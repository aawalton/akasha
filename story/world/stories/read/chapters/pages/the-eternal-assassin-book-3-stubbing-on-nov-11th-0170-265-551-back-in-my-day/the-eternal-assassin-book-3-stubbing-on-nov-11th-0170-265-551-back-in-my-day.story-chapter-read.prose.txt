Dismissing the notifications, Asher activated Comprehend’s second-tier effect before the stranger’s head could even hit the ground. As the man’s shocked eyes stared at him, Asher glanced around, doing his best to take in the room despite his inability to turn his head.

Beyond the stone slab Smiles’ head and now body were lying on, the room was lined with shelves and storage containers that looked as though they held any number of random body parts. Asher saw large, hairy legs, jars of different-colored eyes, frail, elderly hands, and body parts he didn’t even recognize. He felt as though he’d stumbled into a serial killer’s showroom, and even as desensitized to dead bodies as he was from all his time working as a mortician’s apprentice, he had to shiver at the sight.

Or he would have, if his body hadn’t been all but frozen in time.

Past all the flesh, bones, and organs, there was even a shimmering pair of blue wings strung up on the far wall, somehow still in two solid pieces despite the fact that they appeared to be made of pure tritessence. The displayed wings confirmed his suspicions as to who had come to speak with Smiles, and as time sped back up and the freshly severed head hit the ground, Asher stared down at it.

“Weaver. I have to admit, I was hoping for your boss.”

“See! There! Just like that!” Smiles called down from the slab, laughing at Weaver’s startled expression. “It’s weird, right? Not being able to move the rest of your body!”

“You severed our very link?” Weaver asked, staring at his crumpled body and motionless tails that now lie splayed across the floor. “No skills exists that would allow something like that! Only an extremely advanced wizard can mess with tritessence directly!”

“Perhaps you’re not as smart as you thought,” Asher said, feeling something warm run down his face. Blinking, he touched his upper lip and grimaced as his hand came away with fresh blood coming from his nose. He didn’t have to worry about what was causing it either, as his own hand was practically shriveling and growing cankerous right before his eyes.

Damn, I’ve been in the center of the death aura for all of ten seconds. This is no joke.

“Sorry, I don’t have time to chat,” he said, pointing his dagger directly at Weaver’s head. “Where’s the King of Death?”

“Why would I tell you?” Weaver scoffed. “I do not feel pain, and the King will simply create me anew if you are even capable of ‘killing’ me. I hope you enjoy having a slow and painful death, mortal. I will use your entrails to construct ten new masterpieces to replace those that you slaughtered to get here! You will—”

“I think I know where he is!” Smiles said, speaking over Weaver and grinning at the other undead’s look of shock.

“Smiles! After all these years, you would betray our King? The very man who created you? Made you what you are?!”

Asher thought it a tad strange how shocked Weaver seemed by this sudden declaration. He’d only spent a couple of hours with the annoying undead, and as far as he could tell, Smiles seemed like the type to do whatever he wanted at any given moment. Chaotic stupid, if you will.

“If it seems fun, sure!” Smiles laughed. “It’s like a new kind of game! Let’s see if we can take out the King before he horribly murders you! The King has too many people on his team, so it seems only fair that I’m on the other side. It’ll be more fun this way!”

“This is not a game, Smiles!” Weaver shouted, his face straining as if attempting to brute-force his body to respond to his whim, despite having been severed from it. To Asher’s shock, the skin and muscle of Weaver’s face began peeling off one strip after another, swirling around his bare and bloodied skull like some sort of bizarre, undead jellyfish as the many pieces pressed against the ground and shakily forced his head to rise inch by inch. “Not that I think this pathetic mortal could even scratch our King, but the fact that he is even willing to try is appalling! We must rise up and eradicate—”

“Sorry, currently dying, no time for this,” Asher said, wiping the blood from his nose on the back of his hand before elongating Crescent and slicing clean through Weaver’s transforming skull.

“Wow, you guys are really good for leveling,” Asher muttered, turning to stare at Smiles as the two halves of Weaver’s head splattered to the ground, dead for good this time around. “Are you being serious? You’ll help me find the King of Death?”

“Sure, why not?” Smiles grinned, looking excited. “It’s like we’re going on a magic quest to destroy an evil ring, only the evil ring is actually an evil lich! There’s even a wizard involved!”

“Smiles… are you seriously just helping me because you enjoyed The Lord of the Rings?”

“Maybe! Now hurry up! Before you drop dead, grab my head and let’s go!”

Shaking his own head at the ridiculousness of his temporary ally, Asher did just that. After a moment’s hesitation, he also grabbed everything on the stone slab and shoved it into his Personal Rift. He was tempted to run around and see if there was anything else worth taking, but like Smiles said, every second wasted was another one closer to his rapidly approaching death. He’d Recall back to Samantha if absolutely necessary, but he didn’t want to risk it if he didn’t have to.

Unlike the Servants, it sounded as if Weaver was the King of Death’s right-hand, undead man. Asher could only hope killing him hadn’t triggered any sort of special defenses, but Weaver hadn’t really left him with any alternative. He’d planned to just leave the head lying there while he searched for the lich, but that was before discovering Weaver could do whatever the hell he’d been doing with his flesh in his final moments.

Cautiously peering through the door with Astral Dip, his last encounter with a greater demon still fresh in his mind, Asher confirmed the stone hallway was empty before slipping into it. The one downside to bringing Smiles with him was that he couldn’t phase through doors and walls without shoving the head into his Personal Rift for a second, but that was more of a minor annoyance than anything.

Beyond the horrifying murder room that Weaver had been planning to fix Smiles in, the rest of the King of Death’s workshop was fairly plain.

For a psychopath.

Tapestries of woven sinew and muscle dotted the hallway, depicting horrifying scenes of zombie hordes overwhelming innocent villages and devouring their inhabitants. Carved sconces of what could only be bone held brilliant, blood-red gemstones that cast crimson hues across the workshop. There were even occasional glass cases displaying twisted humanoid skeletons that had to belong to either other races Asher had never seen before, or were failed creations that the King of Death wished to immortalize. All in all, it was without question the most messed up building Asher had ever been in.

“Left will bring you to the corpse room and eventually outside, right will bring you farther into the workshop,” Smiles explained, actually speaking in a normal voice for once rather than his usual excited yell. “You’re going to want to avoid messing with any of the skeletons in those glass cases. I think they’re just sleeping at the moment.”

“Are there others I need to worry about beyond Weaver? And the sleeping skeletons, I suppose?” Asher asked as he started down the hall, trusting in his assassin class to keep his movements silent. He was quite tempted to try equipping his new class to see just what it did, but infiltrating the lair of one of the most dangerous people on the continent was not the best time to mess around. He faintly recalled Samantha mentioning the gravekeeper class ages ago, back when he’d first come to this world and had been trying to figure out what he wanted to do with his life. When he’d mentioned working with dead bodies, she’d spoken briefly about the gravekeeper class, mentioning it was dangerous as they often had to deal with the undead.

Certainly explains why I got it at the very least.

“No, not here in the actual workshop,” Smiles said, instructing him to turn left as they hit a bend in the hallway. “The King of Death and Weaver are always creating crazy new Servants and releasing them into the Deadlands, but most don’t pan out. Normally they end up falling apart or getting destroyed within a few years.”

“Wait, Weaver said you hadn’t been back for repairs in almost a century. Hell, he said you were older than he was. How old are you?”

“Eh, I stopped counting a while back,” Smiles admitted. “Any idea how old the Deadlands are? A little older than that. Right turn up here, at the tapestry that shows the King of Death standing over the smoking ruins of some city that spurned him. Impressive detail, that one.”

“What?!” Asher hissed, stopping dead in his tracks and lifting Smiles up to eye level so he could stare at him. He could feel a throbbing headache coming on as his brain probably liquified under the Death Aura, but he ignored it for a moment. “That’s like… fifteen hundred years old! Are you serious?”

“Really? Huh, time flies when you’re having fun!”

“Smiles… are you different from the other Servants? I don’t know, some sort of progenitor for the other undead monsters? You’re definitely messed up in the head, but you were much stronger than they were, that much was obvious.”

“No clue! I mean, the King of Death did used to call us the twelve Plagues. Each of us unique, created as the first of our kind, given the sole task of guarding the Deadlands and killing all who approached, that sort of thing. But over the years the other eleven Plagues died for real one by one, until only I was left. It felt sort of silly to just have the one Plague, so these days I like to think of myself as just one of the Servants.”

Asher continued staring at Smiles’ grinning face, only the bursting of a blood vessel in his eye snapping him out of his stupor.

“You don’t think, at any point, that might have been important to know?” he asked, following Smiles’ directions as he kept running, taking a right at the tapestry that someone should probably set fire to. Other than all the horrible decorations and the supposedly ‘sleeping’ skeletons, it was almost eerie how silent and empty the maze of tunnels was. Without Smiles’ help, it probably would have taken him ages to make his way through the workshop.

“You never asked!” Smiles argued.

“Because you told me you were a Servant!” Asher paused, doubling over and clutching at his heart as the muscle stopped beating for a dangerous few seconds. Gritting his teeth, he gasped and pounded at his chest until his heart finally fluttered back into a flurry of motion, though it definitely felt weaker than before. Coughing, he shook his head. “Woah. Alright, time out. I need to Recall back to the others and recover from the Death Aura. You stay here, I’ll be right back.”

Focusing on Samantha’s mark, Asher triggered Recall. The world began to shift around him like usual, before it suddenly froze, snapping back to normal as though he’d never used his skill at all. Staring in horror at the bare stone walls of the hallway, Asher tried Recalling again, only for his skill to fail a second time. It wasn’t quite the same as how the greater demons locked him down, it felt more like someone was undoing his skill before it could complete rather than preventing it in the first place. But regardless of the difference, the end result was the same.

He was trapped.
