"Are you okay, Junior Brother?" Ling Yinran asked, patting Ning on the shoulder. He had been staring blankly at the field without moving. "You've been staring at the same spot for the past few minutes."

Hearing her voice, Ning slowly looked up and spoke.

"I was merely pondering the inevitability of the universe. To gain something is to lose something. In the end, all things return to nature itself, and someday, even the world will cease to exist."

"Hm?" Ling Yinran studied him carefully. "Did something happen after your match? I only caught a glance, but you seemed to be completely dominating him. Was there some hidden danger I missed?"

"There was nothing like that."

Ning shed a single tear.

The tragic incident played out in his mind once more.

He had lost Courtyard Sixty-Nine.

It was said that numerology was closely connected to fate. If that was true, then perhaps the creation of the Vitality Pill had also been cosmically tied to that courtyard's legendary number. A place of innovation for questionable pharmaceutical achievements.

Gone now. Replaced by Courtyard Sixty-Six.

Okay, okay.

He was spiralling.

But despite this devastating loss, he would press forward. The universe took, yes, but the universe also gave. The road to culture was long, but he would not flinch at setbacks.

Ning took a breath and clenched his fist.

Ling Yinran stared at him.

In the span of roughly three breaths, his expression had cycled through what appeared to be genuine grief, quiet philosophical reflection, reluctant acceptance, and finally something approaching renewed purpose.

"So," she said carefully. "Have you sorted everything out?"

"Yup." Ning nodded.

Hearing that, Ling Yinran just shrugged.

For anyone else, witnessing such strangeness from someone close to them would have been worrying. But at this point, Ling Yinran was already used to it, not only from her aunt, but from her junior brother as well.

Maybe the condition to enter the Spiritual Flower Peak as a disciple should simply be weirdness? Ling Yinran seriously considered it for a moment.

"So, are you going to watch my fights, or will you check out the Heavenly Origin Tower?" Ling Yinran asked. "Since your ranking is quite low, you probably won't get challenged. As for me, I'll probably get a lot more challengers now that I beat Shen Wei."

Every change in the top twenty rankings of a peak drew attention, so Ling Yinran was certain that some disciples would come for her, figuring she was an easier target than Shen Wei.

As for the Heavenly Origin Tower, since this was the week celebrating the sect's establishment, how could there not be special events?

Climbing the Heavenly Origin Tower was one such event.

The Heavenly Origin Tower was the Nascent Soul artifact of the late Patriarch Li Changsou. During this event, any inner sect disciple could challenge the tower, and those who cleared certain floors would receive rewards.

Ning shook his head.

"I can check out the Heavenly Origin Tower later. Today, I'm here to cheer for my dear senior sister. So, unless you force me to go, I'll be right here providing moral support."

In truth, Ning just wanted to relax and watch fights.

He was taking a break, and spending that time cheering for his senior sister sounded perfectly fine. On the side, he could also train his spiritual control using his threads.

Hearing Ning's answer, Ling Yinran tilted her head slightly, then smiled.

"Since my junior brother wants to support me so much, how could I disappoint him? Watch closely as I kick some ass."

"Um."

Fatty Li walked toward the Heavenly Origin Tower alongside the endless stream of disciples flooding into the inner sect.

The streets were unusually crowded today. Outer sect disciples had been temporarily granted access to certain parts of the inner sect during the Heavenly Origin Tower event, and countless people had rushed over immediately. Many even planned to stay for the full seven days, cultivating nonstop to take advantage of the denser spiritual energy within the inner sect grounds.

After all, even breathing here felt smoother. Spiritual qi drifted naturally through the air, far richer than what the outer sect offered, and opportunities like this were rare for most lower-ranked disciples.

Of course, most people weren't here purely to cultivate.

They were here to watch.

The Heavenly Origin Tower challenge was one of the Pure Qi Sect's most important yearly events. Qi Condensation disciples weren't the only ones participating; even Foundation Establishment elders competed for rankings within the tower.

After all, having Foundation Establishment cultivators fight each other on ordinary training grounds would be disastrous. Instead, their rankings were largely determined by how high they could climb within the tower, making the event one of the primary ways to determine their standing and treatment within the sect.

Fatty Li himself wasn't particularly interested in the rankings, though.

With his now towering physique, he moved through the crowd surprisingly easily. His broad shoulders and muscular frame naturally opened a path as disciples instinctively stepped aside.

Eventually, he found the person he was looking for.

Near the tower, several open viewing fields had been set up with large projection screens floating above the crowd through the use of Projection Stones. Each displayed a different floor of the Heavenly Origin Tower, allowing the disciples outside to watch the challenges taking place within.

Fatty Li spotted Ning standing before one of them.

On the screen, a Foundation Establishment elder was charging through a massive horde of puppet soldiers. He raised two fingers, and several crescent-shaped blades of compressed wind immediately formed around him before tearing forward.

BOOM!

The wind blades cleaved through rows of puppets, sending broken limbs and shattered metal scattering across the floor. Yet despite the overwhelming display, the elder's expression remained serious.

Those puppets were merely cannon fodder.

The real challenge would come later.

"Hm. Cleaving Wind Art," Ning commented while watching the projection thoughtfully. "Foundation Establishment wind spell. Pretty strong offensive power."

"Not just strong," Zhang Feng snorted beside him. "It seems quite versatile as well."

He punched Ning lightly on the shoulder.

"Still, if it were me, I'd rather stab things directly than cast spells. Going head-to-head against multiple enemies in close combat would be much more thrilling."

"One of man's great romances, surely," Ning said solemnly.

"I know."

"There you are, Brother Ning."

Fatty Li finally approached the two.

Ning turned. "Oh, Fatty Li. You made it."

Zhang Feng looked over curiously, so Ning gestured between the two.

"Zhang Feng, this is Fatty Li. He's a very skilled spiritual chef." Ning then pointed toward Zhang Feng. "Fatty Li, this is Zhang Feng. He helped train my spear arts and is also from the Mutual Aid Association."

"Oh!" Zhang Feng immediately grinned and clasped Fatty Li's forearm enthusiastically. "Any friend of Ning's is a friend of mine!"

Fatty Li laughed. "Same here. Just call me Fatty Li."

Zhang Feng blinked before openly looking him up and down.

"...Fatty?"

Honestly, the current Fatty Li looked more like someone capable of strangling a demonic bear barehanded. His previous chubby appearance was almost completely gone, replaced by thick muscles packed beneath his robes.

Fatty Li coughed awkwardly.

"Well... this is temporary."

"Temporary?"

"It's because of my physique," Fatty Li explained helplessly. "Normally, I'm actually quite chubby."

"I see." Zhang Feng nodded seriously.

Bizarre physiques were common enough in the cultivation world that he didn't question it further. There were people born with dragon bones, phoenix blood, and even stranger constitutions. Compared to those, temporarily becoming extremely muscular was hardly worth mentioning.

"So," Ning asked, "what did you want to talk about? Please tell me it involves spirit stones."

Fatty Li immediately grinned.

"You're right."

He handed Ning a storage pouch.

"This is the profit from the last three days. One thousand seven hundred spirit stones total."

Even Ning raised his eyebrows at that figure.

"The hot dogs and the other recipes you gave us have completely exploded in popularity," Fatty Li continued proudly. "Especially with all the outer sect disciples visiting the inner sect. You can expect the profits to increase even more over the next few days."

Fatty Li was genuinely impressed by how successful the recipes had become.

Ning had always possessed a strange perspective when it came to food, and now that he had learned some spiritual cooking techniques, his recipes could finally be considered proper spiritual dishes. By incorporating spiritual meat and simple cooking techniques into otherwise ordinary recipes, their value had risen considerably.

More importantly, they were strangely well-suited for mass production.

Ning took the pouch and smiled.

"Thank you, Fatty Li. Honestly, getting even five percent of the stall's profits is already amazing. I thought I'd get one percent at best."

"It's what you deserve," Fatty Li replied immediately.

In truth, there was something he hadn't told Ning.

Fatty Li hadn't taken his full share of the profits. Instead, he had quietly redirected a large portion of it toward Ning without ever mentioning it.

The reason was simple.

Gratitude.

Fatty Li didn't have many genuine friends. Cultivators talked about brotherhood and loyalty all the time, but most relationships within the sect were ultimately built around benefits or status. When you were weak, people ignored you. When you were poor, they mocked you. When you lacked talent or backing, people found it much easier to walk all over you.

Fatty Li understood that better than most.

Xiao Fan had been one of the few people to treat him sincerely from the very beginning, long before strength or opportunity had changed anything.

And now, Ning had become another.

Perhaps Ning himself didn't think much of what he'd done. Helping Fatty Li awaken the Emperor Tyrant Spiritual Body might have simply been an interesting theory worth testing to him.

For Fatty Li, however, that moment had changed everything.

Before awakening the Emperor Tyrant Spiritual Body, his future had been painfully ordinary. At best, he would have remained another outer sect disciple scraping together resources and hoping to someday stumble into Foundation Establishment through sheer luck.

That was the reality for most cultivators.

Now, not only had he gained the recognition of a late-stage Foundation Establishment master, but for the first time in his life, Fatty Li could actually see a future beyond merely surviving in the sect.

That kind of favor couldn't easily be repaid.

It was also why Fatty Li had used his master's influence to secure such favorable business terms for Ning. Otherwise, there was no way an unaffiliated disciple would have received a contract like this from a spiritual food stall operating during the Heavenly Origin Tower event.

Ning, meanwhile, remained completely unaware of the emotional weight behind any of this.

"So," he asked while lightly weighing the storage pouch in his hand, "how's your progress with the Food Qi Nourishment Method?"

Fatty Li's eyes immediately brightened.

"It's incredible. Seriously, this technique fits my physique almost perfectly."

He patted his chest.

"My Emperor Tyrant Spiritual Body already absorbs and refines the essence from whatever I eat to strengthen my physique, but before this, a lot of that spiritual essence was still being wasted during digestion. Now it's completely different."

Fatty Li clenched his fist.

"Before, eating a pound of spiritual beast meat might only let me absorb thirty or forty percent of its useful essence. With the Food Qi Nourishment Method, I can actively guide more of it through my organs and meridians while digesting."

He looked down at his hand, clearly pleased.

"I can actually feel myself getting stronger every day."

Then his expression softened.

"Seriously, Brother Ning. Thank you."

Ning waved his hand as though it were nothing.

"You should thank Xiao Fan more than me. He's the one who found the technique in the first place."

His gaze then drifted toward the distant Scripture Pavilion.

"Anyway, you two enjoy the tournament. I'm finally going to buy my archery technique."

Zhang Feng blinked.

"...You're leaving already?"

"Just for a moment. I'll be back before you know it."

Before either of them could respond, Ning disappeared into the crowd.

Zhang Feng watched him leave before letting out a helpless laugh. "That guy seriously never rests, huh? And here I thought I was obsessed with martial arts."

"That's just how Brother Ning is." Fatty Li smiled faintly before his expression gradually became solemn. "Honestly... it's exactly what you'd expect from a man who walks the Path of the Grind."

Zhang Feng blinked.

"...The what?"

Fatty Li immediately straightened his back and clasped his hands behind him like an ancient grandmaster preparing to reveal a forbidden scripture.

"The Path of the Grind," he repeated gravely. "A supreme philosophy centered around relentless self-improvement through continuous accumulation."

Zhang Feng raised an eyebrow. "Oh? That doesn't sound bad."

"Exactly." Fatty Li nodded approvingly. "As expected of Brother Ning's friend. You grasped it quickly."

He raised one finger.

"The grind isn't merely training. It's waking up weaker than your enemy, getting beaten in the morning, practicing at noon, collapsing in the evening, and then getting back up at night because if you're going to get beaten again tomorrow, you should at least lose three percent less badly."

Zhang Feng's eyebrows rose. "Hah! Now that I understand. If you're going to lose anyway, you might as well make sure the bastard has a harder time beating you next time."

"Exactly!" Fatty Li pointed at him excitedly. "That's the mindset!"

Zhang Feng grinned. He had initially thought this whole Path of the Grind thing was another one of Ning's strange ideas, but the more Fatty Li explained it, the more reasonable it sounded.

Fatty Li continued with renewed enthusiasm. "Some people want shortcuts. Some people are born talented enough that they barely need them. But a grinder only cares about one thing."

"Progress!" Zhang Feng answered immediately.

Fatty Li froze.

Then his face lit up.

"Brother Zhang, you truly understand!"

"Of course!" Zhang Feng slapped his fist into his palm. "What's the point of training if you're not better than you were yesterday?"

Fatty Li nodded rapidly. This was going far better than expected.

"Exactly! Fail a technique nine hundred and ninety-nine times, then fail the thousandth time with slightly better posture. See a genius advance three realms overnight and don't despair. Just think, 'Good. So it can be done.'"

"Hahaha! I like that!" Zhang Feng laughed. "If someone is ahead of you, chase them. If you can't catch them today, train and try again tomorrow!"

"Yes!"

Fatty Li was starting to get excited himself.

"The Path of the Grind doesn't care whether heaven favors you, whether your spiritual root is superior, or whether you were born with some ridiculous physique. At the end of every day, it asks only one thing."

Zhang Feng leaned forward eagerly. "And that is?"

Fatty Li clenched his fist.

"Did you do your reps today?"

Zhang Feng went silent.

For several seconds, he seemed to genuinely contemplate those words before suddenly clenching his own fist.

"Brother Li."

"Yes?"

"I think I've been walking the Path of the Grind this entire time without realizing it."

Fatty Li stared at him.

Then he grabbed Zhang Feng firmly by both shoulders.

"Brother Zhang!"

"Brother Li!"

The two looked at each other with the unmistakable excitement of men who had just discovered that they shared the same Dao.

Fatty Li had expected to explain Ning's philosophy.

He hadn't expected to find a fellow grinder.

"Remember this well, Brother Zhang. Talent is good. A powerful background is even better. But if you don't have those things, what are you supposed to do? Sit around and cry about it?"

"Of course not!" Zhang Feng declared. "You train until your arms stop moving, rest until they work again, and then keep going!"

Fatty Li's eyes practically shone.

"Exactly! You grind!"

"Hahaha! Good! I like this Path of the Grind!"

Fatty Li nodded vigorously.

He had been spreading this philosophy among his fellow disciples for quite a while now and had even managed to convert a few of them, but Zhang Feng might very well be his most promising convert yet.

There wasn't any deeper scheme behind it. Fatty Li simply wanted to give others the same kind of push Ning had once given him.

I'm sure Brother Ning will be very pleased when he finds out.

With that pleasant thought, Fatty Li pulled his newly discovered fellow grinder aside and continued explaining the profound mysteries of the Path of the Grind.

…

Ning, completely unaware of the strange missionary work Fatty Li had undertaken in his name, swiftly made his way to the Scripture Pavilion and finally purchased the manual for the Lightning Cry Arrow Technique, accomplishing his original goal.

The only unusual thing during the entire process was Elder Shen, or more specifically, just how much more of a bum the old man somehow looked today.

Usually, there would at least be disciples coming and going around the pavilion, creating the illusion that Elder Shen was some mysterious hidden expert guarding a repository of ancient scriptures. Today, however, with most disciples having rushed toward the Heavenly Origin Tower event, the old man had completely given up pretending. He was lying sideways across a wooden chair in the sunlight, one sandal half-fallen off his foot as he lazily fanned himself with what looked suspiciously like a low-grade technique manual.

Ning respectfully handed over his contribution token and technique request.

Elder Shen barely opened one eye. "Lightning Cry Arrow..." the old man muttered before glancing briefly at Ning. "Archery? Finally decided to buy a proper martial art after window-shopping for so long?"

"Elder, I was merely considering my choices."

Ning may or may not have annoyed the elder with his repeated questions about various martial arts. It wasn't his fault, okay? People really needed to learn how to write proper introductions with clear advantages and disadvantages.

"You certainly did." Elder Shen slowly sat up, though sat up was perhaps too generous. It was closer to a boneless shift from one lazy position to another.

He took the contribution token, swept his spiritual sense over it, then waved toward the pavilion shelves. A thin jade slip flew out from the inner section and landed in his palm.

Instead of handing it over immediately, Elder Shen glanced at Ning again. "So, was your sudden decision because of the Heavenly Origin Tower?"

"Yes. I wanted to prepare for anything. Having another option for long-range attacks wouldn't hurt."

The old man snorted softly. "At least your head works. Most brats are too busy polishing their boots and imagining themselves shocking the sect. Young people are too carefree these days."

Ning accepted this wisdom in silence.

Elder Shen tossed the jade slip lightly in his hand. "The Heavenly Origin Tower only opens once every five years. For disciples like you who have never entered before, the first five floors are the real opportunity. Once you've challenged them, the tower won't treat you the same way again."

Ning's attention immediately sharpened. Although he had already heard some information about the tower, anything coming from an elder of the Scripture Pavilion was naturally worth listening to.

Elder Shen noticed his interest and yawned. "Foundation Establishment cultivators and disciples who have entered before usually begin from the sixth floor onward. The first five floors are reserved for fresh Qi Condensation disciples. They're both a test and a blessing left behind for the younger generation."

He finally tossed the jade slip over, and Ning caught it carefully.

"A test and a blessing?"

"Mm." Elder Shen leaned back into his chair and resumed fanning himself with the unfortunate manual. "Perform well enough and you may obtain an inheritance. A spell, a technique, a secret art, sometimes even something stranger. But the tower isn't a charity hall. Reaching the third floor is usually the minimum before anything decent starts appearing."

Ning stored that information away. If the third floor was where decent rewards started appearing, then the first two floors were probably some form of screening.

Elder Shen's half-closed eye opened slightly. "And don't think combat is the only thing that matters. The tower tests many things."

"Like?" Ning prompted.

"That's for you to figure out." Elder Shen chuckled when he saw the young disciple's aggrieved expression. Maybe this would teach the brat not to leave his novels on a cliffhanger every damn time.

"Thank you, Elder." Ning still cupped his hands politely.

Now all he needed to do was grind the technique until he could actually use it. The Heavenly Origin Tower was a trial ground, and there was no telling what kind of enemies, formations, or strange restrictions might appear inside. Having another ranged method, especially one with the speed and destructive power of lightning, would only give him more ways to respond.

And Ning liked having options.

Elder Shen watched him for a moment before suddenly asking, "You're at the sixth stage now, right?"

"Yes."

"Good timing."

Ning raised his head. "Good timing?"

"Your master didn't tell you?"

"She did, but she didn't elaborate much. She only said that having lower cultivation could actually be advantageous when entering the tower."

"Then she did tell you." Elder Shen clicked his tongue. "That woman is lazy, but she's not careless."

Ning wisely chose not to comment on his master's laziness.

Elder Shen continued, "The first five floors don't simply reward whoever has the highest cultivation. If they did, every clan brat would delay entering until the ninth stage of Qi Condensation, cover themselves in spirit artifacts, and stroll inside expecting to sweep everything."

The old man pointed at Ning with the manual he had been using as a fan. "The tower judges you according to your cultivation. Your performance is weighed against the realm you're actually in. A sloppy eighth-stage disciple may receive a worse evaluation than a refined sixth-stage disciple."

Ning's gaze sharpened. That was the important part.

A strong cultivator at a lower stage could receive a better evaluation than someone at a higher stage with an unstable foundation. Entering later wasn't automatically better; what mattered was how capable you were relative to your cultivation.

And right now, Ning was only at the sixth stage.

So was Xiao Fan.

They had the lowest cultivation among the inner sect disciples, yet both of their foundations were exceptionally solid for their respective stages. This was a very favorable situation to be in.

But was it a coincidence?

Ning doubted it.

Ling Xin had specifically mentioned that entering at a lower cultivation could be advantageous. Xiao Fan, meanwhile... well, he was Xiao Fan.

Still, now armed with slightly more information, Ning cupped his hands. "This disciple will remember Elder Shen's guidance."

"Good. Now go away. You're blocking my sunlight."

Ning glanced at the sunlight falling freely across the completely empty pavilion courtyard, then back at Elder Shen. He wasn't blocking anything, but arguing with an old man who could probably slap him through a wall wasn't part of his preparation plan.

So Ning respectfully cupped his hands, bowed, and left.

As he descended the pavilion steps, he glanced toward the distant Heavenly Origin Tower. "I can only hope this is one of those arcs where the protagonist beats up a bunch of arrogant young masters and shocks the entire sect."

After considering the matter for a moment, Ning decided there was only one important requirement.

Preferably, he would be standing somewhere safe and watching when it happened.
