“The Paladin of Hyperion, Sir Roderick Vayne, has fallen, Your Grace, as have all six of his Squires,” Isolde reported while standing at parade rest across the table from her father, Duke Rasmus Argent, as he watched her in silence. “After a careful investigation, we found evidence that Priestess Sara Lightspeaker likely survived the explosion and may have been taken by the perpetrators from the chapel prior to our arrival. It is our working theory that she was taken as a prisoner and transported to…” Isolde hesitated by reflex and then forged onward. “To the Necropolis.”

The duke laid down the report he had been reading, written by Isolde’s own hand, beside the map laid out of Blackstone between them, his brown eyes narrowing in thought as he considered her words. He had received Isolde, Hector, Lamonte, Kalaria, and Tricia—all of whom had been brought in at Isolde’s recommendation—the moment they’d returned from the chapel, still smelling faintly of old death and smoke.

Lamonte stood nearest to the door, his expression grim, while Hector had settled against a wall with old comfort and familiarity, having served with the duke when he had been the Wardens’ Captain prior to Isolde’s fallen predecessor. Kalaria and Tricia stood near Isolde herself, both women wearing their own grim expressions at the implications their investigation had revealed.

“The Priestess may be alive,” the duke repeated intently. “But you cannot confirm it?”

“I cannot, Your Grace,” Isolde admitted with a grimace. “Kalaria found indications that one of the Squires in the chapel, a man named Gareth, may have shielded the Priestess when the building detonated. His body had been moved by an external force after death, and there was an empty, relatively clean section of floor large enough for the Priestess to have rested on when we examined the site.”

“Someone took her, Your Grace,” Kalaria added immediately when the duke glanced at the tracker. “The debris was very intentionally disturbed, and the marks in the settled ash showed clear signs of movement. I can’t promise she was alive, my lord, but they carried her out on a wheeled litter or something equivalent. Of that I am certain.”

Rasmus’s expression darkened at her words, and he turned back to Isolde. “You mentioned a Vampire in your report, Captain. Are you certain this was the work of such a creature?”

“Certainty with any of this is impossible, Your Grace,” Isolde answered as duty compelled her to, “but the evidence is strongly in favor of that interpretation. Sir Roderick’s body was exsanguinated when we found it, and there were clear signs of feeding on his neck when we examined it. It also wielded some kind of bladed weapon, likely a sword, and some manner of unknown blood sorcery. Whatever its precise nature is, we are definitely dealing with something Vampirically-adjacent at the minimum, and not merely a feral bloodling that strayed too far from the Dark Forest.”

The duke regarded her levelly after she finished, then turned to Hector. “Hector?”

“They have the right of it, Rasmus,” Hector replied with old familiarity, scowling back at the duke, though his expression was aimed at Isolde’s father. The pair were old comrades, and Hector was one of the few men alive capable of using the duke’s name without titles when he thought it prudent. “This ain’t some little leech we can scare off with a few torches and foul words, neither. It went after a paladin in his own bloody sanctuary. Whatever else this bastard is, old friend, it’s bloody strong.”

“You’re very confident about this, Hector, without even seeing the creature,” the duke observed.

“I saw the Paladin’s corpse, Your Grace,” Hector replied, somehow managing to make the title sound like a nickname more than a marker of strict respect. “I’m no bloody loremaster, but I reckon anything that can drain a fuckin’ Paladin like a midday snack should be bloody powerful.”

Isolde winced at the language internally, but her father showed no outward displeasure, instead turning his gaze back toward her as she straightened just slightly on instinct.

“Proceed with the rest of your report, Captain,” the duke instructed.

Isolde nodded and turned to Tricia, who produced a cloth-wrapped aetherglass instrument from her pouch and set it carefully on the desk. “Sir Roderick was repeatedly healed during the fight against the unknown assailant, Your Grace,” the Warden explained after she stepped back and indicated the aetherglass. “His armor and injuries show multiple signs of it. The assailant, or creature, or whatever it was survived long enough to separate from him from that support despite extreme force on Sir Roderick’s part. Its accomplice appears to have been fighting inside the chapel simultaneously while the first was occupied outside.”

The duke reached out to pick up the aetherglass and examine it, his gaze narrowing as he regarded the dark markers within the gem before he looked back at Kalaria. “Do you know how many accomplices it had, Warden?”

“Only one that I could reliably place at the site, Your Grace,” she answered immediately. “An archer who also wielded bladed weapons, likely long knives or daggers. The two Squires in the housing complex were killed before they could properly defend themselves, and the wounds carried the same magical residue as the energy found near Sir Roderick’s corpse.”

“Magic I cannot yet identify,” Tricia added with mild frustration. “I would rather admit that failing now, Your Grace, than have someone expect a potion or healing spell to mend an injury that defies its workings.”

Isolde watched her father absorb the information in silence as he looked back at the aetherglass, then set it carefully back on the desk. She stepped forward when he did and reached out, picked up the separate checkpoint report, and set it down for him, at which point he read it in silence. Of all the things Isolde and her Wardens had learned, that report was the one that held the most clues—and the most damning report about their city’s security failings.

“Guardswoman Bonnie Gray borrowed a Warden’s name—a Warden missing and presumed dead—and used her authority to allow the suspected killers to evade scrutiny on their way to the Necropolis,” the duke summarized after he finished reading. “That is not a comforting revelation, Captain.”

“It is not, Your Grace,” Isolde agreed grimly while swallowing down the echo of anger the memory inspired. “Even ignoring the implications of our security failings, of which there are several, it also highlights a coordinated retreat plan. Bonnie Gray is not a woman anyone would mark as disloyal, Your Grace. We’ve conducted preliminary interviews with members of the Watch who knew her well, and we’ve all worked with her before. She’s not highborn by any stretch, but she is loyal. We suspect she may have been compelled, or worse, enthralled.”

“Suspect, or know?” the duke asked again carefully.

“Suspect only, my lord,” Lamonte answered. “But it fits the theory too well to dismiss. Bonnie Gray has a near-flawless service record, rose through the Watch through loyal service, and while she was never spectacular, she was no traitor. Enthrallment is the most plausible explanation for what she did, and when combined with the exsanguination and blood sorcery, well…”

“It paints a picture,” Rasmus finished with understanding, leaning back in his chair with a grim expression. “Which means that we have to assume we are dealing with a Vampire, and prepare accordingly, instead of trusting in the rarity of such an occurrence. What manner of creature would you presume it to be?”

“An Elder or Master,” Isolde answered without hesitation. “A fledgling would have been obliterated by Sir Roderick, and any normal leech would have died as well. This has to be something far older and more cunning.”

“Then we must be ready to capture Miss Gray and see what she can tell us,” the duke said while looking from Isolde to her Wardens. “And what of the citadel itself? I take it your report on the oddities around its change are pertinent.”

“They are, Your Grace,” Isolde confirmed while trying not to think about who had last vanished within it. Earnest eyes and a handsome grin flashed through her mind, and Isolde’s hands tightened at the base of her spine.

Are you still alive, Romulus? What did you unleash in that forsaken place?

“Aye, it’s bloody changed,” Hector agreed. “That Light-forsaken itch at the back of m’neck has been gone for the best part of a day now. Not all at once, mind, but it’s a damn sight better’n it has been. The nightmares have ended, too. Had my first good kip in a while.”

“And my instruments have been decidedly less disturbed, Your Grace,” Tricia added. “Whatever happened when the Traveler entered the Necropolis, the forbidding pressure it exuded has eased considerably, and appears to still be reducing. The citizens have already been seen acting far more carefree and smiling more. There are even some Taverns open now, instead of closing at sunset.”

Which does nothing to answer what that fool got himself into. What happened to you?

“What of the Traveler himself?” the duke asked, as if reading Isolde’s thoughts, and startling her for a moment, though she managed to conceal the reaction.

“No word as of yet,” Isolde replied dutifully, her lips turning down at the corners. “He entered the Necropolis, and we’ve received no word since. If he’d died, we’d likely know given the nature of how they reconstitute at Shrines, but it’s not outside the scope of reason that he may be captured or worse inside the Necropolis.”

“It’s a plausible result,” Lamonte added, speaking up at last. “Travelers are rare, Your Grace. It wouldn’t be outside the norm for a Vampire to find great interest in capturing him and seeing what his blood could offer.”

Her father considered that in silence, leaning back in his chair as he glanced at the window facing toward the citadel. “He was very confident in himself when I gave him the task.”

“He was also new to Eternus,” Isolde replied, more sharply than she fully intended. “Confidence does not tell a man which doors to leave undisturbed, Your Grace. We could offer him little to no real guidance, and there’s every chance he unleashed something in those halls by accident.”

Rasmus turned back to her when she finished, and Isolde firmed her lips into a line, already reprimanding herself for her outburst. Romulus had been important as an irreplaceable font of knowledge. His extra-worldly information could have given them unprecedented insights into Blackstone’s future.

“Your concern is noted, Captain,” her father said after a moment, his brown eyes intent. “Though I still believe in his confidence, if nothing else. Every legend agrees that death remains a temporary inconvenience for his kind.”

“To a certain extent, Your Grace,” Tricia added, and Isolde turned to her while the woman glanced between the captain and the duke. “Travelers can die permanently; it just takes some doing. Their reconstitutions are not unlimited.”

“And returning from death is not the same as escaping captivity,” Isolde added after she was done. “Nor do we know which Shrine he would reappear at, Your Grace, nor how long it would take. The stories vary wildly, and all they agree upon in essence is that they do return. That doesn’t mean he can just escape captivity at will.”

“And you believe he is worth recovering, captain?” the duke asked her directly.

“I do, Your Grace,” Isolde answered without hesitation. “When I spoke to Romulus, he revealed things about the System that were unprecedented. He interacts with it in ways we consider exceptional, and he thought them commonplace. It issues him quests, tasks, and objectives normally reserved for Powers.” Her expression hardened, and she worked to keep her own feelings separate. “He has knowledge we cannot imagine, and an interest in making a place for himself here, besides. Losing the opportunity to learn from him would be a considerable loss for Blackstone.”

It was a truthful and defensible answer, and she saw that it had reached her father, even if it was not strictly the entire truth. More than just the loss of his knowledge troubled her. She remembered his sincere delight when she’d given him the provisions, the selfless gratitude he’d offered afterward, and his unerring sense of duty, even if it were buried beneath sarcasm and teasing.

Romulus had been infuriating, inappropriate, overly familiar, and too flirtatious by half.

He had also looked at a portrait of Isolde and her parents and seen a young woman proud to become a soldier, instead of another presumptuous noble daughter, as others had labeled her. She had offered him far too little patience compared to what that sincerity had deserved, and she was determined to correct that instinctive reaction, if only to honor the kindness of his words.

You still owe me an explanation about your ridiculous request, too, Romulus.

“You make a compelling point,” the duke said while she watched him and worked to keep her thoughts from her face. “I see no reason to let him languish in the clutches of a leech if we can be recovered, but we cannot send another handful of people inside and hope they will find success where others have failed. I want a proper proposal.”

Isolde nodded, having expected the request, and stepped forward to indicate the map on her father’s desk with her armored right forefinger. “I have considered the next step. With the Vampire having only one confirmed accomplice and no evidence of Necromancy, I believe a surgical strike force to be sufficient. It will let us move fast, hard, and without the issues associated with rousing the entire Ducal force. I will take twenty Wardens and thirty of the Watch with me in command. That gives us enough to hold the line, and more than enough people over level sixty to breach the Citadel. The creature struggled against Roderick; the battle is evidence of that.”

“So you believe that such a force should be sufficient,” the duke surmised. “I am inclined to agree, and it prevents us from begging General Carstaire for his garrison’s aid to clean up a mess while looking inept in the process. Very well, but why not more Wardens?”

“Because the city does not become less dependent on our forces for law and order when we enter the Necropolis, Your Grace,” Isolde explained firmly. “If we lean on the Royal Garrison, we effectively announce our incapability, as you said. I would rather solve this with our own forces and minimal civic disruption caused by emptying the barracks.”

“Plus,” Lamonte added, “it means we can keep enough forces outside to watch for more potential servants of the Vampire prowling around, Your Grace.”

Rasmus nodded in understanding, and gestured for Isolde to proceed.

“If we select the correct thirty from the Watch, we will have a disproportionately disciplined force—men and women who have stood together against threats from the Dark Forest and guarded against criminal gangs and other troubles.”

“A compelling argument,” the duke conceded. “And the Wardens?”

“My best,” Isolde replied without hesitation. “The four here with me, plus sixteen more, selected from among the best and highest-level. It will make the venture more likely to succeed, and reduce any potential casualties—plus, we will have the discipline required for a rapid retreat if it is required.”

“And equipment?” the duke asked.

“Silver, Your Grace,” Tricia answered after Isolde glanced at her. “Silver blades, arrows, quarrels, chains, potions for each person, and sanctified oils and holy water from our stores—the supply of those in the Sanctuary was too tarnished by the explosion to be reliable.”

“Silver collars and armor bands, too,” Hector grunted. “Give the leech a rude bloody surprise.”

“Also acceptable,” Rasmus agreed, then rose from his chair. “And I, too, shall accompany you.”

Isolde felt her heart seize in her chest at her father’s words, and then immediately spoke before discipline could restrain her.

“No.”

The duke raised an eyebrow. “No?”

“No,” Isolde repeated, realizing her error and pressing on. “We do not know the true extent of the threat, Your Grace. We have no reliable map of the interior, and the creature already killed a Paladin. It is one thing for my Wardens and the Watch to hunt it; it is entirely another for the reigning duke to enter a citadel from which nobody has ever emerged.”

“You are my heiress,” Rasmus pointed out steadily. “Does that same reservation not imply your own lack of participation?”

“I am also your Warden Captain,” Isolde said firmly. “My duty compels my attendance, as yours would have when you were your father’s heir, Your Grace. It is one thing to risk me—it is entirely another to risk the duke. Remain here and let us do the work, Your Grace,” Isolde urged quietly. “If the expedition fails, whether or not I make it out, the city will need its duke to marshal a second hunt, and the General is far more likely to heed you if the need becomes dire.”

Please don’t make me divide my attention between finding our people and keeping you alive, father.

“I sent the Traveler into that fortress, Isolde,” the duke said after a long moment of silence, straightening as he did, and revealing the war-trained body and broad musculature beneath his ducal attire. “A Sanctuary in my city has been destroyed, a Priestess is missing and presumed imprisoned by a Vampire, and the people will soon wake to discover that whatever terrified their forebears may have been released from its confines. You have your duty, Captain, but mine does not end where yours begins.”

The Wardens straightened at his words, and Isolde felt her heart rate increase in frustration.

“No, Captain,” her father continued. “I will not remain safely behind these walls while my Wardens hunt the threat that may have been unleashed and may have plagued this city since before my great-grandfather settled it. I will attend with you, and we will liberate the Priestess, Traveler, and Blackstone itself from this awakened scourge.”

“Father…” Isolde said, trying a different tactic. “Please, I cannot protect you at the same time as—”

“I have heard your objection, captain,” Rasmus interrupted in a stern voice. “I will not ask you to pretend I am just another blade, but I will remind you that I am a capable one. I will remain at the center of the formation; I will not intercede in battle unless required, and I will defer to your field authority while retaining overall command. The matter, Isolde, is settled.”

Isolde felt her jaw lock stubbornly and then paused when Hector set a hand on her shoulder, turning to the veteran Warden.

“I’ve seen that look on his face afore, Cap,” Hector said with a grim smile. “He means it, lass. No point arguin’ now. Let’s figure out how we all make it back instead, eh?”

Isolde took a breath at Hector’s words, closed her eyes for a moment, and then opened them to regard her father. “Very well, Your Grace,” she said finally, each word dragged from her. “As you said: you remain in the center, you avoid engagement without dire need, and you defer to my field authority.”

“As agreed,” the duke concurred.

“When do we go?” Lamonte asked, seemingly eager to get past the ducal family argument.

“As soon as we can muster the forces and provision ourselves,” Isolde said. “We need to open the armory and—”

“We should wait until tenth bell,” the duke interrupted, stepping around the desk and retrieving a key from the wall. “I can open the House reserves of silver and summon the smiths. It will take hours to properly equip the Wardens alone, captain, and haste will only work against our efforts.”

“I am aware, Your Grace,” Isolde said in a steely voice, “but every minute we wait is another one in which Sara and Romulus are—”

“I am aware of your friendship with the Priestess, Isolde. I am also aware of the Traveler’s value, but charging into the Necropolis while night holds sway only aids the creature. If nothing else, daylight will force it to remain within the citadel’s interior, and taking time to properly attire our soldiers will save lives.”

Isolde suppressed a growl of frustration and instead inclined her head, accepting the logic even if her heart railed against waiting. Despite her reservations, it was sound strategy.

“In the interim,” the duke said as he moved toward the door. “Tell me everything you have learned, from the beginning. If we are to hunt a leech, then I want to hunt it properly.”

“Just like old times…” Hector said with dark amusement, while he, Isolde, and the Wardens followed the duke out of the office, and the city bells rang distantly to signal the second hour of morning.

Eight hours, she thought as she followed her father and worked to suppress her frustration at the justified delay. Just hold on ten more hours, Sara, Romulus. We’re coming.
