Chapter V.XXII (5.22) - The Wager

“Do any of you care to take a wager on the victor of today’s race?” Vincent asked them.

The griffins were lined up. Less than a minute before the race commenced.

“Yes. The female black one with a splash of red on the back left leg and overo splotches across its neck,” Ione said instantly.

Unlike the rest of the box, which had been listening in on Aoi and Vincent’s discussion of politics and ethics, Kizu’s date’s attention had been locked onto the griffins flying by ever since they sat down.

Vincent leaned back and examined Ione, eyebrows raised. “That’s Daybreak. You have a good eye. Who are you? A breeder in Hon? Perhaps a member of the Umazo family?”

“Kajima family.”

“The… prison wardens? Fascinating.”

“Ione is the top summoner in the academy,” Kizu clarified. “She knows everything about animals.”

Ione laughed. “If only.”

“Well, regardless of whether or not your knowledge is all-knowing, how about we see how Daybreak performs?” Vincent clasped his hands together, elbows on either side of his armchair. “I’ll admit you’ve stolen my first choice. But my first choice doesn’t win every race. You weigh your odds of success high?”

“Depends on which one you are betting on,” Ione answered.

“Hmm.” Vincent eyed the lined up griffins. “Before I make my selection, do the rest of you wish to choose your champions first? I promise not to claim any griffin you choose.”

Kizu stared down at the marvelous creatures lined up in a row in their starting positions. Their eagle-like heads turned to stare at one another while their clawed lion’s feet stomped the ground impatiently.

“What’s on the line?” Basil asked. “Money?”

“A tad mundane for my tastes,” Vincent admitted. “How about this, a favor for a favor? If my chosen champion wins, you all must show me around your campus. I have been attempting to get a decent tour of it for years now but Gizrim has delayed hosting me repeatedly with weak excuses.”

“Deal!” Basil said quickly. “And if one of us wins, you have to give us a griffin!”

Kizu’s head nearly fell from his shoulders as it spun in the shapeshifter’s direction. Before he could voice a word, Vincent accepted the proposal.

“I do not want a griffin!” Kizu declared. He already had to deal with a monkey, capybara, and a Dragon. There were enough random animals on the island without another magical creature linked to him.

“Then you’d better not win,” Basil said. “I choose the chestnut colored one with the black beak.”

“And I pick the pure white one in the center,” Aoi chimed in. “The one with the pink haired rider.

Kizu quickly scanned the griffins, trying to find the one least fit to participate. He was under no illusion that if any of his friends in this booth managed to win, he would be the one responsible for the creature. But while he couldn’t control their choices, at the very least he could minimize the odds of his own success.

“The one with the scraggly rider two from the furthest left.” All of the griffins looked the same to him, but at least that rider looked disheveled with tussled hair and a crumpled riding outfit. In fact, Kizu suspected the man might be drunk based on how he stumbled over to his mount.

“Interesting choices from you all.” Vincent nodded. “I will pick Thunderclap, a fan favorite. He has the yellow streaks on his wings.”

Mere seconds after his proclamation, an announcer sounded around them counting down the start of the race.

“Three…two…one! And they’re off! Look at them go! Evert and Kingslayer take a swift lead, nosing ahead of the competition! Ronald and Swiftheart just dove, a bold gamble to take the underside strategy in effort to make headway! Gina and Thunderclap are shoving their way forward as well! Ohh! Look at that spellwork, dastardly! Daybreak might be in trouble!”

Kizu only half paid attention to the commentary, his eyes fixed on his chosen griffin and rider. The man’s head bobbed as his griffin kept pace with the back half of the flocks. Not quite last place, like Kizu had hoped for, but far enough back to elevate Kizu’s worries.

Ione’s chosen griffin was struck by three different hexes. While the creature floundered in the air, her rider rubbed the griffin’s neck, leaned in close, and whispered to it. The hexes were removed via some counterspell and they flew on, gaining on Swiftheart, Basil’s choice.

The lead had been taken by Thunderclap, Kingslayer, and Ione’s chosen griffin, Daybreak. Kizu was taking comfort in the fact that Aoi’s choice was even further behind than Kizu’s. But, as he scanned the back few stragglers, he realized with horror that his griffin was no longer among those in last place. Instead, it had pushed its way forward, weaving through spells and attacks from enemy griffins with ease. Never once did its rider send a retaliation attack. Likely because the rider seemed to have fallen asleep.

“Ah, Star, the riderless griffin,” Vincent said, noticing where Kizu was staring. “We decided to try something new this race with Star, I see the fruits of the experiment are paying off.”

“What did you….” Kizu forgot to end the sentence as the griffin stopped mid-flight, dropped, darted up from below, and knocked Swiftheart’s rider off. The man howled as he fell from his saddle but the man’s griffin dove after him and scooped him out of the air before the human made contact with the dirt below. It cost the duo precious moments, but they weren’t yet out of the race entirely.

“Star realized he wasn’t properly buckled in,” Vincent commented. “Sloppy rookie mistake on the rider’s part. But to answer your unasked question, Star is a very honorary griffin and I recently noticed that whenever I put a strong willed rider on his back, he’d retaliate out of spite. So as an experiment, I instead grabbed Carlton, the most drunken and useless rider I knew, and stuck him on Star for this race. Someone with a will so weak that Star could trample over it with ease.”

Kizu’s eye twitched. He hadn’t even bothered to look at the griffin during his selection, focused entirely on the rider. The rider who was strapped in securely and dozing on Star’s back while the griffin twisted in the air and kicked the side of an opponent, propelling himself forward even faster.

“That’s so smart,” Ione muttered, not taking her eyes off the race. “I thought his body language too proud and stubborn to act as a decent mount. I had no idea.”

“Like I said, it was an experiment,” Vincent said. “I wouldn’t have bet on Star, should he have been available. Thunderclap and Daybreak are both better contenders.”

“What about Swift—” Basil started to say but was cut off as Daybreak’s rider created a wall of water and slammed it into Swiftheart. The water expanded into a cube around the griffin and they struggled to swim their way free. Their competition split and flew around the blockade and, in a matter of seconds, Swiftheart went from a top contender, to last in the pack alongside Aoi’s choice.

“Swiftheart’s rider is borderline incompetent as well,” Vincent said. “Ronald refuses to devote time to divination techniques which leaves him blind and exposed for attacks. If he was one of my riders, I would have booted him from my ranks, no matter how talented he might be at stunt riding. Thankfully, he’s the son of a rich noble and Swiftheart is one of the few griffins not under my ownership. So instead he serves as an easy competitor.”

Their loge swiveled under them to continue letting them view the race without needing to strain their necks. A few of the stragglers became harder to keep sight of, but the main contenders remained in the center of their vision.

Everything else faded from Kizu’s vision as he watched Daybreak and Thunderclap’s riders exchange magical blows. Daybreak’s blocked with a bubble of antimagic around them and then popped their own bubble with a return fire of earthen attacks. Thunderclap narrowly dodged with a shift in the air but the griffin behind wasn’t so lucky, catching the attack and finding itself weighed down by the weight of earthen manacles.

With the action on Daybreak and Thunderclap, Kizu lost sight of Star. He leaned forward in his seat and peered back at the back of the flock, desperately hoping that maybe Star had fallen behind.

“Star has taken the lead!” the announcer boomed. “An unprecedented turn of events! The griffin known for being too stubborn to get out the gate is now leading the flock!”

Kizu’s head snapped back to the front. How had he not seen the griffin up there? Star had  a lead of several meters at this point.

“Magic,” Ione whispered. She gripped his arm. Her hand shook in excitement as Star dodged out of the way of an icy missile then disappeared from sight. A camouflage illusion spell. And with Star’s rider passed out on his back, that meant it was either an innate magical ability or some piece of enchanted gear. And Kizu saw nothing that matched that description on the griffin when he scanned him with a flared spellsense.

“We do breed for more than just speed.” Vincent took a sip of tea, looking rather smug.

“Why are you happy?” Kizu asked. “Thunderclap is behind her now.”

Vincent raised a finger. “Hold that thought.” He watched intently as the griffins approached the gate marking the end of the race. Star reappeared, illusion evaporating as he bolted through and the announcer went berserk alongside the roar of the crowd.

Kizu’s heart sank at the sight. Suddenly, the responsibility of a griffin had just fallen onto his lap. He somehow doubted any of his peers in the booth had near enough accountability to care for the creature and he doubted any of them would let him sneak out of this deal. Basil couldn’t even be relied on for the most basic help on their class projects. Kizu was already assisting Ione care for her pet capybara, Dog. And Aoi was a necromancer who might just let it die on ‘accident’ to get ahold of its body. Maybe he was being harsh and assuming the worst of the three of them, but this wasn’t exactly a small responsibility they’d just dumped on him.

Vincent let out a deep breath, a massive smile on his face. “There are approximately a bit over a thousand people watching this race. Do you know how many people bet on Star winning this race today?”

“I don’t know, a few?” Kizu replied dully.

“Zero. Star has never won a race before today. Usually, he comes in dead last. Sometimes not even completing the race at all. This is a major upset. And that means all bets go to the house.”

Basil sat up straight and whistled. “That’s a windfall.”

“Percisely!” Vincent beamed at the changeling. “And I’ll tell you what, after you pick out a griffin, I’ll give you an additional gift if you agree to still show me around your campus later this semester.”

“Deal!” Basil said joyfully.

Kizu barely held back a whimper at the thought of an additional ‘gift.’
