Chapter 54: Bedrock Spire

It took twenty minutes to roll into the station.

Twenty minutes was a long time to see the big city coming, and to realize just how small you were in comparison. All cities were big, but Ambrose had never seen a city so vertical and wide. The closer they got, the more he understood why Stonskarr capitulated without much of a fight. If the Empire could build a metropolis like this in just a few short decades, a monstrous wonder in the heart of a subterranean world, then what else could they do?

The station took up an entire level as trains from each village stopped in. Ambrose led the way as they piled out of the crowded carriage and onto a surprisingly busy platform. As soon as they stopped moving, people of a myriad of races came forward to help unload the tons or ore and lockboxes full of cards, also found deep in the mines.

They got out of the way and closer to a line of benches and shops manned by a mix of humans and kobolds. The goat-lizard-like creatures came in a variety of vibrant colors that put Dexi’Lynn’s bronze to shame, and were animated as they traded food and drink in exchange for stamps in little books people carried.

The way Ambrose understood it, hard currency wasn’t as much of a thing with the Hermanic Empire. It was more about who you were, who you knew, what resources you had access to, and what you could leverage over others. It usually involved stamps for people working under someone bigger who would foot the bill. Individual worlds would always have their own money, but cards, naturally, were a universal coin.

Ambrose breathed in the air, and regretted it. Bedrock Spire had this sharp, iron aroma, with a healthy dose of sulfur and sweat underneath. Frankly, it smelled like a salty fart.

But the people here didn’t seem to notice, and might even have cards to help protect against the rank odor. Jessica held her hand over her mouth and looked as green as he felt. Raeva’s nose wrinkled, but it was nothing compared to Raeleq’s complete lack of reaction. Of course he was tougher than the rest of them.

“Yeah, breathe in that good stank,” purred Dexi’Lynn, wafting the air up with her little hands. “You get used to it after a while. Or you run away and never come back.”

It was clear what Jessica would choose, if she could. The rest took a shuddering collective breath and made peace with it. They found a spot between kiosks, and huddled together for some semblance of privacy.

“A’wright, we’re here,” said Mulch, looking around one more time. “The combat cards’ll be in the Hermanic Quarter, and the Levi-Box in the engineering block. They’re at two diff’rent spots in the city, so who’s teamin’ up with who?”

Jessica raised her free hand, while continuing to cover her mouth. “Me and Dexi will go after the Levi-Box. It’s the only thing that makes sense.”

Ambrose nodded. To him, that had been obvious, but he supposed they needed to say it out loud. “And I’ll go with Mulch to get some combat cards. I’ve got the most experience haggling and getting shopkeepers to let their guards down.”

“I’ll go with you,” said Raeva, without some of her earlier haughtiness. “Between the two of us, they won’t stand a chance. Besides, I don’t trust you to not keep the best cards for yourself.”

“That’s reasonable,” he said with an approving nod.

Luthor sighed and said, “And I suppose I will come along to make sure you two don’t start any unnecessary fights. Which means Jack will be coming with us. Raeleq?”

“Of course,” their gentle giant said. He shifted closer to Jessica and put his hand on her shoulder. “I’ll come along as muscle for the two tiny ones. Nothing will happen while I’m around.”

She looked like she wanted to argue or complain, but Dexi’Lynn was all for it. “Oh hell yeah. We get the big, good-lookin’ beast on our side. Can he walk in front?”

None of them quite knew what to say to that.

With the decision made, all that remained was breaking apart into their two groups, and getting it done. But everyone lingered, no one quite sure what to say. With the danger involved and the delicate nature of their mission, it was too obvious to say ‘good luck’, or ‘be careful’. And they seemed insufficient, anyway.

This was the first time since they’d rebelled and fled that they were going to be apart for any real length of time. Sleeping didn’t count, nor did the feast, where everyone could easily see each other and there was no danger to be found. This was enemy territory, and one wrong move would be the end of all of them.

“Well, don’t fuck it up, I guess,” said Ambrose, unable to suppress his wicked grin.

Luthor sighed, which made Raeva laugh, Jessica giggle, and then they all came apart. It was exactly what they needed, and Ambrose patted himself on the back a little. They parted without another word.

Dexi’Lynn motioned for them to follow her and headed off in one direction, towards a great staircase splitting this area of the station in two. They went downwards, and disappeared from sight after a few seconds, leaving the bigger group alone.

“Okay,” said Mulch, “so, the Hermanic Quarter. Technically, people like me ain’t allowed in there with the rest of you humans and other Imperial races. But if we all stick together, project enough attitude…”

“Leave it to me, Mulch,” said Ambrose in his best sleazy, overconfident voice. “And if you’re a good little guide, we’ll even tip well at the end.”

The moleman’s nose twitched in irritation. “Yeah, we’ll be fine.”

Mulch led them up the stairs, and it was a slower go than Ambrose expected. Their group of five took up one half of the steps, and as powerful as the molekin was, he didn’t move especially fast. Raeva ended up skipping ahead to the next level and waiting for them, and Ambrose was tempted to do the same, but he kept their guide in front of him.

The next level up were largely offices meant for the people overseeing the businesses above and below, and places to buy and sell ritual and utility cards.

“You can get all kinds of cards on Stonskarr, but we’re mostly known for some decent elemental manipulation cards and rituals,” said Mulch, panting and wiping sweat from his brow with the back of his claw. “And creatures. There's a niche market for those tough enough to group up to take on critters and bind them to cards without any combat cards of their own. Me, I always thought that was too dangerous without some good friends at your side.”

He looked over his shoulder pointedly, and Ambrose understood. He and his friends, Mychele and Roy, did that together. They had to get resources somehow, but after seeing him webbed up and useless, the idea of him succeeding was kind of funny.

“So there aren’t as many combat cards?” Raeva asked, looking at a sign that promised travel cards inside.

“There are, but they’re restricted, and harder to find. They’re the ones that are up real high, and real low. There’s probably a trove or two of cards in the Forlorn Pit. You gotta check it out next time you’re down there.”

“After we figure out how to deal with the ghouls,” said Luthor.

They continued up to the next level, and this one immediately made Ambrose’s ass clench. This level was where the guards and guard stations were. They’d no sooner reached the landing when a jail loomed over them, manned by a pair of humorless looking bruisers. There was no telling how strong they actually were, compared to trained wizards, but chances are they weren’t helpless. The entire level looked to be dedicated to the city’s security, making it the least appealing place around.

People moved on, heedless of the presence of law enforcement, and even Raeva had to elbow him in the ribs to keep him moving. “C’mon, Amby-lamb, don’t look more guilty than you already do. You don’t want to sabotage this for us, right?”

Luthor outright chuckled, until Jack spoke up. “And here I thought you were all worried about me,” he said, sounding bitter.

Ambrose looked at the kid, and favored him with a dark smile. “We are. Stay close, and keep your mouth shut. Be a good boy, and maybe we’ll stop for candy.”

“Ambrose…” Raeva started. Jack scowled and looked away.

“Anyway,” said Mulch, “the next two levels up are the Hermanic Quarter. After that is the Overseer’s office, and a bunch of places for high ranking guests and dignitaries and shit. I’ve never been up there, but Roy’s done a flyby a few times without getting seen. Stay close.”

He didn’t have to tell them twice. Keeping an eye on the guard station and the increased number of uniformed men and women, they kept climbing. As they went up, Ambrose tried to count how many uniforms he saw, and lost track after thirty. If there was a fight here, it would not be in their favor.

Luckily, anticipation of their goal won out, and he finally looked upward again. Just as he expected, the beginning of the next staircase was manned to keep the riffraff out. As soon as Mulch got close enough, two large, almost identical bald men with spears crossed them and furrowed their thick unibrows.

“No molekin allowed up here,” one of them said.

“You know better than that. Get back.”

They were bored, with just a bit of dumb, ugly eagerness for violence in their voices. Ambrose knew their type immediately and pushed past the molekin.

“This ugly miscreant is my guide, and we’re already late. I’m tired, sore, and this stupid staircase goes on forever. Get the hell out of our way or I’ll bring Boss Parl down on your asses for obstructing our mission.”

Behind him, Luthor and Raeva stiffened. The guards looked at each other, unsure. Ambrose knew he looked good, intimidating even, in his new spider silk outfit, and he used it to his advantage.

“Come on, I haven’t got all day. Do not test me, we’re running on a tight time-table!” He snapped his fingers at the two. Doing so made him want to kick his own ass, and pray that the guards didn’t feel the same.

The guard on the left snorted in irritation. “Apologies, sir. Keep your molekin close, and do not let him be alone or he will be removed, guide or not. And if you need directions, we can -- “

“If I need directions, that’s what the stinking mole is for,” said Ambrose, motioning with his hand for them to get on with it.

The guard’s mouth snapped shut. Glaring daggers, he nodded and stepped back to the side, bringing his spear with him.

Mulch scurried up between them, and the rest followed. Ambrose felt their burning anger and desire to do him harm, and for once, he didn’t revel in it. It was a necessary affectation and nothing more.

Once they were safely past and out of earshot, Raeva shoved him. “Ancestors sakes, Ambrose. Did you have to push it like that? What if he’d turned us away?”

He couldn’t help but smile and shrug. “Then I would’ve doubled down and made things worse until they let us through or it turned into a fight. But we’re fine, aren’t we? And look at this place.”

A vibrant, colorful sign proclaimed them finally in the Hermanic Quarter. Beneath it was a sign advertising cards for self defense. Across the way was another, promising creature cards from the most dangerous beasts of Stonskarr. It was mostly humans up here now, though there were a few leonine folks, kobolds, and what looked like a tall, thin human, but blue and with four eyes.

They tried not to stare, but here they were, in Bedrock Spire’s center for commerce and combat.

“Well,” said Ambrose, licking his lips, “where do we start?”
