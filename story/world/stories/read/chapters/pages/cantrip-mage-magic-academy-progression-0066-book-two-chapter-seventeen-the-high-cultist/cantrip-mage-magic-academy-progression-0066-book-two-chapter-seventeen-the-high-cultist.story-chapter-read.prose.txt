Not even the first Spellsword Tactics lesson on First-Day of the next week could dampen Eola’s spirits.

It came close, though.

For the last three days, her courtship with Colin had taken a very interesting turn—and one that irritated her familiar to no end. They still studied together, yes. She was still working diligently on the Great Mage Varin’s endless recollections about either his struggles with magic or how much he’d enjoyed his evening meal. But in between pages, she was also getting very familiar with his lips.

Weighed against that was Eola’s thirtieth lap around the gymnasium. At least she was on running today. Sword-Forms was worse—holding a single position until she felt like her arm was about to fall off. She was pretty sure Lieutenant Librarian Fringe didn’t do any of this to make his students stronger. No, this was torture, pure and simple.

She put her head down and kept running. It was making her stronger. But Fringe had lost a lot of her trust when he’d sent her to battle against the Winterstalk without a full understanding of the beast’s capabilities. They had another patrol at the end of the week, and Roth had been busy digging into the library while Colin quizzed Instructor Clearance on the most likely monsters they’d run into. So far, there hadn’t been a letter, so this would likely be a longer, tougher, and colder Patrol Duty than the last one.

So, when a first-year student ran in, looking almost as out of breath as Eola felt, she didn’t pay him much attention. He waited for a gap in the line of running spellswords and jogged to Lieutenant Librarian Fringe. A paper changed hands, and the boy sucked in a few grateful breaths.

“Lemiene!” Fringe snapped. “Come here. Now.”

She ran over. “Yes, sir?”

“This is for you. You’re excused from the remainder of today’s lesson. Try to get some exercise today to make up for it.” He handed her the letter and glared. “Headmaster Tomlish knows better than to interfere in how I run this class. The stakes are too high. But not everyone knows that.”

Eola read the note, blood going cold.

Miss Eola Lemiene,

Your presence is required immediately in my office for a discussion regarding your magic, your attunement, and your relationship with Y’aer.

Headmaster Tomlish

That was it. Nothing else. But Eola—while out of breath—wasn’t stupid. She knew exactly what this was about. Ordered Magic, spell-chains, and her broken attunement.

As she ducked into the locker room and quickly changed into her robes, she thought about slipping away, visiting her room, and gathering both her sword and armor. But no. Her equipment wouldn’t help her against the High Cultist. Atta might be able to. She still didn’t know what the cat’s true capabilities were. But the stone figurine was already in her pocket.

So, with no better move, she did exactly what the letter said and crossed the campus, climbed the stairs past Instructor Tarik’s office and Madame Reyanna’s, and found herself sitting in a chair outside of a tall oak door as two voices talked in muffled tones on the other side.

Atta hopped up from the floor, curled in her lap, and stared up at her.

“I know, Atta. It’s going to work out.” Eola stroked the cat’s blue fur as she waited. One voice belonged to Headmaster Tomlish, while the other—to Eola’s complete lack of surprise—was High Cultist Andrese’s. But what they were saying was a mystery.

After maybe five minutes of waiting, the door opened, and Headmaster Tomlish waved her in. The two glass windows had been shuttered and curtained against the prophesied storm—one good thing about magic was knowing when a storm was coming, and Varin’s Academy had some very good tea leaf-attuned mages. The globe hung overhead, just as it had before, and Tomlish’s orange snake stared from its place in the rafters.

But the pink chair she’d found so comfortable last time was occupied.

“Miss Eola Andrese. Age nineteen. Your student record shows you as being from Greenarbor. A scholarship case, based on your written work and theory. This year, you registered your attunement with the school. Unattuned Mana.” High Cultist Andrese’s masked face looked up at her. “Explain how that works.”

Eola sighed. She hadn’t had to explain her attunement in a very, very long time. “It’s simple. I have an attunement, the same as anyone else. It’s just that, while your attunement is to scent Mana, and my father’s was to rope Mana, mine’s to unattuned Mana.”

“Ma’am.”

“Sorry. Ma’am.”

The High Cultist scribbled a few notes in a book as Headmaster Tomlish took over. “Miss Lemiene, you are aware of the Cult of Y’aer’s, ah, historical position in Alemia, correct?”

“I know the Governing Houses tolerate them, and most towns have a sanctioned lodge or temple. Greenarbor’s is near the docks, close to the gate and road that lead to Lord Card’s keep. I’ve never been to the one in Varin’s Town, though. As long as you don’t push your faith and hunt for new members, they put up with you.”

“Correct.” The mask betrayed no emotion as the High Cultist took over. “And you know all about my family. After all, you’ve been such good friends with my daughter.”

That wasn’t a question, and Eola didn’t answer it like one. But if this woman thought she was friends with Catrine, she was mad.

“Catrine told my husband and me all about her shambolic mockery of a duel of honor she challenged you to. Not that she didn’t try to embellish her way through, but Lord Andrese has a very…interesting…attunement. Do you know what it is?”

Eola shook her head.

“Truth-attuned Mana.” The woman behind the mask laughed quietly. “There are only five Truth-attuned spells in existence. Tying my fortunes to his guaranteed a life of obscurity for both of my sons when they inherited his attunement. He’s as close to a broken attunement as it’s possible to get while still reaching Journeymage. But when Catrine tried to lie to us about what happened in your little spat, he knew every falsehood before it left her lips.

“So, now I know that you—somehow—figured out our family’s spell, discovered a way to oppose it, and made it reliable enough to get you through the trading of spells. And you did this in only a handful of days.”

Her eyes narrowed. “How?”

Eola turned so her front side faced the fire, the better to warm herself. “I can’t tell you.” She’d promised to keep everything she was doing with Instructor Tarik quiet, after all.

“I thought not. You should know that under the neo-Revisionists, the Cult of Y’aer is becoming a major player in Alemia’s politics—and that House Andrese supports them wholeheartedly. You may not talk now, but you will talk later. Now, your attunement. Unattuned Mana. I don’t believe there are any spells higher than Child’s Magic that use that attunement.”

“No, ma’am.”

The mask came off as the High Cultist stood, and Eola sucked in a small breath. This close, the family resemblance between Catrine and Calliene Andrese was painfully obvious. She had more wrinkles under her eyes, and her nose had a bend halfway down from being broken a long time ago, but aside from that, the younger Lady Andrese could easily have been a simulacrum of the older.

“Then why did you come here?”

“To learn magic,” Eola said earnestly. “I’ve loved magic since I was a girl. The languages, the sketches—they’re all so beautiful.”

“And you know your attunement is broken?”

“I passed all of my classes last term. That doesn’t sound broken to me.”

“You’re not a Journeymage yet. Most second-term students should—

Headmaster Tomlish interrupted. “Most second-term students reach Journeymage by Y’aer’s Day, but wearing the hat and tassel aren’t requirements to graduate from Varin’s Academy. In fact, your husband only made Journeymage midway through his third term, and I don’t believe he ever reached Truemage.”

The High Cultist’s face froze, then shifted slowly into one of barely-concealed anger. The cords in her throat stood out for a few moments before she swallowed them down. Eola wouldn’t have been surprised if she’d started sketching a spell and attacked Headmaster Tomlish right then and there.

Instead, she sat back down, picked up her mask, and carefully placed it over her face. “Miss Eola Lemiene, I have no idea why you’re at this school. But I do know that you and the thirty-six other students at Varin’s Academy with broken attunements are a beautiful thing in the eyes of Y’aer. The Saint of Curses and Blessings has given you this strange gift because they want you to be broken. They need you to be broken. By denying that which they’ve given you, you commit heresy and blasphemy all at once.”

“Enough.”

Eola whirled to face the door as it opened. Instructor Tarik stood framed in it, face full of fury. “Miss Lemiene is a student here at Varin’s Academy as well as my apprentice. She’s earned the right to be here—earned it more than your daughter has, judging by their marks last term—and you have no right to—“

“I have every right to accuse her,” the High Cultist said, but she eyed Instructor Tarik warily. Eola’s eyes dropped to his hands, both hovering inches from the twin wands he carried; he’d come ready for a fight.

“Miss Lemiene, go back to your dorm suite. The headmaster, Lady Andrese, and I have a lot to discuss.”

And, under the combined glares of both Instructor Tarik and High Cultist Andrese, and with her heart pounding, Eola fled.

An hour later, Eola sat on the couch, Colin next to her. She’d let him pull her into an embrace as she stared off into space—couch or not, she needed the comfort.

“So the High Cultist wanted to know about your attunement?” Patrice asked, yawning. “That doesn’t seem so bad, really.”

Roth and Colin both glared at her. Eola shivered a little. “It was. She knows exactly how many students here have broken attunements, and if she hasn’t already interviewed you, it’s only because Instructor Tarik interrupted mine. She accused me of heresy and blasphemy.”

“She can’t do that,” Patrice said smugly.

“She did, though.”

Patrice rolled her eyes. “No, I mean, she absolutely can make an accusation in an informal setting like the headmaster’s office at a magic school, but to accuse you formally, she’d need to convene a tribunal in a temple or lodge, and you’d have to be a member of the Cult of Y’aer. Even the Revisionists don’t try to spread cult membership to anyone who doesn’t seek it out, so you’re safe. She and the rest of the cult are harmless.”

Colin’s glare let up a little as Eola relaxed. Patrice was right. All the High Cultist could do was put pressure on her—and she’d done a good job of it. But pressure wasn’t the same as power. Lady Andrese, in her role as High Cultist, didn’t have any power. Not here.

But Roth’s eyes didn’t un-narrow. That wasn’t unusual, except that he bored into Patrice. “And what if you are a member of the cult?”

“Are you?” Patrice asked.

“No. But you are.”

“How do you…so what if I am? I’m a Clerk, and my parents and I are both proud Revisionists. And I only pay lip service to the Cult anyway,” Patrice spluttered. “The High Cultist won’t make a move against me. I’m the daughter of her family’s subordinate house, for Y’aer’s sake!”

Roth only rolled his eyes. “You’d better watch your back, that’s all.”

“We have bigger problems than whether or not she’ll move against you, Patrice,” Eola murmured. “There are thirty-seven of us who interact poorly with Ordered Magic. Last year, there were only twenty-three. That’s fourteen new students with broken attunements.”

“And you think High Cultist Andrese’s going to do something to mess with them?” Patrice snorted. “She’s a monster, and her husband’s not a lot better, but—“

“She took Nathien away from me,” Roth said quietly. “And she’s up to something in the north, with the silver near Norwall.”

“She’s not going to hurt a bunch of toddlers.”

Eola held up a hand. “Please, can we focus? Remember last year, when Instructor Tarik said something about how Ordered Magic was incomplete? And how the lack of new spells was a symptom of that—or of problems with magic itself, and how magic was going stale? The number of children getting strange attunements that don’t work with Ordered Magic is accelerating. Fourteen new students here just this year—and they’re all six to eight years past the moment their attunements manifested.

“We need to talk to Instructor Tarik about this.”

Colin shook his head. “No. Absolutely not. We need to stop talking about this right now.” He stood up and fidgeted with his robes, and Atta hopped off Eola’s lap and into the warm spot he’d left behind.

“Traitor,” Eola muttered. Atta only stared back, yellow eyes radiating smugness.

“Why should we stop talking about it?” Patrice asked.

Colin only shook his head. “I’m going to need your help. All of you. I should have done this three days ago—I would have if I could’ve—but it has to be perfect. It’s a Second Order ritual. Trust me, it’s important. But I can’t cast it alone.”

Eola nodded and stood, bouncing Atta a little more than necessary as she did. The cat whined a little, and she smirked back at her. “What do we have to do?”

“I’m out,” Patrice said quickly. “You don’t need me to help you with magic. I’ll be in my room.”

“Oookay. I just needed help drawing the symbols. The ritual is called Bounded Boundary. Instructor Tarik taught it to me just after I hit Journeymage. We’ll sketch the rune around the whole suite—through all four of our rooms, the bathroom, and the common room. Just echo it over and over. But it has to be perfect. Every error increases the ritual’s cost, and I don’t have the Mana for more than one or two mistakes. Here’s the symbol.”

He sketched a complex array of Forsooth Script and Old Alemic on a sheet of parchment, then grabbed a second one and drew it again. “Trace it exactly like this. Use charcoal. Chalk and ink won’t work, and it needs to be visible. Charcoal will burn off, anyway.”

Then they got to work.

Eola started in her room, naturally, working the spell along the baseboard in wide loops and flowing curves—just like Colin had drawn it. It didn’t take her long at all to find the four Old Alemic modifying marks, or to identify the run in the middle. It was little more than the Child’s Magic she used to alarm her room against intruders. But there was something more to the spell.

It took her most of her room, sketching the symbol over and over, to figure it out. When she finally linked her final symbol to the one Colin had just drawn on the door frame, she noticed it. It had something to do with the intersections.

She’d noticed the connection points for the first time when she’d worked on understanding the Cutting symbol, but this was different. Where the intersections in First Order spells were simply places for Old Alemic marks and Forsooth Script runes to intersect, these carried more meaning.

“Alright. You go start on Patrice’s room. Make her move any furniture she’s got against the walls. It has to encircle everything,” Colin said. “I’ll finish up out here and get my own room done. Roth can help if he’s finished his.”

By the time Eola worked her way around both Patrice—who grumbled and grunted loudly as she moved her furniture around—and the offending furniture, her hands were covered in charcoal smudges, and so was the front of her robe. She stood, popping her back loudly. “Colin, what exactly will this ritual do?”

“Bounded Boundary is an anti-scrying ritual with a long duration. Months long. Instructor Tarik said it’ll stop any scrying of Second Order or below, and warn us against anything but a Worldshaper—and if anyone’s using Fourth Order magic to scry on us, all our secrets are out of the bag anyway.”

Then he took a breath. “Let’s check it. Then I’ll give it the Mana it needs.”
