Hel vanished into the air, reappearing only when Anadis reached the two cores. He hid behind a bush and pushed some branches aside just enough to peer through.

Deactivating Soul Sight, the two figures came clear: a boy about his age, face hidden under a hooded cowl, and across from him a man barely taller than Anadis but built like a bull. A battle-axe hung in the man’s grip, its edge tilted threateningly toward the boy. His intentions were unmistakable.

The man meant to kill him.

“Well, there’s a sight. A fight between two Blessens.” Hel’s tone dripped with enough condescension to grate on Anadis. Her gaze shifted to him. “This has nothing to do with you, so I hope you aren’t planning to get involved. Let’s leave.”

Anadis glared at Hel from the corner of his eye. She pouted, arms crossed as she hovered next to his face. He would have snapped at her, were it not for the fact that he knew exactly how that would end. That would only turn into an argument he didn’t need right now.

“I need to understand what’s happening before I decide my next move,” he muttered right after shushing her.

Hel glared at him, her face actually turning a shade redder. “How dare you shush me,” she gasped. “I may as well let you die here, you ungrateful vessel. Did you even look at that man’s core?”

Grunting, he nodded. It was the first thing he’d noticed. The boy was a Blessen, and his core was red—pretty good, actually. Red was the best type of Divine core most people could realistically wish for, since yellow and white were so rare as to be almost unheard of. The man’s core, however, was grey. That in itself wasn’t anything special, if not for the two threads writhing from it.

Yep. The man was an Emanant.

But Anadis’s mind was stuck on something else as well.

That scream sounded like a girl’s scream. So where was she?

Taking a closer look at the Blessen, he noticed him clutching his right arm. The boy’s fingers were tightly curled around a wound, blood seeping through them and pooling at his feet.

“Thought you could snoop around my camp and not be found out?” the stocky man spoke suddenly, his voice calm but gravelly. “You thought wrong, boy.” The words sounded like a final verdict, as if he already knew the Blessen wasn’t going to make it out alive.

The boy said nothing. His shoulders trembled as he took two steps back, placing himself between the axe-wielding man and a tree. His cowl billowed with each ragged breath. He narrowed his sharp red eyes, fixing the man with a glare full of defiance.

The man inched closer, closing the distance again. “Now, before I deal with you, why don’t you tell me if you’re alone and just how in the gods’ name you found this place?”

This place? Aren’t we just in the middle of the forest?

Anadis swept his gaze around, searching for any structure, base, or sign of a camp—anything that might resemble a “place.” He found nothing. Nothing obvious anyway. He also couldn’t shake the feeling that he had seen the man before. That long scar around his eye—he was certain he would have remembered it, but he just couldn’t place it. And the clothing… he couldn’t be sure with the tunic over it, but was the man wearing a division uniform?

“Answer me!” The man’s commanding voice snapped Anadis out of his thoughts. “Is anyone else with you, little scoundrel?” A weaselly smile spread across his face. “If you tell me, I promise I won’t hurt them. Just be a good lad and speak.”

No response came from the youth. Instead, he raised a bloody finger and flipped the man off.

The man sighed, then swung his axe. The air hissed in its wake. But the Blessen’s reflexes held true. He ducked, barely evading the fatal blow, and dashed away.

The axe buried itself deep in the tree trunk. The man tried to yank it free with a frustrated grunt.

I have to strike now—he’s distracted. Anadis steadied his breathing and shifted his weight, ready to spring from the bush. If he moved fast enough, he could get behind the man and knock him out before he freed the axe.

Yes. I got this.

But just as he was about to dash forward, the man stopped pulling on the axe. He stepped back, lifted his leg in one fluid motion, and kicked the trunk right below where the axe was lodged. The tree shook violently. Bark cracked and leaves rained down as the man caught the falling axe in one hand.

Anadis dropped back into a squat behind the bush. “This guy is one hundred percent an Emanant,” he muttered under his breath. What should he do? Could he leave to find help? No. By then the Emanant would have slaughtered the Blessen. Anadis clicked his tongue. There was no alternative but to fight.

Right then, Hel popped into his vision. “See? This is too much for you. I understand you want to help that Blessen, but not against an Emanant. You’ll just die the same way you almost did against that Unhallowed.”

Hel was right, and it pained him to admit it. But what could he realistically do against someone of that caliber? His eyes tracked the bleeding Blessen as he barely dodged the Emanant’s swings. The man was clearly toying with him, probably waiting to see if anyone else was with the boy.

“Where are you keeping him?”

Anadis’s brows rose as the red-eyed boy finally spoke. His voice was low and tense, muffled beneath the cowl.

A coughing laugh burst from the man’s throat. “Ah, so you do know about that…” He shook his head in annoyance. “It’s a shame to see a young life end over a few careless words,” he sighed. “You know too much, and I can’t have you running off to talk. If this gets out… let’s just say I’ll have nothing left to lose. So enjoy the scent of the air, because this will be the last breath you draw.”

Even with the cowl hiding most of his face, the painful frown stretching across the Blessen’s lips was visible in the way his red eyes crinkled at the corners. With a bloody hand, he beckoned the man forward. “I’d like to see you try and stop me, porky legs.”

No other words were spoken as the man launched his attack. The daring teenager could only run and evade. It was impressive he managed it at all against an Emanant.

Drawing a steadying breath, Anadis prepared to jump in at the perfect moment. He tore off part of his pajama and wrapped it around his head like a mask—there was no point in showing them his face.

Right then, the boy stumbled. His knees buckled, and his face slammed into the ground.

“Hel, there’s something you’re forgetting,” Anadis muttered, a sneer twisting his lips.

“What?” she replied, scowling at him.

“I survived something worse than an Unhallowed, an Emanant, and even a Luminous. I survived you. So this man means nothing.” Adrenaline flooded his veins. Anadis smacked his own cheeks, then dashed forward with reckless abandon. The red-eyed boy’s gaze flicked toward him for a heartbeat, then widened.

The man’s back was completely exposed. Anadis focused on the two whips of soul threads writhing around his divine core—the longest ones ethereally coiling around the man’s neck.

“What the?!” the burly man yelped as Anadis leaped onto his back, wrapped an arm around his thick neck, and summoned his Curved Sword. “Another one?! I knew you weren’t alone!”

With the Curved Sword in his right hand, Anadis didn’t waste a moment. He aimed straight for the man’s neck. But of course, it wouldn’t end so easily, not against such a foe. The Emanant was far too fast. He seized Anadis by the arm, and the Curved Sword clattered against the pebbles as Anadis was yanked off the man’s back.

He knew he couldn’t overpower an Emanant. So with his left hand he reached for one of the two soul threads channeling the man’s Blessing and despite being yanked himself, he pulled hard on the thread.

A burning frost surged through him, ten times worse than any Grull’s thread. Anadis wanted to let go, but that would mean losing his only chance and his life.

The thread shriveled under his grip as he activated Soul Sever. The next second, Anadis flew across the dense alpine undergrowth and rolled violently through the dry grass until he slammed into a tree. The impact drove bile up his throat, and he blew blood out of his nose. The damn bastard was far too strong for a fresh Blessen like Anadis.

Looking at his left palm, Anadis saw it was swollen and red as if he had dipped it in boiling water. Blisters glistened across the skin, yet he laughed—because without the man’s yank, he wouldn’t have been able to tear the soul thread free.

“W-what did you do to me? I feel dizzy—m-my Blessing, I can’t feel it!” the man shouted, his voice rising in hysteria. He staggered, losing his footing as he clawed desperately at the empty space behind his neck. He didn’t know what was missing, of course. But Anadis knew there was no time to relax. The Emanant would snap out of his stupor once the thread reformed in a few seconds.

“We have to go!” Anadis urged the Blessen. “He won’t stay like that for long!”

The brat gave him only a side glance, no words, no thanks, and then ran. Not away from the Emanant, but straight toward him.

“What are you doing?! Are you stupid?” Anadis shouted, bewildered.

A rapier manifested in the silent Blessen’s hands. Small embers sparked from it like faint fireworks as the blade glowed red-hot, like an incandescent knife.

A fire Blessing?

The boy jumped and stabbed. The rapier sank into the bastard just beside his left eye. But the Emanant reacted instantly, driving a heavy punch toward the hooded boy’s stomach. The boy blocked it with his forearms, yet the force still sent him scrambling past Anadis. Before he could plunge headfirst into a tree, he flipped mid-air, planted his feet against the trunk, and dropped lightly to the ground. A muffled grunt escaped him.

“You little shits! I’ll take my time killing you,” the Emanant declared. His face reddened with rage and the blood pouring from the wound beside his eye.

Crap. What do I do? Anadis grimaced, glancing back at the crazy boy. They should have run when they had the chance instead of attacking him.

“Let’s go!” the fool beside him barked, blood staining the outside of his cowl.

“What?” Anadis’s eyes widened, but he ran anyway. “I don’t know if you’re just stupid—actually, don’t answer that—but this man is an Emanant. There’s no way we’re going to outrun him.”

Red irises that shone like rubies stared at Anadis. “Try to keep up.”

“Oh, come on,” Anadis exhaled sharply and sprinted right behind the boy. This was so stupid. So futile. Why was that guy so confident they could outrun an Emanant?

“Foolish brats! You really think you can outrun me?” The Emanant barreled after them, each heavy, stomp shaking the ground like a minor quake.

Hel flickered into view beside Anadis. “I suppose only I can stop him,” she said, biting her bottom lip.

“Wait—are you going to do the same thing you did with the Luminous?” Anadis asked through their mental link. “You’ll get hurt like before. Don’t do it.”

“We don’t have any other choice.”

Is there really nothing else I can do?

Could he use Soul Sever again? His blistered palm told him otherwise. Even if he tried Soul Drain, it wouldn’t drain the man’s essence nearly enough to matter. Besides, there was no way he could reach the Emanant without getting killed first. The man was faster, stronger, and far more experienced.

Think. Think.

“It should’ve been enough time already,” the boy beside him muttered suddenly, skidding to a stop.

“Why are you stopping?!” Anadis shouted, also stopping with a baffled expression.

Pulling down his cowl just enough to reveal his lips, the boy drew in a breath so vast Anadis felt he might drown in it. In the next heartbeat, he exhaled a rippling veil of heat that shimmered in front of him like air rising from a forge. The temperature spiked. Sweat beaded on Anadis’s forehead. The boy’s knees buckled slightly before he steadied himself. As the mantle of heat haze lingered where he had exhaled, he stepped back.

“If you don’t want to burn, step back,” he advised.

Anadis quickly complied.

So… what will this do?

The Emanant laughed dismissively and skidded to a halt. He cracked his neck with a palm on his chin. “Did you finally realize how futile your attempt was?” he snarled, taking slow, measured steps forward.

“Burn in hell, pig!” the boy snapped his fingers.

The left side of the Emanant’s face erupted in flame exactly where the boy’s rapier had pierced him a moment before. The man wailed as the skin there bubbled and blackened. Stepping forward, he walked into the rippling blanket of hot air that caught fire as soon as he touched it, and then the man’s body was engulfed in the roaring flames.

Staring in awe, Anadis’ jaw went slack. Hel was a little surprised too, flames dancing in her golden eyes.

“Done gawking?” the fire Blessen asked in a hoarse voice. “Let’s go.” He turned to run, as if he hadn’t just started what could become a wildfire. However, right after taking that first step, he fainted.

Anadis’s stupor faded, and his feet carried him to the Blessen’s side before his mind caught up. The boy was panting, sweating, and his right arm had gone pale beneath the crimson blood.

“He’s lost too much blood and must have overexerted himself with his Blessing,” Hel pointed out, blinking into existence beside him in a ribbon of light. “Carry him and let’s leave. What he did won’t delay an Emanant for long.”

“Alright. I’m tired of this damn forest anyway.” Anadis lifted the youth into his arms. As he did, the hooded cowl slipped back, and a cascade of hair spilled free.

Anadis froze.

“A… girl!?”
