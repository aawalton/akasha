Hovering at an altitude of 300 metres was a stone island shaped like a manta ray. Its wingspan stretched a full kilometre—massive and slow-moving as it drifted toward the Ogre Gate.

Most of the island's interior was hollow, serving as a mobile stronghold for the Ogres. Right now, eighty of them stood inside, each gripping smooth, granite-like stone spears.

Strapped to their backs were over twenty more rock spears apiece, secured tightly with a pair of thick stone clamps.

“Hoof!”

“Hurk!”

“Hoof!”

The Ogres slammed the shafts of their spears into the ground in rhythm, creating a deep, thunderous beat that echoed through the stone. Their arms were massive, corded with muscle—strong enough to snap any spine in half without effort.

They carried themselves with a brutal, almost elegant menace. Their bodies were covered in shallow scars, too many to count, the result of endless training and battle. Their skin was tougher than most, hardened through years of punishment.

Most importantly, every single one of them had a Control Factor of 2. This wasn’t just a warband—it was a trained army. And right now, that army was gathered at the mouth of the island, eyes fixed on the Ogre Gate ahead.

“Your Highness! We’re almost at our destination.” One of the Ogres turned around and slammed his spear into the ground with a thunderous thud. All the Ogres wore thin rock guards on their joints—knees, elbows, and ankles.

These guards were delicately crafted from the toughest materials, then embedded directly into their limbs. Ogres stood at an average height of five metres, and bodies that size put immense strain on their joints.

In ideal conditions, an Ogre could live up to 200 years. But by the time they hit their sixties, their knees would start giving out. And by eighty, most were bedridden.

That’s why the stone guards were made—to handle the bulk of the stress on their joints. With them, an Ogre could live out its full lifespan. The material used was a rare substance found near the roots of the World Tree.

Long ago, the Elven King had gifted it to the Ogre King, hoping it would help the Ogre Race live longer, healthier lives. After coming into contact with the material, every Level 2 Ogre and above gained the ability to create more of it, which led to the mass production of the stone guards.

Rulite.

That’s what the Ogre King named it—meaning ‘a divine rock that extended lifespan.’

It had a murky green hue with yellow patterns that looked like Damascus steel. Its antibacterial properties kept the joints in pristine condition, even when worn for life.

It was tough enough to handle an Ogre’s full weight, which was why it didn’t crack when one of them kneeled with force.

In the direction the Ogre faced while kneeling, deep inside the island’s hollow interior, were two parallel rows of stone seats, four on each side. Sitting comfortably on them were Level 2 Ogres, their massive frames settled into the stone like they belonged there.

They all looked alike—siblings. Each one commanded ten Level 1 Ogres.

The oldest sibling, Taruk-Tuk, nearly reached six metres tall. As he shifted his weight, the stone armrest beneath him dented slightly. Beside his seat stood two winged figures that looked vaguely human.

Their torsos were humanoid, but their hips were wider. Feathers covered their legs—brown for the male, pink for the female. Instead of feet, they had bird-like claws with talons sharp enough to scratch steel.

Their arms were wings, each with a five-metre span. The feathers shimmered between the green of fresh grass and the gold of sun-dried hay. Their heads and necks resembled those of owls, able to twist nearly 270 degrees.

The Gravity Race of Harpies.

As adults, they stood between 180 to 220 centimetres tall and weighed around forty to fifty kilograms. Once they reached adulthood, they rarely moved around alone—always in pairs.

Right now, one such pair stood by Taruk-Tuk. From the way they held themselves—heads slightly bowed, bodies angled toward him—it was clear they were his servants. In front of him, on a stone stool, sat a bowl filled with a mix of fruits and meat.

A piece of meat floated up from the bowl and landed straight into Taruk-Tuk’s open mouth. He chewed slowly, eyes focused as he assessed the situation. After a few seconds, he stood up abruptly. “I’ll lead the charge.”

Then he turned and bowed deeply toward an elevated platform at the far end of the hollowed island. “Mother, Father, please grant me permission.”

“Permission granted!” came a deep, rumbling voice.

Coiled atop the platform was a massive, lizard-like creature. From snout to tail, it stretched over twenty metres. Its entire body was covered in scales tougher than refined steel.

Two horns curled menacingly from its head, pointing forward like a trident, perfectly aligned with its gaze. Its body was a gleaming white, while the gaps between its scales shimmered sky blue. The horns and talons were a slightly deeper shade of the same.

The Lightning Race of Wyverns.

The creature lay asleep, its snores loud enough to rattle stone. Its wings were tucked in tight, but if fully spread, they’d span over sixty metres. The Wyvern was curled around two lavishly carved seats—thrones, clearly.

Wyverns ruled the skies and saw themselves as the direct descendants of Bolomere. Their pride soared higher than even the Elves. And yet, this majestic beast behaved like a mere pet to the two figures seated between its coils.

They were slightly larger than Taruk-Tuk in size, but in power, they existed in a different league entirely. These were the ones who made the floating island possible—Level 3 Ogres, known across Gangnea for their love of enslaving other races.

The Ogres were the original enslavers. Humans had learned the art of subjugating sentient races by watching them.

Stone Race of Ogres—Level 3 (Relic)!

Taruk-Tuk’s father, a feared figure known as Tron-Tak, gave a light tap on his armrest. It echoed like thunder. “Permission granted.”

At once, the flock of Harpies harnessed at the top of the island stopped flapping their wings and rested. The island came to a halt exactly a hundred metres from the Ogre Gate.

“Thank you, Father.” Taruk-Tuk stomped the floor with joy, then turned and gave a firm nod to his team of ten Level 1 Ogres, all poised and ready for action.

But just before he could step off the island, Tron-Tak’s voice rang out again. “You can play with the Humans within your line of sight. But the moment you feel the temperature of the entire region spike—retreat here immediately. Otherwise, you’ll be ash before you even know what’s happening.”

“Understood.” Taruk-Tuk thumped the ground, fully aware that the warning was about the Warden.

“We’re doomed!” Nancho gasped, eyes locked on the island as it came to a stop. “They could just rain rocks down on us from there—we wouldn’t stand a chance.”

The officers might survive—Level 2 Humans could fly—but the Level 1s would be wiped out.

Nancho spun around, eyes darting down the ash road that led straight to the World Tree. ‘Still no sign of the Warden?’

‘He’d get here fast the moment he got the message. He should’ve already been here—an hour ago.’ A chilling thought crept into his mind, draining the color from his face. ‘Did something happen to him? No! That’s impossible!’

He shook his head hard, groaning. ‘It’s impossible to assassinate him.’

“What do we do now, Officer Nancho?” Rachad asked. “We can’t face them.”

“Have the regular soldiers retreat into the Lava Moat. They can use its flames to fight,” Nancho replied without pause. “We’ll do the same. Fighting anywhere else is suicide.”

At Nancho’s order, the soldiers moved quickly, taking only the most essential supplies. The rest was abandoned in the Ogre Gate, which Rachad set ablaze as he flew out.

“Two of you, head to the other side and be ready,” one of the officers called out, pointing at two soldiers and motioning toward the Sun Stone embedded on the far side of the bridge. “You’ll need to activate it if we want our soldiers to cross it.”

“Also,” he added after a brief pause, “if it looks like we’re losing—release all the Sun Stone’s heat into the bridge. Sink it.”

The officer stepped up to the Sun Stone on their side of the bridge and activated it. The stone began to absorb all the heat from the structure. The two soldiers sprinted across, reaching the other side. As soon as they were clear, the officer pulled out the Sun Stone on his end.

“Officer Nancho, you’re the strongest among us.” With those words, the officer handed over the Sun Stone. “Please wield its power if the time comes.”

“I will,” Nancho said, taking the Sun Stone and immediately charging it with the flames he produced. He pushed the heat to its limit, packing as much energy into the stone as he could manage. The more heat he fed into it now, the more destructive it would be when unleashed.

Boom!

Just as they finished their preparations, a spiral staircase of stone uncoiled from the island and connected with the fort’s terrace. An Ogre, taller than the others by a full head, stepped out and charged straight toward the edge.

At the same time, a stone column shot out from the fort and crashed into the Ogre Gate with enough force to shatter it.

“HUMANS!” Taruk-Tuk bellowed, his voice echoing with sheer force. “Prepare to be crushed like bugs!”

His roar, followed by the destruction of the Ogre Gate, snapped the soldiers into action. They winced in pain as they dipped their feet into the Lava Moat—standing at its coldest edges—and drew its heat into their bodies.

In unison, they unleashed a wave of flames, surging it toward Taruk-Tuk with everything they had.

So focused were they on the threat ahead, none of them noticed the two soldiers on the other side of the bridge suddenly collapse.

A Pinaka clone approached the fallen soldiers, touched them, and split into two more clones. Each one quickly morphed its form to match the appearance of the two dead Humans. Then, without hesitation, it dragged the corpses and leapt into the Lava Moat, erasing all traces.

The two new clones, now disguised as the original soldiers, had already put on their clothes and gear. They stayed in place—not to activate the Sun Stone, but to make sure the bridge remained steady.

‘Even for them, this is necessary,’ Pinaka thought, watching from a distance. His gaze fixed on the massive flock of Harpies perched across the floating island. ‘Judging from the Sky Boulder’s power, a Level 3 Ogre can create floating stone platforms. But they can only float—on their own, they can't move.’

‘The Harpies are what pull them through the sky. But the heat from the Lava Moat will scorch their feathers, no matter how much they try to shield themselves.’ He narrowed his eyes. ‘That means the bridge is essential if the Ogres want to invade.’

Originally, Pinaka had planned to help the Ogres destroy the Ogre Gate and push into the land inside the Lava Moat. But now that the floating island had shown up, he was sure they could easily get through on their own.

So, he no longer needed to worry about this battle. He could focus on his real objective. ‘Now would be the perfect time to alert the Warden.’

Once the Warden left the Elven Prison, the remaining soldiers wouldn’t stand a chance against him. ‘There are still Level 2 Humans inside who haven’t been mobilised yet.’

And those were the ones Pinaka was really after—because the soldiers who hadn’t been called to the front had just one job: to guard the World Tree Fruits.

That had been Pinaka’s goal all along.
