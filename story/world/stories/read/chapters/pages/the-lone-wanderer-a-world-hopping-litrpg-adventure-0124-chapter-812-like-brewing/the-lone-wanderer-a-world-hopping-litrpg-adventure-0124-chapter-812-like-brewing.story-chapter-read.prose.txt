While Percy and Nephthys were taking great strides forward on Remior, the operation on Mapel wasn’t going very well. The plan had looked good on paper, but life in the small settlement was messy and riddled with variables that Percy couldn’t easily control.

By selling his elixirs at a low price, he had attracted several regular customers to his shop, but also annoyed the local sellers who didn’t appreciate being undercut by a bunch of newcomers.

Percy found the situation somewhat amusing, because there weren’t any trained alchemists in the village. The other merchants were just importing resources from outside, while Percy was the only one who had actually brewed the elixirs himself. At the same time, he could understand the apes’ anger at having their livelihood stolen. Unfortunately for them, their heated interactions only provided him with more opportunities to cleanse people with his beam, so he had no intention of stopping.

‘You’d be thanking me if you knew,’ he kept telling himself.

There were plenty of villagers who refused to buy from Percy’s sentries out of loyalty toward their neighbours, regardless of the financial benefits. Some never shopped for elixirs in the first place, either because they were Red-borns who had already reached Yellow and couldn’t advance further, or because they had family members purchasing resources on their behalf.

Worse still, a subset of infected people almost never left their homes. Perhaps they were mindful of spreading the corruption, or maybe they were just introverts. Either way, Percy appreciated the fact that he didn’t have to pay those residents as much attention, but it also meant that he had to seize any opportunity to cleanse them at all costs, since those opportunities didn’t occur very often.

Another issue that seemed obvious in hindsight was the villagers’ disproportional activity over the course of each day. There was a lot of foot traffic early in the morning when the apes left for work, as well as late in the afternoon when they returned home.

Those brief windows were naturally packed with interactions and arguments, but Percy’s formation could only shine on a single spot at any given time, so he had to prioritize the villagers that would have the greatest long-term impact if cleansed. That typically included the most social or easily agitated apes, who were prone to catching and spreading the corruption.

Annoyingly, those were the same people who were likely to piss the wrong person off and get infected again an hour after getting purified, which created a situation where Percy had cleansed the same fifty-or-so individuals dozens of times while ignoring some who had remained corrupted for weeks without break.

In addition to the daily hassle, Percy had experienced his fair share of unexpected setbacks.

For example, there had been a large festival a couple of weeks back, which had brought even the least social apes out of their houses to celebrate at a handful of local pubs and taverns with everyone else. That night had apparently marked the anniversary of the emergence of Mapel’s first deity or something – Percy still wasn’t sure whether that meant the god’s birthday or the day of their ascension, nor did he know whether the Concept mage in question was still active on the planet.

It didn’t matter.

What did matter, was that ten percent of the healthy residents had managed to get themselves infected during the festivities. It would have probably been twice or thrice as many if Percy hadn’t done his best to protect and cure them.

He’d never been more tempted to reveal his presence and knock a few rowdy bastards out as much as he had that night. Apparently, even people experienced in the horrors of demonic infestations would occasionally forgo their caution and leave it up to the authorities to pick up the pieces later.

As a consequence of both the expected and unexpected mishaps, Percy had spent the first two months actually losing ground to the infestation. The village had started off with only a quarter of its residents corrupted, but had eventually peaked at around a third.

Percy might have managed to compensate for the rare slip ups and bad days if the apes had kept themselves couped up inside the settlement and under his watchful gaze, but there were plenty of hunters, lumberjacks, fishermen, and men of other professions who regularly left the village, only to return after getting infected by some wild animal.

His only consolation was the certainty that the situation would have worsened much faster without him. Mapel’s demigods would eventually swoop in to cure the peasants and reset the village’s condition, but Percy had brought them precious time and reduced the odds of someone getting hurt if they lost control of their ailment before then.

Not allowing his subpar performance to discourage him, Percy had pressed on with the operation and slowly improved on all fronts as a result.

The awkward and clumsy behaviour of his sentries had raised a couple of eyebrows during his first week, but Percy had thankfully ironed those wrinkles out and fully blended into the settlement’s social fabric.

He’d also grown better at keeping the village covered by his sensory domain, predicting arguments, and pointing the formation at them before they even started. He’d even developed a rudimentary understanding of which apes were the most important to keep clean, who they might infect if he let them be, and when his next opportunity to cleanse them was most likely to come.

Percy’s biggest breakthrough had happened about two and a half months in, when he had been struck by a strange realization.

‘This problem… it’s almost like brewing, isn’t it?’

He might be oversimplifying things, but his efforts to cleanse the villagers had brought him back to the nostalgic days of studying the three pillars of alchemy in Orin’s lab.

The entire settlement would be the cauldron in that analogy, while the apes would be equivalent to the precious ingredients that he had to preserve. The corruption was similar to the impurities that he purged from his concoctions, and the giant beam was like the heat and pressure runes engraved on his equipment.

That last part was literally the same, in fact.

Percy’s greatest mistake until then had been trying too hard to understand the natives at an individual level. Studying them was both important and inevitable to an extent, but he would never fully unravel the endless complexity of a person’s mind, even if he had a million times as much processing power. Let alone the minds of an entire village.

Fortunately, alchemists didn’t need to completely understand their ingredients and map out every single reaction occurring inside their cauldron. Through endless repetition, they slowly developed heuristics that allowed them to efficiently eliminate the bubbles and solid lumps plaguing their brews.

Along with that realization came clarity.

Percy stopped trying to predict the apes’ every move and allowed himself to only pay attention to things that mattered to his project. He didn’t need to know where a villager’s foot would step the following second, nor which corner they would turn around as they returned home from a shitty day at work. He only needed to know that the ape was in a nasty mood and hadn’t been getting along with his wife lately, which would likely lead to a massive fight a few minutes later.

At first, Percy’s ocular mutation refused to accommodate his changing perspective. Whether in combat or alchemy, his predictions had been fine-tuned to only peer a couple of seconds into the future as accurately as possible, while his social experiment on Mapel unfolded along a much longer timescale. But his sensory domain slowly adapted to the challenge, eventually producing vague glimpses of more distant events.

Much like his momentary predictions, many of these delayed visions proved false or inaccurate.

Contrary to Percy’s expectations, the butcher’s wife didn’t throw a vase at her husband upon learning that he had gambled away their savings. She angrily stormed out of their house to spend the night at her mother’s.

The young couple living at the northwestern corner of the village wisely chose to postpone their fight until after their kid left the house to play with the neighbours.

However, the core of Percy’s predictions tended to materialize more often than not, giving him many opportunities to plan out the activation of his beam. There had even been times when he had been able to act on his visions to prevent the villagers from meeting at inconvenient moments, thus spreading their fights across a more manageable schedule.

Manipulating the entire settlement admittedly felt a bit creepy, but it did allow Percy to cut the number of the corrupted down to a fifth by the end of his third month. It was a modest result, as well as an indication that he still had his work cut out for him, yet also proof that he was finally progressing in the right direction.

And so, he kept up the good work, getting a little better at every tiny aspect of his operation with each passing day.

Percy’s improvements accelerated further when his main body completed the next round of upgrades. Elevating the passive boost of Supercharged Cognition from forty percent to forty-five didn’t reward him with a notification, because the underlying mindset was still the same. However, his efforts to improve it and create the new mindset required by Nephthys had inspired him to create yet another valuable training tool for himself and Micky.

[Congratulations! You have acquired a new mindset: Automatic Motor Control!]

The Command trait didn’t apply to the Huehuan’s sentries because they weren’t true subordinates – just mindless constructs – but there were other ways to manage them more effectively. Automatic Motor Control trivialized the tedious task of maintaining a sentry’s fake appearance: it stabilized the shape of its body, preserved the optical illusion coating its skin, and replicated the subtle movements that living creatures constantly performed while breathing or shifting idly on their feet.

Percy and Micky still had to understand the organism they were mimicking and put in the effort to prepare each illusion, but the mindset would start taking care of the menial tasks once the sentry was ready.

Afterwards, they wouldn’t have to worry about simple things like how the apes’ loose tails were supposed to flail around, or counting how many times they had blinked that minute. According to Nephthys, a person’s brain already handled small actions like those for their own body, so extending that functionality to the sentries was as simple as identifying the correct settings, locking them in as a temporary subroutine, and assigning it to the appropriate constructs.

Inside the starry sky that was Percy’s mindscape, Automatic Motor Control appeared like a vast lake of midnight-blue stars which represented Micky’s entire pool of spiritual blood by default. Whenever he created a new sentry, the corresponding stars would split off and form a constellation shaped like the creature he was imitating.

Percy had even applied Nephthys’ advice about his spectral traits to this mindset as well. He had identified the silver star representing the Mimicry trait and linked it to the blue constellations to strengthen their relationship.

With two new upgrades under his belt, Percy expected to cleanse the rest of the village within another month or two. Some of the most stubborn apes might keep themselves locked up in their homes and never give him a chance to purify them, but he wasn’t above forcing his way in and punching them in the face once he brought the corruption down to just a handful of people.

Well… fine. If things ever reached that point, he could also just raise the intensity of the beam to a more effective – if somewhat unpleasant and suspicious – level, or he could simply leave the planet entirely. After all, his goal had never been to eliminate the demonic infestation – only to improve his spells and accumulate experience. As bad as he felt about these people, he knew that he currently lacked the means to save their world.

Alas, his plans went right down the drain about a week after registering the mindset, when a pair of demigods flew into the settlement.
