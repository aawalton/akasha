Charlemagne almost attacked out of instinct before catching himself.  The woman clearly had a point: the other camp HAD been annoying, after all.  In fact, they had been so annoying that they didn’t even bother giving him anything good to eat.  Maybe he could convince the lady to give him something to eat if he helped her.  And, come to think of it, wasn’t he supposed to be helping ladies in general to earn extra points during the Delve?  He felt like he had heard Josephine complaining about something like that.  Surely, he should at least ask the woman what she had to offer.

“Bawk,” he said, as a strange bashfulness overcame him.

“Well, that is wonderful to hear!  Allow me to introduce myself.  I am Ser Labrissa, and I do enjoy collaborating more than anything.  You know, if we work together, I bet that we could complete this quest in just a few days.  Those pesky Mooks are almost immune to my abilities, but I can affect them given enough time.  With your obvious strength, you could capture a few of them for me and bring them here for a few rounds of…er…convincing.  Once we have a few new friends on our side, we can use them to sabotage the enemy camp while we launch a full-scale assault.  How does that sound?”

Charlemagne thought that the plan sounded good.  Really good.  There was just one problem with it,  but he couldn’t put his pinfeathers on it.  Hadn’t he already….

“Bawk?” he asked.

A puzzled expression creased Labrissa’s unblemished skin.

“Why, the camp of the Mooks is just across the plain.  You couldn’t possibly miss it: just head straight out of the gate, cross the river, and keep going.”

As she spoke, the woman exited the tent, allowing Charlemagne to get a good look at her body for the first time.  While the woman had the head and upper body of a woman, from her stomach down she appeared to have the abdomen and tail of a snake.  She slithered forward gracefully as her hand reached out and patted the rooster on the wing, looking around the camp as she did.

“My, you have been busy out here.  Two of my guards defeated, and so quickly.”

Labrissa stopped and looked around as if noticing for the first time the shouts, screams, and smoke that rose from the outer sections of the camp.

“And you’ve made quite a mess of this camp,” she sighed.  “I should recall the skirmishers.”

“Bawk…” the rooster admitted, ashamed of his actions but not understanding why.

“You killed all of them too!?” the lady yelled.

“Bawk!” the rooster clarified.

“No, only killing most of them is not better at all.  What a mess.  Did the Mooks send you over here?”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne denied.

“That’s a relief.  Okay, well, at least I know what we’re working with here.  First things first, we’ve got to officially make you a Snook before any of the soldiers spot you and get the bright idea to attack.”

The rooster hesitated.  The woman’s offer sounded logical: he needed to become a Snook like her so that the soldiers in this camp wouldn’t attack him.  Not that they could hurt him, of course, but it sounded like she didn’t want him to make a bigger mess.  Well, he could understand that.  On his farm, the humans were always asking him to keep the messes to a minimum, claiming that doing so interfered with something they called “efficiency”.  And lower efficiency meant fewer food and fewer chicks.

Speaking of chicks, the rooster took another look at the snake-woman, feeling himself inexplicably drawn to her.   He wasn’t entirely sure, but he felt like it would be possible for the two of them to have offspring.  At the very least, he knew that snakes laid eggs like chickens did.  Maybe that would be good enough.  Smitten, the rooster looked around for a bit of food that he could offer Labrissa.  He didn’t spot anything promising, but then inspiration struck.

He dragged over one of the dead soldiers and dropped it next to her.

“Bawwk?” he suggested.

“What are you…oh, I see what’s going on here.  Well, I do appreciate the offer, but we’re in a bit of a time crunch, and also I don’t think we’re compatible in that respect, and, well, we just met.  So let’s focus on the war, yes?”

Labrissa clapped her hands as she refocused.

“Right, so what’s your name?”

“Bawak,” Charlemagne answered.

“Do you, Charlemagne, pledge to join me until the Mooks are defeated?”

“Bawk!” the rooster agreed enthusiastically.

Charlemagne felt a strange weight settle over him, as if a rock had been placed on top of his soul.  But, other than that, he felt no different than before.

“Well, now that that’s settled, why don’t you fly on out and bring me back a few Mook infantry or dismounted cavalry.  I’ll get some new guards and have them help you restrain them so that I can work in safety.”

The request finally jogged the rooster’s memory, and he explained what had happened to the Mooks’ camp.

“It’s been destroyed?!” Labrissa exclaimed.  “When did you do that?  No, never mind.  The Floor is going to be ending soon.  Listen, I need you to trust me and stay very still.”

Charlemagne, trusting Labrissa, stayed very still.  Even when she slithered over to him and opened her mouth wider than a human should have been able to.  Two fangs descended from a hidden recess in the top of her mouth, which she used to bite down on Charlemagne’s neck.  Through it all, the rooster did not budge.

“Bawk?” he inquired when the deadly embrace had concluded.

“Oh, that…uh…well…you see, I need you to no longer be a competitor in the Elite Systemic Games.  There’s no way that I can defeat you in a fight, and I’m worried that you’ll develop defenses against my mental abilities, so I’m going to have to kill you now.  I hope you don’t mind.  And, it’s not like it’s permanent, right?”

“Bawk,” the rooster agreed.  He remembered enough to recall that death in the Elite Systemic Games, at least during the events, was not permanent.  But wasn’t Labrissa his friend?  If so, why was she trying to knock him out of the games?

The rooster’s train of thought was disrupted by the Squiggles arriving.

“Wait, nooo!” Labrissa shouted as the rooster read the message from the Squiggles.  He had no idea why she was upset: maybe she hadn’t earned an “A” like he had?  He turned to ask her what the matter was, but the charming snake-woman had already disappeared.  As if to make up for taking away his new friend, the Squiggles caused an open door to appear directly in front of Charlemagne.  He was sure that, if he passed through the door, he would see Labrissa again.  So, he went.

Charlemagne pushed the Squiggles' messages to the side as his blood began to boil.  Molten Rage activated, and huge flames pushed toward the sky as the rooster squawked in anger and frustration.  He had been tricked!

Phatagin sighed as he watched black smoke slowly rise from the camp on his left.

“Do you think that it will take Charlemagne much longer to finish,” he asked Josephine.

“I have no idea, but, based on how that base is completely destroyed, I would say he’s almost exactly halfway done.”

The pangolin attempted to roll his beady eyes and failed.

“Are you just mad that you can’t sense out that far?” Phasima teased.  “We could get closer if you want to.  I mean, I have half a mind to go join him right now.”

“He asked us to stay out of it,” reminded Josephine.

“And Charlemagne does not enjoy being disobeyed,” added the pangolin.

The former Empress of the Forest chuckled as she watched the rooster break the sound barrier with ease.  He was far away and moving quite fast, there was no way that he’d notice if she cleaned up some stragglers.

“Well, then you stay here, I’m going to help Charlemagne mop up,” she insisted.

Phasima pointed at a small group of mounted cavalry that had clustered at the edge of the plain, failing miserably to hide themselves from the trio standing high above them.

Phatagin was unable to make out the soldiers’ colors from that far away, but Josephine’s eyesight proved to be keen enough.
