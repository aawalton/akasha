Beneath the Royal Temple, Salaqa Ik’Balam at night throbbed with drums and flutes and laughter after the end of the coronation ceremony. Up at the flat crown of the temple, however, the wind had room to breathe, and it carried the smell of sweetened maize and roasted fungi up—like Zora was right down there himself, instead of sitting on the edge with his legs dangling over open air.

Warm tea steadied his palms as he held the cup close, letting the steam touch his nose. It was a damn shame neither Enki nor Kita seemed to like tea—the boy preferred his juice, while Kita preferred her local cup of hot chocolate—but he supposed they all had different tastes, and for Kita, something sweeter was probably easier to swallow as they looked down at the city.

Right below them, behind the Royal Temple, a wagon waited there under a tarp. Ifas and Machi and several other trusted hands hauled Eria’s ice onto the wagon, and even from all the way up here the ice felt cold to Zora’s skin.

“Your ring finger’s on the wagon,” Zora said, glancing at Enki. “How long did you say it takes for a clone of yours to grow to full size?”

“One day to become an exact clone of me with one level in all attributes.”

“Only one level?”

“The clone only needs to reapply ice to Eria. High attribute levels are unnecessary,” Enki replied plainly. “I will take manual control of the clone only for a few minutes every week. For the rest of the week, it can stay hidden under the tarp alongside her.”

Zora and Kita stared at him. They were thinking the exact same thing, though Kita blurted it out with a soft laugh first.

“What a strange, strange class you have,” she said. “Where did you say you came from again?”

“Immanu. A village in the Hagi’Shar Mountains.”

“Never heard of it.”

“It is outside of empire territory. It no longer exists.”

Sensing that was about as much information as Enki was willing to part with for tonight, Kita didn’t press it any further. Instead, they kept looking down at the shadowed alley where Eria was being hauled.

“... I hope the research finishes soon,” Kita muttered. “I’d rather not be an old lady when I get to see her again.”

“So do I.” Zora smiled faintly. “But now you have an empire’s weight to place on the scale.”

“Hm?”

“You are the Empress of the Attini Empire now. You can send diplomats over to the Rampaging Hinterland Front to discuss investment and patronage into their research, for you, of all people in this land, have the ability to decide whether ‘slow’ becomes ‘less slow’.”

Kita blinked, turning her head at him as if she’d forgotten that weight sat in her lap now. “I… suppose I do, actually.”

He grinned. “You forgot about your authority now, didn’t you?”

“I’m not particularly looking forward to being garbed in ceremonial robes for the next few decades, no,” she replied, returning a grin. “But if I recall correctly… your Amadeus Academy also takes in children who are Afflicted, right? Can your academy sponsor the research as well?”

It was his turn to stare blankly at her.

“Actually, that is true.”

“You forgot about your own authority, didn’t you?” she teased.

“I’ll write my colleagues a letter, then.”

Kita nodded once, satisfied, then turned her gaze out again. Citylight glittered across rooftops everywhere, and Zora felt all of it. The celebrations sounded endless from up here. It was almost hard to believe—just a month ago—that the same streets had been full of corpses housing zombie ants like bread fillings.

“... A hell of a year,” Kita said softly.

“You have graduated from being a child to an Empress,” Zora said. “It would be an understatement to say it has been anything but a hell of a year.”

Kita’s hot chocolate steamed between her fingers. “Yes,” she said quietly. “I keep wondering… what my sister would say. What my mother would do. What my father would think of me now.”

“Well.” Zora tilted his head towards the wind. “Your father would tell you not to sit up here like this. He’d say the top of a temple is not a place for an Empress to be seen with her legs dangling like a street kid.”

For a heartbeat, Kita looked like she might argue. Then she laughed brightly, almost rude with relief.

“It’s alright,” she said, wiping the corner of her eye with the back of her wrist. “It’s the night after the ceremony. Tonight, I’m still just Kita Salaqa. Even still…” She looked at Enki now, reverent. “Do you have any request you’d like to make of the empire?”

Enki didn’t look at her. He was too busy staring out at the city.

“For what you did as a former Ant Class soldier,” she continued, “and for what you have done for us as the Worm Mage… there must be something you would want me to put into action. If you have anything you want, just say the wo—”

“No more Forward Armies made up of child soldiers,” he answered suddenly. “I am tired of seeing them die.”

Zora raised a brow. He didn’t think the boy would be so resolute with his answer—or that he even had any answer at all—but Kita didn’t hesitate to give him an immediate nod.

“It will be done,” she said. “My father spent the last years of his life trying to end the Forward Armies he helped to create. I will make sure his efforts do not go to waste.”

Enki didn’t nod, but he looked just the slightest bit satisfied.

“Wait. Can I also make a request?” Zora said, raising his hand.

Kita glanced at him, amused. “What do you want?”

“Better infrastructure for the Outer Regions. Gods, marching down here from the far northeastern border through rivers and overgrown fungi forests was a chore. I want more roads and more bridges and fewer muddy deaths by travel. The next time I march down here, I want a highway from Amadeus Academy to Salaqa Ik’Balam.”

Kita laughed. “Okay. Sure. I’ll look into that as well.”

Fireworks suddenly loosed from a hundred unseen cannons and arrived at temple height. The first bloom cracked open almost level with their dangling feet—a flower of heat bursting outward so close that Zora felt it pressing against his cheeks for a heartbeat—and the rest of them came in sequence, almost matching the intensity of the cheers coming from the city.

Sweet cocoa clung to the air around Kita as they watched the sparks fade into drifting ash, and her voice, when she finally spoke, sounded… reluctant.

“What are you two going to do now?” she asked. “Are you going to leave?”

“Regalia was a wake-up call for all of us,” Zora said plainly. “There are powerful bugs out there that do not behave anything like the ones humanity are used to fighting. Morta will likely be no exception. I must find her before whatever she’s planning comes to fruition, because rest assured, she is planning something.”

“I’ll keep soldiers posted,” Kita agreed. “If anyone hears anything about Morta—anywhere in the empire—they’ll send it straight to me, and I’ll send it straight to you.”

Another explosion flared close, and Zora felt the bright heat again. “A kind gesture, but it is unlikely she is here in the empire. My staff isn't vibrating nearly strongly enough to indicate she is anywhere close to Salaqa Ik’Balam. Still…” He lifted his staff and planted it against the stone, feeling it vibrating very, very slightly. “It does whisper west. I believe I will begin my search in the Deepwater Legion Front.”

“The western front?”

“Yes.”

“The one with… the salt-running ships and fleet-devouring leviathans?”

“That is the one.”

“... Can I ask you something?”

“Could I say no?” he teased.

Her laugh was quiet and brief.

“Don’t leave the empire,” she said flatly. “Stay here. Be my advisor. The empire could use your help as we recover. I could use your help.”

Zora smiled. “No.”

“... And I knew that as well. It was still worth a shot.” She sighed, before returning his smile with a resigned warmth. “I am the Empress now. That is why you must remain the Walking Legend that you are, and I must hold the south. Those are the roles all men must play… right?”

“You speak of me as if I am dead. I’ll still visit from time to time, you know?” he offered.

“You better.” She lifted her fist towards him. He leaned in and bumped it with his knuckles, and as a new round of fireworks whistled upwards, he rose slowly with his staff in hand. Enki rose too with his rifle, setting down their cups. “And what about you, Enki?” she asked. “You’re still sticking with Zora?”

“Yes,” he said. Then, after a pause, he added, “I also realized… something, after facing Regalia.”

Kita tilted her head, listening. Zora kept quiet, letting the boy’s mind take the front seat.

“I cannot win my war against the Swarm by myself,” he said. “I am one person, but there are bugs everywhere. Even with ten clones, I cannot be everywhere at once. Therefore, I need more than ten of me.”

Both Kita and Zora blinked. Zora less so, because his eyes were closed.

“... What?” they said.

“I will create an organization composed entirely of elite bug-slayers like myself,” Enki said plainly. “It will be strong enough to rival the militaries of the Six Swarmsteel Fronts, but not beholden to any one of them. They will walk the continent and slaughter every bug they see, connecting information across places that do not share letters, and they will also not be responsible for any annoying paperwork. I do not like paperwork.”

Kita still looked befuddled. Zora, however, was starting to understand what the boy was going on, so his smile turned wry. “And what would you call this group of ‘elite bug-slayers’ like yourself?”

“I do not know.”

“What about the organizational structure? The financial backing? Where will you recruit your members? How will you decide if they're ‘like you’ or not?”

Enki gave him a rare, rare frown. “I will think about it when I find my first member. Kita would be a good first member, though.”

“... Me?” Kita pointed at herself.

“You are strong,” Enki said blankly. “Join my organization.”

“Um. I can’t.”

“Just have two jobs.”

“I can’t. What do you think being an Empress is like?”

“Fine,” he grumbled.

“Oh, but I can be the first to acknowledge you are the leader of that organization,” she offered, rising quickly before lowering herself to a kneel. “When you are ready to begin, I will stand behind you with the full backing of the Attini Empire. You can be sure of that.”

Zora had half a mind to hurry Kita onto her feet—he couldn’t even imagine the sort of stories that’d be written if someone saw the Empress kneeling to anyone—but Enki was surprisingly… surprised as well. Perhaps, as a former Ant Class soldier, the notion of an Empress kneeling to him was also very difficult to believe.

“Thank you,” he said, dipping his head. “I will… hold you to your word.”

With that, he stepped to the edge of the temple and rested his rifle on his shoulders. Zora followed, elbowing him lightly in the ribs. “Look at you,” he teased. “Grand goals and even grander negotiations. I just wanna kill Morta and go home.”

“But you can go home anytime,” Kita called out after him. “Why don’t you just visit Amadeus Academy from time to time?”

“Because I promised myself I wouldn’t until the Magicicada Witches are dead,” he said. “Until Morta dies, my long march continues.”

One last firework screamed so close that Zora felt its heat press along his eyelids, and that was his—and Enki’s—cue to take the plunge.

Night wind rushed up from the city. Gravity seized them immediately, and the world turned into rushing air and distant music.

Above them, Kita’s voice cut through the night.

“Zora!” she shouted, hands cupped around her mouth. “About The Earthen Princess—what do you think of her now that I’ve become an honest-to-god Empress?”

A strange question.

Zora didn’t dignify that with a response, but he did chuckle as he fell.

I’ll see you again, Earthen Princess.

But now that you mention it again…

I did write many short stories for each of the Six Swarmsteel Fronts, didn’t I?

What'd I write for the Deepwater Legion Front again?
