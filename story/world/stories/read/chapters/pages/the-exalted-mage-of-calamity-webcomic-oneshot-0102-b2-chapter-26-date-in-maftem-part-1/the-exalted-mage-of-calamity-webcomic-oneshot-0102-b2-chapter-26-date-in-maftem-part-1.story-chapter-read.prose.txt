The next day.

“What are you doing?”

Nasha opened the door to their room and stopped beneath the archway.

Veronica stood beside the bed, arranging a small collection of items across the blankets: a change of clothes, Gwen’s staff, Selene’s Tear, her identification cards, including the one for the Adventurer’s Guild, and several paper notes covered in writing.

Veronica turned. “Ah, Nasha. Welcome back.” She glanced toward the pile again. “I’m just organizing things for our date today.”

Nasha walked over, looked at the assortment, and sighed. “None of this is going to do.”

“What? Why not?”

“Seriously?” Nasha picked up the staff from the bedsheet. “Why do you need Gwen’s staff?”

“My mana cores are still healing. I won’t be able to use much magic today, so I thought I’d bring it in case anything happens.”

Nasha’s expression sharpened. “What exactly do you think is going to happen today?”

She set the staff down and picked up Veronica’s guild card. “And this?”

“In case we pass by the guild and need to pick up a commission,” Veronica said plainly. “It’s just a card. No harm in bringing it, right?”

Nasha pursed her lips. “And the notes?”

“In case we come across the mage guild or an alchemist’s shop and need to trade for something. We are exploring the city today, correct? You never know what might be useful.”

“You’re not wrong, but also so wrong on so many levels,” Nasha said, crossing her arms. “And you are not going out in those clothes.”

Veronica raised a brow. “Why not?”

“That’s a full dress. The kind you wear to dinner or to some noble’s estate, not to walk around the city and have fun.” Nasha gestured to herself. “Look at what I’m wearing.”

Veronica looked more closely at her.

Nasha’s hair was down, and she wore a light cream blouse that rested off her shoulders, with loose sleeves around her upper arms. A brown corset sat around her waist, paired with dark fitted trousers tucked into leather boots just above her ankles. A simple belt tied the whole outfit together.

“But didn’t we wear something similar during our dinner date?” Veronica asked.

“That was a dinner date,” Nasha said slowly. “We went to a high-end restaurant. Today is casual. If you wear that and I wear this, people will stare at you all day and wonder why you’re dressed like you’re visiting royalty, walking around with your commoner assistant.”

Veronica wanted to protest, but decided against it. Nasha would be leading for most of the day, so it was probably better to listen.

“Fine,” Veronica said. “But I’m taking Selene’s Tear. It’s too important to leave here.”

Nasha shrugged, already resigned to that much. “Alright, that’s fine. Now show me your wardrobe. I’ll pick something nice for you.”

“So… I just pick a horse?” Veronica asked.

“Whichever one you think will win. Choose right, and you win some money,” Nasha explained.

They were inside a large stone building filled with carefully designed archways, each one leading through the halls and lobby. The two of them stood on the second floor in some kind of balcony viewing area. Around them were nearly a hundred other people, men and women, along with a few teens, but no children.

In front of them stretched a large circular track, a dirt course divided into marked lanes.

Veronica and Nasha stood before a betting booth. A plaque beside it listed the names of eight horses, along with several statistics such as breed, age, weight, height, and win rate. The odds were posted beside them as well.

“So this is… just betting?” Veronica asked, glancing over at Nasha as she placed her bet on the fifth horse, Gerald.

“You can think of it like that,” Nasha said. “You told me before that you liked sneaking out to watch Victor gamble in his dens, right? I didn’t want to take you somewhere like that, so I thought horse racing might be a better change of pace.”

Veronica hummed.

It was true that she enjoyed watching Victor gamble. She even enjoyed gambling herself. But that had always been with dice and cards. She hadn’t even known horse races were a thing.

Also… why horses? Why not dogs or cats? Or even human races? It seemed oddly specific to choose horses.

“I don’t recommend betting too much. We still have a lot of places to go,” Nasha said.

Veronica nodded and looked over the information again. She didn’t want to pick the horse with the best odds. That was never fun. Money had never been what she cared about when gambling.

“I’ll pick the eighth horse,” Veronica said. “The slowest one. Bernard.”

Once their bets were locked in, Veronica and Nasha moved to an open spot along the balcony as the betting period ended. People around them shifted closer to the railing, already beginning to shout and cheer as the horses were led to their starting lines on the track below.

“Come on, Gerald! You got this!” Nasha yelled beside her.

Veronica flinched slightly and glanced over. “H-hey. Aren’t you being a bit too…”

“Loud?” Nasha cut in. “Look around you. Everyone is shouting.”

She was right.

Everyone around them was screaming the names of the horses. The whole place had turned rowdy, almost like a tavern packed wall to wall.

Nasha grabbed Veronica’s fingers and raised their joined hands above them. “Come on,” she said. “Get into it.”

Veronica nearly panicked, unsure of what to do. Screaming and yelling in public was improper. It was one thing to shout in anger, or while caught in the rush of battle, but to cheer? In public?

Veronica pursed her lips and steeled herself.

“Come on, Bernard!” she shouted. “Show them you aren’t a loser!”

Beside her, Nasha smiled, watching how hard Veronica was trying.

Then she joined in. “Come on! Show that top horse he ain’t nothing!”

Together, they became part of the noise around them.

No one scolded Veronica. No noble looked at her with disgust. There was no attendant at her side telling her to stop. No rumors spreading about her unsightly behavior.

A man near the edge of the track lifted a flag. The balcony quieted for a brief moment as his voice carried through an augmentation crystal.

“Three… two… one… go!”

The flag dropped, and the horses burst forward.

Noise exploded through the building. People shouted so loudly the floor almost seemed to tremble beneath Veronica’s feet. Nasha’s hand tightened around hers and rose even higher as she cheered.

“Come on! Bernard! Go!” Veronica shouted.

It felt awkward at first. But the longer she watched, the more everything else faded. The crowd. The balcony. The noise. All of it narrowed to the horse she had chosen.

As expected, the top seed took the lead almost immediately. From there, the horses settled into the same order the plaque had predicted.

“Bernard is doing worse than I thought,” Veronica said.

Nasha laughed beside her. “You picked the slowest horse!”

“I know, but still. This seems almost too systematic. How is—” Her words cut off as the order shifted. The second horse pushed into the lead. The sixth passed the fifth. Bernard slipped past seventh.

And just like that, Veronica was dragged back into the chaos.

As the race went on, the gap at the front only widened. Bernard eventually fell back to dead last, but Gerald, Nasha’s horse, was now neck and neck with the first-place horse.

Veronica abandoned Bernard without hesitation.

“Go, Gerald!” she shouted.

Nasha joined her, both of them cheering as the final stretch came. Gerald and the top seed ran side by side, several seconds ahead of the others. Veronica leaned forward, completely focused on the two. Seconds away from the finish, and then—

The first horse crossed the finish line.

Gerald came second.

Bernard came last.

The crowd erupted anyway, regardless of the result. Some cheered loudly. Others groaned and cursed in frustration.

Veronica stood frozen for a second, still gripping the railing. Her heart was racing, even though she had done nothing more strenuous than watch. Somehow, it felt like she had just sprinted across the city herself.

“Ah, damn,” Nasha said. “He was so close.”

Veronica looked over at her and smiled. “Yeah. He was.”

There was a feeling in her chest she couldn’t quite name. Happiness was close, but not exactly. It felt lighter than relief and warmer than simple excitement.

Fun, maybe.

Perhaps she was simply enjoying herself.

The two of them walked through the streets. It was a little past noon; the horse races had taken up most of the morning. Now, it was time for them to find lunch.

“Ah, by the way, what did Gwen say she was doing today?” Veronica asked. “I feel bad since we’re on a date and left her alone.”

Nasha walked beside her, holding Veronica’s fingers in her own. It had been embarrassing for Veronica at first, but over time, she had slowly grown used to it, considering how often Nasha did it.

“She told me last night that she wanted to go to the library and study some public magic knowledge. She felt bad for not being able to heal us more during the fight at the sanctuary.”

“What? That wasn’t her fault, though. And I already told her I would help train her while we were here.”

“Well, she wants to help in her own way. I told her to get some rest and enjoy the city, but she refused me.”

Veronica sighed. “I’ll have to make it up to her later.”

As they continued along the street, they soon entered a large district lined with tall buildings. Many shops were open along the roads, with vendors selling food while others displayed small trinkets and knickknacks.

A few men stared at them, but Nasha immediately glared back. Women also gave them looks, but upon noticing their joined hands, they stifled small giggles, as if they had just heard some scandalous rumor.

They passed by a theater with painted banners hanging above its doors. One showed a woman in a silver mask weeping beside a bloodied crown. Another displayed a group of actors in exaggerated poses, all pointing toward a terrified, horrifically drawn goose.

“What is that supposed to be?” Veronica asked, pointing overhead.

“A comedy, probably.”

“About a… goose?”

“That’s probably what makes it funny.”

Veronica didn’t have any way to argue. But it was intriguing, to say the least. Perhaps later, if they still had time, they could watch the goose play. Though, based on the drawing, she wasn’t sure if the goose was the victim or the villain.

Nasha led her to a stall. On the counter sat a flat heated plate, powered by an internal flame using a mana crystal. Thin sheets of batter were being spread into wide circles. Beside it were bowls of sliced fruit, whipped cream, melted chocolate, nuts, honey, and specialty syrup, whatever that meant. Hanging from the wooden rack overhead were small candied sugar balls, each one a different color and sprinkled with grains of sugar.

“What are those?”

“Crepes,” Nasha said. “The flat folded ones. And those are sugar pearls.”

“Sugar pearls?”

“Candied sugar balls. They’re crunchy on the outside and soft inside.” Nasha tugged her closer to the stall. “Come on, let’s buy some.”

The woman behind the counter looked up as they approached. She was middle-aged, her brown hair tied back in a bun. She wore an apron stained with sticky sugar and fallen batter. Her eyes met their faces, then briefly dipped to their locked fingers.

“Good afternoon, ladies. Something sweet for the couple?”

Veronica froze.

Couple?

Were… she and Nasha a couple?

She looked over at Nasha, unsure. What exactly was their relationship?

They were on a date. They held hands. They had kissed before—and then some. Well, a lot, actually. But was there anything else? Something that confirmed it? Was it just a gut feeling or a mutual agreement spoken verbally?

“We—”

“Yes,” Nasha said before Veronica could finish. “Something sweet for us.” She looked back at Veronica, smiling as if nothing had happened.

The red in Veronica’s face appeared slightly quicker than she would have liked.

The stall owner chuckled, either because she noticed or because she was wise enough to pretend she hadn’t. “Then you’ve come to the right place. What will it be?”

Nasha leaned over the counter, studying the options. “One with strawberries, cream, and chocolate. And…” She glanced at Veronica. “What do you want?”

Veronica cleared her throat softly, forcing herself to focus on the food instead of the word couple. “The same is fine.”

“You can pick something different.”

“I don’t know what any of these are supposed to taste like.”

“Then we’ll share.” Nasha turned back to the stall owner. “One strawberry cream chocolate crepe, one honey apple with nuts, and two sugar pearl sticks.”

“Coming right up.”

The stall owner began working quickly. Batter spread across the hot plate, thin and smooth. She flipped it with a flat metal tool, added the fillings, folded each one into a neat triangle, then slipped them into paper sleeves. After that, she took two sticks of sugar pearls from the rack, one pale pink and one amber-gold.

Nasha paid before Veronica could reach for her coin pouch.

Veronica gave her a look.

Nasha ignored it and accepted the food, passing Veronica’s portion over.

“Come again!” the woman called as they walked away.

Veronica took a careful bite of the crepe. It was warm and soft, with a slightly crisp edge. Sweet honey and cooked apple filled her mouth, followed by the crunch of nuts. It was simple. Not refined like a noble dessert, not layered with rare ingredients or arranged like edible art. But it was good.

Very good.

Nasha took a bite of her own crepe, cream and chocolate smearing slightly at the corner of her mouth. She noticed Veronica looking and wiped it away with her thumb.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You were staring.”

“You had cream on your mouth.”

“Oh.” Nasha smiled. “You could have gotten it for me.”

Veronica nearly choked on nothing.

Nasha only looked more pleased.

They continued walking through the entertainment district, eating as they went. Nasha switched between her crepe and one of the sugar pearl sticks, biting into the candied shell with a sharp crunch. Veronica tried hers after a few more steps. The outside cracked apart beneath her teeth, while the inside was soft and sweet, almost like thick syrup wrapped in sugar.

It was far too sweet.

And yet she took another bite anyway. It was exactly what she liked.

A chorus of cheers rose from farther down the street.

Veronica stopped.

Several people ahead had gathered in a loose circle near the edge of a small plaza. Children stood near the front, while adults lingered behind them with smiles and cups of drink in hand. Every few seconds, the crowd let out another round of gasps and applause.

Nasha looked toward the noise. “Oh. Street magician.”

Veronica blinked. “Street what?”

“Magician.”

Veronica stared at her. “Is he a mage?”

“No, he probably isn’t.”

“Then how is he a magician?” She turned toward the crowd again, watching them cheer.

“He performs tricks.”

“With magic?”

“No.”

Veronica looked at Nasha with disbelief. Was she just lying now? That didn't make any sense.

“Then he isn’t a magician. Nor a mage, for that matter.”

Nasha laughed and tugged her forward. “Come on. You’ll see.”
