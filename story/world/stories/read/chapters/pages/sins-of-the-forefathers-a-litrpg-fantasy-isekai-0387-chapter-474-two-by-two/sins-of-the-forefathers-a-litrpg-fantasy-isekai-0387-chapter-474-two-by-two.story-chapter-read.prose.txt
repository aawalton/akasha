I stood off to the side of the great central pit in the middle of Karag Varla, watching my soldiers file into the plaza. They knew what they were doing, as they rapidly constructed means that we could descend into…

And save all of those that the adherents of the Red Truth had captured.

Including Azarus.

I was alone and unbothered as I considered matters. From what I had been told by my commanders, most of the city had already been cleared, to my surprise. The Korvoks hadn’t actually left all that many of their ‘Skarned’, as I had been told they were named. Likewise, Djarn had been the only warrior of the Karvokar who had still been within the Hold City itself. By the time I was speaking to Vandimar and the surviving, uncaptured civilians in the Hagia Dynamis, Sylvia and Bella had finished mopping up every roving group of murderous cannibals they could find.

Varla was large, yes, but not on the level of something like Blutstein. Or even Rhoscara, really. Before Bleddyn had arrived at the plaza to reinforce Walter, both he and Sylvia had more luck in finding other survivors within the city itself. While they hadn’t been locked up inside of a governmental building being threatened by a hulking mutant, there had still been huddling pockets of Dwarves hiding from the Skarned. The decision had been made that the best place for them would be to join the rest of the survivors within the Hagia Dynamis.

I may not have spent a great deal of time within the building itself, but I wasn’t shocked to discover that its roots went deeper than I had seen. They had more than enough room within to accommodate the people both canny and lucky enough to dodge the Skarned.

Which led us to now. No more pussyfooting around. I wasn’t going to allow us to lose the momentum we had built up in this impromptu campaign against the Korvoks.

It was time to take the fight to them. Right into the heart of Karag Korvok. It had been a bloody day, so far, but it threatened to get even worse.

Not only had the three wings of my forces here in Varla descended on the plaza, but my reinforcements from outside the city had as well. My messenger to the rest of our forces had done his duty admirably, and my orders had been followed.

Including the one to bring everyone inside.

Including the Final Line itself.

I had made the call to bring it into the city itself, instead of waiting just outside of the gates. As twisty as the main entrance was, it had still been wide enough for the converted vessel to carefully maneuver its way through the passage. Once through, it had been directed down blood and offal-tainted streets here to the plaza, where matters were currently underway.

With the Korvoks, what little had been left behind after this Ul-Korvoth had absconded with his captive, driven out of the city, it was time to fortify our position. We had needed not only the engineers and crafters of the expedition to build a sufficient amount of ladders to descend into the great hole in the mountain, but to create out fortifications. The walls of the unstable pit were being shored up at the same time the ropes and ladders were being set, as the plaza itself rapidly became an entrenched position. Barricades, palisades, and walls were being thrown up all over the place, all of them directed toward the pit.

We were going to be going down there, and in the event we were driven back…

Well, we needed an entrenched position to hold the Korvoks off. This was going to be a bloody struggle, I predicted. These maddwarves and their Red Truth wouldn’t listen to negotiations, not when they were zealots.

To not only rescue the captives, but to reach our original goal in the form of Nehushtan…we’d have to slaughter these Korvoks by the hundreds. No doubt that would involve plenty of encounters with more Karvokar like Djarn, as well as the other leaders of the Korvoks.

I had already briefed all of my officers on the encounter with Djarn, as we waited for the frantic construction of our defenses to finish. I actually had full confidence that most of my most deadly could handle a Karvokar, one on one. Of course, the goal would be not to allow the strange, half-skinned bastards that opportunity.

Most Classers weren’t oddities like me. They didn’t hunt monsters like that by trying to go for the single, powerful blow. No, they bled them out by a death from a thousand cuts.

You wore them down through squad tactics.

But that still presented another problem, and that problem was numbers.

The Korvoks were an entire civilization. The forces of the expedition numbered barely a little over five hundred. There had to be thousands and thousands of mad, raving barbarians on the other side of that tunnel, each and every one of them literally hungry for blood and flesh. A brutal assault on their position was our only way to reach their Hold, but the sheer disparity in numbers would see us crushed under the weight of the screaming hordes, normally.

Normally, being the keyword there.

We had our own trump card, and he was already preparing. There was more than one reason we were essentially standing around waiting for the construction to be done.

After all, the plaza was now empty of all the dead bodies left behind by the Korvoks.

That left me in a mostly supervisory position at the moment. With the benefit of my tripartite mind, I was both directing the construction efforts as well as contemplating matters.

Goddamnit, Azarus. What had you gotten yourself into now? You travel thousands of miles on foot, clearing the path through the Long Road with only an oversized house cat and a single mouthy Academy student as your companions…only to get captured a few days after arriving.

Once Torgir Gravelfoot had introduced himself to me, I’d gotten the story out of the formerly injured Dwarf.

All of it, from the moment the Skyfall had descended on Vereden.

It gave me hope, honestly. For a lot of reasons.

Turns out, I wasn’t the only one who had still been in communication with at least one of the Great Spirits. Azarus still possessed a direct line of not only communication with Tarus. As the Envoy of the Great Spirit of the Sun, their connection was strong enough to pierce through the wall blocking the Concord off from Vereden. Not only that…

But Tarus had been able to empower Azarus. Beyond what he had already gifted the Dwarf, as it stood. With nothing else to occupy either his attention or power, Tarus and Azarus had thought of a dangerous gamble on how they were going to breach through the rubble blocking off the Long Road. Back when my friend had accepted the gift of power from Tarus, his chosen boon had been a Skill. Specifically, a transformation Skill, in a vein not unlike my own. It had changed him physically, in much the same way Rhazal’s curse upon me had. Not…quite as monstrous looking as my own, in a way that I was a bit jealous of.

But the Skill itself essentially transformed Azarus into a fifteen-foot-tall walking suit of burning plate mail. The few times the two of us had sparred together, back in the before times, I had found it hard for any of my abilities to even penetrate the steel his skin had turned into. He was, essentially, my natural counter.

He was also really damn strong at moving rocks in that form, which is how he had managed to clear the Long Road. I had considered this possibility a long time ago, but had dismissed it as unlikely. Azarus couldn’t maintain that form for long enough that he would be able to clear that much rock and stone.

Until Tarus had empowered him with every ounce of attention he could spare. Now, apparently, Azarus could stay in his fully transformed state for as long as he damn well pleased. And he essentially had, according to Torgir.

And even with that, when Ul-Korveth had waltzed right into the Hagia Dynamis like he owned the place, trailed by nine other hulking examples of skinned mutantcy…

Azarus and his transformed state hadn’t been able to even scratch the ‘Korvokahn’. Not even when the leader of the Varlayans had, a Dwarf by the name of Jorvik, had joined the fray, did Ul-Korveth bend.

Instead, he brought both of them to their knees, and along with nearly every other warrior within the Hagia Dynamis, had dragged the defeated back to Karag Korvok where he could do…something with them.

I masked a grimace at that thought. I didn’t like to think about just whatever it was that either my friend or the people of Varla were experiencing right now, under the ‘tender’ mercies of the Korvoks.

Which was all the reason I needed to get this show on the road. It looked like the construction was nearly done, which by itself was a testament to the sheer capability of not only Professions, but the above-average skill of our Engineers with them. Without the outright supernatural skill granted by Professions like Carpenter and Mason, this probably would have taken weeks to do. My Engineers had magicked an entire buttressing system all along the walls of the pit. Not only that, they had attached actual stairs to it as well.

Which…wasn’t what I had asked for in the first place, but they assured me that the wide, spiraling stairs that led down into the pit were a better solution. They weren’t pretty, but they did look solid, so I would take their word for it.

It would work, as a way for us to not only transport our troops, and to not only set up a supply line for the assault…

But to transport our secret weapon. Something I would take great glee in unleashing upon Karag Korvok.

Cries of alarm coming from the palisade walls we’d set up from the southern entrance of the plaza caught my attention. But rather than give me pause, it instead gave me a sense of anticipation.

He must have finished.

Good. I was ready.

Bleddyn had already bounded over to the palisade to see what the commotion was about, and I was close enough to watch a grimace of distaste crawl over his features. I knew a decision like this would cause a bit of consternation in the forces, but I didn’t care. I had already received permission to do it from the few in residence officials of Varla not captured, and theirs was the opinion I thought mattered the most.

Besides. My people would get over it when this saved their lives.

Reluctantly, Bleddyn threw open the gates of the palisade…

And on the other side, stretching all the way back to the entrance of the very gates we had first traversed, was an army.

An army of the dead. Hundreds and hundreds of the risen dead, every last one of the Dwarves that the Korvoks had slaughtered through the streets of Karag Varla had been gathered and given purpose at my order.

By Rafael.

These were the slain protectors of Karag Varla, risen once more to defend their home.

I had never forgotten the sight of Rafael’s, then known to me as Tlazo’s, army of undead Orcs during the battle for Elderwyck. They had come spilling out of the tomb underneath Tlatec, the sister city of Elderwyck. If it hadn’t been his ‘assistants’ back then, the Orcish city would have been devoured by Rhazal’s Revenants.

I had asked Rafael if he could duplicate that feat of strength, and he had agreed. And so, the Lich had wandered the streets of Varla, animating the recently dead in a manner that was…unspeakably, bone-chillingly easy.

A simple wave of a hand, and dozens of corpses picked themselves up off the street to follow in his wake. According to Rafael, these weren’t exactly either the hardiest or the most intelligent of undead he could raise, but that didn’t matter.

The only thing that did was that we had our own horde of Dwarves who could act as the tip of our spear, as we pushed into Karag Korvok.

Quantity had a quality all of it's own.

Floating in the air before the legitimate military-sized column of undead dwarves, his black and red robes flapping in an unseen wind, Rafael’s flaming blue eyes met my own across the distance…

And I nodded at him.

The time was now.

The Lich inclined his head in response and tipped his staff forward.

Immediately, the entire column of undead began marching forward as one, single, unified mass. The level of coordination involved was downright inhuman.

Rafael made sure to accompany his assistants from his place floating overhead. The plaza was silent as everyone watched his creations march past. Some were in various states of mutilation, only leading to the grotesqueness of the scene. After all, it’s not as if the Korvoks had been kind to their victims, as they butchered the protectors of Varla. Some were flayed down to the bone, while some were missing body parts. Some, even, looked like they had been gnawed on by wild animals.

And yet…

There was something distinctly…poetic that even in death, the Legions of Varla would fight to save its people.

I shook that thought off. Now was not the time for literary comparisons.

It was time for action.

I took a deep breath and bellowed into the silent plaza, as the risen dead began to descend into the pit. “FORM UP! FORM UP! WE’RE GOING IN BEHIND THEM!”

As my people began to form up into ranks, and my Captains and officers with them, my odd mood found me. I decided to indulge, if only briefly, when my voice would be drowned out by the implacable marching of feet. I couldn’t help but whisper to myself as the thumping of feet echoed down into the dark.

“Down, down, down…

Two by two they went, upon the stair.

The dead to vengeance. The living to war.

And none could say where one ended, and the other began.”
