After drawing Hinlaw’s Presence, I glanced at his body. The thegns had said his body needed to be burnt. At least, they implied it was important. It may have been symbolic, but it couldn’t hurt to follow the custom.

I dragged Hinlaw inside, heaped him on a wooden table, then set it aflame with a candle from a wall sconce. It was difficult to burn human flesh, especially a Path-walker’s, but hopefully the table and its wood would keep the flame going long enough to finish the job.

But I had other concerns.

Again, I tried using my Perception Skill. I knew what to expect, but it didn’t stop my head from feeling like someone had put it into a hydraulic press, and it didn’t stop my vision from shimmering gold.

The pressure was coming from everywhere.

Realistically, it was coming from below. Someone would have to come from below. I’d seen no evidence that Path-walkers could fly.

I’d also never encountered a Path-walker this strong.

Still, I couldn’t make a choice until I knew more. I’d come this far. I’d just beaten a Gold on my own. I had an Art I could merge if I absolutely had to, no matter the damage it would do to my leys.

To buy myself more time to assess, I climbed. I raced higher, taking a tower up into one of the spires. I’d be cutting myself off if I was a regular person, but just like our first fall into the Labyrinth, I could jump off and slow my descent if I got cornered. I still had slightly over half my Presence left. A hundred and four points, to be precise.

I ascended halfway to the top of the spire. I navigated between overlapping towers, always picking the highest. Offices and rooms swished past, but there was no time to stop and investigate what they were. Besides, they were all empty.

I was in one of the two tallest spires, now. At halfway, I stopped. The pressure didn’t change. Was it that strong that no matter how far away I got, it stayed the same?

Or was its source just keeping up with me?

I stopped when I reached the bridge that spanned between the two tallest towers. From the ground, it looked like a razor-thin aqueduct, but from up here, it was with enough to fit two lanes of traffic across—though it would’ve been a tight squeeze. Railings guarded the edges, sanded smooth by centuries of whipping wind, and banners fluttered in the breeze. It overlooked the open ocean on either side, and with enough of a jump, I could make it to the water. There was a way out.

I might need it.

A man stood on the other side of the bridge, watching me.

He stood in a casual stance. The wind parted around him, and it didn’t even touch his hair. He wore a black coat, closed with brass toggle buttons, and a long cloak which didn’t so much as flutter in the gale. Brown gloves, worn with age, wrapped around the hilt of a great warbrand, which he leaned on as though it were a cane.

His hair was brown, and his skin was so pale I could see every vein on his forehead. A golden circlet bit into his head, pressing against his hair, and a sigil hung at the very front. A circle with three bear heads facing inward.

The blood left my face, and my hands stiffened.

This was the Warlord, wasn’t it?

He was plain. He wore no armour, which was probably by design. He didn’t think he needed it.

“Are you here to fight, or to talk?” I shouted, voice carrying in the wind. It didn’t matter how loud the wind was. A Path-walker that strong would hear.

Powerful Path-walkers like Askan and Moonhand hadn’t given off the same kind of pressure. The Warlord was strong, but was he that much stronger? Or had he simply released the pressure, letting it weigh down on me, where the others had restrained it. That must’ve meant it was a threat.

I stepped forward cautiously. The moment I passed through the doorway, the wind hit me like a truck. It wasn’t nearly as bad as it was on the high plains near Castle Urcia, though, and I was used to it.

At the risk of sounding like Hinlaw, I said, “We don’t have to fight, you know!”

“I prefer my traitors to have a spine,” the man countered. His voice boomed louder than anything I had ever heard. It was firm and regal, demanding respect with every vibration.

“I—”

“Do not play the fool with me, Soulstealer,” the man said.

“You’re the Warlord?”

“Do you have to ask?”

I chewed my lip. “What do you want?”

“There is no atonement for traitors.” He walked forward, raising his blade. He swished it through the air, then pointed it at me as he approached. “If you run, I will follow you. I will ensure you are dead.”

“Hold on a minute,” I said. He wasn’t close enough yet. I didn’t have to raise my spear. “I haven’t done—”

“My Subtle Men cannot be so easily fooled.” The Warlord scowled. “One of your comrades believed you were a traitor. That you intended on killing me. You were reported.”

I snorted desperately.

What about the plans? If the Warlord was here to personally deal with me, there was no way I would become Ealdorman.

“I’ve been falsely reported before,” I tried.

“Frythlid has never raised a false report to me before, and she would not do so now. I cannot tolerate ants like you, who think you can hold a dagger to my leg and stab, thinking that I will not notice until you’ve spilled my guts.”

Like a kid caught stealing from a candy jar, I opened my mouth and tried to speak. “I—”

“I am here to kill you, D5333, and there will be no negotiating. Die like a man, or die like a dog, but you will die. If you thought you were being clever, you were wrong.”

“Then why’d it take one of my comrades reporting me to actually catch me?” I countered. “That’s what Habit was doing, huh?”

“You are numbers, and nothing more. I have never heard of this Habit.”

Backing up, I tried, “Your father made us to stem the Dark Lord’s invasion, didn’t he? And look where you are. The invasion is worse now than ever before. You’re burning what your ancestors built to the ground, and for what? For vanity? Greed?”

The Warlord laughed. “If you see everything so simply, it’s no wonder you failed so miserably—”

“I haven’t failed yet.”

The Warlord sighed. “It’s a shame Duplicates needed a soul to wield the System. Far too uppity.”

“Yeah, forgive me for keeping my head screwed on, no matter how much training tried to beat it out of me. I’m a fan of freedom.”

The Warlord scowled. “You don’t know anything about this world. You’ve lived your life in a sheltered corner of Gate. Oh, spare me the surprise. You think Frythlid didn’t also send me every morsel of information we had on you? I know everything about you. I know when you ripped the soul out of my property, and everything that you have done since you arrived. Yes, perhaps you stayed out of my sight…but when my Subtle Men send a letter warning me that a thegn thinks he can defeat me, and has every intention of defeating me, I listen. I will stomp out traitors.”

“The only traitor here is you.”

The Warlord’s face darkened. By now, he was halfway across the walkway. He leaned forward, sword up beside his head in a well-practiced ready stance. His eyes twisted, a mixture of anger and stained honour.

Evidently, calling him a traitor was the wrong move.

I needed to run. But where? If I tried to escape right now, he’d follow me. He’d kill me while my back was turned to him.

I had to attack.

What if I could end this here? What if I could kill the Warlord? If he was gone, no one would oppose me being an Ealdorman. The king would take power, and soon, Alis. I could solve all our problems, right here, right now.

I raised my shield and lowered my spear.

Before I could even think about attacking, the Warlord flashed forward. I would’ve said he had teleported, if not for the rush of wind that blasted off him, nearly throwing me off my feet. He cracked through the air, creating a sonic boom that shattered all the windows in the tower behind me. Although he only swung with one hand, his blade crashed into my shield with enough force to knock me to my knees.

It didn’t break through my shield, but it made my bones quake. My joints screamed out in pain. Yes, my elbow, but also my knees and my ankles as I tried to resist the force.

Before I could register what was happening, an uppercut smashed the bottom of my shield. I kept my grip on it, but the impact knocked me onto my back.

As far as I could tell, the Warlord hadn’t even used an Art.

I rolled to the side, dodging a stomp, then jumped up to my feet just in time to avoid a swish of his blade. It flashed through the air right where my ankles were.

It wasn’t looking good, but I had a few tricks left.

There was no way I was getting out of this without merging my Art. But to do that, I needed to buy myself time. I rolled to the side, then used my Art to pull down on the Warlord. I activated two wells pulling down toward the same point, weighing him down with twice the power.

He pushed through it like he was passing through a curtain.

“Your arrogance is astounding, ant,” he snarled. With a jab, he aimed his heavy blade at my gut. I deflected it down, crossing my spear and my shield. As it fell, it slashed my thigh. I cried out in pain, then sprang back.

I wasn’t going to get a reprieve to merge my Art to a higher tier. I needed to split my attention.

Like I did while forming my memory palace, I divided half my attention toward fighting, and the other half toward merging my Skills.

My Art made the base of the Skill. I added in [Snapping Pheasant] and [Gravity’s Spearhead] for the other two Advanced Skills, then added [Command of Law] and [Crane Guidance], the last of my armour Skills.

It merged together with a pop, but I didn’t have any time to bask in the satisfaction. I had to survive.

The Art was Expert-tier now.

As I merged it, the Warlord kited me around the bridge. He controlled my every movement. No matter how quickly I tried to retaliate with my Art and spear combined, he was too fast. His sword whirled, deflecting my jabs with effortless ease.

I’d landed a hit on Moonhand, and that was before I’d gotten my new armour and my new Skills. All I needed was one lucky hit…just one lucky hit!

Sure, he was stronger than an Emerald. That much was clear. But all I needed was a hit…

The Warlord backhanded me across the cheek with, sending me sprawling to the ground. My helmet toppled off, but I snatched it up and tugged it back on. There was no way I’d let that go.

I used my Art again as I stood up, targeting the Warlord and pulling backward on him with two wells. Something burned inside me. My heart tinged, and my gut churned. The resonance Nodes inside me cracked, threatening to fracture.

This time, he had to lean into it like a strong wind, but it still wasn’t enough, even with it being stronger.

I wasn’t trying to stop him, though. I needed to plan. I visualized what he was going to do, relying on the distant, deeply-buried [Eye For Framing] Skill.

It didn’t help much. I didn’t know enough about the Warlord. I didn’t know how he was going to fight me.

He didn’t have a shield, and he was fast. But if I could bait him…

I feigned to the side, and he matched me. No amount of trickery would get through. I stabbed and lunged, desperately trying to keep up a chain of attacks, even as my limbs flagged and I burned through Presence faster than ever before. Like Lord Ceonwin had warned, the higher tier Art caused the Presence draw to double.

There was only one way I’d get close enough to land a hit. I had to make it count. One hit could be enough to bring him down, right?

Firming up my resolve, I lifted my shield slightly, making an opening around my gut. I could survive a wound like that for a little while, with my enhanced Vitality.

The Warlord took the bait. He plunged his sword in, ramming it through me. At the same time, I aimed for his head, using Presence and my Art to empower my spear’s swing. It was my last thirteen Presence—it had better be worth it. I used twelve to create an angled double well, catching my spear and accelerating it, then one to activate my resonance Skill.

My spear rushed toward his face. His gaze locked onto mine, but deep behind his eyes, a wisp of fear trembled. Was I actually going to pull it off? I was about to win…

My strike connected. It smashed into his cheekbone.

It bounced off.

The Warlord stumbled, pulling his blade out of me. I’d left a nick on his cheek, and nothing more.

There was no way I was winning this.

He wiped his cheek, looked down at his bloody glove in surprise, then snarled, “Pathetic. All that effort, and you achieved nothing.”

A moment later, a glassy, iridescent sheen washed over his cheek. Labyrinth Keepers surged out of his pores, mending his skin and sewing it back together, before disappearing again.

I might not have been able to win, but I had to live.

Alis was counting on me. So many others needed me alive.

Would it mean anything, though, now that I’d gotten on the Warlord’s bad side?

That was a problem for later.

I staggered to the side, leaning on the railing to support myself. It was a gamble, but what were the chances the Warlord was used to fighting people as weak as me?

He slashed down my back. It didn’t cut me in half, but it felt like it did. I screamed as I fell toward the railing and caught myself. My armour broke beneath his blade, and Keepers scurried about its surface, working to repair the links.

Still, I pushed myself up.

“You still believe in yourself?” the Warlord snarled. “You disgust me. You come here, thinking you’re better than us, thinking you know better. Listen to your elders. People need a strong hand. They must be controlled, and ruled for their own good.”

“Nah…” I groaned. “You have no right to rule any more than the petty tyrant I just slew and burnt.”

“And? You’d wear the crown if given the chance. You would take power for yourself. What exactly would you do?”

I was silent. I could think of a few things, but nothing concrete. I couldn’t summarize it with a succinct sentence, nor did I have the energy to.

My head was light. Black sparks whirled in my vision. I wanted to fall asleep from the Presence drain and my injuries, but I couldn’t—not yet. I had to live.

“Can you even tell me? What’s one choice you’d make? You don’t know what you would even use such immense power for. Only that, what, you should have it instead of me? You tell me about freedom, but you don’t care about that. I have served my people—my people, not yours, soulstealer—for a century. My father before me, for far longer. It was my father’s army, not Aelfwine’s, that built this nation. And when the kings grew too useless, we wrenched power from them. We are the only reason Gate exists!”

The Warlord approached, sword raised, poised to behead me. I begged my muscles for one last push. I just needed to get myself up on that railing.

“I would have a traitor like you drawed and quartered, but that would take too long, and to give you a chance to flee would be a mistake. No, ant. You die here.”

“Maybe. But not at your hands.” Mustering all my strength, I pulled myself over the railing. I pressed my feet against the stone, then thrust myself off with as much force as I could muster, aiming for the ocean below.
