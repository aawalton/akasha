Charlemagne squawked in outrage at Phasima’s declaration, but, to be honest, his indignation was tempered by relief.  While he was reasonably certain that he could have overcome Labrissa’s strange mental powers, there was still a part of him that was scared by the other Knight.  Teem and his squire Teem took the announcement in stride.

Thankfully, the Vessarian let the rooster vent his anger without comment, and the moment passed.

“Thank the System you’re here,” Josephine said to Phasima.  “Now that you’re here, our odds of making it out alive are a lot better.  Should we keep heading toward the throne room?”

“Yeah, might as well,” the former Empress of the Forest agreed.  “We better get there before our powers are degraded even more.”

“Are you not close to full strength?  You said that your race was adept at wielding the elements long before the System arrived….”

“Aye, I’m doing all right, but the lack of mana means I have to be frugal.  I don’t have a way to restore my reserves once they are gone.”

Charlemagne was content to let his Attendant and minion determine their next step.  He was deep in contemplation as to how he could replenish the mana within his Ignition Core in case he needed to go all out for an extended period of time.  He knew that there was a special type of energy called radiation that could be turned into mana, but he didn’t know how to find any sources of it

“Bawk?” he asked after exhausting that particular train of thought.

“Uh, I don’t think so,” Josephine answered.

“The System does not abide unstable elements,” Phasima answered almost simultaneously.  “It has something to do with the interaction between mana and matter.  Newly integrated worlds often attempt to hoard such materials, as they are quite useful, but they break down rapidly.”

“Huh, I didn’t know that,” the Attendant said.  “But it makes a lot of sense…unstable matter seems a bit of an oxymoron if you ask me.”

“Well, I’ll be happy to tell you everything I know about the matter, providing we survive the day,” Phasima promised.  “Let’s get to the throne room!”

“I admit I do not recall the location of the throne room,” Teem said, his voice heavy with shame.  “When Teem and I reappeared, our Attendant had already fled.  I did not commit the castle’s floor plans to memory.”

‘Nor did I,” Teem the Squire added.  “It is my fault, and mine alone.  I should have anticipated that Ser Teem would require my guidance.”

“It is not your fault alone, Squire Teem,” the Knight said gently, placing a heavy metal gauntlet on the other’s shoulder.  “Let us atone for our lapse in judgement through service to the System.”

“Yes, that’s all very touching,” Phasima added sarcastically.  “If you’re all done hugging it out, can we get on with it?”

“Lead the way,” Ser Teem agreed.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne added.

“Come, Squire Phatagin, you cannot serve your Knight from back there,” Josephine called as they set out.

The pangolin sighed as he floated through the air to join Charlemagne at the vanguard of the group.

“I do hope that the System lockout is resolved soon,” he complained.  “My metabolism has been enhanced by the System, and it is difficult to move quickly without its support.”

Phasima snorted.

“It’s going to get worse before it gets better.  You should use your Momentum Core to speed up your bodily functions.”

“That is something I could do?” the pangolin asked, perking up.  “Why haven’t you mentioned this before?”

The Vessarian shrugged.

“It wasn’t really relevant before, was it?  But your Momentum Core is a lot more versatile than you might think.  You just need to develop the imagination and experience to use it to its fullest potential.”

“Thank you.  I shall endeavor to improve my imagination…should we all survive this,” Phatagin responded with surprising sincerity.

“Eh, no sweat,” Phasima answered.  “Apparently, we’re all in this together.  Good thing I got a few levels from killing Labrissa.  The extra Attributes are going to come in handy, I imagine.”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne complained.

The group of six did not run into any other Knights on the way to the throne room.  They did, however, run into several more roaming bands of enemies.  Phatagin, putting his Momentum Core to good use, was able to detect the distant footfalls of the creatures from quite a ways off, giving them plenty of warning against potential ambushes.  Charlemagne continued to dominate when it came to getting kills, racking up another two levels in the process.

The extra Attributes were nothing to sneeze at, and they made the rooster wonder what sort of bonuses he would get from his next Class, and then the one after that.  But, unlike his previous level ups, the feeling was somehow muted.  Perhaps it was due to the partial System lockout, but he did not feel like he was getting the full benefit from the levels.

The sounds of combat carried further than the rooster’s mana senses could apparently reach due to the partial System lockdown, meaning that the group heard the fighting within the throne room long before they reached the enormous double doors leading to the enormous chamber.  Bodies belonging to both the Sun Queen’s guards and the mysterious invading forces were plentiful, and even the incredibly sturdy stones that formed the castle showed signs of damage.

“Not through there, then,” Josephine announced, making a snap decision.  “Come on this way, there are secondary entrances into the balcony area we can use.”

The Attendant led the group through a hidden door and up a winding staircase.  There was a fairly wide hallway at the top that ran parallel to the throne room.

“Any one of these should do,” the woman added as she pointed toward the many doors that led into the great chamber.  “Good luck!”

“Bawk?” the rooster asked, confused.

“No, I’m not heading directly into an active warzone!  You guys go ahead and I’ll follow.”

While they were talking, Phasima had already surged ahead and yanked open one of the doors.  The noise of battle filled the hall, muffling whatever it was the Vessarian shouted as she put on a burst of speed and disappeared through the doorway.  Charlemagne, filled with curiosity, followed her.

As he emerged out into the throne room, two things became clear.  The first was that a winner had definitely not yet been established.  There were dozens of the “Elite Royal Guards” near the center of the cavernous throne room, hammering away at a force field that absorbed the punishment without complaint.  Near the throne itself floated the Sun Queen herself, who was firing a continuous beam of blinding solar energy into the barrier that caused the clear shield to glow red around the point of impact.  Although the guards’ blows were fast and heavy, the rooster had no trouble in following them.  Unless they had special abilities or were hiding their true abilities, he felt that he could handle any two of them in a straight fight.

Evaluating the Elite Royal Guards reminded Charlemagne that he had used his Monocle of Refinement on them in the past, and it might be useful to take another look to see how he stacked up against them now.  So, the rooster activated the Item and studied the resulting data.  Although he couldn’t be sure, they seemed to have lost levels, possibly due to the partial lockout.

The second thing that was obvious was that the invaders were in the middle of enacting some sort of plan.  An almost equal number of powerful-looking beings were continuously reinforcing the powerful mana shield that protected a cube-shaped area in the middle of the throne room.  Inside the cube stood a four-armed giant garbed in purple robes, who was chanting words that made Charlemagne’s ears hurt.  In its two lower hands, the enormous figure carried crystal spheres that were difficult to look at directly: the rooster’s gaze seemed to slide right off of them.  In one of its upper hands, the creature held a staff, while the other upper hand held a thin stick.  The gaudily-arrayed giant’s choice of weapons confused Charlemagne, who found them largely impractical for combat.

So great was the rooster’s focus on the battle below that it took him several long seconds to realize that Phasima hadn’t stopped shouting since she had entered the throne room.

“Elidi!  Elidi! It’s me!  It’s me, Phasima!  Hey!  Hey!  Up here! Sister!  Sister!  It’s me!”

Charlemagne looked over at the frantic Vessarian, confused.  The two women did not look at all like they belonged to the same species.  But, he reasoned, Phasima probably knew her sister better than he did.  With that thought in mind, he willed his Monocle to activate.

She also seemed to have been weakened since their last encounter, which was fortunate for the invading force.  Otherwise, the beam would have easily broken through the stalemate and wiped out the invading force.  On the other hand, if the partial System lockout had been slightly less partial, it seemed likely that the Elite Royal Guards would have been cut down by the invading forces.

Feeling curious, the rooster attempted several times to activate his Monocle of Refinement while targeting one of the enemies, but the ability failed every time.  The failure added to Charlemagne’s hesitation.  The Elite Royal Guards were strong, but they were nothing compared to the Sun Queen, who could easily wipe them out in moments by redirecting her solar beam.  Likewise, each member of the invading force seemed to be powerful in their own right, at least within their force field, but their leader radiated a dark power that gave the rooster pause.  For the first time in a long while, he did not feel compelled to jump into combat immediately.

As if sensing his hesitation to get involved, the Squiggles returned with a message, one that Charlemagne didn’t think was possible.

You have been issued a System Quest!  The invaders in the Sun Queen’s throne room have caused a partial System lockout, weakening integrated entities within the dimension.  Worse, they are in the middle of a ritual that will cut off this entire dimension from the System, killing all integrated entities and robbing the System of vital energy.  Defeat the caster of the ritual to end the threat and restore full System access.

Reward: Variable based on performance

Charlemagne was shocked: he had not expected the Squiggles to issue a ‘Quest’ of their own, believing that was beyond their purview.  The realization sparked several emotions that the rooster did not have time to process in the heat of the moment.

“Bawk?” he asked Phasima, who had graduated from hurling words to hurling large icicles at the barrier.

“Yeah, yeah, I saw it too.  Rewards won’t do us a lick of good if we’re dead, though.  We have to figure out a way to help my sister without attracting too much attention.”

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked, confused.

“You think I’m doing this because I think it will help?”  The former Empress of the Forest laughed angrily.  “I got nothing better to do is all.  Well, there’s one thing, but I’m not sure if Elidi would want me to do it…”

“Perhaps, working together, we could overwhelm the barrier with a heatwave?” Phatagin suggested from the doorway.

“Teem has been analyzing the barrier for a weakness,” added Ser Teem.  “Teem has not yet found one, unfortunately.”

“Oh, your great idea is a heat wave?” Phasima responded, her voice dripping with sarcasm.  “Do you not see the power of a living star being bent to my sister’s will?  The amount of heat she’s generating would cook us all if she wasn’t also containing it within the beam.”

There was a moment’s silence as the group digested that particular fact.  Then, Phatagin lifted a claw.
