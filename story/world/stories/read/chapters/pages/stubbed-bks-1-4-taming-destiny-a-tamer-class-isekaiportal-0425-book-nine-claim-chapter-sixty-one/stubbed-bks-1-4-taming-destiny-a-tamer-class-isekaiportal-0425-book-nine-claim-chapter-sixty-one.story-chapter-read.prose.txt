I’ve only just begun the council meeting when I suddenly feel something go very, very wrong with one of my Bonds.

I stop mid-sentence and focus, searching for the source. My thoughts go immediately to Bastet, who is once again patrolling our territory, but a quick check confirms she’s fine. The same goes for Ninja and Lathani, who are out with the samuran group along with the Warriors. The Pathwalkers are fine – currently working with their respective masters. Even Trouble, despite his recent mutterings about doing something drastic to force his Evolution, is not in danger right now.

This is a newer Bond.

“Markus?” Layton asks warily as I shoot to my feet.

“I need to go,” I reply urgently. “Continue without me. Josiah knows what we need to discuss.”

Without giving anyone the chance to question me, I hurry from the room almost at a run. As soon as I’m out of the building, I spread my wings, launching into the air above the command centre.

As I near the exit to the cavern, I’m forced to skim low over the rooftops where the ceiling drops, but the guards take one look at me and throw the gates open without hesitation.

By now, the others in the Bond network have felt it too. Questions press in from all sides – questions I have no idea how to answer.

I don’t know what’s wrong, I tell them firmly. But I’m going to find out.

I follow the pull of the Bond and find Artio, once more half-buried in vines. This time, I know better what to look for. And because I can compare, I would know something is wrong even without the Bond’s warning.

Unlike before when he seemed to be meditating, now he almost seems dead. He’s utterly still, and barely breathing. He doesn’t twitch even when I stride closer to him.

I try to brush the vines away from his face but they cling tightly to the feathers, almost seeming to be part of him.

I clench one hand into a fist that shakes with my fear. Should I cut the vines away? Or is that likely to hurt him?

Torn by indecision, I reach through the Bond to him, only to feel what alerted me in the first place. The steady hum of emotions that tells me my companions are fine is absent. It feels like I’m touching an empty shell – the Bond is still there; his body is still present and warm, but every trace of self-awareness is absent.

I run through every diagnostic I can manage, far beyond what any mundane healer could attempt without tools. There’s no poison in his system, and his bodily functions are working perfectly, though slowly. It’s more like he’s gone into hibernation.

For a moment, I wonder if that’s exactly what’s happened. Perhaps owl-bears hibernate like actual bears do? If so, it’s pretty poorly timed considering how Artio was supposed to be helping me with the food situation.

Then I dismiss the thought as ridiculous. Bears hibernate when the weather turns – it’s just as hot and humid now as it was when we arrived. Besides, bears presumably know when their hibernation is coming – Artio would surely have warned me if it was something he was expecting. Then there’s the fact that he’s not responding at all – I remember reading something about bears being able to respond to threats even during their hibernation. While I’m not a threat, the urgency I’m projecting along the Bond should rouse him if he’s just gone into a deep state of sleep.

Which all goes to indicate that this, whatever it is, isn’t natural or anticipated.

Anxiety almost overwhelming me, I close my eyes and take in a deep breath. On the exhale, I slip into Artio’s Core space.

All of that looks normal – except for one thing: a thick channel that leads from one of the dense collections of lines that symbolises Artio’s abilities and out of his Core space entirely. It sticks out to me because, unlike the deep forest green that the rest of his internal matrix is formed of, this one is a bright green shot through with gold.

I touch it hesitantly, fearing what might happen if I interfere with it too much. As soon as my presence comes into contact with it, I feel him – faint, distant, but more present there than anywhere else.

My stomach drops as I become convinced of what’s gone wrong: he’s gone too deep. Too far into the connection he forged with that ancient rootstock.

I try to reach him through the channel, calling gently, carefully…but there’s no response. No flicker of awareness.

Calling more loudly this time, I wait again.

No response.

I hover for a moment, uncertain about what to do.

I could sever the connection and hope it snaps him back.

But what if it doesn’t? If someone had severed my connection with my body when my consciousness was so far into the fire surrounding me that I lost all awareness of it, would I have returned to my body? Or would I have been lost?

If I sever the connection, Artio could be stranded wherever he is now, unable to make it back to his body.

I don’t dare risk it.

Grumpy as he is, he’s one of us now and he got hurt doing something for us. I won’t risk abandoning him, not like that. But what can I do?

I run gentle fingers over the other channels in his Core space, searching to see if there’s one that calls more to him, testing to see whether touching the deepest part of him might remind him of who he is and where his body is.

Nothing.

I press some of my stored Energy into his internal matrix, pushing it through him in a wave as if I were trying to help him Evolve when he’s having trouble. The Energy pulses through him as it should, but that’s it. It feels horribly like when a body is on life support keeping its physical functions going, even after the soul has long-departed it.

This is at least a little better than that – I know Artio is alive. And his body is still functioning fine by itself. But what happens if he doesn’t return to it? He still needs to eat and excrete waste. His lungs and heart will stop working if they no longer have enough energy to keep going. And if his body is lost, the rest surely will be too.

I have magic now – I can keep his body in good shape for him when he wakes up, I decide, refusing to use ‘if’.

Pulling back from my Core space, my heart starts pumping wildly as I find I’m no longer alone.

For a moment, all I see are the teeth and the way they’re stalking towards me, and I instinctively call my spear to me.

Then both vision and mind catch up with reality and relax – though the two who have approached me are predators, they’re not hostile.

“Were you hunting in the local area?” I ask Artemis and Orion, tucking my spear away in my Inventory again. I belatedly realise how reckless I was – entering a deep trance in the forest with only an unresponsive companion keeping me company.

We were, Orion rumbles. We felt it. Trouble.

What’s happened? Artemis asks anxiously, coming closer and nosing at Artio, inhaling deeply as she presses her snout through the vines. Fear practically pours off her in waves. She and Orion have grown closer to Artio than the others – probably because when Artemis has decided to do something, it takes a strong soul to change her mind. Artio might have managed to push most of my companions away with his standoffishness and his screeches whenever they try to cuddle, but Artemis is made of sterner stuff. And secretly, I think Artio likes it – that someone cares enough to keep trying.

But that does mean that the two deris will be the most affected of the group. That’s probably why they’re here so quickly.

“He’s gone too deep into a connection with the plants around us,” I tell them solemnly. “I don’t think he can find his way back.”

Artemis shivers, and I sense her remembering back to her old pack, slaughtered by an invasion of samurans. She presses her muzzle deeper through the vines and into the hollow of Artio’s throat. There, smelling his scent, feeling his warmth, and hearing his heartbeat, however slow, she relaxes.

What can we do? Orion asks, less emotionally engaged but still solemn.

I sigh.

“I’m not sure there’s anything we can do,” I admit. “Except call to him. Keep the Bond open. Give him something to follow back. And keep his body in good condition.”

We will stay with him, Artemis offers immediately, physically matching action to her words as she lies next to him, pressed against the vines that tie his body down. I was considering shifting him back to the cavern so we can better care for him, but perhaps she has a point – I’m not sure those vines are coming off. Certainly not without a fight. And while they’re holding him firm, I didn’t see any evidence that they’re sucking life from him or something like that. Instinct tells me that they’re a symptom of the problem, not the problem itself.

“It’s probably a good idea that he has a guard,” I admit, then glance at Orion. “Are you OK with that?”

Orion waves his tail in what feels like nonchalance.

Loran is too busy to hunt together. Hunt or guard – it matters not to me.

“All right, thank you,” I tell them gratefully. At least that’s a load off my mind. “I’ll still check in on him from time to time, but if I know he’s being watched over by companions who care for him, then he’ll be as safe as we can make him.”

I glance between Artemis and Orion. “I’ll arrange for some of our companions to bring you food. Perhaps the samuran hatchlings can bring some of their kills on their way back from hunting.”

Orion huffs, the mental equivalent of laughter.

If those hatchlings manage to kill anything at all, he remarks dryly. The last time we went out with them, most were far more interested in the mud, the stones, and pelting each other with both than in actual hunting.

Even though my lips feel stiff, my memory of the state in which they returned the other day forces the corners of my mouth to curve up in a small smile. Everyone was absolutely covered in mud – including the minders. Even Lathani hadn’t escaped – two of the hatchlings had ambushed her with mud while she was trying to break up a scuffle. Orion might be amused now, but he certainly wasn’t at the time – they all had to go and bathe in the sea to be rid of it all.

“They’re improving,” I say diplomatically, then sigh, my amusement fading as I consider the situation.

I know I should return to the council meeting, but I have to admit to some reluctance. After the discovery about Artio, I feel like I need a few moments just to be. I don’t get that much in the settlement – I have too many responsibilities; too many people demanding me.

Josiah does know what to discuss – and they can catch me up later, I justify to myself.

As if the universe is trying to offer me an excuse – or possibly telling me that I can’t have a moment to myself, no matter where I go – the forest fills with noise. Clicks. Chattering teeth. The scrape of claws through undergrowth.

Orion and I exchange glances with Artemis: the samuran hatchlings have arrived, heralded, as always, by their complete inability to be quiet. There are times when I wonder whether the samuran’s way of doing things was effective after all – those who survived the first year were those who were actually capable of both hunting and sneaking. Those that couldn’t were the casualties.

It reminds me that I left the samurans’ world long before the hatchlings in the village grew up. I trust that the same won’t be true here.

They spill into the clearing, now just over half the size of adults, and far more interested in each other than in Artio’s still form. The tension in the air affects the adults far more than it does them.

One – no, two – of them immediately prove Orion’s point, diving into a patch of mud and trying to smear it into each other’s ears.

My brief flash amusement fades quickly as the minders converge on me, expressions tight with worry. No doubt they herded their little group here the moment they were alerted to an issue.

“What happened?”

I explain as clearly as I can, but before I’m finished, Bastet arrives, and then others, and soon I’m repeating myself again and again.

Eventually, I get frustrated and reach out through the Bond to inform everyone at the same time.

Artio has gone too deep, I tell them all. He connected himself to something he called an ‘ancient and extensive rootstock’ and I think it was too strong for him. I can feel him – kind of – but I suspect he can’t find his way back.

Responses come quickly, suggestions of what to do; ideas of how to help.

I’ll speak to Sera Kerst, River says. There may be a potion that can gently rouse someone in a state like this.

And I will speak to Master Mirena, Hunter adds. There may be a rune combination that can locate – or guide – a displaced spirit.

Happy doesn’t offer a solution. Instead, she sends a steady pulse of support and quiet determination – an unspoken promise that she will do whatever she can. Her feeling is echoed by all of those around.

Even with anxiety tightening my chest, I feel a flicker of pride.

Whatever else we are, when one of us is in danger, the others do not hesitate to step forward and help.

We’re stronger together. I can only hope that it’s enough.
