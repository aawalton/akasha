Bone spear in hand, a new lightstone showing the way, Ferris and Jade headed out into the darkness to hunt.

They began by harvesting qi from the Air Passage. It needed to be done anyway, and the place often attracted demons.

Soon after entering, Ferris spotted one of the cloaked demons hovering just above the trench. The vermin were stealing qi chunks, and Ferris finally could risk confronting one.

Leveling his spear, Ferris ran at the thing. About halfway into his charge, the demon screeched and… ran away.

He wasn’t sure if the cloaked demons could see. It had a cloak over its face, without visible eyes. Previously, he’d always been able to notice them and cover up his light without any reaction. Now it appeared that they could detect him somehow. Perhaps they were near-sighted? Or maybe it’d just heard him?

Ferris was surprised to see it run away, but even if he’d been ready, he wouldn’t have been able to catch the demon. It was much faster than him, and soon disappeared into the darkness.

Off balance, Ferris stopped chasing and returned to Jade. He’d been amped up for a fight, only to be let down.

Continuing to circle around the Air Passage collecting qi, they saw two more of the cloaked demons. Both ran away as soon as Ferris got close, easily able to outpace him. Compared to them, Ferris was beginning to feel as slow as the chain demons. Why couldn’t the cowardly things just act like the other dumb demons and blindly charge him?

He gathered much less qi from the Air Passage than from the Digest River, but every bit counted. Ferris needed a new plan to keep the cloaked demons from stealing qi, now that fighting them wasn’t an option. Maybe he could track them to their source, or build defenses for when he was gone. He thought of the refinement device, and its six large ballistae along the walls. The library also had a jade slip on trapmaking that might help, although it cost merit and time to learn. He might be able to hit one of the cloaked demons with a crossbow bolt, but it would likely just run away wounded.

After dropping the qi chunks in the pool, Ferris and Jade headed off towards the chain fort. At least these demons will fight back, Ferris thought with a grumble.

Unfortunately, the first group they encountered had three chain demons. Ferris wasn’t ready to fight that many without a ranged weapon and ended up having to run away. The irony of complaining about the cowardly cloaked demons was not lost on him, but he resolutely ignored it.

Three groups later, he found an acceptable combination: two blue frogs with two chain demons. Ideally, Ferris would fight a group with only one chain demon, but he had never seen the fat things without a matching partner.

The blue frog demons died easily to Ferris and Jade’s combination. He had to re-teach the plan to Jade, but his familiar easily understood and coordinated over the bond. Ferris knocked one down, then focused on the other while Jade finished off the first.

The two chain demons were a larger problem. Jade may have forgotten, but Ferris distinctly remembered how easily one of the monsters had killed the slime with a single swipe of a chain. Jade could not enter within range of the chains unless Ferris had them fully locked down, so he asked the slime to stay back as he fought.

Excited at the chance to test the limits of his new bone spear, Ferris began checking the range of the two demons. He had the advantage of speed, but they had a larger reach and would win in a two-on-one grapple. So, Ferris poked and retreated at the limit of the demon’s range, forcing them to swing the chains at him but not allowing them to entangle him.

The first time he’d fought one of these demons, his weapon had been caught by a chain. Once that had happened, the chain had tightened and locked, keeping the shaft from sliding out.

Now, Ferris danced on the edge. A chain would swing in. He would deflect or dodge, pressing slightly closer each time. Deflecting had to be done carefully, with enough force and angle to avoid getting caught.

Individually, each chain was no match for him. If the demon only had one or two chains, Ferris could have knocked them aside, dashed in for an attack, and retreated safely. The problem was, the demons had at least a half dozen lengths of chain each, all swinging back in as fast as they could.

The dance soon fell into a pattern. Ferris would press closer, dodging or knocking chains aside until he reached his limit, and then be forced back out of range. He would then circle for positioning, then repeat. At first, he was getting closer each time, but soon Ferris hit a threshold. There were too many chains. Once he was in range of both demons, the difficulty increased beyond his skill.

He’d hoped that when the two demons overlapped, they would get tangled, but the magically moving chains would contort and weave around each other. They did appear to prefer swinging attacks, rather than shooting straight at him. Whenever they tried a direct attack, there was much less momentum.

Although deadlocked in a stalemate, Ferris was exhilarated. Each step of the fight was pressing him to the razor’s edge, a single mistake away from failure. The bone spear seemed to respond to his desires with supernatural ease, gracefully moving around him. He reveled in the fight, poking and prodding the chain demon’s defensive range for any weakness, yet as the contest continued… he felt there was something missing.

Ferris considered the only rift he had ever been in, when his uncle and the delver team from home escorted him to Scalefort Bastion. The group had fought the larger tier two clawling with hit-and-run tactics. The first time Valda attacked, she’d been surprised by a powerful ability the clawling used… but it had only worked once. After that, she darted in to bait it out while Dan and Dean took advantage of the distraction to attack from behind.

Compared to them, he was missing the coordination of a team. His enemy had a defense he couldn’t break through. He needed to force a more advantageous situation. Valda had used feints and water magic to provide that opportunity for Dan and Dean, he could do something similar with Jade—albeit without the magic.

Idea formed, Ferris positioned both chain demons between himself and Jade. The slime would die quickly inside the chain’s attack range, but Jade was able to move slightly faster than the demons.

Carefully, Ferris directed Jade to press in on the demons from behind. The slime would never fully engage, but started to bait out attacks. As soon as a chain swung towards Jade, the slime retreated out of range.

The dance continued like this for a couple of cycles. Ferris would press in and retreat while Jade baited one or two chains. Then, slowly, Ferris and Jade began pulling the demons apart. The chain demons naturally tended to stay together. However, they also had an animalistic desire to charge the closest enemy. Eventually, Ferris and Jade were able to separate them just enough that Ferris’ spear could reach one without the other’s support.

Like a viper, Ferris struck the isolated demon. He was just barely able to weave his way through the maze of chains, darting in for a single strike before retreating. One attack was all he needed—a piercing jab smoothly extended from a parry—and the fat demon fell with a wound to the head. Ferris jumped back, unchained.

After the first chain demon fell, the second soon followed. Ferris repositioned it away from the first demon’s corpse—­­­no point in taking risks—and finished it off just like its comrade.

Standing over the bodies of the demons, Ferris took a moment to catch his breath. Most fights were over quickly, but the prolonged stalemate had required him to push his mental body to its limit.

As the rush from the battle cooled, his excitement grew. He’d just gotten better with the spear in an hour. He’d spent years training every day. He was far past seeing any short-term improvements. Cultivation had secrets that defied logic, and each discovery was a new thrill.

Jade had fully consumed one of the blue frogs, but Ferris tied the other frog corpse to his belt. He didn’t have the strength to carry two of the pudgy chain demons, but he wanted all the chains he could collect.

Looking at the extra corpse, Ferris considered his discussion with Serena about the Acid Dismantle ability. Beyond acquiring it for his job assignment, it would provide a lot of utility day to day. He had no current use for the flesh, but the bones and chains were very useful. In the future, he imagined he would find other combinations of intent forming and body parts. What if he made a lightstone with the demon’s eye, or tested the stomach lining for acid resistance… What could Ferris make with a demon’s brain?

Serena had suggested a magical item that punished Jade when the slime ate the wrong thing, providing a problem for Adaptability to solve. Ferris liked the concept, but not the punishment or expensive item. He’d begun looking for a reward to give Jade when the slime did good, giving positive reinforcement. The problem was that all his rewards were food, which Jade could just get by eating the entire corpse. He’d intended to find more desirable food to provide motivation, but now he had a new idea.

Today’s conversation with Jade made Ferris reconsider the plan. Jade was not an animal to be trained, but a partner to cooperate with. Jade was complex enough to have desires and motivations beyond food, even if his familiar still felt a bit juvenile. Assimilate Object came from a desire for freedom, not just avoiding pain or seeking food.

Ferris remembered the night he’d cried himself to sleep after discovering the oath statue was missing. In that moment, he’d hated chaining Jade down as much as he resented his oath.

So, he’d try talking to Jade, and find things the slime enjoyed.

Beyond eating, Jade had shown a love for rating food. Could the two of them use that to provide a framework for Adaptability?

“Jade,” Ferris said, “would you like to play a new game?”

Jade swirled a bit, but then rolled over and rippled happily.

“How does new game work?” Jade asked.

“The idea is to encourage Adaptability to give us special fruit,” Ferris wasn’t sure how much Jade would remember, but he had already been burned by not discussing his plans. Jade hadn’t known anything about the ability earlier, so it wasn’t likely to help. It did, however, feel good to talk about.

“Delicious fruit heal Ferris! I play game.” Jade bounced up and down a bit.

“Great,” Ferris smiled at Jade’s antics, “now, this game uses a rating, just like when Jade rates food.”

“Jade remembers, Ferris’ shoe six out of ten!” The slime kept bouncing.

“Yes,” Ferris sighed, “like that. Instead, this time I will give you a rating after you eat.”

Jade stopped bouncing and started swirling.

“I don’t understand,” the slime said.

“Let me show you,” Ferris took Jade over to the extra corpse, “now, first taste this chain. Try to remember how it tastes.” Ferris fed a length of the chain that was wrapped around the demon’s body to Jade.

“Chain three out of ten!” Jade said with a ripple, eating usually cheered them up.

“Now, this is the demon’s body.” Ferris placed the corpse’s hand in the slime’s body, a little queasy at the close-up view of the hand melting away.

“Four out of ten!” Jade said. Ferris noticed the number was different; in the past, Jade had rated the chain demon entirely as a three.

“Okay, now. Eat as much of the body as possible, but as little of the chain. Then, I will give you a rating based on how much chain is left.” Ferris pushed a lot of mental pictures along with the explanation. He imagined the rating scale, with a large pile of chains at a ten, and then progressing on down the line until nothing was left at zero.

Acid Dismantle should be able to separate highly connected tissue, dissolving a thin line between different parts. The chain was wrapped around—and sometimes gruesomely embedded in—the demon’s corpse. Ferris couldn’t think of a better starting point.

Jade didn’t need to be told to eat anything twice, and barely waited for the explanation before rolling on top of the corpse. The results were… poor. Jade tried, but the slime had a difficult time not eating two things pressed together. They could either make their body permeable or solid, but struggled with close transitions between the two. The chain was tightly wrapped or connected in many places, getting eaten along with the flesh.

Afterwards, Ferris stood over the remaining chains and gave his judgment.

“Three out of ten.”

Jade deflated, then rolled around in a circle a few times.

“Game hard! I want to eat it all!” Jade bubbling in frustration.

“Yes, it is hard,” Ferris shrugged, “but we get better with practice. Try to think about it as a problem you need solved, so that Adaptability helps you.”

Jade was unconvinced, still bubbling.

“Practice,” Ferris continued, “means eating more.”

That got Jade’s attention.

“Must practice!” Jade rippled, “I eat more and more until I get ten out of ten, then eating even more!”

Ferris smiled as the slime started rolling around him again, but this time rippling.

“I start immediately, must practice more.” Jade started rolling towards the second corpse.

“Wait!” Ferris had to stop Jade, who reluctantly listened. “I need that corpse; we will have to hunt you another.”

Jade was back to bubbling, and wanted to go find more chain demons to ‘practice’ on. Ferris talked the slime into waiting, although he was encouraged by Jade’s determination.

The chain demon was heavy, and Ferris had to carry the frog and the extra chains on top of it. He went slowly, but they made it back to his Heartfire without any problems.

It was getting late, and Ferris had another full day of work ahead. He’d gotten distracted after making the bone spear, and then acquiring a new chain demon corpse had taken longer than he expected. He wanted to clear out the fort but was exhausted from the long day.

His only fear was for the oath statue. He’d checked on it the previous day, but not since. He didn’t think he could risk sleeping if it was actively in danger again.

Ferris made a quick trip to the slimespace to water the Core Tree, Jump, and Grow—although the cost hurt his Qi Pool.

Lastly, Ferris carried Jade and ran to check on the oath statue. Thankfully, the fort was unchanged from the previous day. There were slightly more demons about, but the statue wasn’t under attack. With a sigh of relief, Ferris and Jade returned to the Heartfire and woke up.

Looking around the room, the first thing Ferris did was release Jade’s chain from the dresser. It was cathartic, and Ferris never intended to tether the slime to it again. The chain would stay wrapped protectively around the core, but not as a leash.

Jade may still have problems with their memory, but Ferris at least trusted that his familiar would remember not to hurt him. The bond may be influencing him, but he decided to believe his feelings were more than that.

Ferris carried Jade to bed, then spent a moment just relaxing in the slime’s comfortable presence. Sleep took him unaware.
