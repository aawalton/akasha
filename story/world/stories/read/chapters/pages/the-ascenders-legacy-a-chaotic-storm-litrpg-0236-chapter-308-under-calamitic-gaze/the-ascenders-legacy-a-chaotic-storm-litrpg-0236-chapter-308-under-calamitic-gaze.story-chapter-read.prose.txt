The first thing Gump saw was a line of crimson. Then another. And another…

Pain slammed into him an instant later as over a dozen jagged chains of crackling electricity skewered through his torso, practically tearing him apart. Disorientation followed, and the next thing he knew, he was flying through the air in a dazed stupor, a shredded mess of blood and torn tissue.

For a single heartbeat, the entire arena held its breath—silent as they watched his body arc helplessly through the air. And then he hit the ground—his bones rattling as he crashed in a heap of pain, disgrace, and spinning stars.

The arena erupted in a roar of applause so loud the earth itself shook, the announcer bellowing. "UNBELIEVABLE! ABSOLUTELY UNBELIEVABLE! "

Right beside him, Yerxa was already clapping wildly. "By the storm, that was amazing! Isn't that boy a newbie like you, Aodhán?"

Aodhán smiled widely. "He is. He's also my neighbor."

Despite only meeting Crixis that morning, Aodhán felt a surge of pride at his victory. He had known Crixis was stronger than Gump and his friends, but witnessing it firsthand was a different thing entirely. Practically electric!

He hadn't expected the utter brutality with which Crixis had fought—like a man with a vendetta. There was no denying his skill with the spear, but it was those spiked chains that kept erupting from his body that truly shone, their crackling tips blazing with deadly intent.

They were just like the Medusozoa's limb skill he'd discarded, only they functioned on a different understanding of the storm than he possessed. Even now, as Crixis basked in the cheers and applause of the arena, Aodhán could sense that understanding rippling around him, not with his eyes, but with the intuition of a teasing inspiration.

Before he could fully grasp it, though, Rhetta nudged him. "Come on, let's get you a good seat. Gorvak must have registered your duel with the announcer, and you don't want to arrive at your first duel late."

Zarion said something amusing to buttress Rhetta's point, but Aodhán was barely listening as he followed them. His attention was on Crixis, whose face was now displayed on the four huge screens, a massive grin splitting his features as the announcer bellowed again.

"WHAT AN AMAZING TURN OF EVENTS! GIVE IT UP FOR OUR WINNER, CRIXIS MARCELLAN!!"

Another roar shook the earth, and the announcer laughed. "I MOST CERTAINLY DID NOT SEE THAT COMING! AND NEITHER DID MY BUDDY, FREDRICK, WHO JUST LOST A THOUSAND FREAKING SECT POINTS!!!!"

Laughter and boos erupted from the stands as the audience mocked all those who'd just lost money betting against Crixis. Groans and curses followed, and the announcer laughed again.

"OH, DON'T BE SOUR LOSERS, DISCIPLES! THIS IS AN OUTER SECT RARITY! FOR THE FIRST TIME IN OVER TEN YEARS, A NEWBIE BEATS A DISCIPLE SO SWIFTLY IT WOULD BE AN OVERSTATEMENT TO CALL IT A DUEL! THIS IS AN EPIC MOMENTTTT!"

More boos erupted this time, and Zarion chuckled. "Oh, they don't like that at all. As great as the newbie's win is, he's just toppled the social hierarchy, and the announcer isn't helping his case at—"

The rest of his words were cut off by a thunderous roar as Gump's friends raced down the stands, furious snarls on their faces. They rushed toward the ring, not out of concern for their bleeding companion who was already being tended to, but out of rage and stinging embarrassment.

Bloodlust seeped out of their cores in a violent wave, lightning crackling along their bodies as they rushed toward Crixis. The moment they entered the ring, however, the announcer called out sternly. "THIS DISPUTE HAS BEEN RESOLVED! YOUR REPRESENTATIVE HAS FALLEN! THE BOY HAS WON! "

Cries of agreement and disagreement erupted from the stands at the statement—almost everyone present echoing their opinions, speaking over each other in thunderous voices. Even Rhetta and her friends debated letting Gump's friends avenge him.

However, despite the number of those against the idea far outnumbering their counterparts, it was obvious that this dispute was far from settled. If Gump's friends left that stage without a battle, they would only come after Crixis again, perhaps in a more savage manner.

Better to settle it now. Even if Crixis lost the match, he wouldn't lose any face. He'd already earned everyone's respect.

Crixis seemed to have the same idea, and with a sigh, he said, "Let them come. I'd like to settle this once and for all."

His words were like a bucket of cold water on the cheering crowd, and for the second time since his match began, silence settled over the arena. Many looked down at him in sympathy, thoroughly against the idea, while others snarled down at him, muttering something about how proud newbies needed to be put in their place.

A few seconds passed in that silence, and then the announcer boomed. "FINE! I'VE BEEN OUTVOTED! LET THE DUEL COMMENCE!"

The arena erupted with noise as Gump's friends stepped into the ring, their faces twisted in rage.

They attacked immediately, a wave of lightning lashing out toward Crixis, but Crixis was ready for it. He clashed against his opponents in a burst of determination and violence, jagged chains of crackling red electricity erupting from his palms in brutal arcs.

But while the chains had been brutally effective against Gump, they failed against the combined power of his friends.

Kevar, the larger of the two, moved like lightning itself, blocking his attacks with constructs of solidified storm while Torven assaulted him with a barrage of lightning beams that arced out so fast they almost seemed to teleport.

Just from their fighting style, their paths were deducible. But their true strength came from teamwork—one attacking, the other defending, both hemming Crixis in, forcing him to work harder to avoid their attacks.

His exquisite footwork shone in that moment, a complex series of razor-precise movements and mastery, all working to buy him an extra—

Boom!

A beam detonated against Crixis's right leg, and he stumbled, hurled off his feet in an instant. Oohs and aahs rippled through the audience as he struck the ground hard. The announcer began to say something, but Crixis was back on his feet before the man could finish his joke.

And this time, he was angry.

His gaze locked with those of his opponents as they rushed toward him, and once again, Aodhán felt the stirrings of an inspiration deep in his mind. Crixis was doing something that resonated deeply with him, and the moment all three boys clashed, Aodhán finally grasped it.

Crixis held no weapon, had no bloodline, and yet in that moment seemed to grow ten times his original size. Strength coursed through his muscles so mightily and visibly that Aodhán felt like he could crush a man to powder with his bare fists.

And that was almost exactly what he did.

His fists slammed against Kevar's defenses, shattering through an array of storm constructs to clip his jaw. But that small contact was enough to send him flying, blood spurting from his mouth as he collapsed beside a cluster of healers who were still working on Gump.

"WHAT?!" The announcer shouted, his voice nearly buried in the roar of the arena. "DID YOU ALL SEE THAT? AM I CRAZY, OR DID THAT NEWBIE JUST BECOME LIKE TEN TIMES STRONGER?!"

Speculations erupted within the arena, excited whispers and confused mutterings filling the air, but Aodhán barely paid it any attention, his mind fixed solely on the still-ongoing battle. With Kevar now out of the fight, Torven had to face Crixis on his own.

And for the next few seconds, Aodhán had to admit, he did put up a decent fight, but he barely managed to endure a minute before falling to the same fate that had befallen his friends.

The arena erupted once more, excitement coursing densely through the air, and Aodhán himself smiled, unable to deny how impressed he was. Despite Crixis's tier, there was no doubt he wasn't meant for the outer sect. He was too strong. However, what truly set him apart was his understanding.

Aodhán hadn't realized it before, but it seemed there was an aspect to the storm he had been ignoring or, more appropriately, been ignorant of until now. He had always considered the storm a paragon of unpredictability and chaos. That understanding had shaped his skillset and even his control so far. But he realized now that his understanding was only one facet of the storm.

His mind flashed to the tournament trials before his kidnapping to Abyssos, how he'd found himself outclassed in nearly every category except spirit. He had considered his skillset too balanced at the time, but now he realized he was nowhere near balanced.

The storm was more than just one facet. And while he understood it now as movement, chaos, and unpredictability, it seemed Crixis understood it as pressure. As superiority and doom. But most especially, as a paragon of overwhelming, suffocating power.

That understanding granted him an array of skills far different from Aodhán's, and while the arena cheered Crixis's victory, Aodhán couldn't help but wonder just how wide the concept of cultivation really was.

It seemed like he was always picking new things up. However, with each addition, the scope of cultivation and power only got broader and broader. If he'd been working with only one facet of Storm since his arrival in Aeflym, then just how many facets were there? How many truths? How many paths were embedded in one affinity alone?

First, he had learned not to treat the storm as an element but as magic—one that could either be experienced as an abstract, a concept, or an element. And now, he was realizing that even that wasn't the limit. There was more to the storm than just lightning and thunder—so much more that his mind struggled to even comprehend it.

Even now he could feel it, a shudder in his mind—the result of an inspiration stirring yet unable to form because his mind was too weak to understand it yet. He could probably push, force himself to achieve another thing that should have been impossible for his tier, but Aodhán refrained, Az'marthon's words echoing in his mind.

You don't need more skills. What you have is already enough to make you overpowered. Maximize it.

Despite his curiosity, Aodhán knew Az'marthon was right. He would be doing himself a disservice trying to stretch his understanding further when he hadn't yet reached the peak of what he already had.

The unpredictability of the storm was a powerful path, and until he fully embodied it, there was no need to reach for more. Yet.

That didn't, however, mean he couldn't find out more about these… truths, and as Crixis exited the ring, Aodhán determined within himself to find out as much as he could. For that, he would need access to the sect's library, which he doubted he would get until his initiation.

That left him with only one other option.

High Elder Zarxan Al'ruhn.

If only there was a way to reach him.

Aodhán frowned, mind racing as he tried to figure out a way, but his thoughts were interrupted as the announcer bellowed excitedly.

"PULL OUT THOSE SECT POINTS, DISCIPLES, BECAUSE WE HAVE ANOTHER MATCH JUST LIKE THE LAST! CARE TO GUESS?"

Boos erupted from the stands, but the announcer just laughed. "WHAT A SOUR BUNCH WE'VE GOT THIS AFTERNOON! BUT IF YOU WON'T GUESS, THEN I'LL JUST TELL YOU! FOR OUR 91ST DUEL OF THE DAY, GIVE IT UP FOR ONE OF OUR MOST POPULAR DUELERS, GORVAK ASHHIDE!"

Cheers erupted from the stands so massively, Aodhán felt a sudden pinprick of nerves. Had he underestimated Gorvak?

The screen blurred to display Gorvak as he descended the stairs, and Yerxa gasped. "By the storm, they'll show us next. Rhetta, how am I looking?"

"Like a clown," Zarion replied with a smirk. "Have you forgotten, Yerxa? You're not the star of today's show. That's Aodhán."

Aodhán grimaced, but before he could respond, the announcer spoke. "AS FOR HIS OPPONENT, CAN YOU BELIEVE IT? WE HAVE ANOTHER NEWBIE! GIVE IT UP FOR AODHÁN BRYSTION!!"

Cheers and boos erupted from the audience as the screen blurred once more, and suddenly, Aodhán found himself on the screen, surrounded by Rhetta, Zarion, and Yerxa—all of whom looked suddenly constipated except Yerxa, who just looked like a bad model.

"Just go down there and give it your best shot. Don't be nervous," Rhetta advised him, looking far more nervous than he felt.

Zarion nodded. "It'll be like a bad dream when it's all over."

But aside from the spike of nerves he'd felt earlier, Aodhán didn't feel nervous at all. Gorvak was too weak to be a threat to him.

Nevertheless, he didn't want Rhetta to think he was being arrogant, so he said, "If I lose, I want you guys to help me drown my sorrow."

Rhetta's expression softened at that, and she nodded. "We'll buy you more spirits than you can ever drink. Just try not to break a bone. Those take a while to heal."

Aodhán nodded and began walking down the stands toward the ring where Gorvak stood waiting, crimson sword in hand and a furious snarl on his face. Cries of well wishes, mocking laughter, and boos filled the air as he descended.

Gorvak was obviously a fan favorite. Too bad he was about to nail him into the ground.

However, the moment he reached the ring, something very weird happened.

With a sound very similar to that of a bubble bursting, the entire arena suddenly fell silent—not quiet, but a silence so profound one could hear a pin drop.

A glance around the arena revealed that people were still speaking, shouting, and cheering, completely oblivious to the sudden silence he was currently experiencing.

Which meant he was the only one experiencing it. No, he was the only one aware of it.

He looked up, and his breath hitched at the sight above him.

Six Calamitic individuals stood in the air above the arena, the brightness of their runic irises like small suns against the backdrop of roiling storms—watching him.

Aodhán recognized Zarxan among them, and he swallowed nervously as he realized the highest powers of the Storm Sect had come out to watch him fight an outer sect disciple.

Now he was nervous.

Instinctively, he began to bow as he thought he should but found himself suddenly unable to—the motion dying in his nerves before his body could obey. He glanced up again, and Zarxan shook his head sternly.

Their intent was clear. Continue as if we weren't here.

And then the announcer's voice cut through the profound silence, along with the noise of the arena, the sound crashing into his mind like a sledgehammer after such profound quiet.

"AND WE HAVE OURSELVES OUR FIRST STALLER, DISCIPLES! I THINK HE'S WONDERING WHETHER TO RUN OR FALL ON HIS KNEES AND PLEAD!"

Laughter erupted once more, and from a corner of the arena, people began shouting. "PLEAD! PLEAD!! PLEAD!!!"

Aodhán took it all in with a grimace, wondering just how to proceed with Calamities watching him. The High-Elders had probably come for a show. But he sadly had no show to give them.

A show would require a suitable opponent, and Gorvak was most certainly not… that.

Grimace tightening, Aodhán began walking once more, prompting another joke from the announcer that caused the arena to erupt in riotous laughter. Even Gorvak laughed, enjoying his ridicule.

"You really should just plead," he taunted loudly as Aodhán stepped into the ring. "I promise, I'll forgive you."

More laughter erupted from the audience, and Aodhán realized they all thought he'd stalled because he was afraid. Gorvak thought he was afraid of him.

He couldn't have imagined just how wrong he was.

"ARE YOU READY?" The announcer asked, and Gorvak laughed. "Hell yeah! If he won't plead, then he'll bleed."

Another bout of laughter, and then the announcer shouted. "FIGHT!"

The moment the word left his mouth, Gorvak exploded with a dense wave of electricity, his body launching into the air even as a massive storm cloud rumbled into existence above him. Lightning crackled across its surface in violent bursts, and in an instant, a dozen spears of lightning materialized from it, hanging over the ring like a constellation of death.

Aodhán watched the display with a blank expression, wondering how best to handle the situation. For Gorvak, this was just a normal duel, but for him it was a test—his first chance to display his prowess to the High-Elders, which meant he couldn't simply hijack the spears from Gorvak and redirect them at him.

He had to end this battle in a noteworthy manner. Simple lightning manipulation wouldn't cut it. He had to do more.

"Do you feel small yet?" Gorvak snarled down at him, completely confident of victory. And when Aodhán didn't respond, he laughed. "This will teach you to respect your seniors."

With a burst of willpower, he raised a hand to the storm above and brought it down in a sharp arc. Instantly, the spears tipped downward, snarling with electricity. However, just before Gorvak could launch them, Aodhán acted.

Chaos and willpower surged within his spirit like an overwhelming tide, and with a single, sharp motion, Aodhán channeled it all into his newly evolved skill: {Leech Energy}.

But he hadn't realized just how brutal the skill had become, and with so much chaos and willpower backing it, it was practically overkill.

The skill locked Gorvak in place—frozen, spread-eagled in the air like a diagonal crucifix.

"What?!" Gorvak began in growing panic, but the rest of his words were cut off by an agonized scream as every iota of energy and electrical impulse was simply drained out of him in an instant.

It surged out of his core, his pathways, his body, hair, eyes, and toes—not a single part of him was left untouched, and despite the apparent weakness, all that energy together was significant indeed.

Aodhán gasped as the energy rushed into his pathways in an overwhelming flood of boiling currents, lighting his veins on refreshing fire. He directed it all into his core, savoring the ecstasy as it slowly expanded, blazing with a rising intensity not unlike that of a supernova.

And then it exploded, energy surging out of his core in a flood of reinvigorating currents as he broke into the 36th tier. The world bled away in the face of his advancement, the announcer's shocked exclamations a muddled whisper to his senses, and Gorvak's screams a distant echo.

But as significant as all that energy was, it wasn't nearly enough to push him up two tiers, and only a few seconds after his breakthrough, the energy stream cut off, leaving him feeling high and incomplete at the same time.

The world came back into focus with a sharper clarity—a chaotic blend of horrified screams, excited cheers, and absolute shock. Still high in the air, Gorvak elicited one last croak before collapsing to the ground like a sack of stone, still alive, but so utterly drained that he would probably be unable to lift a finger for days.

For a heartbeat, there was silence. And then an eruption of absolute, manic cheers as the entire stadium exploded in applause.

"BY THE STORM!!!" The announcer screamed in feverish excitement. "ANOTHER SHOCKING VICTORY! THESE NEWBIES ARE ON FIRE!!!"

His words incited even more excitement, and despite himself, Aodhán couldn't help but smile. He glanced at Gorvak's body and slowly walked to pick up the sword. Almost impossibly, the audience cheered louder the moment he did, laughter erupting so loudly lightning sparked across the arena.

But Aodhán barely paid it any attention. He looked up instead, expecting to see the High Elders, only to be met with an empty stretch of stormy skies.

The High Elders had vanished.

He glanced back at Gorvak's withered body as healers swarmed him and wondered if they'd been impressed or horrified.
