Aoife and Chad flew toward the desert, Whadesgart slowly growing smaller in the distance.

The dragon began to notice her wounds more now that her adrenaline had started to recede. The pain throughout her body confirmed what she had already begun to suspect: they would need to stop soon. She and the Hero could not fly far in her current condition.

“Shit!” Chad exclaimed, his body twisted as he looked toward their rear.

Then Aoife looked back, too. She felt her heart clench.

Behind them, the undead were boiling up over the walls of the distant town like an angry anthill someone had dowsed with boiling water. They were far enough away that the creatures really did look like an indistinct pile of ants, but it was still disconcerting.

“Shit!” Chad repeated. Then, in a different tone, “I think we woke the necromancer up after all…”

Aoife wanted to reply sarcastically, Oh, really? Mission accomplished, then, I suppose. You wanted to draw him out, right? Well done us!

“Why do you sound excited?” she said instead. “You were running away from half that number, or a quarter, maybe, and they didn’t have the necromancer leading them at the time.”

“Yeah, you do have a point, but there is the opportunity now to just kill the leader and end this once and for all!” She felt him excitedly fingering his sword and immediately needed to dampen his enthusiasm.

“That wouldn’t solve the problem, Chad. It would just free the creatures from his control.”

“How do you know that?” the Hero asked skeptically.

Gods, you know so little about the enemy we mean to deal with! How did you ever expect to win?

“I have already explained that I have some instinctual memories,” Aoife reminded him. “Left to me by ancestors who have dealt with certain problems before. The issue with the undead is not primarily addressing the leader. It is killing every single member of their group so that there is no seed from which the rotten fruit may spring again. Several types of undead can create more of their kind. They are notoriously persistent. It was always going to be a challenge to defeat an army of the creatures, and when we arrived, it was clear that this necromancer had far more under his command than we had anticipated.”

“Notoriously persistent. Wait, were you expecting this?” He gestured back at the undead now running through the sand - but still receding further behind them, since she could glide faster than they could dash.

“It was within the possibilities I considered when I realized what you were running from.”

“But if you knew this pursuit could be a problem, why didn’t you head for the ocean?”

“I need healing, Chad. I just wanted to get a little distance, so you could do your thing, and—”

“Well, we have to fly faster! We can’t afford to stop in the desert. They’re not exactly gaining on us, but just now, something appeared a lot closer! Two shapes—”

She looked back and saw two figures in the dunes perhaps a few hundred feet behind them.

Teleportation?

“Heal my wings, then! The catgirl shot a half-dozen spears through them. I just said - or implied - that I could not handle flying across the water again. You have been free riding for long enough. It is high time to earn your keep!”

He was concentrating his mana, starting to heal her wing injuries. It freed up her natural regeneration, which had been mainly helping her wings hold together, to begin working on her scales. Right now, from beneath, she knew she looked a bit like a giant plucked chicken. Soft, raw, and vulnerable.

Thank the gods. We might actually be able to—

No!
