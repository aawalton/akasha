The common room of the boarding house was a cramped, low-ceilinged space that smelled of stale ale and woodsmoke. Arthur pushed away his empty bowl of watery porridge. The meal was a necessity, a simple task checked off a list before he could move on to his primary objective: the princess. He knew from experience that he couldn't get the answers he needed while she was starving, so with his next step clear, he rose from his seat in the shadowed corner and approached the proprietor.

"I need a meal sent up to my room," Arthur said, his voice the flat, unremarkable tone of Zane. "Enough for two."

The proprietor stopped wiping and straightened up, his eyes narrowing. "You just ate, lad. You have quite the appetite, don't you?"

Arthur rubbed the back of his neck, affecting a weary, slightly embarrassed demeanor. "That just whetted my appetite," he said with a half-shrug. "Haven't had a decent meal since we left the forest yesterday afternoon."

The man grunted, his suspicion giving way to a grudging understanding. He took the coins Arthur offered. "Fine. I'll have one of the girls bring it up."

With the order placed, Arthur nodded and turned away, heading for the stairs. As he climbed, his senses were on high alert. He scanned the hallway, the other closed doors, listening for any sound out of the ordinary. Nothing. Everything was as it should be: the quiet desperation of a low-tier boarding house, where everyone was too wrapped up in their own troubles to notice anyone else's.

He entered his room and slid the heavy iron bar across the door, securing it. He lay on the lumpy straw mattress, waiting. He didn't want any interruptions once his conversation with the princess began. A few minutes later, a soft knock came at the door.

A young woman in her early twenties entered, balancing a tray with two steaming bowls of stew. She was shy, her gaze darting around the cramped room before settling on him. A faint blush colored her cheeks as she placed the bowls on the edge of the mattress, there being no other surface available.

"Will... will there be anything else, sir?" she asked, her voice soft, her eyes holding a hopeful, suggestive glint.

Arthur saw the potential for complication, a thread that could unravel his carefully constructed identity. He had seen it a hundred times in his old life: a casual fling, a moment of weakness, leading to exposure and failure. "No," he said, his tone polite but firm. "That will be all."

Her blush deepened, this time with a hint of disappointment. She gave a quick curtsy and hurried out, closing the door softly behind her.

The moment the lock clicked, Arthur was up. He sent a wave of mana to his ears, extending his senses to capture every creak and breath in the boarding house. The woman's footsteps faded down the hall. A man coughed in the next room. The distant clatter of pots from the kitchen below. The room was not a fortress, but as long as they kept their voices low, it would be secure enough.

He closed his eyes, and the invisible mask on his face dissolved. The sharp, unremarkable features of Zane softened, melting back into his own face. Only then did he reach for the ring.

With a silent command, Princess Ysara materialized in the cramped space.

The sudden transition was jarring. She stumbled, her hand flying to her mouth as a wave of nausea washed over her. She swayed, eyes wide with disorientation as she took in the squalid surroundings.

"Sit," Arthur said, his voice calm.

She hesitated, her gaze flickering from his face to the lumpy mattress, the only place to sit. Internally, a cold knot of fear tightened in her stomach. This is it, she thought, her heart hammering. He's making his move. The room was small, intimate, and she was entirely at his mercy. He was handsome, in a severe sort of way, and she still believed him to be the scion of some powerful A-grade family, but the thought of losing her chastity to a stranger, a captor, was horrifying.

Steeling herself, she sat stiffly on the edge of the cot, her posture rigid, her hands clenched in her lap.

Arthur gestured to the stew. "Eat."

The rich, savory smell of the stew hit her, and her stomach betrayed her with a loud, embarrassing rumble. A hot flush of shame spread across her cheeks. First, she had vomited in front of him, and now this. It was profoundly unladylike.

But she was starving. She hadn't eaten since before the ambush, and though little time had passed for her in the ring's stasis, her body was screaming for sustenance. She picked up a bowl. With a discipline born of years of royal training, she forced herself to eat with a slow, deliberate grace, each movement a small war against the desperate urge to devour the meal.

Her eyes met his a few times as she ate, and each time, she quickly looked away, the blush returning to her cheeks. Arthur simply watched, his expression unreadable, waiting patiently for her to finish.

When the last spoonful was gone, he broke the silence. "Ysara, I have something important to discuss with you. Your response will determine how we work together going forward."

She had been expecting something else entirely, and the sudden shift to a serious, almost formal tone caught her off guard. She quickly masked her surprise, her posture straightening as she gave a small, regal nod for him to continue.

He leaned forward slightly, his gaze intense. "Why did you heal slaves in the past?"

The question was a statement, not an inquiry. He wasn't asking if she had done it; he already knew. He was demanding to know her motive. Ysara felt a chill run down her spine. This was the test. She could lie, try to spin a tale of political maneuvering, but the soul pact was a chain around her words. And besides, if he was going to look down on her, to scorn her as all the other nobles did, then so be it. She was tired of hiding.

"My mother died giving birth to me," she began, her voice quiet but steady. "My family... they consider me an ill omen. A curse that took their beloved queen. My father is the only reason I'm still alive, and only because he says I have her eyes."

She looked down at her hands, the memory a familiar ache. "I wasn't raised by nobles. I was raised by my mother's maids. Slaves. They were the only ones who ever treated me as a person, not a title or a tragedy. The kindness they showed me... it was the only real warmth I knew."

A single tear escaped and traced a path down her cheek. "So I help them. To return that kindness. It's the only thing that feels right." She looked up at him then, her eyes pleading. "Please... don't order me to stop. Don't ask me to be like the others. That would be the same as killing me."

She braced herself for his judgment, for the cold dismissal she had come to expect.

But instead, Arthur smiled. A small, rare thing, but it reached his eyes, softening something sharp in his face.

"Oh no," he said softly, the sound of his voice sending a strange shiver through her. "You misunderstand me, Princess."

He leaned a little closer.

"I'm going to expect the contrary from you."
