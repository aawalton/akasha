“There’s two types; templars are just humans who’ve been trained in combat. They’re harmless. The paladins, though…”

Embriel led us through the frigid tunnel. It curved away on either side, carrying the awful cacophony and choking every breath. Saffine, Kallian, and two other men disappeared down one hallway, chatting and laughing as they walked. Aravis debated for a moment, then tugged Liana along after them.

I watched her go, horror yawning like a pit beneath my feet. But something else came, too. Rage born of sorrow. Wild, righteous wrath, so strong it shook me to my core.

Perhaps I couldn’t help her at the moment. But I would someday, or I’d die trying, and that meant I couldn’t fall to pieces right now.

Embriel led the rest of us into a cold open-air space. We stepped onto an expanse of flat, circular black rock lit by the glowing gray sky high above. And between rock and sky… winding tunnels made up the Pit’s first few stories. They curved around the arena, with hundreds of holes scattered throughout. It looked like an enormous, hollowed-out beehive. Further up, where jagged stone arched towards grim gray, tunnels gave way to lit sections of carved, balconied rock. Daises. Places to watch.

And there was enough blood stained beneath my feet to imagine exactly what they watched.

“Garruck,” Embriel called out, “mind grabbing a pair? One of each.”

A hulking gray figure with great, leathery wings was scraping its way through the arena. Jagged claws screeched an ear-piercing whine against the stone, leaving grooves in their wake.

Familiar grooves, and in a familiar pattern. It’s just like the barrow. He’s carving a giant rune.

The teeth-grinding sound fell silent. The creature turned its flat-nosed visage towards us, stared with hooded black eyes, then prowled close. It moved slowly, almost sluggishly, but the unhurried kind of slow that belied danger. Like a grizzly bear pacing simply because it saw no reason to run. Its wings stretched and curled with each step, as if stroking the air. Sharp black claws tipped their ridges.

Embriel thought for a moment. “Get Fenwick, not Avetta. He’s stronger.”

“Yes.” Its voice was like dead, rattling leaves against hollow stone. Bone-dry, with an empty echo behind it. His face was man-like, though warped and broad, with broken, tusk-like teeth. Long-healed scars hatched most of his massive, muscular body.

I shivered at the creature’s approach. Embriel called him ancient. I wonder how ancient?

Those empty, black eyes slid to me. Slitted nostrils flared. Then he lurched on, disappearing into a tunnel.

“This is your portal?” Velidan sounded stunned. He was tracing the ground with narrowed eyes. “It’s massive.”

It seemed he wasn’t the only one surprised. The other Fae were pacing along, whistling and glancing at their host in obvious confusion.

Embriel swept both hands wide, snickering as he spun a languid circle. “Size counts for something, gentlemen. And of course it’s here; can you think of a better place for a river of blood? But that part comes later; first, we need to meet our competition!”

One of the Fae coughed. “Competition?”

“In defending the helpless humans, of course.” Embriel snickered. “None of you have ever seen a paladin, but trust me when I say that besides Anduin and Thalessa, they’re the only thing we need to worry about. And there’s going to be about…” he tapped one fingernail against his teeth, “a dozen of them, I think my humans said? They’re all riled up and will be waiting for us once the door opens.”

I stared at him, drinking in every word. My skin was practically vibrating. This. This is what we’re here for. It’s not every detail laid out, but it’s something.

Clearly, Velidan was of a similar mind. “And we’re to fear these paladins? Why?”

“Not fear exactly. They can’t kill us. But- oh. Perfect timing.”

Bile flooded my insides as Garruck reappeared, dragging two men with him. One was younger, perhaps around my age, with cropped brown hair and wild eyes. The other was graying, with deep lines around his mouth and a few days’ worth of stubble. Both were shackled, gagged, and smeared by grime and blood. Garruck dropped them to the ground, then stepped on the older man’s chest. Holding the stone-faced figure in place.

The High Fae circled around. Some grinned and chuckled while others bared their teeth.

They’re like a pack of wolves. Blood roared like an ocean in my ears as one hand plunged towards my dagger. Impotently. The hilt felt firm and fierce, but of course it wouldn’t truly do anything. Saints, how can I possibly-

“Heal the old one, Vel. I want to make sure you can before we start.”

“Is that in doubt?” He frowned at the Thalessians.

“It is, actually. Watch.” A twist of his hand sent tendrils of shadow curling around the Thalessian’s gag. It snapped like a thread. Then they slithered up into his matted, shoulder-length hair. After some straining, one gray knot ripped free. “That should have been easy. It wasn’t. I can’t glamour him, either. Go on, see if your magic works.”

Velidan knelt. Silver welled and washed, and the man’s cuts and bruises faded.

“Excellent,” Embriel purred. “Now, gentlemen, these warriors are formidable. They also handle torture annoyingly well, so I don’t know a dawned thing about why they’re so tough. And their subordinates,” he waved a hand at the younger man, who lay white-faced with his nostrils flaring, “can be glamoured, but they don’t know what makes the older ones special. Just that it’s some kind of blessing.” He crouched over the paladin, poking at him like a petulant child. “Obviously it’s something to do with Thalessa, but he won’t tell me what. Will you, Fenwick?”

The man stared skywards.

Embriel rolled his eyes, rising. “Fine. Keep being stubborn. Now, gentlemen… we’ve got a fight ahead of us tonight. Paladins can’t kill us, but they can withstand a lot and trust me when I say they can do some damage. This one nearly gutted me the other day. So we’re going to do a little experimentation; see what magic does work on them in a nice safe space. And feel free to compare and contrast on our little templar, here, so you can get a sense of the difference. We can even make a game out of it; whoever makes him pass out gets a prize,” he laughed, nudging the ashen-faced man with one boot. “Who wants to go first?”

“M-me.”

Stunned silence fell. As one, all their sparkling eyes turned towards me. Velidan’s jaw actually dropped.

I couldn’t breathe. Had I spoken aloud? I wasn’t supposed to do that, but now they were all staring as I stood trembling from so much more than the frosty air. A few smiled wickedly, as if delighted that I wanted to join in on this awful, evil-

“M-me instead of h-him.” The words were madness as they tumbled out. Numb fingers jerked towards the templar. My knees threatened to buckle again but I kept going, even managed to step forward. “P-please, don’t-”

“Vel, she’s so sweet!” Embriel roared with laughter, and several of the others joined in. “Dawn, no wonder you-”

“Ignore her,” he growled back, gaze simmering as it narrowed towards me. The warning in it was clear, but the dispassion was a thousand times worse.

There was no stopping this.

“Gladly. Vendric, have a go.”

I turned away as a red-haired Fae raised one glowing hand and reached for the templar. Then a little voice whispered that, more than anything else so far, this was important; their magic, their powers, things I could warn everyone about.

I couldn’t help these men, but I could help their friends. I would help their friends.

So I watched.

Vendric gripped the bound man’s wrist and hissed something. When he let go, putrid flesh bloomed in the space his hand had been. Slick, glistening black sank in, and the man’s muffled scream nearly made me vomit. Vendric repeated the process with the paladin; the older man’s skin darkened, but the rotted handprint didn’t sink in or seem to hurt him.

“Rog, I ever tell you how I met my wife?” The paladin’s voice was hoarse, as if he hadn’t spoken in quite some time. He cleared it while his companion groaned. “Listen, it’s a good story. Happened back when I was guarding caravans.”

A blonde Fae went next. His fingertips glowed sickly green. When he slashed at the Templar, the man began to writhe. Foam spilled from behind his gag.

The paladin shuddered and paled when struck. He didn’t foam, but his skin grew pasty. Fenwick panted for a moment. Then he ground on, “Went for dinner and a pint, and left my purse behind. Didn’t realize til I had to settle.”

“He finally wants to talk.” Embriel rolled his eyes. “Just my luck.”

Fingers wreathed in icy fog went next. The templar groaned as frostbite whitened the skin of his other arm. On the paladin, there was hardly any effect.

“Wynne was closing up, and she got right mad. ‘Couldn’t afford to get the roof fixed because people kept skipping out,’ she said. Started pointing out leaks while she got all pink and huffy. Cutest thing I ever-”

A murmured word from a slim, sharp-featured Fae turned Rog’s eyes black. He became frenzied, thrashing from side to side and screaming behind his gag. His body bucked, bare feet lashing out at things unseen.

The same happened to Fenwick; his irises filled like spilled ink and he roared, heaving with such force that Garruck teetered before slamming his foot back down. Then a silver glint in his eyes chased the black away. He gasped in air, looking to his still-thrashing companion. “Listen, Rog,” the man panted. “This is the good part. Hey. You listening?”

“He’s having a little dream,” the Fae said, grinning at the black-eyed templar.

“Well, wake him up. He’s gonna miss the good part.”

A chorus of disbelieving laughter followed the order.

Embriel shook his head. “He’s kind of fun, isn’t he? Resilient. I’ve been growing fond of him.”

Rog’s eyes cleared and his body went limp. Moisture glistened on his cheeks. It dripped down mine, too. I felt the weight of a heavy gaze searing into me, but couldn’t tear my eyes from the templar.

“Listen, Rog. That’s an order. I went back early. Got it in my head I’d have her roof fixed before dawn, plus the coin I owed, and wouldn’t she be just as pleased as could be. Thought maybe she’d get all pink again.”

A Fae with eyes eerily like Velidan’s, though dominantly silver with pinpoints of black, raised his hand. Flecks of diamond-bright light swirled in his palm. Then, with a snap of his wrist, they sliced their way along both Thalessians. Blood blossomed from tiny cuts along Rog’s arms, but the templar barely twitched. His head lolled against the stone, eyes lidded and glassy.

“She got pink, alright,” the paladin groaned, watching his companion fiercely. “I climbed up the side of the building, took two steps and fell straight through the thatch. Landed square in her bed. Stars, Rog, you’ve never seen a woman blush like-”

“This is all very entertaining,” Embriel chuckled, “but I need you to pay attention to me, now. Vel, do you mind? Just the old one, don’t strain yourself. Garruck, go get that shiny sword of his.” He clapped his hands, beaming around the circle of Fae. “Now, who wants to let Sir Fenwick try to kill them?”
