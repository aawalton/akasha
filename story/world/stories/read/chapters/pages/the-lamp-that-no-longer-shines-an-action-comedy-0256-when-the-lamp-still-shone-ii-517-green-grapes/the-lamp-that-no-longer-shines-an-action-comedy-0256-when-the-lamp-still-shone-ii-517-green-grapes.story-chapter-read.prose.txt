[Time]: 1980

The Lantern Cats were Ovelia's kin, extensions of her very soul, and by the immutable laws of magic, their glowing eyes were her eyes.

Naturally, the Lord of the Umbral Cathedral would sooner climb into one of Irene's Lantern Cat washing machines than admit she used this omnipresent, fluffy surveillance network to track Irene Habsburg. If anyone dared to ask, Ovelia would cross her arms, tilt her chin to an imperious angle, and declare that a master must always monitor the progress of her disciple. How else could she perfectly time her abductions, snatching Irene away into the Umbral Cathedral at the exact microsecond she was completely alone?

Unfortunately, Irene was now acutely aware of the surveillance. The revelation had occurred weeks ago, when Ovelia, in a moment of unguarded arrogance, had casually critiqued the ending of an obscure romance novel Irene had read through in a single evening at home, with nothing in the room but a Lantern Cat dozing on the windowsill.

Ever since that slip, Irene had been careful. She selected lower-density routes when she wanted privacy, guarded her conduct in their sightlines, and reserved anything she did not wish observed for the deepest chambers of her private estate.

In the depths of the Umbral Cathedral, Ovelia let out a soft, indignant huff, her tail swishing against the cold arm of her throne. Stingy, she thought. What is the harm in letting Cat look? Cat is simply making preparations for pedagogical optimization.

Because Ovelia was profoundly lazy, she opened the internal hive-mind and tossed the task to her Aspects.

Find her.

A moment passed before Daena's voice came through the link, barely above a whisper, and hesitant. Found her.

A visual feed blossomed in Ovelia's mind. She stayed sprawled across her throne, expecting a library, a laboratory, or perhaps a café. Instead, the feed rendered the neon-soaked corridors of the White City's most infamous entertainment district. Irene was seated in the velvet-draped VIP booth of a high-end parlor. A curated selection of golden-haired, emerald-eyed beauties pressed close around her, one pouring wine, another leaning against her shoulder.

Ovelia sat bolt upright on the throne. Her ears went flat, and her tail started thrashing against the armrest in short, outraged strokes, and a cushion went tumbling down the dais steps.

Shameless! It went out across the hive-mind at full volume. Despicable! Indecent! Cat is going to retrieve her. This exact second.

Around the dais, the assembled Aspects looked like a room full of sisters who had been handed a spectacularly messy family scandal, and every one of them had a different and fiercely personal problem with it.

"Why is she simply sitting there?" Avenka demanded, crowding into the feed for a closer look. Her tail swayed once. "She has not asked a single one of them whether there are any unexplored ruins nearby."

At the far end of the hall Shaar came off her cushion and landed on her feet with her ears already forward. "Go," she said. "Go right now. Go exactly as you are."

Allison held a very specific Irene in her head: a sweet, deferential junior with flawless manners and a dimpled smile, whose only real misfortune in life was being permanently and unjustly glared at by Allison's own younger sisters, none of whom had ever once managed to prove a single thing.

Somebody had tried to warn Allison about this at length, with diagrams. She had not listened then either.

She watched the golden-haired girl in the feed lean against Irene's shoulder and be permitted to stay there, with the expression of a woman discovering that her exceedingly well-behaved puppy held a majority stake in a casino.

Then, as the one in the household who handled feelings, she cleared her throat and did her job anyway.

"Leaving aside," Allison said, "whatever it is she imagines she is doing. She is currently in the Red Light District." Every trace of accommodating warmth had gone out of her voice. "A sudden extraction might generate... complications."

Ovelia rounded on her. "So what if it is the Red Light District? It is time for her lesson!"

To call her time in Ovelia's company an ordeal would have been a lie. The trouble was Ovelia, who went through the world thoroughly pleased with herself, chin up and tail swaying, without the faintest suspicion of what was being thought about her at close range. Every smug little curl at the corner of her mouth made Irene's palms itch to catch hold of her and ruin her, slowly and thoroughly. She would not, because then the cat would run. So the entertainment district had become a necessity: one room in the world where she was the one in total control, and where that itch could be scratched at a safe distance from its cause.

Over these frequent excursions, her preferences had crystallized. Her strike zone was technically quite broad, but nothing took the edge off like a golden-haired beauty with green eyes looking up at her and staying exactly where she was put. Whatever Irene did with them, she would not be doing to Ovelia, and Ovelia would never have a reason to vanish into the shadows.

During her last visit, she had summoned seven companions at once. Walking out, she had casually mentioned to an acquaintance that next time she would call for nine. Irene Habsburg was a woman of her word.

She organized the evening's architecture with the calm efficiency she brought to everything: One to establish the baseline. Two to escalate the tempo. Then three. Then the final three to close it out.

The heavy door of the VIP suite clicked shut behind her. The room was all velvet and dim, warm light. Sitting at the edge of the sprawling bed was the evening's first appointment: a vision of trembling, golden-haired vulnerability, green eyes wide and expectant.

Irene offered a slow, devastating smile. She took a measured step forward, fingers rising to the collar of her tailored jacket. She slipped it from her shoulders, draped it over a chair, and reached for the first button of her thin silk shirt.

Then the gravity in the room inverted.

Before Irene's combat reflexes could even register the anomaly, a weight landed against her spine. First came softness, impossible and overwhelming, pressing flush against her back. Then the scent: green grapes steeped in strong shochu, cool and sweet at first breath, with the spirit's warmth coming up underneath.

Ovelia's breath ghosted directly over the shell of Irene's ear. Irene's mana field spiked in the same instant it was already irrelevant.

The neon-lit suite shattered like spun glass.

When Irene's vision cleared, the velvet and warm light were gone, replaced by the towering, bone-cold arches of the Umbral Cathedral.

Ovelia stepped back, releasing her. She crossed her arms beneath her chest, lifted her chin to an imperious angle, and waited for the gratitude.

Irene stood frozen. She looked down at her half-unbuttoned shirt.

Her jaw locked, her face darkened, and without a single word she began fastening the buttons, one by one, from the collar downward, with the slow, deliberate, terrifying precision of a bomb technician. Each pearl button slid into its buttonhole. She smoothed the fabric flat with her palm, straightened the collar, and tapped her spatial ring to produce a replacement jacket.

Ovelia stood a few feet away, watching. Her tail cut the air behind her in a slow, rhythmic sweep. She could read the hostility coming off Irene's shoulders perfectly well. Who is she giving that look to?!

Irene raised her head. "I had previously operated under the assumption," she began, her voice a ribbon of razor-wire wrapped in pure, saccharine politeness, "that you possessed at least a rudimentary, baseline comprehension of not interrupting someone during moments of extreme personal privacy."

Ovelia's cat ears flattened.

"I had assumed you understood the concept of occupied." Irene's cadence accelerated, the icy veneer fracturing to reveal the full boiling fury underneath. "Can you genuinely not distinguish whether you are a Grand Witch or a glorified, overgrown Lantern Cat? Have you truly succumbed to dementia before your first century?"

The sheer speed of it caught Ovelia off guard, and she took a half-step back.

The retreat lasted exactly one microsecond before her pride detonated. "It is entirely your fault!" Ovelia shouted back, her voice echoing off the vaulted ceiling. "You know perfectly well that Cat is going to come looking for you! Why do you insist on going to places Cat absolutely hates?!"

A sharp, throbbing pressure bloomed behind Irene's temples, a biological impossibility for a Witch, manifesting anyway. She looked at Ovelia's furious, completely oblivious face.

Why do I go there? It nearly made her laugh. Every golden-haired girl, every carefully arranged evening in that district, existed to burn off the heat standing three feet in front of her, tail lashing, demanding an explanation.

Enough. If you hate the substitutes so much, you can take their place. Her weight was already on the front foot.

Then, unbidden, her nerve endings replayed the weight of Ovelia against her spine: grapes and shochu still faint on her skin, the yielding softness that had held her in place without a trace of real force.

The step never landed. She would hate it, and there was nothing more to the matter than that. Heat flooded up from her chest to the tips of her ears; Irene pressed her lips into a hard line, crossed her arms to keep her hands to herself, and retreated into silence.

The silence made it worse. Ovelia stood in the vast, empty hall, looking at the woman who refused to look back at her.

Fine, Ovelia thought, her chest tight. Class is cancelled.

With a snap of her fingers, the Cathedral's spatial matrix inverted.

One second she was in the Cathedral, the next she was standing on a quiet street corner in the White City.

The night breeze was cool, rustling the collar of her freshly changed jacket. Irene stood entirely still under the streetlamps. She raised one hand and touched her own cheek. The skin was burning.

She was not nearly obtuse enough to mistake it for anger over a privacy she had stopped caring about three minutes ago. She had arranged an entire nine-woman evening for the express purpose of putting out what Ovelia started, and the cat had walked straight through the middle of it, pressed all that soft, shochu-scented weight against her spine, set her alight at point-blank range, and dropped her on a pavement to deal with the fire alone, like an arsonist with somewhere better to be.

Back in the Umbral Cathedral, nothing moved.

Ovelia sat rigid on her throne, biting down on the tip of her own tail.

Allison came out of the shadows by the nearest pillar and got as far as opening her mouth.

Ovelia's head snapped up. She spat out her tail.

"You!" Ovelia accused. "Are you laughing at Cat in your head right now? Are you standing there thinking that Cat should have listened to you from the beginning?"

"No," Allison said. "I would have gone and got her myself."

The tail came back down. Ovelia sat with her mouth open around the rest of an argument that no longer had anywhere to go.

"Did you hear the tone she used with Cat?!" She threw both hands at the vaulted ceiling. "Cat removes her from a den of absolute iniquity, from women who were practically drooling on her shirt, and what does Cat receive? A lecture on privacy. Accusations of dementia. She stood there and shouted at Cat as though Cat were some misbehaving kitten!"

Allison let her have all of it. "Which changes nothing. There was a very confused escort in that suite who watched the Head of the Grand Witches materialize out of thin air and remove her client, and I had to drag Daena out of hiding to bury tonight, and Vila in behind her to bury the burial. If we had not been running damage control, every gossip column in the Inner Sea would be asking what the First Seat was doing carrying a girl out of a pleasure house at midnight, and the moment anyone starts asking what the two of you are to each other, how long do you imagine it takes them to arrive at the answer that actually matters?"

The Cathedral went dead silent.

Ovelia's tail puffed to twice its normal volume, and then, between one moment and the next, came down flat. She had been handed agreement, a full explanation, and nobody at all to fight.

"I see," Ovelia said.

The grievance had gone out of her voice, and the volume with it, and what remained carried perfectly into every corner of the hall. "If she finds my attention so stifling, then she shall have the space she asked for." The starry black eyes went around the circle of her own Aspects one at a time. "If she requires a master, one of you may go and instruct her. I have lost interest."

A pause.

"Let her sit in the silence she values so highly, and think about what she has done to Cat's heart."

She turned on her heel and went into the shadows, and the hive-mind connection closed, with great care, the way a door is closed by somebody who intends you to notice that she did not slam it.

Allison stood alone by the pillar, then turned and looked at the remaining Aspects hovering in the hall.

"Well," she asked the room. "Who wants to go be her master? It will not be me."

"Cat will do it," Avenka said, instantly.

"You would have her hauling your lab equipment inside the hour."

"What else would she be for?" Avenka said, entirely unrepentant.

Shaar came out of the dark at the edge of the dais. "Has nobody else noticed," she said, "how appallingly normal this one is? She gets annoyed. She argues. She asks for privacy and then she simply receives it." Her arms folded. "I refuse to deal with a functional, well-adjusted Irene. It is unnatural."

From the far corner, Daena, who had been hoping very hard that no one would look directly at her, let out a long, pitiful whimper.

That left Kara, whose ears had been up since the question was asked.

"I could instruct her," Kara said, the instant the room came round to her.

"Absolutely not," Allison said.

Shaar's folded arms dropped straight to her sides. "Banned," she said. "You are banned. We are not dealing with the consequences of your parenting again."

Kara's ears came down. "It is always a failure in my methods," she murmured to the floor. "I did offer to turn myself in." A pause, and a small, sad smile. "She only loved too much."

Allison sighed up at the arches. It was going to be a very long cold war.
