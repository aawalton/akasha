James was ready, but nothing came at him. Not even a goblin crawled in this hellhole. Then, on the horizon, he sighted a city. As he came closer, an orc soldier peeked out from the wall. James was ready for them to turn hostile, but they didn’t shoot any arrows. Instead, they brought out their horses and waved at James. Kouki already had his sword drawn.

“Greetings, traveler from afar. I go by the name Maruk, the chief of this city. What may we help you with?” The orc came down from his horse and bowed to James. His politeness belied his bulky body and scary face. James was hit with a sense of nostalgia seeing the sapient orc.

“I am just looking. We are stranded somewhere there.” James pointed to the wasteland behind him while motioning Kouki to sheath his sword.

“We shall welcome you. If your men are good at labor work, we and Count Goliath are looking for people to rebuild his kingdom,” the orc said.

“A count making a kingdom?” Kouki was miffed, but James ignored him.

“Are you fine with it?” James asked, and the orc nodded.

“As long as they bring order. Our true enemy lies in the deep waste,” the orc said. “You may come to our land or to theirs. We must work together as a group.”

“Understood. Where is this count’s kingdom you spoke of?”

“It is to the east.”

“Where is the east?”

“The moon’s direction is north, the east on its right side, and the west is on its left, while the south is if you flee the moon. May the blood moon bring peace to your heart and anguish to your enemy.” The orc bowed. He then motioned James to follow him. “Before you go to Count Goliath, please have a look at our city.”

“Alright. But I do have your word that we may come and go as we please, right?”

“Yes. You may come and go as you please—except during the Great Tide of Life. During that time, all gates are closed and the barriers are erected. None may leave the city.”

“What happened at the tide?” James asked as they entered the city. The gate was tall, a carbon copy of Bartholomew’s wall he saw in the previous dungeon. This orc reminded him of the orc general James befriended in the novice dungeon. They even had a similar cannon mounted on the wall. He remembered the time they fought together against Nirvok. In fact, James’ mechanical raptor was a gift from that dungeon. James scanned the surroundings. The city here was more lively, though.

“Here is our census bureau. Count Goliath has one as well,” the orc said.

“Oh? Why do you need it?”

“We welcome everyone. But only residents can get a job and contribute to the city. Moreover, poaching residents from each other is forbidden. Once you become a resident here, you may not become Count Goliath’s resident. Only residents are protected during the tide.”

“When will the tide come? And what do you mean by only residents being protected during the tide?” James was suspicious.

“We are not sure when the tide will come, but usually it comes four days from now when the moon is at its peak. At that moment, the moon will shine, covering the wasteland with life.”

“That sounds like a good thing. Why do we need barriers and such?”

“Any living thing hit by the light will be transformed into mindless monsters as worms and other servants of life blessed them,” the orc said.

“Should have known there would always be a drawback,” James sighed.

“After the tide ceases, it will be time for the harvesting season. Hunters and farmers will hunt animals and collect fruits and wheat that grow from the ground. That’s where we get our fruit and water,” the orc continued.

“So, if we do not bow to your rule and become your slaves, will you throw us out of the city?”

“No. You may come and go as you please. Even during the tide. But only residents are given food and water during the tide, and you may not buy them from anywhere else. During the tide and one week before the tide, food and water are scarce, so they must be controlled.”

“I see.” James nodded. “Thank you for your information. I will go to the neighbors. Then we will decide whether we will join you or Count Goliath.”

“Understood,” the orc said. He then whispered to James, “Between you and me, this is mere advice, so you can ignore me. You seem to be nonhuman, but Count Goliath’s men have some distaste toward people like us. So it would be better for you to remain here.”

“I’ll be the judge of that,” James scoffed. The orc seemed nice, but he knew better than to trust him unanimously. James then motioned Kouki to climb onto their mount, and they both rode out of the city. Their destination was in the east, where Count Goliath was said to reside.

The moment they came out of the city, a system message blinked in front of James and Kouki.

[Clock unlocked. Time before the Great Tide of Life: 96 hours.]

“Thank you, system. Are you finally being cooperative now? Any news you can share about how we can advance through this dungeon? How many floors does this dungeon have?”

The system went silent. There was not even the usual reply of ‘Information classified’ for James and Kouki. Only time until the tide. James grew giddy with anxiety at the system’s sudden silence. A few hours later, another city was in sight, just like what the orc had said.

“Greetings, traveler from afar. I am Khan, a soldier of Count Goliath. Welcome to the Kingdom of Goliath,” the soldier saluted. “The Census Bureau is this way.”

“I have yet to decide. Can we look around? We can leave anytime, right?”

“Of course. You have my word that you are free to come and go as you please. I don’t know what lies the other city has fed you, but we do not discriminate against non-humans. As long as they are sapient and are willing to work for the city, we shall welcome them with open arms,” the soldier said. His smile was eerily similar to Maruk's from the previous city.

The city was almost a carbon copy of the previous one except for tiny differences. Here, the stone houses were all the same size, while in the previous city, the stone houses were of different sizes. James understood the reason why.

This city was built for humans. Monsters and non-humans were welcome, but they had to conform to human laws and dwellings. The soldier then took them to the census bureau, the same building Maruk had shown him. James looked over the city and was puzzled. What was the dungeon’s purpose? What was this city’s purpose? Why did they create two similar cities?

James glanced at the knight beside him and asked, “What are the rules here in this city?”

He avoided any obvious leading questions, but the knight answered him similarly. The rules were the same.

“You have to be registered at the bureau to do any work for the city. And during the tide, anyone who isn’t registered will not be provided with any food or water. We don’t want to do it this way, but both cities need residents for hunting and farming jobs, so this is the compromise we set upon ourselves. The other city is the same, right?”

“I see. Yes, you are right. Thank you for your information.”

“So, you have seen both cities, right? Which one will you pick?”

“The truth is that we are not alone. There are over a thousand people stranded here, so we have to get back to our friends first. We shall decide which city we will go to later,” Kouki said.

“I see. How many are you?”

“About a thousand people,” James said, recalling Anderson’s report. That’s how many soldiers and researchers there were.

“What? That’s the size of a village or small town. I thought there were only like ten or twenty of you.” The soldier blinked. He then faced James with a grim face after contemplating. “That won’t do, then. The most we can accept is five hundred. Maybe six hundred if we push it. We can’t accept everyone at once. There are simply not enough food and water. You must split your people.”

“Alright. See you soon.” The soldier waved. James and Kouki then climbed back onto their mounts and returned to the base camp. The clock had been ticking down. It had been almost ten hours, and they were getting hungry.

[Would you like to spend 100 biomass for sustenance?]

The system asked as if it knew James couldn’t get food from anywhere else. James ignored the system prompt and marched to the base camp. There had been little progress. The soldiers were organized, but without weapons and tools, they looked like boy scouts on the outside. There was zero intimidating factors.

“James, you have returned! Any news? Ah, please pour water magic into this bucket we made from nearby dead wood,” Andrew said.

“Yes. There are two cities there, and if you want to get food and water, you must sign there as a resident,” James said.

“I see. A notification suddenly popped up about nine hours ago, so we can assume they were telling the truth about the tide. If we can get food, we must go there immediately.”

“They said the tide was hazardous to us, so we have to get inside the city,” Kouki said.

“Ah, was it like the purple moon? When we couldn’t touch it for an extended amount of time?”

“They said if the moon shone on them, they would be consumed and turned into monsters,” Kouki said. His hair stood on end at the horror story Maruk had told.

“We have no choice then. I will split the people. They will go to the north and northeast.”

James nodded while looking at the crimson moon above. He was confused. The dungeon hadn’t shown him what its purpose was or how to defeat it. No floors, no missions, and no monsters yet. His instincts rang alarm bells inside his head, but he didn’t even know what the dangers were. The hard-difficulty dungeon invasion had just begun.
