(Note: Pronunciation ‘Oisella’ - ‘wuh-zel-uh’)

Elarion stands at the head of our party, creating an ice bridge to the visiting ship – merchants, if their herald is telling the truth. They’re definitely from Moriaxar, though – the language would surely be different if they were natives who happened to have a flag that matched the one Valence recognised.

Elarion has clearly been putting a good deal of effort into showing off his skill with weaving ice. The bridge even has delicate and elegant strands that form railings and lanterns holding light-emitting crystals.

“Won’t we slip?” Josiah asks, concerned.

“You can see he’s made it intentionally rough underfoot,” I point out, the crisscross of strands offering a textured surface.

“And even if Elarion doesn’t keep it cold, I will,” Layton reassures his cousin. “It won’t melt while we’re on it.”

Out of all the options available to us, I decided that this would be the best option, and Elarion agreed to cooperate. Something to walk on is necessary because several of those in our party are unable to fly, and being carried by myself or Elarion wouldn’t be very dignified. That’s an unfortunately important consideration since one never gets a second chance to make a first impression, and these are the first outsiders since we arrived on the Lost Continent.

An earthen bridge would be easy enough to create, but an ice bridge is a better defence – if we have to retreat from enemies, we can melt it easily. An earthen or plank bridge would take longer to remove. Besides, it looks awesome.

Sarran moves closer to me.

“My lord, should we all be going?” he asks me quietly enough that no one else should be able to hear. “It is a risk – if they have set a trap that captures or kills all of us, the settlement will be left completely helpless.”

I send him a sideways glance.

“If all of us are killed, the settlement becomes theirs,” I point out. “But if they have enough firepower to overcome all of us at once like that, then we stand little chance against them anyway. Assuming that they are who they present themselves as, the other lords would take it as an insult if I excluded them from this first meeting – and I could not invite some without giving even deeper insult to those left behind. We will remain alert and keep our eyes out, though,” I assure him.

Sarran grimaces faintly, clearly still not entirely happy with my decision. Then his eyes drift to Loran.

“Consider leaving Loran behind, at least. He is the most vulnerable of us all and he doesn’t need to attend a meeting like this.”

I glance over at Loran and am curious to see him look back at us, his eyes slightly narrowed.

Can you hear us? I ask him.

Just enough to be decipherable, he confirms.

Interesting. After we’ve dealt with the merchants, I really want to take some time to explore exactly what Loran’s new limits are – I didn’t have time before dinner to do more than look at the new developments in his internal matrix. And then dinner was filled with finding out more about the various leaders who have emerged among the various groups in the settlement – not guilds, exactly, but certainly organisations.

Then how do you feel about Sarran’s suggestion? Would you prefer to stay behind, or do you feel up to coming with us?

I feel… He hesitates for a moment. I believe I am more durable than in the past. It feels like I can survive more blows than before. He nods decisively. I would prefer to come with you, if that is your will.

I turn to Sarran.

“Thanks for the concern but he’ll come with us.”

“And if it turns into a fight?”

The bridge is complete, Elarion announces through the Alliance Bond. I give Sarran a mischievous smile.

“Then you might find yourself surprised.”

Sarran narrows his eyes at me.

“Something has happened,” he concludes. “What?”

My smile just widens a touch and I wink at him before striding off towards where the bridge meets the land.

Valence is organising the unit of Warriors who will be preceding us onto the ship. We don’t want to upset potentially friendly merchants, but Sarran isn’t the only one who has considered that this seemingly innocuous ship might conceal a poisoned blade. The Warriors will go and secure the deck space before we approach. The other unit will remain here on the harbour wall, watching for any signs of foul play.

As the first unit of ten Classers stride up the delicate-looking bridge, I half-expect pieces of it to rattle loose under the impact of their heavy boots. But it remains as solid as a marble structure.

“Impressive work,” I compliment Elarion when he comes to stand next to me. He sniffs and flicks one hand as if to say that such craftsmanship is only to be expected of one of his calibre. Perhaps I’ve got to know him a little too well, I contemplate.

The beauty he’s wrought in his construction makes my own efforts in everything but Flesh-Shaping look like those of a ham-fisted brute – all power and no finesse. Something to explore, I decide. Learning how to delicately wield my various Shaping Skills would no doubt be valuable for my progression in them.

With my sharp hearing, I am able to eavesdrop on the conversation between the captain leading the unit and the captain on the ship. There is a protest against the Warriors inspecting the ship ahead of our boarding, and then again when some of the Classers use Skills that probe for magical traps. However, given how quickly they give way on both issues, I take it that the protests are perfunctory – intended only to register their discontent.

“They seem like legitimate merchants,” Valence murmurs, his own ears and eyes clearly trained on the same conversations mine are. “No merchant would happily allow others access to their storage rooms, even those not actually concealing anything. They’d be too afraid of delicate items being damaged, or food stocks being spoiled.”

Elarion snorts contemptuously.

“They protest to earn an advantage in the oncoming negotiations, not of any true concern.”

Valence grimaces.

“That may be part of it,” he admits.

It feels like an age until the captain of the unit on board appears and gives us the signal that everything is clear.

I take a deep breath and then place my foot onto the first step of the bridge. These are ‘just’ merchants, but in this new place where trade and alliances may be the difference between our colonisation succeeding and being driven out, they are important.

The bridge is easy to climb, the steps not at all slippery underfoot. The ship is much like Josiah’s – a galleon that’s wider than it is tall, minus the masts, of course. Its deck is full of people though our Warriors keep a space clear at the point that the ice bridge flows over the ship’s railing. Casting my gaze over the others, I see sailors around the edges of the deck like an audience, and three brightly-dressed figures centrestage. The two men and one woman are obviously the merchants in charge.

The last step onto the ship is the biggest – Elarion had to leave some space below it to account for the regular swaying of the ship. I drop down easily enough, then step forwards to stand before the three merchants.

They quickly bow deeply.

“Great Heir Titanbend,” the one in the centre greets, speaking to the wooden deck, his shoulder-length black hair slipping forward to conceal his face. They stand a moment later, but upon seeing who’s just reached my side, they bow again, just as deeply.

“Great Heir Forestheart.” Not a flicker of expression passes across Elarion’s face, but I get the strong sense that he’s eyeing them with contempt.

After that, they greet Josiah, then Layton, then finally Valence who’s bringing up the rear. I wonder if it’s my imagination or if there’s a slight note of warmth in the merchant’s voice when he greets Heir Fell.

“Clearly you recognise all of us,” I start when the greetings have finished. “Perhaps you could introduce yourselves?”

“Of course, of course,” the lead merchant agrees, nodding his head several times in quick succession, his dark brown eyes sharp in his dusky-skinned face. “I am Ser Gulnis Sale of Trade, Head Merchant of the Mithril Trading Caravan. These are two of my junior partners – my nephew, Ser Rithan, also of Trade, and Sera Oisella of Wakefield.”

He indicates first the man and then the woman. The man bobs another quick bow, his ear-length black hair flopping about his ears – he looks very like Gulnis, but younger. Actually, on closer inspection, I wonder if he’s a teenager still – there’s a gangliness to his limbs that indicates he hasn’t quite grown into them.

The woman just nods at us, her hair dyed a bright blue to match her eyes and tied up in a bun. She’s a lot older – perhaps late thirties. Though it’s hard to tell with Classers, especially those who have put plenty of points into Constitution. It’s telling that neither of the other two merchants are introduced with a surname – and Gulnis has only the generic one claimed by most commoner merchants after proving themselves in the trade.

I give them a faint smile.

“I’m afraid I don’t see any caravans around here – perhaps you should rename if you intend to do more trading by sea.”

Ser Gulnis gives a hearty guffaw that sounds entirely genuine. I distrust it immediately – my weak joke was hardly worth that much merriment.

“Ah, my lord, the rumours did not speak of your sense of humour.” I prevent my eyebrow from rising through sheer force of will.

“For good reason,” I comment dryly. “Now, if you are merchants, I assume you have things to sell – or wish to buy.”

“Ah, straight down to business, is it? Can we not tempt you to share a cup of tila with us?”

I consider it for a moment, then incline my head.

“That would be welcome.” It’s relatively easy to tell if a cup of tila has been doctored, and even if the merchants didn’t show offence, I know they would be if I refused their hospitality.

Within a few minutes, a table and some chairs for us all have been quickly produced, the area lit and warmed by braziers. The merchant correctly read the room and realised that we wouldn’t want to go deeper into their ship at this point in our relationship, so set the furniture up on deck. Taking some time to drink tila, we share some small talk about the weather.

Finally, after the first cup – of perfectly serviceable tila – is drained and refilled, we get down to business.

“Now Mithril Trading Caravans is here to meet your needs–”

“For the right price,” Elarion interrupts seemingly casually.

Gulnis exchanges a brief look with his partners, then turns his head to look at Elarion, his expression smooth and agreeable.

“Of course, my lord. However, I assure you that Mithril Trading Caravans is known for the honesty of their dealings.” He falters as Elarion snorts faintly.

Elarion, what are you doing? I ask silently. Trade will be essential to our future success. If you alienate them now, you may undermine that entirely.

Merchants are vultures, Elarion replies disdainfully. Let them see weakness and they will pounce on it. If they think we are easy targets, they will bleed us dry.

I can’t deny that he does have a point – a slight one. However…

I take your point, but we need to talk later about what you’ve got against merchants, I warn him. It’s fine if he doesn't like them. It’s not fine if it’s causing issues for the settlement.

In the meantime, I give the merchant a neutral smile.

“What sort of goods are you offering?”

They proceed to launch into an explanation and demonstration of their wares. Gulnis leads the presentation, though relies on his partners to demonstrate certain pieces of equipment, produced from an extra-dimensional storage.

Interestingly, most of their inventory is from Moriax – potions, enchanted devices, more mundane supplies such as parchment and ink, tools of various sorts. I note that they don’t have any Class stones, and when I ask if they have any beast Cores, they only produce a few Tier twos – though with the air that these are great finds.

As the presentation continues, I find myself disappointed – and sense that the lords with me are equally unimpressed. Had the merchants arrived a few weeks ago, they might have found more interest, but by this point, the various craftsmen of our settlement have figured out ways of transforming local products into equivalent, or even better items.

The potions, for example – though the alchemists can’t produce the normal healing or mana potions that the merchants offer, they can produce far stronger ones. Which is sometimes the problem, of course, but they’re working on dilution techniques. The single-use offensive scrolls and defensive devices might have also been of interest to us, since we finished the last of those we’d brought from Moriaxar during the beast waves and Tilathis’ arrival, but Lizil and Keshaka are well on their way to replacing the most important.

There are only two things that interest any of my party. Sarran shifts when the merchants bring out some ink – the alchemists haven’t yet got around to figuring out how to make ink from the local plants that doesn’t just burn straight through parchment – and the others perk up slightly when they see the bottle of wine that Gulnis presents with a slightly-desperate flourish near the end of the presentation.

“My lords, clearly you are customers with very refined tastes,” he chuckles, though I can hear a hint of strain in it. “Perhaps if you indicated what sort of things you were looking for, we would be better able to fulfil your needs.”

“Or perhaps you’re not very good merchants,” Elarion offers with only the faintest hint of derision in his voice. “If you do not have any goods that are even worth our time to watch them being presented. Have you even traded with any noble Houses before?”

This time, I stay silent, watching him calculatingly. I have a good feeling I know what he’s doing.

Rithan swells up, clearly taking some affront.

“We have been the trusted suppliers for multiple noble Houses,” he nods at Valence. “Perhaps Heir Fell could attest to this.”

“Rithan,” Gulnis warns, but his words are drowned out by Elarion’s response.

“That is on Moriax. This is a completely different context and, so far, you have not proven a thing.”

“We have already traded with–”

“Rithan!” Gulnis interrupts sharply. Rithan sends him an apologetic look and subsides. Gulnis smooths his expression back to affability, though he can't hide the calculating glint in his eyes. “Of course, information is also a ware we can offer,” he continues almost coyly. Seeing as that has grabbed our attention more than anything else, he gives us a broad smile. “We have sailed further along this coastline than anyone else. If you would like to know who is where…I’m sure we can agree on a price.”
