Two realisations suddenly slammed into me, leaving me reeling. First was the utter certainty that it had worked. Somehow, I was in Salvation. It was not the same version of the city as the world I had just come from, but another version, from some other reality. I had actually managed to find my way there again, and I wasn’t sure how I knew, but I did.

But it was the other realisation that felt more urgent. It stole my focus, my excitement, away from the first and all of my attention quickly became fixated on the fact that I could not breathe.

The hand wrapped around my throat was getting tighter, and I tried to suck in a breath, but nothing happened. It was almost impossible to inhale any more of that deliciously sweet and pure air, and I needed to get him away from me before it was too late.

I had to break his grip, I thought, recalling the self-defence training that I’d been given at the Academy. It had been terrifying to practice, to feel someone reach out and lightly grip my throat. Their fingers had just rested against my skin, barely even touching me, and that had scared me enough, but it was nothing compared to the pure fear pulsing through me as the unknown man strangled me.

The thumb. That was where I had to aim. It was the weakest finger, the weakest part of the hand, and if I could pull that away, I’d be able to get the rest of his fingers away from my neck. Then, all I needed was a well-placed punch or kick, and he’d go down. I’d be able to run away.

My head began to swim as my fingers resumed their scrabbling. It was easy to find the man’s thumb, but I couldn’t get a good enough grip on it. My fingers scratched at his gloves, trying to find purchase, but he was gripping my neck too tightly. I couldn’t pry it away.

Fear soared within me, and my heartbeat grew even faster. I could hear it pounding in my ears, the whooshing beat strangely hypnotic. It drew my focus away from what I was trying to do, and I could feel the temptation to just give up rising within me, but I pushed it away.

It wasn’t going to work, I realised. The man was too strong, and there was no way I’d be able to break his grip. That meant I needed to try something else, and I remembered the self-defence tutor talking about that. She’d said something about what we should do in that kind of situation, but my brain was moving too sluggishly. The lack of air was making it harder to recall exactly what she’d taught us.

Go on the offensive. If we couldn’t break their grip, we should go on the offensive, I finally recalled. My eyes darted back and forth, taking in the shadowy figure before me as I tried to figure out where to aim. His eyes would be best, but his nose would work too. If I managed to hit either of those, it would probably hurt him enough to make his hand loosen a little, or hopefully, he’d let go completely.

But it was still too dark. I wasn’t sure if my vision was fading or if my eyes had never adjusted to the light, but I still couldn’t see his face. There was no way to tell if he was wearing a helmet, like the people who’d attacked the Academy, and I was scared my punch would only cause an injury to myself. Then, it would be even harder to get away from him.

It was my only choice, though. I’d tried to pull his thumb away, tried to break his grasp, and it hadn’t worked. He was too strong, and my chest was burning. My vision was fading in and out of focus, and if I didn’t break free soon, it would be too late.

I thrust my fist out towards the man’s face, praying that it would catch his nose, but it felt weak. It was more of a flail than a punch, and I wasn’t sure whether it was the lack of oxygen getting to me or if the soft, cushy life that version of me lived was to blame. I’d had it easy. Everything had been provided for me, and I wasn’t sure I’d ever punched anyone before in that world. Even just forming a fist felt unnatural to me.

My knuckles bounced off the man’s bristly cheek, but no noise came from him. There was no muffled gasp of pain, no sharp exhale. I wasn’t sure he’d even felt my punch, and it did not have the effect I wanted. His hand did not loosen, and the chilling realisation that I could not get free dawned on me.

Was I about to die, I couldn’t help but wonder. I knew I was about to pass out. My grip on consciousness was starting to wane, but I wasn’t sure what would happen after. Would the man stop choking me, or would his fingers remain laced around my throat until my heart finally spluttered to a stop? I wasn’t sure, and I didn’t want to find out, but something was keeping me tethered to the world.

I should have been safe there.

That knowledge pulled at me, making it hard to turn my back on it and return to my void. I could feel myself clinging to the hope that none of what was happening was real, that it was some kind of cruel test, but I knew that couldn’t be true. The people of Salvation didn’t do that kind of thing. Of that, I was certain.

But why had I been attacked? And how? That was what confused me, even as I clawed weakly at the man’s hand again. My nails were cut short. I was too well-groomed, and they caused him no pain.

Salvation was meant to be a safe haven, protected from those who would commit violence, and there were strict rules against it. Everyone knew the punishment of such actions, and I knew they would not risk it. They would not want to face a fate worse than being cast out into the desert with no hope of survival.

There was no way someone from Salvation would attack another person, I realised. And that meant the person in front of me must have come from the outside. Either he was one of the few people from my bubble who actually survived his attempt and made it to the doorway, or he was one of the others.

That seemed the most likely. Although my memories were still hazy and mostly unreachable, I was almost certain that no version of me had done anything to draw any hatred and anger from someone from my bubble. Therefore, he was an other. They had managed to infiltrate Salvation, and it was no longer safe. The people in the desert, my people, would receive no more help from the city.

My frantically fluttering heart sank. Without Salvation, the bubbles were alone. They would die. I knew that with such crushing certainty that I almost wanted to cry. I could feel the knowledge wrapping around my heart, drawing it deeper into the pit of my stomach, and my fighting became more determined, but still, I could not break free.

A chill slipped down my spine. Everyone I knew, everyone that I had ever loved, would likely be dead within a couple of years, if not sooner. Without the support, without the resources that Salvation managed to send them, they would not survive another harsh season.

I was new to the world. That version of me did not know anyone who lived in the bubbles, but I could remember slightly more from the last world I’d visited. I recalled the friends who’d thrust shoes towards me as I was marched towards the doors by the guards, and the family who had wept whilst silently cursing themselves for allowing me to face my attempt so unprepared. Their love for me was evident. I could feel it, despite the distance.

My arm fell to my side, my body finally drained of the strength to continue fighting. My vision was a blur of flashing lights, visible only to me, but as unconsciousness settled over me, I tried to tell myself that they might be okay. I wanted to believe that Salvation still stood in that other world, that they would eventually save everyone who lived in the bubbles, but my reassurances felt hollow.

The urge to check pulled at me. I could feel that dizziness looming closer as I returned to my void, trying to tempt me to reach out and fall into it, but I knew I could not. It was too dangerous, and luckily, I had just enough clarity to pull myself away from it.

But I didn’t know where else to go. I still couldn’t go back to my reality. Somehow, I didn’t even need to search for it to know that it was still beyond my reach. I could feel the distance, the disconnect lingering at the back of my mind, and that scared me, but I tried to ignore it. Soon, I’d be able to return, I told myself, but that reassurance felt just as hollow and unlikely as the last.

Anxiety prickled in my chest as I drifted through the vast emptiness. It felt as though endless possibilities surrounded me, just out of sight. There were endless worlds I could slip into, but uncertainty bubbled within me, making me hesitate.

Last time, I thought I was going somewhere safe. It should have been, and it had felt safe, but it turned out to be just as dangerous as so many other worlds I’d been to. That made it harder for me, though. I made me far too aware that I couldn’t trust myself, couldn’t trust my sense of danger, but I didn’t know what else to do. I had to go somewhere. The nothingness of my void would drive me crazy if not.

A terrible idea welled up in the back of my mind. If I had tried to go somewhere safe and that hadn’t worked, then perhaps I should do the opposite. At least if I went for a world that I knew wasn’t safe, that felt terrifying, I would be expecting it. It wouldn’t take me by surprise, and surely, I wouldn’t be scared at all.

Immediately, a world appeared before me. The strange dizziness began to spread over my mind, making my body turn cold. A chill seemed to settle in my extremities as white spots burst behind my eyes, and I could feel my fingers and toes turning numb. It felt as though they’d been drained of all blood.

With a jolt, I yanked myself away from that world. I didn’t mean it, I told myself firmly. I didn’t know what was happening in that place, but I knew for certain that I did not want to find out. I never wanted to go there. Instead, I wanted to stay as far away from it as possible, and go somewhere more…

My thoughts stumbled to a halt as something caught my attention, and I hesitated before letting myself float closer, wincing as an almost stinging sensation washed over me. It tickled my nose, making me feel as though I was about to sneeze, but I did not.

I drifted closer, drawn in by the bizarre feeling that seemed to fill my head with static. Something about the dizziness felt almost… fizzy. It was like my brain was being bathed in carbonated water, and obviously, I knew it wasn’t. That wasn’t possible, and it would probably kill me, but it felt like it.

My mind was buzzing, vibrating softly, and it intrigued me. The sensation felt unlike anything else I’d ever experienced before, and that seemed to push some of my earlier fear away. I was still scared, of course. Part of me was still terrified that there was something deeply wrong with the world, that it had been invaded too, but the larger part of me was curious.

It didn’t matter, though. I’d already drifted too close to the world, and panic burst to life within me as I realised it already had its hooks in me. I was being reeled in, and I tried desperately to fight against it, to escape the unknown world, but I couldn’t. I thrashed, throwing myself back towards the void, but there was nothing there for me to cling to apart from the other realities, and they were all just as scary and unknown.

A world lunged towards me, offering itself up, but I recoiled away. I could feel the danger, the threat radiating from it, and I knew it was the dangerous world that had tried to ensnare me before. No matter how uncertain and scary the place I was being dragged towards was, it could not be worse than that world.

The toe of my boot caught on something, and I was sent sprawling. The wooden pole I’d been clutching flew out of my hand as I hit the ground hard, and I heard it thud loudly against the old brick wall. For a moment, I did not lift my head. I just lay there, face down on the compact dirt, as I tried to catch my breath.

But I knew I could not stay there forever. I had to get up, to find the wooden stick again, and I prayed the impact had not damaged or destroyed it. I needed it. It was my best chance of surviving until the end of the timer, my only real chance, and I had to make it. If I did, everything would change. My life would be completely different, and I knew I was strong enough. Or… I hoped I was.

A strong person would not lie there in the dirt, though. Only a weak person did that, and I needed to show I was better than that. I had to show that I was willing to fight, that I deserved the chance I had been given, no matter how slim the odds.

Pain flared in my knee as I began to push myself to my feet, and I glanced down at my legs. They were clothed in a white fabric, just like the rest of my body, but there was no sign of any injury, so I must have just bruised it. If I were bleeding, somehow I knew I would have been able to see it through the material.

Looking up, my eyes searched for the wooden pole, and I found it quickly. It rested against a patch of the wall where a fire must have broken out at some point before. The yellowed stone was scarred with black soot, but the dirt below was untouched.

I crouched quickly and snatched the stick up. It was unexpectedly warm in my hand, and I could feel something pulsing within it, but I had no clue what. I didn’t even know what the stick was. There were no markings. No runes or patterns had been carved into it, and it was not studded with gemstones or precious metals. If I didn’t know any better, I would have assumed it was just a normal staff, but something told me it was not.

Gingerly, I held it out before me as I began walking again, my steps more cautious than before. I couldn’t see what it was that I’d tripped on. The dirt floor appeared flat and easy to traverse, but something had caught my toe, so I knew I had to be careful as I continued…

I wasn’t sure what I was doing. I could not access my memories from that world, so I had no idea where I was or what was happening, but I began to look around before realising something.

There was music. I could hear music blasting around me, the noise triumphant, excited, and loud, but I couldn’t work out which direction it was coming from. I looked around, trying to figure it out, but it sounded as if there were a full orchestra stuffed into the street around me.

The music was making me feel strangely claustrophobic. It felt like it was surrounding me, trapping me in, but I pushed that thought aside. I knew that was ridiculous. Music couldn’t do that, and I was outside. There was no need to feel claustrophobic.

Taking a deep breath, I looked around again and scanned the tall walls on either side of me. They were both made of the same yellowed brick, and I couldn’t work out whether it had always been that shade, or if it had started off white but had been dyed by age. Either way, it looked ancient.

And it was far taller than me. I was almost tempted to climb it. Uneven stones jutted out from the surface, creating perfect places for my hands and feet to go, but it looked unsteady. The stones along the top were crumbling. They seemed weak and unstable, and I was worried they’d send me crashing back down to the dirt below, which would only make me appear foolish.

I couldn’t risk that, and I might drop the staff again in the process. It might not survive the second.

My feet started moving again, taking me further along the alleyway as I continued to scan my surroundings. I could feel confusion pulling at me, growing stronger and stronger until I came to a stop again and turned slowly. My eyebrows furrowed as I took in the walls on either side of me, following them until they came to a curve in the distance.

I had assumed that I was in an alleyway before, that I was passing between two houses or something, but I was not. There were no houses beside me, no buildings rose up over the walls on either side. In fact, nothing was visible above the walls around me. All I could see were the old stone walls and perfectly clear blue skies overhead.

But there was something else out there, I realised as I turned slowly. Floating in the otherwise untouched sky, was a screen. It was bigger than any I’d ever seen before, easily 30 feet tall and even wider, but there did not seem to be any machinery holding it aloft. It was not attached to any scaffolding, and no propellers or engines stuck out on either side. It was just floating there and broadcasting my own confused face to…

I wasn’t sure who the target audience was. Not really. I barely even recognised myself, and I had no idea why anyone would want to see me, but weirdly, I wasn’t surprised to see it. If anything, I think the version of me from that world expected to see it, but that only confused me further.

My eyes remained fixed on the screen for a few more seconds, and I couldn’t help but notice that, despite how big the image was, it looked perfectly clear. The quality was just as good as if I were staring into a mirror, and I found myself looking around for the camera. There must have been something nearby recording me, but I couldn’t see anything jutting out of the stone walls.

Glancing back at the screen, I tried to pinpoint exactly where the camera should be. I was being filmed from above. The angle of the image was sharp, and the picture showed the walls on either side of me. I could see a glimpse of the next alleyway over, and it was just as empty as the one I was in, which meant the camera couldn’t have been attached to any of the walls. It must have been floating above them, but there was nothing. The sky above the alley appeared empty.

No, not alley, I corrected myself as I continued to peer upwards without spotting anything. I was pretty sure an alleyway had to lead between buildings, but there were none around me. That meant I couldn’t be in one. I had to be in a…

Realisation crashed into me, and my heart leapt into my throat. I was in a maze, and that was why I was on the screen. People were watching me, and it was not just those in the maze with me. There were more. Thousands of others stared at my image on a screen, transfixed as they waited to see what I would do, and they were the ones I had to prove myself to. If I survived until the end of my timer, it was their decision that mattered.

But I could not think about them, I told myself, tearing my eyes away from my image on the screen, which was slowly getting smaller as the camera drifted higher, and tightening my grip on the wooden staff in my hand. Although they would choose my fate, it was the other people in the maze that I needed to focus on. They were the ones who could stop me from surviving until the end of the timer. If they found me, my chances would disappear.

My gaze found the screen again, and a dull horror pulled at me. The camera had continued to float upwards, but I was still visible. My bright white jumpsuit seemed to glow in the mid-afternoon sun, and that meant that anyone who looked at the screen would know exactly where I was and how to get to me.
