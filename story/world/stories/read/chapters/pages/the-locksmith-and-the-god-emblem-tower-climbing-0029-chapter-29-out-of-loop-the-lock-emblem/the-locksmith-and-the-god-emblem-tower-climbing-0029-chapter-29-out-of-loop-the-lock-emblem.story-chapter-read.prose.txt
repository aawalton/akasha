In a jump, Harry nodded, gasping for air. One moment he was drawing a Symbol on an obsidian disc, and the next, he felt like he had just woken from a nightmare. He clutched his chest and touched his body in a desperate attempt to prove to himself that everything was alright. After the scare, he closed his eyes and calmed down.

Running his hand along the floor, he felt the cold floorboards and the cracks in the paving stones. He looked around, but found only darkness. Wow! All this seems so real... He thought as he stood up. He was still wearing the same clothes as before, brown trousers and a cheap jacket.

At the end of the corridor, a faint light appeared. Harry, scratching himself, felt like he was inside a dusty book, the dense particles of dust clinging to his clothes and skin. Ignoring them, he felt uncomfortable, almost as if he shouldn't be there.

It doesn't seem like a dream like Devon said... Harry remembered clearly as he tested his motor skills. He punched the air and cracked his knuckles again. I just hope it's simple... I don't want to wake up with a broken finger.

Approaching the light, he saw that the shape of the entire place resembled a lock, exactly as he had drawn and made on the disc. As if it were solid, the light didn't lead anywhere, leaving Harry confused. At the same time, a pressure took over his body, making him look into the darkness behind him. Something was coming.

He couldn't hear a sound from the dim light, but he was certain that if he stayed there any longer, he wouldn't get his Emblem. He began to pound on the light with his body, a desperate attempt to escape the place. As the darkness drew closer, Harry lost patience and pounded harder. Eventually, he realized that force wouldn't get him out of there, and that's when he noticed a metallic part beneath the light—a handle.

Without wasting any time, he lifted the handle, which lifted the light around him. Harry then passed through the illuminated gate and closed it, banishing the darkness from where it came. When he turned around, however, he thought for a moment that it would have been better to have stayed behind.

He was in a room. No. A hall that stretched dozens of meters upwards, covered with shelves full of unfamiliar books. The wooden floor covered the circular hall, which had a sunken area in the middle. Directly pointing towards an open section of the hall, a telescope of colossal proportions stood still, observing the stars.

At its maximum extension, the telescope had a lens three times the size of Harry. Calmly pointed at the starry sky, the object could even be considered a technological weapon if viewed from a certain angle. As if the entire hall had been made for him, the shelves and any decorations in the place did not obstruct his view.

Near Harry, long tables were covered with documents and equations that made his head spin. Refusing to make any sudden movements, he looked around anxiously, wondering what this could possibly have to do with the Lock Emblem. He then spotted a man on his right, a crayon in his hand. He was drawing geometric shapes in various patterns, mathematical equations surrounding the forms.

Before Harry could do anything, the man took three pieces of paper — a circle, a square, and a triangle — and stuck them on the blackboard, forming a kind of symbol that Harry was too far from understanding.

Almost instantly, the hall shook, causing Harry and the mysterious man to fall. In all this mess, the telescope seemed to reflect something in its lenses. The young man, drawn to the shape that craved attention, was then interrupted by the man.

"Don't look!" the man shouted, startling Harry.

Wasn't I supposed to be in some kind of dream? What the hell is going on? How can he see me? Is it a representation of my subconscious? A swarm of questions assailed Harry's mind, and he decided not to look at whatever the telescope had captured.

"...Can you see me?" Harry asked, taking a step back.

"Don't come any closer, Raviel!" The man shouted again in Harry's direction.

Raviel? He looked back and saw a child of about ten years old. His brown hair was curly and his face was chubby. He was clearly confused, and amidst all the chaos, he seemed not to hear what the man was saying.

He's not talking to me... Harry thought. Does this mean this is a memory? I don't know anyone named Raviel, and I've never seen this man in my life. What am I supposed to do here?

The telescope was now trembling, unsteady. Raviel, without the slightest concern, quickly approached the telescope with a bright smile on his face.

"Raviel!" The man cried out in despair once more, his voice hoarse. "No. Don't come near!"

He fell but ignored the throbbing pain in his foot. He had a thick beard and tired eyes, as if he hadn't slept in weeks.

"...What is this, Dad?" And the boy looked through the telescope's eyepiece again, laughing to himself.

Harry, at that moment, felt his heart stop; he had never seen so much sadness and dread on a man's face before. Wide open, his eyes seemed almost to pop out of their sockets. His hands? They trembled as if he were freezing. He stumbled again as he stood up and cried out, tears in his eyes. His melancholy expression seemed to give even more strength to his pleas as he dragged himself to the eyepiece.

The child, however, had already lost himself as he gazed into the infinite. His joyful gaze was consumed by one of inexpressiveness as he refused to take his eyes off the lens. Before the man could reach him, Harry felt the world crack open once more and the young man softened.

Dissolving, the child, now in the man's arms, turned into a green jelly. The man continued to shout as Harry grew increasingly confused. He then instinctively looked at the starry sky. He turned slightly to the right and saw that the telescope was pointed at the moon. Before he could notice any discrepancy, darkness took over his vision.

"...Harry!" He opened his eyes as if nothing had happened and looked at Devon, still confused.

"What..." He looked at Devon's face, who was holding him, and at the obsidian disc, which now floated and emitted a light never before seen. As if it had its own magnetic field, waves circulated around the object, which now had a mythical appearance.

"What did you see? Harry!" Devon asked. "What happened there!?"

"I-I don't know!" Now more awake, he screamed with a terror he didn't know where it had come from. "I don't remember! Everything is blurry, I think I saw—"

There was an explosion. Harry was thrown to the back of the room, hitting one of the shelves which, by some miracle, didn't fall on top of him. He heard doors, chairs, and tables breaking. Shards flew across the room, leaving scratches and cuts on Harry's face.

Getting up, dazed, he then saw Devon's body under a shelf. No. No. He ran to the spot and helped him out from under the rubble. Coughing, the man had a large wooden stake stuck in his stomach. No. Please no.

"...Harry?" He asked, his voice weak. "G-Get out of here... Quick..."

Devon ran his hand over his stomach, feeling the warm blood seeping from the stake hole. Seeing the red on his hands, he looked around and saw the room completely destroyed, his books piled up under the shelves.

"What do you mean?" Harry asked, his eyes wide. "No, of course not. I'm not leaving you here, I'm not. We have to get you to a hospital, they'll—"

"No!" He screamed and coughed up blood in the same sentence. "You can't. If they find out we were messing with Emblems, you'll... you'll be..."

He coughed violently into Harry's arms, who frantically searched for bandages or anything that could stop the bleeding.

"I'm not leaving here, D-man!" Harry shouted, now crying. "I'm not leaving you, damn it!"

"They'll get you, son," Devon said. "They'll pile you into debt and make you work for them. It's worse than going to prison. It's slave labor..."

"I..." Harry cried again. Devon then held his hand, making the boy smile weakly. "I'm sorry, Devon. I'm so sorry, fuck! I-I didn't want this, I never wanted this. I should never have chosen this, I shouldn't have brought you into this. Shit! I'm sorry!"

"There's no need to be angry, boy." The man replied, his voice growing weaker. He wanted to be strong for Harry, but the terror in his eyes was visible; the fear of death. "I was the one who taught you. I knew the dangers... Although I still can't understand how a Symbol like this could have reacted like this... I guess it's one of life's mysteries, isn't it?"

He closed his eyes as if wanting to sleep but quickly opened them again and slightly raised the corner of his lips; he wouldn't last another two minutes.

Desperate, Harry turned to the disc, which now had no Symbol at all, only a handful of Moon Dust beside it. He then left Devon where he was and ran to the disc. I'll make it. I'll save him. I just need to do it fast. Yes! I'll use my powers and I'll save him. The door between life and death, isn't it? Let's go to Mary's house tomorrow, and, and...

Deep down, he knew he could never acquire a power like that. That ability, to interrupt the cycle of life, would never be easily accessible. Life, and consequently death, are elements as important as water and fire. Even then, he had to try.

He took the powder and followed the same procedure Devon had done. He looked to check on the man periodically, who tried to say something to Harry, who only smiled with tears falling down his face.

The obsidian, for some reason still intact, served as a place for Harry to make one last Symbol. He drew the shape of a door and waited for sleep to come.

After passing his trial, which was much clearer and easier than the Lock Emblem, the moment he awoke, he used all his strength to break the Emblem and ran to Devon, whose warm gaze was no longer there; it was too late.

Without the slightest idea of ​​what else he could do, he carefully embraced Devon, kissing his forehead. He hoped something would happen, that he would miraculously wake up, and that the stake would be removed. None of that happened.

Then he felt a force coursing through his veins, a connection to something thousands of times greater than he could imagine. It was the universe's way of communicating with him. He carefully laid his body on the ground while crying silently.

Using the power of thought, he created a door in front of him and tried to open it, but it was locked. He then generated another door and realized he could open them with his power, without having to manually open the doorknob. The door, as if it had always been there, was made of wood and had ornate patterns on its front. A hard, round doorknob remained in the center to the right.

When he opened them, he immediately realized what he could do. Create passages between them, almost like portals. Before he could try anything else, he heard knocking on the door, followed by several muffled sounds.

Of course they've arrived... Wiping the tears from his face, still covered in bruises and dust, he carefully removed Devon's glasses, placed them on his face, and gave his lifeless body one last hug. Before the officers could enter and retrieve him, Harry awkwardly created a door to exit through a hole that had been made in the ceiling during the explosion.

Swallowing his fear, Harry took a step forward and fell onto his own house. He then realized he had made the door too high, so he plummeted to the ground at high speed. Suppressing a scream, amidst the city buildings, he created a door beneath him and another randomly pointed upwards. Doing this a few more times — exhausted from everything that had happened — he eventually managed to stay on top of a door without creating another, a door for which no passage had yet been created.

The Lock Emblem was already being used... He thought about how quickly things had changed.

He gasped in panic at what had just happened and realized he had a lot of work ahead of him. He looked down and saw fire and debris covering the library entrance. Before he could become enraged, he spotted Devon's body on the floor, looking directly at Harry through the hole.

The young man wept once more as he stared at the sky, specifically at the moon. A tattoo was then slowly being placed on his back.

One that would take years to complete.
