Xavier materialised in a vast hallway with doors on either side stretching seemingly forever. He reached out and touched a door to his left, smiling slightly. His tower quarters.

He stepped into the room, saw the small bed, the chest at its foot, the little writing desk.

This is where it all began.

He closed the door and slowly walked to the stairwell, remembering his first few days here, and the time he was using a vending machine as a weight in the hallway.

Xavier’s time dilation field had been cancelled out when the System had teleported him into the tower. He’d activated another one instantly.

He headed down the spiralling steps to the bottom floor and the tavern he knew would be there.

The other Champions he passed all had time dilation fields wrapped around them too, though theirs were nowhere near as powerful. He must have looked like a blur as he flitted down the steps.

There was even a Champion or two without a time dilation field, which made him realise just how little time had passed not only since the rules in the tower had changed, but also since he’d started teaching the people of Earth how to use spell patterns.

Some of the tower floors ran longer than others, and there were still Champions in the tower who hadn’t been trained up in the same way as everyone else back on Earth and in the wider Silver River and Ventorin sectors had been.

I’ll have something done about that.

He threw off a quick communication to John Hammond, knowing the man would be able to get it sorted. Then he pushed open the door to the tavern and paused after stepping through, taking a deep breath of the somewhat stale air.

Candles burned on the tables, their flickering flames frozen outside his field. A fire was bright in the hearth. Adranial hadn’t arrived yet—she wouldn’t for another few minutes. The woman would have materialised in a different part of the long hallway than he had, and her time dilation field wasn’t quite as fast, but as they were in the same Champion of Earth cohort, this would be the tavern instance she turned up in when she opened the door at the bottom of the tower.

There were several different parties sitting around the tables, though there was no sign of Adranial’s old party—a party that was still beholden to the old man, having refused the offer to erase their contracts—but there were a few other familiar faces.

Sam being the most familiar of them all, still standing behind the bar, idly wiping it clean.

Xavier snapped his time dilation field outward, encompassing everyone in the room. It didn’t take long for the barkeep and the Champions to notice the sudden shift. Heads turned to him in, eyes widened, and there were several small gasps.

But what he didn’t feel, from any of them, was fear.

Every single one of the Champions in this room had possessed their own time dilation field, which meant each of them had likely been connected to that portal when he’d shared the power of Silver River Alpha and Silver River Prime to kill the World Destroyer—some of the Champions, of course, might have been stuck in the tower at the same.

Xavier wasn’t so powerful that he could create a portal here.

Every single Champion stood and raised their drinks high as a cheer rang out.

And it wasn’t just the Champions, either. Sam came out from around the bar, a large flagon held aloft. “To Xavier Collins! The Champion of Silver River!”

The cheers tripled in volume.

Xavier stopped himself from shifting his weight from one foot to the other. He stood rooted, relaxed his shoulders, and gave a small wave. He’d never felt terribly comfortable in this position, with people staring at him—whether in admiration or otherwise—but he was getting more and more used to faking it.

He was the leader of two galaxies, after all.

The Denizens here, of all places on Earth, knew he wasn’t much of a talker. They didn’t huddle around him or ask for a speech—god, he was glad they hadn’t asked for a speech. When it was clear Xavier had nothing profound to say, they went back to their business, occasionally glancing over at him but otherwise chatting along as though he wasn’t even there.

Xavier wondered if it was more awkward for them or for him. Here he was, their supreme leader, their king, emperor… just come for a drink in the same tavern they’d shared in the past whenever he was in the Tower of Champions.

He could snap the time dilation field so it was only around him… but no, that really wasn’t necessary.

He shrugged away the awkwardness and headed for the bar, ordering a cappuccino from Sam.

The man was uncharacteristically silent as he made the drink. When Sam placed the mug onto the bar, he gave a huge grin. “You did it! You defeated the World Destroyer!” He released a long sigh, and it seemed as though every bit of tension the man had carried with him over the years left his body. “Seriously, Xavier, thank you. I’ve dedicated my life to helping the Emp—” He frowned. “To helping Larona find you.” He poured himself a drink, took a sip. “Honestly, I never dreamed we would succeed.”

“You thought the Silver River sector was doomed?”

“She had me looking for new Champions, at E Grades. I trust that woman, but I just didn’t imagine the odds of someone so new to the System—only a newly integrated world, no less!—would be our saviour.”

“Saviour…” Xavier sipped his coffee. “People don’t actually call me that, do they?”

“Really? You didn’t know?”

“I haven’t really been around many people lately. Not people in Silver River and Ventorin, anyway.”

Sam sat on a stool behind the bar and peered at him. “Where have you been?” He nodded at the Champions. “I haven’t had a chance to learn your new ways, not here in the tower. But I’ve seen what your powers can do, the way you’ve spread them to everyone… It’s amazing, Xavier. I knew you were becoming powerful, but I had no idea what you were really capable of. Though I don’t have a time dilation field someone here is always kind enough to encompass me in theirs.”

“So decades have passed for you too, since the last time I was here?”

The man nodded. “Which makes me wonder just how much time you’ve passed, and where you’ve been.”

Xavier tapped a finger on the bar. Honestly, he wasn’t really sure how much time had passed for him. He’d last left the tower with Adranial, and then they’d trained with their Prime versions. That had been ten years, but more time had passed beyond that.

He sighed, smiled. “I really don’t know.”

Sam chuckled. “You don’t even know?”

Xavier glanced around the tavern. Adranial wasn’t here yet. He manifested a spell that would allow the two of them to speak privately. “How about I tell you where I’ve been?”

Sam listened raptly. Xavier didn’t tell the man everything, but he told him enough.

“Jhanku? Are you kidding me?”

All the tension that had released from the man’s body returned, slamming into him with full force. His shoulders slumped, head dropping. His gaze fell to his drink and he downed the whole thing. “Jhanku. You’ve pissed off one of the biggest players of the universe.”

“To be fair, he started it.”

Sam shut his eyes. “You have a plan, right?”

Xavier took another sip of his coffee, only to find the mug empty. “I do.”

“Is there anything more to the plan than ‘get stronger?’”

Xavier raised a finger, dropped it. “You know, that about sums it up.” He grinned. “Still think I’m a saviour?”

Sam cursed under his breath. “I think you’re just as insane as you were when you first walked into this tavern.” He sighed. “But honestly, after all you’ve done… it’s no surprise they’re coming after you.”

The tavern’s door opened and Adranial strolled in. Xavier couldn’t help but notice a few of the Champions’ heads turning her way, giving her appraising looks. He frowned, feeling suddenly murderous. He’d never thought he was the jealous type, but he’d also never been in a situation like this. It made him feel the need to announce to everyone in that room that Adranial was with him.

Something told him there was no socially acceptable way to just blurt that out.

She made her way to the bar and pecked him on the cheek before sliding onto the stool next to him. A few people cleared their throats and averted their gazes, staring everywhere and anywhere but at them.

He smiled inwardly at that.

Word will spread.

Sam’s gaze flicked between them, a smile slowly forming, though he didn’t make a comment about them. “Adranial.” He nodded. “The usual, then?”

“Thanks, Sam.” Adranial stared around the tavern. “It’s strange, being back here.”

Xavier nodded. Every moment since he’d arrived, he’d been half-expecting to get a communication from someone back on Earth or elsewhere in his domain announcing a full-scale invasion of his sectors had commenced. It made him want to smash through ten floors as fast as possible just so he had the option to leave the tower at the drop of a hat.

“Very,” Xavier replied. “Though it also feels… right.”

Xavier wasn’t really sure which floor of the tower he should enter. If Roln was there, he might be able to ask the mental construct for advice—the dead Wanderer wasn’t restricted by the System, so he could tell him basically anything.

But of course, Roln wasn’t there—not in any practical sense.

Helping Roln recover was still something happening in the back of his mind. He was always inside that room with him…

Xavier and Adranial ended up ordering a meal and sitting at one of the corner tables. They chatted for a while, as though nothing of note was happening. It almost felt like they were just in the middle of a normal life, and that when the meal was done, they’d return to that home they’d just finished building.

Until, of course, reality settled back on their shoulders.

“So, what floor are you going to start on?” Adranial ran a finger around the rim of her glass. She’d mentioned, or rather tried to mention, a floor that he should enter, when she’d advised him to return to the tower.

He just had no idea which she meant.

He smiled. “I could tell you, but I don’t think we’re going to trick the System into allowing you to tell which one to go to.”

“So many things you can do, and yet still beholden to the System…” Adranial bit her lip and glanced at the ceiling.

Xavier wondered if he should tell her about that time in the Roving Seed Base, when The System is Watching had been written in the sky.

Oh, I fully intend to strip the System of all its powers. One day, he thought, wishing it was something he could say aloud. Adranial, being from the oldest family in the universe, would likely know just how much she should and shouldn’t say about the System, and didn’t say another word about it.

When their meal was over, Xavier felt giddy with excitement.

He’d discarded the plan to smash through ten easy floors. Instead, he was going to continue where he’d left off.

Onward, to the three-hundred-and-first floor.
