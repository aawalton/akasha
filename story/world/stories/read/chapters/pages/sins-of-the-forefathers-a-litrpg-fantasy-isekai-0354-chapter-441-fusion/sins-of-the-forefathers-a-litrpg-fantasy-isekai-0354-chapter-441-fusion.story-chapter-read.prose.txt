The last time I had designed and enchanted prosthetic body parts, it had taken me weeks. At the time, I’d had Grey to help me with the process. Not only that, but I’d had the entire logistics and purse of the Uprising at my command.

This time I didn’t have that. Right now, all I had was a box of scraps and my own experience.

And still, I thought I could get this done in the time it would take the refugee forces from Rhoscara to reach the base underneath Addersfield.

From what an incredibly harried-looking Bleddyn had told me, when he stopped by as we were loading up our cart for the journey, it would take about five days of continuous travel. Everyone who was going to survive their wounds after the battle of Rhoscara had reached the point where they were going to. It was time to move on, and there would be no more stopping to set up camp on the way there. I would have to do all of my work in a cheap wagon barely big enough for me to stretch out my arms to the side, all the while we were on the move.

At the very least, it promised to be a mostly smooth ride. Even after centuries of neglect, the pavestones of the Long Road were still in good shape.

I could do this, though. Even in my weakened state, even when I was in a clone body, I was confident in my crafting abilities.

I may have fucked up when it came to my leadership capabilities, but I could still smith some bones.

Aetherial Melding had no inherent Mana cost associated with its use. I had long grown used to the stamina drain from crafting with my odd Profession, and it very rarely affected me deeply. My skill with it had only grown over time as well, and I didn’t need outside Aetherial assistance in order to smelt and shape molten metal. I still recalled the odd grill-type device that Azarus had created for me, once upon a time, when I had still needed a ready source of fire to help in my manipulation of metal. I had long since outgrown things like that.

Still, though. I didn’t have a great deal of material to work with to craft the new bones that would support my upper skeletal system. Trinkets, jewelry, fragments of armor and weaponry. The Unchained hadn’t actually had a great deal of spare materials on hand for my use. I’d have to get by with scraps. That just made things all the more difficult, because there was no way I would be able to enchant these bones deeply enough to fuse safely to my skeleton unless the base materials were heavily mystical in the first place.

I did have one more source of material, all of my own, though.

After Daffyd had struck me into a coma, and after I had been evacuated to the then base of the Unchained within Rhoscara, Sylvia had ventured out one last time into the city. She had gone out and retrieved the shards of my last retractable spear, which the werewolf had broken. I had noticed the pieces of it sitting in a pile behind my body, after I had woken up, and thought that was curious.

But now they were going to be essential.

You see, when I had originally crafted my first prosthetic arm, it had been forged from Mithril. And that was a great metal, don’t get me wrong. Mithril was an incredibly conductive material to work enchantments with. Strong and light, it was the gold standard when it came to enchanting work.

The problem was, though, that it was too conductive. There was literally zero resistance to Aetherial flow within the metal. Mithril was, to use a term from Terra, a super-conductor.

Which had been a problem.

If we had forged that arm entirely from Mithril, my internal Aether would have come spilling out the end of it because the metal wouldn’t have been able to offer enough resistance to prevent it from doing so. To solve this problem, we had eventually settled on mixing in common iron into the mix of the Mithril, as it was being smelted and smithed. Mystically, iron was the exact opposite of Mithril. Where the silvery metal had zero resistance, iron had zero conductivity. It had turned out mostly fine, though there had been some trade-offs with the bargain. My sense of touch with that first prosthetic had been muted compared to either a real flesh hand or the Primordium one I had right now. It hadn’t quite been as strong structurally as a pure Mithril appendage would have been either.

But with Oninite…

I hadn’t even known about the existence of Oninite before I had reached Helstein, all that time ago, well after my first arm had been forged and grafted. The black and blue metal was a curious contrast to Mithril. It was heavier, for one, while being nearly as strong structurally as Mithril when it was forged. But it was also much more charged from Aetherial and geological processes deep within Vereden that created raw Oninite. It was absolutely loaded with equally raw Aether, making it an extremely temperamental material for a smith to work with. It was the entire reason that most smiths tended to run as far away from Oninite as possible when it came to working the metal.

It tended to explode during the forging process.

Incredibly sharp metal fragments moving at subsonic speed weren’t exactly conducive to one’s health.

Here’s the thing about Oninite, though. It was a metal that was a study in contrasts, something that actually befuddled some of the enchanters I talked to.

It was a terrible conductor of Aether…and yet it was excellent material for enchanting.

Because Oninite retained Aether.

That was the secret I had discovered. Something that had been giving me a number of ideas, ever since I had discovered it, over the months I’d been working with the metal.

Oninite was a natural battery when it came to Aether.

There was a great deal of potential there. Not anything remotely useful for my current purposes, no. But in the future?

God, I hoped I survived this. I hoped Grey survived what was happening to the world right now, at the hands of a Dwarf with more ambition than sense.

We could change the world with that knowledge. I’m sure my mentor would understand the broader implications of my discovery.

That aspect of Oninite wasn’t terribly useful for forging my new clavicles, though. Instead, I was just thankful that I had a good amount of the metal to mix with the Oninite that the Unchained had scrounged together for me. It would work better than the steel I had used for my first prosthetic. Together, the resulting alloy of Oninite and Mithril would be stronger than that had been, and just the right conductivity for Aether that it wouldn’t throw my system out of whack when implanted into my body.

I had just enough of the resulting metal when I mixed them together, in the darkness of the rumbling wagon, that Renauld and I would be able to replace my lost bones.

The result was… different-looking than I thought it would be. I’d almost expected a hazy, smoky-looking silver metal, considering the hues of both parts.

But that wasn’t what I received.

Instead…it was almost purer looking than either a bright silver or a pitch black. Not quite white, but something brighter and closer to that than I was expecting. The blue note from the Oninite had carried forward as well, lending the metal a near ethereal air. I could feel the Aetherial weight of the metal as well.

I almost wanted to compare it to the Primordium of my Orus shaped prosthetic. Primordium itself was far purer, though, a bright, almost shining white shot through with the coruscating rainbow of pure Aether. This…was lesser than that, but there was something about what I had created.

It felt significant, and I deeply wanted to look into it more. But alas, I just didn’t have the time for something like that. We were only days away from reaching Addersfield, and I had to shape and enchant the resulting bones.

This was the part that I needed Renauld’s help with. I wasn’t exactly perfectly familiar with the shape of the Human clavicle, and honestly, neither was my Gnollish Healer. He’d mended more than a few in his time, but that wasn’t the same as being able to map out the bone when asked.

We had to get creative.

First, there were the actual dimensions of what I needed to create. The replacement bones needed to be forged to exacting specifications, if they were going to be the struts that supported my entire skeleton. Daffyd hadn’t exactly been neat and clean when he had ripped my bones from my chest. What had been left behind had been closed up in a hurry, after Renauld had exhaustively removed all of the shards left behind from the forceful extraction. The ends where they had been torn from, attaching both to my sternum and my scapulae, still featured a small amount of residual bone from where the bulk had been torn.

After the debate, Renauld and I reluctantly agreed that these remnants had to come out. We would have to incorporate the removal of this residual bone in the surgery we drafted to replace them. That would have to wait until we reached Addersfield, though. It would be a death sentence for my body if we tried to do it in this wagon.

Still, it wasn’t all doom and gloom. The presence of those fragments still anchored to my skeleton meant that they could be used to provide measurements for the proportions of my replacements. Using a spell that was remarkably similar to the results of an x-ray test from Terra, Renauld and I had carefully marked out these measurements from end to end, for what both clavicles should look like.

We had the measurements; now we just needed the shape.

To do that, we were going to need examples. However, it’s not like there were just examples of a clavicle just lying around. It’s like we could pop into the extremely well-stocked Healing department of the Academy and asked for a free bone or two to examine. Nor could we just rip some out of a poor fool somewhere in this encampment. Also, we couldn’t use dead bone in the first place to model my replacements off of. Healthy, living bone had far different properties than dead bone, and it would be a mistake to do so. No, we needed to map out a living example.

Luckily, Renauld could do such a thing with the same Spell he’d used to examine my chest. We just needed a subject that we could examine. Sylvia was out, for obvious reasons, and it’s not like my clone body actually had bones that could be examined. It was really just a mass of Human shaped Mana, in the end. Renauld himself was a no-go as well, even after he had taught me the x-ray spell. Gnoll bones were just too different from Human ones to count for our purposes, though they did still have clavicles. Our subject couldn’t be a female Human, either, so we couldn’t ask Maria-

(Who was simultaneously relieved to see me and baffled by the circumstances I had found myself in. I had told her I had full confidence in her ability to lead the remnants of the Herztalian expedition while I was laid up, and represent us to the Unchained leadership.

The former spy had told me to shut up and concentrate on getting better.

Good to see at least one person had come out from Rhoscara relatively unscathed.)

-after she finally had time to stop in. No, it was going to need to be a Human man.

Bleddyn was too busy with his duties, now that he was back among his people, so…

I grabbed the first member of the expedition who was free and also unlikely to protest.

That person turned out to be Thaddeus.

The former homeless soldier was baffled by the request but willing to cooperate. Both Renauld and I were exacting as we used the x-ray Spell to map out the exact shape of the man’s clavicles as precisely as we could.

All of this was done in the first day of our travel through the Long Road. It was pretty damn hectic, but by the end of the day, I had everything I needed to get started on the actual forge work. I had no need for rest as a clone, and frankly, no time, so I got to work on the forging throughout the night, while the procession pushed through it as well.

And by the time lunch would have rolled through on the second day of travel…

I’d done it. Two precisely shaped and forged clavicles of the odd alloy I had prepared for this, sitting on the lone, tiny table I had in this wagon. I was barely aware of the world after working for twelve straight, tireless hours as I pushed Aetherial Melding to its limit.

But I was aware enough to be a bit disturbed by the sight of them. It was…eerie to see things that would soon go into my body, lying innocuously on tthe able.

What strange turns my life sometimes took me on.

I soon shook the feeling off, though, and rolled my shoulders. I had more work to do. This had been the easy part. Just creating the bones themselves was only step one.

Step two was fully imbuing them with the prosthetic enchantments that Grey and I had labored so long to create. If I tried to meld those ‘bones’ into my chest as they were, both my physical self and my soul would reject them, and I would…probably die horribly from the rejection.

Time to grind, I suppose.

I took a deep breath…

And fell into my crafting trance once more.
