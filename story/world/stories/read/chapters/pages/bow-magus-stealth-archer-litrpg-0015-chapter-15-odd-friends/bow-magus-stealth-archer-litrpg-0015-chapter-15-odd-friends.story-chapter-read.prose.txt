Rex steadied his breath and tamed his thudding heart. He had to be as calm and quiet as humanly possible, or it would all be for nothing.

There.

In an instant, he loosened, sending an arrow whizzing through the underbrush. For a second, he felt like time had slowed, praying that the arrow was on target.

He couldn’t afford to miss.

The arrow had moved with such speed that when it struck, he didn’t immediately realize. The deer raised its head, then jerked, and toppled over.

“Damn, [ Powershot ] is no joke.”

He walked over to it. The arrow had blasted straight through its neck, leaving a bloody hole behind. It was still groaning too, lurching as it kicked out its final death throws on the ground.

“I’m not a bad hunter, am I?” Rex said, turning to his jiggly blue companion with a wide grin.

But Slimer didn’t reply. Instead, it began to roll toward the kill.

“Hey, hey, hey! What do you think you’re doing, you little thief! We’re going to need some rules around here!” Rex said, rushing to intercept the slime. “That over there is my kill. And while I’m happy to share with a contributing party member, that does not mean you get the first bite. Got it?”

Slimer jiggled and seemed to nod.

“Good. I’m glad we could come to an under–HEY!”

Slimer continued to roll.

“I said stop!”

In the end, Rex was forced to tie the deer up by its legs and hang it from a thick branch. However, he did need to bleed it anyway.

He got to set up a camp for a fire and a meal, but kept an eye on Slimer as it tried to roll up the trunk of the tree.

“I am very glad you can’t do that.”

But as he watched Slimer, he realized that the slime was still much smaller than when he met it. His arrow had obviously done a bit of damage.

“Sorry about the… You know. The arrow. Kind of surprised you're so chill about it, to be honest. I did try to kill you.”

Slimer didn’t reply, so he kept working, moving to slit the throat after putting down his pack. The moment blood dripped out, Slimer was on it, jiggling happily beneath it as it consumed.

“You like that, do you? Well, go nuts. It’s all yours.”

As Rex built a fire and got ready to cook, he could have sworn Slimer got a little bigger. Though he still felt kind of bad, and when he got to butchering, he cut a leg for the slime.

“Yeah, I thought you’d like that. It’s good, huh?”

He was still annoyed about the artifact; on the other hand, he felt like Slimer had a lot of potential if he could figure out some communication with the monster.

“Consider the meat payback for shooting you. I hope you don’t take it too personally. Still, that doesn’t mean you can just steal my artifacts, okay?”

Slimer wiggled happily, though it was almost certainly because of the meat it was dissolving.

Rex cured some jerk by the fire while he ate. After he was done, he stashed away his jerky and wrapped the rest up before burying it some distance away.

“Alright,” he dusted off his hands. “Time to move again. Don’t look at me like that. We made a fire. It’s far too risky to stay around here now.”

It was true that he needed to get moving. But he also needed some kind of plan. He had no idea how large the world he was on was, or how far away they were from any other civilization. And he couldn’t remain on the run forever. Besides, he didn’t feel right leaving Andy behind.

What am I going to do? What if they really are slavers?

Even if the farmers were slaves, there was no evidence that they kept the Classed as slaves. But that didn’t mean he could just leave Andy behind. Or the other recruits, for that matter.

But it’s not like I can take on Jango and everybody else at the farm. I’ll get myself killed for sure.

As if reading his thoughts, Slimer made a slushing sound. Unfortunately, if it was trying to communicate, he didn’t understand a word of it. But it did give him an idea.

Then again, I do have this little guy now…

Rex hadn’t made up his mind entirely. But with Slimer, he had an escape option. Which meant he could take far bigger risks than before.

I could ambush the other hunters. It would cull the slavers down. The problem is, I still wouldn’t be able to take the farm. Not unless I got the other recruits to team up with me…

Even if all of them join him, it would still be a hard battle. They were weaker than Jango and his veterans and didn’t have artifacts.

That said, I don’t have to commit to anything just yet.

There was also his Ambusher Feat, which would make getting closer to the farm without getting spotted much easier.

Okay, I head back toward the farm and try to scope things out. Depending on what I come across, I can decide how to handle things. I just need to find somewhere to put this little guy.

He liked to think that at least some of what he was saying was getting through to Slimer. But if his life was on the line, he couldn’t take any risks.

It took him a while to find somewhere suitable. But finally, he came across a burrow. Slimer followed him inside, and he used [ Harden ] to make a door of woven leaves to keep the disobedient monster inside.

Rex still wasn’t sure exactly how many spells he could keep tethered at once, but he could feel a very weak drain on his mana as he set up another. He had also realized that [ Harden ] gradually wore off. In the heat of battle, he had forgotten just how uncomfortable his iron-like tunic was. But at some point, it had weakened and was just kind of stiff.

Since he couldn’t take any unnecessary risks walking back into enemy territory, he used [ Harden ] on his tunic again, but didn’t bother binding it to a thread.

“So uncomfortable, but better than dying. Alright, time to go see what I can find out.”

Even with his precautions, he knew that heading back toward the farm was a big risk. But so was remaining in the middle of the forest. And while he still didn’t really know what a shadow beast was, he had a feeling that bumping into one wouldn’t be any better for him than the hunters.

It was some trek back. He had walked further than he had realized. Luckily, he was naturally good with directions, found the river fairly quickly, and could follow it most of the way back.

Well before he spotted anyone, he sensed vague auras. They weren’t close enough or strong enough to pinpoint, but easy enough to tell that there were multiple of them around.

Who’d have guessed? It seems they are looking for me.

He decided to try to walk toward them. He didn’t necessarily want a confrontation, but he felt it was better to find them before they found him.

Predictably, he sensed them where the battle had taken place and carefully moved in that direction.

They had set up camp in the goblin clearing, which made sense since it was a perfect camping spot, and he spotted a couple of hunters on watch.

Doesn’t seem like they’ve detected me yet. This Feat really does work.

Rex remained vigilant, though. He knew auras could spike for various reasons, and just because they hadn’t sensed him yet didn’t mean they couldn’t.

Even with Slimer, I don’t think this is wise.

He was stronger than before, but even so, he had only just survived the previous encounter with the help of the goblins. And he was not at all confident that having Slimer as a backup was enough insurance. Especially since they had clearly set up a camp with lookouts. And they were hunters, after all. There were bound to be traps about.

Carefully, he backtracked, taking a wide berth around the hunter camp and heading toward the farmstead.

He wasn’t going to make the same mistake that the hunters had made when they came looking for him.

It was getting late, and the afternoon was waning, which Rex embraced. Darkness would only make creeping around easier. And to his surprise, as he got closer to the farm, he sensed more auras ahead of him.

Wait, those are recruits.

Spying from a distance and shrouded by shrub, he spotted several who had come through the Gate when he did. And they appeared to be combing the forest, looking for something.

I guess that means Jango is looking for me, too.

His chest sank a little. Finding the other recruits was part of his plan, but it seemed dumb now. If they were looking for him, they were obviously working for Jango.

He could jump out and tell them everything, hoping they would listen. But he had killed multiple hunters. It wasn’t like portraying him as the villain would be particularly hard, even if the others were suspicious, like he and Andy were. After all, suspicion was one thing; proof was another.

Keeping an eye on them, he crept around. He wasn’t ready to try to make contact, but he was far less intimidated by the recruits compared to the hunters.

If he could just find Andy, then maybe he’d have someone he trusted to expose himself to. He could explain himself. After all, Andy already didn’t trust the operation; he was the one who first thought they were slavers. Surely he would be on his side.

But as he made his way around, he was mentally verifying each of the auras as he sensed them, linking them to the recruits they belonged to as they came into view, and saw no sign of Andy.

Dammit, Andy, where are you?

He even spotted Neanderthal amongst them, but he sure as the hells wasn’t testing his luck with him.

What about Ernie? Is he not here either? It looks like they’ve rounded just about everyone else up. What’s going on–

As soon as he had the thought, a chilling realization struck. They were the only two he had really spoken to since he arrived, and they just so happened to be the only two he couldn’t see.

If they’ve done something to them.

His fists clenched. What if it was his fault? What if something had happened to them because of what he did to the hunters?

A pit formed in his stomach. He didn’t even know them that well, but he couldn’t stand the thought of his actions resulting in something bad happening to them.

He kept going, trying to scope out the farm as well. But stopped in his tracks as he got closer. He could sense something. It was similar to an aura, but much stronger.

His brow furrowed. It wasn’t just a strong aura, though. It was as if somebody was projecting their aura outward, into the surrounding forest from the farm.

Suddenly, he felt it. The aura struck him. It wasn’t physical. But it was there, and he immediately knew that it had spotted him.

Rex didn’t waste a second, rushing back the way he had come. But he reminded himself to remain calm. Panic would just project his aura for all to see. And he wasn’t ready to use [ Transference ]; that might put Slimer in harm's way, and it was only a backup he would use if absolutely necessary.

The strangest part of it all was that no alarm seemed to be triggered. The recruits were still aimlessly walking about, searching through the brush, and didn’t stand a chance at finding him as he snuck past.

He didn’t see or sense any of Jango’s veterans pouring out from the farm, either. But he was sure of what he had felt. Whatever it had been, it had known he was there.

And so, he kept going. His pace picked up the further he got from the other auras. And soon he was pretty much running. Perhaps moving too fast, as he didn’t sense the aura ahead until it was too late, skidding to a halt. But it had already seen him.

“Wait, Freyja?”

The young freckled girl, maybe a year older than him, smiled as she stepped out of the moonlit bush.
