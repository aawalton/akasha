Seven thrones in the distance, arranged in a semicircle.

Each magnificent and grand, each a peak Origin-grade treasure. One at the very center, with a back that reached nearly to the shattered skies, a back that featured a giant clock with ethereal hands, ticking and ticking.

Eight broken pillars studded the land, four to either side.

The Throne Room.

The Mirror Dragon made landfall a second later. And Stegar, quietly, to its side.

They flanked a certain six-eyed serpent standing at the very center of the realm—in the midst of all the thrones.

The serpent that'd been on Zane's mind since the day he'd locked onto it in the void-tunnel.

The communication charm on his chest was cold.

Reina's avatar had been wiped out in the transition. The last thing she'd given him was a top-up of Sanctified Physique and her Vitality-preserving Blessing—that barrier in his soul he'd seen in his fight against Cromm.

He'd always imagined her here with him, in this moment, but there it was.

It was a safe assumption these Blessings would be the last of the support he'd get, at least directly.

He felt quite coldly calm as he noted Malzareth's state.

It hadn't fully healed, he saw. There was a faded, yet quite prominent Destruction-scar over the scales on its chest—white marring inky black, but it'd recovered quite well.

Its soul was laced with scars, but it still felt most of the way back to its peak.

It was silent here too as they assessed each other—as its gaze swept over Cromm impassively.

It seems, it said. I have traded a Prime and a Supreme Skill for an avatar. And an avatar of a True God, at that...

Two of its six eyes went to crescents.

How fortuitous, it said. For you. Unlocking the soul with the System. Gaining the Title. Stumbling upon the future Mistress, and Aiwe, and the System Store—the Store, which has dispensed treasures upon you as though to a favorite son.

It was silent.

But then... that is the right of the Title. Fate is never fair. It favors certain souls, those chosen few—for so long, it favored I.

Zane stood fully upright and clasped his hands to his chains.

He observed it in the Astral Plane—observed the Mirror Dragon and Stegar, and found streams of essence and lines of Fate connecting them to the thrones in the background. He couldn't determine exactly what the function of those lines was, but it was safe to assume they represented extra abilities or buffs.

Malzareth's Thrones connected mainly to itself, with thin lines bridging to its two Prime Summons. One, what seemed to be a mage, with an obsidian-topped staff. The other an archer, but its bow also held crystals, so he had to assume a magical component there as well.

Above Malzareth were a fleet of twenty or so summoned spears—the same it'd struck the Barbarian Sage with.

But that was the extent of its prepared Summons, from what he could understand. Just two Primes and some weaponry—there wasn't any sign of an army.

It seemed to want to get ahead of his Supreme.

I have considered, it said. How I might approach this moment. What I would say—but then, what is the use?

It cocked two of its heads. Will I intimidate you? Break your spirit? But it took nine days and nine nights for me to bring your master to the brink... how long would it take to do the same to you?

Another silence.

Individuals may fall. Galaxies may fall. But in the end a Cycle tilts toward man and his allies... The System upsets the balance. And so it is. It is useless to resist Fate.

And yet—why you?

As far as what it said—if it wanted to give him time, it could do as it pleased. In the meantime he felt he'd grasped the broad strokes of what he was facing.

Why was it you? Of all men, in all the Galaxies that could've been sent... it seems Fate, in its wisdom, is determined to finish me.

It leveled its eyes at Zane.

There is no reason you should survive this battle, it said. I would like to see how Fate protects you now, Zane Walker.

The points of Destruction on its spear sharpened, brightening, stacking with shards, and shards—the Prime archer drew arrows of pure essence. The Mirror Dragon's throat lit up with crystals, and Stegar was already lifting its ion cannons—

The spears struck first—the very first attack of that final fight.

But Zane was gone by the time they landed. A fleet of ten spears shunted far overhead.

Then a wall of essence, thousands of shards in all, filled the air in front of him.

Crystalline beams. Ion beams, reflected by newly summoned mirrors; blocks of hardened mana barriers flying toward him, conjured by that Prime mage, and the Prime archer was letting loose too, its arrows splitting and cloning even as they left its fingers—

It was a serious test of his Great Sage Mind and his reaction times, all at once.

A test he passed almost perfectly.

The spatial component to his Supernova Step showed a little more of its worth.

He partially phased through a wall of intertwined arrows, and mirrored arrows, and ion beams—then he was through on the other side, barely scratched.

It was a level of spatial aspect he hadn't yet shown—he simply hadn't been pushed to.

Then he took another step, which was sufficient to close the distance between him and his first target.

The Mirror Dragon loomed before him, its body reflecting the shine of his Axes—Axes whose edges carried Destruction and little else. Hardly any true essence.

He knew the playbook against this thing quite well; he'd fought its Endbringer too many times. And once he was in close, it acted as a shield for many of the projectiles coming at him—not unlike its fallen ally Cromm.

He stepped and let loose a series of slashes—making sure not to leave any physical strength on the table—stepped again, climbing around the Dragon even as it twisted to get at him.

He made it about a third of the way up its length, leaving sizable cracks down its crystalline body.

That was as far as he got.

He had to slow down slightly to land strikes, and that opened him up to counters.

The first few volleys, he was fast enough to avoid—Great Sage Mind and his Steps' spatial dimensions worked wonders there.

Then he felt reality ripple around him.

Three Destruction-spears appeared directly in front of him at mach speed.

By the time he realized what'd happened, they'd already struck him. Digging several inches into his chest, snapping off violently as they did, and he felt the Destruction carve straight into him.

They drew bright strings of blood, blood just brimming with Vitality. His Health instantly dropped a clean 10%.

Even as he was falling he realized what'd happened—the spears had been warped directly into his path.

He was lanced with essence arrows on the way down.

At this point, he came to the conclusion that there was little point in holding off any longer.

As he fell, plummeting head-first toward the ground, his eyes went shining silver.

He arrested his momentum with two steps in rapid succession, turned, and charged the Dragon again. The speed of all his actions had instantly increased several-fold.

And this time his Axes were filled with essence.

His strategy changed entirely.

He absolutely blitzed the Mirror Dragon with Star-Crushing Slashes.

It didn't have a hope of keeping up; its head was still turning, crystals still summoning around it, as he raced up its side, slashing it again, and again, and again.

Now fissures riddled its surface, tail to neck, fissures bright with the light of his Destruction.

Six slashes, ten slashes, sixteen, twenty—

Reality warped massively around Zane—but it canceled out almost instantly as it caught the Mirror Dragon too, and Malzareth let out a sound of pure frustration.

Zane focused back on his target.

He stepped away from ion beams as he carried up his path, letting splitting essence arrows clatter harmlessly off the Dragon's neck.

In that time, he lit up the Mirror Dragon like a godsdamned Christmas tree.

It didn't shatter.

He quickly realized the issue, even as he dodged a hyper-powered crystalline beam.

The Mirror Dragon's beam couldn't dump essence fast enough. That wasn't his concern. But it could offload its excess essence to its throne, which seemed to act as some vast battery.

Fine, then.

He stepped out of the way of a fleet of reality-warped spears—easier to feel coming, now that he had his senses trained on reality itself as well.

And then he simply did it the old-fashioned way.

He hit the Mirror Dragon with Star-crushing Slashes, Slashes filled with pure physicality.

A slight modification, in that he took all the essence out of the blow—but his new Supreme'd essence still powered his body, and that meant his movements were still leagues faster.

He climbed up the Mirror Dragon like a jagged lightning bolt.

He still left giant fissures, one after another, on a crystalline form that he knew would do what crystals did when subjected to enough sheer mechanical force.

So far the Mirror Dragon was almost an observer in its own devastation. All its attacks simply came too slowly, too distantly. It had a similar issue to Cromm; if those hyper-powered beams and reality-ripping crystal claws had landed, they might've had an impact.

It managed to hit him with an omnidirectional essence blast which kept him off it for all of half a second.

At this point Malzareth seemed to understand, at least to an extent, the degree to which it was still underestimating Zane.

To its credit, it adjusted. Reality-warping clearly wasn't fast enough, so it quickly paired that Skill with something else—its eyes brightened, and reality seemed to solidify around Zane, just briefly. Slowing him for that shard of time.

It was enough for it to warp five or six spears straight into him.

It managed to do this again and again, forcing him back a step or two each time. It seemed a stalling tactic, based on volume, more than one meant to kill him, which was likely the right call.

At this point, Zane was quite certain very few things could truly down him in his Supreme.

The issue was—he could take these attacks, and dish out a half-dozen more blows in rapid succession.

Its Prime simply couldn't.

In the end they could all only watch as Malzareth's greatest Prime gained fissure upon fissure—fissures that started to leak essence and Destruction-light, all throughout its form...

It still held together, letting loose wanton blasts as it did, a few of which struck Malzareth's Summons.

Its body seemed a kind of superstructure, Zane realized.

It would crack, but it wouldn't shatter unless it was all at once...

And he was pushing it near the brink.

The Mirror Dragon reeled back, so riddled with fissures now its destiny seemed quite clear. If he had to guess its Health was nearing the 20% threshold and dipping fast—

Malzareth screeched at its Prime, and the Mirror Dragon snarled back in defiance.

The six-eyed dragon seemed incredulous.

Then its eyes brightened, and the Mirror Dragon stilled; a shadow fell over it in the Astral Plane. Its own eyes went slack, its colors changing, like the Summons Zane had fought upstairs.

The essence in the Mirror Dragon swelled.

Walls of light came out of the fissures in its body.

Zane was already moving—pushing his body as hard as he'd pushed it this fight, flooding his body with Supreme essence—he knew he'd need absolutely everything his Supreme'd physique could muster for this.

At the same time he pushed the spatial aspect of his Slashes too; a level of Law he could only attain by flooding the Skill with his essence, and its Law, and his Destruction—

He threw a single, brilliant Axe, an Axe whose sheer light drenched the plane in white. It crossed space in a fraction of a second.

It landed at nearly the same instant the essence in the Mirror Dragon's body reached its peak.

An obscene force crushed right through that crystalline mountain body.

It shattered in glorious fashion.

A mountain collapsing, smoking sheer essence as it did—a self-destruct halted at its climax. 20% Health to 0% Health in an instant.

Malzareth's cry was barely audible over the shattering sounds.

The bar on Zane's Supreme collected what was left of the Mirror Dragon's soul. Its self-destruction prevented about half of it from falling into Zane's hands, but the rest he was pleased to claim.

Then the notification, a second later—

A Prime Endbringer has been slain.

Two down.

And without another word, he turned to the third.
