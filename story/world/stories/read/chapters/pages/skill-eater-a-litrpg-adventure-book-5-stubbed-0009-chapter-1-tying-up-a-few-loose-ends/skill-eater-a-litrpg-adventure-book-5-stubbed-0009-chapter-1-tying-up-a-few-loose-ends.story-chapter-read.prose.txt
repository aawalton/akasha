Edge brought Opulence whipping around in a flashing arc as he sprang from the canyon’s wall.

Bolstered by Quadra Slash, Warlord’s Mantle, and Dragon’s Greed, the mastercrafted glaive tore into the hulking monster, cleaving its head from its shoulders in a single strike. He grinned while admiring the insane synergy between the powers at his command and the amazing weapon in his hands.

A little further down the salt-crusted ravine, the rest of the crew was battling another nightmarish creature that had entered this world through the rift to the Ancients’ realm. The monsters had absorbed enough energy to use their skills, but they were disoriented and disorganized—still adjusting to the mazelike sub-biome his team had become intimately familiar with over the last two days, terrain that was strikingly different from the doomed planet they had left behind.

The hunters called out when they needed help and came together when they encountered a powerful stage-three foe. Culling these creatures was a difficult challenge, but it was nothing compared to the ordeal they had endured before closing the portal, experiencing explosive growth and earning a mind-boggling amount of Mortium along the way.

“We’re taking a ten-minute break,” Trapper called out when the vile being was bleeding on the ground, spasming and then going still. “Sakura, see if you can scry our next target with Remote Viewing. Jumo, wall-off the passage behind us with Manifest Mineral, then stand watch until we’re ready to move on.”

“Got it, boss,” the tattooed warrior replied while sealing the entrance to another series of ravines, preventing their prey from escaping when they resumed their lengthy hunt.

By Edge’s rough estimate, his team had killed two-thirds of the monsters while fighting to reach the rift and enduring the subsequent siege while he traveled to another dimension to destroy the anchor bridging the planes.

That left thirty or so to deal with, and the hunters had defeated ten before the creatures regained enough mana to defend themselves. With the five they had killed over the last few hours, there should be another fifteen left to take care of. The crew intended to continue their hunt for the rest of the day and set out for World’s End early the next morning. That would leave them with five days to finalize their preparations for meeting with the Coalition.

While they hadn’t met every goal for their upcoming negotiations, they were in far better shape than before. Sakura and Trapper had evolved to stage three, and the rest of the team had racked up an incredible number of cycles over the last thirty-six hours, along with a massive windfall of Mortium.

Edge had hit the peak of stage two and could evolve in two weeks, once the Epic skill digesting in his inner world hatched. That meant their best bet was to try to buy more time during the initial meeting with King, then return after Edge advanced so he could bargain from a position of strength. It would also give his friends a chance to upgrade their implants from tier one to tier three. He already had the requisite resources sitting in his vault, and they had earned all the lifecoins they needed for the foreseeable future.

In the meantime, they would figure out what to do about the revelations he had received regarding the Ancients and their plans to invade Ord, but that wasn’t a problem Trapper’s crew could handle on their own. They needed to determine who they could trust, send word back to their people in Puppet Town, and forge alliances with powerful factions to deal with the emerging crisis.

On the bright side, one pressing dilemma had been resolved for the time being. The dark passenger had been defeated—bound by the chains of oblivion after losing the battle to take over Edge’s body and subsume his soul.

That train of thought made the memory of what had happened after he returned to Ord enter the forefront of his mind, when he had engaged in a conversation with Skill-Eater unlike any that had come before.

Four Hours Earlier

You have cleared the first of three requirements to evolve to stage 3.

You have cleared the second of three requirements to evolve to stage 3.

You have completed the quest…

Before he could read the rest of the System’s message, he was drawn into his inner world. Rue jumped into his arms and licked his face. Edge chuckled while holding his Companion tight, deeply relieved that the fox was all right, along with the rest of his friends. When he thought to wonder why he had been pulled in here to begin with, he realized something strange was going on.

Over in his skill village, his avatars were going nuts. Everyone was chanting and dancing—more excited than he’d ever seen them before. Rue began barking his head off, latching onto the undercurrent of tension thrumming through his core like a river of lightning.

Thunder erupted along a storm-racked shoreline as whatever was happening rose to a crescendo. Along the far wall of his central chamber, the black door swung open. Before Edge could process what was happening, a familiar figure with piercing silver eyes crossed the threshold and removed her robe.

“Greetings, my scion. My apologies for entering your inner sanctum uninvited. We have grave matters to discuss, and time is of the essence.”

With that, the entity came to a stop in front of him, examining the contents of his core while he wrapped his head around the ravenous lord’s unexpected arrival, internalizing that the lord was awake and they could speak face-to-face with Skill-Eater’s memories intact.

All of that was remarkable, but what left him at a loss for words was what was revealed when the lord removed its outer garment. Not it. Edge corrected himself. She.

For the figure was undoubtedly a woman, although she wasn’t human, a spirit of the land, or one of the natives he’d seen in the Gardener’s vision. That last bit reminded him that the Gardener had referred to Skill-Eater as “she” during their brief conversation, although it hadn’t registered at the time.

Regardless of the details, this was his first true meeting with the being whose heart was beating in place of his own. She was waiting for him to speak, so he said the first thing that popped into his mind.

“Sorry about all those ‘big guy’ comments. I had no idea what you looked like under those chains.”

The regal woman turned to him and laughed. “Think nothing of it. The creature I became after being infested by the Ancient was a hideous monster, and I have worn many forms over the millennia. However, this was what I looked like when my friends came together to begin our grand adventure, and it has always been my favorite.”

The way her words resounded across every fiber of Edge’s being left him with no doubts as to the woman’s identity, although he was struggling to reconcile her appearance with the chain-bound giant he had conversed with before. As she spoke, he took in Skill-Eater’s true form, or one of them at any rate.

While she was striking, it was an alien beauty that didn’t stir his blood like staring at Sasha or Mel. Her limbs were longer and thinner than a human’s, and her silver eyes glowed from within, giving Skill-Eater a penetrating gaze. Her skin didn’t seem to obey the rules of color as he knew them. It was the purest black, pristine white, and everything in between, all at the same time.

She had short raven hair, delicately pointed ears, and ivory nails that tapered to form the suggestion of claws. Her features were angular, and sharp canine teeth protruded from her ruby lips. The fallen Lord of Ord was wearing a simple tunic that would have fit right in at any dojo on Ord.

The fact that he could catalogue Skill-Eater’s features so clearly made him realize that his memory of lords he’d seen in two separate visions was fuzzy. He could remember what their presences felt like and the tone of Death-Walking’s voice, but their appearances were blurry, though he was certain that hadn’t been the case at the time. The manipulation of his memory was so subtle that he hadn’t questioned it until this moment, but it was obvious now that he’d noticed the discrepancy.

“I’m glad that you’ve regained your missing memories, along with a portion of your sealed power. After reliving part of your story, I know how terrible it must have been to be bound by the chains for centuries, trapped inside a monstrous body while the passenger tried to devour your soul.”

“Thank you. You are the only one who understands what have endured, along with my brother, Boundless-Well. While there is a great deal we need to discuss, I fear today’s conversation must be brief. Until you evolve, I can only manifest within your central chamber for short periods.

“I will answer any questions you have, but first, we have two matters of vital importance to discuss. A portion of my past and missing powers have been restored, but there are pieces missing still. Another relic is out there somewhere. You must unseal it before we bring the fight to the Ancients that have invaded this world after destroying another. I have no idea where it might be found, but I will let you know if I sense anything of note.

“The second issue is the identity of the Ancient that appeared as you leapt through the rift. Though his form was warped by the monster’s touch, that was Realm-Traveler, my senior brother. His disappearance marked the end of Ord’s golden age and the downfall of the lords. I had my suspicions, but this confirms that he journeyed to Stithis with Boundless-Well, where they were possessed by the energetic parasites known as Ancients.

“He is the source of the dimensional collision that brought the monsters to Ord, and the Ancients are using his magic to fuel their invasion of this plane. During our battle with the dark passenger, our minds touched and revealed their plans.

“The most powerful of their kind are trapped on that dying world, unable to enter any of the rifts they have created thus far. However, dozens of lesser Ancients have already passed through, paving the way for their dread rulers. If we are to save this world, they must be stopped, and it will only end with the destruction of Realm-Traveler’s body and the Prime Ancient that has consumed by brother’s soul.”

“That explains a great deal,” Edge replied. “Although I have no idea what we can do about it other than search for your relic, grow stronger as fast as possible, and seek alliances with the planet’s major powers. I would love to learn more of the details when you’re up for a longer visit. I look forward to getting to know you better after I evolve.

“Before you go, there was something I was hoping to get your advice on. I have an Epic skill and a Rare domain digesting right now, but I don’t have any free skill slots to bind them. I would hate to give up any of my existing powers, and I was wondering if there were any good candidates for my Triple-Merger Token so I can solve the problem that way.”

“I see. Unfortunately, I don’t sense any compatible matches with your present powers. However, I can offer an alternative solution. My power is still greatly diminished, but this realm is a manifestation of my essence and your will. A minor modification is within my present capabilities.”

With that, Skill-Eater gestured and magic flooded his core. Beside the pillars representing Edge’s skill slots, two columns rose from the floor, glinting with the circuitry that linked the receptacles of his powers to the mana flowing from his reactor.

“These are two of the skill slots you would have obtained upon evolving, hastened by my decree. Now you can bind the powers you spoke of without having to sacrifice anything to obtain them.”

“Thank you. Get some rest, big g… err, friend. We can chat again when you’re back on your feet.”

“Indeed. Be careful, my scion. Dangerous times have descended upon this world, and this is only the beginning. Watch your back and grow stronger still, and I will lend my aid as I can.”

With that, Skill-Eater approached the black door. She picked her voluminous robe off the floor, swung it over one shoulder, and stepped past the threshold while raising a hand in farewell.
