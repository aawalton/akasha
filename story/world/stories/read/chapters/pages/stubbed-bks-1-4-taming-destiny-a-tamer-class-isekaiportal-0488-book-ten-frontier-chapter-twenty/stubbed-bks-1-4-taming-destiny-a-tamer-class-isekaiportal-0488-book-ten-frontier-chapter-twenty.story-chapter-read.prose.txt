“Who do you think it could be?” Valence wonders as we hurry through the settlement together. Since the ship has only just entered the bay, we have a little time. “Someone from another expedition?”

“Who else would it be?” Elarion snaps at him irritably.

Layton catches the grimace that appears on my face in response to that comment.

“Markus?” he asks sharply.

That’s the cue for the other lords to look at me – Loran and Sarran too. Elarion’s eyes narrow.

“What do you know that we don’t?” he demands, accurately reading my expression. It’s sometimes a pain that Elarion and Layton in particular are highly observant.

I hesitate, glancing around me quickly. The streets aren’t exactly crowded, but there are still plenty of people out on them, celebrating the end of a day. I’d rather they don’t overhear, but Seal works best when I’m stationary.

“Let’s get out of the settlement first,” I tell them. Elarion and Josiah look disgruntled at the information being delayed; Layton and Valence are more accepting. I feel Sarran’s gaze heavy on me for a moment before he turns it back onto the route ahead.

I reach out to Nessie. An unfamiliar ship entering the bay is exactly one of the scenarios I thought she would be useful in.

Are you in the bay?

…Yes, she replies, slightly reluctantly.

Please come close to the docks. I might need you to present a show of force.

Very well, she agrees with the feeling of a sigh. I suppose you have upheld your side of the bargain so far.

As have you, I agree – the crew on the ships, while still a bit wary of the large sea serpent, are now appreciating how much easier it is for them to find fish every day. How are your hatchlings?

Tension creeps across the Bond.

A little better, she tells me defiantly.

Are you sure–

No, she tells me finally. They are healing. And when their father comes back, he will help supply them with food that will heal them faster. There is no need for their spirits to be entangled.

My eyebrows twitch upwards. This is the first time I’ve heard of a father.

Alright, I tell her after a moment. Your choice. Just please make sure their father does not pose any threat to my people. Otherwise we’ll have to treat him like one.

…Understood, Nessie replies stiffly, then pulls away from the Bond in a clear signal that she wants to end the conversation.

I find myself lingering on her words a little longer. A father, hm? Another mate coming out of the woodwork. Hopefully less explosively than Tilathis did.

Thanks to all of our supernatural constitutions, we make it to the gates far faster than the average person would. I see Sarran glancing at Loran several times, his expression wondering. I’m not surprised – he’s the only non-Classer of the group, yet he’s managing to keep pace with us without even looking like he’s struggling. Clearly the Evolution has enhanced his whole body even if he focused on his upper body.

That’s another revelation I need to share with the other lords. Truth be told, I’m not sure how they will react. After all, Classes have been the privilege of the rich and the noble, and commoners gaining access to power without needing to align themselves in some way with a noble House is a potential threat to the whole feudal structure.

However, I will have to share it sooner rather than later – but perhaps only once I have proof that Loran isn’t just an outlier. Because I’ll definitely be looking for volunteers to see if we can standardise the method. After all, a whole settlement of Evolved or Classers is far more likely to survive than a settlement of unEvolved and a few Classers. Assuming that this ship isn’t the Blood Fang elves already, that is.

At the gates, we collect a few units – known as shieldwalls here – and take them with us out of the settlement. In the fading light of the setting sun as we hurry down the increasingly well-worn path down to the docks, I spot what the messenger informed us of – a large ship with white, triangular sails most of the way towards our harbour.

It’s flying a flag that’s solid yellow with three round circles in black, grey, and brown lying almost on top of each other in the centre.

“Does anyone recognise that flag?” I ask tensely. I wouldn’t have thought a ‘blood fang’ clan would have a device like that, but Kalanthia indicated there are many different guilds and clans on the Lost Continent. Certainly, I don’t recognise it as one of the noble Houses from Moriax.

“I’m almost certain it’s the merchants’ guild,” Valence suggests thoughtfully.

“Of course!” Layton replies, with an air of realisation. “That’s why it’s familiar. I was trying to work out which noble House it was but…”

“It isn’t one,” Valence finishes. “Then is it part of Lady Goldmine’s faction? They’re known to sponsor merchants.”

“Wouldn’t they be sporting Goldmine’s device as well, then?” Layton points out. “Certainly when we faced Heir Torrent’s fleet, his allies flew his flag as well as their own.”

“Perhaps they’re here independently,” I point out. “Many hope that the Lost Continent will be a goldmine for them. Wouldn’t merchants want to get in at the ground floor?” From my understanding, they tend to be the venture capitalists of this world, always looking out for the next source of income.

Out of the corner of my eye, I see Josiah frown.

“I thought that the competition was restricted to noble Houses?”

“The competition is,” Elarion agrees crisply, looking at the flag with something in his eyes that looks surprisingly close to hatred. “However there is nothing saying that other parties cannot join either as part of a noble House’s retinue – or as an independent party.” His face twists. “It makes sense that those leeches would be here – no doubt selling life-saving supplies at a criminal price.”

“Merchants have to make a living,” argues Valence. “If they take the risk of coming here, it makes sense they would want to make a good profit to justify it.”

Elarion’s lips twist in a faint sneer.

“It makes sense you would be sympathetic to them. Wasn’t your grandmother the daughter of a merchant? A noble title in exchange for saving a House from poverty?”

“Alright, enough,” I say firmly as red rises in Valence’s cheeks. “Elarion, I don’t know what your issue is with merchants, but bringing family into the discussion is out of line.”

The other Great House heir almost glares at me, his normal stoic appearance oddly absent. I make a mental note to find out what the history is here – because with as spiteful as he’s acting, there must be some. And I don’t think it’s between him and Valence – the younger lord is just his chosen target for some reason.

After a moment, however, Elarion looks away.

“I shouldn’t have spoken of your grandmother,” he admits stiffly, looking off to the side as if the cliff in that direction is fascinating.

“Apology accepted,” Valence replies mostly levelly. His fists are still clenched, but the red is reducing from his cheeks. I feel oddly proud of him in that moment. I know he holds his honour highly – after all, his original complaint against me arose from feeling that, by existing as a commoner promoted to Great House heir undeservedly, I besmirched his honour. That he has managed to stop himself retaliating against Elarion despite the poor apology is an achievement.

I turn my head to face Josiah – he’s flicking his eyes from Elarion to Valence, his expression wary.

“Josiah, please signal to the ships that the approaching vessel should not be allowed to enter the docks, but, assuming no movement towards hostility on their part, we will respond in kind.” The wariness clears from Josiah’s face, replaced with agreement.

“Certainly.” Pulling out a communication mirror, he passes on my message to the skeleton crew left on the ships. There aren’t enough people present on the ships to put up a good fight, but they will at least be able to delay any attacker from entering the docks long enough that we can arrive and act. Between the other lords, Nessie, and me, we can fend off a single ship easily enough.

“Markus?” Layton’s voice pulls me out of my contemplations. I glance over at him. “You said that you didn’t want to tell us something while we were still within the settlement.” His expectant tone heavily implies the question that he doesn’t voice. The other lords and Sarran are also looking at me with either curiosity or expectation. Loran, on the other hand, is eyeing the vessel that’s close to the harbour opening with calculation in his expression.

“I did,” I admit. We’ve arrived at the harbour so I really don’t have any excuse not to speak. Sighing, I pull up a Seal. Our escort is probably out of earshot, but I won’t risk this being spread around before I’m ready for it to be. “Very well. I will state it bluntly: the Lost Continent isn’t nearly as lost as those on Moriax believe it to be.”

“You mean…?” Josiah asks with a frown between his brows.

“Yes,” I confirm, holding his gaze. “There are natives here – have been since long before the refugees settled on Iseria.”

“That’s impossible,” Elarion snaps. “The histories indicate that the humans left in a mass-migration event.”

“And many humans did leave,” I agree. “But not all of them. And humans aren’t the only ones who were here.”

Layton joins his cousin in frowning at me.

“Are you implying there are damayar here?”

“No,” I correct. “Elves.”

Elarion lets out a bark of laughter.

“Elves,” he scoffs. “Fairy tales!”

“Markus, where are you getting your information?” Valence asks, more politely, but I see the incredulity in his eyes.

“Kalanthia,” I tell them. “She’s a native of this land.”

“I thought she came with you from your previous world?” Valence questions hesitantly.

“It’s a long story,” I dismiss, “and not mine to tell. But the fact is that she’s come into contact with the local residents on this continent and can confirm that the other side of this vast mountain range is ruled by a mixture of human guilds and elven clans.”

The other lords don’t look entirely convinced.

I sigh, exasperated.

“Look, you saw what state she was in when I teleported her to my side, yes?” Several nods. “You saw the muzzle around her head? The ropes around her ankles?” More nods – and more thoughtful ones this time. “Well, where do you think she got those? Unless you think she somehow teleported back to Moriax.”

“From what I’ve heard from Master Mirena, the enchantments on that muzzle weren’t exactly standard ones,” Sarran murmurs, joining the conversation. “She has been requesting materials to run her own experiments with blood-bound equipment,” he clarifies in response to my questioning look.

“Then…that’s why you relaxed when we identified the vessel as one from Moriax,” Layton murmurs perceptively. “You thought it might be one of the natives.”

“Yes,” I agree. “Contact with them seems inevitable, especially as there’s a clan of elves who will take exception to me stealing Kalanthia out from under their noses,” I decide not to mention the artefact for now, “but the longer we can develop before then, the better.”

Valence turns troubled eyes out to the sea.

“Allies would help too,” he murmurs.

“And trade of ideas and materials,” Josiah agrees.

“Exactly.” I agree. Then, looking at the bay, “The ship has dropped anchor without showing any sign of hostility. Let’s go aboard and find out exactly who’s visiting us.”
