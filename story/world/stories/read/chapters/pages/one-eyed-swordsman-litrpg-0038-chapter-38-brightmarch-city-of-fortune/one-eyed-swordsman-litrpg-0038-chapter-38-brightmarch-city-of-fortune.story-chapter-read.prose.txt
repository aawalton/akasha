Dust stirred around the wagon’s wheels as it rolled to a gradual stop just outside the gates of Brightmarch. Pebbles scattered beneath the hooves of the horses, and a faint breeze teased at the edges of Oswald’s cloak. One by one, he and Selene stepped down from the wagon.

Oswald’s hand briefly lingered on the wooden rail before he let go. The morning sun caught the buckles of his boots and the faint scuff marks that told a story of long roads behind them. Vivi, who was perched on his shoulder, flicked her tail once as she surveyed the towering walls ahead.

The gates of Brightmarch loomed beyond, their iron portcullis raised high. Ivy clung to parts of the outer stone, threading between cracks. Beyond it was the promise of crowded streets and winding alleys.

Selene’s gaze swept over the guards posted at the entrance, each clad in chainmail. One of the guards, a man with a sun-darkened face and a thick beard, straightened as she approached. “Lady Whiteheart. Didn’t expect to see you this far west again. Business in Brightmarch?”

“I am here to see the duke,” she replied.

The guard offered a brief nod, already stepping aside. “He’ll make time for you, I’ve no doubt.”

Another guard, younger, glanced past Selene at the figure beside her. “And him?” he asked, jerking his chin toward Oswald.

“He is my assistant.” Selene answered.

Assistant, Oswald echoed in his head, almost amused.

He said nothing, only adjusted the strap of his bag and let his eye drift across the battlements. One step behind, just like they’d agreed. The guard nodded, not pressing further. As they passed through the gate, the younger one tilted his head toward Vivi. A small smile tugged at the corner of his mouth.

“Cute cat,” he murmured.

Vivi met his gaze with quiet disdain. Yeesh, I need to teach her how to take a compliment.

Brightmarch rose before him like something out of a painting. Stone towers crowned the skyline, while countless rooftops stretched out in dense, overlapping layers of red and gray tile. Ships bobbed in the harbor below, rows upon rows of masts stabbing into the sky. Sailors shouted, hoisting crates, barrels, and ropes while gulls wheeled overhead, shrieking in protest.

Steam hissed from iron grates in the street, and the clang of a forge echoed from somewhere nearby. Various people bustled past, cloaked merchants with coin-heavy satchels, children darting between carts, and guards in armor pushing through the crowd.

Oswald let out a low whistle. Damn, this place looks like it's doing just fine to me.

He turned toward Selene, who had stepped down beside him without a sound.“This place doesn’t look like it’s in trouble."

Selene didn’t answer right away. She stood perfectly as her gaze swept across the crowds. “First impressions can blind you to the truth.”

Oswald opened his mouth, a joke already forming, something about her being dramatic again, but the words caught in his throat. Off to the side, half-hidden in the shade of a narrow alley, a man slumped against the stone wall.

His head lolled forward, chin buried in a scruff of tangled beard. One hand hung limp beside him, the other clutching the neck of an empty glass bottle. His clothes had once been fine, but now hung off him in stained, rumpled folds. A single boot rested beside him, like he’d taken it off and forgotten why.

Oswald’s smile faded. “My bad Selene, you’re right.”

She snorted. A quiet, almost elegant sound. “I usually am.”

With a tilt of her chin, she stepped forward and strode into the city proper. Oswald followed, adjusting the strap of his pack and tugging his cloak into place. Vivi perched calmly on his shoulder, tail swaying like a lazy metronome.

The stone roads widened as they walked, giving way to broader avenues lined with tall buildings wrapped in iron balconies and hanging planters. The closer they got to the city’s heart, the cleaner everything looked. Dirt faded from the stones. The people grew quieter, more refined in their dress. The noise of the docks fell away behind them like a tide rolling back.

Eventually, they reached a wrought iron gate taller than any Oswald had ever seen. The estate beyond it sprawled across the hillside like it had been carved out of the land itself.

Beyond the gate, a massive courtyard opened up, cobbled in elegant patterns, flanked by trimmed hedges shaped into various birds. At the far end, a mansion loomed, built of pale stone with towering windows and sharp, angular roofs. The entrance bore a set of double doors, each one engraved with an intricate golden emblem.

Selene stopped just short of the gate and folded her hands neatly in front of her. Oswald stared at her, then at the estate. Then back at her. "...So we’re just gonna stand here?”

“Yes,” she replied.

“Ain’t we, like... supposed to knock or somethin’? Or ask for whoever’s in charge?”

“They will come.”

“Yeah, but how do they even know we’re here?”

“They will know.”

She didn’t explain further. Just kept her eyes on the mansion and waited.

Oswald sighed and glanced around. The street was eerily quiet now. No hawkers, no crowds, just the echo of his own breathing.

This whole place is too clean for me. Vivi gave a soft meow from his shoulder, almost agreeing with him.

Suddenly, there was the sound of the faint groan of old hinges. A thin sliver of light slipped out from the crack between the massive double doors.

Then the entryway unfurled with slow, deliberate grace, the doors swinging open as if the manor itself were waking from slumber. A line of servants stepped out in perfect formation. Their black-and-white livery clung crisp and precise to their frames.

From their ranks, one man stepped forward. He had a tall figure, lean and upright, with iron-gray hair combed back to reveal a lined but unyielding face.

He came to a halt before them and offered a bow. "Lady Whiteheart, we have been expecting you.”

Selene inclined her head. “We appreciate the Duke’s hospitality.”

The man’s gaze shifted to Oswald. But one brow arched, just enough to suggest curiosity, or perhaps judgment. “And... who is this young man?”

“He is my ally.” Selene said.

The older servant nodded once, then bowed again, shallower this time. “Then he is welcome within these walls. However, the Duke isn’t fond of animals. We can see to the care of your ally's animal companion in the meantime.”

Oswald blinked mid-step, his boot pausing just above the flagstone. Of course they’ve got rules.

He exhaled through his nose, more a sigh than a breath, and reached up. Vivi gave a soft, almost offended grunt as he untangled her from his neck. Her claws didn’t come out, but her ears twitched at the unfamiliar arms awaiting her.

“She might not be too keen with strangers,” Oswald muttered, handing her over. “So, uh... maybe don’t stare her down or try to pet her belly.”

The servant took the cat with both hands like he’d been handed a lit fuse, nodding. “Understood, sir. She will be treated with the utmost care.”

Oswald watched her go, her small head still poking from the crook of the servant’s arm, eyes fixed on him like she was silently judging his betrayal. He scratched the back of his neck, then sighed again and turned to catch up with Selene.

They stepped forward in unison as the great wrought-iron gates slowly pulled shut behind them. Soon, the estate’s interior courtyard unfolded ahead of them.

Stone paths stretched forward in flawless symmetry. The cobblestones underfoot had been polished to a faint sheen, catching glints of sunlight like flecks of glass. Not even a leaf was out of place.

Oswald walked just behind Selene, trying not to look too out of place, but his eyes couldn’t stop wandering. Wide windows glinted under the afternoon sun, reflecting back rows of tall columns and finely carved statues tucked into alcoves. Marble lions crouched at the corners of the courtyard, while fancy looking flower beds lined the sides of the path.

The duke really went all in with this place, he thought, glancing at a pair of gardeners in matching vests gently dusting the leaves of a bush.

Inside the mansion, the air shifted—cooler, scented faintly with lavender and old paper. The floors beneath his boots were polished wood inlaid with long gold lines that snaked into intricate patterns. He caught glimpses of chandeliers hanging high above, their crystals gleaming like trapped stars. Everything—every hallway, every corner—looked like it had been built for someone important.

Oswald’s gaze flicked toward a passing servant who bowed slightly as they walked past. Man, imagine having a place like this. A dozen rooms just to lose your socks in. People opening doors for you, bringing you tea... no chores, no cold floors, no rotten floorboards.

He stuffed his hands in his pockets, watching another pair of servants open a massive set of double doors ahead. Yeah. I could get used to that.

Their escort, still that same sharp-eyed servant from before, led them into a large room. The ceiling was vaulted high overhead. Plush chairs and low tables sat arranged across the rug, which was thick enough that Oswald’s boots sank into it with every step.

A fireplace sat dormant at the far end, its mantle lined with delicate glass ornaments. Everything smelled faintly of lemon polish and something floral he couldn’t name.

“Please make yourselves comfortable. The Duke will join you shortly.” The attendant said.

Selene offered a polite nod and took a seat in the high-backed chair closest to the window. Oswald hesitated, then picked the one beside her.

Another servant approached with a silver tray held steady in gloved hands. “May I offer refreshments? There is hot tea available, with a selection of seasonal tarts and biscuits. Do you have a preference?”

Oswald blinked. “Uh... I’ll just get whatever she’s having.”

Selene, without even glancing up, answered, “White tea with honey. And one of the lemon tarts, if you please.”

The servant bowed and swept away. Oswald finally leaned back slightly, letting out a breath. “So... we’re really just sittin’ here till the Duke shows up?”

“Yes, try not to fidget.”

“I ain't fidgeting.”

“You were.”

He shot her a look, but she didn’t bother returning it. The tea arrived within moments, delivered on porcelain dishes with tiny gold rims.

A curl of steam drifted from the cups, carrying a clean, herbal scent. Oswald accepted his with both hands, unsure whether he was supposed to sip it right away or wait for some signal.

He glanced at the tart placed before him, perfectly round, topped with a thin sliver of candied lemon. Fancy food for fancy people, can't be that nice.

He took a cautious bite. ...Alright, that’s actually good.

He didn’t speak after that. The quiet held between them, stretched by sunlight and the faint clink of porcelain. Until finally, somewhere deep inside the estate, footsteps moved, drawing nearer.

The click of a latch broke the stillness. Oswald’s fingers paused halfway to the teacup, eyes drifting toward the doors at the far end of the room.

When the doors opened, first came the boots, black and polished so clean they caught the reflection of the floor.The hem of a deep green coat swept forward, brushing just above the marble as the man stepped fully into view.

He was easily the tallest man in the room, with shoulders broad enough to blot out the light behind him. The uniform he wore clung to a muscular frame. No excess flourishes marked the fabric; the coat and trousers were crisp, trimmed with silver, and strapped with a ceremonial belt that bore a single ornate badge: a crowned lion wrapped in thorns.

His beard was neatly trimmed, thick and dark, matching the hair slicked back over his scalp. Just above his right temple, a streak of grey cut through the black like a scar. His skin bore the faint bronze tint of a man long used to the sun. And his striking green eyes held a commanding pressure.

That’s the Duke? He don’t look like any noble I’ve ever seen, maybe more like a military guy.

Duke Blackridge Blackridge scanned the room once, absorbing it all with a glance that carried the weight of a battlefield survey. But the moment his eyes found Selene, his expression shifted.

The hardness softened. His back straightened further, not in pride, but out of respect. “Lady Whiteheart, you honour my house once again.”

Selene rose from her seat with fluid grace and offered a smile. “Duke Blackridge. It’s good to see you, Edward.”

He crossed the room and extended his arm. Not for a handshake, but the forearm-to-forearm grasp of warriors. Selene met it without hesitation.

Then his gaze shifted. Oswald froze. The Duke's eyes landed on him, all warmth swept away in an instant. “And who is this?”

Oswald shifted under the Duke’s stare, one hand drifting to scratch the back of his neck. “Uh… name’s Oswald, I’m travelin’ with Selene.”

A single eyebrow rose on the Duke’s face. The silence that followed pressed in like fog.

Selene stepped in before it grew too thick. “He is also my disciple."

The Duke turned his head slightly toward her, expression unreadable. Then, after a moment, he looked Oswald up and down again, as if re-evaluating a broken sword someone claimed was still sharp. “With all due respect, he looks better suited for a brawl in the slums than standing at your side.”

Oswald snorted before he could stop himself, a grin tugging at the corner of his mouth. “He’s not wrong.”

The Duke’s gaze snapped to him. “What, precisely, do you find amusing?”

Oswald blinked. Ah, shit. Opened my big mouth again.

“Didn’t mean nothin’ by it, sir. Just... figured you’re not the first one to think that.”

Before Edward could say more, Selene raised a hand slightly. “Oswald has never spoken to an aristocrat before, Edward. You’ll have to excuse the roughness.”

The Duke exhaled. “Then let me make it simple.”

He took a step closer towards OSwald. .“I can forgive poor manners, but only if you can prove yourself.”

“Prove myself?” Oswald asked.

“You’ll accompany me to the training grounds. Now.”

“Right now?”

“Yes.”

His tone left no room for argument.

Great, Oswald thought, exhaling through his nose as he rose from the plush chair. Barely finished my tea and now I’m gettin’ dragged into a fight with a guy who looks like he's built like a mountain.

He cast a glance toward Selene, hoping for some kind of reprieve. But she only offered him the faintest of nods. Of course, she probably does want to see me fight him.
