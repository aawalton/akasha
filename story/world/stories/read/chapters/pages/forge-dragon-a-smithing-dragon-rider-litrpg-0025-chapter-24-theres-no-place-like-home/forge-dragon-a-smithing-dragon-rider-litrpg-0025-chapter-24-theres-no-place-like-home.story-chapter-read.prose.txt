Caleb loved his private mill. So much so that when we returned to it looking for Kelia and didn’t find her, he quickly became lost in setting up a new project.

Behind his water-mill-turned-forge, he had a small bloomery he’d built. In an attempt to improve the design, he’d tried to induce airflow mechanically, connecting a bellows to his rotating shaft, but he’d not gotten far in the project.

Now, he didn’t need any mechanism to induce airflow.

He pulled the heavy clay lid off the top of the bloomery, and using his Imperium he heaved the old hardened mass of ore and slag and coal out from within. Bog came out to watch and, when he saw what Caleb was doing, joined in. Caleb was cleaning out the bloomery so he could smelt the blue metal. Bog found the remnants within delicious, and helped by licking the walls clean with his coarse tongue.

Together, the task took less than an hour, and then Caleb began the process of filling the bloomery back up with coal, placing his bits of ore in the middle.

“Have you digested this stuff yet?” Caleb asked Bog, pointing to the ore he’d been eyeing greedily.

Bog shook his head, sending frustration through the bond.

The dragon began working something up in his throat, and then opened his mouth, revealing three small chunks of ore, cleared of any caked-on rocks, but not yet refined into metal.

“Want to let me try smelting it?” Caleb asked.

Bog snapped his tongue back into his mouth hungrily, and took two steps back for good measure.

“Fine, fine” Caleb said. “This is enough to see if it worked. Maybe if this works, you can sample it and figure out the trick of it.”

Bog liked the sound of that, and watched eagerly as Caleb set the bloomery ablaze.

“Can you keep this up for me?” Caleb showed the dragon how he was inducing airflow.

Bog, eager to taste the refined metal, agreed and took over for him. The dragon curled up around the bloomery, enjoying the heat of it, and appeared to be napping as he kept up the constant draft.

Caleb stretched and went to clean up in the river before moving on to his next project.

During his constant interrogations in town, he’d told everyone he’d invested his stats into strength and perception. That lie would be easy to perpetuate should he always have gear on hand, but he wouldn’t be able to walk around with his spear, helmet, and hammer at all times. In fact, he was planning on returning the hammer to his master soon, as he suspected the steel-aspected hammer he took from the mountain forge would serve his purposes much better.

To start, Caleb took a billet of cold-aspected steel and began heating it in his forge. While both this steel and the white flecks were cold-aspected, the amount of magic in whatever metal the flecks were was significantly greater. The cold steel Caleb was used to only took a little bit more heat than other steels to get to a forging temperature, but if heated too much, it was more prone to degradation.

Once it was heated, he cut the billet into little pieces, starting from the size of his index finger and moving up to fist-sized.

One by one, he worked, starting with the finger-sized piece, hammering and drawing it into a wire, using all he’d learned the past week to do so faster than even his master could perform. All the while, he worked to push out the chaotic mana and replace it with his own, copying the patterns he’d learned to enchant metal for strength. But, no matter how hard he worked at it, he couldn’t fit his enchantment into the bit of steel.

So he started over, with the next size up. He forged this into a block, three centimeters by eight, and one thick. This time he was able to fit an enchantment of +2 strength within, just barely running out of room. He knew if he improved his acuity and his mana manipulation, he’d be able to fit these same enchantments into smaller shapes, but he also sensed he’d hit a bit of a wall in advancing the skill.

He repeated this again, and on the third one managed to get a +3 strength bonus in. At first he’d thought he’d improved in his skill, but then on further review he remembered this third chunk was slightly larger than the previous ones.

When he was done, he laid out all three bars of steel on his anvil and took in the sight, willing to connect to them on his system.

Cold Steel Ingot of Strength (+2)

Cold Steel Ingot of Strength (+2)

Cold Steel Ingot of Strength (+3)

“This… wasn’t that hard,” he said to Bog.

His whole life he’d thought forging enchanted gear would require rare ingredients or secret methods. All it had taken Caleb was the ability to see what was actually occurring. Sure, he couldn’t figure out how to make upgrades higher than 3 strength or 2 constitution, but he’d only been at this for less than a week. Who knew where he’d be next year.

“Why is this all so rare?” Caleb asked Bog, but he knew the answer.

Regardless of whether or not it was easy to forge enchanted gear, the empire took anyone who could do it and used them to supply their armies… or something. Caleb wasn’t really sure where they went.

He felt an uncomfortable tugging as the items tried to pull more strength out of him, and he quickly relinquished the bond to his other gear.

“Perfect,” he said, stretching, only to realize then that he’d worked all night and the sun was rising.

“Elders,” Kelia’s voice swore from the entrance of the mill. “You are still working.”

Caleb turned to see her coming in with food in hand—uncooked food, but food nonetheless.

“Hug?” he asked, holding his arms out wide.

“Pass,” she said, walking up to the anvil. “What’s this?”

Caleb used his Imperium to lift the largest ingot to his hand, and handed it to Kelia.

“A present,” Caleb said.

She gave him a blank stare.

“I’m not your dragon. I don’t want a block of iron.”

“Just take it, and try to connect with it like it’s my hammer,” Caleb said.

Kelia took it. A moment later, her eyes grew wide.

“How?”

“I enchanted it,” Caleb said, as if it were obvious.

“You can just enchant a random piece of metal? It doesn’t have to be an item or anything?”

“It looks like you can, and I did. I’m going to make enough of this so we—me, you and Gris—can always be at max stats, even if we don’t have equipment on us. I need it so I can pretend I actually put points into strength.”

“Why do I need it, then?” she asked.

“Because I want you to beat my brother up the next time he flirts with you. I think if I can get his perception up to 20 with ingot,s he might finally take the hint that we are in a serious relationship.”

“‘Serious relationship?’” Kelia asked, eyebrow raised. “One involving a gift, maybe? A gift symbolizing commitment?”

Caleb sighed.

“I was making something like that, actually, the night of the ringfall,” Caleb said, sitting down on his anvil.

The joking air left Kelia’s voice, and she sat across from him.

“You know I was just joking, you don’t have to give me anything. I don’t need that silly tradition.”

“I want to give you something. Something good. My first gift… it was a bad idea.”

“What was it?” Kelia asked.

Caleb took a deep breath and then exhaled, stalling for time.

“A knife…”

Kelia laughed.

“Impietor take you, that’s terrible.”

“I know, I know, Gris told me as much,” Caleb admitted.

“Why did you need your idiot brother to tell you a knife was a bad engagement gift?”

“It’s traditional, I wanted it to be very traditional.”

“But why?”

“Because maybe then everyone would stop giving you such a hard time?” Caleb more asked than stated, causing Kelia to laugh again.

“I don’t care what these stupid village people think of me. If you want to make me something, make me something you want me to have. Not something for them.”

“Alright,” Caleb conceded. “I have an idea I think you’ll like. What’d you bring me for dinner?”

“Breakfast,” she corrected, pulling a dead rabbit and two game birds from her back. “Get to plucking.”

As they worked cleaning the food, Avis landed beside them, snatching up the scraps of entrails as they discarded them. Soon, Bog pushed the smaller hawk out of the way and took some for himself.

“Don’t be a bully,” Caleb scolded him. “You’re bigger than her. Share.”

Bog’s head ridges slumped in shame at the chastisement, and he backed up.

“He’s kind of cute when he’s in trouble,” Kelia admitted.

“Then I think you two will get along, he always seems to be getting into some sort of trouble.”

“So…” Kelia said after a long pause. “Do you want to talk about it?”

“Talk about what?” Caleb asked, knowing full well what “it” was.

Kelia didn’t dignify his evasion with a response, and he could feel her gaze boring into him.

“I got offered the class,” he said eventually. “Either I’d already crafted an enchanted item in the past—which I doubt—or it’s not actually a requirement.”

“Was it a hard choice?”

“Yes and no,” he admitted. “If I hadn’t been offered the Dragon Rider class, I don’t know what I would have done—probably would have been stupid and taken Blacksmith—but a rare class… How could I pass that up? And there was Bog. I didn’t know he was a dragon until that moment. That was a lot to take in. In our brief interaction, he’d trusted me enough to offer a bond. I couldn’t abandon him. He’s really just a baby, as big as he is.”

“I’m glad you came around,” Kelia said, grabbing his hand. “I wouldn’t want to see you taken like your mom, but…”

“You think they still might take me?” Caleb asked.

Kelia nodded.

“That’s what I’m afraid of too. I’m going to have to be very careful. Do you think the village could keep it a secret?”

Kelia laughed at the suggestion, and Caleb smiled. As much as he loved his home, they were all a bunch of busybodies and braggarts. It wouldn’t take two weeks before the next village over got wind of the new enchanted gear suddenly appearing in town.

“Do you think Grisim will be able to keep his mouth shut?” Kelia asked.

“Yes,” Caleb said confidently.

His brother was a lot of things—and being a busybody braggart was among them—but he wouldn’t betray Caleb like that. Especially after what had happened to their mother.
