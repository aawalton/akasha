Fay backed through the portal, trying not to rush despite wanting to get away from the Doorkeeper as quickly as possible. She was fairly certain she knew what she'd been dealing with, that she'd run into one of the rare Seer constructs that monitored comings and goings for secure locations.

She'd never told anyone that she'd come across references to them, it hadn't been on purpose after all, and it wasn't as though she'd ever be able to do anything with the knowledge. Except apparently she might.

Seeing the clear edges of the portal she turned, barely bracing herself in time as Anya took one last step and pulled her into a hug. Seb was a few feet further away, giving them the moment, or maybe simply giving her the chance to greet them rather than be pounced upon.

"I'd say she should know better," Anya finished for her. "But she probably didn't think about it."

She blinked, realizing she was all but vibrating as she tried to adjust her thoughts and plans. She hadn't come here expecting this, she hadn't come here planning to almost panic before she had a chance to even look around. She'd come to see her family, to spend time with them, to talk about everything they'd missed over the last six months.

"I'm fine," she said softly before repeating herself slightly more confidently. "I'm fine. I just thought I'd be having to try and explain that there were other students here who might be related to you without ever using the word Fae. And here you are talking about it as though it isn't anything special."

That was confirmation enough. She couldn't remember the details, only the name, but the odds of another Bladesinger of sufficient renown and authority to exist with that specific name were incredibly slim, especially among the fae.

"Can we sit and talk? Somewhere?"

"The dining hall should still have breakfast, so there'll probably be bacon," Anya said, gesturing to two buildings as she spoke. "Our room only has coffee and it isn't as good."

She smiled as Anya turned towards the dining hall without waiting for an answer. She'd had breakfast before she came, but that didn't matter, not today.

Seb pushed the plate with the last piece of bacon towards Fay. She still seemed a little rattled by their knowing about the fae, even though none of them had mentioned anything further about it.

It had almost felt like some of their earliest meetings over breakfast. They had everything to share, details, experiences, opinions, and Fay sat there listening, asking the occasional question when they hadn't clarified some detail.

Dueling and Depths lessons were easy to explain, so were their respective skirmisher and close quarters combat courses. They were mostly drills and practical explanations, details meant to help fill in years of applied learning with opinions from others who had gone down the same path.

His private lessons with Percivus were, by necessity, less discussed. He couldn't explain what was changing about his use of Bladesong because it was more introspective at the moment. He'd acted intuitively from the start, understood elements that gave them advantages, but never pushed further, never realized he could.

Anya's mentorship under Elienne, the close quarters teacher who also followed a Monk path, was just as vague.
