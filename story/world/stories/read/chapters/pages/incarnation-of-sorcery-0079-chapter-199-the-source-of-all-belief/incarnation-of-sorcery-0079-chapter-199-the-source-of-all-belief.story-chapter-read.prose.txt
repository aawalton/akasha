Ryland didn’t let go of the Oracle within the Incarnation of Faith’s form.

“You invited the Incarnation here?” he asked. “Or rather, since I doubt you have the ability to do so, I assume you manipulated circumstances until the Incarnation arrived.”

“Stop chitchatting, Ryland,” Xyke shouted from far below. “The Glyphs won’t hold an Incarnation for long.”

As if to prove that, the Incarnation’s blazing form turned searing bright. Ryland felt his barriers being compressed and placed under tremendous pressure just from being in the proximity of the Incarnation of Faith. He was cracking. Shattering. Breaking.

And yet, he refused to let go. Borrowed power from the Great Gods was not about to defeat him. Not when so much was on the line.

“I took advantage of the opportunities that came before me,” the Oracle said with perfect clarity. It was almost as if she had purposefully relinquished control over herself to the Incarnation just to make it look like she was possessed. When in truth, she had been capable of wresting control away all this time. “Can you blame me after my faith abandoned me?”

Ryland needed a moment to parse that statement. A moment where the Incarnation of Faith continued struggling and Xyke was now on the verge of cursing Ryland.

“The Great Gods ripped away their blessings,” Ryland deduced. “And so, you were essentially powerless. Unless and until an Incarnation possessed you.”

“Correct. I was looking for them. Waiting until the opportune moment presented itself.”

A brief image of Ophelia lying wounded on the ground flashed through Ryland’s mind. The grip of his skeletal right hand tightened on the soul he had caught. “Is that why you manipulated the Mage Knight to come and assist you? All so you could forge the exact conditions needed to summon the Incarnation, even if it cost your protector her life?”

“You should know better than anyone else the price we must pay to protect the whole cosmos. You destroyed Rhel itself just so Mavar Val Arona couldn’t gather all the Living Mana in all the Realms and force himself to become a Great God lording over everyone by existing outside the Overrealm.”

“Don’t tell me this whole ploy of yours is to save the universe too.” Ryland shook his head, implacable eyes boring into the Oracle’s blindfolded gaze. “I faced a man just like that. A man whose megalomania twisted his desire for a utopia into a mad grab for unfathomable power.”

Credit to the Oracle, she didn’t flinch. She didn’t look away. “I am not the Demiurge, Ryland. I am not Mavar Val Arona. I do not seek power for myself.”

“Really? One could be forgiven for thinking you’re lying since you’ve summoned an Incarnation to regain your power.”

“To regain my connection to the Great Gods!” Everything started shaking as the Oracle’s rising emotions made the Incarnation possessing her start to vibrate with ever greater frequency. The energy levels were starting to get untenable, even for the Glyphic traps that Xyke had set. “Don’t you understand what that means? Don’t you see I’m trying to stop the Great Gods?”

Ryland only needed a moment to reframe the sequence of events to see what she meant. It was true. Of course, the fact that she had decided to assist Ryland would immediately cut her off from the Great Gods’ power she pulled from, once her chosen side was known.

It would further make sense that she would then do whatever she could to regain that favour and that power. For what was an Oracle if not a receptacle for the divine?

What was an Oracle without access to her gods?

But Vertha was looking past that. If she could be believed, she was looking beyond her own immediate goals, for summoning the Incarnation of Faith did bring about another possibility—a way to stop the Great Gods from influencing others to attack Ryland, at least until his task was complete.

If Oracle Vertha could be believed.

“You’ve had a plan to perform this… severance all this time,” Ryland said, taking a minor leap of faith. “But how do you intend to achieve this?” Even as he asked it, the answer came to him in moments. “By using the Incarnation’s connection to the Great Gods?”

“Well, of course! But first—” The Oracle’s form shuddered, and she managed to tear free from Ryland. “You must subdue the Incarnation!”

With a shriek that rippled across the world, the Incarnation of Faith once more attacked.

“I told you,” Xyke said as she waved away the dust. The Incarnation of Faith had unceremoniously flung an unprepared Ryland to the ground. He wasn’t hurt thanks to his barriers, but the act was rather undignified, even if he was facing off against a fellow Incarnation. “I told you to finish dealing with the Incarnation before they broke free.”

“It’s alright,” Ryland said, letting his manasoul blaze. “We need both the Incarnation and the Oracle.”

“The Oracle?” Xyke frowned, before her eyes widened. “Wait a minute, Ryland. What are you attempt—”

Their opponent was at it again, launching an all-out, gods-fuelled assault.

Pillars of burning light crashed down. That draconic Great God was once again bursting free from the Incarnation’s back and assaulting Ryland and everyone around him. Then black lightning rained down among them, shooting clods of broken earth everywhere. Ryland’s poor Inscription wasn’t going to last long at this rate.

He fought again. Attacked again. When seemingly “conventional” spells started failing to keep the Incarnation in check, Ryland pulled out everything he had.

“[Concave Nadir],” he incanted. There was no more holding back against the Incarnation. Diplomacy was pointless. Not if the Oracle herself was targeting what Ryland believed she was. “[Eye of the Archfiend]. [Temporal Reflection]. [Endless Expanse].”

His set of spells finally put a stop to the Incarnation of Faith. He was tired of dealing with it on and on, and he wished to get started on the next step of the procedure.

The first spell caught everything the Incarnation was attempting to throw at him. Divine energy or not, all the magic from his fellow Incarnation was gathered and condensed into one, focused location.

Then he was flinging them all back with [Temporal Reflection] while adding onto the effect with [Eye of the Archfiend]. Pure destruction was headed the Incarnation’s way.

Finally, the last spell was one Ryland had figured out in the back of his mind after seeing how the Incarnation of Growth had manipulated time itself. Essentially, just as his allied Incarnation had done to the bits of the reforming divine avatars, Ryland was repeating the moment in time all around the Incarnation of Faith.

They were now trapped in a ceaseless parade of devastating explosions and a sea of energy drowning them completely. Ryland winced. He was still reluctant to actually harm the Incarnation or the Oracle they were possessing.

But the Oracle’s words about the state of Rhel had pinpointed something Ryland couldn’t deny. All of this was to free Acrypha from the Great Gods and the Flourinus Order. If severing a rogue Incarnation’s source of power was what needed to be done, then he was not about to be stopped.

The destruction turned the day into a blast zone. A second sun formed right where [Endless Expanse] was recreating the explosions from all that expelled energy over and over and over again. Only [Omniscience] ensured Ryland didn’t need to shade his eyes.

“Ryland!” Xyke shouted over the ever-rising noise of utter obliteration visited upon the Incarnation of Faith. “Even an Incarnation can’t survive—”

“We don’t need them to,” Ryland said. “We only need a sliver of their abilities to reach the Great Gods themselves.”

“What?” In a flash, a furious Xyke was now in front of Ryland. Viren, upon whom Ryland had already cast a bevy of protective spells to safeguard him against any stray bits of devastating magic, looked on with a fearful expression. “Are you willing to sacrifice an Incarnation for all this?”

Faith. That’s what the Incarnation’s true source of power was. Or rather, what it was supposed to be. It wasn’t necessarily a religion of the Great Gods that this Incarnation needed to draw power from.

“I know what needs to be done,” Ryland said. “I know what I’m doing.”

Xyke still appeared as though she was intent on continuing her protests, but Ryland focused on shutting off his spells. The blazing sunlight in the sky of Rhel disappeared. The Incarnation of Faith crashed to the ground and moved no longer.

Ryland ignored everyone else’s reactions and rifted in.

With [Everlife Spectre] still active, he stabbed his skeletal hand once again into the Incarnation. Into the Oracle entombed within their golden aura. She wasn’t harmed. It was the shell of the Incarnation around her that had suffered the entire brunt of damage, leaving the blazing figure sputtering out only some embers of burning mana.

Which was good because they could now proceed with the real operation.

“Oracle Vertha,” Ryland said as his skeletal arm came into contact with his actual target. “It is time.”

“Indeed.”

It still felt odd that she was perfectly fine despite the Incarnation form she was resting within now broken to a helpless state. But as the figure rose, it was the Oracle enforcing her control.

“The process is rather… difficult to control,” the Oracle said somewhat evasively. “And difficult to understand as well. Do not hesitate to act as you see fit for this will be an exercise as much of intuition as it is of skill.”

Ryland nodded. He wasn’t quite sure what the Oracle was planning, but using an Incarnation as a base—not to mention, another one as a partner of sorts—meant that she had to be aiming for something suitably far-reaching. Something as consequential as removing the influence of the Great Gods from the entirety of the cosmos. Or as far as it mattered, at least.

“Let us begin, then,” the Oracle said. “[Revocation].”

The moment the spell started, the Grand Frame finally decided to pop in.

[ Achievement Recognized: Eternal Saviour

You’ve saved allies against the wrath of gods and Incarnations, despite being merely Empyrean-ranked.

Rank up from Empyrean [87] to Transcendent [3].

Reward: Glyph of Ascension evolved to Glyph of Transcendence. ]

Unfortunately, Ryland couldn’t even begin to figure that Glyph out before the real work started.

The spell started taking effect almost immediately. The world of Rhel started fading away as Ryland’s consciousness was temporarily severed from his body, transcending through the barrier between not just Realms or even Planes, but something in between. Something that existed everywhere and nowhere.

“This is…” Ryland frowned. Or felt like frowning. He had no actual physical body with which to emote, but the mental part of the sensation was as strong as ever. “Strauslire.”

“Indeed,” the Oracle said. Her voice had an ethereal, echoing quality to it just like his own. “I’m not surprised you know of it. Though, if you know even more, feel free to lead the way.”

Unfortunately, Ryland did not know more. He had never visited Strauslire before, having never needed a reason to. This visit was unprecedented for him.

It reminded him of poking into Paralimb. Where thousands of threads of Fate cut through one another, twisting and turning in a chaotic mixture that rapidly grew overwhelming even for Ryland. He had been lucky to be able to find Viren’s pet cat as fast as he had.

Now, Strauslire was giving him the same sensation.

Unlike Paralimb, this wasn’t a world filled with threads. Rather, this was a sea of flames. A world of conflagrations. Everywhere Ryland looked, he saw flames of different colours and consistency rising everywhere. Different forms too. Some fire turned into lightning. Some became streams of liquid magma. Some became pure light.

Without his Incarnation form, Ryland would probably have been incinerated a hundred times over in just the few seconds he had existed in Strauslire.

“I cannot maintain the connection for long,” the Oracle said. “The Incarnation is already burning away.”

She was right. Ryland could already feel their tenuous connection starting to slip. The heat and pressure from the press of flames was hammering him with so much intensity, he felt like he was back in the star he had attempted to move. There was no further time to waste. So, Ryland got to work.

Strauslire was a world of emotions. Or rather, the connection between one emotion and the next. Layer by layer, flame by flame, it created a dense network of the sum total of feelings that living beings could feel.

The sheer level of heat prevented Ryland from doing anything to the actual emotions. For instance, he couldn’t simply throw himself into that raging, multihued inferno in some fantastical notion of removing all hate from the universe. Strauslire hardly worked that way. Rather, this was a series of choices and consequences, actions and reactions, stimuli and responses.

Flames that fed into each other, ash that built into piles that rose higher than mountains, smoke drifting up to form continent-sized clouds. A web of the sum total of all emotions so dense and tangled, there was no hope for anyone, not even a Great God studying this madness for an aeon straight, to hope to unravel it.

But they weren’t here to do any of that. As their forms were slowly consumed by the heat, as the fuel provided by the Incarnation of Faith started running out, both Ryland and Vertha understood their goals perfectly.

“[Omniscience],” Ryland incanted.

“[Divination],” chanted the Oracle in response.

Together, they searched every corner of this Realm beyond Realms. They looked all over, hounded every corner they could think to look into, leaving no figurative stone unturned.

Until, the Oracle finally recognized the pattern they had been searching for.

“The auroras,” she breathed out. Her words had grown alarmingly faint, likely due to the strain they were both under. “That’s what we need to end…”

The way her words trailed off wasn’t great either, but Ryland focused on their goal. He tried casting [Immortal Rejuvenation] on the Oracle, but something interfered with it. Maybe she was already too far gone, or she just wasn’t present in a form that even his Tier [28] spell could interact with and actually heal up. The prospect of that was rather frightening to consider.

Weird that spells like [Omniscience] seemed to work fine on him, but healing wasn’t allowed.

But that just went to prove that focusing on their goal was the better option. The auroras. Ryland wasted no time channelling his trusty Soul Glyph of Sundering on them.

For just a moment, he thought it wouldn’t work.

Then the first aurora snapped. Splinters of iridescence rained into the world and began burning away, becoming more flames to join the endless parade of conflagrations.

“There are many of them,” the Oracle said.

She didn’t sound demoralized, but there were hints of it. A resignation, an understanding that they just might not be able to take care of all the influences of the Great Gods they were seeking to eradicate,

But Ryland refused to be daunted. Aurora after flaming aurora, he took down the Great Gods. He cut through their main means of influencing what happened beyond their Plane of existence. This was the best way of stopping the Great Gods from eternally assaulting his Inscription, and he was determined to see it through.

The heat grew intense. Ryland wasn’t here in person, yet the sensation of pain struck him like he hadn’t experienced it in ages. He forced himself onwards regardless, locating every bit of the Great Gods’ power he could find before obliterating them entirely.

A brief glance to his left proved why he had heard no further complaints or warnings in a while.

Whatever representation the Oracle had come in with was now awash with flames. They appeared iridescent as well, almost as though the rainbow-like flaming auroras they had been destroying—well, Ryland had been eradicating—were retaliating even as they fell. But he hadn’t experienced any of it because the Oracle had transferred all the damage onto herself.

Ryland grimaced, then continued. This couldn’t be stopped, no matter what. They had a golden opportunity to end this.

But even then, there were limits to everything.

“Ryland…” the Oracle finally said after another minute or so. “It ends…”

He didn’t know how many of the auroras he had destroyed. Keeping count had been an entirely thankless endeavour within moments of starting his task. There were just so many.

“Not yet,” Ryland said. “These fires are everlasting…”

“We must end this. Now, Ryland.”

“Not until I’ve found what I seek.”

“You’ve already destroyed enough of the Great Gods! I—we can’t sustain this any longer!”

Ryland didn’t reply. The pain he had begun to experience now rivalled everything in the world. It distantly made him wonder what the Oracle saw when she looked at him. Another burning soul just like her own. But he could take a great deal more than her, unless they had been reduced to a tremendously primal form devoid of their Inscriptions and other integral magic.

And even then, Ryland was certain that his manasoul had a far greater tolerance than Vertha’s.

“Ryland—”

“I have it!”

Triumph flooded him. The auroras were dealt with, had been dealt with for a while, at least as far as he had been able to see. But more importantly, he had found something that would truly help.

Unfortunately, the Oracle was under too much distress to notice it. “We must leave. Now.”

Ryland didn’t protest any further. The flames claimed him, ate him alive, hollowed out his manasoul. It was incredible just how much pain he could experience.

And then they were back. The sudden reappearance, the sudden reinstatement inside his body, made his entire world spin and even Ryland nearly stumbled to the ground. Calling the experience disorienting was doing it a tremendous disservice. It was insane.

Eventually, both he and the Oracle recovered. She didn’t look physically harmed from their little jaunt through Strauslire, but there was something next to her.

A husk. One that had once held an incredible amount of power.

“He may be…” Oracle Vertha didn’t finish her sentence, but Ryland understood what she meant. “I knew this was needed, and I will suffer the consequences. But the guilt… oh, it’s crushing.”

Ryland sympathized. But luckily for her, she was entirely wrong about the situation.

“It’s alright,” he said, stepping forward and pulling out the last thing he had claimed in Strauslire. In the world of intentions and emotions, the chain reactions of the psyche. “He isn’t gone.”

“Isn’t gone? We severed his sources of power. That he even exists in this state is essentially a miracle!”

“Then stand witness to another little miracle.”

The little spool of flames emerging from his hand dripped onto the Incarnation, whose vital essence had been used up in their effort to deal with the Great Gods’ source. This of course left the Incarnation more or less dead. Unless Ryland brought it back.

“What are you doing?” the Oracle asked, eyes slowly squinting. “The Incarnation of Faith needs a powerful source of belief to draw from. If we’ve severed the Great Gods as a source, then what else is left—”

Her eyes turned wide with shock as she stared at Ryland.

He smiled. “The Incarnation can put their faith in me for now.”
