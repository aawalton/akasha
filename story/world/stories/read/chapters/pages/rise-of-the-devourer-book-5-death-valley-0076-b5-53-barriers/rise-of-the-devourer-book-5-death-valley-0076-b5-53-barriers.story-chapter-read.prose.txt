Dylan settled down on Noah’s shoulder as they kept moving through the endless flat plane. For a long time neither spoke. Noah was focusing on maintaining their orientation and not letting himself get drawn away by the lure of the path back to the living world and Dylan had never been talkative at the best of times.

He had to admit, it was nice being free of the Devourer’s constant commentary. The rest of the trip should be downright peaceful. He just kept catching himself curving back whenever his focus wandered.

Unfortunately, their path didn’t remain empty for long. After another indeterminate amount of time that Noah would probably describe as an hour or two of walking, they came across an endless expanse of a large black wall.

"How did a whole wall get here without any of us seeing it?" Noah asked, annoyed at the disruption.

The wall crossed the entire path in the direction they were going, disappearing in either direction without visible end. He moved along beside it for a little, but it showed no sign of changing that way either. Jumping revealed that his ability to fly as a soul in the underworld was almost nonexistent. He could hop and somewhat drift, but while his momentum sideways seemed to be well preserved—as he learned by smacking face-first into the wall at a higher speed than he was expecting—upward was not something available to him.

The wall was too smooth to climb, resisting his best efforts to imitate a gecko. Dylan even tried scrabbling at it with his tentacles, in case he could make himself into a sort of step, but they slid off just as easily.

"Do we go around?" Noah looked first one way, then the other. The wall wasn't perfectly flat across their path, it would push them off a bit to the right, generally away from both the Reaper and the exit, or backtrack the way they'd come.

You're my vessel, are you not? the Devourer spoke up. Simply consume the wall.

“I can eat a wall?”

Yes, we can eat anything. That's rather the point of being called the Devourer, believe it or not.

Noah squinted uncertainly, but leaned up against the wall and stuck his mouth on it. It felt exactly like shoving his face against a window made of basalt. "Now what?" He could lick the stone, or scrape it with his ghostly teeth, but that was about it.

You're not trying to bite the wall, you're trying to consume it. Stop thinking with your body. It isn't here.

Noah frowned at the reprimand. "How exactly do you expect me to eat a wall without biting it?"

Go find something smaller to eat then.

"What does that help?"

If you get used to the feeling of eating, it'll become easier to do it again. It's not something complicated. What works for one thing works for another. You're just not familiar with doing it on your own. Too used to me taking care of everything for you. Or relying on the astral script to make things work.

Noah looked around, but the ground was a flat plane and the wall was equally flat but perpendicular. He finally shrugged and tore a piece of his sleeve. "Will this work?"

Maybe.

Noah put it in his mouth.

Don't do that! Take it out.

Noah himself stopped short of swallowing, choked slightly as he abruptly reversed course, and spat out the scrap of ghostly cloth. "What do you expect me to do?"

Consume it. I told you, stop thinking with your body.

Noah held up the scrap of ghost cloth. "You may as well tell me to levitate it."

We are the Devourer, not the Drifty Floater.

"So get devouring."

I already gave you everything you need. Think. Remember what it is to be the Devourer.

"Isn't that your job?" Noah asked.

Exactly. Now stop making excuses and remember.

Noah closed his eyes and thought back. He started with watching the Devourer consuming the wisps they'd fought back at the city by the river, but the weren't much of anything. It was a visual spectacle, but he didn't feel anything inside. Nothing that would show him what the Devourer wanted from him.

So he thought further back. Abilities he'd consumed. Enemies. His runic chains, the undead dragon's soul. He'd been consuming things as far back as the very start, when he consumed the first Shard of ???.

Much like absorbing mana, there was a difference between eating physically and consuming, devouring. Physical, magical, spiritual, all had their own distinct flavor.

The Devourer chuckled in his mind, and Noah scowled. "Shush, I'm focusing here."

The laughter at the back of his head fell silent.

He tried to tease out the difference in his memories, which ones were attached to each type, what the various techniques had arrived as. The powers he'd integrated into his paths tasted different than the ones he'd integrated into his inventory or that had become perks.

But it wasn't intuitive. The transformation of his Cosmic Wyrmblood bloodline, the transformation of his very race, none of that carried over here though he'd have expected it to do so.

He couldn't tell what separated magic from spirit, soul from spell.

That's why the Astral Script is so monumentally groundbreaking, Shadow commented. People can attain in months what would ordinarily take years of meditation, practice, and trial and error. Knowing exactly what to do right makes it easier to accomplish what would have been impossible for anyone but a master a thousand years ago.

But the Astral Script doesn't change you, it only augments you. Any growth has to come from yourself.

Noah nodded. "That sounds a lot like what Zax said about magic and resistances when we were first training together."

The old coward is still pretending that he is wise. He is weak and afraid. If you were not so sentimental, he would be the first thing I vote we consume.

"No eating my friends."

I have committed and thus it is bound, the Devourer said, almost grumpily. Our pact does not limit my desires, only my actions.

Noah opened his eyes. "This isn't working. You sure I shouldn't eat the thing?"

Yes. Do not.

If you want more motivation, it's on its way.

Noah looked around, suddenly alert, and saw what the Devourer was talking about. A group of wisps was drifting their direction. "I miss Abyssal Awareness so much." He raised his fists, ready to fight.

Don't forget that they will be consuming your soul every moment you stay in their proximity.

"Thanks."

I am not there to save you this time. If you think you cannot handle the challenge, then retreat.

"I'm not the retreating sort." Noah strode toward the incoming group of wisps.

They were more solid than most, wavering at the edges but the spiral maw they had instead of a face didn't lose its clarity. There were only five of them, however, unlike the dozens that Noah and the Devourer had already fought off collectively.

Take it slow, Shadow suggested. Strike one, then retreat.

Noah ignored the advice and threw himself into the fight. The first two wisps shattered as his fist swept through them impactlessly, scattering them into cloudy fragments. The next two shrieked their wordless cry and threw themselves at Noah despite their companions having failed right in front of them at the same endeavor.

They are not companions. There is no company among the lost, only madness and despair.

"How delightful." Noah took out the next wisp with a kick, then followed up with two quick slaps that scattered the rest of its substance in a trail of white around him, almost like his hand was a paintbrush drawing an arc on the air. The fifth came at him slower, its form slightly less substantial than the others, but no less viciously.

Behind you!

Noah ran straight into the fifth, slamming his whole body through it, and spun just in time to see the first two wisps reforming themselves behind him, their hungering faces leaning toward him. And, to his consternation, a thread of purple light drifted out of his own chest toward them in response.

"No you don't." He swiped through them again, then through the other two, then the fifth again. He could just about keep them all at bay, but they were spread out enough now that it left him scrambling constantly. "Now what?"

You're not here to punch them, you're here to consume them. Devour!

Noah tried biting one, without success. He snatched at some of their wispy body substance and shoved it into his own mouth, only for it to drift through his body and out the back of his head before rejoining its originator.

Not with your body! the Devourer hissed. Let me.

Noah hesitated. The internal pressure from the Devourer was gentle, a query rather than a demand, but he still worried about giving away too much of himself.

Don't be an idiot. I've already agreed to protect you and your allies. We're partners here, not adversaries.

Noah shrugged and nodded. "What do I have to--"

His hand flicked out faster than thought and his mouth opened, and a surge of warm fluffy lightning lit him up from inside. He had just enough time to observe the Devourer considering the soul's memories before discarding them and slurping out its raw strength instead.

Then Noah was back in control of his body, no longer a passenger observing. "That was trippy."

Don't stand there, keep moving!

Noah was still grinning in the aftertaste of whatever that soul wisp had been flavored with, slightly dizzied.

Urgh. Do I have to do everything?

Noah's fists struck out as he spun, scattering the four wisps who'd reconstituted themselves already and were helping themselves to his soul just as eagerly as he'd taken the other's.

He fell back into himself with a lurch and had to shake his head to clear it. "Okay, enough of that."

The Devourer mentally shrugged. You know what it feels like. Do it for yourself.

Noah nodded and tried to concentrate, but it was difficult to focus while fighting off four soul-eating monsters. It was hard to resist the temptation of handing control back to the Devourer to give himself the time to think back over the memory.

Then again, it wasn't that much different from giving Tony control of his body, was it? If they were truly the same, why would he need to retain complete control?

Exactly. Watch and learn.

"Nah." Noah turned and ran back toward the wall. Right now it was out of sight, by that strange trick of the Underworld, only appearing in front of them when they were within a few paces of it. He spun back to face the following wisps. As he'd expected, they moved significantly slower than he did.

He closed his eyes and concentrated. What had it felt like to initiate the consumption of that delicious storm, the exact sensations that rippled through him. It felt weird to replicate, almost like intentionally inhaling water against his body's every protest. Forcing muscles in unfamiliar paths.

But at the same time, it felt completely natural. Why shouldn't he be inhaling water?

He placed a hand on the wall, then breathed it in. It tasted oily and sticky, like the stone had been mixed with magic. He didn't know how he knew that was what he tasted, but it was the distinct vivid sensation he got from it.

Not going to clean up our little companions there?

"They don't need to be used as food. Who would deserve that fate?"

If you leave them here, they'll slowly lose themselves to madness and dissolution. I'd argue eating them is the merciful option.

Noah hesitated. Somehow, eating the souls of the dead felt more acceptable when they were physical enemies or literal monsters. These wisps were nothing he could recognize. They could as easily be people like Erwest or Snow as cultists or monsters.

They're not people any more, the Devourer ranted at him. They're dead and empty. Just eat them and get on with it.

"If the Reaper is leaving them to their own devices, I'm sure he has a reason."

Because it's not worth fighting with the Watcher over measly soul scraps like these. But you know he'll be the only one to benefit if they roam free, yes?

Noah didn't know that. He turned back with a frown.

The four wisps drifted toward him with their usual passiveness, deceptively calm until they'd explode into hunger and fury.

"It's different to do it myself," he admitted, "rather than just accept a system prompt."

It's not any different to them.

Noah nodded. He crossed the distance between them in three strides, swept his hand through all four, and slurped in their sparking, comfortingly fuzzy essence. It was even better in concentration like this, the slight differences in their souls blended into a delectable medley.

"This could get dangerous." If he weren't in such a hurry, he could have stood there basking in the inner warmth for hours, turning over each nuance of that delightfully complex set of souls.

If you leave them to dissolve on their own, you'll end up with a minor improvement to your soul grade. Or you can guide the process, choose what to take. Specific attributes, capabilities, memories... anything you can find is free to be taken.

Noah kept walking while they talked, absently eating a path through three different walls as he moved through a stranger, less city-shaped city. If anything, it was built like a fortress. Layers of walls, each thicker than the last, as if to dare anyone to try and break through them all. He couldn't help wondering what needed so much protection, and why they were all so long. He still hadn't found an edge to them, though he was cutting across them diagonally to stay on course toward the Reaper.

By the sixth layer, the wall was so thick that he had to eat a full on tunnel through it in order to pass. If not for the inward focus on the sparkling souls he'd eaten first, the repeated oily walls would have begun to disgust him after the first few, but he was distracted enough to ignore it.

It was easy to find the physical attributes, what would be translated by the Astral Script as constitution or agility. Mystic and power weren't far behind, but the closer he looked, the more details he started to find. There were thinner, fragile things attached to each of them. Nothing he could name, but he'd have bet just about anything that they were abilities.

He grabbed for the first, and the soul dissipated within him, sparking out into undirected energy.

If you're going to take out part of it, you need to stabilize it first. Compress the soul around that part, if you just try to tear it out, there won't be enough of it to survive the transition.

Noah's second try went better, forcing the devoured soul to empower its own ability before pulling it into himself--though without the Astral Script he had no idea what he'd just eaten.

With the third soul, he tried something different and looked deep into the thing as a whole, rather than focusing on the attributes or abilities.

The Devourer gave a pleased laugh and settled back to watch.

It took another several minutes, two more walls, and crossing halfway across a massive dark dome before he found what he was looking for. Deep within the soul, something thin and solid that tasted like refusal.

Noah had a harder time forcing the soul into that shape, they weren't as compatible with one another as the abilities or attributes, but he was relentless.

He'd just finished with the third and about to start on the last when something surged up around him, bright and smothering.

"Do you have any idea how frustrating it has been waiting for you?"

The man who stepped out of the grey nothingness looked inhuman, even compared to the Devourer. He had a faint silver glow to him, especially concentrated around his eyes. He didn't look fully alive, but he also didn't have the same translucent quality as Noah. His clothing was almost indistinguishable from his skin, grey and gleaming in a light that wasn't present. He had a sword of blue-white light in one hand and an expression of intense irritation on his face.

"How have you been, dear vessel?" the newcomer asked, every single word laced with venom and malice. He pointed his sword at Noah "I must say, you look weaker than what I expected, for having caused me such a lot of trouble."

"And who are you?" Dylan demanded from Noah’s shoulder.

"I am Vor Ashen, and I represent the Watcher, in whose domain you now stand." Before Noah could respond, Vor closed the distance between them in a single step. Noah couldn't even register what was happening before Vor's moonblade was inches away from his face. There was a manic edge to his voice that contrasted eerily with his bland affect. "I don't know what you are, Forgotten One, but I don't need to understand you to destroy you."
