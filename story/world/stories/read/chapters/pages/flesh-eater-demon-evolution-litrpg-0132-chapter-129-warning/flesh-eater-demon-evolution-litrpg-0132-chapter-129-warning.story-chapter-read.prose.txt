Pyre gave Pax a long look. At a glance, the crossroads demon looked the same as she remembered. She was still wearing that human’s corpse, lanky limbs outfitted in monochrome clothes and sharp features framed by choppy white hair. Those signature grey gloves remained present, and the demon’s body language was lax and casual. She wore the same vaguely uncanny smile, and her almost translucent pale eyes stared unblinkingly.

It had been a while since Pyre had last seen the other demon. She’d almost forgotten how silent Pax was. Combined with her total lack of scent and temperature, it created the odd sensation that nothing was really there even when someone clearly was.

And then, of course, there was the lingering wrongness that had nothing to do with Pax’s current form and everything to do with their past connection. Specifically, the one involving Pyre’s past life.

She’d half-wondered if Pax would simply avoid interacting with her after their previous encounter in the forest and all the revelations that had come with it. The crossroads demon had left awfully quickly after all but confirming that she had, indeed, known Pyre’s previous life.

Right now, at least, that didn’t seem to be the case. Pax gave no indication that she was at all bothered by the subject of their last conversation, nor did she reference it at all. Instead, she casually waved a gloved hand.

“Pyre, I see you’re doing well.”

The sloth demon raised an eyebrow. “Any reason you’re here? Thought I still had a few months before you needed that item or whatever,” she drawled. It looked like they were going to pretend like the whole “you knew my past self” revelation had never happened. Fine. She didn’t think she could get any real answers from the crossroads demon, anyway.

“You do,” Pax said simply. She cocked her head. “I’m not here for you today.”

Before Pyre had a chance to consider what the hell that was supposed to mean, the vibrations in the ground shifted. Behind them, Llyr exited the kitchen and stepped down the hallway towards the front door, movements quick, but not overly so. He looked surprised.

“Jael? I wasn’t expecting you today. Is it time already?”

Pyre’s brows rose. She glanced between the crossroads demon and the siren. If her memory wasn’t failing her, Jael was one of the many alternate fake name options Pax had listed during their first meeting.

Llyr was looking between Pyre and Pax as well, the curiosity clear on his face. “You know each other?”

“I could say the same to you,” Pyre pointed out. Her eyes landed on the healer’s earrings again. They looked completely unharmed from their “fishing trip,” the blue teardrop-shaped gems gleaming in the light.

Apparently her theory hadn’t been far off the mark.

Llyr stepped forward and shut the door. Pax had, at some point, stepped fully inside. Or maybe she’d just teleported or used whatever other fancy travel magic crossroads demons had. Frankly, Pyre was surprised she’d even bothered knocking in the first place, as opposed to popping up out of nowhere like a monochrome jack-in-the-box. Which was what she usually did when not summoned.

“Oh hell no.”

The three monsters turned. Around the hallway bend, Daphne and Callan were rather conspicuously poking their heads out from behind the corner to watch them. Daphne was scowling at the crossroads demon, and Callan’s face was contorted like he’d swallowed a particularly sour lemon.

Llyr studied the two of them, then turned to face the two demons again.

“Perhaps,” he began, “we should sit down.”

The tension in the dining room was almost tangible as Llyr carefully set down the plate of breakfast he’d made—some kind of egg dish that would probably be forgotten until this discussion was over. A bit of a shame considering it actually looked quite good. The siren’s cooking skills made a lot more sense now that they knew he was a reincarnator.

Daphne was still glaring daggers at Pax, and Callan kept fidgeting in his chair. Pyre suspected the ghost was only resisting the urge to phase straight through out of sheer force of will.

Llyr set down a few cups of tea, then took a seat next to Pax, who was watching them all with that faintly amused expression she often had. The one that made it quite clear that she saw herself as above the rest of them. Which, if they were speaking purely on power level and age, was technically true. It just didn’t improve the tension in the room.

“So, you’ve all met before?” Llyr asked, tastefully opening up the conversation. Pyre took an experimental sip of her tea and was pleased when it, like water, did not make her feel like she was dying on the inside. It still didn’t taste amazing, mind you, but she appreciated the variety. She sipped some more.

“Yep,” she confirmed. “We”—she jerked her head at Callan and then gestured to herself—“made a deal for some anti-monster detection charms a while back. That’s why we’re traveling, actually. We’ve got to fetch some shady item in the Spine to hold up our end of the bargain.”

Llyr’s eyes immediately moved to Pyre’s silver cuff earring, then the bracelet Callan was now fiddling with. He hummed thoughtfully.

“I see, it’s a similar case for me. I also made a deal for a talisman,” he explained. He paused, then added dryly, “though it did not help in the case of Lord Alardice.” His earrings swayed as he spoke. Clearly Pax’s magic hadn’t taken whatever the noble had going on into account.

“Why is it always Pax,” Callan muttered under his breath. “Are there no other crossroads demons?”

Pyre snorted at the comment. Honestly, though, it was a valid question. She already knew why Pax had answered them, though she assumed her reasons for making a deal with Llyr had more to do with the siren’s abilities than any secret past connection. Pax had said that she could afford to be “selective” about her deals, and getting a powerful healer in a deal was probably gold for a crossroads demon.

“What’d you give in exchange?” Pyre asked. She assumed it had to do with why Pax had popped up here now of all times.

In response, Pax idly raised an arm. Daphne and Callan both tensed, but she ignored them. Instead, the demon rolled up her sleeve just far enough for them to get a glimpse of the skin beneath.

Further up her forearm, Pyre spotted what looked like decaying, rotting flesh. The patch was darker in color than the surrounding skin and wrinkled across the surface like a dried tree stump. It clearly continued further up the crossroads demon’s body.

Pyre’s mind flashed back to those bandages Pax had had in the Lightning Pass, then the lack of them during their next meeting on the Bellhein grounds.

“I found a temporary solution,” Pax had said when she’d asked about it. Pyre raised an eyebrow as the pieces clicked into place.

“Getting Llyr to heal your meat suit was your ‘temporary solution?’”

Pax smiled. “It’s rather effective, isn’t it? It saves me from needing to find a new body every time my current one rots.”

Daphne’s brows furrowed in confusion at that. Callan leaned closer to mutter something into her ear that made her face twist even more. Ah, right, she didn’t know about Pax’s whole “wearing a human corpse” thing. That certainly wouldn’t improve her opinion on the demon.

“Of course,” Pax added lightly, “the ideal outcome would be if I could acquire a body that I didn’t need to worry about rotting in the first place. A nigh-immortal one with strong regenerative abilities.” Pale eyes flitted over to Llyr, and the demon’s smile sharpened. “Unfortunately, the siren here seems rather unwilling to part with his.”

Llyr took a sip of his tea with no outward reaction. Pyre assumed that this had already come up in conversation before, considering how calm he was about a body-snatching crossroads demon not-so-subtly suggesting that he was the next target. Well, that certainly explained why Pax had zeroed in on him for a deal.

“As I said, that’s not happening,” the healer said simply. He set his cup down with a light clink.

“Oh, you never know. Desperation can drive people to lengths they would never before consider. Few truly understand their own depths.” Pax cocked her head, long earrings swaying with the movement. Her voice remained easy and casual even as her pale gaze was fixed unblinkingly on the siren. It was a stare Pyre was quite familiar with at this point—one that made it feel like the demon could see straight through you. “You never know what the future will bring.”

Llyr’s eyes narrowed, but he remained seated.

For a moment, no one at the table spoke. It was as if all of them were collectively waiting for someone to make the next move first. Finally, Pax chuckled, and the sound broke through the tension that had fallen over the room.

The crossroads demon raised her arm, sleeve still rolled up even as her gloves remained firmly in place. “Well, future possibilities aside, shall we return to the matter at hand? I assume,” she remarked, waving her arm casually, “that this is still well within your ability.”

In response, Llyr lifted his own hand. His palm immediately began to glow that signature blue, and he closed his eyes and chanted something.

In the corner of the dining room, one of the vases trembled. A stream of water rose from its mouth, flowing through the air like a liquid ribbon that Llyr guided closer. The glowing water coiled around Pax’s exposed arm, and the light grew brighter. Pax watched the whole thing in faint amusement. Meanwhile, Callan and Daphne had both leaned closer to get a better look at the magic.

With a final sweep of his hand, the light faded. In its wake, Pax’s arm was visible again—now perfectly smooth and free of all withering.

Llyr guided the water stream away, coiling it into a tight sphere before lightly tossing it into a basin in the corner of the room. Pyre vaguely wondered just how often this had happened, considering both he and Pax seemed quite used to this process already.

Pax idly inspected her healed arm, turning it around a few times in the light. “Hm, acceptable,” she said. Based on Llyr’s light snort, Pyre suspected that the crossroads demon frequently said this.

“Well then, I’ll be going now.” Pax abruptly pushed her chair back and rose to her full height. She made to turn away, but at the last second, she stopped. Those pale eyes landed on Pyre.

“Ah, right, I should mention this before I go. As I said before, I don’t particularly care about your current detour. It’s quite smart to keep your mage support healthy, fragile as they are.”

Both Daphne and Callan made affronted noises at that, but the crossroads demon completely ignored them and continued.

“However, I do suggest you try and make your way to Pereth within a month’s time.”

Pyre raised an eyebrow at that. “You shortening our timeframe?”

Pax waved dismissively. “Quite the contrary. I’m happy to abide by our original contract terms. This is for your own sake.” The corners of her mouth tugged up in a sharp smile, and some unreadable emotion flashed in her eyes. “It will be much more difficult for you to retrieve this item after the next month has passed.”

The words lingered in the room, ringing with a sense of truth that was difficult to ignore. Before anyone could ask what the hell her warning meant, though, or how she knew about it, Pax had already turned away.

In the blink of an eye, she was gone.

A few beats of silence passed, everyone seemingly holding their breath. Waiting. When the crossroads demon didn’t reappear, the tension in the room deflated like a popped balloon. Daphne’s shoulders slumped, and she rubbed her forehead. “Why did it have to be Pax,” she muttered.

Callan, on the other hand, was staring worriedly at the spot where Pax had disappeared from. “What did she mean by that? Is something happening in a month?” He shifted uneasily. “I haven’t heard of anything.”

Pyre just shrugged. “Who knows.” She took another sip of her tea, which was mostly cold at this point. “I doubt she’d tell us more even if we asked, anyway,” the demon continued. “Not without having to make another deal or something.”

By now, she’d grown accustomed to the crossroads demon’s dodgy nature. If she wouldn’t elaborate on a past life that Pyre literally couldn’t remember, she doubted she’d give more details on this.

Still, the signature vagueness did make it difficult to know how seriously to take the warning. “More difficult” was also a very subjective term. It could mean anything ranging from “this will take a few extra days” to “the item is now basically impossible to reach.”

Either way, Pyre figured it didn’t hurt to hurry once they were out of Serindell. If they found another one of those magic-powered carriages to hitch a ride on, they shouldn’t have any issue reaching Pereth long before whatever thing Pax was hinting at happened. They’d traveled farther to get from Magrath to here.

With that settled in her mind, she glanced at Llyr, who was sipping his own cup of tea. She raised an eyebrow. “You seem to know Pax pretty well,” she commented.

Between Pax’s tendency to make subtle digs at people and the reincarnator’s sometimes hairpin trigger, she’d half-expected that conversation to end much worse. It seemed Llyr was used to Pax’s comments, though, and the crossroads demon herself had acted with marginally more restraint than she had with Callan or Daphne. Possibly because they had an ongoing healing agreement, and despite her talk of Llyr’s magic being “acceptable,” it was clearly quite rare and valuable.

And of course, if she was actually hoping to use his body as a meat suit at some point, then that would be another reason to stay somewhat civil. It would be hard to get a future body-trading deal out of the siren if he completely hated the demon.

Llyr set his cup down and smiled wryly.

“Not really, we simply have a mutual agreement. We don’t interact much regardless.”

Pyre hummed at that, and conversation at the table soon fizzled out again. Slowly, Daphne picked up her fork and began to eat.

Pax’s appearance had successfully made everyone even more antsy than they already had been. Between the ongoing worries about the cult and the wait for the invitation, they now had the crossroads demon’s ambiguous warning to add to the pile of shit to sort through.

It was no wonder that both Daphne and Callan looked about ready to explode by the time they finished breakfast. There was too much energy thrumming in the air right now to be sitting still.

Pyre gave the two mages a long look, considering. “So, about heading into Serindell,” she began idly. “You said we needed clothes or something?”

Daphne immediately nodded, jumping onto the shift in topic. She looked relieved to have something tangible to do while they waited.

“Yes, we’ll need clothes for the ball. I was going to wait until after we received the invitation, but I think it’s fine to go now. Lord Alardice already said what the attire guidelines are, and I doubt Lord Rotham will deny my request.”

Pyre hummed. “Okay, sounds like a plan.” She gulped down the rest of her tea, then set the cup down onto the table. She rose. “Let’s get going, then.”
