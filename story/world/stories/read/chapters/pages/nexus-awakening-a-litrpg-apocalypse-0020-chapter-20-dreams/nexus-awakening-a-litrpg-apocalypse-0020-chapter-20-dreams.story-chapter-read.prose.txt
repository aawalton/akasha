Chapter 20: Dreams

I chose the Path of the Nexus Eclipse.

Maybe it should have been a profound choice. After all, what did that reveal about me? That I wanted to conquer the Resonance Flux? To become what was essentially a god? Yes, it revealed all of those things. Every dark, hungry part of me that I'd tried to pretend didn't exist.

It was a tough truth to try and hide from. In the moment, I wanted to tell myself this was the path that would lead to the most power, which would leave me in the best position to protect Elisa. The noble lie. The comfortable fiction that let me sleep at night without seeing that young initiate's face every time I closed my eyes.

Now, I laughed at myself. What an utterly weak lie. The truth was that I wanted power. I was looking for ways to get it, and treading a path that would lead me to the greatest amount of it appealed to me on a level I didn't want to examine too closely.

I was always going to take that kind of path. The man who built houses might have chosen differently, but I wasn't him anymore. Hadn't been for a while now. That man had died in pieces, a little more each day since the night monsters tore through my front door.

When I opened my eyes from the vision, my status screen was floating before me like an accusation made of blue light. The text glowed steady and certain, no judgment in the cold display of numbers and abilities.

[STATUS] Name: Varus Thorne Age: 31 Flux Level (FL): 10 Race: Human (Awakened) Faction: Nyxen (Initiate)

[PROGRESSION] Flux Experience (FEXP): 1100 Path: Path of The Nexus Eclipse

[TEMPERING] Mind: Awakened Initiate Body: Awakened Initiate Spirit: Awakened Initiate Bloodline: Nexus Bloodline

[ATTRIBUTES] Resonance: 17 Projection: 14 Containment: 4 Perception: 4 Attunement: 4

[ASPECT ABILITIES] Vital: None Primal: Flux Surge (Initiate)

Flux surges within you in response to your anger, granting you enhanced strength and speed. Deepen your connection to the Primal Aspect to enhance the strength of this ability. Flux Crush (Initiate)

Your rage surges through the Flux, allowing you to use it to crush opponents with Containment lesser than your resonance.

Flux: Flux Sense (Initiate)

Your awareness of life, and objects connected to Resonance Flux. Can currently sense the strength of other initiate tier life forms and objects. Deepen your connection with the Flux on a neutral level to increase the power of this ability.

Void: Consume Flux (Initiate)

You can concentrate, tapping into the void aspect of the Flux and consume an initiate level Flux ability.

Echo: None

I had four more attribute points, and had already considered where to spend them. The math was simple enough. Two went into Containment, bringing it to six. One went into Projection, bringing it to fifteen. The final point went into Resonance, bringing it to eighteen.

Numbers. Everything came down to numbers now. How strong you were, how fast you could kill, how much power you could channel before your body gave out. It was cleaner than trying to measure things like goodness or mercy or love. Numbers didn't lie to you or make promises they couldn't keep. They just were what they were, honest in their simplicity.

After that, I considered the new ability I had gotten. Flux Crush. The name said it all, really. I could feel it sitting in my mind like a loaded weapon, waiting to be used. The description made it sound simple enough - use my rage to crush opponents through the Flux itself. Just will their bones to break, their organs to rupture, their life to end.

But I was finding that just because the Resonance Flux told me I could do something, didn't mean I could mentally will it with a thought. The Flux was helpful, but it wasn't magic. Not the fairy tale kind where you wished for something and it happened.

Usually, I needed to tap into the aspect somehow, allow the Flux within me to flow in a certain way. Channel the right emotions, find the right mental state, let the power build until it had somewhere to go. The rage came easy enough. That had never been the problem. It was directing it, focusing it, turning raw emotion into something useful that took practice.

Or in other words, I would need to practice. And practice meant hurting things. Testing the limits of what I could do and how far I could push before something pushed back.

That meant finding enemies. For a while now, dreams had been plaguing me. They called me deeper into the dungeon, past the relatively safe areas we'd been hunting in, toward something I couldn't name but could feel pulling at me like a fishhook in my chest.

The dreams were always the same. Dark corridors stretching into impossible distances. Voices calling my name in languages I didn't recognize but somehow understood. A sense of something waiting, something important, something that would change everything if I could just find the courage to seek it out.

I didn't want to go, as I felt separating from the group was a bad idea. Basic survival instinct said stick together, watch each other's backs, divide the threats instead of facing them alone. It was the smart thing to do. The reasonable thing. The kind of decision the construction worker in me would have made without hesitation.

However, I could feel the insistence now. More than a dream, more than some random neural firing in my sleeping brain. It pulled at me through the Resonance Flux itself, a constant tugging sensation that grew stronger every time I tried to ignore it. Something was calling me, and whatever it was, it wasn't taking no for an answer.

I needed to follow. The knowledge sat in my gut like a stone, heavy and undeniable. Fighting it felt like trying to swim upstream against a current that only grew stronger the more you struggled. My Nexus bloodline seemed to resonate with whatever was calling, making my skin tingle and my teeth ache when I tried to resist.

Finding Kari just outside our cave wasn't hard. She had returned from patrol, her green eyes still alert despite the exhaustion that lined her face. The constant vigilance was wearing on all of us, but she carried it better than most. She'd dropped a dead boar at the entrance to our shelter, its alien blood pooling on the stone in patterns that reminded me of the crimson veins in the dungeon walls.

The boar was a good kill, clean and efficient. It would feed us for days if we rationed it properly. Kari was good at her job, good at keeping us alive and fed and functional. A useful asset to have in a place like this.

"I'm leaving for a while," I stated. No point in dancing around it or trying to soften the blow. She was going to think it was stupid no matter how I phrased it, and I didn't have the energy to pretend I cared about her opinion.

She arched a black eyebrow at me, her expression shifting from tired to concerned in the space of a heartbeat. "Yeah? Nice vacation spot somewhere I don't know about?"

Her voice carried that forced lightness she used when she was worried but trying not to show it. I'd heard Lynn use the same tone when I'd worked late shifts on dangerous job sites, that careful casualness that couldn't quite hide the fear underneath. The similarity should have bothered me more than it did.

I didn't laugh. Couldn't manage it, even to make her feel better. The pull was getting stronger, making it hard to focus on anything else. Like trying to have a conversation while someone hammered nails into your skull.

"Something is calling me. I don't know what, but I'm going to follow it. I need to do it alone."

The words sounded insane even to me. Following mysterious dreams deeper into a dungeon designed to kill us, abandoning the safety of numbers for the unknown. But the alternative was staying here while that constant tugging sensation drove me slowly mad. Better to face whatever was waiting than to let it consume me from the inside.

Kari pursed her lips, her green eyes studying my face with the kind of intensity that suggested she was seeing more than I wanted her to. She'd gotten good at reading people since we'd been thrown together. Survival depended on understanding the mental state of the people watching your back, knowing when someone was about to snap or run or do something that would get everyone killed.

"It's not like I can stop you, can I?" she said finally, resignation creeping into her voice. "Look, it's a stupid idea, but should I even waste time trying to convince you or is your mind made up?"

She knew me well enough by now to recognize when I'd already decided something. There was no point in argument or persuasion once I'd set my course. Maybe that was a flaw or maybe it was just another survival trait I'd picked up in this place. Flexibility was good, but so was the ability to commit completely to a decision once you'd made it.

"It's made up," I stated firmly.

"Very well." She sighed, the sound carrying weeks of accumulated stress and worry. "No guarantees we'll be here when you get back, you know? If this place goes to hell while you're gone, we're not waiting around to get killed on the off chance you decide to come home."

Her concern seemed genuine enough. Maybe it was. But I couldn't bring myself to care about her feelings one way or the other. She was useful, and now I was leaving. That was the extent of our relationship, no matter what she might think.

I nodded. It was fair. More than fair, actually. They didn't owe me anything, and I was abandoning them to chase something I couldn't explain. But then again, I didn't owe them anything either. We'd been useful to each other. Now I had other priorities.

If the situations were reversed, I'd make the same choice without hesitation.

"Tell the others I said goodbye," I said, though I wasn't sure why I bothered. Politeness, maybe. Or keeping up the pretense that I still cared about social niceties. Old habits died hard, even when everything else was changing.

"I will." Kari's expression softened slightly. "Try not to die out there, yeah? Would hate to think we wasted all that effort keeping you breathing."

Then I left, following the call that pulled at me like a chain around my soul. The Nexus bloodline sang in my veins as I walked away from the cave, away from the safety of numbers and shared watches and people who thought they knew me.

The deeper parts of the dungeon were different from the areas we'd been hunting in. The architecture became more twisted, more openly hostile to human perception. Walls curved in directions that shouldn't exist, and corridors led to rooms that were larger on the inside than the outside. The air itself felt thicker, charged with Flux energy that made my skin tingle and my teeth ache.

I moved carefully, resonance blade ready but not activated. Light would give away my position to anything else that might be hunting these passages, and I was already taking enough risks just by being here alone. The pull led me forward through twisting corridors and impossible chambers, past dangers I could sense but not see.

The sounds of the dungeon changed as I went deeper. The constant background noise of distant combat and dying screams faded, replaced by something else. Whispers in languages I didn't recognize. The sound of wind through passages that had no openings to the outside. Occasionally, what might have been laughter, if laughter could carry that much malice and hunger.

Hours passed, or maybe days. Time moved strangely in the deepest parts of the dungeon, stretching and compressing in ways that defied normal understanding. My watch had stopped working weeks ago, another casualty of prolonged Flux exposure. I measured progress by exhaustion and the growing strength of the call that guided me.

My legs ached from walking on uneven stone. My shoulders burned from carrying my pack and staying ready for combat. But the pull never weakened, never let me rest for more than a few minutes at a time. Whatever was calling me, it was impatient. Eager. Hungry for my arrival.

The Nexus bloodline in my veins responded to that hunger with hunger of its own. I could feel it stirring, awakening to something that resonated with its nature. The amber-gold glow in my eyes grew stronger the deeper I went, until I could see clearly even in passages where no light should exist.

Eventually, I found myself standing at the edge of a vast chasm that split the dungeon floor like a wound in reality itself. The pull led down into those depths, into darkness so complete it seemed solid. I could hear things moving down there, massive shapes that displaced air and made sounds like grinding stone.

But I couldn't see the bottom. Couldn't see anything at all except the first few feet of a narrow path that spiraled down along the chasm wall. After that, it was just darkness and the certainty that whatever was calling me lay at the bottom of this abyss.

I stood there for a long time, looking down into that hungry dark, feeling the pull grow stronger with each passing moment. The smart thing would be to turn around, go back to Kari and the others, pretend I'd never felt this calling. Find some excuse for why I'd left and hope they'd take me back.

But I was done being smart. Done being reasonable. Done being the kind of man who built safe houses and lived safe lives and pretended the universe was a place where good things happened to good people.

I started down the path, following the call into the depths, toward whatever waited in the darkness below.
