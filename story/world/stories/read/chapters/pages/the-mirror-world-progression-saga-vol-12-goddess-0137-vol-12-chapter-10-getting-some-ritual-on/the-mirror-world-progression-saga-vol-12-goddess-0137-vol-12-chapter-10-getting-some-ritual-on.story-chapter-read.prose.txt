Twelve legendary figures gathered at the top of Itha Ranon. Some of them were natural spellcasters, variants of the [Wizard] class in some way, like Ash or Azure. Orswyth, as always, was somewhere between cleric and mage, but he lent special ritual benefits just being there, particularly to stability. With the sheer volume of MP about to throw down, that was very welcome, indeed.

Others were there like batteries because they simply had a ton of MP, such as Bast, or the newer ritual target when available, Tashome, who could ‘transmute’ the power in his living artifact arm for that purpose. One Naugite figure, apparently an overly tall wizard, might surprise those who didn’t know the truth: he was the God of War, Zadkiel, and was just using his newer backup Avatar, which with his high [Wizard] levels and various extra MP boosts from innate abilities and quest rewards, was among the highest.

Bast was glaring over at the Naugite figure as if with newly flared-up ire. “It seems we’re working together in a whole new way today. I’ll kill you another.”

The blue-skinned Naugite grinned and shrugged. “And here I thought you’d let that bit of business go, my comrade. I’m glad to see we’ll settle it like men regardless, one day — when our mutual honor is cleared.”

“We will.”

Samantha neglected to say anything and stared significantly at Azure to forestall her saying something, though that got a roll of the eyes.

They likely won’t fight. But it may be more important than ever to let him fool himself. He and his inner self both are quite impulsive. At least Zadkiel isn’t goading him.

“Let’s begin,” Samantha called, and began the initial phase of the ritual, which was to perform alignment incantations and gestures, setting the stage for others to join and link to her. For her method, this was very much like a song, with fluid stationary dances. Due to the obscene complexity and energy requirements of the ritual, this required very high skill levels, at least on average.

Orswyth took it up next, which greatly simplified it as his voice interlaced with his deep, bass notes. He became like the drum to her lead, giving the others, especially the less familiar or skilled, a much better baseline to work from.

Azure became another counterbalance with her sharper notes, lending a pulling, inspirational cadence that drew the rest into form.

Ash, hand and staff lifted high, was yet another elementary contrast to Azure, providing a steady, precise rhythm that never wavered, yet was lighter and more delicate than Orswyth.

On and on it went, each providing their own take. Samantha was somewhat surprised by the contrast of Bast and Zadkiel. Bast provided a raw creative force, chaotic and fluid as he made the entire musical construct wave and swirl in grand motions, forcing adjustment but contributing a massive power increase. This was not surprising per se, but Zadkiel…

The Naugite’s forms were supremely ordered and intricate, balancing out the chaos Bast contributed, and even specifically shearing and contouring Bast’s notes specifically, like bending sails catching the wind.

It very much annoyed Bast, but not enough to disrupt the ritual. By now, the power of it was its own thing, and it consumed most of their attention. It would do this just to see and hear, because it was quite beautiful and wondrous, easy to become fascinated by.

But once all twelve were linked and in sync, the power began to flow. Mana ramped up and fueled the spell, pulled first from them and a smaller portion from the tower’s battery.

Samantha took the energy by her spirit and will and wove it into form, converting it into many different layers as suited the magic schools involved, dominated most physically by the pneumanic components. She gradually added in the Faith Energy equation to the raw brew of swirling energy gathering above their heads — a spell yet to be cast.

When the majority of the energy was gathered, she began to stitch together the actual spell, virtually as if over an army of people, each transformed by the magic into a better version of themselves. The music became more technical as she stopped hearing sounds and toyed with the magic of concepts and raw form.

She had to divide her mind to the maximum here, to properly visualize all the possibilities and attain the realization of [Sublime Art] and its promise. Every soldier was slightly different. She did not desire a total chaos of weird chimeras, but each must have some aspect of themselves represented.

Fate guided her to make the proper ‘fluid’ to fill the future molds.

They churned through it and into the final minutes of their hour-long engagement. It turned progressively back into a song, the beginning becoming like the end. Finally, the notes wound to their final forms, and all of it was captured just before it might cross the last threshold into reality. The magic was force-flowed as a whole through the tower structure and underneath, following the connection matrix into the storage that was the auxiliary tower.

There it was contained, the final five seconds of the casting withheld and bound into a simple activation, made for a later date, and almost hilariously simple to conduct. Even a Level 1 [Wizard] with a secondary control crystal could technically do it, opening the circuit and directing the spell as otherwise legal. Though that could be nearby, their intention was for the focal point to be Samantha, far north, where she’d finish the final physical requirement by shouting loudly and far in all directions.

For leading and casting a great ritual spell, you have gained 12(4) exp in [Magineer]. Note: this type of experience gain is one-time only.

Eh. I’ll take it. Puts me at 65 out of 170.

After it was over, various of the spellcasters sagged and were panting from exhaustion. A couple, including Ash, sat right down on the floor.

Samantha called, “Thank you, everyone, and great job. That is a guaranteed difference-maker. Dax will be elated.”

She’d better be,” Ash muttered, blinking, as she wiped her brow of sweat. “That energy, I… That was intense.”

Orswyth, leaning on his staff heavily and panting, nodded. “Indeed.”

Bast played like he was unaffected, though he was sweating profusely. “It was a good workout, I say! Old man Orsy, you might want to cast a telekinesis spell to haul your bones to the bed. Or do you need a boost? I’d be happy to assist a venerated soul such as yourself.”

Orswyth glared at him without real heat. “Quiet, you. Otherwise, I’ll introduce catnip to your felines.”

Bast laughed. “Now there’s an idea! Hmm. Is there something even I dare not tempt fate with?”

Azure scoffed. “I’d put money on you doing it, at this point.”

“A fair call.” Meanwhile, Samantha was aware of the half-white, half-black halo over Bast’s head making ‘cat eyes’ briefly, a sign they were in there and probably already curious about the subject matter.

Tashome, looking down at nothing in particular, glanced Samantha’s way. “If there’s nothing else?” At her slight nod, he strode immediately from the room.

Bast stared after him with a frown. Once he was gone, he muttered, “I’ve tried with him, you know. Time and again. Thank the heavens for my mother, at least.”

Samantha already knew Marjorie and Tashome were together, but was sworn to secrecy by her adoptive mother on it. Tashome was cagey and oddly sensitive about the subject. She’d assume Bast knew. In any case, they were both very busy, like pretty much everyone.

As everyone began going their separate ways, Samantha sat down next to Ash. “Got your head on straight about what to cast for the opener?”

Ash met her eyes and nodded. “Perhaps simple and stupid, in mage terms. Radiance From Heaven, opting for additional targeting instances with the tower’s modification.”

Samantha raised her eyebrows and whistled. “Radiance? From the Radiancers? Those badass elves?”

“Yes. We conspired with those particular specialists left among the elves. Some died at Matrazere, sadly. Fortunately, Leiande — their foremost expert — survived and was able to help with a custom, hybrid, ritual-only spell. We didn’t want her stuck here for the battle, so we needed to get creative. I had an idea: multiple smaller ritual spells contributing to one greater spell cast together. With her guidance, three of her disciples are adding what is necessary to bestow the anti-undead effect.”

Samantha’s eyebrows climbed even higher. “You’re surprising me with this, right now.”

Ash smiled in pleasure. “I know. I would’ve told you soon, but there are a few final kinks to get through, and I wanted it totally ready.”

“I can help-”

“I’d like to finish it myself, Mother.” She had a stubborn look. “But if I fail in the next day, I will come to you to fix it.”

Samantha nodded slowly and finally smiled. “So be it, then.”

Ash looked away, somewhat embarrassed. After clearing her throat, she said, “Hopefully, we will have the logical hard targets by the time you activate the crown.”

“With this particular spell in Spellhold, I’ll want it out there pronto.”

“You may want to check with Dax. I think she’d prefer that held slightly. Particularly because that cloud wreath ability can be used twice. It’ll inevitably bait them out a bit to use it the first time, especially as it looks like a big one-off thing. Regardless, though, we’ll have targets. We are loaded up on information-gathering like never before, and already have some high-probability suspicions. We will find the undead giants one way or another and remove them from the equation entirely. Even if not, this particular spell will fry other targets just as well.”

Samantha took a breath and nodded. “Alright then. Good work, Ash. I’m hellaciously proud right now.”

Ash smiled faintly. “Thank you, Mother. I’m glad to hear it. Everyone will do their best, just like you, even when you’ve had to suffer to the extreme to make it happen. We will too, if necessary. Whatever it takes.”
