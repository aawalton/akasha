Unfortunately, the wormholes opened by Enki were made in a hurry, so when Zora dashed through the first wormhole, then fell through several more wormholes that brought him far and away from the Divine Temple, he finally emerged… somewhere in the sky.

For half a heartbeat, there was nothing beneath him but wind and distance. His stomach lurched as gravity claimed him immediately. His wings were already open, though, so he snapped them out to catch the wind as he quickly reoriented himself.

Along with Kita, Ifas, Machi, Sora, and the rest of the soldiers he’d entered the Divine Temple with, they were all plummeting towards the far northern earthen wall of the Divine Capital. Enki and Ifas aside—who surely had a way to handle their own landing—the others were flailing and desperately trying to stop their rapid descent.

Zora swept his staff in a wide arc.

“Guide them to safety, winds of the world!”

And a giant hand of wind swept under everyone, cradling them and gathering them until they were close enough to the ramparts of the northern wall—and then it rolled everyone across the ramparts, having slowed them just enough to avoid major injuries on impact. A good ten seconds later, Zora landed on his toes next to Sora and Kita. A good ten more seconds after that, both Enki and Ifas crash-landed next to Machi with nothing but pure strength and resilience in their legs, cracking the ground beneath them.

As the soldiers recovered and tried to crawl onto their feet, Zora turned his head out and listened.

Beyond the great earthen wall, the Reunification Army’s many advancing units were still charging over the dead zones. He heard them sprinting through mud and old shell craters, a hundred meters below, carving trenches and tunnels in real time to avoid artillery fire from the cannons on the wall, firing errant bullets up at the Royal Capital Guards on the ramparts to buy themselves more space. Some of the units were even close enough to the base of the northern wall that he could physically hear the orders being given to ascend the wall with hooks, ropes, and fast-growing fungi.

That was a good thing to hear. It meant, even if Zora and the elite team hadn’t been able to make it into the Divine Temple, the Reunification Army would’ve eventually breached the northern wall.

But when he turned and listened into the Divine Capital itself, his stomach sank.

Fire had a sound. It wasn’t always just crackling. It was the frantic chewing of wood, the popping of heated stone, and the distant, wavering roar that grew whenever panic fed it with open doors and spilled oil—and now there were fires all across the city.

People were screaming. Many of them. Different districts, different streets, scattered like sparks thrown into dry grass. Footfalls ran in every direction. Market stalls shattered. Doors were slammed, giant ant rides screeched, and threaded through all of that chaos were small, precise noises that didn’t belong to panicked civilians at all.

Small groups of soldiers were cutting down civilians across the city, and these weren’t Reunification Army troops. They were moving too stiff in the joints and too unhurried in their cruelty, slitting throats and continuing to walk, firing into crowd and not flinching at any scream. They marched through heavy fire as if smoke was merely weather, and Zora probably wouldn’t believe what they were if he hadn’t already fought a few of them already.

Undead soldiers.

There weren’t many of them across the city. Maybe two, three hundred at most, but the dead-men squads were creating chaos inside the walls, forcing civilians to stampede and forcing the Royal Capital Guards on the ramparts to look inward… and all the while, mindless masses of Giant-Class ants burst up in pockets across the city, ripping stone and timber apart as they surfaced to reinforce the undead soldiers in the thousands.

Carnage.

And then there was the Divine Temple in the distance. The pyramid’s flat roof was completely demolished, fully exposing a single, overwhelmingly powerful ant queen as she stood upon the ruined crown of the empire.

Regalia gazed down upon the burning capital, and then she spoke.

“Crush the empire, my children.”

With that, she began her slow march down the stairs of the Divine Temple, trailed by three shadows that Zora couldn’t hear—or distinguish—too well at all.

Nevertheless, Regalia was heading south.

Cold dread slid down his spine.

This is bad.

As Regalia’s departure thundered away, she was no longer alone in being seen or heard. Across the vast length of the northern ramparts, tens of thousands of Royal Capital Guards faltered. Artillery fire stuttered, then died entirely. Cannons fell silent. Rifles lowered. Men and women turned to stare back into their own city, and even those beside Zora couldn’t look away. Ifas narrowed his eyes. Machi’s hands hovered uselessly near her sheathed knife. Sora stared, pale and wordless with an open notebook in hand, while Kita remained on her knees, tears streaking down her face, grief still hollowing her chest—but even she couldn’t help but lift her head, eyes drawn helplessly toward the Regalia.

All of them were technically enemies with the Royal Capital Guards surrounding them on the ramparts, but no human dared attack another human now.

… She’s going to the southern earthen wall.

The southern earthen wall of the Divine Capital was perhaps the most famous wall on the entire continent, rivalled in size and strength only by the anti-titan wall in the Rampaging Hinterland Front. Since the capital was built on the southernmost point of the continent, the wall was the only thing standing between the capital and the Crawling Seas—the endless mass of bugs packed so densely they resembled and moved like a single black tide. Two hundred of the empire’s three hundred Spore Knights were stationed there at all times, whittling down the bugs of the Crawling Seas day and night, and still that battlefront was known as the ‘Battle Without End’.

If the southern earthen wall were to fall, there’d be no battle to hold the capital. The Crawling Seas would pour in like night itself, swallow the city whole, before spilling northward into the empire beyond. Entire regions would fall in days, and the rest of the empire within weeks.

And if the Attini Empire falls…

The southern earthen walls were famous for being nigh-indestructible from the outside, but from the inside?

Regalia seemed confident she could do something about it.

… So this is what you mean, Decima?

This is… my past played out on a ‘grander’ scale?

He couldn’t help but notice it now, but the sequence of events was a near-exact replica of what’d happened in Amadeus Academy two years ago.

Just like two years ago, Decima—Nona—had allowed him to win small battles across the empire—Amadeus Academy—so she could make him believe he was gaining advantages and building up his strength… only to show her real hand at the end. Back then, Nona had lured him and the others to the northern research building only to flip the table on them and force them to retreat with an overwhelming display of power. Now, Decima had lured him and the Regional Lords of the Reunification Army into the Divine Temple, only to flip the table on them and force them to retreat once again with Regalia.

Here, Decima even managed to replicate the death of a parent and the grief of a child. Nona had killed the Headmistress and left Cecilia motherless, and now…

‘History repeats itself’, huh?

So this is it?

You want me to see how the Swarm will batter humanity down this time around?

You want me to see how overwhelmingly powerful you bugs are?

You want to… break my spirit?

… For the second time today, Zora was out of words.

His hands trembled around his staff as the inevitable image of the black tide poured in—swallowing the capital, then the empire, then the entire continent until the world became one long, gnawing hunger.

The thought came uninvited. Unwanted. And still it landed, heavy as a stone dropped into his chest.

My voice can’t reach the southern earthen wall from here.

My spells can’t save the entire city.

How can we possibly win?

If humanity—

A sudden rifle shot exploded next to him, making him and everyone around him flinch.

Enki furrowed his brows slightly, looking slightly annoyed as he lowered his rifle.

“I missed,” he said plainly. “I need to get closer to get a better shot on Regalia.”

Then he crouched, picked up a few stone shards that he made with his initial crash-landing, and stuffed them inside his bolt-action rifle. He loaded. He chambered. He lifted his rifle and aimed down the wall again, firing seven shots in a single second to nail seven Giant-Class ants below from butchering a family cornered in an alley.

The boy didn’t slow. Didn’t pose. Didn’t even breathe differently.

“Reinforce the city,” he said. “But even if none of you do anything, I will kill Regalia.”

But before he could step off the edge and fall into the city wreathed in flames, someone else found their voice. A Royal Capital Guard Zora didn’t even know the name of.

“How?” the soldier whispered. “How can you possibly beat someone with an aura like… like that? She’s already inside the walls. What… what use is there in fighting until we die?”

“... I made a promise to someone that I would destroy the Swarm,” Enki replied. “I will fight until I win.”

Then he stepped off the wall, and a wormhole opened beneath him. He dropped through it before reappearing lower—much lower—near the street’s edge, spearing his bayonet through the head of a Giant-Class ant climbing a building with civilians screaming inside it.

The ant convulsed. Enki ripped the bayonet free and vanished through another wormhole, reappearing, shooting, stabbing, slashing, and slaughtering his way from bug to bug on his way to the south. He wasn’t just beelining for Reglia, either. He went out of his way to warp to every bug he could see, butchering as many as he could before leaving the rest to the soldiers on the ground.

And everyone on the ramparts—Royal Capital Guards and Reunification Army alike—just watched him go, stunned into silence by a boy who seemed to have no room in his body for fear.

Zora watched, too, as the boy carved through what he’d started to believe was humanity’s inevitable fate—-and then he bit his tongue hard enough to taste blood.

The pain grounded him in the heat of it.

Decima wanted to break his spirit. She wanted him to watch the city burn, to see him tremble, to return him to the shy, fearful boy he’d been back when he first arrived at Amadeus Academy—but now he was furious with himself.

The Worm Mage was but a mere boy, and all of them were doing nothing but watching?

Did Zora not march down to the Attini Empire to challenge humanity’s inevitable fate?

Did he truly believe, for even a single second, that humanity was fighting an impossible war?

… ‘As chains become an accessory, submission becomes second nature, and a child’s posture, their nature, and even their fate begins to take the form of a ‘prisoner’.’

Isn’t that right, Emilia?

If I believe we are prisoners, then we shall be prisoners, but if I don’t…

His fingers tightened around his staff once more.

You’re wrong, Decima.

You may be making my past repeat on a grander scale, but you’ve forgotten one very important detail about my past.

He’d won back in Amadeus Academy.

And beside him, Kita Salaqa began to rise.
