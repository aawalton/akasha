Ryland and Xyke headed through the Wound Corridor opened through time. The act had given Ryland a certain idea, which he might need to execute once he reached his destination. For now, he had to focus on actually getting there. Which proved a minor challenge due to Xyke’s disgust.

“I do not understand the comic evilness of the Order,” Xyke said. “Really? Wound Corridor? Why would they ever come up with a spell with a name like that?”

Ryland pursed his lips and decided against commenting. “Yes… I wonder why.”

“It appears awful too! Look at all this, Ryland. They even made it look like a wound too. It’s as if I’ve been turned into a tiny microbe who’s probing into a sore on someone’s backside.”

Ryland wished Xyke would stop with the abominable comparisons, but if bearing them was the price to pay for getting her through it all, then he would suffer in silence. Thankfully, the Wound Corridors never tended to be long. The Order had places to be and chaos to cause, after all.

Within moments, they emerged from the Wound Corridor into a golden demesne that could be nothing but the Overrealm.

“I sense no Great Gods coming to assault us,” Xyke said. “I’m almost disappointed. They’re happy to trap you and launch endless attacks, yet when I arrive, all I get is silence? This insult won’t go unanswered, mark my words, Ryland.”

“I shudder at the prospect,” he said.

If nothing else, Xyke was good practice for regaining his proficiency in sarcasm.

She was right, however. There was no sign of any Great Gods. Were they really in the Overrealm as Ryland had first assumed?

Of course, like all Planes of Existence, the Overrealm had its own foibles and specific locales that were separate from another and so on. It had its own Realms too, most of which Ryland had never been able to map out to anywhere near the same degree as he had with the cosmos he normally inhabited.

Their current location wasn’t that different from the Transient Realm where the Ephemeral Colossus had fallen. Twisted mountains growing from the sky, pits so dark that it was impossible to say where they led to, and trees growing from points floating in mid-air like giant balls of hair.

If they hadn’t travelled through the Wound Corridor, Ryland could easily have believed that they somehow ended up back where they had started.

That, and the fact that instead of brimming with the gold and red essence of the Colossus, this area was suffused with the sparkling aura of night. A miasma of the dusk had blanketed the whole region in purplish and bluish light, twinkles glinting through them like stars.

“I can read the Glyphs here,” Xyke said. “And the vast majority of them speak one thing.”

“Which is?”

“Tampering.”

Well, that made sense. They knew the Order had come here, and it was likely they had left their mark in this section of the Overrealm. Or pseudo-Overrealm. Because the longer this went on, the more Ryland was certain they weren’t actually in the fully-fledged Plane that was inhabited by the Great Gods.

“Let’s look around and investigate for a bit,” Ryland said. “Considering how closely this region resembles the Overrealm, we may have stumbled across a backdoor of sorts that the Order uses to get in and out. Although, it might just be the abundance of divine energy that has me fooled…”

“Which would make that enormous ritual of yours entirely pointless, wouldn’t it?” Xyke asked.

Ryland frowned. “That is true…”

They began looking around. Xyke was manipulating Glyphs of Time to reveal what had happened here, and Ryland was casting [Rewind] for the same purpose. A few things became evident before long.

The Order had indeed come here, and they had brought a lot of the essence they had taken from the Ephemeral Colossus. It also seemed they had been attempting to coalesce all that essence together to form… something that neither Ryland nor Xyke was able to gain a proper grasp on.

There was one other bit of weirdness, and that was where the Order had gone. They had somehow disappeared after a massive burst of magic, one that made both Incarnations scratch their heads.

“I can’t tell what spell that was,” Xyke said. “Because I saw no Glyphs before that blast occurred.”

Ryland nodded. The only thing they had seen was that the coalescing essence had grown completely unstable.

But if there had been a magical explosion that had wiped out the Order mages, why were there no remains of them strewn about for Ryland and Xyke to find? Where were the physical clues about what had happened? Even looking back in time wasn’t revealing it.

“I have an idea,” Ryland said. He explained what had come to him while they had been travelling through the Wound Corridor. “With time, we can redo practically anything that occurred within a given region. I now believe I can redo who occurred here as well.”

Xyke frowned. It made her draconic visage look quite ferocious. “Are you saying you wish to revive the dead?”

“Not exactly. Not when life and death are not fully applicable concepts in the area I want to apply my sorcery.”

“That’s just legalese, Ryland. Who or what do you want to bring back?”

“The Incarnation. You don’t want a fellow to keep suffering, do you?”

“The Ephemeral Colossus?” Xyke was still frowning, but it had changed from disapproval to begrudging curiosity. “How in the world are you going to manage something like—” Her eyes widened. “Are you going to recreate the coalescing? Right here? Stabilize it somehow and then return it to the new body for the Colossus you created before we came here?”

“I stand in awe of your genius, Xyke,” Ryland said.

“Fine. I can help. But on one condition.”

“I didn’t ask for your help in this particular matter.”

She ignored him and went on anyway, like her participation had already been decided. “You must let me at least sample some of the essence. Even if I can’t have it, I must see just what sort of Conceptual Glyph represents it.”

Ryland sighed. Typical Xyke, living and breathing Glyphs like little else mattered in the entire universe.

He promptly agreed and started working on his little project. While they had come here with the twin goals of escaping the grasp of the Great Gods and also finding out what had happened to the mages from the Flourinus Order, Ryland was now consumed by his new objective. Though, it did leave a gaping unanswered question.

If this wasn’t the Overrealm as he was quickly becoming sure of, then what exactly was this place? A recently-constructed pocket Realm made with divine energy?

Ryland focused on his task. “[Shrine of Divinity].”

The incantation created a tiny dot on Ryland’s outstretched palm, which immediately began drawing in all the local divine energy. There was no one or nothing else using it. He couldn’t sense any tethers or traps linked to the divine energy either.

Even Xyke was certain that the divine energy just… existed here, for some reason.

They had to wait for a few minutes until enough of the divine energy had been gathered. There was a lot to absorb, and Ryland waited patiently until he believed he had enough. Xyke was looking so closely, he could count the blood vessels in her draconic eyes.

When the gathering was done, Ryland cast [Rewind] once more. Now, they just needed to wait until time returned to the state he needed it to go.

“Why did you gather the divine energy before casting [Rewind]?” Xyke asked.

“Because I intend to temporarily evolve my Soul Glyph of Time with what I intend to act on next.”

“Temporarily? Not permanently? I suspect you’ll gain a Soul Glyph of—”

“Must you ruin the surprise for me?”

Xyke considered with unblinking eyes. Then she laughed. “I will leave you alone, then. I’ve been observing the local Glyphs and they tell a tale you might want to hear once you’re done here.”

Ryland couldn’t lie, that intrigued him a lot. “What sort of tale?”

“One that explains just what our Order mages might have been doing here.”

Acrypha was no fool. She was well aware that trusting the Flourinus Order was a highly risky move at best, even if they had guided her into gaining new powers. Nothing in this world was free. Not even in the Plane of the Great Gods, if that was where she really had been transported to.

But she couldn’t lie. The new spells she was learning certainly felt powerful.

“I don’t recognize these corridors,” she said. She had been walking for a long time now. For hours. For days. For weeks. She couldn’t tell. The only thing that kept her going was the presence of her two guardians. “[Lifegiving Imbuement].”

“You will not recognize any of them,” the voice of the Order mage said. “These halls have hardly been explored by us, much less by a little girl who has never been here before.”

The woman’s words were biting. Acrypha was growing to detest her voice.

Her newest spell somehow used the Soul Glyph of Legacy to power itself, even though she knew it shouldn’t have been a spell that her parents had known. Because this was a spell that used Living Mana to power itself.

The thought kind of sickened her. All that time trapped inside the Demiurge’s artificial moon had given her a perspective on Living Mana that made her disinclined to let it come to harm in any way.

And using it to drive her spells had to count as harm.

After all, her new sorceries literally used it as fuel just like regular spells used mana to power themselves.

But what choice did Acrypha have? Complying with the Order seemed to be the only way she could get herself out from whatever imprisonment she had been subjected to, even if it was a deplorable wish.

A part of her wondered what Ryland was up to. What Viren was doing. What Arcoryx Academy was going through. But relying on them was going to get her nowhere. Her Restorer parents had taught her that self-dependence was non-negotiable for survival. Something she was understanding more and more nowadays.

As such, Acrypha had followed the Order. They had plopped her into strange ruins that didn’t even seem to properly exist most of the time. Why they needed her or the Living Mana she controlled hadn’t been explained, but it was clear she wasn’t getting out on her own.

Not unless she did as the Order asked. And right now, they were asking her to map out the whole ruins herself. Sort of.

The new spell, [Lifegiving Imbuement], was eating away at the walls before her. It functioned by injecting Living Mana into whatever she targeted, then converting that substance to Living Mana, which she could then reabsorb.

This process had allowed Acrypha to “eat” through the chamber walls, before she had found the tunnel. She had come across blockages that she had then used the same principles to break down.

“There’s a door up ahead,” Acrypha said. “[Lifegiving Imbuement].”

There were no handles or locks or any Glyphtech that she recognized. In other words, there was no way inside.

But that was fine. Her new spell ate through the door just like it consumed everything else.

And then she was through.

“What… is this place…?” Acrypha asked. She frowned. “And why do you keep evading the question regarding why you can’t explore it yourself?”

“Go on,” the voice said. That hated voice just ignored everything she had asked. “See for yourself. Find everything you seek.”

The Living Mana within and without Acrypha started to grow agitated as soon as she stepped into the next chamber. She stared around, trying to understand what she was seeing. There was too much going on here. Which was an odd thing to say for a room that was almost entirely empty.

Except for the walls. Those weren’t empty at all.

The closer Acrypha got, the more it felt like the walls were alive. It wasn’t merely a mural or some other sort of recording. This was an impression of truth itself. A brand of history.

Acrypha walked along the walls’ length, marvelling at the lifelike images and carvings that seemed to pop out with startling clarity. The first one showed worlds being destroyed. Realms in utter chaos. Lives on an unimaginable scale being torn down utterly. She could hardly fathom the scale of destruction she was being shown.

In one case, a Realm was being eaten by some titanic monstrosity. In another, some sort of strange miasma or plague had blanketed another world fully. Scenes of destruction followed one another in an endless parade of death.

“What am I supposed to understand from all this?” she asked. Her words echoed in the room devoid of anyone else, but she didn’t stop talking. “What am I supposed to find and do here?”

Even as she spoke, though, Acrypha knew that she wasn’t the main entity who needed to be present here.

That honour went to the Living Mana.

It was swiping and swarming over the illustrations on the walls, almost like it was trying to mould itself to them like a living cast. Acrypha had never really learned to control it, but there was another new spell she could wield. [Will of Authority] had the Living Mana obeying her once more, where she magnified the presence of her own life with mana.

But it got much harder to control, even with the new spell, when Acrypha reached a shift in the images. Now, she was seeing the causes for all that destruction represented as twisted living creatures. As Calamities, she realized after a moment.

“You’re living history,” the voice said again. “You’re witnessing a truth long buried. A record that, while most might forget, the Great Gods will never allow to pass away.”

Acrypha was having a terrible time holding the Living Mana back as it tried to assume the forms of the various Calamities. Here, a creature with a gigantic eye that beheld everything in devastating violence. There, a thorn-wreathed monstrosity was rising higher than a mountain.

She didn’t stick long with those. There were more parts of the wall left, and the Living Mana was surprisingly attracted to the Calamities. It was an odd feeling. Acrypha was getting used to thinking of the Living Mana in a positive light, so for it to get close to the Calamities, who were anything but positive, just stung.

The next few sets of images were strange and harrowing in their own way. A figure rose to prominence. One who radiated power and awe. So much so that the Calamities started gathering closer to this person, to this man as far as she could tell.

Oddly, it almost felt like the Calamities were the man’s pets. They were all following his commands, acting as he saw fit.

Intriguingly, Acrypha noted that the destruction had stopped almost entirely.

It was like the man was taming the Calamities. He was keeping them secured away from where they could cause most harm. His power was so great that he was gathering followers of all sorts too. People from all across the Realms, beings who were terribly impressed by everything he was capable of. Even Acrypha was awed. Much like how she often felt about Ryland.

Her breath stuttered. No, it couldn’t be. This could in no way be Ryland, could it?

The realization brought with it a renewed resurgence from the Living Mana. It wasn’t just her breaths coming in short supply. Her control over the Living Mana was quickly disappearing too. Just like the depicted Calamities, they were gathering at the image of the man.

Like they were Calamity-cursed pets as well.

But there was a problem. There was a consequence of the tight control over the Calamities the man was inflicting, especially after all this time. An issue that almost no one else saw from their mortal perspectives, a problem that Acrypha couldn’t fully understand aside from a vague feeling that something was missing.

“Yes…” the voice said. She was subdued. For the first time, it was starting to feel as though the voice was awed too. “This is what we remembered. This is what happened. But even we were not aware of this…”

Acrypha ended up going back just to be sure of what she was seeing. Right. There it was. Something—a lot of somethings—were emerging from the destruction, creating a strange but stable flow at the regularity of the devastation taking place. All of which had slowly ceased after the Calamities were subdued.

And it remained that way until the disruption occurred. Until the followers of the strange man broke away from him, forcing him to relinquish control over those he had corralled.

“It’s not just you,” Acrypha said as she realized what was going on. “The Great Gods…”

“They hate your precious master too,” the voice said. “And now you know exactly why. This world is made of souls. Of manasouls given form. And Marionys Ryland vastly reduced the flow by stopping the devastation wrought by the Calamities. But now—”

The voice cut off abruptly. It was so sudden, Acrypha found herself blinking and looking around, as if that would somehow reveal what happened. Instead, she just heard a screech through that strange mental connection.

“He’s there already?” the woman’s voice shrieked. “Then what are you waiting for? Send in the Incarnation. Yes! The Incarnation of Growth, who else?”
