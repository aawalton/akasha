Lain stayed gazing at her for a few more seconds before following Crow and the others.

The group continued down the seemingly endless corridor, dimly lit by flickering candles. Farther along, the floor was in worse condition. Amy had to watch for cracks in the stone, and moss filled the gaps between slabs that looked much older than the ones they'd walked over before.

They were getting close.

She gripped her satchel strap. Libris was in there with a broken cover, and her memories would be waiting ahead. She couldn't stop thinking about either.

The others were talking. Arguing, maybe? She heard their voices without managing to follow a word.

Amy clenched her fist and bit her lip as hard as she could, forcing clarity into her mind. Right now, it wasn’t the moment to get distracted.

"How much farther?" Iris asked.

"Not much, I think," Crow replied, his eyes narrowing. "I can feel something..."

"I feel it too," Lyra said, pausing mid-step.

Ash's brow furrowed while he stared at them then glanced around, confused. "Feel what? I don't feel anything."

"You aren't as sensitive to magic as us, that's probably why," Lyra explained as she rubbed her arms. "Like... a pressure. In the air. It's getting stronger the further we go."

Amy felt it too—a subtle but growing tension. The hair on her arms stood on end, and a faint buzzing sensation prickled at the back of her neck.

"Is it the temperature? Because I'm pretty sure it's getting colder," Ash observed, wrapping his arms around himself. His breath had become visible, small puffs of white mist.

"Everyone, stay close," Crow instructed as they rounded a curve in the passage.

The walls curved away from them until they were standing in a round stone chamber. An obsidian arch stood in the middle, carved with red symbols. Amy couldn't read them, or see anything through the opening except darkness.

Crow went closer to examine the writing. "It's written in Old Vernacular… The entrance to the Eastern Wing."

"That doesn't look like any wing I've ever seen," Ash muttered, eyeing the darkness beyond with suspicion. "Looks more like a gate to hell."

"It's not," Zayd said quietly. "I’d recognize it anywhere; it's a threshold."

"A threshold to what?" Iris demanded.

"To wherever the Eastern Wing currently exists," Zayd responded. "The Vernal Alignment…" he said after a pause. "The headmistress mentioned it. It only happens once in seventy-seven years."

"Okay," Iris said. "What does that mean for us?"

"It means the barriers between realities are thinner. Even time can change during the Alignment. It need not behave as you expect."

Amy looked at the arch again. Yeah. This was it. In the manga, they had entered through a different place but still the same gate.

I'm not ready.

She didn't feel ready, not in the slightest.

Sure, it's not like the moment she entered she would immediately have to confront her memories; for that it would take a few days. But the previous days weren't any better, even if it all was an illusion…

Crow turned to face the group, his expression turning dark. "I think..." He paused, gathering his thoughts. "I think that what lies beyond isn't just a physical place... It's a nightmare."

Lyra gasped, and Ash stood up straighter. Iris actually stepped back from the arch. Amy stayed where she was, watching them realize what they were about to enter.

"A nightmare?" Lyra asked quietly.

"Yes, I'm almost confident," Crow confirmed, probably through his third ability. "But it's different in nature from the usual. I believe it's the kind that formed naturally, over time."

"Hold on." Ash turned toward Crow. "And the teachers knew? They knew there was a nightmare here and just told us to stay out of that wing?"

"The entrance is here," Zayd said. "The nightmare is in a separate space, even where it overlaps with ours. You can't simply walk into it whenever you please."

"You need the right conditions," Crow added. "Like tonight. The Alignment makes the barrier thin enough to cross."

Iris pushed her hair back. "Why the fuck would you put a school anywhere near that?"

Silence followed Iris' words as everyone stared into the archway. They all knew there was no going back now, but nobody dared to take the first step forward. They just stood there, staring into the darkness.

It was Lain who finally broke the silence. "...we should go through... together," she suggested. "...less chance to get separated..."

Crow hesitated for a second before nodding in agreement. "Link hands," he instructed. "Whatever happens, don't let go."

They formed a chain—Crow at the far right side, followed by Lain, then Lyra, Iris, Ash, Zayd, and finally Amy at the far left. Amy felt Zayd's hand, cool and dry, grip hers firmly. With her other hand, she unnecessarily checked that Libris was secure in her satchel.

Crow looked down the line at them. "Anyone want to turn back? This is your last chance."

They shook their heads, though no one had much to say about it.

"Alright. Together."

They stepped through.

Amy's next step landed in snow. She hadn't seen the room disappear, or the trees appear; they were just there, dead and bare, stretching out across a landscape of white. The chamber had been cold, but it had nothing on this place.

"Holy shit," Ash said, shivering beside them.

Nothing answered. Amy couldn't even hear anything moving among the trees.

She looked up. The sky was gray, with no sun to tell her where they were, or whether it was even daytime. No moon or stars either. There were darker shapes in the distance, but she couldn't tell if they were buildings or mountains.

Despite the absolute cold of the environment, Amy did not feel it much. Something about her clothes protected her.

She winced at the realization. Just how much had Libris been helping her…?

"Everyone alright?" Crow asked.

They'd let go of each other's hands, but nobody had moved much farther than that.

"No," Ash said. His breath came out white, and he couldn't seem to stop shaking. "I'm fucking freezing."

Zayd looked around, narrowing his eyes. "It may be winter here permanently. That seems to be how the nightmare has formed. Interesting."

"Yes, very interesting," Iris said through chattering teeth. "Can you tell us something that'll help?"

Zayd ignored her, turning to Crow instead. "So what now?"

Crow unclasped his cloak and offered it to Lyra, who accepted it gratefully. He seemed to be the least affected by the cold after Amy and Lain.

"We need to find shelter," he stated, his dark eyes narrowed against the dim light. "And the Library. That's our objective; that's why we're here."

"Which way?" Ash asked, gesturing to the seemingly identical terrain stretching in all directions. "There's nothing but snow and dead trees as far as I can see."

Crow reached into his pocket and withdrew a small piece of paper– the letter left by his father. Then he closed his eyes for a few seconds, staying completely still.

"This way," he said finally, pointing to what appeared to be a slightly denser cluster of trees in the distance. "The magical density is stronger that way."

"Do you have a better suggestion?" Crow countered.

Iris fell silent, glancing around at the desolate landscape again before grinning. "How about going to the right?"

"Right?" Crow asked, arching an eyebrow. "Why?"

"Dunno, was just suggesting since you asked."

"..."

"I can't tell," Crow admitted. "Distance feels... wrong here. But we should move. Standing still won't get us anywhere. Let's go."

Without wasting any more time, they began walking. The snow crunching beneath their feet was the only sound in the eerie silence. Amy stayed at the back of the group, her hand occasionally brushing against her satchel where Libris rested.

There were weapons in the snow. Amy saw the broken end of a sword sticking out near a shield, arrows caught in the ice, and pieces of armor scattered between the trees. A helmet here, a gauntlet farther off. Nothing that looked usable.

The snow was blackened in places where magic must have struck. This was about what she'd imagined a battlefield would look like afterward, except... where were the people? There weren't any bodies. She couldn't even find dried blood.

"What happened here?" Lyra asked under her breath.

Crow crouched and cleared the snow from another sword. About half the blade was still attached to the hilt; the rest had broken off.

"There was a battle," he said. "A long time ago. The signs suggest it's been here for years, perhaps decades… But where are the bodies—or more like the bones?"

"Maybe they have been moved, who knows?" Zayd suggested.

Iris nodded emphatically. "Finally, you have a great idea for once. This place is giving me serious 'everyone dies horribly' vibes."

They went on through more dead trees. Amy kept seeing scraps of cloth in the snow, more signs of people who'd come this way and apparently hadn't made it much farther. Otherwise, the place looked exactly the same. She couldn't hear anything beyond their own steps.

The only thing changing was the sky, which gradually became a little more visible, slowly revealing the thing behind the expanse of clouds. Amy was the only one to notice, though, as the others were more preoccupied with their situation in search of danger they would not find.

Hours seemed to pass, though without the sun, time was impossible to measure. Amy's legs hurt. The clothes Libris had given her were helping, but after this much walking she could feel the cold through them, and her nose had started to sting.

[Would you like to talk now?]
