The messenger doesn't need to take me far – only to the main warehouse in the same district as the healing rooms and the command building. Inside, the scene is distinctly reminiscent of the earlier attack in the marketplace. People, many of them bearing bleeding bite or scratch wounds, are hiding or running. Overturned boxes and goods littering the ground are an even more obvious sign of the panic that has only just started to ebb.

This time, the attackers are being held down by civilians who have intervened rather than by guards. One attacker has a particularly massive man lying on top of him, while the other is being pinned down by five more reasonably-sized people.

I narrow my eyes. Both figures on the floor are far slighter than those holding them, yet the people trying to keep them down are clearly struggling to do so. Lack of concern for their own wellbeing like that of the earlier woman – I feel a flicker of guilt that I don’t remember her name – or something else? That this seems to be the same thing is obvious – when one of the attackers turns his head my way as he thrashes to get free, I see the same glazed look in his eyes and feral snarl twisting his lips.

Only a moment after I arrive, I hear the tramp of feet outside and Layton bursts in at the head of a contingent of guards. He's clearly trying to keep his composure, but I can see from his eyes that he's shaken by a second attack in such a short span of time.

"Another one?" he demands, striding over to me. "What happened?"

"That's my question," I reply tersely. "I’ve only just arrived."

Layton's eyes take in the scene, lingering on the two figures pinned to the ground.

"This is exceedingly troubling," he murmurs quietly. "Two attackers, both attacking in the same way as the other, and both so unexpected. Please tell me you have some idea of what's causing this."

I hesitate.

"We're still exploring theories," I say finally, not sure that mentioning parasites is wise when we're not even certain that's the cause. Though given the eerie similarities and the fact that two more people broke down at the same time, it seems increasingly unlikely that this is simply a natural psychotic episode.

It is possible it’s a toxic buildup – we’re mostly eating the same food, so if one person is badly affected, it’s possible that others will be too. But I didn’t notice any signs of it in the first attacker’s body, nor did Healer Thessalanor raise it as a possibility. I didn’t ask, but I have to conclude that he would have spoken about it if he’d noticed.

It’s also still possible that it’s a mutation caused by the manastorm, though that seems increasingly unlikely – given what I know about manastorms, I doubt it would produce the exact same mutation in three separate people with no other mutations evident. If it’s not the parasite, then we’re back to square one when it comes to figuring out the cause of the threat.

"You need to get to the bottom of this, Markus," Layton warns, concern making him sharp. "We have enough dangers outside. If people start looking at each other suspiciously, wondering who's going to be next to snap, our settlement is doomed."

"I know," I tell him tensely. "I'm doing what I can."

And I will – even if it means crossing a line I never wanted to cross. Though as I glance at one of the two attackers here – only one of whom has a silver collar – there are other considerations that will come into play this time.

I turn to Layton's guards.

"Four of you get both attackers to the healing rooms. I want them restrained and ready for examination." Only after I speak do I realise how sharp my own voice is – stress and worry making me less polite than normal. The guards bow their heads and, after a quick glance at Layton who nods tersely, four break away to follow my orders.

Turning back to Layton, I do my best to soften my tone, aware that I just went over his head and commanded his people.

"We need to start patrolling the streets. This has now happened to three separate people. For all we know, it will happen to more – and we need guards in place to respond when it does."

Layton nods again, a little less tense this time, and turns to face the rest of the guards he brought with him.

While he gives out orders, I move through the remaining civilians, gathering eyewitness accounts. I don't record them this time, but it's enough to confirm what I suspected – the same pattern as before, namely, no pattern. No obvious triggers, no similarities with the previous attack except for the complete absence of anything that should have set them off.

In one case, the man had been mid-conversation when his face suddenly twisted and he went feral. The person he'd been speaking with has been badly injured and now has one of the healers Thessalanor sent bent over him. The conversation had been amicable as far as everyone else around is concerned. Even when I interrupt the healing – to the glare of the healer concerned – the person the attacker had been speaking with confirms that they were friendly acquaintances, sharing relief about the end of the manastorm at the time.

I might have thought that conversation itself was a trigger, except that the other attacker this time wasn’t speaking to anyone at the time. In fact, he had simply been stacking chunks of roughly-hewn wood on a rack.

Wondering whether that might have been a commonality – perhaps the wood contains toxins that can be absorbed and cause this psychotic break – I ask more pointed questions about the first attacker. But that’s a dead end too – he works with the wool sheared from the sheep-like beasts brought from Moriax. It’s possible he handled local wood when setting a fire or something, but I would have thought we’d have seen more wide-spread effects if so.

There’s also something familiar about the face of one of the attackers. I’m wracking my brains, trying to chase that hint of familiarity, when my attention is drawn once more to the sound of pounding feet.

Another messenger rushes into the warehouse – this one in armour – and heads straight for Layton. Alerted by the fear on his face, I quickly move close enough to catch the tail end of the exchange.

"–don't know how long we can hold," the messenger says, keeping his voice low enough not to carry.

Layton's face takes on that tense blankness I've learned to recognise as barely-contained terror.

"What is it?" I ask.

The messenger glances at me, then bows quickly.

"Lord Markus." He doesn’t start speaking immediately, looking to Layton with hesitation plain on his features. Then, at Layton's impatient gesture, "A mass of beasts is attacking the wall, my lord." He takes my stunned silence as a cue to continue. "They don't seem to feel pain or fear – they just throw themselves at the wall and keep fighting until they're dead. For now we're holding, but the wall enchantments are already showing strain from the sheer volume of attacks. I don't know how long we can hold."

I blink hard, trying to force my thoughts back into order.

“Excuse me for a moment.”

Raising a Seal around myself to prevent any sounds from escaping, I turn away from the others and brace both hands against the wall.

For several long seconds I simply stand there, breathing hard, fighting down the surge of panic that threatens to overwhelm me.

As if the sickness, low food stocks, and feral attacks weren’t enough, now we have a siege too? And I can’t help asking myself an awful question: can we survive this?

I force myself to inhale slowly, then exhale.

One thing at a time. We will get through – even with this.

Lowering the first Seal, I raise another around Layton, the messenger, and myself. We don't want this news spreading, not right now when everyone is already on edge as a result of these attacks.

“How many of them?” I ask, sharper than I would like.

“It’s hard to count,” the guard admits, “but at least a hundred, if not more.”

"What kind of beasts?"

"All kinds. Small, medium, large. Some I recognise from creatures that have been brought in for eating. Some are completely new to me."

"Tiers?"

"Mostly Tier ones, my lord, but quite a few Tier twos – some of them very large. Especially the ones like the beast that came through the settlement a few weeks back. Those are the ones having the biggest impact on the wall and are the hardest to kill."

I take in a long, slow breath again. The timing cannot be a coincidence. We start facing internal attacks at the same time as a mass assault from outside. Yet how can they be connected?

"Right," I say, my mind racing. “Right.” Then, realising I've repeated myself, I focus and reach out through the Alliance Bond. Valence, where are your people? Still in the settlement, or out on scouting trips?

Mostly still in the settlement, Valence replies, his tone cautious. Given the dangers you reported, I thought only the most capable should venture out.

Good. Have you been informed of the situation at the wall?

No, he answers, his tone curious.

Or the recent attacks inside the settlement?

No, he repeats, sounding more agitated.

Attacks? Elarion breaks in. There have been more?

Two, technically, I inform him grimly, though they happened simultaneously. Where are you, Elarion?

Outside with the farmers, as we agreed, he answers immediately.

I curse under my breath. Such bad timing all round. Coincidence? Or designed to be such?

For those who don't know, I say across the Alliance Bond, I say grimly, I've just received a report that a large group of beasts of various Tiers is currently attacking our gates – seemingly without any concern for their own survival. Simultaneously, we're dealing with attacks inside the settlement that may or may not be connected. These consist of members of our population abruptly going feral and attacking anyone around them with tooth and nail – literally.

I sense both Elarion and Valence absorbing the news with grim unease.

We can’t see the beasts from our position, Elarion contributes. We can round the hill and attack them from behind if that would be the best strategy.

How many dedicated fighters do you have with you?

He’s silent for a moment, then returns. Myself, two Plant Mages who are competent in combat, three Farmer Classers who are a dab hand with a pitchfork, and three guards we brought with us to watch the forest while we worked.

I hum. It’s not as bad as I thought. Though that does depend on how many beasts there are.

Investigate on your end. See if it’s possible for you to get through to the gates – returning to the settlement is the priority, not killing beasts. I don’t have the evidence to prove it, but something is telling me this is all linked to what we were discussing earlier about the wells. Having you here to carry out the investigation would be good – however, it’s not worth your life, nor the lives of your people. If you have to wait until we’ve seen off this beast wave, so be it.

I’m doing my best to sound more confident than I am. I haven’t yet seen this wave of beasts, but I trust the guard’s report.

I'll see what I can do, Elarion promises.

What do you want me to do? Valence asks, his tone worried.

I need you to warn your people in the forest that we're under siege, and if possible, send them to find out what's causing this wave of beasts. Those currently in the settlement should be deployed to patrol the streets and watch for further signs of anyone going feral. That should free Layton's guards to man the walls. All mages capable of either combat or wall reinforcement should be diverted to doing so immediately. We cannot let these creatures get through to our vulnerable population.

I’m on it, Valence replies with determination, and I sense his attention already turning from the Bond.

What are you going to do, Markus? Layton asks, using the Bond rather than speaking aloud even though we're standing right next to each other.

Unless you need me on the wall, I’m going to do my best to get to the bottom of this, I reply – aware it's exactly what I said after the last attack, but the urgency has only multiplied. Every person we have to deploy dealing with internal attacks is one fewer person holding the walls.

Good luck, Valence says solemnly.

Thank you, I reply. I'll need it.
