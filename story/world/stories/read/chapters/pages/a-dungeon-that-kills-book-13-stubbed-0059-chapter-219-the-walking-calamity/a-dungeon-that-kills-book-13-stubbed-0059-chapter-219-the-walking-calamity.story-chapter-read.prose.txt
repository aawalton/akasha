“It’s heading for the chasm on the far side of the desert?” Viktor asked with a frown.

[Yes, Master.]

That confirmed it, then. That heap of burning stone striding across the desert at the moment was not some mindless construct lashing out with the last remnants of its borrowed divinity at anything unlucky enough to fall within its reach. No, there was an actual mind steering it. Whether that mind belonged to Xander or something else entirely was a question he could leave for later, however. For now, one fact mattered above all else: this walking calamity was not a force of nature to be waited out, but an intelligent enemy that could think and plan and choose.

Which led to the next question: why the chasm? The titan was tall enough to see clean over the perimeter wall of the mortuary complex and spot that opening from a distance, so there was nothing particularly mysterious about how it had found the thing in the first place. But finding a landmark and deciding to march toward it were two completely different matters. Was it simply curiosity, or had it somehow figured out the chasm was the way down to the third floor?

None of those was the most pressing question right now, though.

“Do you think it can get through?”

[If we judge by size alone, then no, Master. As big as that tunnel is, the titan is much bigger. However, if it tries to force its way through regardless, if it tries to widen the opening by sheer brute strength...]

Viktor arched a brow. “Is that even possible?”

[Under normal circumstances, no. But we are not dealing with a normal opponent, Master.]

“Fair point,” Viktor said with a nod.

Whenever people debated if something was possible or not, the answer almost always hinged on whether the one attempting the feat possessed the raw power required to make it happen. And what he was dealing with was some sort of avatar of a god. Trying to draw any conclusion about what it could or couldn’t accomplish would just be premature.

[I could try to seal the chasm as it forces its way through, though I have to confess I have no way of knowing how successful such an attempt would be.]

“Or you can just let it pass through unchallenged.”

[Why, Master?]

Viktor chuckled. “Because even if it makes it to the third floor, then what? The Core Room we’re in is still two floors below that, behind two doors that are smaller than even its little finger. Which means the thing would run into the exact same problem it’s having right now, just one floor further down the line. More importantly, the third floor is our water realm, isn’t it? And what is a better place to fight a walking mountain of fire than somewhere water is abundant on every side?”

[Yes, you are right.]

Now then, who could he actually throw at the thing? The forces in the great tomb were mostly intact, thankfully enough, since Celeste had managed to teleport them away just in time. So he still had Templars, tomb guards, spider-women, troglodytes, a fine collection of creatures that could bite and stab and strangle. Not a single one of them could deal meaningful damage to a colossal heap of burning rocks, though. And the same thing could be said for many of the minions the third floor had to offer, like the mermen or the froglings. Sending them to engage the titan in melee combat was not a battle plan; it was a cremation with extra steps.

No, the only ones who had anything useful to contribute were the mermaids and the Acolytes of the Deep, who could at least use their spells to hurl water at the wretched thing from a safe distance.

And speaking of spells, it occurred to Viktor that he might as well send the surviving undead mages down there too. Even though earth and death magic weren’t exactly suited to the task at hand, having more options to choose from was never a bad idea. They could hang back out of the fire’s reach and serve as reserves, to be called upon if the situation ever demanded it.

[Master, there is one more problem.]

Viktor frowned. “Go on.”

[It is... about Cedric’s party.]

Ah, right. He had completely forgotten about them. The Daelin team was also on the third floor, going about their business with no idea what was bearing down on them from above.

“Where are they now? What are they doing?”

[They have finished the skirmish we arranged for them, Master. And now they are about to make their way up through the tunnel with the trophies they have collected, expecting nothing more than an uneventful climb back to the second floor.]

If they did so, they would walk directly into the path of that titan of fire, and the outcome of such an encounter hardly required much imagination to predict.

Viktor mulled it over for a moment, then turned to Galatea.

“You go to them. Stay invisible and have a quiet word with Renee. Let her know what is coming.” Good thing that the girl had volunteered to join Cedric’s team, and she had already known Galatea before this. “Tell her to get the others moving. Away from the tunnel leading up to the second floor, because that’s where the titan is going to come through. And away from the stairs to the fourth as well, because that’s most likely where it’s headed. Then, have them wait it out at the edge of the water realm until the battle finishes running its course.”

He had no idea, though, how Renee was supposed to persuade the rest of the party to go along with it. But he now had bigger fires to put out, literally, so he would just let her handle her people as she saw fit. And if everything worked out the way he hoped, well, then she and the others were about to find themselves with front-row seats to a battle against an avatar of a god, which was rather more excitement than most people paid good money for, all things considered.

Closing his eyes, Viktor sent his vision to the second floor, and he found what he was looking for the instant he arrived. Not that spotting it took any great skill in the first place. Some things you just couldn’t miss even if you tried.

The titan was making its way across the empty half of the desert, and the ground itself seemed to recoil with every footfall. Each print the thing left behind was a crater of fused glass, the sand cooked smooth and dark and faintly gleaming, the raw heat radiating from that impossible body having turned ordinary grit into something else entirely in the span of a single step.

Before long—though it didn’t actually take long at all, given the sheer length of its stride—the titan reached the chasm. It slowed as it approached, tilting that great misshapen head of flame downward to study the black opening below. For a moment, it simply stood there at the very edge, fire dripping in slow ropes from its jaw and chin, each drop hissing into steam the instant it touched the sand.

Then, it knelt down.

The ground shuddered under the weight of that one simple motion. A tremor rippled through the desert, rattling the ruined bones of the mortuary complex behind it and opening fresh cracks across whatever walls still remained standing. The titan pressed one enormous hand, fingers wreathed in flame and tipped with claws of half-molten rock, flat against the lip of the chasm.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened. And then, the fire building along its arm began to change.

The glow shifted, first from its usual dull orange into something closer to a blinding white, and then further still, deepening into a searing blue that hurt to look at even though Viktor wasn’t doing so directly with his own eyes, sharp enough that he found himself trying to blink it away.

The heat radiating outward from that single hand seemed to warp the very air for a hundred paces in every direction, and where its claws dug into the stone lip of the chasm, the rock began to scream.

Yes, it screamed. There was simply no better word for it. A deep, groaning shriek that was dragged up out of the earth itself, as if the chasm were some living, suffering thing being pried open against its will.

Cracks raced outward from under the titan’s palm, spreading through solid stone as easily as ice splitting across a frozen lake. The stone at the edges went from glowing to blistering to sliding, great molten sheets of it sloughing down into the dark like wax pouring off a dying candle.

The titan pressed harder. The stone gave further.

Whole slabs, some easily the size of a house, sheared loose from the chasm’s crumbling walls and tumbled into the abyss, vanishing without so much as an echo. The titan kept pushing, kept pouring that unbearable heat directly into the rock as though it meant to unmake the barrier rather than merely breach it, melting out a path wide enough to accommodate its own colossal bulk instead of wasting effort trying to squeeze a mountain through what amounted to little more than a keyhole.

Within moments, the chasm yawned open into something vast and raw and still glowing at its ragged edges, a wound freshly carved and cauterized in the same breath, wide enough at last for a god’s avatar to descend through unhindered.

The titan rose back to its full and terrible height, fire cascading off its shoulders in slow, heavy sheets. It stood still, gazing down as though it was admiring its own handiwork, the molten doorway steaming and muttering in the dark below.

Then, without the faintest trace of hesitation, without so much as a backward glance, it stepped off the edge and dropped, and in doing so, began its descent to the water realm.
