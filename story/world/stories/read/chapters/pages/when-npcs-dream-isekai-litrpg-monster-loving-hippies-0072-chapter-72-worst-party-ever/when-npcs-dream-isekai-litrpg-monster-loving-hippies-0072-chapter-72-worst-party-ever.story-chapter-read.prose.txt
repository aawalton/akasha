I waited on the giant porch attached to the King’s bedroom in the palace, staring up at the sky, waiting for Anna.

The sky was an oppressive, empty blue. There was nothing up there. Not a cloud, not a bird.

I sighed, a long, rattling sound in my chest.

I didn’t want anything else. I wasn’t interested in politics or war today. I just wanted Anna back. Servants came and went, messengers scurried past with scrolls that were probably important, but I couldn’t bring myself to care.

I needed Anna. I was a different person—a different creature—without her. There was nothing for me here without her. After all, this wasn’t my home. Not even close. I had been kidnapped—twice, mind you.

The first time was from the Old World, taken by the System. That was horrible enough. I had a good life back home; I was engaged, mere weeks away from the ceremony. It broke me when the System took me. It took over a year, but I finally found friends, found a place I could call home in this silly, magical world.

But then I was kidnapped again. This time by the Bamboo.

A flock of mind-controlled giant birds had stormed our city, successfully distinguishing players from NPCs. With a release of spores from their feathers, they put us all to sleep.

We woke up to torture. Dry, impossibly dry air. We were hundreds of meters above a scorching desert, our bodies held firmly by bird talons. I was so dehydrated… I can’t forget that feeling. The thought that you would give anything for blood. For water.

But the worst part was seeing the birds around me surrender to the harsh air. I watched my friends drop to the hellish desert surface—sixty degrees Celsius, no less—and I knew they were down there being cooked alive, suffering through every agonizing second. The fear was worse than the thirst, I remember."

Then we arrived at the Bamboo Continent, and they brainwashed us with their spores. That part I can’t remember well; it’s all fuzzy, though I sometimes have nightmares about it.

And then came the hellish desert ride all over again. Even brainwashed, I remember crying, begging them not to send me. But the Bamboo didn’t care. They only cared about the mission.

I suffered again. We died again. More than half of us didn’t make the trip. It was horrific. I somehow survived, even though my bird gave out in the end and we crashed.

Anna… Anna was the first good thing that happened to me in a long time. I needed her.

Suddenly, a small white dot appeared in the clear blue sky. Then another, and another.

I smiled wide, baring my teeth. Finally.

I waited patiently for the giant birds to descend and drop my love onto the palace porch. As soon as her feet touched the stone, I flew to her—like, really fast—and hit her like a charging bull.

“Wow!” Anna laughed, stumbling back. “You almost knocked me off the porch!”

She hugged me tight and kissed my fluffy head. “I missed you too,” she whispered.

We stayed like that, hugging. I wrapped my tail around her, grounding myself. I could probably stay like this forever. But eventually, Anna separated us and carried me inside.

“You probably already heard,” Anna started, setting me down, “but my mission was a success. The gas mask factory is blown to pieces. It’ll send them back weeks, maybe months.”

“And since their only other defense is that mass training with pressure bubbles,” I added, “they’re basically sitting ducks. Those bubbles are useless against our sleep spore arrows."

I already knew the mission was a success, but I had been sick with worry about Anna. I had received some very confusing reports.

“Have you really been kidnapped by pirates?” I asked, tilting my head.

“By pirates accompanied by Geshem and Shooya, no less.” Anna grinned. “They somehow got themselves there totally by accident. It was hilarious. I actually had a great time with those two.”

“Geshem is one of the deadliest players out there,” I said, my tone serious. “Smart, too. If he’d figured you out, it would’ve been the end for you.”

“Good thing my acting skills were superb!” She smiled cheekily. “And he did discover me, at the very end when I called to give the factory coordinates to the strike team. He didn’t really have a chance against me, though. Shooya, too—she really tried her cute best to attack me.”

“It sounds like you weren’t careful at all,” I scolded.

“Probably not,” Anna smiled and stuck her tongue out. “Sorry.”

I glared at her, but only for a second.

“Oh, but look at you,” Anna said, running a hand through my fur. “It’s been days since we did any grooming. Have you used someone else to…?”

“What? No!” I protested, offended. “I would never!”

Anna just smiled and carried me to the dressing table. She placed me upon her lap and, ever so gently, began to stroke my fur, then my tail, and down to my tummy and wings.

Oh, that was it! Just that! I missed it so much! I purred. Loudly.

“Now a song?” I asked expectantly.

“I made a new one!” Anna said. “When I walked in the forest with Geshem and Shooya, I wrote a song about the forest.”

Anna pulled out her flute and began to play masterfully. The notes floated in the air, perfect and sweet—until the door burst open and a stupid bird player barged in.

“Oh, not this!” I groaned.

“You are the leader now,” Anna reminded me, stopping her song. “You have, like… responsibilities and whatnot.”

“What is it now?” I turned to the messenger and sighed. “We are busy, as you may see.”

“The force is ready,” the messenger squawked. “Like you asked, we gathered the troops available in Val-Rush. Sixty NPC soldiers, twenty NPC archers equipped with sleep spore arrows. Some twenty-four high-level players, including eight mages and three agents. And six birds armed with death spore bombs. All ready for the assault and awaiting your command.”

“Oh, that does sound important,” Anna commented. “Mind updating me?”

“After the gas mask factory was ruined,” I explained, “I wanted to assault a small camp the Order of the Phoenix had in Rishuan, near the border.”

“With that force? Isn’t it, uh, overkill?” Anna asked.

“Yes,” I said. “But it’s the first-ever army assault onto Rishuan ground—into Order of the Phoenix territory. To take ground there means a huge morale hit for them. I need crushing victory, I want them to feel despair.”

“I see,” said Anna. “Good thinking!”

“Why, thank you.”

Anna then took me in her arms and joined the messenger. “What are you waiting for?” she asked him. “Let’s go!”

The messenger led us to the army gathering outside the palace. The NPC troops stood in neat lines, stiff and disciplined. The controlled bird-folk players were lazing around, picking at their feathers, but snapped to attention when they saw me. The agents—Nemm, Vii, and Linee—all glared at me for being late.

“Let’s go,” I ordered. “To the Rishuan border.”

It wasn’t that far, but we still had a two-hour march ahead of us. Not that I complained; being carried by Anna while listening to her music was relaxing as heck. And if I didn’t look very… ah… general-like, that was fine too.

It wasn’t like I felt the need to impress the army. NPCs were NPCs, players were controlled, and the three agents here knew too well that if they had a problem with how I did things, they would do well to keep it to themselves.

Yes… the walk was really, really relaxing.

And Anna's tune were so… calm. Made me sleepy…

“Hey, Zylil!” Anna whispered while rattling me.

“Ah—where? What!?” I came to my senses. It seemed I had dozed off. “Wake up, we arrived!”

“Oh, yes, sure.” I rubbed my eyes with my tail. Yeah, sleeping while marching was very un-general-like behavior.

I flew from Anna’s grip, hovering a few meters above the ground to survey the battlefield.

We could easily see the camp on a small hill, not five hundred meters from us. It was a good distance between armies—way further than the two-hundred-meter effective ranger of archers, and out of range for most magic attacks like gas, fire, and lightning.

The enemy camp had some wooden structures for supplies and sleep, surrounded by a spiky wooden fence. There were probably around sixty NPC soldiers there, and maybe twenty players, I’m guessing.

We could see the frantic movement of enemy soldiers. They weren’t equipped to handle a force our size. I waited to see if they would just run away, which would be the smart thing to do.

They didn’t. Instead, they were… definitely doing something, rearranging some equipment.

I waited patiently. I was curious. After ten minutes or so, it seemed they were ready. They arranged themselves into ten teams of seven men each. When I looked with the magic spyglass, I could more or less understand the tactics.

I gave the spyglass to Anna. “What do you think?”

“That’s smart,” Anna said. “Looks like each group has two NPCs carrying huge wooden shields against arrows. Behind them are two NPC archers. Another is definitely a gas mage, or a player that knows how to cast large positive air bubbles against spores. And the last two are players, probably a battle mage and a warrior.”

“Yes, that is very smart,” I agreed. “Now let’s see how this fares against our sleep spore arrows.”

“It will ruin them,” Anna said. “Completely. Oh, it looks like… they are running toward us.”

Yes, they were.

“Archers! Nock!” I shouted. “Spore Arrows!”

They complied. We were at a 250-meter distance. This was usually too far for an effective shot to pierce armor, but we didn’t need to pierce anything.

I gave the order. “Loose!”

WWWUSSHH.

The enemy stopped running, held their positions, and adjusted the giant wooden shields to form a roof. The mages cast the air bubbles; I could identify the glowing magic circles. The bubbles engulfed the shields and the men beneath them.

This was great because some of the arrows hit the shields and shattered.

PSPSPSSSS.

The sound of cracking and gas releasing from many arrows filled the air.

The gas quickly spread within the bubbles. The magic was meant to keep outside air out, which meant it kept the gas in.

Some enemies inhaled the gas and immediately fell into a deep sleep.

Of course, when the enemy saw their comrades fall to the ground, they assumed the gas was a deadly poison. They screamed and panicked, abandoning their formation and breaking the bubbles to run.

Some brave ones took their fallen comrades with them. Maybe they noticed they were only sleeping, but still, they took a huge risk inhaling the gas themselves. I knew that some gas mages could use precise bubbles to make effective gas masks around their noses, but this was a skill only a few masters possessed.

There were around twenty that fell asleep overall.

The enemy force was now cut in half. Some were on the run back to their camp; others were regrouping, trying to figure out if they should rescue their fallen comrades.

I didn’t give them any time to figure it out.

“NPC soldiers, Charge!” I shouted the order.

Then I turned to Linee, who controlled the birds. “Send three birds to the running enemy army, and three to those trying to help the sleepers. But only drop bombs on the running army.”

She nodded, and the birds were on their way.

The NPC soldiers and birds I sent to the sleepers—and the ones trying to carry them—were only there to scare them. I needed to separate the ones who had fallen asleep from their protectors.

And it worked. The giant birds were terror in the enemy’s eyes, death itself descending from the sky. The enemies left behind started running like crazy. Even those carrying their sleeping comrades dropped them and bolted. Of course, they weren’t even sure if their comrades were alive or dead. It wasn’t worth dying for.

“Second Volley!” I ordered another wave of sleep spores.

That was it. The NPC charge, the screaming birds, and the sleep volley—they broke. They were all running, leaving the twenty soldiers sleeping on the ground.

The three birds I ordered to bomb the running enemy dropped their charges, but the soldiers easily made small groups protected by pressure bubbles. So, no casualties there. This tactic was only effective in combination with other types of assaults.

But what I was really after were the sleeping beauties on the grass, just waiting for my taking.

I arrived there with Anna, the other agents, and some twenty NPC and player soldiers.

“Ah,” said Nemm excitedly when we got to the sleepers. “What have we here?”

“Two mages at least, I can tell,” I said. “Five bug-folks, obviously.”

“But the bird-folk and the naked Nula soldiers,” said Vii, “which are NPCs and which are players?”

That was the important question. After all, we can only control the non-bug-folk players.

“It’s easy to say from the uniforms,” Anna said. “Zylil is correct about the two mages, and you got two more naked Nula players there,” she pointed, “and one more bird-folk player, and one bug-folk player.”

“So five…” I said.

This was huge. Five probably high-ranking players from the Order of the Phoenix would flip sides today. They would tell us the most updated intel, and we would have their power. Some Order of the Phoenix members had really climbed the levels. If we got ourselves a level 4 or 5…

And of course, this was another moral blow. Not to mention the hostage release leverage.

“Vii,” I said, “who will take control?”

“Right now,” she said, “I will take temporary control of all of them. When we get back I will arrange permanent control. I could take one myself. Linee here could take one, as long as she is not required for any more bird missions. Shenn and Rel have spare space for the other three between them.”

Then she turned to me. “And ah… according to my data,” she said hesitantly, “you have only Anna, Regsmil, and Thourax. So you should be able to control two more, that is… if you prefer.”

“Nah, I’m good,” I said. I disliked managing controlled players; it was tiresome. Even if I had only two players under my control—not three as Vii assumed—I didn’t want the hassle.

“Take temporary control and then divide them as you see fit,” I told her. “But only use agents you judge adequate for the job. These are our prize players, and much more important than your average Sin-Ish bird-folk player.”

I would have shouted ‘no offense’ to the bird player nearby, but there was really no need.

“Yes, Zylil,” Vii said.

“How much time will they sleep?” I asked.

“For one or two hours,” Nemm said. “Depends on their size and how much of the spore they inhaled.”

“Tie the five non-bug-folk players,” I said. “Carry them back to our main force, then Vii, start working on them. Leave the rest.”

“Leave the bug-folk player as well?” Linee asked. “We could get a ransom.”

“The bug-folk could be a mage,” I said. “And it’s a headache to transport and contain a non-controlled mage player. Besides, five are enough.”

“Then shouldn’t we just kill the rest?” Linee kept asking,

“She said ‘leave the rest’,” Anna scolded him “It wasn’t open for debate, you dense.”

Linee shut his mouth but gave a hostile glance toward Anna, which I didn’t miss.

We got back to our main force. After half an hour, Vii finished taking control of all five players. She was bloody fast at this, the best of us.

Now I needed to decide if we should give chase to the rest of the enemy soldiers.

They had apparently abandoned the small camp and headed in the direction of Merezeth—the Rishuan capital. It would be hard to catch them on the way, and our force wasn’t equipped to assault a large fortified city. And anyway, the idea was to make a point, and we got the prisoners…

“Let’s just burn the empty camp,” I shouted. “Nemm, take some fire mages and NPC soldiers and take care of it. Don’t forget to loot for valuables before burning it all.”

“Yes, Zylil.”

We then headed back. That was a really quick and decisive battle; I was glad about the result. In only a few hours we had made real progress.

When we got back, I was ready to take the rest of the day off. I think I definitely earned it, and besides, I had gone days without Anna. I needed some compensation time.

Unfortunately, some time after we arrived, another horrible interruption set its course to ruin my day.

“They are having a party,” Anna informed me. “You are expected to attend.”

“Didn’t they have a party yesterday!?” I sighed. “What is it for this time?”

“Why, our grand victory today, of course,” Anna said sarcastically.

“Our victory?” I asked. “Only three agents even came with us, and even they barely lifted a tail.”

“Well, you know them,” Anna said. “They love parties.”

“I hate parties!” I groaned. “At least this time you will come?”

“Oh no!” Anna said. “I couldn’t. With what you guys do at parties, it’s disgusting, really.”

“You mean… drink?” I asked. Anna sometimes got me confused. “I mean, I don’t like parties because I just don’t like the other agents, but what is wrong with the party itself?”

“It’s the animals!” Anna said, shuddering. “All those poor beasts. You fill the room with cute beasts and monsters, tie them up, drug them with calm spores, and then drink their blood while they are still alive until they disappear! Some of the animals you make drink large amounts of alcohol first, so their blood will be intoxicated!”

I looked at Anna and blinked. I thought about every single thing she just said, and honestly didn’t find anything wrong with it. Well, I guess as much as we were connected, we were still from different species.

I went to the party and my sense of smell was immediately assaulted by the mixture of alcohol and copper blood.

There was bad music coming from two bird-folk players. Both played on some skeleton-like structure full of strings seemingly tied randomly. Each also held a large wooden bowl to act as an echo box. It looked hard to adjust while playing, and indeed, they were failing miserably at it.

I saw Nemm sitting on a couch next to a large horned snake. On the floor, I saw a large pool of blood; obviously, someone had opened a drinking wound too wide, and half of the animal's blood had spilled to waste.

“Oh, hi Zylil,” Nemm said, then followed my gaze. “That’s not my doing! This was one of those savages.” His tail pointed at a group consisting of Shenn, Linee, and Te. “Drinking like children! Would you believe it?”

I wanted to take a sip from the snake myself. I flew to a soft-looking spot and gave a small bite, but as I did, the snake suddenly disappeared!

Leaving me biting the air, looking really stupid.

Oh god, this party sucked so much!

“There are honey-induced bears over there,” Nemm suggested. “But… I think they put alcohol in them all…”

“That’s okay, I’m not that thirsty,” I said dryly.

“You know,” said Nemm, leaning in, “this party is not only to celebrate today’s small win.”

“Is it not?” I asked.

“I talked with the other agents,” said Nemm. “They are excited. They feel like we are winning in the overall war. Like we can finally fulfill our mission and bring our Master. And that… that is so exciting.”

“Yes,” I agree “I can’t wait to fulfill our mission.”

“We all feel like it’s really happening,” Nemm said, his eyes glazing over. “Saving our Master from the horrible fate! It just fills me with hope and happiness. And we all feel like we owe this to you, Zylil.”

I raised an eyebrow at that. I didn’t feel that. Then again, I didn’t give a damn what the other agents thought.

“Yes,” Nemm continued. “You are the leader we all needed. The one who will lead us to total victory. Not that fool Nurr—good riddance, we can all see it now. You are the leader our Bamboo Master truly wanted.”

“I just hope to be a worthy servant for the Bamboo,” I said, reciting.

“As do we all.”

Nemm got quiet for now; apparently, he had run out of brainwashed religious things to say. “I will go give the honey bear another sip before this fool Te drinks it dry.”

Nemm was gone, and I was bored. I hated parties. Now I saw Shenn, Linee, and Te coming my way. Oh, I needed to evade them somehow.

“Zylil, here you are.” Anna’s voice! Thank heavens.

“What are you doing here?” I asked her as I flew in her direction.

She moved toward me while glancing at all the bleeding animals with distaste. “Eww,” she whispered. “Disgusting.”

“There is a single enemy soldier outside the palace,” she said. “He wants to talk. White flag and all. We think he is from the Order of the Phoenix.”

“Interesting!” I said. “Lead the way.”

This was an important development, and just as importantly, a valid excuse to leave the party early.

We left the room, and I ordered some twenty NPC archers and some mage players to tag along. Better to be safe.

He was waiting on a small hill a few hundred meters from the palace. When we got near enough, we noticed he was wearing a gas mask—probably one of the few they had left.

We stopped twenty meters from him. The archers drew their bows but didn’t aim.

“Hello,” I called out. “I’m Zylil. Who might you be?”

“Isn’t it beyond pleasure to make my acquaintance?” he asked sarcastically, his voice muffled behind the mask. “I might get offended.”

I smiled at that. “I’m not talking like the other agents. Are you from the Order of the Phoenix?”

“Yes, I am,” he said. “I come as a representative. I speak with their voice.”

“How can we know that to be true?” Anna asked.

Instead of answering, he simply removed his mask.

I knew his face from descriptions even though I hadn’t met him. It was fucking Felix. I was a bit stunned. I looked at Anna and she nodded. “It’s him.”

Felix put his mask back on.

“Why would you come to parley?” I asked. “The second most important man in the Order. Isn’t it a bit reckless of you?”

“You are the sole leader of all your forces,” Felix answered. “You are twenty meters from me, with only twenty-five men. I could slaughter the lot of you. So it was reckless, but on your part, I’d say.”

That got us all tense. Felix was known to be incredibly powerful. The archers aimed at him, and the mages activated their circles. But not Anna; she was relaxed.

“So why wouldn’t you?” Anna asked. “Slaughter the lot of us? Surely it would solve some of your problems.”

“It wouldn’t solve enough of them,” Felix said. “But don’t make a mistake, it is still very tempting. Maybe if the talk doesn’t go to my liking, I will reconsider.”

Felix was very dangerous, I knew. Anna was capable too, and some of my men were strong. But Felix was right. I didn’t expect a monster like him to show up, and he may have had fifty-fifty odds of actually killing me if things go south.

“So what did you want to talk about?” Anna asked. “Don’t leave us hanging.”

“Anna,” Felix said, an icy voice heard even behind the mask. “You got us really good, didn’t you?”

“Well, you know,” Anna shrugged. “In love and war and such…”

“But really, Felix,” I interrupted. “Did you come all this way only to complain? Speak already, or leave. I’m busy too, you know.”

Oh please don’t leave!

“We are losing,” he said simply. Resentment colored his voice, but sadness too. “You got the technology edge, it’s that simple. If we got here five years ago, like the Bamboo, we would have guns and bombs by now, and who knows what magic. We would have wiped you out easily. But the lazy System defense failed. And so we are helpless.”

“So file a complaint letter to the System,” I said. “What do you want with me?”

“We want to leave,” he continued. “To a different continent. We are building airships like you.”

That made us quiet. We knew they were building airships, of course, but we just assumed they were meant for some type of aerial combat.

“Unfortunately,” he continued, “your ships will be ready long before ours. We estimate they have a good chance of getting the Bamboo here before we could set sail. And if that happens, we are not naïve enough to think we will leave at all. We need more time. We need you to delay. Two weeks, at least.”

“And you thought to yourself,” I said, “maybe I will just ask them really nicely, maybe that will work?”

Instead of answering, Felix just pulled a square object wrapped in cloth from his coat and threw it hard at us.

Anna stepped in front of me, protective. But it didn’t explode or anything.

I signaled to one of the NPCs to recover it and open it. It was… just a book.

“This is ISO 42001:2473,” Felix said. “The book with the answers. It is the most valuable object in the continent. And the only thing we possess that your Master actually needs.”

Ooh. It was what Nurr had so desperately wanted. It was a real treasure.

“This is only the first of thirteen volumes,” continued Felix. “We want 500m² of cloth for each book, as well as the release of the prisoners you took today for the first book. And, of course, a general ceasefire. If you don’t agree, the moment your ship leaves the ground, we burn the books. Every copy.”

He paused to let that sink in. “Not many people know and understand the content of the book. Those few individuals have agreed to commit suicide if your ship lifts off. So as long as you believe me, the knowledge will be lost to you without an agreement. This isn’t a suggestion for you, Zylil. This is a suggestion for your Bamboo Master. Kindly pass it along.”

This was… so interesting.

I couldn’t hide a wide smile.

“I will pass it along,” I said. “Even before I get an answer, maybe I'll send you one of the prisoners—you know, as a gesture of goodwill. Maybe try that ceasefire of yours, see how that goes.”

“How much time for an answer?” said Felix.

“You know,” I mused, “seagull mail is such a hassle. Maybe three days each direction?”

“That long!” Felix angered. “Don’t you have a magical means of communication?”

“We tried,” I said. “Doesn’t work, sadly. So you just wait patiently.”

"That’s too long," he snapped. "Release a second prisoner and give the first 500m² of cloth—even before the bamboo approval."

"300 m²" I countered, just for the hell of it. I didn’t really care.

“Fine,” he said with contempt. "So I guess I’m done here."

He turned around and left.

I didn’t return to the party after that. I’m the boss, aren’t I? I can surely do what I like. And I liked to attend a smaller party—just me and Anna, in our room. I’d have her play for me, and groom me, and if she fancied, I could taste her a bit as well.

“So what are you gonna do?” Anna asked mid-groom. “I’m so curious.”

“It’s all gone to perfection!” I beamed. “Better than I could have hoped.”

“Meaning?” Anna prompted.

“The Bamboo,” I said. “They specifically ordered us to do everything in our power to get the books. They didn’t say anything about it being of lower priority than the shipbuilding, even if it obviously does.”

“So that… is really good for us,” Anna realized.

“Yes,” I said. “I could kill two bird-folk with a single rock. I won’t really write the Bamboo about the deal. But I will happily get on with Felix’s deal after six days. The other agents won’t complain or think it goes against the Bamboo's will. I will get rid of my enemy while also delaying the arrival of the Bamboo.”

“And after that?” Anna asked. “Eventually, our ship will be ready.”

“The ship will sail when they are ready,” I said. “But about mid-journey, one of the bird players under my control will execute a special command.”

Anna smiled, knowing what was to come.

“He will burn the ship to the ground, killing all the crew along with himself,” I said. “We will say he was an Order spy or something.”

“So there will be no Order of the Phoenix,” Anna summarized. “And no Bamboo.”

“Yes,” I said. “Fuck the Bamboo.”

“That leaves only the other agents for us to handle,” Anna said. “But it’s doable.”

“And then,” I continued, “only the two of us will remain to rule on this continent. Like you always wanted.”

“Yes!” Anna beamed and kissed me. “It will be so much fun! I can’t wait!”
