The wheel kept spinning for a few more seconds, and the enforcer’s head whipped from side to side so badly that Sakura was already starting to feel like her own neck might cramp just from looking at it.

Then the wheel began to slow. Sakura leaned forward slightly without even realizing why. Everything inside her tightened with tension.

With an unpleasant click, the wheel finally stopped. Burned into the wood directly across from the man’s head was the number 7.

For a second, the hall went quieter. Then everything exploded at once.

“SEVEN!”

“Ah, damn it! I bet on five!”

“Bring him the drinks! Seven for him! Seven!”

People clapped. Sakura blinked, still not fully understanding what exactly was happening here.

“Seven?” she asked quietly to no one.

They started untying the man carelessly, with no concern at all for his comfort. One of the laughing men undid the straps on his arms, and another kicked the fastener loose from around his ankles. The enforcer dropped to the floor like a sack.

The crowd burst into approving laughter. Sakura grimaced.

The man lay there for a couple of seconds, sprawled across the dirty boards, then muttered a curse and tried to get up. It did not work on the first try. He almost slammed his shoulder into the wheel itself, but still managed to straighten up.

Now Sakura could get a better look at him. He was short, a head and a half shorter than Gilbert, but solidly built, with broad shoulders and a thick neck. He looked about forty, maybe a little older. His face was coarse, with short stubble and a nose that had clearly been broken once.

“Damn all of you...” he muttered in a hoarse voice, shaking his head. “I... respect you all very much!”

The crowd answered that with delighted cackling.

“You hear that? He respects us!”

“And we love you too, Harek Harlow!”

“Bring him seven!”

Hands started stretching toward him from behind the counter and from the nearby tables, each one holding a shot. One, two, three... seven. They lined them up in front of him on a table like a reward or a sentence, depending on how you looked at it.

The enforcer stared darkly at the line of cloudy little glasses, then let out a heavy sigh.

“Seven it is,” he muttered. “Going small was never my style.”

Without wasting time, he grabbed the first shot and threw it back. Then the second. Then the third. Sakura felt her brows slowly climb upward.

“He definitely has an iron liver,” she muttered.

“Looks like it,” Gilbert said.

The enforcer worked his way through the rest of the shots without much thought. But before the last one, the seventh, he stopped sharply and turned to the room.

“I...” He forced his face into something meaningful. “Love all of you!”

That declaration was met with roaring delight.

“And we love you too, Harek!”

The enforcer burst into completely drunken laughter, snatched up the seventh shot, and downed it too.

“He’s awful,” Sakura said quietly.

“He’s just a drunk,” Gilbert corrected with a shrug.

They had been standing too close to the entrance the whole time, and that was already becoming noticeable. Gilbert moved first, heading toward an empty table by the wall, not too close to the center but not too far out of sight either.

“Come on,” he said. “Watching is better when you’re sitting.”

Sakura nodded and followed him.

Gilbert sat down across from Sakura so he could keep an eye on her and still watch the enforcer. By then, the enforcer had already made his way over to another table, where several locals had gathered around him happily.

Sakura watched him with distrust.

“That’s really the enforcer?”

“Looks like it. Ogrom’s enforcer in the flesh.”

“He looks like he doesn’t even know where his boots are or what to do with them.”

The noise in the hall gradually began to die down. People drifted back to their tables, leaving the empty wheel alone. As long as no one was tied to it, it was not interesting to anyone.

“Seems like we won’t have to work too hard to kidnap him,” Sakura said with a snort.

“I wouldn’t say that so confidently.”

They watched the enforcer for a while longer as he kept pouring more alcohol into himself and slipping deeper into oblivion. Sakura was still amazed that someone could drink that much and still remain upright. A real talent.

Talent...

She suddenly remembered that her own talent still had not been identified by the System. What kinds of talents even existed in this world? Were they tied to combat, or could they touch any sphere at all? Could the System one day calmly inform her that her talent was, for example, alchemy? Acting? Or something spectacularly useless in this world, like origami?

It almost certainly would not have anything to do with alcohol.

Caught up in those thoughts, she did not even notice how much time had passed, and by then the enforcer, known by the nickname Harek, had finally said goodbye to the loud crowd. He kissed no fewer than six men before stumbling toward the stairs. Whistling some tuneless little song, he was clearly making his way up to the rooms above.

Gilbert watched the enforcer until he disappeared upstairs, clinging to the railing and whistling something completely incoherent. Only then did he speak quietly.

“Time.”

Sakura had already started to rise, ready to follow, but Gilbert gave a short shake of his head.

“Not after him. Outside.”

Sakura was surprised, but did not argue. They got up from the table almost at the same time and headed for the door as if they had simply decided to get some air or take a piss somewhere around the corner, like half the tavern. Hardly anyone paid them any attention.

Gilbert moved at once to the other side of the street, where the upper windows of the tavern were visible. Sakura stopped beside him, trying not to stare too openly upward.

“So now we just wait?” she asked quietly.

“Watch carefully,” Gilbert told her.

A warm, trembling light flared in one of the second-floor windows. Almost at the same moment, that same drunken whistling with its smug little flourishes drifted out through the half-open shutters.

Gilbert inclined his head slightly.

“Him.”

Sakura kept watching the window. Light wandered across the glass as if the man inside were staggering from one corner of the room to the other.

“So now we climb in through the window, right?” she asked. Gilbert gave a short nod.

The mage led Sakura to a wall overgrown with ivy. He tested the stems and handholds with one hand, quickly judging whether they would hold a body’s weight.

“I go first,” he said. “You’re right behind me. Watch where I put my feet and step there too.”

“I get it. Don’t worry.”

Gilbert only gave a quiet huff and started climbing. He moved quickly and astonishingly quietly, which no longer surprised Sakura at all. She tried to do no worse, or at least not slide off.

The window was open wider than they had expected. Apparently, the enforcer really did like fresh air, or he was just too drunk to close the shutters properly and think about his own safety.

When he reached the level of the windowsill, Gilbert froze first and listened. Then he cautiously lifted himself higher, looked inside, and shifted aside a little to make room for Sakura.

“One,” he whispered.

Sakura pulled herself closer. The room was small, with a crooked bed, dusty furniture, and a washbasin in the corner. The enforcer stood sideways to them, trying to pull off his boot without falling over. He had not stopped whistling.

Gilbert climbed through the window first, soft and almost noiseless. Sakura followed him. The floorboards creaked so faintly under their feet that the man did not even notice.

Then he did.

He had only just started turning his head toward the sound when Gilbert was already there. One arm locked across his chest, and the other hand clamped tightly over his mouth. The enforcer jerked and made a muffled sound into Gilbert’s palm, trying to twist free, but his coordination was exactly what one would expect after that much alcohol.

“Chair,” Gilbert threw to Sakura.

Sakura reacted at once. She grabbed the shaky wooden chair and dragged it closer. Together, not very elegantly but quickly, they forced the man down into it. He still tried to resist, angry and drunk.

“Hands,” Gilbert said shortly.

Sakura grabbed the belt lying by the bed, then a second one from the man’s boot. They pulled the enforcer’s arms behind the chair and tied them tightly to the backrest. They bound his legs too, so he could not kick. The man kept making angrier, more and more incoherent sounds.

Sakura snatched a rough rag off the table, twisted it into a wad, and after catching Gilbert’s glance, gave a quick nod. The gag went in with no shortage of cursing. Silent cursing, though, since half the words were swallowed by the cloth.

When they were done, both of them paused for a second and looked at the result. Harlow sat tied to the chair, damp with sweat, angry, bleary-eyed, and with his hair sticking up in all directions. He looked more pitiful than frightening.

“Beautiful,” Sakura muttered.

Gilbert did not answer. He was already checking the door. He shut it and braced it with the chest that had been standing by the bed.

Sakura, meanwhile, pulled a flask of water from her inventory and, without overthinking it, splashed the contents straight into poor Harlow’s face. She hoped it might sober him up at least a little. The enforcer flinched and squirmed in place. An especially outraged series of sounds came from behind the gag.

Gilbert stepped closer and crouched in front of him so he could look him directly in the eyes.

“Well then, Harlow. Enforcer. Listen carefully,” the mage said in a hard voice. “We didn’t take you for entertainment, and not for money. We need you to remove something.”

He held out his hands, showing the bone bracelets around his wrists. Harlow’s gaze did not focus at once, but then it sharpened, staring closely at the bracelets.

“Yes,” Gilbert said more quietly. “Those.”

The enforcer started making more urgent sounds through the cloth.

“You’re going to take them off,” Gilbert continued. “Quietly, without resistance. And then this ends without unnecessary blood. If not, I’ll find a way to make this night much worse for you than the wheel downstairs.”

Sakura stood beside them and watched Harek closely. Behind the water and the haze in his eyes, awareness was already returning. She decided to step in more gently.

“Listen,” she said. “We don’t want to kill you. We really don’t. Just take these bracelets off, and we’ll go. You won’t be hurt.”

They really were acting like a bad guard and a good guard. The enforcer looked at Sakura for a long moment, then back at the bracelets, and then at Gilbert again. He made another muffled sound.

“Looks like he wants to say something,” Sakura remarked.

“Yes,” Gilbert answered dryly. “And I really hope it’ll be something useful.”

He reached for the gag, but did not pull it out at once.

“You’re going to speak quietly, with no stupidity and no heroics. One sound in the wrong direction and the gag goes back in. Understood?”

The man hesitated for a second, then nodded anyway. Gilbert carefully pulled out the cloth.

“You really are filth...” the enforcer rasped.

Gilbert did not move back even an inch.

“I’m glad we’re starting to understand each other,” he said evenly.

The enforcer twisted his mouth into a crooked smile. Water was still running down his face, disappearing into his stubble and dripping from his collar.

“You idiots,” he said hoarsely. “The guard will know about this immediately. They’ll catch you. And they’ll hang me too if they decide I helped.”

“They won’t catch us if you’re smart,” Gilbert answered.

“Runaway mages don’t stay free for long,” Harlow continued, clinging to his own words like the last scraps of authority. “Especially stupid ones who come crawling into Ogrom for a rescue. This is a fool’s plan.”

Gilbert tipped his head slightly.

“The foolish thing here is trying to scare me in the position you’re in.”

The enforcer narrowed his eyes, studying him far too closely. That look made Sakura uneasy. For one second, she was afraid he was about to see something wrong. Recognize something. Almost without thinking, she lifted a hand to her hair as if she were only brushing a strand aside and touched her temple. The raspberry streak was still there. She let out a quieter breath.

The enforcer’s gaze shifted to her. Then back to Gilbert.

This time, Gilbert did not limit himself to words. He pulled a dagger from his inventory. The narrow blade flashed in the half-dark room and immediately pressed beneath Harlow’s chin. The enforcer visibly froze, and only his throat moved against the steel.

“We don’t have time for empty talk,” Gilbert said quietly, almost calmly.

“You don’t understand what you’re doing,” Harlow said now in a very different tone, without his old self-satisfaction. “The city is full of guards right now. You’re not getting out of here.”

“That isn’t your concern,” Gilbert cut in.

“You’re wrong. It is my concern now,” the enforcer shot back.

He studied their faces again, this time more carefully. As if, despite everything he had drunk, something deeply unpleasant was beginning to connect in his head. Then suddenly it hit him. He jerked hard enough that the blade scratched his skin more sharply.

“Wait...” he breathed out, staring first at Gilbert, then at Sakura. “You... are you the ones?”

Sakura felt everything inside her go cold. Gilbert immediately pressed the dagger harder to his throat.

“Don’t ask useless questions.”

The enforcer started shifting in the chair, no longer like a drunk, but in quick, nervous little movements.

“You’re insane,” he breathed. “Completely insane. If that’s true, you never should’ve come here. They’ll tear you apart alive in this city, idiots.”

“Then you should help us faster,” Gilbert said.

Sakura stood slightly to the side, watching all of this with a growing sick feeling. Gilbert was right in the way he handled the interrogation. Everything he did was cold, hard, and without emotion. But it was still difficult to watch the blade being pressed harder and harder into a half-drunk man’s throat.

So she stepped closer.

“Listen,” she said, more gently than Gilbert. “We don’t have a choice, understand? There’s no way for us to take these bracelets off by ourselves. We don’t want to kill you. And we don’t want to make things worse for you. Just help us, and this will be over.”

“Over?” he repeated hoarsely. “For you, maybe. For me, maybe it’ll only begin. You have no idea.”

“Maybe your reputation will be ruined. Maybe they’ll strip you of your post. But you’ll stay alive,” Gilbert said calmly.

The enforcer closed his eyes for a second.

“Filth...” he muttered. “What filth.”

“That isn’t an answer,” Gilbert reminded him.

He exhaled sharply through his nose.

“Fine,” he said at last. “Fine. I’ll do it. Just get that thing away from my throat!”

Sakura exchanged a look with Gilbert. They understood each other without words.

“Oh no, that thing stays right where it is until you’re done,” the mage said.

Sakura, meanwhile, carefully untied Harlow’s hands.

“If you try anything extra,” Gilbert said, “you’ll die before you can say ‘mother.’”

As soon as the enforcer’s hands were free, he did not try anything stupid. He only rubbed his wrists slowly, with a hiss, and looked at Gilbert’s bracelets with something close to hatred.

“Fools. Such fools,” he muttered again, quieter this time.

The enforcer grimaced, then lowered his gaze and mentally reached into his inventory. A second later, a small key appeared in his hand. It was made of the same white bone as the bracelets themselves. Smooth, with faint notches cut along the edge, as if it were not quite a key in the ordinary sense, but part of something ritual.

Sakura leaned in closer. She watched as Harlow lifted the key toward one of the bracelets and held it there for a second. Then he began tracing a rune beside the lock. It did not blaze up with huge light or flare like in a fairy tale. It simply appeared in the air in thin, pale blue lines.

Sakura felt the air beside Gilbert’s wrist twitch slightly. A dry click sounded. One of the bone cuffs opened by itself and fell to the floor in a heavy piece. Sakura flinched at the sound, even though she had been waiting for it.

Gilbert did not move. Only the muscles in his face twitched, as if something painful and long held back had passed through his arm in that exact second.

Then the second bracelet clicked open.

“Done,” Harlow muttered. “And after this you...”

At that moment, Sakura looked toward the window by chance, as if some feeling had pushed her to do it. She did not understand at first what had unsettled her. Behind the cloudy glass, something flashed far too brightly. A sharp white-yellow glint, as if the moon itself had flared outside for a moment.

And then she saw a huge black sphere suddenly forming and flying straight at them.

Everything inside her tightened.

“Get down!” Sakura screamed.

And in the next second, the world tore apart in the explosion.

The floor under their feet lurched, the room convulsed, the glass exploded into white dust, and then everything became one chaos of cracking wood, crashing stone, someone screaming, and a deafening ringing in the ears.

The wall split with cracks. Plaster and pieces of beam rained down from above.

The tavern shook so hard that the chair with the enforcer tipped over sideways, and Sakura was thrown shoulder-first into the bed.
