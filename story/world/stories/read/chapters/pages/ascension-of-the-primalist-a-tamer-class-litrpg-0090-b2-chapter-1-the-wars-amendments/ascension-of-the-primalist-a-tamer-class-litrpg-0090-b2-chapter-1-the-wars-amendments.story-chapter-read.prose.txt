Seth propped his chin on his now-fully-healed arm, the morning sun’s rays warming his face just enough to prevent his golden eyes from closing. Beside him, Elena was beaming with joy, her enchanted quill scratching furiously across the piece of parchment in front of her every time Captain Michaelson opened his bearded mouth in front of the class.

The past week had passed in a flash. Since the three final rounds of the Spring Tournament had been canceled, everyone had been talking about the exact same thing.

War.

Kastal’s higher-ups and the academy’s director had all claimed that the Bridan Empire’s declaration of war was unprovoked, that the Empire simply believed it should be allowed to take whatever it wanted from a weaker nation like theirs.

But Seth, thanks to the Champions of Chaos, knew parts of the truth: House heads and high officers of Trogan’s army had delved into a Rift alongside a traitor of the empire and had found resources that would cause any Gold Wielders—and even a Platinum one—to drool from envy.

How could their northern neighbors sit on their hands in such a situation? They were stronger, and that treasure should have been theirs in the first place since it had been in their territory.

Oh, not this again, Seth sighed internally as Captain Michaelson pointed at the list of sentences displayed on the enchanted board behind him.

The twelve war amendments from the NEVAK.

Every single day of the past week, the man had been hammering those amendments into their heads. Five of the most-powerful countries and entities—the Northern Confederation, the Republic of Eryndor, the Velmark Kingdom, the Aelmyra Empire, and the Kravess Union—together had decided to establish rules for every war that took place in the eastern continent.

Twelve amendments that every nation had to follow if they didn’t want to lose the union's protection, or get crushed by them. The punishment depended on the offense. Still, most countries didn’t complain. The five nations that formed NEVAK were the shield that kept the western continent’s stronger powers from crossing the sea and preying on them. Without the union’s backing, there would also be nothing stopping several nations from joining forces to tear apart a vulnerable country and claim its resources for themselves.

And this mattered even more after a war, when a nation was struggling to rebuild an army worthy of the name.

On paper, those amendments were also meant to ensure some kind of justice, prevent unnecessary civilian casualties and make wars a little more fair, yet Seth couldn’t help but feel that there were other… hidden goals.

With a sigh, he glanced at the list behind the captain.

1. All battles must be fought in the Warfare Rifts, with a war lasting for a duration of 18 months.

2. Battles begin 3 months after a declaration of war.

3. Victory in a particular battle is declared when one side is entirely killed, captured, or forced to leave the Warfare Rift, or by having the largest number of survivors inside once the time limit is reached.

4. Battles have a time limit of 10, 20, 30, or 40 days depending on the number of squads.

5. Each squad is composed of up to 50 Wielders.

6. During the first six months, only Copper and Iron Wielders are allowed to participate. Thereafter, the Tier increases by one every four months.

7. Battles can involve 2, 4, 8, or 16 squads, based on the format decided by the NEVAK, which will take into account size advantages and disadvantages for either side.

8. The war ends when either of the main nations in conflict surrender. If neither has surrendered by the end of the Gold phase, NEVAK will authorize both sides to transition into full-scale warfare outside the Rifts.

9. Battle schedules are preset by the NEVAK, with 5 to 20 battles occurring each week.

10. If a nation wants to use Wielders of an ally, they must inform the NEVAK before the first battle.

11. No external nations may intervene during the conflict or for a 1-year peace period following the end of war.

12. The nation declaring war must state what it seeks to claim upon victory, whether resources or territory. Any territorial claim cannot exceed one-fifth of the defending nation’s current territory. The opposing nation has until the beginning of the first battle to declare a reward of equal value should they emerge victorious. If either nation spends, loses, or otherwise becomes unable to surrender the agreed reward once the war ends, it must provide resources or territory of equivalent value in its place.

Fighting in Rifts with equal numbers of Wielders seemed great, but the sixth amendment made Seth grit his teeth every time. The Copper and Iron Tiers were almost exclusively filled by commoners stuck at the different Ranks, mostly those ending with zero.

The casualties of the first four months of war would be them: the worthless pawns.

According to Toren, there was no way war would go on for more than six months. The crown couldn't afford to lose any Silver Wielders, let alone Gold ones. They’d forfeit before it came to that. And by then, the crown and the Houses would have probably used all those resources they'd acquired from the Rift.

If the spoils were truly as valuable as the Champions of Chaos believed, replacing them with something of equal worth wouldn’t be easy. Kastal might have only one thing valuable enough left to offer: territory.

Seth’s jaw tightened. What will happen to people living on that land? Will they simply wake up one day and learn that everything they owned now belongs to the Bridan Empire? Will they be forced to leave? And if they refuse, what will the King do about it?

Things could turn very ugly, there was no doubt in Seth's mind.

Of course the King could surrender before a single one of his subjects died. A good king likely would have done just that, given the odds. Instead he was feeding his subjects lies and likely intended to draw out the war just long enough to secure the stolen loot and salve his pride. He didn’t care who bled for him to do so. Nor for the lives ruined in the aftermath.

A sharp elbow jabbed his ribs.

"Hey, focus," Elena whispered.

"Yeah, yeah, I am," he answered with a sigh, glancing at her.

The noblewoman’s emerald eyes were almost glowing, filled to the brim with eagerness. She had always dreamed of a military career, so she could follow her father’s footsteps and bring justice to this world—and this was the perfect occasion to prove herself.

But there was more to it than that. She genuinely believed that even if the kingdom might be rotten at its core, its people, its history, and its culture were still worth protecting. That perhaps, once all that corruption was finally torn out by the roots, Kastal could see better days again.

History showed that beloved generals were often the ones to lead successful rebellions or negotiate reforms. Even a king had to be wary of those who commanded the respect of his army. If she made a name for herself on the battlefield, it would be the first step towards her goals.

Seth wished he could share her optimism.

All he saw was a greedy king about to add more bodies to the pile, and he was certain few, if any, would belong to nobles. That was how Kastal had worked for years. The poor suffered while the rich watched from the safety their titles bought them.

And Seth doubted this war would be any different.

We should hunt to prepare," Nightmare said through Link from within Seth’s teardrop necklace. "And find a way to hatch Small Bite."

Seth exhaled slowly, flexing the finger of his previously broken arm. "Yeah… I’ll grab a beast-hunting contract after class… and I'll keep scouring through the library."

He had already searched through half of it for any mention of hatching a beast egg, but everything he had found had been too vague to be useful. Same for the encyclopedia of his father.

At the front of the room, Captain Michaelson stopped his lecture and eyed a communication orb on his desk. Then, his voice broke through the room’s murmurs: "All right, that’s it for today. The director wants everyone at the coliseum, so let’s move."

After a small delay, Seth stood and began gathering his books, but paused when Elena’s hand suddenly grabbed his messenger bag to hold it up for him.

"You know, I can do it myself now," Seth said, gently taking the bag from the noblewoman.

"Oh. Right. Sorry," she mumbled as she turned back toward her own things.

From the noble’s other side, Jenna leaned over with a wicked grin. "So, does this mean Dev doesn’t have to cut your food and clean your ass for you anymore?"

Seth rolled his eyes and slung the bag over his shoulder. "For the tenth time, it was a joke. Only one of my arms was broken. He didn’t have to help me with that."

"If you say so," Jenna retorted with a wink that made Seth groan.

The trio filed out with the rest of the class, weaving through the academy’s halls before stepping outside into the cool air and heading toward the towering coliseum. The building’s arches cast long shadows as students chatted around them.

"Why are you making that face?"

At first Seth thought the question was directed at him, as he had plenty on his mind. But apparently he wasn’t the only one. The question came from ahead, and Seth’s attention shifted toward two male students with the scarlet uniforms of nobles walking a few paces in front of him. They had the usual pampered features of high-born, though one was short and stout, the other tall and lean. They were deep in conversation and appeared not to have noticed him at all. The taller appeared to have asked the question of the shorter.

"I don’t understand how all of you can be so thrilled about this," the shorter one answered, still scowling.

His companion let out a laugh. "Why wouldn’t I be? We could earn tons of rewards. If I perform well enough, it could be good for my House too."

"You could also die."

The cold retort only earned the shorter noble another laugh.

"It’s the weaklings who die. Commoners. Having better equipment and spells has its advantages. If things go south, I’ll just have my retainers get me away or use an Escape scroll."

The stout noble frowned. "That’s not how we win a war. By running away the moment things turn against us."

"Win or lose, I’ll make a name for myself," his friend replied with a shrug.

Seth’s expression hardened.

That was why so many nobles called the eighteen-month Rift phase of a war the ‘War Games’. To them, that was exactly what it was: a game. A chance to distinguish themselves, earn rewards, climb the ranks of their Houses, and return home with stories of their exploits.

Even the system surrounding the battles seemed designed to encourage that mentality. Captain Michaelson had explained that every Warfare Rift was overseen by an impartial spirit associated with Gorrhat, the God of War. The spirit monitored the fighting and awarded contribution points for capturing or killing enemies, which could help soldiers stand out.

It had struck Seth as bizarre when he’d first learned about it. Almost as if the NEVAK had deliberately turned war into some kind of macabre competition. And judging by the excitement around him, it had worked.

Most of the nobles didn’t seem to feel as though they had anything truly at stake, and it did nothing to ease Seth’s view of the whole situation.

"Do you think your House will care about the name you make for yourself if it loses its territory?" the shorter noble asked.

The smile on his companion’s face vanished.

"My House lost nearly half its lands during the Red War," the first continued. "My grandfather still talks about everything we lost. Villages, mines, farmland. Some of it had belonged to our family for generations. And yet the king didn't give us a single coin as compensation."

The taller noble remained silent, and Seth glanced at him.

Perhaps that was part of the problem. So many nobles had a lot to lose, but they simply didn’t know it since the king and the other higher ups had been lying about the reason behind the war. If all the Houses knew how much territory could be lost, maybe the mood wouldn’t be quite so light.

Seth couldn’t help but feel a knot in his stomach; whatever that announcement was, it would likely be about the war.

The moment they entered the arena, Captain Michaelson began to bark out orders, forcing their group to sit before they could meet any of their other friends. Seth spotted Devus waving from another section. The Guardian gestured wildly toward the instructors lining the coliseum walls.

Seth’s eyes almost immediately landed on a man standing a few steps away from Professor Reat. Tall and imposing, with a thick beard and a scar slicing down from his eyebrow to his cheek—it was the new Guardians' instructor who had replaced Lucius’ brother, Sergeant Faertis.

After the announcement of the war, the bastard had suddenly left. Probably to be far away when Toren was finally able to unconceal his name inside the communication orb Seth had picked up from Ronard's body. So far, no one outside of the Black Hounds and its leader had paid the price for the deaths of the commoners in both the Desert of Misery and the Fishlords Empire Rifts—nor for Theodora, the Primalist from his class, whose corpse had been found in a forest near Trogan a few days ago.

The only survivors of the Desert of Misery were himself and Selena, who was now escorted by four guards whenever she left the academy grounds.

The students’ collective chatter faded as all seven board members, clad in dark crimson uniforms, took their place with Director Ryehill on the platform in the middle of the coliseum’s arena. The man, wearing his signature black jacket, surveyed the sea of students with an unreadable gaze.

I still haven’t thanked him for the trial, Seth thought, looking at the director.

Jenna leaned closer to Elena, her voice barely reaching Seth’s ears. "Do you think he’ll announce the squads this time?"

"Probably," the noblewoman answered.

Jenna nodded, her gaze flickering toward the stage. "I hope we’re all in the same one. You, me, Devus, and Seth."

Elena hummed in agreement, then glanced at Seth, who remained quiet, staring at the board members. "You okay?" she asked.

"Yeah," he replied with a nod.

The truth, though, had been sitting heavy in his chest for the past week. This wasn’t his war. Not really. It wasn’t just a lack of patriotism toward a nation that abused commoners—it was deeper than that. This conflict wasn’t about protecting people or their homes.

It was solely driven by greed.

And Seth couldn’t listen to the director call it an unprovoked conflict one more time. Maybe the King was forcing the man and many more to repeat the same lie. Seth didn’t know and didn't care.

What mattered was that thousands were about to be sent to their deaths in this stupid Rift Phase—or the War Games, as so many nobles liked to call it—all because the crown and a handful of powerful Houses had gotten their hands on some resources. Whatever those were, it certainly wasn’t worth the lives of all those commoners who’d be the first to fall.

Jenna scoffed, leaning past Elena forward to catch Seth’s eye. "You say you’re okay, but I think you need to tell your face, you’ve got the same look Dev had when Brandon beat his ass at the beginning of the year."

Seth forced a tight smile. "I’m just… thinking about how things will turn out."

He had already told his friends about the lies Kastal’s higher-ups had been feeding everyone, yet they seemed to be taking it better than he was. Maybe they believed the Escape scrolls would keep most people safe. If a battle turned against them, they could simply leave the Rift before things became too dangerous.

Seth's gut told him not they shouldn't be so sure about it.

Jenna opened her mouth, but a sharp clap of Director Ryehill’s hands silenced her and the twenty-five-hundred students in the stands. Seth’s attention snapped back to the stage as the salt-and-pepper haired man stepped forward.

"Attention," the director began, his voice carrying effortlessly through the vast space. "For the past week, you’ve all learned the basics of war: strategy, formations, survival tactics. But theory alone won’t be enough for what lies ahead of us. It’s time for the next phase."

The man paused and scanned the crowd.

"The Bridan Empire believes its strength gives it the right to take whatever it desires from us. It believes that because Kastal cannot match its armies or wealth, we should simply bow our heads and surrender what is ours. We will not."

Seth noticed a few nobles nudged each other and puffed their chest in the stands.

"This is not a conflict we sought, but it is one we will answer," the director continued. "Noble or commoner, we are all people of Kastal. We share the same homeland, and today, we share the same cause: to protect it from an empire that believes might alone gives it the right to take from us.”

Seth’s jaw tightened. Bullshit. Worse than that he saw even some of the commoner students with fire in their eyes, believing their fight was just one, willing to risk their lives to fend off ‘unprovoked invaders’. These lies will get people killed.

But Seth couldn’t risk calling out the deception publicly, so he grit his teeth, and the speech continued.

"To ensure optimal synergy," Director Ryehill gestured to the board members behind him, "the crown has decreed a specific way we will fight. Squads will not mix students from the three main academies, nor will they mix Wielders from the Adventurers Guild or the noble Houses. As a military institution, our academy has been tasked with forming self-contained units capable of performing seamlessly in the heat of the battle."

Ryehill clapped once again, and glowing lists appeared out of thin air above the stage. Fifty names were displayed under each squad number, which went from 1 to 48. Students craned their necks, murmurs rippling through the crowd as they scanned those lists.

Seth clenched his fists, his nails digging into his palms the moment he noticed a pattern. Squads 1 through 34 consisted exclusively of nobles, while Squads 36 to 48 were made up of commoners. Only Squad 35 had both mixed together—probably because the amounts didn't fit for a perfect separation.

They could use this to send commoners out as cannon fodder, Seth thought as his whole body tensed. Perhaps the king doesn’t intend to risk the nobles at all.

"Squad 48," Nightmare growled in Seth’s mind as he looked at the list through their bond. "Did they put you in the weakest one?"

Seth’s gaze moved to his name, at the bottom of the fifty others. "I don't think the number represents the squads’ strength. But who knows, we’ll find out soon enough."

"It won't matter, though," the direwolf answered. "We’re going to win these battles ourselves anyway."

Seth didn’t answer. His eyes drifted back to the seven members of the administration board before turning cold as ice. If those people tried to send him to a certain death, he could still become a deserter. The enchantment engraved in his forearm might prevent him from leaving the nation, but it didn’t stop him from entering Rifts—as long as he emerged within Kastal’s land. That still left him with countless options.

He'd die sooner or later, but it wouldn't be for that lying, greedy king.
