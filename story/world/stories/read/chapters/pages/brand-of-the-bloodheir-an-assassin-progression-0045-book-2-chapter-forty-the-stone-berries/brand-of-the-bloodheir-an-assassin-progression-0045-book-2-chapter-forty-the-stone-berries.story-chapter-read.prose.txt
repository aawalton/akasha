The world wavered around him as the Quillkey took him through the realms of Windwake in the blink of an eye. Unlike the portal that led him to the Roost or the Under, where the discomfort was real, this passage was merely a momentary journey through impenetrable darkness.

Normally, he simply stepped out of the shimmering black doorway into the sedate field of wild grasses that surrounded his dilapidated home on his floating island in the void.

This time, it was painfully different.

A doorway matching his orientation—that of one falling to his death—opened parallel with the ground a meter above the surface. Thankfully, the crossing slowed his descent, and a fall from this height wasn’t lethal. However, with little time to brace himself, he slammed rudely into the dry, rocky earth.

His breath abandoned him as his mind sought to make sense of his immediate transition from “surely dead” to “alive but struggling for air.” Through blurry eyes, he stared, unfocused through a small cloud of billowing dust.

Risens lay motionless. Catching his breath, he worked through the pain that seemed to coat every fiber of his being. His hands, wrists, and the sliver of exposed skin around his eyes still stung from the searing heat of the explosion, and thin tendrils of smoke twisted up from several places along his clothing. A high-pitched, persistent scream sounded in his ears, shaking his focus and mental ability to diagnose his injuries.

His back ached from where he and the assassin were tossed mercilessly into the edge of the aqueduct. Though he doubted anything was broken, he steeled himself against the pain and rolled gingerly onto his back. Just beyond arm’s reach, the flickering void, the door that the Quillkey had opened, stared back at him. The darkness within was a different shade from the infinite void that surrounded the Barren. Gritting his teeth against the throbbing of his muscles, he closed the portal.

He would have no use for this doorway again. The thought of exiting the hanging portal was off-putting enough. The knowledge that it would take him back to a district that would soon be teeming with soldiers made its usage pointless. His passage back to Windwake would involve another trip through the Roost.

He only hoped he had closed the portal in time. If someone had spotted it, word of the mysterious void would spread like wildfire. It would reach the king’s ears in moments. His Majesty would find it difficult to connect Risens to it, having no knowledge that he had even returned to the city. He had been trained as an assassin, not a mage. It would be a stretch tying him with something more aptly associated with the work of a magus.

It was the Hunt’s behavior that was more puzzling at the moment. They had proven to be unbelievably well-informed and connected. Luckily, none of the killers sent for him would return to file a report. But how many blades and magi did they count among their numbers? How had their presence gone unnoticed for so long?

With their most recent attempt still likely fueling a simmering rage within their ranks, they had ambushed him again. The similarities with the first attempt in the hedge maze were too telling to overlook.

Another question danced in his mind. Had anyone remained at a distance, watching? Were they now en route to their puppet master? The only fact he was assured of was that none who bore their blades had lived to reveal any details of their demise.

Having been handed failure on three consecutive occasions, he expected that they were intelligent enough to realize that their preferred tactic of utilizing the Cimmerian Calcify to sedate their prey was wholly ineffective on him. Their tactics would no doubt shift, but to what devious ends? How many teams lie in wait for him at various waypoints throughout the city? The scope of their effort was impressive but also daunting.

Lathrenon’s alleged stranglehold on the martial forces of Halthome grew more questionable with every new revelation.

With the passage closed, Risens worked himself back to his feet, gently brushing free the dirt and crushed grass that clung to the fabric of his clothing. He stretched as he reached his feet, validating the supposition that while nothing seemed to be broken, virtually everything hurt. He’d survived far worse agony over the years. The immediate discomfort would fade over time and not slow him further.

Having thwarted the ambush, Risens would now return to his quest: the healer. Unfortunately, he was in no condition to embark upon it this day. The man who knew more than he should and divulged too much would live to see another sunrise. The district would likely be on lockdown for some time as the investigation into precisely what destroyed the aqueduct would unfold. The city guards would already be stretched thin, battling a stubborn alchemical fire in the Estates. And now, an unexpected event near the Center Ward?

For one who had sworn his life to defending Halthome, he had done more tangible damage this night alone than any plot against the king had accomplished during his lifetime.

Risen shook off the frustration as his thoughts shifted to the peculiarities that surrounded the evening. Attacks, traps, bloodshed, and death were nothing new to him, though the frequency of late was becoming tiresome. The most startling aspect of the evening, though, was something far less violent, and he was uncertain as to its inherent danger.

How had Aleth discovered the forbidden confines of the Raven’s Court? He was certain that there was no way she could have followed him. Her apparent surprise, curiosity, and investigation confirmed his assumption that she’d never seen the dilapidated space before that moment. If she hadn’t followed him there, how could she have found the Shrine? Why did the stars of fate align to lead her to that one decrepit square in the forbidden and forgotten corner of the city?

He ruled out chance, as it made no sense. It defied any sense of logic. She was led there by someone or something. Could she, too, hear the ominous voice that demanded his obedience? Perhaps it was the ravens that seemed to favor her?

His questions begged answers. The time between his departure from the Raven’s Court and the unplanned destruction of the aqueduct had not been long. He doubted more than an hour had passed. With time frozen here in the Barren as it did in the Roost, he expected that the youth hadn’t gone far, if she’d ever even left the shrine.

Since he would have to wait before tracking down the healer, his mind was made up. He would discover the truth behind the young girl’s trespass.

Subconsciously, his hands moved to the vial that hung around his neck, the narrow, capped tube that he’d filled with the blood of the carrion that lurked within the trials in the Under. Through the exposed glass, he could see that it remained roughly a quarter full and slightly warm to the touch. Unlike when he’d filled it, the heat wasn’t anywhere near the point where it radiated through his tunic clothing to the skin beneath.

He frowned at the holes in the thatched roof. The shattered panes of the windows were an eyesore as much as they would have been a security concern had he not been alone on this plane. The crooked door hung ajar, barely maintaining its hold on the hinges. The cosmetic repairs bothered him more than he expected. If he had the supplies lying around, he figured he could have fixed them himself. Supplies, no, but there was magic here at his disposal. The terrain, structure, and features were his to command, but at the present, the raw materials—namely the black blood of the carrion—were in short supply.

Risens understood that there were more pressing matters to deal with than simple, trivial repairs. He had profound, deliberate choices to make. Stopping in the center of the chamber, he stared at each of the doors as if searching for one to answer the dilemma that plagued his indecision. The quiet crackling of the small fire was the only sound to break the silence of the room as he considered his options. The unexpected trials and secrets of the Under, and more importantly, the danger, were real, yet the rewards seemed more uncertain. Yes, refurnishing his home and upgrading his facilities were important, but the true, unquestionable power that lurked within the hallowed halls of the Roost made the final, convincing argument. The lure of the hidden strength proved too great. There were many more trials, dozens of additional doors to be opened in the Roost, and potential powers to be earned.

He waved his hand and laughed to himself as the dark, endless cold, and pressure of the void opened before him. At least, this time, he would hold true to the promise he made to the ever-present stone ravens. If the darkened portals on the consecutive floors above held more skills or evolutions of the skills he now possessed, he had only just scratched the surface. As it was, he desired to return through the Roost to re-enter Windwake. The extra step would cost him effort and time, though both were expendable here.

The portal to the Barren had saved him from a certain, abrupt death, but his body throbbed with a dull pain. He was no stranger to fighting through pain and battling with injuries, so at the moment, the burned skin and bruises would do little to hinder him. A discrete visit to healer Tawny would likely mend the bulk of it, though he saw no need to make the trip.

He would need to see her soon to restock on supplies. But now, they would do a little good. He was content to explore the powers that awaited him in the Roost.

As expected, as soon as his boots touched solid ground within the Roost, the stoic stone ravens leveled a withering judgment on his decisions as they materialized from the darkness.

As with every visit to the Roost, his vision was drawn to the features of the sacred chamber. The massive Shrine loomed in the darkness at the end of the majestic hall, the dull, wavering light from the candles floating around each pillar casting vivid but grotesquely disfigured shadows. The impenetrable blackness above imparted a dizzying sense of unfathomable height as his mind challenged it for glimpses of shapes. Dozens of disguised entries remained undiscovered.

He suddenly felt crushed by the weight of his inadequacies. He had only just scratched the surface of the limitless powers that remained sealed, waiting for his challenge.

His gaze settled on the second floor, scanning the doorways with a renewed sense of interest. The Dull Wind that was at its command, even with its limitations in distance and application, would easily grant him access to at least a handhold on the ledge of the next level. Bringing delicate flames along with him would be arduous, though. He considered the options. He was certain that if he tried hard enough, he could find the means to light the shadows to illuminate the doorways concealed within, at least on the next level above. It would, however, take considerable time and effort.

Beyond the tangible cost, something about the prospect seemed unfounded. The feeling was pervasive. He expected the cost of cheating the system here would be severe. Risens had felt the sting of his failures on far too many occasions as he learned the method for solving the Roost’s puzzles. His limited experiences in the Under had implanted the idea in his mind that access would be granted when the previous test had been completed to fulfillment. Perhaps that was it? He was never meant to access the floors above this early in his development. Access would be granted when the time was right. When the tests had proven his worth. He eyed the shadowed doorways marking the first floor with added vigor.

The stone ravens offered neither words of encouragement nor disdain as he crossed to the base of the Shrine to collect one of the candles he’d stashed at its base. Their attention followed him as they rotated silently to track his movement.

Risens had no desire to brighten all of the shadowed doorways on the first floor to make his next choice. He cupped his hand, protecting the delicate flame as he moved to the next closest door to the right. There was no careful deliberation inthe choice. This time, he was content to attempt the feat purely for the means of evening out the number of completed passages on either side of the wall.

The instant the glow of the small flame touched the inky black shadows, they peeled away as ice melting before a fire. The face of the panel revealed was similar to its predecessors. It revealed no gaps or seams where the inset door met the frame. A rounded bead contained an elaborate set of etchings and decorations similar in design to the others. Running his hands over the surface, he found no keyholes that would grant easy access to the secrets behind. Again, as with the others, he could feel the power, the pull, and the lure of strength. That alone was enough to draw him forward.

Unlike the other stone panels that had blocked passage to their individual trials, the designs of this door featured several shapes that he recognized immediately. Though they were abstract, it was obvious that multiple birds decorated the face of this door.

Ravens.

There were five of them depicted in various positions, some flying while others perched. One held position at the center of the door’s arc, while the others were spread out, seemingly at random, on the stone canvas. Though it seemed innocuous, he knew there was a purpose built into the design. A crude rune carved into a small rectangular negative space amid the raised embellishments caught his eye.

Though still abstract, it looked like the rough outline of an eye staring back at him, following his every twitch. He stepped back, puzzling over the feature among the others. Each test had proven unique and somehow tied to the sacred ravens. It was a feather that held the answer. It had been used as a key, a pen, and a blade. In this situation, he failed to see how it would assist him with the challenge at hand. He suspected he’d bear some pain or discomfort before the answer was revealed.

Observing the door with a questioning gaze, he traced the swirling spiraling features. The shapes acted almost likebranches where the passing ravens could rest. The designs that had seemed purely decorative now appeared more intentional when viewed in the totality of the scene. Beneath each of the birds, several smaller groups of rounded objects were clumped together—berries or fruit, perhaps, hanging from the branches.

Inspecting the nearest one, he noted a slight variation in one of the berries. As gently as his finger brushed the design, a camouflaged button depressed with a quiet click.

The response should have been expected.

As if he’d been kicked by a horse, the wind was robbed from his lungs as an invisible blow struck him squarely in the chest. The unseen impact lifted him off his feet, sending him careening backward. He skidded across the stone floor, coming to an abrupt end against the base of one of the pedestals lining the central aisle of the room. The flapping of wings heralded the departure of a stone raven.

He worked to avert his gaze, focusing on the darkness overhead. Risens had no patience for the critical, mocking stares that the remaining stone idols would assuredly have leveled on his prostrated form. Here in the Roost, he was free from the prying eyes of other humans, though the solitude was a false front. The focus and intention of the sacrosanctchambers’ silent denizens was unending. His failures and successes were all on brutal display to a rapt audience.

The enduring soreness from his abrupt and lifesaving entrance into the Barren still lingered, the new discomfort adding yet another layer. Having regained his breath, Risens leveraged himself off the ground, maintaining his focus on the doorway as he stalked forward. He avoided looking back at what he knew to be an empty pedestal where a stone raven once perched.

The stone panel that held the test loomed over him. As frustrating as it was, Risens couldn’t help but feel a sense of satisfaction. He had long since passed every test that had resulted in him becoming not just an assassin, but the King’s Rightmaker. It had been a long time since he had been truly tested, mind and spirit. He would have preferred if none of them carried the weight of death as a consequence for failure, as with the ambushes he’d face of late, but he would welcome failure here in the Roost if it meant growth in the end.

His first attempted solution, inadvertent as it was, had been wrong, and he’d paid the price. It was clear that there was a button or a combination of buttons, though the sequence eluded him.

As he observed the door from a few paces back, he noted distinct differences in the ravens on the door. Each was unique in its design—all heads twisted in some manner or another. They might have appeared to be aesthetic choices by the designer, but Risens knew better. Risens followed the gaze of the bird alighting on a branch at the peak of the door. He traced its focus, angled down toward the lower-left quadrant of the stone panel.

He grinned as he realized that the attention of each bird was aligned. All pointed roughly toward the same small bunch of berries carved into the stone. Even with the expectation that his assumption was correct, he clenched the muscles in his core as he pushed the hidden button.

Risens relaxed as a distant, hollow click sounded through the room, thankfully unaccompanied by pain. The grating of stone against stone rumbled as the panel slid into the wall.

As if the stone was a dam that had held the strength contained within the trial at bay, he was taken aback by the force of the sensation. A swell of power rolled over him as he stared into the void of the portal.

Another skill was at hand. It was his time to take it.
