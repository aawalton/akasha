As soon as Arthur stabbed the neck of the second archer using the tusk he got from the Grubtusk monster from behind, the annoying remnant started yapping. Not the cool, ironic kind of yapping. The original, soul-draining kind that made you want to walk into traffic.

Drogan wouldn't stop. He railed about how Arthur's actions were dishonorable, how they brought shame to his legacy. Drogan even called Arthur a coward of the highest order and ordered him to duel his opponents like a true bearer of a Legendary Class should.

Of course, Arthur wasn’t suicidal. He ignored every word. In fact, he’d been training his mind over the past few days to selectively mute Drogan’s voice, for sanity’s sake. The background drone of the meathead's outrage was almost comforting now. Predictable. Like white noise, if white noise constantly accused you of being a disgrace.

With the archers down, Arthur crouched low and scanned the battlefield a few dozen yards in front of him. The dual-sword warrior, Yudursh was holding his own against five warriors. His stance was disciplined, defensive, but his blades carved the air with lethal precision. Each stroke a rehearsed beat in a deadly war dance. The sixth warrior stood a bit away, protecting a robed figure that practically screamed "mage." Arthur’s eyes narrowed. That left one person unaccounted for.

A quick pulse of Mana Sight confirmed it. A rogue was creeping through the underbrush, weaving toward the injured princess. She had already yanked the arrow out of her abdomen and was attempting to patch herself up with raw mana.

Arthur reached into his storage ring.

He released three slumbering Stoneclaw Howlers, nasty ape-like monsters that stood just over six feet tall when upright. Their muscular frames were covered in coarse, slate-colored fur interwoven with jagged patches of compacted stone. Their disproportionately long arms ended in claws as sharp as obsidian blades, capable of tearing through bone and armor with terrifying ease.

But their most terrifying feature wasn't their claws. It was their howl. Infused with mana and channeled through a naturally reinforced chest cavity, the sound they produced was something between a warhorn and an earthquake.

Arthur didn’t even need to goad them toward the battle. The metallic clash of swords and cries of pain was enough. The Stoneclaws awoke with snarls, scanned the environment, and within seconds unleashed a synchronized, earsplitting howl.

The sonic wave blasted across the field like a pressure bomb. Everyone, even hardened warriors recoiled as their eardrums shattered. Most of them dropped to their knees, weapons clattering uselessly to the forest floor. The rear guard, the mage and the warrior had no chance. Before they could even scream, the Howlers were on them. Claws flashed. Flesh tore. Blood sprayed in arcs across the mossy ground.

Arthur, standing behind the monsters, was shielded from the full brunt of the howl. His ears rang, but he stayed upright. As usual, quick thinking and tactical positioning gave him the upper hand.

He weaved around the monsters that were now dismembering the mage and rear guard and sprinted toward the princess.

Ysara was on one knee, blood still trickling down her side despite her best healing efforts. Her face was pale, but her hands moved with trained precision as she pressed against the wound.

Arthur’s gaze found the rogue. He was still moving, but the sonic blast had left him unsteady. The man was limping, sword raised, but only a step away from Ysara. Arthur wasn't about to let some half-deaf assassin steal his thunder.

He surged mana to his brain. His thoughts accelerated. The world slowed.

He saw the rogue take another step, short sword angled for a thrust. Yudursh, behind him, was staggering upright, trying to intercept.

Arthur moved.

Mana Body. Legs first.

He crossed five steps in a blur, then pumped mana into his upper body. The dull, weathered tusk cut through the air, its coarse surface stained from past kills, as he brought it down in a brutal arc, right into the back of the rogue’s skull.

The sound was sickening. Bone shattered. The tusk collided with the rogue’s skull in a splintering explosion of bone on bone. Both giving way in a brutal, mutual shatter that Arthur hadn’t anticipated. Arthur had expected the rogue to crumple forward, fatally injured, and crash into the recovering Yudursh. Clean and efficient.

But the impact was anything but clean. The force transferred through the rogue’s body like a hammer strike, sending both of them flying. The force of the rogue’s body struck Yudursh like a battering ram, knocking him clean off his feet.

Arthur didn’t get off clean either. The counterforce was brutal.

His arm detonated with pain. He felt bone after bone give way as his elbow, wrist, and forearm shattered under the strain.. He’d fortified his core, shoulder, and back with mana, but had forgotten the arm. Idiot move.

Pain flared through his nervous system like a lightning bolt. His enhanced brain, still in overdrive, amplified the sensation. A scream almost tore itself from his throat.

But he didn’t black out.

Instead, he gritted his teeth and triggered Mana Heal. Mana surged to his mangled limb, knitting together some of the worst damage. The pain dimmed. His vision cleared. Not a full second had passed.

He stepped forward and reached out with his good arm, his left, placing a hand on Ysara’s shoulder just as she turned toward the rogue, drawn by the noise. Before she could react, Arthur gave the mental command. A flash of light, and she vanished into his storage ring.

No time to breathe. He turned and summoned a full-sized Grubtusk Devourer between himself, Yudursh, and the warriors behind them.

The lumbering beast screeched and immediately drew attention.

Arthur didn’t wait to see how they reacted.

He admired Yudursh's skill. The man was a fighter’s fighter - disciplined, powerful, probably loyal to the bone. But to Arthur, the princess is part of the ruling machine that built its empire on the backs of slaves. Her father was a king, one who didn’t give a damn about the people dying in mines.

So maybe the attackers had a point. Maybe the people ambushing the princess were actually the heroes.

Didn’t matter.

His life came first. Always had. Always would.

With those thoughts anchoring him, Arthur shoved mana into his legs and bolted into the forest.

He didn’t look back.

Branches whipped against his skin. Adrenaline burned in his veins. His broken arm throbbed with every step, but he didn’t stop.

Somewhere behind him, he heard the Howlers shriek again. Another sonic blast rippled through the forest. Trees trembled. Birds scattered.

Arthur ran until the sound faded.
