As much as part of Don’s brain was excited by the prospect, he tossed the notion. There had to be a better way. First thing that Don decided to do was to find a door to the king’s hall.

He stealthily moved around in the labyrinthine castle. He ran into two more pairs of guards and fought them fiercely. He was getting a hang of how the hobgoblins moved and reacted, and the kills were smoother this time.

These guys are definitely seasoned fighters. They’re as powerful as the bears, but they use tactics.

Don was sure fighting more than four of them in an open space would be a struggle. Five would be dangerous and six a nasty death.

Don passed another flaming brazier in the corner and he stopped by it. The smoke was trailing upwards into some sort of chimney in the roof. Half of the smoke trailed around in the room. Don’s physique was apparently good enough to scoff off a little smoke with a side of carbon monoxide. He assumed that applied to the hobgoblins too.

But the question is… What about fire?

He couldn’t take out thirty six hobgoblins. But a fire in a castle made of wood could. Don imagined the experience drop from such a slaughter. It would be amazing.

But such a plan was risky. He would need a plan of escape, or be consumed by the fire himself. And the Grimoids could also stop the fire if it wasn’t well planned. That was assuming the fire was flammable in the first place. It was a small miracle that the wooden castle full of open fires was still there.

With his mind, Don levitated one of the coals from the brazier. He dropped it on the floor. The floor smouldered, but didn’t really catch fire. Don blew on the hot coal. A bit of an ember started, but it didn’t catch on. The flame died, but the floorboard kept blackening.

Fire resistant wood? At the same time it made total sense and no sense at all.

Okay, no setting fires. Luring them into the corridors is still plan A.

Don wandered around and killed another pair. This time he tried something different. As the braziers were located in every corner, Don started pelting the Grimoids with high-speed hot coals. Turned out the green bastards were not impervious to fire. That was good intel. It meant that if he managed to get something flammable in their vicinity, he could definitely damage the enemy, or at least distract them.

I’m also starting to grow an appreciation towards projectile weapons.

The kills had been smooth. While the Grimoid hobgoblins were clearly able to push through pretty severe pain, the hot coals crashing into their shoulders definitely damaged them. Half a dozen of coals softened them up as they charged at Don, resulting in easy kills.

He passed through another junction and ducked under a slanted support beam. The passage beyond was tighter. No torches. No light. Just the faint shimmer of smoke clinging to the walls. Don activated his Camouflage and slowed down. His instincts prickled at him.

He found himself in a messy kitchen. It was well equipped, as one would expect of any castle. Messy pots and pans piled on top of each other, tables full of flour and meat that smelled a tad bit funky. Don took a closer look and found it was covered in maggots. He swallowed some bile and was glad he hadn’t eaten anything for a long while.

There was a heavy cleaver jutting from one of the tables. Just for fun Don picked it up and tried a few hacks at the table with it. It cleaved through with ease. Don wasn’t surprised, his stats were superhuman at this point. But then he flicked his claws open and took a swipe at the table, it absolutely exploded.

Yeah… I’ll stick to my claws for now.

Don explored around, but the kitchen was empty, apart from a few flickering torches on sconces. Then he found an old and heavy wooden door. The edges were warped, like it had been repaired a dozen times with scavenged timber. He pressed himself against the wall beside it and listened.

Sounds of drinking, laughing, fighting. Definitely the king’s hall. This door was connecting the kitchen and the hall.

Don didn’t open it. Not yet. But he marked it mentally. That was the side entrance. Much safer approach than a big mess hall door. If he could pull a few hobgoblins through that door and bottle them in the hallway, he could chip away at the numbers.

He needed to set up some kind of funnel. Make them come to him in manageable chunks. Or rig the terrain. There were plenty of braziers and beams and half-collapsed walls around. He just had to think like a trapper, not a brawler.

That wasn’t exactly Don’s forte. He preferred a straight forward fight. But this time it wasn’t an option, and either he would need to adapt or back down.

And Don definitely wasn’t backing down.

Could I trap the monsters inside the hall? And maybe use fire? There has to be one more entrance. There is no way that king would even fit through this door, let alone deign to use a kitchen entrance.

Two doors wasn’t ideal for trapping. But if he could find the main door and barricade it, he could funnel the hobgoblins through the kitchen door if he gave them a reason to move.

That’s a lot of ifs…

And even if he could funnel the hobgoblins, fighting dozens of them would be a problem, even if he could manage the amount he fought at once. He would tire out if he had no chance to Devour between fights.

This was a difficult boss room, that was for sure. Don hoped the reward was matchingly interesting.

But if Don couldn’t set the place on fire, he needed another advantage. But what?

Then Don’s eye caught the torches on the wall. His mouth slowly slackened.

“Oh…”

There were torches and braziers everywhere. Around every corner. And Don was sure it wasn’t for the goddamn ambiance.

The Grimoids couldn’t see in the dark. But Don could.

He grinned. Okay, now this was an advantage he could work with. The first thing he did was open up the Evolution interface. He immediately upgraded Night Vision.

[180 Evolution Points spent]

[Evolution Upgraded!]

[Night Vision (E-grade)] -> [Night Vision (D-grade)]

[Night Vision (D-grade)]: Your darkvision has significantly improved. You no longer need external light to see in the darkness. Your ocular tissue has transformed into that of a true Evolutor[+3 to Mental]

Well that was something else. He hadn’t seen an upgrade text change so dramatically before. Suddenly his eyes dimmed and Don was blind. He didn’t panic, he knew this was some bullshit System needed to reboot his eyes for lack of a better description. Indeed in a few seconds he blinked and saw again.

Everything looked relatively the same, except the dark corners of the room were now visible to him. Don walked up to one of the torches. He didn’t want to put it out with his hands. He scanned the room and saw a water bucket.

That was good, but Don wasn’t about to carry that bucket around to put out every torch and brazier in the castle. He needed something else.

So he tried Telekinesis. It was a bit of stretch of the imagination, but he had a plan. He started forming a sphere around the torch’s outline. Then he compressed the sphere gradually tighter and tighter. It took some time and his concentration broke. It was hard and he had to visualize very clearly what he wanted to do. He could feel it working through his pineal gland. The psychic energy was condensing around the torch. If he could form a seal of psychic energy around the torch, it would run out of air. Finally he got the form right and it solidified. Within a few seconds the torch snuffed out.

Don grinned. Okay, now he had all the tools he needed. This would be a great way to train his Telekinesis. He would put out all of the torches and braziers in this goddamn castle, and then he would watch from the shadows as the enemy scrambled. One by one the lights would die. He would have the advantage, and slowly but surely he would snuff the castle out.
