It took them a while to find a secluded place for Pampli.Gilbert led them around the edge of the forest for a long time, looking for something, until he finally stopped near a low slope overgrown with moss and twisted bushes. The ground here was hidden under thick leaves, and above it hung a whole canopy of branches. If you did not know what to look for, your eyes would slide right past it.

“Here,” Gilbert said shortly.

Pampli gave an expressive snort, already unhappy with the whole idea.

“Yes, I can see you don’t like it,” Gilbert replied dryly. “But you’ll have to deal with it.”

Pampli grumbled something low under his breath and turned away. Sakura stepped closer and gently ran her hand along his neck.

“It won’t be long,” she said quietly. “I promise. You’ll be able to sleep, Pampli. And when you wake up, we’ll already be back.”

Pampli glanced at her. There was so much offense in that look that Sakura almost found it funny for a second, if not for the heaviness still sitting inside her.

Gilbert was already breaking long branches and dragging them toward the hiding place.

“Lie down,” he ordered.

Pampli did not move.

“Lie down,” Gilbert repeated, now with the kind of tone that cut off any argument.

Pampli let out a noisy sigh and finally lay down among the moss and bushes, tucking his paws under himself with clear resentment. Gilbert quickly covered him with leaves and flexible branches, concealed the saddle, and adjusted the moss along his side. A minute later, the beast looked less like a predatory obsidian creature and more like a very suspicious mound.

“There,” Gilbert said, stepping back. “That’s enough.”

Sakura touched his muzzle one more time.

“Sweet dreams, Pampli. I hope no one bothers you.”

After that, Sakura and Gilbert continued on alone. The mage did not lead her along the direct path, but by strange detours through hills and thick brush. Sakura had already started thinking they were straying far too much from the road when Gilbert suddenly stopped and motioned for her to crouch down.

“Look,” he said quietly.

They were standing on the crest of one of the hills. Below them stretched a wide plain, and beyond it, Ogrom. At first Sakura looked only at the city itself. At its gray, crooked outline. At the dark roofs and the wall, patched together with whatever materials they could find. But a second later, she realized that was not what Gilbert was showing her.

Below, nearer the city approaches, obsidian creatures were moving.

Sakura froze, peering harder. Some of them vanished among the rocks and dry brush, but she could make out several clearly. Her gaze caught on a black creature crawling along a shallow dip in the ground. Farther off, she noticed another obsidian monster rubbing itself against a dark boulder. And then there was a third, moving farther out, almost by the road. They all kept close to Ogrom, as if something were hiding there that they could not reach.

“There are... so many of them,” Sakura breathed out.

“Too many,” Gilbert confirmed darkly. “They never used to come this close in numbers like this.”

Sakura felt the chill move down her spine again. One of the creatures below had really stopped and lifted its head. As if it were scenting the air.

“Can it feel us?” Sakura asked in almost a whisper.

“Possibly.”

Sakura ducked lower at once. The creature below jerked its head again, then slowly turned in their direction. Sakura could already almost feel those red eyes lifting straight toward them. Gilbert reacted instantly. He caught her by the elbow and pulled her down the hill.

“Move.”

They got off the crest so fast that Sakura nearly slipped twice on the loose earth. Only when they were lower, under the cover of stones and brush, did Gilbert slow slightly.

“Why are they gathering around Ogrom?” Sakura asked dully.

Gilbert did not answer at once.

“I don’t know the exact answer. There are too many possibilities. Personally, I think there’s something under Ogrom.”

They reached the gates of Ogrom about twenty minutes later.

The closer they got, the worse everything looked. The wall was high, but it did not look reliable. In some places the old stone held on by itself, in others it had been braced with wooden supports, and elsewhere they had simply hammered fresh boards over the cracks instead of repairing them properly. People crowded around the gate. Some were trying to drag carts piled with junk through it. And right there on the wall and on the wooden boards near the entrance were posted notices.

Wanted posters.

Sakura saw her own first and held her breath. Then Gilbert’s. Both portraits had been drawn close enough to the truth. For a second, it felt like the end, like they were about to be seized right here. But then she remembered that the disguise from the strange flowers was still hiding them. And that the gray ointment was on their wrists. That calmed her a little.

“Don’t stare,” Gilbert said quietly, barely moving his lips. “That gives you away faster than anything.”

“I’m not staring.”

It was noisy around the gate, where two armed guards stood by the open doors and two more waited off to the side under a small awning. One of them, young and with a face so stupid and lazy it was almost impressive, gave them a slow look.

“What do you want?”

Gilbert changed at once. His shoulders loosened, and his eyes took on a murky sort of insolence. He even twisted his mouth like he was already a little drunk, though just moments ago he had been the most focused man within a mile.

“What, can’t a man visit friends in Ogrom anymore?” he drawled lazily. “Or did they tell you to turn away all decent folk now?”

The gate guard snorted.

“You don’t look decent.”

“I’m not trying to,” Gilbert shot back. “I’ve just got a little business. For friends. Maybe friends of friends.”

He gave the bag over his shoulder a meaningful shift, and the gate guard’s eyes flicked to it at once.

Sakura caught the tone and played along too, trying to sound tired and slightly irritated.

“We dragged ourselves through that cursed forest for half a day. If you tell us we can’t come in now, I’ll sit down right here and ruin your whole day with my singing.”

The gate guard stared at her, then suddenly huffed.

“Pretty face, though.”

“Thank you,” Sakura said with the most exhausted expression she could manage.

Gilbert leaned in a little closer to the guard.

“We’ll be quick. Go in, find our people, leave something useful, and go. Then on the way back, maybe something useful will fall your way too.”

Something childish and greedy flickered over the guard’s face.

“Mmm. And what would that be?”

“Something fun. I promise you’ll like it,” Gilbert said. “I’m not about to explain all the details right here at the gate.”

Sakura barely stopped herself from looking at him in open surprise. He played this so naturally, as if he had spent his whole life talking to low-level guards at filthy city gates and hinting about drugs and booze.

The guard scratched his cheek.

“And what are you called?”

The question sounded lazy, almost asked out of habit. Sakura tensed, but Gilbert only smirked.

“Today? Whatever you like.”

The guard beside him laughed. The younger one rolled his eyes, but without any real irritation. He clearly had no intention of conducting a serious search.

“All right. Get in. Just don’t cause trouble.”

“We don’t bring trouble,” Gilbert said. “We cure it.”

Sakura coughed lightly into her fist to hide a nervous laugh.

They were let in.

Ogrom was so filthy it looked like the city had been built in a swamp. Underfoot was a sludge of mud, water, and garbage. Most of the houses were stone, but every one of them looked like it was living out its last years. Cracks in the walls were propped up with wooden beams. Crooked additions clung to some buildings, and on others whole extra levels had grown out of boards, ladders, and makeshift awnings. Different flags hung between windows and beams: patchwork ones, striped ones, some with embroidered signs Sakura did not understand.

She looked up.

“What are those?”

“Markers,” Gilbert said quietly. “They tell the right people what can be found in a house. The kind of things you won’t see sold openly at the market...”

Sakura lowered her gaze, understanding what he meant.

All kinds of human wreckage crowded the streets. There were beggars, drunks, street kids, and panhandlers everywhere. And not one of them looked like a law-abiding citizen.

At the same time, there were too many guards too. They stood at crossroads and around the main street. The people of Ogrom clearly hated that. Enough that someone decided to express it by throwing a stone at one guard’s face. A commotion broke out immediately.

“It’s fine,” Gilbert tossed to Sakura.

“Oh sure. That’s exactly what it looks like.”

“Just act like all of this disgusts you, but you’re used to it.”

“That’s a very specific emotion.”

“It’s the perfect one for Ogrom.”

He tugged her by the sleeve toward the nearest stall, which looked more like a hole in the wall with a crooked counter. It smelled like cheap alcohol even before they got close. Gilbert quickly bought a clay bottle of cloudy rotgut, uncorked it, took several swallows, and then splashed some on his collar and sleeve.

“The smell,” he explained, handing the bottle to Sakura. “We need the right smell.”

Sakura grimaced, but drank too. Her throat burned instantly.

“That’s disgusting.”

“Yeah. Better not think too hard about what this swill is made from.”

Sakura spilled a little on her cloak and sleeve too. The stink that rose from it was appropriate enough.

“Perfect,” Gilbert said.

Sakura looked around.

“What now?”

“We blend in with the locals. We find out where the local enforcer sleeps. Then we kidnap him.”

Sakura nodded silently, then said on her own,

“I can try to ask someone from the gutter crowd without making it obvious.”

She did not wait for Gilbert’s answer or approval.

She glanced around. In one of the alleys, three suspicious-looking men were hanging around. One sat on an overturned crate, the other two stood beside him, clearly occupied with nothing honest. They were talking lazily and watching the street.

Sakura walked up to them, not too fast, but not timidly either.

“Boys,” she said, trying to sound like she did not care about anything at all, “interested in something that’ll wake you right up?”

One of the men snorted at once. The other two laughed. They looked Sakura over with poisonous curiosity.

“And what do you have?”

Sakura gave a vague shrug.

“Plenty. I’d show you right away, but... I’m not sure that damned enforcer won’t show up here.”

One of the men snorted as if she had just said the dumbest thing he had heard all day. The second let out a hoarse laugh.

“The enforcer? Here?”

“He wouldn’t set foot in this hole even for half a sack of silver. He’s sleeping somewhere in the Drunken Lady and not wasting a thought on people like us.”

Sakura understood at once that he meant some kind of place. Most likely a tavern.

“Wonderful!” Sakura said then.

She held their gaze for another second, as if she really was about to reach for her goods. Then she suddenly froze, slipped a hand into her pocket, and her whole expression changed.

“You’ve got to be kidding me...” she breathed out.

The men looked at her with interest. Sakura started patting her sides, her belt, checked her pocket again, and a second later threw up her hands.

“Oh, you little bastard!”

She spun toward the street as if searching the crowd.

“That filthy rat! He cleaned me out! I’ll kill him...”

One of the men burst out laughing.

“Who?”

“The one who lifted everything off me!” Sakura snapped, now fully in character. “That little worm! My money, my goods! Took all of it! I’ll... I’ll wrap his guts around his neck!”

The one sitting on the crate raised his brows.

“Welcome to Ogrom. You should’ve been paying more attention, girl.”

“Yeah, thanks for the advice!” Sakura shot back. “And if I find the little thief, don’t you dare stop me. He’s mine.”

She was already backing off sharply, still angry, still visibly worked up.

“Hey, so what did you have?” one of the men called after her, now more curious than serious.

Sakura only waved a hand bitterly.

“I had it,” she threw back. “Now I don’t.”

Sakura left the alley quickly without looking back and turned the corner, where Gilbert was already waiting for her, leaning against a crooked wall as naturally as if he had simply been bored and drinking his swill. He looked at her with a question he did not bother hiding very well.

“Well?”

“We need to go to the Drunken Lady,” Sakura said, lowering her voice. “They said our enforcer is sleeping there.”

Gilbert narrowed his eyes slightly.

“That worked well.”

“I hope so.”

Gilbert pushed off the wall.

“Then let’s go.”

They moved farther along the street, trying not to rush, but not to linger either. Ogrom only grew worse the longer Sakura looked at it. The mud underfoot sucked at the soles of her shoes like a bog. Rotten water stank from beneath the crooked wooden walkways.

“There,” Gilbert said quietly.

Sakura followed his gaze. She would have recognized the tavern even without the sign. The building stood a little apart, wider and taller than the ones beside it, with a crooked addition on the left and a heavy door that looked like it was opened with a boot more often than with a hand. Above the entrance hung a sign with a warped image of a crowned lady holding a mug. The paint had peeled, but the name could still be read. The Drunken Lady.

Gilbert quickly checked the entrance, the street, and the nearest windows.

“Don’t stand there like a post. We’re going in,” he muttered.

The door swung open heavily. Inside, the noise hit Sakura all at once. Voices, laughter, and someone cursing rolled over her in a wave. In the dimness of the tavern, everything shifted and blurred together like one huge filthy mass. People crowded around the tables and the walls. Some were already drunk enough to look like furniture, while others were only just getting started. But most of them had not gathered here to drink. They were here for the spectacle.

Everyone was staring toward the center of the room. Sakura looked there too.

A large wheel stood in the middle of the tavern.

It had a wooden rim reinforced with iron. Around the circle, rough marks and numbers had been burned into the wood. On some, symbols had been added, and on others, short words Sakura did not have time to make out at once.

A man was tied to that wheel.

A man in dark guard clothes, already half-open and disheveled but still recognizable. His hands and feet were bound tightly with straps. His head lolled, but he was alive. For now. His face was bruised, his hair plastered down with sweat.

The wheel was turning slowly.

Sakura did not understand how at first. Then she noticed the short lever at the base and two cackling men who were taking turns kicking it, spinning the wheel faster. The tied man’s head snapped and swung with it, and each new turn sent another wave of delight through the crowd.

“Ohhh, there he goes! There he goes!”

“Nine! I’m betting on nine!”

Sakura felt her stomach drop.

“What the...”

Gilbert froze too, but only for a fraction of a second. Then his face went unreadable again, though Sakura caught the way his jaw tightened.

One of the spectators, already drunk to the point of blissful madness, raised his mug over his head and bellowed:

“Now we’ll find out if our enforcer survives!”

The crowd burst into laughter.

Sakura forced herself to look away from the spinning wheel and glanced at Gilbert.

“Looks like we found the enforcer.”
