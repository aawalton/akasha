Arthur reviewed his notification log, brushed himself off, and turned his gaze to the Grubtusk corpse - specifically, the thick slabs of meat still steaming faintly.

His stomach growled.His mouth? Full-on betrayal mode.Compared to moss, this thing looked like a five-star steak dripping with mana-rich gravy.

Maybe just a bite.

“Don’t even think about it,” came Drogan’s voice, dry and disgusted. “Unless you’re aiming to spend the rest of your short life screaming on the floor while your guts liquefy.”

Arthur froze mid-reach.

“What? It looks edible.”

“Only if you’re a C-grade chef with advanced mana manipulation and a death wish. Wild monster meat is toxic unless purified properly. That kind of alchemy takes decades to master and you can’t even fry moss.”

Arthur let out a disappointed grunt and stood back up.

“There are domesticated beasts bred for consumption,” Drogan added. “You’ll find them once you reach civilization, assuming you don’t die in the forest sniffing raw meat.”

Arthur sighed. “So… moss it is.”

“For now,” Drogan said. “But if you’re done drooling, how about you harvest the damn core? It’s worth more than your life and actually useful. Potion-makers pay good coin for those.”

Arthur narrowed his eyes. Opportunity knocked.

“Teacher, would you be so kind as to guide me in the sacred art of core harvesting… and perhaps the noble alchemy of potion-making?”

Drogan made a noise that sounded dangerously close to a sniff.“So now you acknowledge me as your teacher?”

“I always did! From day one. How could I not learn from someone as wise and powerful as you?” Arthur replied with a straight face and a tongue so firmly in his cheek it nearly dislocated.

Drogan huffed, clearly appeased.“I’ll teach you to harvest the core. As for making potions, please. I had minions for that sort of grunt work. I never lowered myself to learn it.”

“Thank you, Teacher,” Arthur said, bowing theatrically. At least in his head.

“First,” Drogan said, “use Mana Sight on the corpse. You’ll see a bright lump inside. Low-tier monsters have small, irregular cores. Higher-tier ones have big, perfect spheres. It usually takes a week or two to train your eyes well enough to see one, but knowing you... give it a couple seconds.”

Arthur focused. He activated the skill. It pulsed.

Mana Sight leveled up to Level 26.

“I see it. Back of the skull, small and glowing. What next?”

“…Wait, seriously? You actually saw it? Hells, you really are a freak. I was joking when I said seconds. Who spots mana cores on their first try?!”

He grumbled something unintelligible and added,“Now you carve, genius. Try not to butcher it. Get the core out whole or it loses value. Don’t ask why. I never listened to those alchemy nerds.”

Arthur drew one of the bloodied tusks and carved through flesh and bone with slow, careful strokes, guiding the tusk until the core slipped free. It was a small brown lump, warm to the touch, humming faintly with mana.

It also looked, disturbingly edible.

A strange urge whispered in his mind: Eat it.

He resisted the urge barely. And tucked the core into his ring instead.

Later, by the creek, he rinsed the gore away, cradled the warm lump in his hand, and activated Identify.

> Common Monster CoreGrade: ERich in Earth Mana

Identify leveled up to Level 2.

And then guided by an instinct too sharp to ignore, one that had served him far better than hesitation ever had. Arthur calmly popped the core into his mouth and swallowed.

Drogan’s horror was immediate. “You what?! Are you trying to sabotage your foundation? There are impurities! Side effects! You absolute cretin!”

Arthur wasn’t listening. Not really. Most of his brain was watching the core inside his stomach.

Now that it was in his body, something remarkable happened. He could manipulate the mana within it.

He pulled the earth mana into his spark. His depleted pool, sitting at 82/200, rapidly refilled to full. The spark absorbed more earth mana, and with a pulse of warmth...

> +1 IntelligenceMana Pool increased to 210

There was still mana left. Acting on instinct, Arthur wrapped the remaining energy around the core’s impurities. He guided them upward. Stomach to throat and then...

Buuuurp.

The air turned foul. A sulfurous death-gas that probably offended gods.

“What in the nine burning hells just came out of your mouth?”

Drogan was torn between scolding and slow-clapping. “I genuinely can’t decide if you’re a revolting little swamp rat or a mana-handling savant.”

Arthur gave no response.

Outwardly, anyway.

But inside?

He was grinning.

Praise from Drogan, even when wrapped in insults, still meant something.

Arthur turned toward the forest, eyes sharp and focused. Now, monster cores weren’t just loot.

They were fuel. Power. Potential.

And he was just getting started.
