The horrific, grinding arithmetic of the siege was slowly turning against Ashenfall.

An hour had passed since Brett had obliterated the first fleshy ramp at the main gates, but the brief, euphoric relief of that victory was long dead. The defenders on the upper timber ramparts were rapidly bleeding their stamina into the freezing winter wind.

Muscles that had been coiled tight with adrenaline were now trembling with deep, lactic acid burns. The civilian archers were drawing their bowstrings with agonising slowness, their fingers raw and bleeding from the coarse hemp. The lower-tier casters had retreated from the wooden parapets, sitting slumped against the inner barricades, their faces deathly pale as they suffered the nauseating, dizzying effects of severe mana depletion.

The undead horde, however, did not experience fatigue. They did not feel the freezing cold, and they certainly did not care about the catastrophic losses they were taking.

They simply kept marching into the meat grinder.

The alchemical pitch trenches below were now entirely useless, smothered completely by a solid, smoking carpet of charred corpses. The golden dust of the destroyed dungeon beasts mixed sickeningly with the blackened blood and rotting meat of the natural dead, creating a foul, glittering slurry that coated the base of the thirty-foot timber walls.

And new ramps were forming.

The zombies were piling up against the solid oak logs with relentless, mindless determination. They used the ruined, burning bodies of their comrades as stepping stones, scrambling wildly over shattered skulls and severed limbs to reach the defenders above.

"Keep the masonry falling!" Captain Aris roared, his voice completely hoarse from shouting over the deafening din of the horde. He swung his heavy broadsword, cleanly decapitating a Crypt Ghoul that had managed to hook its jagged claws over the edge of the parapet. "Do not let them establish a foothold!"

Josh stood near the main portcullis, his chest heaving with ragged gasps beneath his heavy steel plate armour. His left arm, carrying the immense weight of the dark heater shield, ached with a dull, persistent fire. He hurled another massive, jagged chunk of limestone over the edge, listening for the sickening crunch of bone as it struck the writhing mass below.

He wiped a thick smear of sweat and black soot from his forehead with the back of his gauntlet, glancing to his right.

Roughly forty yards down the defensive line, away from the heavily fortified epicentre of the main gates, the situation was rapidly deteriorating.

This section of the wall was manned primarily by conscripted militia members. They were local bakers, young stable hands, and terrified merchants holding crude, unsharpened iron spears. They lacked the heavy boulders and the high-tier magical support of the central gatehouse. They were relying entirely on their shaky spear thrusts and desperate courage to keep the horrors at bay.

And the ramp of bruised, purple flesh beneath them was rising at a terrifying, unchecked pace.

A young human boy, perhaps barely seventeen years old, stood at the edge of the wooden parapet. He wore a heavy, oversized boiled leather tunic that was clearly designed for a larger man. His face was devoid of colour, his eyes wide and unblinking as he stared down at the shrieking, rotting faces climbing towards him. He gripped the wooden shaft of his spear so tightly his knuckles were stark white.

"Hold the line, lad," an older, retired adventurer standing next to him offered, his voice a low, comforting rumble. "Thrust for the eyes or the throat. Do not let go of your weapon."

The highest point of the writhing meat ramp finally crested the thirty-foot mark.

A massive, bloated zombie, its flesh entirely corrupted into a bruised, weeping shade of purple, threw itself wildly over the wooden railing. It did not attempt to climb gracefully. It simply launched its heavy, rotting bulk directly towards the line of terrified milit militiamen.

The young boy reacted with blind, panicked instinct. He let out a sharp, terrified cry and thrust his spear forward.

The strike was remarkably accurate. The heavy iron spearhead struck the diving zombie perfectly in the centre of its chest, punching cleanly through the rotting sternum and protruding a full foot out of its back.

For a normal, living creature, the blow would have been instantly fatal. The kinetic force should have stopped the attacker dead in its tracks.

But the undead monstrosity did not feel the shattered bone, nor did it care about the ruined organs in its chest.

The zombie hit the wooden deck of the walkway with a heavy, wet thud, the wooden shaft of the spear still firmly lodged in its torso. The young boy, holding the other end of the weapon, let out a breathless gasp of relief, believing he had killed the beast.

He was horrifyingly wrong.

The zombie did not stop. It locked its glowing, milky eyes onto the young boy. It grabbed the wooden shaft of the spear with both of its rotting, jagged-clawed hands. Then, with a sickening, wet tearing sound, the monster forcefully drove its own body further down the length of the wooden pole.

It used the spear shaft as a rail, impaling itself deeper and deeper, ignoring the thick, black blood pouring from its chest, simply to close the distance to the terrified boy holding the weapon.

"Let go!" the older adventurer screamed, swinging his rusted sword.

But the boy was completely frozen in primal terror. He could not comprehend what he was seeing. He stood entirely paralysed as the rotting monster dragged itself up the spear shaft, its jaw unhinging to reveal rows of cracked, yellowed teeth.

The zombie lunged the final two feet.

It clamped its jaws brutally around the young boy's exposed throat. The horrific sound of tearing flesh and snapping cartilage echoed loudly over the chaos of the wall. A thick, arterial spray of bright crimson blood erupted across the wooden floorboards, splashing heavily against the faces of the nearby militia.

The boy let out a wet, gargling scream, his hands falling away from the spear as he collapsed backwards onto the walkway, the monster crashing down on top of him, tearing savagely at his neck.

The psychological impact of the gruesome, unstoppable death was absolutely devastating.

The men and women standing around the boy recoiled violently. They scrambled backwards, some of their crude spears dropping from their trembling hands. The fragile, desperate courage that had kept them anchored to the parapet completely shattered. They stumbled away from the edge, their eyes wide with sheer, unfiltered panic.

"Hold your ground!" the retired adventurer roared, desperately trying to rally the breaking line, slicing his sword heavily into the back of the feeding zombie.

But the momentary gap in the defensive line was all the horde needed.

Without the constant thrusting of spears to keep them at bay, the writhing pile of flesh below surged violently upward. Three ghouls scrambled wildly over the wooden railing, their jagged claws scraping against the timber. They were immediately followed by a massive, skeletal hound and half a dozen rotting villagers.

The breach was instantly established.

The undead poured over the wall like foul, black water bursting through a cracked dam. They shrieked and snapped, throwing themselves directly into the terrified, retreating ranks of the civilian militia.

A baker was dragged screaming to the floorboards by two ghouls, his cleaver useless as they tore into his heavy leather apron. A young woman was knocked entirely over the inner railing by the skeletal hound, plummeting thirty feet down into the city streets below.

The tightly packed wooden walkway descended into an absolute, bloody slaughterhouse in a matter of seconds.

Forty yards away, Josh heard the wet, gargling screams.

He dropped the heavy chunk of limestone he was holding, spinning away from the portcullis. His storm-grey eyes locked onto the collapsing right flank. He saw the tide of rotting meat pouring over the parapet, tearing into the nearly defenceless civilians.

The portcullis was momentarily clear, the ramp below them having just collapsed from the heavy masonry barrage. Josh did not hesitate. He did not ask for permission to break his assigned position.

"Brett, hold the centre!" Josh bellowed, his voice ringing with pure, uncompromising fury.

He drew his heavy steel longsword from its scabbard, the metal hissing sharply in the freezing air. He lowered his shoulder, raising his dark heater shield, and triggered Juggernaut's Tread.

Brilliant blue lightning flared violently around his heavy steel boots. Josh launched himself down the wooden walkway like a runaway siege engine. He completely ignored the frantic, fleeing militia members, weaving through the chaos with terrifying, focused speed.

He crashed into the outer edge of the undead swarm with the force of a falling anvil.

The kinetic impact was staggering. His heavy steel shield slammed squarely into the chest of a massive Crypt Ghoul, shattering its brittle ribs and launching the creature completely off its feet. The ghoul flew backwards, colliding heavily with three other zombies and knocking them to the blood-slicked floorboards.

Josh did not stop. He waded directly into the centre of the breach, acting as a solid, living wall of impenetrable steel.

He activated Stormforged Adamantite. Raw, blinding electricity surged down his right arm, engulfing his longsword in a crackling cage of high-voltage static. The sharp, acidic tang of ozone instantly cut through the suffocating stench of rotting meat and fresh blood.

He swung the heavy, electrified blade in a brutal, decapitating arc.

The steel sheared cleanly through the thick, unnatural neck of the skeletal hound. The intense electrical discharge instantly cauterised the rotting flesh of the surrounding zombies, the extreme heat cooking their dead muscles and causing them to violently spasm and collapse.

"Get behind me!" Josh roared at the terrified, bleeding civilians, stepping over the mangled body of the young boy who had fallen first. "Move back to the watchtower!"

A towering, heavily armoured zombie, still wearing the rusted chainmail of its past life, lunged at Josh's exposed flank, raising a broken rusted spear.

Before the monster could strike, a heavy iron throwing axe spun out of the swirling smoke. It struck the zombie directly in the side of its skull with a sickening crunch, burying itself deep into the rotting bone and dropping the creature instantly.

"Mountain and stone!" Bhel bellowed, charging into the fray from the opposite side of the breach.

The dwarven fighter was a terrifying sight. He was missing his upper armour entirely, his broad chest wrapped in thick, pristine white bandages that were already beginning to spot with fresh red blood. He held his remaining throwing axe in his left hand, his dark eyes burning with absolute, battle-crazed joy.

He slammed his heavy, iron-plated boot directly into the knee joint of a charging ghoul, shattering the bone cleanly. As the monster collapsed, Bhel stepped forward, driving the edge of his axe deeply into its neck.

"Can’t let you have all the fun here!" Bhel laughed harshly, ripping his axe free in a spray of dark sludge.

They formed a brutal, unyielding wedge of living muscle and heavy steel. They fought with terrifying, mechanical efficiency, completely ignoring the horrific gore painting their boots. Josh used the immense weight of his dark shield to batter the monsters backward, allowing Bhel to dart in and cripple their legs with precise, sweeping axe strikes.

Josh slammed the rim of his shield upward, catching a diving zombie square in the jaw. He followed the momentum, stepping heavily forward and using the massive, flat surface of the heater shield to physically bulldoze the creature. He drove it relentlessly backwards until it hit the low wooden parapet. With a final, explosive shove, he launched the shrieking monster over the edge, sending it plummeting down to the smouldering pitch trenches below.

They were slowly, grindingly pushing the tide back. The immediate threat to the retreating civilians was neutralised, and the walkway was briefly cleared of the horrific swarm.

But the victory was entirely hollow.

They had closed the breach on the walkway, but they had not solved the underlying problem. The massive, writhing ramp of bruised, purple flesh outside the wall was still rising. Hundreds of zombies were actively clawing at the wooden logs, their bodies crushing together to create an ever-widening platform at the top of the parapet.

The monsters were no longer pouring through a narrow gap. They were preparing to spill over a massive, thirty-foot wide section of the wall simultaneously.

"We cannot hold this wide of a front!" Bhel shouted, his deep voice strained as he deflected a rusted iron club. The pristine white bandages on his chest were now heavily soaked with red, his breathing growing ragged. "There are too many of them!"

Josh knew the dwarf was right. They were two fighters attempting to plug a massive, failing dam with their bare hands. He swung his electrified sword, cutting a ghoul in half, but three more instantly scrambled over the railing to take its place.

It was a desperate, suffocating minute of pure survival.

Every swing of his sword felt heavier. The dull, persistent fire in his left shoulder flared into sharp, blinding agony every time a zombie crashed against his dark shield. He was fighting blindly in a chaotic storm of snapping jaws and rusted iron, his boots slipping dangerously on the blood-soaked wooden floorboards.

He was running out of stamina, and Bhel was running out of blood. They’d need to call for reinforcements soon, or risk losing this stretch of wall.

Suddenly, the suffocating, dark air above the breach point warped and shimmered wildly with intense, rippling heat.

Josh risked a single, frantic glance back towards the central gatehouse.

Brett was standing on the highest wooden platform, his crimson Ember-silk cloak billowing violently in the thermal updraft. The mage looked utterly exhausted, his pale face slick with cold sweat and his hands trembling visibly. He had depleted nearly his entire vast mana pool holding the main gates, but seeing his friends desperately fighting a losing battle on the collapsing flank, he tapped into the absolute, dangerous dregs of his core.

Brett did not unleash a massive, sustained torrent of plasma. He did not have the energy left for a continuous beam.

Instead, he raised both hands, his long fingers curling inward. He drew upon the ambient heat of the smouldering pitch trenches far below, forcefully dragging the thermal energy upward and compressing it wildly between his palms.

He condensed the roaring fire until it formed a small, incredibly dense, fist-sized orb of pure, blinding white light. The heat radiating from the tiny sphere was terrifying, actively warping the air and scorching the wooden railing near the mage.

Brett locked his pale eyes onto the base of the massive, widening meat ramp threatening to overwhelm Josh and Bhel.

He thrust his hands forward, hurling the concentrated orb like a deadly fastball.

The tiny sphere of white-hot fire arced gracefully through the pale morning sky. It sailed cleanly over the heads of the fighting heavy infantry and struck the frozen mud directly at the foundational centre of the massive zombie pile.

The detonation was apocalyptic.

It did not burst into a wall of flame. It exploded with the concussive, terrifying physical force of a massive bomb. A blinding flash of white light completely washed out the vision of everyone on the wall, followed instantly by a deafening, thunderous roar that physically shook the massive timber ramparts.

The explosive thermal expansion vaporised the lowest layers of the undead horde in a microsecond. The sudden, violent displacement of air blew a massive, perfectly circular crater into the freezing mud, sending tons of dark earth and shattered bone rocketing into the sky.

With its entire structural foundation violently and explosively removed, the massive, widening ramp of writhing bodies suffered a catastrophic, total collapse.

A horrific, wet tearing sound echoed over the ramparts. Hundreds of climbing zombies, suddenly lacking any support beneath their feet, simply dropped out of the sky. The entire mountain of rotting meat sheared away from the timber wall, tumbling backward into the smoking crater and crushing the advancing ranks behind them under their own immense, falling weight.

The sudden, sheer drop in pressure against the wooden parapet was jarring.

Josh stumbled forward slightly, his heavy shield meeting empty air where a horde of monsters had been pressing just a second before. He caught his balance, gripping the top rail of the parapet and looking down into the massive, smoking crater left by Brett's spell.

The immediate threat of the breach was gone.

"Clear the deck!" Captain Aris roared, leading a squad of heavily armoured spearmen charging down the walkway to relieve the exhausted fighters.

Josh slowly lowered his electrified longsword, the crackling blue lightning fading away as he dropped the skill. His chest was heaving with massive, ragged gasps, his lungs burning from the cold air and the smoke. The adrenaline that had fuelled his frantic charge was rapidly evaporating, leaving behind a deep, bone-weary ache.

He turned away from the edge of the wall, looking down at the blood-slicked wooden walkway.

The horrific reality of the breach crashed over him like a physical weight. During the desperate minute it had taken him to reach the collapsing flank, the undead had exacted a terrible, bloody toll.

Five members of the civilian militia lay dead upon the floorboards. Their weapons were scattered uselessly around them. They were not hardened soldiers who had chosen this life. They were simply local tradesmen, caught in an apocalyptic nightmare, torn apart because the line had buckled.

Josh walked slowly through the carnage, the heavy clanking of his steel boots sounding unnaturally loud in the sudden, ringing silence of the cleared sector. A heavy, suffocating knot of profound guilt formed deep within his chest. He was a Guardian. He was supposed to be the impenetrable shield. Yet, these people had died violently while he was standing just forty yards away.

He stopped, his heavy boots coming to a halt near the very edge of the wooden parapet.

Lying on his back, staring blankly up at the pale, smoke-filled morning sky, was the young boy in the oversized boiled leather tunic. His throat was completely ruined, his tunic soaked heavily with his own bright crimson blood.

Protruding from the wooden floorboards right beside the boy's head, still pinned securely through the chest of a twitching, half-decapitated zombie, was the crude iron spear the boy had bravely thrust forward.

Josh stood perfectly still, his storm-grey eyes locking onto the lifeless, terrified face of the young human. The boy had held his ground. He had struck a lethal blow, entirely unaware that the rules of biology did not apply to the monsters he was facing.

Josh stared at the boy, the freezing winter wind howling mournfully across the bloody ramparts, feeling the terrifying, unrelenting weight of the siege settle entirely onto his exhausted shoulders.
