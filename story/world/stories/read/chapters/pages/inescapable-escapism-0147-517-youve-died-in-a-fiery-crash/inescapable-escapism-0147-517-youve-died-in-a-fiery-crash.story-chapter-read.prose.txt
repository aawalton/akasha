“Oh, really?” Seth asked.

I felt him glance at me, but I forced myself to keep my eyes on the stairs ahead of me, even as blood began to creep across my chest and up my neck. My heart thudded dully in my ears, and my nervousness turned into anxiety.

“Nope,” I replied, trying desperately to sound as though I was fine and not worried at all.

“Huh, fair enough,” Seth said with a slight shrug. “I mean, it’s kind of nice to think about it sometimes, you know? Like, it almost makes it seem like anything is possible, even if it’s not in this world.”

“Yeah, I guess.”

My breath was coming too quickly. I fought to keep it under control and to hide it from Seth, but I could feel it becoming difficult. We were just climbing the stairs, and there were a lot, but I knew that wasn’t the problem. I’d traipsed up and down them so many times since I’d left the induction wing.

It was the panic that was starting to claw at me. That was why I felt like I was running out of oxygen. Seth’s line of questioning hit too close to home for me, and that scared me. It made me feel like he might somehow stumble across the truth about what I was doing.

Seth moved out of the way as we reached out of the landings, squeezing in behind me for a moment so a group of girls could get past before falling into step beside me again. I watched him carefully out of the corner of my eye, hoping that he’d finished talking about the other worlds, but I wasn’t that lucky.

“It is kind of cool to think about sometimes,” he said, his voice quieter that time. It had taken on an almost wistful quality. “I don’t know. I just like the idea of living a thousand lives, each one completely different.”

I swallowed, trying to force down the lump that had formed in my throat, but it refused to move. I wasn’t sure whether it had been caused by fear or heartbreak, but a wave of emotion slammed into me.

It was the longing in Seth’s voice. He’d spent countless days wishing he was elsewhere, wishing that his life was different, that it was better. I knew he had because I’d done the exact same thing before I started visiting the other worlds or whatever it was that I was doing.

I glanced at Seth again as something began to pull at me. A suspicion, or maybe a hope. Seth didn’t seem to notice my eyes on him. His expression was distant. He was completely consumed by his thoughts, and I felt my heart pound.

Was he like me? The question snuck into my mind and demanded my attention. Could he do what I could do? Was he talking about his lives in the other worlds because he actually knew what they were like? He could have visited dozens of them, if not more. His home life was bad too. Maybe he longed for an escape just as much as I did, if not more.

But immediately, I found myself dismissing that thought. There was no way Seth was like me. He was far too… normal. He wasn’t the kind of person who’d disappear into his head whenever things got bad. He’d…

I wasn’t sure what he’d do, but I doubted he’d do that. It didn’t seem right for him, and if he was actually visiting the other worlds, he would never have mentioned it so casually. He would have been far too scared of someone realising that he actually believed what he was saying and reporting it, just like I was. He would have known how insane it would have sounded to anyone else, even if he suspected that I somehow understood what he was talking about.

He was just using his imagination. That was all. It was hypotheticals and nothing more. He was too normal to be like me, and I was just reading too much into his words because I didn’t want to be alone. I didn’t want to be the only one with that… power? That didn’t sound right. People didn’t have powers in real life; it was the kind of thing that only existed in books or movies, but I wasn’t sure what else to call it.

I had to say something, I realised. I’d been silent for too long, and he’d been the last one to speak. That meant that it was my turn. I needed to think of a way to break the silence. My mind raced as I tried desperately to think of anything.

“Yeah, I guess so. I doubt any of my lives are that different, though,” I blurted out. “I’m not sure if they’d be any better than this one.”

That was meant to be a joke. I’d hoped it would help defuse some of the tension that I was pretty sure only I could feel, but it didn’t. The corner of Seth’s lips twitched ever so slightly, but his expression remained distant.

“I guess not,” he said, sounding strangely disappointed. “I mean, we really did luck out with this one, huh? I’m not sure whether I would have been recruited in any of the other worlds.”

“Mmm,” was the only response I could think of.

We continued climbing the stairs in silence for a few more seconds, and I couldn’t help but feel a burst of relief as we rounded another corner and the top floor came into view.

“Sorry.”

I glanced at Seth in surprise.

“For what?” I asked.

I truly had no idea why he’d apologised. He hadn’t done anything wrong. I was the one who was being weird, and I knew that. If anything, I should have apologised, not him.

“For talking about that kind of thing. I know it’s weird,” he explained before I could say anything.

“No, no—” I started to say, but he cut me off.

“It’s fine. I know it is. I just have an overactive imagination. My dad used to say it was too much time on the internet as a kid.”

Seth forced out a laugh, and I felt my lips pull up into a smile, but I didn’t feel it either. Guilt and disgust churned in my stomach, making it hard for me to even speak. I hated that something I’d done had reminded him of his dad. He was an asshole, and he’d treated Seth so horribly. Every single story I’d heard about him had filled me with rage, and I’d just done something similar. I hadn’t hit Seth or anything, but I could tell that I’d made him feel bad.

“There’s nothing wrong with having a good imagination,” I said firmly. “Rodgers said it helps, right? Especially for field operatives. It helps you come up with creative solutions and excuses.”

Rodgers had said that, but I was grasping at straws. In that moment, I would have said almost anything to distract Seth and make him feel better.

“Right.”

Silence settled over us again as we reached the top of the stairs and turned towards the boys’ dorms, and I found my mind spiralling again. It hadn’t been enough. Seth was clearly still thinking about his dad, and I hadn’t said enough to distract him or convince him that an overactive imagination wasn’t necessarily a problem.

Guilt grew within me, making my stomach ache. I felt strangely empty and hollow, and the need to fix it thrashed in my heart. There had to be something else I could do, something else I could say that would make him believe I was telling the truth.

The truth.

The urge to tell him that I knew exactly what he was talking about, that I thought about the other worlds too, and I’d even visited some of them, rose dangerously within me. I could feel it clawing at my throat as it tried to escape, but I refused to let it. The truth was too dangerous.

Seth would never believe me, and even if he did, he would think I’d lost my mind. He’d report it to Rodgers or Ms Brice or one of the other tutors, and then I’d be kicked out of the Academy. They’d lock me up in some mental hospital somewhere, and the other worlds would be ripped away from me. I’d never be able to escape.

But Seth still seemed sad as we walked along the corridor without speaking. People passed us by, and I couldn’t stop myself from eyeing them jealously. It seemed like every group was talking and laughing even more loudly than normally, and I wished we were doing the same, but it was my fault we weren’t.

The words thrashed even more violently within me, and I had to press my lips together to hold them in. I wanted to say something, anything, but I was scared that if I opened my mouth for even a moment, the truth would slip out.

Neither Seth nor I until we reached the lounge at the far end of the corridor and stepped through the open doorway.

“Huh, they’re not here,” Seth muttered, looking at the table on one side with a confused expression.

Relief slammed into me at the sound of his voice, and my gaze flitted around the room, searching for the cards.

“They were just out this morning?” I asked.

“Yeah?” he said. “I guess we could ask someone if they know where they’ve gone, but maybe they’re all being used…”

I hoped not. We were about to go up onto the roof, and the thought of going up there without a pack of cards suddenly scared me. It would be a good distraction. If Seth was focused on teaching me how to play, he wouldn’t mention the other worlds again, and I wasn’t sure I’d be able to handle it if he did.

“Actually…” I started to say, looking around the room again. It was almost identical to the lounge at the end of the corridor with the girls’ dorms. “I think people normally put all of the board games in that cupboard when they’re done with them, so the cards might be in there too.”

Seth glanced at me in surprise before looking at the cupboard that I’d pointed to. It was just as huge as the one in the girls’ lounge, reaching almost all the way up to the ceiling, and both doors were shut, so we couldn’t see inside. I really hoped the board games were in it. It would have been so embarrassing if they weren’t.

“Oh, really?” he said before taking a step towards it. “Is the whole thing full of board games?”

“Ummmm, yeah. I think so?” I replied, trailing after him.

I hadn’t spent that much time in the lounge. There were always so many people in there, and it was so loud. I could hear the voices floating all the way down the corridor to my room sometimes, and the thought of being surrounded by that many people scared me.

Curiosity had gotten the better of me once, though. I could just about remember my hands shaking as I walked inside and looked around, but I hadn’t stayed for long. It was too overwhelming, and it just made me even more aware of how lonely I was, so I tended to avoid it after that.

I had heard someone say something about games being in the cupboard, though. I was pretty sure they were talking about the big one at the back, but there was another on the far side of the room. That one was smaller, but perhaps it was a better side for board games.

A cheer came from the other side of the room, where a group of trainees were huddled around one of the televisions, and I couldn’t help but jump. My head snapped around to look at them just as a loud groan came from the group.

“What?” someone shrieked. “There’s no checkpoint?”

“You need to do all of the levels at once without dying!” another person insisted.

“Wait, neither of us can die?” the girl holding the controller asked, a note of panic in her voice. “Surely, if only one of us does, the game will revive us… right?”

Mumbles sounded from the semicircle of people as they debated that before a confident voice spoke over them.

“We’ll be fine, El,” the guy said with absolute certainty. “We cleared the first part easily that time, so we’ll definitely be able to do the rest. I’m sure it won’t be that—”

Someone groaned loudly and threw themselves back against the sofa cushions.

“We’re never going to complete this damn secret level, are we?”

Laughter rang out, and I found my lips twitching up into a smile as I craned my neck to get a better view of the television. I wasn’t sure what game they were playing from the glimpses I’d caught, but the style and design were cool, even if the level seemed punishing. That made sense for a secret level, though. I assumed they were normally hard.

I glanced at Seth, who was starting to pull the cupboard doors open, before looking back at the group. I wasn’t really a gamer, but something about the one they were playing intrigued me, and I kind of wanted to find out what it was called. Then maybe, if it existed in reality, I could play it with Duncan. We did have a video game date planned for when I got home, after all.

“Oh, wow.”

The soft exclaim came from beside me, and my gaze lingered on the television for a moment longer before I looked back at Seth. My eyes widened. Immediately, I knew why he’d sounded so impressed.

The sheer number of boxes in the cupboard took me by surprise. There must have been a hundred, if not more. Multicoloured boxes were stacked on each of the shelves, and my eyes scanned the titles, taking them all in. I didn’t recognise a single one, but that made sense. I’d never really played any board games before, with the exception of Monopoly.

They had that, though. I could see at least six different editions, and each box was clearly well-used. I wasn’t sure why there needed to be so many versions or if they were actually any different, but part of me wanted to find out.

“Wow,” I agreed.

“Once the others are out, we have to play some of these,” Seth said excitedly.

“That sounds good!”

He shot me a grin as he reached out, grabbing one of the packs of playing cards.

“Do they have the same games in the girls’ lounge?”

I hesitated, unsure how to answer him. I hoped a memory would reveal itself to me so that I had something to say, but none did.

“I don’t know, actually,” I admitted.

“That’s fair. I guess we’ll just have to check,” he said with a smile before shutting the cupboard again and turning towards me. “Shall we?”

“Sure!”

We started to make our way across the room again, and I found myself glancing towards the television again in the hopes of figuring out which game the group was playing, but I had no clue. There was nothing on the screen that gave it away, and I couldn’t see the box anywhere.

I’d have to Google it, I realised as I looked away again. I wasn’t sure what exactly I’d search for, but surely, if I just looked for a game with two women characters and a secret level with lasers, it would come up. Or maybe I’d just be inundated with inappropriate videos. That happened sometimes when I was looking for something completely normal.

Seth turned towards me, his lips moving as he said something, but his words were too quiet. I couldn’t quite hear them, but I could feel myself responding to him. My mouth opened, and words flowed out, but they weren’t really coming from me.

I was barely in the world. My mind had been consumed by the thought of playing that game with Duncan. Or just any game. Maybe I shouldn’t suggest we play that one. I wasn’t particularly good at gaming, and if the whole thing was as difficult as that level, he’d probably get frustrated at me if I kept dying.

He didn’t normally, though. Whenever we gamed together, he was always really patient with me, even when I was playing awfully. He never seemed to become annoyed or irritated, or at least, he didn’t show it if he was.

My body jolted, and I was ripped out of the world completely. My eyes opened suddenly, and I felt my entire body tense as my heart raced. I looked around, holding my breath as I tried to figure out what had happened to force me back to reality.

Nothing seemed wrong, though. As far as I could tell, everything looked completely normal. Music still played obnoxiously loudly, and my mom was still seated beside me as we trundled along the road at a painfully slow speed. She wasn’t tapping the steering wheel in time to the beat, but she didn’t always do that, so it didn’t necessarily mean that anything had happened.

But then why was I there? My gaze flicked towards her again before I looked around again, paying more attention to everything. The world outside the car appeared the same, though. The sky was still bright, with just a couple of clouds dotted across it, but it seemed a little… duller? It looked almost as if the colour was starting to be leached out of the sky.

Fear prickled in the back of my mind, sending goosebumps creeping along my skin, and I reached out. The dizzinesses at the edge of my awareness crowded closer, eager to be chosen, but I moved through them without touching any.

My world wasn’t there. I must have been in it, and that meant…

I looked around again, trying to work out what had happened when I was thrown to the side again. The seat belt cut into my body as it caught me, stopping me from slamming into the door, and my eyes found the road in front of us.

Potholes dotted the barely maintained country lane, and Mom seemed to be hitting every single one. I watched as we approached another, and the wheel bounced into it, catching the lip.

That must have been intentional, I thought as I rubbed my chest and glanced at her out of the corner of my eye. There was no one on the other side of the road. She easily could have swerved to avoid it, but she didn’t. If anything, it seemed like she aimed for it.

I braced myself again as I spotted yet another gouge out of the road ahead, hoping that would help prevent the seat belt from hurting me again, but it didn’t. The solid fabric still tightened around me, and I was almost certain it was going to leave a bruise.

Why weren’t we on a motorway? We should have been. It was a pretty straightforward journey normally, and as far as I could remember, we usually stayed on the motorway until we were almost home.

There must have been an accident or something, I realised. A groan almost slipped from my lips, and I dropped my head back against the headrest. The road must have been closed, forcing us to use the windy country roads, and that meant there would be no service stations along the way. There were rarely any on roads like that. They just had petrol stations, and the bathrooms in those were always disgusting. It was as if no one ever cleaned them, and I hated using them, but I’d have to. I had no idea how long it had been since we’d stopped last, but a dull ache came from my bladder. I needed to pee.

A silent sigh escaped me, and I glanced at my mom, checking to make sure she hadn’t noticed it. I didn’t want to tell her how desperate I was. She was bound to be annoyed that we’d had to leave the motorway, and I didn’t want to make it any worse. Not when I was going to continue to be trapped in the car with her for hours. It already felt like the journey had taken an eternity, but if we couldn’t use the main roads, it was going to be…

My thoughts trailed off as my eyes found a sign at the side of the road, and I couldn’t help but do a double-take. I recognised the name of the village on that sign, but that didn’t seem right. It wasn’t. We couldn’t be anywhere near it. Not yet, anyway.

There had to be two villages in the UK with the same name. That was the only explanation. There must have been one somewhere up north and another further down south, next to the town that I lived in. We’d been driving a long time, but there was no chance we were just two miles away from it.

But I recognised the road we were on. I’d been driven along it many times. I was certain of that, but it didn’t make sense to me. That must have meant hours had passed since I’d last been in the world, and it didn’t feel like they had. Maybe a couple of hours, but no more than that.

Concern pulled at me as I reached towards the other worlds again, needing to check that my reality still wasn’t there, but I still couldn’t feel it. I must have been in it, but I couldn’t quite believe it. It didn’t feel possible.

Glancing at Mom again to make sure she hadn’t noticed my confusion, I lifted my phone and checked the time. My mind turned blank, and my jaw dropped open. How? How was it twenty four minutes past seven in the evening? That meant that…

There were so many texts from Phoebe, I noticed. I hadn’t even felt my phone vibrate at all when I was in the other world. Normally, I could feel it at least a little bit, but I hadn’t felt anything that time.

I scrolled through them, skimming her messages quickly to make sure nothing was wrong. It didn’t seem like anything was, though. She was mostly just telling me about her day and the cute guy she’d bumped into yesterday, with the exception of the latest texts.

Okay, now I’m pretty sure your mom has confiscated your phone again. Either that or you’ve died in a fiery car crash ahah, she’d written before quickly following it up with, Oh fuck, I really hope you’ve not been in an accident. Let me know that you’re alive whenever you can, but if I don’t hear from you by like half seven, I might have to call.

My head was still reeling, but I unlocked my phone and began typing, So sorry. Long story, but I’m fine! There was no fiery car crash, and I’m still alive. Almost home now, I’ll reply to the rest later, but I’m excited to hear more about the guy. Is he hot?
