Her back is to me, but her visage is forever burned into my memory. Her white hair is tied up in a tight bun, and her pale skin seems to melt into her white-and-red attire. I give her a Check.

[Name: Grace]

[Title: Champion]

[Species: Human]

[Class: Frost Weaver]

[Level: 31]

[HP: 125/125]

[Affinities: Ice]

[Allegiance: Widengra]

She’s speaking to a harpy with blood-red feathers, and I Check him as well. No surprise here: I’ve found Widengra’s other champion. Oullus is Level 28 and has wind and lightning affinities. Peachy.

I grab Cyros’s cloak and yank him back around the alley’s corner, nearly impaling myself on the spear he has strapped across his back. He gasps, but thankfully doesn’t cry out.

“What—” He stops the moment he sees the look on my face, and his surprised expression turns grave. His voice drops to a whisper. “What is it?”

“Widengra’s champions.” I nod toward the corner. “Human with white hair. Harpy with red feathers.”

Cyros slowly peeks around the corner before pulling back. “Why are they here? Can they track you?”

I shake my head; I have no idea. Tracking spells exist, but they require something that belongs to the person you want to track, and ever since learning that I’ve been meticulous about keeping track of my belongings—I didn’t even leave anything at Iski and Gugora’s inn. Is there a different way they could be tracking me? Or is it just bad luck?

Doesn’t matter. Right now we just need to get out of here. Once we’re out of the city, we’ll have a little more breathing room. Cyros gestures toward the other end of the alley, and I nod in agreement, starting after him.

Or…

I hesitate, looking back. Could I take them? Here and now? My potions are fully stocked. Grace might have two levels on me, but Oullus is one below, and I’ve got the System on my side.

It’s not like I haven’t killed one of Widengra’s champions already.

But two at once could get complicated, especially considering one can fly. I’d need to surprise them and take one out before they have time to react so I’m not outnumbered. I run my hand down the bandolier of potions I keep strapped across my chest. Maybe a poisoned smoke bomb would work. Two birds, one—

Cyros yanks on my scarf, causing me to stumble backward.

“What are you thinking?” he hisses, eyes flashing with anger.

“Nothing!” I object automatically.

“You think you can take them?” he continues, barely containing his enraged whispers. “By yourself? Come on, Sal. You’re impulsive, but you’re not stupid.”

I’m so taken aback that I can’t even think of a response. I’ve never seen him this mad before. What does he care? This is my unfinished business.

I tug my scarf out of his grasp, glaring at him. “I wasn’t going to fight them. I was just evaluating.”

Cyros gives me a flat look that tells me exactly how much he believes that.

I maintain the glare for several seconds. Then I huff in annoyance, activate Soft Step, and slink away from the end of the alley. Cyros shadows close behind.

“Besides,” I whisper, “you’re not involved, anyw—”

Vines snap out from Cyros’s sleeve and wrap about my wrist. They stop me in place, causing me to swivel halfway back around to face him.

“I’m your friend, Sal,” he snaps. “Of course I’m involved. I don’t care if you’re fighting champions or gods. You don’t have to do it alone. Why can’t you see that?”

A lump forms in my throat as Talia flashes through my mind. A moment of her sitting at her table, reading a book and sipping tea. Then, her body on the floor at Maru’s feet, blood soaking into my clothes.

As much as I hate to admit it, Cyros is right. We are friends. I tried to put some distance between us since moving to Moonfall, but I’ve gotten complacent. I didn’t fight his attempts to hang out as much as I should have. I should cut ties now, before it’s too late.

But I still have this job I need to help him with. He needs me to complete it, and it would be crappy to back out on him now. Maybe once it’s over, once we’re back, I’ll have an opportunity to slip away. I have to, if I don’t want him to end up in the gods’ crosshairs, too.

The gods’ crosshairs…

My heart jumps, and my eyes go wide. Cyros’s hard glare turns uncertain.

“Álvaro,” I whisper. “Where was he going to meet us?”

Cryos’s eyebrows lift in understanding. We both look back in the direction of the champions.

“Will they be able to recognize him?” Cyros asks. “The way you can learn stuff about people.”

I hesitate. “No. At least, I don’t think so.” Maru hadn’t been able to Check me, which is the only way I’d been able to escape her murder spree. And Piria doesn’t seem to have access to the System either. It seems like only the gods have it—well, the gods and people like me and Álvaro.

But is that the only way they have to tell if someone is a Traveler? If not, then we’re leaving Álvaro to the wolves.

I groan. As much as the guy gets on my nerves, no one deserves to be at Widengra’s mercy.

Wordlessly, we both creep back in the direction of Grace and Oullus. Cyros activates a dryad camouflage spell I’ve seen him use on occasion. Without foliage to blend in with, however, it’s not nearly as effective. His form seems to meld into the shadows of the alley, but it’s obvious whenever he moves.

I’ve got an invisibility potion on me. Unfortunately, its application is not subtle; the potion is infused with a smoke bomb, so when the potion is uncapped, it explosively billows out of the bottle, and anything coated in the resulting gas becomes invisible. It would be so much more useful if I could just drink it, but that’s not how the potion works. I hesitate, fingering the potion’s cork as I consider my options.

Cyros pokes his head around the corner, and after a few seconds I join him. The champions still aren’t looking our way, but they also haven’t left. In fact, they’re glancing around the area, like they’re expecting something. That’s probably not good.

And not far beyond them is Álvaro, hands tucked behind his head, casually strolling right toward them.

Come on, I think. Check them, Álvaro. Check them!

But why would he? Unlike Maru’s ethereal glow, these two look like they could be any other passerby.

Grace tips her head, then looks his way. She says something to Oullus, and he looks, too. Álvaro finally notices them, and a moment later he freezes midstep. Then he keeps walking, glancing away, as if he hadn’t just Checked them and noticed they were champions. The champions sure notice him, though.

Welp. So much for slipping out of the city without a fight. With their attention on Álvaro, I slip into the alleyway and begin to pad their way. Cyros is right there with me.

An excited thrill flutters in my chest. Even if I’d rather Álvaro and Cyros not be involved, I’m eager for a rematch. Both of these champions were chosen by Widengra because they obeyed his order to attack a bunch of innocent spectators. They would have killed Iski and Gugora, given the chance. If there’s such a thing as evil in the world, it’s this lot.

This time, I won’t hesitate. This time, I’ll go for the kill.

“Hey,” Oullus calls out to Álvaro as the human tries to casually stroll past. “We need to chat.”

Álvaro feigns a very poor attempt at innocent surprise. “Me? Why?” At that moment, he catches sight of me and Cyros slinking out of the shadows. He glances away just as quick, but it doesn’t escape Grace’s notice. She follows his gaze, turning toward us. Her mouth opens in a surprised ‘o’.

Or maybe she was going to say something to Oullus. Regardless, it’s at that moment all hell breaks loose.

With her back turned, Álvaro charges forward, checking his shoulder into Oullus’s. The harpy spins away like he was struck by a truck. (Right, some analytical part of me thinks, even as I draw my butcher knife and pull a potion from my bandolier. Harpies are more lightweight than other species.)

A grin splits my face.

Ice shoots up in front of Grace in time to block my attack, flecks of frost chipping off beneath my knife. Oullus has recovered and is spreading his hands, bolts of lightning zapping between them. Cyros intervenes, vines whipping out to grab a wrist and yank it away, causing the electricity to stutter out.

“Álvaro!” Cyros tosses the spear his way, and Álvaro catches it from the air.

He spins it in his grasp with a laugh. “Sweet.” Then he swipes it at Oullus, and the harpy skips away with a flap of his wings.

I lose track of the rest of that fight as Grace monopolizes my full attention. Her ice shoots toward me, and I twist my knife free to chop the spears from the air. I retaliate by throwing my potion bottle, which shatters as it crashes into her ice. The liquid splashes across her, and a blistering heat immediately bursts forth. It burns like lava through the ice, and the heat stinging my face and arms, but with my class’s Temperature Resistance it only feels like a mild sunburn. Based on Grace’s screams, it feels a lot worse for her.

I don’t let her recover. I follow up my potion with more slashes from my blade, chopping easily through the half-melted ice. A sheet falls away, and suddenly there’s nothing between us.

Grace has a hand raised defensively before her, but her face is contorted in a grimace of rage, and her eyes—

They’re just like Maru. Like Widengra. Uniform pits of red, like pools of blood.

Fury erupts from me, and I launch myself at her, swinging for her head.

Pain slams into my left hand—my injured hand—and my attack goes wide. Grace’s grimace turns into a cruel grin, and a wall of ice crashes against my knife hand, knocking it from my grasp. The blade goes spinning across the ground, but I wrench my hand away before Grace can incase it in ice.

My left hand is not so lucky. I can see the ice around it staining red. I don’t know what the damage is, but it can’t be good. Worse still that she’s managed to lock it in place, preventing me from fleeing. Disarmed and partially immobilized, it will be embarrassingly easy for her to drive a spear through my chest.

[Noxious Gauntlet activated.]

The ice around my hand lights up green, and giant, ghostly claws form over the frost. It doesn’t break Grace’s attack, but it doesn’t need to. The claws dig into the ice, and green light begins to leach through it, swirling through the element and down toward its caster.

Grace dashes backward, separating the ice from herself to avoid the insidious crawl of the gauntlet’s magic. Pity. If I’d made contact, I would have snagged a portion of her mana to use as my own.

I grab another potion with my right hand and slam it against the ice around my left. Glass cuts into my hand as the bottle shatters, but pain is temporary and the injuries will heal. The ice hisses away in a cloud of steam, and I yank my hand free. Grace has vanished behind the fog, and I’m about ready to dive after her when a zap of lightning strikes my butcher knife right next to me, electricity burrowing all over its surface like a swarm of worms.

Cyros gives a shout of warning, and I glance up to find Oullus flying in the air above us, sparking with a blinding, yellow light.

“Take cover!” Álvaro shouts, diving away from the champion. Cyros is also retreating. I glance Grace’s way, but I still can’t make her out. The cloud will clear in a few seconds—but I’m not sure Oullus is going to wait that long. Grinding my teeth, I snatch up my knife, ignoring the tingling spark that lances up my arm, and spin on my heels to dive away. I make it behind an alley wall just as a storm of lightning explodes behind me. Flashes of light leave behind black pockmarks on the opposite wall of the alley. I have to squint to keep from being blinded. I wait, but after several seconds, the attack is still going. No way any of us can show ourselves now without being electricuted—of course, assuming Oullus is just going to stay stationary and not come after us.

As the angle of the lightning strikes stretches down the opposite wall of my alley, I realize he’s doing exactly that. Against every instinct, I flee, turning a corner and passing out of sight, lightning licking at my heels.

In the back alleys I find Cyros and Álvaro. They’re both breathing hard, and a trickle of blood is running down Álvaro’s face.

“Let’s get the hell out of here,” he pants.

I want to object. I want to stay and fight. But subterfuge is no longer on my side, and with Oullus acting as a living lightning rod, I don’t have anything that can counter him.

At least, not yet.

“Fine,” I grunt, grabbing a smoke bomb potion from my bandolier. I bite the cork and yank it off, then throw as hard as I can back in the direction of the champions. By the time the square erupts in green, toxic gas, the three of us are halfway down the block.

“Woah,” Álvaro says as we run. “That was wild, right? But kind of fun!”

Cyros groans. “Not you, too.”

“And what is that?” Álvaro continues, jabbing a finger toward my Noxious Gauntlet. “That looks sick!”

“Nothing,” I grumble, frustrated I once again fell back on Shirasil’s gift the second things got tough.

[Noxious Gauntlet deactivated.]

Cyros sucks in a breath. “Sal, your hand.”

I don’t bother looking at it. “It’ll heal.”

My lungs burn as we continue to run, but the champions don’t catch up. I don’t know if we hurt them enough that they need to recover, or if they’re just biding their time. Regardless, we’re the ones on the run.

We lost this fight.

My chest hurts worse than my hand as we continue to flee, angry with myself. What’s the point of all this training if I can’t beat a couple champions this low level. I killed Maru. These should be a cakewalk.

But I’d had a suicidally lethal amount of poison then. I’d both surprised Maru, and she’d underestimated me.

These champions won’t treat me so blithely. They know what I’m capable of, and they’ll be ready.

Next time, I’ll need to be twice as ready right back.
