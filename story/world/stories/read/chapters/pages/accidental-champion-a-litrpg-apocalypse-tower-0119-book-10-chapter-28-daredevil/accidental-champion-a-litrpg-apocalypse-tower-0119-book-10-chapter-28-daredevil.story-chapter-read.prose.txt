Switching off his sight after so long of having that be his only sense was terrifying.

It was a difficult, perhaps impossible sensation to describe. Though it had only been days, perhaps weeks, that he’d been in this state of only using his natural sight, it felt as though for all his life he’d seen and only seen, then someone had come over and plucked his eyes straight out of his head and shoved ears in their places—a strange, clumsy sense that didn’t show him anything.

The sound of his heart pounding in his chest was overwhelming. Like a drum the size of a house was being played a hairsbreadth from his ear, and the drummer was five times as large and had all the strength of an S Grade silverback gorilla—if such a being existed.

Xavier curled up and clutched his hands over his ears. He couldn’t feel where his hands touched. He couldn’t feel himself curled over. He couldn’t see where he was. But he knew that was the motion his body had made.

He could hear his hands cupping over his ears, and the strange sense that he was listening to running water once he had. Though he knew it wasn’t running water but the noises of his own body. The rush of his blood coursing through his veins, pulsing in his ear.

Cupping his ears didn’t help anything. Besides, he didn’t want to deafen himself to the noises out there, he wanted to open up to them. He pulled his hands away from his ears and slammed them on the stone beside him, hearing the audible slap, hearing his rapid breathing.

The transition was a hard one. Harder than only being able to see had been.

He sat there, slowly allowing the rhythm of breathing to steady. For a moment everything he heard sounded wrong. Off. Like it was too much. Before he’d shut off his hearing, he’d thought this cave was quiet, silent even. Then, without the ability to hear, he’d quickly disabused himself of that notion. The cave had not been silent. Not even close.

True silence was what he had experienced without hearing.

Now that hearing was the only sense he had, this “quiet” cave became a cacophony of noise, and as far as he could tell all of that noise was coming from his own body.

At least, that was what he’d been able to tell at first.

He didn’t try to hear the song that energy played in the air, not right away, just as he hadn’t done that when he’d been training his sight. He needed to strengthen his ability to discern different sounds. As far as he’d come in his development, his hearing was incredibly powerful as it was, but just like his sight he knew he wasn’t using it to its full potential.

I can see energy, and it looks different to how I imagined… what might it sound like?

He expected music, but he might be wrong—he hoped he wasn’t.

There was more he could experience. Far more. He knew that now that he’d done it with his sight—he just had to focus.

He just had to listen.

The feeling of being vulnerable lasted a good while. When it had come to only having sight, he’d been afraid of closing his eyes, afraid even of blinking at first. He knew how fast it was possible move. He could easily miss something.

His hearing didn’t have such interruptions. Even in his sleep, he’d still be able to hear, not that someone like Xavier needed sleep.

In the beginning, he couldn’t help but be frustrated by how noisy his own body was. It felt hard to push his hearing beyond himself with his breath and the beating of his heart making such a racket. Of course, it would be easy enough for him to stop breathing—he could do that for a great many hours without anything going wrong. If needed, he could even slow the beating of his heart until it appeared to be still.

But something told him that would defeat the purpose of this exercise. Simply taking away the noise of his body wouldn’t help him use his hearing better. In fact, he should be using the sounds of his own body. He should be locking in on them, trying to understand them better. And there were other noises in his body he might be able to discern—like the noise that energy made when it cycled through his energy channels.

The beat of his heart slowly became more distinct and defined. The rushing of blood, his pulse, too. Even the way he breathed. He could hear things he hadn’t heard before, like when there was the smallest amount of phlegm he needed to clear from his throat, or when his heart started beating ever so slightly more rapidly—just the smallest touch.

There were other things, too. The sound of his hands brushing along the stone floor. Flesh brushing against stone—it made a distinct sound. Everything did. Far more distinct than he realised. And he already had memories of all of these sounds. He’d been hearing them his entire life. He couldn’t feel his body, but he could tell when he was brushing his hand along his shin verses his thigh. There were so many tiny details, soundwaves far smaller than the one he’d been able to see.

Then there was echolocation. Xavier had heard about echolocation, of course, and not because bats were able to use it. The first time he’d heard about echolocation had been because of the superhero Daredevil. Though Xavier preferred reading novels over reading comics, he went through a phase of reading dozens of different comic books a week for a little while there.

Daredevil had been one of his favourites. The way the man was able to “see” even without sight had always fascinated him.

Now, he would get to experience exactly what that was like.

Xavier tapped his finger on his thigh in quite the same way he’d been doing when all he’d been able to do was see. The sound travelled all the way to the walls. That he could tell right away, but he couldn’t tell much more than that.

Tap, tap, tap.

He waited for the world to start making sense. For the objects to form in his hearing.

Tap, tap, tap.

It didn’t work. He couldn’t see anything.

Tap, tap, tap.

His eyes were squinted, narrowed. He couldn’t feel them, but he knew—and could her them move, could hear his very skin—and that was when he realised he was going about this all wrong. Thinking about things wrong. He wasn’t trying to see by using echolocation, he was trying to hear. The way the Daredevil comics depicted echolocation… all they had was a visual medium. It was impossible to show echolocation through hearing unless the person listening could actually perform it too.

Tap, tap, tap.

He listened, straining his hearing.

He tapped harder.

He tapped lighter.

But always he tapped and he heard.

A part of his mind wondered if there was a skill out there he could simply acquire. Wondered if he could take this ability from another’s mind, use their memories of it and transfer that to his own mind. But that was neither here nor there. Hell, maybe he could do that for every one of the senses.

This was something he needed to figure out on his own.

And figure it out he did.

When the trick finally came to him, it was another of those things that was difficult to explain. He couldn’t see around him. That wasn’t it. Wasn’t it at all.

It was more like he could follow the wave of the sound he made by tapping his finger on his leg, and when that wave crashed against the stone of the floor or the stone of the wall of this place, or kept flowing down the Dark Tunnel and onward, he knew. He knew when that wave splashed onto the wall, and how big that splash made told him how far the wall stretched. It was fuzzier than sight by a great deal.

At least at first.

Then, the more he used it, the more details he was able to discern. The echo told him much. Though there were things he couldn’t test. He couldn’t move out there and touch the wall to see if his hearing was telling him the truth—but at the same time, he didn’t need to.

He needed to learn to trust his hearing.

It wasn’t just the wall the echo told him about. It was his body as well. He could listen to exactly where he sat. Eventually, he got up the courage to stand. In the gravity, which must have been well over a hundred times that of Earth, standing was more difficult than it usually would be—not that he could feel the strain of his muscles.

Hell, right now, he wasn’t even sure if he would be able to feel pain.

Strange, how he’d never known if pain was something that could be switched off. Stranger still that he didn’t think switching it off was all that good of an idea, especially considering how many things he’d been through.

As he stood, he kept tapping his finger on his leg, using the echo to discern how his body was moving through space, and using his muscle memory to make sure he was moving in the right ways.

Something strange happened as he tried to stand. A sudden shift. A disorientation he couldn’t place. The tapping stopped even though he kept trying to make the movement. There was a series of sounds he couldn’t at first discern, then a loud thud.

Oh, he thought, with a little humour. I fell down.

Eventually, Xavier was able to pick himself up from the ground and stand. Standing was about a thousand times stranger than sitting had been in his current condition of not being able to feel, see, taste, or smell—though he didn’t think it was the latter of the two senses that made this such an odd experience.

He moved all the way to the wall. When he reached it, he ran his fingers along it. Not feeling but hearing the brush they made. What he discovered was something he’d already discovered with his sight—the wall wasn’t as smooth as it seemed. He could hear whenever the skin of his fingers caught against something.

At first, he wasn’t sure what this could tell him. He’d already learnt something about the walls, something about the entirety of the dungeon. That it absorbed energy. This little fact made it feel wrong to be touching the wall with his bare hands at all, but he didn’t think the Void Dungeon would be able to pull energy straight out of him. If it had that ability, it wouldn’t need to send the Void Dungeon-created beasts after him.

Unless it just enjoyed the hunt…

He didn’t think that was it, however.

There was something strange in what he heard. Something he couldn’t quite identify. Something… familiar. He continued brushing his fingers along the wall, then placed his entire palm over certain spots. What in the multiverse was it that was familiar about this? He dragged up his memories of seeing the walls, comparing them to what he heard, but nothing about it made any sense.

He wasn’t sure what it was, even after days of doing this, of simply running his fingers and palm along certain parts until finally he released a long breath with a little huff at the end and pulled his hands away from it.

It was time to start listening for energy.

He made his way back to the centre of the tunnel and slowly sat himself down, glad to find he didn’t fall over in the process. Once sitting, he sent out small strings of energy, having them criss cross in front of him just as he’d done before. And, as with his sight, he couldn’t discern anything.

Not at first.

But after a little while—far less time than it had taken with his natural sight—Xavier began to hear something. He was quite sure what he heard wasn’t a figment of his imagination. It was too real to be that. The sound was like… notes of music, though there was something different about it. Xavier had never been musical. In all the time he had back on Earth before integration, he’d never learnt an instrument. He’d picked them up from time to time, in the music classes he’d had to do in his early years, but he’d never learnt, and he didn’t have an ear for such things despite how powerful his hearing now was.

Still, he could tell these were different to normal notes of music. That there was something… magical, for lack of a better word, about them.

He sat there for a long while, sending out energy in different streams of thinness and thickness, toward different trajectories, and listened to the magical music it made.
