Rosslyn leaped into action.

Healing magic flowed through her swiftly as she laid hands on the young lord. The energy ran out of her hands and into his chest and head, rushing through his body.

It was not doing anything before when I tried to heal him, she thought. The poison is producing fresh injuries a day after it was administered? Is that how it works?

Her own experience with the Empire’s assassins was a hard baseline for her to use to judge, because she had been unconscious for almost her entire period of recovery. And Carolien had awakened before her. This was nearly Rosslyn’s first time actually seeing the poison in action.

Carolien stood at the edge of the Queen’s peripheral vision, looking like she did not know what to do with herself, but Rosslyn made herself ignore her stepmother.

She tried to sense what exactly her healing mana was doing inside William, where it was going and how effective it was. It was difficult for her, though. His body seemed to be soaking up the energy, and the power was certainly doing something to help, but healing mana went everywhere throughout his system, as if there was catastrophic damage to multiple places.

His brain is taking the most energy, she thought uneasily. That is a very bad sign. Damage there…

She didn’t want to think about what the consequences might be.

She felt Adon settle on the pillow beside her hand, and the butterfly extended his antennae to touch William’s temple.

What are you doing? she wondered. But she kept the thought very quiet. She felt certain that whatever Adon was up to would be helpful, and she didn’t want to distract him.

They both continued their activities for another couple of minutes, until William’s body calmed down. He was still alive, at least, and as far as Rosslyn could tell, the seizure had not added to the existing damage to his body. But of course, it was still an ominous thing.

Frederick slept fitfully beside William, but for now at least, the younger brother did not experience as drastic an escalation in symptoms as the elder.

Adon withdrew his antennae from William’s temple a few seconds after the seizure calmed.

“What were you up to?” Rosslyn asked quietly. A little, irrational part of her insisted that she should keep her voice down so as not to disturb the sick sleepers, though of course, she would have preferred that they woke up.

I was trying to understand what’s happening to him, Adon replied. I used Telepathy to read his mental activity during the seizure. I also compared his visible symptoms with the handful of other times I can remember witnessing people having seizures across my other lifespans.

“Oh, I guess you have a lot of prior experience,” she said thoughtfully.

I would not have even imagined Adon using his vast reserves of memory that way, though I suppose he has done something similar before.

A little bit, Adon replied. I think William has brain damage. Incomplete thought patterns kept moving through his head. Even after the seizure, there are some thoughts I sense that he can’t complete. Even if he survives this ailment, it’s hard to say if he’ll be himself again afterward. I also think he might be near the end if we don’t get him some real help right away.

“That was what I was afraid of,” Rosslyn said. “It is hard to imagine him gone. I knew him since we wore the same sort of clothes; when children are very small, we dress them in shapeless garments with no differentiation of sex.”

I’m ready to take him away to the Empire’s camp, Adon sent. You know I have a much better chance than you or anyone else of escaping the Empire’s clutches after I do it.

The Queen raised a hand to still the objections of the others who were preparing to speak. Both Goldie and Carolien were about to give the butterfly an earful, she could tell.

“You know, Adon, I am not certain he would go to the same sort of trouble for you,” Rosslyn said quietly, lips curled in a gentle smile. “I have slowly developed the sense that the two of you were not fond of each other.”

That just means that I might be a better person, Adon replied instantly. We aren’t particularly close, but we have fought alongside each other. For you. He’s been a comrade.

“I will not allow it,” Rosslyn replied simply. “You can make whatever arguments you want, but I will never allow you to risk your own capture by taking William to the Empire. When I weigh which thought is more troubling to me, William’s death or your capture, the latter is clearly worse.”

You might be the Queen of the country, but I’m not a citizen, Adon sent. You don’t command me. Didn’t you once say I should think of myself as the ruler of my own sovereign nation, with a population of just me?

“I am currently the ruler of roughly the same number of people as you,” Rosslyn said dryly. “When I say I will not allow it, I do not mean that by my great authority as the deposed Queen of an occupied nation, I forbid it. I mean that I will physically stop you. You are orders of magnitude stronger with magic than you were when we first met, but not stronger than me, I think. Certainly strong enough to escape me if I tried to fight or capture you, but not strong enough to drag a large body away with you.” She patted William’s chest. “Since you have no way of getting him past me, you will not be able to enact this complicated plan to destroy yourself.”

It’s not—he began, then paused. You said there’s no argument I could possibly make to change your mind?

“None whatsoever. Not unless you have some great power I do not know about that allows you to defeat me in single combat or guarantee a successful escape even from magic-cancelling devices.”

Fine, he sent flatly.

She could tell he was trying not to be annoyed at her for telling him what he couldn’t do.

He always has to be a hero, always ready to sacrifice himself, she thought. She recalled Samson. He and Adon had been cut from the same cloth.

“I must find another solution,” she said quietly. “I will go out for the moment.” She looked at Carolien. “Until I come back, mother, please watch over the brothers.”

The intent, So Adon does not drag them away while I am solving the problem, did not need to be spoken.

The Dowager Queen nodded. “Good luck.”

Rosslyn walked out of the cottage, a pit forming in her stomach. In the back of her mind, she already knew what the logical solution to the problem was. She just hated it.

He has already given so much, she thought. What I would be asking is monstrously unfair…

But the practicalities of the situation had no regard for what was and was not fair.

The Queen found Sergeant Angus positioned at the edge of the little village. He was standing staring in what Rosslyn was fairly certain was the direction she had come from, though it was hard to tell with the hamlet surrounded by forest.

He is standing guard, she realized. He never got the Army out of him. Has he been out here all night? Did he take it in shifts with another villager?

“Your Majesty,” he said as soon as she got within ten feet of him. He turned to face her, sharply as he could with one wooden leg, and snapped to attention. “Do you have need of me?”

She hesitated a moment, before she forced herself to say, “I do.”

“Only tell me what it is you command, and I will execute.”

She straightened, forced some of the regal demeanor back into her bearing, and thought through the exact words she would speak for a few seconds while he stood there patiently. These words would have to carry him through the next day or two, or longer.

“Sergeant, I have a difficult mission for you, if you choose to accept it,” she said. “I need someone to convey my friends, the heirs to the throne of Dessia, into the Demon Empire’s custody. They are both dying of the Empire’s poisoning. Only the Empire can save them now, given that we have no miracle worker at hand. If you choose to accept this mission, you will take them to the outskirts of Wayn, explain that you are William and Frederick’s bodyguard, and tell how you were selected to accompany them in their departure from the city. Perhaps that is how you lost your leg, if it gives your story more flavor. The same assassins who have William and Frederick near death also took that from you.”

“An old wound, Your Majesty,” Angus replied slowly. “If the Empire’s people examine it, they will see that easily. If they do not, then the lie might pass. It is the only explanation I can think of for why a crippled old man would be one of the Dessians’ elite bodyguards, though. Especially considering that I never truly got a grasp on how to use mana.” He gave her a hard, searching look.

“It is a thin story,” she acknowledged after an uncomfortable moment. “It may well be a suicide mission. If you are unwilling to make the sacrifice, I understand. I will not hold it against you. That is why it is not an order, only a request. You were always, and will always be, one of our finest soldiers."

“What happens if I do not go?”

“The Dessians will die without any intervention, and they have fought to keep my family safe despite being here as diplomats, assured of safe conduct by my family. Honor and the reputation of our country demand that I find some way to make this right. I might go myself if I cannot find someone else.”

“You cannot,” he growled.

“So everyone has told me,” she replied dryly. “But neither can I let the brothers die. As long as Claustria continues to exist as a country, our alliances with our closest neighbors remain crucial. Dessia and Parmonia are particularly delicate. We already chose Parmonia over their historic enemy Orthonia months ago, when my father concluded an alliance with the new Parmonian King. But they might rethink, given the current state of affairs. Galton’s friendship is secured by the fact that all of my siblings are half Galton through Carolien—er, my stepmother, the Dowager Queen. The only one of these we can influence right now is Dessia.”

He nodded. “I understand it well enough. Politics plus honor plus the country. The needs of the few must bow to the needs of the whole. These people are critical for their homeland, as you are critical for ours. I should think of the sacrifice in those terms.”

Rosslyn swallowed and then made herself nod. It was true. It was an excellent summary of where she had arrived mentally in making the request.

“I will do my duty—” the Sergeant began.

But both Queen and Sergeant turned at the sound of a pair of footsteps rapidly approaching. It was Carolien.

I asked her to watch the brothers, Rosslyn thought. What happened to make her abandon that?

The Dowager Queen drew near them and began to speak in breathless tones of urgency.

“William is dead,” she said. “Frederick’s heart rate is slowing down. Adon is applying healing magic, but it does not seem to be working. We—we have to make a decision now. Soon there may be nothing left to do but bury them.”

“Only one Dessian left,” Rosslyn said quietly.

Poor William, she thought. He died without having the chance to say goodbye to anyone or even know he was going. I thought my father’s death was premature, but William was half his age. The Goddess took them both so quickly. Life is a mystery.

“My task takes on even greater urgency,” the Sergeant said in an even, resolute tone. “We should get moving, Your Majesty.”

Rosslyn nodded, and they turned and walked back to the cottage where the new heir to the throne of Dessia lay, his condition deteriorating.
