The King of Death’s main workstation made the room Asher had found Smiles in look like a kid’s playhouse. There were dozens of corpses hanging from the ceiling, each in varying states of disembowelment with organs dangling like dried meats. Half-animated undead in secure cages thrashing about and desperately trying to break through bars of solid bone. Twitching, flexing muscle fibers splayed out across long tables, the strands coming together at the end in one giant fist that was experimentally opening and closing its fingers all on its own.

And throughout it all, right in the very center barely fifty feet away, stood the King of Death.

Asher stared at the man responsible for so much death and destruction. The creator and maintainer of the Deadlands. A man who had killed tens of thousands of people throughout his incredibly long lifetime. An ancient wizard, one who had given up their very mortality in order to cling to this material realm with undead fingers.

Unlike Zygrul or Hylm, the King of Death didn’t actually have much of a presence. Those two liches had carried themselves powerfully, keeping their undead forms sharp and erect, as though they knew they were better than others and wanted to make sure everyone was aware of it. Curiously, the King of Death was the polar opposite.

With a small, hunched over frame draped in a dark robe, the lich barely even reached five feet in height. If he hadn’t been completely immune to Asher’s Identify and Judgement skills, Asher might have assumed he’d simply stumbled upon another random zombie.

Though the fact that he was currently hands deep in the guts of a monstrous warrior gave away his identity just a little bit.

On the table in front of the King of Death laid an eight-foot-tall gladiator of some kind. The corpse wore gleaming steel armor adorned with spikes of bone, and if the equally large soulsteel sword lying beside it was any indication, the King of Death was intending for this to be his most powerful creation yet.

“You actually made it,” the lich called out, not even looking up as he continued fidgeting away within the entrails of the large corpse before him. “I’ve been keeping an eye on you ever since you destroyed Marrow. I have a connection to all my children, you see, and it saddens me to see even a single one of them fall. It is why I try to limit myself to only creating new ones every few years. I did not think my soul could handle losing all of them at once.”

Asher tried to walk forward before stumbling, having all but lost feeling in his feet as though they’d fallen asleep. He only managed to stop himself from falling by grabbing onto a nearby table at the last moment. Feeling something warm beneath his frigid fingers, he glanced down, nearly losing his lunch as he realized the table seemed to be coated in a large, thin layer of what looked like brain matter.

“As it turns out, my soul is stronger than I originally gave it credit for,” the lich continued, still keeping his focus on the corpse before him as he grabbed another tool and went back to work. “Strong enough to push aside the pain of losing so many children at once. But not so strong as to forgive the man who took them from me. Did you know I’m capable of directing my Death Aura? Isolating the regions of the body it impacts?”

Having already righted himself, Asher raised a shaky hand, hurling his living wood dagger with all his might. But his fingers had already gone numb, and his strength was waning fast. Even with his tunnel of compressed space, his dagger fell short by a good fifteen feet, clattering to the ground and sliding under one of the many tables.

“The sole reason you still live is because I wish to see you suffer,” the King of Death explained, grabbing a pinch of gemstone dust and placing it somewhere within the corpse. “Your heart would have given out a few minutes ago had I not intervened. No, I’m keeping your heart and brain alive. For the moment. But in exchange, that extra power from my Death Aura is attacking the rest of your body. By now, you’ve probably realized what’s happening. You’re dying, from the extremities up. That slow, creeping cold you feel? It’s death. Unavoidable, inevitable, death. Hylm was a fool to trust Zygrul, and she lost her soul in doing so, but I am not so careless. You were dead the moment you set foot in my Deadlands. You just didn’t know it yet.”

Throughout the lich’s speech, Asher had been struggling to put one foot in front of the other, ignoring the disgusting brain matter on the table as he dragged himself toward his prey. He was barely even listening to the lich at this point, his sole focus on forcing his failing body to move. Already, just as the lich had explained, Asher could feel his body shutting down. He’d lost full feeling in his arms and legs by now, and if he hadn’t swapped back to his assassin class solely for the surefooted bonus, he almost certainly would have already collapsed. All he needed was to get close enough. If he could just get within twenty feet of his target, a single use of Distortion would be all that was needed to end the King of Death for good and get rid of the Death Aura that was rapidly killing him.

“Your persistence is impressive, even I will give you that,” the lich said, continuing to talk to himself even as Asher refused to answer. “Though I suppose that’s more so the Eternity element’s doing than any sort of willpower on your end.” The lich paused, shaking his head before going back to his work. “Apologies. I let my anger get the better of me. I should not discredit life’s impressive desperation when it comes to keeping itself away from the inevitable jaws of death. Did you know that the sole reason I originally became a lich was because of my own fear of death? Comical, I know, as an owner of the Death element. My Life element did not bless me with any skills of longevity, and this was the only way I could find to extend my life. At first, I figured I’d be able to use my impressive talents to find a way to return to a body of flesh and blood once more. Alas, while I am a master of flesh, I am lacking when it comes to workings of the soul.”

All this time, the lich continued working within the corpse before it, pressing here and there with bony fingers as it sealed up incisions and shifted parts around. As it did so, Asher kept dragging himself forward, his mind strangely lucid despite his body’s refusal to cooperate with his demands. He tried forming a tunnel of compressed space to drag himself closer, only to trip and fall as he let go of the table, smacking his head hard against the ground as his tunnel winked out.

“Careful now, I left your brain alive for a reason,” the lich said, finally turning and giving him a horrifying smile. The King of Death’s eye sockets burned a putrid brown flame, like the color of rotting blood as he stared down at Asher. What little flesh remained on his decrepit skull was yellowed and pulled taut.

“As I said, I am lacking when it comes to workings of the soul. However, my lacking is still leagues beyond what many of today’s ‘experts’ are capable of. I can transfer a soul, of course, I just can’t do it without incurring a modicum of soul damage. I’m not willing to harm my own soul to return to humanity, and thus my research continues. But I am more than willing to harm yours. I bet you thought I was attempting to create another one of my children in order to save my hide, didn’t you? But I would never rush such a creation. No, I began working on this the moment I heard you’d killed Hylm. You see, as one of the few members of The Council of Death who never really bothered hiding my location, I knew you’d come for me sooner rather than later. And when you did, I wanted to be ready.”

As Asher struggled to his feet, the lich turned and walked around the corpse, tapping portions of the body as he called out. “Bones, muscles, enchantments, flesh. It’s all here. All except for two final pieces.” The King of Death turned and grinned at him once more, the brown fires in his eye sockets flickering. “…The brain and the soul. Would you like to take a wild guess as to where I’m planning on procuring them?”

“You’re mad,” Asher managed, a stab of fear shooting through his weakened body as he realized what was about to happen. As he was now, Smiles was right. The King of Death could probably walk over to him and slide a knife right into his ribcage before starting to carve out whatever he wanted. It was all Asher could do to even stand up again, and even that took what little dregs of strength he had left. His body desperately fought against the Death Aura, but it wasn’t even close to enough.

“Mad?” The lich scoffed, sounding genuinely upset. “Do you have any idea how difficult it is to create a Death Aura spanning the length of a small city-state? To then focus a portion of that immense power on a single person, keeping their body barely functional without actually killing them? I am a genius, and even if others refuse to recognize my intelligence in this age or the last, that does not change the fact. Perhaps once I finish the creation of my new Plagues, I will finally expand the Deadlands once more and absorb a few more towns. Your pathetic little teammates attempting to hide along the fringes of my Deadlands would make for excellent additions to my children, don’t you think?”

The mental image of the lich standing over Samantha, grinning as it carved out her still-beating heart to place into some undead abomination filled him with something he’d never quite experienced before. A cold, chilling desire that burned deep within his chest, pressing back against the overwhelming Death Aura. Asher was no stranger to anger. Nor rage, or even hatred. But this new feeling… This burning, unique sensation of wanting, of needing to see this lich dead, came upon him so suddenly and unexpectedly that he jerked back in shock. The King of Death wasn’t the first to threaten his or Samantha’s life of course. But to threaten to transform her into some sort of undead monster? Cursed to live out the next few centuries doing whatever horrible bidding he demanded of her?

Asher would see this man dead even if it killed him for good.

“What is this?” the King of Death asked, frowning at him from across the room. “Some sort of last stand? Your unyielding resolve as you commit to taking me down no matter the cost? Please. You’re not the first hero to come and try to vanquish me. Where do you think I got the materials for my original twelve Plagues in the first place? I suppose I’ve had my fun. Let’s get back to work, shall we?”

The flames in the lich’s eyes flickered, and Asher’s newly kindled desire was smothered out as quickly as it emerged, suffocated by the dense waves of the concentrated Death Aura as it washed over him anew. But just before he lost consciousness. Just as the last embers burning in his soul began to gutter out. He received a welcome surprise. A glowing lifeline dangling from the heavens, just within reach of his drowning hand. A reward for all the pain and suffering he’d forced himself through these past few hours.

Complete Resilience - Lvl 40

As the storm that was the Death Aura washed over him and Asher fell to his knees, he felt a massive chunk of shards evaporate from within his soul, strengthening it and spreading out throughout his body in a flash.
