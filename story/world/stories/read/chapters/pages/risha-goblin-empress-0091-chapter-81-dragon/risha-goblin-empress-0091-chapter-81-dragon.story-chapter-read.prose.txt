Risha woke suddenly, her heart pounding and sweat dripping down her skin. She pushed herself up in the furs that surrounded her, trying to remember how she got there.

After a moment, her memories of the previous night came back to her.

She’d collapsed.

She brought her hands up, looking at the callouses that covered them.

She’d pushed herself too far, and after making her speech to the final camp of Scaled, her legs had gone weak and she’d fallen into Glo.

Risha placed her head in her hands, equally parts embarrassed and furious with herself. Kliks shifted in her hair, the little spiderling gently placing her leg on Risha’s cheek.

With a sigh, Risha reached up and gently scratched the little spiderling. “Risha was too tired.” A soft laugh escaped her, then she pushed herself up.

Someone had cleaned her body and changed her into soft leggings. Risha vaguely remembered Tya’s face.

It was a kindness to be clean. Risha hadn’t realized how much the blood that covered her had weighed her down.

Risha walked to the other side of her tent, and put on a shirt, then pushed her way out of the entrance.

The Goblin guarding her tent bowed to her, and Risha turned to immediately catch sight of a coyote who’d been waiting patiently nearby.

Mist-Prowler stood from where she’d been sitting, and bowed low. “Grand Mother, It’s good to see you.”

“How long was Risha asleep?”

“Two days.” Mist-Prowler rose from her bow, and gave a small smile.

Risha paused, and stared vacantly as her brain processed that information. She hadn’t slept that long in a long time. Risha walked over to the coyote, and pulled her into a hug. “Risha needed a long nap. Where have you been this last week?”

The coyote returned the hug, then let Risha go. “I’ve been busy learning about our newest allies. And…”

“And?”

“I’m glad you’re awake. There’s something you need to see, tonight.”

Risha’s eyes narrowed, as her body stiffened, “Dangerous?”

Mist-Prowler shook her head “No, not immediately at least.”

Risha relaxed at the words, then watched the Kobold for a long moment. “Help me get ready.”

The coyote followed Risha into the tent as the Grand Mother mentally prepared herself.

Risha walked through the Scaled Bastion on a solid wooden bridge just above water, a silk mask hiding her features, and a cloak that blended into the background hiding her from the eyes of any who would recognize her.

Beside her, Mist-Prowler in the form of a Grey Lupus padded along, following her through the city. Bridges spanned the bastion, traveling over the many deep and murky waters and leading to simple houses that stood on wooden beams. Higher, along the cliffs, homes were carved into the mountainside, steep stairs leading to and from them. The people of the city were quiet, the somber mood of grief lying heavy on the large city.

Risha’s people mourned too, a day of sadness that would stay with them for a long time.

Tomorrow, they would celebrate, and Risha would be the first among them to dance with the fires and play for shinies.

Until then, she wanted to learn about the newest people under her. Many of the Scaled still wore their pale scales, and she would leave them to that choice for now. As she walked, a Purple Scaled warrior spoke softly to another warrior of Red Scales.

That was an interesting choice many of them had chosen. Mist-Prowler explained to Risha as they walked that Draconic Ember Saya and the Draconic Sapling Kaldero had accepted several of the lizardmen into their clan colors, an ability that surprised Risha, and apparently had shocked the human riders equally. Thankfully, the human riders seemed to accept their new Scaled allies quickly.

Mist-Prowler pulled some distance from a large gathering hall. With practiced ease, Mist-Prowler shifted, her grey fur turning into black scales, and her snout shortening as her nostrils turned into slits. Her pupils too, sharpened into two points at the top and bottom.

They walked towards the building, past several Scaled warriors who greeted Mist-Prowler quietly before turning their gaze back to the paths leading to the hall. Mist-Prowler opened the door for Risha, and the two of them slipped into the room. No one paid much attention to Risha, [Tribal Hunter] working with her disguise to stop the many Scaled from recognizing her. Mist-Prowler, however, attracted many eyes with her black scales, marking her as a servant of the Draconic Terror who followed Risha.

Mist-Prowler soaked in the attention expertly as she sauntered to the front of the hall, and sat with several of the more important looking Scaled. Risha stood behind her chair, watching the gathered with an interested gaze. Not a single non-scaled was in the room aside from Risha, although a rainbow of the new colors were still reflected in the factions that chose to be here. Risha also noticed that none of the Scaled had purple colors, although several were still pale.

“Sira, I am surprised to see you here. Does your new Clan head not require you?” An old White Scaled Warrior spoke to Mist-Prowler, using the name that she’d taken for her stay in the Scaled’s bastion.

The white scales were quite prominent in the room, and a sign of those who had chosen to follow one of the surviving dragons, instead of following Risha’s people.

Mist-Prowler turned to the speaker with a confident expression, and spoke fluently in the Scaled language, “The Terror does not care what I do, so long as I serve.”

The old White Scaled snorted, “Elderoa would still accept you, if you chose to follow her ways.”

“That’s why we’re here, isn’t it? She’s called us.”

“Yes, but my offer is more personal. She has noticed you, Sira. A star that has risen from the ashes of this war.”

“I have already chosen my Chief.”

“Hmmm. Perhaps. I suggest you keep an open mind.”

“Thank you,” Mist-Prowler bowed her head, “I am always open to the words of the wise.”

Risha watched the exchange with curiosity. Her meeting with the dragons had been put off with her collapse a few days ago, and so she’d yet to meet them personally. The White Dragon Elderoa she still knew by name, and she had seen her from a distance, a striking impression that Risha remembered well. Her scales had glistened in the morning light, covered in the blood of a long battle. Their eyes had met for one brief tense moment.

Mist-Prowler spoke with several more of the Older Scaled at the front, traversing the different conversations with an ease that amazed Risha.

Risha was glad that she could avoid most of the politics in her empire, and trusted her allies to see her will done. The veiled threats, and soft convincing were beyond Risha, and things she would much rather have an expert like Mist-Prowler deal with.

The words of the crowd quieted as a presence approached the room. Risha turned her eyes to the front of the hall, where a young woman walked.

She looked closer to human than most. White scales marking her cheeks, and two horns curled above her hair. She wore a white dress that shimmered in the torchlight of the room, and her eyes roved over the crowd with the pride of a creature that knew her might.

Risha watched the woman, a competitive feeling rising in her stomach as she met the woman’s eyes. Risha’s blood rushed as she saw a dismissal, and a challenge.

This woman dared to challenge Risha? Dared to look down on her?

Risha’s hands tightened, as she controlled her breathing. Her blood still rushed, her body tensing with the desire to destroy the woman who dared to challenge her. As the woman’s gaze moved from Risha, she slowly got her body under control and unclenched her hands.

“It is good to see so many old friends, especially in a world that has taken so much from us.” Elderoa started. Her voice was soft, and yet it easily carried through the room. “The eternal Clan welcomes our brothers and sisters with open arms, wherever they rest.”

The Scaled in the room bowed their heads as one, and Risha copied the movement while keeping an eye on the dragon at the head of the room.

There was sadness in the Dragon’s eyes, and a fury.

“Many of you do not know why I have called you,” Elderoa raised her head, and looked into the distance. “It is for a simple reason. There can be no trust for our new masters. They can not understand the pain that we suffer, or the bonds of our clans. Not as I can. I offer you my protection, so that you need not fear Spark or Goblin.”

“We should slaughter them in their sleep.”

Risha’s head snapped towards the speaker, but she could not tell who it was among the many Scaled who waited there.

“No.” Elderoa shook her head, “No, Amiso, we shouldn’t, at least not now. When was the last time that your hatchlings had a full belly? What did the Spark’s war buy us except more pain? Food, and safety, they matter more than anything right now.”

Risha finally caught sight of the Scaled who had spoken, his scales were white, and he sat with a seething anger that was all too familiar to Risha.

“I am here to fight for you, my people.” Elderoa continued, “And I will use every ounce of my power so that you won’t have to bind yourselves to our enemy. Someday, I will bring you true freedom, and I will not be alone.”

Her words had an effect on the gathered Scaled that Risha could see. Like magic, they drew the gathered creatures in, with a confidence that struck a cord in Risha.

Elderoa stepped forward, then kneeled before them. She bowed her head, “My kind failed you. We will never fail you again. I promise you, on the name of my Mother, and on the honor we once carried with us.”

The room held a breath, the kneeling Dragon carrying with her the pressure of subservient power.

Elderoa lifted her head, “Come before me now, so that I can keep my promise.”

The Scaled rose, and one by one, they walked up to Elderoa, their scales rippling with power as they changed into white.

After they bound themselves, they left, until only Risha and Mist-Prowler were left in the room.

Elderoa turned towards them. Her eyes moved past Mist-Prowler, landing on Risha.

She stepped back, her scales glowing as the pressure in the room increased.

There was silence for a long moment, then Elderoa calmed herself, lessening the pressure.

“Who are you? Sira, why have you brought her here?” Elderoa looked at Mist-Prowler with confusion.

Risha stepped forward, and lowered her hood and the silk mask that covered her face. “I am Risha. It is good to meet you, Elderoa.”
