Callie swallowed hard as she stood at the beginning of the course with Aurelia next to her. They were going to go first, much to both of their chagrin.

"H-How do we go about this?" Callie asked Aurelia tentatively, hoping that perhaps the older girl had already done something like this and would be willing to share something with Callie.

"You listen to my every word and don't fall behind!" Aurelia snapped, not even glancing at Callie.

Callie suppressed a sigh. She had expected as much, especially after the whole incident with Wiggles. But part of her had hoped that Aurelia would be able to look past their differences for this task at least. Apparently not.

"Begin!" Mister Blackthorn announced.

Aurelia instantly ran forward without any regard for Callie, leaving her behind. It took Callie a moment to react and run after Aurelia.

"W-Wait for me!" Callie called.

"Don't fall behind!" Aurelia barked, the distance between the two increasing by the second.

"Aurelia, if you lose your partner, you will fail," Callen warned, running alongside Callie just outside of the course. There was a convenient, narrow trail he and the other boys could use.

"I… Of course!" Aurelia paused to smile at Callen, after which she begrudgingly slowed her pace, letting Callie catch up.

"Maybe we can—" Callie began, when she suddenly noticed her brother pick one mud ball from the pouch on his belt and fling it at Callie.

Callie managed to dodge.

Aurelia laughed. "Keep up, carrothead!"

The next mudball flew straight at Aurelia. The girl was so startled that she just barely managed to dodge it.

"We must have attracted the monster because of how loud we are speaking," Callie said to Aurelia in a whisper and crashed into the other girl by stumbling over a tree root.

"Get off me!" Aurelia pushed Callie, making her fall to the ground.

Another mudball flew at Aurelia, hitting her on the shoulder.

"You're dead! Seen by a monster. Continue the course," one of the boys called.

"This is your fault!" Aurelia snarled and roughly pulled Callie up to her feet.

Callie wanted to snap that it was she who was shouting around and refusing to listen to anything Callie said. But that wouldn't accomplish anything, so instead she turned her attention to the course. "We have to be careful. There's water over there."

"You don't have to tell me! I have eyes," Aurelia said in a low hiss and boldly charged forward, jumping over the first large puddle. She landed in a pile of dry branches and pinecones, making quite the racket. Once again, Callen threw a mudball at her, which the girl barely dodged. Or perhaps, Callen's aim was off this time.

Callie followed after her, although she opted to carefully sidestep the puddle by sticking close to a tree. This way, she managed to avoid the noisy branches and pinecones. In fact, she managed to pull ahead of the startled Aurelia.

"Why you…" Aurelia turned red in the face and picked up the pace to catch up to Callie.

But the further they went, the more water appeared on the obstacle course. It wasn't rare for the only way forward to be over a deliberately placed log or by swinging on a rope that was hanging from above. The heavy feeling of being watched continued to nag at Callie.

One time, Callie thought she glimpsed something white somewhere in the distance and lost her grip, falling straight into the muddy water. Thankfully, it wasn't deep, only up to her thighs. Nonetheless, Callen jumped in instantly to pull Callie out.

"You're dead, Calluna," Callen said with a sad smile.

"What?! But I'm fine," Callie protested.

"Do you really think Mister Blackthorn would bring kids to an actual, dangerous swamp?" Callen said.

Another boy added. "In the Deep Forest, if you fall into a swamp, you will not be able to resurface."

That… made sense. The retired hunter couldn't throw them into an actual dangerous situation. But what were those white things she had seen? Was it really just her imagination? Or was it the eerie lack of birdsong that was getting to her?

The last section was incredibly difficult, requiring the girls to climb trees, swing on ropes, or carefully navigate swampy waters. Aurelia did rather well where climbing was required; however, she made a lot of noises and was often peppered with mud balls. Callie was quieter, but often fell off the logs and ropes, forcing Callen to intervene and pull her out.

By the time both girls got back, they were both, one way or another, covered in mud.

"Yes, well… I don't think I even need to say this, but your performance was atrocious," Mister Blackthorn said. "Aurelia, you completely ignored your weaker partner in favor of charging ahead. You didn't check your surroundings. Had you been in the Deep Forest, you would have attracted a monster in less than half an hour. That is, if you were lucky."

The man then turned his gaze to Callie. "You're too weak. You need to train more and watch your surroundings. While you are quieter than Aurelia, you were hit by almost every mud ball the boys threw. If that were a monster, you would have been its food!"

Callie very much wanted to object. She certainly could have dealt with real arcane beasts. Wiggles and Snowglow proved that they could be talked to, so surely whatever large arcane beast Callie met, she could talk to as well.

Although she couldn't deny that her arms were too weak. She couldn't even climb a rope properly. That was definitely something she needed to work on, if she wanted to pass the test.

"Alright, you two can head home for the day. We'll be going through this course daily for the next week, so be sure to set aside an hour of your time in the evenings," Mister Blackthorn warned.

Every day?! Wasn't that a bit much? She still had a mountain of chores and her apprenticeship. How would she find time to play with Wiggles? Or head into the forest to meet Snowglow? Or try to practice her magic.

This isn't fair!

But Callie didn't dare to complain loudly; instead, she just sullenly dragged herself home alongside Aurelia, who looked equally depressed about the result.

Once Callie was home and had washed up with the cold water, which she had to draw from the well herself, she had to tackle a mountain of tasks. Dad returned home soon after, so any chance of sneaking some play time with Wiggles was gone. She even thought that she had seen a slimy tendril poking out from behind the coop, but Dad had appeared after that, making the mireling hide away.

The next two days passed by quickly as Callie busied herself with work. She did manage to meet Wiggles a little bit in the morning, while tending to the chickens.

Miss friend.

"I'm sorry, Wiggles! I wish I could play more, but I have all these chores… I promise it'll get better once my punishment ends," Callie said.

She desperately wanted to keep that promise. It felt like she was neglecting the first arcane beast friend that she made. Unfortunately, Callie didn't get to keep her promise.

When Mom was allowed to return home, Callie and Callen both received a thorough scolding from her for recklessly charging into the Deep Forest to get her a dandilynx flower. On top of that, Callie's punishment was extended by another week, and Callen, on top of his duties, would have to help Dad in the smithy.

Sneaky Dad got his helper in the end after all.

So this busy routine continued, and Callie despaired. Callie's arms and legs often ached from all the work she had to do, and she always came back muddy in the evening from the obstacle course. Things were also busy at the healer's hut since Granny Ethel was running low on ingredients, so Tristan and Callie were running around in search of them.

She was getting better. She could maneuver over the logs without falling as often, and sometimes she even managed to pull herself up the rope. But it came at the cost of things Callie wished to learn more about—Wiggles, other arcane beasts and magic.

"Congratulations, Gerda, Illia. You have passed. Not a single error today," Mister Blackthorn announced. This was on their fifth training day.

Aurelia gasped, staring at the two girls in disbelief as they cheered at their success.

"I… I can't believe it. If it wasn't for you, I would have passed on the first try!" Aurelia snarled at Callie.

Mister Blackthorn had heard that and turned to face Aurelia. His expression was surprisingly calm, as was his voice. "You know, Callie being the most inexperienced isn't the only reason I placed her together with you. Can you guess what the other reason is?"

Aurelia fell into deep thought, but after a minute, she shook her head.

"Do you know, Calluna?"

Callie thought about it for a moment. "To force us to get along?"

"Not a bad guess and not too far from the truth. But I actually paired you together because both of you are incapable of working with others. You, Aurelia, expect everyone to listen to everything you say and follow your words as if they were a command. You are not the chief; you do not get to command others," the man leaned closer, looking at Aurelia with a stern expression. She nodded in response, albeit reluctantly.

"And you, Calluna, refuse to listen. You go off doing your own thing without any regard for other people. Admittedly, you at least showed some effort to work together with Aurelia," Mister Blackthorn noted. "Before you begin your course today, I want you to talk with each other and figure out how you can work together to pass."

With that, Mister Blackthorn turned his attention to the other team on the course, leaving Callie and Aurelia to talk.

For a long while, they didn't say anything, just occasionally glancing at each other. Aurelia looked angry, and Callie didn't want to anger her further.

"Okay, what do you think we should do? I want to pass this thing already!" Aurelia announced after a while, looking as if talking to Callie was the most painful thing in the world. At least she was talking to her. Sort of.

"Well… You're good at climbing and stuff, right?" Callie began nervously.

"Obviously!"

"And… I'm good at being quiet. I haven't had a mudball tossed at me for two days now."

"I think it's just Callen being nice because you're his sister."

"There are other boys there too, not just my brother."

Aurelia narrowed her eyes at Callie but didn't answer. She knew Callie was right, but it didn't look like she would admit it loudly.

"I-In any case. Let's try helping each other. I'll show you how to walk without making much noise, but you help me where it is necessary to climb," Callie suggested.

"Why should I? You should be able to climb on your own," Aurelia shot back stubbornly.

"D-Didn't you want to pass? It'll be much faster this way rather than you spending time to learn how to be quiet and me spending time on climbing," Callie snapped, feeling rather annoyed at Aurelia's attitude.

Aurelia's eyebrows furrowed, and for a moment, Callie feared that the girl would grow angry again. Instead, she muttered something.

"Wh-What?"

"I said FINE!" Aurelia grumbled. "But only this one time, so we can both pass and be done with this!"

Callie nodded. This certainly didn't make them friends. But this could be considered what adults call a truce. Right?

With that settled, they waited for the current team to finish the course. Each girl got hit by a mud ball once. They were very close to passing. After that, it was Callie's and Aurelia's turn.

"Begin!" Mister Blackthorn announced.

This time, instead of speeding off, Aurelia actually stayed by Callie's side. When the first large puddle arrived, Aurelia was prepared to jump over it. Callie, however, extended her hand in front of the girl, thus silently stopping her.

"What?" Aurelia hissed out, but quietly.

"There are dry branches and pinecones. If you jump, you'll have a mud ball thrown at you," Callie whispered her explanation.

Both girls glanced at the boys, who were already holding mud balls at the ready.

"Then what?" Aurelia asked.

Apparently, she had been so preoccupied charging ahead that she had never noticed how Callie dealt with this part of the course. Callie demonstrated how she stuck close to a nearby tree and stepped on its roots to get past the puddle.

Aurelia looked a bit annoyed to be doing the same thing Callie was, but in the end, followed her footsteps. Somehow, Callie managed to slow down the reckless girl and make her think about where to place her steps to not to make so much noise.

The boys did toss a few mudballs at her, when Aurelia snapped at Callie a bit too loudly. But those were more like warning shots rather than actual attempts to hit them.

When it came time to climb the ropes and logs, Aurelia reached out her hand and helped Callie climb. She was surprised how much easier it was to climb with Aurelia's help. They really should have done this earlier.

However, as they neared the end of the track, it became increasingly difficult for Aurelia to pull Callie up. The girl was likely getting tired, and Callie wasn't in any better shape. Surprisingly enough, they actually managed to get to the end of the course. All they had to do was get back the same way.

As Aurelia climbed down from the tiny wooden platform built at the end, Callie suddenly noticed with the corner of her eye. Something white. She looked around beyond the course, but saw nothing.

Must have been my imagination.

"Hurry up, carrothead!" Aurelia whispered from below.

Callie nodded and positioned herself on the edge of the platform, dangling her legs down to reach a foothold in the tree, which would allow her to safely climb down. That was when she saw it.

It was a good distance away from them, appearing small in the distance. But the moment she saw it, Callie instantly knew what it was. Similar to a lynx and white all throughout, its head was turned towards Callie, watching her from afar.

The glauxis.
