Curiously, Ana didn’t show up that night. Though Cass slept better than he had since arriving, he grew a little worried when he didn’t spot her in the early evening.

Waking up almost exactly four hours after falling asleep, Cass performed his two Stalwart dailies, as well as the Exponential. The moment those were done, he looked through the three options within the new Tempered type, then picked the one he felt he could best do unassisted.

Fine Motor Control: Complete all objectives within one hour.

Finding something he could not only drop without breaking, but bounce as well wasn’t easy. He settled on an odd decorative ball in the corner that was strangely elastic. It took him a few tries to get the hang of it, but eventually he finished the first set. Then, as his reflexes caught up with what he was trying to do, the second and the third quickly followed.

Unlike Reaction Catching, the footwork drills were easy. Between his mother’s training and Tempered’s upgrade, staying on his toes and practicing directional changes was a cinch. He flowed through the sets without a problem, his breathing even as he moved from one aggressive leap to another.

But Object Transfer Control was where he ran into trouble. The first problem he ran into was that the directions weren’t perfectly clear. He’d never seen anyone roll anything across their fingers, and it took him at least ten minutes just to figure out what it was asking of him.

The second problem was that the dexterity required to move his fingers in such a way wasn’t natural to him. Reflexes or not, this wasn’t a skill he’d ever developed or even considered doing so. The coin had to start on the edge between his pinky and ring fingers, then he had to bend and twist them so it would roll over to the next.

The sound of the Crest hitting the carpet began to ring a bell in his mind every time he heard it. He worked at it long enough that, eventually, the first two sets of objectives reset, forcing him to redo them.

Slightly sweaty after performing the catching and footwork drills a second time, Cass was seated on the couch in the foyer when Ana entered. She took one glance at the concentrated look on his face as he tried and failed to roll a coin over his fingers, then shook her head.

“I keep coming in here to see the oddest things.”

The coin fell to the carpet with a Thump.

“Damnit!”

“Sorry, did I distract you?’

He waved her off, “It’s fine. I’m just trying to learn a few new skills, that's all. How was your time with Ren?”

She fanned herself with a hand while walking into the kitchen, “That man is quite spirited.”

Cass’s face shifted from the pinched expression of frustration to one of disgust, “I once heard a stablemaster say that about my horse.”

Ana’s laugh was decidedly naughty as she removed items from the cupboards, “I’ll bet she’d say the same about Ren. A thoroughbred, that one.”

Breakfast passed, and by the end of it, he still hadn’t completed the coin trick. As he got to the Annex, Cass sat there, performing it over and over on both hands until, finally, he managed to complete it on his right. Now, it was time for the left. He was still working at that when Ren and Maggie came in.

Cass looked up, “No Noah this morning?”

“I’m covering the day for him,” The armored man said with a polite nod. “It’s only fair after he managed all of yesterday.”

“If you say so,” Cass said, slowly moving his left hand as the coin refused to budge between the ring and middle fingers. Ren took an interest in what he was doing as he crossed to the Annex.

“I had to learn to do that myself. Who taught you?”

Cass’s tongue stuck out of the side of his mouth as he said, “Nobody. Just picked it up.”

“Huh. Well, it’s a great exercise to improve your finger control. We do it all the time back at the compound where I train.”

“You don’t say,” Cass said, just as the coin slipped through his hand and hit the ground with a thump. “Fuckah you, Crest!”

Ren smirked but didn’t laugh, “The trick of it is to use a heavier coin than normal. The heavier the coin, the easier it is to manage it’s weight.”

Cass picked up the small Crest off the ground, “Thanks, I’ll keep it in mind.”

“Anytime.”

Just as he left, and Cass was once again working on his left hand, a message came in.

>Pellin: Amazing System Engineer to Cassio. Amazing System Engineer to Cassio. Come in, Cassio.

>Cassio: You made me drop my coin…

>Pellin: Life is hard all around. So, quick question for you. How much money do you have right now?

>Cassio: All of you have been spending too much time with Orla.

>Pellin: You’re not wrong about that, but it’s a real question.

>Cassio: I won’t get paid until later today. Why?

>Pellin: I’d very much like to build everyone their own Whispr, but they’re not cheap. Should be able to source some parts from our dungeon runs if Alan isn’t lying, but I’ll still need at least 50 Crests per. You got that kind of money?

>Cassio: Not on me. I’ve got a lot of dockings coming out of my pay, so I might have enough left over to give you the money for one. That good enough?

>Pellin: Yeah, that’ll work for now. Also, Bella and Adya will be waiting for you outside the Tower later today.

>Cassio: How’re they going to get through all the gates?

>Pellin: Adya says she has a plan for that. She’s not 100% sure it’ll work, but she’s going to try.

>Cassio: Roger that. Gotta go, meeting starting soon.

>Pellin: Enjoy the drudgery!

>Cassio: I live for it.

Cass closed out the messenger, then slipped the Crest into his pocket. He only had about 60 Crests left, plus a few bits from his time in Liora. As far as he knew, there were only two dockings applied to him for the previous week. That should mean a paycheck of about 160 Crests.

Since the Guildtower was kind enough to pay for both Ana and his room, that meant he should end up fairly wealthy after only a week's time. Of course, that thought sent him off on a severe recrimination spiral as he considered the poor people of the 5th ring. But soon enough, he’d have something even better to give them than money.

Experience.

And maybe a little money.

The meeting came and went. There was nothing special to talk about, just a rehash of what events were coming, including a slight increase in deliveries needed. Cass knew, secretly, that it meant there would be more deliveries sent to the Private compounds, since the exercises had ended only yesterday. Hungry soldiers needed to eat, and nothing put an appetite in the belly like fighting.

Colin gave him a nod before the end of the meeting, and Cass took that as further progress in befriending his fellow QuestWright. He had no such feelings received from Senior QuestWright Mirelle, but she did end the meeting by stating several delivery Quests would be handed out to Apprentice QuestWrights as needed. He took that to mean she’d be sticking to her word on how many Quests were required of him.

When no mention of payment appeared before she left, he took that to mean he’d find out later, storing it away in his mind.

After all, there’s no way they wouldn’t pay me, right? If everything in Liberty has to do with money, then it’s sacrosanct. Violating salary payments, aside from Dockings, would be like throwing the middle finger at a religious person’s god.

Then again, stranger things have happened since I’ve been here.

He tried not to think of Noah and the pickle as Quirrel appeared with his first stack of the day. Dropping it down, Cass noted with a smile that it was much less than he was used to seeing.

“Fifteen here for you, Cass. I have a feeling you’re going to be just fine on time today,” The Clerk said with a big smile.

“Thank you, Quirrel. Your optimism is, as always, refreshing.”

The Clerk winked at him, then quickly left as Cass got to work. Today was the seventh day since he’d arrived. By tomorrow, he should be able to start tracking which Quests will arrive and which templates to use best. Increasing his efficiency was still an enigma, but he figured he could just ask Colin when the chance appeared.

The day’s candle burned down quickly, and he all but ignored Maggie and Ren in the corner as the Re-Quests came in and out expeditiously. Cass knew he was really starting to hit a groove, as, when each stack was completed, it gave him enough time left over to continue working on the coin trick.

Leaving a daily open and incomplete just wasn’t something he was willing to do. Not to mention, he hadn’t even used Copy a second time as of yet.

I refuse to be defeated by a coin!

Thump…

Damn you!

Ana visited with his food, providing a very distracting conversation as she tried to speak quietly in the too-small room.

“-Of course we can meet again tonight. If you want to.”

“You can bet your sweet ass I want to,” Ana said in a throaty voice.

“I need a quick break.” A red-eared Cass announced as he left the room and stood outside, leaning against the wall. Even with the glares of a few untrusting QuestWrights, he still considered his exit a win. Only a few moments later, Maggie joined him, her red cheeks joining his ears in embarrassment.

“She’s very, um…open about everything.”

“Yeah.”

They stood there quietly until Ana left, blowing him a kiss while laughing.

Moments later, he was back at the Annex, a humming Ren minding his business next to Maggie. He zoned them out and continued to try completing the trick as Quirrel dropped off another stack.

The rest of the day continued like this until something changed. Cass was so distracted by the coin rolling across his fingers that at one point, only two items fell in front of him. Looking down as the silver Crest unmercifully thudded to the ground, he was shocked to see blank vellums.

“It is my honor and privilege to inform you, Junior QuestWright Cassio Vale, that all of your Quests have been completed for the day,” A beaming Quirrel said with his arms spread out. “I knew you could do it.”

Cass smiled as he picked one up, “Well, would you look at that.” Holding the two thick pages up next to his face, he said, “I never thought I’d see the day.”

“You should have more faith in yourself,” Ren’s rumbling voice said from his corner of the room.

“He’s right, you know,” Quirrel said with a smile.

Cass reached into the Whispernet and sent a message, then lifted one of the vellums and stood up, closing the Annex. With his utmost focus, he activated the Exponential Daily, watching the words spread across the page as it finished with a flourish.

[TIER 3 (RARE) EXPONENTIAL QUEST]

Quirrel, you’ve always been nice to me, so this is a gift. Exponential quest-types are different from others you may have heard of. Each day, you’ll receive a new Quest that moves in different directions. This will continue for a while, so be prepared.

If at all possible, I’d like to ask you to keep this quiet and to yourself. If you respect me at all, you won’t tell anyone about this. It’s incredibly important to me that you don’t.

You’re a good guy, in an incredibly shitty place. Thank you for being so kind and accepting of me.

Cassio Vale

Liberty Guildtower

QuestWright

Cass made sure the door was closed and that Ren and Maggie were busy before handing it over to the Clerk. He looked first at the Quest in his hand before looking at Cass, then smiled and activated it. With the glassy-eyed stare of someone looking at a System message, Cass got the pleasure of watching his eyes go wide before a huge smile broke out on his face.

“Thank you for being a great Clerk, Quirrel.”

“Haha,” The man said, reaching out and shaking Cass’s hand, “Anytime, Cass. Anytime at all. This is great! I’ll make sure to keep it to myself.”

“Keep what to yourself?” Maggie said from her corner.

“Nothing at all! Thanks, Cass! Enjoy your evening!”

“You too!”

Cass heard the sound of a pencil tapping on wood, “Did you do something you shouldn’t have?” Maggie said with a suspicious tone.

“No. I did something I’ll be doing a lot more of.” Cass corrected her. “Now, if you’ll excuse me, the city calls.”

“Heading out already?” Ren commented.

“Yep!” Cass replied, taking the extra Vellum and sliding it into his bag before putting it on his shoulder. “There’s lots to do, and I'm finally free of this prison. I’ll make sure I’m back here by morning. See you later!”

Battlefield Memory kept his path true as he quickly made his way to the Tower’s exit. The guards asked to see his pass, and with that, he was out.

And nobody was there.

Moving over to the wall, he leaned against it when one of the guards yelled, “No loitering outside!”

“Yep, right you are, sir. Not a loiterer am I.” Cass said quickly, moving away from the wall. Under the hot sun, he quickstepped away before they could say anything else.

May as well walk to the gate.

He made it about halfway there when, from the side, a rumbling draft-horse mounted by a woman in dark leather made their way over. He smiled at them both as she pulled Bella, who came to a stop and nuzzled him warmly.

“Why didn’t you just wait?” Adya asked.

“I didn’t know when you’d get here,” he said after patting the horse's neck.

“Can’t you feel where Bella is?”

“Hah!” Cass rubbed the back of his head with his other hand, “With all the excitement over leaving whenever I wanted, I completely forgot to do that. Sorry.” Taking the outstretched hand she offered, he hopped aboard the Bellatrain.

Cass put his hands hesitantly on her hips, “So, where are we going?’

“I was planning on leaving that up to you.”

“Then, off to the 5th ring we go. Just a man, his girl, and their noble steed.”

“His girl, huh?” Adya said, taking his hands and placing them around her waist. “I’m fine with that.”

Leaning forward and into her back, he smiled.

This is how it was always meant to be.
