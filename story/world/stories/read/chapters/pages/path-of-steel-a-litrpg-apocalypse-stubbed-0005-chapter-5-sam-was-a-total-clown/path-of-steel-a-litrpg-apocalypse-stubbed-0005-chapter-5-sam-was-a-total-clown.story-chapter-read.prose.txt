Five squirrels shot out of the woods.

“Careful, they’re Scottish!” Gary shouted as two of the squirrels, wearing red-green kilts, played their bagpipes. The other three—machete-wieldiers—shook, then warped their faces into bloodthirsty grins. Their bodies vibrated to the tune of the bagpipes.

Machete Squirrel, Level 1 (frenzied)

What do you get when you take a serial killer, shrink them, and make them cute? That’s right. Ma-che-te Squi-rrel!

They use tiny sharp instruments, so they probably want to trim your toe nails. You should lie down and let them. Unless you’re carrying a bagpipe, in which case they’ll leave you alone.

These little guys are also far-right, die-hard nationalists. When they hear their country’s anthem, they’re filled with national pride and fight without regard for their safety in the name of Squirrtland.

Bagpipe Squirrel, Level 1

You know those guys who play live music at KKK rituals? Yeah, this is them, except they’re nationalist instead of racist. They claim it’s just a way to make ends meet, but deep inside they relish in the blood and death.

That said, they have mad bagpipe skills.

“For the Queen!” the machete squirrels shouted, bouncing forth.

Goran’s hammer swung wide. All squirrels dodged it, too fast to track, and fell on the man’s legs. He cried out in pain.

The rest of the humans joined the fray. Gary and Tom had put away their weapons—a pencil and videogame controller respectively—in favor of their bare hands. Webley was trying to slash the squirrels with his laminated playing card, while Goran swung his hammer around. Bagpipes echoed.

It was less of a battle and more of a scramble to catch the wild animals. The squirrels dove between legs, jumped over hands, and slashed their machetes everywhere they could reach. They weren’t harmless. Deep wounds appeared on the limbs of all humans, who resumed the chase with frantic vigor.

Tom grabbed one squirrel by the tail and smashed it into the forest floor, then stomped on it. Goran swung wide in panic, almost hitting Gary, who was luckily looking that way and managed to duck.

“Careful!” Gary shouted.

“Shut up and catch them!” Goran replied, swinging frantically. Gary gave him a wide berth.

Webley was the one to catch the second squirrel, nicking it with a flick of his card. The beast cried out in pain, then was kicked in the back by Gary and flew into the forest. Only one machete squirrel remained, but the bastard was fast. Its body’s vibration intensified. It sped up, a blur of brown and bloody lines, as all the bagpipes’ magic focused on it.

“I’ll fooking carve ya!” the squirrel yelled.

“Get the damn bards!” Gary cried out, but Goran was already stomping over. His hammer fell like a meteor. The squirrels jumped to the side to dodge it, but they had to stop playing their bagpipes, leaving their machete comrade trapped between Gary, Webley, and Tom. If it realized it was no longer enhanced, it didn’t show it. It resumed its risky maneuvers until Gary grabbed its machete. That was the beginning of the end.

“Did you get them?” Gary asked, wiping his bloody hand—the machete had carved up his palm nicely.

“Sure did,” Goran said, showing off his hammer. Blood was disappearing off its head.

“Goran! We were supposed to capture one to interrogate it!” Gary said.

“Then you get them next time, wuss. Those things were carving me up. I didn’t want to risk it.”

“Do you really think they have magic?” Tom asked.

“They played bagpipes and made the machete guys faster. What else could it be?”

“It doesn’t fucking matter. We get stronger with each kill. That’s the only magic,” Goran said, pacing over like an angry bull.

“You don’t need to get butt-hurt because you didn’t catch them,” Gary said, which was apparently a bad idea because Goran grabbed him by the collar and pulled him in.

“I have the hammer. You don’t tell me what to do!” he screamed in Gary’s face, who froze for a moment before raising his hands.

“You’re right. I’m sorry.”

“You better damn be,” Goran said, dropping Gary to the ground. He turned around. “Come on. We keep hunting.”

Gary looked around. The air shimmered over the squirrel corpses, dripping power into everyone’s chest, contributing to the sitting mass. Their wounds were closing too, turning the deep gashes into what looked more like cat scratches.

This was their second hunt together, and Gary opened his status screen.

Wisdom: 4

Advance your cultivation to access more System functions.

“Yes!” he whispered, seeing his Strength and Speed had ticked up again. Looking around, Webley seemed equally excited, while Tom and Goran were disappointed. Everyone was panting heavily.

“Can’t we rest a bit, Goran?” Webley said as if reading Gary’s thoughts.

“Why rest? Are we weak?”

“We’ll do whatever you say. I just think we can take a moment to make sure we’re ready for the next fight. What if it’s harder?”

“They’re squirrels, Webley. Are you afraid of squirrels?”

“I think Webley has a point,” Tom said. “Maybe the next battle won’t be squirrels. Even these guys would have been much more difficult if Gary didn’t tell us to fight them in a clearing. Between the trees, they could have reached our faces.”

Goran frowned, glaring at the other three. “Fine,” he said, sitting down where he stood. He kept the hammer in hand.

Gary and the others also sat down.

“I still don’t get why they’re far-right Scottish,” Webley said.

“Maybe they had emotionally distant parents?” Gary said.

“I don’t think so,” Tom said. “They’re alien monsters, right? Why would they be related to Scotland? I think all this is just a game, and some engine generated them based on hastily-read Earth customs.”

“Are you saying they’re fake?” Webley asked.

“They’re racist Scottish squirrels. What do you think?”

“You sound like you know games, Tom,” Gary said, pointing at Tom’s game controller.

“It’s how I spent my free time, back before…this,” he gestured around. “PC mostly, but I did have a Playstation.”

“And I’m guessing you were a gambler?” Gary asked Webley.

Webley stared at the laminated jester of hearts in his hand. “Not really. But I did know a couple card tricks.”

“And I used to write stories when I was younger,” Gary said, glancing at the pencil in his hand. They all stared at Goran, who held a sledgehammer.

“I’m a farmer,” he said. “I repair things all the time.”

“So these items weren’t as random as they made us think. Did you guys also meet a robot bureaucrat that spoke in riddles?” Gary asked.

Tom and Webley nodded.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Goran asked. “My guy was a robot, but he was all business.”

“So he didn’t overwhelm you and toss you out?”

“Hell no. He just described how everything works.”

The other three all glanced at each other. “How does everything work, Goran?” Webley asked.

“We just need to survive for twenty days and reach Level 1,” Goran said, looking around smugly. “Your guy really didn’t explain? What a fraud.”

“He didn’t say shit!” Gary said. “What else did he tell you?”

“Not much. That there would be monsters, some stronger than others, and that killing lots is good for me.”

“Did he explain how to level up?” Tom asked.

“If he did, I wouldn’t be level 0. He made it sound hard.”

“I think we need to kill a lot of monsters,” Webley said. “Just like Goran’s guy said. It’s like a game, except leveling up is harder. We need to kill lots to reach level 1.”

“Obviously,” Goran said.

Gary frowned. “I don’t know. I think we’re missing something.”

“That’s the problem: you think,” Goran said. “We’re killing monsters and growing stronger. That’s exactly how it’s supposed to work. Don’t bog your head in the details.”

Gary held back his tongue. “Maybe you’re right,” he said.

“I am right. That’s why I have a hammer.”

A moment of silence followed.

“Why did you guys enter the Tower?” Tom asked.

“To get stronger. Which is exactly what we should be doing right now,” Goran said, getting to his feet. “Up. We hunt now, talk later.”

Tom and Webley rose immediately. Gary followed a beat later, watching the three walk ahead of him. They seemed close-knit, yet, from what Gary had gathered, they didn’t know each other before the Tower.

A few moments of walking later, Tom trailed a step behind the others. Gary came beside him and walked slower on purpose, making Tom slow down as well. When they were several steps away from the others, he spoke.

“Can I ask you something?”

Tom looked at him weird. “Sure.”

“But I want an honest answer, okay?”

“Just ask.”

Gary studied the caution in the other’s face, then glanced at the head of Goran’s hammer.

“Monster blood disappears when the monster is killed. Does the same apply to humans?”

“How would I know?” Tom replied.

“I’m just curious.”

Tom gave him a cold look, then walked faster to reach the others. Gary followed silently. He was just about to start a conversation when Webley raised a hand. “Do you hear that?” he said.

Everyone froze. Gary heard it. Flowing in the wind was a faint creaking, like a wooden wheel rolling on the forest floor. His blood went cold.

“We need to run,” he said.

“Why?” Goran asked.

“That’s a really strong monster. Really, really strong. It will kill us all. We need to run right now.”

Goran barked a laugh. “A strong monster? Perfect! Maybe I’ll finally get to test my hammer on something that fights back.”

“Goran, I’m begging you, we need to escape.”

“We’re here to hunt, coward. If you want to run, feel free. Are you guys with me?”

Tom and Webley glanced at each other, then at Gary. “We’re in,” they said.

“Perfect. Let’s go before it escapes!” Goran said, breaking into a brisk walk in the creaking’s direction. The other two followed. Gary stared at them as they made some distance. His feet didn’t want to move. He remembered the malice he’d felt just yesterday, shivering in that bush.

He grabbed his forearm. It was covered in tiny scars from the thorns.

The three men paused. “Are you coming, Gary?” Goran asked. His tone was off. Gary closed his eyes, took a deep breath, then opened them again.

He’s an asshole, but he’s right. I’m here to fight. There’s four of us, and we have a hammer.

So why is my heart shaking so?

“I’m coming,” he replied, then followed the others. Goran nodded. The others sighed with relief.

The creaking echoed louder. A minute later, it filled the forest, eliminating all other sounds. Gary felt his heart tighten with every step. He grabbed his pencil and kept going.

“Guys—” he said, but the words died in his throat when the front of a cart emerged from behind a tree. He saw the wooden wheels, old and creaking. Saw the cart, thigh-high, filled with roundish red objects.

He held his breath. So did the others. The cart kept moving, revealing two handles pushed by white-gloved hands. The frigid, cruel aura slipped around the tree as the rest of the creature appeared.

A ridiculous, multi-colored suit. A three-pronged hat with bells hanging from its ends, jingling lightly, as did the bells at the tips of the creature’s shoes. A face so heavily covered in make-up it resembled a mask. A permanent, wide smile.

Its hands ended in pristine white gloves, and Gary’s mind finally realized what the cart was carrying. Hearts. Beating, crimson human hearts.

The jester stopped, turned only its head towards them, and smiled wider.

Jester of Hearts, Level 10 (Field Boss)

Let me tell you a little story.

There once was a man called Sam. Sam loved a particular girl. The problem was, she didn’t love him back, but Sam wouldn’t quit that easily. He put on tons of make-up to hide his ugliness, wore gloves to hide the spots on his hands, and bought a hat to hide his growing baldness. He didn’t eat for two weeks to lose some weight.

When he was ready, he went to the girl’s home and rang the bell, only for another man to answer.

Sam was inconsolable. He had given her his heart, and she’d thrown it away. She’d betrayed him.

To keep on living, Sam needed his heart back. He returned to the girl’s home, struck the guy with a hammer, then demanded she return his broken heart. When she didn’t, he took hers instead. A heart for a heart. If he couldn’t go on living, neither should she. He also took the other man’s, since he was already there.

Sam still lacked his heart. He hadn’t found it in the girl’s house or the nearby trashcans. He did, however, have two new hearts, which he stuffed in a grocery bag and carried back with him. They were pretty. He decided to gather more. Since he couldn’t conquer them, he just had to take them by force. He did, after all, have a hammer.

On the eve of his third murder came the Drafting, and Sam received an offer different than most. He’d be given power and all the hearts he could take. He struck a deal.

As for why he’s dressed as a jester… Well, that’s because Sam was a total clown.

Like with that first squirrel, the description dove into Gary’s head and gave him the information instantly. The jester still stared at him. It let go of the cart and turned to face them completely.

“Run!” Gary said, but Goran’s hand was on his shoulder.

“We fight. He’s unarmed! Surround him!”

Tom and Webley fanned out. The jester paced towards them, its aura so thick it was suffocating.

How can they not feel it? Gary wondered, shaking violently. The air is humming. That thing… It’s going to kill us all! We have to run!

His feet refused to move. Webley readied his laminated card—the jester of hearts—while Tom held his controller by its end. Goran grabbed the hammer with both hands. “Attack!” he roared. They converged on the jester.

Gary was the only one who saw it move, and that’s because he was far enough away. The jester blurred. Webley’s card slash met nothing, while the jester’s hand was already sticking out of his chest, holding onto his beating heart.

Webley looked down in incomprehension, opened his mouth soundlessly, then collapsed. Tom and Goran, both standing next to Webley, stood frozen.

The jester zipped back to the cart, deposited the heart on the pile, then turned towards them again. It fished a white handkerchief from its chest pocket and meticulously wiped its glove clean.

Tom and Goran screamed and ran off, but they weren’t nearly fast enough. Bells jingled. The jester was suddenly squatting on Tom’s shoulders, and it stretched out the horror for a moment before its hand blurred. It held another beating heart. Tom collapsed, his blood wetting the grass.

“Stand and fight!” Goran shouted as he ran, but Gary had woken up by now, and he was sprinting hard in the only direction that made sense. “Stop!” Goran shouted, but Gary knew he’d never catch up. They were too far apart.

Something smacked the small of Gary’s back—hard. He felt his spine bend but hold. The impact took him to the ground. Turning his head, the hammer lay beside him, and Goran was sprinting over. Behind him, the jester had already left Tom’s heart on the cart and wiped its hand. It was approaching fast. Slender legs blurred under it.

“Take his heart!” Goran shouted, racing past the fallen Gary, who suddenly realized he was next in line. He grabbed Goran’s ankle, making the other man fall. He jumped over him with strength he didn’t know he had. In that airborne moment, he saw Goran’s hand snake back, and he more sensed than saw the hammer coming. He ducked so hard he fell to the ground face-first. The hammerhead sailed over him, and as he pushed his fingers into the grass to take off, a meaty hand grabbed his calf.

“Gotcha!” came a voice.

Gary tugged with all his strength, but Goran was stronger. He couldn’t break free. He turned around.
