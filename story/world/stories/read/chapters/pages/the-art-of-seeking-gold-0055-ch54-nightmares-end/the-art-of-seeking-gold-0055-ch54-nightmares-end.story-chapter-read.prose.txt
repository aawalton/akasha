Azazel led them back between the shelves. Nobody felt like talking.

Amy stayed near the back with a hand on her satchel. Now that nothing was trying to kill her, staying awake had become a problem. She glanced at the others, who looked about as ready to fall over as she was.

Better finish this fast.

About half a minute later, the shelves opened onto a smaller, round room. A white book lay on a pedestal of white marble in the middle.

“Mmm…” Azazel raised a hand. The book shook, then suddenly there were seven of it, all as white as the first.

After hanging there for a few seconds, the books drifted toward them, one apiece, and waited to be taken.

Amy hesitated before accepting hers. None of this had happened in the manga. Azazel had promised the gifts would benefit them, and she shouldn't have been able to break their deal, but Amy still wasn't particularly comfortable taking something from her.

As Amy closed her hand around the cover, it turned deep red. A yellow triangle slowly formed in the center.

She looked at the others. Their books had changed too, each a different color, though Zayd's was red like hers. He didn't have the triangle, at least. Amy looked between the two books, frowning.

Hers even had the same size and thickness as Libris. With that color and the triangle on the front, she could have been holding a second copy.

Why…?

She cast her eyes back to Azazel, a question nearly escaping her lips before she caught herself. Right. No questions yet. At least, not directly. Instead, she narrowed her eyes and locked her gaze onto the demon in a silent demand for an explanation.

Azazel met her stare for a moment before offering a knowing smile. The demon was the first to turn away as she shifted her gaze toward Crow. “I suppose explanations are in order,” she said lightly. Without waiting for a reply, she stepped past them and walked toward another corridor lined with towering shelves. “Come. I will explain each of your gifts while we head toward the exit.”

After a pause, one by one, they all followed.

“Accompanying us toward the exit…” Iris noted as they walked. “Very generous, especially for a scammer.”

Azazel glanced at her from the corner of her eye with amusement. “That,” she said in an overly bright tone, “is because I possess a very big heart.”

Long silence followed her words.

“And because I simply dislike people wandering around inside my library,” Azazel finally added when the silence stretched on for too long.

“…There it is,” Ash muttered.

Azazel grinned at him and kept walking. “Honestly, sitting around waiting for you to make your way to my desk was far too much for my fragile heart. After so many years without visitors, I had completely forgotten the feeling.”

Iris snorted. “Hearing that almost makes me want to wander around and touch random books just out of revenge for scamming us.”

Azazel stopped dead in her tracks. The change in momentum was so abrupt that Crow and Lyra nearly collided with her.

Slowly, the demon turned her head toward Iris. She wasn't smiling anymore. The killing intent that came with that look made Amy stiffen, even though those white eyes were on Iris. Cold sweat ran down Amy's back. She hadn't even realized how badly her body could still react after everything they'd been through.

“Little girl,” Azazel said softly, “even if the contract expels me from this realm, I will make absolutely sure to drag you to Hell with me for a lifetime of torture before you touch a single one of my books.”

Nobody spoke for several seconds as they were utterly paralyzed by the crushing pressure raining down on them, an aura somehow multiple times heavier than Abaddon's.

Iris raised both hands in surrender once she managed to regain her composure. “Okay, chill. It was a joke.”

For one long second, Azazel simply stared at her. Then, the demon spun back around with a bright, cheerful smile, acting as if nothing had happened at all.

“Anyway,” she said merrily, resuming her pace. “Your gifts. The books you hold are called Archives. Archives are fragments of my library given physical form and bound directly to your souls.”

If anyone had still been feeling drowsy, the previous killing intent woke them right up. It took a moment and a fair bit of courage for the group to keep following her as if everything were normal.

“Crow,” Azazel said without breaking her stride, gesturing toward his book. “That is the Tome of Despair. It will allow you to experience immense despair by reliving your worst memories and witnessing catastrophic possible futures for one hour every day. I don’t think I need to explain how perfectly that synergizes with your peculiar ability, do I?”

Crow remained silent, his lips thinning into a hard line. Amy couldn’t blame him. What sort of gift was that? Useful? Yes, but incredibly cruel.

Lyra looked ready to speak up on his behalf, but Crow stopped her with a quiet shake of his head.

With a heavy sigh, he finally spoke. “Thanks.”

“You’re welcome,” Azazel replied with a smirk, turning her gaze to Lyra. “As for you, girl, that book holds a technique crafted by the Goddess herself.”

Both Amy and Lyra frowned deeply, though for entirely different reasons. Amy’s expression carried pure distrust, automatically wary of anything associated with the Goddess. Lyra, meanwhile, looked deeply offended.

“The Goddess would never leave her teachings behind for anyone but her true followers,” Lyra said sharply. “Try getting better at lying, devil.”

Azazel glanced over her shoulder and smiled. “My, my. Such hostility.” The demon pressed a hand against her chest dramatically. “You wound me, little believer.”

Lyra’s eyes narrowed further. “You expect me to believe the Goddess personally entrusted you with her teachings?”

“No,” Azazel replied lightly. “I don’t care whether you believe my words or not. Moreover, she didn’t entrust anything to me. This Library simply records every spell ever cast in the mortal realm, past and future alike.”

“The Goddess cannot be tracked or copied.”

Azazel rolled her eyes. “Either way,” she said, pointing at the faint green book enveloping Lyra’s hands. “Contrary to what your man-made texts claim, neither 'Hell' nor the so-called 'Heaven' are places where mortal souls go when they die. In fact, even the Goddess cannot bring back the dead. That grimoire merely records one of the spells she used after the death of one of the many lovers she had throughout her eternal life.”

Lyra shook her head again and muttered a quiet denial, but Azazel completely ignored her.

“She bathed an entire dying land in green, transforming it into what we now know as the Great Forest, despite knowing full well there was no one left to heal. Flowers bloomed over corpses for weeks.”

“Fake,” Lyra retorted almost immediately.

Azazel merely shrugged. “Is that so?” She tilted her head. “Then just think of it as a very cool spell that heals wounds over a large area. Who knows? Maybe your boyfriend might even get his arm back if you master it.”

Lyra made a sharp, choked sound at the comment, but Amy was too focused on the deeper implications of Azazel’s words to pay attention to it.

Not even gods can bring back the dead…

The concept lingered unpleasantly in her mind. Had the Goddess not brought back Libris from the dead?

Was the librarian wrong? It wouldn’t surprise Amy since the Goddess just felt like someone who was above everything and anything.

Actually, she did mention that they hadn't technically been dead yet…

The demon’s gaze shifted away from Lyra and settled on Zayd. Azazel was now walking backward, which, given her previous display of killing intent, made her look in Amy’s eyes, incredibly creepy.

“And yours,” she continued casually, “is considerably simpler: a collection of divination techniques and hidden knowledge stolen from the Gaspards.”

Zayd tilted his head, and his face twisted into an expression of confusion. “Why would I need this? Even if I'm currently barred from the greatest secrets my family holds, I'll naturally gain access to them as I grow older.”

“Do you really think you’ll last that long over there?” Azazel asked with a knowing, sharp smile. The remark instantly silenced Zayd, a troubled frown etching itself onto his face.

“Moving on. Ash and Iris, yours are even simpler,” she said, her eyes darting back and forth between the two. “They contain personalized hand-to-hand combat techniques developed by wandering travelers and forgotten over time.”

Ash blinked. “That’s it?”

“That’s it,” Azazel confirmed. “You hit things. Iris hits things. I simply made sure you two can hit things better.”

“Nice,” Iris muttered.

Azazel smiled at her before turning her attention to Lain.

Lain tightened her hold on the book before Azazel said anything. Amy noticed how closely she was watching the demon now. Since the fight, she'd looked almost as absent as Crow, but whatever she'd been thinking about had apparently been put aside for this.

“I had to put a little more work into yours,” Azazel told her, smiling.

Lain frowned at that.

“It covers human behavior. Mannerisms, emotions, how to recognize a lie. Customs, expectations, and the many ways people contradict themselves.”

Lain's eyes widened slightly. Amy was less pleased with where this seemed to be going.

“Somebody gets upset with you, and you're supposed to know why,” Azazel went on. “Even if they've never told you what you did wrong. Their customs and expectations are in there, written out so you can actually read them.”

Lain's fingers were digging into the cover, and so, Amy noticed, were her own.

“If you've done something they find strange, you can read their reasons. The same goes for things they consider inappropriate or unsettling; the book tells you what bothers them about it. Rather more useful than simply being told that you ought to know better, don't you think?”

Lain had gone completely still. Amy scowled at the demon.

Was this supposed to be a gift or an insult? Azazel had promised something beneficial, without tricks, and Amy was about to remind her of that when she looked at Lain again. The girl seemed genuinely interested. More than interested, actually. She hadn't taken her eyes off Azazel.

“And there's some guidance in there as well.”

Lain blinked at her. Iris asked, “Guidance for what?”

“What you might do with your life,” Azazel said, still addressing Lain. “And how you might find your way through other people's. Possibilities, dear. You won't find a set of instructions telling you how to live.”

Nobody spoke, so Azazel went on, sounding rather pleased with herself. “There is also some advice on emotionally unstable friends. The sort you have to keep saving from themselves, whether they appreciate it or not.”

Lain turned to Amy at once. Amy frowned and went back to watching Azazel, deciding she didn't need to know what that was supposed to mean.

The demon grinned at her. “And yours,” she said.

Amy’s frown deepened.

“Your gift is rather personal,” the demon said softly. “I highly recommend opening it only when you are completely alone in your room.”

Amy’s irritation flared instantly. “Ugh,” she groaned loudly. “Damn you, leaving me for last and then telling me to wait even longer…”

“Haha, yes. I did that.”

“That’s incredibly annoying.”

“I know.”

Amy narrowed her eyes, but Azazel merely offered an innocent, mocking smile. Taking a deep breath, Amy forced herself to calm down. She decided it was best to listen to the devil's advice, just in case. Once she was safely back in her quarters and rested, she would use her ability to make sure there were no hidden traps—even if, logically speaking, there shouldn’t be.

As they walked, she did her best to ignore her growing impatience to open the book, which looked so unsettlingly like Libris, and forced her attention elsewhere.

It was then that she caught a faint sound. It was soft at first, but steadily growing clearer.

It sounded like rain.

She frowned, glancing around at the endless rows of shelves. Where could that possibly be coming from?

The sound grew louder with every step they took.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

Suddenly, Azazel halted. Crow and Lyra almost walked into her again. Neither seemed to have the energy to keep stopping whenever she felt like it.

Azazel reached into the empty space ahead and took hold of something Amy couldn't see.

Then she pulled. Amy stared as the air folded under the woman's fingers and opened down the middle, like a pair of doors. She recognized what was on the other side.

Snow, as far as she could see, with that same gray sky over it. The enormous angel still stood above the frozen wasteland, looking down.

Now there was black rain, too. Amy followed it upward and realized it was coming from the colossus's eyes. That stuff wasn't water. The thing was crying.

“I cannot accompany you any farther than this,” the librarian said, turning back to face them. “Now that the colossus has begun to weep, the obsidian archway you used to enter this nightmare has reopened. You can go back to your world from there.”

She smiled as the black rain poured heavily behind her. She reached into the space beside the opening, making it ripple again. This time, seven black umbrellas came out.

“Take them,” she said as they floated toward the group. “I assume none of you would like to become snow.”

Amy fumbled hers before getting a proper hold on it. “…The hell…?”

Azazel looked out through the doorway. Amy followed her gaze, wondering what that was supposed to mean.

Something was moving a long way off. Several somethings, with limbs bent the wrong way and bodies made of darkness, stumbling through the rain.

Chaos creatures. As the drops hit them, their bodies turned to pale stone, then broke apart into snow. Amy watched it happen.

“Living beings before, and now chaos creatures,” Azazel said. “I still find it difficult to believe something eternal can simply disappear after a few drops. No matter how often I see it.” She turned back to them. “Don't worry about the wet ground. It only affects what the drops fall on directly. Just make sure not to stand out there for too long; even umbrellas crafted from the wood of the World Tree won't hold out forever.”

No one spoke. The rhythmic noise of the rain filled the tense silence.

Slowly, Azazel turned her attention back to Amy and Lain.

“I owe the two of you one question each, so please, ask recklessly,” she continued. “And do refrain from trying to combine two questions into one. It is incredibly rude, and only I am permitted to trick people.” She paused, baring her teeth in a sharp smile that highlighted her prominent canines. “Anyone who tries to cheat me gets eaten. It doesn't matter if I'm banished back to Hell because of it.”

Iris visibly took a step backward.

Azazel completely ignored her as she sifted her eyes on Amy. “And before you ask about how to defeat a certain being…” Her smile sharpened almost imperceptibly. “Give up.”

Amy froze. The others looked confused at her words, but Amy knew exactly who the librarian was referring to.

Frowning, she shook her head and took a sharp breath. As if she would ever give up that easily...

Amy cast a slow look toward Lain. For a brief moment, the white-haired girl hesitated, then she gave her a nod.

“How do we stop the angel from destroying the world?” Lain asked, stepping forward after their silent exchange.

For only the second time since they had met her, Azazel’s smile faltered slightly. “To stop them,” the demon answered, “you must enter the lost city of Avalon once it reappears within this realm.”

“Avalon…” Crow murmured under his breath.

“It will return in approximately five years,” Azazel continued. “And the most powerful factions in this world all seek entry. This includes the Blood Emperor, the Tribe of Onyx, the King, the Gaspard lineage, and Abaddon… though I am uncertain if the last one cares much anymore.”

Crow visibly shuddered at the mention of the name.

“Inside the city lies their heart,” Azazel continued. “Find it, and neutralize it in whatever way you see fit to stop this from happening.” She said while pointing outside.

Amy’s brow furrowed. A heart? What does she mean by that? Is that massive thing actually alive?

“What are they?” Amy suddenly asked, drawing the librarian’s focus to her.

Azazel stared at her for several agonizingly silent seconds before her lips curled into a strange smile. “It is better to ask who they are.” Her smile vanished entirely, leaving her expression completely unreadable. “They are the absolute antithesis of the Goddess. Closely related to her… Even she fears them, and consequently, so do I.”

Silence covered the group as they absorbed the weight of her words. Meanwhile, Amy’s mind went into absolute overdrive. The demon claimed that defeating the Goddess was an impossibility, but wasn't the angel in the sky a lead, or at least related to doing exactly that? Sure, it might be a reach from Amy’s part, but still… Hadn't Abaddon’s entire plan revolved around extracting the angel’s power to counter the Goddess?

“Apart from that…” Azazel added softly, “Even I do not know who she truly is.”

That single admission pulled Amy abruptly from her thoughts, disturbing her far more than everything else combined. How could a demon who possessed intimate knowledge of the Goddess’s personal history and the colossus’s ties to her be entirely ignorant of the entity's true identity?

“How are we supposed to enter Avalon?” Crow asked suddenly, drawing everyone's attention.

Azazel was smiling again, apparently finished with being serious. “I've already answered your question, Mr. Thorne. I don't owe you another.”

Crow looked ready to argue.

“However, I will answer this one. It happens to be in my interests.”

Amy studied her for a moment. This sounded familiar. Then she remembered, and despite how tired she was, she found herself smiling.

This was why they'd come here. In the manga, Azazel had given them something at this point, explaining very little beyond the fact that they would have to cooperate if they wanted to save the world.

Azazel put a hand to her chest and drew out a small seal from where her heart should have been. White marble, six sides. Amy stared at it, almost too relieved to believe they were actually getting it.

They'd actually done it.

“Here is your key,” Azazel said.

“Oh Goddess,” Ash groaned. “Another one?”

Azazel ignored him and closed both hands around the seal.

Crack.

It broke into seven pieces. They floated away from her hands and stopped in front of the students, one fragment for each of them.

“It's no use to me,” she explained. “I'm bound to this Library. I can't leave.”

Amy took the piece waiting in front of her. It felt warm against her fingers, which was surprisingly comforting.

“Those will let all seven of you enter Avalon.”

The rain outside was getting louder. Nobody answered Azazel, who looked over them with the same pleasant smile she'd worn at her desk, then raised a hand to wave.

“Well, goodbye.”

And she was gone. Amy was still looking at the place where she'd been standing, trying to work out whether that was really it.

The others looked just as confused. They glanced at each other, then at the umbrellas they'd been left holding.

One at a time, they raised them over their heads and stepped cautiously through the opening. The obsidian archway was still visible in the distance, where they'd entered the nightmare. They started toward it through the snow.

They could finally go back.
