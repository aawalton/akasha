Chapter 76

Inside

And so Shin and Mai waited for a few minutes, though it felt like hours inside that place, where time was difficult to measure, hoping that the ancient mechanism would regret its decision and open again.

The only way they could measure time was by how long the pinecones burned, and they didn't have very many if this went on for too long.

They searched and searched, but the line that marked where the door had been was nowhere to be found.

Nothing happened.

The silence of the place was unsettling, and that only made things worse.

Shin cursed himself for not paying more attention and examining the carving properly on the inside when they had entered. Now they couldn't tell whether there was some mechanism that had been activated, or even whether they had triggered it by accident.

Mai was just as observant, searching the walls with him. But remembering what had happened outside, she didn't seem eager to leave again without knowing what awaited them on the other side.

Shin hadn't asked her how she had gotten there properly. She had only pointed toward the distance. But when she did, her reaction had been one of horror, and for a moment he had almost thought she was going to vomit.

She managed to hold it back.

"I woke up in a river... carrying bodies. Everything was dark, but I saw corpses. It wasn't until I got away because those things were chasing me that the storm disappeared, and the moon guided me here."

"Why did you come here?"

"I thought there would be people."

Shin thought about it.

A river carrying bodies?

That explained why she had been wet when he first saw her.

She must have run like the wind to get here.

He was about to ask her more when they felt a creaking throughout the structure in front of them.

Then came a movement from directly beneath their feet.

Shin and Mai searched for the source, and when they realized what was happening, it only made them more confused.

The wall where the door had been was moving.

Toward them. Slowly.

They looked around, and it seemed as though the entire place was changing, almost reconfiguring itself.

They couldn't be sure how much time they had, but if it continued, in less than five minutes they wouldn't be able to get out of that passage.

Unless they went farther into the corridor they had entered earlier.

"This isn't possible," Shin said.

"What is this place?" Mai asked.

[More importantly, where is the mechanism moving everything?]

They had no answers.

And the truth was that staying there would only result in both of them being crushed, unless they ventured deeper into the bowels of that stone giant, that seemed to have awakened at the intrusion of its two strange visitors.

In the end, they had no choice but to move forward.

Once they entered one of the corridors, the movement continued for another couple of minutes.

But the section they had come from was definitely sealed. They could no longer leave through the way they had entered.

They retraced the corridors they had already seen when they first ventured inside, then passed through stone chambers where their bare footsteps seemed to echo throughout the abandoned place.

The torchlight cast unstable shadows that sometimes appeared to move on their own, but neither of them commented on it.

When they reached a corridor covered in sculpted images, Shin was the first to notice the difference.

He stopped. "...The carvings are a little different."

Mai looked around, confused. "What?"

"The carvings. The stone here is darker."

Mai looked more closely.

The stone really was darker, and the carvings were difficult to distinguish.

But certain sections seemed more organic.

Almost as though forms of life resembling nautiluses and other marine creatures, or malformed skeletons, had been arranged in perfect order across the walls and then petrified.

Or, more unsettlingly, as if they had grown directly from the wall itself.

That was the closest comparison they could make.

The sinuous, curved forms were genuinely deceptive, and it was impossible to say whether they were actually real creatures or merely the product of the imagination and skill of the craftsmen who had built the place.

It truly looked like flesh, bone, and other cartilaginous parts from countless creatures had been assembled together while following the angles of the surrounding structure, creating an overall architectural order.

Yet it contrasted sharply with the carvings and artwork of the completely alien creatures depicted throughout the walls and sculptures.

They passed through several rooms and corridors built in the same style until the stone once again took on a more yellowish tone beneath the torchlight.

It was a relief.

The previous section had left them with the disturbing impression that they had been inside something alive, even though it was made of stone.

Almost like a tunnel of petrified flesh that someone had deliberately shaped according to straight lines and precise measurements.

The corridor they were now walking through depicted, along one of its walls, a river filled with drifting corpses.

It opened into an enormous oval-shaped chamber.

Mai stopped and studied the carvings.

The walls of the oval chamber were divided by golden lines separating several sections.

Each section was covered with large disks of polished black stone, embedded into the walls like mirrors.

They were so dark that they reflected the torchlight like motionless water, deep and without a visible bottom.

But the most unsettling things weren't the mirrors.

They were the images and sculptures. The reliefs covering the walls followed no logical order. They didn't seem to belong to a single culture, or even to a single period.

It was as though someone had taken fragments from countless different stories and layered them together without caring about coherence.

In some sections, that same organic style appeared again, blending into the other artistic styles.

There were figures wearing solar crowns, confronting bat-winged creatures. Dragons coiled around towers without windows. Lions with exaggerated proportions and overly expressive eyes. Warriors with spears fighting gigantic bulls. Serpents encircling entire mountains. Men wearing animal masks guiding ships across seas filled with impossible shapes.

There were other barrel-shaped forms with wings flying above mountains.

Others depicted strange forms of life descending to the earth from the sky.

Everything coexisted within the same space, without hierarchy or explanation.

Mai slowed her pace even further, carefully observing every detail.

"It’s like... everything has been mixed together," Mai murmured.

Shin didn't answer immediately. He had noticed something else. Something that kept repeating.

"Do you see it?" he finally asked.

Mai narrowed her eyes. "What?"

"That."

He pointed toward the ceiling and then the floor.

The ceiling was slightly vaulted, but otherwise fairly plain. It was covered in engraved geometric patterns.

The same patterns appeared on the floor. But one particular shape was repeated far more than the others.

Triangles.

Once they paid attention, they could even find them in the carvings throughout the chamber if they looked closely enough. Small ones, large ones, inverted ones, overlapping ones.

They were everywhere if you knew where to look.

They formed part of shields, mountains, temples. Some even floated in the sky above certain carved figures.

A constant symbol.

Mai slowly nodded. "Yes... they're everywhere. Do you think it has something to do with the pyramid?"

"I don't know."

Neither of them added anything else, but they both felt it.

Mai looked at herself in one of the mirrors and regained some of her modesty.

Seeing the two of them reflected in those dark mirrors reminded her that they were both completely naked inside that enclosed place.

It was probably better not to think about it too much.

Neither of them spoke for several minutes.

How long had they been walking?

The sound of their footsteps was the only thing breaking the silence.

And then, as though the body remembered before the mind did, another concern surfaced.

Water.

Shin looked at the stone head he was carrying, where they still had some of the liquid they had boiled. There wasn't much left after he had dropped it while catching Mai.

They hadn't exactly planned on getting trapped here in the first place.

Mai walked carefully.

Her feet still ached from the cold she had endured earlier, although the floor was kinder now.

The marble, black, gray, and white, covered in layers of accumulated dirt, was no longer quite as hostile.

Her hair was still slightly damp, but at least the air inside was more stable.

There was no wind, and now both of them could feel a faint warmth emanating from the stone itself, as though it held the memory of an ancient heat.

Mai suddenly stopped, slightly flushed.

"I think... I need to pee." The direct way she said it, despite the slight hint of embarrassment in her voice, broke some of the tension of the moment.

Shin stared at her for a moment, then looked away, processing the situation more than necessary. "Be careful..."

She looked at him, confused. "Why?"

Shin hesitated, as though searching for the right way to explain it. "We don't have much water."

The silence that followed was brief, but enough for Mai to blush even more.

She understood.

Her expression changed slightly, and a small shiver ran through her body.

That was the implication. They both thought they were trapped.

And they didn't expect to find a source of water inside this place, unless they resorted to licking the salty moisture from some of the corridors.

If they stayed there longer than necessary, they would have to find other ways to hydrate themselves.

"For now..." Shin continued, his tone more measured, "if we conserve what we have, it should last us a day. As long as we trust that we can get out somewhere else, there shouldn't be a problem."

It wasn't a guarantee. More like an estimate.Mai nodded silently.

Then she looked around, clearly uncomfortable with the idea of doing it in a place like that.

Shin sighed and took a step back."I'll move away."

"No!"

He stopped.

"Stay... don't leave me alone," she said quickly, lowering her gaze. "Just... don't look."

Shin watched her for a second.

He didn't argue. He turned his body slightly, thinking that they couldn't be dead.

Ghosts couldn't pee.

They were definitely alive, but that feeling that they had somehow vanished from somewhere still lingered in his mind. "All right."

"And..." she added, her voice even quieter, "cover your ears."

This time, he hesitated. Really?

But he eventually obeyed, placing the head containing the water on the ground. Not very well, since he had the torch in one hand, he brought both hands to his ears and looked in the opposite direction. He tried to concentrate on anything other than the faint echo that inevitably began to form in that enormous space.

He sighed.

This is taking a while, he thought, trying not to pay attention. The sound echoed more than it should have, and the acoustics of the place definitely weren't helping.

What the hell am I counting? he wondered. No, you! What are you counting?

On the other hand, there was something he didn't want to think about, and it had to do with bodily functions.

He had looked at Mai around the fire near the entrance, and afterward, he had caught himself glancing at her private parts a few times.

He hadn't done it out of lust.

Mai was a woman. There was no doubt about that.

He was a man. There was no doubt about that either.

They both had humanoid forms, but they weren't human.

That was also clear.

The ears of both of them told him they were different from humans.

But there was something else that had caught Shin's attention, something he had noticed with surprise.

Mai had something he didn't.

Or rather, it was something that any person or animal would have.

But he didn't.

He didn't know why, but something told him he should avoid mentioning it for the time being.

Because he could conjecture that this difference was what completely separated him from being an ordinary person. Even more than the armor.

Several seconds passed. Perhaps more.

Finally, Mai spoke. "I-I'm done."

Shin lowered his hands, relieved to be able to concentrate on something more important again.

At first, she avoided looking directly at him, but then resumed walking as though nothing had happened.

Somehow, that helped.

They continued forward among the impossible reliefs and another chamber of dark mirrors, the torch illuminating fragments of stories neither of them could fully understand. At each one, they stopped, and Shin asked her to look through the carvings for anything unusual.

Something that might indicate a map, or something similar.

“Whoever had built this place must have had a great many workers. Surely there had to be some indication for someone in case they got lost.”

It was also important to watch where they stepped.

Shin thought that perhaps they had activated something at the entrance that caused the door to close.

It was better to be careful in case they activated something else while walking.

But one thing was clearer now.

They couldn't stay still.

They had the feeling that the place wasn't deserted. They hadn't found anything, but both of them could feel it in the hairs on the back of their necks.

And the place… It wasn't going to be simple.

Silence wrapped around them again as they ventured deeper into the chambers.

They continued through corridors that seemed to have no end, repeating themselves with a logic that wasn't quite logical. The walls changed subtly. The reliefs varied, but the sensation remained the same:

They were moving forward without ever truly getting any closer to a destination.

Eventually, they reached another room filled with mirrors. But this time, something stopped them before they could reach it.

There was light in the distance, coming from another corridor.

Not the unstable light of their torches, nor the contained glow of the burning resin.

Mai felt like running toward it.

"No! Wait."

"What is it?"

"It could be a trap. Remember the door and how it sealed the exit."

Mai looked at the walls on either side, while Shin examined the ceiling.

The thought of being crushed while making their way forward, simply because they had stepped on or touched something they shouldn't have, made them inspect the walls carefully.

But there were almost no carvings. There were no signs or markings warning them of anything, either.

Finally, Shin decided to take the lead, while Mai carried the pinecone torch and the water.

She would step exactly where he had stepped. And if something happened to him… Well, it was better not to think about that.

All that caution didn't make him regret the decision. After ten minutes of deliberation, he reached the room safe and sound.

It is better to be cautious in a place like this.

Mai arrived a minute later, stepping exactly where he had.

It was a cylindrical room, rather small, with almost no decoration. But that was the least important thing.

Small flames burned inside stone hands protruding from the walls, as though invisible figures were offering fire to those who passed through the place.

The hands had been sculpted in remarkable detail, open and raised at chest height for an average-sized person, holding flames that didn't seem to consume anything.

The two looked at each other. The place was abandoned, or so it seemed.

They had confirmed it in every room, in every crack covered with dust, in every structure eroded by time.

And yet those flames were still there.

Calm. Motionless. Without smoke. Without sound. As though time had no power over them.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

I know what this is, Shin thought when the idea finally surfaced in his mind. "Perpetual lamps," finally said in a low voice.

"Perpetual lamps?"

"Fire that doesn't go out, usually in temples or ancient places. Don't ask me how. I just remember that."

But the word fit.

Shin moved forward first, carefully observing the surroundings as though he expected something to react to his presence.

Nothing happened. The flames didn't move. The air didn't change.

The few carvings in the room were on the opposite side from the stone hands, and the two stopped for a few seconds.

They depicted a naked man holding an oil lamp in one hand and a triangle in the other.

A few meters farther on, the same man was depicted raising the lamp toward something that looked like a dome of stars with several concentric rings, placing the flame inside a square.

“That could represent an exit,” Shin thought.

“Perhaps there is an exit on the other side.”

They should already be around the halfway point through the structure.

Shin looked at the lamp in the carving, then down at the floor. "Could it be true?"

"What?"

In one of the corners, he found a small clay lamp, forgotten beneath the dust. It was intact and sealed, but lying on its side.

Shin picked it up.

There was a seal around the spout. It was made of very old wax, which almost crumbled completely beneath his fingers. But when he removed the wax, a smell reached his nose.

Careful not to break it, he broke the seal, opened the lid, and found oil inside that was still in perfect condition. It had a somewhat heavy smell, but it was definitely oil.

He approached one of the stone hands and brought the spout close to the flame.

The oil caught fire immediately. The light was clear and steady, much more stable than that of the torches.

The two looked at each other, not quite knowing what to say. Now they had another source of light.

"Can you carry it?"

"Do you think someone was here recently?" Mai asked, looking at the floor.

There were no footprints or anything else.

Shin examined the lamp once more and shook his head.

"It must have been here for a very long time. Here, carry it. Be careful if you use the handle. It might become fragile if you grab it there because of the heat."

Mai nodded and took it carefully. She held the lamp with both hands, studying it for a moment before looking up.

Shin picked up the torches, making sure they had them in case of emergency.

One was already extinguished, and he took care of putting out the one he had lit for Mai, extinguishing the flame with his bare hands under her astonished gaze.

"In case the lamp breaks," he said without looking directly at her.

She nodded, slightly impressed. "You're going to burn yourself."

Shin showed her the palms of his hands, covered in scars and calluses. There wasn't a single burn.

"I have tough skin on my hands."

And they began moving again.

Mai walked ahead, illuminating the path with the new light, while Shin kept some distance behind her, attentive to the sides, to every sound, to any change in their surroundings.

Mai tilted her head slightly.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"What is that you use? Those black things around you?"

"I only know that they're part of me."

"Hmm... they're useful."

"Do you have anything like that?"

"No... but."

"But?"

"My mind reacted when I saw them. I was scared the first time."

"I see..."

"Thank you..."

"Huh?"

"I didn't thank you for saving me."

"Thank me when we get out of this."

Mai turned slightly and nodded, blushing.

Certainly, the view of Mai's back was beautiful even with that long hair, but wasn't the time to think about that. He didn't want to make her uncomfortable, either.

It was better to keep watch for anything that might happen.

He didn't like having her walk ahead of him, especially in case of an emergency, but letting her carry the light might calm her down in a situation like that.

On the other hand, if he carried the light, it would mean he would have to walk ahead, turning his back to a threat that could endanger them both. At least with his one good eye, he might be able to anticipate something and leap in front of Mai if anything appeared ahead of them.

Either way, he only hoped that nothing would suddenly appear out of nowhere.

The corridors continued. One gallery after another.

Intersections that led nowhere, and short staircases that went up and down without really changing direction. In one room, they were surprised to find a staircase.

But the staircase was strange to begin with.

It started high above the entrance and led toward the vaulted ceiling, where it seemed to connect to openings.

They both stared at it in confusion. Could it have been a design choice? For what purpose?

They shrugged. It was just another mystery.

They found another room like it, but this one had six openings on each wall, with more marble staircases connecting them. There were carvings of people walking upside down, which they found somewhat amusing given their situation, but they avoided getting too close to the opening in the floor as they made their way back out.

Time became difficult to measure again.

And with it, so did space.

Shin began to notice something strange about the structure.

If his calculations were correct—and somehow, he felt they were, even though he didn't know why—they should have already crossed the pyramid completely.

They should have been close to an exit, somewhere on the other side of the mountain. They should have covered a considerable distance by now. And they could be certain that they were deep inside the pyramid. The exit had to be close.

Shin was certain there had to be another door.

In his mind, he had associated the entrance with an exit on the opposite side, which was why they had been examining the carvings along the way, hoping to find some clue or something that indicated they were heading in the right direction.

But that wasn't what was happening.

The corridors simply kept extending.

As if the inside didn't match the outside.

He said nothing. Then they heard it. And stopped.

A roar.

It was deep, wet, and vibrating.

The sound crawled along the walls, descended through the floor, seeped into their bodies before finally fading away.

Mai stopped dead.

"Was that...?"

She didn't finish the sentence.

"Water!"

The corridor suddenly opened up. They had reached what they thought was a three-meter-wide walkway inside a dark chamber.

But it wasn't.

It was a bridge.

A gigantic cavity unfolded before them, swallowing the light into its depths. The lamp could barely reveal fragments of what lay within.

Wide stone bridges crossed the void to their left and right, spaced roughly six meters apart, stretching from one side to the other.

Far below them, a black river flowed.

It was what had produced the sound that had caught their attention.

There was no foam and no clear reflections.

The bridge was about thirty meters long. Only dark, constant movement flowed on both sides of it.

Mai smiled.

They had found water.

There were narrow channels, barely twenty centimeters wide, running along both sides of the bridge. Water emerged from the walls and flowed through them toward the side they needed to reach.

Mai handed him the lamp and knelt down.

"Wait," Shin warned.

Mai scooped up some water and swished it around in her mouth before spitting it out.

"It's clean. It tastes a little different, but it's clean."

But they barely had time to think about it.

From beneath the bridge and on the other side, among the shadows moving across the underground current, something stirred.

At first, there was only a distortion in the air.

Then came the sound of something rising.

Something heavy dragging itself upward.

Then an indistinct shape appeared in the darkness.

And finally—

a presence.

Something enormous rose before them like a ghost.

A serpent with a pale body and a red head.

Its body was thick and heavy, as wide as the trunk of a tree. Its white scales reflected the light in iridescent fragments.

It moved without haste, but every shift of its body made the air vibrate.

It raised its head and hissed at them.

For an instant, the very space around them seemed to compress.

His body reacted, but he hadn't had time to decide what to do.

And then—

the creature attacked, lunging toward them.
