Chapter 114

Joint Guild Exercise, Part I (Reality)

August 1st

2 days, 9 hours, and 35 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

The first time I flew using my Full Metal Staff, I almost puked. It wasn’t the altitude, or a fear of heights. No, it was because maintaining concentration while straddling a barbell shoved into your nether region was the equivalent of being super sonically ball-tapped. I guess there’s a reason the classic image of a witch riding her broomstick through the night’s sky never had a wizard equivalent.

It took over a week of trial and error before the genius idea of pairing my staff’s new flight capabilities with the perfect balance allotted by my new even crossed my mind. The idea struck me while driving past a skate park one day. Of course, riding a flying barbell like a board isn’t a perfect science. It took about another week to get a feel for it, and develop enough confidence that I wasn’t going to lose my footing and plummet to my death. Note to self: Prioritize learning a spell that prevents death by splat.

Now? Now I’m surfing the sky like the god damned Silver Surfer, only with larger traps and without the chrome paint job.

My Full Metal Staff slices through open air above the city’s lakefront, the black iron barbell humming under my feet as it carries me forward. The clouds below are thick and bright, like someone spilled whipped cream across the horizon. Lake Erie glints through gaps in the white, a sheet of steel-blue glass that stretches as far as my eyes can see. The city is a smear in the distance—tiny buildings, tiny problems, tiny people who don’t know a Muscle Mage is joyriding above their lake like a lunatic.

I’m riding the staff like a surfboard. Boots planted wide, knees bent, arms out for balance. It’s natural now, and I settle into the sensations: the wind whipping around me, the weightless acceleration of the bar beneath my feet, and the joy of absolute freedom. Flying is the best! Don’t let anyone convince you otherwise.

My new cape tugs subtly at my center of gravity, correcting micro-wobbles before they become disasters. When I lean left, it leans with me. When I dip, it shifts. It’s like having an invisible coach whispering, No, dumbass, not like that.

The only issue is [Fly] still requires concentration, and concentration is the whole game. The staff isn’t just moving because I want it to. It’s moving because I’m holding the spell formation inside it steady—like I’m balancing a spinning plate while standing on another spinning plate while the wind tries to slap me off both.

At first, that was torture. My thoughts would drift for half a second—oh look, a bird, or wow the lake is pretty, or I wonder if Clyde could do this without falling—and the staff would wobble like it was trying to buck me. I also need to concentrate with my body. The spell’s activation apparently flows through my abs. After flying, my core is on absolute fire, from my obliques up to my upper abdominals.

A lot of practice late and now my concentration is ironclad—a steady breath, a calm mind that shifts into the background. The world can roar around me and the spell stays tight. I can now admire a flying gull without dropping into freefall.

All of the practice gained me a new passive Skill, too. Two, in fact.

Skill: [Enhanced Concentration]

Description: The User has improved their ability to concentrate! The User has an increased ability to maintain concentration through environmental elements, taking damage, and while performing other tasks. In addition, the User is capable of maintaining concentration on an active Spell while also activating a Skill or casting another Spell (so long as the second Spell does not require concentration to cast or maintain).

I bank hard over a break in the clouds, dropping low enough that the damp chill touches my face, then I rocket up again, punching through the white. For a split second I’m inside fog, blind and weightless, and then pure gold sunlight washes over me, warming my skin. The cloud layer falls away beneath me and I’m in open sky, bright and endless, blue so deep it feels like falling upward.

I throw my head back and laugh. It comes out wild and stupid and full of that same energy I had as a kid when I’d jump off the garage roof into a pile of leaves and my mom would scream at me and my dad would pretend he wasn’t impressed.

I carve a wide arc, leaning into the turn, and the staff follows like it’s an extension of my spine. Wind tears at my shirt and my cape snaps behind me like a flag that says BAD IDEA IN PROGRESS.

I leap off the staff, propelling my self through the air. As I begin to fall, I command my staff to come meet me. It zips by and I snag it with one hand, swinging my body under and over it, landing back on my feet.

“Woo hoo!”

Skill: [Acrobatics] (Intermediate)

Description: The User is capable of performing dexterous acrobatic feats with enhanced efficiency and ease.

[Enhanced Concentration] and [Acrobatics] are total game-changers. When I had first obtained [Acrobatics], it had only been at the Beginner level. Even then, I noticed an immediate upgrade. Like my reactions got smoother, more fluid. Intermediate is even better—subtle improvements, but they stack. There’s less wasted movement in everything that I do. Cleaner pivots, and better recovery when something doesn’t go immediately as I intended.

I check my status out of habit.

Name: Joseph Sullivan (Participant No. 4,432,444)

Race: Human

Discipline: Spellcaster

Class: Muscle Mage

Level: 30

Health: 270/270

Mana: 0/0

Stamina: 685/685

PHYSICAL STATS:

Strength: 98

Dexterity: 78 [+50 from Equipped Items]

Constitution: 60

MAGICAL STATS:

Intelligence: 3

Willpower: 108 [+70 from Equipped Items]

Spirit: 6 [+5 from Equipped Items]

I’ve only gained a single level in July, but I’ve finally reached Level 30! This feels like a massive milestone. And my Stats definitely demonstrate how far I’ve come since gaining my Class! Hell, my Strength is almost at 100. My eyes flick to my Willpower score. It’s odd seeing the three digit number beside the Attribute. Who would have thought Willpower would be my first Stat to break 100? Not me! Of course, most of those points are coming from Equipment. Specifically my evolved jorts, which provide an impressive 50 additional points to the Stat.

I need to find Equipment that boosts my Strength even further.

Even though I’ve only gained a single level since joining Harvest Guild. If I’ve learned anything since, it’s that Levels aren’t the end all be all. I have new passive Skills to work with, and have also honed by Resistances even further. And have spent more time than I thought possible using my Spells and practicing how to most effectively employ them in a number of different circumstances. Jerome made sure he personally oversaw my training on use of my Spells.

And, speaking of Spells. I’ve added a new one to my growing repertoire. This Spell came in the form of a gift from [Pact of the Novice Scribe]. Apparently, the Slime Bros have been passively leveling up this entire time. I suspect it’s because of their connection to Jelly Boy, and a result of his own growth in power. Really, though? I have no idea. When Jelly Boy and I discovered this, I decided to try the Pact. It was meant to be with Jax, only. But the Toms intervened when it was clear my Spell was about to fail. The Pact instead succeeded with all three contributing their mana to the cause. That had definitely been strange. Would my Pact Spell work with multiple monsters at once, or was it only viable because of the slimes’ hivemind connection? I need more testing to find out.

For the experimentation, I was gifted with a highly niche spell.

SPELL: [Slimy Sending]

[Slimy Sending] (Evocation Spell, Level 2)

Casting Time: None.

Stamina Cost: 25 Points

Range: Unlimited (Must be on same plane)

Duration: Instant

Description: The User is capable of telepathically sending short messages to any Slime in which the User is familiar with. The Slime hears the message in its mind, and is capable of responding.

I can send mental messages to any of the four slimes I am familiar with on this plane! Awesome!... Alright, it’s shit. I was really hoping for something in the same vein as [Slimy Shield]. I was at least hopeful that this would give me a way to actually communicate with Jelly Boy and the others. Unfortunately, their responses to my messages simply come across as emotional impressions.

Most of July has been a blur of Harvest Guild work. Little jobs, almost exclusively. Small monster outbreaks in suburbs—goblins terrorizing the local frozen yogurt place, giant bats roosting at the local park, and one particularly nasty troll sleeping under a bridge and causing a long train of traffic during morning rush hour. Somebody’s backyard suddenly having a pack of feral, chrome-shelled beetles the size of dogs. Oh, and a sewer rat thing that shouldn’t exist crawling into a cul-de-sac.

“It’s a lot of little jobs now,” Labonte says, waving a hand dismissively. “But the big break for the Guild is coming. You watch, kid!”

I don’t know what that means. But Labonte is the kind of man who gets excited about “big breaks” the way sharks get excited about blood. So, I take him seriously.

I’m mid-arc over the lake when my phone buzzes. I snatch the phone from my pocket with one hand while keeping my stance locked on the staff with the other, cape tugging my balance back into alignment like a patient parent.

It’s a text. From Labonte.

>Labonte (Boss Man): GET TO THE OFFICE ASAP. NOW.

“Maybe that big break’s finally come through,” I mutter. I type back a quick response.

>Joseph: ON MY WAY. NOW.

I pocket my phone and bank left, pulling my staff as hard as I can and ripping myself towards downtown Cleveland. The staff responds instantly, carving a clean turn through the air. Clouds peel away beneath me as I angle toward Harvest Guild headquarters.

I need Jelly Boy. He’s probably still at the apartment, watching reality TV and judging the human cast members. Most days, he’s joined me at Harvest Guild for training. They technically permitted him under the ‘Familiar Rules,’ even though I tried to explain numerous times that he isn’t my familiar. Today is actually one of his scheduled “rest days.” He won’t be happy about being disturbed on an off day, but whatever is this urgent for Labonte has to be important enough to disturb my jiggly pal.

I focus inward for a moment, keeping the Fly spell steady like a clenched jaw.

[Slimy Sending]

I feel the tug on my Stamina reserves, and then a faint, cool thread snaps into place in my mind—like someone tied a string around my finger and tugged once.

I push a simple intention into the spell, clear and sharp, and form my message:

Meet me at the Guild if you can.

A beat later, an impression bounces back along the thread: warmth, excitement. And the unmistakable vibe of Jelly Boy vibrating with purpose.

“Great,” I sigh. “See you soon, buddy!”

The Harvest Guild building comes into view. I angle down toward it, dropping altitude fast, the wind roaring in my ears, cape snapping like a whip behind me. I aim for the sidewalk near the entrance, where pedestrians are just walking around like a man in jorts riding a barbell wasn’t just about to land on their heads.

I swoop low, slowing hard, knees bending as I brace. The staff dips, hovering inches above the pavement. I deposit it into my Inventory at that very instant.

Ping.

The Full Metal Staff disappears beneath my feet in a cloud of pixelated light. For a fraction of a second, gravity remembers it exists and I drop. [Acrobatics] takes over, my legs absorbing the impact, feet hitting the sidewalk with a controlled thud instead of a bone-shattering disaster. I straighten, cape settling behind me.

A couple bystanders stare. One guy drops his coffee in surprise. I don’t pay them any mind. I just march toward the Harvest Guild entrance like I belong here. Because I do. I’m finally starting to get used to this.

I enter the building lobby, march towards the elevator bank that leads right to Labonte’s office, mentally swapping into my street clothes as I do. My jorts and cape disappear in a quick flash of light. As I approach the bank, a light scans my body. The sliding door blocking the entrance to the elevator bank slides open with a soft click.

The doors open on Labonte’s floor. I walk past conference rooms with frosted glass and vague silhouettes inside, then down the hall to Labonte’s office. Just outside of his office is a little seating area the Guild has arranged. I’m told it’s because Labonte’s meetings are often scheduled back-to-back and often run very long. There’s a coffee table with a few glossy magazines that look untouched. A mini fridge humming softly in the corner. A small station for making coffee. There are three wall-mounted TVs in a row like Labonte wanted to make sure no one ever has to sit with their own thoughts. Perhaps they’re there to drown out any noise coming from his office to prevent eavesdropping. But I know all of the rooms in Guild headquarters are magically soundproofed and protected from eavesdropping, from within the office building and without (apparently Guild-against-Guild spying with the use of magical scrying and the like wasn’t an uncommon occurrence).

On the first TV, sports coverage plays—some talking heads arguing about preseason stats with the kind of passion that feels almost comforting. The volume is low but the energy is loud anyway. A ticker crawls across the bottom like it’s the most important thing in the world. Up next on their segment is whether the commissioner of the league will sign off on permitting System Users to play.

On the second TV, local news is running a segment with urgency—red banners, grim anchor faces, and B-roll of police tape and blurred streetlights playing in the corner.

SYSTEM SERIAL KILLER HUNT EXPANDS IN CLEVELAND, the chyron screams.

This has been the hot topic the past month. Apparently, a serial killer from Chicago had made their way to Northeast Ohio. Or, it was a copycat killer. The theories were endless, and the entire city is on edge. The killer’s victims are all female System Users. All of them had low-to-moderate levels. One thing’s for certain: someone with System access was to blame here. The anchor’s mouth moves, serious and rehearsed, while the footage shows a press conference—uniforms in front of microphones. The Federal System User Enforcement Agency has partnered with local and State police forces to bolster the search. It’s the kind of story that makes your skin crawl. I think about Liv, and Veronica, and a my gut begins to twist like a cold snake’s working its way through my stomach.

On the third TV, global news is on, the colors are cooler, the tone more polished. A city skyline I recognize from travel photos—Barcelona, I think. The headline flashes:

MAN ARRESTED IN CONNECTION WITH INTERNATIONAL GATE-CRASHING RING

A photo pops up on screen— a skeletal man in an all-black suit being escorted to a vehicle by enforcement agents. The man looked like a walking corpse, which was only made creepier when he looked towards the camera with his milky white eyes.

“Ivan Popov, a Russian foreign national…”

I stop paying attention. Three screens, three realities: Sports, murder, global crime. And somewhere behind that office door, something waits for me. My own reality has been quite peaceful as of late. Guess it’s about time that life crashed down on me again.

I sit down on the edge of the couch, elbows on knees, and stare at the TVs like they’re going to give me an answer. They don’t. They just keep playing, bright and loud and indifferent, while I wait for Labonte to open the door and tell me what kind of hell we’re walking into next.

Jerome isn’t at the door this time. That alone makes my stomach tighten. He usually has a way of preempting my arrival. After several minutes of waiting, I grow impatient. I walk over to the door and knock once.

The door slides open. Taking one last breath, I enter.

Labonte is behind his desk. His chair is pushed aside and he’s pacing, short steps, hands clasped behind his back as he walks the length of his office’s window, which takes up most of the back wall behind his desk.

He points at me the moment I’m inside.

“There you are,” he says. “We’ve been engaged!”

I blink. “Engaged?”

Labonte’s eyes gleam. “High profile. Very high profile. This job is going to put us on the fucking map, kid!”

My mind immediately jumps to the worst thing, thinking back to the TVs outside his office.

“The System Killer?” I ask. “You mean… we’ve been brought in to help find and capture the System Killer?”

The room goes a shade colder. Labonte stares at me like I just suggested we spend Guild funds on a clown statue.

Then he shakes his head sharply. “No! Not that… There’s no money in that, as great as it would be to find that fucker and make him pay!”

Jerome would normally say something like Mr. Labonte, technically we don’t know the killer’s sex, but Jerome isn’t here.

Labonte jabs a finger toward the floor like the real problem is directly beneath us. “Remember those meetings with the AEA I’ve been having? The AEA is having some serious issues with the Diplomatically Maintained Gate.”

A Diplomatically Maintained Gate—or DMG—is actually something I know a little about. They’re Gates that are left open. They’re monitored and maintained for a number of reasons, usually involving a relationship with the Realm on the other side. Governments trade resources from Earth, or services, in exchange for resources from the Realm. They’re extremely rare, only a handful throughout the United States, and are an extremely valuable resource. I knew Cleveland had one but frankly don’t know too much about it.

I swallow. “Okay. What kind of issues?”

Labonte’s voice takes on that sharper edge. “Cleveland’s DMG provides the Guilds with eight different kinds of mana shards. High volume too.”

“That’s… a lot,” I say.

“It’s a gold mine,” Labonte snaps. He glances toward the door, then back at me. “The AEA representatives are in a conference room in our office right now. We’re going to meet them now. They’ll explain whatever’s going on with the DMG.”

My stomach drops again. “Here?... Now?”

“Yes,” he says, like it’s obvious. “And you’re joining the meeting. That’s why I called you here.”

I point at myself. “Me? Why me?” It seems above my paygrade.

Labonte’s grin turns sharp.

“They requested you by name,” he says. “Which is part of the reason I suspect Harvest Guild was one of the two Guilds brought in on the job.”

I stare at him, processing.

I exhale slowly. “Okay.”

Labonte claps his hands once, delighted. “Good. Let’s go make a good impression.”

The conference room is one of those glass-walled boxes with a view of downtown that’s meant to make you feel important while simultaneously reminding you that you are very small in the face of corporate architecture.

Labonte strides in with all the confidence of a man four times his height. Jerome is already inside, seated at the far end of the table with a legal pad and a pen, calm and unreadable. Across from us sit two women in crisp professional attire—dark suits, neat hair, the kind of posture that says they’ve been trained to walk into rooms full of powerful people and command the room’s attention. I hope we haven’t been keeping them waiting.

Labonte reaches across the table extending a hand. When neither takes it, he cooly withdraws it and, clearing with throat, takes a seat. He nods at me to join between him and Jerome. I slide into one of the wheeled office chairs. It dawns on me that I’m woefully underdressed for this meeting. I sink into the chair, trying to make myself as unnoticeable as possible. Which is impossible with the two women currently drilling into me with their eyes, which subtly glow with power.

“Mr. Labonte,” one says. Her voice is clipped, polished. “Mr. Sullivan.”

I feel my shoulders tighten at the use of my last name. I’m used to being “Joe.” “Joseph.” “Hey dumbass.” Not Mr. Sullivan. Memories of being called to the principal’s office bubble to the surface of my mind and I similarly feel like I’m about to be in a lot of trouble.

The other woman nods. “I’m Agent Keene. This is Agent Alvarez. We’re with the AEA. We’re handling the current situation at DMG OH-NE-003, which I believe Mr. Labonte has apprised you on.”

Labonte’s smile is all teeth. “Agents Alvarez. I’d like to thank you and the AEA for your time. Always a pleasure. Yes, I’ve caught Mr. Sullivan up on the relevant details.”

Did you? I think, but I keep my mouth shut.

Jerome gives a polite nod.

Agent Keene taps a thin glass-like tablet on the table between us, and a document flickers to life on the screen embedded in the table. “We’ll be direct,” Keene says. “The Gateborne we have treatied with on the other side of the Gate have warned us of an emerging threat from other residents of their Realm. As you know, we’ve been monitoring this threat for the past several months. The situation has… rapidly evolved. And we will need to act swifty.”

Agent Alvarez continues seamlessly, like they’re reading off the same shared script. “We are standing up a joint task force comprised of members of Harvest Guild and Pegasus Guild. The mission objective is to address the threat while maintaining the DMG and further securing it from future incursions.”

Pegasus Guild. My stomach tightens. Does that mean there’s a chance Sarah will be on this task force? I want to vomit. My eyes dart towards the conference room door, and I almost bolt for the exit. I keep my face still, but inside I’m a coiled spring. Jerome gives me a subtle, but clearly concerned look.

Labonte, oblivious, looks pleased. Like he just got invited to the cool kids’ table. Jerome’s eyes flick briefly between us and the agents, measuring our reactions.

Agent Keene turns her attention directly to me.

“Mr. Sullivan,” she says, and the room shifts. The air feels a little tighter. “We have an additional consideration.”

I nod slowly. “Okay.”

Agent Alvarez pulls out a small tablet from her suit jacket. It’s thin—almost like a clear playing card, similar to the device the Municipal Enforcer used on me back in Chrome Forest.

She taps it twice.

A hologram blooms into the air above the table. A sharp, bright, and disturbingly clear miniature figure stands there in glowing projection. It has a cephalopod head, a tentacle beard, and unmistakably broad shoulders. Arms crossed in front of his chest like he’s unimpressed with the conference room interior design.

Yomhalde?

My heart does a weird hiccup.

“Wait,” I whisper, barely audible. “Yomhalde?”

The hologram animates, eyes narrowed, posture rigid with indignation. I realize that this image isn’t actually him, and he can’t actually see or hear us.

Agent Keene’s voice is calm, but there’s something faintly amused behind it. “You apparently know one of the Gateborne. An influential figure within his tribe. He demanded you be included in the Earth-bound group assisting the joint effort against this threat.”

The hologram’s mouth moves, and the audio comes through the tablet like a tiny speaker. I realize that this must be a kind of recording.

Yomhalde grumbles, voice thick with disdain and pride.

“We will not accept your offered assistance without your warrior, Joseph Sullivan, lending his fist in battle!”

Labonte’s eyebrows climb. Jerome’s lips twitch, almost imperceptibly.

And I just stare at the tiny hologram.

What the hell did I just get recruited into?
