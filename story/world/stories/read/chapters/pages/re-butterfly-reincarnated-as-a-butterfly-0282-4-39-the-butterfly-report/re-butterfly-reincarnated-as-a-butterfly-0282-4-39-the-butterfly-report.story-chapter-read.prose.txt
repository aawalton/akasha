I think he might be right, the butterfly sent to Rosslyn.

The Queen gave off a feeling of skepticism but had no visible reaction.

All right, she thought after a moment. Why?

It’s better if I explain it out loud to everyone, Adon replied reluctantly. That way I only have to do it once.

He did not add that poor Randall was squirming under the intense gaze of the combined command leadership.

The butterfly opened his mouth; he had been sitting there in humanoid form this whole time, just trying to keep his mouth shut and not give too much away on his face about what he thought of events. Everyone else had, in turn, kindly ignored him since he did not speak up. Until now.

“Actually, I think the demons do have an enemy,” Adon said. “It’s one that we have a little bit of knowledge about already, not a mysterious new faction.”

All heads turned to look at him. The butterfly successfully fought through his desire to turn back into an insect and make himself a smaller target.

“Where are you getting this idea from?”

“What enemy?”

“What do you mean we have knowledge of them already?”

Multiple generals started talking over each other at once, peppering Adon with questions more quickly than he could move his mouth to answer, until King Janus silenced them by clearing his throat.

“Prince Adon, I understand you have experience of other worlds,” he said, “but I was under the impression that you have lived in ours for just a few months, almost all of which was spent in the royal gardens of Wayn.”

“That’s true,” Adon admitted.

“So you have not explored the Empire or navigated the border with this other power that you and Randall are telling us about?”

“Not in this life.”

King Janus’s eyes widened slightly in understanding.

“Is this something that must be kept secret from others?” he asked.

Adon shook his head. “No, I can talk about it.”

“All right. You have not explored the Empire in this life but in another, I take it.”

The butterfly nodded and then remembered there were other people in the room. He turned his head to look each of them eye to eye in turn, though it felt both unnatural and uncomfortable. It was something Rosslyn had mentioned he could do when he asked for tips on speaking in front of others.

“I remember all my past lives,” he said as he made the strange eye contact. “I think at least one or two of them were lived in this world.”

Adon briskly recalled two of his prior lives in particular, just flashes to refresh himself. How he had been a dragon, an intelligent beast, stealing sheep, running away from the possibility of social interaction, killed before his time by a noble hero hired to do something about those missing livestock. That life had been a failure. It was connected to this world; Adon remembered flying over an older version of the city of Wayn when he had been a dragon. Still, it was a failure, and it was a little painful to remember it now.

He had a terrible feeling that he had been a part of the doom of the draconic race in this world. He had never reproduced, and he knew dragons were extremely rare. Now there seemed to be none, from snippets that he’d heard from Rosslyn and others.

But worse by far had been his life as a monster, which Adon had relived during his transition from a caterpillar to a butterfly.

He had been a sort of reptilian creature with long, vicious claws, living in a dungeon. The beast had seemingly lived to ambush and kill other creatures. The monster had no friends, no mate, no purpose. It wasn’t exactly his best life.

Then it had received a command inside its head, ordering it, Go forth and destroy this place.

The order wasn’t verbal; the monster wasn’t a verbal thinker in the first place. But it had carried the intent of eradicating the indicated location, and that was a concept that was at least familiar to the monster’s brain. The creature ended up being part of a flood of monsters flowing out of the dungeon and rushing toward the target place.

The defenders of the village were horned humanoids. Monster-Adon and his fellow beasts had slaughtered them brutally. Now that Adon had seen them in person in this lifetime, he had confirmed that those horned humanoids had been the race called demons. The same beings that Claustria was now at war with. So the monsters and the demons were not on the same side, unless things had drastically changed from the time of Adon’s incarnation that had gone to war with the demons.

At the same time that he was briefly speeding down memory lane, the others in the room were having quasi-religious reactions.

Multiple lives in our world? Does that mean he was destined to return? Is his soul pulled back to this place?

It must be the plan of the Goddess…

He was sent to enlighten us as to the true nature of the Demon Empire in our darkest hour. Praise be to Her name!

The Queen was not immune to this, though she at least didn’t assume Adon’s whole purpose in this world was to regurgitate his previous incarnations’ memories.

Maybe this was why Adon was given the gift of remembering all his past lives, Rosslyn thought.

King Janus coughed, and that seemed to break the spell over the room. Adon decided to keep talking so none of the others could go into religious speculation about the meaning of his past life memories.

“In one of my lives, I was a monster,” he began.

“So they do have souls!” General Muller exclaimed.

“That is a shame,” said General Forbes dryly. “We never have a choice about killing them.”

“Yes, that is what I wanted to shed light on,” Adon said, seizing back the thread of the conversation. “They do have souls, but they also have a central figure controlling them, giving them commands.” He explained the details of his past life as a monster, in as much detail as he thought everyone could stomach. They didn’t need to hear about his horror at ripping demon women and children to pieces, or how blood seemed to mat his entire body as the night of slaughter wore on. Those were the details Adon had fixated on when he was forced to live through the vision, but they clearly were not what the Goddess had meant to show him by apparently making him experience it during his metamorphosis.

“So, the monstrous ruler or Dungeon Core or something got you to attack a demon village?” summarized King Janus.

Adon nodded.

“Military scholars and historians had developed a sort of theory before this that the monsters and demons were working together,” Rosslyn said. “That would have explained why sometimes, dungeons would emerge at the same time that the Empire became militarily active.”

This was a theory the Queen had outlined briefly to Adon before they delved to the bottom of the dungeon and pushed the Dungeon Core into destroying itself.

“It was only ever a theory,” remarked General Forbes. “The rule of the world seems to be that the more we learn, the more we realize we do not know.”

“That reminds me, there is another piece of evidence about this other power existing,” Adon said. “When we destroyed the Dungeon Core outside Wayn, I intercepted a telepathic transmission it was trying to send. I got both the message and the location the Dungeon Core was attempting to direct it toward.”

Rosslyn gave Adon a subtle look, and he heard what she was thinking: Careful. Unless you intend to reveal how powerful your Telepathy is, you might not want to say too much about this.

Probably too late for that, the butterfly thought. He had to press on in any case.

“The message was in code, so I couldn’t understand it at the time, but recently, I broke it.”

Everyone just looked at him expectantly until Adon spoke the transmission.

“The humans have bested me outside of wayn. The Royal Princess and a monster in the shape of a butterfly. Recommend attacking after the butterfly has passed on. It is unusually strong-willed for a creature not affiliated with the Monster Kingdom, and the creature has the aura of a reincarnator. Had dialogue with the monster and confirmed that it was a reincarnator. Mental powers are too strong to dominate it with magic. It is not amenable to joining the hive. Will side with humans over its own kind for unknown, possibly sentimental reasons. Will attempt self-destruct to kill them both, though efficacy is doubtful. I die for our victory. Hail to the King!”

That seemed to suck the air out of the room for a minute. Adon heard the trains of thought of the generals.

The Monster Kingdom is our enemy as well as the Empire’s enemy, then…

Will we be invaded once this butterfly dies?

Are the mystic beasts truly monsters, as this Dungeon Core thought? Can we trust the butterfly as we have been doing to this point?

Is this Monster King a rival to the Demon Emperor? Are the monsters related to the demons, or purely enemies?

Most of the periods of peace with the Empire have been when we assessed they were suffering under internal strife. What if they were fighting this Monster Kingdom instead?

The monsters seem hostile now, but is alliance possible? They have an intelligent ruler, and the Dungeon Cores clearly possess intellect as well.

“You said you had the location this message was going to,” King Janus said. “Where?”

“Deep into what the maps show as the Imperial heartland,” Adon said.

Everyone around the table exchanged meaningful glances with each other. It was obvious what that meant.

“So, maybe the relationship between dungeon outbreaks and aggression by the Demon Empire does exist,” Rosslyn said, verbalizing what most people at the table had quietly concluded. “But the causation goes the other way. These are moments when the monsters, guided by their King, chose to wage war upon the surface world, including both the human lands and the Demon Empire. Imperial military doctrine, aggressive by its very nature, simultaneously sought to defend against these attacks and to steal additional territory and treasure elsewhere to make up for the blood and soil they were losing to the monsters.”

“If that is the case, then the Empire is vulnerable right now,” King Janus said, visibly excited. “At this very moment, they are fighting desperately against this Monster Kingdom within their own borders. They are leaving Wayn and much of Claustria ripe to be retaken!”

“They must be quite desperate to have actually abandoned their defense of the territory they have taken,” General Saxon said slowly. “Have they ever done that before, during any of these periods when the scholars noted apparent coordination between the timing of dungeon and Empire attacks? Perhaps they are in even worse shape than we realize.”

“No, they always meet these situations with aggression, I am almost certain,” said General Quirinus, who had remained silent to this point.

“What if the monsters win?” asked General Saxon. “What then? Prince Adon, do you know what that would look like?”

There was a sudden silence around the table. Eyes darted to Adon, and others looked back at each other. The butterfly sensed that most of them did not actually expect him to have an answer. Most of them were a bit perturbed at the idea of having to fight another enemy dangerous enough to give the Empire pause.

But several people had a mindset more like, Who cares? The Empire was already an incredibly difficult obstacle for them to overcome. If the Monster Kingdom had failed to defeat it over centuries, then the Monster Kingdom was probably not more dangerous than the Empire.

Before Adon could begin to answer, one of the people thinking along those lines spoke up.

“We can only worry about what happens if the monsters win once we are in a position where the Empire winning would not be a disaster for us as well,” Rosslyn said. “Once we retake Wayn and the rest of this country from the occupiers. I am curious about what would happen if the Empire loses, but we can make our plans for that later. First, I would like us to direct our attention toward a plan for retaking Wayn itself.”

King Janus gave a little nod of agreement. He also badly wanted them to retake Claustria. That would allow him to go home and respond to any sudden military emergencies that might come up relating to the Monster Kingdom. He would even be able to return a hero who had represented the Holy Kingdoms honorably.

The generals moved on following Rosslyn’s directive. They began discussing how soon they could attack Wayn and surround the city, now only defended by a relative skeleton crew.

Barely chiming in with her own thoughts, the Queen observed them until they were clearly heavily invested in this task.

Only then did Rosslyn silently ask, without looking at Adon, So, what do you think will happen if the Monster Kingdom wins?
