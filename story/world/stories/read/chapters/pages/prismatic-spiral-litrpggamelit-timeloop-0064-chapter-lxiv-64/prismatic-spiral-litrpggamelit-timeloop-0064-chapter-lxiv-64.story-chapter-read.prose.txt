Chapter LXIV (64)

The golem’s arm smashed down in Mitsuko’s direction. Dodging its attempt to crush her, she rolled to her feet, and sliced at the construct with her blade. The sword shattered when set against the stone, but not before slicing through a bit of the viny vegetation that the construct used to hold itself together.

Mitsuko flicked her wrist and created another sword. Her goal was simple. Slice through everything green and let the stones fall. But that objective wasn’t particularly easy to pull off. She only had the opportunity to cut the vegetation as a reaction. Going on the offensive left her small body too vulnerable. Not to mention her balance continued to tilt her to the side from her missing ears.

Her first attempt to step up, close the distance and cut the rocky legs of the golem apart failed. For her effort, she earned a blast of stone to the chest as the construct shot out projectiles among the rocks that made up its body. The impact crushed several of her ribs and sent her tumbling backwards. As she collapsed on the ground, she felt a shard of a rib puncture a lung within. She sucked in a breath of air and found only pain as a result. A quick cast of Mend healed her body in less than a second, but she needed to exercise more caution. If a rib pierced through her heart everything would be over in an instant. There would be no respawning back at the start of the loop.

The stone projectile that had punched into her zipped back, refitting itself back into position on the body of the golem.

“Give up,” Anemone called from her position on the golem’s shoulder. She’d taken a moment to physically shout at Mitsuko rather than play her flute. Despite Mitsuko’s lack of ability to hear, the guardian used both her verbal and internal voice. “Your magic is useless against my creation. Just lay down and die. Let these children live on. I’ll protect them far better than you ever could. In fact, you could even join forces with me and send more children to settle in my flock. That will be your legacy. An assistant to the savior of children rather than the one who doomed them.”

The words stung. But Mitsuko pressed forward, intent on breaking the golem as much as possible before Anemone could resume playing her song.

Sadly, Mitsuko’s childish body lacked speed or strength. By the time she positioned herself back in front of the golem, she only sliced through a single vine before Anemone attempted to crush her under the golem’s full weight.

Mitsuko cast Mend on her foot wrappings and let the cloth carry her backwards. They retraced her steps at a rapid speed and she barely dodged out of the way as the golem smashed down. Once clear of the danger, Mitsuko ended the spell and went on the offensive before the golem could recover. But rather than cutting at the weak points holding it together, Mitsuko went for a far more vital prize. With the golem hunched over after its attack, Anemone was only a few meters above her.

Mitsuko heaved her sword, swinging it with both hands before releasing it. It spun through the air.

Unlike knives or axes, the weight of a sword rested in its pommel. They made for poor thrown weapons, but still far better than nothing.

Anemone’s eyes went wide at the attack. Rather than duck or dodge, she grabbed the girl beside her and held her up as a human shield. Mitsuko’s breath caught.

Before she could cast Mend to pull the blade back to her, the hilt of the sword punched into Hallie’s stomach. Then it clattered down the golem until it hit the temple floor, broken in shards. That attack would leave a bruise, but not a serious wound. Mitsuko released her breath.

“She will not play fair,” Sterling warned from the sidelines. “Anemone is known for kidnapping and holding children hostage. She cares only for her own ego.”

“You have the gall to lecture me about ego?” Despite Anemone’s words being silent, she still managed to scream the sentence in Mitsuko’s mind.

Anemone tossed the girl back down to her position on the shoulder then began to furiously play her flute again, her fingers a blur as she blew into the instrument.

At her magic’s prompting, the golem moved with unprecedented speed. But, rather than attack Mitsuko, the golem advanced on Sterling off by the temple’s far wall.

The cat yowled and leaped. The golem’s stone fist still caught him, crushing Sterling’s tail into the wall and pinning him in place. Sterling cried out in pain, the noise echoing in Mitsuko’s head.

Rather than stand by as a spectator, Mitsuko took advantage of Anemone’s change in target. But this time she didn’t go for the faun, instead sticking with her original plan. Mitsuko chopped at the vines and flowers at the rocky joints. Finally, she managed to slice through one at shoulder height. A boulder tumbled a few centimeters from the golem’s structure.

When Anemone commanded the golem to turn on Mitsuko, the bottom rock remained on the floor. It stumbled but recovered before Mitsuko had an opportunity to take advantage of the weakness in its foundation. Its walk now hobbled, it came after Mitsuko with a wide swing. Too wide for Mitsuko to properly dodge. Not in this body with a child’s stride.

Now disconnected from the main body and outside the control of Anemone’s magic, Mitsuko attempted Mend once again on the boulder she’d sliced out of the golem. It flew backwards and slammed into the back leg of the golem. Before the swing could meet Mitsuko, the golem’s weight fell forward. It was forced to catch itself with the stone arm rather than follow through with the attack.

The smart call would be to back up and wait for more safe opportunities to slice her opponent to pieces. But Mitsuko could feel her blood running lower and lower with each cast of Mend. Sterling had warned her before that this child’s body wasn’t built to hold her blood expenditure. What would be inconsequential in her adult body was life threatening here. And she already felt the light headedness of bloodloss, something was acutely familiar with from her years uselessly attempting other branches of magic.

Arriving on this floor had already sapped her of a lot of blood. The challenges in the temple required quite a bit of blood to be used. She silently cursed herself for not conserving the resource better.

Now she needed to take risks to end this quickly.

Mitsuko advanced on the prone figure of the stone golem, a new oversized blade of ice clutched in both her small hands. This time she didn’t attempt to slice out a boulder in the lower leg, instead she went for the waist. While missing over a meter of its height on one side and slouched forward with its current posture, Mitsuko just barely reached the mid-section of the construct. It required an upward cut, which stole some of the attack’s momentum, but Mitsuko managed three swift, consecutive blows before the golem recovered.

Overhead, Anemone was screaming, though Mitsuko ignored the internal onslaught of insults. Since the golem continued to move, that meant the faun had discovered that her actual mouth wasn’t required to yell mentally at Mitsuko. Disappointing to lose that advantage. But that still meant that part of the guardian’s attention was torn between playing and shouting at Mitsuko, which was excellent for Mitsuko. She could already see the lethargy in the golem’s movements as a result of the split focus of its commander.

The golem attempted the attack from earlier when it shot out head sized stones from within its main body, but this time with its reaction time reduced and Mitsuko now expecting the attack, she ducked under the assault before it landed a blow on her.

Mitsuko continued to ignore Anemone’s words. She wasn’t about to play into the guardian's own folly and let the conversation distract her from what needed to be done. Still, she heard a bit of the guardian’s ravings. The faun was upset about her lot in life. She seemed to believe it all to be unfair. As if the world was colluding against her. That these parents she’d stolen the kids from didn’t deserve the innocence of children. That they needed to be punished for their impurity. She’d be a better mother to all of those under her care and lead them to greatness.

Nothing particularly important to Mitsuko’s current objective.

Again, Mitsuko cast Mend on her foot wrappings and allowed them to carry her backwards, ending the spell before it could reverse back towards the golem.

With a moment of respite on hand, Mitsuko tossed aside her chipped and battered sword and replaced it with a flick of her wrist.

“You believe you can save these children?” Anemone yelled at her from on top of her crumbling golem. “You’ll fail and doom them all. You’re not a true mage. You’re nothing more than a scraggily girl with a sword. I am far from the greatest guardian! You stand no chance against the others. You’ll perish time and time again as you shove your face into their fists. Then, without me here, you’ll sentence these children to a fate worse than death.”

“I—” Mitsuko stopped her response. She licked her chapped lips and her heart beat faster and louder, the only true noise in her head. Could she gamble with the lives of more children? If she failed….

“Mitsuko is more than the Champion of the Prismatic Spiral,” Sterling said simply. His body was limp in the corner, but he spoke crisply. “She is a hero.”

Mitsuko’s eyes widened at the declaration. Stated with conviction and certainty.

Anemone commanded her golem forward, clearly intent on ending Sterling’s life and silencing the sage. But as the golem moved its lopsided body forward, the top half of it shifted. Then it collapsed, the upper body careening down as it separated from its legs.

As it fell, Anemone steadied herself, playing a song that caused the stone to mold around her hooves to better her footing. Hallie, on the other hand, slipped from where she sat on the construct’s shoulder and fell. Rocks careened down to crush the young girl.

Mitsuko cast Mend on Hallie’s shirt and every bit of the stone surrounding her, yanking the girl back and narrowly preventing her death. But now Hallie was suspended midair, and in a moment she’d be falling to the ground.

Mitsuko ran forward, her child body wobbling as she scrambled over the golem’s wreckage. Then she dropped her Mend spell on Hallie, her blood now all but spent. Blinking black spots out of her vision, Mitsuko dove.

And caught Hallie before the girl hit the temple floor. Mitsuko absorbed the brunt of the fall with her own body, softening the impact to a nonlethal degree.

“I…caught you,” Mitsuko said, panting. Her head swam and her body ached. “You’re safe now.”

The girl’s lips were moving, but even if Mitsuko’s head wasn’t roiling, she’d never been particularly talented in the art of lip reading.

Then the girl lashed out. Mitsuko shifted at the sudden movement and dropped the girl as pain blossomed in her left shoulder. Mitsuko looked down and found a shard of ice embedded in her flesh. A piece of one of her old swords. Blood gushed out of the wound. She touched it. Then cast Mend, deciding that the blood spent on the spell would pay for itself if it meant ending the blood flow from the wound. Her body nearly collapsed from the spell’s backlash, but Mitsuko clung to consciousness.

“Pah, you missed,” Anemone said, clearly annoyed. “Try again, sweetie. This girl’s death will be our salvation. She is the hunter seeking me.”

Hallie leaped forward, another shard in her hand. The blade sliced through the girl’s palm in her grip, but she didn’t appear to care as she attempted to stab Mitsuko in the chest again.

Mitsuko stumbled backwards as Hallie swung to stab her. The girl’s eyes were glazed over as she sloppily struck at Mitsuko. Even woozy, in a child’s body, and low on blood, Mitsuko had no trouble dodging the attack and grappling the girl’s arm. She pressed down on a pressure point and Hallie’s hand popped open, releasing the shard of ice. Mitsuko then pushed the girl backwards and watched as she tumbled over a large stone and collapsed on the floor. When the girl attempted to stand, Mitsuko pulled her back to the floor with Mend.

“It’s over,” Mitsuko said. Her words felt numb in her mouth. She didn’t fully turn her back on Hallie, but her focus shifted over to the faun, struggling to free herself from the broken golem’s wreckage. She had sealed her feet to the stone with a spell but never thought to release herself from it.

As Mitsuko stepped forward, the golem’s arm swept forward to swat her. She didn’t even bother with a spell. Instead she ducked the awkward attack and continued on.

“You’ll fail,” Anemone promised as Mitsuko advanced. Instead of playing her flute, she swung it in Mitsuko’s direction like a weapon. As if warding off her death from afar. “You’ll never beat all of us.”

Mitsuko flicked her wrist, the weight of her sword immediately in hand, heavier and more unwieldy than she was used to. She swayed. Everything felt surreal like a dream.

“Look at you.” Anemone opened her mouth and her shoulders jerked in a chaotic rhythm. It took Mitsuko an eyeblink to realize the faun was laughing, the noise muted by Mitsuko’s injury. “You look like a deranged monster. Blood on either side of your face. Not even walking in a straight line while you drag that sword. You’re a gremlin that’s crawled out of a sewer. I’ve seen friendlier looking banshees. You can’t be a mother to these children! You’re barely even clinging to life as is.”

Mitsuko continued her approach. “I never asked to be a mother.”

“I’m their mother!” Anemone screamed. The faun’s face was twisted in outrage.

“No. You’re not.”

Mitsuko, her head slightly clearer now after the half a minute of reprieve, dashed forward to close the final bit of distance, forcing her stumbling body to its limits.

Then, an arm’s reach from Anemone, the faun flung her flute at Mitsuko, who easily deflected the weapon. Then plunged her sword into the guardian’s chest.

Anemone struggled, grasping at the sword skewering her with one hand, the other reaching out towards Mitsuko. But Mitsuko was out of reach. The guardian’s hooves remained stuck in the stone. Her protection from the earlier fall now sealed her in place.

Anemone gnashed her teeth. Mitsuko noted they were flat and smooth, the teeth of prey rather than predator. Another lie from the woman who believed herself so fierce.

“You’ve…doomed them.”

Mitsuko shook her head. “A life with you would be a doomed existence. Your fight declared that to me. Consider your children fucking emancipated.”

Then the guardian on Verdant Island died.
