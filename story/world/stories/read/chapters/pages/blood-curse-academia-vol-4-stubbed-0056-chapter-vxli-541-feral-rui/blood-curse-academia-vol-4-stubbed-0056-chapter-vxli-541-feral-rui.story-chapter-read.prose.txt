Chapter  V.XLI (5.41) - Feral Rui

While Kizu had been distracted, taking care of the bruised girl, it seemed that Anata, Mae, Rui, and Kon had decided to take justice into their own hands. Mort, the traitor, had never even alerted Kizu to the chaos that spilled out throughout the beach.

A week ago, in their last meeting, Stina had insisted to Kizu that she was only teaching Mae the most basic of witch folklore and foundational knowledge necessary. Kizu now suspected that to be a lie. Or else, Stina’s definition of ‘foundational knowledge’ differed wildly from a normal person’s.

“Mae!” Kizu shouted, darting forward. “Release that lady from your hex, now!”

Dean’s mother stood upside-down, her feet having been replaced by her hands. She chased after the two girls, her entire body wobbling as she ran. Mae must have done something to her tongue as well, because the woman spoke gibberish that even Kizu’s earring couldn’t properly translate. Mae and Anata giggled as they ran away from the angry, incoherent woman. They were running in the direction of the nearby forest.

Unfortunately, all he could do for the moment was yell at the girls. Another, more pressing, problem tore away his attention from them.

He forced Mort to jump. As the monkey was riding on the back of the current problem, he was able to force Kon to join his familiar at the academy beacon. The other chaperone lady with the glasses, collapsed in the sand. Kon had managed to chase her a hundred or so meters down the beach. Kizu spared only a few seconds to watch the woman bring her hands up to her broken spectacles and weep, her body shaking with either relief or terror.

The third troublemaker was the one Kizu personally jumped over to.

Rui had his teacher pinned. Ms. Reads barely held the boy back by his shoulders while he snapped his teeth at her and growled. Kizu had absolutely no idea what had come over the boy, he’d gone utterly feral.

Kizu grabbed his shoulder to pull him off and the boy turned on him. He sank his teeth into Kizu’s arm. While he winced in pain, his grip on the boy’s shoulder only tightened. Kizu then shoved the boy off the woman.

Rui took a chunk of Kizu’s flesh with him as he tumbled to the sand. Kizu wasted no time constructing another barrier box around the rabid boy.

Only at the sight of her uncle’s bloody arm did Anata finally stop giggling. From across the beach, Kizu saw her eye go wide as his blood stained the beige sand. She reversed course and ran back in their direction while Dean’s mother continued to chase after Mae into the foliage.

“Was he hit by a hex?” Kizu asked as he used his good hand to help Ms. Reads back to her feet. It was the only thing he could think of that might cause Rui to turn this way. It could have perhaps been Mae accidentally targeting the wrong person.

“Not… that I know of.” The teacher gulped. She continued to stare as Rui threw himself repeatedly into the barrier wall that separated him from them. “Th-thank you. He just went berserk. Ms. Harrow was reprimanding the children and saying… well, she said something extremely inappropriate about Mae’s mother. I was about to step in and take control of the conversation when Rui threw himself at me.”

“The same way he attacked Dean earlier?”

Ms. Reads slowly shook her head, not taking her eyes off Rui. “Not at all. He said nothing. It was like… someone replaced his mind with that of a beast’s.”

Anata reached them, panting as she grabbed onto Kizu’s sleeve. She joined them in watching Rui smash his face into the barrier wall. His nose was now bent at an angle, having broken in a self-inflicted wound. Not all of the blood across his mouth and dribbling down his chin was Kizu’s anymore, his own joined the mix.

“Anata, do you know what happened to make Rui like this?” Kizu asked his niece. His voice was colder than he’d ever used on her.

He was extremely upset and, judging by the way her already pale face drained of color, Anata understood this was not something to be laughed off. She shook her head so vigorously her eyepatch slid across her face and she had to correct it.

“I’m going to use a draft to put Rui to sleep,” Kizu said, drawing out a potion from his spatial storage ring. “Once he’s asleep, I’ll bring him to Taroe to be inspected.”

“Thank you,” Ms. Reads said, her shoulders slumping. “I don’t… I just don’t understand how things can get like this.” She hiccupped and stiffened, struggling to keep herself together. “What am I supposed to do? I’m not a professional mage. I don’t know how to deal with witch apprentices and feral children. And that’s to say nothing of the mundane burden of overwhelming parents. It’s all just adding up. My entire class is slipping out of my control. Every day, I wonder if this will be it. Will this be the day I finally break? Should I have run off with that Tainted boy five years ago?”

Kizu decided that Ms. Reads didn’t require him to be present for her ranting. She wasn’t really speaking to him, as much releasing words into the void itself.

He jumped into his spatial box behind Rui. The boy whirled around and Kizu slammed his fist into the boy’s bloody mouth. The glass broke and Rui coughed as a decent percentage of the liquid went down his throat. He took a step towards Kizu and weakly snapped his teeth. Then the boy collapsed. Acting with enhanced speed, Kizu just barely managed to swipe his good arm under Rui before the boy hit the sandy ground.

“Did you just… shatter glass into the boy’s face?” Ms. Reads asked in horror.

“Made of sugar,” Kizu explained. “It’s edible glass. Designed to break easily.”

Rui would be incapacitated for a couple hours, so Kizu decided to take stock of everything else before his departure.

The other children all cowered. Some hid under their blankets while others peered at the chaos from around tree trunks further inland. Nobody else appeared hurt.

While he scanned the area, he silently got to work repairing the damage dealt to his arm by Rui’s teeth. With this level of superficial damage, Kizu could easily take a healing potion to mend his body, but he decided not to let the chance of practicing healing magic slip away. The skin knit itself back together and sealed into a smooth surface again. It left behind a scar, but nothing too intrusive. Plus, Kizu was confident that if he wanted to, he could easily concoct a salve to remove this light of scarring. Several potential recipes flitted through his mind in just the moment it took for him to consider the scar.

Just as he had with the bruised girl from Dean’s attack, Kizu used an elemental water spell to wash off his bloody wound and clean off Rui’s face. When he first learned the spell to create and manipulate water with magic, Kizu had never imagined that this would be how he primarily used the spell in day-to-day life.

Unfortunately, when he departed, all of Kizu’s barriers would instantly drop. He didn’t have enough control to maintain something like that while kilometers away. That could create more issues. Kizu turned his attention to his remaining active barriers. The one who kicked all of this off to start with was thankfully not involved with the escalation, though only due to Kizu’s interference.

Dean remained trapped inside Kizu’s barrier box, exactly where Kizu left him after the initial fist fight. He had his face pressed up against the barrier and was shouting something inaudible while slamming his fist repeatedly. Kizu decided that with that sort of body language, the boy wasn’t ready to leave time-out just yet.

Kizu mulled over the problem. Perhaps with a scrying spell he could keep the barrier active for a while longer. He tapped his foot while he considered. He was already running a little low on blood, and it would require him to actively use his scrying orb. This was not ideal.

“It’s fine,” Ms. Reads said, finally breaking free of her rambling. “I’ll take care of Dean. At the end of the day, he’s just a normal boy. Perfectly within my qualifications to handle. Just get Rui to a medic.”

“Thank you.” Kizu nodded his head to her. “I’m sorry about all of this.”

“It’s not your fault. Well, maybe in the future refrain from telling stories with obvious unflattering comparisons.” She offered him a weak smile.

Kizu set his newly healed hand on Anata’s shoulder, Rui tucked under his other arm. As he jumped away, carrying them with him, he couldn’t help but think he was forgetting something.
