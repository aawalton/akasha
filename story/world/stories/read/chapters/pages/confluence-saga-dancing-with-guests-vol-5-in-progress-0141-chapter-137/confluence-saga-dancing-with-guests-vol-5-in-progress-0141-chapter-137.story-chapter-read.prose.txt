"In the end it was rather simple," Fay said before trying to take a sip from her empty coffee mug.

She'd been sure she had coffee left, she remembered Briana filling it for her a moment ago. She'd been explaining how she'd found records pointing to an answer that Valene's colleague had asked for regarding Pond discovery patterns. It had taken her weeks, not to find the answer but simply figure out how to trawl the EDN for anything relevant.

"So we discover Ponds at regular intervals?" Briana asked. "Every few decades a new one simply appears and then we-"

"No," Fay said quickly. "They don't appear, and it isn't every few decades, it's… I'm not actually sure how often it is, I didn't run the calculations only found the records of past discoveries that seem to point to a trend no one had considered in the last millennia. Divers go missing and then show back up talking about a long journey, or a new Gate connection gets established because they happened to find a Construct with one just sitting idle…"

She saw Briana smile and shake her head slightly. It was all clear wasn't it? The patterns were there, the historical records were there. The coincidental discoveries of infrastructure that simply wasn't connected was unusual, but so were the Combat Pathers who managed to lead entire expeditions to new Ponds and incorporate them. There wasn't any pattern as to what Energy density was most likely to lead to a new Pond.

Some claimed the Ponds were all the same place, but different versions, but that didn't make sense when there were varying sizes. But then again there were so many similarities between some of them that the premise kept being given weight. It was all so- When had she finished her coffee?

"Fay," Briana said gently before slipping out of the booth. "I know how much you love your work, how much you enjoy digging into these things, but I only understand the smallest parts."

"I can explain. It's-"

"And I'll listen, later. I have to get back to work."

Fay nodded.

"I don't want you to feel like you need to listen."

"I want to listen," Briana said. "And I know how much you enjoy talking about figuring out answers to those problems."

"Anya thinks it's boring."

"No, silly. Anya just likes saying that to tease you. If she thought it was boring she wouldn't stay around to listen."

She'd never thought of it that way. Anya could have wandered off at any time, she could have never engaged in the conversations. She did sometimes let her and Seb spend additional time rambling, but she'd always end up showing up and joining in at some point.

"I miss them," she said.

"It's been three days."

"They were back for less than three weeks after being gone for three months."

"Fay…" Briana said with a soft smile. "I'll get you a coffee to go. I know you planned to get stuff done today."

Fay smiled back before taking a proper glance around the cafe. The place was packed, had she really spent so long talking? She hadn't meant to use up all of Briana's time, not that way at least. She refocused her thoughts as she stood, leaning in for a quick kiss before heading out the door and back to her office.

Seb could feel his eyes trying to glaze over as Seer Marten continued to speak. He'd known this lesson would be a challenge to get through from the opening introduction.

Where both Leo and Sarna had put effort into making their initial lesson engaging while still working up from the basics, be it by asking for the answers and then expanding or by sprinkling in anecdotes, Marten had made it clear he would be starting from a remedial level since there was every indication from Seer records that not everyone would be up to the typical Widepond standards.

He hadn't allowed for questions, simply told those who tried to hold their peace and listen, while at the same time refused to allow anyone to even pretend to lose interest. The first one he claimed to have caught acting distracted, someone from one of the other dorms, had been asked to explain why she thought he didn't deserve her attention, the next had simply been berated for yawning.

How much longer? Anya sent.

I wish I knew.

The information would have been moderately interesting in any other situation. Seb knew the basics of Awakened society, of how Awakening worked, of why Holdings and Affiliated were a common structure that was then expanded into Families and Clans when discussing Widepond lineages. He hadn't realized that the age of full Awakening followed a standard distribution. He hadn't cared to learn the names of all the Great Families, nor the reason some others were considered pre-eminent in a given field. Did it matter that some Families had been notable for millennia while others had under a century of visibility among the self-proclaimed elite?

It probably did, at least to some of the other students. He'd noticed the Hammerswell contingent straighten as they were mentioned, the Blehmans doing the same when pointed to as an example of newly minted fame. Despite that the lecture was beyond dull. The Seer knew what he was talking about, or at least had clear notes to work from, however he had never shifted out of a condescending monotone except when singling out an unfortunate individual who had caught his attention.

He knew how restless Anya was getting, how hard they were both struggling to pretend to remain focused. They were barely even letting their thoughts mingle over the band, the temptation to sink into a conversation or simply meld awareness rather than remain distinct a challenge in itself. But they were aware they still had tells, that when they got too focused on one another something always showed.

They'd both realized from the start that Marten was aiming the lecture at them. There had been too many mentions of feudal standards, of incomplete educations due to an over-reliance on non-Council Seers, along with one too many moments of his eyes lingering on them, for them to have any doubts.

Not that it mattered, they were stuck here along with everyone else.

Is he actually done? Anya sent.

Seb blinked, he'd apparently lost track of the last few moments. Seer Marten was already halfway to the exit and it seemed everyone was finally free to stand and leave.

That was the most boring, bland, droning explanation I've heard-

Fay would have covered it in half the time, Anya interrupted.

Seer Phelann would have, he answered. Fay would still be talking for another two hours because she was going into the details she'd found interesting even though they didn't really matter.

He felt Anya resisting the urge to mock his answer, to make some comment that they would both know was false. He flicked her, the Energy technique brushing her far side as she stood just sharply enough to catch her off-guard.

Hey!

He flicked her again, the impact gentler even as his thoughts turned taunting.

Admit you enjoy those days.

Or what?

He flicked her in both the prior spots simultaneously, leaving her half glaring half grinning as she spun towards him.

Fine, she sent. I enjoy it and agree I'd have sat through twice as long because of how she explains things.

Think we can get a private training hall? he sent.

It took less than a moment for her thoughts to re-align with his. She didn't so much as answer as take the lead to find them somewhere to spar, to unwind after having had to endure the last few hours.
