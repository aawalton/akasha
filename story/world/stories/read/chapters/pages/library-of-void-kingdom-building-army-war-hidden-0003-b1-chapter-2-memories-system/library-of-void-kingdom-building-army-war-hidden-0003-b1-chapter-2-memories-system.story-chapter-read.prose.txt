While one section of Carnatic City basked in elegance, its architecture gleaming under the rising morning light, another part of the city breathed an entirely different existence.

Open drains ran like festering veins through narrow alleys, carrying blackened sewage that reeked of rot and decay. The stench clung to the air—thick, suffocating—a nauseating blend of waste and damp soil

Houses leaned against one another like exhausted beggars.

Most were little more than mud-packed shells reinforced with sticks and straw, their roofs stitched together with brittle thatch. Others were worse—walls fashioned from thick, weathered cloth, scraps of leather stretched and tied into crude barriers against wind and rain. These homes sagged under time and poverty, yet they endured, sheltering lives that refused to vanish.

It was the Slums.

And within one such mud-and-thatch house lay a secret.

Beneath its uneven floor, hidden from careless eyes, stretched an underground chamber.

Vast.

Five… six times larger than the house above it.

Stone supports reinforced its walls, carved and reinforced as if recently. The clean and bright walls told, they were newly built, a year at max or months in earlier. Cleverly designed shafts allowed Aethel’s light to seep down from above, while a network of oil lamps cast illuminated across the chamber revealing stone walls, weapons stacked along the walls, and rough wooden tables scarred by years of use.

This was the Resistance base in Carnatic City.

Normally, the chamber buzzed with activity—whispered discussions, urgent strategy sessions, clashing opinions. Especially in the mornings, it pulsed with life.

But now—

Silence ruled.

Every concerned gaze was fixed on a single room at the far end of the chamber.

The infirmary.

Inside, Vector lay unconscious on a simple bed.

His chest rising and falling in a steady rhythm, each breath shallow but firm. Healers moved around him, examining him repeatedly, but not a single wound could be found. One raised Vector’s eyelids checking his pupils while the other checked his pulse, silently. All of them performed one examination after another.

After sometime…

One of them finally spoke. “Sir Sebastian… the Young Master is only unconscious. We couldn’t find anything wrong with his body. In fact… I” He hesitated, then glanced at the others. Receiving their nods, he continued, “…We believe he will wake up shortly. As… his body shows activity beyond that of a normal Blood Awakened.”

“Believe?” Sebastian asked quietly.

He reprimanded them for their careless remarks. He strictly ordered them to check again.

While Vector’s body lay unmoving, his mental world roared with turmoil.

⚜

Images... or memories... flooded Vector’s consciousness rapidly.

He saw a world where no one refined their bodies with Blood Energy.

Instead, people refined machines.

Metal beasts raced across roads of black stone—square-bodied constructs sealed in vibrant colors, rolling on small rubber wheels, carrying people faster than any horse-drawn carriage ever could. They moved in endless streams, orderly yet relentless, moving to their destination.

Smaller machines appeared next. They fit into the palm of one’s hand—flat, black mirrors through which voices traveled across cities, across continents, as though distance had surrendered entirely. Voices without wind. Words without messengers. Events unfolded in real time, visible from countless miles away. Even moments long past could be summoned again, replayed at will—all contained within that tiny device.

Another memory surfaced.

Larger black mirrors emerged, thinner than stone slabs yet broader and heavier than the smaller ones. Words and symbols attached beneath the black screen. The way to control them. It was similar to the palm-sized mirror he saw previously, yet vastly more powerful. Fingers danced across the inscriptions attached beneath, summoning images, knowledge, entire worlds of information. Libraries, entire civilizations of accumulated wisdom, were compressed into something light enough to rest upon one’s knees.

He had seen fragments of these images before—in dreams that were vague, fractured, and impossible to piece together. Never was he able to make head and tail about these dreams.

But now they all appeared crystal clear. He understood what they were.

Then… another memory surfaced, one that stunned him.

Flying metal tubes.

Enormous and hollow, they pierced the sky like sharpened spears, tearing through clouds as they carried hundreds of people within them. They crossed hundreds of miles in mere hours, making vast distances trivial. For the common populace, transportation had become effortless.

They could not compare to the speed of a Third-Stage Warrior—let alone a Fourth—but they demanded no exhaustion. A warrior might cross thousands of miles in a day, yet the aftermath would leave their body screaming for rest. These flying tubes, however, carried people the same distance without draining even a breath. Some were even luxuriously built, filled with entertainment, sleeping quarters, and comfort beyond imagination.

Turning the vast world into a Village.

Then came weapons.

Weapons capable of erasing cities.Weapons… That massacred without discrimination.

Yet among all these revelations, two memories shocked him far more than the rest.

The first…

A person identical to him.

The same face.The same hair.The same eyes.

He saw himself as a child—thin, poor, no older than nine. He recognized it instantly. His parents had died when he was nine, leaving him completely alone. There were no grandparents. No uncles. No extended family.

Only him.

He couldn’t imagine life without his parents. His grandparents. Or his lovely cute sister.

He only shuddered at the thought of this.

Only one word came to his mind thinking this… Life would be hell. As simple as that.

He continued to digest the memories further.

Life afterward had been harsh, but he had not been destitute. His parents had left behind savings and insurance money. Yet living in a residential care center, what people called a children’s home, could not heal the wound they left behind. No amount of comfort could fill the hollow space carved by their absence. As no one had adopted him. Foster parents.

He lived a largely normal life, except for one thing—

The absence of familial love.

The memory continued, stretching into his university years, into his twenties.

Then surfaced the same dream he had seen dozens, perhaps hundreds, of times.

He stood on a vast platform of stone and steel. Chaos churned around him. Crowds surged and voices overlapped, echoing through the open space. Announcements boomed from unseen sources, amplified to reach every corner. Massive iron serpents moved along parallel metal paths. They roared forward on metal wheels, carrying enormous cargo and thousands of people toward distant destinations.

He stood still, waiting.

Waiting for his metal behemoth to arrive.

As it approached, the chaos intensified.

Then—

A shove.

Sudden.Violent.

His balance vanished.

The world lurched.

He fell on parallel metal tracks.

A deafening horn screamed.

Blinding light flooded his vision as the colossal machine thundered toward him, growing larger with every heartbeat. Metal wheels shrieked. The force was unstoppable. Merciless.

It struck. At the impact, his body was reduced to pulp.He died.

But his soul—

In that split second, space itself cracked.

A tear opened, and from it emerged a swirling portal—black intertwined with countless shimmering colors, like polar auroras trapped in a violent storm. It expanded violently, dragging his very soul into its depths before snapping shut, vanishing as though it had never existed.

The existence of the soul was no longer a matter of belief or legend.It was real.

No theory or scripture could have explained it more clearly than the sensation of his own soul being torn free.

This was the memory that delivered the second-greatest shock—far surpassing even the first.

He felt himself seized by a primordial force.

Held.Suspended.Floating.

Thought itself became difficult. Before he could grasp what was happening, an inexplicable pain crashed over him. His soul turned chaotic, flickering violently like a lamp battered by a raging wind, as though it might be extinguished at any moment.

Then a blackish-purple aura enveloped him.

It wrapped around his soul, layer by layer, steady and firm. Slowly, the violent flickering subsided. The chaos calmed. His soul stabilized—and then began to fuse with that mysterious energy.

His thoughts gradually settled, emerging from disorder into clarity.

He recognized the sensation.It was the same feeling he had experienced during the attacker’s confrontation.That mysterious energy… was connected to this place.

Only then did his perception expand.

He observed his surroundings.

An endless Void.

Black space stretched in all directions, silent and immeasurable, offering no sense of origin or boundary. Within that vast darkness, swirling lights emerged—brilliant and fluid, moving chaotically, radiating hues identical to the auroras that danced above the world’s northern skies.

He drifted through the infinite expanse.

Within the darkness, countless luminous structures appeared—spirals upon spirals, vast and elegant, woven together into an immense web. Auroral colors threaded between these radiant bodies, and a single realization struck him with quiet certainty.

Galaxies.The universe.

Countless galaxies and stars filled the Void—spiral, elliptical, irregular—each harboring billions of stellar bodies. They expanded endlessly, drifting away from one another as though the universe itself were alive, breathing in a slow, eternal rhythm.

Between these colossal structures lay profound darkness and absolute silence.

On a grander scale, the galaxies traced a vast cosmic web—filaments and voids interlaced like a colossal spider’s net. The voids separated the universe, maintaining balance, preventing collision even as everything moved and expanded in ceaseless motion.

Then—

A primordial, boundless, and unfathomable energy surged through his soul.

The pain was unbearable.

It seared through every fragment of his existence, threatening to unravel him completely. The energy flowed like a second heartbeat, carving itself into the very framework of his soul. It permeated every part of his being, reshaping him, rewriting his essence at its most fundamental level. Soul.

Time lost all meaning.

His awareness tore free from the memories—the echoes of his first life on Earth dissolving into nothingness.

“Ahhh—!”

He screamed wildly, before he even had time to curse that ‘you idiot doesn't know the word pain… people could feel pain’. Yet even as the scream ripped from his throat, realization followed swiftly.

No one could hear him.

He was within the vast sea of his own consciousness.

With the relentless influx of energy, it felt as though eternity passed. Slowly, imperceptibly, the agony faded.

His screams ceased.

The power had not destroyed him.

It had strengthened him.

Tempered him.

First the body.Then the soul.

Vector opened his eyes.

A familiar ceiling greeted him—the stone roof of the underground resistance base.

The infirmary.

“Young Master…”

“Do you feel any discomfort?”

“Please rest a little longer.”

Only the first voice was clear. Sebastian’s.

The others’ voices blended together like an hodgepodge, their words overlapping until he could no longer tell who was speaking. His senses were still slow, dulled by his sudden awakening. And exhaustion from the suffering of pains.

He turned his head toward the sound.

Sebastian stood closest to the bed. Beside him were Marcus and Tristan—the two who had remained with him since childhood. Playmates, perhaps, though the age gap of seven to ten years made them feel more like guardians carefully arranged by his family.

All three wore the same expression.

Worry.Anxiety.

Their eyes never left him.

Marcus—the foodie of the group, but its effect could not be seen. Whatever he consumed seemed to vanish without a trace, digested into a body of well-defined muscle. His build was athletic rather than bulky, strength honed and efficient.

Tristan stood silently beside them, ever serious. A man of few words, one who moved in shadows. An assassin by profession, and the one responsible for intelligence operations across Carnatic City.

Hehee… Not that his official duties as the leader were insufficient, but a good commander needed to know the battlefield. Vector occasionally helps Tristan with a small part of the intelligence gathering. He called it "getting some field experience," and the cover was perfect: the unassuming gardener at the City Lord's Estate. It was the only way to truly gauge the ground situation, and honestly, he enjoyed the thrill of being the unseen hand.

Then something tugged at his awareness. One more presence was missing. He searched the room. Where is she? Hearing he was injured, she would have been the first to rush in—without hesitation, without thinking.

Suddenly…

A cool sensation brushed his forehead.

Clothes. Wet.

Clothes soaked in water were placed on his forehead. He didn’t sense it unless it was disturbed. To change,

Someone had just replaced it.

Vector slowly tilted his head upward.

There she was.

Rhea.

The woman he called his elder sister stood closest to him.

They shared no blood, no lineage—but the bond between them was unquestionable. To him, she was simply a sister, and nothing more needed to be said.

All three of them—Rhea, Marcus, and Tristan—appeared to be in their mid-twenties.

Then came the questioning.

Careful. Persistent.

Vector answered patiently, addressing each doubt, each concern—choosing his words with caution. But there were truths he deliberately withheld.

He spoke nothing of his special condition.Nothing of the mysterious energy.Nothing of the transformation.

Instead, he offered them a safer version of events.

Because he knew—Sebastian would have already sensed it. The moment Sebastian arrived and saw the aftermath; he would have reached the same conclusion Vector had.

There had been another presence.

So, Vector told them that when he was suddenly attacked, an unknown force had intervened—shielding him from the blow and striking back at the assailant.

Sebastian confirmed part of the account himself. The attacker’s hand had been unnaturally fractured. He pressed further, asking whether Vector knew anything about that force.

Vector answered truthfully.

He didn’t.

Not even a fragment of understanding.

Could a Fourth-Stage Warrior produce such an attack? He voiced the question aloud, allowing uncertainty to mask the deeper truth he himself could not yet comprehend.

The questioning continued.

Then, midway through the questionnaire, Vector spoke again.

“Sorry… to trouble you all,” he said quietly. “But could you wait outside for a while? I’ll call you back shortly.”

They resisted at first.

Rhea refused immediately. Marcus frowned. Tristan’s gaze sharpened.

But Vector insisted—calmly, repeatedly.

At last, they relented.The door closed.Silence settled.
