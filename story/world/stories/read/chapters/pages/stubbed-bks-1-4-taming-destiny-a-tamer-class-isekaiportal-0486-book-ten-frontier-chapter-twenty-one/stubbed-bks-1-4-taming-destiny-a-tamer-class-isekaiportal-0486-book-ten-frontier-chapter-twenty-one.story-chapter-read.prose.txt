Power pours through Loran’s limbs, feeling like the ocean they have so recently sailed across. As he’d expected, it is painful, but it feels different from the pain he has suffered so often in the past for one reason: that was meant as punishment; this is meant for transformation.

Loran doesn’t know how he can feel it so clearly, but, in that moment, he doesn’t care. All that matters is what is – and what can be.

Because he also senses that the power, so plentiful, is not unlimited. Even while heady with possibility, he feels the old urge not to let any bit of it go to waste, an imperative born of a starving orphan who scrambled for scraps on the street.

I need to focus, he thinks, feeling like he’s submerged in rich, golden honey that he must somehow shape into enduring power.

‘Does it reflect who you wish to be…?’ Markus’ words echo again in his head. Who I wish to be. Who is that?

It feels like a question he must answer – yet one that seems almost impossible to pin down. Loran has never spent much time on considering who he might be in the future – he’s always been too focused on surviving the present. When he bought out his contract and Lord Nicholas removed his collar was the first time he’d ever really had the choice.

At that time, he’d chosen to remain part of Markus’ group of companions, he’d chosen to become a carpenter, and he’d chosen to continue serving Markus as his manservant.

Now, he dares to project himself into the future, dares to consider what he might become if he follows each of those paths forward.

The power that saturates his body and internal matrix can do almost anything – but not everything. And if he seeks to be too greedy, he will end up attaining nothing special. Which leaves him with a choice to make.

If he wants to dedicate himself truly to carpentry, he can. He can pour power into his fingers, increasing their dexterity, into his eyes to magnify their sharpness and ability to see the smallest of details, into the muscles of his upper body to be able to split even the hardest of wood. His internal matrix will follow suit. He can almost see what it will become in his mind’s eye – a web that is slightly unbalanced, but stable despite that.

However, he hesitates before committing to it. Master Ulim has been dissatisfied with him recently. Becoming a Master Carpenter takes many years even when practised day in, day out, and Loran is certainly not doing that. Some days, he doesn’t even take his whittling tools out of their case – a beautifully worked piece of art from his brother.

And why? Because Master Ulim is not his only mentor. And carpentry is not the only demand on his time.

A different picture forms. He could follow in Sarran’s footsteps. The steward is servant, guard, and friend to Lord Nicholas. He serves his lord with fierce loyalty, obeying his commands to the best of his abilities, yet not failing to speak out when he feels his lord is making an unwise choice.

Loran could pour power into his brain, enhancing his ability to determine what his lord is doing – and to spot the faults his lord has not. Or he could pour the power into his body, creating of it a shield and a sword in one. He has not the strength of Sarran – this could change that. Or he could lean more into his natural gifts – litheness and agility – and sharpen the knife of his combat ability.

Yet neither of those pictures truly speaks to Loran. He feels gratitude towards Markus – of course he does. Without his former master, he would still be languishing as a slave under one master or another, ever more bitter as his loneliness grew. It’s only since becoming part of the pack that he realises how much he had been longing for company, for acceptance, for love. Especially after his siblings turned their backs on him and his illegal lifestyle.

But, even as he thinks about enhancing himself so he can be a better packmate, he dismisses the possibility. Not because he doesn’t wish to be such, but because that is too small a vision for him. It is once more a vision of the present – and not the future. What he is, rather than what he can be.

The power cradling him even as it urges him to use it forces him to see himself with far more clarity than ever before. There’s a reason why his vision of himself as a Master Carpenter, or loyal steward, or loving packmate do not appeal to him beyond a moment of wistfulness.

Because they’re too small.

It’s like a key has turned a lock and released a wave of realisation. Each of the roles he plays is only a small facet of what he wishes to be.

And that vision has its roots within his past, but, like a tree that grows from a seed, is immeasurably greater than what he used to be.

And, as this new picture comes to mind, he finds himself almost smiling. Because yes, this is right. This is who he wishes to be. And even if he never gets there in the end, what he will achieve in the pursuit of it will be reward enough.

As if sensing his readiness, the power cradling him swirls with what feels like excitement. Where before it had seemed willing to be patient, now it reminds him of looking after the samuran hatchlings – each eager to act in different and chaotic ways.

Reaching out with senses that he never had before learning to use the Bond that connects him to everyone else in the network, Loran catches the swirls of power and corrals them firmly. This is his Evolution – the power will obey him.

First, he needs a Core. He senses that with a certainty that he doesn’t question. The power resists his pull – not because it doesn’t want to form his Core, but because its nature is to test and challenge.

Loran tugs again with a firmness that he has learned from Sarran – a no-nonsense approach that assures those he is speaking to that he has the authority to order them around. Best used with crafters unwilling to complete their obligations, citizens who insist on complaining about something after he’s already answered their complaint, and hunters who have already been told not to put their kills there. He tugs at the power, not willing to take ‘no’ for an answer.

Finally, the power responds, even more golden light flooding through his channels – from the opened fists into the space in the middle to join the streams that had already met there.

Loran feels a sense of pressure that grows ever more uncomfortable the more power he pulls in. Yet he knows this is the right direction, so he just pulls in more.

Even when it feels like he can’t breathe, like he’s about to explode from how much power he has concentrated at the centre of his being, he continues pulling.

And then he reaches another tipping point when he can’t pack any more power into the centre. If he were aware of his physical body, he’d be holding his breath. As it is, he just waits, hoping, praying that he’s done this right.

Then, with a sharp cracking sound that he can somehow hear even without ears, the power crystalises. A shining orb far smaller than the ball of power that had been there a moment ago appears and Loran feels it settle within him with a naturalness that implies it had always been there – that he had discovered rather than created it.

A moment after it appears, a shockwave of power pulses through him, stronger even than the initial wave of power that had crashed through his internal matrix.

Loran scrambles to grab the power and direct it in pursuit of the vision he can still see with crystal-clear quality.

To his eyes to see more clearly, even that which is not usually visible.

To his mind to think more clearly, to be able to process what he sees, to draw the right conclusions about it, and to find ways around the obstacles that will inevitably block his path.

To his arms and his hands, to build wisely. And to his body to strengthen it. Because even if he doesn't want to dedicate himself solely to becoming a Master Carpenter, he will need strong hands to build his vision – and to defend it.

The power bucks his grip, but he once more holds onto it with determined firmness. Because if he wants to bring his vision into reality, he will need every little bit of it.

I watch as Loran’s eyes slide shut, his expression going slack in a way that I know indicates he’s entered his Core space. Something about my words clearly resonated with him. I’m tempted to enter his Core space to see what he might do about those tight knots of channels, but I resist. Something tells me that he needs this time alone to process things.

Loran’s skin starts to glisten. At first, I think it’s just the changing light on a faint coating of sweat. And then it grows in intensity, the light clearly not coming from reflections on his skin, but from somewhere deeper within.

Is he…? I ask myself with excitement as it almost looks like his bones are shining from within, his eyes glowing even behind his closed lids.

I almost jump out of my skin when a crash hits the door to my study.

Let me in, Markus! Artemis demands a moment later and I realise exactly who’s just tried to burst bodily through the door.

I’m coming, I hurry to the door, and am almost bowled over by the two deris as they practically sprint inside, skidding to a stop in front of Loran, their ears pricked expectantly.

I look up at my manservant and my breath catches. He’s surrounded by bright silver flecks of light, more and more and more until they obscure his figure from my view entirely. My eyes trace the path of the sparks with urgency. When I see that they all seem to be even, nothing sticking out, I breathe out, my shoulders relaxing. If the previous Evolutions I’ve seen are any indication, so far so good.

What’s happening? Bastet demands in the Bond network.

Is someone Evolving? Lathani follows up quickly after, her voice full of excitement.

Loran is, I tell them, feeling so many different emotions I can barely identify them.

Voices immediately start babbling in the network, but I tune them out in favour of staring at Loran.

I had hoped this would happen. Yet I hadn’t been at all sure it was possible. Loran’s matrix development seemed promising, but when Kalanthia had indicated that the local humans, unlike the local elves, were unable to advance ‘like beasts’, I had thought that there would be a final obstacle, something that we would find difficult, perhaps impossible, to get past.

Yet in the end, it seems that Loran was so close to the edge that he only needed a little encouragement.

I try to temper my expectations. Not every Evolution goes well, after all – though all appearances indicate that this one probably will. And even if Loran successfully completes it, there can be vast differences between one Evolution and another. But I can’t stop feeling excited and hopeful.

Because if Loran successfully Evolves, that has huge implications – for all of us.

The room darkens, the silver sparks revolving around Loran soon the main source of light. I glance at the window and see Artio’s owl-like head shoved inside, watching the events with a great deal of curiosity.

Movement at the door then catches my attention and I turn my eyes in the other direction to see a whole load of samurans pouring in.

I exchange excited – yet trepidatious – looks with the adults. All Evolved, they know just what Loran must be going through – better than I do, even.

Little by little, the sparks begin to disappear, absorbed into Loran’s body. As he becomes visible – first his silhouette, and then his form properly – I see the glimmering on his skin once more, like the sparks have transformed him into a silver statue. Then they are absorbed too, and Loran is back to normal.

Yet not entirely. He is more muscular than before, his hands still thin, but looking far stronger than they were. And then he opens his eyes and I see a thoughtfulness and sharpness in them that is new.

He blinks, taking in all those surrounding him with some surprise and, if I’m interpreting his emotions correctly, a good dash of gratitude and…love?

In the time it’s taken for his Evolution to complete, most of our pack has managed to join. The only one absent who is actually part of our inner circle is Kalanthia.

“How are you feeling?” I ask, doing my best to keep my nerves from showing on my face or through the Bond.

Loran doesn’t answer at first, his emotions becoming a maelstrom that’s hard to interpret. Instead, his eyes drop to his hands and he turns them this way and that, flexing and clenching them in turn.

Then he looks up at me with determination.

“I know now what I’m meant to do,” he tells me with a certainty and intensity that are uncharacteristic. “And I will work towards it even though I may not accomplish it in my lifetime.”

I smile at him.

“Good for you. What is it?”

“I don’t want any other child to be forced to make the choices I was because it was that or starving. I don’t want any other child to have to watch their siblings waste away. And those who have committed crimes and been sentenced? They, we, still feel, hope, and dream. Those still bound by the collar don’t deserve to be treated like objects, used, broken, and thrown away.”

“Well said,” I agree. “I take it you want to work to improve life for the vulnerable – slave or child?”

Loran smiles in a way I’ve never seen before – with a relaxation and certainty that has always been missing.

“I do. And I believe you do too.”

I nod.

“It’s what I’m already working towards.”

Loran fixes me with a firm glance, then goes down to one knee before me. Unlike the last time he knelt to me, it doesn’t make him seem smaller – quite the reverse.

“Then, Lord Markus, if you swear to tirelessly work towards revolutionising the conditions of the collared and vulnerable children – those who have been thrown away by all ‘right-thinking’ people, I swear in return to place my eyes, my mind, and my hands in your service. To see the obstacles that stand in the way of our shared goals, to think of ways around them, to build shelters for those with none, and to defend them from those who would seek to destroy what we have built. Do you accept my vow?”

I give the moment the solemnity it deserves, feeling a fierce pride that he dares to demand this of me. He’s come a long way since that bedroom where we first met.

“I do.”

He gives me a fiery look, one that promises unwavering support as long as I uphold my word, then rises to his feet.

A moment later, he’s surrounded by our companions who each want to congratulate him on his successful Evolution. I trace the woodgrain pattern on my desk thoughtfully while I wait for my own turn.

A human has successfully Evolved through Core absorption alone. Time will tell whether this discovery is for better for the world – or for worse.
