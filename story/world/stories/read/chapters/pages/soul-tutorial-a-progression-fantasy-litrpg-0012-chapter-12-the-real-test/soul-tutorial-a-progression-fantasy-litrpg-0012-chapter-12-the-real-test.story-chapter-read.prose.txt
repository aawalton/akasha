Luke opened his eyes, starting off with blurriness. Shaking his head to get rid of the dizziness, he tried his best to stand up.

A crude idea struck him.

What if I could store some of the soul’s energy inside myself and then release it later?

Even though he had just woken up, training wasn’t over yet. Until he could get strong enough, he wouldn’t stop training. Pulling his thoughts back to the present, there were close to twenty souls left.

He tried to gather it. Tentatively, he pulled one soul inward, letting it hover inside his chest. The hum changed steadily; shivers went to his right arm and legs, providing some support. The energy was still messy, still rough, but it didn’t threaten to destroy him.

He tried pulling in a second soul, then a third. His body quaked, sweat pouring off him in rivers, but he kept them inside him. The hum in his arms and legs continued, soothing, as if the energy were a part of him.

He leaned back, using the pedestal as support, panting, chest heaving, sweat and blood soaked him. His arm twitched slightly, but it was all right. Both his legs responded. The stump… Well, it was still a stump, but he could feel the energy adapting, spreading into his body in a crude, awkward way.

A small slime appeared at the thought of growing even an ounce stronger without having to spend points.

It wasn’t perfect. The process was not smooth. It was messy, unstable, and painful. But he controlled it; it was something he could build on.

For the first time since arriving on this floor, Luke had the impression that perhaps he wasn’t entirely powerless. The battering had taken a toll on his body. His left arm was gone. His body was devoid of any power. But he had a special energy now. He had souls. He had a way to continue onward.

He just needed that initial boost to keep striving.

The hum vibrated through his arms and legs.

He gritted his teeth, forcing himself to focus. The first experiment had shown him something: the energy could move through him even if it was weird.

He could direct it and rotate it. But controlling it fully? That was another story. Every twitch, every vibration, every pulse of the hum had made him feel as if his chest might burst.

“Okay, this is harder than I thought,” he muttered, teeth clenched. His left arm had twitched again, leading to tiny movements at first, then flexing more deliberately. The hum had pulsed stronger, almost painfully, vibrating through his shoulder and down into his fingers. He willed it to keep pushing; the arm had responded just slightly.

Next, he tried his left leg. The first attempt had been harsh. The soul energy flowed down awkwardly, like pouring water into a clogged pipe. His thigh had spasmed, and he had nearly toppled over from the imbalance. He had slammed his hands against the pedestal to steady himself and tried again. Gradually, with discomfort, he perceived the energy moving through his limb; not smoothly, but it was something.

The stump was different. There had been no muscle, no limb to respond, but Luke had realized he could still channel energy into it, though he had limited himself once again. It had been strange; although he could push the energy out, Luke had always willed himself to stop, almost like a sensation, a connection that told him in the back of his mind that it wasn’t right. Even missing a limb, his body could adapt to the energy, but he could only keep trying to improve his control.

Minutes had passed, or maybe hours. Time had not existed in that haze of pain and sweat. He had tried sending energy in distinct patterns: first to the arm, then to the leg, then back to the chest, then into his other leg. Every shift had made the hum spike; every movement had made his muscles scream. He had folded over several times from the strain, gasping, but he had forced himself to continue.

He had experimented crudely with rotation. One soul would orbit perfectly while the others wobbled. He had tried nudging them onto smoother paths, but they had resisted, bumping and colliding against each other. Each collision had sent a jolt through his chest, almost knocking him back against the pedestal. He felt as if he were tearing himself apart from the inside.

“Yeah, maybe let’s not do this yet,” he muttered, rocking slightly, sweat streaming off him. He clenched his teeth as the hum spread through his left arm again. It was sharper now, almost painful, but he forced the pulse into his fingers. They twitched, flexed. He could make them move on command, even if just barely.

He tried pooling the soul’s energy inside himself, letting it hover closer to his core. The hum changed, spreading through his chest and radiating into his arm and legs. It wasn’t perfect. The souls resisted, wobbling and shaking, but the vibration in his left arm grew stronger. He could feel muscles flexing before he willed them, responding almost automatically to the energy circulating inside him.

Luke knew it was time to change his approach; even with all his practicing, he needed to manage soul energy while in a fight. So doubling down, he brought his exhausted body to redo floor 0. The safest floor he could currently do at the moment, starting it, the floor energy began swirling around to condense, forming into the slime.

Luke approached it once more. It’s weaker than the usual slimes he had to fight on floor 1, but he could not let his guard down. Its acidity was way stronger than blue slimes; he still felt the residual souls within him and let them orbit to spread the energy throughout his body. Keeping most of his attention from preventing the souls from colliding.

This took a lot more effort than he would admit, but he had to make his way to the green slime. He needed to grow under stress, and this was the perfect situation. Gathering some energy into his legs, he sprang into action. Each step felt a lot more powerful; each fiber in his thighs bulged and hardened.

It was like a shockwave went off, blitzing toward the green slime. While it might look like a good thing, Luke couldn’t control his speed, moving past the slime. It struck him in the back, sending him face-first into the ground. Smearing his eyes and peeling the skin off his face.

Maybe I should have practiced some more dramatic movements first, he regretfully thought. He found that his back wasn’t as sizzled as he had expected. Could be the effects of the soul energy or just his constitution points.

Luke was stuck in the ground and took a long time to get up, only to realize the slime was already near him. It leapt at him, trying to tear away at his flesh with its acid. He instantly went defensive, guarding his face the best he could with only one arm, expecting some of it to go through his guard.

I have to get out of here. He pushed off the ground with his legs, trying to get the slime off his guard, shifting his weight to the left side to slip the slime off his left stump. Unexpectedly, it worked. The slime didn’t remain stuck on his body much longer, and that gave Luke the time to dislodge his foot.

Trying once again, he focused his soul and spun it a lot slower, not trying to generate as much energy as he did before. It gave him the boost he needed to try rushing the slime once more that remained on the ground. He used the disheveled ground to his advantage, allowing him to get a better footing and rush towards the slime with better-controlled speed. That was all he needed, generating enough power to slam his fist directly into the gelatinous body of the slime. Obliterating a part of its body. Acid poured out, spraying over the ground; it looks a lot weaker and smaller than. But its core lay intact within its body; it hopped towards Luke.

But he didn’t let this time pass him by; planting his feet, he shifted his body into a low stance, setting a foundation he needed to drop his leg into the slime, destroying its core, turning it into particles.

It still took a lot of his focus to even keep the souls moving, but it was barely manageable; at least he had gotten a boost out of it. It was a very productive fight for him as he looked at his battered arm, skin burnt, but still usable after all that fighting.

The souls in his space became a lot dimmer but still held up. He let it sit there as the energy dissipated from his body, feeling the effect of the adrenaline disappearing, he crashed towards the ground, trying to hold himself up. His head throbbed under all the exhaustion, his focus dwindled, passing out on the floor with blood flowing out of the torn flesh on his face.
