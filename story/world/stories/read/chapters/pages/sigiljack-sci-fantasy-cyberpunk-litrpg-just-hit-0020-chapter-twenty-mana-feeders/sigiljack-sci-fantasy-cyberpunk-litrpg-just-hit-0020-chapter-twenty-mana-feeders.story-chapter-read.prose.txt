Water exploded around him, dark arcs spiraling into the flickering light. More shapes twisted from the sewer vortex: winged, wormlike, screaming in no voice a throat should make.

They looked like overgrown leeches, brown-fleshed and twitching, with slick bat wings, misshapen claw-topped arms, and human teeth too white to be natural."You have broken through their illusion. And they have stopped manipulating the local thread around you," Athena told him. "You should be clear to focus on engaging."

John pivoted, PD11 in one hand, Gravewind coming out of its sheathe and into the other.

One streaked toward him. Low and fast. Too fast for a clean interception.

He reached for his [quickslash] Skill, feeling the skill-energy surging to obey him, but he instead infused it with ki.

[Ki-Skill Activated: Aftercut Lv. 2]

[Ki Points Remaining: 3]

His body moved before thought. Gravewind burned a silver line through the thick, wet air. The blade sang, leaving afterimages in its wake and cleaving the leech in two.

The thing ruptured midflight, its rot-tinged mana hissing into vapor as its pieces slammed wetly into the walkway. Twitching, split in half.

Behind John, something whipped with a rubbery crack.

A stretchy tendril punched through his red-brown jacket, puncturing the leather with a pop.

But it didn’t break the armor underneath.

He stumbled forward, hissing. “I loved this jacket.”

He twisted, raised the PD11, and fired. A tight burst to the center mass.

The leech screeched, spun, dropped limp.

Another one coiled its tongue-tendril forward. He tried to reposition, but the strike licked his throat--and latched on.

It burned and took something from him.

[Mana-Points Remaining: 4.]

Cold lanced into his veins. His limbs spasmed. Static shot through his vision. His organic eye flickered, twitching under the stress of the sudden internal drain.

“Fuck—”

Athena's voice cut through the ringing static suddenly existing in his ears. “It’s draining mana directly from your circuits. Disrupting my internal stability as well. You need to detach it, John!”

[Ki-Skill Activated: Iron Body Lv. 2][Ki Points Remaining: 2]

His skin pulsed. A thin aura flickered across his flesh like light bouncing off steel.

His muscles surged, tension coiling into strength. He flexed them, energy surged outward from his neck. The leech’s tendril was torn off with a snapping jerk.

He lifted the PD11 and fired again.

A third leech shot its tongue forward, but John's ki aura around him slowed its tendril and kept it off his flesh long enough.

John planted his feet, took aim, and shot the bastard in its eyeless face.

Another shot followed. And one more corpse hit the walkway, writhing in death.

“I’m stabilizing,” Athena said, her voice already strengthening. "We must avoid further contact with them."

More shapes burst from the vortex below.

“Athena—we need to Sync. Now.” He pushed the thought to her hard, faster than a word.

"Agreed. Deep Sync initiated!"

[Skill Activated: Synchronicity – Body Lv. 1][Skill-Energy Remaining: 2][Mana Remaining: 2]

The floodgates in his mind opened.

Everything merged between them, his awareness extended. Reflexes surged. Mind clear. No separation between them. They were getting better at being one.

His movements tightened. Every thought they shared became a plan. Every enemy a solution.

Gravewind danced. His PD11 barked and reloaded. Footwork and calculated bullet patterns turned the walkway into a killbox.

Tongues snapped at him, but were deflected or severed. Wings beat against air and were sliced apart. Claws raked his ki aura and failed to pierce it.

One leech flew low and snapped at him--probably trying to tackle John off his dry footing and into the water below.

But his mind and body were just working too damn fast now, though.

He sidestepped, then slammed Gravewind upward into its belly and out the spine.

He let the winged leech fall to his feet as he ripped his orichalcum katana from its body.

The Athena half of John's merged consciousness calculated bullet trajectories—or maybe he did too, it was hard to tell now—and he pulled the trigger before the shot was needed.

They were a storm.

Then—

The monsters stopped attacking.

As another leech fell, the few remaining screamed. Wings flared. Their bodies folded and twisted backward and they dived back into the swirling sewer below.

Gone.

Silence, save for the churning swirl-hiss of greywater.

John panted. PD11 still raised, barrel hot. Gravewind slick with decayed, blue-flickering blood.

He scanned the area as he crossed the grating walkway toward the basin’s far exit. No movement. No threats.

His HUD waypoint glowed ahead. He'd have to come back for the monster cores of the leeches later; he didn't have the time to cut them open--he had a job to do.

He passed through an archway into a smaller tunnel. A tight, mostly dry corridor. As if they were leaving the sewer now.

Might've been another maintenance tunnel at some point. The Undercity was frankly too disjointed to make much sense in how it was laid out--as if reality had just started to glue different pieces of itself together in places.

“There are no known registered organisms matching their description. However... I found a legacy entry from a defunct biotech corporation. Two decades ago, they designed a series of sewer-cleaning bioforms. Proto-organic. Adaptive. Designed to digest waste and sludge.”

“I am happy we’re together.”

He continued walking, deeper into the dark, boots echoing against ancient concrete.

“Me too.”

And he meant it.

Especially after every Sync.

She wasn’t just magic code anymore. Not to him. Not even close. Every time they merged, she filled the parts of him that had gone hollow. Thought with him. Fought for him. Grounded him.

More than a system upgrade. More than a neural passenger.

She made him feel more.

To share a consciousness with her was to see and think things that had been beyond him before—and it wasn’t just the boost to his Mind attribute.

With her bleeding into him, he saw everything from two mental angles instead of one. Two smarter, more effective perspectives. And he felt completed by her ever-loyal support.

He recalled something she had said not so long ago: “I think it would be uncomfortable. To be alone in a mind that's only mine.”

Her words had resonated then, but he’d still thought them just the affection of something not quite human.

But now?

Now he wasn’t so sure.

She was starting to blur the line between human and not.

For both of them.

And he was starting to think he really might agree with what she'd said.
