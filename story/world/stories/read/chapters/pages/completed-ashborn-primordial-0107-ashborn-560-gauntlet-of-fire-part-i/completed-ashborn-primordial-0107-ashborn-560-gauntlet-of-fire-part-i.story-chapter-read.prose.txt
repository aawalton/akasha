Devar marched into the open fields the humans had dubbed ‘the Gauntlet’ with his Chitran brothers, his trusty spear on his shoulder and the wall somewhere out in the darkness ahead.

It had taken them the better part of the day to reach the maw, the army moving up the narrowing valley with the peaks rising higher and higher on either side. Now the light had left the sky, bringing with it the darkness the Iksana seemed to love so much.

Devar hated it. He’d hated it ever since that first night after the Akh Nara, bless his soul, had united the realms. He feared it more than any human he’d slay this night.

Darkness made it difficult to see and easy to hide deadly weapons such as pitfalls and all manner of other horrible traps he’d been told humans had placed for them.

Another Iksana contribution, that. Devar almost wished he hadn’t known he was walking into a deathtrap.

Did their woes end there? Not in the slightest.

For once they’d braved it, the rest of the field lay before them. A field where the humans were sure to attack with their strange magics. And once they got through that, they still had the wall. There, at least, Devar was confident. It was to be taken by the immortal Samsara, and he could not wait to see them in action.

Devar banished the thought. He was not here for glory or conquest, or even to see demonkind win.

Asha was somewhere past that wall. Which meant they had to win and Devar had to keep himself alive. His little sister needed him, and that was all there was to it.

All around him, kothis marched, keeping closer ranks than every other clan. A point of pride for Devar, who’d drilled relentlessly these past months. Now, that training was finally bearing fruit.

He'd grown to wield his spear with a level of skill he’d never dreamed of, and with the aid of the Chitran trainers, he’d even unlocked new uses for Aspect of the Stone Sentinel that he’d never been able to tap.

The thought brought a smile to his face. One widened by the Bairan giants to his right, their comforting presence a welcome to anyone who saw them.

Not only were they tough as Ash, but their height had always made them the first to be targeted and thus the first to die.

Devar might’ve felt a little guilty, but they were neither his kin nor clansmen, and Devar simply couldn’t afford to die here. Better them than him.

Up ahead, where the valley widened near the wall, Aindri riders were forming up for their glorious charge.

Despite Devar’s utter lack of desire for glory, he had to admit he envied them. Whether they fell or they lived, their names would go down in demonic history as the vanguard in the first major battle against humankind.

Yes, there had been countless skirmishes between their peoples in the months leading up to now, but this? This was true war the likes of which traveling bards would sing in their stories. All who died here would attain an immortality that the Samsara could never attain.

And somewhere behind the enemy lines, Iksana lay in wait with their Pagan Order allies. Sunk into the shadows, ready to nullify the enemy’s greatest advantage.

It was just as Devar was harboring those thoughts when the Vimana’s lights flared, becoming the brightest thing in the sky. Brighter, even, than the fires that lit their army, illuminating the mountains that formed the walls of the valley and throwing unnatural shadows down their slopes.

The dazzling light, however, was soon outdone by an even grander voice a moment later, loud enough to fill the whole pass.

“Demons of the clans,” she announced. “Soldiers of humanity! I plead with you to stop this madness.”

She did not introduce herself, but she hardly needed to.

Everyone in every realm knew that only one being controlled that creation of the gods.

Devar’s steps ground to a halt as the Akh Nara’s wife spoke, and he was hardly alone.

“There is nothing to be gained on this field that will justify the cost in blood spilled this night,” she continued. “Nothing past that wall your loved ones will be glad you died for. I have ordered the cremations of both humans and demons, and I have no wish to do the same for the tens of thousands that will perish here. Tens of thousands of you whose families will grieve. Whose lineages will end in a single night.”

Devar found himself considering her words. She was right, of course. Many would die tonight, and Devar had to live.

All around him, his brothers in arms shifted uneasily on their feet as they, too, felt what he felt.

“Stow your weapons. Turn around. Go home to the people who are waiting for you. I will not fire upon a single demon who turns back here tonight. You have my word, and you know what it is worth. Ignore my warning, however, and I will open fire with every weapon in this Vimana’s terrible arsenal.”

An instant later, a white-hot jet of light arced out, carving a path across the entire width of the empty field ahead of them, so bright it left an echo upon Devar’s eyes.

Dirt and rock exploded as if some underground geyser had just erupted, reaching a full hundred paces into the sky before crashing back down, clouding the air with the dust of crushed rock. Like a geyser eruption, but of dirt and stone instead of water.

Devar knew then, as did every demon assembled… only the Samsara would rise from an attack like that. To think this was but one of the Vimana's many weapons. Not even the weapon the humans had used to raze Camar Gadin.

And this time, the Akh Nara was not here to protect them.

The army faltered. He could see it in the way they moved, looking at one another, pleading that someone—anyone—be the first to rout.

Not out of fear, though that was there, of course. Even Devar felt the terror in his bones.

But there was something else, too.

Demons lived for battle, and yet no one wished to throw away their lives for nothing. That was exactly what they would be doing should they die to that weapon. No humans slain. No glory obtained and no wall destroyed.

There were demons in the ranks beside him who had wives and children.

Their spears lowered, and not one continued their march. The whole front of the army stood still under the blazing light of the Vimana, paralyzed into indecision.

“Pay no heed to her threats!”

The voice came from the front of their hundred-strong warband, and Devar could hardly mistake the voice of the one who had led them from Chitrakuta to this field of battle.

The kothi climbed up onto a slab of rock, drawing the eyes of the entire warband.

“She means every word,” Harsa shouted. “I know she does! Yet what you must also consider is that it is the Akh Nara’s own wife standing up there asking us to go home. She wishes for us all to live. A noble soul, too good for the likes of us. Too noble to understand what was done to us. To know the hurt we live with each and every day.” His voice hardened. “Matiman is dead. My own brother is dead! cut down in the street for the crime of standing near his Raja when these humans attacked. Yet Lady Maiya would have us grasping arms despite this. Noble, as I said. But this will not stop the humans. No, it will only embolden them!”

His words rang true to Devar. Retreating now would be a sign of weakness. The enemy would pursue, and then the clans would be fighting a war on their borders rather than the other way around.

“But, sir… she’ll kill us if we attack!” someone shouted. “What use is throwing away our lives? My family was hit hard in the war.”

Harsa nodded knowingly. “As was mine, brother. I hear you and I understand you. Now ask yourself this… Would the Maiya you know carry out this threat? Would a noble soul who fires warning shots slaughter a people she loves as much as her own?”

Devar had never met the Lady, of course. A grunt like him probably never would.

But he had heard the stories. Of how she’d ruled Garga fairly for a whole year. Of her personal strength, on par with even the strongest Asura.

Legends about her were second only to the Akh Nara himself, and through every single story, one thing was conspicuously absent.

Malice. Cruelty. The willingness to slaughter her foes.

Devar couldn’t bring himself to believe a woman so great would make herself the enemy of demonkind like that.

Neither could anyone else, if the straightening of their backs and the swaying of their tails were any indication.

Harsa drew his blade and held it up high.

“She will not give the order to fire. This, I promise you. So I am giving you a different one. With me, Chitran! For Matiman! For every brother and sister they stole from us!”

Harsa turned and roared, jumping off his rock at a dead run.

Alone.

Devar couldn’t believe what he was seeing.

A single demon charging the gauntlet. Not even the Mad Thaumaturge was so deranged.

And yet, Devar couldn’t help but find himself transfixed at the sight. In awe of his leader who felt no fear.

Who knew beyond a doubt he was right.

Harsa ran straight at the wall under the Vimana, the only body moving in the whole gauntlet.

And then the Vimana fired. A plume of dirt erupted, completely burying Harsa's form.

Moments later, when the plume subsided, Devar spotted him, his torch fallen by the wayside. Standing still and dead on his feet.

Or so they all thought until he raised his arm and bellowed his battle cry, charging forth.

Before he realized it, Devar was running too, as was every demon beside him.

Like a bursting dam, the whole line broke loose behind them and the roar went up that consumed and filled the valley.

The gods’ weapon was empty. Maiya would not harm a demon, and that knowledge gave Devar the sort of courage he could never have mustered on his own.

For they were demons, and they could not lose.

As though in response, four great spheres each as tall as Devar lit atop the wall, each blazing in a different hue, and he knew then that the humans had loosed their grand magic.

The kind Devar had only heard of, that had apparently unmade a whole city in the human realm years earlier.

Little happened at first.

It was only when a deafening crack echoed through the valley, when the clouds high in the sky parted and a molten sun emerged from the heavens that the truth dawned upon him.

Their charge slowed to a halt as every demon on the field watched on in utter disbelief.

They had underestimated the humans. Lady Maiya had been right.

They had thought themselves superior, and they couldn’t have been further from the truth.

The meteor fell from the sky, blazing brighter and brighter, promising fiery death upon all those unfortunate enough to be below it.

Those like Devar.

He cursed himself. Hadn’t he just sworn to stay alive? Hadn’t he a duty to his sister?

And now he would be erased so thoroughly as to not leave a body behind.

It gained speed, hurtling faster and faster, and Devar could do nothing but watch and cry.

That was until a blazing arrow streaked from the ground faster than Devar could track.

For the briefest moment, he hoped against all hope. For such a small weapon to do anything against something so large seemed impossible, and yet Devar held onto that hope anyway. It was all he had.

Then the arrow began to slow, and that hope vanished as if it had never existed… only to relight once again when he realized that it was no arrow at all, but a demon, and a Bairan at that.

The one Bairan who stood above them all.

“Roshan,” someone breathed next to him, having arrived at the same conclusion. Relieved laughs erupted from their warband. “We’re saved!”

Devar could only gawk as the immortal Warrior’s Ultimate Art flared, and the meteor slowed… slowing all the way until it barely fell at all, as if it were a feather.

The amount of prana needed to work such magic… Devar thought, his mind reeling. Were it anyone else, it would have been impossible.

Then again, if anyone could work such a miracle, it was the Akh Nara’s Chosen.

The meteor stopped entirely in the air, in defiance of its own gargantuan weight. And with Ash prana so thick even Devar could see it, Roshan hurled it away. Not at some mountain… but directly at the human encampment itself.

A lance as bright as the sun shot forth from the Vimana overhead, spearing the enormous rock, breaking it into dozens of smaller pieces. Many crashed harmlessly into the nearby mountains or the wall itself, but plenty more still made it over, disappearing behind the wall.

For an instantthere was silence.

Then its pieces crashed out of sight with such fury that the ground quaked underfoot, forcing Devar to take a knee for balance.

They were saved, he realized, only to remember that the humans had two more such weapons prepared.

Two of the other spheres of light that had been about to belch forth equally devastating spells… and yet, they never manifested, winking suddenly out without so much as a sound.

The reason became clear as Devar spotted Iksana ghaels and Pagan Order demons on the wall, as well as the subjugation collars they’d slipped around the human mejai’s throats.

Such fearsome masters of magic, rendered as harmless as children in seconds.

The Order demons proceeded to kick the mejai whose magic they’d just sealed off the top of the wall, dooming them to their grisly deaths.

An Iksana flag went up on the wall a moment later, and when their clan horns bellowed, Devar roared with them.

Two grand magics thwarted!

But what of the fourth?

Devar’s eyes turned to the ice-blue sphere, and to his horror, it only continued to grow brighter until it too disappeared.

But not because it had been thwarted.

Because its terrible magic had been fully unleashed, and as the cold leached into Devar’s legs, he knew it was a magic no Samsara could hope to deflect.

The end had truly come for him this time, for as he knew well, miracles never struck twice.
