Micky didn’t have much spare spiritual blood to practice with, since the clone on Tenefyre had yet to return. Forced to test his borrowed brainpower out in a more mundane way, he grew a third pair of arms and used them to brew some healing potions while playing a kik’lit fashioned out of ice with his remaining four hands.

Percy was quite pleased to watch his friend handle both tasks flawlessly. ‘We’ll have an easier time managing your sentries now – among other things.’

Strictly speaking, Percy and Micky had already had access to three minds before awakening his third core, so the new mindset had technically only quadrupled their cognitive capacity.

Still, that should let them see through almost fifty sensory domains at the same time, on top of controlling Micky’s entire army of sentries. Unfortunately, Percy’s soul clones would only be able to manage a fraction of the constructs until he figured out how to lend them his extra processing power as well.

The final thing Percy wanted to check was how well the mindset would respond to the destruction and creation of his mind clones. There didn’t seem to be a cost to having them permanently active, so he didn’t see himself deactivating his bloodline often. However, he couldn’t help but worry that he’d need to waste some time connecting himself to his future mind clones every time his capacity increased.

To test that, he emptied and assimilated one of his mental pockets while watching the corresponding constellation vanish without a trace. Activating his bloodline again, he was excited to see the orange stars reappear in their modified state as the cognitive pocket within his brain formed anew.

‘Good. This proves that the changes to my current mind clones are permanent. I suppose we’ll have to wait a few months before we know whether they’ll carry over to future clones.’

‘There’s a good chance,’ Nephthys said. ‘Like I mentioned, mindset creation relies heavily on one’s intentions.’

Percy nodded. Pulling up his Status, he read the changes to the mindset section.

___

Mindsets [688/1,200]:

___

The description of the new mindset was simple enough. Percy was more surprised by how differently his mental capacity was represented. Before registering his latest mindset, his Status had listed the number as a percentage.

Evidently, Phoebe’s Decree had decided that the flexible nature of his mind warranted a more thoughtful classification.

Performing a few quick calculations in his head, Percy confirmed that his mindsets still occupied about fifty-seven percent of his capacity. The numerator and denominator of the fraction had simply been multiplied by twelve, which was the number of minds that he and Micky had access to.

The new mindset was apparently as cheap to maintain as Ludwick’s Compass and Shimmering Constellations. Enhanced Cognition was the only one that strained Percy’s limited resources, which was quite ironic given how poorly designed it was.

‘My next priority should be to fix that,’ Percy declared.

The current version of the mindset consumed over half of his capacity and only boosted his thinking speed by about fifteen percent. It was better than nothing, but those were no longer his only options.

‘Agreed,’ Nephthys concurred after he shared the information through the cord.

‘How difficult will it be to improve?’ Percy asked.

‘Harder than creating Parallel Minds, that’s for sure,’ the reincarnated goddess warned. ‘You’ll have to rework the central part of the mindset, and you’ll be fine-tuning something that actively converts your mental capacity into a tangible boost. That’s a lot more complicated than connecting a few minds handed to you for free by your bloodline.’

Percy nodded, having expected that. ‘How much better do you think I can make it?’

‘Depends on how long you work on it,’ Nephthys replied. ‘The current mindset is pretty bad, because it was passed down through a plaque. If you just clean up the fluff and scale the effect to occupy one hundred percent of your capacity, it should accelerate each of your minds by about forty percent. That would typically require a novice at mindset creation who’s been training for about a year, though you might be able to pull it off after a couple of months because much of the training is modular and can be sped up by your extra minds.’

‘And how much better would it be if I were an expert?’ Percy asked.

‘There’s no upper limit that I know of,’ the Ruby Princess replied with a mental shrug. ‘Minds are immensely complex, so there’s always room for improvement. However, you’ll quickly run into diminishing returns. One can typically expect about a flat five percent improvement over the initial boost whenever they accumulate ten times as much training in the field. For you, that would mean a forty-five percent boost after a couple of years, fifty percent after twenty, fifty-five percent after two hundred, and so on. Oh, but keep in mind that asking another person to do it for you would greatly weaken the effect. It’s generally better for a mind mage to produce their own mindsets.’

‘What about you? What could you have done in your prime?’ Percy asked – mostly out of curiosity, since recovering his familiar’s power wasn’t exactly on the table.

‘Around sixty percent?’ Nephthys replied, sounding unsure. ‘I lived for hundreds of thousands of years, but I didn’t have your mind clones, nor did I spend all of my time practicing mindset creation. Given what Zoris told us about Celestials, I suppose that even the oldest mind deities would struggle to achieve a seventy percent acceleration before losing control of their internal world.’

Percy frowned. That didn’t sound like much, though he quickly remembered that they were only talking about the base acceleration of the mindset. A mind user could supercharge it with additional mana, and a demigod with that affinity would presumably be able to amplify the effect by thousands of times. Concept mages could likely do even better than that.

‘I see… Well, I can’t afford to waste my life on mindset creation either, but spending a couple of months to increase the boost from fifteen percent to forty sounds like a good idea,’ he concluded. Even pushing the effect to fifty percent after a couple of decades made sense, though anything beyond that would have to be weighed against the opportunity cost of working on other projects. ‘Unless you think that there are more useful mindsets to invest my limited capacity on?’

‘Not necessarily,’ his familiar replied. ‘Raw cognitive acceleration is about as simple as it gets, but it’s not a bad thing to focus on. In theory, there might be situations where you’d benefit more from spending your capacity on mental resistances or automated spellcasting, but those results can be replicated to a lesser extent by just having a lot of extra brainpower to throw at your problems. Considering the synergy between your mind clones and Enhanced Cognition, I would recommend going all-in on the latter.’

Percy was satisfied with the explanation and with that short-term goal, though he knew that upgrading his mindset wasn’t the only priority. The cognitive acceleration would also depend on how much mana he could feed into it, so working on his boosting art was also important.

Not to mention that the sooner he mastered Circulation and the Dance, the sooner he’d be able to start working on the Cradle as well, which would not only improve the boost from Enhanced Cognition but also increase his clone limits and allow him to consume his lifespan more slowly.

‘I guess I can start working on Circulation already,’ he realized.

His mind clones had spent all of the past week clearing and tempering channels, which would translate into months of progress for a regular person. There were still a few pathways that weren’t ready yet, but they wouldn’t take more than another day or two to complete.

The even-better news was that the internal pattern for his new affinity was known, because Percy wasn’t the only mind user in the alliance. The external flow was still being worked on, since none of his subordinates had figured it out yet, but some of the Starry Commanders and Breeders had been practicing for over a decade, so they should have plenty of insights to share.

Considering his experience with other versions of the boosting art, Percy felt confident in his ability to master Circulation, the Dance, and even the Cradle within a single year.

The only question was how to best split his mental pockets between the boosting art and mindset creation, but that could easily be determined after some trial and error.

If there was one thing that Percy had been slacking on lately, it was his soul clones. He’d been too busy figuring out his mind affinity to plan his adventures out properly, which wasn’t ideal. In addition to the clone that had returned from Melodia and the new cord that he had acquired after activating the Void Decree, the two that had been working on Archibald’s and Galahad’s cores had also returned.

To avoid wasting precious time, Percy decided to send a clone to Gallimus – to hitch a ride to the Sapphire Pavilion’s branch. He wasn’t planning to make any big moves in the next auction, as he’d had enough excitement to last him for quite a while, but he didn’t mind buying a few more affinity-granting treasures for his allies and keeping an eye out for new karmic compasses.

As long as he didn’t try to sell something he wasn’t supposed to, making a few anonymous purchases shouldn’t raise any eyebrows.

As for the other three clones… he decided to send them on random adventures for now. Visiting Mithril to plan out the acquisition of his Focus, or the planet with the rich heritage in sound magic might have been better uses of his time, but he wanted to be able to retrieve his clones on short notice in case he needed them during his experiments with his new affinity.

He had a hunch that he’d be uncovering more interesting applications in the near future, and delaying those adventures by a few months or years wouldn’t make much of a difference.
