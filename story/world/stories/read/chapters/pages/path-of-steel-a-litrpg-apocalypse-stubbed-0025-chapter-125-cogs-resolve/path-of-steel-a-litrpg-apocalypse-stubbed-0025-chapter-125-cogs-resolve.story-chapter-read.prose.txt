Gary, Cog, and Alicia sat with the crowd. They were less relaxed than usual, and it wasn’t just the tournament. Gary had told them about the inspector. They’d memorized their story, and how to respond to a series of possibly incriminating questions.

Gary himself was exhausted to the bone. This wasn’t what he hoped would happen. The operation was supposed to be dissolved long before anyone looked into them. They were small fish. If not for the upsets during the tournament, they’d have slipped through the cracks.

This was all on top of an upcoming Duel of Honor which required one hundred percent of his focus to even stand a chance.

There’s nothing more I can do. If we’re caught, we’re caught, and if we escape, we escape. At least Cog and Alicia never sold, only bought. Even in the worst case scenario, they should be fine. The inspector can’t possibly execute everyone who ever bought from us.

He was sliding in his seat. His eyes were closing. Not only had he fallen asleep close to dawn, but his own match would be soon after the start of the day, meaning he had to wake up early. At least, if he won, he’d have reached the final day of the tournament’s first phase with zero losses. He could just not show up tomorrow, and he’d still pass.

“Everyone left is really strong…” Alicia muttered.

“We’re strong, too!” Cog replied. Like Gary, he had a lossless record so far, though that was in part because he kept getting matched against weak opponents. He just needed one more win to pass, but of all the disciples remaining, he was at the lowest level.

Dozing off, Gary almost missed his name getting called. “Gary Steel versus Sean Cog. Disciples, please attend!”

Gary’s eyes snapped open. He stared at Cog, who stared back. The metallic lifeform chuckled. “Well, I have no hair to lose. I’ll try to at least fight back,” he said.

“Wait, Cog, no. This is your chance! I’m more likely to win tomorrow. You might not. I can give this match to you, and then you automatically qualify for the finals. I know how much you need that!”

Cog was frozen for a moment. His eyes took on a sad hue. “I can’t do that, Gary.”

“Why not?”

“Disciples, please attend!” the deacon repeated. They were only holding one match at a time now, so the arena was empty as it awaited them.

“I want to win with my own worth, not because you let me. Plus, the better I do in the tournament, the more likely it is that I’ll be questioned. The inspector will certainly find me if I reach the finals.”

“Cog…”

“Don’t say anything. It’s fine. Plus, you really need the extra training, right? If you lose today, you’ll have to show up tomorrow, and that’s a few hours of training you’ll miss. You’ll be fighting for your life, Gary. That’s more important than me reaching the finals.”

“Sean Cog and Gary Steel, please attend, or you’ll both receive a loss!” the deacon commanded. He was looking at them. Cog stood up.

“Cog, wait!” Gary said. “You don’t have to do this. We can at least fight.”

“What’s the point? I can’t beat you, and you need to save energy. Hey, Deacon!” he raised his voice. “I, Sean Cog, surrender this fight!”

“Are you certain?” the deacon asked.

“As certain as the contents of a thrown-away diaper.”

In the audience, awkward chuckles mixed with boos and cries of disappointment about missing the baldinator’s fight. “Don’t worry, guys. You won’t see him tomorrow either,” Cog whispered, sitting back down.

“Thank you,” Gary said.

“It’s alright. You earned this by being stronger than me.”

“No, it’s not alright. You’re helping me, Chloe, and Asim, while you were never at fault. We can’t thank you enough.”

Cog turned, beaming a bright smile at Gary. “Isn’t that what friends do?”

Gary burst out laughing.

“Don’t worry,” Cog said, thumping his chest. “I’ve trained hard. Tomorrow, I’ll do my best, and if my opponent isn’t a monster… I promise you, for my people and my dream, I will definitely win! I will earn my place in the finals!”

***

Gary departed the arena and headed straight for Master Colorful’s residence. The city’s scenery was lost on him. He was in do-or-die mode. On arrival, they started work on perfecting the first two songs, then launched into the third song in the afternoon. It was as hard as Gary remembered. Thankfully, with Master Colorful giving pointers, he was making steady progress.

The sun gave way to the moon. It illuminated a sweaty, shirtless man, perfect muscles gleaming, eyes hazy as he struggled to focus on the advanced aura manipulation required for the third song.

“You’re done,” Colorful said eventually. “Sleep for two hours, and we’ll continue.”

Gary lay on the grass where he stood. He awoke to moonlight still, with Colorful standing over him. “It’s time,” he said.

Gary stood. He picked up his spear, inhaled sharply, then dove into training. The hours blended together. It was so grueling he almost quit. He didn’t even care about survival. The only thing overpowering his need to sleep was a dark fire burning in the core of his being—the image of a world on fire, building collapsing, people screaming. It birthed hatred in his heart—the desire to make the Network pay for what they did to his planet.

Clara, his friends, his family… Everyone he’d ever known was dead or suffering. They’d taken everyone's lives and ripped them to pieces, stomped them down, burned them into the ground and laughed about it while tossing the survivors to silly monsters.

Gary thrust and thrust, his anger inflating. Black lightning brewed. Ruin screeched, demanding pain. Demanding death. Unbeknownst to Gary, his eyes turned black. Colorful said nothing. Goldie also said nothing, focused on enhancing Gary’s power.

The sun rose. The third song began to take form. The day’s duels had started, but Gary neither knew nor cared when he was scheduled to appear, because he’d forfeit his fight by no-show. He’d already passed, anyway. He needed every training hour he could get.

Colorful stayed tirelessly by his side, giving pointers. The amount of skill required for the third song was mind-blowing, but bit by bit, Gary was chipping away at it. The only question was…

Would he make it in time?

***

While Gary painstakingly trained, the first phase’s final day was in full swing.

A gladiator-looking man smashed into the walls, rolling away from a spear. He flourished his own spear, thrusting thrice in quick succession, then rode a wave of sand into his opponent’s face.

His opponent, a white-haired woman wielding twin short spears, jumped high into the air and span, unleashing a storm of strikes. The gladiator met them. Spears clashed. Auras exploded. A boom washed over the crowd, which cheered to the heavens.

“What a battle!” Cog exclaimed.

“They’re both really good!” Alicia replied. “How can they have one loss each? Who beat them?”

"Someone better,” Cog replied, licking his metallic lips. Of the almost two hundred participants, just under thirty were left. The cream of the crop. Each was an accomplished cultivator, using unique styles and advanced aura attacks.

“You know,” Cog said, “I don’t think I can beat even one of these guys.”

“At least you have a chance. You didn’t get matched against Feng fucking Shen,” Alicia said.

“That was bad luck for sure.”

“What could I even do? He blocked all my attacks with pure aura and used two fingers to bash me into a wall. It’s unfair, Cog. I really thought I had a chance.”

“You know what would be fun? If you’d gotten a regular opponent and won, and then we matched against each other today. That way, at least one of us would make it to the finals!”

Her gaze softened. “I’d forfeit. I know how much it means to you.”

“It does…but I have to earn it! No forfeiting!”

She chuckled. “I guess you also got a monster opponent yesterday… It was my fault for losing once before. This is exactly why we get two losses.”

“That, and to increase the fight days. I bet the clan racks up a ton of aura stones.”

“Well, it isn’t always this crowded…” Alicia said. The arena was packed. There wasn’t a single empty seat outside the VIP area, and the hawkers selling snacks and drinks had to push their way through people. Her gaze rested on a corner of the stadium flying a banner that read, “Take their hair and pride, BALDINATOR!”

“I bet they’ll be upset,” Cog said, following her gaze.

“It’s for the best. Gary has enough enemies to fill a village already. Hey, Cog?”

“Yeah?”

“Are you considering throwing the match?”

He glanced at her. “Why do you say that?” he asked.

“If you win here, you’ll make a huge impression. You’re a nobody. The inspector will come to question you, and then you might incriminate Gary and the others…”

A wave of cheers washed over the arena as two mighty fighters clashed. Aura flew everywhere. People exclaimed in wonder.

Cog’s mouth tightened. “I considered it…but I’m not going to throw the match. You know how important this is to me. Reaching the finals will elevate me in the eyes of my people, giving me the support I need to go further. I’m a nobody now. I can’t change the tower like this. I need power. I need to become someone, and the only way to do that is to reach the finals. I have no illusions of winning even once there, but not everyone fighting today is a monster. If I get a weaker opponent… If I can just get this one win… My dream will come this much closer to fruition.”

“And the inspector?”

“I have my story. I’ll consider all possible questions and come up with good answers. I believe it will work, but it’s a risk I have to take. If I asked Gary, I’m sure he’d tell me the same.”

“Yeah, he would… Because he’s an idiot,” Alicia said, sinking into her seat. She was already eliminated, so she could relax. Cog clenched his fist.

A red spear crashed into the chest of a challenger with the force of a sun. There came an explosion. A mangled body slammed into the arena walls under the sound of cheers, and healers were already rushing as the winner, panting heavily, raised a fist. The audience erupted again.

As both disciples left the arena, the deacon read the next names. “Sean Cog versus Lord of Pot. Disciples, please attend!”

Alicia’s brows shot up. She exchanged an incredulous gaze with Cog. “Isn’t that…” she said.

“Yeah. The guy who beat you up.” Cog stood in his seat. Leaping into the arena, he landed on the sands and leveled his spear at his opponent, a tall and lanky person wielding a spear in one hand and a pot in the other. Seeing his taunting pose, the audience cheered.

“You’ve been an asshole to one of my friends,” Cog enunciated slowly but loud enough for everyone to hear. “I’m not too strong, but you’re one of the few people today I can defeat. I will do that. I need to see the finals, Lord of Pot—and you stand on my path, so you must fall.”

The audience cheered again. The Lord of Pot chuckled. He swayed from side to side, his jives flying around. “Try me, robot,” he said. The audience gasped.

“Fuck you!” someone shouted—that word was a slur against metallic lifeforms.

Cog’s eyes darkened further. His gears whirled. He drew his silver spear into a thrusting position. “Watch me,” he whispered and charged.

The pot swirled in his opponent’s hand. For the first time, it had a lid. There was a sloushing sound as if it contained some liquid, but the lid held as the Lord of Pot threw it forward. Cog dashed out of the strike. He approached the Lord, made to thrust, then shot sideways to dodge the pot on its return trajectory.

“I’ve seen this before!” he exclaimed, launching into a flurry of thrusts similar to Gary’s Nail Gun. His mechanical physique was well-suited to precise, repeated movement. The silver spear pushed his opponent back, but every thrust was calmly parried. Occasionally, the pot would come in Cog’s way, blocking strikes like a gong.

“What stupid fighting style is this!” Cog yelled.

The Lord of Pot chuckled. “One that hasn’t lost yet. Behold, the beauty of my pot!”

The pot was chugged at Cog again. He ducked under it, right into the Lord’s rising spear. If this was a month ago, Cog would have been hit. However, he hadn’t trained this hard for nothing. He wasn’t the weakling he used to be. He saw the strike coming and leaned out of the way, losing his balance but simultaneously using his momentum to slash.

“I got yo—”

Something hard fell on Cog’s head. The world went white. He stumbled, losing his footing, and the butt of a spear thrust into his belly, bending metal. Cog went flying. When he managed to stand again, he had one hand on his belly, where oil was flowing. His mind was still on the previous attack.

“How?” he muttered.

“As if I’d tell you,” the Lord of Pot replied smugly.

“He put his spear through the pot’s handle and flicked it at you!” a loud voice came from the audience. It was Alicia. Cog turned thankful eyes at her, while the Lord of Pot snorted.

“Losers should know their place,” he said. When he turned to the front, Cog was already charging. There came a flurry of blows. The Lord of Pot defended but was gradually pushed back. He threw his pot backward, where it bounced off the wall like a tennis ball and ricocheted towards Cog. He was ready this time. He brought his spear down on the pot, burying it in the dirt. When it tried to rise, he placed a foot over it.

The Lord’s face darkened. “That’s mine,” he said.

“Come get it,” Cog said.

The Lord attacked. They traded blows in the center of the arena, Cog matching his opponent for a bit, then earning an edge. His Vibration of Geometry let him move in lines that confused and outran his opponents. In a straight battle, he was superior.

Suddenly, the Lord pretended to lose a step. Cog realized it but charged in regardless, focusing his aura perception behind him. The second he sensed a massive item enter his range, he ducked to the side, then calculated the pot’s trajectory and thrust right next to it. The Lord’s hand appeared exactly where he’d thrusted—of course, he’d try to catch his pot.

Cog stabbed the other’s wrist. The pot went flying. The Lord screamed. Cog ducked under a wide slash, shooting his spear straight. Somehow, he missed. He then realized his feet were horizontal, completely off the ground. A terrible pain rose from the left side of his head.

What?

The pot crashed into him, from the right this time, turning his world white. Cog was too lost to scream. The pot flew around him in sharp angles, pummeling his head, rocking it from side to side. Cog didn’t understand. The shaking belonged to someone else. There was no pain.

“Stop!” a distant voice yelled.

Through his hazy eyes, Cog saw the pot dive out of view. He caught his opponent’s mocking glint.

No, he thought. I worked so hard.

He was collapsing now. His body leaned forward, and he had no strength to stop it. Suddenly, the pot rammed into the underside of his jaw, sending it flying high—and still attached to the rest of him, thankfully. The pot shot from side to side, pummeling his head again, while Cog was powerless to defend. It didn’t let him fall. Clangs echoed in the arena. The audience was silent.

Somehow, sand got into his face—had he fallen, or was it flying? He couldn’t tell. Cog saw light. So much light.

His fingers were tight around his spear.
