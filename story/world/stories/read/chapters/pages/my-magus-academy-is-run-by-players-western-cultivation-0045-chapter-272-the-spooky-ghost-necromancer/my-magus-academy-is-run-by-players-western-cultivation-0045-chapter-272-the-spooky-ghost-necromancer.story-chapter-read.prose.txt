Inside the Headmaster’s Building, Victor — who had been in the middle of an experiment with Lillie — suddenly received a transmission feed relayed directly from the mechanical golems he had gifted to Frederick. The image quality was poor, but still clear enough for him to grasp the situation. It showed Frederick’s body, bloodied and broken, being lifted by one of the golems and being brought back to the academy.

Who would have thought those spy golems would prove to be useful so soon?

After reviewing the report transmitted through their data cores, Victor let out a long sigh. So, the inevitable war with the Arcane Radiance College was this all along… No wonder the mission hasn’t been marked as complete.

Had he not been bound by the curse, perhaps he could have intervened — maybe even prevented this chain of events entirely. But there was no use dwelling on what-ifs. At the very least, the envoy’s life had been preserved, thanks to the golems’ autonomous protocols. Still, judging by Frederick’s condition, it would take months — if not longer — before he recovered even a fraction of his former strength. His survival would have to remain a secret; otherwise, the College’s assassins would surely come to finish what they started.

“Master, is something the matter?” Lillie asked, tilting her head curiously.

Victor shook his head and offered a faint smile. “Just some troublesome matters. Let’s call it a day and continue our experiment tomorrow.”

“Mm-hmm.” Lillie nodded with her usual enthusiasm, unaware of the growing storm brewing beyond the academy walls.

As Victor returned to his office, Lizbeth burst in from the adjoining bedroom, flying straight toward his face with a look of alarm.

“Master, something’s approaching the Sanctum!” she blurted. “A powerful presence, stronger than anything we’ve faced so far! It’s coming fast from the west!”

What?! Is it the enemy who almost killed Frederick? Victor creased his eyebrows in thought, but he felt that it was too soon.

His mana flared instinctively as he extended his senses outward, sweeping across the entire academy. A vast surge of Necro-elemental energy pulsed on the horizon — heavy, ancient, and unmistakably terrifyingly powerful. For a moment, he nearly unleashed his full power to intercept the intruder, but then he froze as he recognized the mana signature, as well as the alert given by the System, was all too familiar.

He exhaled softly and shook his head. “No… It’s not an enemy.”

Lizbeth blinked, confused. “It’s not?”

Victor’s lips curved into a faint, knowing smile. “No. That’s—”

Before he could finish, a spooky, green-skinned ghost materialized beside them, wearing an apron and an oversized chef’s hat. She raised her elongated arms dramatically, her distorted face twisting as she let out a playful wail at them both. “Boo!”

“Eeeek!!” Lizbeth squealed, her wings flaring as foam gathered at the corners of her mouth. She dove behind Victor, trembling violently. “A scary ghost!”

“—Valeria,” Victor finished his sentence, unamused by her antics.

“Well, well, well. What have we here?” Valeria grinned broadly, her face morphing back to normal as the eerie ghostly glow faded. “The headmaster and his little fairy. Look who’s finally decided to move into your charming little academy.”

“She’s… she’s back!” Lizbeth’s eyes widened as she peeked out from behind Victor, still visibly shaken. “Y-You scared me half to death! Geez. What’s with that entrance?”

Valeria crossed her arms, smirking. “If you can’t handle a little ghost prank, you wouldn’t survive five minutes in the Bone Plane.”

“Valeria,” Victor greeted calmly. “Good timing. I actually need your help with something.”

Valeria placed her hands on her hips, tilting her head with a mischievous glint in her crimson eyes. “Uh-huh. Sureee. What is it? No, wait, let me guess. I already know what you’re about to ask.”

“Yes, it’s about the cu—”

“My cooking service isn’t free of charge,” she interrupted swiftly, wagging a finger. “I’m losing a lot if I have to cook for you without compensation. Hmm… or are we talking about something else?”

“I’m talking about the curse,” Victor said, partially releasing the seal that restrained his power. Black tendrils of corruption slithered from beneath his right hand’s skin, writhing in the air like living things.

Valeria blinked, her expression twisting into one of disgust as she studied it. “Ugh… no wonder I kept sensing that foul stench clinging to you. From the looks of it, that’s the Soulrot Curse, isn’t it?”

“Correct.” Victor nodded. “Can you dispel it with your power?”

Valeria was a powerful ancient Magus, her strength surpassing even the Sigil Conversion rank. Her knowledge was equally vast, as she recognized the curse with just a single glance, a feat Victor himself couldn’t have accomplished without combing the grand library inside the citadel. If even she couldn’t do anything, then Victor had no choice but to continue his current experiments with Lillie and hope for a breakthrough.

Valeria’s expression turned serious as he pondered for a moment before replying, “Hmm… dispelling a curse born from an eldritch being isn’t as simple as having another equally powerful entity remove it. Perhaps if we were within the Bone Plane, it might be possible to dispel it by borrowing the plane’s power, but you’re not a native there, so that’s out of the question.”

“So… not even you can lift this curse…” Victor sighed dejectedly, full of disappointment. There went his hope of resolving this issue easily.

“Don’t be so down,” Valeria said with a mischievous smirk. “While completely dispelling it is impossible without some equally queer and elusive power like dreamforce, or perhaps an even higher authority such as the power of laws, I just so happen to know a way to suppress it. To make it so that you can unleash your full power without the curse progressing at all. Of course, it’s a bit dangerous. So, I wouldn’t recommend it unless you are at the end of the road.”

“I see…” Huh, did she just say that dreamforce could resolve this?

The implication lingered in his mind like a spark. It was the very energy he had been experimenting with through Lillie — the same force that transcended elemental classification, bridging dreams, reality, and consciousness. If what Valeria said was true, then the key to breaking the Soulrot Curse might already be within his reach.

But he decided to keep that thought to himself for now. “Tell me more about your method,” he said instead.

Valeria placed a finger on her lips as she looked upward thoughtfully. “It’s something I learned — or rather, stole — from a dying Necro Lord in the Bone Plane. A sealing technique that binds the portion of one’s soul contaminated by the curse to a stabilizing anchor or medium. It doesn’t destroy the curse, but it traps it in a loop of false equilibrium. The curse still exists, still festers… but it can no longer spread or feed on your essence.”

“Seems a bit risky…” Sealing away a part of one’s soul meant severing it entirely — it was no different from losing a piece of oneself. Essentially, it was like amputating a decaying limb to save the rest.

“Because it is,” Valeria admitted. “If done improperly, the curse will merge completely with your soul instead of being sealed. And instead of killing you, it’ll turn you into something even worse than those eldritch spawn you’ve read about. Also, I should mention that the process does give excruciating pain — worse than anything a human could possibly endure.”

“Oof.”

Valeria chuckled softly at his reaction. “But if done properly, you’ll regain your full power and won’t have to worry about the curse eating away at you for a long, long time. Long enough, perhaps, to find a permanent solution.”

“Yeah… let’s not go down that path unless I’m absolutely forced to,” Victor replied wryly.

“Well, your choice.” She shrugged. “I’m just looking out for you. After all, if you die, who else is going to provide me with cooking ingredients and guinea pigs for testing my new recipes?”

Victor sighed, half exasperated, half amused. “Only you would think of cooking jokes during a conversation about curses.”

“Hehe, that’s me. One and only,” she replied with a wink.

“Oh, right. There’s one more matter you need to know about,” Victor said as he brought her up to speed with the current state of the academy and the impending confrontation with the Arcane Radiance College and the surrounding Magus forces.

Valeria listened quietly, and when he finished, she nodded. “Noted. I’ll uphold my promise to protect your academy. My power is still limited here in Seraphia, so I can’t leave the Sanctum to deal with those arrogant kids hiding behind the Arcane Radiance College. But if they dare to bring the fight here…”

A faint, predatory smile appeared on her face. “Vanquishing those Sigil Conversion Magi would be like squashing bugs.”

With Victor’s strongest ally, Valeria, having returned from a plane of death itself, he felt the lingering tension in his chest ease. If he had to leave the academy for an extended period, he could do so without worry. Should the enemy dare to set foot upon the Sanctum’s grounds, they would find themselves swiftly and brutally crushed by her hand, reduced to ashes before they even realized their mistake.

With nothing else to discuss, Valeria said goodbye and headed off toward the Magus Tower to settle in.

At last, Lizbeth crawled out from behind Victor and let out a relieved sigh. “Phew… she’s finally gone.”

“Ha, why are you so scared of a little ghost? I’ve never heard of a fairy being such a scaredy-cat,” Victor teased.

“Hmph, I’m not scared! I was just startled, that’s all.” She crossed her arms, puffed out her cheeks, and turned her head away like an angry little child.

Shaking his head in amusement at the sight, Victor activated his Master Shadowlink Mark and opened the academy’s main tab to check on its current progress.

Nine days after the academy had upgraded to level six, nine hundred new players had joined its ranks. With Valeria’s arrival, the requirement for one dean was finally fulfilled. At this rate, in another month and a half, the academy would likely level up again.

Victor could hardly wait for that moment. But for now, his priority remained finding a way to undo the curse—

“Uh-oh, Master…” Lizbeth’s voice broke his train of thought.

He turned to her. “Is something wrong?”

“I think the new envoy is here… and the players are confronting her. It’s already turning into a fight.”

“…What?”
