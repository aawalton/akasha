“I’ll go in first. Give me a few minutes, then follow me in.”

“Wilco. Five minute delay.”

The Fracture itself is partially submerged, and I slowly march my way inside. The mud and peat that makes up the bottom of the marsh pulls at me with every step. Only the cloak Morgan made for me keeps me from sinking in. I step into the Fracture, and I fall into the marsh with a splash.

The water is crystal clear, and I see small fish of a dozen different species darting among the huge roots that hold up the towering trees. We’re lucky that the trees’ supernatural size keeps them away from each other. The ACVs should have enough space to maneuver without too much difficulty.

Looking around, I don’t see any immediate threats, though I do move out of the way. I don’t want Second Squad to run into me. When the ACVs are swimming, most of their height is below the water. They’d hit me, and none of us want to figure out who’d win a fist fight: me, or the vehicle. We all know the answer, but I’d rather it not confirmed with empirical evidence.

Nothing tries to attack me, at least at the moment. A few nosy fish and a normal-sized watersnake with more courage than sense are all that bother me. I flick at the snake and it hurriedly swims away. After five minutes, the convoy of armored vehicles comes through the Fracture.

Unlike for me, their entry is much smoother, and they fan out upon entry, their sensor systems swiveling as they search for threats.

“Tom, you there? Can you hear me?”

The magical nature of the Fracture has a tendency to interfere with radio for some reason, which messes with radar systems incidentally. More importantly, it can mess with long range communications. Throwing enough power at the transmitter can overcome that limitation though. Vehicle-based comms have enough power to break through, but infantry radios are often too scrambled to go more than a few hundred yards and still be intelligible.

“We can hear you, loud and clear. We’re ready to get a move on.”

I can actually hear Eric’s leg jittering in his seat, hammering a staccato tap-tap-tap against the hull.

“Eric, I can hear you out here,” I tell him. “If I can, something else probably can, too.”

“Shit, sorry.” He stops.

“Alright, follow my lead. Keep an eye out for crystals or anything funny looking. Remember, this is a smash and grab. Science is taking a back seat for now.”

“Wilco,” Tom acknowledges.

A squadron of automated drones weave inbetween the swimming vehicles. The forward team was happy to let us have them. They had been pushed out of the back of a few Chinook helicopters and loading them back up would have been nightmarish.

The drones spread out in front and around us by my command. Hopefully they’ll give us early warning on any monsters, or spot something valuable. Their automated systems should help mitigate the jamming effect Fractures have, and if necessary I can throw enough power at my internal transmitter to burn through it anyway. Just have to be careful not to microwave anyone.

I clamber up a giant Cypress tree, one that would easily rival the largest redwoods of California. Rough bark creaks and groans as I haul myself up and out of the water. Air plants, flowering bushes that would be the size of smaller trees in a more normal place dot the trunks of the Cypress trees. Spanish moss covers their branches, shading the water below.

A few members of Second Squad are half out of the top ACV hatches, scanning the flooded forest. I can hear birds chirping, though I doubt anyone else can. The ACVs’ roaring engines are likely swallowing up any noise any of them could possibly hear.

Sergeant Callisto eyes the water line with suspicion, though when it comes to Fractures that’s her default state. For good reason, too. Fractures are extremely dangerous for Typicals. Cat-3s to such a ludicrous degree I’m surprised they came along. Heavy weapons and anti-tank missiles seriously tip the scales, though.

“Hey, Mach,” she whispers over the radio.”Do you see that thing on my eleven o’clock? Forty meters, give or take. On the water line.”

I lean away from the tree to get a better look, and the Cypress groans.

“Just a log, Sergeant. Still clear. Tom, we need to go deeper into the Cypress dome. The water will be deeper the closer we get to the center, and I hope we’ll be more likely to find something valuable.”

“Roger, sir.”

The convoy changes direction at my command, and I drop back down into the water to follow them. The trees are far too weak and flexible for me to leap between them. Even at their size with magical durability on top, I’m sure they’d snap or drop debris on the heads of Second Squad. They’d certainly make a racket.

The bed of the marsh is a mat of dropped Cypress needles, mud, and sunken logs. It crunches and squelches under my boots as I hurry to take the lead again. The propellers that power the ACVs furiously churn the water above me. The woosh-woosh-woosh noise they make almost sounds like chanting. Sticks and moss caught in their unrelenting blades are ground up and spat out with the bubbles and foam.

A bird swoops for the flotsam behind the convoy, probably seeing lunch. It’s so odd to see the Fractures replicate the immense complexities of an ecosystem. Especially so since they sometime fail with the most simplistic of structures. There seems to be little rhyme or reason to when a Fracture is accurate to when it’s dreamlike. Maybe I just don’t know enough about Cypress domes to tell the difference? Reading a few dozen research papers an expert does not make.

“I still don’t see anything, Sarge,” Eric reports.

“Neither do I,” I say.

“That’s… good. Right? No, wait, that’s bad. I think.”

I hear a mighty chuckle from Thor. He’s also scanning the Fracture from outside the hull. He has his massive sniper rifle laying on the topside armor, ready for use. I can hear it clattering against the ACV as the vehicle bobs in its tireless charge through the water.

Tom’s lead vehicle nudges floating moss and plants out of the way as we make our way deeper into the Cypress dome. The water gets deeper and the spongy matt of needles grows thicker. The trees get taller which darkens the water. Long shadows start to dominate the forest, and Second Squad grows more nervous. Their eyes snap from dark corner to dark corner. Any one of them could hide a lurking monster, and in a Cat-3, it could tear apart an ACV apart like tissue paper.

I hear a faint splashing noise, which draws my attention. As I move around a tree, I find a huge snake swimming on the surface towards Second Squad’s convoy.

“Contact,” I report. “A Giant Cottonmouth water snake.”

It’s thirty feet long, which is as long as the ACVs themselves. I jump up just high enough to grab it and pull it under the water. It squirms in my hand. With it’s superior size it might unbalance me given the time to do that. I crush its neck, killing it instantly. I leave it behind. It’s too big to store in one piece, and I’d rather not attract anything by butchering it into smaller chunks.

“Where is it? I don’t see it.” Tom says.

“Dead, I killed it.”

The body floats to the surface.

“I’m leaving it behind. If I butcher it, it might attract something else. Let’s keep going.”

“This place is huge,” Eric mutters half an hour later.

“Approximately fourteen miles across,” I tell him. ”It’s hard to say for sure, distances get a little messy the closer you get to the edge of the Fracture.”

“And hot, holy hell.”

“It is quite humid,” Thor agrees. “It is not so bad, I think.”

“I don’t know how you’re not sweating buckets, you’re wearing the same gear as I am,” Eric grumbles.

“Perhaps I am just more acclimated—do you see that?”

Both myself and apparently Thor, somehow, notice a swarm of more Giant Cottonmouths swimming towards us at the same time. They burst out of dark crevices made by fallen logs, still living trees, and shrubbery. A few even drop down from Air plants to splash in the water. All of them are hungry.

“Fire at will!“ Tom shouts. Callisto, Thor, and Eric all duck inside the ACVs, slamming the hatches shut behind them. The 30mm autocannons start thundering out hate at two hundred rounds a minute. My sword forms with a boom that rocks the ACVs, though I doubt they notice. They’re already shaking violently in the water.

I start off slowly marching towards the monsters, but I keep speeding up. I swipe upwards, catching three snakes in a single strike. Blood fills the water. 30mm rounds slam into another, and take its head off. The experiments with infused armor-piercing rounds has paid off. They might be in extremely limited supply, but the National Guard coughed up enough for a full load.

Despite the withering fire, for every snake that goes down, another takes its place. Some of the 30mm rounds that miss skip off the water with a bwwm. They blast chunks out of trees down range. I move like a living blender, carving up multiple snakes with each strike. The water becomes clouded with gore.

Tom lets loose with a Javelin ATGM , and it blows a snake in half. The boom echoes throughout the forest. The blast wave slams into me, but I don’t slow down in the slightest. I kill and fight and kill and fight. One snake gets close to the rear vehicle, and underneath the autocannon’s maximum depression. The hatch flies open, Thor squeezing out with his huge sniper rifle.

The snake lunges for him, venom dripping off bared arm-length fangs.

Boom.

The first .50 caliber round crashes through a fang and flies straight down the snake’s gullet. It blows through the back of the throat with a burst of blood. A violent hiss filled with the promise of an agonizing death becomes the panicked gurgle of a dying animal. With its spine cut, it becomes a dumb missile. It slams into the ACV, which rocks dangerously to one side.

The ACVs prove their worth, and it just barely manages to recover. Thor doesn’t stop with just his first kill. His sniper rifle barks again and again. Rounds slam into open mouths bared wide and carve into eye sockets. The 30mm next to him barks out again, and though Thor winces, he keeps firing.

I leap off a submerged root and breach the surface with a splash. In just a few seconds, I slice a dozen times. The air screams in protest with each attack, and the snakes fall silent, carved into tiny pieces. Armor-piercing rounds slam into my armor. Some explode into shrapnel that peppers the few remaining snakes, other skip off with a twang and disappear into the forest. Milliseconds later I can hear the crack of them taking a chunk out of a tree far in the distance.

Falling down, I stomp on the last watersnake, shattering its spine. I drag it under the water, and my weight crushes it into the muddy lakebed. Bubbles and blood pour from its mouth as it hisses in agony. It dies a moment later when my sword plunges through its skull.

Finally the forest falls silent. We killed over a hundred snakes, and the water is crowded with their bodies. The stench of death fills the air. Thor wrinkles his nose before retreating back inside his ACV.

Tom calls for an ammo check, and the results are grim. The single engagement consumed a little over half of the ammo the squad brought along. Of the twelve hundred rounds between the three vehicles, they now only have six hundred or so. Tom, commanding the lead vehicle, is the worst off. They only have a hundred and fifty of their initial four hundred.

“How many more do you think there are?” He asks me. “We can only afford maybe one more engagement of that size. If that happens, we’ll be dry on ammo, and won’t be able to help much with the boss.”

“Not sure, the drones initially spotted alligators, but I haven’t seen any yet. That was a lot of monsters though.”

“Are we continuing?“

“We’ve got to,” I tell him grimly.”I don’t know if what we’re looking for is here, but if it is we have to try. The stakes are too great to give up.”

“Roger, Second Squad, Charlie Mike.”

“Tom, we should probably book it before something else comes looking.”

“Wilco. All vehicles, ahead full.”

I manage to store away a few of the smaller chunks of monster before I follow the rest of Second Squad. Maybe it’ll be enough to experiment with.

Even on the outer edges the Cypress trees rivaled the greatest of the California Redwood giants, but just a few miles in they’ve grown to truly fantastical sizes.

Each tree is wider than the ACVs are long, which is incredible. The largest tree I’ve ever seen was the General Sherman, a giant sequoia in California. It was twenty-five feet in diameter. The Cypresses here are just shy of forty. Not only that, but they’re taller, too. From the base under the water—which is just over twenty feet deep at this stage—to as far as I can see. That would make them probably over three hundred feet tall. It’s hard to tell, as the Fracture’s boundary is probably around the tops of the trees themselves.

“Wow,” Eric says, looking at the truly jaw-dropping scenery. “I don’t think I’ve ever seen trees this large.”

“You probably won’t see larger. These are even bigger than the largest redwoods in California. Trust me, I’ve seen them. These are the largest trees ever by volume.”

“If taken, these could easily fix the lumber shortage.” Thor says.

“I’m not sure they’d even fit through Fracture’s exit,” I admit. “Not to mention just how difficult logging in twenty feet of monster-infested water would be.”

“A common problem with Fractures. They hold such wealth in terms of material value, but most of it is near impossible for us to truly extract.”

“One day we’ll figure out how to strip Fractures for all they’re worth. I’m sure of it.”
