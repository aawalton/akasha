Don’s break was brief, but it was well needed. He grunted and got up and used Devour on the Snarlak.

[200 Evolution Points acquired!]

[Evolution trait “Boss”: Consumed]

[Health at 100%]

[Energy at 100%]

[Debuffs: N/A]

Boss, huh?

That was interesting. Don wondered if he had gotten one of these from the Grimoid King. He had used Drain Life to take its life essence, but it was basically the same thing, right? Drain Life was a variation of Devour. His psychic abilities gave it range, but it was a slow drip, instead of an instant thing.

Well, either way, he at least had one ‘Boss’ trait consumption under his belt.

Don wondered whether there was still more dungeon to explore, or if there was only one thing left to do.. Morshoff…

He pushed the swamp horror from his mind and spent his Free Attribute Points.

Don put one point in Energy. He needed it more and more if he was to integrate his psychic abilities into his combat style. Right now the psychics drained him fast. While Devour could keep him topped up, a protracted fight might spell trouble. Better to be wise.

And that puts it at a nice round 10. That totally didn’t affect my decision making…

Since he had two points left, he figured he’d use one for Agility and one for Power. Those two were his primary Attributes.

Then he turned towards the boss. The cracked exoskeleton had turned gray from the Devour. The cheeks had sunken and the mighty bat demon looked old and frail. Don wanted its speed. He had almost been able to keep up with the absolutely monstrous speed Snarlak had been capable of. He figured if he had like ten, maybe fifteen more points in Agility, he could have moved as a blur.

Don reviewed the fight. He had performed pretty well. While Snarlak had been fast, it didn’t have the stamina needed for such a long fight. Don had worn it down. In retrospect it had slowed down in the end.

It made sense. Snarlak had been a dive bombing ambusher type. The boss had had great speed and offensive ability. But when something could take a punch and stay in the fight, the boss had been less threatening.

Still, Don needed to improve. His mind scanned over his moves and decisions.

There was the case of learning further Telekinesis. Don was good at using it as an augmentor for his physical abilities. It worked especially well when he could use the psychics as a burst. In a single boosting pulse, like when he made a jump or threw something.

But Don wanted to learn to use it constantly. Like a power armor, that could augment all your movement. Obviously such a thing was unsustainable with its massive Energy drain, but he could surely figure a workaround.

Of course there was also the case of Drain Life. It was an excellent ability. Don wouldn’t have had aforementioned staying power without it. The boss had used devastating power and some of the hits had been direct. He would have died, or at least risked going into berserk mode without the new ability.

It was useful, but he would need to master it further. Don could feel that both the range and the throughput of the ability could be improved. It would grant him a massive tactical advantage in a pseudo-ranged weapon. In fights against superior physiques, he could keep the drain active and dance around.

Don resolved to focus on practicing Drain Life when he got some downtime.

Well, downtime wasn’t really an option, but rest on the other hand… Rest wasn’t optional. He would need to rest soon.

Don had no idea how long he had been going, but he had been fighting for hours.

Don had watched professional boxing sometimes on TV. If those guys went the distance, they would fight half an hour and look like absolute death. Don couldn’t blame them. Fighting was fucking hard. The intensity and effort required was almost always at a maximum. That took both a physical and a mental toll.

But Don did have a vastly superior physique to a pre-System human. He was becoming more and more of a monster by old standards. But by the new standards, he was still small fry, he knew this.

There was an ocean between him and Mal’asha. But that was an ocean Don was eager to cross. Unfortunately tactical rests were part of an efficient strategy.

Don compromised and decided to rest a little bit. Just an hour or so.

So he took a nap, then and there.

Maybe it was an hour, maybe two. Whatever it was, it was short but effective. Don could feel he was again ready to fight some more. Not for a full day, but definitely for several hours.

This means I can be fully ready even if it’s Morshoff I have to face.

Don was still feeling some trepidation at the idea of fighting the swamp horror. But he was quite a bit stronger now since he had entered the dungeon.

It was also possible Snarlak had something to deal with with the swamp horror. Don took a glance, and was pleased to see there was a shining box in the remains of the boss.

It was time for some fat loots.

He noticed something interesting. There was a shiny box embedded in the giant corpse. It hummed faintly and looked like it was made from pure diamond. Like a footlocker with a half-pipe cover. A chest. A dungeon chest, for killing a boss.

Don stepped closer and put his hand on the surface.

The moment he touched it, three items materialized in front of him. A folded cloak, a dark fang hanging on a chain, and a flat metal case the size of a brick.

He went for the cloak first.

[Cloak of the Echo Predator] +3 Constitution +2 Agility +1 in Stealth SkillA skill buff, huh? That’s new.

It was a good item. The stats were exactly what he needed and the Stealth increase was welcome. Sure to come in handy. Not to mention the cloak would go well with his black leather pants.

Don ran a hand over it. The outside was a matte, scale-like texture. Tough as boiled leather, but flexible. The underside was smooth, almost warm. He swung it over his shoulders and fastened it with the simple clasp at the collar.

It settled across his back like it belonged there. Balanced. Light. But it pulled close when he crouched, like it knew what he was trying to do. That was nice. Might keep him alive later.

Next up was the necklace.

[Fang of the Black Scream] +3 Power +2 Mental Unique Passive: Predator’s Alertness— Grants a temporary boost of +5 to Mental when out of combat.

Holy shit.

That was a lot of stats. Don could really appreciate the passive. It was powerful. Sure it wasn’t available in combat, in which, Don admittedly spent a lot of his time in. But it would help him avoid getting ambushed and he would be better at picking up environmental details when preparing for a fight.

As a bonus it would also make his psychics more powerful when out of combat.

Powerful.

Don examined the necklace.

It was simple. A chain of dull black iron, holding a curved fang the length of a thumb. The etching glowed faintly when Don held it up.

He slipped it over his neck. Cold at first, then warm. He felt the difference almost instantly. His thoughts sharpened, just a bit. A quiet focus settled in the back of his mind. No noise. No clutter.

He nodded once.

Last, he picked up the metal case. It clicked open, revealing three dark gray spheres nestled in black foam.

[Sonic Disruptor Bombs]

The name was simple, but there was a long description.

Single-use thrown item. Detonates in a high-frequency shockwave that pierces thick organic tissue. Ignores armor. Effective against enemies with regenerative or amorphous physiology.Developed by the ancestors of Snarlak, when their species had not devolved into mindless beasts. The bat people, Shila’ari, were integrated into the Multiverse eons ago. Instead of prospering, they regressed. While some of their species were able to evolve, most became beasts. The last of the ancestors was consumed by Morshoff. This is their vengeance.

Don whistled, low. He’d never seen anything like them. Heavy for their size. There was a simple red trigger line running across the top of each sphere. No fuse. No countdown. You pull it, you throw it, it detonates.

Don grinned. He figured Morshoff probably didn’t know how to make a vacuum around its head to block sonic waves.

He tucked the bombs into his satchel and closed the case. He could only hope they'd do what they were made for. But it was something. A chance. Better than nothing.

Don stood up, cloak shifting at his shoulders. He took a breath and checked his gear one last time. New cloak. New necklace. New tools. He was in better shape than when he walked into this hellhole.

He glanced once more at Snarlak’s corpse. Nothing left now but scorched ice and ruin.

“Thanks for the gear,” he muttered. “And the lesson.”

Don looked around and waited. No trap doors opened. No hidden passageways appeared. This was it. The end of the dungeon.

He turned away and started heading down from the spire. It was time for one more pit stop before leaving the dungeon.
