Their first stop was at Greta Kaufmann’s hotel. It was a quick one too. She had simply intended to get her things and check out. After that, she urged Kang Do-Won to help her to the airport on the basis that she intended to ask Merlin some questions and it was safer for their driver to be someone affiliated with the Consortium Guild than otherwise. She also emphasised the point that her interrogation of him would still continue on eventually, but, at the moment, Merlin was of greater importance.

The part about Greta interrogating Kang Do-Won caught Merlin off-guard, but considering he was not exactly in the right phase to ask questions not concerning his situation he kept his mouth shut.

The sky was a lot darker now as the sun had vanished in the horizon and night was just around the corner, but Merlin’s mind was still plagued with the exact same fright it had had an hour or so ago. He occasionally closed his eyes, wishing that when he opened them again he would awaken from a bad dream. But reality wasn’t so kind to him. It was real. It was all real. And it was high time he surrendered to facts and not fiction. Enough of being a reader who lost himself to fantasies. It was time to face reality.

“Begin,” said Greta as she concluded all her calls and the messages she was sending; she was finally settled.

Merlin turned to her; all he was met with was her side profile. Her eyes were fixed on the road, watching calmly as Do-Won drove past cars as though in a hurry to get to the airport.

Merlin had no clue which country they were headed to, and he was too frightened to ask. In fact, he wondered just how he was to get through the border without his passport with him, but he came to the conclusion that the Council of Mages definitely had their ways.

Deciding not to keep Greta Kaufmann waiting for an answer any longer than he already had, he turned away and said, “I have no idea.”

“Everyone always says that at first,” she replied. “Okay. I’ll give you five seconds. You can tell me the real answer then.”

Merlin’s brows twitched, but he fought back the urge he had to glance at her and frown.

Her lackadaisical reaction frightened him. She instantly came across as the type to torture him in some sort of magical way if he insisted he had no idea as to the changes occurring within him.

But what else was he to say? There was no way he could tell her about the System. That would only worsen his situation.

Goodness. What was this dilemma he was in?

“Your time is over,” Greta reminded him as she crossed her legs and folded her arms. “Now, please cooperate.”

Merlin pressed his lips together as several lies rushed through his head in half a second. In his desperation, he settled on that which he believed made the most sense to him. He could only hope that it would be the same for Greta.

“It started after the headmaster’s maid told me about attunement,” Merlin began. “Apparently it’s a second year course and I wasn’t supposed to be—”

“Don’t joke with me, Merlin,” Greta cut in as she turned her gaze to Merlin; there was a frightening glint in her eyes, forcing Merlin to shudder and reconsider his lie.

But he had started on this path already, and he was not stopping now because of some pressure. And, besides, he had nothing better to say.

“I’m not lying,” said Merlin. “If you don’t plan to believe anything I say, then to hell with this. Do whatever you want.”

Greta raised a brow; after a second or two, she turned her attention back to the road.

“Go on,” she said.

Merlin silently exhaled in relief and continued, “I’ve always been a big fan of meditation, and make sure to partake in it at least five minutes every day. I was doing just that in the headmaster’s personal gym when Maid Eun-Wol met me. She mentioned how my meditation was in alignment with attunement, and gave me tips on how to further strengthen my knowledge of it. Ever since then, I’ve been practicing meditation even more intensely, but instead of focusing on the peace it brought, I shifted my focus to the small traces of mana present in my body.

“I’ll be honest, I have no clue what exactly changed in my body. I have no clue why even. But I felt my mana become stronger. That is all I know. Why was I able to cast magic? Don’t ask me. I am just as clueless as the next guy. And, truth be told, just as frightened too. I’ve always wanted to be able to shoot fireballs out of my hands for as long as I can remember, and now the fact that I’d done that scares me. Because I don’t know what’s happening to me.”

Silence draped over the car for a couple of seconds.

“Casting magic is one thing,” Greta Kaufmann began. “But being able to cast two different spells, without a Grimoire is a complex scenario that goes beyond any understanding.” She turned to Merlin. “Attunement is a part of magic that has been present since the Cataclysm. Whatever’s happening with you is not an effect of that. A Deficient Mage doesn’t just gain the ability to nullify magic out of the blue, and then suddenly has the ability to use varying types of magic without a Grimoire. This might sound a tad insensitive, but understanding what’s going on with your body will go a long way for the world of magic and in curbing whatever evil might plague our world in the future. Do you understand?”

That was a bitter pill to swallow.

What about him? Was he not important? His life, his future, did that all not matter?

He had already promised to give his life to prevent the Cataclysm from happening again, and to die fighting if it did; was that not enough?

Merlin pressed his lips together.

“What will happen to me?” he asked.

Greta stared straight into his eyes and shut hers. “That’s for the council to decide.”

Merlin nodded and turned away. Perhaps it was time to accept his fate and just hope that Professor Jung was right about the headmaster having a plan.

The car suddenly jerked into an abrupt stop on a lonely road as a loud screech sounded from beneath, echoing through the inside. Merlin and Greta were thrown forward, only held back by their seatbelts preventing them from slamming their foreheads into the front seats.

“What’s going on?!” Greta voiced as she peeked from behind Do-Won who was breathing heavily.

Merlin did just as the lady, his heart pounding heavily in his chest, as he stared through the windscreen.

The sky had long gone dull, and darkness had descended upon the road, but with Do-Won’s vehicle's light blazing through the night, he was able to make out the figures standing before a black motorcycle just a few ways away from the car. And both of those figures he recognized.

One was a lady dressed from head to toe in black leather which was accentuated with straps and belts. She had a lithe shape Merlin couldn’t forget, and black short hair. For the first time, however, he was seeing her face. She looked to be in her twenties with an oval shaped face and her lips colored purple, just like her eyeshadows. Her ears were filled with piercings, and all her features combined came together to give her a beautiful but terrifying goth-like look.

As for her companion, Merlin had never seen his face before, but his body instinctively remembered him to be the man who had paid him a visit in the academy. He was, like the lady, dressed in black. His, however, was a leather jacket and wide pants. He had a calm expression, with narrow eyes that made it seem like he was bored of everything. His hair was black and he had a lean build. But what made Merlin’s eyebrows jump up was that the lad looked very young. Merlin couldn’t help but think that he was still a teenager.

Greta Kaufmann realized that the situation had taken a drastic turn. Immediately, she reached for her handbag and retrieved her Grimoire. It was dark red, signifying that she was an A-Class Mage, and had the symbol for air etched into its cover.

She stepped out of the car.

Do-Won reached for his Grimoire as well; it was the same color as Greta’s, but the symbol etched into it was of a solid, singular shape that Merlin interpreted as the structure for bone.

The man opened the door of his car, stepping out, but opted to tell Merlin something before he did. However, Merlin had already jumped out of the car.

Seeing the two present with him spring to action, Merlin had decided that it would certainly be stupid of him to remain hidden. So he had made a move. And besides, he had a nagging feeling at the back of his neck, considering his interaction with the two people on the opposite side of Do-Won’s vehicle, that they were here for him.

“Whoever you are, whatever you want, I suggest that you move aside!” Greta Kaufmann voiced through the night. When their ambushers didn’t say anything, she channeled her mana. Her Grimoire flipped open, and the symbol of air on its cover glowed. “I’m an agent of the Council of Mages. I repeat, and for the last time, whoever—”

Her words were never finished. One second she was speaking, the next Greta’s brains were raining down upon Do-Won’s vehicle and the road.

Her head had exploded.

Merlin took a deep breath, shaken by the sight, as he stumbled away from the car.

Greta Kaufmann’s Grimoire dropped to the ground just as her body fell down like a log of wood; blood spilled out of where her head used to be, painting the road red.

Do-Won froze at the sight and so did Merlin.

They both glanced at the two people across, noticing the blue shimmer in the eyes of the man.

Then Do-Won frowned as his gaze rested upon the lady.

“You,” he muttered. “From Club Spiral.”

The lady clicked her tongue. “See,” she said to the man standing just a few steps ahead of her. “I told you I should have hidden my face.”

The man waved her away. “When have you ever hidden it? It doesn’t matter now anyway. We’ve moved on to the final phase.” Then his gaze shifted towards Merlin with utter disregard of Do-Won. A smile appeared on his lips. “Come, Merlin. It’s time for us to leave.”

Merlin’s stomach churned as the display of Greta Kaufmann’s head exploding played in his head over and over again.

He had just witnessed an A-Class Mage die without as much as a struggle. It was like she was an ant stepped upon by a giant. There was no way he could escape these people, and if he tried to resist, perhaps Do-Won would die too.

Merlin bit his lower lip.

“What do you want with me?”

The man dipped his hand into his pocket and shifted forward.

“I told you,” he said. “I want to speak with you.”

Merlin stole a glance at Do-Won. The man’s muscles were tense, but he was not channeling his mana. He was scared.

“Then talk,” said Merlin. “I’m listening.”

The young man raised a brow, then glanced around and sighed.

“Not the optimal location. We have to leave. It’s not like you’ll be returning to a normal life ever again, so what’s the point in dawdling here or trying to prove stubborn?”

Merlin sucked in a deep breath, realizing that the man’s words were true.

The Council of Mages wanted him, Greta Kaufmann was dead, and these people, who he was certain were members of the mysterious organization Professor Jung referred to as the Blackguards, wanted him too. He was like a prize sought by all pirates.

It was a mess. This was a complete mess.

He couldn’t go with them, but he couldn’t tell them that. What if they killed Do-Won? No. What if they killed him?

Fuck!

“He’s not going anywhere with you!” Do-Won voiced and channeled his mana. Immediately, the man of the Blackguards raised his hand and his eyes glowed blue. Merlin’s heart skipped a beat.

“Stop!!” Merlin voiced.

However, to his relief, Do-Won’s head didn’t explode. The mana he had channeled simply dwindled drastically as if being sucked out of his body. It only took half a second for him to become completely exhausted as he fell to the floor, panting heavily.

“I’m not going to ask again, Merlin,” said the man. “Let’s go.”

Merlin gulped hard. He took one more glance at Do-Won and realized that he was completely lost here as to what to do.

No one was coming to save him; he would have to make the tough decision on his own.

Merlin took a deep breath and walked forward. Do-Won shook his head in disapproval, but Merlin turned his eyes away from him.

Several thoughts flashed through Merlin’s head as he approached the villains. They were filled with questions of what his life had become and how it would turn out from here on. Each step he took forward felt like he was pushing a boulder up a mountain. They got heavier and heavier. And before he knew it, he was just a few steps away from them. The lady was smiling, pleased with the development, while the man just stared, stoic, silent.

Then a loud screech split the sky apart.

Merlin broke from his reverie as his eyes turned upwards. In the distance, something approached quickly, sweeping through the air. No one would have guessed what it was, but Merlin knew that screech anytime, anywhere.

His eyes widened.

“Blue?”

The dragon appeared, a long body like that of a snake’s, with wings that carried him through the sky. He had not kept himself hidden, and he further made his presence known by breathing a blinding swirling blizzard directly from his lungs.

Blue’s attack slammed into the ground before Merlin, creating a thin wall of ice that separated him from his ambushers. Then the dragon landed beside Merlin and purred.

Merlin gulped hard. “What are you doing here?” he asked. Then he glanced at Do-Won who was staring at him in shock.

Before any other words could escape Merlin’s lips, the sound of an engine revving came from behind. He turned around to see a car zoom towards them, stopping just behind Do-Won’s vehicle.

The occupants alighted.

Sunny appeared from the driver’s seat, dressed in a leather jacket, and wearing a tense expression. From one side of the back seat emerged Nora in her tracksuit, her face tight with all the anger in the world; while from the other seat emerged someone Merlin had never seen before—at least personally.

It was a lady who looked to be in her mid-thirties with porcelain skin, pixie haircut, and an expression that turned nasty as soon as she set her eyes on the lifeless body of Greta Kaufmann.

Merlin had seen her on TV several times. She was Kim Haneul. The Guildmaster of the Hero Guild.

Her Grimoire flipped open and took to the sky as the air around her turned hectic.

Merlin shifted uncomfortably, trying to warn her not to try anything rash. She was a B-Class Mage, and an A-Class Mage had been swatted down like a fly.

But before he could utter a word, the barricade of ice Blue had created shattered into pieces.

“A dragon,” said the man as he walked out of the mist. “You keep making me fascinated, Merlin Tyrrell. Did you successfully tame a monster?” He chuckled gently.

Merlin shifted back slightly as he placed himself before Blue and everyone behind him.

“Stop it!” he voiced. “Don’t harm anyone else.”

“You have the power to put an end to that,” said the man. “Just come forward.”

Merlin pressed his lips together; he glanced at Sunny and Nora, taking in their expressions. Then he heaved out a deep exhale.

They had come all this way to rescue him. How could he get out of this without causing them any harm? Did he really have to give in to the Blackguards’ request? Nora would never let him be. She hadn’t even let him be with the Council of Mages, there was no way she would allow him to leave with a villain organization.

Just as Merlin’s head was filled with countless ideas on what to do, a gentle hand fell on his shoulder. He turned to his side and there stood Kim Haneul.

“Don’t make any stupid decisions,” she said. “Let the adults make them. All you have to do is remain a child.” Then she directed her attention to the Blackguards as the mist hiding their features cleared, specifically the man. “Right, Lee Jiho?”

Lee Jiho? Merlin’s brows fell.

The man who was referred to as Jiho chuckled.

“You really don’t forget a face, do you, noona?”

“I told you I didn’t,” Haneul replied with an unfriendly tone.

Merlin was lost, but he kept his mouth shut.

“Just hand the boy over,” Jiho said. “There’s no need for me to kill you. At least, not now.”

“Do you think you can?” Haneul replied coldly.

Jiho stared at her, his expression turning grim. Haneul raised a brow as if to tell him to dare.

She was a B-Class Mage, but for some reason this man, Jiho, feared her. He had not even blinked at the thought of killing an A-Class Mage just some minutes ago. Was it Kim Haneul’s ability, or was it their familiarity?

The lady of the Blackguards stepped forward as if to take on the challenge, and so did Nora in response.

Merlin’s heart skipped a beat.

Jiho raised an arm, stopping his companion from doing anything.

“We’ll take it from here another day,” he said. Then he glanced at the dead body of Greta Kaufmann, a vile expression replacing the calm one he was wearing a second ago. He turned that vile expression towards Haneul and the rest of them before settling it upon Merlin.

“We are meant to be together, Merlin,” he said. “We are two sides of the same coin, and I’ll make certain that you see that.”

Merlin swallowed dryly at those words.

In the next instant, the sky rumbled. There were no signs of rain previously, and suddenly it began to pour heavily. Landing on the broken ice from Blue’s barricade, mist spread out, blocking the Blackguards from the eyes of Merlin and the rest. When the mist cleared, they were nowhere to be found.

Haneul breathed out in relief as she hunched over with a grin, the tight expression she’d been wearing disappearing just like the mist.

“Gosh, that almost took my heart out, heh.”

Merlin’s brows twitched as the rain slowly petered out.

He didn’t know what to say. Everything still felt so surreal. So he turned around to rest in the arms of his sister, but was met with a resounding slap.

“What the fuck were you thinking?” Nora voiced. “You wanted to go with them?”

Merlin rubbed his cheek as he stared quietly at Nora. The slap didn’t hurt, he was just dazed by the fact that his Perception hadn’t notified him of what was coming.

He listened to her ramble on and on, pouring out all the complaints she had gathered in the past hour or so, and a smile spread his lips apart.

Nora paused.

“What’s so funny, nerd?” she badgered. “You almost died—”

The rest of her words caught in her mouth as Merlin hugged her.

“Thank you,” he said. “For coming after me.”

Nora was quiet for a moment then she scoffed.

“You owe me one.”

Merlin sighed and tightened his embrace across his sister. Until she pushed him away.

“That’s enough out of you,” she said with a harrumph.

Merlin turned to Sunny next and pressed his lips together.

The ginger-haired man waved whatever Merlin wanted to say away.

“Don’t sweat it. Just owe me like you owe that devil.”

Nora’s nose twitched but she kept silent.

Haneul, who had been watching the interaction silently, approached then.

“Do you understand the situation, Merlin?” she asked.

Merlin turned to her and nodded. “To an extent.” He looked at Greta’s lifeless body. “Now that they have killed a high ranking official, the Blackguards will be going all out with their plans.”

“And you are one of their plans,” she said. “You are also being sought out by the Council of Mages. And now that things are going to turn hectic around here, they’ll want you a lot more than they already do. What I’m saying is—”

“I’ve become a fugitive,” Merlin cut in and everyone fell silent. Blue rubbed his face against Merlin’s leg and purred, being the only one to make a sound for a very long second.

Haneul sighed. “Precisely. You will lose access to your family, friends, and everything that makes you you.” Merlin instinctively glanced at Nora to see if she was fine with that. Haneul added, “Headmaster Im Ilseong already explained the situation to her. She’s stubborn, but she’s not foolish. If anyone knows about your whereabouts, it’ll be easy for the Council of Mages to track you. Which is why I’ll take you away.” She turned to Do-Won who was now on his feet, listening in. “The rest of you, Mr. Do-Won will take you back to your places. Always be on guard from here on out. Understood?”

“Yes!” Sunny and Nora said in unison.

Then Merlin glanced at Blue. “And him?”

Haneul shook her head. “A tamed dragon brings too much attention. He’ll be with your sister.” She took a pause and glanced at her wristwatch. “Now that that’s all said and done, say your goodbyes.” She turned around and gave Merlin some space.

“Just wash the dishes when you’re done eating, okay?” said Nora. “Don’t go and become lazy just because I’m not there to caution you anymore.”

Merlin scoffed. “It’s not like you’ve been living with me in the dorms.”

“Chima and glasses have,” said Nora. “Whatever, just be safe, okay? I’ll tell mom and dad that you’re fine. God, they’re going to pester me so much.”

Merlin smiled and turned to Sunny.

“Yeah, same thing Nora said, kid,” Sunny said. “Ah, and don’t be so stubborn. Listen to your elders sometimes. Nah. Most of the time. Yeah, that’ll be better.”

Merlin nodded. “I will. Thank you once again for coming.”

“Pleasure.”

A thought flashed into Merlin’s head and he opened his lips to let it out, but immediately thought against it.

“Want me to pass a message for you?” Sunny asked just as Merlin was turning away. “To Janeth.”

As much as Merlin would have loved that, he shook his head.

“There’s no need,” he said. “Not like it matters anyway.” Then he leaned towards Blue and caressed the dragon’s chin. “Don’t be a dick to people, okay. Reserve that part of yourself for me. Freeze your own meat and don’t burn electricity. Also, remember to stay hidden always. I’ll let you off the hook this time because you saved me, but never again. Understood?”

Blue whined hesitantly, but Merlin would have none of that. He raised a finger, pouting, and the dragon finally heeded.

When that was done, Merlin stood up, took in a deep breath and nodded at himself. He walked up to Haneul where she was waiting for him at the car Sunny had driven and nodded at her.

“Is there anything you’ll need from your dorm? Besides your gadgets of course,” she asked.

Merlin mulled over it for a second and nodded. “Yes. A few.”

Haneul nodded. “List everything to me on the way, and I’ll get them for you. Get in,” she said as she hopped into the driver’s seat.

Merlin turned one last time to Nora, Sunny, Blue, and Do-Won, and waved them goodbye. Then his eyes landed on Greta’s headless body and his heart skipped a beat.

As he entered into the car and it zoomed off, he couldn’t help but think about how things would turn out.

However, one thing was certain. He had changed.
