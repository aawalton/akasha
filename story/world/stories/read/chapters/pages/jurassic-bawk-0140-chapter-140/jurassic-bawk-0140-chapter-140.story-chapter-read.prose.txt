Hyperborea was having a great time in the Delve.  Her Class, Special Abilities, and Skills were largely built around supporting a group, making her a very competent healer, tamer, and buffer.  Ordinarily, she would be at a disadvantage in a tournament-style competition, but the Elite System Games fortunately allowed her to bring her animal companion as a Squire.  This allowed her to bulldoze through most of the challenges that required brute force.  The Boss at the end of Floor One had given her the most difficulty, because she had been forced to fight alongside her companion against that Drago fellow.  She shuddered as she recalled how close she had come to being set on fire by the incredible heat emanating from the Boss’ sword.

To be fair, being on a boat was not really her cup of tea either.  As a Phytari, she preferred to remain on or in the ground most of the time.  The deck of her ship felt completely unnatural underroot, a vast departure from the lovely plain that made up Floor Two of the Delve.  The warm sunlight helped at least.

Turning her photoreceptors toward the fake sun and drawing in the real warmth and sustenance it provided, Hyperborea sighed.  She knew that she should feel more grateful to the System for the opportunity to be here.  She was incredibly lucky.  She had been born with the greatest nature affinity that her race had even seen, and then been well-cared for and trained her whole life.  Natural treasures, elixirs, training, tempering, she had been given everything that she could possibly need to thrive.  Not that she had been spoiled, far from it.  She had been carefully taught balance in all things, neither being given too much nor too little comfort.  Her caregivers had also instilled in her a sense of duty.  She had received incredible natural gifts, so it was only natural that much should be expected of her.

She had even been lucky inside the Delve.

On Floor One, she had come across an Item deep inside the Empress of the Forest’s territory that had increased her resistance to extreme temperatures, which was probably the only reason that she had survived her encounter with the Boss.  Then, on this Floor, she had started right next to an island with an unusual mana signature.  Upon clearing out the monsters that inhabited the sandy beach, a treasure chest housing a Life Fruit appeared.  Hyperborea had immediately consumed the powerful treasure and received a message from the System that, over time, it would increase her affinity to life-aspected mana.  Great balls of sap had run down from her photoreceptors at the news: perhaps she could use the insights that she gained to save her people from the plague that was slowly killing her world.  And, even if she couldn’t, perhaps she could make an alliance with a group powerful enough to solve the problem.  That was one of her goals here at the Elite System Games.

Even here in the Delve she was hoping to form some bonds.  The System’s wording on how to complete the Floor meant that, should she succeed in creating a strong coalition, she could help other Knights clear the Floor.  That would create goodwill she could later leverage into formal agreements to send researchers and healers to aid her people.

Shouts and the trampling of heavy boots brought her back to reality.  The sailors were in an uproar about something up in the sky.  Hyperborea, not having the greatest vision, couldn’t tell what they were getting so excited about.  Floopy, her six-legged animal companion that looked like a cross between a bear and a rhinoceros, suddenly appeared at her side.  He grunted as a flurry of emotions flooded through their empathic bond.  Hyperborea could only make out an impression of winged doom among the scattered thoughts and feelings.  One thing was clear, however.  Floopy was terrified.

Floopy was never terrified.

Not by Draco, not by the Empress of the Forest, and not even by the icy wizard that guarded the final stage of the Battle Simulation.

Was there a powerful Skill or Special Ability at play here?  Or had Floopy somehow become infected by the simulated sailors’ cowardice?

“To battle stations, you louts!” she heard the ship’s captain cry.  About half of the sailors responded, pulling out an array of cutlasses, sabers, and even a few epees.  The other half continued to panic.

Hyperborea activated one of her Skills, Calm Voice, using her mana to vibrate the air so that she could communicate with the crew.

“Everyone, listen to the captain’s orders!  Whatever it is, we can face it together!”

She then used a Special Ability, One of Us, to bolster the fighting prowess of the entire crew.  With that done, she began buffing Floopy in earnest, using Skill after Skill to bolster his Attributes, attack power, defenses, and rendering him immune to fear effects and mind control.  With that done, she tried to spot the incoming threat without success.

Which way is it coming from? she finally asked Floopy after having failed to spot anything.  But no sooner had the thought left her mind than her mana sense finally picked up something.  It was like a second sun was approaching, blazing through the sky at a speed that no creature she’d ever encountered could achieve.  Surely that wasn’t actually a creature, she told herself.  Perhaps it was some sort of ultra-advanced airship.  She didn’t have long to wonder, because the mana signature was headed straight toward her.  Moments later, she finally spotted it.

It was a bird.

The avian landed on the deck with a thud that reverberated through the entire ship.  The crew, which had largely held it together thanks to Hyperborea’s efforts, fled from the intruder.  Floopy’s grunts took on a new fervor, and he began to tremble uncontrollably.  Nevertheless, he remained by her side.

The bird looked around the ship but otherwise made no aggressive moves.  Hyperborea, who had much experience at gauging an animal’s intelligence by the way it observed the world, could tell that the creature was clearly intelligent.  Not that she needed this particular skill to make that assessment, since the avian was wearing a suit of armor.

It was another Knight.

Emboldened by the lack of aggression that the bird Knight had displayed, Hyperborea used another of her Skills, Verdant Growth, to grow a tiny pink flower from her trunk.  Using one of her prehensile roots, she plucked the flower and offered it to the bird as a gesture of goodwill.  While the other Knight was clearly intelligent, her Empathy Skill suggested that this non-verbal gesture would please the Knight.

It turned out that the Skill was correct, but not in the way that Hyperborea had hoped.  The bird blurred into motion.  Floopy bayed and leaped forward a moment later, but the Knight batted him away with ease.  Hyperborea stared at the place where her flower and the appendage holding it used to be.  The sound of her companion’s body hitting the water startled her into action, and she began to activate her most powerful healing ability.  She was too late.  Her mana sense caught the bird’s movement as its beak destroyed the central processing center located in her upper body.  By the time that she had processed that she had been killed, she had been removed from the Floor.  A single question rattled around inside her mind as she struggled to come to grips with the enormity of how badly she had just let her people down.

Why did he eat me?

Phatagin felt rather than saw Charlemagne return to the boat.  His Knight’s return brought a measure of relief.  Not because he was actually happy to see the rooster, but because he would finally be able to take a break.  As the pangolin unhitched himself from the rope that tied him to the ship, he wondered what had brought the rooster back to the ship so soon.  That was, until he saw what Charlemagne had returned with.  He quickly joined the others as the four real occupants of the boat examined the rooster’s booty.

“Wow, that was a good find!” Phasima said enthusiastically.

Josephine the Attendant tried to nod, but her complexion turned green as she did, causing her to run over to the railing to vomit over the side of the boat.  Phatagin was almost certain that Josephine was faking her symptoms in an attempt to get sent back to the castle, but he had no proof…yet.

“Bawk!” the rooster exclaimed with pride.

“I agree that you found a novel solution, Charlemagne,” he replied.  “Perhaps now we will be able to make full use of the ship’s capabilities.”

“Well, what are we waiting for, let’s see if we can get it to work,” Phasima suggested.  “Go on, get!” she demanded, prodding the very scared and very confused ship captain that Charlemagne had stolen from another Knight’s boat.  The captain looked around, the panic in his eyes appearing anything but simulated.  However, once he spotted the captain’s cabin, he immediately calmed down and began to head toward it, barking orders to the crew as if they had been working together for years.

“Swab the decks!  They’re filthy!  Check the rigging for frayed lines!  And someone check that the ropes are properly tarred, it feels like rain soon.  And you!  Sand that railing over there!  I can see the splinters on it from here!”

Charlemagne puffed himself up with pride as the boat began to move faster just from having a captain on board.

“Bawk,” he boasted.

“Yes, you did a good job,” Phasima praised.

“But wherever did you find another ship’s captain,” the pangolin inquired.  “Not that it is an unwelcome find, but did you have to eliminate another Knight to obtain him?”

“Bawk,” the rooster told the group.

“You…ate…a plant person?” Josephine moaned as she once again leaned over the railing.  “I think I’m going to be sick!”

“I must remind you that this is hardly the first, last, or even worst creature that Charlemagne has consumed,” Phatagin responded with a laugh, moving a bit to get a better view of Josephine’s face.  Maybe he could catch her pretending to retch.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne agreed.

“Well, that was horrifying,” Phasima responded before shrugging off the mental image and refocusing.  “But if you eliminated another Knight and stole their ship’s captain, does that mean that their boat is still out there?”

“Bawk.”

“Well, you took the captain off of the boat, so the crew doesn’t have anyone to give them orders, right?” the former Empress of the Forest continued.

Charlemagne merely stared at her until she sighed.

“We can take the rest of the provisions from the boat if you just show us where it is,” Phasima explained patiently.  The rooster immediately raced after the captain, and, after a few loud and demanding bawks, the ship’s heading was adjusted.  Then, unable to sit still while a windfall of food was in peril, he flew over to the other ship in order to guard its supplies.

It took them a few long hours to reach the other ship.  While they were making good speed, the body of water they were traversing was quite large.  It was also mostly empty: the group only saw one island off in the distance, which the captain dutifully recorded on a map that appeared to magically keep track of where the ship had been and what the sailors could see.

During this time, Phasima practiced her mana manipulation, with Phatagin joining her.  The Vessarian proved to be quite the teacher when it came to using mana, and the pangolin felt that he was getting somewhat close to acquiring the Mana Manipulation Skill.  His progress felt glacial compared to Charlemagne’s, but Phasima repeatedly told Phatagin that it was unhealthy to use the rooster as a yardstick when it came to using mana.  Apparently, talented individuals could train for thousands of years and never achieve the level of subconscious control that Charlemagne exerted.  It didn’t make the pangolin feel any better, but he politely lied and said that it did.

A cry from the deck notified the duo that they were getting close.  Phatagin set aside the tiny mana ball that Phasima had given him to play with and headed to the front of the ship to get a better look at the other vessel.  He immediately realized that he needn’t have bothered: the ship was a carbon copy of the one they were on.  But, just as disappointment set in, the ship began to rumble, and its timbers started to glow with a silver shine.

Something interesting was happening.
