Ethan had a conflicted smile on his face as more and more Bishops passed through the rift. On the one hand, they were desperate for help, and this was a plan they’d been working on since he’d left Michael to recover on the family farm.

On the other hand, this was his family. All of his family, and he’d brought them to hell, to fight devils. People would die today, and that was a difficult thing to square against what he was seeing.

“Hi Aunt Margret, Uncle James! Cousin Tim, you look great! Is that little Lina, I haven’t seen you since we were kids. You fight monsters now, I see. Gary! Bonds have treated you well; you’ve lost weight! Grandpa Mark, you seem to be perpetually on fire now. That’s new.”

It took several minutes for them all to make it through, and even longer to assemble into ranks. It was a strange process. Say hello, sometimes exchange hugs, then tell them where to stand for the final battle against invading aliens and a demon army.

“I still can’t believe you pulled this off,” Selina said. “So many Dusks…”

“It wasn’t easy,” Michael said. “First we had to call in Bishops from every corner of the globe–we didn’t have time to find other people we could trust with this power.”

“Who also had training as soldiers,” Ethan put in. Bishops fought in half the armies in the world.

“We used up every last monster that you left for us,” Michael said. “Then we began hunting for what monsters we could find that made it through from Nexum.”

Ethan smirked. “I can’t imagine Elias De’Val thought he was giving me an army when he left me that vault.”

Before leaving Nexum, the Chosen had given Ethan access to a lifetime of acquired items from one of Terranova’s lost Champions. Among the contents had been the collection of rare monster cores that ultimately transformed Fable, as well as hundreds of monster eggs, carefully stored away.

Michael nodded. “I’m just glad I had something to do while recovering from my…unusual addiction. Training soldiers was the perfect distraction.”

Ethan grinned. “So you admit I was right to bench you,” he teased.

Michael gave him a flat look. “No one doubts how often you’re right, little brother. We just don’t appreciate how smug you are about it.”

Ethan laughed, slapping his older brother on the back. “Well, the important thing is that you did it. And that I was right. I can’t believe you managed to get so many to Dusk in less than two years.”

“It wasn’t easy,” Michael said. “I needed to push them hard, but between the Dimensional Bleeds, and the Follower attacks, there was nearly enough time. As it stands I still had to force them to fight above rank almost exclusively, and I still left behind nearly fifty Dawns.”

He turned to look out at the army the Followers had assembled, and his voice became even more serious than usual. “I wish I’d had two more years. I might have brought a few Twilights with me.”

“It’ll have to do,” Ethan said, “we’ve lost the luxury of time.” He took a steadying breath. “So, what’s the play?”

Michael considered the battlefield for a long moment, eyeing the two Champions with a raised eyebrow. “That…thing is with you?”

“With us,” Ethan said, referring to the Leviathan. “Apollo is in control of it.”

Michael nodded, as if that was a perfectly normal thing to hear. “Then we’ll have to assume the Champions cancel one another out. I doubt either side has the power to shift that balance anyway. That leaves what looks like dozens of Twilights to us, plus a small handful of Follower generals.”

“With the ultimate objective of taking out the Emperor,” Ethan added. “But I’m fairly certain he’ll be distracted guiding his own Champion.”

Michael’s jaw shifted as he seemed to grind his teeth. “We can’t count on him ignoring us if we attack him directly. Worse, those Twilights will pounce on us if we do. Whittling down his army will need to be the first objective.”

“My people will help,” Selina said proudly. “We have a few hundred Nator, all at Dusk rank, and all ready to fight for our world. They don’t have much more than claws, strength, and determination, but they’ll fight to the end.”

Michael looked at the milling group of alternately furious, then frightened group of Nator. “We’ll need them,” he said. “More than two thirds of my people are ranged fighters–their powers largely emulated their training as soldiers. They’re capable of staggering amounts of raw damage, provided we can keep the Twilights off of them.”

“That will be our job then,” Selina said, sounding determined.

Michael nodded. “The Bishops are trained to work in fire teams. A large number of squads are built around dealing as much damage as possible in a short time, then retreating while another group takes their place. We have specialist teams who focus on mana regeneration that will top up the first group, then the cycle repeats.”

“Oh, I get it,” Ethan said. “Like the British army. One line fires muskets, then ducks to reload, while a second line behind the fires.”

Michael gave him a pitying look. “What a topical reference,” he said. “After working with so many Bishops, it’s nice to be reminded that some of us chose a different path. A very different path.”

Ethan laughed. “Remind me how different it is as I wade into the thick of it with you. I assume that’s the plan?”

“Largely, yes. Coordinate with Roth and Banshee. Don’t go for the kill–like I said, the fire teams will surprise you with the damage they can output, but try to keep them alive, and distracted.”

“Understood,” Ethan said. “I assume you’ll do the same?”

This time Michael smiled. It was a wicked sight. “Not quite,” he said. Then inky black liquid spread across him, and his form grew until he was a head taller. “Orion and I are going to give the Followers a taste of their own medicine.”

With that, tendrils shot out, each one striking a Bishop softly. None reacted, suggesting this was a common occurrence, and soon the entire army of soldiers was coated in a thin layer of Michael’s Familiar, enhancing and protecting them.

Ethan nodded to his brother, then gave a quick hug to Selina before she ran off to join her people. At last, he turned back to battle, locking eyes with the Emperor, despite the miles between them.

“It is time, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo whispered.

“It’s time, my friend,” Ethan agreed.

Above him, the Leviathan bellowed its challenge, and like a starter pistol, the battle began.

Ethan leapt away from a demon that looked like a two-headed elephant, having cut through several tentacles on its back, and hobbled its back legs. The creature howled with fury until wave after wave of magical bullets tore through it like tissue paper.

Ethan landed in a controlled roll, needing to duck under an attack from a gorilla-like demon on his other side. Tomo was above him in an instant, leaping upward and carving through two of the beast’s four arms. Another hail of bullets drove it back, granting Ethan a few brief moments of peace.

He waved to cousin Andrew, the older man’s squad of ten absolute brutes from Glasgow already charging away to a new target. The black of Orion’s protection made it nearly impossible to tell Ethan’s distant relatives apart, but he couldn’t forget Andrew’s distinct accent.

Ethan took a moment to survey the battlefield, frustration biting him from nearly every direction.

The battle was proceeding in an aggressively medium way. The most eye-catching sight was, unsurprisingly, the two titanic Champions battling away not far off. The ground shook continuously from their thrashing, but at least the Emperor and Apollo had seemed to silently agree to leave the two colossal demons to fight in the deep trench surrounding the massive rift.

Neither side wanted to be wiped out by a single errant attack from those two monsters.

So far, the duel seemed relatively even. The scorpiursi had two pincers and its stinger, but its horrific head had proved too inflexible to be a major threat. The Leviathan had fewer weapons at its disposal, but it had coiled around its opponent like an anaconda, and its countless tentacles were doing a good job keeping the immense stinger from doing any real damage.

That battle seemed like it could go on for days, as expected from two such ancient powerhouses.

The other battle, Ethan’s battle was the real problem.

Selina’s Nator had proven every bit as dedicated and ferocious as she had promised, and they hurled themselves bodily at Twilight demons without hesitation. But passion didn’t make up for power disparity, or a tragic lack of combat experience.

They were dying rapidly, and more and more Selina was needing to pull them back for healing, or leaving the Bishops to cover their retreats.

That wasn’t the only thing going wrong, sadly. The Nator had made it abundantly clear that they didn’t know how to fight around hundreds of soldiers with rifles, miniguns, and even more deadly projectile weapons.

More than one Nator had fallen to friendly fire, or simply run directly between a fire squad and a demon, forcing the career soldiers to call for ceasefires at the worst possible moments.

The Bishops were proving themselves every bit as effective as Michael promised, thankfully. Their focused firing techniques and commitment to burning through absurd quantities of mana was proving more than enough to whittle down most of the Twilight demons. Already nearly a quarter of the Follower army had been brought down.

But not without casualties. And these were extremely personal losses.

All around him, Ethan’s family was dying, and he’d brought them to this. He’d grown up with so many stories of heroism, and glory, that he genuinely hadn’t paused to think of what he was bringing them to.

Bishops died in wars. That was what they were for.

The emotional toll revealed that the borderline family motto was nothing but a way of coping with a terrible reality. More and more, Ethan was forced to look away to avoid letting the realities of war tear him apart.

The saving grace had been King Rothavaro, the Banshee, and Ethan himself. Each one had pushed themselves to their limit to target the Follower generals, and keep the enemy off balance. Of course, there would be three times as many casualties if Michael wasn’t hovering above the battle as well, directing his army with deadly precision.

But looking around, Ethan could immediately tell there was something wrong in the battle. He’d developed something of a sense for them over the years, even if he lacked the typical Bishop expertise, and something tickled his mind now.

The Twilight demons he’d been facing were…odd. He’d faced many of them before, and had a sense of how they should look, yet he’d swear there were changes.

Then he noticed that several of the Follower generals were missing. Yet they hadn’t been killed–their respective groups of black-clad demons were still fighting.

Ethan quickly scanned round, finally finding one of the few remaining Followers, he did so just in time.

“What the hell am I seeing?” he asked over his communication rune.

“The generals?” Rothavaro responded immediately. “Watch for yourself.

The Follower in question had moved slightly away from the battle, and was lowering itself onto a Twilight crustacean. No, not onto, into.

“I’ve seen two others do this,” Rothavaro said. “Their groups turtled, then…there.”

Ethan kept watching, the demon that the Follower now…inhabited was quivering, and the ones around it were moving in more tightly. Abruptly, the first demon stilled, then the group surged forward.

In perfect synchronicity. Ethan had seen this before, when the generals had controlled armies of their own kind. The effect had been devastating…and that was with Dawns.

“This could be a problem,” Ethan whispered.
