Not all who Descend come back.

Not all of them should.

The black coin felt almost slick between his fingers, though Harker didn’t have a problem holding it. It was also surprisingly heavy for its size. More than a bit nonplussed, Harker carefully stored the strange black coin in his Seed Quiver. He had no real use for a sharp circle, but he wasn’t about to throw it away.

Harker looked around, getting his bearings. A mist hung low over the water and docks, cut only slightly by the lamplight. He walked down the boards, gait hitched by the ever present soreness in his thighs and knees. The mist obscured the path, making what might have been a short walk into an eternal jaunt of lapping water and creaking boards. Sovereign Sight helped. He held it around himself and moved slowly, keeping the five-foot radius going as he had in the boat. It was a lot, especially with the river sloshing within range, but Harker was happy to find it entirely manageable.

Each step was a flurry of creaking boards, water-logged fibers, and the bitter taste of the white flecks that covered them. An anti-rot compound, no doubt, to keep the docks functional for longer. Harker knew the taste of stemmik and oil of acriba. There weren't more than a dozen recipes that used those reagents, and unless they coated the boards with a paste to attract polflies, anti-rot was the only thing that made sense. So despite the ache in his muscles and the hunger that gnawed at his belly, a smile curled Harker's lips.

Separating sensations was far easier now, even at walking speed. Touch, taste, smell, sound, and sight still tended to intermingle, but in a way that only made them more understandable. It reminded him of the soup Ironwine had made him eat not a day ago. The flavor on his tongue was passable, but it was the smell of it that elevated the meal. Taste and scent worked together to create a greater whole. That was how everything felt to him now. The world was a puzzle, and he finally had all the pieces.

The dock continued onward, well beyond his five-foot reach, and curiosity got the better of Harker. What was beyond the mist? How far did the docks extend? He reached for his Talent, squeezing those spiritual muscles within himself. It came far easier than before, though squeezing at his Talent was still like seizing a slime eel. It wriggled in his hands, fighting against the direction he gave it—but it compressed anyway. Slowing his steps at the same time, he shaped the sphere into a cone.

A brief pain stabbed at his head and chest alike, but it dulled by the second. He held the shape of it intact. What was surprising, however, was that it pushed further than five feet. Almost extending seven feet from his chest, the far end widened until it was nearly ten across. Mist, board, and wave vyed for his attention, each of them carrying a slew of sensations that soaked, stabbed, and chilled his very bones. Disorientation followed, nearly as much as hitting his max range would have done months before. He cut it off.

“Unpleasant,” he croaked. His voice still wasn’t back up to snuff. It rasped sometimes when he least expected it. “Sovereign Sight.”

Water rushed through him again, restoring his standard sphere.

If a cone is too much, what about my direct line? He’d tried it before with great success, though he’d never managed anything over a couple feet. No time like the present.

He gripped his sphere once more, flexing his spiritual muscles, and squeezed. This time, he applied it in even pressure across the whole of the sphere, except the very end. Harker wasn’t entirely sure how he managed it, only that it felt right—sure enough, the end shot outward in a four-foot diameter line as the rest of his sphere collapsed forward.

His breath whooshed from his chest and black spots sprinkled his vision, but Harker held his ground. The length of the line stretched onward, well past his previous limit and even the seven foot cone.

That’s…hoo, that’s strange.

His awareness had fled along the line as well, gathering details from where its length penetrated the mist, but the bulk of his attention was at the end. There he could taste the condensation, feel the flutter of faint air currents, all while smelling the stink of vegetation, wet wood, and distant smoke. It was strong and more precise than he'd experienced before. The mud bath had truly changed him. His blood was strengthening him, no doubt, and his organs—

My brain. It must be sifting everything better because of the Boon. Harker ran his tongue over his teeth. What if he refined his organs further? How good could he get his Talent before Descending to First Depth?

It was an exciting thought, even if he had a broken soul and a weak Current. Now wasn’t the time to test this…and yet…

Coming to a full stop, Harker planted his feet and squeezed. The line narrowed more, down from a four-foot diameter to three feet. Just as before, the length burst outward as his internal muscles pushed and pulled, and the squirming eel of his Talent shot free.

Six feet. Eight. Ten.

No dizziness or interference swept over him. Harker smiled, and compressed harder.

Fifteen feet. Seventeen. Twenty.

His pulse raced, his breath came rapid and heavy, and his head pounded. Spikes of sudden pain dragged at his neck and jaw.

Twenty-five feet.

Thirty feet.

A sudden strangeness tugged at him—a cold lurch through the fog—but it passed as quickly as he sensed it. In its place, bolts of lightning crawled from his nape to crown and exploded behind his eyes. Harker gasped, but his chest felt compressed by a giant’s unrelenting fist. He dropped his Talent, letting the line snap back and vanish altogether.

Harker was on his knees. He didn’t remember falling, but that was fine. The pain was fading and his breath returned.

That was…incredible. No pain could dent his enthusiasm. The real pain didn’t start until I went beyond twenty feet…but already that’s massive.

He now had an effective range of twenty feet—or thirty if he wanted to risk his head exploding again. The ways in which Harker could utilizing that were…uncountable. Extending his senses alongside that line was a bit of an issue, but it was also a boon. Ideas gathered in the back of his mind, eager to be tested. Unfortunately, the time for that wasn’t out in the middle of nowhere where anyone could spot him.

That movement I felt… Harker eyed the fog. Without his Talent, it was impenetrable. I need to be careful.

He stood back up, and, with a wince of expectation, manifested his Talent. It spun out around him, taking up space at the standard five foot radius. No pain accompanied it, thankfully. He did, however, feel a pang in his tributaries.

Depths. It used that much?

Nearly half of his Water had been spent during that attempt. On the trip across the river, he had determined that he produced enough Water to keep his Talent going almost constantly. Sovereign Sight didn't use much, even sustained, and even less when he pulsed it. Since his recovery, the full five-foot radius did little to even dent his reserves.

The cone and line versions, however, were clearly much more Water-intensive. He'd only used his thirty-foot extension for a few seconds. It was likely the cone shape was less of a drain, but more than his sphere. With the Vestige pumping the untempered Sea into his reservoir unpredictably, there was a high chance he’d run out entirely if he leaned on those techniques too much.

It’ll be far safer, for now, to hold my sphere and pulse.

Most of the water in his tributaries was unrefined, which was yet another problem he needed to solve. Had he been using fully refined water, he expected that his Talent would be far more efficient, even with his expensive variations. His baby Current had deepened slightly since his recovery, but it was far from enough.

Harker walked a while more, until his thoughts began to circle around on himself again. He pondered his Talent, his blood, and the unnatural treasure that clung to his broken soul. Leaving the Vale had always been the plan, eventually—but not like this. He rubbed at his chest, as if that would assuage the ache around his reservoir and Lock. His steps picked up. Things were getting worse, and he was running out of time.

Soon enough, his five-foot radius sensed a blush of greater illumination beyond the fog bank, and Harker put on a burst of speed. He cleared it in seconds, and the dissipating mist revealed starlit countryside.

Before him sat a series of low buildings clutched around a rocky cliff of green stone. The river extended to the left, while the path rolled straight through the buildings and wove its way up the distant cliff. There, shrouded by darkness and distance alike, was a large tower dozens of stories high. It was dotted by amber lights, but it was the top that blazed. A great beam rotated at its peak, a slow illumination that swept across the cliff, river, and a set of distant piers on the far side of the river.

Closer to hand, the green stone cliffs were cut up, chunked out with blocks that had clearly been used to make the squat buildings before him. There were ten of them, separated by series of white gravel paths, but they were laid down as if a drunkard had planned them. Some crowded close, while others were twice as far as the others. Yet every single one was made to the same dimensions, with black tiled roofs that were heavily covered in thick yellow moss.

Waist-high walls surrounded them, dividing the docks from where the odd little village began. They were built of stacked stone where the thin grasses met the sudden slope of the riverbank and looked to be painted with more of that anti-rot solution. Harker skirted around them, looking for the opening that would lead him up the cliff. He found instead a large circular entryway. It bore no gate or door of any kind, just a wide and inviting path between the buildings.

“Hello hello, newling. Come to see the Lighthouse, have you?”

A crooked man leaned against the half-wall. He was dressed in a sort of robe, though it was stitched from sackcloth. His hair hung lank over his face, as if he’d just got out of a bath, or the river. Eyes like mud stared at him above a pallid complexion, but his smile was wide. Friendly.

Harker reached for his knives.

Thin hands lifted, fingers splayed. “Ho there, boy! I’m no riverside robber. Just kindly Duncan, payin’ a passin’ traveler a good eventide.”

Harker scanned the wall. Somehow, two more figures had appeared just on the other side. They were dressed in sackcloth too. “You all live here?”

“Hereabouts,” Duncan said, that grin still stretching his lips. “What brings someone like you to our parts?”

“The Lighthouse.” Harker pulsed his Talent. The two others had gotten closer. “Just like you said.”

“Ah yes. Old Pellus. My favorite Ancestor, you know.”

“Not me,” said a woman’s voice behind him. Harker angled his body, trying to keep them all in view. “I was always partial to Anuan. Those chains…”

She moaned, but it was more like a gurgle. The woman was, for lack of a better word, bloated. Her skin was pale but her arms, belly, and legs looked swollen as if she suffered from a sickness.

A hand touched his arm, and Harker yanked it back. “Chartermarked…”

“Keep away,” Harker warned, knives drawn.

“Oh, the academy boy is dangerous.” The bloated woman tittered, and five others joined in.

Five? He counted them again. Seven of them now, including Duncan.

They were surrounding him.

Where had they come from?

“You look tired, newling.” Duncan gestured with his long fingers. “Come. Rest a while.”

The door across the wall was open. It led into one of the green stone houses, but Harker could see nothing inside. Even his Talent didn’t pick up details. But he was tired, that much was true. Perhaps, a short rest…

He stood near the door now. How had he gotten there? The fog had swept in again, but this time it clung to his senses like a wet sheet. All he could feel was the slick flesh of bloated hands, too cold to be anything alive. They grabbed at him, pulling his arm, his leg, his waist—

“Gah!”

One of them howled, a sound like an injured animal, and Harker’s Seed Quiver was cast onto the ground. Items spilled free—seeds, vials, and a dark circle no bigger than a coin.

The creatures hissed, backing away from it. The cloying shroud thinned, and Harker blinked. “Sovereign Sight!”

His Talent slammed forward, flared with all the might he could muster, and his senses sliced through the haze that surrounded him. Harker pulled back, away from the door and the cringing things that huddled there. He could see them now, fully and truly.

Crooked and swollen, they were dead things wearing empty flesh. Claws broke through skeletal fingers and wide, flat teeth gnashed at him from inside the green stone hut.

“Drowned,” he whispered, the horror of it more than enough to wake him up. “You’re already dead.”

The creatures howled, anguish tearing at their malformed features.

“We live!” Duncan snarled, leaping onto Harker’s back. “We only need your heart!”

Harker’s thoughts whirled, but his body was already in motion. Blood pumping a strange, tingling strength through his limbs, he seized the wretch by the wrist and twisted his hips. With nothing more than a grunt, he threw Duncan onto the ground.

Directly onto the black coin.

Duncan burst, his pallid flesh erupting into a geyser of dark Water. The rest scurried away, their disjointed limbs vanishing behind the shadowed doorways before every single door slammed shut at once.

Harker gagged, the scent around him as putrid as sloughing flesh. The taste of it was thick on his tongue, and his ears still rang from the wet shriek of that bloated woman. His Talent fed him too much, but he feared to turn it off.

He scanned the green huts. There was no movement in any direction nor sound, save the faint whistle of the wind. It was as if there had never been anyone there at all.

Dark Water still remained. It clogged the grass, matting it down beneath its thick flow that couldn’t quite flow in any particular direction. More than anything, it reminded Harker of the Stitcher’s power—foul and rotten. It even smelled stagnant, and the bubbling hiss—

At the center of the dark Water was a swirling maelstrom. He pulled his blue dagger free and moved closer…only to watch as the dark, murky Water vanished entirely. All that remained, surrounded by a circle of twisted grass, was the black coin.

Harker picked it up carefully. It was warm to the touch and just as heavy as before. It was also bone dry, without a single droplet on its smooth, featureless surface.

“What in the Abyss are you?” he muttered.

“Halt!” The voice was loud and deep. It came from the other side of the wall. “Incandescence!”

A flash of brilliant white light appeared in the air, shining like a fallen star.

“Who stands among the dead?”
