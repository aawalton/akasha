Kyle looked down to where his left arm had been, then back at Marcus.  His right hand instinctively reached up to feel the gnarled wood.  It was warm to the touch, practically thrumming with energy.

“It’s something we’ve been working on,” Kyle said after a moment.  “As you probably understand, my second class is a variant on Nature Mage, and some recent breakthroughs have allowed us to keep refining the original prosthesis schematics we developed during my time on Kertan Six.”

Marcus’ eyes lit up at hearing the word “schematics,” and the old engineer leaned forward, the last sips of his liquor forgotten for the moment.  “You’ve been working on a few different variants?  Can I take a look at what you’ve done so far?”

Given Marcus’ skill as a craftsman, Kyle didn’t see any reason to refuse.  With a quick mental affirmation to C.H.A.D.D., the drone projected schematics of the various prosthetics they’d created.  Marcus looked at Model: Shrike and Model: Cannon with interest, before turning his attention to Model: Spear and Model: Shield.  Tapping furiously on his tablet the entire time, his attention finally rested on the unnamed prosthetic that Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. were most recently developing.

“Now this is a complicated piece,” Marcus said with a whistle.

[WE ARE STILL IN THE DESIGN PHASE, BUT IT SHOULD BE A USEFUL EVERYDAY PROSTHESIS,] C.H.A.D.D. replied.

Marcus nodded, gesturing for the drone to expand the image.  Unlike the other prosthetic arms, which were designed to mimic their namesakes, this one was modeled after a standard human arm.  Kyle used Infuse on thousands of tightly-wound vines that acted like muscle fibers, being drawn directly into the World Tree Seed.  The fine control was difficult; requiring him and C.H.A.D.D. to individually lengthen and contract different vines to provide the movement.

“Any reason that you didn’t add a skeletal framework like you did with the others?” Marcus asked, looking at the twisted cord of vines serving as the core.

“We considered it, but ultimately preferred the flexibility the vines provided. Plus it looks enough like a normal human arm that the range of motion might take somebody by surprise.”

“Interesting idea, but I can’t help but think some modular bones might be helpful.  It would improve your lift strength and provide more leverage, likely improving striking force as well.  Do you still plan on using the baton-based fighting style?”

Kyle sighed and gently shook his head.  That was one of the hard realizations he’d come to during his time on Kertan Six.  As his skills developed, along with the advent of his World Tree Tender class, the foundational escrima skills he’d picked up from Frank no longer served as functionally as they used to.  The blunt force damage from the batons was reliant on his Strength attribute, which was lagging well-behind his other attributes.  His skills also lent themselves far more to mid-range combat rather than the aggressive close-range focus the dual baton style favored.

“I’ve been shifting away from it.  The skills I’ve unlocked don’t lend themselves to supporting it, and the time I’d need to invest in adapting and refining it could be put to better uses.”

“Then I take it you’ve got answers for close-range combat?” Marcus asked as he continued examining the prosthetics.

“Model: Spear is the prosthesis I’d use, though I’ve got plenty of tools to disengage back to a more favorable range as needed.”

[WE HAVE ALSO BEGUN DEVELOPMENT ON SPECIALIZED CHADDLINGS FOR CLOSE-RANGE COMBAT TO HELP SUPPLEMENT DR. MAYHEW’S FIGHTING STYLE.]

“Chaddlings?  You mean the little acorn things?” Marcus asked skeptically.

[THE VERY SAME,] C.H.A.D.D. confirmed as the drone pulled up some of the highlights they’d been working on.

“It’s an interesting plan,” Marcus said as he shifted his attention to the other schematics.  “I do see a flaw, though.  Trying to get these automatons functional at low-C Grade power is going to be nearly impossible.  The material costs alone will be astronomical, topped off with trying to find an efficient method of infusing it with enough energy to function.  When I’ve worked on projects like this, they either work as high-cost trump cards, or need to wait until I’ve tiered up a bit to make something a tier lower.  Keep experimenting, just keep that in mind.”

Kyle thought about the chaddlings in D Grade, and was about to comment before he got a quick warning from C.H.A.D.D.  What’s wrong? He sent the drone through Pack Leader’s Instinct.  What he got in response was a memory from his time on Pokke, then specific scans the drone had taken of Ma’Sai’s card-based casting system.  Then Kyle understood.  While the method C.H.A.D.D. used was certainly different, the mechanics of both recharging and surpassing grade restrictions were based off the same technology.  Technology, it seemed, that Marcus had been unable to replicate.  Of course, Ma’Sai is following a B Grade summoning heritage, so it makes sense that they’d have some more refined means.

Turning his focus back to Marcus, he smiled.  “I’ll keep it in mind, thank you for the feedback.”

“Any time.  And if you don’t mind, I’d like to propose some enhancements to the design of the prosthetics, assuming you don’t have an urgent need to get them built.”

“We can wait,” Kyle said, thinking of the endless list of projects they were working on.  It’s a good thing we’ve got so much time in C Grade.

“Excellent.  Only one other item of business for us to work through, though it can wait if you’re in a hurry.  Are you planning on staying on Kertan Six permanently?”

This was a question Kyle had considered before, and one that wasn’t unexpected.  “I’d like to have a permanent residence,” he said.  “However, I’d still like to continue to travel and explore.  It would be nice to have a place to come back to that was mine.  That said, I also understand that might not be feasible depending on what your design for the planet would be.  And I wouldn’t want to be causing problems for you in any event.”

“You’re not planning on leading whoever takes us up on the offer to work, are you?”

“Definitely not,” Kyle said.

“Then I don’t think it’d be a problem.  We’ll get you a spot a little bit away from the primary worker’s settlements and teleportation hub, and set up a standard local governance clause.  I think that should take care of us pretty well.”

“So long as it has a provision for Earthlings to come and find work, that sounds good to me,” Kyle affirmed.

“Excellent.  I’ll have documents related to the auction and schematic ideas sent to you as I get them done.  Do you have anywhere to be now?  You’re welcome to stick around for a while, if you’d like.”

Kyle checked his nav bracelet.  The contact from the Pillar still had their job on pause, with no update on when the next phase would begin.  Similarly, he hadn’t seen a special job come through from Duroc quite yet.  And, after all the chaos since his return to the Hub, it would be nice to have a bit of normalcy.  “I’d really like that.  Thank you, Marcus.”

The older man shifted uncomfortably for a moment, then placed a rough hand on Kyle’s shoulder.  “You’re welcome.  And I’m glad things worked out.  You’re welcome to take a train to the residence, or otherwise train in any of the usual spots.  I’ll be back at the Manufactory if you need anything.  And make sure you’re on time for dinner.”

“Will do,” Kyle said with a nod.  “And if you have any C Grade tech you want to test…”

Marcus let out a snort.  “I should have known you were still a glutton for punishment.  I’ll get a range set up.”

Kyle beamed.  “Then I’ll get ready.”

Less than an hour later, Kyle found himself down range of a turret battery, four large cannons pointed at him from nearly two kilometers away.  A robotic voice rang out from his nav bracelet, a familiar sound as he prepared for the exercise to start.

Mr. Mayhew,

The purpose of this test is to measure both the destructive power and range of the new MK-III Stationary Support Battery.  You will begin at a two kilometer range, and move progressively closer as instructed.  You are welcome to use evasion skills or defensive skills at your leisure; be warned that these are designed as early C Grade defenses, and as such possess a high degree of power.  Caution is advised.

The exercise begins in five seconds.  Four.  Three. Two.  One.  Begin.

Now, Kyle had been primed on this before, and knew the expectation was for him to practice using his defensive skills, building up to a point where he either could no longer dodge consistently, or was overwhelmed by the attacks.  However, part of him wanted to really experience how his body would hold on against true C Grade attacks.

As the countdown proceeded, Kyle pushed power through his Ferric Augmentation, covering his body in the biometal.  At the same time, Model: Shield was withdrawn from the World Tree Seed and attached at his left shoulder.  This model involved a highly-mobile socket to position the massive wooden tower shield he and C.H.A.D.D. created.  Naturally, the exterior layer was made of stonebloom bark plates enhanced with Infuse.  Springy vines acting as shock absorbers behind the plates, attached to another thick layer of bark, this one containing further shock-absorbing properties from the trees in the first phase of Kyle’s Practicum.

He left Aura Force and Genesis Engine inactive for now, trying to get a good sense for what he could handle using this as his base.  The moment the countdown ended, a lance of crimson energy rushed toward him.  Raising his shield to intercept it, he took the blast square in the center, knocking him back nearly a meter.  Three more lances followed in rapid succession, and soon the battery was firing at full speed.  Model: Shield held up better than Kyle had hoped, withstanding thirty-one direct blasts before the material began to fail.

Withdrawing the prosthetic back into the World Tree Seed, Kyle accepted a single blast to his chest as he activated Aura Force.  The heat from the cannon battery singed his flesh despite his incredible durability, requiring an activation of Adaptive Regeneration to help ward off the worst of the damage.  With Aura Force active, the game changed.  Kyle pushed himself out of the way of the next few blasts from the cannons, then met the next barrage with compressed barriers of force.  While these barriers were insufficient to entirely block the cannon battery, they were more than enough to disperse the focused energy.

Destructive power bathed the area around Kyle in the wake of the attacks, the damage quickly healed away by Adaptive Regeneration.  He was more than pleased with the result of the defensive capabilities of Aura Force, though one issue was made abundantly clear.  The mana cost from maintaining a constant barrier with the skill was astronomical.

With Genesis Engine active, that would be mitigated, but as things stood this was not a tenable solution for longer fights.  When he’d tested it against the environmental effects, he’d been able to quickly reabsorb a large portion of the energy expended, so long as it was within his domain.  Now, the energy from the cannon barrage disrupted his mana, preventing him from being able to draw that power back into himself.

Soon, the attacks stopped, and the robotic voice began to speak.

Mr. Mayhew,

Please advance two hundred and fifty meters, and we will resume testing.

Kyle smiled at the prompt, and began to walk forward.  It was good getting an opportunity to train again, and he intended to take full advantage of Marcus’ hospitality while he was here.  When he reached the designated area, he braced himself for the next batch of attacks.  This was going to be fun.
