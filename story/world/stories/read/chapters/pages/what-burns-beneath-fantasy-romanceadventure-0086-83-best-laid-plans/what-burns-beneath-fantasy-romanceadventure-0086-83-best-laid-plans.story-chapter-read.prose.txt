An hour passed and the pleasant, heady warmth from my mead hadn’t fully faded when Renner walked back in the inn’s front door. The room was a bit brighter than it should have been and his edges were a bit fuzzy. The effect was quite jarring.

I blinked, marveling at this, with my chin propped in one palm. Teela sat slumped against my side, drinking a cup of water and yawning.

Renner paced through a sparsely populated common room and settled opposite my chair, examining the both of us. “Well. Didn’t expect this.”

Teela elbowed me in the side and whispered, very loudly, “He thinks we’re irresponsible.”

My face grew warm. I spent a moment straightening, trying very hard not to think of the man’s arms or eyes or the slow smirk crawling over his face or anything, really. “We’re not.”

“Never said it,” he braced both elbows on the table. “Are you two wallowing or panicking?”

“We were having fun,” the young woman mumbled through another yawn. “Mm. Sounds like it’s time to be responsible again, though. Any news?”

“Nothing good. Order’s fanned out through the city, putting all the Watch on alert. Dame’s with a group north, looking for this… well. The barrow’s obviously gonna be an ambush, but there’s a dozen paladins looking for blood and a dozen more being held hostage. They’ll meet the fight head-on, which is probably exactly what this Embriel wants. Sounds like a few Thalessians have heard the name before, and they expect this to get ugly.” He grimaced, flexing one hand. “And interrogating the pacted’s getting us nowhere, so no one’s got any new information.”

My shoulders tensed. “Interrogating? As… as in…?”

The gaze that met mine was cold. “They killed people in the streets. They have hostages. No one’s playing nice right now. Did you learn anything?” He fixed me with a critical eye.

“Nothing good.” Both arms curled around my middle as I quietly relayed what little information I’d gleaned.

His scowl darkened with every word. “So… just so I have this straight. Instead of us having fun killing pacted and one stray monster, the city’s gonna be a damn bloodbath tomorrow and you’re gonna get kidnapped? Willingly or otherwise?”

I examined my hands. The bubbly, fuzzy mirth from the mead was fading quickly, indeed. “Something like that.”

He thought for a while. Then, with a shake of his head and a glower aimed at the table, muttered, “We should just leave. Grab horses and head north at first light. Tonight, even.”

I almost thought I’d misheard him. “You want… but Joselia-”

“Is gonna do what she thinks is right,” he snapped. “Always has. There’s a few hours of daylight left. I’ll risk sleeping in the woods if it gets us more distance.”

“And then tomorrow we get a repeat of this morning.” Teela addressed the ceiling very matter-of-factly. “Where Brin’s… you know shows up looking for her. Except this time he’ll be mad, so maybe instead of telling Royce to go sit in a chair he’ll rip his head off.”

“Might be an improvement.” Renner ignored my indignant noise, turning the glare towards Teela. “So you’d rather sit around and wait?”

“I’d rather be back home, actually,” she snipped. “But I don’t think running away is the answer, here!”

“I do.”

They both looked at me.

My teeth sank into my lower lip. “N-not me. I don’t… I think that’ll end badly. But I think you two and Royce should run.” The statement earned me a long, frigid silence. “Think about it. Either my… either he was lying and this is just some little problem that the Order can handle, or he was telling the truth and it’s something catastrophic. Either way, I don’t think running is a bad idea.”

Teela looked away, nose scrunched in thought. Renner just frowned at me.

“I’m… look, you’re not going to like it,” I looked between them, hands tight around my mug and feeling almost shy. “But I’m thinking of… going. Agreeing. To his… idea.”

Copper eyes narrowed. “To being kidnapped.”

“He said I’d be safe. Just as long as I… behave.” A helpless, bitter laugh choked its way up my throat. My vision blurred. “And it… it sounds like I either go willingly and maybe find a way to help stop all this, or… or…”

“Or he shows up in the middle of a bloodbath and drags you off anyways,” Teela finished. She looked at me with hooded, shining eyes.

I couldn’t seem to look back at her.

Renner’s face was stormy. “You’d be asleep, right?”

“What?”

“For this… idea. You’d just be dreaming?”

I thought for a moment. “I think so. He said that I could wake up and warn the Thalessians about what they’re up against. Why?”

“Well… salt, I’m not saying I like it, but we could take shifts keeping an eye on you. Shake you awake if things get ugly.”

“And…” I eyed him skeptically. Waiting for the argument, the insistence that this simply wasn’t an option, the… well, the tantrum. The words felt heavy on my tongue. “And… when I warn the Order, they’ll… well, they’ll know about me, and-”

“I get it,” he said flatly.

“And…?”

“And I don’t see a lot of other options, ladyship. I also don’t see much way of stopping you.” One gloved hand dragged over his face. He looked impossibly weary for a moment, then the scowl returned in full force. “We’ll… spin it, somehow. Say the prisoners started talking. Salt, maybe you can write it all down, hand me a letter and run while I fill them in. There’s options. Not good ones, but…” he swept a hand out.

“But better than anything else we’ve got.” I sighed.

Renner grunted. Teela groaned into her palm.

“He said I’d be safe,” I murmured again, eyes downcast. A meager offering of reassurance to the two of them as much as to myself. “He needs me. He wouldn’t bring me somewhere if… I’ll be alright.”

“Right.” His voice was… flat. Empty.

I rubbed my eyes. “I… I still think you two should consider running. I’ll give you my coinpurse, you can buy horses at dawn and just go. Maybe you should even start back towards Pinnacle, take word of what’s happening in case this is a massacre. And then… and maybe I’ll be able to catch up once everything... I… saints, I’m certainly not in charge of either of you, but… I want you both to be safe. I’d like you to just leave me behind.”

Teela said nothing. She hunched forwards, expression shadowed beneath the hat’s brim and knuckles white around her cup.

Renner regarded me for a long while. Then, stone-faced, looked away and muttered, “Tried that already. Didn’t take.”

Something in my chest fluttered, but before I could even begin to think of a response to that the inn door swung open.

Jareth strode towards us, absent his armor and with his head held high. Deep-set blue eyes practically sparkled as they settled on Renner’s tense shoulders and, with a positively wicked grin, he boomed out, “Ready for round two?”
