As it turned out, Rex had strolled into the Red Rose at just the right time. The moment he accepted the contract, Credo’s people rushed him out of the room and pulled him into a crowded tent that belonged to Credo. This was where they were preparing their journey to the farming station.

He had even managed to avoid Lavrian on his way out.

Not all Gates were the same. Some portals, like the ones at the centre of cities like Dorog, were permanent passages to other worlds. These were the Gates opened by dragons and required taming to colonize. In contrast, others required channelers, reagents, and lots of mana to summon them, all of which were rather expensive.

Rex watched as channelers working for Credo placed the required reagents to open the Gate on the points of a pentagram that looked to have been drawn with blood.

“Don’t worry. It’s only goat blood.”

Rex looked to the boy who stood at his side. He was a broad, sandy-haired boy who stood a good foot taller than Rex with a strong chin and a sword dangling at his side.

Rex nodded. Goat blood was better than human, he supposed.

“I guess you're going to the farm as well?”

“Yep. Me and the others,” the boy said, gesturing to seven others standing around them.

They were all young like Rex, although some, like the sandy-haired young man, looked to be at least a couple of years older.

“We’re all going? That’s more than I expected,” Rex said.

“Yeah, I was a little surprised too. It’s been quite the experience. One moment I was in the market haggling for a sword, and the next, one of Credo’s men said he could buy it for me. Not long after, I got roped into this. How’d you get here?”

He seems rather upbeat, considering it sounds like he got scammed into this.

Rex explained what happened at the Red Rose.

“Ah, I see. So they picked up a couple of others in there, too.”

“I don’t get it,” Rex said. “Are you suggesting we’re getting scammed or something?”

“Ha! No idea,” the boy shrugged. “Only just arrived a few days back. And my tribe doesn’t have a lot of Classed. This is all rather fascinating. And a little scary.”

Oookay, so he’s just going with the flow then? Well, at least he seems nice enough.

“So, you’re a first-timer as well then?"

The boy nodded. “Yep! Nice to meet you. My name’s Andy. I’ve got your back if you’ve got mine."

Rex tilted his head. He hadn’t expected to make allies so quickly. Then again, they were a bunch of newbies about to pass through a Gate into a dangerous outworld with roaming monsters. And finding a few people you could trust to have your back made a lot of sense.

“Oh, yeah. Sure. That sounds good. Oh, and I’m Rex,” Rex hurriedly blurted out.

Why not? There can’t be any more harm in making allies with this guy than with Credo, he thought. After all, he had no reason not to try to form some alliances.

“Good. I kinda want to survive this whole thing. There are people counting on me,” Andy said, whispering the last part. “What’s your Class, anyway? You’re a bit small and skinny for a front liner.”

“Mine? Oh, I’m a Versatile. Rear guard, I suppose," Rex said.

Generally, people only shared what archetype their Class was, rather than the Class itself. It was an unwritten rule, as having a stranger knowing exactly what Class you were could be dangerous.

“Versatile? Don’t hear that often. I’m pretty sure you’re the only one in our group.”

“And you?”

“Vigor, of course.” Andy grinned and thumbed his broad chest. “And we’ve got pretty much everything covered now that you’re here. We even got Freyja over there with an Arcane Class.”

Rex glanced over at the cute freckled girl, who smiled and waved back.

Maybe taking this contract wasn’t such a bad idea after all…

“Alright, be at the ready,” one of Credo’s men said. “The Gate is almost stabilized. We’ve got wagons to bring through it as well, and every second it stays open, the more it costs us. So be quick, dammit!”

“Sounds like that’s our cue,” Andy said.

Beams of flowing mana had begun to pour out from the channelers, curling together in the middle of the pentagram. Rex watched as the reagents turned into motes of light and were consumed by the Gate. And within seconds, a portal that looked like the surface of calm water appeared.

“Go, go, go! And remember, look for Jango on the other side!” Shouted Credo’s lackey, and Andy immediately ran straight into it.

Rex stood frozen for a second, watching as several others passed through the Gate. Everything had happened so fast. And he didn’t snap out of it until Freyja stopped before the Gate and gestured for him to go first.

“What, you want me to go?”

“I SAID, GO, GO, GO!” Credo’s lackey roared again, and Rex jumped to attention, his legs marching him through the Gate before his brain had fully processed what he was doing.

Bursting through the Gate, Rex almost ate shit, skipping over a mound of earth, but managing to find his footing.

“Not too shabby, huh?” Andy said, hands against his hips as he took in the view.

They found themselves standing in fields of wheat and barley. The fields went on for a while in all directions, eventually stopping when they met a thick wall of trees.

Looks like we’re surrounded by forest.

The trees were massive, towering as tall as any building he had ever seen, and could be seen in all directions above the manicured fields.

At the far end of the fields, some distance away, was an impressively long and large homestead.

Gotta be a fair few rooms in that thing.

“Is that fresh blood I smell?”

Pushing aside a row of wheat aside, came a thin, but dangerous-looking man as he twirled his mustache. He wore light leathers with curved daggers at his sides.

“You’re big; you should be useful,” he said, eyeing Andy.

A moment later, Freyja and the last couple passed through the Gate, followed by a couple of wagons.

“You lot, hurry it up! We don’t have all day!” The man yelled and clapped his hands. And a second later, a couple of wagons filled with produce came rattling down a narrow path between the wheat and rolled straight on through the Gate.

Once the wagons were through, the Gate closed, and the man started clapping again, continuing until he had everyone’s attention.

“Welcome to my little outpost. My name is Jango, and from here on out, what I say goes. If I say jump, you say?”

A silence followed until Andy barked up in his oversized puppy dog kind of way. “What kind!”

“Close enough. Now, let’s get you miserable lot to the homestead and settled in.”

Jango didn’t miss a beat, turning back toward the house immediately and marching off. Andy was only a step behind, and the rest quickly followed.

As they passed through wheat and then rows of corn and other vegetables, Rex spotted the weary gazes of several farmers working the fields. They were dressed in rags and looked up with dirt on their faces. Their dishevelled look reminded him of Dorog. Except the miners on Dorog didn’t need a bunch of Classed to keep them safe.

I guess the unclassed have it rough everywhere.

Reaching the homestead, they walked up and onto the deck, where there were a few other men standing about. Rex had gotten some basics down when it came to sensing auras, and now he could tell Classed from unclassed with a look.

No shortage of muscle around here.

“These guys were once like you, greenhorns. They earned their stay and made something of themselves,” Jango said as he passed by the men and into the house without stopping.

Rex eyed them, spotting a couple of items on their persons that resonated with weak power.

Artifacts.

His eyes brightened as he spotted the valuable items. They represented exactly the kind of treasure and glory he hoped to secure for himself. And he felt a rush of adrenaline as he imagined himself claiming an artifact for himself.

Stopping in the foyer, Jango turned to the newcomers. “Here are your new leaders. They’ll show you to your rooms. Put your stuff away and return here, and I’ll complete your induction,” he said, pointing to a man and a woman who stood on staircases to either side of him.

“Separate into two groups and follow,” the woman said, her lip curling oddly against the gnarly scar that split her face.

“You and me, buddy,” Andy said, wrapping an arm around Rex’s shoulder and walking with him. They walked up the right-side staircase and followed the man.

Rex glanced back, spotting Freyja as she joined the other group, and then continued up.

“Here you go. This will be your room. It’s usually four to a room…. But there are five of you, so somebody is sleeping on the ground. Don’t worry, though; I doubt all of you will last. Pick a chest and put your things away. Jango won’t like being made to wait, so don’t dawdle.”

The man finished his little speech and walked off, leaving the five of them in the small shared bedroom.

“I think this mattress is actually better than home,” one of the boys said as he sat down on a bed and pressed his hands against it. “Not bad. Not bad at all.”

“You’re on the ground, runt!” A bigger boy said, grabbing the boy’s arm and pulling him off the bed.

“Hang on, that’s not fair!”

The boy looked around and realized he was the smallest. When nobody came to his aid, he slumped his head, grabbed a pile of linens set aside, and started making a bed on the floor.

“Hey!” Rex said, stepping toward the big boy.

“Huh? Got a problem?”

The large boy who looked more neanderthal than human crossed his thick, furry forearms as he glared down at Rex.

“Leave it, Rex.” Andy grabbed his shoulder. “Remember who we’re working for. I don’t think either Jango or Credo are going to take too kindly to us fighting amongst ourselves.”

Rex felt bad. He was not fond of bullies. But Andy had a point. He was hoping to make the most of this contract and to move on to something better as soon as he could. But he couldn’t ignore the fact that his employers were shady and didn’t need to give them any reason to try to screw him over.

“That’s right,” the big boy said. “Listen to your boyfriend, and run along.”

“We’re playing nice for now. But keep yapping, and you might just have an accident somewhere out in the forest,” Andy said.

The big boy’s eyes widened a little at the threat, and then he huffed and turned around. “Whatever. I’m doing you a favor anyway. Or do you wanna sleep on the floor?”

Rex had to admit he was a little surprised by Andy. He had taken him as cheerful and straightforward. But perhaps he was more aware than he realized.

“You can take my bed if you like,” Rex said to the smaller boy.

“No, it’s fine.” The kid smiled back and fixed his glasses. “The floor is cozy enough.”

“You sure?” Rex said.

“Yeah. Consider it payment for sticking up for me. I really appreciate it. I’m Ernie, by the way. Looking forward to working together.”

“Rex.”

“And I’m Andy.”

The door slammed, interrupting the trio.

“Looks like Neanderthal has left the room,” Rex said.

“And the other kid,” Andy said, noticing there were only three of them.

“We-we had better hurry too. Remember that warning about keeping Jango waiting?” Ernie said.

“Right you are,” Andy said and started shoving his things away.

Rex didn’t have much, and it only took him a second to throw his sack full of old clothing into the chest.

“Done,” he said, brushing his hands, and walked up to the door. He could have left, but he decided to wait for Andy and Ernie. “Don’t you think there’s a lot of Classed here just to defend a simple farm?”

“Eh, it’s outworld,” Andy said as he stuffed the last of his things away. “People go missing on outworlds all the time.”

Outworlds were worlds that were barely tamed. New Gates appeared all the time, mostly spewing out monsters when they did. Humanity, alongside other intelligence races, had fought back against the monsters a long time ago, in turn, colonizing the worlds beyond the Gates. But some were more settled than others, and some had only recently been discovered.

“Sure, but all this just for wheat and veg?”

“Carsus isn’t too good for farming,” Ernie said as he folded the last of his things away. “Towns like Tartarus pay a small fortune to keep food and drink flowing. And with all the contracts passing hands in the city, they’ve got more than enough coin to pay for it.”

Rex nodded. He supposed it did make sense. Carsus was the world Tartarus was on, and as far as mid-tier adventurers went, there was no better place to find contracts. Which no doubt meant good tax revenue for the lords.

Still, there had to be safer places food could be sourced from. But he kept the thought to himself. They already needed to get a move on.

“Come on,” Andy grabbed Ernie. “You can fold later. Or do you want to make Jango angry?”

“R-right. Fold later,” Ernie nodded.

By the time they finally got downstairs, Jango was already irritably tapping his foot against the floor. “You’re late. You three just got here. Try not to piss me off just yet.”

The neanderthal from their room, and the others were already standing beside Jango, making matters worse.

“Well, hurry up then!” Jango snapped his fingers, and the boys rushed down in unison to join the others. “Don’t keep me waiting again. Anyway, now that you’re all here, we gotta decide what jobs you’ll take. And to do that, I need to know what you’re good at. If you’re equipped and ready to go, come see me ready to tell me your archetype. If you’re not, head through that door over there and see Ulysses. He’ll sort you out with some gear. Remember, it’s all on tick. Anything you break comes out of your pocket.”

Credo didn’t mention anything about owing him coin for the gear. Rex wanted to sigh, but he honestly wasn’t too surprised. Once again, nothing came free. But he would have appreciated a warning.

Inside the next room, Ulysses was waiting hunched over a table covered in various bits of equipment. He was a big man with an eyepatched, and a stump with a mechanical hand connected to it.

“Archetype?” He grunted.

“Versatile,” Rex said, stepping up to the table.

“Versatile? Haven’t had one of you in a while. Alright, in that case, you pick. What kind of weapon do you want?”

Rex thought about that for a second. While he didn’t actually have an Archer Class, a bow was what he was best with, and the reason he had been able to defeat the guardian.

“A bow, please.”

“A bow, huh? You know how to use one?”

“Better than anything else,” Rex shrugged. “Been hunting since I was a little boy.”

“Have you now? Well, then, here you go,” Ulysses grabbed a shortbow that was a damn sight better than the old bow he had back on Dorog and placed it down on the table. “Simple, but decent quality yew. Should keep you going for a while. You owe me for it, and break it, and you’ll owe me twice. Got it?”

“Yeah, got it.”

Ulysses then passed over a quiver of arrows. “Arrows ain’t free either. I recommend you don’t miss. It can get expensive if you do.”

“Good to know,” Rex said and gathered up his new belongings.

“Boots?”

“Huh?”

Rex glanced down at the cloth he had wrapped around his feet, and Ulysses craned over the table. “Boots,” he repeated, and placed a pair on the table. “Leather coat and armguard.”

“All of this is for me?”

“Yep. Ain’t free though. You owe twenty gold coins now.”

Great. Twenty gold? That’s a bit rough, isn’t it?

Shaking his head, Rex trudged outside. The new gear was great, but he didn’t feel like he’d gotten the best deal.

The moment he was out of the room, Jango spotted the bow over his shoulder. “Boy, can you hunt?”

“Yeah. It’s about the only thing I’m good at.”

“Good. You’re an easy pick then. There’s been an empty position amongst our hunters for a while now. You’ll be joining them. Oh, and what’s your archetype?"

“Versatile.”

“Oh?” Jango’s brows perked. “Interesting. I haven’t met many, but I hear Versatile Classes make for good hunters. People hunters normally. But I can’t see why the skills wouldn’t transfer to hunting game. I guess we’ll see. If you don’t bring back any meat, you can always be moved to guarding.”

People hunters? Is that what people think about Versatile archetypes? Rex thought.

His brow crinkled, and he wondered if it was perhaps better that he didn’t go around telling everyone his archetype…
