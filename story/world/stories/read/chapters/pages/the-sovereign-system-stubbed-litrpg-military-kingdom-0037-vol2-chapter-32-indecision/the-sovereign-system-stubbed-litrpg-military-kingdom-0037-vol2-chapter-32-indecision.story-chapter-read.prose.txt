The entire group of fifty men stood frozen in shock, their eyes wide as the scene unfolded before them. Watching the bratty and arrogant second prince get humbled by a single, flawless front kick felt almost surreal—like a shared dream finally come true.

But none were more stunned than the minister himself. His mouth hung so wide open it looked as if his jaw had come unhinged.

Unfazed by the stunned expressions around him, Luke stepped forward and extended a hand, helping the prince to his feet. Unlike his father—who was built like a small horse—Prince Lyon was lean and wiry.

“There should be a river nearby. We’ll get you and the carriage cleaned up before we move on,” Luke said casually, as though he hadn’t just delivered a strike that nearly bordered on regicide in broad daylight.

“T-thank you,” the prince stammered, fear still flickering in his eyes.

Without delay, Luke ordered the group to head toward the nearest river. It would be a minor detour, but there was no way he could force the prince to ride ten more days in a carriage soaked with vomit.

Though he had just struck a member of the royal family in full view of fifty witnesses, Luke knew he wasn’t entirely in the wrong. Within the army, his command had to be absolute. By putting the prince in his place, he had established a clear hierarchy—and made it known that orders would not be questioned.

Still, forcing the young royal to sit in his own filth for days would be seen as a grave insult to the entire royal lineage. Luke had no illusions—such an act would earn him severe punishment upon returning to Ralis City, regardless of how many accolades he might win during this campaign.

Fortunately for the prince, there was room next to the coach for the short ride to the river, sparing him the indignity of enduring the stench-filled carriage.

When they arrived, Luke dismounted and issued orders in a sharp, commanding tone. The men moved swiftly, scrubbing the carriage and airing it out as best they could.

Unfortunately, with no access to modern cleaning agents, water alone did little to save the ruined upholstery. Though they could have returned to Ralis City to fetch a new carriage, Luke dismissed the idea. Time was precious, and he wasn’t about to waste it.

“You’re quite brave, young Luke,” Tristan remarked as he approached, his voice calm but laced with admiration. “I never imagined I’d witness someone treat the second prince like that.”

Luke let out a weary sigh and shook his head. “He didn’t leave me much of a choice. If I hadn’t stood up to him, I’d have lost the authority to lead before we even began. I just hope he’s not the vengeful type…”

He meant it. Though the prince had yielded—for now—Luke couldn’t shake the worry that Lyon might try to sabotage him later.

If it were just personal retaliation, he could handle it. But if the prince began meddling with the mission itself, it could spell disaster. Fifty thousand lives depended on Luke’s ability to lead, to make sound decisions, and to act without hesitation.

If he were undermined at a critical moment...

Luke shook the thought away. There was no time for doubt. He would remain vigilant, but his focus had to stay on the mission—on ensuring the safe retreat of their entire army.

Tristan wore a conflicted expression as he replied, “The second prince isn’t exactly known for his magnanimity, but I doubt he’ll act—at least not while we’re outside the capital.”

Luke’s eyes shifted toward the river, where the prince was being dutifully tended to by the minister, who fluttered about like a mother hen.

“I should be able to manage the consequences once we return,” Luke murmured. “Assuming, of course, we succeed in our mission.”

“If anyone can, it’s certainly you, young Luke,” Tristan said with quiet confidence.

Luke offered the older man a grateful look, but quickly redirected the conversation.

“What are your thoughts on the mission?” he asked, careful not to reveal his own strategy before hearing Tristan’s perspective.

Tristan’s face hardened. “It’s troubling that we haven’t heard from the army in over a month,” he began. “Combine that with Xiangdong’s proximity to Lhair… it doesn’t bode well. I suspect foul play.”

Luke gave a slow nod. He’d reached the same conclusion. Had he not defended Clayton City and reported Lhair’s movements to the king, the Marxx Kingdom might still be in the dark about the invasion.

The enemy had already seized Valand City and the northern reaches of the kingdom without sounding a single alarm. That alone was a chilling testament to their capabilities.

“I propose we travel south along the far side of the river until we reach the border,” Luke said. “Then head east toward Xiangdong.”

“What?” Tristan’s voice jumped with surprise. “Wouldn’t it make more sense to go to Yunxi first and reinforce the army stationed there? At least then we’d have troops at our side if we run into enemy forces.”

But Luke shook his head. “It would cost too much time. Xiangdong is likely under siege as we speak. If we delay, we’ll lose whatever narrow window we still have.”

His reasoning didn’t seem to fully convince the librarian. Tristan’s brows pulled together in a heavy frown.

“Young Luke,” he said slowly, “how are we expected to do anything with just fifty men? If the enemy has already surrounded the city, we’ll be powerless without reinforcements.”

Luke remained calm. “We have one mission, Tristan: retreat. Our goal isn’t to win a battle—it’s to withdraw as many soldiers as possible. A pitched fight would only waste lives and vital resources.”

Tristan fell silent, clearly weighing the risk.

“But if they’re encircled, how do we even coordinate a retreat from outside the fortress walls?” he asked, the uncertainty still etched into his voice.

At that, Luke gave a wry smile. “How are your climbing skills?”

“HUH!?” Tristan nearly shouted. “You want to climb the cliff from the southern valley?”

His outburst drew the attention of nearby soldiers. Realizing his slip, Tristan cleared his throat and waved a hand, dismissing their curiosity. Only once they had returned to their tasks did he lean in, voice hushed.

“Have you seen that cliff face? It’s several hundred feet high. One wrong move, and it’s a straight drop into the afterlife. Even if I were twenty years younger, I doubt I could scale it.”

Luke understood. It wasn’t a perfect plan—not even close. But the alternatives were even worse.

His eyes drifted to the horizon, where the river curved and the mountains loomed in the distance.

“You’re right. We don’t even know if Xiangdong Fortress has fallen yet,” Luke said, attempting to ease the man’s growing anxiety. “We’ll revisit this discussion once we’re closer. Maybe the situation won’t be as dire as we fear.”

“Let’s pray we don’t have to resort to such dangerous tactics, young Luke,” Tristan replied, exhaling a deep sigh.

Luke gave the older man a reassuring pat on the shoulder and turned toward his horse—only to freeze as a familiar chime rang out in his mind.

[DING]

[New Quest Received]

A sinking feeling settled in his gut. Forcing his expression to remain calm, he approached his gelding and gently stroked its neck, whispering quiet words of encouragement to the beast while masking the turmoil inside.

He silently willed the system window to open, already bracing for something unreasonable.

Auxiliary Quest: Preserve your forces

Details: Even when forced to retreat, the Sovereign does so with dignity.

Task: Successfully retreat from Xiandong Fortress with more than 70% of the initial forces remaining.

Progress: 87% forces remaining.

Reward: Skill Selection

Failure: Unknown

Luke’s brow creased as he read the quest details. Just as he suspected, the fighting was already underway at Xiangdong. Whether their forces were holding the fortress or attempting to reclaim it remained unclear.

Then the translucent window flickered.

Progress: 86% forces remaining.

Luke swore inwardly, a cold pressure building in his chest. Men were dying. Whatever was happening at the front, it wasn’t good—and they didn’t have the luxury of lounging beside a river.

“Pack up! We’re moving out!” Luke shouted, swinging into his saddle in a single fluid motion.

His voice startled the group by the riverbank, where the minister was still scrubbing vomit out of the second prince’s robes. There was a moment’s hesitation—just long enough for Luke’s grim expression to reach them.

They said nothing.

If we follow the southern border, it’ll take half a month at best, he thought bitterly.

The horses were swift, but the second prince’s insistence on bringing a carriage was a major hindrance. Not only did it slow them down, but it would be a liability during a retreat with injured or demoralized soldiers.

Luke made a choice—one that would certainly earn him more ire from Prince Lyon.

But he didn’t care.

Steering his gelding toward the coachman, Luke straightened in the saddle and issued his command with finality.

“Saddle the horses. We’re leaving the carriage behind.”

The driver—a mere hired hand—went pale. But after witnessing Luke send a royal flying with a single kick, he knew better than to argue. Without a word, he dismounted and began unloading what he could.

However, not everyone accepted the decision so easily.

“General, what is the meaning of this?” The minister’s high-pitched voice rang out, grating on Luke’s nerves.

“The carriage stays. The terrain ahead is rough, and we’ll lose too much time dragging it along,” he replied curtly.

The minister scowled. “The plains stretch for miles from here to Yunxi! That’s exactly why the carriage was brought in the first place, General.”

Luke’s eyes narrowed, carefully studying the man before him. The minister clearly served in the royal court, though likely from the lower rungs. These types were often silver-tongued—quick to speak, slow to act.

“We are not going to Yunxi,” Luke said flatly.

“What!?” the minister's voice shot up an octave. “Our mission is to recover the remnants of our armies. Ignoring Yunxi is direct rebellion against the king’s orders!”

Luke felt his patience beginning to fray.

“Those are my orders, Minister. If you take issue with them,” he said coolly, a wicked smile curling at the corner of his lips, “I’d be happy to discuss it with you—right after delivering your punishment for insubordination.”

The color drained from the minister’s face. He looked like a man who had just realized he was standing at the edge of a cliff.

“Of course,” Luke continued casually, “it might be a little difficult to speak without your head attached to your shoulders.”

“T-the king will hear of this!” the minister squealed.

“Aye. I’m sure he’ll mourn your passing... if he remembers who you are.”

Without breaking stride, Luke turned to Richard Boyd and gave a simple nod.

Richard cupped his fist in silent acknowledgment, then stepped forward. His massive frame cast a looming shadow as he reached over his shoulder, gripping the hilt of his greatsword in one fluid motion.

The steel groaned as it left the sheath, and the intention was unmistakable.

“W-wait!”

The minister fell back in a panic, landing hard on his rear as he scrambled away on all fours. But there was no escaping the armored knight who stalked after him at a measured pace, the heavy blade gleaming in the light.

“I-I’m sorry, General! I’ll obey—I swear!” the man cried.

The entire camp watched in silence. Some turned their eyes away from the pitiful sight, while others observed with cold detachment. Even the second prince looked as if he might be sick, his face pale with unease.

The minister crawled until he backed into the carriage—his escape route vanishing. As Richard approached, the man’s breath hitched, and a high-pitched whimper escaped his lips. Then, without warning, his body went limp.

His head hit the dirt with a dull thud, and an acrid stench wafted through the air as a warm puddle formed beneath him.

Luke wrinkled his nose.

“That’s enough,” he ordered calmly, ending the spectacle.

Richard shot the collapsed man a look of disgust before sheathing his greatsword and stepping away. Some of the soldiers seemed genuinely surprised the general hadn’t followed through.

Tristan, however, merely sighed. He had known Luke never intended to carry out the execution—though as a general, he would have been well within his rights to do so.

“Leave him here,” Luke said, his tone indifferent. “He can walk back to Ralis City when he wakes up.”

With that, he turned to Prince Lyon, whose complexion had not yet returned to normal.

“It’s a wonderful day for a ride, don’t you think, Prince Lyon?”

The prince swallowed hard and gave a meek nod.

“Y-yes, General.”
