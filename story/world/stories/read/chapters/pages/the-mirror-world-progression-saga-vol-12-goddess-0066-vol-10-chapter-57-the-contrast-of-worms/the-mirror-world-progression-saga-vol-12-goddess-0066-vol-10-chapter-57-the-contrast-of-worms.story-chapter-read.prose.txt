From above, from too far, and too slow to change anything, Nyomel watched in horror as the sword of the undead champion was slowed in the final decapitation it might wage on Deikmorn, as his blades trapped it, but, with his blood oozing out of his neck, and entropy clawing across his skin, he weakened almost instantly and the follow-through continued-

No!

Like a bolt of lightning to match the speed and power of the moment, another shape appeared from above — massive in total size — and a blade flashed, instantly recognized as dangerous enough to force the wraith to retreat and jerk away with the blade, snarling in annoyance as he did so. Indeed, it turned out to be a wise act, as the flashing blade effortlessly chopped off half of a shoulder piece.

Odysseus rocketed by on his hippogriff after the swooping pass of his blade, now in the full, gleaming armor of a resplendent Heaven Knight, casting the reflection of the burning star above from his chest. His hippogriff let loose a piercing cry of challenge even as it slowed down and swooped in a loop above. Though the fight raged on, the common zombies paused as one, somehow, and gazed at this, their former champion in another life. Awe was there, though dumbfounded, as if seeing a vision of a past life they couldn’t fully fathom, but felt the echo of.

Felt and feared, as he was now their cardinal opposite.

In the grand distraction and space created, Nyomel took advantage, swooping in to engulf her lover in a telekinetic field and pull him away. Seeing what was happening, anxiety and dread filled her.

“Help!” she called into the gestalt. “He won’t make it to the tents!”

She dragged him beyond the enemy line into safety and dropped herself to his side. Already, Resemblants swarmed and were flinging healing as well as resistance boosts, vainly hoping it would help. Deikmorn, meanwhile, had his swords loose as his sides caught in the telekinetic sheath, and his hands grasping his ruined, blackening neck, his face looking as if under tremendous pressure as he struggled for breath.

Nyomel pulled out a potion worth a small fortune and poured it directly over the neck…

It did nothing. The entropy progressed unabated.

The truth hit her like a dagger in her gut — he was dying, and there was nothing anyone could do. Samantha alone had the power over this extreme, and she was barely aware of the world, barely hanging on to herself. Even with her speed, she might not make it in time if she’d heard the call.

Beginning to sob over his form, trying not to lose it completely, she took his hand and squeezed. “I’m sorry… I failed you…”

Deikmorn was looking up at her, his mouth working, though he couldn’t speak. Instead, he managed to connect through the gestalt, direct to her. The impression, the echo of a mountain of pain, first hit her, and she couldn’t fathom how he was conscious through it. He sent, “I wouldn’t worry; death is an old friend. I’ll see you on the other side, sometime, doll.”

Sobbing uncontrollably, she nodded. “I love you, Deikmorn Brakka!”

The blackness spread. The last thing he did was smile, his teeth coated in blood.

Swallowing her grief and pain and shunting it away, per her training — to which she was never more grateful — Nyomel stood up from her lover’s deteriorating corpse and closed her eyes, centering herself. Focusing. Her hands slowly clenched into fists.

I’ll follow you soon, per the signs. But first…

She opened her eyes and spoke the mystical words of what must be her final act, exposing herself as never before.

Before he could do it himself, Dax commanded in the gestalt, “Everyone, create space, move to the edges, and play defensively!”

She knows what I’m about to do. Sharp.

Odysseus replied, “Some worms instead transform into something greater; others feast on death instead of dying.”

“And which are you?”

“Both.”

Suddenly, a giant, long snake burst from the lines, charging right for the champion. He tried to use his prodigious speed to move away, but such was the snake’s reach, and the way it could fling itself, that its tail came unerringly to engulf him. He couldn’t bring his whip to bear, so he slashed it with his sword. Instead of ending things right there, though, the entropy of the blade seemed only to disintegrate a shed layer of skin, and the snake got bigger. It entangled him and wrapped around.

His armor wraiths quickly rushed forward to aid him.

I don’t think so. I hope the snake can handle this. Odysseus slashed his sword, Cloudbreaker, sending a wave of cascading energy wide enough to engulf the whole of the area. Like a heavy cloud, it fell, hit the ground, and ‘splashed’ outward, hitting friend and foe alike and striking them with lightning-like veins, paralyzing them.

Odysseus was already phasing through the field to his first target — an armor wraith struggling against the substance, functionally like a thick gel to those not greatly resistant or weak. In a storm of slashes, he cut apart the torso armor with his blade, parting every piece like a hot knife through butter. Each of the pieces fell away when ripped apart, and the wraith dissipated when it was shredded enough.

This happened in the merest fraction of a second, and it recharged him to do it again. That they were mere immaterial spirits didn’t matter — that was the sort of reason he used the sword in the first place, and he’d wiped out small armies of spirits this way.

On to the next; he cut apart armor with impunity, none able to move quickly enough to stop him, even at their still-enhanced speeds — because they were, quite simply, arrested. He began a circle, going around the battle of the snake and the champion in the middle, cutting down the armor wraith minions before they could reach their master. Again and again and again, he shredded them, passing fully around twice and half again. Near the end, they began trying to back away, but it availed them nothing. They were far too close together, and he tore them apart all the same.

Meanwhile, the snake was holding the champion and wrestling with him, wisely focusing on preventing the whip from doing anything. Even restrained, even further inhibited by Cloudbreaker’s field, the champion was still able to resist and buck against the aggression, snarling and forcing the sword to touch the snake’s scales and cut. The sword cut the snake again twice, only serving to cause the same as before: a shed skin and a minor growth in size.

The snake, for its part, was biting and absolutely pouring out acrid venom from its fangs, which was quite helpfully dissolving the armor bit by bit, fumes of its substance, and the reaction to the venom rising up like smoke.
