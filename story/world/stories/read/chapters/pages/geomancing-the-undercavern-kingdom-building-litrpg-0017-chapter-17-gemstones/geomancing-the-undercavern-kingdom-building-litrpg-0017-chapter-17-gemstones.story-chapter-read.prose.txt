“Get back to work, you slackers!” Growled a four-armed insectoid with long, moustache-like antennae. “You call this a harvest? Do we look like paupers?”

“No master,” came a weak groan from several miners draped in torn rags.

Pouncing down from a huge rock, the insectoid landed between a dozen ragged humans, their blistered hands weakly clutching pickaxes.

“Lord Hasibara demands his emeralds! Don’t make me tell you again! Put your back into it, soft skins!”

The people could barely talk through their dry lips, murmuring apologies as they struggled to wield their pickaxes with their famished bodies against the hard stone.

The open mines were spread across the floor of a giant cavern, above which dozens of huts were spread across a network of natural bridges and ledges. The four-armed insectoids watched the few hundred human prisoners from above, themselves only numbering a few dozen.

These insectoids were yaddiks. Merciless slave running insectoids who were known for their ability to sniff out gemstones and force other races into slavery to extract them on their behalf. Their carapace was as hard as plate metal armor, and their four arms, usually each wielding scimitars, could turn a melee engagement into a deadly barrage of endless strikes.

Watching from above, the yaddiks rode atop giant beetles—vivek beetles. These hardy, blue beetles were the size of hippos and capable of creating enough lift with their buzzing wings to fly with two average humanoids and gear upon their backs.

Vivek beetles were rare and valuable, even within their natural environment of the Undercavern. They were prized flying mounts, comparable to pegasus and griffons. A status symbol that spoke to the wealth of the yaddiks that ran and ruled the small mine.

Across the cavern, and sheltered amongst stone, Mae looked up from her hiding spot. She carefully eyed her yaddik captors, mentally noting their every movement.

It had been three weeks since James had left, and she couldn't bear waiting another day.

Her older brother promised he would find help, bring with him the knights of Golden Jade City, and save everyone… but she hadn't seen any sign of him or any rescuers since he left.

I'm going to find you, brother! And if anyone dared to hurt you, I swear I'll return the favour ten times!

Mae knew she was being silly. Her brother was two years older than her at eighteen, he was taller, bigger, and had been training with the militia for a few months before those damnable bugs had flown out from their holes in the ground and captured them while harvesting their parent’s fields. There was no way she was hurting anyone who could hurt James.

But that didn't deter her. James had been her rock, and without him, she couldn't bear to go on another day. Whatever was out there, she would face it. She had long forgotten her fear of death.

Patiently, she waited and watched. The dumb bugs couldn’t tell the humans apart. They hadn’t even noticed James or her disappearing; all she had to do was not get spotted escaping.

Waiting for the moment her captors had their backs turned, she sprang free from her hiding spot and sprinted for her escape. It was only twenty-odd meters away, and despite her malnourishment and broken body, she flashed past across the rocky floor in the blink of an eye and dove into the adjoining tunnel.

Pulling herself behind the cover of rocks, she pressed her back up against the cold stone and tried to calm her panting breath. She couldn’t hear a thing over her own racing heart and heavy breaths.

The seconds passed by excruciatingly slowly.

Did they see me?

Gradually, she calmed and her senses refocused on her surroundings. There was nothing. Just silence, and then slowly the monotonous thuds of pickaxes in the distance.

I’m free… they really didn’t see me, did they?

She wanted to jump and celebrate, but the crashing reality of her situation sank in a second later. She was far from free. This was still the Undercavern, and she had no idea how to reach the surface, let alone Golden Jade City.

Okay, calm down, Mae. There has to be a way out. You just need to find it.

Glancing around the tunnel's bend, she stared into the chamber. It was business as usual.

There were several unguarded tunnels connected to the cavern. Such relaxed security might have seemed weird to most, but the yaddiks were so terrifying and had broken down their slaves to such a degree that they just didn't need to bother. That and James had managed to loosen their shackles. Up until her escape, Mae had kept the loosened shackles on, but she removed them before escaping.

Emotions tugged at her. She actually felt bad leaving everyone behind. Why did she deserve to escape more than them? But there was no practical way to save anybody. If she tried, she'd no doubt get caught.

Sorry…

Steadily, she exhaled and turned deeper into the tunnel.

The light of the mining cavern quickly disappeared behind her back, but that occasional, faintly glowing mushroom provided just enough hazy light to make her way through.

She didn’t know which way to go, but perhaps thankfully, there was only one direction she could. The tunnel climbed, dipped, and turned every which way. Her emaciated body felt weaker with every step, but the thought of her brother James drove her onward. She couldn’t give up, not when there was a chance he might still be alive. Not when there was a chance he might need her.

Grunting and hissing, she forced her blistered feet onward. Her thin, torn footwraps provided less and less protection against the hard stone, and she could feel the wet of blood coating them.

Keep going. You can’t give up.

Hours passed, and she grew increasingly delirious. Her bones shook, and her muscles felt just about ready to fall off.

We need predictable monsters to train these men with.
