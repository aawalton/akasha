The congregation broke up soon after, with the fastest of the Asura skyships in Vir’s armada heading for the human realm. Vir, Maiya, Siya and a few dozen other Asura were aboard the lead vessel while a half dozen more airships carrying an additional hundred demons surrounded them in formation.

Cirayus broke off from the group early, heading north to retrieve Saunak from his tower.

With luck, he’d retrieve the Thaumaturge without too much difficulty. Vir knew from experience just how arduous that task could be, but thanks to their long and tangled history together, Cirayus had always held more sway over Saunak than Vir ever could.

Siya leaned over the railing watching Cirayus go, her hair gently drifting in the breeze. Thanks to a clever design and application of wind orbs, the main deck on the airship was largely untouched by the wind despite its considerable speed.

Maiya’s Altani vessels kept pace alongside. Though technically an Altani asset, Maiya was able to appropriate practically any military resource she needed, no questions asked. Such was the clout she had earned within the realm over the past years.

Vir could only pray that it would be enough if the worst came to pass.

The airship soon left the Ashlands behind, the scenery changing into the familiar terrain of the human realm.

The flight to Sonam was the longest single stretch of travel any of them had attempted since the reunification, and Vir spent most of it ruminating over outcomes he didn’t especially care to share.

The rogue Vimana was moving due east, which was the shortest path to the demon realm. That meant the closest Vimana to its projected course was Vimana Kin’jal, which had floated above Sonam since the Age of Gods. Geographically, it was their best hope.

Whether Vimana Kin’jal would respond to Vir’s entry code, however, was an entirely different question.

He had fired a series of prana bursts in a particular order to open the Vimana in the doomed universe, but not only was that a different Vimana, it had been a different universe altogether. He had no reason to believe the same code would work here, especially when it had failed on Vimana Matali.

Even still, it was the only code he had.

Feeling the oppressive prana core once again press against his chest, Vir abandoned his thoughts to disperse the prana once again.

He didn’t know what was more worrisome. A rogue Vimana captained by a man with a hatred for demonkind or Vir’s own worsening situation. The former had the potential to ruin cities, whereas the latter might very well destroy the world.

Vir moved some distance away from the others on the upper deck and began expelling prana into the air.

Thankfully, it wasn’t too long before Sonam came into view with its overbuilt walls and dense, squat buildings. A stark contrast to the beautiful lake and idyllic forest that bordered it to the east.

Vimana Kin’jal hung above the city as always, casting its large shadow over it. Unlike most other Vimana, this one bore more silver and steel on its hull, using gold for accents rather than the main metal. It somehow fit the Kin’jal aesthetic, Vir thought. Or perhaps he had it backward, and the Vimana had influenced the civilization that developed under it, with people staring up at the floating fortress every day for centuries on end.

It was strange to think that this was once hostile territory, considering its shift in recent years. Nowadays, its buildings were painted in every color imaginable, and its people wore outfits one would only have seen in Rani.

While that was the most obvious change from the sky, Vir knew from having walked its streets that a cultural renaissance was well underway, transforming Sonam into a hub of the arts and commerce.

So much so that the Rani Queendom had even approached Ira, concerned that Kin’jal competition was crippling their own economy.

They’d come to an agreement where both countries supported and promoted the development of the arts, but it just went to show how vastly different Kin’jal under Ira’s rule had already proven.

Vir couldn’t wait to see what became of the country in another decade or two.

“I’ve signaled Ira,” Maiya’s voice said over Vir’s communication orb. “They’re expecting us, so we’re cleared to proceed to the Vimana as we please.”

“Thanks Mai,” Vir replied.

That was the other difference. Kin’jal’s arsenal was as good as Maiya’s, and that meant it was practically Vir’s as well, though Maiya would never admit to such a thing in public, of course. It was important for public opinion that she champion human interests above those of demons, in the same way that Vir did for his people.

In reality, they each cared for humans and demons equally.

The fleet came to a halt some distance away while Vir boarded Iteration IV, his personal airship,  and approached the enormous structure alone. Incredibly, Vimana Kin’jal was even larger than the gargantuan Vimana Matali.

Not that size meant much other than its capacity to house people. Vir hadn’t a clue whether its weaponry matched up or outstripped their foe.

“If this works,” Maiya said over the orb, “the rest of the fleet will follow you in. If it doesn’t, well, we’ll regroup and figure something else out. Just be careful, yeah?”

“Understood. And you know I will,” he said with what he hoped was a reassuring smile.

Vir brought the airship to a safe distance away from the Vimana’s hull, level with the activation panel about a third of the way up. Though invisible to the naked eye, through Prana Vision, he could clearly see the twelve nodes arrayed around the hangar door’s perimeter.

He took it as a good sign that the Vimana’s defense systems hadn’t yet activated.

Not wanting to push his luck however, Vir wasted no time in firing a concentrated dart of prana at the first node, then waited a half-second before firing the next one, until he’d finished the code.

If it didn’t work, in the best case, he’d have to come up with another plan. In the worst, the Vimana’s defense mechanisms would target him, and then he’d have to fly away as fast as he possibly could.

He was just about to turn his little ship around when the hull began to split open in front of him, expanding outward as the bay doors parted, golden light spilling through the gap.

Vir heaved a sigh of relief.

By no means were they in the clear—on the contrary, even if they did everything right here, they wouldn’t be—but at least now, they had a chance.

“It worked,” he said into his orb. “Bring the fleet in.”

The hangar was more than big enough to swallow all half dozen vessels, and even then, the vast space felt empty. It made Vir wonder just what sort of craft the Imperium expected to berth here.

Though, with five thousand souls aboard, he supposed there must have been at least a few thousand personal vessels alone, to say nothing of military ships and cargo haulers.

They set down on the deck, and within minutes a hundred Asura warriors and a handful of Altani mejai had disembarked, clearing hall after hall and securing positions before Vir’s arrival.

Though Vir found it exceedingly unlikely there would be anyone aboard, he appreciated their diligence, following after with Maiya and Siya to the central lift in the grand main chamber, which they took up to the command center with several other demons. Most hung back to secure what they could of the massive fortress.

The room was thankfully almost identical to the others Vir had seen, differing only in aesthetics and decoration. A circular projection table sat at the center, and every inch of wall was covered with inscriptions Vir didn’t even try to decipher.

Without hesitation, Siya walked up to the projection table and placed both of her hands on its surface, bringing the console to life.

One after another, displays appeared as she moved her hands across the surface, and soon every panel hummed with prana.

A dozen projections appeared on every wall showing the Vimana, the surrounding airspace, and the land below them.

“All systems are now online,” Siya announced, and Vir didn’t fail to catch the hint of pride in her voice, no matter how well she thought she’d hidden it. “Including weapons and shields.”

Vir had to admit, for Siya to do in seconds what their enemy still hadn’t figured out despite their numbers and the time they had made him swell with pride..

“Thank you, Siya,” he said, squeezing her shoulder. “You truly are a wonder. We are all in your debt.”

Siya had no hopes of hiding the blush that ensued, though everyone did their best not to notice, suppressing their smiles as best they could.

Vir admired the array of displays, hardly believing this stroke of luck.

Vimana Kin’jal, it seemed, was theirs to do with.

“Please set course for the rogue Vimana, Siya,” he said. “As quickly as this Vimana can go. I’d like to intercept it before it reaches the demon lands.”

Siya’s hands were moving across the projected controls even before he’d finished speaking.

“Done,” she said.

“Are… you sure?” Vir asked. “I don’t feel anything.”

It was only when Siya pointed at the speed indicator, which showed a non-zero number, that Vir’s stomach dropped.

Not from the sense of movement, but because this Vimana was just as slow as the other one.

He’d suspected as much, of course. Vimana were many things, but swift was not one of them.

He’d just hoped, however stupidly, that Vimana Kin’jal might be the exception, or at the very least, slightly faster than its counterpart.

The displays in front of him dashed that hope entirely.

Maiya reached the same conclusion he did. “It’s not enough.”

“No,” Vir said, his lips taut. “No, it isn’t.”

They would intercept the enemy Vimana eventually if it slowed or stopped. Barring that, however, they’d arrive well after Vimana Matali reached the demon realm. By the time they caught up, whatever was going to happen there would be long over.

“Well, at least we have a solid contingency now,” Maiya said, attempting to comfort Vir. “That’s far better than what we had just a few minutes ago.”

“Right.”

Vir felt the communications orb at his belt light up, and he brought it out to find Cirayus’ face on the other side.

“Vir? I’ve brought Saunak. Mind if we come aboard?”

“By all means,” Vir replied. “We’ll meet you in the main hangar.”

Siya was already moving toward the lift, and Vir followed her, with Maiya right behind.

If there was any hope for a solution, it was on Siya and Saunak to find it now.
