As he watched a particularly belligerent yellow and gray triggerfish give chase to some purple crabs, Henry came to a realization: fusing Aspects was a lot easier in theory than it was in practice. Perched and camouflaged atop of a reef, he continued watching for a couple more seconds as the purple crabs suddenly turned around and gave the triggerfish chase as soon as they got within reach of their submerged tree-trunk homes, as more of them streamed out in reinforcement.

Letting out a slow breath, Henry shut his eyes and tuned out the images and sensations coming through his blood clones. Especially the one watching over Maurice. The crab was once more hanging out with the farmers, bugging them and helping them out in equal measures. He’d gone there directly after their lesson with Arisia, while Henry had elected to head down to the water and continue his practice.

I can’t believe she called this easy. Then again, she always does that, Henry grumbled inwardly. Arisia’s explanation had been both surprisingly simple and annoyingly frustrating at the same time. It turns out that fusing his Aspects would be as simple as combining the two concepts without having them reject each other. It involved meditation, long hours of thinking and visualizing the possible combinations, and finally, to really trigger the fusion, he had to combine both essences of the Aspects seamlessly, without either of them overpowering the other and be comfortable enough with the process, at which point the option should be unlocked in his interface, and he can begin the process.

The System would sort-of give him a hint that he was ready by unlocking the feature. Until then, he had to keep practicing. As for combining skills, it was a bit similar, although a little more complex. He wasn’t ready to tackle that part, though. Once he managed to mix the essences perfectly, he could then move on to planning out skill fusions and unfortunately, Henry was nowhere near that point just yet.

Looking down at the last concept shard he’d created, Henry snapped his beak in frustration. It was, in a way, as if an indigo-colored gem had grown black, leaky pustules that emanated a haze of ink. His creation—the result of pooling both essences of Misdirection and Swiftness—was breaking down much faster than the stable shards Arisia had allowed him to examine.

Not even close, he thought before chucking the failed attempt away. The triggerfish who’d been chased away by the crab lunged at it, only for it to explode into a cloud of ink.

Alright. One more try, Henry told himself as he sent his consciousness inward, feeling his remaining stores of essences. There were only so many times in a day he could do these exercises. While his mana recovered fast enough thanks to Draining Touch and Mana Nexus, the essence generated by his Aspects didn’t. He liked to compare the Aspects to trees, and the essence of the Aspects to the sap that flowed through the trees. Just like maple trees, you could only get so much sap in a day, and then you had to let the tree recover.

I’m out of essence for Shiftiness and Vitality. Also out of Fury and Hunger, and I have one more attempt to combine Swiftness and Misdirection, he quickly summarized. Henry paused for a moment, and he wondered if maybe, maybe he shouldn’t be pursuing all three all at once. But then again, this was the current next step he’d chosen for his progression. While this was usually done at B-rank, why not doing it now if he could manage it? Arisia and Zerathstra had only said it might be more difficult, but Henry thought that if he consolidated before his next jump in power, he might get something good out of it, to which the two senior said it might be worth a shot.

It just happened to be a little more challenging that he’d expected, even when he had multiple little subminds helping him out. Still, he was sure he’d get there. After all, the fusions were now taking whole seconds before they destabilized. Heck, he even got them to take solid form.

That was still progress. Plus, getting this process out of the way would make the evolution to A-rank easier.

Eyeing the triggerfish as it swam further and further away with its beak empty, Henry took a few more moments to center himself before one more attempt. It had already been a week since the fight against Tevarius, and Henry had had the chance to learn a couple of new things in the time he’d spent with Arisia. One of which was that the evolution to A-rank required one of his Aspects to become the glue that tied them all together, and that would be much easier to do with four fused Aspects instead of seven. Henry had thankfully already fused one with the System’s assistance, but there was no way he’d have enough evolutionary point to give the others the same treatment Thaumaturgy had gotten and in any case, it was good practice.

The final jump to A-rank was bound to be even more of a challenge. There would be no System to assist him then.

Still, when Henry had heard about those tidbits, he couldn’t help but rethink of the System’s presentation in his last evolution. Back then, it had shown him a collection of fragments hovering in the air, held together by the frame that represented his class. In retrospect, it made sense that the final product would be a complete, fused new gem that represented all of his Aspect. Henry just had to slowly melt and fuse everything together one piece at a time.

And by then, we’ll hopefully have some answers about what comes after, Henry thought. Otherwise, he either had to slow his progress when he got to the mid-A-rank levels, or, like Zerathstra, refuse to take the next step. But these were worries for another time.

For now, focus, he told himself. One more attempt, then I’m calling it for today.

Henry wasn’t sure what he was going to do after, but it might be worth heading back up. Hunting and exploring could be nice, but with his Aspects tapped out of their essence, he’d be playing with fire. While he could survive most B-ranks, you never know when an A-rank could show up and ruin his day, and if he was out of essence when that happened, he’d be screwed.

With a deep breath, Henry pulled the remaining essence out of Misdirection and Swiftness into the center of his being, picturing the bright energies collect somewhere in a softly-lit space around his stomach. The essence of Misdirection was like flitting, shifting shadows. A flow of dark ink that morphed and danced its way toward its destination, while Swiftness was like a jet of purple glowing energy that just wanted to flit and teleport toward its target. The two concepts met, and Henry pulled on his Octominds to help him control the fusion, mainly by buffing his focus and keeping away distracting thoughts.

The two energies collided violently at first, and as he recited the meditative note he’d prepared in advance, the essences began to swirl around each other. He pushed his mana to them, both to give them a medium in which they’d mix, and to have something to crystallize later.

Speed and teleportation are part of misdirection. Swiftness is not just about travel, nor is it only about reaching a destination. It’s about the smallest of movements. The speed of my thoughts and reactions. It can be about making sure my enemies never really manage to grab hold of me. Every ink and blood clone can be the real me, and if they get lucky enough to find the real one, then Misdirection would allow me to take another clone’s place. The real combination is not just about speed and misdirection, but it's about blurring the line between what’s real and what’s fake. Just outrunning my pursuers isn’t enough. Not when I can completely neutralizing their perception of me. It doesn’t matter if they happen to discern which body is mine. It doesn’t matter if they know where the real me is. So long as I have clones, I can disappear in their midst. I can be in any one of them. I can’t be caught.

The two essences slowed their rotation and slowly began to merge. The black, ink-like Misdirection gained a purplish hue and accelerated, as if Henry had pressed fast forward. It had done that in the past but this time… something felt different.

Now, stabilize, he softly urged it. Henry maintained the mental space he’d invoked with the note, and he kept his breathing even as the two energies began to separate and re-merge. Small clumps of black ink formed, then they dissolved again as he reasserted the image of the two concepts.

You two are one and the same, he told them. What’s speed without misdirection? What’s misdirection without swiftness?

Henry wasn’t sure how long it took him to coax the essences together, but after a while, he knew instinctively that the process was now coming to an end, so he began condensing the resulting mixture. The cycle of merging and rejection was coming to a stop, and if his experience had taught him anything, this was the moment where he would know if it succeeded or not. Usually, this was where things fell apart. Henry had tried giving the mixture longer before he concentrated it, but that didn’t seem to matter.

Still, he had a good feeling for this one. This time, the essences had mixed faster than usual. Which might be because he was getting a better feel of the result he wanted to see. Henry didn’t know for sure. But he would figure it out, sooner or later.

With a practiced push, Henry coalesced the essence-infused mana a couple of inches in front of his eyes and, without relinquishing control, he pressed his will around it even harder, until the nimbus of dark purple began to take a physical form. It took a few seconds, all in all, and when all of the mixture was crystallized into the shard, Henry took a deep breath and loosened his grip on his Octominds, which made his awareness of his other clones flood into his mind. Already, a few images passed through his inner gaze, and one of them made him immediately frown.

Why was Maurice sitting behind a merchant’s stand?

Henry pulled his attention away from the crab’s shenanigans and examined the shard that fell on his arm.

Huh?

Henry blinked at the… decently formed gem. It looked surprisingly good. Much better than his usual. It wasn’t perfect, but the blemishes and residues of Misdirection were minimal. If he were to put a number on it, he’d say only 5% of the matter hadn’t mixed, which was much, much better than his last attempt.

I’ll be damned, he grumbled to himself. It’s working.

Henry was at a loss for words for a moment. He wished he could make another attempt right away, feeling that he might actually come out with something even better, but he was out of essence and it would take him at least 4 hours to have enough for another shot. Still, this was a win.

Alright, he decided, chucking the resulting gem in his Maw for now. Let’s go see what Maurice is up to. And why he should never sell fish and fruits at the same stand.

Honestly, the fact that the crab had put both meat and fruits on the same damn counter was bugging Henry more than anything else. Focusing on the clone sitting atop the crab, he began channeling mana into the recalling Blink, only to pause when he saw the feed of another clone.

Was that a ship?
