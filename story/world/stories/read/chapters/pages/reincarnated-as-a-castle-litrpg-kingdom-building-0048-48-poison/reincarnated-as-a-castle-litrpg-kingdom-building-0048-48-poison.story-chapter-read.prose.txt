The moment Gopuran came crashing down from above, the poison coated dagger sank deep into the giant bear's neck with a sickening thud.

The beast let out a piercing, agony filled shriek that tore through the forest.

"ROOOOAAAGH!!!"

Dark, steaming blood burst from the wound and splattered across the leaves as Gopuran clung desperately to the knife's handle, refusing to let go. Her body was nearly swallowed by the monster's massive frame, yet her grip remained iron tight, one hand locked on the blade, the other buried deep in its thick fur. Every muscle in her body strained with all the strength she had.

The beast reared up in a frenzy, then twisted violently from side to side, trying to shake her loose. Its claws tore deep furrows into the earth as it thrashed and bucked like a creature gone mad.

But Gopuran held on no matter what.

Failing to dislodge her, the beast grew even more desperate and furious. With a blood curdling snarl, it charged straight toward a nearby tree at full speed.

BOOOOM!

The thick trunk exploded apart under the devastating impact.

Wood splintered in every direction as the entire tree snapped like a twig and crashed sideways through the undergrowth.

But Gopuran was already gone.

At the very last moment, she kicked off the bear's back and flipped away, landing in a low crouch several steps behind it.

The giant beast rolled twice across the forest floor, then pushed itself up, and spun around. Its eyes were bloodshot now, blazing with pure fury. Blood dripped steadily from the wound in its neck, staining the black fur dark and wet.

Every ounce of its murderous attention locked onto Gopuran.

"RUN!" Gopuran shouted toward the other goblins.

But they were still frozen in fear and shock.

The evolved goblin stood there like a green statue, the smaller female still clinging tightly to his back, while the others stared wide eyed at the raging monster.

Gopuran clicked her tongue and cursed under her breath.

"RUN!" she shouted again, this time louder, filled with urgency.

That finally snapped them out of their daze.

The goblins jolted into frantic motion and bolted through the trees, stumbling and crashing over one another in blind panic as they fled deeper into the forest.

The bear noticed the movement immediately, its massive muscles tensed like steel cables.

For one heart-stopping moment, I thought it would chase after them.

But it lunged straight at Gopuran.

The ground shook violently beneath its thunderous charge as it barreled toward her like a living avalanche

High. Much higher than I had expected!

The bear skidded to a halt and immediately spun around.

Perfect.

“Just wait until the poison takes effect!”

The giant bear lunged at Gopuran again with terrifying speed. She rolled to the side at the last possible moment, narrowly avoiding the swipe of its massive claws once more.

The ground exploded where she had stood a split second earlier, sending dirt and leaves flying in a violent cloud.

Then it came again.

Another thunderous charge.

Another furious, bone-crushing swipe.

And once more, Gopuran twisted away at the last instant.

Again.

And again.

Even though the bear was fast, Gopuran was faster!

Time after time, her smaller frame and superior speed allowed her to dance through the trees and brush. She stayed light on her feet, always keeping a safe distance, never lingering in one place for more than a heartbeat.

“Keep it up, Puran!”

Against something this huge and powerful, direct confrontation was pure suicide. But the moment the poison kicked in, we would finally be able to counterattack.

However…

Ten seconds passed.

Then twenty.

Then thirty.

Every single second felt like an eternity.

So why was it still moving? Why did it still look so full of savage energy? Could it be that... we got the wrong mushroom?

But then, finally, after around forty agonizing seconds…

Its movements began to slow.

The bear staggered.

"Yes! It's working!" I exclaimed in joy.

Still, this was not the time to rush in yet.

One mistake here, and Gopuran would die.

Gopuran seemed to understand that too.

Patiently, she bent low and picked up the axe she had dropped earlier. Her other hand raised the small shield into position, and her eyes never left the bear for even a second.

A few seconds later, the giant bear finally gave in. Its legs trembled as it tried to force itself forward.

One step.

Then another.

Its body lurched sideways.

Still, Gopuran did not move.

One second.

Two seconds.

Five seconds.
