The interior was far larger than Veronica expected. A wide chamber stretched before them, filled with neatly arranged tables. Soft mana-filled lanterns lined the walls, casting a warm glow across the room. Overhead, there were large metal fixtures with clear sheets of crystal embedded in them to serve as spotlights. At the far end stood a broad stage framed by deep red curtains over polished wood.

Voices blended into a steady murmur as people began taking their seats; servers went about taking orders and delivering trays of food.

“Wow… it’s so much cooler in here than I thought.”

Veronica gave a small, agreeing nod.

They made their way to the front. One waiter stopped them, but let them pass once Veronica showed them the black VIP card.

Several unoccupied tables remained, some with four chairs, some with three. Gwen chose one with three seats and quickly sat down, smoothing her dress beneath her.

Veronica took the seat opposite her.

From here, the stage loomed close, the lighting dim and moody, giving them an almost intimate view of the stage. The rest of the hall was softly shadowed behind them.

A server came over, placing down two menus.

Veronica opened hers calmly.

Gwen opened hers and immediately her mouth went wide.

“Wow… the prices here are…”

“Pretty cheap?” Veronica asked.

“N-no," she replied, brows furrowing. "They’re too expensive. I mean… a steak for three hundred vix? That’s… I’d be lucky to make that much in one day at the restaurant I work at.”

Veronica hummed. So three hundred is expensive for some meat?

In the future, she’d dined on many delicacies, prices reaching tens of thousands for some of the most trivial dishes. Back in Greystone, sticks of roasted meat on sticks cost as little as three vix.

Where exactly was the line?

What was the normal price of food?

Veronica scowled lightly. She really couldn’t understand it.

Even now, she could clear a simple extermination mission and afford quite a few things on the menu. Though, of course, that didn’t mean everyone could.

Gwen was a healing mage, after all. She wasn’t suited for missions of that degree, without a party, of course.

“Get whatever you want. I’ll cover it,” Veronica said.

“Really? You mean it?” she asked, almost nervously.

Veronica wasn’t the richest right now, by any means. But if it helped push things in her favor, she could afford a bit of a deficit.

She smiled. “If you become my student, that is.”

Gwen chuckled. “Wow. Bribing me already,” she said jokingly. “If I do, I won’t hold back then. I also want a drink. A real drink.”

“Aren’t you just seventeen?” Veronica asked.

She looked up at her. “Are you older than me?”

“Yes,” Veronica replied. She paused for half a second. For a moment, she forgot that she had regressed. “I’m nineteen.”

The number felt strange on her tongue.

Gwen’s eyes lit up. “Perfect. You’re an adult. The legal age for drinking here is eighteen. As long as you’re with me, it should be fine, right?”

Veronica shrugged. Technically, she was right. As long as she was supervised, the girl could have drinks in moderation.

“Just don’t go overboard,” Veronica said.

She smiled. “Yes! Then I’ll have the emberberry liquor.”

Once a waiter came over and took their order, Veronica decided to raise the topic while there was still time before the show.

“So… have you decided yet?” she asked. “Whether you want to become my student?”

Gwen looked at her with a serious expression. Her lips pressed into a thin line as she considered her response.

“You said… you could get the Mage Tower here to take care of Rocky?” she asked slowly. “That he’ll be safe?”

Veronica nodded. “The association won’t be able to refuse any request I make in the near future. The entire organization will owe me a favor soon enough. The Ronswick Mage Tower certainly won’t be an exception.”

She paused briefly.

“Ensuring Rocky’s safety will not be a problem. I can assure you that.”

Gwen seemed to relax slightly, though the concern had not completely left her face.

“And… I get a stipend, right?” she continued. “This would basically be a job too, even if I’m your student? I’ll get paid for expenses and things like that?”

“Of course,” Veronica said. “As I mentioned before, traveling with me is part of the arrangement. I will cover any necessities you might have along the way. Food, lodging, clothing—anything reasonable.”

She added calmly, “In moderation, of course.”

Gwen nodded slowly and began idly stirring the straw in her glass of water.

“And… vacation?” she asked after a moment. “Will I get time off or anything like that?”

Veronica hummed quietly as she considered the question.

“Well… we’ll be traveling most of the time,” she said. “So returning all the way to Ronswick may not always be possible. But if you want a few days to yourself wherever we happen to be staying, that wouldn’t be an issue.”

She hummed, gaze drifting down to the table, seeming to accept the terms. Though another thought quickly crossed her mind as she looked back toward up.

“Are you really able to teach me anything?” she asked. “I follow the Path of Blooming and the Path of Longevity. You’re… I’m guessing the Path of Ruin?”

“Just because our paths are different doesn’t mean I can’t teach you,” Veronica replied. “In fact, I’ve studied the theory and application behind all of them.”

"What?" she asked, blinking.

“My field of research is spell compression,” Veronica continued. “To understand it properly, I had to study the spell structures of every path. They all function differently.”

“Spell compression?” Gwen repeated. “What’s that?”

“Exactly what it sounds like,” Veronica said. “It’s the study of condensing higher-tier spells so they can be cast at a lower tier. For example, I’m currently a Tier-3 mage, but I can cast spells that would normally belong to the fifth tier.”

Her eyes widened.

“You—you can do that? I’ve heard of mages casting one tier above their level, but… two?”

Veronica nodded calmly. “Yes. I’m not exaggerating. Even the head of studies at the Mage Tower here would confirm it.”

She took a sip of her water before continuing.

“Anyway, even if our paths differ, I still have a great deal I can teach you. Practices, theories, techniques… things that most people our age haven’t even heard of. Knowledge that could accelerate your growth by years.”

Gwen stared at her for a moment, her lips slowly pressing together again.

“How do you know this much?” she asked. “You said you aren’t a noble. And you’re only two years older than me, but the way you talk… it feels like there are decades between us.”

Veronica suppressed a small chuckle.

That’s because there are, Gwen.

“It’s a secret of mine,” she said lightly. “One I might share with you in the future—if I trust you enough.”

“Trust me enough?”

Veronica nodded. “When I think you’re ready.”

Gwen frowned. “That sounds incredibly cryptic. Can’t you just tell me now?”

A short laugh escaped Veronica. “No, unfortunately. It’s a rather significant secret.”

“That sounds so cliché,” Gwen replied, crossing her arms.

The moment the words left her mouth, a server arrived carrying their food and drinks.

Gwen had ordered a plate of roasted riverfish, along with the emberberry liquor she had chosen earlier. The drink arrived in a slender glass, its deep crimson liquid glowing faintly in the dim lighting.

She stared at it for a moment with visible excitement.

Veronica, meanwhile, had ordered a small platter of honey-glazed meats served with candied roots and sweet herbs. Her drink of choice was yggdrasil’s tears, a very sweet, green-colored liquor.

The name itself was a bit blasphemous—but Veronica knew it was out of respect, not discourtesy.

As if on cue, the lights in the hall dimmed further. The quiet conversations around the room softened as attention gradually turned toward the stage. Nearly every table was filled with people; by Veronica’s estimate, there were roughly three hundred people here to watch the show.

A man in a well-tailored suit stepped out from behind the curtains. He walked to the center of the stage and tapped a finger against a small bracelet around his wrist. A faint wisp of glowing light formed near his mouth, amplifying his voice so it carried clearly throughout the hall.

“Ladies and gentlemen! Thank you for joining us tonight!” he announced warmly. “We hope the food and refreshments are to your liking. We have four wonderful performers for you this evening.”

Polite applause spread through the room.

“Our first act tonight will be a musical duet from the talented Mattem and Martha. After that, we will have a performance from our in-house violinist, followed by a special dance presentation. More on those shortly.”

He paused briefly, smiling.

“And to close the evening, we will be welcoming one of the Crimson Court’s most beloved performers.”

Gwen leaned slightly closer to Veronica, whispering excitedly.

“That’s probably Nasha.”

Veronica glanced toward the stage but said nothing.

“Please sit back, relax, and enjoy the show,” the host continued. “Let us begin.”

He stepped aside as the curtains parted once more.

Two performers entered the stage, carrying instruments. One held a slender harp while the other took a seat beside a polished piano. The lighting shifted subtly, focusing on them as the hall grew quiet.

Soft music soon filled the room.

Gwen, however, was only half paying attention. Her eyes remained fixed on the glass in front of her. She slowly lifted the emberberry liquor and held it up to the light, watching the deep red liquid shimmer.

“Alright,” she murmured. “Moment of truth.”

Veronica glanced sideways at her.

The moment Gwen took her first sip, her face immediately twisted.

“…that tastes awful.”

Veronica chuckled and calmly took another sip of her own drink.

Her face also soured slightly, though she tried not to show it.

Ugh… this still doesn’t taste right. Damn younger tastebuds.

Time passed as they watched the performances on stage. The first act was… decent, in Veronica’s opinion. She had seen far better during her years attending noble theaters and mage gatherings. Still, the pair performed well enough, and the audience applauded politely when they finished.

The violinist who followed was far more impressive. His fingers moved with practiced precision, drawing long, elegant notes from the instrument. The melody filled the hall with a soft, sweeping rhythm that lingered pleasantly in the air.

Even Veronica found herself listening more closely.

Yet both performances were soon overshadowed by the third act.

Two dancers stepped onto the stage, a man and a woman, moving together in a carefully choreographed routine. The lighting shifted, dimming until the entire hall was bathed in a warm, orange glow. The upper fixtures, all powered by mana, rotated slowly overhead until their light converged on the stage, forming a focused spotlight.

The dance itself was elaborate and intimate. Their movements were close, deliberate, sometimes nearly intertwined as they spun across the polished floor.

It was a bit… exotic for Veronica’s tastes. Not the sort of performance one would see in a noble’s theater.

Still, Gwen seemed completely engrossed, leaning forward in her seat as she watched. Veronica decided not to comment.

By the time the dance ended and the applause faded, their food was gone and only their drinks remained. Veronica noticed that Gwen couldn’t finish her drink past the first several sips.

The final performance was next.

The man in the suit stepped back onto the stage, the light catching neatly along the edges of his tailored coat.

“And for tonight’s final performance,” he announced, his amplified voice echoing through the hall, “please give a warm welcome to the our dangerous red beauty…”

He smiled.

“The Crimson Court Chanteuse… Nasha!”

He stepped aside as the lights dimmed again, the hall returning to its moody crimson glow.

The curtains slowly parted.

Nasha stepped onto the stage.

Veronica’s eyes widened. Beside her, Gwen’s mouth fell open without her even realizing it.

Nasha looked completely different from how they had seen her in the forest. Back then, she had looked like any other rogue or ranger who took adventuring work.

Here, however, her entire presence had changed.

Her dark hair had been styled carefully, falling in smooth waves that caught the red light. She wore a slender crimson dress that fit close to her body, the design unafraid to reveal the long lines of her legs when she moved. Her smile was soft and subtle, yet alluring all the same. She was stunning.

Along her wrist was the same metal bracelet the announcer wore.

With a light tap against the bracelet, a glowing wisp floated near Nasha’s mouth, amplifying her voice.

The hall immediately grew quiet.

Behind her stood a small band of three musicians: one man and two women. Together, they played a deep bass string instrument and a set of low, steady drums that filled the room with a slow rhythm.

Nasha took a slow step forward, her heels clicking softly against the stage.

She drew a calm breath as the music began. Then she smiled, tilting her head slightly as her voice slipped effortlessly into the opening line.

“Careful now, don’t look too long. Some hearts break when the night runs strong…”

Her voice was smooth and velvety. The bracelet’s enchantment carried every word cleanly through the hall. Everyone was quickly focused solely on her and her singing as the spotlights honed in on her.

Unlike the earlier performers, Nasha’s performance was a bit different.

She didn’t remain on the stage.

As the next verse began, she stepped down gracefully from the platform. The soft red light followed her as she moved between the tables, her voice never wavering.

“Some come here for a quiet drink,some come here to forget and think…But if your eyes should meet with mine,you may not leave the same tonight.”

A few guests chuckled softly.

As she passed the first table, she brushed her fingers lightly across a man’s shoulders before gliding past him. At another table, she paused just long enough to take a woman’s hand in hers while continuing the lyric, spinning away in one smooth motion.

At a nearby VIP table, she lifted a playful hand and blew a pair of teasing kisses toward a well-dressed couple.

Soft laughter and scattered applause rippled through the room.

Gwen’s eyes were locked onto her performance, but she leaned toward Veronica and whispered, “She’s… really bold.”

Veronica said nothing, though she gave a faint nod as her eyes followed Nasha moving through the hall.

What the man at the door had said earlier suddenly made sense.

Chanteuse.

Veronica had not expected this kind of performance. Yet it was obvious Nasha possessed talent as a performer. Every movement, every glance, seemed carefully measured and practiced to keep the audience watching her.

The difference was almost startling.

It was hard to believe that this was the same woman they had found in the forest with a stab wound across her stomach.

“The night is young, the lights are low,So tell me something no one knows…Lean in close, don’t be afraid,Some promises were meant to fade…”

As Nasha moved through the hall, her eyes lifted and briefly met Veronica’s as she journeyed back to the VIP section.

There was a spark of recognition there.

Without breaking the flow of the song, she turned and strode slowly toward their table.

The music softened further as she approached, voice lowering ever so slightly.

“And if I find a daring heart…Perhaps we’ll play a reckless part.”

She reached Gwen first and held out her hand.

Gwen swallowed and hesitantly placed her own in it.

Nasha clasped it lightly, smiling as she slowly spun, allowing Gwen to twirl her. She then let go, moving over to the other side of the table.

She stopped beside Veronica’s chair, still singing.

“Split us apart? Says who?”

Nasha then leaned down.

From the audience’s perspective, it looked as though she had simply leaned down to whisper something playful to a guest.

Her hand brushed lightly along Veronica’s shoulder as she bent closer, her lips near Veronica’s ear while she continued singing.

Then she pulled away again, straightening as she delivered the last line of the verse.

“A secret like this is best kept for two.”

The lights shifted, drawing attention back toward her as she began walking through the hall again. The music swelled behind her as she made one final pass between the tables before circling back toward the stage.

Gwen’s eyes were engrossed on Nasha, but still, she leaned close over the table, whispering excitedly.

“What did she whisper?”

Veronica didn’t answer immediately.

When Gwen glanced over, she noticed Veronica sitting strangely still, one hand lightly touching her cheek.

“…Veronica?” Gwen repeated.

Veronica blinked once, still watching the stage as the music built toward the final crescendo, signaling her performance’s end.

“She… didn’t whisper anything.”
