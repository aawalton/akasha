March 21, 2021

Hayden Proctor watched the red numbers on his desk. He’d been watching it for what felt like the last several hours.

Obviously, he knew it couldn’t really have been that long. It hadn’t even been a minute.

4:59.

Hayden knew the number would change eventually. That is what clocks did after all. He was still watching the clock when there was a light knocking on his cubicle wall.

The clock hit 5:00.

“Good, you’re still here.”

Of course.

Hayden swiveled in his chair.

“Technically, you’re not wrong. But I am about to change that, so talk fast.” Hayden grumbled, reaching for his backpack.

His coworkers warned him he should be more careful with Corey. The floor supervisor wore his customary grey wool suit and a black skinny tie. His mid length auburn hair parted cleanly at the side aided by copious amounts of product.

“I wouldn’t get too excited.” Corey said, casually blocking him in. “Brady wants you in his office, he said it couldn’t wait for the weekend.”

Corey’s smug grin wasn’t lost on Hayden. He was the kind of supervisor who worked late on tedious work for optics and then made the rest of the office feel guilty if they weren’t doing the same.

They both worked for the Department of Homeland Security, or more accurately, a very ancillary branch not even officially recognized.

Office of Non-Conventional Risk Analysis, or ONCRA for short. Hayden was convinced the entire branch was propped up to cover government laundering.

“Right.” Hayden replied sardonically.

He ignored Corey and shoved his laptop away, slinging his backpack over a shoulder and awkwardly squeezed between Corey and the cubicle wall. Everyone who wasn’t part of ONCRA knew it was a joke, if they even knew about it at all. Whatever Brady wanted to talk about, it could absolutely wait until after the weekend.

He took a few steps. His cubicle was at the back of the room, and he could see everyone else was already on their way out.

“You know, for someone who wants to get out of here, you really don’t show a lot of initiative.”

Initiative?

Did Corey really just say that?

Hayden rolled his eyes. He’d spent the better part of his day writing hypothetical procedural risks for a zombie apocalypse.

While the rest of DHS was analyzing actual potential threats to our country, he was in a damn basement, analyzing fairytales. He’d slaved away earning his M.A for this?

Hours researching cascade failure models, power concentration models, resource allocation, and Game-theoretic state collapse. He’d expected to pay his dues, but this was ridiculous.

So how long was he really willing to spend here? As much as Hayden hated to admit it, Corey might have a point.

Hayden sighed. Another thirty minutes wasn’t going to kill him.

So, before he could change his mind, he veered off towards Brady’s office peering through doors window. The department head was at his desk, feet up, paperback book resting on his lap.

Hayden tapped the glass, and Brady swung his feet down and waved him into the room enthusiastically. Brady was less polished than Corey and his white-collar shirt had its top button undone, tie pulled loose around the neck, sleeves rolled up.

In his mid-fifties Brady had a youthful look that belied the laugh wrinkles at his brown eyes.

“So, Corey was able to guilt you into staying?” He asked, gesturing for Hayden to take a seat in front of his desk.

Hayden stepped into the room and took the offered seat across from the older man.

“So, it seems.”

Brady seemed to be anticipating the snark and grinned solemnly. The look was just enough to tug at his conscience, and he realized he was being unfair to Brady.

“He said you had something urgent for me?” He said, correcting the former snarkiness.

“Hayden, I know you don’t want to be here.” Brady leaned back in his chair.

“That’s not exactly true.”

“Oh, I don’t doubt that you want to be part of DHS, I mean ONCRA.”

He slapped a pile of papers on the desk that read “Analysis of Strategic Necessity.” and pointed to it.

“Do you know what this is?”

Hayden grimaced. Of course he knew. The whole floor knew.

“It’s Corey’s work on potential zombie apocalypse scenarios, specifically related to high priority points of in the North American continent.”

Corey’s pride and joy. An entire years’ worth of work that had essentially morphed into the man's entire personality. At least twice per week he’d brag about his time briefing the defense secretary himself on the project.

Brady grunted and produced another much, much smaller stack of papers that joined the former.

“How about this?”

Hayden didn’t need to look at the title.

“Alright Brady. You’ve made your point.”

“No, I don’t think I have. What is it?”

Hayden studied the wall, and mumbled.

“The “You’re a wizard Harry” Hypothesis.”

“Can you speak up a bit? I didn’t catch that.”

Hayden shifted a bit uncomfortably but complied.

“The “You’re a wizard Harry” Hypothesis.”

Brady rubbed his eyes. “Do you think that my job is a joke?”

“No, of course I don’t.”

“Are you sure? Because you clearly think that your own job is a joke, I mean seriously Hayden, what is this garbage.”

Well, if he was being honest, after he’d received the project, he did feel his job was a bit of a joke. He also was fairly confident that once he wrote his hypothesis it was going to the back of a drawer somewhere never to see the light of day again.

It was essentially Hogwarts fanfiction.

“I’m sorry sir....” Hayden knew he should keep his mouth shut. It’s just, what did they expect? “But do you really feel my time is best spent considering possible scenarios, should humans someday develop enough to somehow gain magical abilities?”

“I don’t give a single shit how you think your time is best spent.” Brady leaned in. “Can you imagine if someone other than me found this?”

He reached for my hypothesis and fed it to the shredder. He’d no doubt already deleted the file from the database. The copy he was shredding was part of the redundancy policies.

“It is not your job to decide what is relevant or not. Your job is to provide our government with essential source material for the scenarios given. That’s it.”

“But...”

“No, dammit. Maybe, and that's a huge maybe, if you can show yourself competent enough to provide anything even remotely adequate, you’ll find yourself working on projects that you consider more worthy of your precious time.”

Brady stuck his finger in Hayden’s face.

“But until that day comes, you will either shut up and do your job, or you can go find something else.”

Hayden slumped in his chair.

“Now, if you decide you can live with that, I’ll give you one last chance to get this right. But if I get even a single whiff of you not taking it seriously, I will personally see that you never work another day in DHS ever again. Are we clear?”

Hayden nodded awkwardly, feeling small.

“Good.”

And with that Brady pushed back in his chair and kicked his feet back up onto his desk.

“Close my door on your way out.”

Hayden stood up mechanically, and shuffled out, pulling the door shut behind him with a crisp “click”.

He glanced up at the uniform black and white hand clock on the wall.

5:13.

From across the room, he could feel Corey staring from over his cubicle wall. The truth was nobody would ever use “Analysis of Strategic Necessity.”

It was near statistical impossibility. They didn’t have Corey write it because the government was preparing for an impending zombie apocalypse or had insider information they were hiding from the rest of the world. It was barely even a contingency.

And yet, he took it seriously.

Hayden made his way back to his own cubicle and pulled his laptop back out of his back. He opened it to a fresh word document and watched as the cursor pulsed.

No one would ever read this outside of ONCRA. No one was ever going to spontaneously gain superhuman magical powers.

But if this was Hayden’s ticket out, he would right the best damn thesis that bastard ever laid eyes on.

His fingers moved, typing the title of his thesis.

And thus, “The Directorate of Equitable Power” was born.

***

Three months post-mana induction.

The President of the United States died exactly five days into the introduction of mana on earth. It was determined that the risk of entering the trial, even with the secret service, was too much of a threat.

Instead, the Vice President entered at exactly four and a half days in. There were a few groups of cabinet members who immediately entered the trials together that strongly encouraged the president to enter the trial.

They were ignored.

So, after five days, the president and all his secret service members entered the trials solo once the five-day grace period expired. Only one survived.

With the President dead, Vice President John Carter was sworn in. The system respected the decision, and President Carter became the first faction leader of the free world.

And yet, there was little left.

The east coast was a jumbled mess.

New York City, Philadelphia, Boston, Baltimore, and every other major city, gone. Same with every other human technology. Guns, tanks, planes, cars, and electricity all vanished.

Humans were forcibly thrust directly back into the stone age. A stone age where magic and monsters were all suddenly real.

Millions of humans all crammed tight in skyscrapers were now spread across tens of thousands of territories all filled with dungeons and monsters.

From old D.C., a massive reclamation project began.

President Carter made it his priority to unite the surrounding factions under the banner of the United States government. It meant dealing with literally thousands of raids as territories were claimed.

After months of conflict and assimilation, millions flocked to the rising power of the United States. It hadn’t happened overnight but slowly but surely people were starting to rebuild.

Now what was left of the United States government congregated in the newly minted house of representatives.

Hayden fidgeted in his seat.

In front of him sat some of the most influential people in the old world. He recognized many of their faces from television and internet streams before those technologies disappeared.

To his left, the Faction leader of the United States, President Carter sat calmly.

“We’ll now hear from President Carter, who will introduce our guest.”

The air shifted in the room into one of anticipation. A hand rested on his knee.

“Looks like I’m up. Don’t be nervous, they’re all a bunch of blowhards once you actually get to know them.” The president whispered before taking his place behind the podium.

Sitting here reminded Hayden of when he was in high school waiting to give his commencement speech. It helped that they were actually in an old high school auditorium. It was one of the rare buildings from the old world that happened to make it through the change.

Hayden used his lap to dry the sweat from his palms and tried to use his breathing to slow his racing heartbeat.

It helped, and he turned his attention to the tall balding man now addressing the crowd.

“Thank you, Allen, for setting the agenda today. And thank you everyone for attending today.”

John Carter, former CEO of American Trust Financial chosen as VP for his proven financial experience and his approachable easy-going demeanor.

“I’d like to extend a special welcome to the representatives from the newly joined Trenton faction. We’re happy to have you on board.”

The President led a polite round of applause.

“We are always grateful when we can peacefully assimilate new members back into this great country. What a world we live in..... There should be more here.”

He scanned the crowd and shook his head.

“Every single day, the brave men and woman of this country risk their lives fighting monsters outside our borders to save as many human lives as we possibly can. But what happens when the threats no longer only exist from outside our borders?”

President Carter let the question linger in the room before continuing.

“Yesterday morning, a man, no different than you or I, woke up and decided he’d had enough. He rolled out of bed, laced up his shoes, walked twenty or so steps to where the neighboring family still slept peacefully and, with the family still inside, incinerated their home in seconds.”

Hayden was already briefed on the incident, but hearing it again, combined with the shouts and gasps from the members of the room reignited his feeling of disgust.

“The man needed no gun, used no propellant, and made no plans. He used nothing but his hands...well, his hands, and the use of mana.”

At the mention of the word, there was a palpable shift in the room.

“Twelve more people died before the psychopath was finally put down. Sadly, this was not an isolated incident. In just three months, dozens of incidents just like this have occurred, right in our own backyard. Ladies and Gentlemen, we are in unprecedented times. We are witnessing a shift, the likes of which the great founders of this country could ever have imagined in their wildest dreams.”

The President, turned around, gesturing directly at Hayden.

“Which brings me too why this young man is here with us today. Hayden Proctor was hand-picked straight out of Harvard, to assist the DHS in analyzing incredible situations just like the one we find ourselves in today. For the last several years he and his department head have worked tirelessly, becoming the foremost experts on what they call “The Directorate of Equitable Power”, a robust and bold new system of governance tailored to our unique needs.”

Hayden narrowed his eyes at the Presidents back.

If they weren’t in front of over a thousand people, Hayden might’ve tried to correct the Presidents blatant embellishments of his qualifications. Hand-picked? Hayden took the job because it was the only option available at the time in the DHS.

He supposed, given the situation, he understood the reasoning. The President was applying credibility where Hayden had none.

With a subtle wink, he gestured for Hayden to stand and join him at the podium. As soon as Hayden arrived, he wrapped his arm around his shoulder.

“Are you ready?”

The President whispered. Hayden gulped but bobbed his head in confirmation.

“Let me introduce to the floor, Hayden Proctor.”
