All night long, I watched as Greg typed and retyped his letter.

He started with, “I have made mistakes.” Then deleted it. Tried again, “During my tenure as CEO, I’ve had to make some hard decisions.” Delete. “There has been–” Delete. “Due to extenuating circumstances there is some information that will be coming out that paints me in a particular light.”

“Oh, what a fuck nugget.” I muttered.

After almost a full minute the cursor stopped blinking and Greg highlighted the whole thing and deleted it.

Good. At least we weren’t going down this path.

He started again, “Recent allegations regarding my conduct.”

I groaned, and took a gulp of my coffee. This guy better get it together.

He deleted and started over again, “I have recently become aware that some of my actions could be perceived.” He deleted that one before even finishing that sentence.

Greg opened his internet browser and checked his bank account. He opened up his checking account followed by his savings.

I leaned in closer to my screen, “What are we doing here?”

Another tab opened and he google searched a flight purchasing website. He clicked the first booking site that came up. He selected one way.

“Oh no… We’ve got a runner.”

He searched in the destination box. Mexico. Delete. Aruba. Delete. Switzerland.

“Oh Greg, are you picking these based on the world’s most obvious places to run to?”

He opened another tab and typed, ‘countries without extradition to the united states.’

“Oh, come on. Seriously?”

I closed the tab. After a moment he opened it again. I closed it again.

He minimized the browser and opened his finance folder. He selected everything in the folder and right clicked selecting delete. When the ‘Are you sure you want to delete 2,820 items?’ message came up I overrode and selected ‘No.”

He tried an additional two times before I sent him a song to his iphone. “No” by Megan Trainer played out and he began writing his confession letter again. The tab that he had open for international flights was silently closed out.

“Good. Glad we understand each other.” I monitored the rest of the night until he finished attaching his files to the email and sent it out in the morning.

To whom it may concern,

I can no longer live with what I’ve done.

For years, I’ve used my position at Harrison & Sons Design to take advantage of the people who trusted me. I’ve manipulated, threatened, and paid off anyone who tried to hold me accountable. I’ve destroyed careers to protect my own. I’ve hurt people who deserved better.

Attached you’ll find documentation of my actions. Emails, financial records, signed agreements. Everything you need to understand the scope of what I’ve done and who I’ve hurt.

I’m sending this because the guilt has become unbearable. Because I’ve realized that no amount of money or power is worth destroying more lives.  Because the people that I’ve wronged deserve to have closure.

I am regretful of my actions and I am ready to face consequences.

Greg Harrison CEO, Harrison & Sons Design.

The news broke faster than I expected. Kate was providing me live updates from the office.

Kate: Something’s going on!!

I rubbed my tired eyes. I’d only been asleep for maybe two hours but the text from Kate caused my heartrate to spike. I hadn’t expected to hear anything until the evening if I heard anything today at all. I decided to play it cautiously.

Me: What do you mean?”

Kate: They called me in to cancel my PTO. The c-suite has been in the conference room all morning… They posted security in the hall!!

Fuuuuccccckkkkkk. So much for it not negatively impacting Kate.

Kate: Shit they just called everyone to a meeting!!!

Oh shit.

Me: You had to cancel your PTO??

Me: Keep me updated.

I waited for well over an hour before the next reply came in. I gave up on sleep and sat on my couch doom scrolling.

Kate: Check the news

I turned on the tv and simultaneously pulled up the news on my phone.

Me: What am I looking for

Kate: You’ll know…

I found the article pretty quickly. The headline read “Harrison & Sons Design CEO Confesses to Years of Workplace Abuse.”  My stomach somersalted. I felt a little like I was dreaming.

And then I read it.

The article mentioned more than I figured it would. Documents, NDAs and emails were all released with names blocked out. There was a disclaimer at the bottom, “We are still reaching out to the alleged victims to verify and collect statements.”

Me: Holy shit. What?

Kate: I’ll call tonight…

Tonight. Tonight was forever from now. I spent the rest of the day checking and refreshing every news outlet that he sent the email to. By that evening the story was trending. Every social media had it plastered everywhere.

Former employees were reposting it on linkedin.

My mother rang shortly there after. I knew this would happen, I’d prepared for this. I took a deep breath and answered the phone.

“Hello.”

“Oh thank God. We’ve all been so worried.” Her warm fake overly caring voice purred through the phone. I could almost hear her fake veneered smile. Fake concern, fake happiness, fake teeth, fake lips.”

“What were you worried about mom?”

“You of course! Your Aunt Catrina and I were just on the phone when we saw the news about Greg Harrison. You never returned my call after Aunt Harriet told me that you’d been fired.” Her voice became curt at the end of the sentence.

She took a breath and returned to her honey sweet purr. “I realized that you must’ve been a part of what that horrible man did. No wonder you didn’t call me back, you must have been so distraught and ashamed. Don’t worry honey. Your aunts and cousins will all be super understanding about your situation this holiday.”

I started to talk but she cut me off before I could get anything out. “Like I was saying sweetie, I was talking with Aunt Catrina just a moment ago and we’re going to be organizing a prayer circle this Sunday.”

“Well gee, mom. That’s really thoughtful, but you know I’m not religious.”

“It’s not for you, dear.” The way she hissed the word ‘dear’ made it sound almost like a curse.

“Oh good, I thought–”

“It’s for me of course. The amount of stress you’ve put me through, I’ve barely been able to eat dinner. I expect you to make this up to me. Bring your boyfriend to Thanksgiving. Okay dear. Nice chatting. I need to get back to Catrina on the other line. Ta.”

The line clicked and I realized that my vision had begun to darken, and that I was gritting my teeth and holding my breath.

The evening news featured a statement announcing that “Greg had willingly taken a paid leave of absence.”

Kate called me shortly afterward.

“Sam.” Her voice was tight. “Did you see the news?”

“Yeah. How are you doing?” I kept my voice as concerned as possible.

“I don’t know Sam. This entire thing is insane. They had me come in first thing this morning. They cancelled my PTO. Greg has been preying on people at work.” She paused. “He never did anything to you, did he?”

“Nothing other than fire me, don’t worry.”

“Did you do this?”

“How would I have done this?”

“I don’t know. I don’t know anything anymore. It’s just… our conversation yesterday got me thinking. It’s probably just paranoia. But, maybe you spoke it into existence. Karma and all that.”

“People do have crises of conscience sometimes, right?”

“Do they? Do people who would do that kind of thing have a crisis of conscience.”

I thought about what I’d found in his files. The meticulous record-keeping, the almost serial killer vibes of the trophies in that finance folder. The lack of remorse at anything he’d done to me, or anyone else.

“Maybe the guilt got to him. Or maybe something else happened. He doesn’t seem like the remorseful type.”

“Yeah.” Kate was quiet for a long moment. “Yeah maybe. I just, I don’t know what to think anymore.”

“Do you want to meet for coffee tomorrow?”

“Yeah, yeah I’d like that. Our usual place?”

“See you at noon.”

I hung up and stared at my phone. Kate suspected, or she knew something was off.  She wanted to believe me. But at the end of the day, he’d confessed. I hadn’t really done anything other than encourage him to do the right thing. Certainly she couldn’t blame me for that.

Right?
