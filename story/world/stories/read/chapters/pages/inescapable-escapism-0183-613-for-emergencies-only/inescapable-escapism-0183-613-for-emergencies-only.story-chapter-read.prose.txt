I laughed, my gaze flicking towards Seth. I hadn’t intended to go to the Academy, and I could still feel myself lounging on my bed in my reality, the book still clasped in my hands, but I had no desire to go back. All I would do if I returned was continue reading, and the story was okay, but it wasn’t gripping me.

But I was trying to persevere. I was attempting to stick with it and finish the story, and it had gotten better. It was more interesting, and at least that meant I was less tempted to allow myself to be whisked away to that other world I’d so briefly visited. Some of the allure still lingered, though. Part of me longed to return and see if I’d made it to the summit, whether the dragons had accepted me and allowed me to pass the first trial, but I forced myself to ignore that urge.

It was impossible to resist the call of the Academy, though. I didn’t even feel myself starting to slip into that world until I was there, and by that point, it was too late. I could have returned to my reality, but I didn’t want to do that either.

“Right?” Seth asked, his voice still shaking with laughter.

I had no idea what he was talking about. My mind felt half-awake and muddled from my sudden return to the world, and I could have searched my memories to find out, but I was too tired. It didn’t seem particularly important anyway. I knew I could get away with not knowing.

“Yeah!” I agreed.

Seth shot me a grin, and my chest tightened. It wasn’t the same as when Duncan smiled at me. Seth’s was smaller, slightly more reserved, but it still made my heart pound and my breathing hitch.

I had to look away. If I held Seth’s gaze for any longer, he’d realise something was up. He’d notice the way I was gazing at him and work out what I was thinking, and I couldn’t have that. Instead, I quickly scanned the room.

We were in the canteen, I realised, but it wasn’t very busy. There were only a few scattered groups dotted around the tables. It seemed to be breakfast time, if the food on my tray was any indicator. I had half a croissant and the remains of a piece of vegetarian bacon that I’d taken a bite of before realising I didn’t like it.

Glancing at Seth out of the corner of my eye, I reached for the croissant and tore a piece off. It peeled off easily, the outer layer so crisp it shattered into shards, and a smile pulled at my lips. The food at the Academy was always so good, and the pastries were no exception.

Eagerly, I swiped the piece of croissant over the small mound of butter on my plate before dipping it in the raspberry jam and shoving it in my mouth. My eyes fluttered shut as I chewed, relishing just how delicious it was.

“So, what do you have first today?” Seth asked, interrupting my thoughts.

Embarrassment crashed into me as I opened my eyes quickly and looked at him, worried that he’d noticed how absorbed I was in the food, but he didn’t seem to have. He didn’t look confused or weirded out, which sent a wave of relief through me.

“Uhh,” I said, buying time as I racked my memories. Luckily, I had been in the world for a little longer by that point, and the answer came to me easily. “Observation and memorisation.”

“Oof, rough,” Seth replied with a sympathetic grimace. “Hopefully it’s not too bad today.”

I sent him a doubting look. It wasn’t that I didn’t like the class. It was fine. I just always found it… intense, and I hadn’t expected that. When we were in the induction wing, it had never been that bad. It was tough, obviously, but that was fine. However, now that we were out in the main building, it had become worse. I always left the class drained, and my brain felt mushy. It seemed as though I’d pushed it to the limits.

That was true, kind of. I was pushing myself in that class. Although I felt like I was a fairly observant person, I wasn’t used to having to focus on every single little detail and immediately memorise it, and that was exhausting.

The teacher was understanding, though. She’s reassured us that it was a skill that took time to master, so not to worry too much when we couldn’t recall something she asked us about, but I still felt awful every time, and that didn’t help.

“We’ll see,” I said darkly. “What do you have?”

“First aid.”

“Lucky.”

Seth looked down to hide his smirk. He was silent for a few seconds as he shoved the last of his waffle into his mouth and chewed before speaking again.

“What do you have after? Tell me it’s something less awful at least?”

I cocked my head to the side, trying to remember. I was pretty sure it was something I was looking forward to, but it took me a moment to recall.

“Cooking,” I said.

“No way!” Seth cried, his face lighting up.

“What?”

“I have cooking then too!” he told me. “Wait, you’re in the kitchens on minus one, right?”

“Uhh… I think so,” I said, reaching for the timetable in my pocket and scanning it quickly. “Yeah, I am.”

“Me too, obviously,” Seth said with a laugh. “I guess that means we’re in the same class for something after all!”

“Yeah!”

I grinned at him, glad yet surprised that we did have a class together. We’d compared our timetables a few evenings ago and hadn’t noticed any, but I was pretty sure we hadn’t missed it. Surely, one of us would have spotted it.

“Did it change?” I asked before quickly clarifying, “I mean, did you get a new timetable or something?”

“Rodgers brought it to me this morning,” Seth explained. “Apparently, some things were moved around, or some groups were going to be smaller than they thought, so they’re having to combine some classes or something?”

A hint of worry edged into my heart, and I felt my eyebrows draw together.

“Oh, really?”

Why would they happen? Had more people failed the induction than they expected? Surely, they didn’t intend for anyone not to make it through, right?

“Yeah,” Seth said, concern clouding his expression for a moment before he wiped it away. “I wonder if we have any other classes together now.”

That thought pushed away some of my worry, and a tentative smile tugged at my lips as I reached for the timetable I’d shoved in my pocket that morning. There was no need for me to carry it around. I’d already memorised it, but anxiety forced me to bring it with.

“Oh, maybe!” I replied as I pulled it out.

Seth smiled at me as he pushed his tray back and pulled out his timetable before glancing up. His gaze landed on the clock on the wall, and his hands fell still.

“Ah… we should probably get to class, actually,” he said reluctantly.

My eyes flicked towards the clock as well, and I felt my posture sag slightly. He was right. We still had ten minutes or so until it started, but we both liked to get there early. The thought of being late made me too anxious to risk it, and although I’d been in the main building of the Academy for a while, it still felt like a maze to me at times.

“Yeah, we probably should,” I agreed, my disappointment audible.

“We’ll check them at lunch, though,” Seth suggested as he started to stand.

I nodded.

“Yeah, okay.”

My legs were stiff as I stood, my muscles still aching from some strenuous exercise I’d done the day before that I barely remembered, but the pain subsided as I followed Seth towards the buffet counter again. We put our trays away before making our way out of the canteen.

As we reached the foyer, I couldn’t help but glance towards the giant front doors. They were open, as they always were, and I felt my steps slow for just a moment as I stared out, surprised by how beautiful it looked out there. It just seemed like such a nice day. The sky was a bright, vibrant blue, and I couldn’t see a single cloud.

Immediately, I was struck by the sudden desire to be outside, but I couldn’t. All of my classes were indoors that day, so the only time I’d be able to be out in the sun was at lunch, and I wasn’t sure what to do about that. I didn’t like eating outside. There were too many bugs, and I was always paranoid that one would land in my food and I wouldn’t notice until it was too late.

But maybe it would be worth it, I mused. Perhaps I’d put up with it. If it meant I could spend just a little bit of time outside, I could deal with it.

“Do you want to take the long way?” Seth asked as we neared the far side of the foyer.

I glanced at him in confusion.

“What?”

There was only one way to get to class, and it was straight down the corridor in front of us, so I had no clue what he was talking about.

A small smile pulled at Seth’s lips, and he came to a stop.

“I mean, there’s a door next to the botany classroom, right?” he asked. “The fire door?”

I fell still as well, hesitation washing over me.

“Yeah, but…” I started before trailing off.

Fire doors were only meant to be used for emergencies. Everyone knew that, and the locks and security systems on that fire door were just as scary as the door in front of me, so it felt dangerous to even risk using it, but I had seen other people going through it. I’d definitely spotted trainees using the door without any fear or hesitation, so… maybe it was fine at the Academy.

“It’ll probably be open, right? Like, I doubt it’s locked,” Seth said, but he sounded unsure.

“Yeah, I doubt it. I mean, I don’t know,” I replied, indecision still pulling at me.

Seth glanced over his shoulder at the classrooms before looking back at me.

“I mean… we could just go that way and see if the door is open,” he suggested. “If it’s locked or something, we can come back and go the normal way. We still have a little bit of time until class starts, so it’s not the end of the world if we have to do that.”

Once again, I knew Seth was right. We wouldn’t be late if we had to double back, but I was still unsure. As much as I wanted to be outside, the thought of going through the fire door worried me. It was silly because I knew other people did it, and they had been fine, but I was scared of getting in trouble.

Perhaps they were allowed to use those doors, a small voice in the back of my head whispered. Maybe that was a benefit they were granted after being in the Academy for a certain amount of time?

I doubted that, though. That didn’t seem like something they’d do. There was no reason for it, and every single other rule in the Academy made sense. There was always a reason or explanation for things, so I knew if other trainees were allowed to do it, we should have been too.

Plus, Seth looked so hopeful. It was clear he was trying to hide it, but I saw the way he glanced towards the front doors. His gaze was so longing, and I didn’t want to be the reason he couldn’t do what he wanted to. I would have felt awful for stopping him, especially because I didn’t really have a good reason for it.

“Okay,” I said, still not entirely comfortable with my decision.

Seth’s face immediately broke into a smile.

“Yeah?” he asked, taking a small step towards the doors.

“Yeah,” I repeated. “You’re right. We have enough time to go this way if needed.”

He beamed, causing happiness to flutter in my chest as I followed Seth towards the front door, and I couldn’t help but feel glad I’d agreed to his plan. As soon as I stepped through the door, I felt that gratitude grow even stronger.

A contented sigh slipped from my lips as the sunlight caressed my face, and I had to fight the urge to fall still once more. I longed to shut my eyes and bask in the warmth that immediately wrapped itself around me, but I forced myself to continue walking.

“Damn, it’s so hot today, isn’t it?” Seth remarked.

“Yeah. I didn’t realise it was this warm.”

“Right? Like, the aircon in the Academy is so good, I had no clue,” he said with a laugh. “I love the summer, though.”

I glanced at him out of the corner of my eye, taking in the easy smile that had settled on his lips.

“Oh yeah?” I asked.

“Yeah. It just makes me want to, like…” he trailed off, his expression thoughtful. “I don’t know, go swimming or something.”

“I mean, you have swimming later, right?” I pointed out as we walked along the side of the building.

Seth laughed.

“Yeah, but that’s not the kind of swimming I meant,” he said. “I want to do… fun swimming. You know, just messing around, not…”

“Fighting against that damn wave machine?” I suggested, finishing his sentence for him.

A snort came from Seth, and I felt my smile widen.

“Yeah, exactly. It’s not exactly the most relaxing experience, you know?” he asked.

“Yeah, I do.”

The corner of Seth’s mouth twitched into a smirk before his eyebrows rose.

“Oh, shit! I didn’t tell you!” he cried as the fire door came into view. “I got to the end yesterday.”

My eyes widened, and I felt my mouth drop open.

“What?”

Somehow, Seth had managed to reach the end of the pool, beating that damn machine that imitated riptides, and I had no clue how he’d done it or how he’d not mentioned it before. If I’d been the one to do it, I was pretty sure I would have told him immediately. It felt like an impossible feat, and I’d yet to see anyone make it there.

“Yeah! I mean, I feel like it doesn’t fully count,” he explained. “I got caught by the current right at the end, and it slammed me into the wall. If Jenna hadn’t turned the machine off so quickly, I fully would have lost my grip and gone under.”

He laughed, but I didn’t join in. I was still too surprised that he’d actually done it.

“That still counts!”

“Kind of,” he allowed. “Although I have this giant bruise already forming all the way down my side. It looks so gross.”

“Oh no!”

“Yeah, it’s so dark already,” he told me as he reached for his shirt.

My eyes followed his movement, watching as his hand closed around the fabric and started to lift. His shirt had barely lifted before he froze, his gaze flicking to my face, and he let out an awkward chuckle.

“I… hope it feels better soon?” I said, sending him a smile that felt like more of a grimace.

I looked away quickly, feeling my cheeks begin to heat up. I knew that I was blushing, and it was silly. I had no real reason to feel quite so flustered. Seth hadn’t actually lifted his shirt or anything.

But I kind of wished he had. I wanted to see the bruise, I lied to myself. I was worried about him, and I wanted to check that he was okay. That was the main reason, at least. It wasn’t just that I also did kind of want to catch a glimpse of his body. That would be weird. It would be creepy, right?

“Thanks,” Seth said without meeting my gaze, before muttering, “Sorry.”

His apology caused my cheeks to burn even hotter.

“For what?” I asked, trying to pretend I hadn’t noticed just how red I’d turned.

“For… you know,” he replied before looking up. “Oh, there’s the door!”

His tone was relieved, as if he were glad we were so close to it, and I was too. I felt so awkward, and the urge to apologise to him as well was burning within me, but I was worried that it would just make things weirder.

“Oh, great!” I said, hurrying towards it.

We both reached towards the handle at the same time before freezing and laughing.

“Sorry,” Seth apologised again. “Please.”

He gestured towards the door, but I let my hand fall to my side.

“No, no. It’s fine. You do it.”

Seth began to reach for the handle again before glancing at me.

“Are you just letting me do it because you’re worried the alarm is about to go off or something, and you don’t want to be the one to cause it?” he asked.

That hadn’t been my reason. I just felt awkward and wanted to let him go first, but as soon as he mentioned it, that worry came back to me. It wasn’t just the alarm that concerned me, though. That would be the least of our problems. It was the security system that we should be worried about.

Rodgers had explained that to me early on. I’d seen the demonstration of what the building could do to defend itself and the people inside against intruders, and I was scared of triggering it. It was smart, though. The whole thing was much more high-tech than anything else I’d ever seen, and the chances of it thinking we were trying to break in were low. Rodgers had said it had never made a mistake, and he was pretty confident that it wouldn’t. I had to trust that.

“I mean…” I said, pushing my worries aside and sending Seth a smirk. “It was your idea, after all.”

Seth pursed his lips, his eyes narrowing at me.

“Fine,” he muttered after a moment. “I guess it was, but if the alarm goes off, I’m running.”

Despite my anxiety, I laughed.

“I’ll race you,” I told him.

He snorted softly before letting his hand close around the metal handle. My heart seemed to skip a beat as I watched Seth take a deep breath to prepare himself before pulling the door open just a crack.

I couldn’t breathe. It felt as though time stood still as we waited for the alarm to sound, but it didn’t. We were met only with the chatter of voices that spilled from the hallway ahead, and I let out the breath I was holding.

Seth looked at me and shrugged.

“I guess it’s fine?” he said.

“I guess,” I replied, feeling relief crash into me.

It was so strong that my head felt as though it was swimming. My knees were weak, and I almost fell as I followed Seth through the door into the building once more. I longed to collapse into a heap and stay there until my heart returned to a normal speed, but I could not.

“Oi! What are you two doing? You shouldn’t be here!”

Panic slammed into me, immediately flooding my body. The relief I’d felt moments before was ripped away from me, and my stomach leapt into my throat. Ice gripped my heart, spreading through my body as I spun around to face whoever it was that had shouted at us.

“What?” Seth asked.

“You two shouldn’t be using this door,” Rodgers told us as he pulled it open and joined us in the corridor. “Don’t you know it’s a fire door? For emergencies only?”

I couldn’t breathe. My chest was heaving, but it wasn’t helping. I felt as though I were being choked, or a heavy stone had been dropped on me, and I couldn’t push it off. No matter how hard I tried, I couldn’t take in enough oxygen.

“Surely you shouldn’t be using it either, then?” Seth pointed out.

A good-natured grin appeared on Rodgers’ face.

“I would never,” he said, despite having just come through the fire door.

He was joking, I told myself. It was obvious he was. We weren’t really in trouble, and everything was fine. I knew that, but I was still terrified. I could feel adrenaline pulsing through my body, and I was on the brink of hyperventilating or bursting into tears. Panic was welling up inside me, the bubble getting bigger and bigger, and it was going to burst. Soon, something would cause it to pop, and then what?

I was dizzy, I realised. The lack of oxygen was starting to make the corridor around me spin, and I tried to inhale slowly, but the best I could do was a gasp. I tried again, but that breath was even more shallow. The urge to run slammed into me. I had to flee, to get away from there, but I was frozen. My body refused to listen to me, and I couldn’t move at all. I was stuck. Trapped.

Voices sounded around me, but I couldn’t make them out. They were distant and warbling. It seemed as though the people speaking were far away, too far for me to reach, and I tried to look around, tried to glance at Seth, but the movement caused vertigo to close around me. White spots burst in my vision, blinding me, and I tumbled backwards into nothingness.
