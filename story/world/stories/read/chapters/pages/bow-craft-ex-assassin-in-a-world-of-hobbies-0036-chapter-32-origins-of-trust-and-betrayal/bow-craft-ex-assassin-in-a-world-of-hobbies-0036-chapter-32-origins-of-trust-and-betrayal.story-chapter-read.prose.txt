Chapter 32Origins of Trust and Betrayal

Dane had agreed to take a message back to Solace and hopefully bring back some help to deal with the outsider. “Be careful out there, alright?” he said, waving goodbye as he and his carriage vanished around the curb.

Craft turned around and beheld the entrance to the trail. It was just dirt, rocks, and a wall of green, yet he found himself needing to summon courage to start the trek. Though the forest was thick enough to feel hostile, and the trail was too narrow for a gork to maneuver, it wasn’t the place nor the trek that bothered him, but the fact that he was heading in with a teammate. He saw flashes of scenes where he’d point his finger at her, accusing her of losing her mind, thus condemning her to die.

But this wasn’t his other world anymore, and here, there wasn’t supposed to be any risk associated with getting close to his teammates. “At least we won’t be needing any machetes,” he remarked.

Just as he was about to continue, he noticed Lei-rei had stopped, too. All this time, they had been shoulder-to-shoulder, frozen, staring at the mouth of the forest.

He’d had his reasons to freeze. What were hers?

“Hey,” he called.

She flinched and looked at him. “Right. You’re more used to jungles, right? Lead the way.”

“Scared of the forest or something?” he said.

Only her eyes moved to glance away. If Craft hadn’t been paying attention, he’d have missed it.

“That’s not it. We’ll need to find Rimmel,” she said. “No matter how carefree he normally is, he can help us with Nightshade, and that bridge is close to home for him. He’ll help us with the outsider, too. I think.”

It was so unlike her to be indecisive, and it sounded to him like she was distracting herself.

“Do you think it’s that impostor?” she continued. Apparently, she was also trying to distract him. It somewhat worked.

“I can’t really say,” he said. Impostor, impostor, impostor—it was becoming an obsession at this point, like a piece of tape stuck to the bottom of his shoe, making an annoying sound with every step. He wanted to be rid of it, but he couldn’t be bothered to stop and take five seconds to do so. He had more important things to do. But it was just so, so infuriating. “Well, it should be alright.”

Lei-rei let a moment pass. “Is everything really alright?”

Are you asking me or yourself? “That’s a weird question, don’t you think?” he replied. He backed away from the entrance and found a thick, ugly root to sit on. They’d probably be stuck here for a while. The root felt harsh, digging into his sit bones, but it was tolerable. “Most people aren’t ever completely fine.”

Lei-rei joined him, hunching forwards and anxiously playing with her thumbs. “You might be special. I wouldn’t know.”

“I’m pretty sure I’m just a slightly-above-average human being.”

She chuckled. “Let’s put it differently, then. What percentage of you is fine?”

He’d never heard it put that way before, but it was phrasing he could get behind. “Fifty, I guess. Seventy on a good day.”

Lei-rei slowly turned his way. “Are you aware that’s shockingly below average?”

“Huh? What’s average, then?”

“Eighty.” She faced forward again.

“Are you kidding? That’s high.”

“Sixty on a bad day.”

“I’ve been self-aware for a while, but—really?” He paused. How about her? She had been acting strangely.

He observed her, looking for any sign that she might be dipping below sixty, but when she started to lean away from him, it occurred to him he’d never bothered to just ask someone how they were doing. Anyone on a dive team would try lying their way out of the hot seat, but he didn’t need to think that way about her.

“What about you?” he finally asked.

“Me?” She leaned back. “On average?”

“Yeah.”

“I don’t know… Sixty-five bordering on seventy.”

Craft’s disbelief was written in cursive on his face.

“What?” she said.

“You don’t look the part.”

She narrowed her eyes.

“What? I’m just saying—”

—and reached for his face and pinched his cheeks, saying nothing else.

He blanked out. She was still pinching his cheeks. Am I…supposed to just take this? Unclear. If training manuals for socializing existed, they probably wouldn’t have covered this situation either.

“Some people learn to smile before they even have a reason to do so,” Lei-rei said. “I don’t want to be a liar. I present myself naturally and raise my sword if there’s a problem. That’s all.”

A liar, he thought, and before he could call himself one, she let go of his cheeks, and he felt the snap-back on his teeth. A stinging buzz lingered and left him rubbing his cheeks and flexing his jaw. It was a good distraction.

He was a liar, and of the third kind: the omitter. There were things he couldn’t let himself say, and other things—half-truths—he would use to ‘test’ others.

Trust was never guaranteed as long as not all the cards were on the table, so of course he had ways to test others and try to guess what cards they still hid. On the other hand, he was good at keeping his cards safe.

“Can I trust you, Craft?” Lei-rei said.

Never would he have imagined having them outright swiped from him.

He should’ve expected those words. Lei-rei was someone of the shadows. It should have been a given for her not to fully trust him—so why was it that a syrupy kind of sadness grew in his mind?

There was no one who could tell him this emotion was called ‘loneliness,’ and it showed on his face too. All he knew was he wanted to say yes, but if he did, what would happen afterwards? He didn’t know. He had never known.

“I-I do trust you with some things,” Lei-rei quickly added. He looked at her, hopeful, but for what, he still couldn’t name, and he found that she couldn’t meet his gaze. “Yesterday and the other day were new experiences for me, yet I’ve only known you for this long. I’m just a little farther ahead than you, after all. I can’t be complacent.”

He knew that rationally. To hear it said was another matter. “I’m not sure how to feel about that,” he said. There was a constricting feeling in his chest. “Why’d you bring this up now?”

“Each step closer to Monstera is getting harder for me to take. I can’t tell you nor myself why. That’s why I”—she paused, but made up her mind—“I realize I need you to do something for me, which I can’t do for myself.”

He didn’t want to let the heavy feeling coast along. “I’m shocked.”

“I know, I—”

“You could’ve just asked normally. My cheeks aren’t free pinchin’ real estate, you know?”

He chuckled, even if he still felt bad.

Part of Lei-rei’s fear turned into a chuckle. “No, well, that just happened. I don’t know.” The smile from her chuckle disappeared. She met his gaze for a just a moment, checking if he was still with her, before looking away.

“So why?” he asked. “Why do you need to go further?”

How they were was supposed to be enough.

It took her a while to answer. “There are things I can’t ask you to do as long as I’m not sure of where we stand.”

He sighed. “I owe you a lot already. You can just ask.”

If you owe someone, you pay them back—that was the only way he knew to live freely. He wouldn’t let others trap him with debts.

“Don’t do that,” Lei-rei said pointedly. “Don’t feel like you owe me.”

But he did. He genuinely believed he should reward her patience. “I’ve been relying on you too much.”

“Don’t put it that way. If either us started to feel trapped just because we didn’t have the correct image of each other, then I wouldn’t be able to forgive myself to live in a future where I didn’t even try to stop it. I’m telling you already; I don’t want your repayment. You’ll make yourself a life I don’t want to live again.”

He forgot to breathe. He’d almost forgotten that she, too, was someone who wanted to leave her past behind, and that he wasn’t the only one trying to live differently. The fact that she was the one who’d opened this topic meant she was stronger too—that she had practiced going through these same motions he also was, over and over, until the ghost that haunted her had become a mere shadow.

That shadow humbled him. Even if he had no idea how to move forward, he at least had to show he was not choosing to go backward.

“How else are we going to be equals?” he said. He just didn’t want to be master and slave.

A crack grew in her expressionlessness: the slight pull of a corner of her lips, the pronounced rise and fall of her shoulders, and the tired squinting of her eyes. The whole time, she didn’t want to look at him. He’d been thinking maybe it was because she was afraid of his gaze, but now, he witnessed one side of her face trying to keep from frowning and furrowing. Maybe she just wanted to hide the face she was making.

“We don’t really need to be. Rather, I’m...already beginning to care about you. Being equals doesn’t really matter to me right now.” She paused. “I know it’s out of the blue. I’m sorry.”

She was right to apologize. It scared him.

How could he prove her words? How far would one have to go to prove it? He didn’t even know what it took to do so.

“Wait. Sorry,” he said. “Give me a moment.”

In the corner of his vision, Lei-rei nodded.

He bowed his head. ‘Care’ was a big word to him. It was the prelude to betrayal, and it wasn’t something he easily believed. This time, however, if Lei-rei was telling the truth—the whole truth—and he didn’t reciprocate, then he’d be the one doing the betraying, not her.

His feelings towards her leaned closer to ‘concern,’ but wasn’t that just a seed of ‘care’ ? He admired her in some ways and was surprised in some others, but summing it all up, the word he would rather use was ‘grateful.’

Always and forever, he considered himself lucky just to have met her. Even if she’d disappeared tomorrow, her mere presence these past few days had already drastically changed his future.

If she was asking him to care about her the same way, he had to beg a certain question:

“Why do you care about me?”

He expected the awkward and sometimes verbose Lei-rei to take a while dancing around the point, but that wasn’t to be.

“Despite who I am, you don’t push me away.”

Her words were precise and right to the heart. He realized that she hadn’t waited for him to lay out his own cards first, and she was just laying hers down on the table without a care for the outcome.

He could reject her right now. He wanted to. He didn’t want her to die because of him—but, wait, this was Amatoria. People only ‘died’ here.

“Same goes for me,” he blurted out. It surprised her and himself, and they met eyes at that.

Lei-rei closed her mouth, not knowing she’d gasped.

Moments passed. They’d put each other through some difficulty there, but…forgiveness went unspoken. They found it easier to look at each other now.

“What did you need me to do?” Craft continued.

“I can’t face Rimmel alone. Be my commissar. Just make sure I can’t run away.”

He reassured her with a smile and nodded. “I’ll make sure.”

They were all smiles, but they weren’t standing.

“I want to go home,” Lei-rei said.

“Have you ever experienced a princess carry—”

She stood up.

Some time before Lei-rei had met Nightshade, she still wore the white coat of the Exorcisa. It would supposedly give her half a structured life—ranks, uniforms, achievements, and rewards—and half an unstructured life: out of one-thousand days in an Amatorian decimal year, the angels might request her skills in one or two of them.

The rest of it was free time, and she was supposed to grow into it. Alas, old habits died hard, and without the familiarity of structure, she filled in the void with just more of the same.

It was another good day to train. Lei-rei finished the three-on-one mock fight, standing tall as her opponents laid sprawled on the ground, panting and bruised. One member massaged a strained shoulder, while another dabbed a cut above their eyebrow.

One of the members, a gray wolfman, was still in good shape. He was still catching his breath when he approached Lei-rei with a forced smile. “Nice job,” he said, extending a hand for a handshake. “I didn’t think anyone could move that fast.”

Lei-rei’s gaze flicked to the outstretched hand. “It’s not needed,” she said, her voice flat.

The member’s smile disappeared, and he let his hand drop. “That’s why you’re a lone wolf,” he muttered.

“Hey, hey, say that again,” a boyish yet high-pitched voice said. A head of long, blue, liquid hair came between the two, but only the top of his head reached their eye level. He was also the last person to be pissed off among all the Exorcisa.

The others’ faces paled, and the wolf turned white. “R-rimmel—er—good match,” he said, turning tail and running. The rest had said nothing, but they still shied away and picked up the pace back to the locker room.

Rimmel turned to Lei-rei, his expression softening. “Hey, hey, bad day, newbie?” he asked, his eyes crinkling at the corners.

Lei-rei’s gaze remained distant, her eyes fixed on some point over Rimmel’s shoulder. She still thought herself alone in the courtyard, the wind the only sound. “It’s not a problem,” she said.

Rimmel nodded, his smile unwavering. “Yeah, yeah, you’re tough. Anyway, I’m here.”

If she stayed quiet for long enough, he’d go away. They all did. Her thoughts drifted to the long hours she’d spent alone, training to fight enemies that only appeared once a year. Really, there was nothing else to do in the base except be pestered by Rimmel every day.

But if he weren’t here, there really would be nothing to do—because then there’d be no one to tell her what to do. Until she could figure out how to be pulled towards the gravity of her own desires and will, her voice couldn’t be her own, and she could only continue to live by being pushed.

“You’re the leader,” she said quietly. “I just follow.”

Rimmel’s smile grew, and he nodded. “Yep, yep, that’s how it is.” He turned to leave, waving behind him. “If you’re showing up again tomorrow, call me! You need a tougher training partner, right?”

...But as Rimmel went back into the halls of the Exorcisa’s headquarters, greeting his subordinates with the same, cheery smile, he affirmed it to himself: only Lei-rei could possibly know my pain.

All these ‘others,’ they had only lost one or two people in their lives, and that had been enough to drive them mad and straight into Enthusia’s pitying embrace. He saw all these people smiling and laughing—well, how lucky of them. How can you all move on so quickly? It boggled the mind. At least Lei-rei…yeah. Only she and I know what it feels like to lose everything.

His grip on his emotions loosened, and his madness felt good to give into. Only they could heal each other. He’d never let her go.
