One minute, I’d been conversing with the Rat God.

The next, I’d found myself waking up in a bed with white sheets.

I blinked as the world came to focus. Yellow walls, a sterile room, a pot filled with green plants beside my bed…

And some kind of tube in my left arm. What the fuck was that?

I tensed my arm by instinct, only to realise I could move it again. It was stiff, somewhat unresponsive, and it ached a little, but I could move it. It worked. Could whatever this strange silvery liquid was, still flowing into me, be the cause of that?

I was alone in the room. There was no one standing watch over me. Besides the tube sticking out of my arm, I wasn’t restrained in any way. As far as my physical state went, I felt well-rested. I had no aches or pains besides my arm. It was like all my previous bruises had somehow healed.

Panicked, I opened my system to ensure the contents of my [Hoard] still remained. They did, including the broadsword I’d been given, and beyond that, there was something else awaiting my attention.

Something big.

[Major Advancement unlocked!]

This was it. I’d never heard of one of these before. I had no clue what to expect. For a moment, all worries about my situation, where I was, how I’d gotten here, and what would happen next… they froze. If I needed to escape, this might aid in it. That was my excuse. I read the appearing messages without delay.

[Your mana capacity has increased by 50%.]

[Mana Capacity exceeds Tier 0 limitations. Your core has been improved to Tier 1.]

[Tier 1 core has slightly bolstered all physical attributes.]

[Mana Control heightened. You can currently control 37% of the mana in your body.]

Seven skills with levels between eight and nine had just shot to ten. The ones at nine, as well as the three already at ten, promised me extra points in whatever skill I combined or refined them into. Already, I was wondering if those extra points stacked up if I combined multiple skills with the same line of text. Could I start a new skill at level five? Higher?

Then there was my core. Tier 1. I couldn’t believe it. I’d had no clue how to advance my Tier before, and wasn’t sure if I would progress past Tier 0 for years with my lack of class and levels, with zero training on how to evolve my core, and yet…

I almost wanted to cry, despite this mess. I refocused myself on the text before me. There’d be time to react later. Right now, things were quiet, and I was alone. I needed to read this over quickly, and be aware of what I could do before I stepped out of that door, just in case things went the wrong way.

They appeared to have given me medical care, to have cleaned me and even bathed me, but that didn’t necessarily mean that I’d be allowed to leave. That I wouldn’t be interrogated, tortured, experimented on. Who knew if these Drassians had ever seen a human before, and that they wouldn’t want to find out where I’d come from and what I was made of?

I imagined it was an extremely low possibility, at least considering what I’d seen of their society through Maya’s dreams, but I wasn’t completely discounting that something could go wrong—paranoia continually served me well, and those dreams were meant to be idealised. Plus, while I knew the mist had driven the research team insane, I had no clue just how much of their behaviour could be attributed to it. Likely all of it, but maybe not.

I shelved that thought, considering Maya.

I wondered where she was. If she was okay. She’d seemed in pretty bad shape when I’d brought her through here. If it turned out she didn’t make it, then all of this had been for nothing.

I hoped that she was alive and recovering. Regardless of anything, she hadn’t deserved that world, and she’d risked herself to save my life.

I glanced back to the system, where yet another sequence of messages awaited:

[As part of your Major Advancement, please choose two of the following options:]

[Heightened Growth: Choose one subset of skills from a list of previously used skills. Your growth in related activities will be accelerated by 20%.]

[Reduced Restrictions: Your skill hard cap will raise from level 10 to level 20. Skills will still be eligible for refinement or combination at level 10, but will be up to 25% more likely to receive higher-class upgrades if refined/combined closer to level 20. Raising a skill from 10 to 20 takes, on average, 2-3 times as long as raising it from 1 to 10.]

[Mana Matrix: Choose an elemental mana type. Your body will learn to generate mana of said type, and will use said mana 15% more efficiently than it does otherwise. You will gain Extreme Resistance (epic) to that element, also affecting spells used against you.]

[Intuition: Choose three subsets of skills from a long list. Passive observation of said skills will now majorly contribute to skill growth.]

That was… a ton of options. Too much to realistically consider right now. I couldn’t just pick at random. Better, or worse, I got to pick two of these. That meant I could potentially miss out on an incredible combo if I rushed this. I needed to hold off.

But, that being said, could I afford to?

I yanked the needle from my arm, simultaneously cursing at the pain and relieved that I could feel my arm again. I knew just from a quick glance that [Channel Cleansing] was likely one of the worse long-term options for sustained growth, considering I’d been chipping away at my mana control for some time already, but if anyone did end up attacking me in here or trying to prevent me from leaving, I’d lock it in out of necessity. Hopefully, the burst of power from being able to access more of my mana would help me fight off the Drassians.

Still, I needed to find that portal. I had no clue where I was, or even how to begin navigating this place…

I pressed my ear to the closed door. When I didn’t hear anything, I moved to the window.

I recognised the city immediately, from the incredibly tall buildings to the flying vehicles, the heavy storm clouds, the silver and blue architecture…

This was where Maya had gone to school. Where we’d been in her dream.

Looking straight down, there was a grey stable where people seemed to park their vehicles, and a road leading off into the city.

I could see other parts of the building I was in from the window, it seemed to stretch off a fair distance in multiple directions. This was some kind of infirmary? It was huge.

The drop from here to the ground was about forty feet. There was a small strip of grass between the wall and the hard grey, but it’d be difficult to land on.

Was there any way I could shimmy my way down the building? My climbing skill had improved, but I wasn’t wearing my spike suit, the wall looked sheer with nothing sticking out.

I had some rope in my [Hoard]. If I could find somewhere decent to tie it, I could descend the building like that. But where would I go next? Even if I found a map, I didn’t know my destination.

If I walked through that door, I was consigning myself to the Drassians and whatever they had in store for me. What were my choices? Run away blindly or trust I’d made the right decision. Both were scary.

I thought about it a little longer. Breathed some and resisted the urge to panic.

They’d brought me into this place and healed me. I hadn’t woken up in restraints. I was in a room I could feasibly escape from and I wasn’t being actively monitored. They probably weren’t planning to do anything nefarious to me.

I needed more information before I decided what to do. I moved back to the door, opening it a hair and activating [Moonlit Grace].

Immediately, I detected mana signatures a small distance away. Somewhere between four and six of them. Three appeared to be talking. Their sounds were carrying in the air, garbled, but heightened compared to what I’d be able to hear without my ability.

Tuning them to make out words was difficult. There was the language barrier—I didn’t understand every word a Drassian said, even with the multiple books I had stored—and then there was the noise itself, overly amplified and filtering through an ability that still made me dizzy whenever I attempted to use it.

That said, I could feel the improvement two more points in the ability had granted. I could pilot the space beyond me easier than before, even if it was still difficult, and I felt far less nausea.

I heard the words ‘alien’ and ‘still asleep’ through the murmuring echo, and attempted to pin down the conversation, working to eliminate external noise. Like in the facility, the whir of machinery in this place was a constant, vibrant hum, and the more I made an active effort to push it into the background, the more my mind felt strained, and the shorter my breaths became.

That said, the filtering had its desired effect, and I was able to ‘turn up’ the conversation a little more. Enough that I caught a handful of words.

As I released my ability, leaning against the wall and catching my breath, I reviewed what I’d heard: ‘hope he wakes up’, ‘asked us to notify her of any changes’, and ‘hero’ were what stuck out most.

It helped me feel far calmer about my situation. I could’ve been misinterpreting what I’d heard, or coming to conclusions too quickly, but the word ‘hero’ made me think I’d gotten in these guys’ good graces, which made me hopeful about Maya’s condition.

I was surprised I even knew the Drassian word for ‘hero’. I had pocketed a fair amount of books. Perhaps my time in Maya’s dream had helped with expanding my vocabulary?

I didn’t get to ponder it long. I heard footsteps coming my way.

Shit. I didn’t even need [Moonlit Grace] to tell me this person was close.

I didn’t think about what I did next. In retrospect, it felt childish. I clambered back into bed in a snap, laying there with an eye peeking open, watchful for whoever entered.

A short Drassian woman with long pink hair and wide hips entered the room, peering at me through the door.

“Hello? Um, human?” she asked, her voice low.

I opened my eyes further, signaling I was awake.

“Ah! You are up… Okay, don’t be scared… Rasha, come here please?”

She called through the door, and a few moments later, the biggest Drassian I’d seen entered my small room.

He was easily six and a half foot, and fucking solid. His forearms were about half the width of my waist. I stared at him, alarmed, as the two of them drew closer.

When I moved, both of them flinched, as if I were a poisonous snake.

“Okay… I was told to show you this…”

She reached into her pocket. A few moments later, she withdrew a set of cards with writing on them, her hands shaky. She placed them before me as I stared between the two, my mind still reaching halfway into my [Hoard], just in case anyone tried something.

My eyes focused on the cards before me. To my surprise, the words on one of them were written in Tellos’ common language, in neat, cursive handwriting.

Hello, human.

If you’re reading this, you’ve likely woken up in a bit of a daze. The first thing you need to know is that no one here is going to hurt you.

We’ve written this message in every language we know of that’s associated with humans. Hopefully, you can read one of these!

Our local government has been notified of your presence and will wish to receive you once you’re ready. You’ve done Drassia a great service in saving one of its citizens, as well as re-opening a vital mining location. We wish to reward you for your service.

You were unconscious after arriving, and our medical examiners discovered that there was a substance buildup in your left arm, which we are attempting to treat with stem cell therapy. If you ripped the needle from yourself upon awakening, it would be best to allow our medical staff to reattach it. They will only do so if you permit them with a nod.

Please treat the staff kindly. They are not used to humans. I’ll be there to receive you within the hour, try not to wander off hospital grounds.

I finished reading, and upon looking at the two Drassians, I could tell that they were visibly tense. The big guy was sweating. Was I really that intimidating to them?

After a few seconds of shared silence, the girl motioned toward the tube across the room, the one I’d predictably yanked out.

I gave her a deliberately slow nod. She set to work grabbing the needle and cleaning it off with a wipe, giving me a notably wide berth as she did so.

“Okay… this is going to sting a little…”

She sounded as if she was bracing for the pain rather than me. Oh well, I took it like a champ, or so she said, smiling down at me before hightailing out of the room just about as fast as she could walk without bumping into anything.

The big guy, possibly a guard assigned to ensure I didn’t go on a rampage once I woke up, walked back outside, standing somewhere near the door.

Was he the only one? Were there more just in case I’d overpowered him?

I suppose I’d been right that they wouldn’t leave me unguarded, but it seemed as if the letter I’d been left was legitimate.

After all, why bother making me feel safe if they were just going to torture me? They could’ve not bothered healing me, or sedated me somehow. It felt as if the Drassians were attempting to tread as lightly as they could around me, while still keeping wary.

Why? Because they didn’t know what I was capable of?

Come to think of it, after I’d come through the portal with Maya, and the Rat God had grabbed me…

Had I just disappeared before their eyes? And then reappeared much later?

Yeah. Maybe it made sense that they were wary of me.

That being said…

The Drassians working at this infirmary—or, hospital—didn’t seem to know much about humans. Their government knew at least enough that they were able to write me a letter in a language I recognised. But they’d said they knew multiple languages associated with humans.

From where? Were those other languages on Tellos, or did humans exist on other planets besides my own?

Also, those two, at least, hadn’t known I spoke their language. They hadn’t even attempted to speak to me in mine, which indicated they didn’t speak a word of it.

It might benefit me to not admit I could speak Drassian just yet. Or, if they spoke my language well enough, ever.

I was stuck in bed for the next hour. The drip in my arm was meant to be kept in, and that meant laying here. Thinking.

I was less worried about the whole situation now than when I’d woken up. One thing that had stuck out to me in that message was that I’d ‘saved’ a Drassian citizen. At this point, I was almost convinced Maya was alive and recovering, but I still wanted proof.

The other made me worry. ‘Recovering a vital mining location’ or something to that effect.

Did Drassia know what they were doing? Would they send another team to those mines and have them go insane again?

I’d seen what those mines had done to a Drassian team first hand. Men, women, and children  killed. I wasn’t sure how much mist exposure one needed at this point to lose their sanity. I didn’t want to be responsible for more ferals.

I knew how to combat the mist. At least temporarily. That information was valuable, but I had no interest in holding it back. That facility didn’t deserve to claim more lives. I resolved to tell someone the moment I had a chance.

I was stuck here in the meantime. I had plenty to do, however. So many skill increases that it was boggling to even consider how I might begin to refine and combine them. A [Hoard] filled with gear, some of it useful, some just expensive. I was already considering the use of my new [Loan] tab. I’d categorise the items later today and see what I was planning to use.

Then there was my Major Advancement. I could feel the increase of mana inside me without even attempting to visualise it. I could also better feel just how much of my own mana was unresponsive to my instructions. Before, it’d been like peering into an ocean and being unable to see the floor. Now, I could better gauge the depths of my own power, but knew it would take time to properly utilise.

I had Spirit Stones for that, but I was hesitant to jab anything into myself right now. If someone came in and my arm was bleeding, they might think I was violent.

When I did look inward, my core was more apparent too. It was like a swirling golden ball, awash with wispy blue mist that swirled around inside of it. When I focussed on the ball, I could make it pulse, releasing mana from my core in waves.

That felt funny. Kinda soothing across my body, but also like I was prodding myself in the stomach. I decided I’d figure that new discovery out later and shelved it, opening my [Skills] tab and once again staring at the contents.

I had ten skills at level ten. That was incredible. Not only that, but a majority of those skills offered me further benefits upon advancement. I was imagining combinations as I stared, dragging different skills into the [Combine] tab one after another to catalogue every combo I could conceive of.

An impulsive itch told me to combine all ten. I decided against that near enough instantly. I still had zero clue what combining two completely odd skills would do when it came to generating a new one, and I was by no means going to risk that with some of my best ones. It was something I wanted to test eventually, but I’d wait until I’d learned a couple of skills I deemed completely useless before I tried something that bold.

In the meantime, this was about finding combinations that made sense. That being said, some combinations, while they seemed clever on the surface, soon had me thinking twice.

Combining [Tinkering] and [Trap Making], for example. Combining those might result in me gaining options that were highly specialised, like a skill that made me better at re-arming traps, but I wasn’t sure if I wanted to lose out on the opportunity to refine both [Tinkering] and [Trap Making] as separate skills. Rolling them into one could have benefits, but it could also leave me with a skill that leaned heavily towards [Trap Making] and lost me some makeshift repair skills in the process, or the opposite.

I was less worried about combining physical skills. I felt as if doing so was more likely to result in generalised boosts and my previous results weren’t anything to scoff at. Considering that, I was heavily considering rolling my [Throwing], [Running], and [Perception] skills into one, and possibly adding something like [Marksmanship] to the mix. I also thought about adding [Climbing].

I wanted to be able to visualise what I might get out of a combination before I committed to it. In this case, a [Throwing], [Running], [Perception], [Climbing] mix sounded like it might be the skill profile of a warrior or a performer, or at least something adjacent to that. I’d not ever had to rely on my climbing skill during combat, but if I were more proficient, and the terrain allowed for it, I imagined I would.

A part of me was also considering that the more level ten skills I combined, the stronger the result might be, assuming the skills were complimentary, but I didn’t know that for a fact. I was willing to test it here, if only because I had so many to utilise, but not with more than four.

I could throw one skill below level ten into the mix too, and considered it to be between [Jumping] and [Grappling], and I was sure which one I included would somewhat shift the result. A skill with the previous four plus [Jumping] was likely to be more movement-oriented, while [Grappling] would be more likely to land me with options centred around combat. Then there were the cores I included. They too might shift my results, alongside giving me more options.

I was sure that Beast Cores were likely to shift my combination options further into the realm of fighting, but I was wary about using them, or, at least, using them as the main core in my material sacrifice due to the difficulties that [Savage Reflex] had put me through. It wasn’t lost on me that [Savage Reflex] had only appeared as an option to me because of the multiple Beast Cores I’d shoved into that combination.

I would definitely use some core in my combination regardless. From what I could tell, using them was a benefit, and until I had plenty of time to experiment and determine whether that was false, I’d stick with what seemed to be giving me results.

I had Rest Cores of different grades, multiple Beast Cores, a couple of Slashing Cores, and two of something I’d picked up a good while ago, an uncommon Prey Core.

This left me a pretty big decision to make. Offensive, defensive, or somewhere down the middle? The final skill I chose to put into this medley, as well as which cores I selected, would greatly influence what I got in turn. What was I currently lacking in?

I’d apparently had my physical attributes ‘bolstered’ by my core evolution, but I was still underweight despite my slowly increasing muscle definition. I wasn’t under any illusions regarding my physical strength, nor my durability. I’d likely struggle almost as much going forward tackling strong enemies and weathering big attacks as I had before, and I couldn’t always rely on a health potion to bail me out.

For now, I seemed to be able to supplement my offensive capabilities using projectiles and magic. Not only that, but this new increase to the capability of my [Hoard], something I was itching to test, led me to believe that I’d be that bit better at using its contents to aid me in fights.

Considering that, I felt as if heavily indexing into offensive skills now was a bad idea. What I needed was mobility. I’d lost at least a portion of my agility from giving up [Savage Reflex], and that had forced me into increasingly physical confrontations where I couldn’t outspace my opponents, and my durability was still immensely lacking.

I slotted [Jumping] into my new ability, which now consisted of five total abilities, the others being [Climbing], [Running], [Perception], and [Throwing]. I placed two E Grade Rest Cores and one D Grade Prey Core into the material sacrifice section, then bolstered that with about five hundred gold’s worth of ore and gemstones, utilsing pretty much anything that I assumed would be difficult to work into a more valuable material, then threw in two D Grade Spirit Stones and a single C Grade to cap things off.

I was investing heavily in this new skill, which made sense to me, considering the resulting combination would need to do a lot for me. Not including the greatsword, which I very briefly considered sacrificing too, but thought better of, this was over 7 percent of the materials I owned in terms of value. Over a decade of wages for anyone earning a gold piece a week.

Here’s hoping it was worth it.

The moment I pressed accept, I felt the familiar buzz of energy I’d come to associate with power. The bed rattled and shook. My mind raced with electric sensation. I felt a sudden urge to jump out of the nearby window and run a mile, the energy eclipsing from my core and cascading through my body in waves.

Once my heart had settled and my mind had cleared, I finally read the screen before me, hands tremulous:

[Material Sacrifice of Aspects has resulted in increased combination choices.]

[Please choose from the following options:]

[Freerunner (semi-rare): user is increasingly adept at navigating difficult and vertical terrain, able to move fluidly, grip easier, jump higher, break falls, and retain balance more easily, as well as spot potential hazards and incoming danger while moving at high speeds. Aching muscles recover faster.]

This sounded extremely useful in the underground. The agility boost in general sounded solid, though the ability to sense traps or incoming attacks was even better.

At least, I thought it was a flat agility boost. It might’ve been more to do with having control over my motions? Either way, anything that made moving easier for me was a plus.

This ability didn’t seem to give me any [Throwing] related benefits, however, and despite the fact my [Hoard] was growing more versatile, a part of me suspected my [Throwing] skill might affect my [Hoard] accuracy, and I’d rather not take a skill that had nothing to do with the skill if I could help it. I decided to check my other options.

[Deer in Headlights (rare): Detect mana signatures of creatures within fifteen feet of you who cannot mask their mana, alerting the user. While retreating from said creatures, your mobility is significantly heightened. All ranged attacks against creatures ten feet or further from you are slightly easier to aim.]

This was potentially abuseable and kinda exciting to think about. It all came down to what ‘retreating’ meant. Did I have to be actively running away, or did dodging an attack count? How many creatures could mask their mana? Was that common? I’d never heard of it.

Crucially, could I get a buff like this by travelling with a friend and running away from them? What about a pet? If two enemies were coming at me from opposite angles, could I move toward one of them faster because I was technically retreating from the other? Could I manipulate my surroundings when fighting multiple enemies to use this skill offensively?

That speed of movement, combined with [Kinetic Shell]’s barriers…

This skill incorporated every component I’d put into it, though I already had a means to detect mana signatures using [Moonlit Grace]. That being said… it was useless in enclosed spaces. What if I couldn’t maintain ten feet of distance? What if I couldn’t run? I thought back to some of my previous fights and determined this wouldn’t have been useful in all of them. Maybe more than half, but certainly not all. Fighting the marilik, useful. Fighting a feral? I highly doubted this would be as effective.

Well, unless I was planning to simply book it and run a mile.

I read my next option, [Deer in Headlights] currently at the top of my list.

[Trailblazer (rare): You don’t just run things, you set trends. Your first ten seconds in combat afford you double effective agility and thrown weapon accuracy. Using the same attack or maneuver twice results in a minor penalty to your coordination.]

That sounded pretty cool. Was I biased because of the name? Maybe. Did I realise that while ten seconds was a long time in combat, it was also a very short amount of time to win, especially versus multiple enemies, or even to secure a large advantage? Also yes.

But I liked the name. And I liked how such a skill would reward my creativity, which seemed to mesh quite well with my existing fighting style.

Still, it was specifically thrown weapon accuracy. This wasn’t doing anything for guns or other projectiles, and I wasn’t sure how it’d interact with [Hoard].

I wasn’t sure about it. I checked my last option.

[Performing Clown (rare+): A juggler, a knife-thrower, an acrobat, a comedian. User receives a substantial buff to all agility/accuracy-based feats while at least one sapient creature is watching, and a minor buff otherwise. User can sleep while standing up.]

I stared at the last skill offered a little angrily. It reminded me of the Uncommon [Court Jester] class I’d been offered months ago, way before all of this.

While I recognised that a circus performer might require literally all of the skills I’d placed into this combination, I still felt something was amiss. I considered the options presented…

Was it possible that Thymos determined what skill choices I received?

I had no clue if that was true. From what I knew, gods presided over class selections, but I had no clue about skills.

Was he trying to test me on my pride and ego? Expecting me to pick [Trailblazer] over [Performing Clown] simply because I liked the sound of it better, because the skill’s text was complimentary?

Well, if that was the case, he was wrong. I’d pick [Trailblazer] if the choice itself wasn’t so cocky. Expecting to finish a fight that quickly was stupid, and I wasn’t stupid. But I also wasn’t convinced that [Performing Clown] was the best option. If I was, I’d take it in a second.

I had no pride. Fuck him. How dare he.

I took a breath. [Performing Clown] required a sapient creature to be watching, a clarification on the word told me it meant human-like intelligence. That discounted a lot of the monsters I’d faced, at least as far as I knew.

It wasn’t lost on me that it gave me a small benefit regardless, but I had no clue how minor ‘minor’ was. [Deer in Headlights] was impossible to use without something to retreat from, but I imagined that’s mainly when I’d need it, and I couldn’t only think about this within the purview of the underground.

When I left the underground, I’d go back to the mines. When I left the mines, once my contract was finished, I’d go back to Melusia. In both of those places I would be surrounded by people, and the ability to run around and climb would be less important than the ability to fight effectively and stay alive. For more than ten seconds of a fight, preferably.

[Trailblazer] was out. So was [Freerunning]. Between [Performing Clown] and [Deer in Headlights], which was more useful? An ability that always promised me a minor benefit or a more conditional ability with a higher defensive capacity?

Even tapping into that higher benefit required me to be ‘watched’. What if my goal was stealth? What if I lost line of sight with the opponent? It was a lot to consider.

I’d ended up down here because I’d almost been killed. That doctor could come and attack me again at any time, anyone could. It wasn’t lost on me that despite [Moonlit Grace]’s ability to detect mana signatures, I was still inconsistent with it, and had to have it toggled on in order to use it. Not only did [Deer in Headlights] not require a toggle, apparently it ‘alerted’ me. That could save my life if I was sleeping or distracted.

I’d wondered if I’d try to invent justifications to take a less insulting option. I didn’t. Honestly, both were pretty damn insulting. I’d rejected the only cool-sounding names because the skills were risky and lacked versatility. Maybe that was the point.

I wanted to stay alive. That was all I gave a fuck about. That and growing strong. I’d do whatever it took to do it.

I locked in [Deer in Headlights]. I breathed a sigh.

[As a result of your combination, new skill has been attributed six bonus levels. These levels will not contribute to your level 10 skill cap. Skill text has been updated to reflect this.]

[Deer in Headlights (rare), level 1 (+6 ): Detect mana signatures of creatures within twenty-five feet of you who cannot mask their mana, alerting the user. While retreating from said creatures, your mobility is significantly heightened. All ranged attacks against creatures eight feet or further from you are slightly easier to aim.]

I blinked as I reread the text.

Holy shit. This was far better than what I’d thought I was getting. And I could raise it another nine levels? Because of my Major Advancement, this skill was capped at sixteen?
