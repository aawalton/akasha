Max gasped.

Sweat dripped from his face. He stared at the packed earth before him, on both hands and knees. Trembling shakes and cramping overtook his entire body every time he tried to shift. For all intents and purposes, he was stuck in this position until his body decided otherwise.

Never before had he felt so exhausted.

Never before had he felt like someone was actively beating him with metal rods.

Someone must have take a club and beat him senseless while asleep the night prior. It was the only excuse he could think of. Alas, Max wasn’t so disillusioned that he did not know the exact reasoning for this suffering he was facing today, and if he was being frank, he would not trade anything at all if it resulted in the same sequence of consequences time and again.

This was what happened when you stayed up all night, worked unfamiliar muscles you never knew existed in the form of Mana Manipulation, fought against surging tides of mana trying to escape the body and mana channels, and finally using every wisp, except the last few, unlocking a single percentage point in his Skill Shard.

It didn’t seem like he had done much at the time, but he knew better now.

And then, it only got worse.

Mainly because the Optio did not care one bit that he was gasping and struggling to keep up.

All the workouts continued with added intensity.

Max picked his head and looked toward the Optio—

He cursed under his breath, which he regretted because he face protested the attempt immediately. The Optio was staring dead at him, arms crossed, balanced standing position, and eyes narrowed. Senior Casius stood beside him, looking at Max as well.

The old man leaned toward the Optio and whispered something.

The Optio frowned, but nodded nonetheless.

That made Max even more suspicious. Why were they standing there and staring at him? What were they whispering about? More importantly, why wasn’t the Optio shouting at him to get up? Where were the extra team punishments for Max being slower then everyone and not keeping up? What about the individual examples he always made?

What is going on? Why isn’t he acting normal?

A shadow loomed over him. “You look terrible.”

“Thanks, Arnie. Don’t move. I need your shadow right now to block out the sun. I can’t move.”

“You look like someone took a club, wrapped you up in bed, and then beat you with it.”

Max threw himself to his back and spread his arms out.

“You aren’t going to die on me, Max? Right?”

“I’m not going to die—”

Arnie got down and grabbed Max’s shirt. Max’s chest protested the action. “You can’t die after you gave me that speech about brother hood!”

“Arnie—”

“I’ll literally feed your body to the jackals if you think you can go the easy way out!”

“Arn—”

“And then I’m going to cut them up and take your body out of them and—”

“That’s enough. He’s not going to die.”  Caleb stepped beside Arnie and held his shoulder. He looked around, surveying the entire camp around them including the recruits, the Optio, the legionaries, and even the Record Keeper. “Haven’t you all noticed? Something weird is about to happen.”

Max and Caleb shared a look. Both of them had noticed the strange reactions from the Optio and Senior Casius.

Arnie looked up. “What do you mean?”

“Max came in last, right?” asked Caleb.

Arnie nodded.

Max sat up. He had been thinking about the same thing.

“Why isn’t the Optio punishing us as a group? Why isn’t Max standing next to him right now and doing ups and downs while the Optio shouts at us? Has that ever happened before? They’re acting like what happened, him being last and not keeping up… hadn’t happened? Why? What are they planning? How does that involve Max? More importantly—”

Caleb looked around their regiment.

Arnie did the same, his eyes widened. “The rest of our barrack noticed.”

Max didn’t look up. He could feel the dozens of eyes staring at his back without having to look up. He had been doing his best to ignore it, but it was only growing worse the longer Max didn’t end up getting punished just like the rest of them. It would breed resentment, much of which he had nothing to do with at all.

This is worse than being punished. This gets everyone’s attention and makes them start asking weird questions.

“Most of us have been punished for being unable to keep up with the group, leading too far, coming in last, coming in first without the group, and a dozen other petty reasons. And then Max does the same but doesn’t get the same treatment,” Caleb sat down next to Arnie. “They still remember Senior Casius and Max walking together during the goblin fights. Everyone is thinking it, Arnie. What makes him special and them not? Why is Max getting special treatment and they aren't? Is he better? He can’t be better. He always came in dead middle on everything and never stood out.”

Max frowned and ventured a side glance toward the other recruits. It was exactly as he thought it would look like. Many tried to hide their stares and looks of confusion, some didn’t bother and looked visibly upset. It made sense. They had been severely punished for the same thing Max had done, except he wasn’t.

What happened to everyone was the same?

What happened to everyone was equal in the legion except for glory and honor on the battlefield and rank?

What happened to being one unit that breathed, fought, slept, ate, and died together?

“Max is Max,” Arnie said. “He does everything not to stand out—”

“You know that. I know that. Do they know? Do they care? Joel was basically shunned by most of the barracks after standing out too much and it made them, us included, run a few times. Don’t you think he is seething? Why was he punished and not Max? What about his current crew? And the crew that left him because of that?”

Max closed his eyes.

There was no way to control this situation. He would not stop grinding. There was no way he would allow himself to slowdown his development just because the rest of the recruits were getting upset at the Optio’s reaction. He couldn’t make the giant man do anything and his decisions were his alone.

All he could do was continue to put hard work in at all times, especially with his Patron Inheritance.

Max noticed Arnie and Caleb get up. “Where are you guys?”

Arnie nodded toward something behind him.

Max closed his eyes for a second, knowing full well that whatever had been happening, finally came to a head and arrived.

“How goes the breathing, young man?"

Max stood up, much to the displeasure of every muscle in his body, and saluted the Record Keeper.

“At ease, young man.” the Record Keeper smiled wide at him, his eyes vanished in the wrinkles around his face. “You never answered. How goes the breathing?”

“Not very well, Senior.”

Casius laughed. His already wide smile grew wider and made his prodigious crow’s feet even more prominent.

Max kept his face as stoic as possible. He could already feel the grumbling behind him from the other recruits as they asked a dozen questions and stared in confusion as the Record Keeper, a person of importance within the camp that made veteran Legionaries straighten up and salute, made chummy with a little kid no older than they were.

A recruit that didn’t even seem that special in any way they knew.

Senior Casius wiped his right eye and took a long moment to study him, looking up and down his exhausted frame inch by inch. He paused to stare at a bruise on Max’s forearm, he fell during the fifth lap and foolishly braced himself with his arm, then once at his eyes, and then finally completed the circuit by staring at his bare feet. No one had been allowed to use the perfectly made boots yet, having not earned it based on some otherworldly criteria the Optio made up.

“You know, it might be the technique,” said Casius.

“The technique? What’s wrong with the breathing exercise?”

“Well. Nothing really. For most people. For you? It might be limiting your success and recovery.”

Max blinked. An intense urge to learn and grow overtook him. He knew everyone was watching and listening in on the conversation, but, if he was bing honest with himself, he didn’t quite care. He was there to improve as much as possible and as fast as possible without causing any future issues for himself.

What that consisted of? He wasn’t quite sure, hence his burning desire to learn from the Record Keeper and anyone else that knew more than him.

“Limiting my recovery? What do you mean?” Max was fully focused on Senior Casius now.

“You mis-understand. That breathing exercise I provided you with is a low level breathing technique given to the lowest levels of underground brawlers and prize fighters. Nothing special compared to what is out there and can be learned. There are real special ones out there, real breathing techniques that are far more expansive.” The old man leaned in and whispered for only Max to hear. “One’s borne of magic to help restore and even gather mana.”

Max’s eyes widened.

He hadn’t known that was even a possibility and considered Mana Manipulation, Mana Gathering, and Core Formation as the only ways based on his limited knowledge. This opened up an entirely new world to him. One that he could use and tailor to fit his strengths and weaknesses perfectly.

More importantly, this was an opportunity he could not let slip by him, even if everyone listening would grow to resent him even more.

Max nodded, eyes growing wide and intense.

Senior Casius smiled again at his reaction. “These breathing techniques have existed for generations. Generations more shall make do with them even if cities rise and fall during the Endless War against the Hordes. Some are mythical and legendary in make, used by the Emperors since the birth of this glorious empire. Others, well, they are all but made for the dredges and masses. What do you want to learn? How far do you want to reach?”

Max stared into the eyes of the Record Keeper for a long time, fighting the desire to scream that he wanted a mythical and legendary breathing technique that even the Emperors used. It was a battle he almost lost and only won once he snapped his eyes shut and bit his lip.

Even he could see how unwise it would have been to shout something as ridiculous as that.

“I, yes, I—”

“How far do you want to reach?”

Max kept his eyes closed shut and grit his teeth. “As far as possible,” he whispered.

“Good enough of an answer. Not to ambitious, but not to conservative—”

If only he knew. Emperor level breathing techniques? How do I get my hands on something like that? Its going to bother me until I figure it out now.

Senior Casius patted his shoulder. “—A flexible goal that can grow of slow down if necessary.”

Max opened his eyes and nodded. He recalled Caleb’s words just before Senior Casius came to talk to him. Yet, he could not let this moment escape him. Anything that could potentially boost his mana recovery and gathering was a boon he would be foolish to let slip from his grasp, even if it resulted in a bit more resentment than normal.

He would need to keep an eye out more often though. Jealousy and envy caused even the sanest to commit insanity.

“Come with me, I have someone you should meet. A teacher, maybe? I’ll give you the breakdown once we are heading toward them. There is much you should learn before we get there. Mostly for your own safety. Some personalities are far larger than others and this one, this one is the biggest of them all.”

Senior Casius turned and started walking.

Max hesitated for a second, then turned to his friends. “I’ll be back.”

Caleb and Arnie both nodded, they knew what he meant. Once he was back they would get the full scoop including what he learned. That was what it meant to be a team, even if their were secrets amongst them. The general vibe Max was fostering was one that included them all growing.

Max took off on a slight jog until he caught up to Senior Casius.

It wasn’t until the group of recruits vanished around a bend did the older man speak.

“You are going to meet the Centurion—”

Max stopped dead in his tracks.

Senior Casius stopped and turned back to him. “Do you understand what that means?”

Max nodded.

“No you don't.” Senior Casius stepped closer to him. He shook his head. “You will not speak unless spoken to. You will not act unless told to do so. You will not stand, sit, shift, move, or even think unless the Centurion demands it of you. You will do exactly what he commands you to do and you will not make a fool out of Samson, Ignacious, Petor the Patron mage, or me.

Your talent of reading and writing elevate you. Do not give him a reason to demote you. The Centurion is a busy man, worked endlessly with one purpose in mind. Killing orcs and saving humanity. But thats not all…”

Senior Casius spun on his heels and began walking again. Max followed.

“The Centurion is the strongest individual this side of the Red Sand River. The Centurion of a Cursed Legion is no joke nor can anyone pull any strings to take such a role. Only the most hardy, veteran, quite frankly, insane, and powerful of all Centurions volunteer for this mantle, and even then, not all get accepted unless they meet the power criteria…”

Max followed without saying a single word as Senior Casius went on to tell him a hundred different things he needed to memorize to do his best in serving such a powerful individual. When to speak, when to stand, when to sit, when to brew tea, how to brew tea, why he should never use more then two leaves of mind and when five was the correct answer, and so much more. Max’s head was spinning by the time they crossed into the Cursed Legion’s real camp.

Said camp was enormous. Thousands upon thousands of legionaries marched and walked and trained and sparred and cleaned their armor. Each one scarred and battlehardened. None looked new or green in any shape or form. Even the youngest and least wounded among them had an edge to them that made Max gulp.

Here, there were no real structures.

All of them were tents and pavilions of various sizes, prepared to move at any notice. Nothing but banners were set with foundations that would take some time to remove. Everything else was easily gathered, packed up, and then set upon carriages or bag to begin a new march toward a new destination.

“...I hope your penmanship is good enough. The Centurion wishes all his messages and letters to be readable by even the least literate commanders and generals, which is more than a few. Being good at killing and leading men does not stretch into the scholastic foundations after they are trained in the Grand Military Universities by the best professors and teachers these lands have to offer after all…” Senior Casius paused as he looked back toward Max. “Are you listening, young man?”

“Yes, Sir! Grand Military Universities are the best places to learn military doctrine and strategy. Not so good for reading and writing.”

Senior Casius laughed. “Yes, that’s exactly what I was trying to say!”

They kept moving through the camp until they reached the dead center of the thousands upon thousands of tents. There sat the largest of them all. Four massive banners of the Golden Claw sat on each corner of the pavilion. Yet, what caught his attention was not the size of the thing or the banners or even the space allotted to it.

It was the lack of legionaries.

There were no guards except a lone legionary at the tent flap, likely placed their to open and close it as people walked in and out. Said legionary was missing a leg that had been replaced by a metal peg that ended in a blade at the end. He held onto a long spear that had to be eight feet long, its tassel was swayed with the wind in the colors of the Legion.

“Well met, Record Keeper. What brings you here?” asked the one legged legionary.

“Senior Yorv. I bring the Centurion his requested aide. The one we spoke about a few days prior.”

“Ah! The potential scribe.” the one legged legionary, Yorv, looked Max up and down. He did not look impressed. “Scrawny is he not? The Centurion was expecting more considering the… tales and titles he’s garnered.”

“The most impressive men tend not to look so. I am sure you would recognize that above many others.”

Yorv smiled. He grabbed the tent flap and opened it.

Max saw nothing from the inside, which he attributed to magic.

“Indeed. Strength has little to do with how impressive you look. Come on in. The Centurion is discussing tactics with a few strategist as we speak, so you may have to wait until he is done. News from the Cloud Piercing Mountain Passes has not been the good sort. The orcs are moving earlier then expected.”

Casius’s face turned grim. He said nothing as he nodded and crossed the threshold.

Max hesitated for a second, looking back at Yorv for a second, before he walked in without looking back.
