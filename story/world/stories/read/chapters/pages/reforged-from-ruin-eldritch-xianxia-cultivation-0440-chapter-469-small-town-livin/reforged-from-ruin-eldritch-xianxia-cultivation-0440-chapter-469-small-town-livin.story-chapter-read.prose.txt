In times of great upheaval, one of the most important and foundational elements one must hold closely to their heart are the people least important to the conflict.

Contradictory, perhaps, but only on its surface. Great upheavals come from specific conditions, for specific reasons, and yet, the world is not so limited as to possess only those elements. Just as we exist in a multifaceted madness, so too must we acknowledge, understand, and control that madness, rather than succumb to the temptations of becoming myopic. It is easy to become lost to the moment, to the immediate, to what is happening in isolation, and thus to lose the understanding that nothing ever happens in true isolation.

And the heart is a fragile thing. Just as one’s mind suffers from short-sightedness, one’s heart suffers from a lack of space, from the compression of focus and chaos. People are multifaceted creatures, reflecting the multifaceted nature of reality, and to neglect this can be as lethal as to ignore the conditions which bring about and necessitate such immediacy of focus. Survival is well and good, but the tools to actually engage with the world as more than a base organism come not from combat or from adrenaline– they originate, at their core, from peace. From interactions with others. From hunger as well as satiation. Without the whole picture, there is nothing that can be done save react, half-blind, and eventually, succumb.

It is a hard learned lesson, and one I impart with the gravest of gravitas. Remember why you survive, not just how. Remember what you fight for, not just the fight. Embrace your loved ones, even if only in your mind’s eye, even in the midst of fire and war. Hold tight to your joys, even amidst the darkest and most insistent of despairs. Focus and willpower may allow you to take the next step forward, but it is joy, love, peace, satisfaction, as much as their absence, that allow you to begin walking in the first place, and that tells you where to go.

--Original Writing of Titan-Philosopher Tao Zudong, post-quarantine by the Fool Of Fools Emperor Of Emperors.

“Alright, take three of these a week, preferably with a day in between each dose. If you start growing a bunch of hair in unexpected places, come back and I’ll fix you up, but if you suddenly start seeing new colors, that means it’s working.”

The young woman nods nervously, her eyes pointed resolutely down at the floor rather than at the speaker. In her hand is clutched a small clay jar, stoppered with wax but unable to contain the potency of the scent of medicinal herbs and blood wafting out from within it. Clad in simple, rough-spun clothes and threadbare sandals, she looks out of place amidst the bustling jungle of ingredients and strange organisms growing out of the walls, but to her credit, she stands firm.

“And… for the other thing, honored one?”

“Oh! Right.”

The giant in the room turns to one of the strange plants of the small cabin, which eagerly droops down and severs its own leaf to land in her palm. With a single, spasming crush of muscle and digits, she crushes it into a rough powder before wrapping it in a soft napkin.

“I got most of the water out, but it will probably lose its potency after a month or so. Grind it up and make tea with it, but only a pinch at a time. If you or your partner drink it an hour before bed, it should help alleviate some of the problems you’ve been having. And if he stays erect for more than two hours, come knock on my door, it means his bloodflow got messed up.”

The blush on the young woman’s face only deepens, turning near crimson, but she bows politely enough, taking the little packets of powder.

“Many thanks!” she says, and before Raika has a chance to say anything in return she’s already dashed out of the cabin like her feet were on fire.

She sighs, sitting back onto the rocking chair, shaking her head.

“You’d think no one’s ever wanted a longer pounding, the way she was acting. Yeesh. Next time I get a customer like that, remind me to write down the instructions, huh? They always make it so damn awkward.”

The cabin lies empty of anyone but her, but she still nods like someone’s responded.

“Exactly! It’s all just so exaggerated. Honestly, I don't know what they’re all so panicked about. I’m not that tall, and it’s not like I’m going to tattle to anyone. Hardly anything to be nervous about.”

Another pause, followed by her scoffing.

“Yeah, yeah. An hour, you said? Fine. I can manage that. See you then.”

With a groan, she gets up off her chair, shuffling over towards the door. She grabs a little pouch on the table closest to the door before taking hold of the walking stick resting against its frame, squinting as she steps out into the bright midday sun.

On one side of her home she sees the farms, spreading out past the edge of the woods, surrounded by little runestones, bright and lush in the height of harvest season. In the other direction, a few minutes walk away, she looks out over West Laketown, half-hidden behind one of the sloping hillsides that dot the landscape, clear blue water glinting behind it from its namesake lake.

Which is, hilariously, named “the lake”. There’s probably someone out there that’s catalogued this place, named the lake, marked it on maps, and then completely forgot about it, but to the people who live along it, it’s just… the lake. The only one anybody really knows, because even in this day and age, traveling unprepared through the wilds is a recipe for disaster, and when one has all they need so close to home, why bother?

She’s pretty sure they don’t even pay taxes out here. If they do, it’s certainly not very much.

No, this particular lake and its denizens are rather happily passed over by the wider world.

Grunting, she hobbles down the dirt road leading from her front door, making her way down the hill and towards the near-identical buildings of the town below.

It’s a pretty ramshackle situation, but she’s managed to keep it running alright. Building it originally took a lot longer than she expected, but when she offered to pay for someone to help her make a place to live, the flood of volunteers was… well, it was as baffling as it was humbling. In weeks, the finished product was ready, and while what few coins she had were gratefully accepted, it was made clear that a better form of payment would come from her presence about town.

In spite of herself, she agreed. It helps that no one here knows shit about proper cooking.

Well. That’s mean. They’re decent chefs, they just don’t quite have her palate.

She clears the edge of her “property”, marked more by where the wildflowers stop growing and where the chicken coop is located. Some of the hens cluck aggressively at her, aggrieved at this change in their usual pattern and more than willing to share that wrathful annoyance. She ignores them, until the stupidest one starts trying to put her head through the fence again, and she has to hobble over and poke the stupid thing back in, at which point she has to toss them some seeds from a nearby bucket lest they get even more suicidal.

It’s stupid, and so are they, but she still finds herself smiling as she does it.

Her knee aches already, even under such slight strain, but she grumbles under her breath, picks her walking stick back up, and gets back to walking.

She’s a little out of breath when she gets to the bottom of the hill, passing some of the other families she shares it with. Lai Shu is outside hanging up laundry, and waves at her. The matronly woman’s gaze is still a little too alert to be entirely friendly, but it’s friendly enough, and familiar by now. Raika waves a few fingers back, nodding over at one of the other woman’s kids as he does his level best to imitate her chickens and shove his head into a bucket full of soapy water. Lai Shu turns, letting out a loud cry of “Lai Mi! You get your head out of there this instant!”, which leaves her youngest startled and on the verge of tears but successfully not neck-deep in the bucket.

Raika huffs out a chuckle, nods at her neighbor, and keeps walking.

It’s not the only encounter on her way. On such a lovely day, in the middle of harvest season, there’s plenty of people out and about, working on chores, coming home for lunch, or otherwise enjoying the nice weather. Kids play in the streets, one particular group kicking a rough-hewn ball across the road back and forth, stopping to let her by. Old folk sit on stoops and in gardens, gossiping or watching the world go by, and farmers covered in dirt and sweat wander in and out as they get back to or come from the fields.

It’s a small place. Self-sufficient, for the most part. She feels dramatically out of place.

It doesn’t help that she’s at least a foot and a half taller than the next tallest person in town. Or that her walking stick is more of a staff. Or how she looks a different shade of sun-kissed than everyone else, with hair much longer and braided in patterns unique to her.

Or the scars.

She’s honestly lost track of them all. There’s the scars of cuts, the jagged star on her forehead from a bullet, the slash coming from one side of her mouth back to her cheek, the marks of jagged claws and the burns. It’s easier to find someplace on her skin with a scar than without, and it’s not close.

They’re pretty useful, though. Without them, the looks she’d probably get would be a lot more fearful. A seven-foot titan, clear stranger, blatant outsider, is a lot less scary when they’re missing chunks. The fact that one of her knees doesn’t really work, forcing her to limp even with the aid of a walking stick, makes her easier to stomach. The missing arm, severed at the bicep, marks her out as victim.

And she’s useful.

She uses her walking stick to knock on a screen door, tapping on the wood three times and waiting.

The house looks like any other. A bit closer to the center of town, maybe a bit older, but perfectly normal. There is a small garden of tea plants out front, as well as a tired old dog, laying lazily in the shadow of the building and paying Raika not one bit of mind.

She knocks again, wincing as the doorframe rattles a bit.

Even as she is, it’s easy to forget how easily she could break some of these buildings.

Before the thought annoys her any further, she hears a cough, then a grumble, then the sound of shuffling feet.

She elects to wait instead of knocking again, though the shuffling stops and starts once or twice on the way to the door.

Finally, it slides open, revealing an ancient-looking man who glares at her with all the fury of a man whose sleep has been interrupted.

“What’dya want?” he snaps, sun-darkened skinned wrinkled enough to make him look perpetually sour beneath a hair of thinning salt-and-pepper hairs.

She huffs, smiling down at him. “Is that any way to greet someone here to offer gifts?”

He grumbles again, like a cantankerous old cat, before spitting into the dirt off to one side. “It’s the way I damn well please to treat anyone interrupting my afternoon nap. I don’t knit and I don’t carve, so the least you can do is leave me to my hobby in peace.”

This time she laughs a little, her smile softening. “Apologies, elder. It’s only that I heard from Wu Jie that you’d been coughing a bit more than usual lately, and seeing I’d rather not get kicked out of town for laziness and witchcraft, I decided it would be best to help sooner than later.”

He snorts, glaring at her through squinted eyes that make him look positively ancient. “You outsiders, always full of so much fucking yap. You got a house, don’t ya? Who’s kicking you out? No one but this Bi Jao would dare, and I will dare if you don’t leave me be!”

She raises an eyebrow at that, before pointedly looking at his size compared to hers.

He seems entirely unbothered by the two-foot difference between their sizes, simply continuing to glare at her with the sort of utter confidence that comes from being old.

“Then I’ll have to make sure not to bother you,” she tells him. Letting her staff rest against the house’s wall, she reaches into her robes for the pouch she grabbed on her way out. “I just figured, if you’re having a bit of a cough, you could use a bit of medicine.”

“Bah! Who needs your herbs? I’m old, woman! Old people cough! It comes with being close to dying! And in this heat? As if I have time for your medicine!”

“Not even if it’s the sort of medicine that fits in a pipe?”

He pauses at that. Considers.

“Go on.”

She chuckles before handing him the package, a little cloth pouch filled with dark blue weeds. “It’s an herb of my own, from where I came from. Rolled up or put into a pipe, if you smoke it, it’ll fill your lungs with cooling smoke, rather than hot. I mixed in some a simple cough remedy with it, and it should help alleviate your lungs. Tastes weird at first, but I’ve found that it’s an acquired taste.”

Bi Jao looks down at the pouch. Then up at her. Then he grumbles, turning to stagger back into his house. “Well fine then, come in since you’re here.”

“Ah, apologies, elder, but I’m afraid I can’t. I might have to be gone for a day or two, so I figured I should bring this down to you before I get too busy and forget.”

“What’s this? You come all the way to my house, knock on my door, foist this concoction on me, and won’t even accept a seat? Are you trying to disrespect me, woman?”

“I wouldn’t dare, old man. I’m afraid I’m just too generous for your hospitality to catch me.”

“Wha- You dare? And who are you calling an old man? Why I–”

“Make sure not to smoke too much at once, or it’ll knock you out for hours! Just a pinch should last a while, it burns slow!”

She’s already clacking her cane against the dirt road and stepping on her way back, in spite of old man Bi Jao’s annoyed shouting echoing out behind her.

“You’ve got him in a proper heat this time, Rai,” Wu Ji says as she passes by her house. The young man raises a glass of tea to her in a salute. “Careful, or you’ll give him a heart attack before his time!”

She scoffs, smiling as she does. “Please. Looking as I do, I’ve had plenty of experience giving men heart attacks. I know the symptoms too well to endanger such a refined gentleman.”

“And you see if I save you any fish next festival! I’ll give them away to the children, the foxes, and right back to the lake!”

Raika and Wu Ji both chuckle, and she waves at Bi Jao before turning and walking back.

She makes it home with a few minutes to spare.

She lands heavily on her bed, feeling the wood of the floor creak as she does. In spite of reinforcement, she’s still heavy enough that she’ll have to replace it in another year or two.

It’s a bit annoying. If she’d known she would lose her powers the way she did, she would’ve made herself a bit smaller. Or at least skinnier. As it is, she had to cannibalize whole chunks of her anatomy to keep herself functional when everything changed.

Sighing, she lays down, letting herself relax and slowly releasing the tension in her muscles. It took the entire hour just to walk down the hill into town and back, and she’ll be feeling plenty sore about it the rest of the day. She makes a note to prepare some anti-inflammatory tea later, whenever she wakes.

In the bright sunshine of the afternoon day, she closes her eyes, drifting off to someplace dark.

When she opens them again, she is elsewhere.

Before her, in a rather dramatic circle, are seven chairs. Six of them are already filled.

“You are late,” intones the metallic obelisk making its chair creak dangerously.

“Cut her some slack,” says the woman made of writhing worms, silk, and acidic beetles. “It’s pretty last minute, and we’ve all got things to do.”

“No judgement registered,” says the pillar, speaking in spite of the total absence of a face or mouth. “Simple statement of fact. She is late.”

“Yeah, well, not all of us have clocks inside our guts, Raika says, coming around her seat and settling into it with a groan. “Can we start this up already? Last time we did this I was out for like three days. I’d rather we get to the important things first.”

“Of course,” says the woman of bloody sunlight, fleshy flora and strange skies. “To the first issue, then. It's been a few years, but the time has come. We stand on the precipice. It is time we decided the next act. It is time... that we prepare for War.”

Raika sighs. She can be so dramatic sometimes.
