Barry had both arms buried in a half-reassembled power conduit, something he'd picked up on the road.

"Yep, this is crap," he said, pulling one hand out. It came away slick with a strange, clinging oil. "Nothing useful in here."

He rubbed the oil between his fingers. Hmm...

Then his bracelet buzzed, and a red light started to beep.

[Level 1 security breached]

"Loki again?" he said. "Hope not. My security will eat her alive."

[Level 2 security breached]

Barry kept glancing at his wrist. The only person who could ever do that was Thor, and Thor wasn't here. Still, he wasn't worried. His last layer was just that impossible to breach.

[Level 3 security breached]

"Oh, would you look at that," he said. "Someone just breached all of my security. All at once."

The air in the workshop began to change. The door was built to resist: wards, alloys, a few clever tweaks of his own, traps layered everywhere. None of it mattered now. A thin thread of blue Bifrost light slipped straight through.

He didn't need to think twice. Only one being could punch through everything he had.

Barry stood, wiped the oil off on a rag, and said simply, "Lord Odin."

Thud.

When he turned, Odin was just there, standing in the middle of the messy room as if he'd been there all along. The ruler of Asgard, in his golden robe, one eye glowing faintly with Bifrost and fixed on Barry. It wasn't a look, exactly. To Barry it felt like a glare.

"Engineer," Odin said, crossing slowly to the sofa. "You have been useful." He settled onto it. "The Bifrost road. The magic crystal. You even improved the very foundation of our magic."

Barry dragged a chair over, set it down near Odin, and sat, perfectly at ease. "Well, that's what I'm here for. Fixing things."

"You must already know about Ragnarok by now."

Barry nodded and raised a hand. With a soft magnetic whir, the book flew off the shelf and into his palm. "Yeah. You fighting a wolf, Thor fighting a serpent, and everything turning to shit."

Odin nodded. "I suppose you do. But I still can't trust you. Not yet."

Silence.

"More than that, I still don't know you well enough." He stood.

"That's a compliment I'll take, Lord Odin," Barry said, rising with him. "So why are you here? To start some banter? Try a little ancient manipulation?"

"No, no." Odin stepped closer. "I speak the truth, engineer. The truth. If you are to become greater, to help us further, you need to understand our world better."

"Which means what?" Barry muttered.

"You will. Soon enough." Odin waved his hand, and in that moment Barry saw it: Odin's thumb, glowing gold.

Barry's mind went straight to the slow rot eating through Odin's body, that shining decay no Aesir healer had been able to stop. It was dangerous on more than one level, but he kept his face calm.

"Forget about my understanding," he said. "That sickness of yours is going to eat you alive one day."

Odin said nothing. He lifted one hand, and a door of pure Bifrost energy opened beside them. A blade of cold wind sliced through, sharp enough to sting.

"Come..."

Barry hesitated for half a second, then stepped through.

They landed on ice beneath a sky that looked cracked. The wind came in waves. Mountains of stone jutted up in the distance like shattered teeth, and closer in lay the town: abandoned homes of stone and ice, frozen corpses, children hauling firewood, empty food stores, a prayer shrine buried under thousands of offerings.

Odin didn't need to explain. Barry already knew.

"Jotunheim..."

"Indeed."

Farther off, frost giants moved between buildings built to their own scale. Some stood three times Barry's height, others were nearly his size. All of them had pale skin, white hair, and light eyes, and they carried clubs, spears, and axes of bone and iron. They wore animal hides and rough cloth. Nothing here ran on magic the way Asgard did. Nothing looked more advanced than hammer and fire.

Odin's robe never stirred in the wind. "They have prayed for a long time. Now they will be answered. You are the answer to their prayers."

"Simply put, you want me to fix their problems."

Barry looked toward the town. A group had spotted the Bifrost door. Some stopped to stare, others didn't care. It was his first time seeing another species up close, and the questions came on their own: what had made them evolve so large, and able to withstand this freezing air?

But something else was nagging at him. "What's the real issue, Lord Odin?"

"Just..." Odin's voice cut him off. He was already turning, the door closing behind him. "Don't die..."

"Huh? Wait. You're leaving me here? Just like that?"

Then he was gone. The door sealed with a soft sound, and Barry stood alone on the ice, already annoyed at how much time this was going to waste. Typical. Handed a problem and told to figure it out himself.

The closest giants started walking toward him. Barry backed up a step and activated his jacket.

Odin sank back into Hlidskjalf with a long, heavy sigh of relief. The hall was empty except for Freya, who stood at the foot of the steps, arms crossed.

"You left him there, didn't you." She stared into his one eye as if she meant to rip it out.

"Yes." He couldn't meet her gaze.

"They'll eat him. Or freeze him. Or both. They're savages, Odin. They turned down your magic."

"Barry is stronger than that."

Her jaw tightened. "I know what you're doing. You're sending him away so that..."

"Yes," he cut in. "I am using him as a way out. The giants have been calling to the Tree for years, and by now their voices are nothing but noise. He will make sense of that noise." His eye narrowed. "And while he is busy, away from Thor, tell Thor to stop wasting time and get ready for the throne."

"You want the change to come faster," Freya said. "You're supposed to be the wise one. You know what that will cost."

"I want it ready. That is all." Without another word, he left. The great doors closed behind him.

Behind the throne, in the narrow gap between stone and wall, Loki had been standing there the entire time. The smile she usually wore was gone. Her face was completely blank.

"He wants my brother on the throne," she said. "Not me."

She waited until the hall had fully emptied, then slipped out and teleported to the Bifrost observatory.

"Heimdall," she called.

Heimdall was watching Jotunheim when she arrived. He didn't turn. He kept his gaze on the Nine Realms and did his best to ignore her.

"I need a ride. Now."

"To Jotunheim."

"Yes. You see everything, so of course you know everything."

His golden eyes stayed fixed on the ice. "You will not cause chaos again. That is the rule. Keep it, and I will let you go."

"Of course," Loki said calmly. "I understand."

"Hmm." He looked at her for one full second, then slammed his spear against the ground. The Bifrost flared open in front of her in a flash of rainbow light, and Loki stepped onto the bridge without looking back.

Barry didn't wait for the giants to reach him. He angled sideways, neither toward the town nor away from it, heading for the hills above, where he'd get a better view. The ice was rough and treacherous, but his shoes held. The cold was creeping in. His jacket could handle Antarctica and Everest without a problem, but this place was something else. He filed that away for later.

He glanced back. The giants were gaining faster than he liked. Three of them, the tallest a good four meters, carrying iron-tipped clubs of wood bound with frozen sinew. They called to each other in a low, rough language he'd never heard, but the tone was clear enough: challenge, curiosity, hunger.

He had no intention of becoming dinner.

"Okay, they're gaining. Time to run." One look at those strides told him how that would end. "Where's that bulky Thor when you need him?"

Barry stopped and held up both hands, open and empty, to show he wasn't a threat.

They reached him and halted. The one in front stared at him for a long time, then finally opened his blue lips and spoke. The others kept their weapons ready.

"Ko haaaaaau iiiimi, eeeeta iiiina aaoooo?"

"Okay..." Barry adjusted his goggles, matching their tone, frequency, and gestures. He broke their speech into sounds, then built the sounds back into words, until the words started to make sense.

"You, small thing. Are you a soldier? Angel of the Allfather?"

"Me? No. NO. I'm an engineer. The Allfather sent me. Don't eat me, don't eat me!"

The goggles were still working on the language, so the giants just stared back at him.

One of them stepped closer, reaching to grab him. Barry backed away. "No touching. You touch me, you die."

Ting!

The translation locked in, and a small mic unfolded beneath his goggles.

"Can you understand me now? Hello. Don't eat me."

The giant's eyes went wide. "You know the giant tongue?"

"Yes. Yes, I do."

Barry needed information, and fast. One look at the town gave him most of it: cooking fires, tents, houses built from bones. Nothing resembled a proper forge beyond a few pits. They had the wheel, which was a good sign, but no metalwork beyond hammered iron and bronze.

Maybe Bronze Age, just starting to touch iron in places. It didn't take long to see that whatever was wrong here, it wasn't a technology problem. Resource distribution, energy infrastructure, agriculture, a shrinking population, magical infrastructure: all of it was either missing or failing.

"The whole system is rotting from the inside," he muttered.

One of the others moved closer, a female giant maybe two and a half meters tall, her face marked by frost and old cuts. She spoke slowly this time, pointing at Barry, then at the spot where the Bifrost door had opened.

"You don't have to talk slow," Barry said calmly. "I understand you. I'm not food, and I'm not here to fight."

She tilted her head, reached out, and poked his chest with one finger. It landed like a hammer and sent him back a step. She was smaller than the others, but for some reason everyone waited on her. She made a low sound and spoke to them, and their weapons dipped a little.

"Welcome, soldier of Odin. We welcome you to our humble town."

"Thank you, I guess."

Barry used the moment to look for something he knew had to be here. He found it near the temple: a stone platform half-buried in ice, with statues of Odin and Freya at its center and offerings already heaped on the ground in front of them.

And there it was, the familiar crystal, green and gold, set into the stone. He pulled out his scanners and swept the whole statue. He'd been right. Of course he had. The crystal was linked to other channels, all of them running back to Yggdrasil. It wasn't just receiving prayers. It was storing them, routing them, compressing them, and sending them up through the Tree.

"I'm going to touch this, if you don't mind."

The others had wandered off by now. Only the female giant stayed with him, and she simply nodded.

Barry hooked a wire to the crystal and ran it into a white sphere he'd been working on, a prototype filter, then linked the sphere to his goggles. He slipped them on, and they went hot almost at once.

"What the..."

The lenses glowed red, ready to blow. He ripped the cable free. After some quick work on the goggles' processor, he started the process again.

This time a prayer-relay monitor flickered to life.

"Okay, that worked. Of course it did. It's me." He took the goggles off and set them on the ground. "Okay, you. What was your name again?"

"Bela..."

"Weird human name for a giant. Okay, don't freak out. Everything's gonna be fine."

She nodded.

"Project the data."

The goggles flickered, and a ghostly glow rose above the lenses.

"Ha! What is this?!" Bela scrambled backward and fell onto the ice. "A daemon? Magic?"

"Oh, right. You lot don't even have the concept of science, let alone this." Barry pulled out his gun and pointed it at her.

"No, no..." she pleaded.

"Don't worry. It's for your own good." He fired. The injector vial pierced her skin, and she slid slowly into unconsciousness.

He knew that in a place this dysfunctional he needed someone, someone he could use to reach the people. And there was no better candidate than the unconscious giant in front of him.

Offerings surrounded the platform: bones, broken tools, scraps of hide, a few animals. Barry knelt, pulled out his tools, and cleared the ice away from the statues and the crystal. Once enough was clear, he touched two points, sent a check from the device he always carried, and, voilà, the goggles on the ground started projecting the data.

Everything came flooding in. The principle was simple. The execution was insane. Odin had said the giants had prayed enough, which meant the problem lived inside those prayers, all that faith stored and passed along through the statues and the crystal. And now every prayer they had ever made began to pour out.

"Okay, we have one, ten, hmm, three hundred..."

He paused as the numbers kept climbing. And climbing. And climbing.

It wasn't one problem. It was problems stacked on problems, and still growing. War, hunger, illness, hunts gone wrong, no children, raids that never ended, whole families lost to cold or hunger or each other. Prayers for food. Prayers for revenge. Prayers that only asked for the day to be okay.

None of it had ever been fixed. None of it had ever been answered. The debts just got older and heavier, and each new generation of giants was born owing problems their great-grandparents had never solved.

Barry looked away from the projection and sat down on the ice. "...Damn you, Odin."

This was pure negligence on Odin's part, a mess he'd left for Barry to solve.

He leaned back, his breath fogging in the air. The scale of it was more than one person could hold. It was easier to look at the tent beside him, the one with three fires for twelve people and food for only eight. That was what the big numbers looked like on the ground.

Behind him, the female giant stirred as she came around. "What did you do to me?"

"Enhanced neural processing, accelerated learning, improved magical compatibility. Basically, I upgraded your whole genetic makeup."

"Huh? What do you... wait. How do I understand what that meant?"

Barry grabbed his goggles and stepped closer, scanning her from head to toe. Her brain frequency had changed, and for the better. He offered her a hand and helped her up.

"Congratulations. You are now the smartest giant in all of Jotunheim."

"Th... thank you, I guess."

"Now come here. Help me."

"Help you? With what?"

"Saving your whole realm before it destroys itself."

She looked toward the starving town, at her people, and then back at Barry. Something like hope flickered in her eyes.
