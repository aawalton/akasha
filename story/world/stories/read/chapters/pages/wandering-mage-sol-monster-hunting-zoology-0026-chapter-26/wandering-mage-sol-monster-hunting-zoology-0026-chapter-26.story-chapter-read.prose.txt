Tressa had thought the bridge looked massive from a distance, but actually standing on it was something else entirely. She had to crane her neck up to see the enormous stone pillars above them. Thick iron braces ran between them, and they looked so thick she doubted she could even get both hands around them. She could hardly even imagine how any of this was created.

It was wide with plenty of room for multiple horse-drawn carriages to pass in both directions, and ahead of them, it stretched as far as their eyes could see. Below their feet was beautiful polished stone, hardly showing any wear from what must have been centuries of travel, so it must have been cleaned and maintained regularly.

“This is amazing,” Tressa said with a low gasp.

Lisha and Briadon walked on either side of her while the merchants they agreed to escort were just behind, leading their oxen and carts.

“How did they even build this?”

“With stone, I imagine,” said Briadon.

“Obviously,” she said, putting up a middle finger.

Lisha’s tongue flicked as she tried not to laugh.

“I mean, how,” Tressa continued, running up ahead and then jumping onto the railing to look down at the massive water below and focusing on the stone pillars supporting it that disappeared into the water. The sun had mostly fallen, so the water reflected the starlight above beautifully, making its dark waves against the support seem otherworldly.

“Look at it! The water is deep, yet the pillars go far down! They would need to build under the lake. How is it possible?”

“Dwarves,” Briadon answered as Tressa hopped down from the railing and rejoined them.

“That explains nothing,” Tressa complained.

“It explains more than you’d think,” Briadon said. “Dwarves are the best builders in the world.”

Tressa’s ears twitched under her hood, and as she heard one of the merchants’ paces quickening to catch up, she drew her hood down further.

“Aye, he’s right about that young lady.” She looked over to see it was Mitchel. “Have you never even heard of the Carrion Bridge before?”

“I have not,” she admitted.

Luke looked at her a little skeptically as he led an ox. Below them, the water splashed around from the wind and hit the bars of the support beams.

“Come to think of it, I can’t place your accent either. Where are you from?” Luke asked.

Tressa stiffened. “Oh, heheh. I’m from the far northwest, near the Thimberman Mountains.”

“Huh. The only cities I know from there are the fallen dwarven kingdom and the capital of the high elves—Faldin or something like that. Then, to the far, far northwest are the Bloodlands. No one dares travel there. It's treacherous and, well, the blood elves are even worse about visitors than High Elves, from what I hear. But are there other towns around there I am not aware of.”

Tressa coughed and pulled her cloak closer around her. “Please do not ask. I do not wish to speak of it.”

“Hmmm. Well, everyone has their reasons for not saying where they are from, and I don't blame ya if you live near the damn high elves. Must be hard. Probably still have some humans and dwarves held captive.”

“That’s not tru—”

Lisha nudged Tressa, trying to stop her from saying anything that might give her away.

“I’m sorry young lady, what was that?” Luke asked.

“I um… I know not of this happening. In truth, the things I hear of High Elves when I began traveling don’t match what I’ve known.” She let out a deep breath. “But the world is vastly different from what I expected.”

“I guess you didn’t leave your village much?” Luke asked. They passed by one of the basin’s of fire lighting the bridge and Tressa turned away from him.

“Yes. My father was none too pleased that I had left home. Mother was supportive, but he had wanted me to stay and follow in his footsteps.”

“Sounds rough, kiddo,” said Mitchel. “I left my home as well at an early age and met Luke here, who had been training under his master. He found me hungry and wandering around when I was no more than maybe thirteen winters old. Well, they fed me and gave me clothes, and the master told me I would need to pay him back. It turns out I was quite good at being a merchant, so I joined them and became his apprentice as well. When we graduated from our master, we stayed in business together.”

“That sounds amazing,” Tressa said. “You must have seen so much in your travels.”

“Yes, but not as much as you monster hunters, I’m sure. How long have you been traveling?”

“Just six months.”

“And you’re a mage, aren’t you?” he asked.

She nodded. “I am. I was talented at it and practiced hard. I’m just glad I can assist Lisha and Briadon. They are far more seasoned than I as monster hunters.”

“Yeah, but she comes in handy; don’t let her age fool you,” Briadon laughed.

“Still quite the mystery,” Luke pondered, leaning over to try to see under her hood. Thankfully, the darkness helped hide her, and whenever they passed under a basin of fire, she’d turn towards the shadows. She didn’t want her being a High Elf to cause Lisha and Briadon to miss out on yet more coin if she scared these merchants.

“But your story sounds amazing too, beginning as an apprentice,” Tressa said. “My story is far more difficult than that. My father had a plan for my future without consulting me or my mother and was very strict about how I was to appear in publicts.”

“Public,” Lisha said.

“Puldicks?” she said, furrowing her brow as she tried to get the word.

“No, public. Like society.”

“Socetaties.”

Lisha laughed as Tressa struggled to say the word, making more sounds than she needed to and causing the others to chuckle.

“Tell me, lass, is the common tongue not yer first language?”

Lisha sucked in a breath and Tressa looked at Luke.

“No, it's not.”

“Hmmm, might you be from overseas?”

“No, Luke, she said she lived near the mountains. So she...” he trailed off and stopped. “Wait a sec, you’re an elf, arn’t ya?” he asked, putting the pieces together.

Tressa looked at Lisha, and the Statiki nodded.

“I am,” Tressa admitted.

“Well, wood elves are perfectly…” Mitchel began.

“She’s not a wood elf. Look at her hair,” Luke clicked his tongue, speaking more sternly. “Lower your hood, lass. I won’t travel with someone who hides themselves from me.”

Tressa let out a breath, looking between Lisha and Briadon. Lisha’s tongue flicked, and Briadon let out a huff. “Might as well at this point,” Briadon said to her.

“Just don’t misunderstand, please…” Tressa said, finally pulling her hood back, revealing her blazing green eyes and silvery blonde hair. The wind blew, tousling her hair to reveal her pointed ears.

Luke tensed, as did Mitchel.

“High Elf. You’ve deceived us!” Luke spat at her. “I thought you were human!”

“I’m sorry, but I never deceived you,” Tressa said. “You never asked my race; you assumed. You asked where I lived, and I told you the truth of where I was from, and you filled in the pieces yourself. Not once did I say I am not an elf.” She looked away. “You just assumeded.”

“Assumed,” Lisha corrected.

“Is that not what I said?”

“Not quite,” Lisha said with a chuckle.

“Ahahaha, sorry there, merch, but she has a point,” Briadon cut in. “She never stated she was human. But I’ll tell you, she’s as capable a hunter as either of us. You want a safe escort, then don’t turn away a good deal just because you can’t deal with her being an elf.”

“A wood elf I could deal with,” Luke grumbled… But as he looked at Tressa, the way her posture was downcast, and her eyes lowered, he seemed to soften. Tressa did look young, after all, and not just in appearance but in the way she spoke and acted.

“I’m sorry, but I had nothing to do with the Mad King,” Tressa said, turning towards him. “I was born towards the end of his reign. I was very young when he died; even in human years, I’d have been considered young.”

Luke studied her for another moment, his expression still guarded.

“So you're really a member of the Hunters Guild?”

Tressa nodded. “I am.”

Luke glanced toward Lisha and Briadon, then back at Tressa.

“That’s strange.”

“What is?” Tressa asked.

“A High Elf traveling around with a Statiki and a minotaur like this. Especially after everything I've heard about your people.”

His eyes shifted toward Lisha. “She's not bound to you somehow, is she?”

Lisha's head turned. “What exactly are you asking?”

Luke hesitated before saying it. “Is she your slave?”

Lisha immediately bared her teeth. “No. She is my friend, a hunter, and an ally. And if anyone tries to touch her in that way, I won't be afraid to use my full powers to protect her. She is a member of the guild and therefore protected under Unison Law.”

“Hey, hey, hey!” Luke said, raising both hands as they came to a stop. “I was only asking.”

“You asked poorly,” Lisha growled.

A noise came from Mitchel's throat as he stepped back and stared at Lisha.

Tressa walked up to her and placed a hand on her shoulder. “It’s okay, they didn't mean any harm. But are slaves still a thing?”

Everyone turned and looked at her, astonished.

“Elf, are you saying that there are no slaves among your people today?” asked Luke.

“My name is Tressa, and no, there are no slaves. They were freed after the Mad King was killed. King Faldin, our current ruler helped everyone get home who wished to leave and give them gold to get them started with their lives again and to make up for what his father had done.” Tressa said. “There were some who thought he gave too much, as my people were recovering from the wars as well, but he said it was no choice, as it was the responsibility of the High Elves to right the wrongs done by the Mad King, and the council agreed.”

The two merchants looked at each other, shame coming into their posture as they saw how Tressa’s hands were shaking. She was holding back tears in her eyes and looked away.

She was just so tired of it. Being blamed for something she had no control over. For being a burden on her friends. Constantly having to prove she wasn’t dangerous when everybody assumed the worst. She’d smile and put up with it and shrug it off when treated poorly. But suddenly it just wouldn’t hold back.

She was on this beautiful bridge, further from home than she ever imagined being, seeing an incredible world she’d only dreamed of. But still, Falidorn’s past came along with her like a dark shadow. And for once she just wanted to not smile but be angry because it wasn’t fair!

“I didn’t do anything,” she snapped, pulling away from Lisha and wiping at her eyes angrily before the tears could fall. “I did not start the wars. I did not enslave anyone. I did not hurt humans or dwarves or anyone else! I was a child! Yet everywhere I go, people see my ears and suddenly think I am dangerous or cruel! You thought I was a normal person ten minutes ago. You were talking with me and laughing, and then you saw my ears and suddenly everything changed!”

Luke shifted, looking away and clearing his throat.

Tressa tried to calm herself down a little as she said, “I understand why people hate the Mad King. I hate him too. I hate what he did to everyone. My people suffered under him as well. And even hundreds of years after he died, he still follows us everywhere.”

For a time, only the wind lapping waves filled the air.

Finally, Mitchel said, “You’re right.” As Luke opened his mouth to say something, Mitchel said, “She’s right, Luke.”

He gave Tressa a smile. “We hired you three to protect us, and you’ve done nothing to us. So I apologize.”

Tressa looked away, feeling at least a little relieved to get that off of her chest. She had been holding it in ever since her travels began, trying not to let her friends see how it bothered her.

“I’m sorry… for creating such an unsightly moment,” Tressa said, wiping at her cheeks.

“Not at all,” Mitchel said softly, seeing how truly young she seemed to be. “In truth, I never actually met a High Elf. But it’s clear I may have misunderstood some things. Right, Luke?”

Luke nodded. “I apologize as well. Truly.”

“Thank you,” Tressa said.

Lisha patted her back and said to everyone. “Well, you are all right to continue with our agreement as planned, then?”

“Yes, of course,” Luke said.

“Good, then the three of us will protect you to the best of our ability. Now let’s continue before the night grows any deeper.”

Tressa smiled and pulled her hood back up over her head, though her hands were still shaking as they continued across the bridge.

But as the others began talking again, an old nightmare resurfaced in the back of her mind. A room full of chains. Laughter. Screams. And a dark figure standing over her, grabbing her face, fingers digging painfully into her cheeks, and whispering to her.
