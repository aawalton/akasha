Hayden’s ship lost electronic function the moment that it exited the rift. The analog safety systems kicked in just as the wet, cloudy landscape of Heline disappeared, replaced by a sunny, colorful landscape.

Rifts contained microcosms of different worlds. Most were about ten to fifteen square miles if they had open or forest terrain, four to eight if it was dense. Some were smaller if the rift contained a cavern.

Grand rifts were different.

Most were 25 miles wide and across, for a total area of 625 square miles. Many contained ghost cities from ancient civilizations who tried to survive and spread to alternate dimensions. Others contained open worlds—microcosms of world landscapes.

This was one of those worlds—but it was far larger. The Heline Rift was the largest rift in recorded history, a staggering 2,500 square miles of colorful mountains and foliage. The microcosm looked like a painter’s palette, a peacock world that screamed “poison” at every step. If Hayden didn’t have a treasure hunter, finding anything in the rift would be like searching for a black grain of sand on a beach. Even with one, it would be impossible to find something specific unless someone knew where it was—and Hayden didn’t.

Thankfully, he remembered the big things about the rift—

Things that were impossible to forget.

Hayden took a snapshot of the rift from the ship’s windows as it flew above the trees. He saw the distinctive purple mass that looked like a regular mountain covered in purple flora, and a chilling black section surrounded by massive, circling birds. He couldn’t see the last landmark—but that was expected. It was fifty miles away—at the far end—a location that required one to traverse the entire rift.

Hayden committed other geographic markers into his memory—and then the shuttle plummeted to the forest below.

The ship hit the forest canopies with a loud crunch and then flipped as it fell through the trees. It usually would have been a bumpy experience, but the team was strapped into gyroscopic chairs that remained relatively motionless as the shuttle flipped and crashed onto the ground. The chairs were designed for launches—and they performed the job well.

Hayden was amused that he was still sitting normally, despite the fact that the rest of the ship was vertical. If he unbelted, he’d fall into the pilot seat.

“That’s as good as she’ll get,” Kalia said. “So let’s get going.”

The team unclipped from their chairs and scaled the seats to grab their packs, chutes, and survival equipment. Lent then pried open the shuttle’s door, and they jumped outside onto the lush, forest ground cover.

Fringe threw Hayden an ultra-thin pack that went underneath his backpack. “This is your extra chute. Don’t fuck it up. If the Sickles steal our backup chutes, we’re stuck here.”

“I understand,” Hayden said. He glanced at the wormhole. Unlike on Heline, where it was in the sky, that same massive mercury sphere was before them, touching the ground and extending a thousand feet upward. To get out of the rift, the team needed to dive out with a parachute. To ensure they had one, rifters had a parachute on their backs and another at the gate. Usually, there was a team member waiting at the mouth of the rift with the backups—or RDA officials, in the case of grand rifts—but they had neither. So they needed to hide their backups. Otherwise, if they damaged their main parachutes and the Sickles—or any other antagonist—stole their parachutes, there would be no way to escape the rift. Therefore, they would be at the mercy of anyone who had their parachutes.

“Do you?” Fringe pressed sarcastically.

“Fringe,” Kalia warned.

“No.” He turned to her. “In ten minutes the Crimson fucking Sickles are gonna launch through that rift, and when they do, we’ll be at war with elites in the deadlist place in the universe. And this kid’s orderin’ us around with that bored fuckin’ face like this is a field trip. This isn’t a game. If he doesn’t listen to us—we’re dead.”

She grimaced. “Yeah, that may be true—”

“That’s not true,” Hayden interrupted. “I foretold this rift, and since then, I’ve acquired billions in resources and governmental support. If you don’t think I’m qualified to lead a rifting expedition, you’re a fool.”

“Lead?” Fringe asked. “You want to lead? I don’t care who you are. You hired A-rank rifters—”

“Correct.” Hayden stopped him with a cool gaze. “I hired you. As mercenaries that were willing to face off against the Sickles in exchange for a ridiculous sum of money. You accepted; thus, until you fulfill your contract, your lives belong to me, and you will do as I say.”

Fringe’s face burned, and he wasn’t the only one. Kalia, Photel, Yaka, Tibbi, and Lent stared at him coldly. No one liked to hear that they belonged to someone. Kalia spoke first.

“We’re going to be together for quite some time. It would do everyone good if you were a bit more friendly. Good relationships breed better results.”

“I agree,” Hayden said. “That’s why you should be chastising the belligerent man who’s done nothing but yell since we set out for the rift. Or do you all lack professionalism?”

“What’d you just say?” Fringe snapped. He took a threatening step forward, but Kalia hit him in the stomach with lightning speed, making him fall to his knees.

“That was a mistake, yes,” Kalia said. “He has been out of line, and we’ve all been too stressed to chastise him. That said, you should respect our situation. Just because we agreed to this doesn’t make us any less human. If you treat us as pawns, we will naturally become bitter and hostile.”

“No, I’m treating you as subordinates,” Hayden said. “The fact that you’ve treated me as a child instead of your leader is your mistake, and I’ve chastised you rightfully. Do it again, and I’ll send you home. Just be aware that you’ll have to bear the full weight of my connections if that happens—and I’ll show you no mercy.”

Hayden’s eyes were so frigid that the rift’s tropical heat seemed to cool. The group stiffened in response.

“I hired you to be my subordinates. Treat me as your leader, and I’ll treat you as humans. Until then, you’re nothing but unprofessional liabilities who are refusing to bear the weight of their decisions.” Hayden glared at Fringe and then swept their gazes. “Who has wind magic?”

Lent folded his arms. “I do.”

“Did you see a large dead tree to the northeast, and the meadow to the northwest?”

“Yes.”

“Good.” Hayden put on his backpack, clipped his chest and waist bands, adjusted them, and then turned to the team. “Give the parachute backups to Lent. We’re walking a mile west while he buries them at the tree. Once he finishes, we’ll meet at the meadow.”

In this case, the RDA and Golden Belt teams would soon arrive, but Hayden didn’t want them to hold power over him either. So hiding the parachutes was a must.

Despite the logic, Fringe laughed roughly. “You want to split up? Now? And by a mile? Are you insane?”

“Everyone here should be able to navigate a forest,” Hayden said. “If they can’t, they won’t be of any use to this team.”

Fringe snarled, but Lent put up his hands and spoke. “I’m all for it. Kiddo’s threw a thinker on that one. I think it’s better to split and do the divert before the Sicks get here, and double track it to catch their eye. I wouldn’t sleep a wink if we didn’t.”

“I agree,” Tibbi said. “The first thing they’re gonna do is find a way to control us. Avoiding that should be our first priority.”

“Agreed,” Kalia said. “Any other complaints? If you have any, voice ‘em now.”

No member raised objections. They simply gazed at Fringe, who snorted and said, “Fine.”

“Cool. I’ll meet you up north.” Lent ran into the forest, light wind blasting underneath his boots to remove his footprints.

Hayden then turned to the team. Let’s go.”

The group complied, walking west in an area that was just thin enough for the incoming ship to see them. The idea was to capture the other team’s attention, so they wouldn’t search for Lent. It seemed to work.

Not fifteen minutes later, the Crimson Sickles’ shuttle launched out of the wormhole. Hayden's group could see the ship, so the occupants could see them, too. That was good.

Things were going as expected—
