Adabelle had been right; with Trevor’s enhanced Dexterity, learning to ski didn’t take long at all. His body connected with her instructions much easier than he had thought it would, and she taught him with patience and skill. He wouldn’t be beating any Olympians at their own sport, not yet anyway, but he wasn’t crashing into anything and he was having a great amount of fun.

With Lora guiding them, it didn’t take long to reach the cozy mountain village of Froslan Heights. To the newlywed’s surprise, it was a verdant sanctuary in the sea of snow and stone. Some of the greenest grass and trees Trevor had seen in this world grew here, and tiers of terrace garden fields rose all around the settlement.

Some he guessed were for rice, but he could also see wheat, tomato, watermelon, corn, beans, and some fruit trees. All were unbothered by the cold. In fact, they seemed to be thriving. A stone aquifer from higher on one of the peaks brought melted ice water down to where they needed it most.

The buildings were constructed inside of the hills. They seemed small at first, but one near the village entrance had its door open and it appeared as though the home went deeper underground. There were livestock everywhere, and Trevor was once again reminded of Vilderchtif though in far less inhabitable lands. They even had tasktas, sheep-like creatures with flat faces and clawed feet.

A pale, white-haired elf approached the group as they removed their skis. His eyes were the same color as frozen stone, but there was a warmth to them as he smiled and waved. It seemed as though he was under the same effects as they were, which seemed to extend to the entire town, because he wore light wool clothing with no jacket.

“Welcome to Froslan Heights!” he greeted, stopping in front of the group and bowing. “Asalorana, it’s good to see you again.”

“Mayor Nester, hello!” the teleporter returned with a grin. “These are the ones I talked to you about. Lady Adabelle, who is also a mayor, her husband Trevor, and his familiar, Clay. This is Nester. He’s in charge of making sure that Froslan Heights keeps chugging along.”

“The pleasure is ours,” the demonkin said, matching the elf’s bow. Clay shifted on her shoulders to match her movement. “This is a lovely village. I’ll be honest; I wasn’t expecting everything to be so green and full of life.”

“Your expectation is a reasonable one,” Nester replied with a sage nod. “Shaman Brown and I work together in order to make sure that everyone is comfortable. She takes care of the cold while I make the land livable through magic. As it turns out, the hearts of the golems up in the peak make for a fine conduit, allowing me to set our boundaries.”

“That’s incredible. Even against the supernatural cold that Shaman Brown warned us about?” Adabelle asked.

“Yes, even against that,” the mayor answered. He gestured for them to follow him, and they did. “Froslan Heights has recently become self-sufficient thanks to the funds we’ve gained as our borrowed Summoned brings back materials from the dungeon. He is a little overzealous sometimes, but he does good work.”

Trevor tilted his head. “Borrowed?”

“There used to be adventuring parties sent every so often to collect, but the mountain fights back,” Nester explained. “The supernatural cold is one way of doing so, and there was a stretch of time where the ice golems would roam Froslan Peak because we hadn’t received enough help. Thankfully, one came and settled down nearby. He takes great joy in being able to knock them around.”

“Nearby,” Adabelle repeated. “That sounds like he’s not a part of the village.”

The mayor shook his head slowly. “Hans has some trouble controlling his strength, which makes it hard for him to coexist sometimes, but he also prefers the cold. I’m still not sure if it’s because of what he is or if the mountain just likes him.”

“No spoilers, though,” Lora said, and Trevor pouted at her.

“You’ll meet him soon enough,” Nester chuckled.

As they walked through the village, it became clear that most of its inhabitants were dwarves and pale elves. There were a few humans and orcs, hardier folks, but only a handful of families. The mayor led them around a deep gouge in the middle of the road right before they arrived at the only structure above ground.

“This is my office, as well as a gathering place for the village,” Nester explained. “I was told you were bringing supplies?”

Trevor looked at Lora, who nodded. “Number six,” she informed him.

“Yes, sir. Do you want them inside?” he asked.

“If it’s not too much trouble,” the mayor answered. “Just inside, please.”

“Nah, I’ll only be a minute,” Trevor said with a smile before heading inside.

The main room was large, reminding him of a school gymnasium with random markings on the floor, and there were a few doors leading deeper in on the far wall. Most of the delivery here came in smaller boxes of medicines and talismans, though there were crates from everyone he had visited so far.

Once that was done, they moved on.

There was a flower garden filled with roses, mums, carnations, and others all corralled by waist-high hedges behind town hall. They entered under an archway and followed the stepping stones towards a sitting area. There were metal benches with fluffy cushions around a square table with cups and a container of what looked like lemonade. Everything sparkled as if it had just been cleaned.

“Please, take a seat,” Nester said as he sat down. “Drinks?”

“Yes, please. Thank you for your hospitality,” Adabelle replied as she did so, and he poured her a glass. Trevor seconded that as he sat beside her. Clay jumped down, laying across both of their laps.

“I’ll just take a glass for the road, if that’s fine by you,” Lora said. “But I’ll be back later. This is the last stop on the honeymoon trip, and I have a couple more errands to run.”

“We’ll be heading home after this?” the courier asked.

“Yup, sorry. Tomorrow. Show’s almost over,” she replied with a sad smile, taking the glass Nester offered her. “I hope it’s been a good one, though!”

“It’s been very memorable, Lora,” Adabelle reassured her. “You can get going. We’ll be fine under Mayor Nester’s care.”

Nodding, the teleporter snapped off a small salute while drinking some of the lemonade before disappearing.

“She’s always coming and going, isn’t she?” their host said with a chuckle.

“Lora definitely keeps busy, that’s for sure,” Trevor agreed as he took his glass.

Nester settled onto his own bench, shifting to better face the newlyweds. “First, I would like to congratulate you on your wedding. Asalorana has shown great excitement about your trip every time she comes.”

“Thank you,” Adabelle replied with a light nod. “Our trip has certainly been something to be excited about, so I can’t fault her enthusiasm.”

“Good, I’m glad it’s living up to her expectations,” the mayor said, smiling. “Second, I was told that you three are a great team of adventurers, even going so far as to complete a dungeon with the same general makeup of our own.”

Trevor let out a low laugh. “Well—”

“We are,” Adabelle interrupted, slightly squeezing her husband’s thigh before he could deny it. “Trevor here actually made a new record for completely intact armor pieces from the ghosts in our last dungeon. He was incredible.”

“Only because these two protected me,” he pouted, lifting up Clay from his lap. The earth elemental slumped, as if going limp would give their master more of the glory. “They’re ninety-nine percent of our combat strength. Plus, without my wife’s sword skills, they wouldn’t have given up when they did.”

“My contribution was no less important than his,” the demonkin said confidently. “The record is still Trevor’s, no matter what transpired.”

“The record is ours,” Trevor corrected before setting Clay back down before they decided to ooze through his fingers. “I’m not going to hog the glory. Don’t want it, don’t need it. We did it together.”

Nester laughed as he watched the dynamic. “There is nothing wrong with taking on a support role. In fact, I’ve heard that it is quite a vital one to fill.”

“You have heard correctly, Mayor Nester,” Adabelle said. “While my husband might be right about our combat capabilities, we still would have never performed as well without him. That’s true no matter how humble he wants to be.”

Trevor pouted, and Adabelle patted his leg gently. Clay followed suit, amusement flitting through their bond.

“That’s good. I’m curious, then, how you’ll fare against the ice golems at the peak of the mountain,” Nester said, sipping his drink. “Our Summoned’s tactics are generally to go in fast, break everything that moves, and then return with the chunks that we get to sort through. A lengthy process, but a necessary one. I know this is just temporary as you aren’t looking to move anywhere, but I’m looking forward to working with whole pieces again.”

“Is your guy that bad?” Trevor asked, frowning. Adabelle turned in her seat to look off into the distance.

The mayor shook his head. “He’s not bad. In fact, he's quite well loved by the people of Froslan Heights. As I said, he has trouble controlling his—”

A whistling noise caught his and Trevor’s attention. It was coming from the direction Adabelle was already looking in, and Nester sighed.

The path of stepping stones leading up to the garden terrace were suddenly three feet deeper into the ground as a hulking beast landed amongst them. They were at least nine feet tall hunched over with thick, grayish-white fur covering everything except for their black face and hands. The only clothes they wore was a pair of blue pants.

Adabelle was the first to move, standing up with Clay in her hands. She had pushed the metal bench back with ease and stepped in front of Trevor. He had barely reacted to the newcomer before there was a shout.

“Hans!” Nester yelled, exasperated, as he stood up. “How many times have I told you not to land in the garden? You know it’s going to take a lot of effort to fix that hole, and we still have one in front of town hall that you left!”

The giant fuzzy creature turned to the mayor, taking a few steps towards him. Even with his huge size, his knuckles dragged along the ground. This didn’t change after he left the small crater his landing had made.

He scratched the back of his head. When he spoke, it seemed to come through a series of grunts. “Sorry, Nest. Jumping faster. Heard challengers here. Excited.”

“Well, I suppose it’s my fault for bringing them here,” Nester allowed, though he was clearly upset about his garden. He sighed again and turned back to the trio. “This is Hans. He’s our Summoned.”

“You are a yeti,” Adabelle stated, her eyes still locked onto the newcomer. Trevor sat up a little straighter. He knew about those.

“Yes,” Hans confirmed. “You warrior. Can tell. Stance, strong.”

“I am Lady Adabelle,” she replied before setting Clay upon her shoulders. They laid low against her, looking up at the giant. “And yes, I am. Shaman Brown mentioned you would likely challenge us to a competition.”

“Yes,” he said with a nod. “Heard challengers. Fight dungeon together. Big ice monsters. On peak. But, that boring. Fight separate. Keep count. Exciting. Not you, though.”

The demonkin raised an eyebrow. “Not me?”

“Not you,” Hans repeated. “Told man would win. You not man. That man.”

The yeti pointed at Trevor, and he snorted. “They told you I’d beat you?”

“Yes.”

“That is my husband, Trevor, and this is his familiar, Clay,” Adabelle introduced. “And, yes, he will defeat you.”

This statement made Hans all the more excited. He lowered himself, moving almost like a gorilla to get a better look at the courier. Trevor stood up so he wouldn’t be towered over. They came face to face, and the yeti squinted at him.

“Not warrior,” he declared sullenly, the excitement draining from his voice. “Not warrior at all. No challenge. Probably mage. Gross.”

“Must I remind you again that I’m a mage, Hans?” Nester asked. “Please don’t be rude to our guests. You can’t judge them based on appearances.”

“Nest good mage. But, words true,” Hans allowed. He reached out and poked Trevor with a finger as thick as the man’s bicep. It nearly made him stumble, but his stats and skills helped keep him balanced. “You try beat Hans?”

“I mean, I guess so,” the courier said with a chuckle.

“You try real hard?”

“Yeah. I’ll try real hard.”

“Good. Let’s go.”

“Go whe—Whoa!”

One moment, Trevor was standing with his feet planted firmly on the ground. The next, an abominable snowman had wrapped his fingers around his chest and jumped, taking to the air. They soared towards one of the peaks at breakneck speed, and Hans cheered with excitement.

Apparently, it was time to visit a dungeon.
