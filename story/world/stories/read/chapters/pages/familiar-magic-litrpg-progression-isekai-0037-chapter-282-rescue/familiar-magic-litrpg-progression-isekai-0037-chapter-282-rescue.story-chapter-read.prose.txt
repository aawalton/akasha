A torrent of emotions crashed through Ethan as he looked up at the unlikely rescue. He could never forget his first impression of the mighty Familiar, it had been the most majestic thing he’d ever laid eyes on, and a stark contrast to the brutality and ugliness of Potentia. Even now he could close his eyes and see it perfectly, the feathers, white silver and blue, the two sets of wings, a tail drifting in the wind. Even that odd face, with its too-human eyes, and surprising absence of a beak, for a creature so bird-like.

Yet, as the Champion rank monster drew closer, and all eyes turned toward it, Ethan almost believed this was something else entirely.

The creature was barely holding itself together–in fact, it wasn’t. Heavy chunks of it were falling to the ground, disappearing into wisps of magic as they fell. One wing was entirely gone, and the others struggled to keep the remarkable beast aloft. Ethan had a terrifying moment where he almost believed the creature was undead, but closer inspection revealed that it was merely injured well beyond what he thought anything could survive.

That didn’t mean it wasn’t a threat, and the Follower army had already turned from Ethan and the Basilisk toward this new danger. Blasts of energy and spiny projectiles shot into the air, they flew toward the challenger like arrows, and Ethan wondered if even a Champion could survive such an onslaught, given how injured it already was.

But the attacks never reached his titanic savior. Instead, a single forward-flap of its wings sent an answering wave of ice cascading toward the army. The attack seemed to devour the various projectiles as it rushed forward, and Ethan felt the temperature drop around him as the wave crashed into the front-line of the army. He was far enough away from the point of impact not to be harmed, but dozens of Twilight demons found themselves encased in ice.

“We need to go,” a hoarse, feminine voice whispered from behind him.

Ethan spun, eyes widening when he recognized Allyara only a few paces away–or what remained of her. She was easily as injured as her Familiar. The silver runes he remembered from their first encounter were still present, stretching all the way to her midnight hair. But he could see where they ended prematurely, as her right arm was entirely missing, and a significant portion of her torso along with it.

She looked like she’d been bitten by a great white shark, and he couldn’t understand how she still stood. The glow of raw magic was all he could see of her wounds, the exposed flesh lost to burning light. She was obviously pained, and weak, but a normal human would be missing a lung, half their ribs, and more.

The rest of her was covered in the silver armor he remembered, though it was cracked, bent, and rent in a hundred places. Ethan had to resist reaching out to heal her, forcing his mind back to the situation at hand.

“I can’t leave!” he said, hearing the near-manic tone in his own voice. “That man there,” he said, pointing at the inky-black figure of Prince Calevaro, still holding onto the Basilisk as the Emperor worked to dominate it, “he’s my friend. We can’t leave him here!”

Allyara didn’t speak, but her Familiar crashed to the ground behind them a heartbeat later, looking all the worse from close up. Adding to the surprises of the day, Selina dropped down from the beast’s back, rushing up to them.

“Ethan! We have to go, now!”

“You don’t understand,” he pleaded, “Cal is–”

“I know!” she said, and he spotted the tears in her eyes. “I know,” she repeated, “but trust me, we don’t have the time.”

Ethan opened his mouth to say more, but a grip like a vice landed on his wrist, and suddenly he was being hauled through the air as Allyara leaped onto her Familiar’s back.

“Fly, Silvera,” she croaked. “Fly us home.”

Three great wings beat at the air, waves of frost extending from each one. The beast let out a terrible cry as it forced its injured body back into the air, and turned back the way it had come. Ethan settled into the surprisingly soft feathers as he stared down at the army. The occasional attack still shot up after them, but the Follower forces were in utter disarray. They’d barely begun recovering from the Basilisk’s attacks when a greater enemy arrived, and Ethan could still see dusty remains everywhere he looked.

But his eyes strayed to the place he almost didn’t want to look. Calevaro seemed to be staring back at him from within the cocoon of the alien Familiar. The gaze couldn’t be considered accusing, not from this distance, and his features covered, yet that was how it felt.

This hadn’t been a victory at all. He’d handed them a far greater weapon than any they’d possessed, and he was leaving behind one of his closest friends.

Selina put an arm around his shoulders, leaning close, and Ethan let his head fall, exhaustion and pain so long-ignored finally overwhelming him.

***

Ethan awoke hours later, finding Selina still next to him, diligently trying to hold back the petrification that still threatened to overtake his side. Groaning, he realized with disturbing clarity that he’d never have awoken without her.

“Thank you,” he whispered, gently moving her hands away. Selina nodded, but didn’t speak right away, clearly having burned through most of her mana. Conversely, a few hours rest had replenished his own reserves higher than they’d been in days. He clamped his healing rune down on his ribs, and in a few minutes, he’d finally banished the persistent affliction.

“You have a lot to explain,” he said, then took some deep, long-overdue breaths.

“More than you know,” Selina responded, slumping back into the feathers.

The sun was setting, but Ethan didn’t need much light to take a better look around them. They were situated at the base of the Familiar’s neck, the first set of wings stretching out to either side. From this close, Ethan realized that the creature was in even worse shape than he’d thought. He could actually see through its flesh, the magic holding the beast together seemingly deteriorating before his eyes.

“What the hell happened to you?” he whispered, his healing rune questing out automatically to get a sense for its injuries. Of course, it wasn’t a true, living creature anymore, but rather a magical manifestation, and all he could sense was the corruption of Potentia, worming through it.

“I’m not sure, myself,” Selina said, seeing what he was doing. “Champion Allyara brought me to you only moments after we met. I tried to speak to her, but the entirety of her mind is focused on keeping Silvera from disappearing entirely.”

Ethan looked up higher on “Silvera’s” neck, to where his savior hung on by one hand, head bowed in concentration. “Shame,” he said, “I could really use the distraction of some answers. You saw…?”

“Prince Calevaro,” Selina said. “I don’t know how it’s possible, but yes, I saw him.”

“I have to save him,” Ethan said, not caring how desperate he sounded, or how obvious the sentiment.

“We will,” Selina assured him. “For now, there’s much I can tell you. Ethan, I found Cara.”

His head spun around to stare into her deep purple eyes. “Cara? How’s that possible? What’s she doing on Potentia? Is she okay?”

Selina was already shaking her head. “It’s definitely her, but I’m not certain about the rest. Ethan…she died. She died and came back.”

Ethan’s eyes widened. He knew that was possible, but little more than that. “She didn’t explain?”

“She couldn’t. She’s been changed by the process, I believe. Ethan, she had horns! They were quite fetching. But also, her throat was damaged somehow, and I couldn’t heal it. I’m hoping your rune can restore her speech. Maybe leave the horns though.”

Ethan blinked rapidly, trying to take that in. “She couldn’t write something down?” he asked.

“We didn’t have time!” Selina said. “I found her in the woods, or she found me, I suppose. But she took me back to where she was staying. Ethan, she lives in a city! A city full of Nator! They’re not well, of course, so many have been changed and twisted, but they’re alive! My people are alive, and living on Potentia!”

“That’s incredible,” Ethan said. “I’m happy for you, Selina.” He was also happy to have potential allies, but didn’t want to spoil the purity of her exuberance.

“The city is called Bastion, and it’s underground. We’re heading back now. The people there think there might even be more like it, possibly all across the world, but they can’t say. But maybe not… This one has only survived because of her.” Selina nodded in Allyara’s direction. “They called her a ‘benefactor’, and introduced us. She was with another woman, as well. But that one is in even worse shape.”

“Wait, another Champion?” Ethan asked. Injured or not, he felt a tiny spark of hope.

“So it would seem,” Selina said. “Her runes were silver, and reached as high as Allyara’s, but I could only see her face. She was in some kind of capsule, possibly to heal. Allyara had been sitting with her, the two spoke after I explained why we were here. Somehow, the other Champion was able to find out where you were, and told us we needed to help you. Cara wanted to come, but she’s sworn some kind of oath to the people of Bastion–they saved her life.”

Ethan nodded. “So there’s answers waiting for us,” he said. “I can’t wait to see Cara–although I don’t look forward to telling her about Cal. Those two were a thing, weren’t they?”

Selina shrugged. “Hard to say. They’re both so private, but they were definitely growing closer when I last saw them. And yes, if you fix her throat, she is very much going to scream at you.”

Ethan nodded. “No more than I deserve,” he whispered. “I can’t believe I left him—”

“Enough, Ethan. Enough. You’re injured and exhausted, but you’re still rational enough to know that you had to leave him. And he’s not lost to use. He’s not! We’ll find a way to save him, and we’ll do it together.”

Ethan made himself nod. “You’re right,” he said. “We’ll get back to him, and–”

His words were cut off as Silvera let out a pained whine, then began plummeting toward the ground. Ethan moved to grab Selina, but suddenly he felt nothing beneath him. He passed through a cloud of silver mist, and realized that their ride had just disappeared back into Allyara’s soul.

Selina was already conjuring a disc of light to bring her safely down, and Allyara actually seemed to be trying to fall faster, arms and legs held closely as she shot toward the ground.

Ethan used an old trick, hurling a dagger beneath him, then flashing through a rift, his momentum reversed. Abruptly, he was shooting back upward into the air, and at the apex of his climb he flashed to a second dagger on the ground, landing softly.

The silver Champion crashed into the ground a hundred paces off, the soft damp ground of the forest absorbing the impact. Selina landed last, hopping off her disc, then joining Ethan as he rushed toward Allyara.

She was already standing when they reached her, though she looked like he she belonged in a hospital bed. She inspected Ethan with tired eyes, looking over his armor, weapons, and finally his healing rune. Then she nodded once, as if in approval.

“That survival kit I gave you certainly lived up to its name,” she said. “We have much to discuss, Chosen.”
