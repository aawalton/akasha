Darius

Darius stood atop the tower he was building. It was massive, hundreds of stories tall, and getting bigger by the day as countless undead worked tirelessly without break.

From there, he watched over the land. The corruption spread further and wider by the day, the land itself twisting and changing—mutating.

There was no stopping it. His undead empire was growing larger and spreading. It conquered more and more land. And with each obelisk he corrupted, the land became poisoned, and life fled.

With the top rankers of his world gone, it was too easy. The corrupted aether generated from his corrupted obelisks strengthened his minions, while weakening everybody else.

Of course, there were rebellions. There always were. But each and every time, they were crushed, and their numbers integrated into his own.

“Soon. So very soon.”

In a short time, his army had spread across a third of his gigantic new world. The numbers of his forces were staggering, and he hadn’t even replaced his champion yet.

Better still, as a minion directly under his command, he earned experience from their achievements, and while it was only a fraction of what he would earn fighting himself, the size of his army was well and truly making up for it. And now he was getting dangerously close to C-grade.

His rise was exponential, and inevitable. Every moment the top rankers were gone, his rise became more cemented. A reality that they would find impossible to resist.

And while there were still dozens of settlements along the frontiers under siege from both the corrupted aether and his minions. They wouldn’t last long.

The reality was, they couldn’t do anything to save themselves. They were too weak to fight in his corrupted lands. Corruption that was spreading around their very settlements, kept out only by their barriers that would soon fail.

In fact, if he so chose, he could go out and destroy them himself. But he had more important things to do.

While his armies fueled him with an endless stream of experience, leveling his Class, Darius was busy working on his Profession.

Every soul consumed by the obelisks was feeding him. Energy that poured into his deathly cauldron. Energy that could be used to create more monstrosities to lead his army, and in the process of their construction, provide more levels for Darius’ Profession.

He laughed.

He had been a little disappointed by how quickly Johan had died. But even that seemed so meaningless now. Sure, he would miss out on some treasures, but so what? What did any of that matter compared to converting the world in his image?

He did need to find himself a new champion, though, and the world was decisively short on worthy candidates with them all inside the dungeon.

But soon they would be out, and soon he would claim another. It was too late now. They could try all they wanted. But they would not turn back his mark on this world.

The Death God had shown him what needed to be done, and he was well on his way to completing it.

What fools they were. Leaving the world in my hands undefended while they go and chase treasures.

Darius inhaled the deathly air deep into his lungs and turned to descend back into his fortress.

Enjoying the view was always so pleasant, but he needed to get back to work now.
