Arthur returned to the creek and began to undress. Only to freeze halfway through, staring down at the outfit he’d worn.

It was stiff with sweat, grime, and the unmistakable crust of dried Grubtusk dung.

“Oh, for the love of...” he muttered. “That was the clean set.”

A tactical blunder of the highest order. His old instructors would’ve called it “situational blindness.”

“Genius move,” Drogan drawled. “Truly, the mind of a tactical prodigy.”

“Bite me,” Arthur muttered.

“Tempting, but I don’t nibble on filth.”

He sighed. At least he’d stolen backup.

Stripping down, he shoved the soiled set into his ring and pulled out a battered metal bowl, a bar of sand-colored soap, and a folded outfit from the Guardians’ survival kit.

“Bless those overconfident idiots,” he muttered.

Using the bowl, he poured cold water over his head and shoulders. The water was freezing, but after being covered in Grubtusk dung, it almost felt like mercy.

He scrubbed hard. At first with his hands, then with the rough side of the soap, chasing away sweat, dirt, and monster gunk. By the time he’d rinsed off the third cycle, he could actually smell… air. Forest. Himself.

He still didn’t exactly smell good. But at least he didn’t smell armed anymore.

As he stood drying in the forest breeze, he caught his reflection in the water’s surface. For the first time since his exile, he truly looked.

The body he inhabited had changed.

Lean. Taut. Roped with compact muscle. His skin was impossibly smooth, unmarred by scars or callouses. Even his fingernails looked buffed. If you squinted, he almost looked noble-born. The absurdity of that hit harder than he expected.

How the hell did he look this clean, given what he’d been through?

“System perks,” Drogan grunted. “High mana users repair quicker. Skin doesn’t crack. Flesh doesn’t rot. Cuts heal prettier. You’re welcome.”

Arthur blinked. “That... actually makes sense.”

“Of course it does. I’m your System-assigned mentor, remember? What, you thought I was just here for the one-liners?”

“I figured you were a failed experiment in motivational abuse.”

“Not failed. Still abusing.”

Freshly dressed, Arthur stepped into the trees, mana flaring into his eyes and ears. The forest came alive - sharp detail, layered sound, every rustle a thread on a giant living web.

No more rationing. He had monster cores in storage. Plenty of fuel.

He moved fast, but silent. A few minutes later, he caught voices. A group of five. Young. Armed. Overconfident. Typical.

Arthur trailed the group like a shadow, listening in as they joked, argued, and bragged. Nobles with more ego than caution, just his type.

Behind his thoughts, Drogan let out a disgusted scoff.

“This again? Lurking. Listening. Creeping around like some underfed voyeur.”

Arthur didn’t look up. “It’s called intelligence gathering.”

“It’s called wasting potential. You have a Legendary Class and you’re out here pretending to be a bush.”

“They talk. I listen. The more I know, the fewer surprises later.”

“Surprises build strength. Predictability builds softness,” Drogan growled. “You want power? Go fight something. Bleed for it. This recon crap? You’re squandering your evolution.”

Arthur kept his voice calm. “And yet here I am, still alive.”

“For now,” Drogan snapped. “But legends aren’t written in hiding spots.”

He stuck with the group for a few hours, logging names, accents, team dynamics, power reveals, magical affinities. By the time they made camp, he’d already built a mental profile on each.

The next day, he repeated the process with another group. And the day after. He was patient. Cold. Efficient.

He didn’t just want targets.

He wanted the whole map.

Four days into his surveillance, something changed.

A spark flared on Mana Sight, brighter than usual. Female. Stronger than the typical Guardian. And surrounded.

A five-person team moved like they were trained. Disciplined. Coordinated. There was a healer in the center, protected on all sides. Arthur kept his distance, curious.

The four guards were efficient. One rogue. One tank. Two warriors.

They never referred to her by name.

Just Princess.

Interesting.

He watched longer than usual.

She healed with speed and precision. Her mana control was excellent. Her guards took risks others wouldn’t dare. They fought monsters aggressively, always circling back to protect her without needing to speak.

Then came the moment that locked his attention.

Two E-rank beasts ambushed them.

The tank drew aggro. The rogue flanked. One of the warriors held the line.

And the second warrior. The tall one who hadn’t moved in the last three days, drew his twin swords.

It was over in three seconds.

Arthur’s mouth went dry as the twin swords flashed - one clean arc, two dead monsters, no wasted motion. He didn’t fight like someone trying to win. He fought like someone who’d already decided the outcome.

“Damn,” he muttered. “That wasn’t a fight. That was art.”

Drogan groaned audibly. “Ugh. Spare me.”

Arthur blinked. “What? You’re not impressed?”

“He dances pretty. Great. Should we throw him a parade or book him a stage?”

“He killed two E-ranks in under three seconds.”

“And? So what? Flashy kills don’t mean jack if you can’t take a punch.”

Arthur frowned. “You’re seriously telling me that wasn’t enough?”

“I’m telling you it wasn’t strength. Pretty blades and clean form don’t win wars. Pressure does. Pain does.”

Arthur fell silent for a moment, eyes still locked on the swordsman.

“…Still. Hell of a thing to watch.”

Drogan grumbled, “Watch all you want. Just don’t start idolizing dancers.”

He stayed on them for days. Every time they killed, he checked the leaderboard.

One name matched the timing.

Ysara.

_________

Three days later, his Mana Sight pinged again.

A single spark. Moving low. Controlled.

A solo participant? Maybe a scout?

The figure spotted the princess’s team. Watched them from afar. Then vanished into the woods.

Arthur frowned. “Straggler? Or scout?”

“Wanna bet on it being innocent?”

“Not remotely.”

Hours later, more sparks entered his radius. A new group. Stealth-trained. Experienced. They didn’t engage. They just began tailing Ysara’s team.

But they weren’t close enough to be spotted. Yet.

Arthur’s heart quickened. He dropped even further back, staying out of everyone’s mana perception range.

And now, he was tailing them.

The watchers had no clue they were being watched.

Ysara’s team had no clue they were being hunted.

And Arthur? He had every clue that this was about to spiral.

“This gonna be your big move?” Drogan asked.

“Not yet,” Arthur said, crouched on a ridge. “Let the sheep keep grazing. I want to see what kind of wolves we’re dealing with.”

“And if the wolves are stronger than you?”

Arthur’s smile was cold. “Then I learn. Then I adapt. And when I do move? I’ll make sure no one even knows I was here.”

Below him, the forest moved.

Two paths. Two teams. One Princess. And a man with a ring full of monsters, a growing list of grudges, and no patience for anyone else’s plans.

Whatever was coming?

He wasn’t just going to survive it.

He was going to own it.
