I studied the panopticon from the dark tunnel that connected to it midway to the ceiling. Creeping toward the opening overlooking the panopticon, I saw kobolds filling the sides of the chamber with my new infravision necklace. Ratfolk occupied most cubbies, although some stragglers still climbed the walls around the bunks.

The guilt for leaving Fabulosa weighed on me. She’d clearly objected to another dungeon crawl, and my disappearance probably hadn’t improved her disposition. The sooner I could garner her attention, the sooner she could relax.

Signaling my partner without alerting the rat pack was crucial, but luckily, I possessed a handy new antenna, an appropriate device for a communication conduit.

My lower stats came from Exhaustion. Forgetting to cast Heavenly Favor­ exemplified why Fabulosa had been right about not attempting another dungeon crawl without getting a full night’s rest. It made for a careless error, and unless I wanted to run back into the communications conduit, I wouldn’t be able to buff myself without creating unwanted attention.

I looked for other ways to get Fabulosa’s attention without revealing our hiding spots. One power offered a spark of hope. Scorch produced no spell effect trails like Fireball or Shocking Reach that would give away my location. I couldn’t target my partner because she lay well outside its range.

Normally, my Scorches inflicted 40 damage, but with Aggression in the middle of this underground city, I wasn’t sure if instantly killing one would wink me in and out of combat too quickly to plant the battleground standard.

After deciding to use Scorch, I edged to the lip of the tunnel and found a kobold with the most health, one asleep in his cubby. The rude awakening wouldn’t erase all of its 95 health—it would survive the attack. Spells didn’t critically hit, so I’d have enough time to plant the battle standard.

I cast the spell.

It squeaked as it writhed in the cubby, putting the flames out with its bedding. Unfortunately, the creature rolled out of bed and plummeted to the floor below.

/You Scorch Brackwater Digger for 70 points of damage (10 resisted). /Brackwater Digger takes 32 points of falling damage./Brackwater Digger dies.

Or maybe it wouldn’t survive.

I grunted under my breath. “Sorry, Mr. Kobold, but The Book of Dungeons isn’t a game for sissies.”

Tumult erupted at the panopticon’s floor. None of the kobolds pointed in my direction, so I ignored the commotion. Either way, my combat state allowed me to activate the battleground standard.

Again, I expected Fabulosa to say something like, “Yeah, just gimme a beat,” or, “Ready to rock,” or something fun. She wasn’t kidding about not being in the mood. Something felt off about her.

The window stood too far away for one Slipstream. To get within thirty feet, I had to climb halfway around the silo and a few stories up.

A Fireball streaked across the room, interrupting my thoughts. It exploded below me, leaving me just outside its range of damage. As brilliant as Fireballs appeared, they caused minor damage, but at least the flames preoccupied the locals.

The room erupted in hundreds of squeaks and whistles. An arrow hit something above the opening of my oval tunnel.

My partner’s voice carried over the clamor. “To me!”

When the projectile flew back toward the window, distinct squeaks of panic rose as a corpse landed atop other climbers, dropping more bodies to the floor with fatal thuds. Fabulosa caught it in her outstretched hand, nocked it, and fired at another target. A similar sequence of sounds followed as she one-shotted rats off the wall with the Returning Arrow.

Her distraction gave me time to move. In gripping the lattice by the vent, I narrowly avoided being bitten by a nearby kobold crawling toward me from a neighboring cubby. After Shocking Reach dislodged its grip, it crashed headfirst into other climbers beneath it like a bowling ball knocking down pins.

I grabbed the nearest buttressing and pulled myself into the panopticon, ignoring enemies climbing beside, below, and above me. Winterbyte’s claw weapons might enhance my strength, but I’d need to drop Creeper to use them—and that wasn’t happening.

Casting Heavenly Favor boosted my stats +4—I couldn’t believe I’d nearly forgotten to cast it again. Sleeping less than eight hours wasn’t good for business.

While climbing the roots supporting the chamber walls, I assayed the situation. Kobolds filled the cubbies, running up to the ceiling. They scrambled quickly and closed in on my position at all angles. I decided the higher I climbed, the safer I’d be. Unfortunately, this route meant fighting critters above me. If they jumped on top of me and held on, a Grappled condition would prevent me from casting Slipstream.

As if to confirm my fears, a kobold landed on my shoulders and dug its teeth into my neck. I rolled into the cubbyhole, grabbed it, and kicked it out before it could get a better grip. The airborne varmint plummeted to the floor below.

Being mindful of what climbed above me, I swung myself back onto the walls. After kicking another hard enough to lose its grasp, I cast Compression Sphere on the cubbyhole above me and knocked off two more.

Spells and abilities suiting my predicament were scant. Move Object wasn’t strong enough to loosen grips and couldn’t target creatures. Lightning Bolts could reach any point in the room, but it was an inefficient use of mana.

Fabulosa’s cadence of Fireballs wasn’t frequent enough, and the Returning Arrow killed and dislodged only one target at a time. I still had to admire her accuracy—her very shot proved dead on. Whenever her arrow struck something above me, I hugged the wall to avoid a falling body.

Using the chat made me remember I’d forgotten about the battleground standard. I’d left it sticking out of the dirt in the communications conduit. Where had my brain gone? It counted as my third dumb mistake since entering the chasm.

I couldn’t leave something as valuable as the battleground standard behind. It wouldn’t help me communicate with monsters or NPCs, but it might help me avoid confrontations with other players—a potential game-changer in the contest. Abandoning it over a horde of filthy kobolds would not happen. Still, they had a home-field advantage and climbed faster than me.

I switched the battlefield chat to be instantaneous instead of real-time, freezing the world and silencing the bedlam of the agitated kobolds.

When I unfroze my interface, the high-pitched roar from the chamber overwhelmed my eardrums, reminding me how much noise frantic kobolds could make.

Arrows picked off kobolds above me as I backtracked. With the bulk of them cutting off my escape to the window, Fabulosa had fewer targets on my side of the room.

I returned to the tall, oval tunnel covered in scratches, bites, and wounds, but nothing Rejuvenate couldn’t handle.

Kobolds still occupied the tunnel when I arrived. Fabulosa’s Returning Arrow and Creeper cleared them away, allowing her to save Fireball for a large group climbing from the floor.

It felt good to be on my feet again, and after clearing the remaining kobolds, I hurried. I grabbed the battleground standard and lit up Presence to blind my attackers.

The crescendo of protests testified to its effectiveness. Unfortunately, my eyes had adjusted to the dark, and I had to squint. When an arrow buried into my arm, I realized I wasn’t the only human blinded by the brilliance.

Fabulosa hit me for 70 damage—doubled because of Aggression—giving me a new appreciation of her character’s strength. The missile ripped from my bicep and flew toward her sniper window. I winced at the pain and popped a Restore on myself, hoping she wouldn’t blame herself for the miss.

I doused Presence and Slipstreamed down the tall, oval corridor. Extinguishing the spell plunged the silo into darkness, blinding the multitudes for a second time.

Using my infravision necklace to navigate, I sprinted down into the deep, dark unknown of the communications conduit.
