The Vimana lumbered into position above Camar Gadin some hours later, casting its long shadow over the entire city, its dark hull blotting out the low sun across half the city like a harbinger of death.

Vir ensured he kept his airship at a respectable distance, and it was with a myriad of roiling emotions that he monitored the massive structure as it moved, watching for any sign of the prana buildup Siya had assured him would precede weapons activation.

Of everything he felt, though, it was anxiety that dominated. When was it not?

He had grown well acquainted with it over the years, and he could manage it well enough by now to function despite its crushing weight. Anger as well, though that was muted by the knowledge that the men aboard the Vimana were not entirely responsible for their own actions. They were tools, wielded by something they could not see, let alone comprehend. It was hard to hate a tool, no matter how deadly.

Even still, the tragedy about to unfold made Vir's heart break.

The Bairans had been Garga’s staunchest allies since long before Vir had taken his crown. They had outright refused to partake in the Chitran uprising that destroyed his clan and killed his father. They'd even snuck aid and information to the Gargans whenever possible.

While not an outright alliance, Vir now understood that it was as much as they could have reasonably done without condemning their clan to the same fate as his.

Thaman was a good demon and an excellent ruler, and Vir had yet to meet a single Bairan he disliked.

They didn't deserve any of this. The chaos, the panic... being singled out by an enemy whose misplaced grievances had absolutely nothing to do with them.

It was just one of the many reasons he could not fail.

And yet, what he hated the most by far was that his success or failure today was partially out of his hands.

In the past, he'd simply driven himself past his limits until he broke through.

He'd reforged a shattered soul through sheer stubbornness and crossed entire universes. He'd ventured to the deepest depths of the Ash—a place that killed all of his predecessors.

And he’d not only survived, he'd thrived, turning the Ash and even Mahādi itself into a base of operations. A home, as ridiculous as that idea was.

All of it had required strength, certainly, but more than that, he'd had the chance to try and fail. Several chances to apply those learnings to his next attempt.

This time, he'd have just one chance to get it right.

No opportunity to redo this moment a thousand times in a Crown chakra mindscape. No mentor standing over his shoulder ready to correct him when he went astray.

The outcome would come down to quick thinking, instinct, and more than a little luck. The one thing he wanted to depend the least upon.

Who could say? Perhaps the humans aboard that Vimana would never figure out how to activate the flying fortress’ weapons systems. Perhaps they were, even now, fumbling through controls made for a far more advanced race, growing so frustrated they simply gave up, with Vir floating here for another six hours waiting for an attack that would never come.

That would be nice.

His communication orb pulsed against his belt, and the face that appeared on the other end made him smile.

“Hey, you,” Maiya said with a smile.

“Mai,” Vir replied, letting out a relieved breath.

“Look, I know I’ve said all this already,” she said. “And I don’t want to distract you, but I thought it was important. All of us believe in you, Vir. You can do this, even though I know you’re probably thinking you can’t.”

Vir chuckled. That was his Maiya alright. She could read him like a book, and he her. His most precious existence in any universe.

Coming from anyone else, he might have brushed off the sentiment. He certainly brushed off most reassurances and accolades these days, given how many he received. From anyone else, the words would have been empty platitude.

But this was Maiya, and when Maiya said she believed in him, she meant it down to the bone.

Which was, of course, the problem.

Not a single person in the entire realm thought he would fail.

Not Maiya, not Cirayus, not Thaman, not even the hundreds of thousands of demons watching the skies over Camar Gadin. Trusting that the Akh Nara would do what he always did.

They simply could not comprehend him failing, having convinced themselves that he was some sort of infallible god who never erred. Who simply showed up when all hope was lost to make the impossible possible.

If only they knew just how many mistakes he'd made. Against Annas. In that doomed universe. Against an Automaton he had once fought... the list went on.

Vir had failed his way into more solutions than he could count. Just that the world had simply not seen most of those.

“Thanks, Mai,” he said quietly. “As always.”

She held his gaze for another second before looking away, as though listening to someone.

“Vir, something’s happening. Siya says the Vimana’s weapons systems are activating.”

“I see it,” he replied.

The surge of prana accumulation at the bottom of the Vimana was unmistakable.

Whatever hope he had that the humans aboard were not hostile, or that they would fail to enable the weapons system evaporated as he watched a flat panel extend from the underside of the Vimana, perhaps twenty paces square, its surface a perfect mirror inscribed with patterns like on the external weapons he had destroyed earlier.

Just larger and far, far more numerous. The sort of weapon that did not need to be aimed. Its enormous blast area eliminated that mundane need.

“I’ll leave you to it,” Maiya said, all levity gone from her expression. “Go save that city.”

Vir pocketed the orb and sent prana into Iteration IV’s control inscriptions, bringing the airship directly beneath the extended panel some twenty paces away below the Vimana’s underside.

The positioning had been Siya’s choice. It seemed the panel beam emitted by the weapon would expand as it descended, and that the closer Vir was, the easier a time he would have of blocking the whole thing.

That, of course, also meant he would be hit with a far more concentrated beam than if he were lower.

He engaged the autohover—another of Saunak’s additions in the latest iteration—and unlatched the top hatch, climbing out onto the airship’s roof.

While capable of hovering just fine himself with the use of Balancer of Scales and Wind Affinity orbs, this task would require every bit of his attention and then some. Best to minimize the distractions.

The wind buffeted him at this altitude, cold enough that he felt it even through his Imperium metal armor, and he’d just barely had a few seconds to begin cycling his prana when the Vimana’s weapon directly above him began to glow.

On a whim, Vir fired a concentrated blast of prana at the weapon, but just as Siya said it would, it bounced off. The weapon would only become vulnerable while firing—its prana shielding overwhelmed by the discharge of the weapon itself. Vir would essentially be adding more of his own on top to melt that shielding and thus the weapon itself.

Ash prana began accumulating within the enemy weapon at an atrocious pace… and then kept growing, far past the point of any conventional weapon discharge Vir had ever witnessed. Past anything he had thought even a Vimana would be capable of producing.

The Akh Nara of three years ago would have been vaporized simply by standing on this airship as he was now.

The Akh Nara who had walked into Janak’s tower might have survived that, only to die miserably against the weapon once it activated.

The Akh Nara who had reunited the realms, however, was something else.

I can survive this, he thought, and it wasn’t simply hope talking. The amount of prana above him was vast, almost incomprehensibly so, but it was nothing he could not match.

Whether he could save the city below, however, was another question entirely. For matching the blast would not be enough. He would have to overpower it. To destroy the weapon itself.

Vir opened every Chakra he had.

The figure of a many-armed and many-headed god emerged behind him. His prana cycled to its absolute limit.

Aspect of the Demon God flared and Yuma’s Embrace stood ready to absorb the damage that he would inevitably take.

Every art he had spent his life mastering was now active, ready to conduct an impossible quantity of prana the moment the Vimana fired. A quantity that would have left the strongest Ash Beasts quivering with envy.

And yet, it wasn’t enough.

He could feel it in his bones. Instinct told him that despite everything he had, he could not produce a counter beam dense enough to overpower what the Vimana was about to release.

That the discharge would tear through his attack, and if not through him, then definitely the city below.

He needed more.

Above him, the prana accumulation reached a threshold that made the very air around him warp, a deep wrongness in the atmosphere that made Iteration IV’s hull shudder and his hair stand on end.

Vir closed his eyes.

He knew what he needed to do.

He released his hold on Siya’s prana absorber and unleashed the prana of a destroyed realm.
