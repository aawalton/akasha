Sixth Moon, Second Mist Day

“We can finally relax!” Sue cheered as she and Aida approached the familiar Torii gate. She twirled in her pink sundress, basking in the late morning sun. “I’m going to splurge on all the treatments today.”

“Are you planning on staying here all day?” Aida asked, amused.

“We might as well! Our only other appointment is with my dad,” Sue said cheekily. She waved vigorously as the group standing just inside the gate noticed their approach. “They’re here already!”

“It feels like it’s been a whole moon!” Lily said enthusiastically as she bounded over to greet them. “How are you?”

“Productive!” Sue struck a victory pose before giving Caleb a quick hug. “I got my room set up in the Gullbeaks Headquarters, and we talked about putting me into the rotation for some mini patrols with the members so we can learn to work together. I think they’re going to schedule me for something on Ore Day at the earliest.”

“Things are happening fast,” Levi commented. “Are they that desperate for help that they need to start you before the third star cycle?”

“I wanted to start early!” Sue declared. She rubbed her hands together with relish. “The sooner we figure out our synergies, the better off we’ll all be.”

“You should take it slow,” Caleb said worriedly, his hand resting on her shoulder. “Don’t rush into anything.”

“It will be fine! The reason we’re doing mini patrols to start with is so that we Fires don’t have to be out for long stretches of time,” Sue reassured Caleb, leaning against his broad torso. “Lula and Kuri said we can go at whatever pace I need, and they’ll pick up the slack because if I even take just one patrol, that will give them plenty of time to recover.

“Oh, and the mini patrols really are mini. Dev’s group—they’re calling themselves the Talonwings—are going into the rotation as well,” Sue added to the group at large. “We’re planning a slow ramp-up to make sure we don’t exhaust ourselves.”

As Sue started elaborating on the Gullbeaks’ strategy to grow their new talent, Aida listened with increasing emotion.

Perhaps it was because she hadn’t been in any classes with Sue, but she didn’t recall the beautiful, fiery girl having this sort of passion for anything that required planning; or at least, the only planning the girl had been willing to engage in was for her matches against an opponent. The rest of her life had instead been devoted to spontaneity, and doing what felt right in the moment, with a strong aversion to thinking about the future beyond abstract thought.

But now, the fervor and detail Sue spoke with about her future with the Gullbeaks—even though the topic was nigh irrelevant to the rest of the group—captivated her listeners. Everyone laughed at her absurd predictions of how certain members of the Talonwings would get along with the Gullbeaks (“I can’t wait to see when Kawa puts Pritchard in his place”), while in the next breath she theorized the feats of magical accomplishments that could occur with such a strong pool of gifted practitioners (“Shon has incredible environmental awareness; I bet he and Lula could sweep a whole region by themselves”).

If Aida didn’t think too hard, she found herself nodding along with the young Fire woman’s confidence. She was like a warm bonfire on a chilly night: you knew if the flames went out the cold would settle into your bones, but with how large, steady, and reassuring the flames were, you weren’t afraid of that ever happening. Sue’s gusto for her future assignments struck the perfect balance of confidence and grounded realism, making Aida believe the heroine they needed was standing right in front of her.

Is this the power of the Main Character’s charisma?

“Yikes! I’ve totally overdone the conversation,” Sue laughed, embarrassed. “Shall we go check in?”

“The Tulver’s hot springs are a lot nicer than I expected!” Lily floated on her back through the third hot spring. “I love that they have herbal pouches infusing in the water.”

“These are new,” Sue said, scooping a thick canvas sachet out of the water and lifting it to her nose. She inhaled the aroma. “They definitely didn’t have these the last time we came here.”

“I’m glad business is going well enough for them to invest in these benefits,” Vanita said dreamily. “Their prices are quite low for similar levels of service elsewhere.”

“Shall we move to the next spring?” Aida asked. Unlike the first time she had visited the bathhouse with Sue, she had quickly acclimated to the water’s temperature this time. And she was excited to see how much more nutritious the water in the hotter springs would be; she could feel the thicker energy pouring over the waterfall into their current spring.

“Let’s do it!” Sue stood up, stretching luxuriously in the relatively cooler air as the water cascaded off of her body.

“I’m okay staying here,” Lily said, swimming to the edge of the wall so she could sit along one of the seats on the side. “But you go on ahead!”

“I’ll stay with Lily,” Vanita said, sinking lower into the water and closing her eyes as she rested her head against the rim of the spring.

“Looks like it’ll just be the two of us,” Sue said brightly to Aida, who kept her gaze lowered.

One day, I’ll get used to being around naked people.

Unlike the third hot spring, which only had Lily and Vanita in it now, the second-level hot spring had several women of middling and older ages relaxing in it, making Aida and Sue squeeze uncomfortably close together to maintain a respectable distance away from the other women.

Unexpectedly, Sue wasn’t bothered in the least.

“Even though I miss when we had the whole place to ourselves, it’s so nice to see so many satisfied clients here,” she whispered in Aida’s ear.

“They deserve it,” Aida agreed. “Connie looks so much happier now.”

The two of them basked in the heat some more, and Aida slowly relaxed. As she gave herself over to all the energies permeating the water, she found it easier to let go of the renewed worries that had been plaguing her ever since she met Ernie and Agnes.

When held in the embrace of the bubbling springwater, completely saturated with mana, yet still pliant and submissive to Aida’s idle whims… everything outside of the bathhouse seemed so inconsequential. Especially with Sue’s strong, steady aura beside her. Between the two of them, Aida could almost believe they would be able to handle anything that came their way.

Sue’s form stirred beside Aida. “Shall we move up to the next spring?”

“Mhmm.”

Unlike the second hot spring, there was no one else in the hottest spring. Upon reflection, Aida supposed summer was unlikely to be a busy season for the hot spring business.

“Hey, Aida?”

“Yeah?”

“Remember the conversation we had when we first came here?” Sue’s voice was soft, as if she didn’t want to disrupt the peaceful moment.

It took a moment for Aida to pull herself out of her lethargy. That felt like a lifetime ago.

“Yeah.”

“We’re on the right track, aren’t we?” Sue finished her question with hesitation, as if her first words had been intended to be more of a reassurance to Aida, before her courage failed her.

And now she was the one seeking reassurance.

“Of course we are,” Aida said softly. “You’re part of the Gullbeaks. And I have a job that will allow me to grow my abilities.”

She reached through the water for Sue’s hand, which was floating limply in the bubbling water. “The last time we talked about our dreams, we never expected things to go this smoothly. But I think we’re doing well, don’t you?”

Sue didn’t respond for a moment, though she squeezed Aida’s hand tightly. Finally, she sighed.

“You’re right. Things have gone so amazingly well for the two of us.” Aida could hear the smile in Sue’s voice. “I’m so proud of you, Aida. You’ve changed so much. For the better.”

Aida chuckled. If you only knew.

The water lapped at Aida’s cheek as Sue repositioned herself so that she was staring right at Aida. “I’m serious! We’ve gone through a lot—I’ve watched you shrink into yourself when we first went to Maglica, and then somehow you found the strength to fight through your demons and catch up to the rest of us in our last year of school.”

Aida’s eyes watered as she struggled to maintain eye contact with Sue, trying to keep any sign of guilt out of her face.

“You’re so strong, Aida. I’m so glad… and proud… to call you my sister.”

Sue’s lilac eyes were bright with emotions threatening to spill over her lashes, and Aida could see how important it was for Aida to reciprocate the sentiment.

The two of them grew up together… best friends, basically sisters. And she never ditched Aida, even when no one would blame her for doing so.

Aida forced a happier smile on her face, straightening in the water so she matched Sue’s posture.

“Thank you for sticking by me this whole time. Sister.”
