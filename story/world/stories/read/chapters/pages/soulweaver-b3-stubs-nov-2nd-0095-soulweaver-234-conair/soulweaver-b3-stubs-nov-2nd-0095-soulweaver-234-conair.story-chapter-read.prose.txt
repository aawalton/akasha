Yashas closed his eyes, but I didn’t get the feeling it was in acceptance of his death.

But whether it was a prayer or whether he was just tapping into his awareness, whatever he did worked.

At the last possible instant, Yashas's boot lashed out and kicked Galia right in her flank.

The phoenix squawked in indignation and veered sideways, her wings flaring instinctively to correct the sudden imbalance.

Bone-Eskil's greatax whistled through the space where they'd been a heartbeat before, close enough that it sliced off a handful of feathers from Galia's tail.

How Yashas had managed that, I had no clue. If Galia had moved the other way, they’d both be dead.

Real Eskil was already moving, his roar of pure rage making my ears hurt as his own ax came around in a vicious horizontal slash. The blade caught his counterpart before he’d recovered from his own strike, and cleaved the bone giant nearly in two, splitting it from collarbone to hip.

For a second, I thought it was over. Because surely, no matter how much these things were cheating, that was a lethal wound.

But then I realized Bone-Eskil's skeletal halves hadn’t been cut apart… they’d split before Eskil’s actual strike, and I watched in disgust as the pieces started snapping back together.

“Of course it can do that,” I muttered, wanting nothing more than to launch a Siege Bolt Core at the thing, but doing that would have taken out Eskil, too, considering how they were locked in melee combat.

“Eskil! Get away from it. I can attack it!”

“Shut your mouth, manslave!” Eskil thundered back, and I guess I should’ve known better. I was tempted to fire the thing anyway, and if the world no longer had an Eskil in it, well, I wouldn’t lose any sleep at night.

I glanced at Yashas to see if he had any ideas… and did a double take.

His left eye was gone. Not bleeding or cut or anything like that. Just gone, replaced by an empty socket, like his eye was never there. The general swayed on Galia's back, one gripping her feathers unsteadily..

“Go,” he said, and he sounded like he'd just aged a decade.

And then I knew. The reason he’d only guessed at Aerion’s identity instead of knowing for sure.

He’d mentioned paying a price to use his information broker ability, but I'd never imagined the cost would be this high. I couldn’t say why, but I had a hunch no amount of Galia’s tears would bring his eye back.

Fuck.

I wanted to argue. Wanted to refuse, to tell him we could finish the fight faster with me around.

But the look in his remaining eye said it all. There was no point winning the battle to lose the war.

I nodded in response. But that didn’t mean there wasn’t anything I could do, either. One of the perks of Soulweaving—I had a small army of something even better than soldiers.

I pulled my flying swords from my inventory and sent them streaking toward the bone clones, their [Blade Projection] ability making them deadlier than they had a right to be. The chain whip followed, its [Buzzsaw] shrieking as it chased Bone-Aerion, buying Ohana a few precious seconds.

With my counterpart down, those weapons would not be countered so easily.

Senara was still manifested too, the Aralez harrying Eskil's counterpart with her teeth and claws as best she could. She might have been little more than a mosquito to the skeleton, but she still made for a decent distraction.

All of that against just the two remaining Bone Champions gave me some confidence. Sure, they were two of their most dangerous fighters, but it was every advantage we were going to get.

With everything I could do done, I bolted.

Not because I thought they had a decent chance if they all ganged up on Bone-Eskil. Not because they had Yashas who had more battlefield experience than me, and not even because taking out that core might actually have been the fastest way out of this dungeon, possibly ending it before we'd even taken down all the bone clones.

Those were all good reasons to leave the fight to them, of course, and they did factor into my decision. But there was no way in hell I'd have made that decision that fast if I didn't have Aerion tucked safely away in my inventory.

That probably made me a terrible person, caring about her more than the Champions who'd save Axius and stop millions from dying, but hey, I was just some guy.

I pumped my legs, sprinting as fast as my legs could take me. Which was quite impressive due to my insane 5000 plus points of Vigor, but still not nearly as fast as I moved normally with my Grace suit, let alone with [Spacefold].

Bone-Aerion came after me, of course, but while she hadn't passed out like she should have, at least her [Origin Reave] had ended, and she was nowhere near as fast as she was even before she'd activated it. She might've been a cheater, but it seemed even cheats had limits.

Ohana intercepted her with a volley of her thrown daggers, which exploded in a line between us, forming a sort of firewall that thwarted Bone-Aerion's advance. I didn’t know if she spontaneously generated those daggers as part of her Blessing or had a similar inventory to mine, but in that moment, I was just thankful she'd saved my ass.

I threw her a nod of appreciation as I vaulted up the stairs to the Sensō-ji four at a time, diving through its massive doors and rolling back inside.

However long it had actually been, it felt like I'd just been here moments ago. Except this time, there were no skeleton soldiers waiting for me, and this time, stealth was the furthest thing on my mind.

I sprinted down the stairs before sequestering myself in the same utility closet Yashas and I used before, my armor peeling open almost before I'd stopped as my Grace suit appeared from my inventory.

The familiar surge of enhanced agility flooded through me, and I didn't waste a second. I [Spacefolded] again, and reality twisted as I warped down the stairwell. Again. And again, as soon as the cooldown ended.

The skellies that were in my way weren’t for long as I blasted them apart with Light of the Fearless. Yet again, the enemy seemed to be in disarray, with everyone running this way and that under orders to mobilize their air force.

The hangar opened up below me, and I hit the catwalk at a dead sprint before vaulting over the railing. [Spacefold] caught me mid-fall, taking me toward the hangar floor near the row of F-35s.

Handy ability that. So long as I was in this suit, fall damage was basically a thing of the past.

Some of the jets were missing, while most still sat with their canopies closed, but I could work with that. Taking out the service tech skeletons along the way, I picked the closest one, scrambled up the ladder, and grabbed the external canopy release. The cockpit hissed open, and I threw myself inside.

Which was where the hard part began.

Assuming I could even turn the thing on, could I really pilot this thing to the dungeon core before they shot me down?

I located the eject handle—at least that was there in case shit hit the fan.

I pulled the IFF transponder I'd looted from the skeleton pilot and jammed it into the reader on the instrument panel. The cockpit displays flickered to life with various symbols and amber and green status lights appearing on the screens.

I'd expected to have hours to familiarize myself with this stuff and to run through pre-flight checks, figuring out which buttons did what. Now? Now I had seconds. Maybe less before someone wised up to the fact that someone had just hijacked an $80 million dollar aircraft.

My hands almost moved on their own as things I’d seen a thousand times in sims meshed with reality.

Battery master switch to on.

Throttle to zero.

APU master on.

The auxiliary power unit hummed to life, and I started working through the engine start sequence, my brain already overwhelmed by the relatively simple displays of the F-35 compared to the ocean of levers and knobs older jets tended to have.

The sims had been good, but this was different. Obviously. The actual startup sequence wasn’t exactly public domain knowledge, and there were more than a few prompts that didn't match what I'd practiced. Not to mention all the Japanese text, which I had to blindly guess at.

I punched in a command to initialize the weapons systems… and the entire display went red.

“Fuck!” I slammed my fist on the console. The screen was locked, flashing something in Japanese that I assumed meant 'unauthorized access' or maybe 'fuck you.'

I tried rebooting the system. Tried every trick I could remember from my time in the sims.

Nothing worked.

Either the IFF transponder wasn't enough or maybe I’d just inputted an illegal series of commands. Either way, this jet was useless to me now and Initializing it was out of the question with it costing thousands of points of Essence. Even if I had that much to spare, I had no clue if it would do anything to solve my current problem.

I’d just popped the hatch and was about to try again on another one when I spotted another F-35, fully initialized and on the catapult launch rail just fifty feet away and ready to go.

The skeleton's bony hands gave signals to the ground operators and I heard the whine of the engine spooling up.

He was about to launch.

I looked at my locked-up jet. Looked at his functional one. Looked at the catapult system that was about to launch him.

A grin spread across my face.

“Fuck it.”

I had a much better idea.
