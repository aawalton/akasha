Back in the Storm Sect, things had finally begun settling down once again.

Three days had passed since Calamity Ghaltris’s ascension, and many of those who had gained seals were finally regaining their senses. None of the other Specials or their underlings had been released yet, so Aodhán had been forced to spend the last few days largely alone.

He hadn’t been idle, though.

After cutting his way through the ice rift, he and Lilian had gone directly to the Elder Hall to discuss his immunity with the High Elders.

Needless to say, they had been shocked—both by his presence and by what he had told them—and had immediately begun speculating how such a thing was possible.

High Elder Mystral was the first to offer a theory, asking if it had something to do with the interface—if perhaps, after nearly a full year of interfacing with chaos, something within him had simply stopped bending to it.

Aodhán replied with the only answer he had.

He didn’t know.

High Elders Tharn and Velora took over after that, quizzing him extensively on his relationship with the Origin Plane and pressing him on what made it so different from everyone else’s.

Aodhán answered as best he could, describing how different his last visit to the Origin Plane had felt and how one of his latest notifications proved that the Origin Plane was proud of him. Though, to be honest, his immunity had begun long before he ever received the notification.

Still, the mere fact that he had received such a notification gave them pause, and in the end, they were forced to agree that his relationship with the consciousness of the Origin Plane was unlike anything they had ever encountered.

Nevertheless, none of them were willing to accept that a simple shift in understanding and perception could cause such a drastic change in a person, altering the very consequences of cultivation itself.

The logic simply didn’t hold.

And honestly, Aodhán agreed.

His perception of the storm wasn’t something he had arrived at on his own. Az’marthon was the one who shared that insight with him. And as far as he knew, Az’marthon himself had never been immune to the twisted effect.

He hadn’t even considered that before.

But that alone was proof that his immunity had little to do with his understanding and far more to do with who he was.

For a moment, he wondered if perhaps it had something to do with his mother being an Ascendant. But then again, he wasn’t the first descendant of an Ascendant in ÆFLYM.

He had taken Az’marthon’s advice a few months earlier and read up on the descendants of Ascendants. Though they were rare, enough was known about them to confirm that even they experienced the twisted effect.

For hours, the High Elders proposed one theory after another, their fascination obvious. Aodhán indulged them without complaint, wanting the answer just as much as they did.

At one point, High Elder Kaelthorn suggested that perhaps the uniqueness of his situation had something to do with his path of authority. But that line of inquiry died just as quickly when Aodhán revealed that he had gained the immunity long before he ever created the path.

In the end, no one knew why this was happening or how he had come by his immunity. But they were determined to find out, and thanked Aodhán for bringing it to their attention.

He then took his leave and spent the rest of the day consolidating the gains from the ascension.

He had gained so much in so little time—not just skills, but insights and ideas—and if he was to wield his power effectively, he first needed to understand it.

And the starting point of that understanding had been his newest aspect.

Time.

It had taken him a while to accept the aspect, and even now, several months later, he was still somewhat cautious of it.

Perhaps it was because it was an entirely different power set from what he was used to, or perhaps because of how energy intensive it was, but Aodhán largely stayed away from it, only using it whenever he unleashed his aura.

And even that had been done sparingly.

Unlike the storm, time was not merely an element or force of destruction. It was the silent law beneath reality itself—the unseen current carrying everything in existence ever forward toward its inevitable ruin. Every breath, every heartbeat, every rise and collapse of creation moved to its rhythm.

No one stood above it.

No one could escape it.

And somehow, that power now rested in his hands.

It was a terrifying thought. But Calamity Ghaltris’s ascension had offered him a new perspective, and the truth was whether he liked it or not, the power was now his. Not using it—refusing to train it—was a recipe for disaster.

One day, when he truly needed it, he would find himself unprepared and unskilled once again, only capable of rewinding time by a mere second.

The thought made him grimace, and without hesitation, he proceeded to the time aspect chamber to meditate on the gift his mother had given him.

The aspect hummed to life the moment he stepped into the room, resonating within him like a distant song. It wasn’t quite as loud as the water aspect usually was, but somehow it felt stronger, echoing from somewhere deeper.

Settling into a meditative pose, he did his best to listen and follow the leading of the aspect, trying to understand how it worked.

So far, he had only been able to rewind and slow time within his domain. It was a strong start—perhaps too strong, judging by how energy intensive both skills were.

This time around, he wanted to start with something small. Something he could use without draining himself completely.

And that had begged the question: what else could one use the time affinity for?

It wasn’t a question Aodhán had given much thought to before, but now, with the aspect of time humming within him, the answers slowly began bubbling to the surface of his mind—things he never would have considered or thought possible.

He lost himself in it. Hours blurred into each other as he chased half-formed instincts through the aspect, and before he realized it, the entire day had slipped by without him grasping any new skill.

But Aodhán hadn’t given up.

For the next two days, he worked diligently, leaving the chamber only to eat or shower. Everything else he needed to do, he did within the room, watching the essence move and interact with his will.

Insight came in trickles. Understanding even less. But each scrap of comprehension kept him going.

And now, four days later—as the first ray of dawn streamed into the room—Aodhán finally pieced together everything he had been learning into a skill.

It was nothing special… at least, not compared to rewinding or slowing time. But for the past few days, his mind had circled around one question above all else.

If time could be slowed… then why couldn’t it also be sped up?

Rewinding time or slowing it was about going back, pushing against the forward momentum of the river that was time.

But rather than resist the current… couldn’t he ride the wave instead?

Couldn’t he accelerate it?

Grinning widely, he raised a hand and summoned a small cloud into existence. The cloud barely spanned the length of his arm, but size was the last thing he cared about in that moment.

Channeling energy into the cloud, he watched as the roiling mass darkened and began flashing with lightning. It thundered an instant later before beginning to drizzle softly.

The droplets splashed against the wooden floor, quickly pooling into a shallow puddle that darkened the edges of his robes. But Aodhán barely noticed. His attention remained fixed on the drizzling rain as he reached a hand toward it, feeling both the aspect of water and time stir within him.

He focused his mind on the latter, and his breath hitched slightly as time essence surged through his spirit—vast and ancient, the sensation strangely similar to the storm itself.

It felt like plunging his bare hands into the current of a raging river.

A slow smile spread across his face as he fixed his gaze on the cloud and whispered,

“Accelerate.”

The falling droplets blurred immediately, striking the ground seconds earlier than they should have.

Nothing else in the room sped up. Just the cloud.

Aodhán’s eyes widened as he watched the droplets shoot downward as though their speed had tripled, the sounds of their impact snapping through the chamber like tiny cracks of thunder.

His smile widened into a grin.

It had worked. And the line of text that crawled across his vision a second later only confirmed it.

Congratulations! You have learned a new skill. Accelerate [see full details below].

Accelerate: You may now accelerate time affecting objects and phenomena within your domain. Duration and intensity depend on willpower.

Aodhán stared at the notification for a moment before dismissing it with a satisfied smile.

The skill wasn’t nearly as flashy as the previous two, but the fact that he could use it without feeling like a collapsing dam filled him with immense relief.

Varéc hummed within his spirit in agreement, speaking up for the first time in over a day, and Aodhán’s smile widened a fraction.

“You know, I really should try it on a person before getting too excited.”

Varéc growled immediately, flooding their bond with images of people he thought would make excellent test subjects.

Aodhán barked out a laugh.

“I’m pretty sure Nymera would have my head if I tried it on her, Varéc. And Seamus is just…” He grimaced. “Seamus is a terrible idea.”

Varéc suggested a few more names, his enthusiasm entirely undeterred, and Aodhán chuckled.

“Sophran and Darien would probably agree out of curiosity, but a rift monster is still the better choice.”

Varéc rushed out of his spirit then, his inky black scales gleaming beneath the chamber lights, and Aodhán shook his head the moment he realized just how much larger the Fury had become once again.

He seemed to grow with every tier Aodhán gained, and even with the mansion’s adjustable walls and ceilings, Varéc’s horns were already nearing the roof again.

Aodhán sighed, amusement plain on his face as he placed a hand against the dragon’s neck.

“We really need that size-altering perk, buddy. I’m not sure this ceiling can go any higher.”

Varéc huffed and folded his wings more tightly against himself in an obvious attempt to ‘shrink.’ The effort made very little difference, and Aodhán found himself shaking his head again.

It had been a while since the two of them had gained any perks. Hopefully, the world would hear his prayers for the next one.

He left the time aspect chamber after that, and Varéc followed, phasing through walls and curved spaces to avoid tearing the entire house apart.

They reached the living room a few minutes later, and Aodhán made his way to the bathroom, leaving Varéc to practice their latest skill on a plant in the corner.

He hadn’t had a proper bath in the past four days, relying on Ruinous Baptism instead of taking an actual shower. A quiet groan escaped his lips the moment real water touched his skin.

The last few days, despite being so productive, had really opened his eyes to just how much work Crixis did around the house—and just how much he had come to depend on him for a surprising number of things.

Chief among them was good company.

But beyond that, all the time alone had forced Aodhán to confront just how much he had changed.

Not physically or magically, but emotionally.

Gordon had changed him. The wars, the gore, the deaths—all of it had left its mark on him. Some of the changes he likely hadn’t even noticed yet.

But the few he could see were enough to make him feel like a different person whenever he looked back at the boy he had once been.

The boy who had entered ÆFLYM with nothing but hopes, dreams, and stars in his eyes.

He was no longer that boy.

He still had hopes and dreams, but the stars had dimmed. The naïveté and blind trust he had once placed in others had withered away.

Principal Zatya, Ayisha, and a host of others had made sure of that.

Yet despite everything he had been through, everything he had learned about himself, and everything that had changed, Aodhán was glad the core of his being remained untouched.

Glancing up at the bathroom mirror, he smiled crookedly at the foggy reflection staring back at him and whispered,

"I GAIN STRENGTH TO GIVE IT."

The ideal resonated within his mind for a moment, clear as thunder, and his smile widened slightly.

That was the core of his being. And that would never change.

He would never allow it to.

Exhaling softly, he stepped out of the bathroom, pulled a fresh robe from his spatial storage, and slipped it on.

There was so much he needed to do that day—things he had neglected in Crixis's absence. But he couldn’t afford to put them off any longer. Crixis would be returning home soon, and it wouldn’t do for him to find the house in a worse state than he had left it.

That meant finally taking care of all the errands he had been pushing aside over the past few days.

First, he needed to get food and a few supplies for the house. He also needed to cook himself a proper meal, considering he hadn’t eaten anything decent in days. Then there was the matter of his runic practice, something he had neglected even before Crixis left.

Lastly, he had to visit Elder Gumi’s workshop to repair his camera.

The thought of the camera brought a smile to his face, and he chuckled quietly as he imagined all the pictures he could take once it stopped electrocuting his friends.

“Are you ready to go?” he asked, calling out to Varéc as he laced his robe and tied it at the side.

Varéc growled in response, thunder rumbling faintly through the room as he abandoned the now-overgrown plant and turned toward him.

Aodhán looked at the poor plant and chuckled. “Alright. Let’s go. We’ll stop at the market first.”

Varéc rose to his full height, tail swishing excitedly as he proposed taking a flight through the sect before heading to the market.

“Fine,” Aodhán said, rolling his eyes in amusement as he made his way toward the exit. “Just one lap, though.”

Varéc growled excitedly and immediately turned toward the door, his wings spreading slightly in preparation for flight.

However, just as Aodhán reached the door, he felt a cluster of familiar cores suddenly enter his range.

His eyes widened instantly, a smile blooming across his face, and without hesitation, he yanked the door open to reveal Sophran, Darien, Crixis, and Su Tianan just as they stepped onto his front porch.

“Ugghhh,” the boys groaned dramatically while Crixis laughed. “I told you we could never catch him off guard.”

Aodhán laughed, genuine happiness bubbling within him as, all at once, the world felt complete again.

He had missed them.

Truly.

Not just their company, but them. And though he was a little disappointed not to see Nymera, Seamus, and Ashscar among the group, the sight before him was more than enough to brighten his mood.

“I’ve missed you guys, truly,” he said with a wide smile, stepping aside to let them in. “These past few days have been boring.”

Sophran chuckled as he walked past him. “I thought you liked being alone.”

“I do,” Aodhán admitted. “But it seems I’ve grown to like company more.”

“You make it sound like we’ve been gone for ages,” Darien said with a laugh. “It’s only been four days.”

“Four very long days,” Aodhán corrected before turning toward Crixis with a beaming smile. “Welcome back.”

Crixis chuckled and raised a hand to scratch beneath Varéc’s chin as the Fury rumbled happily in greeting, his tail swishing excitedly behind him.

“It feels good to be back,” Crixis admitted.

Varéc lowered his massive head toward Sophran next, growling softly as Su Tianan immediately reached out to rub the scales along his neck.

“You missed us too, huh?” Sophran teased, earning another pleased rumble from the fury. “How would you like to say hello to Halo?”

Halo rushed out of Sophran’s spirit then, lighting up the room with her ethereal glow. A moment later, Caelion—Darien’s sky serpent—emerged with an excited hiss of his own.

Varéc growled eagerly in response, lowering himself slightly as the two familiars circled around him in greeting while Sophran shook his head in amusement.

“Seems to me like you might have to expand this house to twice its current size, Aodhán.”

Aodhán chuckled, his smile widening at the sight. But just as he shut the door, another pair of familiar cores entered his range.

This time, his body moved before thought could catch up.

He yanked the door open once more and immediately saw the person he had arguably missed the most.

Nymera.

She smiled brightly; somehow even more beautiful than the last time he had seen her.

For a heartbeat, neither of them moved. Then Aodhán opened his arms wide, and with a soft laugh, she stepped straight into his embrace, wrapping her arms around him tightly.

Warmth flooded him instantly, bubbling up from within like sunlight breaking through storm clouds. It had only been four days since he’d seen her—since he’d seen any of them—but somehow it had felt far longer, and—

“Hey!” Seamus called out, glaring at him with exaggerated annoyance. “You’re hogging the entrance. Care to let me through?”

“Or me?” Ashscar muttered dryly, rolling his eyes.

Aodhán grinned and finally pulled back from the hug. “It is good to see you both.”

“And you too,” Seamus said with a smirk as he stepped inside, Skip suddenly in tow. Aodhán wasn’t even sure when the familiar had emerged from his spirit.

Ashscar, on the other hand, simply snorted, looking far too exhausted to bother with a proper response, and Aodhán found himself smiling as his once-quiet mansion filled with sound once more.

His family had returned.

And for one unguarded moment, the world felt whole again.

Far from the Storm Sect, in a hall of dreams, another sibling reunion had just set something in motion that would not let the world stay that way for long.
