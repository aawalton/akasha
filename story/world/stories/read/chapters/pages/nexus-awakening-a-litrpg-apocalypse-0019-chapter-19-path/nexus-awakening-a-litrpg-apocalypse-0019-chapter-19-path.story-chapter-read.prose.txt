Chapter 19: Path

Weeks passed, blurring by like a hand out the window of a car. You could feel the wind streaming between your fingers at speeds you didn't even think about at the time. Each day bled into the next in ways that made me forget what normal time felt like. Back when I had a job to go to, a wife to come home to, a son who needed help with homework. That life felt like something I'd read about in a book once, not something I'd actually lived.

Sometimes I'd catch myself reaching for my phone to check the time, only to remember I was in a place where phones didn't exist and time moved differently. Where days were measured not by clocks but by how many things you killed before you could rest. Where the only schedule that mattered was the rhythm of violence and survival.

Kari had settled into being the leader of our little group, a role I was happy to allow her to take. There was something comforting about following instead of making decisions. Let someone else worry about where we went and what we did. I could focus on the simple things. Kill what tried to kill us. Stay alive another day. Don't think too hard about what we were becoming.

The weight of leadership was something I'd never wanted anyway. Back on Earth, I'd been content to frame houses and pour foundations, to build things that would outlast me. Simple, honest work with my hands. Now my hands were weapons, stained with blood that never seemed to wash off completely. The calluses I'd earned from hammer and saw were being joined by new ones from gripping my resonance blade.

In my old life, the life of the man I used to be, I had observed many leaders on construction sites. The good foremen, the ones who got things done without drama. A good leader knew how to delegate, but also knew when to step in. They understood the skills of the people working under them and let them do their jobs. They didn't yell at people unless the building was about to fall down. They just knew what needed doing and made sure it got done.

Kari was a good leader. With just a few words, she had us moving with purpose. She'd found us this hidden area between the crack of a wide, dark mountain that towered over the dungeon like some ancient god's broken tooth. The space was narrow but defensible, with clear sightlines and multiple escape routes. It was the kind of tactical thinking that kept people alive in a place designed to kill them.

How it was possible felt beyond me, but the dungeon's ceiling still hung above us even here, black stone that looked like it was weeping blood. The dark veins running through the rock created the illusion of tears, crimson streaks that caught what little light filtered through the impossible architecture. Sometimes I'd stare at those patterns and see faces in them. Lynn's face. Jackson's. The faces of people I'd killed.

The dungeon played tricks on your mind like that. Made you see things that weren't there, remember things you'd rather forget. The Flux that permeated every stone and shadow seemed to feed on memory and emotion, turning them into weapons against your sanity.

I had learned the names of my companions during the long hours we'd spent together in this place. The armored initiate was called Vathwin Zar. His voice came from somewhere inside that suit of his, filtered through speakers that made him sound more machine than man. Sometimes I wondered if there was still anything human left inside all that metal, or if he'd already completed the transformation the rest of us were just beginning.

The plant-like initiate had a name I could not pronounce. It was beyond human vocal capabilities, all harmonics and frequencies that made my throat hurt just trying to form the sounds. So I called her Zoey. Mostly because the start of her complicated name sort of sounded like that. She didn't seem to mind. Maybe she was used to people simplifying her existence to fit their limitations.

Zoey communicated mostly through gestures and changes in the color of her bark-like skin. When she was content, she'd take on warmer hues, browns and golds that reminded me of autumn leaves. When she was agitated or sensing danger, she'd shift to darker greens and blacks that helped her blend into the shadows. It was beautiful and alien and made me feel very small in a universe that contained things I couldn't begin to understand.

Our little camp between the mountains was about as peaceful as anything could be in this place. We had gathered meat from the boars and other Flux beasts that roamed the forest areas. The meat tasted wrong, metallic and strange, with flavors that didn't exist in any earthly cuisine. But it kept us alive. That was all that mattered now.

Sometimes I'd remember Lynn's cooking while I chewed the alien flesh. Her roast chicken with herbs from her garden. The way she'd hum while she worked in the kitchen, completely absorbed in the simple joy of preparing food for people she loved. Those memories felt like they belonged to someone else now, to a man who had died the night monsters came through our front door.

I didn't know what the consequences for eating this strange Flux-infused meat would be, but there was little choice in the matter. Not if I wished to survive and return to Elisa. Each of us had wracked up fifty initiate points so far. Fifty points. Fifty dead things that used to be alive. Some of them had probably had families too. People who loved them, who would never know what happened to them in this place.

The mathematics of survival were brutal and simple. Each point represented violence, pain, the ending of something that had once drawn breath and felt fear. I tried not to think about the initiate points as a score in some cosmic game, but it was hard not to see the appeal of that interpretation. Numbers were clean. Numbers didn't have pink eyes filled with tears.

Kari had us patrol the area in shifts, maintaining watch against the threats that constantly prowled the dungeon's depths. Soon we knew we would need to leave this temporary sanctuary, venture back into the more dangerous areas to search for the four keys that would unlock the cathedral entrance. The mission was simple enough in concept, but execution would mean abandoning the relative safety we'd carved out for ourselves.

We had already seen the cathedral during one of our scouting expeditions. It was a massive structure that dominated the center of the dungeon, somehow giving off an ancient aura despite being made of that same dark stone I had gotten so used to seeing. Looking at it made my skin crawl, as if the building itself was alive and watching us with invisible eyes. The architecture was wrong in ways that hurt to contemplate, all angles that shouldn't exist and proportions that defied rational explanation.

I was sitting on the bed of leaves Kari had woven together for me with surprising skill. It wasn't much, but it was better than the hard stone. If only slightly. The simple comfort reminded me of camping trips with Jackson, of teaching him to make shelters from whatever materials we could find in the woods behind our house. He'd been so proud when he managed to weave his first lean-to, his small face lighting up with accomplishment.

A dim part of me considered sleeping on the stone, discarding the leaf bed entirely. Seeking comfort reeked of weakness. Every concession to softness was a reminder of the man I used to be, the man who had failed to protect his family when it mattered most.

Yet I would be vulnerable if I wasn't as rested as I could be. Poor sleep meant slower reflexes, impaired judgment, mistakes that could get me killed. I decided that it was a matter of survival, and kept the bed. Always with the justifications. Always finding reasons to make myself feel better about the choices I made.

It was easier to tell myself I was being practical than to admit I was clinging to whatever scraps of humanity I could find in this place. The leaf bed was a connection to the man who had built things instead of destroying them, who had created shelter instead of taking lives. Even if that man was dying a little more each day.

Many Flux beasts, and a score of other initiates had died at my hands in the last few weeks. I tried not to think about it too much. The faces of the dead. The sounds they made when they realized they were going to die. How some of them had begged. How their voices reminded me of Jackson crying out for help the night our world ended.

Each kill was supposed to get easier, but that wasn't quite right. It wasn't that they became easier, exactly. It was that they became more automatic. My body learned the motions, the precise angles and timing required to end a life efficiently. My mind learned to disconnect from the act, to treat it as just another task to be completed.

The first time I'd killed another initiate, I'd thrown up afterward. Vomited until there was nothing left but bile and self-loathing. By the tenth, I could eat a meal within the hour. By the twentieth, I'd stopped counting. That progression should have horrified me more than it did.

Factions had already been forming amongst the surviving initiates, temporary alliances based on mutual benefit and shared enemies. With Molech leading the largest and most aggressive group, of course. The draxi had proven to be as ruthlessly effective as I'd expected, gathering followers through a combination of charisma and brutal demonstrations of power.

His people hunted us endlessly, tracking our movements through the dungeon with persistence that bordered on obsession. But they often got caught up in battles with other initiate groups, their attention divided between multiple targets and objectives. The dungeon was eating us all alive, piece by piece. Molech's faction might be the largest, but it was also the most visible target for everyone else.

Dreams had been plaguing my sleep for the past several nights. Visions that felt more real than memory. Of a Nyxen man who looked like me, but much older, wearing a dark hood that somehow made his amber-gold eyes more prominent. He called to me across distances that felt both vast and intimate, beckoning me to go somewhere I couldn't quite identify. The dreams carried a weight and presence that suggested something beyond normal subconsciousness.

I pushed the thoughts to the side for now. Dreams were the least of my problems. Reality was dangerous enough without borrowing trouble from my sleeping mind. But the dreams lingered, leaving behind impressions and emotions that didn't fade with morning light.

Now was the time to address my advancement, and to choose my path forward. Flux Level 10 was a turning point for initiates, I had learned from conversations with the others. It was the first step down the path of true mastery in the Resonance Flux. The moment when you stopped being a student and started becoming something else entirely.

It required deep meditation, and the Flux would respond to you in a unique way. Or so I had been told by the others, though none of them had reached this threshold themselves. The process was supposed to be intensely personal, a moment of connection between the user and the fundamental forces that shaped reality.

My eyes were closed, and as I had done many times before now, I meditated on all I had done since entering this place. The memories flowed through my consciousness like a river of blood and violence, each scene perfectly preserved in crystalline detail. I couldn't forget any of it, even when I wanted to.

Dead beasts with their alien anatomies and impossible physiologies. Their blood on my hands, under my fingernails, splattered across my clothes and skin. The cries of an initiate woman begging me to spare her, the war in the pit of my stomach on whether or not to do so. Her pink eyes had pleaded with me, so filled with tears that they seemed like liquid jewels.

She'd been young, probably no older than twenty-five, with features that spoke of a species I didn't recognize but a desperation I understood perfectly. Her weapon lay broken several feet away, her defensive capabilities exhausted, her fate entirely in my hands. She spoke in a language I couldn't understand, but terror and hope were universal languages.

Weakness.

That was all that had come to mind when I looked at her. Not cruelty or evil, but simple, pathetic weakness. The inability to do what was necessary for survival. The sentimental attachment to ideals that had no place in a reality where strength was the only currency that mattered.

Yet, the lies whispered to me with seductive sweetness. It was the right thing to spare her life. Elisa would want me to show mercy, to remember the compassion that had once defined me. The good man I used to be would have found another way. A hero would have demonstrated that power could be used for protection rather than domination.

A strange discordant echo had rippled around me in that moment, a disturbance in the Flux that I didn't understand then. I didn't know that it had been something, or someone, using the Flux to observe me, to measure my choices and their implications. The sensation had been like standing at the center of a vast, invisible web while something immense and alien tested the vibrations.

Sobs wracked the initiate's body as she waited for my decision, her shoulders heaving with the effort of breathing through her terror. She was young. Maybe younger than I was when I'd first met Lynn at that coffee shop downtown. She had a family somewhere, people who loved her, people who would never know what happened to her in this place of nightmares.

Her tears reminded me of Elisa crying after a nightmare, reaching for me in the darkness of her bedroom. The way she'd cling to me like I was the only solid thing in a world gone mad. The absolute trust in her eyes, the certainty that Daddy would make everything better.

Elisa's face had flashed through my mind in that moment of decision. I latched onto that image, to the justification it provided, to the comfortable lie I told myself. For her, I would become the villain. How much more noble could you get than sacrificing your own moral purity for the sake of protecting those you loved? It was almost heroic when you looked at it the right way.

No.

The truth cut through my self-deception like a blade through silk. She wasn't an obstacle to overcome or a moral test to pass. She was power. Killing her would give me points. It would advance my position. It would make me stronger. I wanted her dead because her death would benefit me, and everything else was just lies I told myself so I could sleep at night.

Truth could not be denied, only hidden from. My blade flashed in a deadly arc, its white light leaving trails in the air like the final chord of a song's end. The break at the end of a story the author would never finish. Her head separated from her shoulders with a wet sound that seemed to echo for far longer than physics should have allowed.

Her head rolled across the stone floor, eyes still leaking tears that caught the light from my resonance blade. The expression frozen on her face was not one of hatred or anger, but of disappointed sadness, as if she had expected better from me right up until the moment I proved her wrong.

What sweet death. I committed every detail to memory, storing the image alongside all the others in my growing collection. Maybe I was becoming something that could enjoy this kind of thing. Maybe I already had. The thought should have horrified me more than it did, but horror required a level of humanity I wasn't sure I still possessed.

You are now Flux Level 10. You have 4 attributes to assign. However, first you must choose a path... standby...

The notification appeared in my mind, blue text that pulsed with its own inner light. This was it. The moment I'd been building toward since the first time I'd activated my resonance blade. The choice that would define not just my abilities, but the fundamental nature of what I was becoming.

My consciousness twisted, reality bending around me as some force beyond my understanding seized control of my awareness. The sensation was like being shot out of a cannon made of pure light, traveling impossible distances in the space between heartbeats. I couldn't breathe, couldn't think, could only experience the impossible speed and the certainty that I was traveling somewhere that shouldn't exist.

The starlight road stretched out before me, a highway of crystallized possibility that led to destinations I couldn't imagine. Colors that had no names flashed past, and I could hear music that wasn't quite music, harmonies that spoke to parts of my mind I'd never known existed.

Before I knew it, I was standing in an utterly black room that seemed to extend infinitely in all directions. There was no visible source of light, yet I could see perfectly, as if the darkness itself was illuminated from within. The air tasted of possibilities and choices yet unmade, heavy with the weight of potential futures.

On four pedestals arranged in a perfect square were four versions of myself. Each one looked like a powerful Nyxen Lord, radiating authority and menace in equal measure. They wielded violet resonance blades with crimson cores that pulsed like captured heartbeats, the weapons humming with barely contained power that made my teeth ache.

They looked like what I might become if I kept walking this path. What I probably would become, if I was being honest with myself. The transformation was already beginning, I could feel it in my bones, in the way my thoughts were changing, in the ease with which violence came to me now.

All versions of me wore dark clothes cut from materials that seemed to drink in light, with even darker cloaks that moved with their own internal wind. My eyes in each version were amber gold, burning with inner fire that spoke of deep connection to the Flux. My features were harder than they were now, weathered by experience and marked by choices I hadn't made yet. Older. Colder. An aura of power hung around each one that made the air itself feel heavy with potential.

I approached the first pedestal, and the version of myself standing there turned to meet my gaze. When he spoke, his voice was deeper than mine but unmistakably the same, carrying undertones of loss and corruption that resonated in my chest.

"I follow the Path of Fallen Stars. I will corrupt the light of the Flux into flaming darkness. Hope will become despair, love will become obsession, and all that is pure will burn in the fires of necessity."

The next version stepped forward, his movements carrying something that sounded like the man I used to be, but twisted into something harder and more protective. His voice held echoes of paternal authority, the kind that brooked no argument and crushed all opposition.

"I follow the Path of the Iron Father. My defenses are absolute, my passion blazes my path. I will shield what matters through overwhelming force and unbreakable will, and those who threaten what I protect will learn the meaning of true fear."

Then the next version spoke, radiating authority like heat from a furnace. This one carried himself like a judge rendering final verdicts, like someone whose word was law and whose decisions were absolute.

"I follow the Path of Crimson Judgment. My authority reigns supreme. I will bring order through strength and peace through decisive action. Those who cannot be saved will be destroyed, and those who resist will be broken."

Then, finally, the last version stepped forward. There was something different about this one, something that felt more complete than the others, as if he represented not just one aspect of potential but the synthesis of all possibilities into something greater than the sum of its parts.

"I follow the Path of the Nexus Eclipse. I will eclipse the Resonance Flux itself, bending all aspects to my will and becoming the center around which all power revolves. I will be the point where light dies and darkness is born, where hope transforms into something harder and more useful."

Choose.

The word hit me like a physical blow, echoing through the infinite darkness with weight and finality that made my bones vibrate. This was the moment that would define not just my path forward, but the fundamental nature of what I would become when this was all over.

I stood in the center of the four pedestals, looking at each version of myself, seeing the potential they represented. Each one was powerful, dangerous, capable of protecting Elisa and ensuring our survival in a universe that had proven itself hostile to everything I'd once held dear. But they were also each a step further away from the man who had built houses and coached little league, further into territory where return might no longer be possible.

The choice stretched out before me like a chasm that could only be crossed once. On the other side waited power, strength, the ability to ensure that what happened to Lynn and Jackson would never happen again. But the price would be the last remnants of the man I used to be.

Looking at those four versions of myself, I realized the choice had been made long ago. Maybe the night monsters tore through my home. Maybe the moment I first killed another thinking being. Maybe the instant I decided that Elisa's safety mattered more than my soul.

The man who had loved unconditionally was already dead. Now I just had to decide what would rise from his grave.
