I took a read of the room before divulging my plan. Yashas sat on a desk, his arms crossed as he gave me an appraising look. I figured he’d probably be the most onboard with my plan. Well, Aerion aside, who looked ready to accept whatever harebrained scheme I came up with.

That level of trust was humbling, endearing, and more than a little terrifying. She wasn’t always that way, of course. I remember the days when she’d question everything, scoffing at my ideas.

The fact that I had it meant so much, but at the same time, I wasn’t infallible.

Richard looked more amused than anything, and I put him in the same camp as Aerion for the most part. He’d seen my ideas play out, and knew there was a method to my madness. Usually.

Arianna would be the toughest to convince, but overall, that was a pretty receptive audience, especially considering how genius my masterplan was.

“Yeah, so, I’m thinking Lemmings.”

“Lemmings?” Richard asked, blinking.

“That’s… ah, right,” I said, scratching my head. “None of you would get the reference. It’s a game where you lead a conga line of braindead creatures to safety by sealing off all paths but one. In this case, we kinda want the opposite. Can you all do me a favor and grab all the pens you can find?”

Richard and Yashas shrugged, looking around for pens, while Aerion sprung into action, darting around the room as though it were a competition.

Arianna simply watched, but that was fine.

I moved around the room, instead picking up a half-dozen coffee mugs whose year-old contents had evaporated long ago. It really did look like the dungeon just picked up the city, deleted its people, and plopped in skeletons and zombies.

I sincerely hoped I was wrong. That it was just imitating that aesthetic, and that Earth was fully intact.

I brought the coffee mugs back to a large empty desk, motioning for everyone to huddle around it.

“Alright, so…” I arranged the pencils into a hasty map of the main lines of the Tokyo metro, and placed the mugs at their very ends. “The pens are the lines and the mugs are the trains.”

“Coffee mug trains mate?” Richard quipped. “Now I’ve truly seen it all.”

“My apologies for not raiding the nearest hobby train store,” I fired back, grabbing a nearby computer keyboard and turning it upside down before whacking it with a fair bit more force than a regular human would’ve been capable of.

The keys all launched from their housings, clattering all over my makeshift map.

“Let’s assume the keys are the zombies. They occupy every train line,” I said, moving the keys around. “From what we see on the cameras, that’s more or less the case, with higher concentrations further north.”

I grouped more keys towards the north end of the map.

“I’d initially thought we could just send an unmanned train from here up north, trampling the zombies, and that would be that. But the cars will get stuck and shut down well before they reach the critical mass.”

“So we can’t ram the zombies,” Richard said. “I assume you didn’t draft up this whole map just to tell us that?”

“Ye of little faith,” I said, picking up a key and moving it down south. “If we can’t get rid of them, why not have them get rid of themselves?”

“If only it were that easy,” Arianna said with a frown that looked like it’d been etched onto her face. “But what could—”

“Of course!” Yashas exclaimed, catching on just as I thought he would. “We get the shamblers to move of their own accord.” He moved a bunch of the ones up north down south. “Through a line we’ll not use. Then we simply board a train and take it where needed. Brilliant.”

“Well, yes,” Richard said with a frown. “When you put it like that it almost seems simple. But pray tell, how do you plan to get those corpses to move? That is, without using one of us as bait, which I sincerely hope you’re not suggesting. Moreover, if we do succeed in moving them, how do we ensure they do not return for us?”

“By using the trains,” Aerion said before anyone else could get a word in. “We already know the zombies react to sound. By activating the PA system in the stations, we can herd nearby zombies to a prepositioned train. Then we just turn the trains’ own sirens on and drive them away. The zombies will follow.”

That was my Aerion, learning things at terrifying speeds. That should have scared me, but for some reason, it just made her even hotter in my eyes.

I had a sudden urge to throw Aerion upon the map and make out with her then and there, but adult Greg managed to get a hold of hormonal animal teen Greg and put him in his place in time.

Coughing, I nodded sagely. “Aerion’s exactly right,” I said, throwing her a wink. “Just that we’ll likely have to pull this stunt several times. I don’t think we can get a train close enough to our destination without clearing out the zombies south of there.”

“And your Lemmings analogy,” Richard said, pointing at the map. “I take it you mean to clog all paths other than the one you want the zombies going down with other trains?”

“Exactly,” I said with a nod. “Chances are high they’ll just blindly rush after the moving train, but I’m sure some stragglers will find their way down other tunnels. Last thing we want is some zombie charging down the wrong tunnel, only for half the horde to follow.”

“This can work,” Yashas said, looking clearly excited. “In fact, I doubt there is any better way of handling the situation. Not only does this accomplish our goals, it puts none of us in danger. We can simply control the trains from here!”

It was reassuring to have Yashas onboard. He might have hailed from a different time, but a military commander was a military commander. Unlike my experience, which came from video games, his was real. He’d lived it. And in a much more brutal age, at that.

“While I admit this plan has merit,” Arianna said, pointing her hand to the keyboard keys representing zombies on my map. “There is one critical flaw. The trains can only run so far, and some lines loop back on themselves. How do you intend to keep the undead you draw away from coming back?”

Yashas’ eyes narrowed in thought, but I waved her concern away. “Oh, that? That’s easy. With another train.”

I brought another coffee mug behind the mass of zombies chasing a traincar, sandwiching them in place.

“We can literally ram another train into the horde from behind, creating a blockade of dead bodies. The zombies that don’t get splattered will have nowhere to go.”

And the pile of bodies from the crash would likely form a literal wall, preventing the zombies from clambering through the glass windshield and into the train proper. Not that they were smart enough to do that. Without anyone nearby to chase after, they’d have no reason to pursue and every reason to mill aimlessly around.

“That answers my questions,” Arianna said. “I am fully onboard with this plan. Especially since we can simply stay here and execute it.”

“Indeed,” Richard said. “Thought we’d be in for a nightmarish jaunt in the subway, running for our lives. This’ll be nothing but a stroll in comparison! Good on you, Greg.”

The others threw in their accolades, while Aerion simply looked on warmly. Maybe even proudly before turning to a computer to check on something.

Of course, I knew better than to let it get to my head. To think that my plan would go anywhere near the way it was supposed to. That old adage about first contact with the enemy popped into my head, but the good thing about this was if it did break somewhere, all we sacrificed was a train. Maybe a station at worst. That was alright since there were plenty of stations in Tokyo’s network, and tons of different routes we could use if we did screw it up.

The plan was flexible in the way all good plans should be.

Which was why I never thought it’d all fall apart less than a minute after I managed to convince everyone.

“That’s…” Aerion muttered, averting her eyes. Was that shame in her expression? But that made no sense—

“Lifting the lock down seems to not have restored autonomy to every train line. Not all trains may be remotely controlled,” Aerion said. “And if I am reading the map correctly, we have to control the trains we want manually. In person.”

Well, shit.

I sighed. “So much for Plan A, I guess. I don’t suppose any of you would feel like joining me out there? No? Thought so.”

Yeah, this was going to be great.
