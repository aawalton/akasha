Interlude V - Ione

Ione awoke from her nightmare in a horrific mood. Unlike most dreams, this one did not immediately start fading into a vague glum atmosphere. Instead, she recalled every detail with vivid detail. The constant wave of issues brought to her, the crushing responsibility on her shoulders. Living the life she’d have without Sene to act as her other half. Of course, out of all the dreams she’d experienced in her life, this was the one to stick with her.

She sat up and immediately wanted to go back to sleep, regardless of the threat of more nightmares.

Someone had tossed her in a cage. That part alone wasn’t too horrible. She knew more than a few different prison break tricks, it came with the territory when raised by famous jail wardens. Plus, for a cage, it wasn’t all that bad. A straw bed with a lumpy pillow. A suspicious gray liquid in a metal dish. Even an extra moth-eaten blanket. Borderline luxurious.

Outside of her cage was where all the problems were located.

A massive, seven-headed hydra loomed over Basil. Three of its mouths struggled to open, contained by an odd adhesive. Each head clearly was associated with an elemental type of damage. She’d seen a lesser version of this beast back in the Hon Basin when the crone’s hut had been assaulted. Whoever had summoned it had leveled up their skills and met more hydra types. She suspected this wasn’t actually a single summon, but an advanced technique that combined magical creatures during the summoning process with a unique circle. The more similar the monster, the easier to combine.

Basil looked half-dead as he dodged lightning bolts and blasts of flame. As she watched, a spike of ice pierced his hip, spewing out a bit more of his mass as it fully penetrated. At least he’d had the foresight to seal the three most dangerous heads, the soul ripper, the petrification blaster, and the concussive mental screamer. The four that remained were the basics. Fire, ice, electricity, and acid.

She winced as Basil stepped in a blob of acid on the floor. Acid was especially effective against Oozes. The fact that he prioritized the soul ripper, when it likely couldn’t hurt him due to his unique heritage, and left the acid head, said a lot about him as a person. Basil might be a bit of a fop, but he cared about his friends.

Weirdly, whoever stuck her in the cage hadn’t bothered to remove her equipment. She found her enchanted chalk in her back pocket, where she always kept it.

By the time she completed her first summoning circle, Basil had lost his leg up to his knee and staggered, barely capable of movement. He’d be dead in another 30 seconds.

Ione wasn’t about to let that happen.

She channeled her magic into her circle. One of three summoned goroles popped its head up. Then the mole-like monster disappeared from sight. From what Ione could see at a glance, the dungeon was constructed from stone, but that would barely slow her magical creature. She’d studied a great deal about their capabilities these last few months. They consumed dirt and stone alike in their burrowing.

Mere seconds later, one of the hydra’s feet collapsed into a pit trap. Another moment, and Basil disappeared into a newly formed tunnel. Safely tucked away in an underground pocket where he could recover.

Ione felt the connection between herself and her summons. At her level of expertise, she had the capability to tap into their very senses, should their distance not be too far. Granted, goroles were a bit trickier to comprehend, as they mostly relied on a unique magnetic sense to comprehend the tremors of dirt and rocks that surrounded them.

Increasing the sensational connection was just one of a dozen ways a summoner could advance their skills after learning how to conjure a creature. There were obviously modifications to the creature itself, though those largely came from excess blood use and additions to the summoning circle, but there were actual techniques involving the use of magic that could expand limitations as well. Last year, she’d learned how to keep her summons active, even while unconscious. More recently, she’d been expanding the range of her summons to greater distances. She assumed that the hydra’s summoner had likewise mastered that technique, as they were nowhere to be found at the moment.

She suspected the summoner to be the other soul slice of Kizu. That would make some measure of sense. They’d come down here with the intention of confronting the Blood Lord and he’d enslaved Kizu’s soul to his service. Not unlike her sister, Kizu was hopeless at conjuring as a whole, so that implied that his rogue soul fragment might have some capacity for the spellcraft.

Her boyfriend, while present, was safely tucked into a far corner alongside a few random doll-like pieces of a person. She wondered if he’d died fighting a golem or something. She wanted to investigate, just in case it turned out to be another rare magical monster. Kizu had a knack for attracting those. Sometimes, she wondered if she, too, was among their number. Another weird creature pulled into his aura.

Unfortunately, the giant hydra now lacked any other visible targets and turned its four unbound heads towards her. That meant she was at a severe time deficit. Examining the weird limbs scattered alongside Kizu would have to wait. She spotted Mort crouched on top of him, so decided to trust in her boyfriend’s familiar to keep him safe while she took care of the looming threat.

While lost in thought, her hands had not been idle. She’d already dismissed two of the three goroles, as the number of summons she commanded remained limited and they’d served their purpose. The remaining goroles was actively protecting Basil down in the tunnel it created. It would keep the shapeshifter safe until she finished dealing with this nuisance of a beast.

A crack of lighting shot at her the same moment that her two-headed bear pulled itself out of the summoning circle. The electricity, while annoying, was a far cry from lethal to her creature. It was a notoriously resilient beast and her go-to when seeking out a brute force solution. One of the heads sneezed and shook its snout while the other growled at the hydra.

However, she reflected that her bear being her go-to might very well change once she mastered the cherufe summoning circle. Likely overkill for some situations and maybe a massive lava beast would occasionally cause more problems than it solved, but damn did she love the thing. She was tempted to sneak into the World Dungeon on creature-seeking expeditions more often. Maybe if they all got out of this alive she’d be able to convince Kizu to delve with her.

Another crack of lightning that would fry her insides and pop her eyes out if it connected with her. Instead, her bear shrugged it off, standing between her and the hydra. She knew through her connection that it wasn’t completely immune to the jolts. Its reaction time and reflexes were stiffer than before. Thankfully, that wasn’t what she needed from this summon. Today, it was tasked as her shield and the breaker of prisons. That task, it accomplished with full marks.

Her cage’s metal bars split under her bear’s strength as it pressed its body against the prison. Once free of containment, it roared its victory, echoing Ione’s own internal jubilation.

This was very low on her list of potential viable prison break strategies, as it attracted way too much attention for her to slip away without getting caught. She’d brainstormed it as a way to draw attention away from herself as the bear rampaged and caused problems, but discarded it because a truly powerful mage could track a summoner through a discarded summon. That type of divination was a rare talent, especially since it required the mage to force the summon to remain active and defy the summoner’s dismissal, but prisons tended to have those annoyingly unique mages around.

Thankfully, this prison break seemed to only require simple brute strength. No weird mages in sight… other than Kizu.

The bear charged forward, a frontal assault on the hydra. That left Ione momentarily defenseless, but she had enough experience to know her timing.

Ione completed her next summoning circle, creating a blue creature with stubby legs and bat-like wings. It darted out of the circle, its bulbous blue body wobbling as it flew in the direction of the hydra right as the red head opened its mouth. This particular summon’s species was related to kokokokyu, like the one Professor Arclight held on retainer as her assistant. Unlike Gulp, however, his distant cousin had several useful applications in battle.

Right as the red head of the hydra blasted a beam of fire, her summon got right up in its face and practically kissed the beast. The stream of flames transferred from the hydra, directly down her creature’s throat. The sight was reminiscent of a mama bird feeding her baby.

The stream of flames halted and the head tried to snap at her summon. It swerved, keeping its body outside of reach of the monster’s bite but close enough to keep sucking in its next stream of flames.

The downside of having one body for all those heads was that the fiery hydra head remained tied back by its core. It attempted to whip its long neck around to shoot fire from a different angle, but her summon maintained the perfect distance to keep consuming its attacks.

That head nullified, Ione’s hand moved in a practiced blur. This would need to be her final summon. She already had three active. Two of them barely sapped her blood supply, but her bear was another matter. Plus, maintaining control and focus of so many creatures was taxing on her mental fortitude. She’d lose control if she juggled too many summons at once.

Her two-headed bear wrestled with the ice and electric heads, digging its claws and teeth into the beast’s flesh while tanking spikes of ice and repeated jolts of electricity to its furry body. She had faith in her creation. It would hold its own for at least another half a minute.

That left the acid head active and capable of killing her.

A feathered form burst out of her final summoning circle. Rather than a defensive measure like with her other summons, Ione opted for pure offense this time.

The bird twisted in the air around a glob of acid.

Ione threw herself to the side to avoid the projectile. The green liquid splattered on the sad prison’s pillow and immediately consumed it with a hiss.

Darting forward at a terrific speed, her summoned bird ripped its talons into the acid hydra head’s eye sockets, tearing and splitting the weak external organs.

One of the sealed heads whipped around to slam into her bird summon and knock it off the acid head’s face. Rather than maneuver off of its perch, Ione’s summon steadied itself and the neck of the hydra met its beak. The power behind the blow split the sharp beak into its flesh, burying the bird’s head halfway into the monster’s neck.

Ione needed to be careful not to allow the hydra to fully lose a head. That was basic hydra knowledge. Never cut off a head. If it got even halfway severed, it could grow another head from the wound.

The beak’s piercing didn’t quite meet the damage qualification for new head growth, but Ione decided to be cautious from this point forward. The bird’s head popped out as the hydra twisted and writhed.

She commanded her fire-eating summon to pull back and dart over to the side.

A stream of fire shot out. However, her summons were too fast to get hit. Instead, the hydra simply scorched its own flesh, searing the exposed wound closed.

Ione grinned. This was fun. It had been a long time since she needed to out-maneuver a beast like this. There were so many pieces that went into this puzzle.

Against her better judgement, Ione’s chalk sketched out a fifth summoning circle. The temptation to add one more player overwhelmed her sensibilities. By this point, nearly the entirety of her cage’s horizontal surface area was marked up by chalk.

This summon was one she had zero battle experience with and she hoped that, since the hydra was currently on its backfoot, it would be enough of a controlled environment that she could test it out with minimal risk. A few modifications to its circle should potentially add to its combat capabilities. Though, Ione needed to be careful or risk the spell resulting in nothing more than black smoke.

Out of the glowing circle, crawled a massive tortoise with a shell formed of pure ice that jutted out in an angular shape.

A common magical creature in the northern reaches of Tross, this species of tortoise occasionally utilized its below-freezing shell to freeze its nearby attackers in cocoons of ice. Even polar bears avoided approaching it.

She was willing to bet summoned hydras lacked that self-preservation instinct.

Sure enough, the fire head shot forward, jaws ajar and teeth glittering. It had learned not to rely on fire with her other summon nullifying its efforts, so it went with the classic crushing bite.

Ione clapped her hands in delight as it made contact with the shell and the immediate backlash sent a wave of ice across its face and halfway down its serpentine neck. The frozen head sagged to the side, rendered useless. She’d successfully augmented the summoning circle to amplify the freezing capability.

With that head out of the way, she dropped her summon for fire protection. She no longer needed it and controlling the fifth summon already caused a splitting migraine in just the first twenty seconds. She was afraid she might pass out from pain if she had to command them all simultaneously for any longer.

The stupid hydra slammed one of the sealed heads into the tortoise, as if expecting to shatter the shell through brute force. It, likewise, froze on contact.

Next, she used her bear to shove the remaining heads into the shell. The remaining underground gorole collapsed the stone floor underfoot, helping her bear heave the bulk of the enemy beast into the shell. Her bird’s talons dug into the hydra’s flesh and assisted in getting its faces exposed to the ice.

It was a team effort, but soon the hydra was nearly entirely sealed away in a frozen cocoon. Only the ice head remained immune to the cold’s grip. However, her bird removed its eyes, tongue, and most of its teeth. All it could do from there was thrash about uselessly.

Feeling rather smug, Ione crawled out of her broken cage and surveyed her work. Far better than when she’d faced the beast in Hon. Just as she was about to bring Basil back to the surface, when a beagle bounded over.

The massive ears were a clear sign it held magical beast blood, so Ione brought her guard up and readied herself for a continuation of the fight.

Instead of attacking her, it tripped over its ears and tumbled. The brown and white hound fell into her tortoise. Instead of freezing on contact, as expected, the dog got back its wobbly feet and happily panted at her.

Ione’s heart leapt at the indication. This seemed to be a magically immune beast. She’d heard about these creatures, but never seen one for herself. She ran over to it, not caring about the danger it imposed.

Right as she fell to her knees beside the beagle, it, along with the hydra, flaked away into nothingness. A summoner’s dismissal.

“Nooooo!”
