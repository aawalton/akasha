The shadows in Jameson’s reception room seemed to drink the lamplight, growing thicker around the corners of the ancient map spread across the table. Cade studied the layout of Cathedral Point, mentally cataloging entry points and potential ambush locations while Jameson’s skeletal finger traced the outline of what appeared to be a subterranean crypt. A part of him chastised whatever unoriginal moron named a destination after the building that was built on it.

Art was dead. Again.

“These lower chambers,” the draugr lord said, tapping a section marked with faded warning glyphs, “contain several sealed rooms. Our intelligence suggests the Crown rests in the central chamber, beneath what was once the high altar.”

Cade’s gaze flicked toward Bernard, catching the gold-ranker’s calculating appraisal of the same location. His eyes narrowed infinitesimally—he was seeing the same tactical problems Cade was. The approach was a killing zone, a perfect setup for an ambush. Though whether that was from monsters or each other would remain to be seen.

“Previous expeditions?” Orro inquired, his voice neutral in that way that meant he was already planning how to kill everyone in the room. “Surely we’re not the first to attempt retrieval.”

A current of tension rippled between the two crews. Smart question—Orro never wasted words.

“Several,” Jameson admitted after a quiet sigh. “None successful.”

“Convenient,” Nash remarked, Bernard’s scholarly lieutenant adjusting his spectacles with deliberate care. “One might wonder if the survivors reported anything of value before their... presumed demise.”

“Hallucinations, mostly,” Astrid replied with practiced disinterest. “Singing trees. Dragons the size of mountains. An upside down duchess who only speaks every other syllable. The usual trauma-induced delusions.”

The rotund Augustus grunted. “Say they weren’t delusions but real horrors. I remember a lad from them Arbiters. Fingernails torn out. Eyes bleeding. Mind was shattered, it was. Said the mist in the Blightmarshes remembers your name. The longer you stay, the more you become one with the marshes. Stay too long, and you just might.”

A chill slithered down Cade’s spine. Wonderful.

Rayka shifted Chubbs to her other hip, the toddler’s golden head lolling with exhaustion. “We need specifics on environmental hazards.” Her eyes met Cade’s briefly—unlike Bernard’s crew, she wasn’t playing games with their lives. “What kills people before they even reach the cathedral?”

Jameson’s gray lips twitched in what might have been respect. “Straight to practical concerns. Refreshing.” He exchanged a guarded look with Astrid. “Our intelligence suggests a variety of ecological aberrations. Localized toxic phenomena. Predatory flora and fauna.”

“Aberrations,” Elena repeated flatly. “Could you possibly be any more vague?”

“Deliberately so,” Jameson acknowledged without pretense. “This operation rewards resourcefulness over spoon-fed intelligence.”

“In other words,” Bernard interjected, stroking his immaculate mustache, “you don’t actually know what’s in there.”

Something flickered across Astrid’s perfect features—irritation? Fear? The expression vanished too quickly to interpret.

“We know enough,” she countered smoothly. “The cathedral’s layout. The Crown’s location. The general topography. The rest is... fluid.”

Glenn, the perpetually smiling dark elf from Bernard’s crew, had been unusually quiet, studying the map with unnerving concentration. Now his head tilted slightly, as though listening to distant music.

“This place whispers to me,” Glenn breathed, his smile taking on a cruel light. “It sings of secrets the gods forgot to bury.”

Chubbs, who had been drowsing against Rayka’s shoulder, suddenly giggled—a sound like silver bells that raised the hair on the back of Cade’s neck.

“Funny elf man hears them too!” she exclaimed, golden eyes momentarily flaring with divine luminescence. Cade coughed, drawing attention away from the goddess.

The temperature in the room plummeted. Glenn stared at the toddler with an expression caught between reverence and terror. Bernard’s crew collectively tensed, Elizabeth’s hand drifting to a concealed blade at her hip.

“What an active imagination,” Astrid interjected with practiced smoothness, though her own gaze had sharpened with predatory interest. “Children say the most curious things.”

Cade angled himself subtly between Bernard’s crew and Chubbs, feeling destruction magic simmer beneath his skin despite Jameson’s suppression field. Through their mental bond, he sensed the others shifting into defensive stances—Nora’s hand on her sword hilt, Orro calculating kill trajectories, Gavin’s telepathy stretching outward like invisible fingers.

“Right. Curious, indeed. Well. first to retrieve the Crown claims their respective prize,” Jameson concluded, producing two identical wooden cases, each containing rolled parchments. “These maps contain what information we’ve deemed appropriate to share.”

“How magnanimous,” Rufus muttered, still nursing his burned arms.

“Our generosity extends precisely to our interests,” Jameson replied without pretense. “No further, no less.”

The draugr rose with the finality of a coffin lid closing. “This meeting is finished. I suggest beginning preparations immediately. The Blightmarshes become completely impassable after the dry season ends in six days.”

As he swept toward the exit, Astrid lingered, her perfect posture betraying nothing. Only when her father had vanished beyond the doorway did she turn to Cade, voice dropping to a whisper that barely disturbed the air.

“When you’re ready to depart, come to the eastern courtyard. There’s information our intelligence couldn’t include in the formal briefing.”

Before he could respond, she glided after her father, leaving behind a subtle perfume that couldn’t quite mask the underlying scent of preserved flesh.

“Well,” Elena broke the silence, expelling a breath she’d apparently been holding, “anyone else feel like we’ve just volunteered to leap head-first into our own graves, or is it just me?”

The eastern courtyard of Jameson’s compound was a monument to elegant decay—once-beautiful statues worn by time into grotesque approximations of their original forms, fountains burbling with water tinted faintly green with algae. Moonlight spilled across crumbling cherubs and weathered nymphs, creating shifting shadows that seemed almost to breathe.

Orro and Cade led their exhausted crew through the silent gardens, every sense alert for ambush. Gavin walked slightly apart from the others, the weight of his revealed past visible in the tight set of his shoulders.

“You planning to brood all night, or just until we reach certain death?” Cade projected privately.

Gavin’s mental response carried a ghost of his usual humor. “I thought I’d aim for all night, but I’m flexible.”

“Bernard’s crew doesn’t know you like we do. Their opinion means less than nothing.”

Before he could respond, shadows coalesced near a crumbling angel statue, and Astrid emerged like a specter materializing from mist. She’d changed into a gown of midnight blue that seemed to absorb light rather than reflect it, making her pale skin glow with unnatural luminescence.

“I wasn’t certain you’d come,” she said, her gaze sweeping over their battle-ready postures.

“We’re naturally suspicious people,” Cade replied easily. “Occupational hazard.”

“A survival trait,” she corrected, glancing over her shoulder before moving closer. “My father hasn’t told you everything about the Blightmarshes.”

“Shocking,” Elena deadpanned.

Astrid ignored the interruption. “The swamp is suffused with a mist—colorless, odorless, but lethal to living tissue. Exposure causes rapid degradation, starting with the lungs.”

Nora stepped forward, her paladin training evident in her bearing. “Why tell us this now, and not during the briefing?”

“Because my father and I have... divergent interests in this particular venture,” Astrid admitted with surprising candor. “He’s content to send both teams in unprepared, to see which is more resourceful. I prefer to stack the deck.”

“In our favor?” Orro asked skeptically, his dark eyes unreadable. “Why?”

Astrid’s smile was enigmatic. “Let’s just say I have my reasons to want Bernard’s crew to fail.”

“Something personal with Bernard?” Rayka guessed, her inherent honesty cutting through diplomatic pretense.

“Something like that.”

“How do you plan to stack the deck, then? Weapons? A cool vest?” Cade inquired dryly, leaning against a nearby wall.

“The Arbiters who patrol the boundary use specialized masks—enchanted filters that neutralize the mist’s effects.” Astrid produced a small scroll from within her sleeve. “This is the location of their nearest outpost. Small garrison, minimal security. The masks are stored in a locked chamber when not in use.”

Orro took the scroll, examining it briefly before tucking it away. “You’re suggesting we steal from Honor’s Arbiters. The same people who execute trespassers on sight.”

“I’m suggesting you want to live,” Astrid corrected coolly. “Without those masks, you won’t last two hours in the deep marsh. Hallucinations, remember?”

“And Bernard’s crew?” Cade asked, unable to resist the question. “Do they get this helpful tip too?”

“They have their own resources,” Astrid replied cryptically. “Elizabeth still has connections among the Arbiters, even if she was excommunicated.”

“For what?” Elena demanded in disbelief.

Astrid met her question with a flat stare. “Extremism.”

“Fuck me,” Elena breathed. “Anyone too zealous for those upright pricks has got to be a cut above. Or below.” The lockpick scrunched her nose. “Hard to say which would be worse.”

Chubbs stirred in Rayka’s arms, her golden eyes opening to fix on Astrid with unusual clarity. “Pretty lady smells wrong,” she announced with a toddler’s bluntness. “We should cover her in icing.”

Astrid froze, something almost like fear flickering across her perfect features. “What an... observant child.”

“She has a gift. Some call it a disappointment, but I prefer the term imagination,” Cade responded, watching Astrid carefully. The draugr princess seemed genuinely unsettled by Chubbs’ attention.

“Indeed.” Astrid backed away slightly. “One last piece of advice—don’t enter the marshes from the main quarantine points. The patrols are heaviest there. Use the northwestern approach, through the Brittle Hills near Westmire. The boundary is weaker there.”

“Why are you helping us?” Rayka asked directly, her inherent honesty pushing past diplomatic niceties.

Astrid’s expression hardened. “I have my reasons. Don’t mistake this for kindness.”

With that, she melted back into the shadows, leaving them alone in the moonlit garden.

“Well, that was decidedly ominous,” Jer observed cheerfully.

“And suspicious,” Nora added, her hand resting on her sword hilt. “We’re being manipulated.”

“Obviously,” Cade agreed, “but the question is whether her information is accurate.”

Orro examined the small scroll Astrid had provided. “The outpost location seems genuine. I recognize the area—remote enough to attempt infiltration, but still accessible.”

“Enchanted masks,” he murmured, rolling the scroll back up with careful precision. His dark eyes met Cade’s with pointed accusation. “Specialized breathing apparatus. Imagine that.”

Cade winced. The memory of accidentally destroying Orro’s prized assassin mask was still fresh.

“I said I was sorry about your mask,” Cade muttered defensively.

“Sorry doesn’t filter poison,” Orro replied..

Rayka stepped between them, Chubbs still cradled in her arms. “Before you two start bickering again, can we please find somewhere to rest? Chubbs is exhausted, and frankly, so am I.”

The divine toddler did indeed look drained, her usually vibrant golden eyes dulled with fatigue. Even Bunny, perched on Cade’s shoulder, had his head tucked beneath one wing.

“Rayka’s right,” Nora conceded, her own exhaustion visible in the tight lines around her eyes. “We need to regroup, assess our supplies, and make a proper plan. Not here, though.”

“Agreed,” Cade said, casting one last suspicious glance at the shadows where Astrid had disappeared. “Let’s find those rooms Jameson mentioned and get some rest. Tomorrow’s going to be... interesting.”

After questioning a particularly austere draugr butler, they found their rooms. Jameson’s guest quarters proved surprisingly comfortable, if somewhat eccentric in their décor. The Clover Clan apparently collected art from across the centuries, resulting in rooms that mixed Renaissance paintings with modern furnishings and ancient artifacts displayed in glass cases.

They had been given two adjoining chambers—one for the women, one for the men—connected by a small sitting room equipped with a large table perfect for planning. Cade spread the map across its surface, weighing the corners with various weapons from their collective arsenal.

“Alright, let’s break this down,” he began, once everyone had gathered. “We need to raid an Arbiter outpost for masks, then sneak into a deadly swamp, find a ruined cathedral, descend into a crypt, grab a crown, and get back out. All while avoiding monsters, toxic mist, and Bernard’s crew, who will definitely try to kill us.”

“When you put it like that, it sounds almost easy,” Jer quipped, sprawled in an overstuffed chair with one leg dangling over the armrest.

Rayka had settled Chubbs on a small palette in the corner, where the toddler now slept peacefully, thumb tucked in her mouth. Even in sleep, faint golden light pulsed beneath her skin, a reminder of the divine power contained in that small form.

“The Arbiter outpost is our first obstacle,” Nora said, pointing to the location on Astrid’s scroll. “According to this, it’s manned by a small garrison—typically eight to ten soldiers.”

“Too many for a direct approach,” Orro assessed. “But their patrol patterns create a window around midnight when the interior guard is minimal.”

“So we sneak in, grab the masks, and sneak out?” Elena asked, clearly skeptical. “These are Arbiters, Orro. They’re not exactly known for lax security.”

“We could create a diversion,” Gavin suggested, speaking up for the first time since the meeting with Jameson. “Something to draw them away from the outpost temporarily.”

“What kind of diversion?” Cade asked, genuinely curious.

“Something that would require multiple Arbiters to investigate, but wouldn’t immediately suggest a raid on their supplies,” Gavin explained, warming to his idea. “A magical disturbance, perhaps, or evidence of a monster breaching the quarantine perimeter.”

“We could use my fear magic,” Nora suggested. “Create illusions realistic enough to trigger their response protocols.”

“Too risky,” Orro countered. “Your magic leaves traces that seasoned Arbiters can detect. We need something more... tangible.”

They debated options for nearly an hour, proposals becoming increasingly elaborate and impractical as fatigue clouded their judgment. Evie suggested using her siren voice to lure the guards with fate-riddled truths—quickly rejected due to the risk of permanent mental damage. Jer proposed an acrobatic infiltration through the roof—dismissed when Orro pointed out the magical wards likely in place.

“This is getting us nowhere,” Rayka finally said, rubbing her temples. “We’re all exhausted, making sloppy plans. We need rest.”

“Rayka’s right,” Cade conceded, recognizing the wisdom in her words. “Fresh eyes in the morning might see what we’re missing.”

“And what about Bernard?” Gavin asked quietly. “He won’t be wasting time sleeping.”

“Let him wear himself out,” Cade replied with forced confidence. “Besides, they have their own preparations to make.”

The decision made, they began organizing watch rotations—old habits die hard, especially when surrounded by potential enemies. As the others dispersed to their respective sleeping areas, Cade lingered by the window, gazing out at the moonlit grounds of Jameson’s estate.

Movement caught his eye—a group of figures crossing the main courtyard with purposeful strides. Bernard and his crew, already departing. The gold-ranker glanced up, somehow sensing Cade’s observation, and their eyes locked across the distance.

No words were exchanged, no gestures beyond Cade’s raised middle finger and Bernard’s contemptuous smile. Yet in that brief moment, a cold certainty settled in Cade’s gut—a knowledge as clear as if it had been spoken aloud.

Only one crew would return from the Blightmarshes.

Cade turned away from the window, returning to where his own crew waited. They were a strange collection of misfits—an assassin, a fallen paladin, a siren prophet, a pair of thief twins, a telepathic werewolf, his loyal sister, and a toddler goddess. Against a gold-ranker and his battle-hardened team, the odds seemed impossible.

But then, Cade had never much cared for odds.

As he settled into his own makeshift bed, one hand resting protectively near Bunny’s sleeping form, he allowed himself a grim smile. Bernard might have experience, power, and resources on his side.

But Cade had something far more dangerous—a crew worth fighting for, and absolutely no hesitation to blow shit up if it meant keeping them all safe.
