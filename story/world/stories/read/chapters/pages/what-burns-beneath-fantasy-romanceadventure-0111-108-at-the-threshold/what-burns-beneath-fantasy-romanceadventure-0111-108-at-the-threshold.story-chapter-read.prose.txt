I expected distant screams and trails of blood to greet me when Velidan sliced his palm, murmured, “Caravain, felinor,” and nudged me through a swirl of blue light.

Instead I found myself in a small cave as the ozone smell of magic faded to chilly damp. Crystals glowing with dusky blue light all along the walls and ceiling bathed Velidan as he appeared beside me. The glow illuminated a time-worn slope expanding out to a much larger cavern barricaded by great iron doors. One of them was propped open, with the faint sound of music emanating from within.

Velidan strode down the slope and I hurried after. Stalactites and stalagmites, some as small as my arm and some much larger than the Fae beside me, loomed around us like shadowy teeth. A few of them shuddered as we walked past.

We were halfway to the door when pale light and a little gasp came from behind. Turning showed the rune’s glow dimming once more, and atop it stood a high-browed Fae with long golden hair. A brunette woman in a belted dress stood beside him, turning as if in a daze. The Fae watched her for a moment, arms outstretched as if to catch a faint. Then he saw Velidan, smiled, and the pair started towards us.

“Aravis.” The voice at my side was even. “Glad I’m not the only one late.”

“Fashionably late.” The man winked. “Say hi, Liana.”

The woman blinked at us, pupils dilating in the light. Blue and shadowed black danced along her soft features and modest satin dress. She gave a shy smile. “Hello.”

I swallowed, looking her up and down and clutching the folds of my mantle as the two Fae conversed. She’s human. I suppose she must be pacted? I searched Liana’s face as she turned this way and that, examining her surroundings with knitted brows. She certainly doesn’t look frightened. Saints, she looks calmer than me!

I offered her a hesitant wave. She returned it and we regarded each other uncertainly until two voices called us on.

Harp music and dulcet chatter trailed from ahead, swelling louder as we passed the doors. Once again, my expectations were not met; I’d braced myself for iron spikes and the scent of gore, but instead found myself in a tapered stone hallway lit by more crystals. Arched openings in the walls loomed ahead, and the echoes reverberated from one further down.

A familiar, bronze-eyed face appeared in that archway. Then Embriel was simply in front of us, wearing an elegantly embroidered vest and a broad smile. “There you two are! Any sign of Kallian lurking out there?”

Velidan’s brows shot up. “You invited him?”

“Of course not. But that’s the best way to get him to show up, isn’t it?” He chuckled, as if at a private joke, before beckoning us forward.

Expectations were shattered once more at the next threshold. Cold stone sloped down to a massive cavern lit by more crystals and echoing with the sounds of voices, laughter, clinking glasses, tapping boots, and music. Two dozen figures stood in small clusters, sipping long-stemmed drinks and chatting. The walls shimmered with dusky cerulean light that waved and rippled. At first I thought it was simply from the masses of crystals, but one step into the chamber- and a swallowed scream- showed that it came reflected from below.

The floor was smooth, polished glass. And beneath, sending the bottom dropping straight from my stomach, was water. It stretched the length of the cavern and went down and down, fading from vivid jade to endless black. Dark seaweed swayed and the occasional shadowy form- some as small as a minnows and some so large it made me dizzy- glided far beneath.

The shock had me scrabbling at Velidan’s sleeve, but he ignored the gesture and walked away. Voices rose to greet him and he responded coolly.

I stood rigid for a very long time. Eternity crawled by as I stared, heart in my throat, past frozen feet and into the endless expanse. Waiting for it to swallow me. My ears rang. Every breath was far too loud and far too fast.

“-at her. She’s terrified! Since when does mindless cruelty darken your step?”

I became vaguely aware of a forceful, feminine voice echoing not far ahead. Chatter continued unbothered around the room, but Velidan’s response- and the wildly sickening comfort that came from his proximity- commanded my attention.

“Don’t start, Alora. She’s perfectly fine.”

“She’s scared out of her mind.”

I could practically hear Velidan roll his eyes. “She’s just being dramatic.”

I wanted to cry. I’d made it all the way here only to fall apart at the threshold. I wasn’t afraid of water- water was a silly thing to worry about! I was in a room full of monsters, predators who could kill me in an instant, and it wasn’t the water that should have me panicking!

Yet I couldn’t look away. Water wasn’t supposed to be so dark and endless, so full of shadows and right there, just beneath my feet, and all it would take was one little crack-

“You should be ashamed of yourself,” the woman hissed.

“You should mind your own business.”

“By the moon, I will not! I’ll not stand silent after you brutalized a helpless young woman!”

He scoffed. “Well, I fixed her afterwards.”

The voices around them broke into laughter. Embriel’s rich tone was among them.

A resounding slap rang off the walls. “You’re lucky I wasn’t there, you arrogant, upstart brat. I would have beaten you a thousand times worse. I still might!”

I finally managed to look up and saw a tall, striking woman with an avalanche of silver hair snarling in Velidan’s face.

He took a deliberate step back. “Alora-”

She advanced. “What would your mother say? I knew her for centuries, whelp, and I can make a guess!”

He spat something in a tongue I didn’t understand. More laughter echoed, then died as Alora rounded to snarl at the other Fae for their inaction the other night. For simply watching me bleed, for not tearing through the door to untie me. Tears filled my eyes at the sight.

Then Velidan was looming over me with a darkened, slightly confused expression. Blood trickled from one corner of his mouth. “What is wrong?” he hissed.

My lips felt numb. I stared, unable to speak past the thickness in my throat as Alora’s fierce admonishments echoed around us.

He seized me by the upper arm and gave a little shake. “Answer.”

“I’m f-f-frightened,” I managed to gasp, fully aware of what a ridiculous thing that was to say given the circumstances. Obviously I was frightened. I gestured to the water and, as the grip clenched to bruising, squeaked, “My l-lord.”

Velidan looked at the floor, then at me. “Well, calm yourself. You’re drawing attention.”

Something far, far too big glided beneath us. I whimpered.

He stared in obvious frustration, then dragged me to my toes. We were so close he blocked out the room, and the growl was more hot breath and the shape of moving lips than actual sound. “I will keep you safe.”

The irony of who exactly was offering that reassurance, and the wildly threatening position he was offering it from, brought the hysterical urge to laugh. Instead I clutched at him and choked back, “From the floor?”

Endless eyes blazed at me. He looked torn between anger and absolute bafflement, as if me falling to pieces so quickly, and because of something so silly as a few panes of glass, had never once crossed his mind. Then, after a moment of heavily furrowed brows, the expression smoothed. Velidan dragged me towards the group of Fae.

“Here, Alora.” He shoved me into the silver-haired woman’s arms. “Since you’re feeling motherly.”

Alora caught me carefully even as she shook with rage. Then, after snarling once more that Velidan should be ashamed of himself and hissing at the little crowd, she firmly drew me away.

“I am sorry,” the Fae murmured as both hands braced my shoulders. It was a very tentative touch, as if she was ready to pull away at my first flinch. “For what mistreatments you have suffered. He has no right.”

I walked along in a daze, heartbeat drumming in my ears. We passed beautiful faces and mellifluous voices that all blurred together. I registered High Fae, small gray-skinned creatures, and a few more figures that appeared to be human. It all seemed very insignificant compared to the expanse of ocean beneath each step.

“I’m sure your lord,” she spat the word, “wants you to attend him, but you will sit until you feel ready. And I have more words for him. Dear Bernard will keep you company. If anyone bothers you, come straight to me.”

There are kind High Fae. More than one. She would have helped me, if she’d been at the other party. She’s going to go yell at Velidan more because she thinks he hurt me. Saints, she hit him. I pictured the blood on his lips and gave a little shudder. She hit him hard.

I half-wondered if I’d fainted at the threshold, hit my head, and was imagining this entire sequence of events.

We were nearly at the other end of the room when I finally realized I was not, in fact, dreaming, and managed to find my voice. I looked at the striking woman and croaked, “What’s a dear Bernard?”
