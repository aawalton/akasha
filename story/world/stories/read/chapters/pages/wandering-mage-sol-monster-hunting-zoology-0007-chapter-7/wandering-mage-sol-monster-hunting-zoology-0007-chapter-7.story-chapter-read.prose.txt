Another two days passed, and Tressa found herself resting in a tree near a cliff to the northeast, carefully filling in the details of a Torrent Bee’s wing. The day before had been somewhat fruitful. After finding a patch of thistle berries and mixing hera herbs into a salve for her wounds, she had continued on and found another Torrent Bee hive. She observed it from a distance, adding several pages to her research.

By now, she had learned which flowers they favored for pollen, when they started and ended their work days, and interestingly, she observed that most other forest creatures fled the moment they heard the hum of their wings.

The Torrent Bees certainly didn’t seem aggressive when they were just going about their work days and returning to their hive. But Tressa, of course, knew all too well how that could flip if they were disturbed.

But the frustrating part was not knowing what was inside the hive. She got to see the construction of the walls by observing damaged portions, but knew nothing of the inner chambers. She could only guess that the queen was in there somewhere, but how did she produce larvae? How were they kept and hatched? How long did it take them to grow and then get to work? If she could just get one quick peek inside, she would have a much clearer picture!

She tapped her chin with her quill.

“Perhaps I could find a way to smoke them out? The opening seems large enough to get my head inside of. I’d have to experiment with what sort of smoke might be effective, and perhaps push it to them with a wind spell… But I wouldn’t want to accidentally hurt them, either.” She sighed, scratching that idea from her mind. “Maybe some kind of spell to see through solid objects?”

That thought made her think of the dragon, and wondered if a spell like that might be acceptable for her trial? No, she doubted it. If the dragon awoke to speak to her personally, surely it wanted something far more profound.

If only I’d brought the rest of my magic research notebooks.

Thinking of this made her heart sink. She wondered what was going on back home. Had her room already been ransacked? Were the elders, or worse, the high council, currently flipping through every page she had ever written while her father stood over them with that terrible, disappointed look in his eyes?

She could almost hear what he might mutter under his breath, shaking his head. “Tressa, what have you done?”

She imagined strangers picking through her notebooks, judging her based on her thoughts, her mistakes, her half-formed theories and failed diagrams. The things she would have to answer for once they arrested her.

Would her mother be blamed? Tressa had been born in the castle under the Mad King’s rule, after all, and she could imagine the whispers and rumors that would spread. That maybe her mother was never right after what she’d been through, and Tressa was proof of it.

Her grip tightened around her quill.

“Damn it, Aldmern. Why couldn’t you just listen to me?” she whispered. How he had betrayed her trust… that hurt more than anything.

She shook off those thoughts and steadied her hand, adding another thin vein to the Torrent Bee’s wing.

She couldn’t just run away, she knew that. One way or another, she would eventually have to return for the trials. But maybe, once she did so, if she could give the dragon what it wanted and pass her trial, people would realize she wasn’t just a reckless girl toying with dangerous runes with no regard for what it might cause. And just maybe, the fact that she had broken their laws might be forgiven.

Tressa was brought back to the present as she heard the hum of a bee. She closed her sketchbook and flipped around to straddle the massive branch and pressed herself low. The sound of the wings was different. A young worker, or maybe one that left the hive too early?

But no, it was a different species altogether. She stared. A Blazing Bee? But she thought that species wasn’t supposed to be anywhere around here. Could she be mistaken and it was something else? No, the red stripes along its abdomen were unmistakable.

She had hit a dead end for the time being on learning of Torrent Bees, so maybe she should observe different species for a while instead?

Using a burst of Levant, she propelled herself to another branch, landing carefully to avoid her injured leg, and then she used Levant again to jump to another. She held her sketchbook tight to her chest as she trailed the bee from an inconspicuous distance, jumping from branch to branch, and just as she had hoped, it eventually led her back to its hive, and she knew for sure these were indeed Blazing Bees.

She found another good, sturdy branch to straddle as she took out her sketch book and quill and began scribbling her observations on a fresh, blank page.

The Blazing Bee hive was massive and set into the side of a cliff, not a tree as the Torrent Bees were.

“I can't believe it. Blazing bees aren't normally on this side of the continent. From what I read, they are pretty far to the west. Their main diet is Balngo tree sap. So how is it that they are this far East? Do they have another diet? Different sap? Hmm, would their honey be as spicy as if they were eating that sap?”

Their sap was a delicacy that normally only reached Falidorn through trade with merchants.

They weren't as large as Torrent Bees, and were colored black, red, and yellow with fuzzier bodies. They didn't have beaks but large pincers and a stinger. She knew that their stingers would inject a venom that would make your skin feel like it was on fire, and could last several hours to days. Unlike the Torrent Bees, who would die hours after losing their stinger—and their victims as well not long after that—the Blazing Bees could regrow the stinger, but they often only stung as a last resort. They mainly used their pincers to rip into the skin and inject a different type of venom that could paralyze the victim for hours. The very worst cases, when attacked by many Blazing Bees, could shut down the entirety of the nervous system and cause the victim to die of suffocation as the body would be unable to properly function.

Tressa gave a slight shudder, thinking about how she needed to be careful, as they were also known to be more aggressive without provocation than Torrent Bees and, for that reason, could be just as deadly as their larger cousins.

She vaguely thought of the idea of allowing herself to be stung once—just once!—so she could see and study first-hand what the effects were.

She watched from a distance as they buzzed around, expanding their hive. Thoughts swirled in her mind, each one leaving her as puzzled as the next. She had no clue why they were this far east.

“And why is it on the face of a cliff? Don't they build their hives in caves? Could they just dig them into the ground? Actually, no, probably not due to rainwater.” She whispered to herself as she jotted her thoughts down and began sketching one of the bees.

She paid close attention to detailing the fuzziness of their bodies, and in truth, she wanted to touch it to see the texture.

Noticing a few bees take off, she slowly moved her sketchbook and quill back into her satchel and stood, summoning her staff. She floated into the air and followed them at a steady, safe distance to see where they were going. After nearly an hour of following, she watched as they approached a massive ash tree.

“An ash tree? Why an ash tree?”

They rested on the bark and began to dig. Burrowing into the face of it. A trickle of sap escaped the tree. She watched as the bees rolled in it, collecting it like pollen, coating their bodies and legs.

“Wait, that's an ash maple, not a standard ash tree.” She landed softly in a crouch on an adjacent tree branch, balancing precariously as she reached into her satchel, quickly pulling out her sketchbook and uncapping her metal quill, and began writing down her observations.

“The outer bark of the ash tree is thick. They use their pincers to rip it off and get to the soft inner live bark. Or they must be going after the sapwood after the cambium. They must take the sap of ash maple much like the Balngo tree sap.” She watched as they also took bits of pollen from the flowers the tree produced and sticking it to the sap that already coated their bodies

Suddenly, the bees took off heading back to the hive, and she followed. She watched as other bees swarmed them, carefully collecting the pollen from the sap then flying into the hive with it. Then, the sap-coated bees continued to removed the sap from themselves and used it to strengthen the outer walls of the hive.

“Interesting,” she whispered as she landed in the tree once more and continued to write down her thoughts.

“So the sap is used to bind the hive walls to the rock face. So interesting. Hehehe, this is nowhere in the encyclopedias of monsters back in the library!”

She revisited her thoughts on creating a smoke spell, but soon abandoned them for the time being. She’d have to think of how to experiment with such a thing without causing too much harm to her test subjects first. For now, she was content to observe and sketch as many details as she could, while also considering if the hive itself might be edible. Since it was made with sap and not wood shavings as the Torrent Bee’s.

“It would likely be spicy at the very least, as is their honey,” Tressa mused.

She was cut off from her sketching when a high-pitched screech rang out, forcing her to cover her ears, and she looked up to see the same massive bird that had attacked her two days prior!

It shot down out of the sky and took a massive chunk out of the wall of the hive with its razor-sharp black talons.

“Whoa!”

The bees grew angry, quickly swarming after the bird. But it seemed that the feathers were too thick for the stingers and pincers to get through. She would have to write that down later. Dropping down from her branch, she flew after the bird. She had never seen this species of monster before, so she wasn’t going to miss the chance to study it!

She didn’t fly in bursts this time, going from branch to branch, because the bird would be too fast to keep up with that way. Instead, she came just above the canopy and flew steadily after it.

Then, something flew at her, and she gasped in alarm as it hit her face.

Instinctively she snatched it and realized she was holding the corpse of one of the blazing bees, which was big enough to fit snugly in her palm.

Her heart sang with excitement as this meant she could study the body!

She giggled excitedly as she stroked its furry abdomen. “So soft!” she giggled.

Her leg struck a branch as she’d lost focus on Levant and dipped into the canopy, but she quickly corrected herself and continued her pursuit of the bird as she stuffed the blazing bee corpse into her satchel.

She used the air to propel herself faster after the bird, hoping to somehow catch up, but its massive wings helped it fly at unbelievable speeds, and she realized her mana was draining fast. Running out of mana while this high off the ground would be both embarrassingly stupid and potentially deadly.

“Damn it,” she muttered, knowing she had to give up the chase.

She let herself down to a safe branch, but noticed something black floating down from the sky. It caught on the thin top branches of the tree nearest to her.

Jumping over with a burst of Levant, she pulled it out of the branch to see it was a massive black feather that was the length of her arm and had a bit of weight to it.

“No way! One of its feathers hahaha! It’s huge!” she said, looking it over as she moved it around in her fingers. The feather was a sleek, raven-black color. She ran her fingers up the shaft, watching as it bent and jumped back into place. “It’s silky smooth!” She noticed the shaft was blue while the vane was actually blackish purple, whereas she had first thought it was merely black when she’d compared it to that of a raven's feather.

“So many shades,” she said as the colors of the feather changed as she moved it back and forth with the light hitting it at different angles. She was completely captivated by the feather. It was beautiful! And it made her want even more to hunt that bird down so she could study it closer.

She closed her eyes and focused on her mana. She guessed she was maybe half full at the moment.

Plenty of mana if I’m conservative, she thought as she stared off in the direction the bird had gone and focused on a tracking spell for the feather. It was a simple spell every hunter knew, and she had learned this one from Aldmern years ago. But she quickly cast off any thoughts of him or other elves for the moment.

Casting Levant, she continued zipping through the sky, following the spell, jumping from tree branch to tree branch, feeling she was becoming quite skilled with Levant and traveling in this way.

Finally landing in another tall tree, she found herself on the north edge of the forest where the trees grew larger and thicker than in Falidorn. She’d never imagined coming this far on her own before. It was at least a full day of travel for someone on foot.

Patches of snow still lay about the trees, and she figured if she continued north, she would come across the Thimberman mountains. To her left was a steep, vast drop off a cliff that led to a ravine with a river flowing far below. There she perched on a large branch as she looked around and checked with her tracking spell. It had dimmed its ever-glowing aura, weakening because the feather had been detached from the bird for too long now. No longer of any use to her, she ended the tracking spell.

After a while, she jumped to another branch, hoping that she could spot the bird. Instead, she found another feather, and her heart sang with excitement. She was getting close.

Scanning the surrounding trees, her eyes caught something white, and she glided over to it, only to cover her nose at the stench of bird feces. She continued northeast following the trail of feathers and poop until finally she came across what she was hoping for—

The nest.

It was huge, made of branches, feathers, rocks, and several types of plants. High-pitched screeches rose from within as the chicks cried for their mother—or perhaps simply for food.

She landed in a large but scraggly tree growing out from the cliffside, its thin branches bobbing beneath her weight. From there, she had a clear view into the nest.

Tressa pulled out her book and began sketching as quickly as she could.

There were seven chicks in total, along with a few unhatched eggs tucked near the edge. She suspected those eggs had failed to develop, which meant the others had likely hatched recently. If that was true, then the mother must have laid them during winter and sheltered them through the coldest days as spring slowly crept closer.

“Maybe they mated midwinter? I have no idea how long it takes for the eggs to hatch. Hmmm.”

She chewed on her metal quill momentarily after finishing the rough sketches, then quickly scribbled some notes on deeper descriptions of the chicks so she could fill out the details later.

A loud screech hit her ears as she covered them and looked up to see the mother circling overhead, the broken bits of hive in her talons. The chicks cried out for her and the food she had brought them. Their beaks open wide and their small wings flapped with joy. The mother swooped down and landed, one talon holding the hive and the other gripping the nest’s edge.

The chicks were about half the size of Tressa herself, and the mother bird was several times larger. Its beak pecked into the piece of the beehive, then it seemed to be spitting the honey into the chicks’ mouths. As Tressa looked closer, she could see the mana flowing around the bird. It was a dark yellow and purple as it mixed and blended together.

"Wow,” she said, getting distracted as it ripped open the hive, extracted more honey, and then fed it to the chicks. “So... I wonder if something about the honey is good for chicks?" she whispered as she was jotting down some more notes on a fresh page labeled “feeding.” She quickly made a very loose sketch of the massive bird holding the honeycomb and tearing into it with its beak.

“If it's feeding them chunks of the hive now, what effects does it give the bird? Fire breathing? Ooh, what if it's like a phoenix and bursts into flames? Maybe spicy breath that melts stuff?” She giggled, thinking of possibilities and some more ridiculous ideas, imagining it spewing molten vomit, causing its prey and victims to burst into flame, or melting like a candle as the molten vomit turns them into meaty, goopy piles. Her lip curled up in disgust as she tried to pull her mind away from those gruesome images.

When she was done sketching, she checked back over her notes.

Giant bird (name tbd)

Mother feeds chicks honey and pieces of Blazing Bee hive, possibly larvae as well. Appears to mix it with saliva before feeding, perhaps to soften it or reduce the harshness. I suspect the hive contains rich nutrients or magical properties to help the chicks’ grow. Mother has not eaten any herself. Male not seen. Nest appears large enough for both parents, though size difference between them is unknown.

Tressa tapped her quill against her chin.

“So where is the male?” she whispered.

Did this species even keep paired mates? Some birds did. Others left the mother to care for the chicks alone, such as the Blue Mimic. But the nest was enormous, easily big enough for two adults to settle in with the seven chicks.

She added another note.

Observe whether second parent returns. Unknown pair-bonding behavior.

Tressa leaned forward and sketched, trying to capture the curve of the mother’s beak and the bright yellow patch beneath her throat.

Then, a shadow passed over her, but she was too focused, and in the back of her mind, she thought it was a cloud. That is, until a screech suddenly tore through the air above her.

She looked up and froze for only a moment as she saw a similar bird, but larger, and it was diving right down towards her! She instantly dropped from her branch to a lower one of the tree, shoving her sketchbook into her satchel mid-fall and trying to fix her grip on her staff.

“Levant!”

Mana began to gather around her, but the bird struck the tree before she could lift away. Branches whipped around her, and leaves exploded into the air as the whole tree lurched beneath her.

She fell, hit her back on an even lower branch, and as the jolt of pain shot across her spine, her staff and quill both slipped from her fingers.

“NO!” she screamed, watching both tumble through the branches and vanish below. Without her staff, she couldn’t cast a spell as complex as Levant. But she had no time to mourn those losses as she scrambled closer to the base of the tree, taking whatever shelter was available to her.

“Okay, okay! I understand! Too close! I’ll leave!” she pleaded with the bird as she clung to the tree.

The bird didn’t seem interested in her apology, however. It slammed into the tree again. Wood cracked, and the entire tree gave a terrible groan as its roots tore free from rocky soil.

“No no no no no!”

For one breathless moment, she hung there with her arms around the trunk, staring down at the nest, the cliff, the furious mama bird and the wide open air below. Then the tree let go, roots ripping free all at once.

She could only cling to the tree and scream, the world spinning chaotically around her as it tumbled down the slope, and she prayed she wouldn’t get crushed in the process. The slope ended in a sudden cliff, and she was then falling past the bird nest, below the other oversized trees of this part of the forest, and down, down to the forest floor below.

Before she could think to jump free, it hit the ground. Pain burst through her body, especially her leg, and for a moment, she could not even breathe.

By the time she managed to suck in one sharp gasp, other smaller branches and loose stones rained down around her. But she wasn’t worried about that; her eyes shot along the canopy, looking for the bird.

Another screech rang out, and she saw it tearing down through the canopy.

“Oh no!" She tried to pull her leg free, but it was stuck.

Without thinking, she thrust her hand up and yelled, “Lumas!” throwing as much mana as she could into the spell.

A scatter of brilliant orbs of light emerged from her palm, flying chaotically upward without her staff as a catalyst to control them. They exploded in the bird’s face, not injuring it, but blinding it. She hoped that would be enough to make it retreat from its attack, and while focusing on channeling her spell, she continued trying to yank her leg free.

But she couldn’t keep it up, the lights were fading, and in the end it seemed to only convince the bird more that she was a threat worth dealing with.

Tressa screamed, trying one last time to pull her leg free as the bird’s vision cleared and it dove down on her. “NO DON’T!” she yelled.

Then—another voice. Spoken in the common tongue.

“Get away from her!” the raspy female voice shouted.

A pink blur of motion jumped over Tressa, some kind of long steel weapon—a halberd?—spinning over her head, a thick, pink tail trailing behind.

The attack didn’t quite connect, but it caused the bird to back off as the woman landed, poising her halberd defensively. And she was unlike any woman Tressa had ever seen!

Pink scales and semi-webbed, clawed feet. A mane of darker pink spikes and feathers around the back of her head and neck. She wore leather armor over her chest, her bare stomach showed whiter flesh without the same thick scales, and lower she wore a sectioned skirt of thick strips of leather and hole cut for the thick base of her spiked tail to go through.

She wasn’t a troll, an orc, or… or anything Tressa had ever seen or even heard of. What species had pink scales like that?

But whatever she was, her attack wasn’t enough to stop the determined bird, only back it off, and as it swooped back towards her, she lowered her stance defensively, halberd out in front of her.

Her weapon, however, wouldn’t be needed as another much larger figure jumped out of the thickets, raising a wooden club over its head and shouting, also in the common tongue. “Back! BEAST!”

The club stuck true, the collision knocking the bird into the forest floor along with a spray of feathers. The giant, fuzzy man began to chase after it for a follow-up attack, but it seemed the bird was finally convinced that this wasn’t worth it as it flapped its wings and carried itself to safety, soon disappearing through the canopy above.

“Hmmmph,” the massive man grunted, placing his club over his shoulder. A thin tail whipped behind him, and as he turned, Tressa noticed he had horns and a snout that looked like a… like a cow…?

A minotaur! She had only heard of those before!

The lizard woman eased her posture, putting the butt of her halberd to the ground and turning to look over her shoulder at Tressa. One yellow and black eye watched her as a thin tongue shot in and out of scaly lips like a snake.

“What is it?” the minotaur asked. “An elf?”

“Of course, stupid. We are near their territory, after all,” the lizard woman responded, though Tressa didn’t understand all of her words, as she was not fluent in the common tongue.

The lizard woman then turned fully and walked over to Tressa, pointing down and saying things Tressa couldn’t understand at all, as complex sentences totally flew over her head.

The minotaur let out an annoyed huff, and as for the lizard woman, Tressa couldn’t guess what she was thinking as her strange reptilian face betrayed no emotion.

They were both huge. Bigger than Tressa or any elf. The minotaur, of course, was the far larger of the two, but as they looked down at her, Tressa remembered stories of bandits and how distant nations would take humans, elves, and other sentient species to sell them as slaves. Or worse…

Lying there helplessly with her leg pinned under the tree, she wondered if she had just found herself in even greater trouble.
