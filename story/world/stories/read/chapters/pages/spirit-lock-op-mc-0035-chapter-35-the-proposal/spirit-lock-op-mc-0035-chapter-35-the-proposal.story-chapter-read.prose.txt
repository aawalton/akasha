“Join our team,” Tiasha said. “No… don’t join our team. I’ll get you an audience with Sairan Ikol. He’s a legend who runs the Golden Belt’s universal team.”

“What?” Aysha cried.

Tiasha shot her a silencing glare and then turned back. “Hayden, I'm serious. If Sairan saw what you just did… He'd let you into the Blind Monks—guaranteed.”

Dead silence.

No one could believe what they had just heard.

It was the defining moment of an absurd event, the moment that turned a fevered dream into cold, hard reality. Hayden Reskco wasn’t bluffing when he started this scheme. He was a hidden genius, perhaps one in a billion. No, he might have been a once-in-a-millennium genius—universe-wide. That's the way Tiasha presented it.

Red recording lights danced among the crowd like fireflies. This would doubtlessly be a historical moment—and everyone wanted to capture it—on video.

Hayden answered smoothly. “While I am humbled by your offer, I’ll have to decline.”

The celebratory cheers of spectators rang true, only to abruptly grind to a halt when people processed his words. Everyone was confused, but none was as perplexed as Tiasha, who was staring at him in a state of surreal suspension.

-

Did he just hear what I offered? Tiasha wondered. She couldn’t believe that he turned down her offer. Then, it hit her. Oh… this kid’s poor… He’s probably never heard of the Blind Monks.

“Allow me to clarify,” Tiasha said. “The Blind Monks are a multi-galactic all-star team of blindfold boxers. If you were to get onto that team, you’d have some of the best combat trainers in the universe. You’d also be offered a salary approaching a billion credits, and luxury travel and food accommodations as you travel the universe. As for school, you’ll naturally have the best teachers. This is an absurd opportunity—but it’s worthy of what you just did. That suppression technique…  was groundbreaking.”

-

Groundbreaking? Hayden thought. Just how far behind is this universe?

He was vexed to hear that his neutered suppression technique was “groundbreaking.” Professional, absolutely. Top-tier doubtlessly. He needed to keep people interested in him—make them see him as more than just a student without raising too much attention—so he showed off.

He expected a reaction—but this? This was far beyond expectation.

It wasn’t as if he did anything special. He used the same strategy Aysha did. He created a crude fist and bit her with his real fist after she dodged. The only difference was that he suppressed the cora in his real fist to prevent her from seeing it. After all, throwing random blasts of cora wouldn’t even make a professional flinch, right? He certainly hoped not! That’s why he hid his real head during the first match, and his fist in the second, but apparently, basic techniques were revolutionary.

It couldn’t be clearer that blindfold boxing was infantile compared to Elysian’s shadow dancing.

-

Tiasha saw Hayden’s gob-smacked expression and grinned. Yes! she thought. I got him! She was so convinced that she said, “Come on. Let’s go talk more in my office.”

Hayden put up his hand. “As I already said, I am truly humbled, but I’m uninterested at this time. My life is in delving rifts; that’s why I’m on this planet. Now if you’ll excuse me, I need to go. I’ll be back tomorrow.”

He bowed his head, took his sign and pay pad, and pushed through the riotous crowd.

“Did he really hide his fist?” Aysha asked as he left.

“Hide his fist?” Tiasha laughed. “That man just hid the cora in his entire head. If he taught us that, the Monks would sweep the Universal Fold.” She laughed breathlessly, adding, “I need to contact Sairan… immediately,” under her breath.

Mayle pushed herself into Hayden’s room and crashed onto his couch, cupping her face—forearms on her knees.

“You needn’t be dramatic every time you bring me bad news,” Hayden said.

“Okay…” Mayle took a deep breath. “There’s an auction at the Nebula Auction House in two days. Loricks convinced the owner to hold it and let you participate. That said, I can’t take you without breaching my contract. But there is a group that’s willing to take you…”

“If it’s the blindfold boxing team—no,” Hayden said.

“Why not?” Mayle snapped. “Why are you so opposed to everything?”

“Because it’s unnecessary. I already have someone. Just send me the details.”

Hayden walked to the door.

“Hey, where are you going?” she asked.

Hayden sent her a sidelong glance. “To talk to that someone.”

The person he was referring to was Jora Hedra, the antagonist who coerced the old Hayden to drop out of Junolyx. Jora's steps were unsteady as he walked into the back alley Hayden called him to. He wouldn’t even look Hayden in the eye. “What do you want?” he asked.

His body language conveyed his desire to be forceful, but his voice came out in a bitter whisper.

Hayden stared at him apathetically. “I hear your family can get skybox access to the Nebula Auction House.”

Jora’s eyes shot open. “No. No way.”

Hayden lifted a piece of paper and handed it to him. Jora’s throat went dry when he unfolded it, hopeful it was the blackmail note—but it wasn’t. It was just a string of numbers.

“What’s this?” he asked.

“It’s the address to the blackmail videos. I’m not entirely sure how it works, but my contact said it’s on blockchain, and they assured me it's undeletable. They also assured me that a single message with this strange string of characters would allow people to see, copy, and share the video. I informed them that I’d call them if you said no.”

Jora gritted his teeth. “I’ll see what I can do.”

“Good puppet.” Hayden left the alley and returned home.

The next morning, he dressed and went on his run before setting up his table again. No one thought anyone would fight him after he beat the blindfold boxing team, but he had raised the price to ten thousand credits, and changed the rules to allow third years, and illusions—if requested.

One third-year student approached him. “Yo. I’ll fight ya, but I’m not doin’ illusions. Even that teacher looked messed up about… whatever you did. It’s unfair.”

“The sign clearly states that you can use illusions, if requested,” Hayden replied. “I won’t use them otherwise.”

“Yeah, but how do I know you won’t?”

“No offense, but I could beat you if I were actually blind.”

The early morning mob’s oohs shamed the man into action. He clicked his tongue. “Whatever. It’s only 10K,” he said, but Hayden doubted he meant it.

10,000 credits was 10,000 credits—and he lost it. Spectacularly. He didn’t even get close!

Similar scenes played out in rapid succession. Soon, news of Hayden “bullying” third-years started another rush. There weren’t many competitors, but he only needed twenty-five matches to make his goal—and he had nearly completed it by noon.

The closer he got to his goal, the larger the crowd became. A full group of women was out front in white t-shirts, with the words “Hugs to Millions!” painted on them. Gluttony flew around them as Mani Westia, a well-known drone operator, captured the event from above. It was festive—then he got to the last battle.

His sign read, “Betting pool size: 994,000 credits,” and people were up in arms about it.

“Come on, Hayden,” Emmi pleaded. “I know you have principles, but you need to drop it to six thousand for the last match. If you don’t land on one million exactly, my OCD will haunt me in my dreams.”

Hayden thought about it and nodded. “Sure.” His fangirls went wild, hugging each other as they jumped up and down. It was amusing.

As he was about to say, “Who wants to be the last fight?” Kayline approached him.

“Can I speak to you for a moment?” she asked.

“I’m currently busy becoming a legend, but if you wait five minutes, I’ll be happy to indulge you.”

Kayline pouted, but she complied and waited.

A man pushed his way to the front.  “My guy! You gotta pick me. Trust me—I’ll vindicate everyone with one...” He whirled into an exaggerated kung fu stance. “Punch.”

The onlookers were hyped by his actions, so Hayden shrugged and presented the pad. The man paid, and the recording lines blinked to life as they squared up.
