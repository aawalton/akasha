"...Interesting, you know?" A raspy voice could be heard. "And the cool part is that the tide is—"

"Harry!" Someone called to him, sitting on the edge of the bed. It was Madeline. "Damn, how are you?"

Remembering when he fought Dracno, Harry found himself in bed again. This time, his head wasn't resting on a cozy pillow, but on a pile of rough leaves; he was also on a bed hastily made of twigs and wood. For some reason, however, his heart didn't feel like it was going to explode anymore. Besides, it was even easier to breathe.

"...I don't think fainting like this is good for my health." He said with a slight laugh. "Seriously, this is what, the fourth time?"

At least I didn't lose a hand... He gestured with his fingers, relieved. He then touched his chest and felt his heart slow and steady. His expression changed as he remembered what had happened.

"How do you do?" Goldman said, the source of the gruff voice. "Tell me if you feel this here."

And he proceeded to scratch the man's left arm. Harry let out an Ouch and pulled his hand back.

"That's wonderful, he's alright!"

"I don't think he's alright, doctor," Brun said, narrowing her eyes and placing herself between the two. "Don't mind him; we still don't know if you're alright."

Ah, what a relief... The Locksmith smirked.

"Well, if there were to be any after-effects, we would have seen them by now," Madeline said, taking a step back. "He was without oxygen to the brain for a while, wasn't he? That should have some effect, I imagine..."

And instead of responding with her usual smugness, Brun looked directly at the young woman. "Good point, but that's not all that would allow for a sequela. In a normal situation, its automatic response and memories would certainly be a good indicator of its health. Not that they aren't now, of course, but the extent of Emblem exhaustion is still largely unknown. Each Emblem acts differently, so it would be good to wait a little longer."

"But that wouldn't matter, as long as he didn't die. Right?" Madeline stood up. "If the loop resets the physical conditions, then we have nothing to worry about."

"I wouldn't go that far, Madeline..." Brun continued, checking a blank piece of paper. "I think you underestimate the impact that brain damage can have from one loop to another. You'd return to 'normal', certainly, but what is normal for you now? Your consciousness and your memories have already been on the other side. Often there's no way to come back from that. Scary, if you ask me."

Harry remembered the state he left Dracno in. He was already a man with difficulties, blind because of his Emblem and with an unstable mind. He felt guilty for the further damage he had caused to him. But again, what could they have done, killed him? And this time, the young thief felt he had made the wrong choice.

"...Very cool, doc." Manes said from a corner. "But can you continue what you were talking about? With the Metas and shit?"

"You are interested in this?" Madeline asked, incredulous. "You. Of all people."

"It's the first interesting thing we've heard all throughout this climb. I almost slept while listening." Manes replied.

"Oh, now it makes sense." The young woman rolled her eyes.

"What happened for me to get this well?" Harry asked. "I was probably a goner, right? So, what? Your faces are telling me something I'm not keen on figuring out on my own, actually..."

"...Brun put Metas inside your stomach." Goldman replied with an apologetic smile.

"Goldman!" Brun turned to look at him, irritated. "I mean, he used the wrong words—"

"What!?" Harry suddenly got up to the group's surprise. "A-And you were all okay with it? What's it going to do with me? Shit, I—"

"You were going to die, Harry!" Madeline said as she came closer. "Each second that passed made your heart beat faster. It eventually stopped working altogether and then... It was all we had, trust me."

"It is not you that I don't trust." It's her. He added inwardly, but soon realize it was a lie; he trusted Brun. "It's... the Metas. We don't even know what they do exactly!"

"Listen here, Locksmith," Brun came closer. "Emblem Exhaustion is basically a death penalty. The amount of people I've seen resuming their lives as normal after it are... scarce. Goldman told us what happened in the last floor for this reaction of your body, but it's still weird. Although you pushed yourself, Emblem Exhaustion is for extreme cases."

"Teleporting a mountain isn't an extreme case!?" Harry replied.

"You did only the tip of it. Goldman should've been the one to feel the effects, but apparently his body can take a lot more than yours."

After hearing this, harry wasn't broken by the evaluation of his poorly maintained body since he was being compared to, well... the Goldman. What struck his perplexity was his fragility. He never felt like that outside the tower, even after hours of evading officers in the wee hours of the night. Was the tower doing something to him?

"...And the after effects." The Locksmith only heard the last part of Brun's talking.

"...What side effects?" He asked, worried.

"Um, nothing. Just..." She looked around with an sheepish smile. "I suppose it'll be different for the individual, based solely on Meta's adaptation skills. The point was to get a tiny piece that rests on the logs of the trees and put them in a warm space where they can, well... evolve? It'd patch up the holes in your organs and slowly get you up and running. I'd use the ones in the ocean but it wasn't practical... I suppose they will be gone in your... in the next time you go to the bathroom."

"Fuckin' hell..." Harry sighed, scratching the back of his head. "At least I'm okay, right? My heart's not a bomb anymore?"

Bombarding him with questions he could answer, Brun felt relief. With a now soothing voice, she replied. "You are, yes. It was an intrusive operation and quite risky, but it was the only one I had. The piece was joined together with other elements so as not to damage your insides too much. Even a tiny piece of them can change depending on the surroundings and give birth to a whole new life form. It is basically mitosis, but on a whole different level. I think they converge and split constantly, losing a piece of themselves to give life. It's quite poetical, if you ask me."

It's disturbing, that's what it is. Harry added inwardly. "So I have a parasite inside me?"

"Not quite." She replied. "A larger piece would surely have a stronger effect on you, developing alongside your body, but with the brew you drank, it's not even perceptible. It'll fix your damages and cure your internal bleeding, but it won't have time to develop inside your body before it is thrown out. A larger one, however... I don't even want to think what could happen if one were to ingest a larger piece of a Meta..."

"Let's go out now that Harry's better. I'm getting bored."

"...You're a real bummer, you know that?" Madeline said.

"Actually, that's a good idea." Harry replied, a hint of a smile in his face. "Where are Daniel and Vela? We need to find the hatch to the next floor and... How much time do we have left, Brun?"

"Um... We have about two hours."

"...Two hours?!" Harry asked. "What the... For how long have I been out."

"Two to three hours at maximum..." Madeline answered, equally confused to why they had so little time.

"Look, I'm not a mathematician, but I don't think twenty four hours have passed." He said.

"I'm not sure, either..." Brun replied, staring at the watch. "I've felt this since the last floor; it was a couple hours short of the usual twenty four. It wasn't a huge deal, so that's why I didn't mention, but now its getting ridiculous. I assume the 4th floor is in a 12 hour loop."

"That doesn't make sense..." Madeline muttered.

Maybe she was miscalculating, but Harry wasn't sure. As he thought about it, the loop on the last floor did seem faster; he only realized it now after Brun mentioned the decrease. But why? He asked himself. Are there people who are stuck in like a five second loop on the 8th floor, unable to go back? An eternal torture? He imagined the worst case scenario, not yet sure what they'd encounter on the upper floors.

"...Either way," Harry began. "We have to move on, then. The next floor will definitely hold more answers, correct, you guys? We just have to keep going."

As everyone looked around and tapped their feet nervously, Manes came out of the shadows and said. "You need to stop trying to act like Goldman. His optimism is contaminating because he has strength and presence to back it up. You, on the other hand, have a bad-shaved beard and wears a bard's stolen coat."

And just like that, Harry's smile was ripped off of his face.

"...Fuck you."

With his hands behind his back, a tall, thin man watched over the island. He appreciated the distant clouds that may as well be ornamental as he gazed upon the waves, silent but deadly. In them, sometimes a shadow the size of a ship could be seen, guaranteeing the safety of an empty sea.

"Shouldn't we go looking for them?" Estela asked, anxious. "Maybe I could talk to Harry and not—"

"Why would you ever need such closure?" Alexander asked, now darting his eyes through the woods, searching. "You know they'll die, right? If Gale and Cal can't take care of the job — which, to be honest, would make sense — then the island's demise will do it."

"Y-Yeah, of course." She replied, still shaking slightly. "It's just... I don't like keeping secrets, you know? It'd make sleep easier at night knowing that I told them the truth."

"...That doesn't make sense." He said. "You lied and betrayed them. Why would telling them the truth or whatever reasons you had for such betrayal change anything?"

"I'm not like you, boss." I'm not a fucking weirdo. She almost added.

"Indeed, you are not." The man replied, frowning. "You have changed."

"When will we leave?" Prism replied succinctly, trying to change the subject. "I don't like all of this... waiting."

"Then why didn't you went with them?" He asked, turning around to face her. "They could've used your help."

Prism mumbled an answer and evaded the man's gaze.

"We have time." Alexander answered her. "I want to show them something when they get here."

Turning, he could now glimpse a few strangers walking through the woods.
