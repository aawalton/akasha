Samantha was fast, but Eupher had been thrown like a baseball from a professional pitcher. If it weren’t for the fact that the lich was already dead, Asher had a feeling the g-forces alone would have taken care of the lich for them, or at least knocked him unconscious. But seeing as he wasn’t reliant on the flow of blood through his body for survival, Asher knew it was up to them to finish the job.

The one good thing was that while Eupher had a head start on them, he didn’t have any further force propelling him forward. Eventually, air resistance and gravity caught up with him, and Asher pointed excitedly at the descending lich as they quickly began to catch up.

“There he is!” Asher called out, watching as the lich managed to catch himself with a spell at the last second before hitting the ground, instead landing lightly on his feet. The ocean was barely a quarter of a mile away, and already the grasses and thin trees were being replaced with dunes of sand. “Bring me in close!”

“I’ll provide support from up here,” Samantha said, flapping her wings as she shot over toward the lich. “Once you’re do—”

The lich turned to face them, the golden flames in his eye sockets flaring brightly, and Samantha’s entire body went limp. Her wings stopped beating and her eyes rolled back into her head as the two of them immediately began plummeting out of the sky. It took Asher a moment to realize what had happened, and he cursed as he clung to her.

Jameston is in my rift right now, which is a separate dimension. His mental defenses aren’t in play to help with Eupher!

At the moment, Samantha was relying on nothing more than a single artifact to keep her mind protected. And while that might be enough to keep the lich from turning her into a permanent vegetable or one of his puppets, just as Asher had feared, it wasn’t enough to stop such a powerful wizard from knocking her unconscious. The two of them spiraled toward the ground as her wings fluttered limply in the wind behind them, and Asher grimaced as he realized he was going to be on his own.

I could risk trying to bring Jameston and everyone else out, but I bet the lich is faster on the draw than he is, and I can only bring them out one at a time. I had to pull them into my rift to protect them from Amanda’s attack and take them with us, but now that choice is coming back to haunt me.

If Asher even had just a couple of minutes to spare, he would have turned and hurled a marked dagger away from the lich as hard as he could, regrouping and bringing everyone out of his rift to help with the final attack. But he didn’t have a couple of minutes. Eupher was within sprinting distance of the ocean, and while Asher didn’t know how long it would take him to do whatever he was planning to do, he wasn’t about to risk millions of innocent lives for a little added help.

Mako and Cora hadn’t even taken the mind-protection artifacts from Groln, which meant the moment he pulled either of them out of his rift they might very well be mind-fried right in front of him. Tightening his grip on Samantha’s shoulders as the ground rose up to meet them, Asher couldn’t help but laugh when he realized yet again, regardless of how well he tried to prepare, it had come down to him versus the lich in a one-on-one battle.

Still, he had a fairly good track record for such fights.

Yanking Samantha into his rift, he twisted around in mid air and hurled a marked dagger straight for Eupher’s face. For his part, the lich didn’t seem all that surprised that Asher hadn’t been knocked unconscious like his friend. Without even moving a muscle, the lich held up his staff, and the thrown dagger froze in place a good sixty feet away from him. It wasn’t nearly as close as he would have liked, but Asher’s alternative was a hard and fast introduction to the ground. Rather than growing far more familiar with sand than he’d ever imagined, he triggered Recall, erasing his momentum while he teleported into position beside his dagger.

Yet the moment he appeared, he gasped as the very same dagger he’d marked jerked forward and buried itself up to the hilt in his back.

Asher slipped into the astral not a moment too soon, dropping the last few feet to the ground and watching as his dagger began spinning violently in midair like a deranged and sharpened top. Had he not shifted between realms, his insides would have been eviscerated in seconds.

Some sort of telekinesis power… he thought, frowning even as he felt the wound in his back slowly began to seal itself shut. It had been a miracle that the lich had missed severing his spinal cord, but based on the fact that he was struggling to feel his legs and barely able to stand, Eupher hadn’t missed completely.

“The Reaper,” Eupher called out, his dry voice chuckling as the golden flames in his sockets flared again and the dagger stopped spinning. The distant sounds of crashing waves and crying gulls were audible from behind the lich, but Eupher paid them no mind. “I thought I still had a few weeks before I’d get the pleasure of dealing with you, but here you are. I’m not sure if I should be angry that you waited until now to come for me, or proud that you thought such a powerful force was necessary to take my life.”

Asher gritted his teeth, happy to let the lich drone on as his nerves knitted themselves back together. He didn’t know why Eupher wasn’t just making a mad dash for the ocean, but he’d take whatever time he could get. The lich didn’t seem bothered by the fact that Asher continued to hide within the astral, as he simply continued talking to empty air.

“I know you are there, for the record. In fact, you would not even be the first Spatial element holder I’ve killed. He served as an entertaining minion for a few decades before dying of old age. I animated his corpse and kept him around of course, but people always change after death. Their minds decay, their emotions turn harsh and chaotic. I imagine it has something to do with the lack of a soul, though I’ll be the first to admit I was never one to research the concept of souls. No, I was far more interested in what one could do with their mind. A pity I cannot show you what I mean. That immunity of yours is truly a sight to behold. I normally deal with heroes and other such fools by forcing them to fight their own nightmares within their minds as their bodies fail around them. Alas, I will have to resort to other methods to deal with you.”

While Eupher continued talking, Asher’s brow furrowed as he began to notice something strange. He didn’t know if it was because of Secret Seeker or some part of his subconscious picking up on tells that he didn’t recognize, but something about the lich seemed… off. As soon as his dagger wound finished healing, Asher sprinted forward, running as far as he could through the astral before being forced to pop back into the material. The moment he did so, he hurled a new dagger forward at Eupher’s face, compressing space to give the lich less time to react. While his aim was true and the dagger didn’t freeze and catch in the air like the first one…

The dagger soared straight through Eupher’s face, rolling on the sand behind the lich before coming to a stop. While a small hole had been carved through the lich’s face, from the way the entire wound flickered, it was obvious that the Eupher standing before him was some form of illusion.

Asher’s stomach dropped at the discovery that he’d been played, and he ignored the laughter coming from the fake lich as he looked past Eupher over toward the coastline. While he couldn’t make out anything larger than the occasional crab, he felt Secret Seeker direct his eyes downward, toward what were clearly soft footprints running through the sand. They’d known that Eupher possessed the Mind and Death elements, but his third one had been a mystery. Yet based on the illusion currently laughing at him and the fact that the real Eupher was now invisible, he felt safe in guessing it was Light.

A guess he felt really confident in making when the fake Eupher suddenly transformed into a blinding humanoid made of light that made Amanda’s false sun look like a dim lightbulb in comparison.

Asher cursed and stumbled backwards, pain stabbing through his eyes as he was forced to not only clench them shut but block them with his arm as well. He at least had the forethought to slip into the astral, dodging his still-floating dagger by the sounds of things as something whistled through the air where he was currently standing. Despite the fact that the colors of the material realm grew weaker within the astral, light itself had no such problem. He tried to crack his eyes open and immediately suffered for it, tears streaming down his face as he was blinded anew.

Eupher doesn’t intend to fight me at all, he’s going straight for the ocean! And I can’t stop him if I can’t see!

The fact that Eupher’s win condition meant he could completely ignore him put Asher in a tough position. He had to track down and kill an enemy he couldn’t even see, all the while dodging one of his own daggers that seemed hell bent on cutting him to ribbons. The blinding, searing light was the perfect deterrent for most warriors, and it would have been perfect for Asher as well.

If not for one tiny, experimental potion he’d purchased from Polly a few months back in Dormaul.

Ignoring the chunk of energy opening his Personal Rift within the astral realm cost him, Asher grabbed his potion of Bright Sight that had miraculously survived Watcher’s rampage in the Deadlands and downed the thing in a single gulp. The potion tasted like sunlight and morning dew, which didn’t make any sense to him, but that didn’t matter. Far more important than the taste was the fact that the moment the liquid passed his lips, the blinding light before him dimmed to a normal level. Asher could somehow still tell that staring at Eupher’s illusion was like staring directly into a floodlamp, but the light simply didn’t bother him any more. He could see through it just as easily as he could see through darkness from his evolved resistance, and he grinned as he let his arm fall from his face.

Returning to the material realm, Asher’s arm shot out through a compressed tunnel of space, and he grabbed the hilt of his dagger, yanking it back into his Personal Rift before it could try to stab at him again. The fires burning in the fake Eupher’s eyes flared in confusion when Asher stared right through the blinding light radiating from its body, trusting in Secret Seeker to help him pick out the sandy footprints as he sprinted forward, using Distortion to compress space and catch up with the lich.
