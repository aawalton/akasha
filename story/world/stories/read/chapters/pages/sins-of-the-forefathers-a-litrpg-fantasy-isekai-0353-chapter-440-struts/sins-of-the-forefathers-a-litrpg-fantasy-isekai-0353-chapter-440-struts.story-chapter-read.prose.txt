“What?” I asked, pure confusion in my voice. “What exactly do you mean, he won’t see me?”

The pair of Elven guards, standing before the entrance to their own little portion of the refugee encampment, exchanged looks at my tone.

Not long after I had left Olag to his own devices, within the makeshift concentration camp of suspected mind slaves, I’d asked Sylvia to direct me to our next destination. There were…a lot of people I needed to catch up with, after my coma had left me bedridden for the last week. I’d already seen to most of them at the meeting, and done more on the walk through the encampment. Sylvia hadn’t known exactly where Maria was, but she had known that the former spy and current head of my Stewards had taken control over the forces of the expedition in my absence. Our fellow former Nocturne was incredibly busy running around the camp, putting out fires, and making sure people were being properly cared for in the aftermath of the battle for Rhoscara. That was why she hadn’t been present for the meeting with Bleddyn earlier. The best I could do was drop a word with the few members of the expedition I could find throughout the camps.

Hopefully, word would spread that I was up and about, in a manner of speaking, and she would come and find me.

For now, though, I had arrived at our final destination before I had to hunker down and start brainstorming what exactly I was going to do to fix up my body.

Only…

I was being turned away.

Just like the Dwarves who had been sequestered away because they were suspected of being mind-controlled, the Elves had separated themselves from the bulk of the camp as well. I had been surprised and relieved to discover that before Sylvia and her co-conspirators had come to rescue me, they had advised the large Elven contingent of the expedition to depart and seek shelter with the Unchained in the Long Road tunnels beneath Rhoscara. Because the now deceased Marcel had had no interest in rescuing either myself or Alveron, they had been all too happy to do so. Thus, the Elves had been able to avoid any of the slaughter that had come in the wake of Daffyd’s assault on the expedition.

I had been told that, after I had been maimed and forced into a coma, Alveron had separated from the group and made a beeline for the safety of his own people.

In the week following, he hadn’t been seen since. And the Elves themselves were being cagey about what this meant. More than usual, they were closing in on themselves and refusing to engage with everyone else within the encampment.

That…wasn’t ideal, but I typically had more luck than most in communicating with them. I had thought I might be able to broach the top of just what was going on, and possibly even get close enough to Alveron to have an actual conversation. To the best of my knowledge, Anguis had visited the elderly Elf and taunted him with the same knowledge he had done to me.

There was a lot I wanted to talk to the last Paragon about.

Only…

The guards had just told me that Alveron didn’t want to speak to me.

I was being turned away. Upon seeing me, one of the guards had left further into the tight mass of Chamel led house-wagons, only to return what felt like moments later and tell me in no uncertain terms…

That I wouldn’t be allowed inside.

“Why?” I asked the guard, utterly baffled.

The green painted wolf mask Elf shrugged his shoulders at me, staying silent. Despite that, I could see just how uncomfortable he was, if only from his body language. I’m sure I would have seen the same thing on his face if it wasn’t covered.

Sylvia and I exchanged a confused look at that. I was gratified to see that my lover was just as perplexed as I was.

I turned back to the guard. “Can I at least get a reason?”

The guard took a deep breath and spoke in the halting, broken Herztalian that I had come to expect from most Elves. “Elder, seclusion. No see any. No see you. Much sorry, Precursor.”

My brow furrowed in thought. Alveron was in seclusion? Why? This was an incredibly inopportune time for the elderly Elf to decide to isolate himself. We had only just barely survived fleeing our loss at Rhoscara. I…had kind of been hoping to get his thoughts on our next move. I hadn’t quite informed Bleddyn about my intention to steer us north toward the Dwarven Mountain Holds, where apparently Azarus was venturing, just ahead of us down here. I had been intending to do so after we had reached the Unchained’s main base underneath Addersfield. Perhaps a few days after, when people had a few days to themselves to destress and ponder our next moves.

I had wanted to get Alveron’s advice on this, perhaps ask if he had ever heard of or interacted with Nehushtan…

But if he wasn’t seeing anyone, then he wasn’t seeing anyone.

“I’ll…be back later, then,” I told the guard slowly. “Just to see if that changes. Goodbye.”

The guard nodded stoically, but not without a hint of relief in his shoulders.

I shook my head and turned away, fading into the crowd of the encampment.

Just as well, I suppose. This just gave me more time to work on myself.

Quite literally, at that.

………………………………..

I had to say…

It was quite unnerving, staring down at your own comatose, critically injured body.

Now that I had ‘woken up’, so to speak, Sylvia, Renauld, and I had moved my body to Sylvia’s personal tent. There was no point to leaving me in what was the unofficial Healer’s section of the camp, which was apparently where I had been. Instead, ‘I’ would remain in Sylvia’s tent while I quite literally tinkered with my own bones.

This proved to be…quite a challenge.

I contemplated my supine form upon the medical cot, mildly better than the previous one. I couldn’t help but notice that I didn’t look very well, to be honest. I was pale, drawn, and unnaturally still. My true body was naked from the waist up, but my flesh wasn’t exposed to open air. Instead, my entire torso was wrapped in suspiciously clean bandages, covering every inch of it. And my torso itself…

I shuddered just to look at it.

It was very obvious that something was wrong with my neck and my shoulders, just from looking at me. Instead of defined collar-bones, which you didn’t realize just how much they supported the upper torso until they were gone, I instead looked alarmingly soft. The muscles of my upper torso didn’t have anything to latch onto, and the result was that they just kind of…sagged in place.

Frankly, it was a wonder I was still alive.

“You know…” Renauld mused from the left of me. “You really should be dead.”

I turned and gave the Gnoll a dirty look. My personal Healer just smirked at me in response, though I noticed a shadow of his complicated grief still lurking behind his eyes. When I had asked for his presence, Renauld’d had no issue coming to my aid.

But.

“I asked for your help, not your backtalk,” I said sarcastically.

Renauld just shrugged in response, unbothered. “You know it’s true. I mean just look at that,” He waved a hand at my true self’s neck. “It’s kind of gross, like a big soft, uncooked roast. Just…wobbling there.”

I almost shuddered at the mental image. Instead, I just glared at the Gnoll.

Said Gnoll chuckled one last time at my expense before he sobered up. “But it’s true, you know. The only reason you’re still alive is because you’re Awakened. Just think about what this would have been like for someone without a Status. Even if I managed to heal the initial trauma, they wouldn’t have survived long in the aftermath without bones to stabilize the area. Honestly, they might have choked. I can’t imagine the pressure it’s all putting on your trachea.”

“Hmm,” I grunted. “I suppose. I didn’t feel anything before I transferred over to this clone, but I was focused on other things.”

“Your breathing has been pretty regular,” Fade popped over our bond. The Spirit Wolf was on his stomach on his own commandeered cot in the corner of the tent. He lounged upon it like it was an enormous dog bed, keeping watch over my incapacitated form.

I repeated his words dutifully to Renauld, and the Gnoll hummed in response. “That’s good, I suppose,” He said, a note of doubt in his voice nonetheless. “Still, it doesn’t solve the main issue. If you tried to stand up like this, with nothing in your neck to stabilize your upper torso? Boom.” He clapped his hands together dramatically. “Your shoulders would cave in with nothing to support them, your neck would flop about everywhere, and you would either choke, pass out, or tear something else in seconds. Probably all of that at once. You kind of need clavicles to live, Nate.”

“Yeah, no shit,” I muttered under my breath, before sighing. “So, you closed up the wound? I’m not interested in getting a better look at it under the bandages yet, but…”

“Yup,” Renauld popped the word. “At the very least, you’re not bleeding. Besides, I wouldn’t let you take the bandages off anyway. They’re the only thing compressing your upper torso together enough that it’s not sagging more than it is.”

“Alright already, I get the point,” I held up my palms defensively. “But that means we need to do something about this. Like you said, I need clavicles to live…and that means I’m going to have to replace them myself, since they can’t be regrown.”

“Which means you need to forge a replacement,” Sylvia pondered, speaking for the first time in nearly half an hour. She was sitting on one of the chairs that looked suspiciously similar to those I had seen in Bleddyn’s command tent, next to a rack holding both hers and my weapons. “You need to become even more Sculpted than you were previously.”

I grimaced. “Not…how I would have put it, but essentially correct. I’m going to need to forge replacement clavicles and then surgically graft them onto my skeleton at the correct points. It’s going to be hard, though, for a number of reasons.”

“The first of which,” Renauld deadpanned, crossing his arms. “Do you even remember the intricacies of the ritual you and Honoka did, to graft your first prosthetic onto yourself? Second, I’m not Honoka. If you’re asking me to step in for her place, then we’re going to be in for a rough time, Nate. I’m…willing to give it a try, but it’ll be more dangerous. I’m definitely going to need more help than just you if we’re doing this, when it comes to the procedure.”

“Okay, let me address that,” I said patiently, ticking things off on my fingers as I spoke. “First off, yes, I remember the ritual. It was…essentially all self-driven on my case, after Grey helped me create it. Second of all, I just needed medical assistance from Honoka for my arm, and Renauld, I have full confidence that you can do what she did. It’ll be hard, yeah, but you can do it. Finally…there’s no way we’re going to do this on the move.”

“Oh yeah, that would be a death sentence,” Renauld immediately agreed, nodding his head.

“Why?” Sylvia asked quietly, drawing both of our attentions. I looked at Reanuld, and he just motioned to me.

I rolled my eyes at him, but acquiesced. “Well…this isn’t exactly a clean environment, Sylvia,” I said delicately. “For a major invasive surgery like what we’re going to do, we need one to ensure success. Not just physically, because that’s still an issue, Status or no status. You might not realize this, my love, but us flesh beasts are very susceptible to life-threatening infections when it comes to something like this. That might very well kill me itself, never mind the procedure. And secondly, we need it to be Aetherically clean as well. It just really, really helps when it comes to intensive Magical rituals. Less chance of environmental interference mucking things up.”

Sylvia’s mouth ‘ohed’ in realization, and she nodded quietly.

“It’s impossible to set up a clean room on the move like this,” Renauld said frankly. “We’re going to have to wait until we get to Addersfield, and we have a week or two to burn on the purification. Then we can do a major procedure like this.”

“Right, right,” I nodded along easily. “And that’s likely where we’ll find a third set of hands to assist. Bleddyn’s people were saying they have a Preceptor on hand, over there, and most Preceptors are trained Healers. They need to be familiar with Healing even a little bit, in order to regenerate lost limbs. But in the meantime, I need to get to work on creating replacement clavicles. And…it’ll have to be from that.”

I gestured with one hand, and everyone else in the tent followed it…

To find a pile of assorted Mithril jewelry. No raw ore, but when Bleddyn had asked, people had stepped up. Rings, necklaces, bangles, and even a few small weapons like daggers sat on the very valuable pile.

As well as the shattered remains of my last Oninite dagger.

“I’ll need help creating them, of course,” I said, contemplating both metals. “I’m not quite as familiar with the correct shape of a clavicle as I was with an arm. I’ll need to forge them right, then enchant them. That will take…a while.”

I took a deep, deep breath, holding it for a moment…

Before I let it out in a rush.

“But I can do it. And I need to get started now.”
