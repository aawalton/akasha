I drew in a deep and pointless breath, then spat a torrent of fire at the walls, hoping like crazy that I wouldn’t accidentally get a kill and have to be rolled the rest of the way to the heart as I turned into a dragon-blob.

While I had a vast array of useless shit in my pocket dimension, for some unknown reason I had failed to plan for this particular eventuality.  Which is why my business card would only declare me a budding genius.  I’m a humble dragon.

I looked up at the melting stone of the ceiling and had a thought.  Shoes and socks went into my storage space, and I rolled up my trouser legs like I was going paddling at the beach.

“You guys wait here a sec, I’ve had a thought.”

“He’s a fucking—” Granvil cut himself off as he received a level nine glare and also backed away slightly.

“I’ve got this.”

Indecision was not one of my many flaws, so once I figured it was good enough, I shrugged.

“Fuck it.”

The beam of light pierced the ceiling above me, shooting upwards and to one side.  I snapped it off and waited, muscles tense and ready to move.

Nothing.

“Missed it!  No worries!  I’ll get it next time!”  I called back to my companions, who now wore extremely sceptical expressions.  I ignored their quiet mutterings about madness and idiocy, but made a note of the latter.  Granvil wasn’t quick on the uptake, and he was going to have to learn the hard way.

Wiping the scowl off my face, I tried again.  Another magma-lined borehole appeared in the ceiling, at a slightly different angle this time.

“Third time's the charm!”

Bighorn mooed mournfully, as though grieving my sanity.

The third time was indeed the charm.  As soon as the light died, a power washer of river water shot out of the hole, instantly cooling the sides and stopping the molten stone from closing the passage.  The high-pressure blast of frigid subterranean water doused me with chilly mist as I hurried back towards my friends.

The mental image of an underground river suddenly disappearing down the foot-wide hole I’d just burned through the rock and stone that loomed above me filled me with a weird sense of glee.  Startled fish, swimming for their lives, perhaps a surprised turtle suddenly finding itself swimming against the unexpected current.  Realistically, it would most likely be weird, be-tentacled blob-things, pale white and eyeless from having evolved in permanent darkness.
