Across from him, Venomjaw had collapsed to one knee, listing forward in the sand. His breath rasped through the slats of his mask. The hood that once shadowed his face had fallen halfway down, baring strands of sweat-plastered hair stuck to his temple. His eyes darted wild and wide, locked in panic. His remaining hand clutched the ragged stump at his elbow, fingers digging in, trying to hold in the blood even as it pulsed between them.

Venomjaw rocked forward once, like he meant to stand, but his legs buckled under the weight of pain. He dropped again, his breath turning to wheezing gulps that rattled louder than the crowd. Or what was left of it.

The stands above had fallen quiet. Just the kind of hush that didn’t belong in a place like this. It was an awkward, heavy silence.

Oswald lowered his sword. Shit, this is bad. Silence means I killed the fun. Need to think of something fast.

The organizers wouldn’t care that Venomjaw lost. They wouldn’t care that Oswald won. That wasn’t the point. What mattered was the energy, the coin behind it, the mouths in the stands opening to cheer or curse or shout names. It was the show they watched, not the skill.

A fighter could win every round and still vanish between matches if the crowd didn’t care. He stared down at Venomjaw, then slowly turned his blade sideways to let the last drop fall. If I wanna survive this place, I gotta give ’em a reason to keep lookin’.

Oswald pivoted back toward Venomjaw and strode around him, the tip of his blade trailing through the sand. He paused just behind the injured man. Venomjaw flinched.

A few cheers and whistles returned in pulses. Not a wave yet, but building. The quiet was breaking. The audience was watching again.

He looked down at Venomjaw, whose head now hung low, the blood from his stump soaking into the sand like spilled ink.

Venomjaw twitched, trying to shift into a crouch, but his balance broke under him. He slumped sideways and coughed through his mask, blood splattering in front of his knees. His remaining hand reached toward one of the small vials on his belt.

Oswald kicked it away. The glass clinked as it spun into the dust, a pale green liquid leaking from the cracked rim.

“No tricks,” Oswald said.

Cheers trickled back in, scattered claps and hoots rising into something louder. Oswald could feel the current turning now. He glanced up at the crowd, lifted his sword, and spun it once.

Oswald raised the bloodied blade and pointed it at Venomjaw’s back. He took a step closer. “Not so deadly without your poison, huh?”

Soon, a cheer broke loose from the stands. Oswald let his mouth curve into a faint smirk, just enough to sell it. He didn’t grin. Just showed enough edge to let them feel like they were in on the win.

He stepped slightly to the side and lifted his boot, then drove it straight into Venomjaw’s ribs. The sound was dull, almost muted. The man folded with a grunt, his body dropping fully to the ground. The mask muffled whatever sound came out of him.

His stump dragged against the sand as he twisted, smearing red in wide, clumsy strokes across the pale grains. The crowd drank it in. Keep the rhythm, just a little bit more.

Oswald crouched beside him, resting the sword across his knees like it belonged there. Blood from the blade streaked across his trousers, warm and sticky, but he didn’t give it a glance. “You drag all that poison in here, and you don’t even get to use one? What were you thinkin’? You stupid or just slow?”

Oswald stood again, raising his sword high enough for the crowd to see it catch the light. Then he turned slowly to face them, letting the silence curl around the moment. A hush settled, just enough to draw the tension tight.

“What do you think?” He called out.

He pointed the sword down at Venomjaw, who was still dragging himself across the sand, elbow shaking with every inch gained. “You want him dead?”

At first, only a few people answered Oswald's call. But the chants picked up fast, swelling like a fire caught in dry brush. The stands shook with the weight of their bloodlust, no hesitation, no mercy left in them now.

“Yes!”

“Take his head!”

"Kill! Kill! Kill!"

Oswald didn’t look down. He walked forward, and when he reached the side of Venomjaw's broken body, he raised the sword in both hands.

The blade came down in one clean arc. Steel cut through flesh and vertebrae without resistance, the sound of it sharp and final. Blood sprayed upward, droplets catching in the air before falling like rain. The body jerked once beneath him, then stilled.

The crowd lost its mind. They screamed until their voices cracked, fists pounding against railings, feet stomping in a rhythm too wild to follow. The arena rang with approval,

Oswald stood in the center of it all, sword still dripping. His eye flicked downward one more time.

After his fight, Oswald made his way back to the Pit Lounge. Part of him wanted to go back to the fighter's waiting room instead, to see the faces of the two bastards that made fun of him. However, the staff insisted that he could not go back there until his next match.

Just inside the entrance to the pit lounge, a woman in a red-trimmed uniform stepped into Oswald's path. She couldn’t have been much older than him: sharp eyes, clean posture, dark hair braided tight behind her shoulders. Her hands moved quickly: a towel over one arm, a drink in the other.

She offered both. “Nice match.”

Oswald accepted the towel and took the drink without answering right away. The glass sweated in his grip, chilled, probably fruit-laced water, sweet enough to clean the blood from his tongue.

He wiped his face with the towel, then gave a noncommittal nod. “Thanks,” he muttered, stepping past her before she could press further.

They always talked after the first fight. Threw you a nod, maybe a grin, like you were already part of the club. Like you’d made it. Doesn’t matter what they say now. They’ll forget my name the second I drop dead in the sand.

The thought drifted quiet in the back of his mind, but Oswald didn’t slow. He moved deeper into the lounge, passing under low-hanging lanterns that flickered with heatless arcane light.

Rough-cut benches hugged the walls. Armor stands slouched beside them, draped in half-stripped gear and sweat-stained tunics.

A few fighters glanced up as Oswald passed. One held a cloth to his busted nose. Another cradled a bruised arm, his knuckles swollen and raw. Eyes flicked his way, then back down. No one spoke.

His breath moved slow through his chest, deep and steady, like nothing going on in the pit surprised him anymore.
