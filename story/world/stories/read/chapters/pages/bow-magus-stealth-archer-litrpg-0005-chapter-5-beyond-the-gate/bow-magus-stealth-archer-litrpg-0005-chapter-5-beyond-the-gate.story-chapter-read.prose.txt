For a moment, Rex really regretted letting himself get pulled out into the celebrations. That was until Ike started questioning him. The boy knew what his plans had been, and he wanted answers.

“You’re alive?”

“Why do you say it like that? Of course I’m alive!”

“So, you didn’t challenge the Shrine then?”

“No! Wait, I mean. What are you getting at, Ike? Yes, for your information, I did. I challenged Shrine!”

“Hmm,” Ike looked him up and down suspiciously.

“Don’t believe me? Fine, check this out!” Rex huffed and fired an acid arrow into an empty keg, and it melted straight through it.

“Hey! What was that? That arrow just melted straight through the timber!”

Within seconds, the crowd that had been surrounding Lavrian and hanging on his every word had turned to Rex.

“You got a Class too, Rex? Why didn’t you say anything?”

“What kind of level 1 Class can do that with an arrow?” Another man asked. “That ain’t no Veil or Vigor Class I’ve ever heard of.”

“You ain’t never heard nothing, Mal!”

“No need for anybody to yell. It’s a Versatile Class,” Rex said casually.

“Hey, everyone, did I mention how I–”

“Oh, excuse us, Lavrian. But would you mind piping down a little? I ain’t never heard about a Versatile Class, and I kind of want what Rex has to say.”

“But I—”

“Shh,” an old woman hissed at Lavrian. “You’ll get your turn. Go on, Rex. We’re waiting.”

Hehe. Maybe tagging along wasn’t such a bad idea.

“Thanks, but I don’t really know what to say. I don’t know any more about Versatile Classes than any of you. That said, I can cast spells.”

“Cast spells, he says, like it’s just another day.”

“Hear that?” Another yelled, calling over other partygoers. “Rex says he can cast spells!”

“Well, I’ll be. Two spellcasters from our little borough in one day! What are the chances of that?”

“To Rex and Lavrian,” another cheered, raising a mug to the air.

It was a long night, and Rex was feeling a little rough in the morning. But the moment he remembered what was waiting for him, the fatigue seemed to melt away, and he bounced out of bed.

If he were quick, he would also hopefully avoid seeing Lavrian on his way out. The boy couldn’t be so smug now that he had a Class, but he still preferred not having to talk to him.

All of Dorog Targ’s permanent Gates were scattered around the central courtyard of the town. Walls had been built around it, and nobody could pass without first going through the guards.

When Rex arrived, two raggedy, bored out of their brains guards were checking papers as they processed a small number of people passing through the Gates.

“Damn, they’re even more beautiful up close,” Rex muttered as he got closer to the Gates.

As he spoke, one of the guards turned to him with a strange look.

“Oh, I didn’t mean you. I meant the Gate,” Rex pointed.

“Strange kid.” The guard shook his head and went back to work.

The Gates were a marvelous thing—a metallic disk of incredible power, marked by intricate runes few understood. And within the disk was a rippling water-like substance that could transport Classed people between worlds.

He looked at the ominous Gate that had opened when he was a kid. It was further away than the rest, with extra guards nearby. That said, a lot had changed. The world beyond the Gate had many outposts these days, and the monster's grip on the world had greatly weakened. Still, it was considered an outworld as it was not fully tamed yet.

Suddenly, the Gate rippled, and a couple of people walked through. For a moment, he had expected, or rather hoped, it was the elven mage. However, it was just a couple of men escorting an empty cart, no doubt coming for a coldrock shipment.

It was all very procedural. They showed their papers or had their System checked by the guards and moved on. When they passed, Rex stepped up to the guards.

“You’re a miner boy, aren’t ya?” One of the guards said, eyeing his dirty, coal-stained rag-clothing.

“I am—”

Rex stammered, losing his sentence as one of the guards stepped closer. He felt a strange pang of something emanating from the man.

So that’s what it feels like?

He had read in the tomes about how Classed could sense the Shrines' blessing on one another. But feeling the sensation for the first time was different than reading about it. It was as if he felt something warm pulsing from the man. It reverberated with a weak thud, rippling through the air like a heartbeat.

I can sense both of them.

Classed men were rare on Dorog. But if there was one job that required a little more muscle than the rest, it was minding the Gates.

“Classed, huh? So, you beat the Shrine. Good for you. But I’m sure you know that Lord Ferris demands his dues before you access the Gates. Come, follow.”

The guard waved him over and took a seat at a nearby table not far from the Gates.

“Take a seat. Good. Now sign this,” the guard handed over a scroll.

Rex unrolled it and began scanning over the tiny text. It was quite something, with all kinds of conditions written into the details.

“Oh, don’t bother reading it all. It basically just forces you to return here to Dorog on every fifth Gate jump so Lord Ferris can make sure you pay him back what you owe.”

Rex frowned and kept reading. But hurried along as the guard started to huff irritably. He didn’t read through all of the details as well as he probably should have, but he didn’t see anything that contradicted what the man said. Besides, he already knew that everyone had signed a contract before leaving.

“Fine,” Rex said and scribbled his name.

“You’re all good to go then. Word for the wise: make sure you’ve got coin on you before returning. We don’t want to confiscate your equipment, and you don’t want to lose it, right?”

“Right,” Rex nodded and rose.

The entire exchange felt a whole lot like extortion. But he was so damn close. So close to finally leaving Dorog for the first time in his life, and he wanted nothing more than to step through the Gate as soon as humanly possible. And there were many ways to get out of such contracts—vassalship to a lord, membership in a powerful guild, and so on.

“Alright then. Off you go.”

Rex didn’t waste a second and rushed straight up to the Gates, and then promptly skidded to a halt.

“Err, which one to Tartarus?”

“That one.”

“Thanks. Catch ya!”

He felt a little bad not saying goodbye to Greg. They’d worked shifts together for a while. But after the amount he drank the previous night, he wouldn’t be getting up for a while.

“Goodbye, Dorog!” Rex saluted as he took one last good look at the pluming smoke and shanties that covered the depressing horizon of Dorog Targ and stepped through the Gate.

On the other side, he found himself standing on a muddy dirt road looking up toward a cliff-side city surrounded by high stone walls.

Rex swung around. No one was even bothering to guard the Gate. It was just here, unceremoniously beside the muddy road.

Okay, I’m certainly not in Dorog anymore.

It wasn’t just the lack of Gate guards that caught his attention, though. The sun was shining, the air was warm, the sea beside the city was perfectly blue, and the clouds were white and fluffy. And most importantly, not a single coldrock mine.

“This ain’t no Dorog. It’s fucking beautiful. Real, blue skies. I can barely believe it.”

He was close to crying. It was the best damn day of his—

“MOVE IT, BOY! Can’t you see I’m walking here?”

In his excitement, staring up at the sky, Rex had wandered straight into the middle of the road.

“Oh, excuse me,” he bowed his head and stepped aside for the old man, who snorted and kept pulling his little wagon up the muddy hill toward the city.

The man had been a little rude, but it didn’t bother him. He stepped to the side of the road and glanced around in awe. There was an endless hum of activity everywhere he looked. This place was nothing like Dorog. Back home, there were just downtrodden miners and guards. Here, he spotted people in fine, satin robes walking with staffs and books over their shoulders. Others were dressed in shiny armor, and caravans rattled by, loaded with all kinds of exotic goods he’d never seen before.

But then his gaze settled on a vast tent settlement beside the city. The tents rolled across the hills and flatlands beneath the perched city of Tartarus, and by his guess, looked to be twice the size of the city itself.

I guess not everyone is living it up.

The tent city didn’t come as much of a surprise. There were likely as many unclassed here as there were on Dorog. And he doubted the people were any more charitable.

Okay, well. Here’s to a new life. Time to go and make something of it.

Pulling on the straps of his bag, Rex made his way up the muddy hill to the famed city of adventurers.

But he didn’t make it far. A few guards manned a small outpost a short distance down from the main gates to the city, and everyone had to pass through them, including Rex.

“Oi, you there!” One of the guards shouted.

He didn’t think things would go that smoothly, but Rex had still tried to stroll right on past with his head lowered.

“Yeah, you. Over here, come here, lad.”

Rex immediately felt a pulse of power from the guard as he stepped forward. He had no idea what level this man was, but he was without any doubt far stronger than the guard who had stopped him at the Gate on Dorog.

“Me? How can I help, sir?”

“Hah-How can you help? You registered or not?”

“Registered?”

“Newbie, huh? Alright then. Place your hand on the stone over here.”

The guard gestured to a stone plaque resting on a table with a nod.

When Rex placed his palm down on the stone, it hummed and light emanated from it. A few seconds passed, and the light projected a 1 above him.

“Level one, huh? Well, that ain’t gonna do it. If you ain’t a citizen, you ain’t registered in a guild, and you ain’t a Freedman, you ain’t getting into the city.”

A Freedmen was the title given to those who were Classed and had reached level 6. There were certain level thresholds that, when reached, provided Classed with additional Attribute points. And level 6 was the first threshold, and represented a considerable power boost.

“I have to reach level six? But how am I supposed to do that without a contract? I need to get into the city to find work.”

The guard pointed toward the tent settlement. “There’s a big wooden tavern in the middle of the tent city called ‘The Red Rose.’ Go there. Plenty of contracts for newbies like you. If you survive to level six, come see us and we’ll let you into the city.”

Rex slumped; he could already see the thatched roof and smoking chimneys of the tavern above the tents. It seemed that he wasn’t as close to Tartarus as he had thought. Then again, it wasn’t that surprising. His mother had always said, “Nothing worth having in this world comes freely.”

Everything quickly changed when Rex left the main road and strolled toward the tents. The shiny armor and fine robes he had spotted on the road toward Tartarus were quickly replaced by tattered clothing and dirty rags.

In fact, the tent settlement wasn’t any better than Dorog, from what he saw. But at least it held promise. A path to something greater.

It’s not so bad. I just need to find the tavern, get a contract, grind some levels, get some coin, and get into Tartarus. Then, I should be able to find a real party.

His first step toward greatness was perhaps not as illustrious as he had hoped, but it was only his first step. There were still plenty to go, and he smiled at the thought, only to have it soured by a terrible smell seconds later. “What is that?!” Rex coughed and squeezed his nose shut.

It was a dead man. Lying face down in a foul puddle of green water only a few steps off the path.

“Gross.”

The tent settlement wasn’t all bad, though. As he kept walking, he heard the throng of people hard at work. Crafters banging away at equipment in nearby tents, curing leather, cooking, and just about every other job required to keep a settlement going.

He also spotted a good number of armed people glaring from the alleys between the tents.

Ignore them and look straight ahead!

Now wasn’t the time to be starting fights with strangers in alleys. Rex had work to find. And it didn't take long to reach the tavern. There was only one proper path, after all, and it led him straight to it.

Outside, a couple of drunken men swung poorly aimed punches at one another to the cheers of several others, most of whom had weapons at their sides.

And it didn’t get much better when he walked through the door.

Rex felt small stepping into the tavern. He was still only sixteen, hadn't filled out, and desperately hoped he still had at least a couple of inches of growth left in him. But still, the patrons were… rather mean-looking, and mostly big ones at that. Brawly dudes with muscled arms on display, and hands resting on hilts.

They're not so big. Certainly not any more intimidating than the meat centaur was.

Rex puffed his chest out and kept walking. He was a Classed man now… well, not quite. But sixteen was old enough to be treated like a man, at least on Dorog. But more importantly, he wasn’t some kid to be bullied. And he held his head high.

He wasn’t looking for a fight, but if someone wanted one, he wasn’t about to cower.

Spotting an empty stool by the bar, he took a seat and looked around. The people here were rough. More so than he had even realized at first. Just about everyone sported a scar or two, while many had missing fingers, limbs, and wore eyepatches. He even spotted a few dark dwarves and orcbears.

I couldn’t have ended up anywhere more welcoming, could I?

“What can I do you for?” Came a grizzled, coarse but clearly feminine voice.

“Huh?” Rex swung around.

Standing before him was a broad, powerful-looking brunette in a flattering corset behind the bar. She was attractive, in a dangerous kind of way. But it wasn’t her looks that caught his attention. No, it was the pulse of menacing power he felt from her.

She is… definitely the strongest person I’ve met.

Rex swallowed. The only Classed people he’d seen fight were the adventurers when he was a child, and he hadn’t had a Class back then to sense their auras. He really wished he had something to compare these people to; nonetheless, he knew she was strong. Much, much stronger than he was.

“Something to drink?" She said with a half-lidded gaze, clearly not wanting to repeat herself.

"Me? To drink? Oh, I don't have a copper on me.”

“Then why are you hogging up one of my stools? There are paying customers."

“Well, I was actually just here looking for a contra–"

“Over there, now get out of my stool," she said, pointing toward a noticeboard.

"Oh, right. Thanks. I guess I’ll be leaving then. Nice meeting you, ma’am.”

She rolled her eyes and moved on to the next customer.

Walking over to the noticeboard, Rex glanced back at the woman. She was just a bar lady, but she had such an incredible presence.

“Don’t stare too long, boy. See that hook? You do not want to know where I've seen her put that before. Suffice to say, she don't take too keenly to lingering eyes."

Rex realized that she did indeed have a hook hand and then looked over to see who was talking. It was a smiling old man who was tipping his hat to him as he slipped on an ale.

“Roseanna’s her name,” he continued. "She owns this place. Used to be a hotshot adventurer. I heard she even worked for Lord Castarian once. But she lost an arm during a contract a few years back, and decided to settle down and open this place.”

Rex looked around. The tavern was a far shot better than the tents outside, but it still left quite a bit to be desired.

“Why here? Why not a tavern within the walls?”

“Ha! Within the walls? We ain’t all made of money, kid! A tavern inside the walls would have cost her ten times as much, maybe more. Besides, it's a charming place, ain't it?"

Beside them, a man downed his ale in one, promptly threw it up into his flagon, looked down at it, then drank it back down.

“Oh, that's just Scotty. Don't mind him."

“Right, right,” Rex said as he slowly stepped away.

But he barely managed to make two steps towards the noticeboard when a strong hand grabbed him by the shoulder.

“Hey, kid. I saw you come in. New to town and looking for your first contract, are you?”

“Yeah,” Rex said without looking back.

What now?

“Look, word of advice? Don't waste your time with the noticeboard. These are just trash contracts nobody wants. If they wanted them, they wouldn’t just be sitting around waiting for someone, now would they?”

“I suppose that makes sense…”

“Right, you seem like a smart lad. Since you're new here, I'd be happy to give you a hand. I could introduce you to some people. Get you a nice, good contract. Much better than crappy contracts you can find out here on the noticeboard."

Yeah, right, buddy. You totally don't sound dodgy.

But despite the man making him immediately uneasy, Rex wasn't so quick to do something that might offend. He could feel the power coming from the man, and while it didn't compare to Roseanne or the guards, he was still no doubt stronger than he was.

But before Rex could come up with an excuse not to follow the man, he heard a commotion by the entrance to the tavern. He looked over to spot a robed boy leading a party into the tavern.

Wait, is that Lavrian? How’d he form a party so quickly?

Not wanting to be seen, Rex lowered his head.

“Something wrong?”

Oh, right. The creepy guy.

Rex was distracted, still trying to keep one eye on Lavrian and avoid being seen, and blurted out the first thing that came to him.

"A contract?”

“Oh yeah. A great contract. It’d be my pleasure to help out a young man like you. You know, you actually remind me of myself back when I first arrived here."

“Um, yeah, I mean, what?” Rex said, turning and covering his face.

“Come, this way.”

“Wh-what?”

The man was already leading him away from the bar, and he didn’t know what to say. Also, there was Lavrian, talking loudly to his party, and he ducked to avoid his gaze as they passed across the room.

“The name’s Arnold, by the way. And you are?”

“Rex."

“Rex is it? Well, you’re about to meet the big dogs around here, Rex,” Arnold said, leading him to a curtain that he pulled back, and as they passed through, he got his first proper look at the man.

Arnold was a wide, raven-haired man who towered over him, with dark, sunken eyes and grayish, clammy skin.

“Over there, to that booth,” Arnold said, pointing as he pushed Rex’s back.

Oh shit. What'd I get myself into?

He didn’t know how to refuse now, and so he nodded and followed his lead. It was dark behind the curtain, and if people looked mean out in the main room of the tavern, they looked worse in there. The nestled booths along the wall were masked by curtains, but he caught enough glimpses of the people within to know he didn’t want to go pissing anybody off.

Arnold led him to the booth and pushed the curtain back. Inside, a big, grizzled man sat with girls on either side of him, and men who looked like bodyguards sat on either side of the girls.

The big, grizzled man with a fuzzy black beard smiled with golden teeth. “What have you brought me today, Arnold?”

“This is Rex,” Arnold said, pulling Rex into the booth. “He’s recently Classed. Tells me he’s looking for a contract.”

“Is that so, Rex?”

“This is Credo, Rex. Answer his question. He’s a bit of a big shot around here. Don’t want to keep him waiting.”

Rex didn’t need the explanation. He could feel the strong pulse of power coming from Credo. Although, if he had to guess, Roseanne was still stronger.

“It is,” Rex said.

“Come, take a seat,” Credo gestured, and one of his men quickly got up and made room for him.

“Go on. Don’t keep him waiting,” Arnold said.

Suddenly, Rex felt surrounded and far from home. What would these men do if he refused?

Then again, maybe they do have a contract for me. That is what I came looking for.

Perhaps he was being a little naive, but he still wanted to hope something good was going to come from meeting Credo, and so he took the seat.

“You look like a strong lad. I’ve always got jobs for the strong. Opportunities for people to prove themselves, if they’re willing to seize them.”

“I am, I think,” Rex said. “The strong part, I mean.”

“Haha. Funny. I like it. It looks like you brought me a good one, Arnold. Well, then, here’s the job. It’s a simple one, really. All you need to do is march through a Gate for me and make sure nothing bad happens on the other side of that Gate. Sounds easy enough for you?”

“What do you mean, nothing bad happens?”

“First, let me fill you in a little on how I fit into all of this,” Credo said, gesturing around with his hands. “You see, I supply this here tavern and many others on this world with food and drink. But to supply them, I obviously need to get that food and drink. And that’s where you come in. I own a small farming settlement on an outworld. It’s a beautiful place. Safe for the most part, but accidents do happen.”

“Accidents?” Rex probed.

“Oh, nothing too serious. Only the occasional goblin, wolf, or other small beasts. Nothing a good, strong, young adventurer like you need worry about. But the farmers, they’re not Classed like you. They’re weak, vulnerable. They need protection. Sounds easy, right? Basically a holiday. You get to stay in a nice cabin with fresh air and open fields. And all you need to do is make sure no weak beasts harm my employees. So, what do you say, Rex? Want the contract?”

Rex had to admit it didn’t sound like a bad first job. He needed something to ease himself into this lifestyle. Something that wasn’t going to get him killed. And goblins and wolves were typically a great place to start. It sounded like the perfect opportunity to gain a few levels and earn coin while he was at it.

Also, he didn’t have anything to lose. Perhaps if he had valuables on him, he might be worried about getting robbed. But he had nothing. Most of the people meandering about the tent settlement would’ve had more valuable possessions than he did.

“A couple of questions. Can I quit whenever I want? And I’m… kind of short of a weapon at the moment,” Rex said, giving his belt a shake.

“Quit? Why, of course. Haha. I’m not going to own you, Rex. If you decide to leave, that’s fine. You will have to wait for the next Gate opening. That means committing to at least two weeks of work. Surely that’s not too much for a young up-and-comer like yourself, though, is it? And after those two weeks, if you choose to leave, you’ll leave with a sack of coppers in your hand. If you choose to stay for longer, you’ll have your fair share of gold and silver before long. How’s that sound?”

“That’s… that actually sounds pretty good.”

“Good. And don’t worry about a weapon. One will be provided to you.”

“Really?” Rex blinked.

“Oh, of course. Can’t have you going out there bare-handed, now can I?”

Rex had stumbled into the meeting and, at first, hadn’t really wanted to take whatever contract Arnold was trying to get him to sign up for. But the deal sounded good. Too good. Sure, he would only be a glorified guard, which wasn’t exactly what he had in mind when he got a Class and left Dorog. But he needed coin to repay Damo and get himself a bow. Besides, what was a few weeks guarding farmers to set himself up for a glorious future?
