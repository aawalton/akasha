Chapter 185 / B3-30: The Erlking

Exiting the portal was a bit of a surprise.  Joe thought they were going to appear on the top floor.  Instead, they were deposited at the base of the staircase.  Zigging through a gap between Naragash and a severely battered bone golem, Joe bound up the steps and through Tezeno’s barrier, straight into a wall of undead.

Joe slammed [Heavy Hand] into the wights in his way, flaring the spell as hard as he could muster with [Morphic Form] swelling the muscles of his back and right arm.   Fifteen Strength was good, not superhero good, just good.  Yet when enhanced by a decent degree of momentum, an increase from [Heavy Hand] and a pair of amber telekinetic paws, there was enough force to punch a hole through the line of enemies.

Bursting out of the undead around the stairway, Joe found himself in a chamber so huge it boggled the mind.  He was expecting a throne room or some other expansive space. Instead, it was as if he had stepped into another world.  The room was lit by a pervasive blue hue that seemed to emanate from floating balls of glowing mist.  There was no specific pattern to the fey lights, causing odd shadows to stretch around the enormous hall.

Sounds were also distorted.  As Joe sprinted in an evasive dash, he could hear through the gurgling roars and the clack of the loom, an eerie melody wafting through the vast chamber.  The song somehow evoked the idea of pastoral beauty and the glory of dawn, yet there was something insidiously wrong with the aria. There was a sickness in the song’s melodic garden, one that made Joe’s hackles rise.

Even with the maddening melody playing, Joe’s first thought would have made the BBC proud. ‘It’s bigger on the inside.’

‘What was that?’ Taylyn and Yuk asked in unison.

‘Nothing to worry about. Just a stray thought. How long do you need to charge your bomb, Taylyn?’

‘I just started,’ she replied. ‘I needed to see the room up here to know what we were dealing with. I’ll need another forty seconds to get the explosion powerful enough.  Can you get close in that time?’

‘I’ll try.  I still have to find him.  What happens if I can’t?’

Finding the Erlking was going to be more of a challenge than he expected.  The undead at the top of the stairs were far from the last of the Spring King’s horde. There had to be well over a hundred wights and mummies in the massive chamber. Several clusters of them were bounding towards the small guild strike force; clawed, rotting hands reaching for the team.

‘I can hold it for about ten more seconds. If I don’t release the [Seed Blast] in that time, I’ll have to add another layer of power to it to reset the timer. Keep in mind, each time I add another layer, the effect gets more powerful and less stable. We can’t delay indefinitely.’

Joe locked his eyes on an open spot of floor and activated his talisman, just as three bands of putrid hunters slammed together in the spot they had just vanished away from.  Forty seconds was both too long and too short.  While it doesn’t sound like a lot of time, in combat, that was an eternity - especially when you were outnumbered fifty to one.

On the flip side, finding one body in the stadium of attacking monsters in under one minute was a severely daunting task.  Joe decided that there was a constant he could target.  The loom.  Even if they didn’t get the Erlking this time, blowing up the loom would stop the endless spawning of wights.  Joe focused on the clatter of the shuttle and the frames.  When he had a direction, he dashed through the throngs, hoping the fey lord would not move the tool away when he got there.

Yuk tried to summon swarms of spectral rats to help out and provide additional targets, but the team was moving too quickly and erratically for the rodents to keep up.

Volleys of arrows filled the air, seeming not to care if they hit their own allies. Joe thickened his hide over [Grimoiriguard] and kept the shield up.  His lower legs were the most vulnerable, but with the sheer number of creatures around him, there was no easy shot at his shins and calves. The worst arrow strike so far was one that found his elbow, a bit above his hairy resistance and just below the edge of his armor. The shaft punched through the skin and lodged into the cartilage of the arm joint. Swearing, Joe yanked it out and healed before teleporting to a safer spot as he closed in on the loom.

Then a long, elegant arrowshaft hammered into Joe’s shoulder, punching right through both the shield covering and the armor on his chest. This shot was several times more potent than any missiles launched so far by the weaselly Grievous Creepers. Joe numbed the area before pulling out the wide, silver arrowhead.  Meanwhile, Yuk scanned the enemies to find the source of that extraordinary attack.

‘Got him, Joe.  It’s the Erlking!  He’s by the loom, look a bit to your left and up on that stage.’

Joe followed his friend’s directions, spotting a being beyond comprehension.  Standing on a large raised dais was the abomination that had once been the King of Spring.

You have failed to resist the Erlking’s [Dreadful Beauty]. [Lucid Juice] has negated the effect.

You have partially resisted the Erlking’s [Ardent Servitude]. You are swayed.

You have resisted the Erlking’s [Obsequent Submission]. You are not dominated.

Just looking at the twisted lord almost overwhelmed even Joe’s enhanced mental defences.

The Erlking: Level 68 {Elite}: Undead Fey (Dirge Lich): Controller: Spirit

Given the massive presence the man exuded, Joe was surprised by the figure on the raised platform.  The Erlking had a lean build and was of elfish height, maybe a few inches taller than Joe’s emerald-eyed friend.  Like the Mournful Thralls, he was bound in ancient, stained, and tattered wrappings.  The swaths of sepia-colored cloth covered much of his face and arms, giving him a twisted, scarecrow-like appearance. What flesh was visible on the fey was as black as frostbitten skin.

In one hand, he held a sweeping dark wood bow. With the other, he was drawing a new arrow from a gem-encrusted quiver. Beside the arrowcase on his belt hung a long, thin, straight blade, equally adorned with jewels. A flickering glow surrounded the rapier’s sheath, hinting at powerful Feyland magic.

“Kneel!” the Erlking commanded, pointing his ornate recurved bow at Joe. The power of that demand washed over Joe like a surging stormy tide; it had a weight that pushed down on him like a titan’s hand across his back. Before it could crush his will, it vanished, freeing Joe’s mind again.

‘One bead spent.  We have four more.’

‘Thanks, bud. You’re the best,’ Joe praised Yuk.  He summoned another [Lucid Juice] with his [Alchemy Belt] and downed it like a shot of liquor.

‘Good thing I can’t see him and he can’t see me,’ Taylyn declared. ‘I didn’t lose focus on my casting. Halfway there.’

‘It got a big part of us.  But we are so physically broken up that it only partially worked. Half of us are currently genuflecting all over you, Joe.  Luckily, the other half had one of the [Beads of Disspelling].’

Joe tried to only look side-eye at the royal figure on the raised stage.  As he caught a glimpse of the bow swinging toward him again, he used [Heavy Hand] and the paws to make a gap so he could dive behind a screen of wights.  Even with the erratic jog and almost full cover, the Erlking’s second arrow still skimmed across his thigh, the broadhead arrow opening a long, deep groove in Joe’s leg

‘Son of a bitch!’ he swore as he healed and teleported.  ‘I was not expecting his bow to be this lethal.  Tay, what would have happened if you had lost focus?’

‘At this level of energy compaction,’ the timid mental voice replied, ‘it would be very painful, but we should have been mostly ok. You two are tough to damage, and I am largely bomb-resistant. At least to my own creations.’

Somewhat reassured, Joe tried to cut around another pack of mummies and wights when the ground under his feet erupted into a swath of burning sticky ooze. Thankfully, Joe had given up on boots, or they would have been dissolved in an instant. Unable to free his sizzling toes from the caustic substance, Joe prepared to use the [Medallion of the Medic] when a pearl tumbled from the shaggy hair covering his chest. The moment the opalescent orb hit the ground, a flash of light erased the corrosive goop and freed Joe’s acid-scorched soles.

‘Hey.  Save those for the mind-stuff, bud.  I can teleport out of bindings.’

‘Not always.  Some constraining spells include anti-teleport, but we’ll wait on your call next time.’

Joe gritted his teeth and sent healing to the tender feet, which were now slapping against the stone floor. Stopping was not an option. Even that momentary pause in the caustic glue had allowed the archers to hammer him with barbed missiles. One had lanced under his wolf-headed hood, slicing his cheek.

There was no chance of initiating a serious offense.  He only popped off a few [Casting Claw] delivered [Dispel Noxa] to keep from being boxed in.

Joe dashed in a long, looping, zig-zagging path, not even really aiming for the loom rattling away on the Erlking’s dais. He was just trying to give Taylyn the time she needed while not getting overwhelmed by the enemy.

Other spells followed the acid patch, but thankfully, none were as effective as that first magic entangle. Joe was seared with fire, shocked with lightning, and splashed with ice. The morphic pelt grew increasingly singed from the incendiary and electrical burns, but [Wellspring] and even a few direct heals kept it strong.

The Erlking attempted to control or dominate Joe more than once, but Joe or Yuk burned resources on the few legendary enchantments that managed to land.  They were getting low on potions and beads, but the clock was running down any second now.

Just as Joe was about to ask how much longer Tay needed, a stunning attack blasted his thoughts to ribbons.

Yuk cleared it, only for another brain-shattering daze effect to land right on the heels of the first one. As Joe lost speed, balls of fire and bolts of lightning honed in on where he stood still. Wobbling on gummy-feeling legs, Joe stood under a rain of arrows - the spearing missiles punched through gaps in his armor and his now bare feet.

Yuk’s shouting voice solidified and then blurred in Joe’s head as the tatterdemalion burned through their supply of dispels. He managed to catch a couple of Yuk’s mental shouts each time, and then the next stunning spell would kick his mind around like a football.

The Lord of Larks and the mournful thralls had switched entirely to stunning attacks. Joe’s mind turned into a speed bag being pummeled by a world-champion boxer.

‘ grubb uuhshoo wuwoojug … YOU OK? huuhvum luddud … JOE!! The king is directing them! …. uvsasoo ascuvo dabbama … WE SHOULD BAIL! ….. wogugga duugdawa dubbu … erstand me?’

Joe had his head clamped in his hands. He could feel blood streaming down his cheeks from his nose and eyes, but also from where his own claws had sliced through his scalp, digging the talon points into the bone of his skull. His mind was on fire, as was his body. Yuk was screaming. Taylyn was screaming. Joe had no clue whether they were trying to focus him or crying out in pain.

He just knew they were screaming.

… and he wasn’t doing anything.

A marauder’s claw ripped into his throat, and Joe couldn’t remember how to heal.

A body tackled them, and they fell, slamming onto the stone floor of the ziggurat.

Blows were raining down on them as Joe curled around the little woman whom he was sworn to protect, pulling his hands from his head to shield her.

His mind just fumbled and stuttered as one stunning blast after another sent his brain spinning.

Joe felt his talons punch through his reinforced palms, unleashing spears of pain, but it was an agony he controlled. Right behind the grinding feeling of his claws against the bones in his hands came a roar of fury. The wildness howled through Joe. Its savage presence swelled, trying to fill every part of his uselessly numb brain and body. For once, Joe didn’t try to contain it.

He let it all the way out.

A howl more ferocious than any belonging to the dead roared from under the pile of putrid bodies on top of Joe. He felt the lupine marauders recoil—some dove off, fleeing from the challenge that bayed out of the wildness.

You have designated your sub-pattern as the controlling entity.  You have expended your Skill Evolution and 1 of your open points of Vigor.

You have partitioned [Morphic Form] into its base usage and the rare skill [Skinwalker].

Joe barely understood this message, mostly because his mind was still spinning like a top, but there were some very strange things happening within him as well.  Somehow, the wildness was spending his advancements.  Also, the name of the skill it was creating was alarming. Skinwalkers were horrific creatures from Earth stories.

As much as he wanted to question or even outright reject these changes, he couldn't do anything about them at the moment.  He was not the one in control.

The wildness pushed right through the dazing assault. It skipped over the skill’s description, seeming to know precisely how the new ability worked. Joe felt the [Hide of the Hunter] settle more tightly around his body, merging—becoming no longer two separate things but conjoining into one unified form.

The hood slid down his face, settling onto his cheeks. The eye sockets formed around his own. The pelt’s jaw-bones found his mandibles and melded into them.

Moments later, the wildness flicked a long tongue over their new mouth, filled with sharp white fangs. It howled with a rage that overshadowed even the dead king's authority.  The undead, trying to restrain them, cowered and slid off.

Your skill [Pack Master] has increased to rank 17.

Heaving upwards, the primal lupine skinwalker rose from the cringing bodies around their feet. They bayed once more, before fixing baleful eyes on the only being in this chamber worthy of challenging them.

Kicking away the cringing curs at their feet, they charged at the Erlking.
