The next morning we returned to the dead city together; its archway appeared before us, and I heard that stressed wail from way back in the city. It echoed like a warning horn, almost, and I could only imagine the type of creature it was coming from.

“So I’ll lead the attack,” Themiel said. “I’m a higher level, and I’ve defeated one of these things before. We’ll try to keep Luke out of it, but it’ll be good experience for him if he can get at least some involvement. Maybe even an achievement, even if we do most of the work.”

I looked down at Luke, who patted along between the two of us. He was at least oblivious to the tension between his companion and I, as his tongue lulled out as if he were having the time of his life.

“There are certain powerful skills I possess,” I began explaining. “But if I resort to them, I won’t be able to turn into a human again for two days.”

“Really? What kind of skill is that? I’ve never heard of that kind of condition before.”

“It’s hard to explain,” I said. Or rather, I just didn’t feel it necessary to explain that I could actually change my Paths. Though, depending on how good his Analyze skill was, I supposed it was possible he would figure it out anyway.

“Well, all right, I’ll keep it in mind,” he said. “Honestly, if you have something that’s really that strong, don’t skimp on using it. This thing we are going to fight is around level 30, so I am higher than it, but it is a great foe. It will be stronger than any typical level 30, and you—and especially Luke—are quite a bit lower than it.”

“I’m sure it will be fine,” I said. “Let’s just make sure Luke stays safe.”

The fact of the matter was, I’d defeated a great foe that was more than five levels higher than me before, and I’d done it solo. This was the Earth Tyrant, of course. That battle really did always come back to my mind as the true turning point in my power…

I stopped walking as we were under the arch of the dead city. “I think I’d like to try to take it down by myself, and with a little assistance from Luke,” I said.

“That’s not going to be possible,” he answered.

I looked at him out of the corner of my eye. “You’re underestimating me. But I came out here to become stronger. The system rewards you for taking on harder challenges.”

“Yes, but if you die, then you will not only fail to get any rewards at all, but your journey will be at its end,” Themiel said back.

I looked down at my brother, who looked between us with his head turned, clearly not understanding our words.

“In any case, I want Luke to get stronger as well,” Themiel said. “And even if you fighting this thing solo is incredibly reckless, I’ll admit it is within the realm of possibility. Luke, however, I won’t risk his life like that.”

“Luke is a pretty high level for his age,” I commented. “I know my own growth is abnormal. But he is still higher level than most I know to be his age.”

Themiel stared at me for a moment. I came a little too close to admitting I knew where at least one of our other siblings was. I didn’t want Themiel to know that in case it turned out I really couldn’t trust him. Of course, I would tell Luke the second we were able to communicate.

“I’ve taken care to train him well,” Themiel said. “Not that I had to try too hard. Luke’s a natural.”

Luke let out a bark and breathed heavily, his tail wagging, apparently understanding that he’d been acknowledged. Themiel grinned and reached down, scratching Luke’s head.

My eyes narrowed. Getting his head patted like that while his tail wagged away…

Themiel—I could not say I felt like he was a bad person, but I still couldn’t accept this. I couldn’t believe Luke would accept becoming someone’s pet.

I brushed back my cloak and moved ahead. “Come on. Let’s get moving.”

Themiel jogged to get ahead of me and joked, “You’re acting as if you’re the one who knows where to find it.”

I pointed north, into the deeper, darker parts of the city—the parts of the city I’d only begun to traverse. “I assume it’s going to be in that direction, no?”

“Okay, I guess that was easy enough to figure out, but still. We ought to approach it in silence first, so I can show it to you and explain some things.”

“I’m a very capable hunter,” I said, giving him another side-eye. “I’ve made it this far by myself.” That wasn’t entirely true. Lily and Grick helped a bit along the way, but at the same time, it was true. I was always the one pushing the limits of what was possible. Sure, I likely wouldn’t have survived without them, but I was the deciding factor in all the incredible feats we’d managed to achieve. That was just the truth of it.

“I can tell you’re capable,” Themiel said. “But information is a resource, and if it is coming for free, why not take a free advantage in your fight?”

“Fine,” I answered, not slowing my pace as he walked beside me.

Themiel lagged behind and muttered something under his breath, and Luke let out a soft whine.

Was he just complaining about me?

I shot him a glare.

“I was supposed to make friends,” Themiel said with a grin. “I’ll be honest, it seems like it’s going to be quite a challenge.”

I almost wanted to laugh at that. I would have laughed at that. But not while Luke was looking up at him like that, his tongue lolling from the side of his mouth and a subservient gaze in his eyes, as if he were waiting to see what Themiel might do next or tell him to do.

Themiel jogged ahead to catch up with me once again. “Anyway, don’t take it the wrong way. I was just trying to make a joke. I imagine, with everything you’ve been through, it’s only natural that you wouldn’t trust strangers and that you’d be on edge.”

“Let’s save it for later. We’re on a hunt. But you’re right; I’ll take your information.”

“All right, then. Follow me.” He jogged ahead a few steps and then jumped, the single leap carrying him impossibly high—just high enough to reach a second-story window and swing himself onto a roof from there.

I looked down at Luke, who had stopped and was staring at me, still with that stupid lolling tongue and happy dog smile.

I couldn’t help smiling at him as well. He was still my brother, and I didn’t want to make him feel bad. But eventually, I’d have to make him see that this wasn’t right, that his place under Themiel wasn’t right. That he had to raise his head like a proud Hell Hound and stop accepting orders from a master.

I knelt down and pushed my face toward his. We nuzzled each other for a moment, his doggy muzzle against my human face.

Then I turned and ran to build momentum. I picked a signpost to jump for and swing from, getting my feet high enough to hit a ledge that marked the beginning of a second story. Then I jumped from there, grabbed the edge of a roof, and swung myself up.

Themiel was already another building down. He waved his arm for me to follow and then whistled. Luke, in turn, was like a bullet below us as he ran along the sides of the buildings to catch up with Themiel. I got moving as well.

Keeping up with Themiel was a little difficult. I couldn’t tell if he was showing off or if he was just that good. I thought about my sparring sessions with Fox and returned to my thoughts of how I mostly did everything on my own.

Genzo helped a little, as did Lily and Grick in some critical moments. What about Fox? I’d say not really. At least not with combat.

I frowned, thinking that she really had been helpful in her own ways. As much as she irritated me, her little lectures were usually pretty worthwhile. Sparring was really just her having fun and trying to prove to me how much weaker than her I was… which…

I mean, she acted like she was doing it for a reason, but I hardly believed her! That stupid Fox just wanted to bully me!

Still, I felt a little better knowing that she was there taking care of Lily and Grick. I don’t think I would trust them completely in the empire’s hands otherwise.

Yes, if anything else, Fox was pretty smart. She would certainly be able to help Grick and Lily navigate living in and working for the empire. I had to trust she wouldn’t let anything bad happen to them.

I jumped from one building to the next. Themiel ran up a wall that partly collapsed beneath his steps, so I picked a spot on the wall a little farther down. All the while, I glanced below when I had the chance to make sure Luke was keeping up.

And I have to say, I was quite impressed. Luke was like a high-speed bulldozer that never slowed down.

If any skeletons got in his way, he’d either barrel right through them or ignore them. He was faster than them, after all, so it’s not like they could catch him. They would just run in his direction, shrieking for a little while before losing their aggro and then shuffling back the way they had originally come.

Some lingering Lisha part of me found that amusing, realizing this world really was quite like a video game. Though, to be fair, this dead city in particular had felt more video-game-like than most other places I’d been so far.

Themiel reached a long gap between roads and jumped it easily. I’d normally slow my steps and assess it more carefully, as I hadn’t taken a jump that far yet, but I wasn’t too worried. After all, Cat Footed would stop me from getting hurt in the event of a fall.

I leaped when I was at the very edge, soaring through the air for a moment, cloak billowing behind me, but quickly realized I would, in fact, not clear the jump. I set my sights on a third-story window, shifting my body and tucking in my shoulders.

I came right through, my foot catching on the sill as I spilled inside, but the landing was a little sloppy. The angled descent onto an unexpectedly close floor in a dark room had me stumbling. Stumbling toward—

I waved my arms and kicked at the ground to slow my momentum, almost like a cartoon character.

I managed to stop with my body leaning halfway over a pair of those creepy jars. Staring into their tops, I could see these ones were alive, as there was bubbling along their surfaces.

Themiel dropped into the window behind me. “My apologies. Are you alright?” he asked.

“Of course I am, why wouldn’t I be?” I said, a bit irritated by his concern.

He rubbed the back of his neck, his eyes drifting down to the jars. “Try not to jump so carelessly through windows. I’d assumed you’d just find another path up to the roof if you couldn’t make the jump.”

I huffed at him, calling me careless. But, I knew he was right. I probably shouldn’t do that again.

I carefully walked towards one and knelt over it, looking inside. When I saw a bubble rise to the surface and pop into a tiny smoke cloud, I shuddered and stepped away from them. “So where are these things coming form anyway?” I asked.

“I assume the undead are distributing them through the city,” Themiel said. “There are some that will try and throw the pots at you, so watch out for them.”

“I haven’t encountered that yet,” I said.

“No, it is usually the ones deeper in the city that try to do that. As I’m sure you’ve noticed, back the way we came, the skeletons are not as intelligent. The deeper in you go, and the more the city wakes up, things that are both stronger and smarter start waking up.”

Then, that long moaning wail echoed through the city once more, and I felt the hair on the back of my neck stand up.

“We’re getting close,” Themiel said. Then nodded towards the window. “Come on, I don’t want to keep eyes off Luke for too long. He’s too low-level to be left alone here.”

I agreed and followed Themiel back out the window.
