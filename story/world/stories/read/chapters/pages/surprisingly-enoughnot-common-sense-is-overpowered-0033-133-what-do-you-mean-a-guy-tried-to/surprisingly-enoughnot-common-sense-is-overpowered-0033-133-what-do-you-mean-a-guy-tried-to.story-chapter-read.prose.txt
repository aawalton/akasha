It was the most anticipated day of the season for Ning.

Harvest day.

He walked along the perimeter of his six acres with steady, unhurried steps, taking in the sight before him. Five acres were filled with basic spiritual grain, while the remaining acre was dedicated entirely to Frozen Breath Grain.

Managing all six acres had pushed him close to his limit, but now, after months of work, he could finally see the results.

Ning stood at the edge of the fields, his eyes practically shining as he looked over the rows of ripe spiritual grain.

Yes, he really was that happy.

After all, being a spiritual farmer was not easy. First came the weeds, then the pests, and then, even worse, the plants themselves decided to become a pain in the ass.

Planting spiritual crops was practically a science.

Some stalks grew unevenly, some absorbed qi too greedily, while others suddenly stopped growing because of some tiny imbalance in the soil's spiritual flow. A problem that looked completely insignificant could become serious if ignored for even a few days.

Fortunately, situations like these were exactly why the Withering and Flourishing Technique existed.

The technique sounded profound, but its basic principle was surprisingly simple: take a portion of wood qi from a healthy stalk and gently guide it into a weaker one, allowing the vitality between them to balance until both stabilized.

The scroll even described the process poetically: "Where one withers, another flourishes; where one flourishes, another sustains."

Of course, such prose was common enough that he didn't even bat an eye at that.

Ning crouched beside a pale patch of basic spiritual grain and took a slow breath. In his mind, he visualized the flow of qi as thin green threads, carefully shifting from one plant to another.

Visualization was his greatest strength, with spiritual energy control coming in a close second.

With those two advantages, combined with his decent elemental affinity, it was no surprise Old Zhou kept saying Ning had a "holy farmer's body." Compared to other new spiritual farmers, Ning's progress was downright explosive.

[Withering and Flourishing Technique: Starting (34/100)]

He rested one hand on a healthy stalk and carefully guided a thin stream of qi toward the weaker one. The pale stalk slowly straightened, its color gradually returning as though life itself were flowing back into a sick patient.

Ning exhaled softly.

It was a clean, successful transfer.

Of course, the technique wasn't perfect. There were several requirements for it to work properly: the plants had to be of the same type, at roughly the same growth stage, possess similar vitality, and be close enough for the transfer to remain stable.

If even one of those requirements wasn't met, the technique could weaken or, worse, backfire.

He had learned that particular lesson after losing two plants at once during his early attempts.

Ning placed a hand solemnly over his heart at the memory.

"Brave soldiers. You will be remembered."

Everyone had their quirks when they were in a good mood. Ning's was harmless; he simply became a little dramatic, even if most of that drama remained safely inside his own head.

After all, a little drama never hurt anyone, right?

After checking the remaining rows and finding no further problems, Ning finally began harvesting.

By noon, the totals were set.

Seven hundred kilograms of basic spiritual grain.

One hundred and twenty kilograms of Frozen Breath Grain.

Just as he finished counting everything, a familiar figure appeared at the edge of the field.

On harvest day, who else could it be but Fang Zhu?

The man always seemed to appear at times like this. Was it some sort of merchant's instinct? Or could he simply sense whenever a fresh "leek" was ready for plucking?

"Brother Ning," Fang Zhu greeted with a polite smile. "You've had a productive season."

"I tried my best." Ning gestured toward the sacks. "Please, go ahead."

Fang Zhu knelt beside them and began inspecting each batch. He checked the grains by color, luster, density, and even the faint aroma unique to each spiritual crop before finally weighing each sack.

"Basic spiritual grain, seven hundred kilograms," he said approvingly. "Frozen Breath Grain, one hundred and twenty. Excellent quality."

Ning smiled modestly. "I was lucky."

Fang Zhu nodded before turning his attention to the calculations. "The sect takes seventy-five percent. You keep twenty-five. And before you say anything, those are the sect's rules, not mine."

“I know.” Ning tilted his head, wondering why that needed to be said.

"Good. You'd be surprised how I'm treated despite that being common knowledge." Fang Zhu sighed as he began writing several lines in his ledger. "Every harvest day, someone looks at me like I'm personally taking their spirit stones. I've even had a few try to fight me over it."

Ning glanced at the mountain of grain he had spent months growing, then at the considerably smaller portion that would actually belong to him.

...Well, he could see how that happened.

"Your share comes to one hundred and seventy-five kilograms of basic spiritual grain and thirty kilograms of Frozen Breath Grain."

Ning felt the kind of pain only someone who had watched seventy-five percent of their hard-earned loot disappear could understand.

Somewhere, every gamer who had ever lost hours of progress was surely nodding in sympathy.

But outwardly, he remained perfectly calm.

"Alright."

"Do you want to sell the usual amount?" Fang Zhu asked.

“Yes. I’ll sell one hundred kilograms of basic grain and...” Ning thought for a moment. “Let’s do ten kilograms of Frozen Breath Grain.”

“Very well.” Fang Zhu nodded. “For the basic spiritual grain, at one spirit stone per kilogram, that comes to one hundred spirit stones.”

Ning hummed in acknowledgment.

He could have sold more, but fifty kilograms were already set aside for Su Fan, while another twenty-five would remain with him for his own use. The arrangement with Su Fan was more profitable than selling directly to the sect, so naturally, Ning had no intention of handing everything over to Fang Zhu.

If only Su Fan could handle a greater volume, Ning would have gladly sent him more.

“And for the Frozen Breath Grain, ten kilograms at eleven spirit stones each...” Fang Zhu added another line to his ledger. “One hundred and ten spirit stones.”

He counted out the payment and placed a pouch into Ning’s hands.

“Total: two hundred and ten spirit stones.”

An amount that would once have seemed unimaginable.

But with cultivation pills costing forty spirit stones each, it suddenly didn't feel like much.

Frozen Breath Grain was worth eleven times more than basic spiritual grain, but there was a reason for that price. Its growing cycle lasted six months, twice as long as basic grain, and nurturing such a high-grade spiritual crop drained qi like a sponge. The amount of care required was easily more than double as well.

For now, Ning could only handle around two acres of high-grade spiritual plants if he gave up growing other crops entirely. Any more than that and he risked burning himself out, a mistake he had already made once and had no desire to repeat.

The obvious way forward was to earn his spiritual farmer certificate, which would reduce the sect's seventy-five-percent tax to a fixed fee.

Unfortunately, that was easier said than done.

He needed three farming spells at Great Accomplishment and cultivation at the fifth stage of Qi Condensation before he could even qualify.

Ning looked over his freshly harvested fields and sighed.

Alas! There was still much work to do.

“Junior Brother, I saved this mission for you,” Si Sihua said cheerfully, glancing at the battle-geared Ning.

“Thank you, Senior Sister.”

Ning discreetly slid two rice puddings he had made across the counter. To an outsider, it might have looked like some shady back-alley deal.

“Hehe, good.” She snatched the puddings immediately.

Ning picked up the mission slip and checked it, more out of courtesy than necessity.

[Kill the black boars in Black Boar Forest. Each pair of tusks earns one spirit stone.]

“Junior Brother, this is already your fifth batch,” Si Sihua said. “You’re really going ham on those boars.”

If she knew she had made a pun, she didn’t show it.

“After all, this mission is quite profitable.”

Ning received one spirit stone for every pair of black boar tusks he showed as proof of a kill. Better yet, he only needed to show the tusks rather than hand them in, which meant he could sell them afterward for even more income.

It was basically getting paid twice for the same kill.

“Oh! Junior Brother, do you know the story behind this mission?” Si Sihua suddenly asked, leaning closer.

“No.” Ning shook his head.

“Then you’re in luck.” Si Sihua lowered her voice as though she were about to reveal some top-secret sect gossip. “Junior Brother, I heard that one of the inner sect elders was humiliated by a black boar when he was just starting out. Apparently, he became determined to exterminate the entire species afterward. That’s why he’s been issuing this mission for so long.”

“That…” Ning blinked. “Makes far too much sense.”

Wanting to exterminate an entire species of boar because one of them had humiliated him in his younger years?

Yup. That was a cultivator, all right.

If Ning had to be honest, the elder’s pettiness didn’t surprise him much. After all, this was a cultivation world where someone might kill you simply because you looked at them the wrong way.

What actually surprised him was the depth of the elder’s pockets.

Issuing missions like this cost a pretty penny, you know?

“It gets even better,” Si Sihua continued. “At first, he offered five spirit stones per pair of tusks. People swarmed the forest so fiercely that Black Boar Forest almost had to be renamed.”

Ning imagined the scene and nodded seriously. “I can see that happening.”

Five spirit stones just for showing a pair of tusks?

Damn, that was practically free money.

“It got so bad that some disciples started buying tusks from outside and smuggling them in. Others even tried to trick the Mission Hall by showing the same pair of tusks multiple times,” she whispered dramatically.

“For a mission like this, that was to be expected,” Ning said with complete understanding.

“One day, the elder caught them and annihilated the entire cheating ring. After that, everyone behaved.” Si Sihua paused before adding, “But apparently, that still wasn’t enough for him. The elder went and created some kind of marking technique specifically to permanently mark every pair of tusks that had already been shown, so no one could cheat the system again.”

Ning felt a faint chill run down his back.

This was definitely a man you did not want to cross.

The fact that he had successfully created an entirely new technique just because a few disciples had cheated his boar-killing mission said everything Ning needed to know about him.

“Eventually, the Sect Master had to intervene,” Si Sihua continued. “He forced the elder to have the black boar population restored so Black Boar Forest could actually remain Black Boar Forest.”

The more she talked, the more unhinged the story became.

“Oh, and the elder happily agreed,” she added. “Apparently, he plans to annihilate them, repopulate them, and then annihilate them again. He intends to repeat the cycle forever so the black boars can suffer for all eternity.”

Ning fell silent as he stared at Si Sihua for several seconds.

“…Senior Sister, what exactly did those boars do to him?”
