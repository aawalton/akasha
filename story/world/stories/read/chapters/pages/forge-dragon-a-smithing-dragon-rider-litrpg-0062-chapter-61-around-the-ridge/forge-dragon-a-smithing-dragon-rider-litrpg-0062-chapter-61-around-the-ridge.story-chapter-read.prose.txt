“That Magnus guy is an Impietor damned traitor,” Grisim said to Caleb when he came by the forge to check in on his brother.

Caleb had told his brother Magnus’ reported version of events and they hadn’t matched up with what Grisim had seen.

“It was all dark, and then suddenly the light returned and I saw him standing over the dead body of the guard. I didn’t even hear a commotion. Then, he just left without looking back at us.”

“I’ll admit,” Caleb said, “That doesn’t line up with what he reported to Canus, but it doesn’t contradict what he said about his orders.”

“I’ll admit,” Caleb said.

“Why are you defending this guy?”

Caleb thought back to his recent conversation with Mettius. Mettius seemed to trust Magnus for some reason, and unless Caleb was sorely off, he thought Mettius to be trustworthy himself. He was willing to give Magnus the benefit of the doubt until he learned the truth of the matter.

“Mettius trusts him,” Caleb said.

“And you trust him?” Grisim asked.

“He hasn’t given me any reason not to and he helped save you,” Caleb pointed out.

“I didn’t need saving,” Grisim said automatically with little conviction behind it.

“Right, well anyway. He helped us in our totally unnecessary daring rescue attempt. And…”

Caleb looked around to make sure Drusus was occupied at his forge. He spoke in a low whisper only an initialized with enhanced perception could hear, “he knows I have a full metal affinity—not just steel—and he’s keeping it a secret.”

Grisim’ s eyebrows shot up. Caleb had informed his brother about the implications of his affinity, and left unspoken the between them that he could be taken away like their mother if it got out.

“Alright,” Grisim said. “Maybe he’s alright, but I’m not extending that to Magnus.”

Grisim pestered Caleb while he worked a while longer, leaving eventually when Caleb grew too engrossed in his work to respond.

Caleb was at work studying Cassandra’s armor, probing his senses in and following the lines of magic within. Mana filled his inner mind in intricate patterns from large bold lines to intricate traces he could barely perceive. He knew that if his perception increased, he’d be able to make these out better, but he equally knew there were details within he couldn’t even catch a whiff of until he further improved his skill and acuity. With each level up of his skill and increase in his acuity, more nuance of the world was revealed to him. The more he saw, the more he realized how little he had seen before.

He had no doubt that there were worlds within worlds of detail in all things if only he had had the ability to see it. It was the same as with mundane metal. To a layman, steel was steel. It was strong and solid. But to a smith, there were countless varieties of steel, all of different strengths and characteristics. Those characteristics were based on the composition of the ore it was smelted from, the type of coal used to smelt it, and the temperature at which it was forged and the speed at which it was cooled.

Knowing that didn’t tell you about grain structures, which was part of the inner working behind the material properties. Caleb could now detect these structures in a way he couldn’t before, and an itch at the back of his mind told him there was more below this that he was not seeing.

Enchantments were like that, but far more complicated. Caleb was certain that without his enhanced mental stats, he wouldn’t be able to comprehend a fraction of what he could at the moment, even if he could somehow have detected them.

The armor, according to Mettius, absorbed magic and converted it to mana of whatever aspect the spell was that would fill her pool. Every magical attack made against her would replenish her and also fuel the material enhancement enchantments within. Mettius was certain there was more to the armor than that, but that was all he knew.

Knowing that there were multiple effects helped Caleb as he tried to piece it all together. He identified the two effects, the mana absorption and the material enhancement, and tried to wrap his mind around them. Once he’d found them it was clear these were the simpler of the effects on the armor. Some of the details of each were beyond his ability to perceive, but as he worked, his perception improved.

Magical Perception has increased to level 8.

With that, a little more of the hidden world was revealed to him, and he began to finally understand how it worked. He worked at it the whole day, not daring to even try to replicate it.

The next morning they were roused by an early muster as the legion headed out to the next village in the valley. Word had come that the Legate and his two additional centuries were needed elsewhere and that they were to advance alone to crush this new and different rebel threat.

Caleb and the other equites were given horses—ones that had been acclimated to dragons as Bog walked along beside them. With they enhanced constitutions, the horses were more status symbols than anything else. The empire had one unit of initialized cavalry, made up of the few mounted classes they had at its disposal. This unit, the Dragoons, was used as a rapid response force that regularly travelled the length of the empire. Outside of them there were mundane calvary, but they were not attached to the Thousand Blade Legion and anyone riding a horse amongst their numbers was marked as someone of import.

Caleb suspected that most Equites were simply lazy despite their initialized bonuses and didn’t want to walk. He’d have preferred to walk normally, but in this instance he took advantage of the mount to further study the armor. They would be marching all day to reach the next village of Forgard’s Rest by nightfall. Caleb had brought a small piece of the armor with him to study as they marched, and lost himself in the work, only noticing when his Magical Perception increased when the view within his mind expanded.

Magical Perception has increased to level 9.

His studying was brought to an end by the polite tap on the shoulder from Kelia.

“The village is around the next ridge,” she told him as he blinked the magic from his eyes.

He placed the armor in his saddlebag and joined in dismounting as grooms came to take their mounts as they  prepared for the coming battle. Caleb still wore his lorica, while it restricted his movement, he’d learned the benefits of keeping it on. While Kelia struggled to find materials that were her aspect, Caleb had all the metal he could want. It would have a two fold effect. First, it would increase the proportion of his mana pool that was metal aspected even without him exerting any effort. Second, it would slowly over time work to improve the grade of his affinity. Most humans gained affinities not through bloodlines, but through long term close proximity to a thing. But, it took more than just holding a thing to gain an affinity or everyone would be walking around with wool affinities.

The act of using and focusing on a material is needed. In this way crafters often gained affinities for the materials they worked with while soldier could gain affinities to steel. Without a class and the skills it came with however, these affinities were weak and good for little more than giving their wielders a subtle sixth sense for their aspect.

Using magic accelerated this affinity growth, which was the principal behind Mettius’ strategy to develop affinities and so long as Caleb continued to use his, he would eventually upgrade his major affinity for a grand one.

When they rounded the next ridge Caleb saw the village arrayed out before them a crude wall even still being put up by the villagers. He was directed to his ready position alongside Kelia and Mettius. They were to stand at the ready in case a hero arose for them to counter. Flavius was also on hand at a different part of the line to serve the same purpose.
