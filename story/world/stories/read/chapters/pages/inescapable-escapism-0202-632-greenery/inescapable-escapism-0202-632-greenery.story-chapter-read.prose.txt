My breath was shallow and shaky as I turned slowly, clutching the wooden staff before me. I half expected to see people, dressed in the same black uniform as at the Academy, barrelling towards me from both directions, but there was no one there. The maze was empty.

Relief nudged at my heart, but I was still on edge. My eyes darted towards the screen again, spotting my bright white silhouette still on display. The camera must have drifted even higher because I could see even more of the maze, but, to my dismay, I realised that I still couldn’t see anything to hint at which way I should be going. I must have been too far away from the way out to even see the edge of the maze.

If there even was a way out. I couldn’t remember how I got to where I was or whether I’d started at one of the entrances, but perhaps there were none. The edge of the maze could be nothing more than a solid wall that wrapped around it, entirely engulfing the walls within. I wasn’t sure, but I found myself wondering that if that was the case, maybe I should have been trying to get to the centre of the maze.

I barely had a chance to consider that before a shudder slipped through me. Instinctively, I turned to face the direction I’d been running in when I entered the world and began walking before my heart leapt.

A faint trill of excitement sparked within me, and I didn’t know for certain, but I was pretty sure I was moving away from the centre of the maze. I still couldn’t access my memories from that world; they were locked away from me, but for some reason, I knew that I wanted to avoid the centre. It was dangerous.

But that realisation just made me more curious, even as fear tightened around my throat, threatening to choke me, and I couldn’t help but wonder why. I longed to know what was so dangerous about that place, but I couldn’t recall anything. All I knew was that I did not want to go there, that if I did, it would be the end, but I didn’t know what that meant. Would I die if I set foot in the centre of the maze, or would it just be the end of… a game?

My curiosity grew stronger. Was that all it was? Perhaps the maze was nothing more than a game, something I was doing for fun, and if I got caught, I’d just be sent home, wherever that was. The questions lingered in my mind, waiting for the memories from the new world to come forth and answer them, but nothing happened. No new information was revealed to me. It remained stubbornly out of reach.

I was a little hopeful that it was just a game, but the fact that I didn’t know concerned me, and I felt myself speed up, just in case. I’d been to too many worlds recently that were full of people who wanted me dead. They did not even hesitate before attacking, and I was worried that I’d just found myself in another world like that, but I wanted it to be different. It felt different, but that just made it harder to know what to expect from the world.

My eyes darted towards the screen again, where I was still proudly on display, before I glanced over my shoulder, checking to make sure I was not being followed, but I was not. So far, I had yet to see another living person in the world, and that was… worrying.

But perhaps they were busy, I told myself, trying to be reassuring. Maybe they were just preoccupied with whatever was happening in the centre of the maze, or I might not have been a priority to them yet. They could have been focusing on hunting down some of the others first.

That thought made my steps falter and sent a pang of pain through my heart, and I grasped at the sensation, trying to gather more information about the world, but nothing else volunteered itself. Still, at least I knew there were more people there. There were others in the maze with me, both hunting me down and being hunted. And I cared about some of them. I could feel myself worrying about them, hoping that they were safe, but that wasn’t strong enough to make me turn and try to find them.

Part of me did want to, though, I realised excitedly. There was a temptation pulling at me, a desire to run through the stone passageways. I longed to let my feet pound on the dry, compact dirt below as I searched desperately for…

Frustration washed away my excitement as my thoughts trailed off. I wasn’t sure who it was that I wanted to go find. No name came to mind, and I didn’t even get a flash of a face behind my eyes. There was nothing to indicate who the person was. I just knew that there was someone I was worrying about, but that wasn’t enough for me. It wasn’t enough to force me to go in search of them.

Even if I tried, I wasn’t sure how I would do it. The maze was too confusing, and the screen was still only showing me, which was unhelpful. Maybe the image would change at some point, but for now, it provided me with nothing. If I did attempt to find whoever it was, I’d probably just end up more lost, and I’d probably end up coming face to face with…

I couldn’t finish that thought either. My mind fell silent once more, and a sigh slipped from my lips. Irritation flashed within me, but it faded away quickly as I looked around at the junction I’d come to.

My heartbeat grew faster as I stopped and peered up at the screen, checking whether there were people waiting around the corners for me, but I saw nothing. For some reason, I didn’t quite trust that, though. I forced myself to hold my breath, listening as hard as I could for any hint of footsteps or anything to indicate that the screen was wrong, but I heard nothing beneath the swell of music that continued to blast from unseen speakers.

Stepping forward, the staff trembling in my hands, I emerged from the shadow of the wall I’d pressed myself against without even realising. My muscles were tight, ready to attack the moment I saw anyone, but I did not.

My body sagged as I let out a sigh of relief and glanced in both directions. Both of the passageways, or whatever they were called, looked identical to the one I’d just been walking along. The walls were the same yellowed stone, and I could see nothing of note. They both appeared empty, and I had no idea which way to go.

Hesitation washed over me. I still didn’t know what I was trying to do or the stakes of the world. I wasn’t sure whether it was life or death or just fun, but the version of me who had been in the world when I arrived must have known. They’d lived there for longer than I had, and I pulled away from the world ever so slightly. I wasn’t trying to leave; I just wanted to let my body or whatever it was make the decision for me.

There was a moment of pause. The music playing around me grew distant, warped, and for a second, I did nothing, but then my hand twitched. The staff bumped gently against my tummy, and I bit my lip as I turned left.

I could feel my anxiety gradually rising as I walked, my head turning constantly to scan my surroundings. The longer I went without seeing anyone, the more worried I got. It felt like I should have seen them by that point. Especially with my face and location plastered on the screen floating above the maze. Anyone could have looked up and spotted me. Even if they had been on the other side of the maze, it felt like enough time had passed that they should have been able to hunt me down by then, but I wasn’t sure. There was no timer on the screen, no way to know how many hours it had been or how close I was to my goal.

My attention was so fixated on the maze around me and making sure I wasn’t being snuck up on, that I almost tripped once more. My toe caught on something, and I stumbled, but I refused to let the staff fall from my hands again. I clung to it as tightly as I could as I regained my balance and spun around, brandishing the weapon before me.

But there was no one there. I eyed the path, expecting to see some kind of trap or a tripwire waiting behind me, probably emitting gas or about to explode, but instead, a pair of shoes stood on the dirt. One lay on its side, probably having been knocked over when I’d kicked it, but there was nothing else there.

Equal parts confusion and anxiety pulled at me as I stared at the shoes, trying to work out what was going on and why they were there. I knew they must have been placed there for a reason. Everything in the maze was there for a reason, but I couldn’t work out what that was.

They looked just like normal trainers, though. I was almost certain I’d seen people at my school in reality wearing them, and I couldn’t see anything particularly special or interesting about the white fabric. If anything, they were kind of boring.

But there must have been something that I was missing. If they had been placed in the maze, they must have been able to give me some kind of buff or passive ability, I realised. If I equipped them, they could probably boost my speed stat or grant me some other kind of skill, like the rest of the weapons in the centre of the maze, and that tempted me.

Chewing on my bottom lip, I grappled with the urge to pull the shoes on as I glanced around, checking to make sure there was nothing but old stone walls surrounding me, before looking back at the trainers. I felt my eyebrows draw together as something pulled at me, making me glance up at the maze again.

Something had caught my attention, and I couldn’t quite put my finger on what it was at first, but I knew something had changed. It was… wrong.

My gaze moved slowly, scanning the light coloured dirt path I’d just been walking along before finding the walls. My confusion grew stronger as I stared at them. The once white stones on either side of me seemed to be growing darker before my eyes, and I couldn’t look away. It seemed like a shadow was slowly being cast over them, and I glanced up at the sky, checking whether a cloud had drifted in front of the sun, but the sky was still spotless. There were no clouds, but the walls were definitely getting darker.

I looked around again before stepping towards one of the walls. It felt foolish. I could feel the reluctance to do so pulling at me, trying to force me to move away again, but it was overpowered by my curiosity. I needed to know what was happening, even though that could get me killed. If I knew, I would be able to fight it. I could figure out what to do and how to combat it, I tried to tell myself, but that felt like a lie, an excuse designed to placate me.

Still, it was enough to quiet my mind so that I could focus on the darkening stone, and I leaned forward, my face moving close to it as I stared. The shadow was changing, I realised. It was turning… green?

Moss. There was moss growing on the walls around me. Within seconds, it had transformed from nothing more than a smudge on the light brick to a thick coat of moss, and I couldn’t help but watch, entranced, as the walls were overrun by nature.

The smell was changing too, I realised as I inhaled deeply. It had transformed just as much as the walls had, and the maze no longer smelled like the sun-baked streets that I was used to. It smelt more natural, more similar to the world I had come from, and I couldn’t help but breathe in again.

Somehow, the air was filled with the soothing scent of freshly cut grass. It was as if I were walking through a meadow. The perfume of grass, flowers, and nature filled my nose and brought a smile to my face. It was so soothing, so filled with promise, and I had to blink quickly to clear the tears from my eyes.

Why had the scent made me well up, I asked myself, but I did not know. Perhaps it was just how unexpected it was? Or perhaps that version of me had never actually been surrounded by so much greenery. For all I knew, she had only ever lived in a city. She may have never seen anything as vibrant as the leaves on the vines that had begun creeping over the top of the wall, stretching down towards the ground.

The path had begun to darken too, I realised as I watched one of the vines reach the dirt. Like the walls, a shadow had appeared on the ground, but it was not turning green. No moss blossomed on the floor. Instead, it just turned into… mud.

An unexpected giggle slipped from my lips as I watched a puddle appear in the centre of the path, and the urge to run towards it seized me. I longed to jump into the water with both feet, to feel it splashing up around me, and the desire was so strong that I felt myself take a step forward before I remembered the shoes.

I spun on my heel, almost slipping on the mud that had appeared beneath my feet, and had to grab onto the vines for balance. Thankfully, they were strong enough to hold my weight, and I quickly extracted my hand from the still-moving mass before glancing down at the shoes.

Hesitation washed over me, stopping me from reaching down towards them. The shoes were exactly where I’d left them. One still lay on its side, just as it had moments before, but they were untouched by the mud. I’d been worried that they would have been engulfed in it, dragged down and impossible to find, but they were untouched.

I could see the mud forming beneath them. It was shiny in the bright sunlight, showing just how fresh and wet it should have been, but no mud had stained the perfect white fabric of the shoes, and they rested perfectly atop the surface. It seemed almost as if they were floating there.

Suspicion pulled at me, and I edged backwards, moving away from them. The thick mud pulled at my boot, trying to steal it from my foot, and I had to look down, carefully extracting it so that I wouldn’t be left barefoot.

It felt like too much of a coincidence, I realised slowly. The fact that there were shoes there, that they were so perfect and tempting, and that the mud had tried to steal mine, was too good timing. It felt like someone had orchestrated it, like they had planted the shoes there, knowing they would distract me so that they could make the vines grow and the mud appear, and then they’d trap me. Maybe they wanted me to be forced to put the trainers on for some reason, or perhaps the moment I touched them, I’d be ensnared and unable to move or let go until they appeared.

I wasn’t sure, but I knew I couldn’t do it. I had to get out of there, and I turned, moving more carefully than before. The mud ahead was thick, though. It looked deep, but I didn’t want to turn back. If it was a trap, and I was pretty sure it was, they probably wanted me to turn around. Perhaps they were trying to force me to confront them, and I didn’t want to risk that.

But the mud at the edges of the path wasn’t as deep, I noticed. It looked almost dry in places, and I didn’t hesitate before stepping towards the nearest side. I was right, thankfully. The mud didn’t reach the top of my foot, but it was just as slippery. My foot slid, my boot unable to find grip, and my hand shot out, grabbing a fistful of the vines once more.

My heart leapt into my throat, and I froze, my eyes fixed on the plants around my hands. They were still growing, thick tendrils stretching out over the walls so quickly that it looked as though the walls were writhing, but the cluster beneath my hand was motionless.

Still, I didn’t trust it. I knew the growth was not natural. I had not heard anyone shouting a spell, but someone must have done it. The walls had been bare less than a couple of minutes ago and the floor dry, and it was not normal for plants to grow that quickly, but there was no way for me to make it across the mud without holding onto them for balance.

I moved as quickly as I could, my eyes darting between the mud in front of me, the maze behind me, and the vines to the side. I expected to see someone appear or for the vines to reach for my wrist or ankle, trapping me and pulling me into the wall. I could almost feel them creeping across my skin as they grew over me, covering me completely before their roots pierced my flesh. They’d burrow deeper and deeper, absorbing my blood, my energy, and my life, and I would be left with nothing.

But that didn’t happen. Whenever my hand touched the plants, they fell still around me, and I had to believe that was a good sign. I clung to the hope that it meant I would survive, that the maze wanted me to prove myself. Perhaps it had already decided that I was worthy.

After what felt like an eternity, the ground became more solid beneath my feet. My boots didn’t even slide as I took my next step, but I didn’t risk stopping just yet. I forced myself to continue onwards for a few more paces before glancing around in search of whoever had just tried to capture me.

But I saw no one. The maze was still empty, but I must have been missing something. There must have been someone there, someone that I couldn’t see for some reason, and my head snapped from side to side, searching for them, before my gaze rose to the top of the walls around me.

They were empty too, but something else caught my eye, and I felt my mouth fall open as I stared at the screen. I was still being featured on it, but that wasn’t what had shocked me. It was the sight of the maze around where I stood.

Greenery was slowly spreading out in all directions, but it was clear that it was centred around where I had been moments before. Even on the giant screen, I could still see the white dot of the trainers I had left behind, but the walls on either side of them were entirely covered in vines, and it didn’t stop there. The vines had begun to reach into the maze, slowly engulfing the passageway on either side of the one I was in, creating new walls and dead ends.

They were trying to trap me, I realised as I watched a wall of thick vines materialise in the middle of the next path over. Whoever it was that had caused the plants to grow in the first place had realised I’d escaped the mud, and they were trying to cut off my escape. They wanted me to be stuck there with no way out so that it would be easy for them to hunt me down, but I refused to let that happen.

Still clutching my staff, I dove forward, breaking into a run.
