Sometimes, Jessie regretted being unawakened in a world with people as powerful as her brother. He’d always been stronger than her, physically speaking. That hadn’t changed so much as the gulf between someone like him and someone like her had gotten bigger. And yeah, there were other types of power. She had a network of internet friends who knew things Kade couldn’t even begin to understand about computers. She had more political power than all but a handful of people, awakened or not. She was plenty powerful.

But at the same time…

What would the world be like without delvers?

She thought about that sometimes, when her brother was doing some stupid crap. It was insane to have an entire subset of people in a city who could, without any real consequences, level that city. The only people keeping the S-Rankers in check were the S-Rankers themselves—and they were all maniacs in one way or another.

For example, Vern Kellen’s first thought when she’d listed Kade’s achievements hadn’t been to acknowledge them, compare them to his own, and use his words like the school counselors tried to get kindergärtners to do. It hadn’t been to negotiate with the Governing Council, or to back down. He’d gone straight from zero miles an hour to one hundred—straight from talking to fighting.

And Kade had rolled with it. That was very Kade. Of course, Kade was more of a maniac than the average S-Ranker. Angelo Lawrence had been up there, but Kade was a whole different breed.

“It’s stupid, isn’t it?”

Jessie turned her head slowly. Everything hurt. Councilwoman Harriet Myers sat next to her, staring with eagle eyes at the fighting. “They’re supposed to be the protectors of Phoenix, and of Earth, but as soon as there’s an opportunity, the weapons come out. The entire point of the Governing Council was supposed to be avoiding this kind of pointless violence and redirecting it into the portals where it belongs.”

Something cracked. The Dad-shaped lightning avatar appeared, slamming his weapons down onto Vern Kellen’s aura. Jessie winced.

“It’s worked for years,” Myers said sadly. Then she turned to face Jessie. “Your brother’s holding back, isn’t he?”

“Yes,” Jessie said.

Myers frowned. “That’s what I thought. He’s going to disrupt the whole balance of power. Are you ready to deal with that?”

Jessie didn’t have an answer.

The sparring match ended pretty much how I’d expected it to.

Vern Kellen was tough, but either he was holding back, too, or he didn’t have a chance of handling me. I was…better…than him in every way. Faster, stronger, tougher. More magic. More tools. It wasn’t just the difference between S-Rank through Paragon cores and S-Rank through Path advancement. It was everything.

In the aftermath, he vanished. I’d hoped to talk to him, but he didn’t give me the opportunity. That was fine, though. We’d gotten what we wanted, and Jessie wouldn’t stop beaming through the pain, even when I had to leave and get her chair. Once she was settled in, she shot another grin at Myers. “I don’t have an answer for your question. I might not ever have one. But I need an answer to mine. Can we announce this now?”

Myers looked defeated, but simultaneously victorious. It was hard to get a read on the woman or her machinations—she was probably a few steps ahead of Jessie, but I had no idea what those steps were or if we’d need to counter them. “You’re fine with the announcement happening before we get those guns for you?”

“Yes,” Jessie said. “We need things moving, and we need them moving as soon as possible. Ellen explained the situation, right?”

I glanced at my partner, raising an eyebrow.

Ellen shrugged sheepishly. “It was the best way to get them to understand.”

“Yes. We’ll do the announcement in half an hour, in front of the GC building,” Councilwoman Myers said. “I’ll get the announcement out so we have media coverage, then work on a statement. Terrel, you’re on camera. You too, Kade. Non-negotiable.”

I sighed. “Fine.”

For the next thirty minutes, we watched as the space just past the tower’s revolving doors transformed into a stage, lectern, and gaggle of reporters—most from news organizations associated with Ellen’s dad, but a few from the handful of independent presses. A major announcement on short notice could only mean a serious scoop, and clearly, none of the channels wanted to lose out to the others. The Phoenix flag hung behind the podium, flapping in the hot mid-morning breeze, as Councilwoman Myers kicked the announcement off.

“Hello, and thank you for getting here on short notice. As you’ve probably noticed, the city of Phoenix is down a pair of S-Rankers. These heroes gave their lives to ensure that the Carlsbad Portal Break was defeated and a dagger aimed at not only Phoenix, but the Texas cities, was removed from our world. We will honor them at the proper time. However…

“The deaths of The Light of Dawn and Bernard the Wall leave two of the major guilds substantially weakened, and there have been rumors that the Roadrunners and Coyotes may struggle to fill their defense obligations. In order to fill the void left by those S-Rankers, the Governing Council has entered into an arrangement with the Tier Three guild known as the Desert Wind.”

She adjusted her notes. “The Desert Wind occupies the Surprise area, and can therefore offer assistance to the Roadrunners’ territories as needed. They are also, delver for delver, one of the strongest guilds in Phoenix, with access to two S-Rankers and another A-Ranker on the cusp of ascension. Further, they are backed up by the Portal Tyrants and the Phoenix Reborn.

“This alliance between the Governing Council, Portal Tyrants, and Desert Wind is meant to help keep Phoenix safe and stable while the 303 Wall is rebuilt, we reevaluate the world in the aftermath of the Carlsbad Portal’s closure, and until such time as one or both of the Roadrunners or Coyotes returns to their former strength—or until another guild takes their place in the top five. This is not the beginning of a guild war, and should not be treated as such.”

Councilwoman Myers breathed in slowly. “The Governing Council continues to be, and will always be, a check against the guilds’ power. We are the allies of Phoenix, and of humanity, and in entering into this agreement, the Desert Wind and Portal Tyrants reaffirm that they are as well. Thank you.”

The questions poured in, and I was thrilled that all I had to do was stand there and look intimidating for the camera. Myers fielded most of them, with only a handful leaking over to Terrel Young. Without Jessie on the Podium, the councilwoman took all the questions aimed at the Desert Wind Guild. That was a relief; Jessie was already pushing herself far too hard.

And then, after almost twenty minutes, Councilwoman Myers held up a hand. “Unfortunately, there is much to do. The Desert Wind and Portal Tyrants both have portals to clear and monsters to kill, and the Governing Council continues its work in the aftermath of the Siege. Thank you for your time.”

Then she slid through the revolving doors, and we followed her back up the elevator and to the conference room. Only after the door did she collapse into a chair. “I hate talking to the press. ‘How will this affect portal clears in Surprise?’ ‘What does this mean for independent delvers operating in former Roadrunner territory?’ ‘Do you intend to build a statue in honor of the Light of Dawn?’ It’s the same every time.”

Then, before I could respond, she turned to me, eyes hawklike again. “You’re aware that you’re going to ruin everything, right?”

I blinked.

“I’m serious, Delver Noelstra. The entire system we’ve developed is based on a few assumptions. First, that S-Rank is the pinnacle. Second, that all S-Rankers are more or less equal. Obviously, one like the Light of Dawn is stronger than someone like Vern Kellen—though he couldn’t give it his all there, just like you. But the only reason the Big Five guilds shared power so willingly was that we’d convinced everyone involved that they were on equal footing.”

Jessie snorted. “You had a five-way cold war going, and you were calling it stability?”

“Yes,” Myers said.

Ellen stared at her briefly, then cleared her throat. “Why tell us that?”

“Because it’s over,” Nathan Anders said. “It’s bad enough that we’ve seen SS-Ranked monsters now, but some of them are Paragons. The Archqueen of Madness is impossible to root out of the Amazon. The Carlsbad Portal Break lasted for literal years, even against the best Phoenix and the Texas cities could throw at it. We’ve been planning for this since you brought us to meet the Crone. Preparing for this guild war. Hopefully, it’s not a bad one.”

Jessie’s face shifted from incredulous amusement to seriousness over the course of about three seconds. “You’ve expected this?”

“Yes,” Anders said. “If monsters can hit SS-Rank, there’s no reason delvers couldn’t, too. We didn’t know it’d be Kade, but all five guilds have been trying to figure out how to do it. Not that we’d make much progress in less than three weeks, but if it’s possible, every guild wanted to have the first one. The writing’s been on the wall for the GC since that day.”

“Right,” I said quietly.

In high school, we’d studied the United States of America and the post-World War Two world. The Cold War, with the Soviet Union. The race to build ever-larger atomic weapons. Angelo had been one of the first bombs, and he’d been the most powerful. I hadn’t thought about what the power I had would mean for the rest of the city.

“Why not take the path forward from me?” I asked carefully.

Terrel rolled his eyes. “You just wrecked another S-Ranker, and I know at least two abilities you never attempted to use. There’s a world where three or four of us could beat you, but that doesn’t mean you’d give us your secrets. Shit. I should have been more aggressive about getting you into the Tyrants. I could be an SS-Ranker by now.”

“You couldn’t. Sorry. And I’m not planning on sharing my method of reaching S-Rank with anyone until I’ve had a chance to figure out the risks.” I didn’t mention that I’d give Ellen that information the second she was ready, without reservation, and no one looked her way, but everyone in the room knew it was going to happen. I could tell. “Now, if you’ll excuse us, we’ve got a war against some extremely powerful monsters to plan and win.”

“Get those cannons to us as quickly as you can. Tonight would be good. Tomorrow morning would be acceptable. The Desert Wind will pay what’s necessary,” Jessie said. Then I wheeled her out of the conference room, and we rode the elevator down to the waiting Deimos. We piled in and rode in silence until we were back in Surprise. But it didn’t stay silent for long after that.

“What happens when someone on Earth hits SS-Rank?” Jessie asked as the AI-driven car worked its way toward the Desert Wind’s headquarters.

I swallowed. “Two possibilities. Neither of them are good.”

“What’s the best outcome?” she pressed.

“Well, Retan. If we get lucky, the SS-Ranker starts to be driven by a need to leave this universe, and they start gathering the materials to ascend. That gets worse once they hit SS-Plus-Rank, and it turns into an all-consuming need. Earth becomes collateral damage in their quest to break through and ascend. That means every delver on Earth becomes fuel for their project. Then, when they do leave, that process breaks part of the universe behind them.”

“Oh.” Silence, other than Deimos’s engine revving. “That’s almost demonic. And what’s the other one?”

“Our world can’t handle a being of that power level, either when they hit SS-Rank or SS-Plus, and the earth literally comes apart.”

“Seriously?”

“Seriously. The God of Thunder was in our world just long enough to drop Ellen off for the healers before he left, and there were immediate earthquakes across Phoenix.”

Even more silence.

This time, it lasted until Deimos pulled into the parking garage. Just before we got out, Jessie reached up and touched my shoulder. “Are you planning on hitting SS-Rank?”

“No.”

“Good. What about other people?”

“I can’t speak for anyone else,” I said. “That’s why I can’t let my method for reaching S-Rank get out. Ellen’s the only one I’m going to give it to, and that’s because—“

“We’re partners, and we’re in it for the long haul,” Ellen said. “I think we can find a way to avoid SS-Rank, Kade. Once we’ve dealt with the God of Thunder and the Placid King, that needs to be our priority.”

“I’ll talk to the Crone about it,” Jessie said. “If she told you all of this, she’s probably got some ideas. But in the meantime, there’s one more thing I’d like your help with, Kade. It won’t take long, I promise. Half an hour, and you’ll be back to getting Ellen stronger, or whatever it is you’re doing in your super-secret portal.”

All I wanted to do was start hunting for a portal or two. The sooner Ellen reached S-Rank—or that we were able to push our Dual Skill up another rank—the sooner I’d feel comfortable having her in the same world as me when the Placid King attacked. I needed her help, too, because as much fun as it had been to fight Vern Kellen with limited skills, an SS-Plus monster was a whole different animal.

But Ellen grabbed my wrist as I opened the car door. “You should hear her out before you go rushing off. Remember, she’s your sister, and she’s doing a lot of work for you already.”

“Okay,” I said.

“Great. So, the Desert Wind Guild’s gotten about ninety delver applicants in the last twenty-four hours. I was hoping—“

“No. Absolutely not.”

“Come on! At least let me finish my ask before you say no,” Jessie whined. I wilted under her look, and under Ellen’s, and my sister continued. “I was hoping you could help me handle one that’s kind of tricky. He’d be a good addition to a C/B/A team, but…Well, I can’t handle him the same way I want to handle the others.”

“And how do you want to handle ninety guild applicants?” I asked.

“Oh, you know? Bring in Jeff, Sophia, and maybe Yasmin, hold job interviews, and pick the best candidates. The usual process,” Jessie said. She opened her door and got out, wobbling a little with her cane, and I hurried to the back and unfolded her wheelchair.

“And what makes this candidate so special?”

“His name.” She sat down. “Thanks. You might know him as Caleb Richter or as Carter Richards, but either way, you need to be involved in deciding whether to bring him into the guild or not. I can’t do it on my own.”

“And he’s here now?” I asked, fists tightening against my will.

“Yes. He got here twenty minutes ago. He’s waiting in the lobby.”

I unballed my fists, then wrapped my hands around Jessie’s handlebars. One deep breath. Two. Breathe until I had myself under control. Five. Six. Then, at least, I started pushing the chair. “Alright. Fine. Let’s see what ‘Caleb Richter’ has to say for himself.”
