“So you were right,” Ryland said to the Oracle.

She didn’t reply at first, only looking at the Juren gathering with her blindfolded eyes. The light behind the blindfold seemed all the more intense in the darkness of the [Void Seal].

“We’ve answered everything you’ve asked,” the first Juren mage said. “Please, keep your end of the bargain. You must let us go free!”

Ryland was pleased to hear the please. Such a far cry from his mannerisms when they had first met, though he still retained some of his old arrogance. Still. It was nice to see him humbled.

And it was true, they had extracted a handy amount of information from the man. From the entire congregation, to be exact. They had learned the titles and names of their superiors, of the people said superiors met with and were generally seen to be influenced by, on who and who else had been targeted by all this.

“Letting you go would be foolish, wouldn’t you agree?” the Oracle said.

“But that isn’t what we agreed!” the man said, trying and failing to summon up enough outrage. There was far too much resignation in his tone for it to surface properly. “Would a holy woman such as yourself lie to us?”

“Lies count for naught when they occur against those committing misdeeds such as yourself. Lies in support of the greater good are never truly malicious.”

“Stop preaching to us.”

Ryland shook his head. “I have no qualms against letting them go. After all, if they wish to leave, they must abide by the conditions I will lay out for them.”

“Perhaps it will not cause issues,” the Oracle said. “But have faith. Those outside have what it takes to handle matters for the time being.”

Ryland didn’t press the issue. Admittedly, he was a little worried about what was going on all over his Inscription. His Glyph of Detection and other Glyphs had noted the presence of foreign entities, had heard the clash of aggressive sorceries, and had felt the desperation of bloody battle spiking through the air.

But if the Oracle didn’t foresee any real danger, then perhaps he could relax for the time being. After all, he had already passed the most pertinent information on to Devas.

However, there wasn’t much left for them to discuss or deal with regarding these Juren mages.

Except for one thing—hostages.

“There is no point,” the first Juren mage said, having already sensed why Ryland and the Oracle were refusing to just let them leave. “You’ve already sensed that the others are attacking, haven’t you?” He seized it as if it was a point via which he could regain some sort of advantage. “You could have prevented it, you know. We didn’t need to come to blows.”

“You’re a fool if you think we would ever believe such a thing,” Ryland said. “But you do have a point. They certainly don’t seem to care enough to figure out your condition before launching their assault.”

Besides all that, this was further proof that their adversaries weren’t just the Flourinus Order. The Great Gods had a great deal to pull from.

“Yes,” another of the Juren mages insisted. “Which is why this is pointless.”

A part of Ryland tended to agree with that assessment, but another part of him was slowly coming up with another plan. One where the Juren mages in his presence could play a very important part…

“Why are you helping this blasphemous tyrant?” a third Juren mage asked, shoving himself to the front to face the Oracle. Ah. There came the zealotry. Vertha had already accused and determined that they were religiously motivated. The Great Gods had their hooks in these mages, one way or another. “He’s a monster! A creature who has caused untold devastation!”

“Is that so?” the Oracle asked.

“Yes! His infamy stretches all across the Accorded Realms. His name echoes along with every warning we have ever heard.”

Ryland wasn’t going to lie. He too was curious why the Oracle had chosen to assist him, when she should clearly have been on the side of the Great Gods. Yes, she had mentioned something about stemming the tides of destruction and that she believed Ryland’s heart lay in the right place.

But still. Was she really content with that justification when it came to explaining her stance to the rest of the cosmos?

“I have mistakes I need to make up for,” the Oracle said. “Mistakes I won’t make again, for this is far too serious for me to act unwisely.” Her eyes sharpened on the mage facing her. “But please, name one such infamy that you seem to be accusing Marionys Ryland of.”

Ryland suppressed a wince. Intelligent entities would have no trouble picking one from a long list of incidents Ryland had been a part of. For while he had never willingly caused massive destruction out of malice, a less scrupulous accuser could simply pick one of dozens of incidents like the Living Mana Incursion on Arcoryx City that he himself had stopped.

A smarter individual could just point at the state of Rhel as a consequence of allowing Ryland’s powers to flourish unchecked.

But the Juren mage faltered.

“I, um…” He shook his head, trying to regain his earlier determination. “I have seen a great many things! Light that ruptured entire worlds. Storms that raged for millennia. Calamities that caused civilizations to end.” The man pointed an accusing finger at Ryland. “He is the source of much evil!”

“Seen,” the Oracle said rather perceptively before Ryland could point it out himself. “Where exactly have you seen these things?”

“Um, in my… I saw them as visions! Recollections of the past. Prophecies of the future! If we do not act now, then we will doom the rest of the Accorded Realms. If we don’t stop this madness, then we—”

He suddenly realized that everyone within [Void Seal] was staring at him, and not in a kind way. The man spoke of madness and then proceeded to exhibit just what it looked like. Plus, he had already blown his cover. All that talk of visions and everything else was merely proof of just what the Oracle had been saying from the very beginning.

These Juren mages, knowingly or unknowingly, were indeed affected by the manipulation of the Great Gods.

“Let’s come to a decision,” Ryland said. He was speaking democratically, but in truth, he was the only one coming to a decision. The rest of them were simply bound to follow it. “Help me stop the ongoing chaos, and I will let you go.”

“You’ve already promised us once,” the first Juren mage said. “Why should we trust you now?”

“Well, what other choice do you have?”

No one said anything to that.

Ryland nodded. “Good.” The plan he had been fomenting earlier now became crystal clear in his head. “Then let’s begin.”

The appearance of the other mages had quickly forced Ophelia to go on the defensive. She might have stood her ground against a Primordial-ranked mage for some time, but that was in a one-on-one scenario. With multiple mages reinforcing her opponent, she had no hope of surviving by herself, much less fighting toe to toe.

Good thing she had Devas to count on. It was almost annoying how reliable the Turnalian was turning out to be.

“[Saviour’s Bulwark],” Ophelia incanted. Her Soul Glyph of Vanguard enforced her with resurgent power, making her armour and shield sizzle with a gleaming aura.

That helped her tank the spells flying in from above. Boulders glowing with a silvery sheen crashed and shattered against her shield. Waves of liquid earth trying to mire just got repelled by her near-solid aura. Her sword sliced through fireballs, deflected lightning bolts, and pummelled back physical blows from an assortment of suicidal summons.

In other words, Ophelia was very much on the back foot, but she was managing to stay alive. She was keeping the mages’ attention on her too.

Which allowed Devas to try and hammer in with potshots.

“[Rent],” he incanted while flying high in the air, the wake of his passage leaving behind exploding, shockwave-inducing tails. The mages attacking them were constantly forced to disperse and seek new locations from which they could continue casting.

That afforded Ophelia some room to constantly call upon the power of [Unrivalled Call], which allowed her to strike back from afar. She’d have preferred to rely on [Stalwart’s Retaliation], but this was a ranged battle for her. Not ideal but she could make it work. She had to.

The problem was the first mage who had attacked. The Primordial-ranked prick cast some sort of strange, living Inscription that moved on its own before attacking, only activating when they were close to their targets. Very sneaky. Very annoying.

It was all just a massive mess, really.

Ophelia was struggling to find a way through their stalemate. The only reason their first opponent hadn’t managed to deal a debilitating blow was because his reinforcements weren’t particularly powerful. Ophelia was certain the strongest among them were barely Titan-ranked at best.

And yet, that wasn’t letting them make a breakthrough. That wasn’t allowing her to deal a blow that would end this battle.

It didn’t seem to bother Devas. He was happy to toy with them, happy to have them desperately defend against his spatial magic before they were forced to disperse at his far greater power. In fact, the longer this went on, the more Ophelia grew certain that he could have taken them all on by himself if he wanted to.

Instead, he just wanted to play cat and mouse with people who had nothing but nefarious intentions. Maybe once this was over, she could try and drill some sense into the man, but then, he was a legendary Turnalian. Who was she to tell him what to do?

“[Space Clasp],” Devas incanted. “[Warped Realm]!”

A tidal wave of broken space tried to corral the opposing mages into one location before he unleashed an actual beam of what felt like the energy released from breaking all that spatial matter. The strongest of their opponents forgot all about Ophelia and raised an Inscription which successfully blocked it. Not perfectly or permanently, but just long enough for the rest to escape.

The Inscription shattered, spraying its lines all over the battlefield while the surging laser of energy carved a fathomless trench through the Inscription on the ground. The one that Ryland had left.

Ah. He was not going to appreciate that. This also explained why the Turnalian was constantly holding back against their opponents.

But that just meant it was Ophelia’s turn. She was quick to recognize opportunities in a fracas such as this, so she did her best to capitalize.

“[Unrivalled Call]!” she shouted.

The violent swing of her sword unleashed a wave of power and light, slashing through the intervening space to reach one of the unwary reinforcement mages. It worked! Her slash cut into his back, sending him flying to the ground with a scream tearing from his throat. The robes he wore tore apart, revealing armour underneath with a bright crest she faintly recognized.

Where was that from? She was sure she had seen it before, and not that long ago either.

The intensifying battle dragged her thoughts away. Now, Ophelia was forced to endure an even worse cavalcade of magic, which crashed upon her like an avalanche. Even with [Saviour’s Bulwark], she barely managed to remain standing under the onslaught.

Even Devas wasn’t having much luck. The fall of the first mage had incensed the rest, and now they hounded after Devas as one with tremendously practiced coordination. He was having little luck repelling them despite his greater power on paper.

All of which shifted again when Marionys Ryland decided to show himself.

A thunderous crack split the air, and a presence that seemed to weigh on them all appeared. His presence. Ryland had arrived, and his aura swamped everyone.

It forced the battle to come to a grinding halt. Devas and the attacking mages disengaged, while Ophelia finally had some reprieve. She even felt like she’d be perfectly fine if she dispelled [Saviour’s Bulwark], though long years of battle prevented her from doing so.

“What…?” It was only the second time since the battle had started that the Primordial-ranked infiltrator spoke. “What have you done?”

Ophelia tore her gaze away from the offending mages to check up on Ryland. Her eyes widened.

He hadn’t come alone. Several more mages had come with him too, though not all of them. Just their decapitated heads dangling by their hair from his hand.

Ophelia swallowed. Just how many insurgents had been active here? Why hadn’t she sensed—

She gasped. Death had cast the faces of the bodyless heads into a terrible state but that didn’t mean she would fail to recognize who they were. One of those was the Juren mage who had come in with those refugees. What had they done to incense Ryland to this level?

Oh! The crest. Now she knew where she had recognized the crest on the fallen mage. Things were starting to fall into place now…

“Enough,” Ryland said. His imperious voice landed on the assaulting mages like a heavy waterfall from the peak of a mountain. “You’ve done enough. I will warn you just this once—depart Rhel this instant or face the same fate as your allies.”

Going by the Juren mages’ reaction, it seemed Ryland wasn’t bluffing. Those really were their comrades’ heads.

Ophelia wasn’t sure how to feel about this. Ryland had never been temperamental. In fact, one of his strongest and possibly most underappreciated qualities was his ever-present equanimity. She had never seen him get properly angry, certainly never vengeful enough to ruthlessly kill his opponents.

Even when they had captured that first infiltrator the other day, Ryland had refused to kill him in an effort to learn more from him.

So what had caused him to become so mercilessly final today?

“You…” The Primordial-ranked mage was shaking where he floated. Rather than being cowed, he was growing increasingly enraged at the fate of his companions. “You murdered them in cold blood!”

“That is what one does when intruders assault one’s home,” Ryland said. His glimmering gold eyes held nothing but contempt. “But it seems you’re intent on ignoring my warning. Perhaps I should do the same as I’ve done to one or more of you before you get the message.”

“You will not. Not while I stand. Your evil intentions on Rhel will not be allowed to come to fruition!”

Evil intentions? What sort of mad command was this man following? The Oracle had suggested that there were deeper motivations at hand here.

“Fine, then,” Ryland said. The pressure emanating from him started intensifying, growing frighteningly stronger every second. “Have it your way.”

Before he could cast a spell, however, the enemy mages had already acted. Their bodies glowed, power swirling with thicker and thicker strands around them. Something strange was happening, and though Ophelia couldn’t quite determine what it was, her senses tingled like she was being injected with the golden, miasmic mana sprouting from the mages.

But for all her alarm, Ryland simply looked on without much concern. He seemed entirely uncaring of what they were attempting.

Even when the world cracked, bleeding virulent auric power the likes of which Ophelia had never seen before.

“Now you die, Ryland,” the first mage said. “[Divine Avatar]!”
