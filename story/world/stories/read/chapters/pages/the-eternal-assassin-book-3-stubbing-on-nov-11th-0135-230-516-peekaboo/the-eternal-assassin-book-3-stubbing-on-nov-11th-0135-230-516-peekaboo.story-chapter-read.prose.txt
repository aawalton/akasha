One of the questions that had been rattling around within Asher’s mind was answered immediately upon entering the lich’s lair. He’d been curious if the tower that he’d been originally summoned to was the lich’s main base of operations and this mountain location was a backup, or if it were the other way around.

He quickly realized the tower they’d all escaped from months ago was the equivalent of the lich’s ramshackled shack in the backyard.

The moment they stepped through the shattered remains of the wooden door Rulfar had broken down, the cold, dreary stone of the cave instantly gave way to gleaming marble floors and warm gemstone lights. From the barebones interior of the lich’s old tower, Asher had assumed that the undead wizard didn’t care for aesthetics in the slightest, but it seemed that wasn’t quite the case. Though rather than paintings or sculptures, the lich seemed to have a fascination with old suits of armor and ancient weapons. None of them were magical, according to Asher’s Identify, and nearly all of them were actually heavily damaged. They seemed more like pieces of gear one found scavenging the remains of a giant battlefield after a few decades of rain based on the deterioration, and Asher finally paused by one suit of armor that was barely still clinging together.

“Why all the rusted suits of armor?” he whispered, unable to make heads or tails of what made all these pieces so important. “I mean, look at this! It goes on for the entire hallway!”

“I don’t know, but it isn’t rust that damaged the metal,” Rulfar said, examining an old axe with a critical eye. “It is something else… Something beyond me.”

“In case you guys have forgotten, we’re not here to look at old weapons and armor,” Moxy hissed, frowning at them as she motioned for them to continue moving. “I can’t punch a decaying sword!”

Nodding, Asher put the strange arsenal of equipment behind him as they continued walking. Or at least, he tried to, but the suits of armor and hung weaponry continued on throughout the entire hallway. He wasn’t able to keep himself from staring at the different pieces as they walked by, and he noticed something else odd about a few of them.

They’d occasionally pass a weapon or helmet that clearly had a gemstone inlaid in the metal, as though it were enchanted. But according to Identify, those pieces were as magic-free as all the rest. Either they were weapons and armor that had been prepared to take on an enchantment and simply never received one, or they had somehow had their enchantments stripped from them.

While he was busy trying to solve the mystery of the lich’s strange decorating style, the hallway eventually came to an end, revealing a beautiful, ornate door standing before them. Though rather than another wooden door like the one Rulfar had so kindly opened for them, this one was made from what looked like marble at first glance. It wasn’t until Asher looked more closely that he shuddered.

[Ivory Door] - Masterwork

“It’s ivory,” he said, a chill going down his spine as he stared at the piece of ivory larger than he was. “I don’t know about you guys, but I don’t want to meet the monster that something like this came from.”

“Everybody ready?” Rulfar asked, hefting his axe as he took a deep breath. “This hallway feels like it’s just some sort of entrance. Whatever the lich truly has in store for us, it’ll be behind this door.”

“Ready,” Samantha nodded, a firm look on her face as she prepared herself for anything.

“More than ready,” Moxy grinned, hopping on the balls of her feet and shaking out her arms. “Come on, throw that bad boy open!”

“We’re still going for stealth for as long as possible, so no battle cries or explosions if we can help it,” Asher reminded her, ignoring her eye-roll as he stepped up to the door. Seeing as this one wasn’t enchanted, he slipped into the astral and stuck his head through.

Coming face-to-face with the jet-black teeth of a grinning greater demon.

Not even Comprehend gave him time to react. The greater demon, who had been crouching down directly on the other side of the door, had to have known they were coming. The moment Asher peered through the door, the demon snapped its massive claws around his head, yanking him fully through the door as it laughed. The fact that Asher was in the astral realm meant absolutely nothing to the being that inhabited the material, the astral, and the realm of ages all at the same time. Asher immediately went to lash out with his daggers in panic, but the demon’s other hand snapped forward and grabbed both his wrists, halting the attack as easily as if they were just playfighting. Asher did his best to twist out of the demon’s vice-like grip, when the crushing pain of his head nearly being crushed like a grape within the demon’s powerful claws made him stop.

“Would you look at that, you did come right to us without us having to do a damn thing,” the demon chuckled, its forked tongue slipping out from between its black teeth as it licked its lips. “Normally, I’d be a tad irritated at the fact that our summoning didn’t include any allowances for slaughtering innocents. But you are a special case. Your skill has thrown the nine hells into something of an uproar, and it can not be allowed to continue.”

“You say that, but you haven’t killed me yet,” Asher pointed out even as he felt the warmth of his own blood dripping down his face from the demon’s claws. He prayed that his team would rush through the door after him any moment now when he failed to return from the astral, but it could be entire minutes before they grew concerned. All of them knew that so long as he was just looking around he could stay within the astral for as long as he wanted thanks to his Ring of False Death, and none of them had ever considered that a greater demon might take advantage of him peeking through a door to launch a surprise attack.

“Very astute of you,” the demon grinned, licking its lips again as if it was even now contemplating simply tearing his head off and eating it. “We have our reasons, but you don’t need to worry about those. And your friends certainly won’t have to either.”

As the greater demon turned and began walking away from the ivory door with Asher firmly within its grasp, Asher gasped as he finally got a look at the room they were currently standing in. It was large, like a small auditorium, empty of anything even closely resembling furniture or a stage. No, the room was filled with one thing and one thing only.

Demons.

As he and the greater demon snapped back to the material realm, Asher could only stare at what had to be at least fifty lesser demons jostling one another as they stared at the ivory door, clearly waiting for some sort of signal. There were more than twice their number in imps running around as well, hissing at one another and occasionally getting backhanded from the impatient lesser demons when one of them was bumped into. Seeing so many demons simply standing there, waiting with both annoyance and hunger on their faces, made his heart skip a beat as he realized exactly what it was they were waiting for.

“As soon as they break down the door…” the greater demon said, walking through the crowd as the lesser demons parted before him. “...kill them.”

Rather than shout or curse or beg, Asher activated Comprehend’s second-tier effect, slowing time to a crawl as his mind raced for a way out of his current situation. With one set of the demon’s claws firmly latched around his head and the other holding his wrists, he was trapped. He couldn’t slip into the astral to escape, as the greater demon also existed within the astral realm. And unlike Clare’s freaky binding chain skill, this wasn’t some sort of effect he could purge with Complete Resilience. Recall was off the table as well, as the greater demon’s impossible weight was firmly locked into place, preventing him from twisting the fabric of reality to that degree. He briefly contemplated trying to slip into his Personal Rift, but he could tell without even trying that the demon’s weight would prevent him from doing that as well. As his mind bounced from one idea to the next, his heart sank as he came to a horrifying conclusion.

There wasn’t a damn thing he could do.

Now Asher began shouting at the greater demon, but nothing he said mattered. His curses and promises to end the demon’s long life were met with nothing more than a chilling black grin, the demon strangely not even wasting the breath to respond. It appeared this greater demon was far less talkative than the previous one he’d fought and killed, which didn’t exactly bode well for his chances of getting it to release him.

Seeing as the greater demon’s hands were full, one of the lesser demons broke away from the rest of the pack and opened the door for them, following them as they continued further into the lich’s true base of operations. Asher barely even looked around, his focus entirely on the grinning face of his captor as he was dragged farther and farther from his team. While the greater demon hadn’t ripped off his wrists for whatever reason, it was still carrying Asher along by the head, which was far from a pleasant experience.

After a handful of twists and turns, the lesser demon opened one last door for them before turning and running back to the horde of others waiting to ambush Asher’s team. Ducking under the too-small doorframe, the greater demon walked into what appeared to be some sort of foyer. The marble floor had been replaced by lush carpet, and a small, crackling fireplace cast warm light across the finely crafted furniture carefully placed throughout the room. Based on the old china lining the mantel and beautiful paintings dotting the walls, Asher could have tricked himself into thinking he was simply in some rich person’s study, sitting down for a cup of tea.

If it weren’t for the two liches sitting before the fireplace, each one turning to stare at him with small, burning fires in their own eyes.

Asher’s breath caught in his throat as he recognized the first of the two liches immediately. The dark purple robe and matching purple fires burning in its eye sockets were a sight he’d never forget for as long as he lived. The gnarled wooden staff leaning against the couch beside the walking corpse was further proof this was the exact lich that had summoned him from his own world, and as he and the greater demon entered the room, the lich motioned for the demon to come and stand before them.

“Fascinating,” the lich said, the fires in its sockets seeming to burn brighter for a second as the lich stared at him. “The subject’s growth over the past two months is incredible. As I hypothesized, allowing the subject to progress and grow on its own has allowed it to advance far more quickly than it would have under controlled conditions.”

“Don’t act like this was all part of your plan, Zygrul,” the second lich snapped, fidgeting with the tiara on her head as she stared at him as well. Unlike Zygrul’s simple robe, this second lich looked as though it was some sort of fallen queen. She wore a large, puffy dress that had so many layers of fabric Asher couldn’t help but wonder if the style at whatever time she’d died had been half created to stop assassination attempts, as he wasn’t sure if a regular blade would even be capable of stabbing through so much fabric.

“I merely came here to finally have my phylactery returned to me!” the lich demanded, the fires in her own eyes burned a bright pink as she leaned forward and pointed an accusing finger toward Asher. “Why must I sit here and witness you carve the element of Eternity out of this child’s soul?”
