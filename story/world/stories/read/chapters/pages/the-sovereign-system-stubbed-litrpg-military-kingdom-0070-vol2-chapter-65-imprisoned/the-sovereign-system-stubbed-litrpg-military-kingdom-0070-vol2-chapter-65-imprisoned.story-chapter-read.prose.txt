Dim flashes of light flickered from the hallway as footsteps echoed off the cold stone walls. The sound stirred the figure slumped on the bench, prompting a low groan as he sat up with some effort.

He turned toward the approaching light, his eyes glinting faintly in the darkness. The man was gaunt, his robes damp and stained from days spent in the cell. A gray goatee framed his chin, aging his otherwise weary face.

As the footsteps drew closer, the man stretched, his joints cracking audibly. He released a satisfied grunt.

“That was a good nap,” he muttered, a hollow chuckle escaping his lips.

The glow of the lantern spilled through the cell bars, sharp and blinding after so long in darkness. All he could see was a silhouette—broad and imposing. Only a handful of people could cast such a shadow.

“You look like shit,” rumbled a deep voice, devoid of amusement.

“Yes, I’d imagine so,” the prisoner replied with a faint grin. “Though, on the bright side, I can finally empathize with you, old friend.”

There was a pause, broken only by a short exhale through the nose—a half-hearted laugh.

“Never thought I’d see you end up in here, Tristan… You truly are a fool.”

Tristan’s grin widened. “Aye, I am a fool. Why do you think we got along so well in the first place?”

“Enough,” the voice cut in sharply, the warmth gone. “Why did you do it? If it had been Richard, I could have pulled some strings—lightened his sentence. At most, he would’ve rotted in here for a few months until the Crown Prince returned. By then, the king’s anger would have cooled, and things could’ve gone back to normal.”

There was pain in his voice—hidden, restrained—but unmistakable to someone who had known him for years.

Tristan shook his head. “Riann… After all this time in the court, how can you still be so naïve?”

With a grunt, he pushed himself to his feet and stepped into the light. Thick iron bars separated them—the gap just wide enough to slip an arm through.

Tristan raised his hand and rested it against the armored shoulder beyond the bars, his expression softening.

“Old friend, you must know something’s wrong by now. You’ve always pretended not to see, but now is not the time for that.”

Riann lowered the lantern, the flame’s glow dimming enough for Tristan to see him clearly.

The Master of Arms looked pale and drawn. Fatigue hung from his frame, and the scar running down his face burned red against his skin. He looked unwell.

“It has been wrong for a long time,” the big man admitted with a small nod, ignoring the last comment.

“Still, I fail to see how that justifies your idiotic self-sacrifice. If you had just kept your mouth shut—”

Tristan raised a hand, cutting him off. “The Crown Prince is dead, Riann. Killed by the traitors who plotted to place the Second Prince on the throne.”

Riann flinched. The lantern slipped from his hand and clattered to the floor, its flame sputtering. The sound echoed through the damp dungeon, followed by the rhythmic drip of water striking stone.

Silence hung thick in the air before Riann began to shake his head.

“No. That’s not possible. You weren’t even with the Crown Prince—how could you know if he was dead or alive?” His voice lacked its usual weight, the deep resonance faded into uncertainty.

“It’s true, my friend. General Drakon had a guard stationed to protect Leo. That guard was gravely wounded. The only reason they’d be hurt… is if someone tried to kill the Crown Prince.” Tristan said quietly, omitting the full details.

“Then you have no definitive proof, Tristan. It’s unlike you to make claims based on a few vague words from another,” Riann replied, studying him closely.

“I trust him with my life.”

Again, silence stretched between them.

Riann’s eyes suddenly widened, his expression shifting from confusion to realization—then to disbelief.

“I see...” he muttered after a moment.

He bent down to retrieve the lantern, its flame now a weak ember, and straightened to his full height.

“I should go. The King will be finishing the mourning ceremony within the inner palace tonight. Once he returns, I’m sure the imperial court will convene to discuss what comes next.”

“Thank you for the visit, old friend,” Tristan said, patting his arm lightly before stepping back from the bars. From within his robe, he withdrew a small piece of parchment and handed it to the larger man with a faint smile.

The lantern’s glow no longer reached Riann’s face, cloaking his features in shadow. He accepted the paper wordlessly, turned away, and slowly unfurled it.

“Remember, old friend: All warfare is based on deception,” Tristan said, his voice low and eerie.

Riann’s broad back stiffened. For a brief moment, he seemed frozen—then he opened the lantern and placed the parchment inside. The paper caught fire instantly, the lantern flaring with sudden brightness before dimming once more.

“I’ll send someone down with a meal later,” Riann said, his voice neutral. “It might be the last one you get for a while.”

His heavy footsteps receded into the corridor, echoing off the stone as the flickering light faded into nothingness.

Tristan didn’t seem discouraged by the lack of a farewell. He was smiling softly. Only once the hallway was consumed by darkness did he step forward and rest his hands gently on the cold iron bars.

He pressed lightly on the bars, applying a touch of pressure until they groaned in protest. The moment they did, he eased off, lowering his hands with a satisfied chuckle.

“I should probably wait for my meal first,” he muttered, returning to the makeshift bed in the corner of his cell.

Much sooner than expected, another light began to approach from the end of the corridor—its flickering glow cutting through the darkness. Through the usual damp and stagnant stench of the dungeon, a surprisingly pleasant aroma drifted in.

“That smells like food,” he muttered.

“Vice General,” a voice called out, catching him off guard.

Tristan squinted into the gloom, taking a moment before recognizing one of the newly appointed Lieutenant Commanders who had joined Luke during the campaign.

“I no longer hold such a title,” Tristan said with a soft chuckle, stepping back toward the bars. “Good to see you, Jason,” he added warmly.

The young man grinned wryly and passed a large wooden bowl through the gap.

“You’ll always be the Vice General, Vice General,” Jason said matter-of-factly.

Tristan didn’t argue. He took the bowl and began devouring its contents with methodical speed. The stew was rich—chunks of tender meat, soft root vegetables, and savory broth. It lacked rice, but more than made up for it with flavor.

In less than ten seconds, he’d emptied the bowl. A contented belch followed.

“Would it be too much to ask for a few more bowls?” Tristan joked.

Jason laughed. “I’m here on my own accord. There should be someone else coming later, on Master Boyd’s orders.”

Tristan raised an eyebrow. “You came all this way just to bring me stew?”

Jason’s expression turned more serious. “No. I came to deliver updates. The noble houses are in disarray after word of the Second Prince’s death. They’re scrambling to align themselves with the Crown Prince’s faction—trying to avoid being culled during the ascension.”

“The Black Tiger Gang is leading the charge. Christian says Ming Hu is cutting deals left and right, trying to secure his position in the city no matter what happens.”

Tristan nodded, stroking his goatee. “And the imperial court? Any movement there?”

While he’d just spoken to Riann—a member of the court—he hadn’t dared press his old friend for details. Doing so would only put the man in a difficult position. Jason, however, was merely a Lieutenant Commander. He carried no such risk.

“The Right Minister’s taken over in the King’s absence, like always. But King Julius is expected to finish the mourning ceremony tonight and should return soon. From what I hear, the court will be meeting tonight.”

Tristan nodded again, eyes narrowing in thought as his fingers idly traced his goatee. “And what of the General?”

At the mention of Luke, Jason’s face lit up with a grin.

“He’s been meeting with Christian every morning. From what I gather, he has a plan—but he doesn’t seem too happy about it.”

Tristan let out a cynical chuckle, shaking his head.

“Then it looks like we’ve come to the same conclusion. The king is probably already dead, and the meeting of the court will turn into a bloodbath.”

If Jason was surprised by the statement, he didn’t show it.

“The traitors haven’t revealed themselves yet,” he said grimly. “Which means we can’t make a move until they do.”

“We wouldn’t have had a choice either way, Jason,” Tristan replied, a weariness seeping into his tone. “We have no forces stationed in the capital.”

Jason’s grin returned. “I think you’re forgetting something, Vice General. You saved ten thousand men that day. If that doesn’t count as a force, I don’t know what does.”

Tristan paused, eyeing the weathered commander carefully.

“Weren’t our men dispersed among the city’s forces?” he asked, incredulous.

“Ai, they were,” Jason admitted with a nod. “But all it takes is a word from you—and they’ll follow you anywhere.”

Tristan spread his arms, glancing around the confines of his cell.

“As you can see, I’m in no position to give orders. And even if I could, mobilizing the men might interfere with Luke’s plan. I was planning to break out during the chaos, but even then—I wouldn’t risk it.”

It looked like he was about to deliver a lecture, but Jason’s grin only widened.

“I never said you should lead the army, Vice General. I just pointed out that we have a force in the capital.”

A moment of silence passed before Tristan laughed, shaking his head in amusement.

“Right… I was getting ahead of myself. Did the General leave any orders for me?”

Jason nodded. “He mentioned something about the Chu army at Yanling?” He scratched his head. “Didn’t really make much sense to me…”

But Tristan’s fingers were already stroking his goatee again, a familiar gesture when he was lost in thought. After a moment, he snorted.

“Well, shit. I guess we don’t really have any other choice…”

He sighed, the weight of it seeming to age him in an instant.

“Tell Luke I heard his message. You should go—things are about to get messy. May we meet again…”

Jason responded with a silent salute, cupping his fist respectfully before turning to leave. His footsteps echoed down the hall, growing fainter with each step.

Tristan watched him go, then let out another soft sigh as he returned to his bench.

“To think the Marxx Kingdom would fall so far,” he muttered, gazing down at his hands.

Though he’d spent years as a librarian, his palms still bore the calluses of a soldier—faint echoes of countless campaigns and battles. He traced a fresh scar along his palm, a remnant of the brutal climb up the southern mountain in Xiandong. The blisters had only recently healed.

He smiled faintly, running a finger along the scar, as if caressing an old memory.

“Perhaps only you can pull this off, Luke…” he whispered.
