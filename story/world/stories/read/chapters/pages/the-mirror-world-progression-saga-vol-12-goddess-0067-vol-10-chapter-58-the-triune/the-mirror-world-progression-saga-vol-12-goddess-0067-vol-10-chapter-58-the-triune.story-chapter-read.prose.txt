Samantha grimaced, her still-shut, blood-caked eyes squeezing down a little more at the news delivered by Jeeves. She’d been a split second from rocketing over to the battle like superwoman cavalry, but the reality had to forestall her. Even as it pained her, as it ached so desperately in her to run to them in support, to strike out in revenge for the likes of Deikmorn and Nyomel, it would all be for naught. It would be squandered time; a traitorous waste of the purpose they died for.

There is only one thing I can do to end this. It’s time.

“We’re going to unleash the energy,” Samantha declared grimly in the gestalt. “Bast, fall back and get your ass over here.”

They needed to strike the tower instead of struggling with mere dead flesh on a dead plain, and before the tower could be struck at all, they had to make it strikable.

When true wisdom is gained, the material becomes what is immaterial. What we fight is a concept, not flesh; spirit, not the frames it resides in.

Eyes still closed, she turned her spiritual vision to focus on the tower — the rune. The tower loomed like a second sky, pulling vision and reality toward it like a black hole. The rune, in contrast, was a brand like everlasting energy bound through the structure of the environment, holding it fast. Refusing all entry, all denial of its absoluteness.

It felt somehow wrong how much being in Matrazere had created Sympathetic links. And so soon; it truly seeped into you, as if swimming in an ocean of putrefaction. Perhaps ‘irony’ was the word, because it was an advantage for what they intended, as it required familiarity. Their resonance and attunement to the place were particularly useful for Azure’s part. Elements of this aura were made even heavier over them for what they’d all suffered through.

As she meditated and prepared herself, she was aware of Azure handing off a potion to an Angel — most likely, a destructive one. Samantha didn’t bother focusing on it anymore, but she hoped that it would help, while knowing it was minor… another physicality in a now spiritual battle. Despite her decision, shame filled her that she was not rejoining the battle on the ground. Was that not her calling? Was she not the foremost warrior, time and time again?

I’m whoever I need to be for the moment — a warrior, a fool, a wizard, a daughter, a mother, a wisewoman. It doesn’t matter; I serve.

“Are we close enough, Chestnut?” Azure asked hesitantly.

Samantha didn’t even twitch as she replied, “Actual distance is as immaterial as what we aim to do. Focus, Azure.” She squeezed Azure’s still-held hand. “Meditate and reach out. You know where that rune is even without me, don’t you? This place is drinking you in like wine.”

After an uncomfortable pause, Azure said, “Samantha, I know what happened to you is unthinkable, but you’re worrying me. You haven’t opened your eyes since…” She trailed off, then cleared her throat. “Sorry. I’m being insensitive. C-can I at least clean your face? That blood is horrible.”

“That’s a waste of precious time as people die for us,” Samantha declared. “Close your eyes and focus. Please.”

Azure sighed. “Of course.”

Bast arrived, carried along by his sphinxes. He felt… harried. Hollow. She was aware of his eyes more in the spirit than as physical orbs, and they had the look of someone who’d seen too much. His usual cheer and pomp were clearly subdued. “Your Majesty; Madame Azure,” he said in polite greeting.“I can honestly say I’m very motivated to put this battle behind us. I will do my part.” Meanwhile, his sphinxes quietly positioned themselves like protective sentinels around the circle, one facing north, one south.

Samantha nodded and held out her other hand insistently, which Bast quickly took. They were accustomed to what was best from the practice they’d conducted, so Bast and Azure also held hands, making a triangular circle.

The Goddess steeled herself and reached out to the Bridge of Edges, and further reached out to Matrazere, pulling the concepts of them toward each other in the spirit world, through [Deific Pneumamancy], primarily. She bent reality and bridged them, breaking through thin divides and then overlaying the ideas and the visualizations into the minds of the spiritual kin connected to her. She gave them the vision, the realization, the frame, of who they truly were. From that barest framework, she, as the bridge’s foundation maker, reached her spiritual hand to take Azure’s and invite her to enact her own power of creation.

That she did — instantly — as if a child hopping back onto a bike. All the difficulties in practice were banished like cast-off training wheels getting in the way. Azure, and the aligned entity inside her, were primed for the moment and perfected in the presence of that horrible place, which was ever bathing her and loudly proclaiming its supposedly dominant existence.

Being there, suffering it, hearing the lies of a darkness she had complete authority over spiritually… she’d already won that battle.

Samantha was almost scrambling to accommodate her lover’s intricate, latticework-like, crystalline germination of the connecting bridge, warping spatial contours to stretch the edge of the ‘poison sac,’ the raw destruction trapped in containment on the Bridge of Edges. She twisted it right into the face of the glaring brand continually screaming at them all in the spirit world.

They both couldn’t help letting out soft, coughed-out laughs of surprise and glee at what they’d just done together. It was… beautiful. It asked the question: how much more could they do together, in a calmer world?

And just like that, it was all up to Bast. He struggled mightily. A moment passed, and the containment writhed once. It shook, and then went still.

“Sorry,” Bast muttered. His palm felt sweaty.

“Take your time,” Samantha said smoothly.

“It happens to lots of guys,” Azure quipped.

“Azure!” Samantha admonished.

Bast burst out in nervous laughter. “I can’t blame her for taking the low-hanging fruit. After all, in my case, the fruit is delectable.”

“Fucking hell,” Samantha breathed like a curse.

“Lord Bast never fails to deliver his vitality in time…” started the sphinx, Moonrise.

“Though sometimes, he is early,” finished Sunset.

Samantha and Azure exploded in such disturbed, inappropriate laughter that they almost lost their focus. In fact, to Samantha, the laughter had a spike of pain to it. It was ridiculous, the situation was ridiculous, and the world was ridiculous.

Humor at the gallows was the only sane thing left to them.

“I thought we spoke about oversharing, girls,” Bast muttered.

“We apologize, Lord Bast… we’ll try to correct this, Lord Bast,” the sphinxes offered. Though it wasn’t detectable, something about it made Samantha doubt their sincerity.

“Please focus, you bastard,” Samantha managed, pain from contained laughter quite clear in her voice.

Bast caused more rumbles and shifts, and yet continued to struggle. In the distance, the sounds of war were a terrible backdrop. Someone screamed.

“Damn it!” Bast exclaimed. “Should’ve practiced more…”

Rolling her eyes internally, Samantha reached out to take hold of his mind. Like an embrace, she pulled him into her presence, enveloping him. She felt him tense, hold his breath, briefly… and then he quickly began to relax, as if warmed from a freeze. She said, “It is the same for all of us, Brother. Take your authority and act in violence; let go, directed in one mere direction. Slay the world, your enemy! Enforce your rule over the now. Unleash all that you are!”

“Yes!” Bast exclaimed excitedly, as if having a eureka moment. A new grasp, a new supernatural harnessing, quite suddenly spawned within the containment bubble, and it shifted; squeezed. The flow of almost liquid-like energy raced down the new angle. “Oh, shit, here it cooooooomes!”

The containment ruptured, and the raw, seething energy spilled forth with a sound like a detonating volcano. It shook the ground.

Samantha was not prepared for how insanely difficult it was to prevent the whole structure from unraveling. Her and Azure’s hands both shook with the strain with which they gripped each other and struggled to hold the directing structural ‘nozzle’ together for as long as they could. Samantha couldn’t even begin to focus on the results with all of her mind bent on control.

“Yes, ahahahaha!” Bast cackled in mad glee as he unleashed the greatest act of destruction ever seen on Calrenazzod, and certainly squeezed it for all it was worth.

Damn him, he’s enjoying this far too much!

Dax continued managing the battle. Fortunately, it had become surprisingly easier to avoid losing people to the wraith, as ‘non-clone’ Odysseus alone was almost a stalemate against him. The rest in support of him made for such a reduction in chances of taking out the stronger pieces that they might hold out for many minutes. Hopefully, it was enough. At any time, though, someone could make a mistake.

More armor-wraiths had risen from the remnants of decent pieces of the damaged armor, once again re-formed. The group successfully organized a tactic with Dax’s help to get them bunched up as much as possible from multiple pulling angles, and then an Angel dropped Azure’s explosive, acidic potion on them, disintegrating most of the armor and wiping the majority out in one fell swoop.

Not bad, if I do say so myself. Thanks again, Bluetail. Immediately after the thought came, she realized she’d never called Azure that — not in this lifetime.

At the sudden flash of energy from the tower and the ensuing eerie, cacophonous sound, Dax and almost everyone couldn’t help but stare in amazement.

A tremendous luminescence flared over the whole central tower, and the rune on it, always drab, was now glowing brighter, and brighter, and brighter. The sounds were like earthquakes deep in the ground finding unseen fissures to reach the ears. And the ground was shaking.

The rune became so bright that everyone had to avert their eyes, and it lit up the entire battlefield like the sky was on fire, with bizarre, shifting shadows playing across everything. Heat could be felt, irregularly wafting, as if sliding only halfway through cracks in reality.

An anxious pressure stirred in the air, and a high-pitched whine began, its source uncanny and unlocatable.

Darkness itself seemed suddenly nervous, as if it wanted to flee.

The rune flickered once.
