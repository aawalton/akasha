Two Days LaterSimon flopped down into his chair, the stress flowing out of him with a long, drawn-out sigh. In the privacy and silence of his personal quarters, he allowed his overtaxed mind to come to a rest.Why does winning always have to be more complicated than losing? he mused. Maybe I should've let the Queen crush my skull a second time. At least then I would have less meetings to sit through.The days since Queen Ashora's death had been hectic, to put it mildly. Simon couldn't just kill her and saunter on back home – that would be leaving too many loose ends behind. The demise of a normal monarch already tended to incite unrest. For someone who'd been a foundational pillar of her nation like Ashora? Far moreso.He'd started off by swearing the Queen's surviving loyalists to Contracts of fealty. Most of them hated him for what he'd done, but Ashora was a land of might makes right, so it didn't take long for their resistance to crumble. Some of them were Lords of their own cities, meaning that Simon's spoils of war included Subjugating over a dozen territories.Next came the after-party cleanup. By teleporting to his newly-Subjugated cities, then making use of their Waystations, Simon gained unimpeded access to the vast majority of the country.He targeted the cities without Lords first. Due to the Demons' catastrophic civil war, quite a few places now had vacancies open for leadership. Subjugating them was as simple as declaring himself ruler, having eyewitnesses confirm that he'd slain Ashora, and then humbling any would-be competitors who stepped forward.The cities that still had Demons in charge took longer to sort out. While the surviving rebels were thrilled to hear that Ashora was gone, none of them were super enthused to learn that it was less 'freed from tyranny' and more 'under new management'. They opposed Simon's takeover with a brand of childish spite that was common among uppercrust types who believed they'd been denied what was rightfully owed to them.The transmigrator swatted them aside like flies, but their defiance, futile as it was, did slow his pace somewhat. It seemed that even in defeat, they were committed to being massive pains in the ass.Simon had forged so many Contracts of obedience that he'd lost count – each one its own individual headache to navigate. Apparently, heavy-handed binds of servitude were considered a major faux pas in modern Demon society. The Lords seemed to take greater offense at being forced to sign Contracts than over having their titles stripped. If Ashora had attempted to do what he was doing now, the rebels would've mutinied decades earlier.Although the situation did make for one amusing moment."This treatment is outrageous," a Demon Lord had spat. "Queen Ashora would never have abused her power to such an extent!""Huh." Simon tilted his head. "If she wasn't so bad, then why did you try to kill her?"They didn't have a proper answer to that.Installing new leaders in each city also slowed his pace. However, it was necessary to prevent Ashora from imploding into a second civil war the moment he returned to the Isles. Simon couldn't be everywhere at once, after all.He fast-tracked the process by compelling the local higher-ups to inform him of who they thought was competent. From there, rapid-fire interviews were conducted, which would result in a Demon being raised to the status of provisional Lord – usually the person who had already been doing most of the work while the former Lord sat on their laurels.Tempting as it was to install at least one human as a Lord, that would be painting a glowing red bullseye on their back. Simon wouldn't be there to protect them from assassins, and his influence had its limits. Sure, he'd placed many of the Demons in Ashora under Contract, but not all of them.Yet.Overall, ensuring stability just took time. If Simon had only been concerned with Subjugating the nation, he could have finished the job in a single day. It mirrored his experiences back home. Usurping Duke Helmund had been several hours of mayhem, but maintaining the Isles entailed thousands of hours of tedium.As things stood, he hadn't even gotten around to visiting every city yet. Ashora wasn't a small nation. Still, he'd made significant headway in a relatively short period. Within the next week or two, the rest of the country would fall in line.Afterwards...that was when matters would start to complicate.Simon had little interest in governing two nations. The Severed Isles was plenty enough already, thanks. His ideal scenario would be to install a puppet ruler in Ashora, give them broad, sweeping mandates from afar, and leave all the legwork to them.Life would be swell if he could just order them to establish equality between humans and Demons, then go about his own business, only checking a few years later to verify that they'd carried out his will. Like putting the entire country in a microwave and pressing a button labeled 'Reform'.But he knew it wouldn't be that easy, even if he exercised more unilateral authority and control than the Queen ever had. Social norms changed at a glacial pace. There would probably be some humans who opposed his reforms, so accustomed to the collar around their neck that its absence felt unnerving.Then there was the issue of foreign powers. The Oscium Empire and the Grand Duchy of Ustrad.Simon's misadventure in Oscium hadn't endeared him to the people there. They wouldn't be pleased when they heard that the Demon who assassinated Lord Edrick was now the de facto King of Ashora. Not that he'd actually assassinated Edrick, but aside from Steiner, the Lord that Simon briefly held hostage, few people in Oscium knew the truth.As for Ustrad...he didn't know enough about that place to guess exactly how they would react. It was time to start learning, and fast.Honestly, Simon felt half-tempted to let those two nations invade. He wouldn't shed tears if most of the Demons were wiped out. Still, there were some decent folk among them, like Marcus from the City of New Dawns, and the loyalists who had sided with Queen Ashora. There was also no guarantee that Oscium and Ustrad would be good to the humans of Ashora.If anything, the opposite was much more likely. Being overrun by rival nations never worked out well for the civilians caught in the crossfire.Suppose there's no real choice in the matter. I've made my bed – time to lie in it. As much as he foresaw many sleepless nights on the horizon, Simon wouldn't turn back. The problem of Ashora wasn't something he could merely pass off to other people.He had assumed responsibility for that country the moment he struck down its Queen.A chill raced up his spine as unbidden memories rushed to the forefront. Queen Ashora, breaking through Fell Harvest, grabbing his head, squeezing. That was certainly a sensation he wouldn't be able to forget anytime soon.Simon had faced stronger opponents in the past. Duke Helmund in his prime – before the mana-bomb – ranked a notch above Queen Ashora. And the Ancient One, even in its larval state, was still the most powerful existence he'd encountered in Valtia thus far.Yet neither of them had managed to kill him. Queen Ashora did. For the first time, and what would hopefully be the only time, his HP had reached 0.If he didn't have Endure Fell to revive him afterwards...Skin of my teeth doesn't even begin to describe it. I think if I'd chosen any other strategy against her, I wouldn't be sitting here right now.Simon's logic had been simple: fighting Queen Ashora on her terms would be a mistake. She was too strong, too fast, and possessed enough mana for a hundred Demons put together. Even if he attached the Mana-Bind to her, he doubted that it would hobble her to the point where he could win.Maybe he could've fought her directly if the rebel Lords severely weakened her, but that concept was a bit of a Catch-22. If the rebels proved competent enough to severely weaken Ashora, then they could probably just straight-up kill her. And if they were competent enough to kill her, they would likely be too much for him to handle as well.Ideally, Ashora would've barely defeated the rebels and been left on death's door in turn, but he couldn't count on events playing out perfectly in his favor. That sounded more like a pipe dream than an outcome he should realistically expect.Betraying her mid-war and siding with the rebels was another option that he'd thought up, then promptly discarded. Allowing the insurgents to win would be worse than letting Ashora live. The short-term safety of the Severed Isles depended on most of the Demon population being culled, and he required the Queen's strength to help thin their numbers.He was looking for a scenario where the rebels and Queen Ashora died, in that order. A way to thread the needle without relying on luck or providence.But while Ashora was nigh-unbeatable in physical combat, she'd been far more vulnerable to another type of warfare – psychological. Though she put up a strong front to the public, inside, she was a mess, her psyche fraying at the seams. Others may have seen a demigod, more of an impervious statue than an actual person, but that statue had cracks running just below the surface.And Simon knew precisely which cracks to jab a chisel into. However, doing so would've rapidly depleted his mana reserves, and sustaining the Nightmare on her had been taxing. He'd wound up burning through all the mana-battery Artifacts that Victoria loaned him by the end.Simon also couldn't be sure that an attack would amount to anything. What if he tried a Landmine on Ashora, and it caused her to snap out of her trance and dodge? What if he sent a Laser piercing straight through her heart, and she just refused to die? He didn't know exactly how durable she was – even a direct hit might do little better than tickle her.No, safe and steady would win the race. All he needed to do was keep Ashora preoccupied with Nightmares until the Mana-Bind devoured her soul....Granted, it took much longer than anticipated. The Fell Beasts he'd tested the Bind on had shriveled to husks in a span of seconds. While there was an ocean of difference between a generic Beast and Queen Ashora, she still must have done something to slow the enervation process.He did wonder what that 'something' was, but it wasn't like he could ask her anymore.I also screwed up by jumping the gun with my initial Fell Harvest, Simon thought, chastising himself. He'd justified it in the moment as Ashora being weakened enough already, but in truth, it was because her self-pitying drivel had been grating on his nerves.Just one surge of emotion – and it led to his brain matter splattering onto the ground. Definitely a lesson to take to heart.Besides that minor snafu, his plan had gone off without a hitch. The Mana-Bind and the Nightmares worked in tandem to imprison Ashora in a hell forged from her own regrets. Only her loyalists could have rescued her then, but they were exhausted from the war. Katarina, Bastian, Cyna, and Esen didn't have any trouble fending them off.Getting his Boon-Bearers on board with backstabbing Ashora had been easier than expected. Simon figured they would argue against him, and he'd prepped counterarguments in advance, yet their conversation on the topic lasted a grand total of three minutes.Cyna had been low-key eager to depose another monarch. While she admitted that Ashora was nice 'for a Queen', the former rebel still retained a near-pathological distrust of authority figures. It was honestly touching that she seemed to view Simon as something of a partial exception there.Bastian just went along with Cyna. Wouldn't be the first time he got swept up in her current. He hadn't been enthused about the prospect of fighting the almighty Demon Queen, but he saw the merit in what Simon was proposing. At the very least, he didn't feel strongly enough otherwise to play devil's advocate.Esen hadn't said much, but the fervent gleam in his eyes was loud and clear. The moment that Simon described how he would be ambushing Ashora when her guard was down, the boy produced a sheet of parchment and started taking notes.Katarina ended up being the lone voice of dissent – if you could even call it that. After Simon presented his plan in full detail, including his rationale behind why they needed to remove Ashora from the board sooner rather than later, the Arcane Rogue fell silent. With a lowered gaze, she asked him one question."Do you truly believe that this is the right course of action?"When he answered in the affirmative, she had nothing more to say.Kat had been quiet and withdrawn during their Subjugation road trip these past two days. She'd then retired to her quarters immediately upon returning to the Isles. Simon was considering suggesting that she go visit Gerold in Springwater Village tomorrow. He knew this had been difficult for her, and seeing her father always lifted her spirits.The transmigrator sank deeper into his chair, closing his eyes. What's done is done. Have to look forward to the future. I won't pretend we'll have an *easy* time of it, but we stand to gain much from our victory.Personally speaking, he'd already gained much. Harvesting the Demon Queen had been a tremendous windfall all on its own.For the umpteenth time, Simon opened his log of system notifications and re-read the messages he'd received two days prior, a smile cracking on his face.Alert: Queen Ashora has been slain!Your Level has increased!Level: 87 → 13086 stat points added to Unspent Points!A monstrously powerful life has been Harvested!30 bonus stat points added to Unspent Points!278 Fell Evolution Points obtained!Before, Simon had assumed that his Level increase from killing Duke Helmund would forever remain the record-holder for most Levels in one sitting. Queen Ashora then graciously proved him wrong. The possibilities weren't actually endless, but in this moment, they almost felt that way....More Vitality first.The thought came to him suddenly, accompanied by a flash of phantom pain in his forehead. He found himself wholeheartedly agreeing with the notion. Not that having more HP would've stopped Queen Ashora from pulping his skull, but...yeah. And how could he further that goal by allocating Points in specific areas?Right now, the only true threat in Valtia that remained was Kirkelas the Conqueror, Sealed Demon of Ruination. Dealing with him was Priority #1. Simon had roughly ten months to do so. but he shouldn't procrastinate. Would be embarrassing to keep kicking that can down the road until he woke up one day to discover Kirkelas knocking on his door.Fortunately, Queen Ashora had given him an idea to build from. Proxia Village counted as a territory for Subjugate Territory even though it was a ghost town. For a brief, absurd moment, he wondered if it really was Kirkelas stopping by to say hello.Chuckling at himself, Simon stood up and walked over to the door. He opened it without fear, because there was nothing left in this world for him to fear.Or so he'd thought. As it turned out, he could be shaken to his core by just a single spoken sentence.Katarina was standing outside. Despite this being in the middle of the night, she obviously hadn't slept. Her posture was rigid, and her face was an ill-fitting mask of stoicism that was hanging just askew enough for the anguish beneath to peek through."We need to talk."
