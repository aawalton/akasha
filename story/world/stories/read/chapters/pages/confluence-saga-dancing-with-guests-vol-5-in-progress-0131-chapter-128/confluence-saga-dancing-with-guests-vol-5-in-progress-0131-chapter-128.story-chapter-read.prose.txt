Why is a Doorkeeper?

We serve as introductions. We ensure those who must pass do so appropriately.

If you meet us you have a reason, if we show you the door you are permitted to enter. Do not confuse us with guardians, we do not interfere. Do not confuse us with someone who cares. We show the door to those who need to see it, nothing more, nothing less.

What comes next is none of our concern, why you are to be greeted does not matter. We serve, we route, we are.

Continue on your journey, enjoy your lives. If we meet again you will confuse us with another. We are not here to be recognized.

Seb paused in the doorway and looked around the suite. Anya was already poking her head into the rooms and expressing her opinion. From the looks of things it wasn't any bigger than his old apartment, a tiny kitchen area off to the side of the main room, a good sized bathroom, and a pair of bedrooms.

Do they expect us to sleep in separate rooms? Anya sent.

I suspect they saw that we were two people and made sure we had two rooms.

Beds are both the same size, rooms look identical other than where things are placed.

Seb followed her into the room she was looking at. A desk, a wardrobe and a twin bed. Plenty of space for the two of them, at least after years in a crowded tent. The second bedroom meant they should have all the space they needed. And they had plenty of time to themselves before dinner.

Valene has a Seed? Anya sent.

It explains what we saw. Especially after we got to the school.

That Doorkeeper. He was-

-trying to provoke a reaction and not give us any answers?

Exactly. At least he wasn't Seer Marten.

Seb nodded in agreement. The Seer had managed to provoke the urge to taunt, to imply something that wasn't entirely untrue. Valene had been a guest, but wasn't exactly a friend or close acquaintance. His presence when they had established the Holding was the only reason they had thought to ask him to officiate. His connection to Yshe and Fay had likely played a factor in his accepting.

He's going to be one of the teachers, Anya said. Knowledge curriculum.

Doubt he'll be able to tell us much, Council Seer rules.

I don't care, Anya said. He didn't like Fay.

Seb wrapped his arms around his wife, holding her close as they let their irritations at the Seer wash over them. There wasn't anything they could do, they wouldn't even be able to ask Fay about him for months. At least she'd be able to visit as family, spend actual time with them and not just have a quick conversation.

Six months… Anya sent.

She'll be fine, they all will.

And us?

Seb smiled and gave her the slightest of nudges. She shifted forward and somehow all their coordination and grace left them. She stumbled, her arm catching on his where he still held her. They tripped over one another, limbs tangling and adjusting as they toppled onto the bed to land nestled together.

There was no one to see how awkward they'd looked, no one to hear them laugh at one another for being so intentionally clumsy. Seb rested his chin on the edge of her shoulder as they both relaxed.

We have each other. No one is going to change that.

They still had to unpack, to arrange their quarters to their liking. But he was in no hurry. Neither of them were.

~ * ~

Anya opened her eyes. Seb's arm was still around her, his breath still warm against her neck. They'd dozed off, not quite fully asleep, but close enough that time had slipped away from them. They weren't late, at least she didn't think they were, but they had spent longer on the bed than intended.

Seb?

Hrm? he sent back, his thoughts barely coherent enough to qualify as anything other than a mumble.

We need to get up, get ready for dinner.

We- Dinner?

She felt his attention snap into focus. A moment ago he was barely there, now his thoughts were crystal clear. She twisted in his arms to face him, grinning as their eyes locked. A slight push and she rolled across him, eyes never breaking contact until she straightened to stand on the far side of the bed.

She knew he could have stopped her, that he likely would have if time had permitted. But they both knew they had to get ready. They'd dressed sensibly for their arrival, should they change? A shared thought said yes. No questions as to what Seb would wear, slacks and a dress shirt were a staple she knew he'd never give up. Her choice was more complicated.

More than half the clothing they'd brought was hers, more than half of the clothing she'd brought was varying outfits that would work for anything from occasions such as tonight to formal affairs such as an Annual Convention. She didn't need extravagant, she just needed something fancy.

She blushed at the suggestions Seb sent, each one a mental impression of how he saw her, of his opinion of her in the look, of a memory that fit. She pulled out one outfit he knew he wouldn't expect. He'd been there when she put in a custom request for it, he knew it existed, but she'd never found reason to wear it. A grey sleeveless blouse, darker than her robes but only slightly and matching pants. Over it a mostly sheer forest green top, flame-like vines creating patterns that were barely visible against the blouse but looked almost alive against her bare arms.

Her decision made she went and changed in the spare room. She didn't care if he saw, what he saw, but there was only going to be one chance to see his first reaction. He knew what she was doing and why, his feelings made it clear he'd wait impatiently.

His reaction when she stepped back into view was everything she hoped for. He didn't say anything, he didn't even move. But she saw herself through his eyes, saw him trace every pattern, follow every fold, linger on the effects until she wanted to shift, then look into her eyes, let her feel how he felt about her until she did squirm.

Then he offered her his arm, his projection of his sight fading away as the moment passed, and gestured to the door.

Shall we?

Do you know where we're going?

She knew he didn't, they had been told when dinner would be, but not where. Hopefully someone would be able to direct them, or be waiting for them below.

"What, only two of you?"

Anya's head swiveled as she turned to find the speaker. A young woman was being shushed by a mountain of a man. Two men stood next to them looking between the pair. The three were clearly related, same sandy brown hair, same facial structure, same gold and blue styled coats. The last, the mountain, stood head and shoulders over them and was easily the size at least two of the three combined.

"Marie, you cannot simply say things. You must be.. polite."

"Sasha," she answered. "Remember your place."

"My place, it is making sure you and your cousins not offend or be injured."

"Peter? Paul? Am I wrong? Are there not only two of them? Did they come alone?"

"We all came alone," one of the two answered.

"Yes," Anya said. "Only the two of us. I presume you have the ground floor suite?"

"Is that what you call it? Four bedrooms, a privy and the smallest common room I've seen?" the other man said.

"Paul," Sasha rumbled. "Not offend."

"Enough Sasha," Paul answered. "I don't understand why father didn't tell us about the accommodations. Had we known we might have made them more suitable. A Hammerswell has standards."

"Hammerswell?" Seb said. "Is that your family then?"

Anya saw Sasha's hand cover Marie's mouth and nose as she went to answer, cutting off whatever she was about to say. The look on her eyes made it clear she was outraged, the rising shrieks escaping Sasha's hand a sign of what was likely to come.

"You must not be well-versed in the families." Peter answered with a sneer. "The Hammerswells are one of the pre-eminent families of Combat delvers."

"Why are you all standing about like louts?" Master Leo said as the door to the dorm slammed open. "Dinner is in five minutes and you haven't taken your seats yet."

"No one told us-"

"You were told to get to dinner. If you can't find your way there without someone holding your hand how can you expect to explore a new Depths passage? Out the door, the squat building across the field. Now go."

Sasha released Marie who spared a moment to glare at him, at Seb, at everyone except the teacher, before the four were out the door and on their way.

"You two as well. The Dean doesn't stand much on protocol, but is strict when it suits him. First night it suits him."

Anya straightened her spine as the stepped past Leo. She was fairly certain she recognized the Hammerswell's type. Arrogant coddled heirs. It didn't matter if they were talented in their own right, they would use their family name regardless.

At least two other groups we haven't met yet, Seb sent.

Second floor and 3b, and possibly the top floor.

I'm surprised they didn't take the top.

She smiled as she sent back her agreement. They were set above the family that was supposedly well-known. Although perhaps the ground floor was more convenient?
