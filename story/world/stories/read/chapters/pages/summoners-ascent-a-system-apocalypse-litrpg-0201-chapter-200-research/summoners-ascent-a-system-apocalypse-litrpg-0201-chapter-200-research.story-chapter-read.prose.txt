As the door shot open, Millie ran back up onto the rooftop. She looked around, spotting Ashton standing by the railing and quickly ran up to him.

"Ashton!" she stared up at him with wide open eyes. "They have a guy here that can jump super far! He went from one end of the room to the other just like that!"

Ashton took a short breath. He let go of the railing, and quickly hid the bent and cracked metal behind his back. Unclenching his teeth, he quickly started smiling. "Oh, really? With just skills, that's pretty impressive," Ashton pointed out. "But did you know, you can do exactly that with magic as well."

Millie's eyes grew even wider, something Ashton wasn't even aware was possible. "Really?!"

"Mm-hm. With the right magic and spells, you can even fly," he pointed out, not adding that flight magic was actually famously difficult. There was a reason why Ashton was planning a different route to achieve it.

"Whoa! Can you teach me?" she asked giddily, and Ashton just smiled at her. He waved the Backpacker over, and had them give Millie a small item. "Huh? Isn't this a magic stone? Can I fly with this?"

"Nope. But with this, you can start learning how to manipulate your mana. If you can do that, we can start working on more exciting things. And down the line, if you're skilled enough, I'll teach you all the cool magic you want, okay?" Ashton suggested, and Millie was already nodding along gleefully.

It seemed that back at the primary school, the adults were really trying to give their kids some semblance of 'normalcy', so they would never practice their own skills when the kids were around. Now that she was here and finally seeing people use these special abilities so skilfully, Millie's excitement clearly skyrocketed.

Ashton quickly explained what she had to do with the magic stone, and what the goal was. With that, Millie quickly ran off back into the tent, and happily got started. Due to her own special ability, Ashton was positive that her sixth sense was already developed enough to just get started at this point right away.

The entire time, Sam and Clementine were just standing there, staring at Ashton. Now, the trio was standing there in silence. Sam walked forward and looked at the railing. Someone who did this out of anger was able to just turn around and act that sweetly with a child the next moment?

"Show me where the constructs are later. I need to see them for myself," Ashton said before the silence could be broken by his friends.

Sam rushed a nod. "Right, of course."

"Oka also told me about the different types of constructs and their weaknesses, so I'll check out which ones are there and then update you on that," Ashton explained. He turned away and slightly pulled up his mask, drying off the frustrated tears that had built up just now.

"That's perfect. Oh, and the place where they showed up is somewhat close to the university. You said you wanted to stop by there anyway, right?" Sam asked, hoping to change the topic. It was clear to him that Ashton didn't really want to keep talking about this topic right now.

Ashton turned and pulled his mask back down. "Yeah, there are a few things there that I wanted to check out. For one, I know that the university kept a ton of my parents' old research after they retired, so I want to take a look at that," Ashton scratched his head.

"Hm? Their research?" Clementine asked, surprised. "Do you think it could be important right now?"

"Probably not, but maybe. Like... there's a fairy sanctuary in my backyard with a nymph that has been there at least since I was a toddler, and a brownie that was attached to our home. Plus, I ended up like this, so I figure my parents might've known more than they let on," Ashton pointed out. He tapped his finger on the railing behind him.

"Right, but would their research really help?" Clementine started, clearly trying to remember something. "Weren't they professors for uh..."

"Well, my dad was a professor for literature with a research focus on mythology. I think his final paper before fully retiring was about the correlation and patterns between old and modern myths and legends. I'm sure you can see how that might be relevant here," Ashton said, pushing his hand into his pocket to grab his cigarette pack. He pulled out one that was good for stress relief and mana flow, and quickly lit it up.

Sam was thinking for a moment as well, before remembering. "Right, and your mum was a botanist, wasn't she? Do you think her research could help you with potion stuff?"

Ashton nodded as he blew a puff of smoke behind him. "That's what she was a professor for, yeah, but she also had a chemistry degree. She was specifically focused on how to improve and make use of chemical compounds in plants. They didn't really tell me many specifics about her research, but my dad would brag about how special it was. Usually to make her embarrassed. But even after she passed, he would keep talking about it, though he never said any specifics."

"Damn... it's almost like you were bred to be this super magical mythical potion-making genius, huh?" Clementine laughed a little, trying to up the mood.

While Ashton didn't feel like laughing right now, he loved that his friend was trying to cheer him up with a dumb joke, and smiled along. "Yeah, almost."

Sam cupped his chin in thought, slowly rubbing his fingers against his beard. "So, you're going to use your dad's research to figure out more about the nature of magic on earth, and your mum's to let you more effectively use plants from earth for potions?"

"Spot on for the first part, but for the second, I also just want to incorporate modern methods into my worldview to improve potions in general. I may have spent a century in Lumia, but I didn't spend all that time researching different plants. The things that botanists and chemists have figured out through precise, controlled research is far more than anything I could have learned on my own," Ashton took another drag of his cigarette. "As much as it seems the opposite, my potion-making is largely improvised and vibes-based."

"Huh. Watching you with all those enchanted cups and stuff made it seem more like you're some expert alchemist." Sam raised an eyebrow. He turned around to the Living Abode. "Honestly, you seem like an expert at a lot of things."

But Ashton just shrugged. "I mean... I am. I'm an expect; hell, I'm the foremost expert on potion-making on earth right now, and I can say that for a fact. But that's going to change as people figure out how to make potions. Chemists or doctors will figure out how to make things that are much better than anything I could ever dream of making. You know I'm not that smart."

"Guy that knows 47 languages says what?" Clementine scoffed in slight disbelief, and Ashton sighed.

"Listen... I know a lot of things. I've experienced a lot of things. But I'm not particularly smart. Things like languages are easy, it's just remembering the meanings, spellings, and intonations of words. That's easy. I can remember formulas, I can tell you the atomic weight of every element we know about–"

"Barium?" Sam interrupted.

"Hm? 137.327u, but shut up, that's not the point. Like, I know stuff. But I don't know what the fuck to do with that knowledge, nor do I really care to. I just don't have the drive to be a fucking chemist or whatever. My brain doesn't work that way. Now with magic, sure, that's the one science I can get behind, and I know I won't fall behind here ever. But with the rest? I'll be overtaken soon enough," Ashton put out his cigarette. "And it's good that way. It's a massive world, and I can't be the only expert when it comes to this stuff."

He started to walk over to the tent. "Plus, there will be people like Millie, who have special, innate abilities."

As he spoke, Millie flinched. "Huh? What?" she glanced at Ashton's friends nervously. "Is it okay?"

"It's okay, I promise. I trust them with my life. They won't ever tell anyone," Ashton explained. "What do they look like to you?"

Millie was clearly nervous to say it out loud, especially to them. Until now, Ashton was the only one that she told about this ability, but she didn't even know these people.

"If you don't want them to know, it's okay," Ashton reassured her. It was her ability, so she didn't need to share the details with anyone. All that Sam and Clementine really had to know was that there was something special about her, so that they could help Ashton keep her safe from those that were guided by constellations to try and exploit her.

Millie shook her head, clutching the magic stone in her hand. "Uhm... okay."

She faced Ashton's friends, who were still quite confused about what was happening. Ashton noticed something stirring in the girl, like a whirlpool of power was flowing toward her eyes. "Miss Clementine is... it's like she's made of wind, I think? It's wild and like a storm, like it doesn't want to be stuck here. It just wants to go away. Not to anywhere though. It just wants to go and move and be free."

Ashton focused on his friend. She wasn't quite sure what was being described right now, but she clearly felt a bit apprehensive to this information. Not that it didn't suit her; it did. But something about hearing this made her feel the need to pull her hand to her chest, as if she was trying to hide from Millie's eyes.

"And he... Mister Sam, he looks..." Millie tilted her head slightly. "Like a knight, I think? He's wearing armour. Really thick and heavy, but it doesn't look uncomfortable. It's pretty, too, white and gold and shining. Something feels empty, though." she concentrated for a few moments. "Oh! The sword, it's missing. A knight needs a sword, right?"

Ashton laughed a bit. "Yeah, a knight does need a sword. Is there anything else?"

Millie looked back at Sam. "I think so. There's something floating around his head, but I don't know what it is? It's like, light? I think it's supposed to be a glowing crown, but it's broken, and now the pieces are just... there."

Immediately, Clementine stared at Ashton. He slowly nodded, holding back a laugh.

"No! Shut up! I don't know what this is about, but you told her to say that, right?" Sam said loudly, pointing at Ashton accusatorily.

"Nope, I genuinely haven't," Ashton chuckled out, and Clementine reached up and swung an arm around Sam's shoulders.

"Come ooon, big guy! You like it, don't you? I mean, you even made your username 'Pendragon'," she pointed out.

"That's just because my last name is 'Penn', and I was really into dragons when I came up with the name. It was a total coincidence! It had nothing to do with that!" Sam protested, moving away from his friend.

Millie looked around confused and scared. "Uhm, what's wrong? Did I say something wrong? Is he angry?"

Ashton laughed and shook his head, sitting down next to the girl while Clementine kept teasing Sam.

"No, it's just..." he held a hand in front of his mouth to stop himself from laughing. "We've teased him about this for forever, and you saying he looked like a knight with a crown to you, even if it's broken, just adds to that."

Millie was still confused.

"Right, sorry," Ashton apologised as he leaned his head on his hand. "We've just always said he's the modern King Arthur."
