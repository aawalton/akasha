The sun climbed slowly higher into the pale, cloudless winter sky, casting harsh, stark light over the horrors of the northern plains. But on the timber ramparts of Ashenfall, the atmosphere had undergone a miraculous, fundamental shift.

The frantic, suffocating panic of the dawn assault had entirely evaporated. In its place, a grim, highly efficient industrial rhythm had taken hold.

Josh's radical tactical restructuring was working flawlessly. The decision to pull half of the defenders back from the parapets had instantly saved the city from a total collapse. The claustrophobic crush of the walkway was gone. The remaining militia members now had space to either side of them. They could plant their boots firmly on the blood-slicked wood, brace their hips, and thrust their iron spears with genuine leverage, rather than simply poking blindly over the shoulders of their neighbours.

Furthermore, the new, unobstructed running lane directly behind the fighting line was proving to be a masterstroke of defensive engineering.

Josh had not stepped back to rest with the first rotation. He had stubbornly refused to leave the wall until he was absolutely certain the new system would hold. He watched as Brett, looking completely hollowed out and shivering violently from magical depletion, was led away by Carcan to sit against the inner barricades.

With the elemental mage resting, the magical defence of the central gatehouse was handed over to Caistina Tharistrar.

The elven mage trainer was a terrifying, awe-inspiring sight to behold. She did not cast with the raw, chaotic, emotional fury of Brett. Her magic was an exercise in perfect, flawless arcane geometry. She stood at the edge of the parapet, her sapphire-blue robes perfectly still in the howling wind, her pristine white staff raised.

She watched with cold, amethyst eyes as the writhing ocean of zombies began to construct another massive, fleshy tower against the timber wall. She waited patiently, letting the undead climb higher and higher, completely ignoring the nervous shouts of the nearby guardsmen.

She waited until the tower was twenty feet high, teeming with hundreds of interlocking ghouls.

Then, Caistina tapped the base of her staff gently against the floorboards. A brilliant, concentrated ring of pure violet arcane energy manifested directly at the foundation of the undead tower. The ring contracted violently, acting like a magical guillotine. It cleanly severed the load-bearing corpses at the bottom of the pile, shattering their bodies into fine golden dust and ruined meat.

The entire twenty-foot tower sheared away from the wall and collapsed backward in a massive, tumbling avalanche of flailing limbs, crushing the advancing ranks beneath it. Caistina simply lowered her staff, took a measured breath, and turned her serene gaze towards the next forming pile, having expended a fraction of the mana Brett would have used for a similar result.

"Clear the lane!" a hoarse voice shouted from Josh's right.

Josh turned, instantly stepping back into the three-foot running highway. He had formed an ad-hoc strike squad with two other high-tier veterans who had been pulled from their static positions: a dual-wielding beastkin named Korg, and a towering human halberdier wearing heavy chainmail.

Fifty yards down the wall, the relentless tide of zombies had managed to overwhelm a small section of the parapet. A hollow tower of flesh had crested the wooden railing before the rotating mages could destroy its base. Four Crypt Ghouls and a massive, corrupted skeletal hound spilled over the barricade, shrieking as they lunged towards the retreating militia.

"With me!" Josh commanded, his deep voice carrying easily over the din.

He lowered his shoulder, raising his dark steel heater shield, and broke into a heavy, thudding sprint down the clear lane. He did not trigger his lightning skills, strictly conserving his mana for extreme emergencies. He relied entirely on his raw, passive physical strength.

He reached the breach point in seconds, crashing directly into the flank of the skeletal hound like a runaway boulder. The immense kinetic impact shattered the beast's brittle ribs, throwing it completely off the wooden walkway and back down into the smoking pitch trenches below.

Korg arrived a second later, a blur of spinning steel. He ducked beneath the wildly swinging claws of a Crypt Ghoul, driving his twin axes cleanly through the monster's kneecaps. As the ghoul collapsed, the human halberdier stepped forward, bringing the heavy, axe-like blade of his polearm down in a brutal, decapitating strike that permanently ended the threat.

Josh slammed his shield against the chest of the final ghoul, pinning it hard against the wooden parapet. He drove his longsword through its skull, twisting the blade sharply to shatter the magical core before kicking the dissolving, golden dust over the edge.

The entire skirmish lasted less than ten seconds.

"Breach closed!" Josh roared, pointing to the terrified militia members who had fallen back. "Step up! Lock your spears! Do not let them climb over the lip!"

The bakers and merchants hurriedly rushed back to the parapet, their confidence instantly restored by the brutal, rapid intervention of the veteran strike squad. They thrust their spears downward, preventing any more zombies from cresting the cleared section.

"Good clean work, Guardian," Korg grunted, wiping a thick smear of black sludge from his cheek and offering a fierce, tusked grin. "This roaming tactic is infinitely better than standing in one spot waiting to be eaten. Let’s find another hole to plug."

They spent the next two hours running the length of the central ramparts. They acted as a rapid response unit, a heavy, blunt instrument used to smash the fingers of the horde every single time they managed to grip the top of the wall.

They fought dozens of minor skirmishes. Josh battered ghouls off the railing, crushed zombies with his heavy steel boots, and physically dragged exhausted civilians out of the path of snapping jaws. It was exhausting, brutal labour, but it was incredibly controlled. The desperate, suffocating panic of the morning was entirely gone. They were managing the meat grinder.

Finally, a sharp, piercing blast from a brass horn echoed from the central watchtower.

It was the signal for the first shift rotation.

A massive, ragged cheer rippled down the length of the timber walls. The exhausted militiamen who had been thrusting spears for the last two hours practically collapsed with relief. They stumbled backward into the clear lane, leaning against the inner barricades to drink heavily from their water skins and bandage their raw, bleeding hands.

Moving up the steep wooden stairs to replace them came the rested defenders. They looked significantly better. Two hours of sitting in the quiet, relatively safe space behind the front line had allowed their adrenaline to reset and their stamina to passively recover.

Josh walked back to the central gatehouse platform, his heavy steel armour completely covered in a thick layer of freezing mud, golden dust, and black gore. His chest was heaving, and a deep, persistent ache had settled into his sword arm.

Waiting for him on the platform was his party.

Brett looked vastly improved. The pale, sickly exhaustion that had hollowed out his face earlier was gone. His mana core had regenerated significantly, and his crimson Ember-silk cloak was shimmering with a faint, eager inner light. Beside him stood Carcan, her pale green robes slightly stained from kneeling in the blood of the triage lines, but her silver eyes were bright and alert.

And standing right at the edge of the stairs, looking like a furious, caged bear, was Bhel.

The dwarven fighter marched directly up to Josh. He did not offer a greeting. He simply placed his thick, calloused hand flat against the centre of Josh's heavy steel breastplate and forcefully shoved the massive human backward.

"Sit down," Bhel commanded, his gravelly voice carrying absolute, uncompromising authority.

"I am fine, Bhel," Josh argued, though he allowed himself to be pushed. He was genuinely exhausted, the heavy weight of his Adamantite-infused bones dragging at his muscles. "The strike squads are working perfectly. I need to keep the momentum going."

"The momentum is going to break if you collapse from exhaustion in the middle of the pathway," Bhel countered, pointing a thick finger towards the inner barricades. "Carcan patched my stitches, and I have been sitting on a supply crate for two hours staring at the wood grain. I am out of patience. Give me the lane. It is my turn to swing the iron."

Josh looked at the dwarf, seeing the fierce, unyielding battle-lust burning in his dark eyes. He looked at Carcan, who offered a small, firm nod of agreement.

"Fine," Josh sighed, unclasping his heavy dark shield and letting it rest against the wooden floorboards. He rolled his aching shoulders, feeling a profound wave of fatigue wash over him the moment he officially surrendered his post. "But keep the roving squads tight. Do not get bogged down in a prolonged melee. Hit the breach, clear it, and move on."

"I know how to crack a skull, lad," Bhel grinned, drawing his two heavy iron throwing axes from his belt. "Enjoy your nap."

The dwarf turned and marched eagerly down the clear lane, instantly taking command of a small group.

Brett stepped up to the edge of the wooden parapet, taking his place beside Caistina. The elemental mage looked down at the writhing, screaming ocean of rotting meat continuing to crash pointlessly against the solid oak logs of the wall.

"They are incredibly persistent, you have to give them that," Brett muttered, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose.

He looked down the line of freshly rotated militiamen taking their places on the wall. They looked rested, but they were still terrified civilians facing an apocalyptic horde. Brett decided they needed a strong, undeniable psychological boost to kick off their shift.

"Lady Caistina," Brett said respectfully, glancing at the elven mage. "I believe the front lines are growing slightly too crowded. With your permission, I am going to clear the board."

Caistina raised an elegant eyebrow, stepping slightly to the side to give the young scholar room. "Do not overextend yourself, Brett. The battle is still young."

"Just a brief flare," Brett promised.

He stepped to the very edge of the wood, his crimson cloak whipping wildly in the freezing wind. He did not aim at the climbing towers or the base of the wall. He aimed further out, targeting the dense, tightly packed masses of zombies surging across the open plains towards the pitch trenches.

Brett opened his hands wide, tapping into the vast, renewed reservoir of his mana core.

He did not use a compressed beam of plasma. He unleashed a massive, sweeping wave of pure, roaring elemental fire.

The spell exploded from his hands in a blinding, terrifying arc of orange and white heat. The wave of fire expanded rapidly as it flew through the air, completely engulfing a section of the plains roughly fifty yards wide.

The impact was catastrophic. The roaring inferno struck the advancing horde like a physical tsunami of heat. Hundreds of zombies, ghouls, and rotting villagers were instantly incinerated before they could even reach the base of the wall. The dark, freezing mud of the plains flash-boiled into thick clouds of foul-smelling steam, and the golden dust of the destroyed dungeon beasts caught the thermal updrafts, swirling into the sky like a shower of glowing sparks.

A massive, awe-inspiring gap was instantly carved into the front lines of the enemy.

A deafening, triumphant cheer erupted from the rested militia members all along the ramparts. They thrust their spears into the air, their morale absolutely skyrocketing as they watched the horrifying monsters reduced to harmless ash in a single, devastating heartbeat.

Brett immediately dropped his hands, cutting the spell. He let out a sharp, ragged gasp, stumbling backward a half-step.

He instantly felt the severe, punishing cost of the display. That single, massive wave of fire had instantly drained nearly a quarter of his freshly regenerated mana pool. The sharp, warning ache in his temples returned with a vengeance.

"Right. That was incredibly foolish," Brett scolded himself quietly, wiping a line of cold sweat from his pale forehead. He looked at Caistina, offering a sheepish, apologetic smile. "Consider the board temporarily cleared. Back to the budget."

Caistina offered a small, amused smile, shaking her head as she returned to her precise, targeted arcane strikes.

Brett settled back into the disciplined, conservative rhythm of the siege. He ignored the individual ghouls and the chaotic charging lines, waiting patiently until a tower of interlocking zombies reached twenty feet before firing a single, highly compressed bolt of plasma to melt the base.

The siege ground on.

The shifts were extended to four-hour rotations, maximising the rest periods while ensuring the defenders on the wall did not completely lose their edge. Josh rested, forcing himself to drink tepid water from a leather skin and eat a handful of dry rations. He listened to the constant, rhythmic sounds of the battle above him, his mind completely focused on the fact that the plan was working.

When Josh finally rotated back onto the wall late in the afternoon, the atmosphere was entirely surreal.

The sun was beginning to dip towards the western horizon, casting long, bruised purple shadows across the smoking, cratered expanse of the northern plains. The temperature was plummeting rapidly, the winter frost returning to bite at their exposed skin.

Yet, the morale on the timber ramparts was higher than it had been since the warning bells first tolled.

The defenders had settled into a comfortable, almost mundane routine. The sheer, existential terror of the ten-thousand-strong army had faded into a manageable, grinding chore. The militia members were actually conversing between spear thrusts. They cracked grim jokes about the smell of the burning zombies and placed wagers on which high-tier mage would topple the next tower of flesh.

They were surviving. The massive, thirty-foot timber walls of Ashenfall were holding strong, entirely unbreached.

Josh stood near the central portcullis, leaning against the wooden railing beside Brett. The heavy fighter looked out over the endless sea of rotting meat continuing to crash against the base of the wall. The horde was visibly thinner in the immediate vicinity of the gates, the sheer volume of magical and physical destruction finally beginning to carve noticeable dents into the lesser infantry.

"We are actually doing it," Josh murmured, a profound sense of settled, genuine hope blossoming in his chest. He looked at the elemental mage. "If we can maintain this rotation through the night, they will eventually run out of zombies to throw at us."

Brett pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his pale eyes tracking the distant, dark horizon. He did not share Josh's optimism.

"We are doing it because the enemy commander is allowing us to do it, Josh," Brett corrected softly, his voice completely devoid of his usual sarcasm. "We have spent the entire day fighting the absolute weakest, most expendable units in their army. We have been fighting farmers and stable hands."

Brett pointed a long, trembling finger over the massive, writhing sea of zombies, directing Josh's attention to the distant northern hills.

"Look at the back lines," Brett whispered.

Josh followed the mage's gaze.

For the entire day, the thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights and the two dozen towering Bone Goliaths had stood perfectly, terrifyingly still. They had remained exactly out of bow range, silent sentinels watching the lesser infantry die by the thousands.

But as the bruised purple light of the late afternoon began to fade into twilight, the dark horizon suddenly shifted.

The ambient temperature across the entire valley plummeted violently, a sudden, suffocating wave of necrotic cold that pierced straight through Josh's heavy steel plate armour. The deep, rhythmic thudding of the zombie charge faltered, changing pitch and tempo.

The endless, writhing ocean of rotting meat suddenly parted.

It was a horrific, coordinated manoeuvre. The shrieking ghouls and stumbling zombies forcefully divided themselves, creating wide, clear, and perfectly straight avenues directly through the centre of their own chaotic horde. They scrambled over each other, eager to clear the path.

"Oh, Light preserve us," a nearby guard captain gasped, dropping his heavy broadsword onto the wooden deck with a loud clatter.

Stepping forward into the newly created avenues were the heavy assets.

The two dozen massive Bone Goliaths began to march. They lumbered forward, their iron-plated boots shaking the frozen earth with every step. They were carrying the massive, shattered trunks of ancient oak trees, heavily banded with rusted iron. They were true, unstoppable siege rams, and they were heading directly for the main iron portcullis.

Flanking the towering behemoths were the thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights. They marched in perfect, terrifying lockstep, their rusted halberds lowered into a devastating, impenetrable wall of sharp steel points. They were not a mindless swarm; they were a highly disciplined, unbreakable military phalanx.

The Bone General had finally grown tired of the meat grinder. The game of attrition was officially over.

Josh gripped the leather handle of his dark steel shield so tightly his knuckles screamed in protest. The fragile, blossoming hope he had felt just moments before was instantly and violently crushed. He looked at the massive, terrifying army of elite monsters marching relentlessly towards the timber walls.

The true siege of Ashenfall was just beginning.
