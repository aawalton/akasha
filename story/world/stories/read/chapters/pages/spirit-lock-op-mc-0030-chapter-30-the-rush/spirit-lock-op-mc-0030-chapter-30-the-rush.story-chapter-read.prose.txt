Hayden had barely relearned how to use the internet and record blackmail, so he didn’t have time to learn about things like group chats. That said, he knew the power of advertising, and organic traffic was the best. Not fifteen minutes later, he was swarmed by people on all sides.

“Oh my God, it’s true!”

“There’s no way…”

“Dude, look at the amount he’s raised. He can officially buy a boba.”

Male students roared with laughter. He didn’t mind the mockery, but they were getting in the way.

Hayden snapped his fingers with an amplification technique. It instantly captured everyone’s attention.

“Gossip is encouraged, but get in line or step off the path.”

In a fit of confusion, twenty people got into line just to clear the path—and then stayed there.

A woman up front laughed in bewilderment when Gluttony started flying circles around her head. “Wait… should I do it?” Her friend said, “Uh… yeah. Hold up, I’m going to record it.”

Hayden knew that there was such a thing as bad advertising, but this wasn’t that.

Not long after students began taking videos, and the men started buying the women hug-time, Hayden's business sales ballooned. It wasn’t even 9:00, and the sign read:

Credits raised: 2,920

Time remaining: 00:55

The line was long, and students complained about it nonstop.

“I’m going to miss my class.”

“Right? This is so stupid. He should just raise his prices.”

“Yeah, but that’d be the smart thing to do. How much do you think people would pay for this, anyway?”

Another group said, “I bet you this is the most money he’s ever had.”

“No doubt. He’ll probably send money home to his family.”

A third was just laughing, saying, “Dude… do you think it actually likes being held? Or is he just hoeing it out on the cheap?”

Hayden didn’t mind the gossip. Gluttony loved being held, and ninety percent of its huggers were females, as men were mostly buying hug-time for them. When males did hug Gluttony, the crowd took videos of how grumpy the little thing got. But no matter how Gluttony was admired, the spirit was thrilled about it. It was a glutton for attention, after all.

That said, some attention wasn't good.

The trouble came during lunchtime. A woman squealed as she hugged Gluttony and yelled, “Oh, my… God! I want it so bad!”

Leo Strister was with that girl. Her name was Nara Lasli, a so-so businesswoman’s daughter that his father wanted him to hook up with. His old man was trying to seal a distributing contract with her mother, so he wanted Leo to get buddy-buddy with the family. Not too close, of course—just the right amount. That's why he was playing the game. It was business. And business required shows of appreciation. Small gestures that show character and care. So he stepped forward to address Hayden with a friendly smile.

“How much?”

“For what?”

Leo nudged his head at Gluttony. “It. I don’t care how much it costs.”

“My spirit’s not for sale.”

“Everything’s for sale. And a horix like this is top-shelf three mil. I’ll give you nine.”

“No.”

The entire line froze, and red lights started blinking as people recorded. Leo didn’t like being on video, but people were saying, “Did he really just turn down nine million? Is he stupid?” That gave Leo confidence that he was being reasonable.

“Listen, man,” he said. “You can do math, right? You can get three horixes for the one you have. I even made the math simple enough for you to understand.”

His girlfriend snorted with laughter.

“It isn’t for sale,” Hayden repeated. His expression didn’t change, but his eyes turned threatening.

Leo’s lips curved into an offended smirk. “You’re unbelievable, you know that?”

Hayden didn’t respond.

“Okay, since you clearly can’t do math, I’ll make it easier. What do you got so far? Six grand? Okay, double that number and put three zeros. That’s how much I’ll offer you.”

The people in line didn’t even want to hug the beast anymore—they just wanted to see if Hayden was stupid enough to turn down twelve million credits for his spirit.

“It doesn’t matter if you triple the number and put five zeros at the end—I’m not selling it.” Hayden turned to Nara. “You’ve well exceeded your twenty seconds. That’ll be an extra forty credits.”

Leo laughed viciously. “Do you want to die?”

Leo couldn’t believe what he just heard. Not only did Hayden turn him down, but he charged his girl for the time they spent negotiating. It was unbelievable!

Hayden didn’t react to his threat, and that made Leo snap.

He lunged at Hayden. To his shock, Hayden dodged, grabbed his wrist, and did something terrible to it. His cora channels spasmed, and his fingers twitched. It felt like his arm was made of glass, and it cracked in webs before healing and then breaking again.

“Did you just touch him?” Nara screamed when she saw Leo stumble back.

Leo gritted his teeth and subtly summoned his Scatter Blade spirit from his good arm. He was blind with rage and ready to strike. He almost did, but just as he was about to attack, a stern voice cut through the noise. “What’s going on here?”

Hayden turned to find Carian Raina, his interviewer and Fundamentals of Crisis Management teacher, rushing up to him. “Ms. Raina. I was hoping I would run into you.”

Carian glanced at Leo’s spirit, which immediately melted away. She then cocked a brow when she read his sign. “What the hell’s going on here?”

“Fundraising,” Hayden said.

“For what?”

“To buy a spirit.”

A collective cackle of laughter rang out, and many students left the line to get a better view of the chaos.

“Twenty credits at a time?” Carian asked.

“Yes,” Hayden replied humorlessly.

She studied him carefully and glanced around. “And who said you can do this?”

“Mayle Haya.”

“Don’t lie to me.”

“Call her. She permitted it.”

“She’s busy.”

“Then it seems you have extra time while you wait.” Hayden recalled Gluttony into his chest and summoned him into his palm. “Would you like to spend it hugging a spirit beast? It’s one credit per second.”

The quad rocked with laughter. Carian chuckled sinisterly as she looked around. “You’re coming with me.” She grabbed Hayden by the wrist and dragged him away as the crowd whistled and cheered.

Carian was grateful for an excuse to drag Hayden off; she was also glad he wasn’t fighting back. He was smart enough to know that she wasn’t pulling him away because of a stupid fundraiser. She had a problem that could ruin her position at Junolyx—and that wasn’t permissible.

Carian motioned Hayden into her office and then shut the door. “Take a seat,” she ordered.

Hayden took a seat in a swivel chair. He spun it around to face her and then put an ankle over a knee with a smug smile.

“Oh, you think this is a game,” she said.

“No, I just find it… amusing.”

“Amusing? Jora Hedra is in the hospital right now. Apparently, if a healing spirit heals a kneecap directly, it also heals a… spring selection of tendons, ligaments, cartilage, and… retinacula. And when it does, it must restretch and readjust before someone can walk normally again. To quote the healer: the patella isn’t the most complicated area to heal, but it’s the most painful, immobilizing, and traumatizing.” She leaned against the door. “I take it you understood that?”

“Oh, yes,” Hayden said.

Carian scoffed. “So let me get this straight. The day after you traumatized the son of one of the most powerful women in the universe, and were transferred to the South Tower for your safety, you came to my class and traumatized a president’s son?”

Hayden’s face returned to an apathetic mask.

“Let me be clear,” Carian said. “I don’t care what you did. I care that you said you would do it during my recorded interview. And then you came to my class and actually did it. Do you get what—” Carian paused when she got a message from Jora Hedra. “What the?”
