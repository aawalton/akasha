James first went to the commercial district for foreigners. When he found out that they did not have a blacksmith, he looked for Kouki and Kaina.

It turned out they were lazing around the city, drinking beer. James looked at him, bewildered.

“Hey, can you drink beer? You are a minor, right?”

“Huh? What are you talking about? I am an honored survivor. I bust my ass every day, defending humanity. It is only right that I deserve a little entertainment,” Kouki said. “It is a Japanese tradition for the working class to have beer after work!”

“Kouki! Stop drinking! Geez, it is not healthy!” Kaina barged into the tavern, pouting.

“Eh, Kaina? Why are you here?”

“Why? We are teammates! Let’s go home! I have cooked for you!” Kaina said as she cocked her hand on her hip. James rolled his eyes at the rom-com situation. Part of him envied Kouki for having a girl cooking for him. Alas, romance was not for him. James had decided that dungeons and work were more important than girls.

He refused to have romance manga inside a dungeon.

“Since Kouki is drunk, can I ask for your help, Kaina?”

“Oh, James, hello. You need my help?” Kaina, who saw James as someone much more intimidating than even Kouki, looked baffled when James asked for her help. “Sure!”

“James, Kaina is my woman, alright! Don’t you lay a hand on her. If you need help, why not ask me? Or is it something only a girl could do?”

“I know you guys have been an item for ages now. Normally, I would have asked you, but since you are drunk, I don’t want your help.” James rolled his eyes. He knew they were a bit too close to each other, but speaking it so brazenly? Did he finally escape the stubborn and oblivious Japanese protagonist’s curse?

“W-we aren’t an item… not yet.” Kaina blushed. Her eyes turned away from James.

Apparently not.

“…Let’s move out. Professional matter.” James pointed toward the exit door.

“Ah, yes.”

James moved out, and when they arrived in the alley next door, Kaina took a few deep breaths to calm herself. James looked around cautiously. His raptor carried valuable goods. System-registered weapons were sought after by survivors. Moreover, he also had ten gold bars. These gold bars were worthless to him, but there were reasons for survivors and locals alike to rob him.

“So, Mr. James, how can I help you? I am just your average survivor. I am not proficient with swords like Kouki, so I don’t think I can help you with adventuring.”

“I don’t need help with adventuring. You see, I found these weapons inside a ruin. Can you see their descriptions?” James handed her a rusted axe.

“It says it can be repaired.” Kaina inspected the rusted axe.

“So, do you know if a local blacksmith could repair them? I am not registered with the city at the moment, so they refused me.”

“Ah, I see. I will negotiate in your place then. Um… do you have money? I mean… gold coins?”

“Here. I have ten gold bars. Do you think you could exchange them somewhere?” James asked.

“Whoa! Gold bars! Amazing! Um… let’s see. We need to go to the bureau first to exchange these,” Kaina said.

“Bureau? The Census Bureau? Why?”

“The Census Bureau functions similarly to a town hall. So any restricted goods, such as gold bars and food, need to go through them first,” Kaina explained. “Where did you find these?”

“Inside a ruin. Five days’ walk to the north,” James said.

“Whoa, amazing. It’s going to be tight, then. Just going there and back would take twenty days,” Kaina said. “The hunting quests were over a week ago.”

“Over? Why?”

“There are no more monsters around. You see, after the first week, all plants died, and monsters began eating each other. Then, in the second week, they all died of starvation. That’s what the locals said. I also saw them gradually disappearing,” Kaina said.

James looked at the Census Bureau before them. His impression of this bureau grew worse and worse. They acted more like a totalitarian government than simply registering people into the city.

“Huh? Starvation? Even humans could last more than seven days without food. Are they rats?” James frowned. Many animals on Earth could last months without food by hibernating. Why didn’t they do it here? How could they last only seven days?

James thought for a moment, but he soon knew the cause.

“Ah. Water.”

“Yes. Thankfully, we have a mage who can use water magic inside the city, so water is not an issue.” Kaina nodded and went for the receptionist. “Ah, excuse me. I wanted to trade these gold bars for coins. Can I do it here?”

“Yes, you may. We will give you 50 coins for these gold bars.”

“That’s a bit low, isn’t it?” James looked over the gold bars. Five gold bars, each over a kilogram in weight, translated to 50 coins. That meant 10 coins per bar. Each coin was about the size of a penny. No matter how James thought about it, it was too little.

“Um… the Census Bureau does not accept negotiation. All prices are final. Will you trade them, Mr. James?” Kaina said.

“Sure. I don’t mind. As long as it is enough to repair the rusted weapons,” James said. Kaina then nodded toward the goblin receptionist. He put the coins into a pouch and gave it to her.

When James received the coins from Kaina, he suddenly felt the eyes of the entire building lock onto him. At first, James wondered what that was about, but then a question came to mind.

“Say, what happens if you commit a crime here, like stealing, robbing, or murder?”

“Exile or manual labor, depending on how much value was stolen. Exile or a death sentence is guaranteed for murder,” Kaina said. James felt relieved.

“But they existed, right? The system didn’t ban us or the locals from committing a crime.”

“Yes. Even the locals committed crimes. The system didn’t interfere,” Kaina nodded. “Ah, two survivors were exiled yesterday because of murder.”

“Huh? Why?”

“Some people wanted to rob them, and these survivors ended up killing them. The local guard saw it, and after the Census Bureau gave the verdict, these two survivors were exiled. They will be killed on sight if seen inside the city again.”

“Even though it was self-defense?”

“Yes,” Kaina said.

James parsed her words for a moment. The system did not prohibit crime. Only the guards and the city council did. The system also didn’t investigate or notify anyone about the crime.

“Here we are.” Kaina went toward a local blacksmith.

“Did you get your spear here too?”

“Yes. But it is not a system-registered spear, so I couldn’t use any spear skills. I have been a bother to Kouki because of it,” Kaina frowned.

“I have an iron spear at the back of my raptor. You can have it. Take it as a thank-you for your help. I do not use a spear, so maybe you or Andrew could use it,” James said.

“Really? Thanks!” Kaina skipped toward the blacksmith. With confidence, she stated her business to him.

“So, do you think you could fix it?” Kaina asked.

“Oh!! This was an ancient weapon of the gods! It would be an honor! It would normally cost a hundred gold coins, but I will reduce it to ten. However, I need more than just gold to fix this axe. I need iron ingots, starmetal, and a block of water nectar.”

“What are those things? What about this sword, spear, shield, helmet, and bow?” James asked. The blacksmith ignored him, so Kaina reiterated his words.

“We need iron ingots for all of them except the bow. For bows, you need wooden planks. All of these can be bought inside the city. However, I also need something more. I need starmetals. You need to search for these unique items, or I couldn’t fix them,” the blacksmith said. James rolled his eyes at the upcoming fetch quest.

“For the shield, I need a Guardian Core. For the sword, I need the Essence of the Spirit King. For the bow, I need a Branch of Evergreen. For the spear, I need Mountain Icedew, and for the helmet, I need Liquid Metal. As for starmetals, you can mine them in the mountains to the west.”

“They sound incredibly rare,” James sighed. “except the starmetals, any idea where to find them?”

Again, Kaina repeated James’ words.

“I don’t know. I have never left this city,” the blacksmith said.

“Well, they are still system-registered weapons. Maybe we can use them as they are.” Kaina was eager to show off her spear skill against monsters, but the blacksmith snatched all the rusted materials away from her.

“No! If you break them, they would be irreparable! Please! I beg you! Keep them here until you find the materials! They are useless. Even ordinary iron weapons and armor are better! I will give you my steel weapons in exchange!!”

“No,” James said.

“In that case! I will steal them! I won’t return them!” The blacksmith hugged the rusted weapon for dear life. He then shouted for guards.

“Is there a problem?” A passing guard came inside after hearing the commotion. James wondered whether the guard was attracted by the noise or enticed by the system to come here.

“I am stealing these worthless weapons. In exchange, I will compensate them with steel weapons.” James blinked his eyes. He had expected the blacksmith to lie and accuse him of nonsense, but he was actually honest.

“Understood. The crime and reparation have been noted. You are free to go. Kaina, here are the reparations offered by the blacksmith for your loss. The value of these steel weapons is much higher than the value lost. Please accept them.”

“Wait a minute! You can’t do this! I want those weapons back! I don’t want reparations!” James was bewildered. Kaina had assured him that any crime would be met with adequate punishment. He never thought there would be someone who would rob him in broad daylight like this.

“The law has been upheld. The case is closed!” the guard said.

“You fucker!” James was about to draw his sword and deal his vengeance personally, but Kaina held him back.

“No. Threatening a guard is a crime, James. Don’t do it.” Kaina tried to calm the rampaging James.

“I was scammed and robbed in front of these assholes! Are you saying this is legal? Those rusted weapons are worth more than this junk!”

Hearing the keyword, the guard turned to James. “Oh? Are you saying the reparations are not enough? These rusted weapons seem far inferior to the steel ones the blacksmith gave you.”

“It is a system-registered weapon!”

“Hm? System? What’s that? By the way, things such as sentimental value are not acknowledged here. We must evaluate the stolen goods objectively. Are you saying these rusted weapons—ones that might break in a single strike—were more precious than the steel weapons the blacksmith gave you?” the guard asked.

James thought hard. The rusted weapons were not merely system registered, but it could be repaired to be much stronger weapon.

“Yes! The blacksmith said these rusted weapons could be repaired! If they are repaired, the value could exceed these steel junk several times over.”

“I see. Is that true, Blacksmith?” the guard asked.

James held his breath, wondering whether the blacksmith would lie.

The smith spoke.
