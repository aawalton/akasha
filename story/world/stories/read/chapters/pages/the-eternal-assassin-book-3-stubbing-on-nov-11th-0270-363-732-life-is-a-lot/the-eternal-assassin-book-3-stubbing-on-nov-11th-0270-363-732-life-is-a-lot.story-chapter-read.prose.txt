While the warm meal was very much appreciated after their eventful day, Asher realized quickly enough that rest was a tad harder to come by. From Lanra’s funeral earlier that morning to all the different battles they’d fought that day… Rescuing people from a burning town and discovering that there were even more secrets he wasn’t yet privy to…

Asher wasn’t surprised in the slightest to find himself lying awake in bed later that night, sleep the last thing on his mind as he stared up at the ceiling. One of the weird things he’d had to get used to about having an evolved darkness resistance was the fact that nighttime was never really dark for him anymore. A small part of him missed the days where he could have his eyes wide open but still fail to see anything, and he let out a quiet sigh at the thought of just how many changes he’d gone through since coming to this world, both physically and mentally. The vast majority of them were changes he was one-hundred-percent on board with, but every so often he found himself feeling nostalgic for the past. Not for his old life specifically, because this new one was an upgrade in just about every sense. But for how simple things were.

“You okay?” Samantha murmured, yawning as she scooted closer and draped an arm sleepily around his chest. “You’re normally out like a light after big fights like these.”

“I think dying has a tendency to tire me out,” Asher chuckled softly, the absurdity of the statement not doing anything to help his strange mood. “I didn’t actually die at any point today. Hell, beyond chopping off my own hand and a few full-body burns, Eternal Regeneration didn’t really have much to do.”

“I still don’t know how I feel about your Remote Control skill,” Samantha admitted. She frowned slightly, no doubt as she thought back to all the times she’d witnessed him lop off his own limbs while he practiced. “It makes me sort of nervous about my own blended skills.”

“I’m pretty confident your blended skills aren’t going to require you to chop your own limbs off,” Asher said, leaning down to kiss her head before letting out another sigh. “Sorry for waking you. It’s not really any one thing in particular that’s got me wired and awake tonight. It’s just… today was a lot.”

Nodding in understanding, Samantha let out a sigh of her own as she laid her head against his. “No kidding. For what it’s worth, I thought what you said at Lanra’s funeral was beautiful. Especially seeing as you barely knew the woman. And then to go from that right to a lich hunt… I’m sure you wanted to get your mind off of her death by throwing yourself right into the thick of battle, but I’m not surprised you can’t sleep. You haven’t had a chance to really process her death yet.”

“We’ve been through so much these past couple of months,” Asher said, thinking back to the countless friendly faces he’d encountered throughout his time on this world thus far. “Not just you and me or our team, but everyone that’s fought by our side. Donvath and his truthseekers… Draken and his gang members… Everyone we’d worked with up until now… I hadn’t really thought about it before, but nobody ever died. We’ve had close calls for sure. Not all that surprising considering the enemies we’ve faced. But I’m the only one that’s ever taken the real hits before now, and I can just shrug death off like a coat that doesn’t fit quite right. I’m not so naïve as to think it wasn’t going to happen sooner or later, it’s just… I always expected it to happen in the heat of battle. There one second and gone the next, you know? It pains me to say this, but I think the fact that Lanra hung in there for so long… The fact that I got to have that one last conversation with her and witness her in that terrible state…”

Asher trailed off, not willing to voice his inner thoughts due to the shame creeping up his body. In what world was it acceptable to admit that part of him wished Lanra had died the moment the magical explosion hit them rather than dragging it out the way she had? Certainly not on either of the two worlds he’d lived in thus far. For her part, Samantha didn’t say anything, choosing instead to wrap her other arm around him and hold him as he stared up at the ceiling. The two of them laid like that for a few minutes while he worked through the unpleasant feelings rolling around inside him. Eventually, he continued.

“I think I finally understand why so many people we worked with at the funeral home chose to go with a closed casket for their loved ones,” he admitted at last. “I’d always thought it strange, not wanting to get one last look at someone you cared enough about to drop a few grand on a fancy coffin and hold a whole gathering for. But I get it now. I want to remember Lanra as the amazing realm wanderer she was. The person who was powerful enough to lead a team of warriors not just through the different realms, but even into battle against a deadly lich threatening the whole continent. Instead, every time I think back to her, all I can see is her frail body lying there on Richard’s couch. Her skin so pale it was literally translucent, and her muscles barely able to function as she clung to life just long enough to give her parting words. And what’s almost worse, is I can’t even fully focus on my mixed-up feelings around Lanra and all this, because my mind is busy racing with all the secret crap Amanda and Groln kept alluding to about the ocean!” Asher snapped, clenching his fists as he thought back to the terrified looks the two greater powers had exchanged with one another. After witnessing just how much power each of those two warriors contained, that just made him even more curious and concerned about whatever it was that had the two of them take on such expressions.

Sitting up in bed, Samantha pulled him toward her so that he was leaning against her. Wrapping her arms around him and resting her chin on top of his head, she hummed in acknowledgment.

“That’s definitely a lot for anyone to think about. Do you want to see if the kitchen can’t help whip up something to help you sleep? There are certain teas that can help with that, and I’m sure the palace would have access to that kind of thing.”

“I doubt it would work. Complete Resilience sort of makes me immune to most weaker poisons,” Asher snorted. Letting out another sigh, he turned and buried his face in Samantha’s chest as he wrapped his own arms around her. Clenching his eyes shut, he continued. “Sorry to bother you with all this. And then sorry for saying sorry. I know you’re just going to tell me that being here for one another is part of the whole being engaged thing. But that doesn’t stop me from feeling bad about it.”

“At least you’re aware I think you apologizing for sharing your thoughts with me is ridiculous,” Samantha teased, running her fingers through his hair as she thought. “For what it’s worth, I’m more annoyed at the fact that these big secrets are being kept from us than I’m letting on as well. Maybe it’s just a difference in perspective. In typical fashion, Moxy put it best earlier. It’s only natural for kings and queens and people in power to have all sorts of secrets they don’t bother sharing with commoners like us. I’ve been struggling with the thought for a while now, but it’s the fact that I’m not ‘just a commoner’ anymore that keeps giving me pause. The fact that I could pull out my own sending stone at any point and not only call the High Prince of the entire kingdom, but the fact that he’s almost all but guaranteed to actually pick up, still keeps me up late at night every now and again.”

“I told you before, you should just start prank calling Donvath every now and again to help with that,” Asher said, laughing as he imagined the confused look on Donvath’s face in response to being asked if his refrigerator was running. “You still feel like that?”

“Not nearly as much as I did at first. But still a little,” Samantha admitted. “I think that’s sort of the point I’m trying to make. I’m not telling you to give up on figuring out these secrets or anything, because I desperately want to know what’s going on with our continent as well. But when it comes to stuff like Lanra’s passing… I always hated it when the other villagers told me this while I was grieving over my parents, but I have to admit, they weren’t wrong. Time heals all wounds. Not completely, and certainly not quickly. But it heals them nonetheless.”

“We had the same saying in my world as well,” Asher said. Sitting up, he gave Samantha a loving kiss before cupping her face in his hand and smiling at her. “Thank you. Just for listening to me ramble on about everything. I promise I’m fine, it’s just a lot sometimes.”

“I’d argue it’s a lot all the time with the way we live our lives,” she snorted, leaning forward and giving him another kiss. “Did I ever tell you about the future I used to imagine for myself after I’d somehow managed to pay off Trellis’ loan and Brian grew up? I’d live my life hunting in the Lavash Forest, using the skills my parents taught me to keep food on the table and sell pelts at the roving merchants that came into town every month. Maybe use the excess shards to patch up the cabin and make a few improvements here and there. I’d meet somebody nice and quiet, who enjoyed the forest as much as I did. Have one or two kids of my own when I was older, probably after Brian himself was married and out of the house. Details changed here and there depending on my mood, but the overall picture was pretty much always the same.”

“Nice and quiet, huh?” Asher asked, getting a giggle from Samantha before she gave him another kiss.

“Yep. A quiet, peaceful life, where I didn’t have to worry about potentially losing my family home just because I couldn’t make a monthly payment on the loans I was saddled with. For the longest time, that was all I really wanted. Horntho Village was tiny and out of the way. Nothing exciting ever happened there, and so nothing exciting was ever part of my daydreams about the future.”

“So… what changed?”

“Something exciting happened,” Samantha shrugged. “It was horrifying and terrible and despite everything good that came from it, I still wish I could go back and order Brian to remain at home rather than join us when I took those alchemists into the forest to hunt for rare herbs. No twelve-year-old deserves to be kidnapped and locked away in a lich’s tower. But all the same, even with all the bad parts of that situation… it was probably the first truly exciting thing that had ever happened in my entire life. And then obviously you showed up and saved us, and then the excitement just kept on coming. I killed demons with my bow, even if they were just imps. I helped you find an awakened beast and was suddenly free of the debt I’d expected to spend at least another decade or two to pay off. Again and again my entire world view was thrown into chaos, and you know what the weirdest thing was? I liked it. I liked not knowing what the day was going to bring when I woke up in the morning. The world is filled with so many bad things, but it’s filled with so many good things as well.”

“It’s definitely got some good things,” Asher agreed as he gave her another kiss. Samantha hungrily accepted, running her hands through his hair as she pulled him close.

“That it does,” she murmured when they eventually broke apart, her gaze sending shivers down his spine as she looked at him. Every now and again the hunter seemed to awaken within her, and Asher got a front row seat to knowing just what any piece of wild game felt like in its last few seconds before she pounced. “If we’re going to spend the rest of eternity together, I figure we should probably get used to trying to focus on the good stuff over the bad. What do you think?”

“I am very on board with this plan,” Asher said seriously. That got another amused giggle out of Samantha before she grabbed him and rolled over, ending up on top of him. Leaning down, she whispered in his ear, her hot breath sending a wave of involuntary goosebumps across his flesh.

“Good. Maybe that tea won’t help you get some sleep, but I’ve got one or two other ideas that might work even better. And I know you’re not immune to what I have in mind.”
