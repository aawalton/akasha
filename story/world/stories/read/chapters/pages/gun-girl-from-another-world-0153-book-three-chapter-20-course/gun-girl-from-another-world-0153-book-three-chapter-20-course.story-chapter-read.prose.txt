Chapter 20

Course

Yorin didn’t come into the trial with us. She wanted to stay outside in case something went wrong and do a second audit on the pavilion’s supplies while she waited.

Trial, that’s what we’re calling the section of the dungeon where the stuff actually happens. You can’t really call it the actual dungeon, since back outside was also totally in the actual dungeon. But trial? Yeah, that makes sense. It’s a test, an exam, a ritual you undergo in hopes of snagging fat loot with a little side of bragging rights.

The Guild is free to change it if they want; they’re the ones in charge of marketing after all, but I think it’ll stick.

Gungeon.

No, I’m not calling it the gungeon. It’s the trial. It has nothing at all to do with guns unless I’m in it. Or the golem boss rolls something fun. I’m not calling it gungeon, shut up!

So, anyway, Yorin is outside in the park area, hanging with Angel (and probably interrogating the poor kid), and the rest of us are in the trial, where the heavy stone portcullis of the entrance just slammed shut behind us. Ten out of ten, very dramatic.

Now that we’re sealed in, my team turns to me. “Alright, Remmi,” Ayre takes the lead in asking, “if this is a troubleshooting run, what should we be expecting to find?”

I cast my gaze around the entrance chamber as I compile my answer. It’s about a hundred square Remmis in size, and looks to be made out of granite bricks and paving stones. There is one exit, on the “North” side of the room if we assume a vertical orientation, in the form of a heavy wooden door.

The decoration is sparse: Just a few of the golden crystal torches along the walls and the angelic harpy statues occupying each corner. The harpy statues have a lot of detail in their feathers and diaphanous robes, practically looking as if they could leap from the corners and scoop the participants up if they fell in battle.

“Well, the rooms are randomly generated,” I reiterate. “Including whether or not there are monsters or puzzles and what kind. It’s still a bronze dungeon, though, so it won’t ever be anything we can’t handle, but expect it to get rough for an unlucky bronze team. Size will be between eight and twenty-four rooms, not counting the entrance here and the boss room.”

“Is the boss randomized, too?” Korrigan asks.

I shake my head. “Still Mr. Golem, though it occurs to me you haven’t met.”

Ayre frowns a bit in concern. “If the dungeon lord is always the same, won’t that make the last step too easy after a while? The golem was tough, but even when we were bronze, it wasn’t really an obstacle.”

But I grin, wink, and give a thumbs up. “Not to worry! I thought of that! If all goes as planned, he should have a modular weapon system now! Whatever he’s carrying is random, though, two to four mounts of assorted weapon categories. If a team gets really lucky, he might not have anything but a couple swords! And if they’re unlucky, well,” I second-guess sharing that one, “I think I’ll leave that for a surprise.”

“Just remember what Sacred Yorin said about your surprises,” Ayre cautions me.

“It’ll be fine,” I assure my best friend. “If everything goes as planned, even the worst possible outcome just means starting over.”

“Right,” he says, “the crisis response. Do we test that now?”

“Nah, we can wait until we’re near the end,” I reply. “For now, we don’t even need to pick a direction!” I motion to the door. “Who wants first dibs?”

Korrigan’s hand shoots up. “Oh, I wanna do it!”

I step out of her way, and she darts for the door, grips the handle in both hands, and throws her weight into pulling it open.

The child peers inside for a long moment as the sound of rattling maracas reaches our ears, then she suddenly slams it shut again, throwing her back against the door.

“I changed my mind,” she says quickly. “Someone else can get this door.”

Ayre and I trade a look. That wasn’t a new sound for us. I nod to the archer as I start changing out my magazine for explosive rounds. He nods back and heads for the door.

“Prioritize explosive magic,” I tell the oni. “Don’t worry about accuracy, except for avoiding us. Just blast into their mass whenever you’ve got the shot. Kyuuga, you’ll want to go for smashing attacks. Spines, hips, and skulls, with priority in that order.”

My words rally them, not because the words are brilliant, but because they’re bland. It’s the magic of a ranking figure looking at something out of a nightmare and giving clean, clear instructions like it’s a Tuesday. No matter how scared or disturbed you might be, your brain decides, if it’s just a Tuesday, what is there to be worried about?

The moment Ayre throws the door open, we charge in like a storm. Sure enough, a massive pile of skeletal rats. Explosive rounds and fireballs collide with the mound immediately, and Kyuuga charges in behind, bashing through the rodents like they owe him money.

Honestly, they … don’t last long. We’ve cleared the entire room in under a minute, but the catharsis is worth it. And, hey, this time, nothing rises out of the shattered remains, so that’s a plus. Instead, the room that had been dark and gloomy is illuminated by the glow returning to its own torches, and a bright, heavenly light manifests into a chest before our very eyes.

I give my gun a little T.J. Lazer spin as I return it to my holster and move for the chest. “Don’t mind if I do!”

“Isn’t it a little too unbelievable for a chest to be dropping right away?” Ayre asks worriedly.

I wave the elf off. “Any event room we clear will reward us with a chest. Don’t get too excited, though. Most of them are going to be junk draws. There’s nothing guaranteeing anything useful for the specific participants except for some generic pity coins and semiprecious stones. Most loot is going to end up traded in at the guild booth for points toward what they really want. At a reasonable mark-up, of course.”

“Of course,” Ayre repeats in drawled sarcasm.

There’s an enchanting silver light that comes out as I open the chest, before it fades so I can see what’s inside, and, sure enough, it’s not much. There’s what I estimate are a few tins’ worth of general detritus and a pretty ring with a band of blue grandidierite flanked by rims of nordic gold.

RING OF MINOR COLD RESISTANCE

---------------------------------

A weakly enchanted, technically magical ring devoid of crafter mark or other identifiers. Such items are often produced in large, market-flooding quantities by first-year enchantment students. They are basically worthless, but are also both easy to come by and inexpensive. Theoretically better than nothing, then, they still find brief use on the fingers of new adventurers that have yet to ascend to anything better.

While wearing this ring, you gain the following effect:

10% reduction in non-magical cold damage sustained

I lower my hand from the Identify spell to look the ring over again, twisting it in my grip as I do so. Wow, okay, so it really does suck. It doesn’t even apply to magical ice attacks! I guess it’d help prevent frostbite? Maybe let you grab something really cold out of the freezer without your hands stinging? Well, it may not be good for anything but looking pretty, but I do know who it would look pretty on.

“Here you go!” I toss it to Ayre.

With his superhuman Agility, he catches it easily, but still looks it over in confusion since I didn’t explain anything. “What does it do?”

“Practically nothing,” I reply without reservation, “but it’ll look good on you. And it’s not like that guild shop is open for trading it in yet anyway.”

I’m treated to the sight of Ayre’s face gaining a few shades of red, but he slips it on his ring finger anyway.

With that done and the rest of the little bit of loot stashed, I jump back to my feet. “Alright, so now we’ve actually got two different ways we can go,” I point out, gesturing to the door to the north and the one to the east, if we’re keeping the same assumption about cardinal directions.

“A dungeon isn’t a maze, and the boss room could be somewhere in the middle, but if we pick a hand and stick with it, we will, at the very least, not lose our way and end up with a very clear idea of the full layout of the dungeon by the time we find that out. One of us should be making a map as we go, too. Who wants to do that? Might be progress toward a cartographer skill!”

Korrigan volunteers again. “I want to do the map!”

“Alright,” I agree, and pull some paper and a charcoal pencil out of my bag for her. “Here you go. Don’t forget to include the starting room, too, and mark every exit we know of, whether we go through it or not.”

She nods quickly and begins doodling.

With that well in hand, I turn to Kyuuga and hold both of my hands out to him. “Alright, Kyuuga, do we follow a left-hand course or a right-hand course?”

He seems to think it over for a long moment, tilting his head as his jaw twitches, and then he jumps to drop a somersault kick into my left hand.

“Agh! Alright, left it is!” I shake my hand out as I stand up straight. “That means we’re taking the north exit first! Korrigan, let me know when you’re ready to move.”

She tucks the paper into her robe. “It’s just the second room, so I’m ready!”

I nod and head to the northern door, check my ammo, and throw it open.

“Wow,” Korrigan coos from behind me. “It’s so pretty!”

In this new room, there’s no battle waiting for us. Instead, fresh air blows across our faces as we step out onto a balcony. There are several very comfortable benches available, all oriented toward the open air, where we find ourselves looking down over endless treetops under charming sunbeams. In addition to the refreshing winds, songbirds drift by, occasionally perching on the stone railing to share with us the stories of their people.

“Pity it’s so early in the dungeon run,” I agree. “It’d be a great place to stop for lunch.”

Ayre, however, is looking around and seeing that the only door out of here is the one we just came through. “More importantly, it’s a dead end.”

Korrigan looks to me at that. “Does that mean the left hand was wrong?”

But I shake my head. “No, it just means that we found our first turnabout. When you pick a direction for navigation of a set structure, the most important thing is not to alter it unless something forces you to do so. Otherwise, you’re adding one more thing you have to remember: Where you switched hands. If you always went left, then you don’t need to ever wonder if you went right. Consistency is key to navigating an unexplored location!”

“Well, alright, then,” Ayre replies, sliding his longbow over one shoulder as he turns back toward the door, “guess we’re consistently heading back the way we came.”
