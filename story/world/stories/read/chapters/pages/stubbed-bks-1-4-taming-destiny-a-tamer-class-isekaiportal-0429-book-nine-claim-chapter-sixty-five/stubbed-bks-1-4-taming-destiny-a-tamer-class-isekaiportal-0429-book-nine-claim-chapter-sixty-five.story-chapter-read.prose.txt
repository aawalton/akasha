"Everyone's worried," Alyna tells me quietly.

She's sitting in my office, her fingers tapping restlessly on the desk. The only reason she's comfortable sitting at all is because I'm not. Instead, I'm standing by the window, looking out onto the city below my third-storey office. It's been five days, and by now it’s no secret that food is getting tight. The only thing I can hope for is that they don’t know just how tight.

"Have there been any fights yet?" I ask.

She hesitates.

"Some," she admits.

I turn my head and look at her sharply.

"Over food?"

"Not supposedly, but I suspect hunger don't help,” she points out. “If things continue like this, some o’ the strong will start takin’ rations from those who are too weak te defend them."

I nod slowly, unsurprised, but dismayed all the same. If people start preying on each other or using their positions to advantage themselves….

"Is everything still being handed out equally, or have there been cases where some people have been given more than others?" I ask, hating to think that it might already be happening, but needing to know.

Alyna shrugs.

“Classers pushin’ their weight around a bit. Sayin’ they should eat first. But cooks’ve been pretty good with that. So far, they haven’ given in.”

“That’s one good thing,” I murmur. “Anything else?”

Again, Alyna hesitates. I send her a sharp look. "Tell me the truth, please," I urge her her as the pause elongates. "I can’t deal with it if I don’t know about it. And it does need dealing with – for everyone’s sake."

She eyes me thoughtfully for a long moment, and then nods fractionally. I feel her decision to trust me flicker through our Bond.

"Ferrell – one o’ the cooks. I saw him bringin’ extra food home to his family. His son’s sick and his wife’s pregnant. But I don' think he came by it legally," she admits.

"I'll speak to him."

Alyna's expression tightens.

"He was just tryin’ to help his family."

"I know," I reassure her gently. "But quite apart from the fact that we've instituted these rations for the good of everyone, and him taking extra risks the future of all of us, we're also currently testing ways to remove the excess Energy from the local plants. For all we know, he took food from an experiment's stores, or from stores that haven't yet been treated, and instead of helping his family, he's poisoned them."

Alyna's expression relaxes a little, turning thoughtful.

"I hadn' thought o’ that," she admits. "It does seem likely he would’a taken food from less guarded stores rather than those with everyone's eyes on them."

"And the less guarded ones are the ones that are no good," I point out. "That's why they're not guarded. Don't worry, Alyna, I'm just going to speak to him – if his intentions are as you say, I’m not going to haul him out in chains. You should know me better than that by now. But you need to tell me if people are sneaking food – or get them to come to me. Hopefully we won’t be on such short rations for long, but stealing just puts everyone at risk."

"I understand, an’ I will pass on the message," she promises, though her eyes are troubled. No doubt she, like me, is wondering just how long we can hold out – and when the threat of the manastorm's mutating effects will become less risky than starving inside.

She updates me on a few more aspects of the colony, then leaves my office with an instruction to send Ferrell to me. I can only hope he'll come quietly. At least if I send Alyna to fetch him, he won't think he's being arrested. Though it's tempting. Given the situation in the colony, one of the cooks essentially stealing food from everyone else's mouths is a serious matter, no matter his intentions.

Progress over the last few days has been maddeningly slow. The mages assigned to the kitchens have managed to remove some of the acidity from the current treatment, but at the cost of replacing it with their own mana. As a result, the food for non-Classers has to sit out for some time to lose that mana – and then it’s always a balancing act to stop it going off. It’s always immensely frustrating when that happens given how much effort is needed to treat it in the first place.

Another concern is that the supplies for the Energy-absorbing tablets are not infinite. We're fortunate that River and the other alchemists harvested so many local components before the manastorm hit – mostly because we were already in the testing phase and knew we needed them – but they weren't expecting to suddenly be barred from the forest. Even the quantity they collected will run out, and sooner than I'd like, especially at our current rate of use.

Unable to sit still, I get up and pace by the window again, desperately trying to work out if there’s something I’m not doing that could help. The problem is that I have no Skill relating to plants – not for want of trying – and have already proven that I can’t pull the Energy out of either the plants or the concoction made from them. Nor am I practised enough at Alchemy to be able to help River and the others. I can help the healers, but even then only by becoming another healer. I’ve failed to discover the source of the sickness every time I’ve tried, and it’s driving me insane.

So, by the time Ferrell arrives, looking exceedingly nervous I’m not in the mood to be generous, though I remind myself to at least be fair. He enters with his cook's hat twisted between his fingers, his front dusted with flour – he’s clearly come straight from the kitchens.

I don't waste time beating around the bush.

"Ker Ferrell. You've been taking extra food from the kitchens without permission to feed your family. Correct?"

The way I look at him makes it clear I don't expect a denial. He swallows, his eyes falling to the ground, the light making his hollow cheeks even more obvious. I don’t have a Bond with him, but I’m pretty sure he’s intending on lying. “Let me suggest that you tell me the truth. I’ll find it out in the end anyway, and I’ll be more lenient if you’re up front with me,” I tell him almost conversationally.

Ferrell freezes for a moment, then I see him sigh and deflate.

"It were for my wife, my lord. She's pregnant, you see, and my child – he's sick. We feared he might die if he didn't get more food."

"Why didn't you take him to the healers?" I ask, forcing myself to be at least a little sympathetic.

Ferrell shrugs slightly.

"Everyone knows the healers are busy. It’s just a childhood illness – we didn't want to bother them."

"Why wasn't the food ration sufficient for your family?"

"Cause he’s sick, sir. He keeps vomiting it back up. My wife too; she suffers badly from the morning sickness, and it's only got worse since we arrived."

I gaze at him slowly for a moment, then nod. Sickness, again. Morning sickness might be nothing – pregnant women are known for it. But then again….

By this point, a full five percent of the settlement seem to have had the symptoms of this mysterious illness and gone to the healers – but we’d always expected that the actual numbers were higher. Ferrell’s words just prove that not everyone with symptoms has chosen to seek help.

"Take your wife and child to the healers," I instruct him firmly. "Especially with your wife pregnant, we can't know if this is something more serious unless the healers check her over."

"Aye, my lord," Ferrell replies, a burgeoning hope in his eyes that he might not end up in the non-existent dungeons or with his head on a pike.

"As for your theft of the food," his expression tightens again at my renewed glare, "I will not demand that you take fewer rations in recompense this time. However, I do expect you to offer your aid voluntarily to the healers for a few marks everyday over the next tenday. You can go after your shifts and once you've taken your rations home to your family. They are doing important work and need every hand they can get. Your contribution can help make up for what you’ve taken from everyone else by stealing."

"I will, my lord," Ferrell replies, sounding relieved. He bows several times, still seeming frozen in place.

My glare softens.

“Make sure to tell the healers that they might have Energy-poisoning – and you if you ate any of the food.”

“My lord?” he asks, paling.

“It depends on which food you took.” I raise a hand as he moves to speak. “No, don’t tell me. But get the healers to check.”

“Yes, my lord,” he replies immediately, a newly-worried look in his eyes.

"Then you're dismissed," I tell him. A few seconds later, as his right foot is about to cross the threshold, I speak up again. "Ferrell."

He turns back.

"My lord?"

"Next time you’re in difficulty, speak to someone before you start taking matters into your own hands. If I hear of you putting our colony in danger again, no matter your intentions, I will not be so lenient. Do you understand?"

"Yes, my lord." He bows again, so deeply his nose almost touches his knees. "I understand. I promise you, it won't happen again."

"See that it doesn't," I reply, and watch him disappear out the door.

I sigh, feeling like a villain for punishing a man just for trying to feed his family. It's not like I would do anything different in his position, though I'd like to think I wouldn't steal extra rations and put everyone else at risk in the process. I can't say that for certain, though.

Still, at least his punishment will be useful. I wasn't lying about the healers needing support. Even non-magical people can help fetch and carry water, tend fires, and prepare fresh bandages or wash out bowls filled with vomit. Ferrell won't find the work nearly as pleasant or fragrant as the kitchens, but it's just as important.

I cast an unwilling look at the piles of parchment on my desk, but find I don't have the motivation to sit back down. I've already spent all morning catching up on administration I'd been neglecting for weeks, and I can't summon the enthusiasm to start again now. Instead, I decide a walk would do me good – and allow me to see Alyna's report in action.

I'm aware, of course, that people act differently when they know the lord is watching, which is partly why I asked Alyna for reports in the first place after the first time on the ship when she came to me letting me know of some minor spats that had a possibility of turning into major personnel issues. I have a feeling that her initial reports were something of a test to see how I would react – and I don’t think that she’s stopped testing me. But I don’t mind this kind of testing when it results in useful knowledge.

As for right now, I might manage to catch people off guard – I’m not wearing my formal robes today.

Nodding to myself, I stand, tuck the parchment away, and activate the enchantment on my desk – a protective dome that blurs the writing on everything within it so that no one can read or retrieve it until deactivated. Only Sarran, Loren, and I are authorised to do so.

Arguably the other lords should be more involved in the day-to-day running of the settlement, but although they're closely involved in the decisions made here, especially since those will be replicated across their own settlements, they've managed to avoid getting caught in the administration. Lucky them.

I head out of the command building and walk through the district. I notice the thin faces and haunted expressions of far too many of my people. They were excited and eager when we first made landfall, relieved after so many months at sea to finally reach solid ground.

That enthusiasm has largely died away – first by the dangers we've encountered on land, and now by the struggle to generate food. The beasts were one thing; we were all expecting that. But I don't blame them for being discouraged by the unexpected issues with the food supply. And having the very skies turn against us, trapping everyone inside away from the sun and open air, only makes it worse.

A boom shatters the air as a powerful lightning bolt strikes somewhere above us, our hill once more taking the brunt of the storm. In reaction, I see several people flinch, some even hunching over and casting wary glances up at the ceiling as if expecting it to fall on their heads.

That was probably one of the loudest strikes I've heard so far, and I can't help wondering how many more our wards can take. Hopefully the Energy absorption function is working as intended and the lightning is adding to the wards' power rather than draining it — but I'd have to ask one of the Enchanters to be sure. I hope that Energy lightning bolts can’t do to enchantments what electrical lightning bolts do to circuits that they hit.

I continue walking, noting those who seem one crisis away from breaking and those who seem to be taking the situation as a challenge they're determined to overcome. For every person who looks despairing – face thin, eyes hopeless – there's someone putting on forced cheerfulness or doing their best to pretend nothing is wrong. Their attempts are betrayed by the way they flinch at each new boom rumbling through the cavern, but I'm glad to see them trying. It means they still have hope. They still expect to come out of this and want to be able to say they were never afraid for a moment that we would fail without being immediately labelled a liar by those who saw their fear.

In the middle of the District One marketplace, I see people drawing from a well. My feet slow and I come to a halt without truly intending on doing so.

Thessalanor said he’d checked it and found nothing, I remind myself, thinking of a different well. Yet still I hesitate.

Then I turn my feet in the direction of District Five and begin walking with more purpose. I had originally intended on double-checking – it’s just that with everything else that’s been happening and all the other issues that have taken priority, I never managed to find the time to go and look at something that Thessalanor has had already investigated. But the truth is that I have specific Skills, ones that are different from most people – it’s always possible I’ll see something the previous investigation missed.

Plus, it’s good justification to avoid the paperwork for a little longer.
