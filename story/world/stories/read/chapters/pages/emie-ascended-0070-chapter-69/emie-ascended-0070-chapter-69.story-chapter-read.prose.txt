“Are you feeling any kind of essence drain while we’re inside the sanctuary?” Stephen asked me out of the blue a few days later.

He still hadn’t managed to partially shift, but he’d been working on pushing his skill in that direction almost nonstop since he’d made the decision to do so.

I thought for a moment before answering, “Not that I can tell. Why?”

“I noticed all of my practice wasn’t draining my essence like I expected it to, given that we’re in the demon realm,” he replied. “I even tried using a couple of other essence skills, and there was no issue. They worked just like normal. That made me realize that there was still ambient essence inside the sanctuary.”

“And you were worried that it was somehow being fueled by me?” I guessed. Just looking at his expression, I could tell that I’d guessed correctly. “That’s sweet of you, but it’s not draining me at all. I’m not quite sure how the space works aside from the fact that it’s soulbound, so maybe there’s some kind of connection to Dia or another space with essence?”

I honestly hadn’t thought about it all that much, nor had I consciously noticed that my demesne hadn’t changed at all after crossing into another realm.

“Do you think maybe the garden has something to do with it?” Stephen suggested. “I’ve heard that plants and animals release essence, and the ambient essence seems a bit richer there than it does in the house.”

“Maybe,” I replied, though my mind instantly homed in on the Golden Oak sitting at the center of the garden. If any of the plants in my garden were responsible, the Golden Oak would be most likely. “Are you having any luck today with adapting your shapeshifting skill?”

“I am, actually. The way my shapeshifting skill works now is quite different from how it worked in my old realm, so it’s almost like learning an entirely new skill instead of adapting an existing skill. I managed to shift just my arm, so it’s only a matter of time before I can manifest a hybrid form.”

That was good. Though it had only been a few days, I was already starting to get pretty bored being stuck inside my demesne.

I’d considered enchanting some accessories that might be helpful during our time in the demon realm, but the fact that each one would need an essence core to function had ultimately stayed my hand.

I’d left a few times to study the worn markings on the old ruin, but even that left me more restless than settled. I couldn’t help but wonder if they really were a form of enchantment. If so, I wanted to learn more, even if I wouldn’t ever be able to use it myself.

Several more days passed before Stephen finally achieved his goal. Surprisingly, the system itself recognized his accomplishment and upgraded his shapeshifting skill.

I’d been worried that being in another realm, the system wouldn’t work properly, but it seemed that worry had been unfounded.

“Before I head into the city, we should see if our communicators work here,” Stephen said.

We already knew that there was ambient essence within my demesne, so my communicator would likely work with no issues. However, I wasn’t so sure the same applied to anything outside of my demesne.

I also had no idea how the devices worked. If they used ambient essence to transmit signals, it wouldn’t matter if both devices could be powered since there wouldn’t be a medium for the message itself to traverse.

But if it used some other form of signal, there was a possibility the devices might work as intended, even in the demon realm.

“Since I’m the one who will be exploring, can you send me to the ruins?” he asked. “If you don’t hear from me after ten minutes, go ahead and assume I couldn’t get it to work.”

Though I wanted to explore a bit as well, I agreed with his reasoning and didn’t argue.

I had barely retrieved my communicator from storage and sat down when the communicator chirped.

“Stephen?”

“Wow. It actually worked.” His voice was as clear as it had ever been.

“Yeah? What did you do?”

“Nothing. I just used it like normal,” he replied. “I figured it would be worth a try just in case.” Through the device, I heard a secondary chime. “Oh! You might want to come get me.”

I didn’t wait and immediately joined him.

“Hold on one second. Let me move somewhere a bit more secure,” Stephen said, holding the device against his ear like a phone.

I realized that Stephen must have tried using the emergency beacon feature just before calling me. Because I was in my demesne at the time, I didn’t receive the signal. In fact, I’d never received an emergency signal while in my demesne before.

Since the connection was already made, I shifted us into the sanctuary without delay. Stephen immediately changed the settings of the device to project the voice on the other side, basically putting it on speakerphone.

“Okay. We’re here.”

“There is more than one of you?” an unfamiliar masculine voice asked through the device.

“Yes. My name is Stephen, and my partner’s name is Emie. We are both Porters, though we are fairly new,” Stephen replied. “We happened upon an incursion breach while we were exploring to expand the network outside of Sorenguard and were asked to gather more information. Unfortunately, it seems we were too close to the breach when it shut down, and we were pulled inside.”

“That isn’t much of a surprise,” the man grumbled. “What rank are the two of you? You said you were fairly new. Do you mean new IRT members? I can’t imagine the guild would have a normal member scout an incursion breach.”

“Yes and no,” I said, entering the conversation. “I’m Emie. Stephen and I are new to the guild and to the IRT, though we only just completed IRT training. We’re only early bronze rankers, but we are both Ascenders, so I guess the guild made an exception?”

“That’s ridiculous. Even if you’re exceptional Ascenders, the guild should never have sent two bronze-rankers to scout an incursion breach. What in the world were they thinking? And you said this happened near Sorenguard? I’ve never heard of it.”

“It’s a city with a fairly new Ascender Dungeon in zone SR12. I think we were in SR11 when we found the breach,” I replied.

“Shit. That means you’re probably on the other side of the realm, then,” the unknown man replied quietly. “It’s too bad I can’t access the network’s location feature through my device to be sure.”

“I don’t mean to offend,” Stephen said, interrupting the man’s musings. “But is there any way we can know for certain that you aren’t just a demon who stole someone’s communicator?”

He made a good point, and it was one I really should have recognized right away. Even with my vast experience, I hated how I still overlooked certain things sometimes. It was probably one of the many consequences of the way I’d progressed through my previous realm.

The voice on the other side of the call barked out a laugh. “Probably not. If you’re new, you probably don’t even know my name. It’s Daver, by the way.”

“The mithril Space Magi?” I asked, instantly recognizing the name.

“You know me?” he asked, sounding surprised.

“Stella told me about you when I first joined the guild. Solan mentioned you as well. He also mentioned that you’d gone missing recently.”

“Stella? You mean the guild receptionist?”

“Yes.”

“And you know Solan as well? How is it you’ve already met the Guild Leader if you’re just a bronze-ranked new Porter? Unless… you’re also a Space Magi?”

“Yep.”

“Well, that’s good news,” he said with what sounded like genuine pleasure. “I’m glad to hear the guild has another Space Magi. Especially an Ascended Space Magi. How long have the two of you been in the demon realm, anyway?”

“A little less than two weeks,” Stephen replied. “We were going to wait until the next incursion breach opened and slip through, but we discovered the demons have a bunch of Dian and other slaves held captive in the nearby city.”

“At your level, you should just escape. Trying to save others will just get you killed. Maybe when you evolve a couple more times, you can come back and try to save everyone, but right now, you’re just too weak. You’ll both get yourselves killed.”

“Maybe, but we have a couple of advantages that I think will keep us safe enough,” Stephen said.

“And neither of us is okay with escaping and leaving a bunch of enslaved Dians behind,” I added. “We’ll be as careful as we can, but we aren’t just going to abandon them.”

“Then you’ll probably die,” Daver said harshly. “I’m not saying this to be mean, but the demons of this realm use Divine Enchanting, which works completely differently from the enchanting of our realm. They use it for everything, too, including slave brands and collars.

“Even if you’re a Space Magi with dimensional storage capable of holding living beings, it won’t be enough to hide them from the demons. Divine Enchanting bypasses stuff like that. Trust me,” he said gravely. “I learned that the hard way.”

“Can you tell me more about Divine Enchanting?” I asked eagerly. “Do you know how it works? Can only demons use it?”

“I’m… not actually sure what I can say with regard to that. I’m sorry.” There was a pause, then he sighed and said something that sounded remarkably close to ‘screw it’. “Actually, never mind that. Divine Enchanting is just that – enchanting that uses divine power instead of essence or any other type of energy.

“Because it is divine in nature, the effects go well beyond normal constraints. In fact, we are only able to communicate right now because of Divine Enchanting.”

I looked at the upgraded communicator in Stephen’s hand, and the information clicked.

“You mean the people of Dia can use this kind of enchantment as well?” I asked.

Not just could, but were.

“As far as I know, anyone can use it if they know how it works. But it’s a secret that only the highest levels in the Enchanter Guild know about.”

“Then how do you know? Are you an Enchanter?” Stephen asked.

“No, but I happened upon the information a while back, so I’m a bit of an exception. I’m not sure if Solan even knows about it, so I’d appreciate you not mentioning anything about it.”

After a moment of quiet contemplation, I said, “Hey, Daver? Did you intentionally come to the demon realm, or was it an accident like with us?”

Sounding completely caught off guard, the mithril-ranked Porter asked, “Why would you ask something like that?”

“Well, you’re mithril-ranked,” I explained. “I find it hard to believe you’d get pulled into an incursion breach if you didn’t want to. Even if you did, you’re also a Space Magi, so I’m sure you would have been able to find another breach to exit through quickly enough. For you to still be here after several months, it has to be intentional.”

The silence was awkward as I waited for him to respond. I had just started to question whether I’d made a mistake in voicing my suspicions when he finally replied.

“You’re right. It was intentional. At least, initially,” he answered bluntly. “I’m not inclined to discuss the reasons behind my actions at the moment since, like you, I have no real way of verifying that you are who you say you are. However, you’re right in thinking that I could easily return to Dia if I wanted.

“In fact, I’ll likely be returning to Dia in the next month or so to refresh my stock of essence cores.” Voice firm, he said, “I strongly suggest you do whatever you can to leave as well. I understand that you feel bad about leaving those who were kidnapped behind, but unless you have a real plan, you’re just going to end up stagnating or dying here otherwise.”

While I had a decent stock of essence cores in storage, most of those were copper- and iron-ranked. Even if I didn’t need to concern myself with Stephen, the cores I had on hand wouldn’t last very long.

We could probably go several months before risking stagnation in normal circumstances, but given how the use of skills actively depleted essence in this realm, I wasn’t so sure the same would apply.

“If you can find an incursion breach to exit through, I suggest you leave as soon as you are safely able,” Daver continued. “We can meet up back in Dia when we both arrive and discuss potential options if you are really serious about rescuing those who have been kidnapped.

“Oh, and keep in mind that not every breach leads back to Dia. The demons of this realm appear to be targeting a few different realms. That’s why not all of the slaves are Dian.”

“We understand,” Stephen said. “It looks like the demons here are building a fortress in Dia, though, so we are fairly confident we’ll end up in the same location as before if we exit when they reopen the breach.”

Through the device, I could hear Daver releasing a heavy sigh. “Well, I guess that is fortunate for you.”

“Have you heard of demons doing something like that before? The fortresses, I mean?” Stephen asked.

“Yes. It happens every so often. We’ve known for a while that demons could target specific locations for their incursions. It ends up working in our favor sometimes, while also serving as a signal that a new dungeon has appeared when a breach suddenly appears farther away than expected.”

I was only half-listening as I mulled over the implications of Divine Enchanting. I was deeply interested in learning more about it, but if people in Dia were basically sworn to secrecy about it, I doubted I’d be able to learn anything there.

Eventually, the conversation with Daver drew to a close. We promised to put off our plans to attempt to rescue the slaves for now, and agreed to return to Dia the next time the nearby incursion breach was opened.

“It still doesn’t feel right,” Stephen lamented once the call was concluded.

“I agree. I understand what he was saying about not being able to do anything to help for now, though. If even the sanctuary isn’t enough to hide any slaves we save, we’d only be putting ourselves at risk.

“Also, the thought of the demons being able to locate and target the sanctuary is really scary. The last thing I want to worry about is demons attacking us inside.”

“I didn’t even think about that,” Stephen admitted. “But I’m not ready to just leave, though. I spent all that time pushing my skill so I could blend in. Maybe it would be worth exploring a bit?”

“Are you sure it’s worth the risk?” I asked uncertainly.

“I think so. At a minimum, it would allow us to verify that my disguise works. That alone makes it worth it in my opinion.”

“Well… If you’re planning to do it anyway, I’d really like to get my hands on something that would help me learn Divine Enchanting. I feel like if I learn it well enough, I might be able to block or outright dispel any tracking they use. That’s not to mention the fact that I’d be able to create enchantments that weren’t dependent on essence to function.”

“Yeah. It’s hard to believe there are people using this technique in Dia,” Stephen said with a frown. “Shouldn’t something like that be better known?”

I could only shrug since I didn’t have any more answers than he did.

“Alright. Well, there’s no time like the present. Are you okay staying here until I return?” Stephen asked.

“Sure,” I agreed. “Take your time.”
