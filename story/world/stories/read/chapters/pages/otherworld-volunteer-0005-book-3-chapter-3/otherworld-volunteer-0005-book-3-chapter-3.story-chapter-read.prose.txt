Noah stood panting, a grim laceration was scored from his shoulder to his bicep. From the health potion he had consumed a minute ago, the wound had already stopped bleeding. He ignored the feverish irritation the bit into his wound. Don’t itch it, Noah reminded himself.

Next to him lay the monstrous beast that took all of them to slay. Although it had the name of a shark, it was anything but. Where its smooth, tough flesh once was, was thick, wiry hair.

“We need to hurry up,” Noah said. He could already hear the jungle from the beach. The beasts had stirred. Time was of the essence.

He got Thraz to split the Mammoth Shark’s skull open, revealing a piercing blue marble. It was a core. Judging by the colour of it, it held the water attribute. It wasn’t the same attribute as his own core, but since it came from a high Levelled monster, it would be handy to keep.

Who knows, maybe his next tamed companion would be a water monster?

Noah smiled at the thought. It would be handy being able to traverse the waters with ease, although it was certain to be dangerous. He would need to be strong enough to fight against the sea monsters.

As Thraz was finishing with the monster, Noah glanced at his Stats for a minute. His Willpower Stat was nearing one-thousand. What would happen once it reached that threshold? Noah didn’t know. Most likely he would be granted a World First Title. Maybe even a sponsor. He was looking forward to it.

The beasts were getting closer.

Around three to five miles out in the jungle, the trees thrashed from side to side. Some fell, while some managed to stay upright. Whatever was approaching had claimed the scent of the Mammoth Shark.

Noah still had time. Pillow was nervous. Even with the fight with the shark, he was hesitant in getting involved. Did the little black panda not trust them, or was he scared of getting injured? Noah wasn’t certain, but he decided not to trust the panda back. Having someone that was hesitant in a fight wasn’t a situation Noah wanted to catch himself in.

After Thraz stripped chunks of flesh off of the slain beast, they retreated back into the jungle. They didn’t have enough time to get back to the town. Noah shuddered as the mysterious monster reached the beach. Even from a distance, Noah could hear the sickening chomping. It sounded like plates being ground against each other.

Pillow was wary of heading straight back. He said that if whatever was consuming the Mammoth Shark’s corpse headed their way, they would lead it straight back to their temporary home. Noah didn’t want that. So instead, Pillow found a small cave to hide in.

It was a chance to reset their tracks, hide their smell. After a couple hours and Pillow deemed it safe, they left for the town once more.

Once they arrived, a blast of air smacked Noah in the face. It was getting dark and that meant one thing. The apocalyptic storm would ravage the city. Noah and the others dashed into Pillow’s house. It was a tight fit, but Krall soon found himself lying against the wall, right underneath the massive metal window.

Noah sat down at the table. Massive slabs of Mammoth Shark meat lay flat on the surface. With the bait acquired, tomorrow would be a long day of fishing, and fighting.

“Do you have to have that meat sitting on my table?” Pillow spat. “It smells like shit.”

“It doesn’t smell right, does it?” Noah said He sniffed the meat and recoiled in disgust. “Well, the description says it’s healthy. Maybe it’s the natural scent of the shark?”

“Yeah, well it stinks. I don’t want it here.”

Just as Pillow's words died down, a blast of wind shook the building.

Noah smiled. “Sorry. We were cutting close on time. Don’t you have a peg or something to block your nose?”

“Whatever,” Pillow grumbled. “But tomorrow you can select another building out of the hundreds that are here.”

“Deal,” Noah said, leaning forward, looking at the little panda huddled in the corner of the building. His voice was loud now so that he could be heard over the battering winds. “We’ve been together a full day now. Mind telling me a bit about your name? I must admit, the name Pillow leaves a lot to the imagination.”

“A day.” Pillow laughed. “Do humans usually trust those they meet in the wilderness in a single day?”

“You’ve got me there,” Noah said. “I was just curious, that’s all. Really, Pillow? If your parents give you a name like that, you’re lining yourself up for others to ask questions about it.”

“It wasn’t my parents that named me,” Pillow said with a vacant expression. He shook his head, then turned to face the wall.

Shit, did I offend him? Noah tapped the desk. He didn’t really think beyond his curiosity of the strange name. Maybe the name has a personal significance? Good job, Noah.

Noah sniffed the air. It really smelled terrible…

Not even sunlight managed to break through the massive metal windows. In fact, it was pitch black. It wasn’t an issue for Noah, or anyone else in the building for that matter as their eyes were naturally attune to the dark.

The only reason Noah knew that it was morning was because the noise had stopped. Noah activated Shadowed Presence the moment the noise stopped. He quietly opened the door and went out hunting alongside Thraz, Krall, and Gluttony.

It wasn’t long before he returned with a healthy jungle rabbit carcass. It wasn’t a regular rabbit. It was at least quadruple the size, and the thick muscle it had meant that there was a lot of meat to go around. In truth, he wasn’t that hungry. But Pillow liked to eat. Maybe the little panda would open up to him, trust him, if he had a belly full of warm rabbit stew?

Food always made himself feel better. Talking animals were the same, right?

He grabbed the pot from another house and set up a fire in front of Pillow’s building. In another few minutes, he had the rabbit skinned and cut up into pieces ready for the stew. Thankfully he had water. After spending a few points in the Grace Shop for salt and other seasonings, the stew soon arrived to a boil.

“What’s that?” Thraz pointed at the glass container holding salt.

“Salt,” Noah said, he picked it up and threw it to Thraz. “You use it to season food. You’ve never heard of salt?”

“I’ve heard of it,” Thraz said. “But you use it on food? It’s too… salty.”

“So you tasted it on its own and decided that it was too salty to be used on anything?” Noah asked.

“Well, yeah,” Thraz said as if Noah was stupid. “If it doesn’t taste nice, why would we ruin our food with it?”

“Okay, that makes sense,” Noah said. “But if you only use a small amount, it really enhances the flavour.”

Noah mixed the cauldron of cooked rabbit stew. He spooned out enough for a few small drops of the stew, alongside a sliver of rabbit. Noah handed it to Thraz. He tasted it. His lizard eyes widened. He nodded, clicking his tongue.

“Amazing, so you just use a small amount and it alters the flavour?” Thraz asked.

“Not alters, but enhances,” Noah said.

“Humans and their inventions,” Thraz said as he plopped the wooden spoon back into the pot. He was quick to bring back another spoonful to his mouth. Noah snatched the spoon back. “When my mother cooks, she just shoves the meat onto the open fire. Roasts it for two minutes and it's done. But mostly we eat our food raw. The natural taste of the meat is the best, after all.”

“Better than my stew?” Noah asked.

Thraz didn’t answer.

“Thraz?” Noah said again, looking closely at the dragonborn.

“I–” Thraz looked around. He spotted the broadcasting eye in between a high tree. He leaned in closer and whispered, “Your cooking is better than my mothers. Please don’t mention it aloud. My mum would kill me.”

Noah laughed and patted him on the shoulder. “Your secret is safe with me pal.”

He was happy to share in his love for food. Unfortunately for him, the dangers of the wild hadn’t given him a chance to savour anything he shoved in his mouth. It was one, two, chew, and swallow.

The smell of cooked meat wafted through the air. Seemingly through the building as well as the door squeaked open. Pillow rubbed his eyes and walked out. His little nose twitched.

“Wakey wakey,” Noah said with a smile. “I made enough for all three of us.”

“What is it? Wait, don’t tell me,” Pillow said, sniffing the air. “Rabbit stew?”

“It took a while to hunt the damned thing,” Noah said. “But yeah, bang on the money.”

“Money,” Pillow mumbled. He sat next to Noah and dipped the spoon into the pot. He took a sip, then took a chunk of tender meat, and chewed it. “That’s good,” He said with slightly curled lips.

“Thought I’d say thanks for letting us stay, and bringing us here,” Noah said. Then he remembered something. He took out a few Uncommon shards, and even two Rare ones. He placed them in the dirt next to Pillow. “And the payment along with it.”

Pillow snatched the shards from the ground. He gazed at them in his hands. His eyes glossed over. It was as if he was reminiscing about the past. Noah had seen it a few times before.

Without moving his attention from the shards, Pillow said, “Pillow is the name given to me by a friend.”
