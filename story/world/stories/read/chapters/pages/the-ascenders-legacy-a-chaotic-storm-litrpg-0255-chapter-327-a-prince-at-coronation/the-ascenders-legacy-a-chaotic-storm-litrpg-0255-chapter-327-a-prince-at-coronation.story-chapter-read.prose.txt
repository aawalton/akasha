Sophran Thunderstride was the lifeblood of the special sect—its emotional anchor, its unspoken center. The unofficial leader everyone listened to and respected, despite insisting he would make a terrible one.

He earned that regard not through power, but through care.

More than the others, Sophran cared, and that care allowed him to understand most people even more than they understood themselves. He understood Darien's buried trauma, Seamus's brusque exterior, and Mirelle's relentless need to be perfect and poised.

And he understood Aodhán's distrust.

Not because Aodhán had ever spoken of it, but because Sophran could see it in the way he moved, in the questions he asked, and in the words he didn't say.

There was no doubt the boy was struggling. And over the past few days, he'd even seemed to be doing better. He'd smiled more and laughed more. The shadows in his eyes had dimmed.

He'd been doing better.

But now, as Sophran watched him pull a massive amount of electricity to strike Darien down, it was obvious that all that fragile trust had been undone. And in its place was a look of betrayal so deep it cut to witness.

A betrayal that shouldn't have been there because the boy just gained two SEALS!

But that was a matter for another time. Now, though, he had to save Darien—another thought he couldn't believe he was thinking.

But he knew he couldn't just rush in, as that would only complicate matters. So instead, he fell to his knees and shouted, knowing full well the action would draw Aodhán's attention.

"We surrender."

His voice boomed across the training room, and just as he'd hoped, Aodhán stilled, just for a moment, but it was enough.

"We surrender," Sophran repeated, his voice quieter. "But we are not against you, Aodhán. We are not hunting you. This isn't Lutia."

The mention of Lutia was like a knife through the tension, and Aodhán staggered, a monstrous beam of electricity only inches from Darien's chest. He looked up at Sophran, confusion plain on his features.

"You're attacking me."

It wasn't a question. It was a statement of fact. Wrong facts!

"No." Sophran shook his head, doing his best to hold Aodhán's gaze. "We are not attacking you, Aodhán. You just gained two seals, and we thought you'd be taken by the twisted effect. We were trying to save you." He glanced at Darien, then back at Aodhán's lucid gaze. "We see now that you do not need saving."

Varéc growled, and though Sophran couldn't understand what it was saying, it seemed to be corroborating certain parts of his story.

Sophran glared at it. "This is all Varéc's fault. He wouldn't let us get close to you, and after two seals, we feared the worst. We had to fight."

Varéc growled again, and the tension in Aodhán's shoulders slowly eased. "You weren't attacking me?"

"No, we weren't," Seamus spoke before he could, a wary frown on his face. "Though if you do not let Darien go this instant, that might not be the case much longer."

Aodhán glanced down at Darien, who stared wide-eyed at him, and immediately loosened his grip, the reality of what he'd almost done dawning on him.

He didn't need words to confirm Sophran's sincerity. He could sense it wafting from his core like smoke.

Sophran was telling the truth. And he had almost obliterated Darien for nothing.

"I…" He stumbled back, the surge of electricity around him dispersing. "I'm sorry, I didn't realize—"

"You misunderstood," Sophran said softly, a small smile breaking through the tension. "Maybe we need to have a talk. All of us?"

Aodhán glanced down at Darien once more and slowly nodded. "Maybe we do."

***

They all gathered in his living room a moment later, including Varéc, and after they'd all sat down, Sophran spoke. "Tell us about your time in Lutia. Tell us why you find it hard to trust us."

Aodhán glanced at the four boys, lips trembling for a moment before nodding. "It's not just one thing. It's a lot."

Sophran smiled gently. "We have all day and night, Aodhán. You almost hurt us today because you didn't trust us. The least you can do is tell us why."

Aodhán stared down at his hands for a moment and then began to speak. "When I arrived in Lutia, I joined an academy. …"

Half an hour passed as he summarized his relationship with Principal Zatya and a few of the things she had put him through, leading up to the moment of his kidnap by someone he had trusted. He explained how he had been relentlessly hunted by his fellow captives in Abyssos and how his own continent had rushed to save him for their own nefarious reasons.

It wasn't a sob story, but it was one that put into perspective why he was wary of people—warier of the sect's friendliness and accommodation. He understood that here in the sect, they were bonded by affinity, a bond that was practically absent for him back in Lutia, as he hadn't known anyone else with the storm affinity.

But if blood brothers could betray each other, how much more those bonded by affinity alone?

"I'm sorry," he said in conclusion, looking directly at Darien. "Where I come from, it's every man for himself. Friendships are forged, not pre-established like they are in the sect. Everyone I've met in the sect so far is so nice and polite it's jarring."

"So, you want us to be impolite?" Seamus asked with a frown.

"No," Aodhán shook his head. "That's not what I'm saying. It's just that I've lived the past year on a continent where everyone is looking after their own interest and advancement, regardless of what consequences might come to you. It's instinct to distrust everyone at this point. But it's something I'm working on."

"We know," Sophran smiled at him. "And I can imagine the sight you woke up to didn't help matters."

Aodhán chuckled. "No, it didn't. I opened my eyes to find Crixis on the ground, shuddering from what I realize now was my aura. You were fighting against Varéc. And Darien was binding me in chains. There were very few conclusions I could have drawn, to be honest."

They all chuckled at that, and Darien finally spoke. "I guess it does seem kidnappy now that you mention it, but we truly meant you no harm."

"I know that now," Aodhán grimaced. "I truly am sorry, Darien. You're all very strong. I didn't think I would have a chance without reacting with full force."

"Oh, you had a chance all right." Darien snorted, his expression shifting to amazement. "That ball of lightning you conjured out of nowhere, and that aura of absolute ruin?" He shook his head. "Is that your new path?"

Aodhán smiled. "It is. Apparently, I had three different paths warring in my spirit."

Everyone paused, the casualness with which Aodhán had just hinted at a three-path merge jarring. It was Crixis who finally asked, "These paths… did you merge them?"

"Yeah," Aodhán nodded, not quite realizing the gravity of what he'd done. "I merged them all with a fourth path to get my current path, and it's amazing."

A fourth?

Seamus blinked as his shock intensified, and in a squeak, he muttered, "Four paths? You merged four paths?"

The tone of his voice forced Aodhán to notice their carefully hidden shock, and he realized he had done the extraordinary once again.

"Oh, you know," he tried to backpedal, but it was a futile endeavor. Darien, Crixis, and Sophran were like dogs with a bone, and though Seamus didn't ask many questions, his interest was undeniable.

They asked question after question, trying to understand how Aodhán had done it. The merging of two paths was impressive. Three was outstandingly rare. But four was never before heard of, and Aodhán had just casually done it in a few hours.

But then, with the impact his presence and knowledge were having on the sect and thus the Origin Plane, was it so surprising that he was being treated with an unfair amount of favoritism?

The Origin Plane was a living consciousness, after all, and with how greatly Aodhán had affected its expansion the past few days, there was no doubt the consciousness had taken note of him—if it hadn't already done so before.

Their conversation continued for a few more minutes, the tension of their battle easing away until they were all comfortable again. Aodhán apologized again, and they all waved it off, laughing and sharing their own experiences of distrust and betrayals.

However, as they spoke, one matter nagged at Darien, and the moment Seamus finished his story of how a sailor had once betrayed him and his crew, simply jumping ship and leaving them without a sailor, Darien spoke. "I have a question for you, Aodhán. You may not like it, but I have to ask. I can't not ask."

Aodhán tensed slightly. "What is your question?"

Darien nodded, his gaze intent on Aodhán as he asked, "How are you sane right now? You just gained two seals. You should be overcome by the twisted effect, yet here you are, laughing and lucid. How is this possible?"

Aodhán hesitated, glanced at the others, then exhaled heavily. "That's… that's not a question I can answer. I'm sorry."

"Why not?" Darien pressed. "This isn't normal. You know that, right?"

"I know. I know this isn't normal, and it's not like I don't want to answer—it's that I can't. I don't know how to."

"You're not shocked by it, though?" Sophran asked, his expression curious. "You fully expected to be lucid."

Aodhán opened his mouth to speak, closed it, then nodded resignedly. "Yes. I fully expected to be lucid. I've been immune to the twisted effect for a while now."

His words went off like a clap of thunder in the silent room, and this time, Aodhán knew why. He had just admitted to being immune to one of the greatest inconveniences of advancement in all of AEFLYM. An inconvenience that even early Mythics suffered.

At first, he had thought the immunity had come as a result of his understanding of the Origin Plane, of chaos, and of how it wasn't a taint but a facilitator. But now, after that conversation about his pendant being an interface, Aodhán wasn't sure any longer.

He had tried to understand it back in Abyssos, but everyone had been too focused on fighting and surviving to give him any useful feedback. Perhaps they had taken his words to heart and were now experiencing the same thing. Aodhán couldn't tell, but he wasn't inclined to test it out again.

At least, not until he spoke to a few elders about it.

The thought of permanently curing the twisted effect made him smile, though. But Aodhán feared that more likely than not, this was a phenomenon peculiar to him and him alone. How proud would he be to think he could come to an understanding even Mythics lacked?

Not too proud, apparently, considering the interface and all, but he really doubted this was something else he could share.

Still, Darien and the others were very curious, and he couldn't just brush the matter aside like it wasn't important.

"Is it a skill?" Sophran asked carefully. "I know you've taught us a great deal, but if this is also a skill, I bet the sect will be more than willing to give a dozen blessed treasures for it."

The thought sent a tingle of greed through him, and he smiled. A dozen blessed treasures. There was so much he could do with that. Unfortunately…

"It's not a skill," Aodhán responded sadly. "I'm not sure what I did to gain this immunity, but as soon as I do, I promise I'll share it."

His words diffused their excitement, and hesitantly, they relented, shifting the topic back to other things. After a few minutes of mundane conversation, Seamus, Darien, and Sophran left for the library, eager to gain more knowledge for when they got their own miracle rain.

Crixis followed them, citing his own need for knowledge too, and Aodhán was finally left alone to his own devices.

He pulled up his status screen immediately, eager to see the changes his new path had wrought within him. And it was a lot.

Congratulations! You have chosen a third-tier path. [New Title Gained]

[STATUS]

Name: Aodhán Ashoka-Brystion.

New Title: Prime Converger.

Path: Path of Ruinous Authority: Increase storm control and manipulation by 15%.

Tier: 38—3%

Aspect: Water.

Aspect Mastery: 15.07%

{New Skills}: [Edict of Shattering] [Ruin’s Obedience] [Ruinous Baptism]

Bloodline: Origin Storm Authority. {Unique+}

Prime Converger: This is a title given to any cultivator who achieves a third-tier path merge.

[Edict of Shattering]: All storm phenomena within your area of influence reinterpret their existences to facilitate the destruction of every living creature within the same area of influence. Willpower intensive.

[Ruin’s Obedience]: All storm, lightning, and electrical phenomena in your area of influence are forcibly classified as subordinate constructs.

[Ruinous Baptism]: Cure spiritual strain, trauma imprints, and spirit curses with a baptism of ruin-infused rain.

Not only had he gained a new title, but he had also gained three new skills, one for each seal and the last for his aspect. But more than that were the additions the system had made to his status screen, the first of which was his path.

The sight of it made Aodhán smile, but it was its impact on his control that made him truly excited. A fifteen percent increase in his control was massive. Coupled with his bloodline and other control-buffing skills, he was working with at least a thirty percent increase in control.

His aspect mastery had also increased, nearly doubling and thus increasing his affinity for water.

The last change, however, wasn't something Aodhán could have imagined, and he frowned as he stared at it.

Bloodline: Origin Storm Superiority>>>>>Authority.

It was only a single word change, but it felt weighty—fundamental in a way he couldn’t explain. And then he glanced at his aura.

Aura of Ruinous Authority.

Frowning, he glanced at the mirror in the corner, wondering if any changes had been made to his appearance, but he found none. His bloodline had changed, but its manifestation was still the same.

Or not…

He glanced down at himself and, with a slight flex of willpower, unleashed his aura. However, rather than the invisible ripple of pressure that usually emanated from him, only an invisible ripple emanated this time.

There was no pressure, no color, and no tension of reality holding its breath. The walls didn't bend, runes didn't flare, and lightning didn't even spark. It was as if he hadn't unleashed his aura at all.

Frowning, he leashed the aura and unleashed it again, waiting to catch something different, but there was no difference.

Just as he began to fear that the change had ruined his aura, Crixis walked in. "I forgot—"

The rest of his words died the moment his gaze fell on Aodhán. He didn't stagger. He didn't flinch.

He simply stopped.

The sentence he'd been forming dissolved before it ever reached his tongue, his lips parting soundlessly as a strange stillness seeped through his body.

"Aodhán?" he whispered, feeling displaced in his own body. "What's going on?"

Aodhán frowned at him. "What is it?"

Crixis's eyes widened in growing terror. "I… I can't feel the storm. I can sense it, but I can't access it at all. What's going on?"

Aodhán stared at him in confusion, then shock, then excitement.

"You can't feel anything at all?" he asked excitedly. "Explain it to me in detail."

His excitement cut against Crixis's fear, and he stuttered. "I can feel my skills and my connection to the origin plane, but I can't access it. It's like I have no authority to do so."

"No authority," Aodhán breathed, and then a smile bloomed across his face, so wide it stretched from ear to ear. "Oh, this is amazing."

He leashed his aura one more time, and his smile widened even further when Crixis suddenly gasped in relief, conjuring a bolt of lightning to test that his abilities were still alright.

But Aodhán was too excited to notice. His aura wasn't broken at all.

It had evolved into something dangerous.

And its applications were near infinite.
