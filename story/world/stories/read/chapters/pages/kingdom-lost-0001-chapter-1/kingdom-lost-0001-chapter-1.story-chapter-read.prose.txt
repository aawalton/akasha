Riley woke on her back in the grass, staring up at a sky the wrong shade of blue. Too bright and too clean. None of the smoggy haze she was used to seeing between buildings. Even the clouds looked off. Crisp in a way clouds weren't supposed to be, like someone had cranked the saturation and walked away.She had no idea how long she'd been lying there. For a few seconds she just breathed, waiting for the memory of how she'd gotten here to slot into place.Her head felt light, like the world might tilt if she moved too fast. She knew the feeling. Fainting wasn't a hobby exactly, but it wasn't a stranger either. She rolled onto an elbow, then pushed herself up so she was sitting. She paused there with her legs out, palms flat against the grass.Gym class had taught her this much; sit first and let the blood catch up. If you went down again, sitting was closer to the ground than standing.She blinked into the sunlight and looked around. A clearing, near-perfect oval, ringed by trees whose leaves stirred in a breeze she could barely feel. The sun was high and warm on her arms. Postcard stuff. The kind of place couples brought a blanket and overpriced cheese to.But Riley was not an outdoors person. If life had offered her a "choose your battlefield" screen, she'd have picked her bedroom, charging cable and a bag of chips. With phone in hand, a new event running, and enough energy drinks to wreck her sleep schedule."Where the hell am I?"Her voice sounded thin in the open air. She squinted at the sky again, then around the clearing, half expecting a street sign to have spawned in. Nothing. Just grass and trees and the faint sound of water somewhere off through the trees.Okay, last thing she remembered.She frowned, rewinding. Her bedroom, the glow of her phone. Maybe a notification from Warfront: Kingdoms. Alliance chat screaming about a mass rally. A warm friendly coffee. Her hoodie, she wore like a second skin. Annoyance at someone's terrible troop comp.Had she eaten? Slept? Her stomach growled, long and offended."Yeah, yeah," she muttered.Then she looked down. The hoodie was gone, no jeans. Even the community-college logo she didn't have any right to wear anymore. In their place: a loose, off-white tunic with ties at the neck and sleeves shoved to the elbow. A rough dark skirt to mid-calf. The fabric was coarse, somewhere between discount Halloween costume and handmade Ren Fair booth. A length of rope cinched the tunic at her waist. It was not her first fashion choice, or her seventh."Where are my jeans?" Her voice climbed. "What the hell is going on?"She plucked at the tunic, half expecting a tag to fall out that said PRANKED in Comic Sans. There was no tag. Just stitches and rough weave. Riley wasn't picky about clothes. Clean and comfortable usually cleared the bar. But she would absolutely have remembered putting on something that made her look like an underpaid extra in a Thursday night junior high production of Hamlet.She wasn't an actor, that implied socializing. Sharing a stage. Riley's preferred activities were aggressively solo and mostly indoors.A cold pressure started building behind her ribs. She pushed it down and started patting herself."Phone. Please tell me I still have my phone."She checked the rope belt. No pockets. Checked under the tunic. Patted her sides, her legs. Ran her hands through the grass where she'd been lying. No rectangle, no weight and no phone."Shit."The word bounced flatly off the tree line.Riley hated being away from her phone. The thing was an extension of her body. It came to bed with her, to the kitchen, into the bathroom balanced on the edge of the sink, which was, she'd admit privately, why her bathroom visits ran long. The longest she'd been separated from it in the last year was the time it slid behind the couch and she'd had to move furniture.Gone, really gone. The thought sat on her chest like something heavy."Okay…breathe. Maybe it's just... over there."She pushed up to standing. Her vision narrowed for a second, then cleared. Their faces looked like someone had sculpted a monster from memory and then let it sit out in the rain.They saw her.She felt it the way you felt an enemy army's movement arrow swing toward your base.
