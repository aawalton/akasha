Chapter V.LV (5.55) - Coloring Pages

Kizu hadn’t had dinner with Anata since the incident with Taroe. Honestly, he wasn’t quite certain how much his niece understood of what had occurred between the two of them. For her own peace of mind, Kizu had never revealed that the ex-Elite had physically harmed him. That would only serve to put a wedge between her and one of her most powerful protectors. Still, Anata had certainly seen the wounds, but never addressed them beyond poking his bruises.

As he walked up to the fancy sushi restaurant, both he and Anata wore two of the nicer outfits Basil had designed for them. Not the fancy kimono they’d worn in Hon, but definitely nicer than his usual academy uniform. Kizu took a moment to prepare himself, using the window display of fake sushi as a mirror. His father was easier to deal with than his mother, but that didn’t make the man less of an ass. Just a more manageable one.

“Let’s do this,” he said, trying to sound more confident than he felt. As he turned, he heard the ding and just barely caught sight of Anata’s foot as it disappeared inside the restaurant.

Cursing, he scrambled after her. This was her first real chance to represent herself in front of their family. While he had told her that their opinion didn’t matter to him, and that was true, it certainly would make both of their lives easier if his parents took a liking to Anna’s daughter.

He caught Anata by the shoulders before she escaped too far into the restaurant. After a quick scan of the tables, he then spotted his father and Finn. Before leaving, Kizu had triple checked the time, to make certain they would be at least fifteen minutes early. Unsurprisingly, both of his family members arrived even earlier.

As the two of them approached, his father nodded in his direction while Finn refused to even look up from his menu.

“Shoes off,” Kizu whispered to Anata. They’d been over the custom a few times, but hadn’t needed to put it into practice since spring break.

“So, this is my daughter’s child,” Kizu’s father said, eyeing Anata from head to toe, like she was a griffen he was debating purchasing. “Take a seat, both of you. We have a great deal to discuss. And Finn’s excellent performance today to celebrate.”

At the praise, Finn actually looked up and Kizu saw a very elusive smile tug the edges of his brother’s lips.

Kizu purposefully avoided mentioning the fact that this was technically the second time his father had met Anata. She’d stood next to him at Emperor Sasaki’s banquet in Hon back over summer break. He figured it was best to simply move on from that fact and try to keep things amicable for their meal. They’d only just begun, after all.

“I brought you a gift.” Kizu’s father bent down and pulled out a rectangular package from under the table. It was pristinely encased in bright yellow paper with blue flowers, a lilac bow further sealing everything in place. It was frankly ghastly and complete at odds with the serious man holding it. Even to Kizu’s eyes, it was garish.

Anata’s eyes lit up with excitement and she held her hands out to accept the gift. Immediately upon accepting it, she looked to Kizu, the question evident in her eye.

“Go ahead,” he assured her with a shrug. “You can open it now.”

“It was something of your mother’s,” Kaga Kubou told his granddaughter. “I found it a few weeks back when sorting through the underground bunker.”

Kizu raised a hand to stop the conversation. “Hold up, underground bunker?”

“Yes, of course. We have a sizable bunker underneath the villa.” His father frowned and met Kizu’s incredulous stare. “Hm. I suppose you wouldn’t have been privy to that information at your age, as Anna was under strict orders not to reveal it unless there was an emergency. Best not to spread our secrets around in case enemies happen to overhear.” He winked and leaned back. “Of course, due to your niece’s… ailment, that’s not a concern of mine with her. Now that you’re older, you should know our family has many enemies. You’re far safer on this island than most places, but remain vigilant. For example, half of all assassination attempts are through poisonings. Drinking from a flask nobody else has access to is a key survival technique. Another would be to secure your sleeping location with one easily traceable enchanted alarm with a secondary one in place with far more complex divination concealment enchantments.”

There was so much to parse from that statement. Kizu started with that bit about Anata first. “Ailment?” “Oh, yes. Finn was kind enough to fill me in. Whoever it was my daughter decided to procreate with left a rather odd mark on her child. She lacks the ability to communicate. Along with an eye, it seems.”

Anata looked up from the ribbon she’d been tugging on, desperately attempting to tear off of the tightly packaged gift. She paused in her endeavor as she reached up to touch her eye patch, but then seemed to decide better than expose her scarlet eye. The moment passed and she resumed her fervent yanking.

“She’s not mute. It’s a conscious decision,” Kizu said, surprising himself. This wasn’t necessary information for anyone else to know. But it galled him to let his father continue making wrongful assumptions about Anata.

“Is it now? Hm.” His father leaned forward and tapped a thumb on his clean shaven cheek.

Before he continued that thought, Anata finally freed her prize from its paper and ribbon prison. She ripped it free, glee glittering in her eye. The paper fell away to reveal a book. She then froze, staring at the cover.

Likewise, Kizu was rendered speechless by the sight.

“It belonged to her mother,” his father said proudly. “I used to sometimes walk in on our nanny reading it to her at bedtime. A foundational brick in every child’s education. I just so happened to come across it when I was rummaging through Anna’s old things, sealed down in the bunker far out of sight of your mother.”

It was an extremely basic educational book on the 60 most common Universal Script words. Plainly speaking, it was a book designed for toddlers.

“When was there a nanny in our household?” Finn asked, speaking for the first time. He was equally as uninterested in the book as he was in Anata as a human. “I recall no such person.”

“Of course you don’t. We decided after Kizu turned one that we could rely on Anna to take on that responsibility. There was a… reallocation of funds that your mother insisted on. Why pay for the young woman on staff when we had a teenager of our own?”

There was definitely more to that story. Kizu’s normally unflappable father glanced to the side and shifted in his seat. Before Kizu could dissect his father’s statement, a waiter arrived with their food and drinks.

“I took the liberty of ordering us all a meal delivered in four rolls. The first being eel,” his father said, changing the subject. “I do hope you’ve learned slightly better table manners over the last year, Kizu, otherwise I fear your mother might faint in our next outing together.”

Kizu dexterously lifted the eating sticks and picked up one piece of sushi. He flawlessly delivered the food into his mouth. He was so smug about the achievement, that he got sloppy and his second piece ended up in two pieces in his lap. Thankfully, he was spared embarrassment by Finn simultaneously distracting everyone at the table with a retelling of his fight from earlier in the day. Well, ‘everyone’ might not have been quite accurate. Anata ignored her uncles as she flipped through the pages of her new book, doggedly studying each Universal Script character, as if to uncover a secret message hidden within the lines. That was probably a better result than having her take it as the unintended slight to her intelligence that Kizu interpreted it as. Plus, it meant she also missed him subtly sliding the broken sushi into his spatial ring.

He winced as Anta flipped to the back half of the book, which was nothing more than half-completed coloring pages. She stared at the colored scribbles of the Universal Script character for ‘river’ for a long minute.

“Are all of Anna’s belongings stored under the villa?” Kizu asked, partly to distract himself from the second-hand embarrassment he felt staring at the coloring pages.

“Most,” his father replied. “Your mother is rather sentimental. As such, she doesn’t take kindly to seeing artifacts of a failure in plain view. Since she also very infrequently enters the bunker, I thought it an ideal location to order the objects transplanted to.”

“What else is down there?” Kizu asked, poking at his fish and rice. His confidence at using his eating sticks had dwindled and he was waiting for an opportunity when the rest of the table was distracted to attempt it again. He thought he’d mastered this stupid utensil. It had been months since his last flub with the sticks.

His father waved a hand. “The usual precautions. Stockpile of food and water to last a few years. Magical enchantments to keep the structure secure against the most devastating assaults. My parents’ old pet tortoise.”

“Pet… what?”

“Neither your mother nor I am fond of reptiles. The creature attempted to bite off my toes on multiple occasions throughout my childhood. I deemed it best to keep out of sight where it can act as a protector for our storage.”

Kizu’s urge to unpack whatever baggage that was unfortunately got interrupted by Anata.

“Show me,” she demanded.

Everyone, equally surprised, turned to face the small girl. Kizu assumed this was another Comet situation and was about to insist they were not going to take care of an ancient tortoise. Instead, he found her not addressing any of them, but the picture book sprawled out on the table in front of her.

Her exposed eye flashed a brilliant blue, then body went limp and her head smacked into the book.

By the time Kizu shot to his feet and set a hand on her back, Anata had already recovered. The small girl had a frown on her face as she continued to stare at the river symbol.

“Anata! What was that?” Kizu knelt next to her, grasping both of her shoulders and forcing her to look him in the eyes. “What did you do?”

“Father never showed me images,” Anata said simply. “I wanted to see her.”

“See her?” Kizu’s father leaned in over his plated sushi to eye the girl closer. “Who did you see? What spell was that?”

Kizu bit his lip and his mind raced to develop a lie to tell his father and brother. Unfortunately, Anata’s usual tight lips proved uncharacteristically loose today.

“Mother as a child.”

“Fascinating.” After studying her for a bit more, his father finally leaned back in his seat and nodded his head in understanding. “So, you’ve a knack for divination, just as Anna did. Good. We can use that. Powerful divination is an aptitude often found throughout my family’s lineage. That is one of the reasons I’ve expanded our family’s trade networks to the degree I have. I knew bringing you into the fold would be wise. Though, some people clearly need to improve their research capabilities, lest those counting on them will be caught by unlikely surprises.” He spared an irritated and disapproving glance for Finn, who wilted in his seat.

“Anata is not a resource for you to milk,” Kizu said bluntly. He’d been scrambling to find footing since her outburst. This was a truthful, simple, and easy statement to make.

“Her value as a member of our family has increased, Kizu. Surely you can understand how this benefits her?”

“You’re not going to take advantage of her. She’s my responsibility. You made that clear when I asked for your help and you abandoned her.”

His father sighed and smiled, as if dealing with a petulant child. “Kizu. Let’s not be difficult now.”

Kizu and his father continued in a back and forth for several minutes, the older man seeming to simultaneously get more exasperated and amused by Kizu’s arguments as they continued.

While that went on, Finn finally spoke to Anata for what Kizu thought must be the first time ever.

“Who is your father?” he asked. “I know it’s a taboo. But you look so weird. It must be someone from Edgeland. Except I don’t see any scales. It’s been bothering me.”

“Otochi is my father,” Anata answered simply.

Thankfully, that seemed to be where Anata’s chatty streak ended for the night. Despite several more prying questions, the Blood Harbinger wisely remained mute for the rest of the night and Kizu did his best to deflect all further questions.

When they finally departed the restaurant, Anata clutching her new present, Kizu found himself thinking back to that question Finn asked.

Who was Otochi? What did Kizu truly know about Anata’s father? If he was going to truly face the Blood Lord and challenge the monster for Mae’s mother, then he desperately needed to dedicate more time on research and approach with a plan of attack.
