Chapter 147: Amplified Miracles

Despite the incident with Délight and Bo, the group collectively put it behind them, and the next series of simplistic tests of ‘Allotment re-jiggering’ went rather smoothly. It became second nature to Jack to release his potential for the collective group. He watched others perform simple demonstrations of the power amplification, specifically Solvent into Conflux+Other, which was heinously and generically potent. Sadly, Permafrost had to sit this one out for the most part due to her strained powers, but she was already familiar with this, so it wasn’t strictly necessary.

And then there was Highfive. Under amplification, his psychokinetic hand got bigger and denser, and his charges were devastating. He charged a simulation of a reinforced concrete wall and just turned it into rubble, laughing like a kid in a candy store the whole time. Briefly, a giant mutant creature was simulated, which looked like some unholy combination of demon, ape, and reptile — with everyone else a safe distance, he fought and wrestled it with his psychokinetic sheath around him, finally tossing it through a wall as Solvent called for an end.

Rad. Highfive is a great wrecking ball.

Another brief demonstration involved Snapper as he targeted a hardened sim creature like a demon-rhino under the amplification, quickly turning it into a deteriorating sack of ruined flesh.

Scary shit. Glad he’s on my side.

Lattice and Crash had their powers demonstrated, and they were just as advertised. Lattice could encase someone, or everyone, in an extra layer of armor, or totally surround someone in transparent, diamond-like full cover. She could easily do the whole team with space to spare while under even a slight boost.

Crash moved water around something like a controller, but far less precisely. He seemed a bit bored, and his demonstration wasn’t too exciting, but it didn’t take much observation to know how dangerous he’d be in the ocean.

Lighthouse had her bulky backpack apparatus already, presumably to get used to it. She and the two boosters had to confer in discussion about her ‘energy limits’ without sky access, making how to improve her a bit trickier than others, for a hypothetical incident inside contained environments under the sea. She showed off her precision in this situation, as dozens of simulations of lesser demon-apes got zapped in quick succession. With more time, she could focus her power and perform a much nastier electric zap that burned a hole clean through a tougher enemy.

I’d guess most of the reason she’s here is the ‘no collateral damage’ potential. She can control these discharges absolutely. She’s like a damn laser. The nickname is ironic — a legacy restriction, now probably just confined to the ‘extra’ she can dish to a locked-down foe under the right circumstances.

Finally, it was Jack’s turn. The feel of Solvent’s reinforcement paled in comparison to the juiced-up juice of Conflux. It felt like a river of power washing through him, begging for direction, and very difficult not to unleash.

His first task was a scattered assortment of mook-style demon-apes. Jack lifted up a mass of metal and divided it into darts rapidly. When he fired them all en masse with Throw like grapeshot or a giant shotgun — at the highest strength of merely reasonable output — the pieces all broke the sound barrier, which made many of the gathered cry out from the ear damage in the contained chamber.

“Shit!” Jack cried out, mortified, looking over and forgetting the assignment when the friendly fire aspect became evident. “Sorry.”

“Well, the targets are sorrier,” Solvent said wryly as he nodded over at them.

When Jack looked, he saw only destruction. The rocky environment had made the projectile impacts even worse, sending shrapnel across the whole zone. All of the targets were mince-meat and quite dead.

“Hell yeah, baby!” Highfive called with a thrust up fist. “That’s Big Iron shit, right there! Shotgun loads right in their face!”

Jack winced at the looks that got him.

“Big Iron, is it?” Permafrost said with a thick tease in her voice, still wincing slightly as she pulled her hands from her ears. “Wanna tell us what exactly that refers to, Ferro?”

“I didn’t come up with it, damn it!” Jack said defensively, pointing emphatically at Highfive. “And it's based on an old song about a gun!”

“Aw, come on!” Highfive called, laughing. “Just own it, bro! It’s awesome!”

Solvent cleared his throat. “Let’s move to lifting, shall we?”

Suddenly, in the thankfully large chamber, there appeared the realistic simulation of a typical military Humvee, a dump truck, and a medium-sized jet aircraft.

Jack blinked at them, feeling the power coursing through him getting antsy. “Can I really lift a jet? It’s huge.”

“Size matters not,” Solvent said. “Use the force within you.” Some groans came from the group at this, but Solvent only smiled. “Start with the lighter stuff first.”

Jack started with the Humvee, and it was piss-easy to lift. He put his maximum effort into tossing it and watching it roll sideways after impact, like a bad crash.

Hell yeah.

The dump truck wasn’t much harder, but tossing it was. After it plopped unceremoniously a short distance from the launch point, Jack decided he wasn’t done with it, and ripped off the back part of it — detached it with his powers was a more apt description — and then tossed that, which sailed through the air quite nicely.

And then there was the jet. Immediately, his first touch only made the metal groan, and it didn’t budge.

He pumped his output up to 105%. Giving his fingers a little wiggle, he stretched his hands out for the extra small focal benefit and began again, collapsing a vast swarm of memorite bits into every metallic pore of the vessel. Slowly, he began pushing it upward.

With several metallic groans, the jet finally lifted off the ground, and a round of claps came from the others.

“Remarkable,” Solvent murmured. “You are barely strained even over the one-hundredth percentile, at your experience level? Not unheard of in general, but this suggests you have room to grow there, which is astounding.”

Jack didn’t even try tossing the jet. He set it down and reduced his output quickly down to 90%, feeling a sheen of sweat coating his skin. Despite this, he hadn’t yet hit a fatigue state. After nodding his appreciation to Conflux, he said, “Overcharge is handy, that’s for sure. So, are you going to tell me what that weighed? The equivalent Create that equates to, Agent Solvent?”

“I’d be honored to, though your Mini could do the same by now. It’s something over ten-kay kilos, which is largely the same as the Allotment value. You’re somewhere slightly above a main stat of twenty-four in equivalency.”

Jack’s eyebrows went up. “Damn.” Jack let the memorite flow over to him and transmute into its semi-liquid form. The difference in this case wasn’t quite as dramatic. It was only about double the maximum mass he could produce, from something over 18 to 36 kilos. But in a pinch, without metal around, that was quite significant.

It was interesting to feel the difference in how the extra power reflected — the memorite progressively seemed to stretch thinner even as it accomplished more. What each ‘bit’ did amplified more than the material increasing.

Solvent eyed the material flowing around him and said, “Don’t worry, Ferro, we’ll have lots of extra metal around for you to play with, trust me. Can’t have you stealing submarine parts, after all.” The last was said non-seriously, of course.

Jack grinned. “So, what, solid steel torpedoes just for me?”

“Amongst other things? Yes. But your optimal weapon size is yet to be determined.” Solvent turned and gestured at the open chamber before them, still in its relative default setup of a destroyed urban environment. The virtual vehicles vanished suddenly. “You sense the real metal around, correct? Collect it and experiment with what you need the most for performance, so you have a go-to setup. Just to warn you ahead of time: be careful. We have the potential to exceed the safety limits here.”

Jack nodded as he turned around, and immediately began feeling out the metal in the environment, breaking down his memorite and then collapsing it in all directions, ready to collect. “Can I ask you something about your powers? Conflux, too, actually.”

“Of course.”

“Go ahead,” Conflux replied.

As Jack pulled out steel support structures, collapsing buildings in the process, he asked, “Are you actively helping me while I do this? It feels very close to natural, like normal power use.”

“It should,” Solvent said. “I can’t say things weren’t shakier in my youth — sometimes embarrassingly so — but I keep things smooth these days. A well-oiled machine, you might say. Actually, the analogy that works best is that I am merely holding up the material for the artist, the sculptor, to do the real work. It does take effort and focus in a sense, but I’m not truly conducting your powers with you on an even level, not even close. I’m conducting mine, in my own way, which is to support yours, which is quite different. My artistry is in the mixing; also, in managing the aggregate flow from the others, but that’s second nature by now unless a problem occurs.”

“Like someone resisting?”

“Or getting interrupted. It can break the whole thing, which will strand everyone very quickly with just their default individual powers. I have to make quick adjustments to mitigate the danger to the mixture.”

“I see.”

Conflux waited a moment to make sure their exchange was finished and said, “For me, it isn’t that subtle or complex at all. Good thing, too, because then I’d be hopeless! I just firehose my power at you, and boom, you’re off to the races. All I gotta do is keep the flow going, and I’m just along for the ride, more or less. I have to constantly resist saying ‘wee!’ Yeah, I wanted to especially when you were tossing stuff.”

Jack chuckled. “Well, you can say it whenever you want, in my case.” Out of the aggregate metal, Jack transmuted and ‘melted’ it down, collecting it into one of his telltale spheres. It was quite big after all the collecting. He sloughed off a bit of the material. “Do you both prefer that? What you’re doing.”

Conflux was initially puzzled. “Huh?”

“I think Ferro is asking if we prefer the role we have,” Solvent said. “Very much so, in my case. I enjoy learning intimately about new powers and how they tick, and watching them bloom in new ways. My enjoyment has only increased with my years, able to guide and teach the younger generations. In short, I love it, and it's quite rewarding.”

“Oh! Yeah, me too. Totally. I wouldn’t want to do anything other than zoom, zoom, zoom with people. It’s like I have my own private water rapid ride. In other words? It rocks.”

All three of them had a laugh at the pun. Meanwhile, he just barely heard Bo mutter ‘dorks’ in reproach.

Jack made a dense metal ball, something around two metric tons, slightly bigger thanks to an inner hollow in the middle. The diameter was longer than a man, certainly.

He moved it around with ease, but it didn’t exactly whip around, and it was exceptionally troublesome to change course once it got going. He demolished some buildings with it before deciding to reduce the material more, getting a feel for the weight, and then reducing it more again. Rinse, repeat, rinse repeat.

As he continued working, he mused, “You guys made that decision at a pretty young age. It must've been difficult.”

Conflux answered first to this. “Nope! I knew exactly what I wanted. My advisors said Catalysts were highly valued, and mine was one of the best ever seen! Mostly in terms of its broadness. I was so excited back then.”

Jack resisted pointing out that Conflux was still excited. “Advisors, eh? That sounds handy.” He tried not to let himself be too bitter. After all, he was older than anyone else had ever been by at least a decade when they got powers.

“The worst part was that we couldn’t choose for a week. Actually, no — the absolute worst part was that we had to write three different papers as if we were selecting each class. I wanted to cry trying to come up with why I’d ever want to be a lame ass Blaster.”

“I heard that!” Snapper called. He shook his fist in mock anger, then formed it into a threatening ‘almost snap.’ “You’ll pay for this, hero!”

“It was lame, Snap! Your class is totally not!”

“For my part,” Solvent said quietly, “I had some trouble deciding between this and Artisan. It haunted me at times — all the potential it represented — but it faded with age. I know I’d never have met the people I did, or have had the incredible experiences I’ve had, being cooped up in the tower my whole life.”

“That’s a lot on a kid,” Jack commented.

“It’s certainly no perfect system, just the one we have to live with. I’m sure things would be different in Memoria’s ideal state of affairs.”

“Hopefully we’ll get there.” Jack began to feel more natural and in control with a wrecking ball in the sub-2k kilograms range, while operating at 94% output, which was getting him very close to the first fatigue status after everything else. “Did your advisors pressure you at all, Solvent?”

“I know where you’re going with this, Ferro. Your case was unusual, and you probably only understand a fraction of that class combination’s relevance. It’s regrettable, but your circumstances seemed to at once beg for careful rationality and quick decision in an unstable time. Those aren’t innately compatible when you’re ignorant. Ideally, you would’ve simply been saddled with the week-long restriction like the rest of us, but then, we wouldn’t be here doing this, not right now. It does feel like a failure of our system in some ways. I’m sorry if you feel you were strong-armed here.”

“Ah. Not exactly.” Jack paused to collect his thoughts and found he ultimately had nothing to say about it. “In any case, I chose what I wanted and what we needed, both.”

“She was testing you.”

“Oh, I got that much, trust me.”

“Let me put it another way: she was testing an unknown variable. That sounds more impersonal, but that situation would’ve been almost terrifying to her. Perhaps as close as it gets for an Archon. Those were existential matters. And the tease of miracles behind all the danger? It must’ve seemed unreal. A trap. I’m not surprised she poked so hard at everything, including you.”

It checked out when Jack thought of that first time he passed back into Memorial airspace. He was sure there was a consideration from Memoria to destroy him at that moment. The promises of miracles had won out in her calculations, though.

She’d claim more personal motives, too, but I know she’d have done it if she thought she had to. How many would do the same in her shoes? Would I?

It was definitely uncomfortable to answer. Jack didn’t bother.

Instead, he formed the perfect metal wrecking ball. He zipped it around with relative ease, and the strength he could leverage was enough to stop it almost on a dime. 1400kg seemed like the sweet spot between speed and power at this level. It was surprisingly small with density-enhanced steel. Because he liked symmetry, he ensured it was one meter in diameter with a hollow inside.

He spent a little time branching out effects such as tendrils and projectiles from it, but soon tired into the first fatigue status, which seemed like Solvent’s cue, as he immediately called for a halt to move on, instructing Jack to sit down and rest. He did so, moving back to the small cluster and sitting down on a cut of canvas someone had found and cleaned off, which a few others were already sitting on.

Délight sat down next to him, looking over hesitantly. Neex pointedly sat down next to him on the other side, which Délight certainly noticed but didn’t react to. She finally leaned in and whispered, “Are you mad at me, too?”

Jack did a combination shrug and shake of his head. “You just did as was logical with your powers amplified.”

“Can’t expect a scorpion not to sting?”

Jack, not in the mood, gave her a reproachful glower.

Grinning, Délight held up a hand in surrender. “Sorry. I see you didn’t mean it that way. Force of habit on my part.” Looking away, she added, “I noticed you were barely affected compared to most. Quite a resistance you’ve got from this… whatever it is.”

“Sure, but you weren’t boosted by Conflux at the time.”

Délight’s eyebrows rose. “Oh. Good point. You think that would be enough?”

“No comment.”

She laughed. “You know, Solvent told me we’d practice under more isolated scenarios to work on my control and comfort with it more gradually. Care to be the guinea pig? I could recommend you as a hard target.”

“I don’t think Jack is very interested in that,” Neex said with cool composure, though without pulling her eyes from the exercises ongoing. Despite this, he felt a bit of testy annoyance from her in the bond, and her head tentacles were flicking around.

Oddly, this improved Jack’s mood. Laying it on a little thick, here, Neex, but that’s fine.

Délight smiled, eyeing both of them. “Oh? Is that so, Agent Ferro?”

Jack let out a breath, almost turning into a laugh, and shrugged. “I’m sure they’ll accommodate you with a more appropriate target, Délight. As for me, I’ve never been one to take on extra studies I don’t need.”

Délight, deeply amused, gave an indolent sort of shrug. “Suit yourself, of course.”

Meanwhile, Neex was very pleased, leaning slightly into him in a faint sign of approval. Jack responded by leaning back on his splayed hands as he stretched his legs, subtly curving an arm to be ‘happenstantially’ around Neex and creating more body contact. That got more approval through the bond.

She appreciated the subtle things, he noticed. Expanding the ‘PDA’ any further wasn’t necessary in that particular situation. It simply got the message across.

If he didn’t know any better, Jack might’ve thought Délight had nudged this particular result out of things deliberately.

Aw, who am I kidding? I really don’t know any better, and you can’t put any social engineering past this one. She’s always playing some game.
