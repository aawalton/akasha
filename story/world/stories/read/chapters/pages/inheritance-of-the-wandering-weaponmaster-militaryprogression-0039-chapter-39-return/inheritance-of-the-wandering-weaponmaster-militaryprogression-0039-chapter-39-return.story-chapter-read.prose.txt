“You are still on campaign!” Captain Riyal shouted as the view of Villy became clearer and clearer as the morning’s mist burned off. “You will be granted leave upon arrival at headquarters and given your payment. However, if you leave formation before arrival, it will be considered a dereliction of duty. For your own sake, wait a few hours to be dismissed.”

At his command that carried over the remaining fifty-nine soldiers, the remainder of the one hundred that the city had sent to fight the goblinoids, the movement and excitement that had threatened to send them running towards home died down, though they all remained talking to each other excitedly. For his part, Ezra wasn’t excluded from the other elites’ considerations of what they would be doing, and he had Boyd to thank for that. The large man’s willingness to talk had saved Ezra from an even more lonely journey home, and when Ezra had found an opportunity to thank him away from the others, he did so.

“Look,” Boyd shrugged, “you’re a good guy. I think you’re an awkward little training-obsessed weirdo, but I also think you’re right to focus on training given your natural talent.”

“Awkward?” Ezra replied, laughing a little.

“Yes,” Boyd nodded, unapologetic. “However, my brother’s too quiet for his own good and Leah is jealous, so it’s not surprising this is how things went when you went all broody and quiet while answering fewer and fewer questions. But the fact is that you’re different from us. I think you know that too, ’cause Cord and Leah can only barely move aura, much less bring it into themselves. And we won’t talk about my fuckin’ failures. Meanwhile, you’re leaving us all behind without seeming to break a sweat.

“I like you, but I’m not the kind of idiot who thinks that you’ll just stick around here because you like us. You’re going to be a whole lot more than any of us can dream to be, and it makes sense that you’re getting ready for that. I think you should’ve said something, maybe mentioned why it was that the scary woman who fist-fights dragonkin took an interest in you. I think that would’ve gone a long way, but honestly, it’s probably too late now. So, happy to be your friend, even though it probably won’t last forever.”

That answer left Ezra squinting at the gregarious man, who shrugged, clapped an arm around Ezra’s shoulders, and changed the subject. “So what do you do to actually feel the aura?”

Instead of giving slightly dismissive answers like he had been, Ezra tried to answer the question completely once again, though it bore no results.

And now, standing on the outskirts of his hometown, Ezra felt much of that frustration from his own inability to balance what was necessary with what would make him happy in the short term bleed away. Leah and Cord’s friendly but short conversations could be replaced by reuniting with some of his friends, given that he was to be given leave. Right?

Unfortunately, the path that took them back to headquarters didn’t go anywhere near The Dragon’s Toenail, and Ezra was left growing more and more impatient as they made their way forward. At the final step, they walked into the headquarters. Until then, the guards who had seemed so willing to look down on the soldiers stayed very clear as citizens, especially those within the inner city, congratulated the arrival of the soldiers. The celebration that they had in greeting the soldiers took Ezra by surprise. It was basically a parade, with shouted cheers and excited cries filled the air.

However, among all the excitement, there were plenty of faces that didn’t reflect that celebration. It was simple enough for Ezra to realize these faces were looking for sons and daughters, fathers and mothers, husbands and wives, and those people grew more panicked by the moment when they didn’t see who they were looking for.

Nearly half of the soldiers had been killed in this engagement, and Ezra felt his stomach drop. He and Adam had survived, being healthy enough to make the way home, but the levies, of course, had sustained quite a few casualties as well as the soldiers who’d taken the brunt of the damage and deaths.

Fortunately, Ezra didn’t have to talk to any of the families of the dead, and he just walked into the headquarters, where a small pavilion was set up. Under it, a table with three clerks and four guards had several different parchments laid out. Behind the table, where the clerks sat, was a large chest, and Ezra smiled as he started to walk forward.

“If your name is in the first third of the alphabet,” one of the guards at the table shouted, “you’re on this side. Second third, in the middle. Final third, you’re on my left, your right.”

He couldn’t help but thank the Madam in his mind as Ezra was reminded that his name was in the first third. He started to walk towards that side, where a line was swiftly forming up. Then he realized that Boyd, Cord, and Leah hadn’t moved.

“Brothers,” he said, walking forward, “you’re with me. Leah, you’re in the middle.”

They all nodded their thanks as they walked forward, Ezra becoming the third in line with the brothers behind him. Leah was about that same place in the second line, and the lines moved quickly. Before long, Ezra was there, standing before a clerk that he was fairly certain had been there on the day that he signed on with the army in the first place.

“Name?” she asked, not looking up.

“Ezra.”

“Eight weeks of payment, four large silver.” Again, she didn’t look up as she turned and grabbed four pieces of silver, each one about five centimeters across and with a nice heft. She took them in her hand, looked up at him, and offered them in her hand.

“Thank you,” he said, smiling widely.

She nodded, writing some numbers beside his name on the parchment in front of her and waiting for him to walk away.

He did so, pulling out his money pouch that he kept on his belt but flipped inside of his trousers’ waist. With the money that he’d saved and been given by the Madam, it was a nice heavy weight there on his waist, and Ezra rolled his shoulders as he walked forward. There still hadn’t been any dismissal, so, looking at the others still being paid, Ezra found one of the nearby sergeants. Laurel, a sergeant who had come back from the fight with a new scar along her right arm from her shoulder to her elbow, was nearby, moving her arm with a pained expression.

“Sergeant,” Ezra greeted, hoping she could give him the answer he was wanting.

“Ezra,” she nodded, continuing to stretch her arm. “What can I do for you? We’re about to be given leave, so I’m not sure what you’re wanting from me.”

“Just info on the baths and information about the uniforms,” he answered. “If I’m going on leave, I think it would probably be better if I don’t smell like… this. In hand with that, I wondered if I could get the uniform washed or replaced, since we didn’t have the water to do that on the march.”

“You can get it washed,” she answered with a shrug. “Won’t be dry anytime soon though, and you’re not going to be given a free second uniform. I wouldn’t buy one if I were you. As for the bath, there’s the water around here you can bathe yourself with, but you’ve just been paid. I’m going to hit a bathhouse before anything else. There’s one nearby, only three small silvers to enter, and they provide soap and warm water. Would you like me to show you where it is?”

Suddenly, the four large silvers in his money pouch felt much smaller at the idea of just throwing away three small silvers for a bath. That was thirty copper of the four hundred he’d been paid. The look on his face seemed to be read by Laurel, and she shrugged. “It might not be full winter anymore,” she said, “but the water is still icy. If you want to break open a frozen barrel to wash yourself to save a couple silver, that’s up to you. For me, it’s worth it to actually enjoy my bath.”

At that, the sergeant waved him off and walked away. Ezra called out his thanks and walked back towards the rest of the soldiers, who were milling around in the middle of the square and waiting for their dismissal. As Ezra got closer to everyone else, he could better smell the sour stink of sweat and hastily and improperly cleaned blood. He was again reminded of when he tried to use his looks to get Amy not to ask him questions and how his smell had kept him from being successful. If he smeledl as bad as everybody else here, he couldn’t blame her.

“Ezra,” a cold voice said as he wandered forward.

“William. Can I help you?” Ezra tried to keep his tone reasonable and relatively kind as he spoke to the self-obsessed soldier. He’d lost two fingers on his right hand in the fight against the goblinoids. Apparently, one had slipped past his fellow’s shield and straight bit his fingers off before being put down.

“It seemed like you were approaching me,” William answered, his face no longer sneering but still unfriendly.

“Just looking to get dismissed,” Ezra answered, continuing past William. Ezra noted that the inner-city kid seemed to want to say something else, but he didn’t care to stick around and wait for him to make up his mind. Instead, he stood back by Leah, Boyd, and Cord. They made quiet conversation, but it was obvious that all of them were focused on the prospect of being able to talk to the other people from their lives. They all had reasons to join in with the army, and it wasn’t just a passing fancy or desire to be a soldier. They were slum rats, and they’d come here for a reason.

Finally, after what felt like the fifth repetition of the same conversation, Captain Riyal stood in front of the soldiers.

“I know you’ve been promised leave, and I’m not going to talk long enough to impact your ability to get things done today. However, I do have a few things to say, so listen and I’ll get through them quickly.”

A few of the veterans chuckled at that, some joke that none of the recruits knew or understood contained in those words. The captain smiled, nodded, and continued.

“Until now we’ve called you veterans and recruits, and if you want to, you can continue to think of yourselves as that, but now you’re all blooded veterans in the eye of the County and the army. I’m sure all of you have noticed that most of our casualties were among recruits. That is always a danger, and I extend my personal apologies for my own failures in preparing you all sufficiently. A weregild will be paid to the surviving family of all those who have died, so if you are familiar with any person, levy or soldier, who was killed in the line of battle, please notify us of their next of kin.

“Now, you’ve all fought and bled and killed for the citizens of this county and this duchy, and I’m proud of you. You fought hard, and none of you broke in the face of danger. Regardless of how much experience you’ve had, consider yourselves equal in seniority, except for in terms of command, to any of your fellow soldiers, sergeants, lieutenants, or even myself. There is a brotherhood that comes from standing on the front lines, trusting the people beside you, and doing whatever is necessary to protect innocents.

“Next, your payment. There will be hazard pay; the Count simply hadn’t sent it here at the time of our arrival. We made good time, which is the reason for the delay, so don’t go getting your panties in a twist. Within the next two days, you all will receive an additional four weeks of pay. Don’t go spending it before it’s received. Buying things on credit looks bad on the Count and on the army, so don’t go making yourselves nuisances, or we’ll have you running and drilling until you puke, and then we’ll keep going for another day.”

Again, some laughs spread throughout the veterans, though a couple knowing looks and teasing elbows let Ezra know that there were not a few people here who had done just what the captain was instructing them not to do.

“That’s all from me for now,” the captain said. “Now, you’ve all been given two days of leave. You’re free to do with it whatever you will, just make sure that by tomorrow night you’re all sleeping here. Dismissed.”

At that, every one of the soldiers snapped out salutes and were free to go do whatever they wanted. Ezra smiled, having already decided what to do himself.
