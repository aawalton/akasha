The good news was that Asher and Rulfar managed to find the two men right where they left them.

The bad news was that Asher’s gemstone dagger didn’t work quite in the way he’d hoped it would.

Recalling Richard’s clear instructions, Asher shrugged before ramming the dagger straight into the first man’s chest, feeling the pulse of energy radiate out from the blade as it shocked the man’s soul. Instantly, he went from blank-faced and practically comatose to screaming at the top of his lungs.

“Is he supposed to do that?!” Rulfar shouted, wincing at the man’s continued wail.

“I feel like Richard would have mentioned if he was!” Asher said, pulling his dagger from the man’s chest and watching curiously as the flesh knitted itself together again. He’d already cancelled his Sever the Thread effect that he’d inflicted upon the man’s muscles earlier, which meant his freaky body should have healed up and been capable of moving again. Instead, he just laid there unmoving, screaming bloody murder at the ceiling.

“I know you said he still has his aura and his class… but maybe his mind is simply too far gone,” Rulfar proposed, his tone somber as they stared down at the poor man. “Based on how old he looks, he had to have been down here for a good ten years or so. That has to take a severe toll on the mind.”

“Maybe… but we can’t just kill him because he’s gone a little crazy,” Asher argued. “What if he can be saved?”

“Asher, this isn’t some random man you rescued from being lost in the woods for the last few years,” Rulfar frowned. “He’s been transformed into a literal weapon. You know just as well as I do how deadly he is. Natural healing and durability, near invulnerability to most common elements, incredible strength… if you drop him off at a nearby village and he goes wild, he’ll probably kill everyone there. And then continue on to the next village and keep killing until someone powerful is sent to put him down.”

“So we just kill him?” Asher asked, continuing to stare at the man even as the poor former-minion kept screaming. “It just feels wrong. What if we call up Donvath and see if he can do anything to help?”

“By all means, give it a shot,” Rulfar shrugged.

Doing just that, Asher stepped to the side and created a Spatial Lock to block out the screaming as he pulled out his sending stone and pressed the rune assigned to Donvath. It took a few seconds, but the High Prince answered faster than he thought he would.

“Asher! What a coincidence, I was just thinking about you. Want to know why?”

“Honestly, I don’t think I do,” Asher admitted, deciding to just cut to the chase. Depending on his mood, Donvath could be more than a little annoying to put up with. But before he could so much as say a word, Donvath interrupted him.

“Sorry, Asher, but I think they’re long gone. Trust me when I say I have some experience in this field. I’d recommend trying to make it quick. They’ve suffered enough.”

“What? I didn’t even say anything yet,” Asher said, frowning as he picked up on what had to have happened. For some reason, Donvath had to have let him get out his explanation and ask if there was anything that could be done, before using Timeline to jump back to before he’d actually done so. “…Was that really necessary?”

“I just thought it would be fun for you to experience at least once,” Donvath said, his voice teasing over the sending stone. “With that out of the way, I’ve got my own news. News I’d rather share in person instead of over a sending stone. Hello all of you beautiful people who are listening in on this!”

“You still think people are listening to your secured sending stone?” Asher asked.

“I know there are people listening in on my secured sending stone. What I don’t know is if they’re actually beautiful or not,” Donvath admitted. “Anyway, come say hi once you finish up over there. Ta ta!”

With that, Donvath ended the connection and Asher stared at the inert stone in his hand. Shaking his head, he put it away and dropped his Spatial Lock, wincing as the wailing hit him once more.

“He’s still screaming?” Asher asked as he walked over to Rulfar. “Don’t they need to breathe?”

“Not much it would seem. No doubt because of the enhancements,” Rulfar said, frowning as he nodded toward the giant, fist-sized runestones embedded all over the man’s chest. “What did Donvath say?”

“He suggested we give them a quick and painless death,” Asher sighed. “It just feels bad, you know? After all the effort we went through to save them, they just die anyway.”

“Asher… these people have been down here for years. Over a decade at least. As much as it pains me to admit, perhaps you are doing them a favor,” Rulfar said, holding up a hand to stop him before Asher could argue. “Think about it. Their friends, their families, the people who once knew them, have probably all moved on. I took a good look around that village, and Ulma was the only one there I witnessed still grieving, potentially because Grant had only been taken three years ago. What if we did manage to restore these people’s sanity to them. How do you think they would feel returning to find wives remarried or children all grown up?”

“I think they would enjoy another shot at life regardless,” Asher countered, before shaking his head. “But it’s a moot point anyway. If Donvath thinks it’s for the best, I’m willing to do what he suggests. Though I’m not giving up on Grant. His aura was the most lively of all three of them, I think we have a real shot at saving him.”

“Once we’re done here, we’ll track him down,” Rulfar promised, tapping the side of his nose. “I still have his scent from his arm. No matter how far he ran, I’ll find him.”

Asher did his best to end the screaming minion’s life as painlessly as possible, before driving his gemstone dagger into the second one’s chest. His reaction was identical, and Asher sighed as he laid him to rest as well.

With that, he and Rulfar headed farther into the tunnels on a merry hunt for who they could only hope was the last of The Lost Lady’s minions. It took them almost an hour, but eventually, they tracked him down.

“What do you think he was doing?” Asher asked, scratching his head as they came upon the tall man standing aimlessly in a tunnel.

“Look at the chalk mark,” Rulfar said, pointing to a familiar mark on the tunnel wall. “We’re near the surface. Maybe when we attacked her at the end her control waned over her minions slightly. Maybe some part of him actually is still alive in there and he was trying to leave the caves.”

“Why Rulfar, that almost sounds like optimism,” Asher teased, getting an eyeroll from the bloodhound. “Are you feeling okay?”

“Shut up and stab him with your dagger.”

Trying not to let himself get too excited, Asher did just that. The moment his gemstone dagger sank into Grant’s chest, the man screamed just like the others. But before the dread of yet another failed rescue sank in, Grant stopped screaming, his eyes going wide and panicked as a spark returned to them. The man stumbled backward and fell to the ground, scrambling until his back was against the stone wall of the cave.

“What’s going on?!” Grant shouted, breathing hard as he looked back and forth between the two of them. “Where am I?! Ulma?! Boys!”

“It’s alright, Grant, we’re here to help,” Asher said, holding up his hands placatingly and nudging Rulfar to do the same.

“Help?! You stabbed me in the chest!” Grant shouted, his breath catching and his face going white as he looked down at himself. “And I’m not… I’m not dead? I’m fine? Oh, realms above, what are these stones? Where is my arm?! What did you do to me?!”

“You were taken by The Lost Lady,” Asher said, preemptively throwing up a quick Spatial Lock in case Grant decided to try and bolt. Just because he’d regained his mind, that didn’t mean he’d lost any of the incredible speed or power that came with his weird new body. “We rescued you and restored your mind, but your body… Well, it’s a bit beyond us.”

“Ulma,” Grant repeated, seeming to get a handle on his panic somehow as he stared hard up at Asher. “Where is Ulma? Where are my boys?”

“Safe and in the village,” Rulfar promised, looking surprised that he could understand the man. Before, Rulfar had mentioned that he only had a weak understanding of the language that was spoken in the village. Seeing as nothing had changed with Rulfar, Asher could only assume whatever crazy augments The Lost Lady had had done to Grant, somehow they had effected his mind as well. “We spoke with them only a few days ago. Ulma misses you, Grant. She’s been waiting for you all this time. Once you’ve calmed down, we can help you get back to her.”

Despite clearly being freaked out by whatever had happened to him, the thought of his wife and children seemed to ground Grant somewhat. After Asher yanked the gemstone dagger out of his chest, Grant sat there, holding his head in his hand as he took deep breaths and tried to get a handle on his situation. From what Asher and Rulfar were able to figure out, to Grant, almost no time had passed at all. The last three years had felt like some horrific nightmare to him, and now that it was over, he wanted nothing more than to return to his family.

Asher even found an outfit that was pretty close to what he’d seen Grant wearing the day he’d been taken three years ago, pulling it out of his rift and letting Grant dress himself. He figured helping the man feel more like himself again and less like some wild animal living in a cave was a good first start.

Once Grant was dressed and able to run a hand over the runestones embedded in his chest without looking like he was going to faint, the two of them led him back up to the surface. Asher’s Permanence didn’t have any sort of proximity requirement he needed to worry about after being set, and Moxy was going to need a long time before she was in anything resembling decent shape, so they weren’t exactly in any rush. Eventually, they reached the mouth of the caves, where Grant stopped dead in his tracks. The sun had already set and the village was illuminated by pale moonlight, but that wasn’t anywhere near as pale as Grant’s own face.

“I can’t see her like this,” Grant whispered, his hand still clutching the front of chest as his fingers brushed one of the many runestones. “I’m a monster.”

“Grant, I’m going to level with you here,” Asher said, deciding not to mince words. “I’m pretty decent at stabbing stuff, but I’m no therapist. I wish I had a bunch of flowery things I could say to make you feel better, but I don’t. What I can tell you is that even we were able to see just how much Ulma misses you when we spoke to her. I don’t think she’s ever stopped dreaming about you coming home to her and your children, and I can all but guarantee you she’ll want to see your face again. Yeah, your body might freak her out a little bit, but so what? What’s the alternative? Spend the rest of your life living in that cave, wondering what’s going on up on the surface? That’s no life.”

“Family is something to be treasured,” Rulfar added, the softness and pain in his voice sounding foreign from what Asher was used to. “Go to them, Grant. Trust me. We'd be more than happy to go with you if you want.”

“…No,” Grant said after a few more seconds, taking a shaky breath and steeling himself. Turning to face them, he gave them a nervous smile. “Thank you both. For the offer to come with me, and for bringing me back. I'm more scared now than I am the night The Lost Lady stole me from my family, but this is something I need to do on my own. I don’t know what The Lost Lady forced me to do while I was under her control, but I’ll never forget this. I may just be some farmer, but if you ever need my help, for anything, you know where to find me.”

“Just live a good life,” Asher smiled, still feeling a bit bad about Grant losing his arm. Technically it had been The Lost Lady who had taken it from him, but she’d only done that because he’d shoved an Eternal Mark onto it. In theory Grant’s strange body might be able to regrow it, but only time would tell. “Tell Ulma we said the same!”

With that, Asher and Rulfar waved Grant off, watching as he moved quietly into the night. Rather than heading for the village, Grant made a beeline straight for the edge where his house was located.

“You think they’ll take him back?” Rulfar asked, sounding like he wasn’t asking about Grant as much as he was somebody else.

“I think so,” Asher said, clapping Rulfar on the back. “Now come on. I’ve thought of at least three more jokes I can make at Moxy’s expense, and I want to make sure I get the chance to use them before they get that hole patched up.”
