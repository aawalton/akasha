Chapter V.XI (5.11) - Echoes of Factions

“Why do you have a copy of my sister’s head?” Kizu asked coldly. His eyes remained locked on the lifeless clay eyes of his kin. She looked so much like both himself and Anata. Older than he remembered, but unmistakable his kin.

“Your sister?” Vincent leaned over and fiddled with his monocle. “Oh! Kaga Anna. I suppose it only makes sense that she’d be related to Kaga Kizu. She leads one of our factions. Out in the far west, beyond Edgeland.”

Kizu blinked. “Your…factions?”

Vincent glanced at him with a surprised look. “I assumed as the courier you would be privy to this information.”

“He is a child,” Yvonne’s disembodied voice said. “Of course he’s not going to know what’s going on.”

“Tell me what you know,” Kizu said, his demand sounding a bit too much like a beg for his liking.

“Absolutely not,” Yvonne said.

“Obviously Gizrim is preparing you to join us. But not yet,” Vincent agreed.

Kizu’s fist clenched and he narrowed his eyes. “Why not?”

“We lost enough members recently for caution to be paramount. If your headmaster doesn’t believe you’re ready yet, then I will not press the issue.”

“You lost members?”

Vincent sighed and looked over the array of clay heads. “Tell me, young brother of Kaga Anna, how many Tainted faces do you see here?”

Kizu turned his attention back to the table. He tried not to focus on Anna's head as he examined each of the faces.

“One.” Kizu pointed at a scaled woman with a severe look on her face.

“There should be over a dozen here. And yet she is our remaining member in Edgeland.”

“The Dragons killed them all?” Kizu’s stomach churned. A genocide he could have prevented should his priorities have been different.

“They acted as the final defense,” Vincent said. His earlier cheer dwindled and his posture slumped. “Not enough. They failed to prevent the rise of that Calamity.”

“Does that mean you were in charge of stopping the Kitsune?” Kizu asked.

“Oh? So you do know of the return of that Calamity?” Vincent’s sorrow was replaced by a quizzical curiosity. “An interesting assortment of info you’ve been granted. But, to answer your question, we in Tross and Hon have different priorities due to our unique Calamity. Some of us have actually argued for helping the Kitsune escape the World Dungeon. It’s a contentious debate.”

“You are revealing far too much,” Yvonne warned.

“Yes, yes. You’re correct. I will now close my mouth” Vincent stuck out a hand and another wine glass materialized. He downed it in a single swig. “Now, if you wish to turn to happier topics, such as griffin combat, I am happy to chat.”

“griffin combat?” That was a random direction for Vincent to turn the conversation down.

Vincent laughed. “Are you not aware? I am the organizer of the griffin races and competitions here in Port Kallis. I own over three hundred griffins.”

“421,” Yvonne clarified.

Vincent waved away the correction. “While my racing days are now behind me, if you have questions or thoughts on the sport, I am one of the leading experts and am the guild head here in Port Kallis.”

“I’ve…seen posters. And a brochure,” Kizu replied. That was the extent of his knowledge on the subject. He knew the races existed, but nothing else.

“Yvonne! Let’s get young Kaga some tickets for the race in an upcoming weekend! How many do you need? At the very least, I expect a sly young man like yourself to have a pretty girl in mind to take on a date. Any other friends?”

“Um.” Kizu quickly went through his options. He decided that Anata wasn’t an option. Vincent knew about the Calamities and it wouldn’t be much of a leap in logic to think he also knew of the Harbingers. And just because the headmaster was fine with Anata, didn’t mean the rest of his pals were. Vincent already brought up that there were disagreements among their members on how to proceed. But Basil, as the headmaster’s ward, was probably a safe pick. “Maybe..four tickets? Would that be fine?”

Strips of paper materialized in front of Kizu and he caught them as they started to drift down to his lap.

Then the old man launched into a very detailed description of the different griffins he bred over the years. While interesting, Kizu barely kept up with the conversation. Every time he successfully managed to ask a relevant question, he let out an internal sigh of relief. It went on for nearly an hour as Vincent filled his head with the different status markers and signifiers of certain breeds and the qualities of riders.

Finally, Vincent clapped him on the back. “I look forward to meeting your companions. And thank you again for the delivery. If you ever need anything, please don’t hesitate to call on me. Any friend of Gizrim is a friend of mine!”

Kizu understood this to be a polite dismissal. “Before I go, can you answer one more question? Not about griffins.”

“Not about griffins? You wound me.” Vincent’s eyes twinkled. “I won’t say anything else about my work with your headmaster, if that’s what you’re after.”

“No. I mean, it’s tangentially related. But not exactly about Calamities or anything.”

“Tangentially related?”

“Did you ever meet my sister? What do you know about Anna?”

Vincent leaned back in his seat. The old man considered for a minute before answering.

“I did meet Kaga Anna a few times after her expulsion from Shinzou Academy. She was a pleasant girl. Fond of Midnight.”

“Midnight?”

“A griffin,” Yvonne clarified. “Which, against my judgement, Vincent decided to gift away.”

“A fine beast. And intelligent. I’m certain your sister is putting her to good use.”

“You gave Anna a griffin?”

“She’s one of the few in our group who has use for my specialty.”

“Everyone has use for your money,” Yvonne corrected.

“Money is money,” Vincent said dismissively. “A dozen others could provide that service. I know griffins. Regardless, I deemed your sister a kind and responsible young woman with a fierce drive propelling her forward. And Midnight would never allow a fool to ride her.”

Kizu hadn’t known what he’d hoped to get from the conversation, but he felt disappointed. He had learned that his sister was accompanied by a griffin. And that some people thought her a decent person.

Still, Kizu thanked Vincent profusely for the hospitality before leaving the haunted home, empty burlap sack in one hand and a few paper tickets in the other.

After a quick ride on the driveway, he was spat out of the property and back into the city. As he wandered the streets he thought more on what Vincent had told him and what he should ask in their next encounter.

During his musings, Kizu found himself walking by one of the Tainted refugee camps. Upon seeing it, he instinctively turned down a different street to avoid passing near it. But then something held him back. He stopped midstep, looked over his shoulder, and examined the camp.

The energy of the camp felt different from his previous encounters. Rather than the disorderly assembly loitering around, the Tainted he saw were mostly children and mothers. And in far fewer numbers than before.

Curious, Kizu cautiously approached the camp. It took less than a minute before he found the culprit for the camp’s abandonment.

A flier was pasted to a stone wall across from the camp. And a dozen identical to it could be spotted stuck on nearby surfaces.

“A call to arms?” Kizu muttered, reading the words. “They want to bolster their military might?”

“Without risking their own lives as fodder,” an elderly Tainted woman agreed.

Kizu turned his head. The woman sat on the edge of the street, her feet in the mud. Her silver hair was bound up and concealed behind a headscarf. One eye was a glassy white while the other glittered with a sharp intelligence. Silvery scales dotted her face like freckles.

“They’re recruiting Tainted. Is that…good?”

The woman chortled. “Good? Ah, goodness died from my sight long ago. Perhaps one young like you can still see it in the world.”

“I mean, has the overall stance on Tainted shifted at all in the city? Is your situation better now that more people are employed by the local government?”

“It has changed. Change always comes.”

This felt like an unproductive conversation. He scrutinized the flier further. From what he understood, the local government were integrating the Tainted as a fighting force and making use of lives they likely otherwise saw as nonessential and bothersome. He didn’t like it. But at least until the borders reopened, the starving Tainted were safely off the streets and training.

He wondered if there was anything he could do to interfere with the looming civil war. Could he contact Sasaki and convince him to help smooth over relations between the split nation? But did Sasaki truly understand the political climate of Port Kallis and Tross well enough to truly help or would his interference cost more lives?

Kizu watched the children playing with a ball made of rags. Tossing and catching it. Beyond them, he spotted two others sword fighting with sticks.

“What can I do?” he whispered into the streets.

The old woman’s chortles were the only reply.
