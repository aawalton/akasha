Rhea shifted her gaze away from Vector and swept across the battlefield.

The terrain was broken and uneven, carved by low hills and shallow depressions that now lay littered with bodies. Blood soaked into the dirt, dark and glistening beneath the faint glow of torches hung from the two oxen carts.

Around fifteen enemies were still alive—but not for long. All fifteen bore injuries, shallow and deep, varied across each of them. Gifts from the earlier arrow volley.

Every member of the Astrum Forces was already locked with their opponents. Tristan had taken advantage of the darkness to cleanly execute his enemies, moving like a shadow among shadows. Marcus pressed forward with brutal efficiency, his blade finding throats and hearts with mechanical precision. And Sebastian remained a looming presence at the rear—not lifting a blade, but watching, assessing the battlefield for unknown.

Rhea chose her own target.

She stepped forward with a sharp inhale and engaged the nearest enemy with her Basic Sword Art. Her body moved as one with the blade as she unleashed a flowing sword dance, swinging overhead toward the enemy soldier's head. The sword sang through the air with controlled precision.

The man reacted just in time, raising his blade to block.

Clang!

Steel met steel, the sound echoing across the battlefield. The impact forced him back—three full steps—while Rhea remained perfectly still, her stance unbroken. Stance maintained.

Balance perfect.

That alone told her enough.

He doesn’t know sword arts.

If he did, he would have read the force behind a basic technique instantly. She had already exceeded his strength by a significant margin with only this finding—that the enemy didn't excel in close combat.

Making this fight's outcome inevitable.

She sprinted forward, closing distance with explosive speed, the tip of her sword moving into position with fluid grace.

Thrust stance.

The opponent swung his blade horizontally this time, trying desperately to deflect her sword's trajectory, muscles tensing for impact—

But his blade didn't meet the resistance it should have upon contact.

A feint.

Her sword lowered—too low—in the heartbeat before collision, shifting to an ideal position for an underhand attack. Her blade followed the motion with predatory, merciless intent.

The blade surged upward.

It entered through the lower abdomen and continued its terrible ascent without slowing, carried by unstoppable momentum and flawless execution.

The sword exited through his skull.

He didn't even have time to scream.

His body split apart along a grotesque vertical line, separating cleanly into two equal halves that peeled away from each other like a nightmarish flower opening beneath the moonless sky. Flesh parted. Organs spilled. Blood poured freely, darkening the earth as the inky night amplified every horrific detail.

Making the already suppressed environment filled with dread.

For the enemy. Not for the Astrum forces.

Rhea withdrew her sword in a single smooth motion and let her breathing settle as she exited her combat stance. Her eyes immediately swept the terrain once more, sharp and searching, scanning for threats that still breathed, still moved, still posed danger.

Only a few appeared to remain.

Her gaze found Vector, who was now dealing with two enemies simultaneously, moving between them with explosive bursts of speed. His previous opponent lay a few arm-lengths beside him.

Headless.

Rhea's breath caught. She'd forgotten that her own execution had been just as gruesome. The skull lay at a distance from the body, as if Vector had used pure brute force to send the head flying.

As she observed Vector's battle with his two opponents, she noted that his footwork was erratic—appearing raw and chaotic, following no regular pattern she could discern from the training manuals. Simple brute force was at work, power overwhelming technique, speed compensating for lack of refinement.

But still—

His movement was fast.

Explosive.

Devastating.

She diverted her attention from him to scan and assess the battlefield's current state, counting bodies to ensure none remained standing, none fled into darkness to bring word of this ambush.

One was missing.

Her instinct flared instantly.

Her eyes darted across the terrain, piercing shadows with the aid of scattered torchlight, tracing the folds of the land until the gap in her count revealed itself.

There.

The last soldier.

He was moving slowly toward Vector, using the chaos and the terrain's natural contours as cover, slipping forward under the guise of confusion for what could only be a desperate, final strike.

Rhea moved.

Instantly.

With her sword raised, she appeared behind him like a shadow given form and intent, preparing to drive her blade between his shoulders—to end the threat with a clean backstab.

But before her sword could descend—

Before steel could pierce flesh—

The enemy froze.

His body went completely rigid.

Immobile.

A sword tip appeared at the back of the soldier's left chest—silent, precise—the point emerging from his flesh with a faint metallic sheen that caught the torchlight even in the depths of darkness. The blade pierced through vital organs without hesitation, driven by someone who knew exactly where death resided within the human body. It was positioned with surgical accuracy, aligned perfectly over the heart.

Thud!

The man collapsed like a puppet whose strings had been cleanly severed, limbs folding uselessly beneath him as life fled in an instant.

And then the wielder of that sword stepped into view.

Vector.

He gave her a smile. "Thank you, Rhea."

She barely heard his words before an absurdly vague question tumbled out, professional discipline unable to stop it. "Young Master..." She hesitated, disbelief bleeding into her voice.

"...are you already a Second Stage Warrior?"

She swallowed and pressed on, unable to restrain herself.

"Were you hiding your strength—your First Stage breakthrough—from us all this time?"

Because there was no other explanation.

The power.

The strength.

The speed.

Everything Vector had displayed on this battlefield pointed unmistakably toward a Second Stage Warrior. Even if his movements lacked polish—whether in archery, sword technique, or footwork—those imperfections couldn't conceal the raw force behind them. Clumsy or not, the strength he wielded was unmistakable.

The answer she received shattered even that assumption.

"No," Vector replied calmly. "I only broke through to First Stage today."

The words echoed across the suddenly quiet land, hanging in the air like a challenge to reason itself.

Rhea's eyes widened. Her lips widening involuntary after hearing that he truly broken through. That Vector was also like them now, a Warrior.

Still—

"But your strength?" She voiced the question instantly, unable to suppress the need for understanding that made sense of what she'd witnessed.

"Oh," Vector said lightly, as if the matter were of little importance. "That might be due to my Body Refinement manual." He paused briefly, then added in the same casual tone, "Or it could be the overflowing energy that comes with advancement. The body sometimes hasn't adjusted yet—strength surges beyond its usual limits before settling down."

Rhea studied him closely, eyes fixed on his face as torchlight flickered across his features, searching for hesitation, deception, or even uncertainty.

She found none.

And yet—

She didn’t buy his explanation.

Not even slightly.

Manuals could vary—some refined flesh more efficiently than others, some emphasized speed, others endurance. That much was accepted truth. But no manual, no matter how exceptional, should grant someone an entire stage’s worth of power beyond their cultivation level—certainly not power wielded with the casual dominance of a seasoned Second Stage Warrior who had lived with that strength for years.

The logic didn’t hold.

The numbers didn’t align.

Something else was at work—something Vector either couldn’t explain… or refused to.

She opened her mouth to push further, to demand answers that made sense of what she had witnessed—but she finally closed her mouth.

As Vector had already turned away.

He ignored her pointed stare entirely—and Tristan as well, who had stepped closer, his expression sharp with the same unspoken questions burning behind his eyes.

Instead, Vector shifted seamlessly into command, his voice steady and authoritative, carrying the weight of someone who had fought not one battle—but many.

“Calm the oxen,” he ordered, gesturing toward the carts.

The massive beasts were bellowing in distress, eyes rolled white, hooves stamping violently against the blood-soaked ground. The stench of death had driven them into panic, their raw fear threatening to turn the carts into uncontrollable hazards.

Marcus and the others moved at once.

Rhea remained where she stood, sword still warm in her grip.

Her eyes stayed on Vector.

She felt that he had changed somehow, though she couldn't quite name what was different. Something fundamental had shifted—not just in his strength, but in the way he carried himself, the way he moved through the world.

Something she couldn't point to.

But could feel.

…

Vector's gaze shifted toward the two carts.

They stood quietly at the center of the ravaged clearing, their wooden frames untouched by the chaos that had consumed everything around them. Four oxen remained harnessed—two assigned to each cart—standing before the T-shaped wooden yoke that connected them to their burden. The beasts snorted softly, steam curling from their nostrils in the cool night air. They had calmed now, almost eerily so, considering their bellowing panic only moments earlier. Crates stacked neatly in the carts. Secured. Intact.

Firearms.

Vector studied the carts in silence, his mind contemplating—

Should I store them?

The thought came naturally.

He had done it once already—earlier that evening, after meeting with Sebastian and the others. Back then, he had quietly transferred crafting materials, furnace components, and various ores into his Storage Space, reducing the physical load the group would need to carry on their march to Vantara.

No one had questioned it.

No one even knew.

He had merely told them that another Astrum force unit would transport the supplies later, once they reached Vantara—a half-truth that served its purpose well. Lightening the team’s burden meant speed. Speed meant safety. And safety meant survival.

But now—

These carts presented the same problem.

Oxen were slow.

Carts were loud.

And the road to Vantara was anything but safe.

Dragging this consignment through the wilderness would slow them to a crawl, making them vulnerable not only to unwanted eyes but also to the beasts lurking in the deeper stretches of the Naga Forest. Yet abandoning the firearms was not an option.

Not when they could be used in the coming battles. They would be vulnerable to Lionheart firearms otherwise. And this was the first time they had gotten intact firearms in their hands—weapons they could study, dismantle, understand.

Reverse engineer.

Suddenly a thought struck him—

Scanning.

There was no need for something as crude as reverse engineering anymore.

With the Void Library System’s Scanning function, all he had to do was analyze the firearms once. Their internal structure, mechanisms, materials—everything would be recorded instantly. The complete blueprint would be stored within the System’s archive.

And more importantly—

It would unlock the firearms technology tree.

The implications sent a quiet thrill through him.

Once unlocked, the knowledge would no longer be limited to crude muskets and flintlocks. The System would provide access to the entire evolutionary branch—refinements, improvements, optimizations.

Firearms… redesigned.

Firearms… adapted.

Firearms… built for the Astrum Forces.

Vector’s lips curved slightly upward at the idea involuntarily.

And with that realization, the problem of transportation solved itself.

There was no longer any need to haul this consignment all the way to Vantara.

After scanning, the weapons could be moved—quietly and methodically—back into Carnatic City, transferred to the Resistance base, where they would serve as armament. Though with some caution.

Vector turned to Sebastian.

“Inform the Resistance,” he said calmly. “Have them take control of the carts and weapons once we leave.”

"But Young Master, they could be—" Marcus was the one who voiced the concern, stepping forward with a furrowed brow.

Vector raised his hand to stop him.

He knew Marcus's reason was sound. The weapons could be used against the Lionhearts in the upcoming battle at Vantara, providing crucial firepower in what promised to be a desperate engagement.

"We will produce our own firearms soon," Vector answered, meeting the confused gazes around him. "And if needed, we can always transport these weapons later. We have more than a month."

Sebastian nodded without hesitation and moved to carry out the order immediately.

Vector had assigned him this task especially because he was the only one truly capable of executing it. With his Third Stage strength and movement techniques, bypassing Carnatic City's towering walls would take him mere minutes. No patrol would detect him. No sentry would even sense his passage through the darkness.

While waiting for Sebastian—

Vector stepped toward one of the carts and halted before a reinforced wooden crate bound tightly with iron bands.

He placed a hand on the crate, fingers brushing the rough grain of the wood, and drew a slow breath. The battlefield around him had fallen quiet—only distant movements and low voices carried by the night wind—but his focus had narrowed to this single moment.

Raising his gaze slightly, he spoke within his mind.

System. Scan the crate.

[System Notification]

Scanning…

A faint ripple passed through his consciousness, as though invisible threads were spreading outward, slipping through wood, iron, and steel alike—probing, dissecting, understanding. Within the crate, every mechanism was laid bare to the System's perception: springs, firing pans, barrels, trigger assemblies, tolerances measured down to the finest margin.

And just as quickly as it had begun—

It ended.

[System Notification]Scanning Complete.

[Technology Acquired]Flintlock Musket — Added to Database.

The faint smile on Vector’s face deepened.

Not by intent.

It was deliberate.

A reaction he didn’t bother suppressing as the next notification surfaced before his eyes, glowing softly against the darkness.

[System Notification]

[Technology Database Update]

[Firearms Technology Tree — Unlocked.]

Associated Technologies Unlocked:

• Bullet design and casting methods• Gunpowder composition and refinement• Ignition mechanisms and firing systems• Metallurgy and alloy compositions for barrels and chambers• Structural tolerances and durability optimization

Not just the weapon he had scanned.

But everything connected to it.

Gunpowder.Alloys.Bullets.Propellant mechanisms.

All of it.

Knowledge that had taken Earth centuries of experimentation—of failed designs, catastrophic miscalculations, and oceans of blood spilled across battlefields—had been compressed into instant comprehension. Every formula, every manufacturing process, every pathway of refinement and improvement now lay open before him, layered neatly within the vast archives of the Void Library.

And more than that—

He now possessed the blueprints to create modern firearms.

Not these crude flintlock muskets.Not weapons bound by slow reloads and unreliable ignition.

But true instruments of war.

Sebastian returned shortly after.

The entire task could not have taken more than ten minutes—delivering the message, coordinating the transfer, and bypassing Carnatic City’s defenses without drawing the slightest attention. As expected, he reported success without embellishment.

The carts were secured at a prearranged location, one discreet enough that Resistance members could later move the weapons into Carnatic City without raising suspicion.

With that matter settled, Vector gave the order to move.

Leaving the carts behind, the group turned northwest—away from the city’s distant glow and toward the looming darkness of the Naga Forest.

The land grew wilder as they advanced.

By the time they reached the forest’s periphery, they were no longer alone.

Waiting for them were the remaining members of the expedition.

Four combatants—guards assigned specifically to protect the non-combat personnel—stood ready. Unlike the ones that were with him, they had not joined Vector’s side in the ambush, their role strictly to guard the non-combatant.

Behind them stood the true foundation of what was to come.

Blacksmiths.Scholars.Architects.Alchemists.Healers.

Men and women whose value was not measured by blades or bloodshed, but by what they could build, heal, and create. They had come to assist in the construction of a base in Vantara. A stronghold. A foothold deep within forest.

During the evening meeting, the decision had been unanimous.

It would be fortified. Defenses would be layered. And would even turn it into manufacturing base for firearms.
