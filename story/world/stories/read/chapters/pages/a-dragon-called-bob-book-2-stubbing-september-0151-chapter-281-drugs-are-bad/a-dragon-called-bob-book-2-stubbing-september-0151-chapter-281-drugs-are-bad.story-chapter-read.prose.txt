I had seen many wonders in my time on Helstat.  Hell, I was a wonder; a giant, glorious creature, covered in glittering scales and able to breathe fire and work magic.  The rocking horse in front and above me was something else.

The trees used to make the supports, joists, and whatnot must have been enormous.  They must also have had an exemption from the laws of physics because nothing that big could be built from wood; it ought to collapse under its own weight, let alone that of the rider.

Perched atop the brushed fur that covered the horse's body was the largest giant I’d yet seen.  The hundred and fifty-foot-tall construct barely came up to his waist, judging by how his legs dangled.  He was also seated side saddle, for some reason, and unlike the rest of his species was not a perfect figure writ large upon the universe.

Rolls of bloated flesh, covered in grease or sweat, wobbled as he rocked back and forth.  He also had two heads, which seemed barely worth mentioning in light of the rest of his appearance.  A pipe was held in each hand, thick smoke curling up from both bowls.

“Sure ya don’t want a puff?” his left head boomed, jowls shaking.

“I’m fine, thanks,” I replied.

“I’ll have a go!” Saalkönig added cheerfully.

Our escort had plucked the dwarf from his soft, fleshy transport and deposited him on the ground next to me.  Without a word, she’d nodded jovially to the crettin and headed back towards the seafoodless barbecue.

Gnarly Wave leaned over and offered a pipe stem as broad as the dwarf's body to the little man.  He fanned the bowl with his other hand, and a wave of smoke surrounded Saalkönig.

“Good shit, bro,” the dwarf coughed.

“If we could set aside getting high for a moment, I’d like to ask you for a favour… Mr Gnarly Wave.”

“I’m Gnarly, he’s Wave,” said the left head.

“Well, I guess I’m asking the pair of you.”

“Bad vibes?” asked Wave, looking at his fellow noggin.

“Just messy, bro.  His karma’s all fucked.  Bit of a canugget, though.”  After mere minutes among the other giants, my brain was now automatically filtering the C word into canugget.  They used it like a bloody comma.  It seemed to fill the same space as sir, friend, dude, mate, brief pause in speech, and bastard all at once.  An impressive linguistic trick, pleasingly economical.

“I am not a canugget.”

“When a three-hundred-feet-tall two-headed giant says you’re a canugget, that’s what you are, man,” Saalkönig added helpfully.

“Are you high already?”

“He offered?”

Turning away from the stoned dwarf, I marshalled my thoughts, or tried to.  The rolls of fat hid the worst of it, but Gnarly Wave shared the rest of the giants' aversion to haberdashery.  He was starting to look wobblier than he should.  My vision was slightly blurry.  I suppressed a giggle.

“Look, bro, I need your help.  You guys would be, like, totally tubular in the war against the outsider.  You dudes would save the world!”  Visions of Saturday morning cartoons were flashing through my mind.  I was a hair's breadth away from shouting Thundercats Ho and seeing if that worked.

“We don’t dally with the little people, my guy.  Bad for the flow,” Gnarly said.

“Canuggets just keep fucking our shit up when we try to help out.  They nick our stuff, then kill us when we try to get it back,” Wave added.

“No one’s gonna kill you for helping us out.  Damn, the corners are moving.  What the hell is in that pipe?”

“Good shit,” the dwarf said, nodding wisely.

“Why are you sparkly little lizard?” Gnarly asked.

“Bulb screwed me over.  I got an item, became his champion, and now I sparkle.”

“That’s cool.”

“No, it really isn’t.  There’s gotta be something we can do to, ya know, get you off your giant flabby arses and helping out the rest of the world.”

As a brave and noble dragon, I did not flinch at the double-glare I received for those ill-thought-out words.  It was at least an eleven on my own glare scale, and I was quietly impressed.  The big guy, battered as he might be, knew how to give “the look”.

“You see any fat giants around here?” Wave growled.

“Do you count as one or two?”

“One, canugget,” they both snapped at the same time.

“How come you’re so big?” asked Saalkönig.

“Everything is big to you,” I muttered.

“I am the repository of all our power.  The bros and sisters channel their magic into me to help with the feng shui.  It’s, like, a harmony thing.”  The heads were taking turns to say each sentence.  I was convinced they had some kind of shared brain.  Maybe the left and right lobes of their brain just happened to be in separate skulls?

“And we need that power, oh toned one.  The people of the world below need you,” I tried.

“Which world?” Wave snapped, then took a long draw on his pipe and sighed out a cloud of smoke.

“The world.  Helstat.”

“Oh that one.  Nice goats,” Gnarly said.  He looked at his other half and smiled.  “Be a shame to lose the goats.”

“We don’t need ‘em, bro.  Got plenty of options from the other places.”

“There’s more than goats down there dude. Dudes?  There’s like gambleprics and janglebonks, those things… I could really eat a janglebonk right now.  And people and stuff, but they taste funny,” I rambled, happy to find a point of agreement.  “The people, not the stuff.”

“You’re a gourmand?” Gnarly asked.

“I’m a fucking chef, mate.  Studied under the Dreadchef himself.  His phaalis was something else, I can tell you.  You guys mind if I?”  I produced a lump of meat, I think it was from the Wyrm I’d slain on Brokebone Island, and waved it at them.

“You brought some to share?”

“Sure thing, Wave.”  A pile of Wyrm slices appeared in front of me.

Saalkönig took a nibble, after breaking off a small chunk, but spat it out and crossed his arms.  The crettin however, was more impressed with the flavour.

“This ain’t bad.  Think it would smoke well?” Gnarly asked Wave.

“Reckon so fellow head dude.  We should get it down to chef-bro and see what he thinks.”

“You guys want more?” I asked.

“Sure do little reptile.  How much ya carrying?  It’s got that mellow flavour that brings out the vibe in the smoke.”  He took another pull on his pipes between mouthfuls.

“Oh, plenty.”  I had stolen almost all the corpse and had hardly taken a bite out of it since.  “Won’t come cheap though.”

“We ain’t fighting downstairs for some scraps of wyrm man.”

“He wasn’t a wyrm man.  He was a very long… thing.  A failed dragon.”

“Nah scaly-bro.  Wyrms ain’t canuggeting fucked up dragons.  They’re different.  No legs.”

“Well I’ve got more.  And even better, I can get you whatever you want! For a perfectly reasonable price.” Even with my blood telling my brain to chill out and eat some snacks, Greed was always looking out for me.

“You want us to fight.  What more could you want?”

“Well, like, dude-bro, food ain’t cheap.  Not the good stuff.  I got a chef.  Gorboon.  Not as good as the Dreadchef.  But he was one of a kind.  I got that dudes apprentice as well.  Makes a mean curry.  Always screw up the rice though.  Anyway, I can get you some real grub.  I’ll cut the price if you come help us against the Changer?”  Even stoned, that physically hurt to say.  Cutting the price indeed.  Gross.

“What did chef-bro say he wanted?” asked Gnarly.  Despite his intoxication, there was a shine of keen intelligence in his massive eyes.

“Seafood!  Oh mate, I can sort you out there.  Gimme a sec.”

I pulled a portal stone out of my storage space and thought for a moment.  The river at the heart of Ankmapak was right out.  Any aquatic organism capable of surviving in that sludge was likely half sewage by body weight.  I opened a portal to just north of the Brokebone Isles, hopefully well away from where the merfolk hung out.  Then again, that nice mermaid with the big… I wouldn’t mind if she plopped through.

What I got was a torrent of salt water that washed away the pile of meat I’d provided.  When I snapped the portal closed, dozens of fish, some the size of a human torso, were left flopping about on the grass.

“Sorry about that.  Pretty sure salt water is good for plants, so the grass will be fine.”  I waved a paw airily, needlessly confident in my pronouncement.

“It fucks them up, canugget.  What kind of canugget doesn’t know that?” Gnarly said, but Wave held up the hand on his side of the body to silence his… brother?  He reached down and plucked a particularly large fish, covered in silver scales that I had to respect from an aesthetic point of view as the light danced on them.

“Fish?” Gnarly asked.

“Nope.  It’s a truck,” I chuckled.  Damn, I was funny today.

“Defo a fish, bro,” replied Wave.

“We can get more?” Gnarly asked.

“You guys are really that desperate for seafood?”

“Sure thing lizard-bro.  No seas up here.  All goes into the clouds, and the interdimensional gravito-flux tears.”

That last part had sounded suspiciously un-hippy-ish to me.

“I know a guy who talks like that.  An Orlic from Earth.  Not from Earth, he was born an Orlic here, but his, ya know, soul was from Earth.  Originally.”  I produced another lump of wyrm meat and took a bite.

“Earth?  Those guys are dicks.  Killed Feefifo. After the canugget nicked his big chicken.”

“Goose,” I corrected.

“What’s a goose?” asked Saalkönig.

“Big chicken,” I replied, earning sage nods from the twin-headed giant.

“Biggest.  Did golden shits too, the fucking giant canugget.”

“Eggs,” I suggested.

“Nah, they were shits.  Did that prick who stole it say they were eggs?  Haha poor canuggets he conned into eating that stuff,” Gnarly boomed.

“I think he sold them?  It’s a little hazy.”  Everything was a little hazy for me.  The world was a kaleidoscope of coolness, and any urgency I’d once felt was long gone.  I knew I’d come here for a reason, but right then all I wanted was to go check out what chef-bro was cooking, and stare at Falling Blossoms galaxy-class cleavage.

Granny Wave slipped down from the rocking horse and took a long step back the way I’d come.  “We need to sort out the fishies, scale-dude.  Once we’ve eaten, we can talk more.”  Both heads exhaled massive clouds of smoke at me, and I felt all tingly.  Saalkönig leaned back in the grass and grinned at the sky.  Scooping him up in one claw, I beat my wings and lurched into the sky.  My usually smooth and graceful flight was jerky and uncoordinated.  My wings seemed to want to flap at different times, but I managed to snatch a claw hold most of the way up the giant's back.  He didn’t care as my claws bit into him, and once I was perched on his shoulder like a parrot on a two-headed pirate, he reached up and patted me on the head with his pipestem.

I didn’t mind at all.  All I could think about was smoked fish and literal acres of female flesh.
