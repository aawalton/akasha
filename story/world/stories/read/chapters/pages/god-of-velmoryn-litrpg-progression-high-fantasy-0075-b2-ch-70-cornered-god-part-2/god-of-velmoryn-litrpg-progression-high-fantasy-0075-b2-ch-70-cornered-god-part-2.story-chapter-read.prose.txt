[The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility Wishes to Form a Divine Contract!]

Seeing the golden window, my focus immediately darted toward it, realizing something significant must have happened.

But before reading it, first, I needed to take care of the goddess sprawled on my floor.

I stopped my divinity a fraction of a second before it reached Athena. I made the basilisk behind my manifestation slither forward.

Its giant body coiled around her, its red eyes now fixed on Athena, tongue flicking as if testing her fear.

“Do not move!” I ordered, then finally shifted my focus to the divine contract Athena had offered.

[The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility Wishes to Form a Divine Contract!]

The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility proposes the following terms:

She will not act against the God of Velmoryn in any manner, including assisting any entity she knows to be hostile toward the God of Velmoryn.

She will use her innate abilities in service to the God of Velmoryn without cost.

She will share all information in her possession with the God of Velmoryn.

In exchange:

She requests that the God of Velmoryn never use force against her, unless she violates any of the conditions above.

She requests protection should she face conflict as a result of fulfilling her obligations to the God of Velmoryn.

Penalty Clause:

If the God of Velmoryn breaks this promise, they will lose their Authority. If the Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility breaks her promise, she will lose her Authority.

Reading the text, I felt both alarmed and intrigued. Her offer, especially the part about sharing all her information with me, seemed like a double-edged sword.

I valued information above all else. I knew so little about godhood. If Athena shared her knowledge with me, it could only be beneficial. At least, that was how it appeared at first glance.

But if I combined that with what she requested from me… what if she shared something trivial, then claimed it had cost her a war with another deity? I would be forced to fight or face death. And that risk applied to almost everything.

Even if the information was not trivial and held real value, it was completely unreasonable for the cost of that information to be a war with another deity.

I needed to be careful with this contract. And with Athena as well.

Divine contracts were not like promises made half-jokingly. Agreeing to the terms would bind me to them and force me to fulfill every single one without exception.

“I have considered your request,” I said at last.

Athena, trembling as she avoided the basilisk’s scarlet eyes, finally managed to focus on me.

“And? Is it… is it not to Your liking?” she asked.

I let the moment stretch, making the basilisk coil tighter, its head now hovering in front of her face. Athena retreated, but as she felt the serpent’s slick skin brush against her back, she jumped.

Satisfied with how her fear deepened, I announced my decision.

“I am not against forming a divine contract with you. However, the terms you suggested do not entice me.”

“We can work something out!” Athena practically shouted. “Please, I’m sure we can. But… please remove Your Lordship’s guardian.”

I was amused. After what she had done, after her attempt to betray me, I no longer felt anything positive toward her. But I did intend to use her to my advantage.

I tried to be merciful and fair with you, but you did not deserve it.

I made the basilisk retreat. The massive creature circled Athena once, its forked tongue tasting her skin, before slithering back toward the throne and coiling around it. It lowered its head beneath the God of Velmoryn’s hand, as if asking to be rewarded.

Keeping it near her would not have increased her fear. If anything, it might have angered her, which would have made my goal harder to achieve. Now that I had shown restraint and allowed her a moment to breathe, she would see me differently.

“Thank you,” she said as she stood, her eyes still fixed on the basilisk.

“The information I seek from you concerns my enemies, or anything I may ask of you directly. Anything else might be of no value to me.”

Athena nodded.

“I agree, and—”

“I am not done,” I cut her off.

Even that would not be enough. Simply specifying the information I needed would not guarantee that I would remain unpunished if one of her enemies got to her. I needed to secure my own safety above all else.

I was about to demand that the punishment for me not mirror hers. She would lose her Authority and die, but why should I? I was the one in the advantageous position, after all. But as the thought formed, a blue window flashed before my eyes.

[Divine Contract Rules]

Fairness is a fundamental condition of all divine contracts.

When a contract is formed between deities of unequal rank, balance must be preserved. A lower-ranked deity cannot be bound by a lesser penalty, while the higher-ranked deity faces an ultimate consequence such as the loss of Authority.

Such imbalance will render the contract invalid or trigger intervention.

What the hell?!

I was angry, but more than that, I was confused. What kind of justice system favored the strong and protected them from the weak?

Sure, I was stronger than Athena, but that was only because of my Radiance and the fact that she was in my dominion. And considering that pure Radiance was rare, in most other cases, she would have been the stronger one. Who knew what would have happened if we had fought outside, where she could channel as much of her divine power as she wished? Would my Radiance be enough against her larger reserves?

Then it hit me. Perhaps the rules favored the strong because that was the intention. Maybe those who created them had safeguarded themselves, in case they ever found themselves in a situation like Athena’s, where a weaker deity could force a divine contract on them…

It sounded plausible. But whether it was true or not changed nothing for me.

Without reducing my punishment, the contract was far too dangerous. No matter how much I didn’t want to let this opportunity slip, no matter how much I wanted to bind a god to my service, my safety came first.

Is there no other way?

I looked at the system window again, weighing my options, what Athena had requested, and what she offered. No matter how I considered it, guaranteeing her safety was far more valuable than the information she could provide. This contract clearly favored her.

I glanced at Athena. She waited patiently for my response, not daring to speak.

How different things might have been if we were not in my dominion. Outside of it, even if she failed to defeat me, she could at least flee.

Of course…

The idea came to me suddenly.

“You must always accept my summon. Whenever I call you, you will enter my dominion.”

She flinched.

“Your Lordship…” she muttered, struggling to find words that would make me reconsider.

“This is not negotiable!”

“But Your Lordship, I am also a deity!” she said, her voice rising.

You should’ve thought about that before you decided to betray me!

My dominion trembled as the God of Velmoryn on the throne leaned forward. The basilisk slithered toward Athena, its massive body moving slowly, drawing out the tension.

“Damn… What am I supposed to do now?” she cursed inwardly, her thoughts racing as she searched for a solution.

The basilisk lunged, its jaws wide open.

Athena retreated, faint pink energy covering her body, but it scattered the moment my divinity surged toward her.

She fell, her eyes wide.

“He will actually kill me…”

Desperation overtook her as she made her decision.

Before she could even speak, another golden window flashed before my eyes.

The contract had been formed, its terms exactly as I had demanded.

A star formed in my dominion, just beside the throne. It was larger than all the others, closer in size to Pintre’s star than to those of my believers. It shone with a faint pink hue and was not tethered to Orrvyn’s manifestation.

Still, I could feel that I had absolute control over it. A single thought would be enough to forcefully drag Athena into my dominion.

“I’m dead…” she thought. “They are all bastards! I shouldn’t have visited him… no, I shouldn’t have visited that snake…”

Her head hung low as she cursed her fate.

I didn’t care. It was her fault.

Ignoring Athena’s curses, I was about to question her regarding the army when Aurelion’s voice snapped me out of my thoughts.

“My Liege, we found him!”

Shit, I’ll have to wrap this up…

I could not afford to waste any more time on Athena, so I channeled my divinity.

“You may leave,” I said, removing her from my dominion.

Athena’s seated figure faded as my divinity wrapped around her and forced her out, leaving me alone in my realm.

I flew toward the Window.

Now that I could summon her into my dominion whenever I wished, I was not concerned. Even the part of the contract that required me to guarantee her safety felt trivial. If it came to that, I would simply drag her here and keep her. No one could harm her if they could not reach her.

I felt both amused and satisfied. Not only had I removed the greatest drawback of the contract, but I had also gained a spy. And she was a god.

I was slightly disappointed that I had not found a way to place my velmoryn within Noah’s army, but that could be dealt with later. Athena would not be going anywhere.

Now, I needed to closely monitor Aurelion and the vampire he had finally found.
