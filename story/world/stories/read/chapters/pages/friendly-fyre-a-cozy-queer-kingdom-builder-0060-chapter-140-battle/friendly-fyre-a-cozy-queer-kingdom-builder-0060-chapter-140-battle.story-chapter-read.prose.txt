I open Psionic Sense, watching through Ollie’s eyes as he circles the tower. Accessing the Dungeon Core next, I have it take hold of all the stone in the walls, refusing to let it budge as Ollie flares his wings and drops around the tower. His tail whips around it tight, and his claws scrape down the stone, catching on a ledge. If the Dungeon Core hadn’t been tending to the tower, Ollie would have almost certainly tore through the wall. To keep himself from sliding down, he wraps his wings around the tower as well.

Dizzi appears at the top floor balcony. “Ollie?! Stars above, what are you doing?”

Before I can listen to more of their exchange, Lorata’s sharp tone brings me back to my own body. For one panicked moment I think she realized what I’m doing, but her attention is still on Gardi.

Lorata shakes her head at the felis. “I understand the choice you made, even if it is a disappointing one. It is easy to miss the forest for the trees, especially when their blossoms smell so sweet. You’re not wrong to care for the individuals here, but the danger they house poses a much more existential threat. If you have seen the things I have, you would not be so quick to defend these people.”

“And that’s the crux of it, isn’t it?” I say, hoping to buy Ollie some time. “You expect others to act in accordance with information you withhold. You ask everyone to take your word on faith, but provide no evidence to substantiate your claims.”

Lorata glances sidelong at me. “That is how faith works. It should not require evidence—merely obedience.”

“I disagree,” I insist. “All of my faith is founded in evidence. When I place my faith in someone, it’s because I’ve witnessed what they’re capable of. I don’t know that they’ll be able to achieve what I expect of them in the moment, but I’m willing to take that chance. Because I’ve seen their compassion, or expertise, or strength, and I know they have the potential to rise to such heights again.”

Lorato sniffs at this, returning her attention to Gardi. “In that case, let’s put your faith to the test.” She lifts a hand.

A blast of wind and a flurry of feathers whip past me. Salvia lands in front of Gardi, their wings fully extended like a pair of shields. They grip their spear with both hands, whiteknuckled.

“You will not lay a hand on them,” Salvia says, their voice level.

Lorata scoffs out a disbelieving laugh. “Clearly the pantheon has been too idle over the years to allow such misplaced confidence to breed among mortals. Enough of this. If your plan was to foster empathy for your people, you’ve severely missed the mark.”

It would have been nice, I admit. Luckily for us, however, that was never the real goal.

Lorata freezes, then turns to me with a frown. I’m uncertain if she also just reached the same conclusion, but I’m not about to wait and find out.

“Ollie?” I prompt. “What is Dizzi saying? Is the spell ready?”

There’s a pause as I can sense him speaking with her.

“UM, SHE’S SAYING ABSOLUTELY NOT, WE NEED TO CAL—CALI—CALIBERATE? IT FIRST. AND THEN WE NEED A CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT. AND THEN—”

“Will it work?” I interrupt as Lorata narrows her eyes at me. “If I activate it, will it work?”

“UM, SHE SAYS IT PROBABLY WON’T BLOW UP?” Ollie replies.

That will have to be good enough. I open the flow of mana, allowing everything to pass through the Dungeon Core and spread into the spell network. The Core greedily laps it all up, ecstatic for its unrestrained access to the Isle’s magic. At the same time I shut off all non essential spells, increasing the mana flow rate. I direct the Core to pour everything it has into the new spell, and it giddily obliges.

The dome around the city dissolves into orange motes of light. A hush runs through the gods’ ranks. There’s a moment of uncertain silence, as if everyone is holding their breath, waiting to see what new spell takes its place.

And… there’s nothing. If a new spell has permeated the city, I didn’t feel a ghost of its touch, and Echo has reported nothing.

“Enough of this nonsense. Seize her,” Lorata snaps, breaking the spell. She flicks a hand in my direction. “Widengra, go retrieve her remnant.”

Shirasil slides in front of the orc god. “Now, hold on just a—”

Widengra roughly elbows Shirasil out of the way, who is thrown back into the ranks of the pantheon like a ragdoll. A few gods and champions stumble back as they unwillingly break his fall.

A dracid girl rushes to his side. “Lord Shirasil! Are you alright?”

Widengra stalks ahead, ignoring the scene unfolding behind him. That is, until Shirasil starts to cackle. The orc god frowns over his shoulder as Shirasil accepts help to stagger to his feet and wipes his wrist across his nose. He starts laughing even harder, an unhinged edge creeping into his tone.

“Lord Shirasil?” the dracid asks, her voice wavering with uncertainty. She watches him rub his fingers together. “Is… is that blood?”

“I certainly hope so, dear.” Shirasil spreads his hands, vivid red dripping from his nose and smeared across his palms. He spits some more blood through a laugh. “I’d be concerned if it were something else.”

I attempt to Check Shirasil’s health, and it’s only then I recognize the absence: There is no Echo in my head. No visuals when I reach for them, no words or stats superimposed over my vision. The System is down.

The gods also must be figuring it out, because right that moment, all hell breaks loose.

“The remnant!” Lorata shouts, and Widengra turns his back on the unfolding chaos to race up the steps.

“Mir—” I cry.

She’s already sprinting after him, taking the steps four at a time. “On it.”

“Be careful,” I mentally call after her. “He doesn’t have the System to rely on, but he’s still dangerous.” And the frightening part is I have no idea how dangerous. Without Stats, I don’t know if his health or mana is just as high as it was before, or if they were being artificially inflated by the system. Given Shirasil’s currently bleeding, I have to assume that they’re at least more vulnerable, but—

I catch the movement out of the corner of my eye. A blazing white spear stabs toward me, and I barely manage to jerk to the side, Mirzayael’s training the only thing that saves me. Lorata flicks the head of her spear off to the side. “I have had more than enough of you.”

As I retreat, I focus inward, reaching for my flames. It feels different from casting a spell with Echo—that’s automatic, structured. But plenty of people do magic without access to the System; I’ll also just have to do it the old fashioned way.

“FYRE?” Ollie calls. “WHAT’S GOING ON? IT LOOKS LIKE PEOPLE ARE FIGHTING, BUT ECHO ISN’T SAYING YOU’RE IN DANGER. OH! SHE ISN’T SAYING ANYTHING! I CAN’T FIND HER! WHAT’S—”

“Ollie, stay where you are,” I quickly tell him, keeping my gaze locked on Lorata. “It’s not safe.”

It doesn’t work on all of them. Some have been Attuned to their wielder, or are currently imbued with their magic. But for the artificed armor that weren’t yet activated, for the weapons that were impressively decorated yet mundane in nature, they vanish from their bearers in an instant. Chainmail disappears, shields dissolve into thin air, brandished weapons dissipate between clutched fingers. Hopefully, her ability to float was also an artifact of the System.

Lorata’s feet leave the ground, and she flies after me.

That’s less than ideal.

Below, Widengra has gained a new weapon—a giant, red axe, formed from the blood that flows from his open wound. But every time she leaves another cut, more blood flows into his axe, growing larger by the second. All it would take is one well-placed hit to take Mirzayael out.

A beam of light cuts through the air, and I narrowly dodge by flipping the direction of my Jets. Lorata narrows her eyes, aiming her spear again as it glows white. I jerk to the side, missing the second shot, too. She’s not used to a harpy who uses rockets in place of wind arcana—she’s anticipating where I’ll be based on classical aerodynamics. My flames are unlikely to do much damage to Lorata: her magic is more honed, and she’s already displayed her capability to dispel my attacks. Her spear means I can’t get close, either—not without a weapon of my own, and I wouldn’t know how to use one, anyway. Which leaves—

Another beam of light fires straight at me, this time with far greater accuracy. I reach for the Dungeon Core, and before I can even articulate what I want, it’s read my mind.

A boulder appears in the air before me, and the beam crashes into it. I dodge to the side as Lorata’s line of sight is obscured. When the beam cuts out and the boulder begins to fall, the Core swallows it whole once more. Lorata finds me a moment later with a look of surprise.

Time to go on the offense.

I know she’s known as the Seer and supposedly has some sort of foresight abilities, but I’d hoped that was myth. How is she doing it? Without System cheats, too. Because I have before. I gave up on my last family. I didn’t fight for them. They pulled away, and I let them, because I knew deep down I wasn’t worthy of their love.

“Fyre,” Mirzayael’s voice comes again, a little louder than before. “Don’t listen!”

Listen to what?

A fog briefly clears from my mind. There’s a heavy weight pressing down on me. Suffocating my thoughts. Lorata—it’s her influence. Mind magic.

She burrowed into my memories, seized upon my fears. But she made the mistake of assuming the person I was yesterday is the person I am today. I seize the foreign magic, and I rip it in half.
