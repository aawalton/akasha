The green goo didn’t make a noise as it suddenly overflowed from the hole, then spilled all over the banks. A silent, terrifying, glowing wave.

I leaped high into the air and landed on a taller boulder, farther away from the waterfall area. Anything that could move fled the area. All of the hounds took off at once, except for the massive ones that were still fighting with the jagged predatory rock-creatures, completely oblivious to anything else going on.

A massive hunk of rock went flying as one of the hounds cracked a rock-thing open.

Random boulders, when splashed or submerged with the liquid, took on a neon-green appearance, then the goo started to ripple.

Thinking of the nests from the cliff side, I moved away from the area faster than before. I needed to get up to the floating rock, no matter what, and this felt like a distraction. The end of one of the chains wasn’t far, and my wings should be pretty healed at this point, I just didn’t want to have to fight that drake in the air.

Or really at all.

I wanted to get this quest done, and go get some noodles.

Which kinda of shocked me. I almost paused, but the commotion behind me caused me to keep going, leaping from one boulder to another as I moved ever closer to the bottom of the chain.

Something screamed behind me, but I didn’t even glance. I just hoped across the landscape. The dots on my map scattered from the waterfall area. Nothing paid any attention to me.

The end of the chain tied into the ground with a massive bolt. Again, the entire thing throbbed with the green energy, yet as I switched to second sight, the actual chain pulsed with that other source. The same stuff the chain in the above had, ancient and far more powerful than this chaotic energy around me.

Energy surged from above, and the Drake launched itself off the floating island. This time the collar stretched longer, letting it fly much farther than before. I pressed myself into the shadows at the base of the chain, pulling harder on my stealth.

The Drake headed directly to the river.

Fire gushed from its mouth, incarcerating the green ooze with every pass.

I studied the creature as it went back and forth, removing the overflowing river. The river kept gushing, though the Drake kept slowly pushing it back.

This was my chance.

With a deep breath, I launched myself up. Wings sprouted from my back as I took to the air and flew. I stayed close to the chain, keeping it between me and the drake, but the great creature’s focus remained on the river.

I sprinted through the air, aiming for the damn floating rock.

All I heard from behind was the drake’s fire, as it focused on its task.

Time slowed down as I gained altitude, then finally reached the island.

I didn’t dare set down just yet, but the rush of fire from below paused. Quickly, I searched for an area to hide within the rocks on the island. The chain attached to the ground anchored into a thick metal band encircling the entire island, so that didn’t help.

The mostly-flat surface led to a set of three jagged points, stretching up to the crack in the sky. All three had openings at their bases as I approached from the left.

The chain attached to the Drake went to the one on the right, while the one in the center opened to a massive, pitch-black entrance.

A roar from behind caused me to dart inside the left-most one and press myself against the edges of the entrance.

The ground shook as the creature landed, sending a burst of dust across everything. The metal clang of the chain rattled as it crawled along the surface.

“The river will fail,” said a deep voice. “The time has come.”

The following sigh rattled my ears.

“Soon…”

The red dot on my map moved away from the opening and into the cavern on the far right. I didn’t dare let out a breath of relief. Instead, I tried to figure out if I could reach the Mana Lens from here, or if it was in the central spire.

This opening remained empty, though there were several tunnel openings coming off of it. I snuck deeper into the room, heading for the left-most entrance. If I stayed to the left, I’d eventually find what I was looking for, I hoped.

Having a map helped.

The tunnel could fit a massive creature in it, but dust coated the floor indicating nothing had been this way in a while. I carefully continued down the passage, watching my surroundings and my map. Eventually, the tunnel opened up even farther, to a stone cavern with cages that stretched to the ceiling. Each was empty, with the doors torn off.

I quietly retraced my steps and headed down the next tunnel.

Again, dust coated the floor.

It led to another set of empty cages, and I picked up my pace to head back. Something glinted in the corner of the room, though, and I paused before heading that way. A broken chain link bigger than my head rested against the corner of the cavern.

The same energy that pulsed through the massive chains outside pulsed inside it. I lightly touched it with a finger, yet nothing happened.

Then, I yanked it into my inventory.

There was no chance I’d leave it behind. John would have a field day with it, if nothing else. I headed back to where I’d started.

Then, I only had one more route to take.

Much like the others, the cavern at the end contained cages and nothing else.

Without much of a choice. I inched back toward the opening to the outside.

Nothing appeared on my map, though I knew the Drake couldn’t be far away. All I needed to do was wait until the Drake needed to fly out again, and then I could sneak into the center entrance.

I stayed within the cavern and off to one side, so I couldn’t be seen from the other entrance, and I sat down.

Time to wait.

I hated having to wait.

Closing my eyes, I pulled up my stat screen.

So much progress, though my advancement had slowed down. Before this ranking, I’d flown through the levels and gained so much, so fast. Now things moved differently. Each choice I made with skills had a heavier weight to them. My stats increased with each level, but it didn’t have the same thrill. The last couple did, with the nests, but that was the exception.

Instead, I felt weary.

Sitting here on the stone floor, all I really wanted to do was go get noodles and maybe take a nap. The urge to keep moving forward leached out of me.

The rocks behind me were nice and cool, slowing my breathing a bit.

Just a short nap, then I could get moving and check out the thing.

“I wouldn’t do that if I were you.”

My eyes snapped open to see a massive nose in my face. I didn’t dare move, as the snout moved back, eyeing me. Green light flickered along the Drake’s collar.

“Sleeping here is how it gets you,” the voice said. It didn’t come from his mouth, but echoed inside my head. “Next thing you know, your body is gone and you are made of bone.”

Still, I didn’t want to move.

I focused on my hands, and it took a few moments to get my fingers to twitch.

“There you go, you can do this,” he added, then he nudged my leg.

Warmth blazed inside my body for a split second, and I scrambled to my feet.

“Much better.” The massive eyes pulled back to stare at me again. “What is a little devourer doing here?”

“I need to finish a quest,” I said, seeing no reason to lie. “The thing I need to destroy is in the center of the mountain.”

The Drake nodded. “You better get moving. Something changed on the other side of the world, though I bet you know more about that than I do.”

It was a leading statement.

“I destroyed the Mana Lens, and the purple crack in the sky started to close.”

A deep chuckle came from its throat, making the floor rattle.

“Indeed.” His head yanked back, and he moved out of the room. “You need to hurry if you want to save this place.”

I inched closer to the entrance slowly, but he wasn’t even looking at me. Instead, the Drake peered over the edge at the land below. I tried to use insight on him again, but it completely failed, making me wonder about the results I’d seen before.

“Hurry.” This time his voice came out as a squeak, like something had choked him. The green light around his neck flared, and a massive roar broke out from his mouth.

Everything inside me froze for a second, then he leaped off the mountain. The ground shook and I just dodged his tails as they swept back and forth.

Turning, I hurried into the center entrance. I shook my head, trying to hold onto the warmth that had come from when the Drake had touched my leg. Yet, each step into the darkness grew colder.

My armor thickened on my feet, and it helped.

In the back of my mind, all I could think about was noodles.

While I loved some good noodles, normally I wasn’t nearly so fixated. This had to be something in this area wanting me to leave. I pushed back against the thoughts, and it was like something shattered.

The distracting thoughts vanished.

I moved faster down the tunnel. The stone underfoot transitioned to rough-cut to tiles. Then torches appeared, though none of them were lit. Doors appeared, blocking off the rest of the way, much like the doors that led to the council chamber.

Finally.

I approached the doors, then they swung open at my approach.

Not good.

Not good at all.

I darted to the corner of the wall where the edges of the door met the stone.

A figure stepped out with a halo floating in front of him, lighting the way. Bright green flames twirled in a circle, reminding me of one of the council members.

[High Councilor Valerius Prime, The Sovereign, Level 295, Unknown. OFFENDER]

What the ever living fuck? We killed him. I ate his core. The bitter taste came back, like something had been off about it.

Now, he walked down the tunnel heading away from me. He didn’t even appear as a dot on my map. Absolutely nothing.

[Valerius Prime]

His name remained on the list. Even though I’d already earned the bounty.

The floor shook as a roar echoed from where Valerius had gone. The bigger point was that the door remained open, and I inched closer to the opening. No one appeared in the room, and I stepped inside.

Another roar echoed from the tunnel. This time it was filled with pain. I paused.

The Drake had saved me. It had sent that burst of heat into me, and urged me to get up and move.

Last time, the Mana Lens was set above the orb. Inside the room, soft blue light floated down from an orb in the ceiling. One I recognized from the world above.

This again.

I knew what to do.
