From within the haze, three hulking shapes emerged through the smoke and red fog. These were orcs, and each possessed nearly the bulk of two men. Their guttural snarls rolled through the din as they stomped over the bodies of fallen goblins, trampling their smaller kin without a second thought. The goblin horde had thinned under the party's relentless assault, but the sight of these new monsters renewed the dread in every adventurer’s chest.

Brett spotted them first. His pulse hammered against his ribs as he raised his hand and started chanting. Small tongues of flame licked across his fingers, hungry and hot. He could feel the weight of every spell now; it was a physical pressure in his skull. The ache of a mana pool running close to dry was a hollow, cold sensation, but he could not stop. Not with those brutes bearing down on his friends.

He hurled a Fireball into the centre of the orc trio. The spell arced through the smoky air and exploded with a crack that shook the nearby trees, engulfing the beasts in roaring flame. The heat washed over the battlefield, lighting up the clearing like a sudden, violent dawn.

Goblins caught near the blast vanished in screams and ash. Their crude weapons melted in their hands before they even hit the mud. But the orcs were different.

They did not fall.

Brett swore under his breath. The flames clung to their skin, blistering and blackening the grey-green hide, but the orcs simply roared louder. They were furious instead of afraid. The smell of burning flesh carried on the wind, thick and cloying. One of the monsters slapped out a patch of fire on its shoulder and raised a massive cleaver. Its single good eye glowed crimson under the haze.

“Bloody hell, they’re still coming!” Brett shouted. He pulled another Fireball into existence, the magic tugging at the last dregs of his strength. After this, he realised he would need to switch to Firebolts in an attempt to conserve what little mana remained.

Perberos was already moving. He darted along the tree line with liquid silence, his silver eyes narrowed and his bowstring drawn tight. He ignored the goblins now; they were a distraction from the larger threat. He lined up his shot on the lead orc’s broad chest and loosed.

The arrow thudded into thick muscle but barely slowed the beast. The orc snarled and kept coming.

Another arrow followed, this one higher, but it glanced off a heavy collarbone. A third buried itself deep just below the creature’s neck, and dark blood spilled down its chest. Still, the orc did not falter.

Perberos ground his teeth and adjusted his stance. He drew again, feeling the familiar weight of tension in his bowstring. “Come on, come on,” he muttered to himself.

The orc lifted its head, mouth open in a triumphant bellow.

Perberos let fly.

The arrow screamed through the haze. It vanished into the fog for a heartbeat before burying itself with a wet crunch right through the orc’s eye socket. For a split second, the beast simply froze. It looked as though its brain had not yet caught up to the fact that it was dead. Then it let out a choking gurgle and collapsed backwards. The impact shook the ground, spraying mud and bloody grit across the grass.

“Holy shit!”

For a brief second, the adventurers cheered, but the other two orcs did not even glance at their fallen kin. They simply kept marching forward through the carnage. Smoke curled from their burned flesh, and their eyes remained fixed on the line where the adventurers waited.

Josh braced behind his shield. He could feel the tremor of each heavy footstep vibrating through the earth and into his boots.

“Right, one down,” he muttered under his breath. He tightened his grip on his sword. “Guess it’s our turn.”

The remaining two orcs pushed their way through the surviving goblins, scattering them like leaves in a gale. Smaller creatures were sent flying. Some were crushed underfoot, while others were knocked aside by sheer brute force. One of the orcs swung a spiked club lazily at a goblin in its path, smashing the creature into a nearby tree with a wet crunch. The orc bellowed again, and the sound rattled Josh’s ribs even from a distance.

Brett’s fire still clung to them, licking along their shoulders and backs. The flames crackled where flesh had blistered and peeled away. Their skin bubbled and split under the heat, but the pain only seemed to drive them further into a frenzy. The smell of scorched meat filled the air, thick and nauseating.

Josh grimaced behind his shield. His throat felt dry and tight. “They’re not even slowing down,” he muttered.

One of the orcs slammed a goblin aside with its massive forearm, sending it tumbling into the dirt. The beast locked eyes on Josh and roared. The sound hit like a physical shockwave, rattling through the trees.

Brett, standing on a slope behind the front line, threw a final Fireball. The spell streaked across the battlefield and burst between the two orcs in a flash of molten light. For a moment, both disappeared in the explosion. Goblin corpses went flying in every direction like macabre confetti.

When the smoke cleared, both orcs were still standing. Their hair had burned away entirely. Their skin was blackened and cracked like charcoal, but their muscles flexed powerfully beneath the charred surface. Steam hissed from their bodies as their wounds sizzled, glowing faintly red with a mana-fuelled rage.

Brett stumbled. His breathing was ragged and shallow. “That should have hurt them a lot more.”

Perberos cursed under his breath. He drew another arrow and loosed it, though he knew it would do little against things like that unless he secured another lucky hit. The shaft buried itself deep in one orc’s side, but the monster did not flinch. It did not even look down.

The pair charged forward now. They moved low and fast despite their size, their heavy footfalls pounding across the dirt. Josh could feel the vibration through his boots as they closed in. The last few goblins dived out of their way, fearing their own allies as much as the enemy.

Bhel grunted beside him and tightened his grip on his twin axes. “Time to earn our keep, lad.”

Josh swallowed hard. He raised his shield and braced himself. “Aye. Let’s see if these bastards can bleed.”

The orcs broke through another group of goblins, pushing others forward as meat shields. The fight began anew: closer, louder, and far more deadly.

Josh drove his sword deep into the chest of another goblin. He felt the resistance as the blade met bone before it tore free with a wet rip. The creature slumped to the dirt and twitched once before going still. He took a half step back, raising his shield and peering over its rim. Through the haze and smoke, the two orcs were closing fast. They were monstrous shapes cutting through the chaos, their hulking bodies framed by firelight and shadow.

He knew they could not let both reach the line at once. If they did, the weight of the charge would break them in seconds.

“Bhel!” Josh barked. His voice was hoarse from shouting. “I’m pushing forward. We take one before the other gets close!”

The dwarf’s answering roar was like thunder. “About time we stopped waiting!”

Without another word, Josh surged forward. His boots churned up a slurry of dirt and blood. The goblins in front of him barely had time to react. He slammed into the first group like a battering ram. His shield crunched into ribs and skulls, sending bodies flying left and right. Green blood splattered his faceplate as he drove on, carving a path through the swarm.

Behind him, Bhel followed like a living avalanche. Every goblin Josh knocked down was met with a brutal end. The dwarf’s axes rose and fell in wide, heavy arcs. Each swing was punctuated by a grunt of effort and the wet crack of bone. He moved with grim purpose, splitting skulls and severing limbs as easily as a woodsman chopping firewood.

Around them, enemy arrows fell like rain. They thudded into the ground or bounced off plate armour. Some hit hastily thrown up magical shields, while others drove deeply into the backs of retreating goblins.

“Keep moving!” Bhel shouted. His voice boomed over the clash of steel. “Don’t slow down, lad!”

Josh did not. He felt the burn in his legs and the strain in his shoulders, but he pressed on. The orc on the right had broken ahead of its companion. Its charred hide glistened with blood and soot. It swung a cleaver the size of a man’s torso, scattering goblins as it came. It was heading straight for him.

Josh adjusted his grip, hefting his shield and sword as he charged the last few metres. His heart hammered against his ribs. Every instinct screamed at him to run, but he buried the fear deep. If he stopped now, if he faltered for even a moment, that thing would plough through their line and kill everyone behind him.

Carcan’s breath came in shallow bursts. The air was thick with smoke and the metallic tang of blood. Sweat trickled down her neck, plastering loose strands of silver hair to her face as she called out warnings between spells.

“Left, Josh, shield up!” she shouted. She thrust her staff forward, and a shimmer of golden light flared just in time to catch an arrow before it could skewer his exposed side. The impact rippled through her arm, a dull ache that spoke of her own exhaustion.

Her heart hammered. Every cast was a drain she could feel deep in her chest, a hollow tugging at her soul. She forced herself to take a half-step back, drawing in ragged breaths and trying to steady her shaking hands. She had already used one mana potion; the glass vial now lay somewhere at her feet, shattered. The second sat in her palm, her thumb resting against the cork.

She hesitated.

If she drank it now, she would recover enough to keep fighting, but that would be the limit. A third potion in such a short time would overload her system. It would make her magic unstable or even burn her mana channels entirely. The thought of mana toxicity made her stomach twist.

Her eyes darted back to the fight. Bhel was a whirlwind of brute strength, his axes drenched in black goblin blood. Josh was charging through the enemy line like a battering ram despite a fresh gash across his shoulder. She had patched a handful of scrapes and cuts, but nothing life-threatening had occurred yet. That could change in a heartbeat.

“Damn it,” she whispered, gripping the potion tighter.

Another arrow whistled toward Josh. Carcan reacted on instinct. She swung her staff and poured what mana she could spare into a quick barrier. The arrow hit, splintering harmlessly against the shimmer of light before vanishing into sparks.

Her knees trembled from the effort. She could feel the drain and the growing emptiness inside her chest that no amount of rest could fix right now. Around her, the forest glowed with firelight and chaos. She realised that this was not even the worst of it yet.

She forced herself upright, her voice sharp as she barked another warning. “Josh! Two on your right! Bhel, that one behind you is still alive!”

Her mana was running low. Her hands shook. But she refused to falter. If she gave in now, someone would die.

Josh slammed his shield forward once more. The impact cracked bone and sent another goblin tumbling lifeless to the dirt. He barely had time to breathe before the press in front of him parted. One of the orcs had forced its way through, its bulk towering above the sea of green bodies. The other orc lagged behind, tangled or slowed by something unseen in the fog.

With a growl building in his chest, Josh braced his boots against the blood-slick ground and charged. The orc met him head-on, bellowing, both combatants unwilling to give an inch.

The collision was brutal. Shield met muscle and iron with a sound like a hammer striking an anvil. The shock rippled up Josh’s arm, and his shoulder screamed in protest as the impact drove the breath from his lungs. The orc staggered a half-step but did not fall. Its tusked snarl was inches from his face, and its hot breath stank of rot and smoke.

For a moment, it was raw strength: man against monster, will against weight. Josh dug his heels into the mud, his teeth gritted. He felt the strain in every muscle as the two locked together like clashing titans.

Then Josh’s shield met the orc’s cleaver mid-swing. The impact exploded through his arm like a bolt of lightning. He staggered, his boots sliding back through the mud, but he did not fall. The orc’s red eyes flared. It bellowed, bringing its weapon around again in a wide, brutal arc. Josh ducked under the swing and drove his sword up, stabbing deep into the creature’s side. The blade cut through burned flesh, and hot, black blood sprayed across his arm.

The orc roared in fury, not pain. It tried to grab him, its massive fingers closing around the rim of his shield. Josh yanked back, twisting his body and kicking out with his boot. He slammed the edge of his steel-lined greave into the orc’s knee. The creature dropped slightly, enough for Josh to rip his sword free and thrust again. He aimed higher this time, toward its ribs.

Behind him, Bhel’s axe split another goblin in two. The dwarf was closing the distance fast. “Keep it busy!” he yelled.

“I’m bloody trying!” Josh shouted through clenched teeth. He blocked another blow that sent a fresh jolt up his arm. The metal of his shield warped under the strain, the edge dented where the cleaver struck.

He shoved forward with everything he had. He felt the static thrumming beneath his skin, a restless energy building in his muscles. He forced the orc back a step. Its massive chest heaved, and its breath came out in snarls that stank of blood and smoke.

He had to hold out until Bhel reached them. He had to be the wall.
