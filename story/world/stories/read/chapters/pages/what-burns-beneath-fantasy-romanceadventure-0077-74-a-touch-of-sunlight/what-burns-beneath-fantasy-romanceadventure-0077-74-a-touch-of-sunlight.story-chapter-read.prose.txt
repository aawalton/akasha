Fire tore through my chest. A scream ripped its way up my throat only to be choked away by a flood of burning copper. My vision narrowed to a handsome, startled visage, then to the red of blazing agony, then to merciful black.

Dark. Pressing, endless dark. Breaths too slow. Limbs too heavy. Sinking. Sinking. Sinking.

“This.” There was a voice in the blackness. One I had heard before.

“Vow.” Distant and strong, like the rumble of an oncoming storm.

“Did.” Its echo thrummed against my bones.

“Brin. Brin.”

The world came back in pieces. Wet, ragged breaths. A haze of gray dust in the air. Silver light licking along my skin. My fingers iron-tight around… something. Something that blazed with fierce heat, tethering me to consciousness even as my skin hummed with the memory of pain.

Large, strong hands on my shoulders. A velvet voice saying my name in increasingly firm tones.

“Mm… wha…” I blinked. Even that felt sluggish.

“There you are.” Cool fingers clasped my chin, tilting my face up.

Too close.

“Can you move?”

He’s too close.

“Don’t…” My tongue swam in sticky heat. “Touch…” Warmth slid from my lips. “Me.”

Silence. Then, with deliberate slowness, his grip tightened.

I felt every fragment of that pressure. Part of me went numb with fear.

And part of me knew exactly what was in my hands.

I swung.

And was immediately shoved backwards. My head thumped against cloth and squishy wool.

“I’ll take that as a ‘yes.’” He plucked the shard away, sounding so unconcerned, so amused, that it made my blood boil. “Stop trying to kill me.”

“Never,” I gasped, watching the ceiling spin.

His laughter made the quilt tremble. “As you wish. And well done.” One pale hand held the sunlit steel up and he examined it hungrily. It was larger than before. Two separate pieces of a divine sword now joined, forming a jagged, thin wedge of fiery gold. It was nearly the length of his hand. “Truly, mortal, you’re just full of surprises. I hadn’t even thought this possible.”

I didn’t answer, other than to press one hand to my head and moan.

“Poor thing.” Veldian’s eyes glittered as they fell to me, and the hungry look did not fade. Dark haired swayed as he tilted his head to one side. “Although… mm. If it weren’t for all the blood, I’d quite like you in this position.”

Ice flooded my insides and I twisted, getting an elbow beneath me. This made the world spin anew, and nausea hit alongside the realization that my gown was bunched up around my thighs. I clutched the fabric, yanking it down as he chuckled. His laughter died a moment later when my shoulders heaved and fresh, hot crimson spilled down the front of my bodice.

“You said,” he tossed the shard to the table, then fixed me with a stern look, “magic makes you faint or fall ill. Not nearly die. How have you survived all these years?”

I found myself asking that very same question. It’s never been that bad. Never. I’ve fainted, I’ve gotten sick, but… ashes, that hurt. That hurt.

My throat felt like it was coated in mud. “It’s… n-not… usually…” I shook my head, then whimpered as that motion sent me into a fresh, dizzy spiral.

Three rhythmic, heart-stopping thumps and a tentative baritone rumbled from the door. “Brin? Are you awake?”

I froze, gaze settling on the Fae’s in wordless panic.

Silence. Then another two thumps and an apologetic, “Brin? Sorry, I know you haven’t been sleeping well. The pri… er, Renner says we need to get moving soon. There’s breakfast downstairs.”

“We’re not quite done.” Velidan appeared entirely unconcerned by the intrusion.

I dragged a hand to the headboard, clambering upright and fighting a fresh wave of nausea. “W-what?”

“We’re not finished. Dismiss him.”

Another knock. “Brin?”

“Y-yes.” I straightened, forcing some semblance of feigned, frantic calm into my answer. “Yes, sorry, I’m awake. I’m… I’ll be… down s-soon.”

I waited, breath caught in my throat, for his footsteps to trail away. They did not.

Royce’s voice held a pained note. “Look, I… I feel really bad about last night.”

“Royce-”

“I shouldn’t have yelled at you.” The door rattled, as though a huge frame had just slumped against it. “Saints know you have enough to worry about without me piling on. Salt, even without… I shouldn’t have yelled. I’m really sorry.”

My vision grew blurry. I blinked back tears, all-too-aware of the questioning frown being directed down at me, and choked out, “You were upset. It’s okay.”

He grumbled for a moment. “It’s not, though. Salt, Brin, I acted like… hey, can I come in? The, uh, prostrating thing is easier face-to-face.”

An exhausted laugh bubbled up alongside fresh panic, and they came out together in something like a sob as the figure before me rolled his eyes skyward. “N-no. I’m not… decent.”

“Okay. Prostrating through the door it is, then.”

“Royce, you don’t need to… I’ll come out s-soon. You should go have breakfast.”

“Nah, I already ate. Look, I did a lot of thinking last night. I’ve got a whole speech ready. It’s pretty good.”

Velidan’s brows inched higher. The look on his face could only be described as incredulity. “For dawn’s sake, Brin, get rid of him or I will.”

“Royce, please-”

“I’ve kinda had this picture in my mind for… well, almost forever, I think. Of you and me.” His voice turned wry. “I’ve always thought we’d… I don’t know. Well, no, you probably do know.”

“I do,” my voice cracked, “I do, but, ashes, we can talk about it later. Please.”

“What, with an audience? Really don’t want that, Brin. This is hard enough without two people staring at me.”

The mattress shifted as Velidan rose, now looking thoroughly annoyed. He took a step towards the door.

“No,” I gasped, crawling forwards to catch the green silk of one sleeve. Spots danced across my vision and tousled hair pooled down around my arms. “Don’t, please.”

Royce sighed again. “You know, ever since we were kids I imagined we’d grow up and… wait, this isn’t part of the speech. Sorry. Are you almost dressed?”

Hands as cold and strong as steel pushed me back onto the bed. “Be silent and I won’t hurt him.”

I didn’t believe that for a moment. Velidan stepped to the door and I clambered off the bed, ready to throw myself at him- for whatever that would accomplish. Pressing black clouded the edges of my vision and I stumbled sideways, world spinning and a scream rising in my throat. But if I screamed, other people would come running up the stairs, and it would be worse-

One pale hand closed over the knob and yanked. Royce had indeed been leaning against the door, and the motion sent him toppling in with a grunt.

“What-” blue eyes widened to the size of saucers, landing first on the Fae and then on me.

I lunged. Not towards the monster; every ounce of my strength wouldn’t even slow him down. Not towards the shard; it was too far away.

Towards the window.

Waves of dizzying black sent me to the ground, but I seized the drapes like a dying breath on my way down. Pink light flooded the floorboards beneath me as a series of hideously fast sounds came from behind.

The sharp crack of a door slamming shut. Sizzling skin and the sickening smell of burnt flesh alongside a pained groan and panicked, muffled cries. Stifled thumps, like fists beating against dense cloth.

Then… chilling quiet. It closed around my very heart.

A silver glow lit the edges of my vision, casting the shadows of two figures standing almost intertwined onto the wall in front of me.

Standing. He’s still standing. He’s alive.

“Royce, run, you have to-” I clawed at the bedframe, trying to right myself amidst a fresh swell of nausea. More heat dribbled down my chin.

An impossibly soothing voice snaked through the room. Thick with pain, but settling to velvet and smoke. “That’s it, mortal. No, don’t look at her. Look at me. You’re perfectly safe. Calm yourself and be silent. Was anyone else in the hallway? Answer honestly.”

Silence. Aching, choking silence. Then the slow rustle of hair and fabric against wood.

“Good. Be still.” Velidan’s tone faltered with a groan. More light flickered, and his voice settled back into its hypnotic purr. “Go fix the curtain.”

I made it to my knees as Royce drew near. Jaw slack, eyes wide and glassy. His chest rose and fell in a heavy rhythm. He looked down at me, mouth moving with what seemed to be a strained attempt at speech, but said nothing and stooped to collect the fallen drapes. His movements were sluggish. As if he was in a daze.

No. Not a daze. As if he’s in a dream.

I remembered that feeling- or at least, the beginning of it. The numbing haze of the Fae’s glamour. The trusting complacency, the foggy calm. The slipping absence of my own will.

“R-Royce?” Tears spilled down my cheeks.

“Is behaving himself very well, unlike some people.” Velidan stood almost flattened against a wall, just out of the window’s glow. One forearm and the side of his face were a violent shade of scorched red, though quickly fading back to their usual ivory. Furious, dark eyes fixed on me. “That hurt, Brin. And you’re still bleeding. Get back in bed.”

Triumph surged through my veins at the sight of him. So imposing, so frightening… and trapped by a few beams of sunlight. He’s hurt. I hurt him. He really can’t touch the light. Or he can, but only for a moment. No sooner had the realization dawned than a shadow fell, and with it fresh horror. Royce had affixed one drape and was starting to raise the second.

“No!” I grabbed his arm. It was like trying to pull down a tree branch. “Royce, stop! You need to leave!”

“Ignore her.”

I seized the heavy curtain with both hands, looking into bright blue eyes that were just screaming. Fresh, wracking sobs shook my shoulders. “Stop, please, just let him leave, don’t hurt him-”

“I haven’t,” Velidan snarled. “If you start wailing, that’s going to change.”

“You s-said-”

“I said a dozen humans barging in will end in bloodshed. I meant it. One is manageable.” Gloom settled as the cloth was hefted into place, and a moment later his furious visage loomed over me. “Now get back in bed.”
