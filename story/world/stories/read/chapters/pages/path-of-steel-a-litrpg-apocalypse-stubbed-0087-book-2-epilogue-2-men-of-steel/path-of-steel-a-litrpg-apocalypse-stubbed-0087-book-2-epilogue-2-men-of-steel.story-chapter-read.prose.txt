Gary walked into Bayonet Gym for what was probably the last time. Cheers and celebrations still echoed behind him. The army had clinched a desperate victory. They were still alive. They had cause to celebrate, and Gary most of all.

He’d rejoin them soon, but for now, he needed to spent the massive amount of merit points he possessed.

Victory!

Congratulations, climber. Thanks to you and everyone else’s efforts, but especially yours, we have expelled the horrors from our holy land of gains. For your contribution to the last mission, as well as the overall war objective, you have been awarded an extra 50,000 merit points.

For your participation in the defeat of the Thousand-Face Puppeteer, you have been awarded half its bounty, which is an extra 500,000 merit points.

Gary still couldn’t wrap his head around it. Just one mission ago, he’d had to borrow merit points from Feng Shen to buy a two-thousand cost piece of ore. Now…

Merit Points: 701,897

“My points are starting to look like my phone number,” he muttered.

“What’s that?” Goldie asked.

“A ten-digit code people could use to reach me remotely. Like my climber name.”

“That’s so silly. Why use numbers instead of names?”

“We gave names to our contacts, but we needed the anonymity of numbers so you could reach people you weren’t— You know what? Yeah. Earth is weird. Let’s stick to that.”

“Okay!”

Gary eyed the reward board. He took a deep breath. “System, give me—”

“Who’s out there?”

Gary paused as a small man stumbled out of the office. He was tiny—wizened, shrunken. Nothing like the old but sharp man Gary remembered. However, as soon as Gary saw this man, he immediately rushed over.

“Coach, please. Don’t walk. Let me help you.”

“I’m not dead yet, little bull,” Coach Bayonet said, coughing into his hand. The other hand shivered, briefly left alone at the handle of his cane. Gary grabbed the coach and gently led him back into the office despite his complaints. He sat him down into the couch, which had been made like a bed.

“Back in my day, we respected our elders. I’d punch my coach before treating him like this,” Coach Bayonet said.

“You heard Satan, Coach. You need rest.”

“Bah. I should have been dead like my brothers. I’ll rest when the time comes.”

“Come on, Coach, don’t say that. Everybody’s so glad that you’re alive. You saved them.”

“Then how come nobody’s here?”

Gary didn’t know what to reply to that. The three coaches had sacrificed themselves to hold back the army long enough for everyone else to escape. Coach Bayonet was the only survivor, but the exhaustion had turned him into a tiny old man.

“You looked like you needed rest, Coach. The others went outside to give you some peace and quiet,” Gary replied.

Coach Bayonet chuckled. “My body’s done for, boy, but not my mind. Do you think I’m senile? Of course nobody wants to celebrate with a crippled old man like myself, in a small office that smells like my urine.”

“Coach…”

“Leave me alone, kiddo. I don’t blame you. How could you live your youth and climb while taking care of me? I did the right thing, and now you’re doing the right thing. Just leave me alone. I want to rest.”

Gary sighed.

“Tell him he’s the best,” Goldie whispered in his mind.

“You’re the best, Coach,” Gary said.

“I know. Now get out of my office!”

“Hey, boss, you here?” a voice came from outside.

“Over here,” Gary replied.

Osteo’s head popped into the open office door. “Hey Coach. Hey boss. We’re getting ready for the banquet, will you come?”

“Yeah, just a sec,” Gary replied.

“I came in your mother,” the coach said.

Osteo’s eyes widened. “I’m your son!?”

The coach rolled his eyes. “Get this imbecile out of here,” he told Gary.

“Can I talk to you for a second?” Gary said, taking Osteo aside.

“I can’t believe the coach was my father the whole time,” Osteo said.

“Yeah. Listen, Osteo, I want to say that I’m really fucking glad we fought together. You and the boys were all absolute pleasures. You won’t despawn when I’m gone, right?”

“No, boss. We exist.”

“Perfect. I want you to take care of the coach from now on, okay? He deserves it. Make sure he’s safe and happy, but not to the detriment of your own happiness.”

“You got it, boss.”

“I’m also leaving you the clown.”

“Please no. Anything but the clown.”

“Well, I sure as shit aren’t taking him along,” Gary said. “You can cut him loose in the forest or something. I’ll leave standing orders for him to not harm anyone except in self-defense.”

“As long as I don’t have to be friends with him, boss.”

“You don’t. And, Osteo? Thank you. For everything. You and the guys were the best comrades I could have asked for. I’ll make sure to tell them all.”

“No need for that, boss.”

“Why?”

“Because I had them wait behind the door,” Osteo replied with a wide smile. The door swung open to reveal all of Gary’s hired beef boys, who stormed in and grabbed him in a massive group hug.

“We love you, boss!”

“Thank you for everything!”

“Reach the top for us!”

“One of us! One of us!”

Drowning in their chant, Gary couldn’t respond.

“Hey, is the boss crying?” someone asked.

“Of course not, you idiot. We just squeezed him too hard.”

“Shit! Sorry, boss! Here, I’ll pull your arms to unsqueeze you.”

Gary laughed as he dragged them back in. “Come here, you lovable oafs,” he said, then chanted louder than them. “One of us! One of us!”

It was only after they left that Gary settled down. He felt warm. Tender. “You really liked them, huh?” Goldie asked.

“They weren’t trying to kill me,” Gary said. “I didn’t think that would ever be my highest standard, but here we are.”

“Don’t lie to me, Gary. I can see your soul.”

Gary smiled. He glanced where Coach Bayonet lay on his couch, snoring loudly, then turned back to the merit board. He had seven hundred thousand merit points to spend, but thankfully, it was a no-brainer.

“Do you even know what that lifetime pass does?” Goldie asked.

“No,” Gary replied, “but I bet it’s going to be worth every penny.”

REWARDS

Flying Fools Card: 10,000 merit points

Helm of Mind Shut: 50,000 merit points

Lifetime VIP Satan Pass: 200,000 merit points

Talent Serum: 500,000 merit points

There were more, but these were the entries Gary found most tempting. The rewards costing less than ten thousand, like the Satan Shakes, had been erased off the board. Gary assumed it was to avoid running out with the whole end-of-war inflation. He didn’t mind. Of the remaining dozen or so rewards, he knew exactly what he wanted.

“System, give me a talent serum, and a lifetime VIP Satan Pass.”
