The first person to learn of the removal of our restrictions was Chien of course. He'd been busy with me, and while I knew he'd been grinding his teeth at the restrictions, he had abided by them since I'd asked it.

“We're go,” I said simply as I entered his office, he had several now.

“Now?” he asked, looking up sharply.

“This moment.”“Excellent.” With that he rose, moving to a cabinet and pulling out his own stack of files. “I've had people on standby for months now, and a few projects already ongoing. Some of the things here don't quite make sense though,” he pointed out.

“I know, but it's fine, guesses on timelines?”“For the first ones we're mostly just waiting on the cores, once they're up we'll have the prototypes ready to go in a few days. From there it depends on how things look. Let's separate out who is doing what parts,” he said, passing me over a few sheets.

“Good to hear,” I said looking over them.

“And Isha?”“Asleep,” I explained, though didn't go into it deeper, she was in a good mood, and that was all that needed to get out about it for now.

Over the next few hours we began, people who needed to start production were alerted, and the two of us together began to make new magic items ourselves. I was faster than Chien at that, cranking out ready components at an insane rate compared to anyone else who'd learned the technique. For a bit I considered why that might be but it was clear enough to me soon enough.

When it came to the cores I knew them better than anyone, I'd designed them from the ground up, each and every connection seared into my mind. Perhaps one day someone would come along who understood the underlying architecture in some way I didn't, some insight I'd missed, but for now I reigned supreme on that understanding. My magic was based on understanding, on knowledge, so it should be as easy as breathing to me.

Well, there was one other person who may be able to make them faster, as her understanding in an intuitive way was in some ways more intimate than my own, but she hadn't made any magic items in an age. At the beginning of the use of them she'd outpaced me, but now I was faster than she'd been. Would Isha be faster than me if she tried again? I didn't know.

Regardless, the shells had been pre-built, with only a few bits here and there that needed finishing, and with those done, the first set of new things were rolling out within a day. There was a backlog of more common items that people needed, smaller things, things that were fairly simple but helped the world go round. Pumps, lifting tools, heaters, and coolers for all and sundry. Those things seemed simple compared to what else we were making, but they were needed no less for being simple. In some ways they were more important than my desire to see what the world held.

Though there were few, very few, of us who could make magical items the desire for them had been near limitless. Proper magic users weren't always common, sure, people had them, but a small village might only have one or two my own home village had only had three until Isha and I came along. This meant that depending on what they had, certain items could fill critical holes in their infrastructure. In larger places this was less critical, but still a potent thing.

There were some downsides to making a magical item, primarily being that it required complete concentration. We always did it in a safe place, but still. This mattered because about eight hours into the mass of work I came to and upon looking up received a fruit to the side of my head, it wasn't hard or heavy, but still.

“Eat,” my wife demanded. “You also need to sleep, when was the last time you did?”“Well, we've got a lot of requests to catch up with, and...”“...”“Though I suppose I could take a small break. I see you've woken up.”“To my childish husband gone and burying himself in work again.”

“I'm not childish,” I objected.

“I've known you since you were a child and you've barely changed Justin, or should I go about recounting some of those memories to you?” That was a real threat, since we both knew we could both remember each one like watching a movie.

“Okay... well I was a mature child at least. Even if I've not changed too much...”“Hardly the point, now, you, me, food.”

With a smile I got up and moved to join her. Apparently she still wanted all of my attention, and I was weak, I'd hardly been able to deny her anything she wanted.

“It is indeed a good time for a break, did you have something in mind?” I asked.

“I don't know, you pick what you want and we'll figure it out.”

With a smile I pulled the fruit she'd brought to my hand and took a bite. “Fine then, let's start with this, and maybe head to the kitchens for something. Not sure what they're cooking today, but whatever is there will do for me.”

Perhaps I hadn't been planning to stop at this point, but it was as good a time as any. Most of what I needed to do for the moment could wait. We had a few basic things to test the next day, a simple radio-esque enchantment, along with a floating one and the camera. All of it was very, very simplistic so far as those things went, but it would give us some idea.

“Attention, right,” I said, looking down at my wife as we walked, realizing she'd turned to stare at me with a furrowed brow.

“Good, you're learning.”“I'm always learning.”“Not all the things you should.”
