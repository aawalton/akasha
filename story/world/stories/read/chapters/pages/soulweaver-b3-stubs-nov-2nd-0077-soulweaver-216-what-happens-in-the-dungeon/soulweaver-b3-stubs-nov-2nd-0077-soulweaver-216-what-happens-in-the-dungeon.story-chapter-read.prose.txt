After our ‘dinner’, which both had cooled enough and was at the right hour to pass for breakfast—especially with the dawn that broke just as we finished—we all collectively decided a day of rest was called for after recent events. There was a Cataclysm on, and yes, people were dying every hour it existed, but burning ourselves out served no one.

Worse, going into an extended battle—more like a small war—with exhausted minds was liable to get us all killed. Besides which, Yashas needed some time to revise his assault strategy now that we had 5 Champion-level beings excluding Galia. After relaxing a bit first. Even ancient warriors needed their rest.

“I feel you should feed her as fast as possible,” Yashas said to me as we sat in the rotemburo—the name for the large communal bath Aerion and I had enjoyed privately earlier. Currently occupied by Richard, Yashas, and me.

All of us fully naked, of course, as was only proper in a Japanese bath like this. And what a bath it was. Deep enough to cover everything up to our necks, and so hot I had to take breaks, even with the stat-boosting rings I was still wearing.

“Gotta agree with the General there, Greg,” Richard said. ‘The General’ was Yashas’ new nickname, and one that had caught on pretty quickly. “If you can get her big enough, we might all just ride her to their base. Drop some ordnance on their heads and be done with it!”

I laughed. “Yeah, why don’t we just nuke ‘em from orbit in the process while we’re at it? Save ourselves the trip entirely.”

Richard paled. “They… they can do that in the future?”

I grimaced. “Yeah… Yeah they can. But I don’t know if Galia’s ever gonna grow that big. She’s a phoenix, not a dragon, and they’re not exactly known to be huge. I won’t know how much weight she can carry until she’s grown, but I wouldn’t bet on any more than me. And Aerion, but only because she weighs nothing. Which I suppose would apply to you too, Richard?”

Richard shrugged. “Being absurdly light has its downsides, mate. Ones you’d never consider. Just not as strong as you lot.”

Ah, right. Richard didn’t have the stats Aerion did to make up for her weight and frailer frame.

“Still, the point is noted,” I said. “Don’t worry, we’ve been gorging her on as much as we feel is safe. Spoiling her outright, honestly, but we need her functional. That’s actually what Aerion’s doing on the roof right now.”

“Will the helicopter truly be alright?” Yashas asked with a raised brow.

“It’ll be fine. Galia’s a bit unruly, but she’s not that bad. Besides, none of us can even use that thing. It’s just dead weight unless you can unload the missiles without detonating them.”

Yashas shook his head. “I’m afraid my knowledge is lacking in that area. Even otherwise, I wouldn’t dare, should something go awry.”

“Yeah, thought so.”

Which meant we’d be going on foot. Or Humvee, rather. That would speed things up considerably, and we’d be traveling during the day to minimize the zombie threat.

That still left the military faction, but there wasn’t much we could do about that. We were about to assault their HQ, after all. We’d have to deal with them sooner or later.

That didn’t mean we couldn’t go in better prepped this time, though.

“I for one can’t wait to see that arsenal you said you’ve prepared,” Richard said, stretching his arms.

“Yes, well, it is but a pale shadow of the one I stole from. I could only fit so much in the Humvee, you see,” the General replied. “Even still, it should suffice. Had I known any of you were capable of firing one, I’d have kept one in the Humvee as insurance.”

I sheepishly looked away. “Not like I’m qualified,” I muttered.

The conversation turned to Earth and its wonders as Yashas tried to mine us for information on what life was like. I came away surprised by just how different things had gotten in the past fifty or so years. Richard got up before dawn, spent a good deal of time making breakfast with his wife for his family, before reading the newspaper and setting out for work.

No computers or internet or social media. Memes weren’t a thing and information could take days to propagate, even for the biggest things. He had a TV, of course, but even that was somewhat foreign to me. Cable programming had all but died out to online streaming platforms.

But none of that compared to Yashas’ own life. Of living without running sewage, of planning for months on end for battles, riding from town to town on horseback… fighting tens of thousands of enemies on an open battlefield.

Some of the stories he told made my blood chill, and it just drove home how much cleaner war had become lately. Hell, half the time, there were no humans fighting on the ground anymore. Just robots and machines with AI brains.

We traded stories like that for over an hour, and after soaking for far longer than we ought to have, we got out and showered off before trading with the two women, who used the bath next.

The guys migrated to the pool table where we killed more time, talking about all sorts of mundane topics, before Yashas threw out an idea.

“How about an outing for tonight after dinner?”

“Where?” Aerion asked. “It is not as if the streets are safe.”

“Not far,” Yashas assured us. “A nearby tower, but one that has the most magnificent bar you will ever lay eyes on, and a selection of liquors larger and more exquisite than this penthouse has to offer.”

I was sold at the mention of alcohol, which earned me squint eyes from Aerion, and after a bit of discussion, we decided to go for it. What was the point of being a Champion if we couldn’t deal with a few zombies?

For our little jaunt, we didn’t take the Humvee, though I did suit up in my Grace build. Between Yashas’ near-perfect awareness and our experience with the zombies’ daytime and nighttime perception zones, it was becoming almost easy to avoid them.

Even still, we left before dusk just to make our lives a bit easier. We’d be returning after dark of course, but we could at least make our lives easier on the way out.

The air was brisk but not cold, and the tower was a fifteen minute walk, made only a little slower by having to move aside and wait out the random shambler. For the first time since arriving in this dungeon, I felt like a tourist. A regular person going to visit something cool with my group of friends.

If I was honest? It felt good. Real good.

We made good time, arriving at the base of the tower within a half hour.

“There are a handful of shamblers in this tower, and unlike ours, this one has no security systems. Partly why I did not choose it,” Yashas said, though his expression said otherwise.

“And the real reason?” I asked.

Yashas smirked. “Well, the penthouse isn’t quite as nice.”

Thought so.

We entered the building through the broken glass door and caught the elevator, which whirred up to the 46th floor.

When we stepped out into the top floor of the bar, Richard whistled, Arianna frowned—she always frowned—and Aerion gasped. I admit, even my breath caught.

Neon blue lights mounted wavy vertical lines that spanned the height of both black-painted walls, which in turn ran three floors to the bottom level of the bar, where a number of couches and sofas had been placed. While extremely nice and with a definite vibe, that wasn’t what caught our eyes, though.

That honor went to the floor to ceiling-wall-to-wall glass window that backed up the wide bar counter that offered an absurd view of the Tokyo skyline responsible for the awe.

Not only was the view better than ours, with more towers and a view of the Tokyo Skytree in the distance, it was one of those walls that leaned outward as it got higher, giving the impression it was perched over the city.

Oh, and the bar that sat directly in front of it? That was nothing short of epic.

Hundreds of bottles sat out on lit glass shelves, looking like they glowed from within, and boy what a selection it was.

Once we took the spiral staircase down to the first floor and I began perusing the many bottles, I realized there wasn’t a single one that cost less than $500 here, and several in the thousands, or even the tens of thousands.

I didn’t dare guess what the drinks normally cost in a place like this.

“It’s an open bar!” Yashas announced, sweeping his arms as if he owned the place. “Name your drink, and I can likely make it. I promise to use only the most expensive ingredients.”

I winced at the thought of using $10,000 scotch in an Old Fashioned or an Extra Anejo in a Margarita, but hey… drinks were on the house. Or, the Dungeon in this case.

Between bartending that gave Cosmo a run for his money and the exquisite ingredients, it wasn’t long before lips loosened and we were all laughing, shouting, and slapping backs.

We migrated from the bar overlooking the view to one of the cozier low chairs set around a table, and it was there that we learned Arianna was a surprisingly flirty drunk and that Yashas was an absolute master player who was absolutely vicious in swooping in for the kill. I feel like his ancient appeal only added to his aura, and I could see how Arianna fell for him.

It didn’t help that Richard brought out a deck of cards, which of course, soon turned into a drinking game.

“Arianna! Your turn!” Yashas cried, putting a hand on her thigh. Which I thought was quite forward, but which she didn’t seem to mind one bit. “You must tell us!”

“Well…” Arianna began, looking bashfully at us… but mostly at Yashas. “My story isn’t nearly as gallant as commanding a charge into battle atop a horse…”

“Bah!” Yashas said, dismissing her concern. “It sounds more impressive than it is. I tell you, I was scared shitless! I just screamed harder so my men did not notice!”

Yashas burst out laughing, which Arianna mimicked, and soon we were all laughing at his stupid jokes.

“But enough about me,” the General said at length. “Now, tell us!”

“I was in the middle of a raid. We, uh… we bombed a building. Full of military personnel, mind you. I did not expect to make it out alive. We armed the detonators. But something went wrong. I went out to investigate and… oh lord,” she said, her voice suddenly full of terror. “How... did I not notice?”

“What, Arianna?” Yashas asked. “What is it?”

“The cold,” Arianna muttered, hugging herself. “In my chest. I… I think I was shot.”

Oh, shit.

“No. Nonono. How could I not have—I mean I—I… forgot?” Arianna asked, her face screwed up in agony as tears streamed down her cheeks.

“Figures. Vigor must've erased the memory.” My alcohol-addled brain looked around, trying to figure out which idiot had blurted out that hot take, but the looks of horror and disgust directed my way revealed the terrible truth.

Yep, that was me.

Fuck.

“Uh, sorry,” I said. “A little drunk right now, haha.”

Luckily, so was everyone else, and they all went back to consoling Arianna. Which really just meant Yashas making her more drinks until she got so drunk she forgot about that little detail entirely.

“Aw!” Richard slurred, pointing at my chest. “Your turn, Grug! You gotta tell ush how you got your ass summoned, mate!”

“That’s not my name goddammit!” I swore, slurring equally as much. “But fine. I, uh… walked into a bar. Shitty day at work. Y’know… Yeah well, turned out I’d been fired. Turned out the bar I walked into wasn’t any old bar, but Cosmo’s. Order’s. You know what I mean. Fucking dick narrated my life story to a fat biker… who was just another him, apparently.”

“Bloody ludicrous!”

“Your god’s got a screw loose! Or ten! Or… or twenty!” Arianna shouted, more boisterous than ever. Had I been sober, I'd have noticed the undertone of desperation in her voice.

“Uh huh. Get this, I try to leave… and I get shot out into outer space! Fucking suffocated!”

Arianna, Richard, and Aerion all laughed at my expense, while Yashas frowned.

“What is this… outer space, exactly?” he asked. “I came across it in some of your books, and I see its mention in several places, but the concept eludes me.”

The bar went deathly silent… and then we all burst out laughing at Yashas.

“Got ourselves a flat-earther here mate!” Richard shouted. Which of course, would’ve been wholly inappropriate were we sober.

But we weren’t, and so we spent the next hour incoherently trying to explain the concept of space to Yashas, all of us talking over each other. Even Aerion joined in, sounding like she was some expert.

I don’t remember a ton of what happened after, but at some point, I found myself madly making out with a half-stripped Aerion who was sprawled over a table covered in broken glass and the remains of half-empty drinks.

As was Yashas in another corner, doing pretty much the same with Arianna.

As for Richard? Well, he’d passed out on the floor before any of that madness began. Good thing too.

The old man wouldn’t get it.
