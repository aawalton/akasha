Gilbert made it to the infirmary in worse shape than he had made it to the cell. The weakness was real now. The blood from his nose was in no hurry to stop, and the core beneath his skin had begun to beat faster, as if it had decided to take advantage of his body’s broken rhythm. The mage tried not to focus on what his body was doing, and that was exactly why he kept studying everything around him, taking in the doors, the locks, and the guards all over again. It was not only useful, it also distracted him from how terrible he felt, because at times it seemed like he was about to pass out.

The infirmary turned out to be a long room with three tables, cabinets along the walls, and two narrow cots behind a screen. Inside, it was far too bright after the corridors.

“Who did they drag in now?” the healer asked without even turning around.

This thin man in his fifties with a short beard stood at the far table, wrapping someone’s arm.

“New one from intake. Nosebleed,” answered the runic warden who had been opening the doors along the way.

“After interrogation?” the man asked without turning around.

“What do you think?” said the second guard, the same one who had been holding Gilbert by the arm. His voice was more insolent.

“I think if you keep trying this hard, pretty soon there’ll be no one left to interrogate except the dead. And I’m a healer, not a priest.”

“He’s alive.”

“Sure. Until the next interrogation.”

The healer finally turned around. Gilbert let the guard sit him down on the edge of the empty cot. The healer came over, took Gilbert by the chin, and gave his face a quick examination.

“Keep your head up.”

He pinched the bridge of his nose, then glanced at the blood-soaked shirt.

“Sit him straight. And get your hand off him. Don’t press on his shoulder.”

“He can barely stay up.”

“Then hold him up. Might as well get some use out of the guard,” the healer muttered irritably.

Someone coughed quietly behind the screen, but Gilbert did not turn his head. He could not have done it anyway, not with the healer gripping him like that. He could hear other guards talking in low voices behind the screen. One of them kept shifting from foot to foot, and even that gave away how impatient he was.

“All right, up.”

“Already?” a young voice asked.

It sounded tired and lost, like someone still trying to understand where exactly his life had gone wrong.

“You’ve rested long enough. The exam is over.”

“But the healer said I still should...”

“I said get up.”

The healer threw back over his shoulder in annoyance, “I said don’t yank him around. Or do you want to drag him back here again in half an hour?”

The guard muttered something under his breath, but apparently loosened his grip. Then the screen shifted. Sitting on the other cot was a gaunt young man in a gray prison shirt. Very thin, with an exhausted face, and clearly not yet adapted to prison conditions. Old marks from restraints showed on his wrists. Two guards stood beside him, so much larger and broader than he was that it already looked like more than enough to control him. One of them took the young man by the shoulder.

“Come on, Lir. Move.”

Gilbert did not turn his head, but something inside him came to a sudden stop when he heard that name. Lir. The name matched what Sakura had told him about the boy’s appearance. He did not look anything like a dangerous criminal. He really was just a fisherman’s son who had ended up in the wrong place at the wrong time.

“I can walk by myself,” Lir said quietly.

“Then walk.”

“I just... if I move too fast, my head spins,” he said, and the embarrassment in his voice was obvious.

“If you fall again, I’ll drag you by the hair.”

“Say that one more time,” the healer cut in sharply, “and I’ll write down that you’re interfering with keeping a valuable prisoner in one piece. Then we’ll see which one of us still thinks this is funny.”

The guard let out an angry breath, but kept quiet.

Gilbert allowed himself one brief glance in Lir’s direction. Not a long one and not too intent, so it would not give him away. But now Gilbert was certain that Lir was alive, that he was here, and that from time to time they brought him to the infirmary. The infirmary could be the first connecting link for him.

Lir was taken away almost at once. One guard walked ahead, the second stayed at his side, no longer touching him unless he had to after the healer’s warning. Lir himself walked slowly, limping on his right leg. At the door he hesitated for a second, as if he wanted to glance back at the healer or say something else, but the guard gave him a short shove.

“Move.”

“I am moving,” Lir answered quietly.

Gilbert lowered his eyes before the boy could notice his interest. Lir did not know him, of course. And right now they were both in the wrong place and under the wrong conditions for Gilbert to reveal himself to Lir and warn him about the escape plan. It was still too early, and far too unreliable.

The door closed. For a few seconds footsteps still sounded beyond it, then the voices in the corridor faded. From the direction of those steps, Gilbert roughly mapped the route and realized they were taking Lir down a staircase. He remembered that. Based on the new information he had just gotten, Lir was indeed not being held in the intake block, not near the ordinary prisoners. But they did still bring him to the infirmary.

“And stop turning your head,” the healer said, this time to Gilbert. “I’m not holding your nose for decoration.”

Gilbert obediently went still. The healer worked quickly and irritably. He looked like a man who had been doing this longer than he cared to remember, and who was very tired of patching up criminals.

The healer wiped the blood from Gilbert’s lips and chin, then pinched the bridge of his nose harder than was pleasant, shoved a clean cloth with a sharp herbal smell under it, and told him to breathe through his mouth. After that, he checked his pupils, pressed fingers to his neck, and frowned.

“His pulse is a mess.”

“That happens after interrogation,” the insolent guard said.

“After interrogation, all kinds of things happen around you except good sense.”

The runic warden gave a quiet snort and quickly pretended it was a cough. Gilbert said nothing. The core beneath his skin was beating unevenly, and the healer seemed to have sensed at least part of that. He did not understand it, but he had clearly noticed that something in Gilbert’s body was seriously wrong. Still, he was unlikely to go any farther after such a surface-level examination. A pulse that was too fast and out of rhythm after questioning, weakness, cold sweat, and trembling could all be explained by pain, blood loss, and exhaustion. To guess that there was another magical core sewn beneath the skin near Gilbert’s collarbone, the healer would have had to know such a thing was even possible. That definitely was not part of an ordinary prison healer’s experience.

“Can he walk?” the runic warden asked.

“Let him sit a little longer,” the healer answered dryly.

“We were ordered to return him to the intake block.”

“You’ll return him in ten minutes,” the healer cut in.

The guards exchanged glances. They clearly did not want to argue with the healer. Judging by everything, this grumbling old man had some influence here, probably the paper kind.

Gilbert sat on the edge of the cot with the cloth under his nose and listened. The infirmary held more life than it had seemed to at first. Glass vials clinked softly in the cabinet behind the healer. Water was dripping somewhere. Someone passed beyond the far door without entering. The healer carried no keys on him. But Gilbert noticed another useful detail. On the wall beside the healer’s table hung a bundle of small metal plates marked with symbols, most likely tags for medicine or records. Another useful detail. It might matter later.

The healer took the cloth away from Gilbert’s face, checked whether the bleeding had started again, and looked displeased, though not surprised.

“In the cell, make sure he doesn’t lower his head. If it starts again, bring him here instead of waiting until he floods the whole floor.”

“Maybe we should bring him a pillow too,” the insolent guard muttered.

The healer looked at him without expression.

“You can.”

The guard clenched his jaw angrily, but kept silent. A few minutes later they hauled Gilbert back up. The weakness swayed through him again, but this time he was ready for it and kept his face almost motionless. On the way back, the guards handled him a little more carefully than they had on the way there, which meant they had, in fact, listened to the healer.

On the walk back, Gilbert fixed the staircase in his memory, the one they had taken Lir down. It was the only one near the infirmary, which narrowed things considerably. Gilbert almost did not doubt anymore that Lir had been taken that way.

Gilbert now had far more useful leads. Lir’s approximate location. The fact that the infirmary was a connecting link between the lower level and the intake block. The fact that the healer could keep prisoners there for some amount of time. The fact that the guards were wary of the healer, while the runic warden still handled his work lazily and mechanically. Gilbert had also memorized the placement of the runes, the guard posts, and every door with a runic lock on it. Fortunately, there were still very few of those. For the most part, they were the main doors, the kind escaped prisoners would try to use if they were attempting to break out. Which meant that to get out, he would need a runic mage again. Their power was not blocked the way the power of ordinary mages like Gilbert was.

Gilbert had been back in the cell for less than half an hour before the locks started opening again. Then the door swung wide and another prisoner was shoved inside.

The man was large and broad-shouldered, with a split brow. Gilbert immediately felt the aggression and danger coming off him. The danger, however, was not the dark sort worth paying attention to. It was more performative and cheap. There was also very little intelligence in his eyes. Aggression without intelligence was an awful combination. He wore the same gray prison shirt, only it fit him worse. The fabric stretched tight over his shoulders and chest, and the sleeves barely reached his wrists.

“Sit quiet,” the warden said.

“Yeah,” the prisoner muttered.

The door shut. The locks clicked again, and the guard’s footsteps faded away.

Gilbert sat on the cot and stared silently at the floor. The new cellmate took a couple of seconds to look around the cell, then turned his eyes on him, on the bloodstained shirt, and on the split lip. Naturally, the larger prisoner came to the simplest conclusion available to him. Weak.

Gilbert could practically see that word form inside the man’s head.

“Move,” the prisoner said roughly, already feeling his power in this new arrangement.

Gilbert did not lift his eyes.

“No.”

The man blinked in surprise. That was not what he had expected to hear.

“What did you say?”

“You heard me.”

“This is my cot.”

“I’m sitting here. So it isn’t.”

The prisoner gave a short huff and stepped closer.

“Listen, smartass. I don’t know who you are, but right now you’re going to get up and sit on the floor. Got it?”

Gilbert finally looked up at him. He did it slowly, lazily, and on purpose.

“I just came back from interrogation,” he said calmly. “My head hurts, my nose is broken, and I have very little patience left. Think it over.”

Of course Gilbert’s words had no effect on the prisoner except to annoy him even more. The big man lunged forward and grabbed Gilbert by the front of the shirt, clearly meaning to yank him up. That was where it ended.

Gilbert let himself be pulled for a fraction of a second, then slipped to the side, caught the man’s hand with both of his, and bent the thumb in a direction the joint should never go. At the same time he drove his heel into the inside of the prisoner’s knee. The man grunted and dropped to one knee.

Gilbert was already beside him. With one hand he kept hold of the thumb. With the other he pinned the man’s wrist to the stone wall and twisted it just a little more. Something cracked, but nothing had broken yet. Not yet.

The prisoner hissed in pain.

“Quiet,” Gilbert said into his ear. “If you call the guards, I’ll have to do much worse. Bad enough that it’ll take you a month to get back on your feet.”

The man went still. Gilbert leaned in closer. His voice stayed low and almost tired.

“Now you’re going to listen to me very carefully. I’m not fighting you over a cot. I’m not interested. You sleep by the door, you don’t touch me, you don’t ask questions, and you don’t keep me from thinking. If you do that, you get to keep all your fingers and maybe even a little pride.”

The prisoner was breathing hard. His face had gone red, but now Gilbert could see what he needed in the man’s eyes.

Understanding.

“Understood?” Gilbert asked.

The man gave a sharp nod.

Gilbert held him there for another second so the answer would not feel accidental, then let go and sat back down on the cot. That movement cost him more than he wanted it to. Pain struck through his temples at once, his vision darkened for a moment, and Gilbert felt again how his pulse had split into two separate beats, both of them pounding out of sync. But he did not show that weakness.

The prisoner rose slowly, clutching his hand to his chest. For several seconds he stared at Gilbert with hatred, but he did not come any closer. He simply lowered himself to the floor by the door and turned silently toward the wall.

Gilbert closed his eyes. Everything suited him. One look at the brute had been enough to tell him there would be no use in trying to make an ally of him. So the only useful thing Gilbert could do with him was establish a boundary quickly. With pain, since words clearly meant nothing to him.
