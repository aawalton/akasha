Charlie

The three of them heard Orielle leave the house suddenly. A moment later, Arkady followed as well. Tomlin looked up from playing with the toys he’d brought over and then shrugged. It had almost sounded like they were arguing, but Charlie couldn’t be sure.

Merlin was still recovering from being woken up so suddenly. He wiped his eyes and groaned. “Ugh, that was the best sleep I’d had all week.” He looked up toward the door, noticing something, and his expression paused. “Who is that?” he asked. Charlie sat atop the living room table, and so he turned to look out the window where Merlin was.

On the table, Orb sat still. “Hey, pick me up! I’m pretending to be a ball, so I can’t really see! I want to see!” They hadn’t fully let Tomlin in on their secrets yet.

Charlie’s eyes widened. Hex knights were walking through the streets, and it looked like they were heading right this way. Right toward their house. It wasn’t Obie or Thespin. A pit ran through his stomach.

Merlin hurried over to the window and peeked out. “Great. Those guys are headed right this way, and they look pissed,” he said. He ran back and grabbed Charlie, setting him in the baby carrier. Tomlin looked up, wondering what was going on. “Tomlin, come with us; we’re going out the back!” Merlin called out. Orielle’s little brother followed without question.

Charlie had a thought. “Wait, Merlin.”

Merlin skidded to a stop. “What? Why? Charlie, if we get caught now then saving—” He hesitated, looking down at Tomlin. “Then, our plans this evening will be much more difficult. We have to run.”

Charlie shook his head. “Grab the blueprint of the fort,” he said.

Orb groaned. “Do I even want to know where this is going?”

Merlin looked unsure, but placing his faith in Charlie, he hurried into the kitchen. He noticed the still-bubbling pot full of broth, but ignored it. Instead, he took the fortress blueprint out and held it up for Charlie.

“Lay it out,” he said.

Merlin did so, and Tomlin tilted his head when he noticed it was his father’s work. Charlie told Merlin his plan telepathically. Merlin played his role immediately. “Tomlin, I need you to do me a favor. It’s a secret mission only you can do,” he said.

Tomlin’s eyes lit up, and he got excited. “Okay! What is it?”

Charlie sat Orb down on the map. Only Charlie and Richard knew where the hidden entrance to his dungeon was. Richard, because he was the only one staying outside the fort, and Charlie because he’d placed it. Arkady had suggested the others shouldn’t know until they needed to. He didn’t think anyone would talk if they were captured, but in this case, the less they each knew, the better. “Show Tomlin the spot,” Charlie said to Orb alone.

Orb rolled over to the place where the dungeon was hidden. “Tomlin, can you take this map with you? I need you to show Arkady this exact spot. Tell him he needs to let Richard know that they’ve found us. We might need his help, but we’ve found what we were looking for,” Merlin said.

Tomlin nodded.

The front door of the house banged. They weren’t even bothering to knock; they were trying to break the door down from the start.

“Charlie…” Orb warned.

“This is really important, Tomlin, and no one else can find out about this. It’s a super-secret mission. Can I trust you with this?” Merlin asked.

Tomlin saluted. “Sir! Yes, sir!”

Merlin handed him the blueprint and sent him towards the back door.

The front door burst open.

Tomlin ran and escaped out the back. Charlie called out. “Upstairs!”

Merlin hurried through the living room, just in front of the knights, who were still gathering their bearings, and bolted up the stairs.

“Bedroom!” Charlie cried.

The knights weren’t far behind. “Stop! You’re under arrest!” one called out.

Merlin shouldered through the door and turned around to slam it shut behind him. “Okay…now what?” he asked.

Charlie looked toward the window and smiled.

Merlin groaned. “You’ve got to be kidding me.”

“Charlie, I can’t carry Merlin’s weight if that’s what you’re thinking,” Orb said.

“I’ve got this!” Charlie said.

A sword carved through the door of the room. Merlin gasped and sprinted for the window.

Charlie held his hands out. “Dungeon Manipulation!”

A chunk of rock took out the window and a foot of the wall on all sides of it. Merlin ran, and when he reached it, he didn’t hesitate. He jumped. He trusted Charlie. Though he did flail his arms a little.

Charlie focused on his technique once more, and a portal opened below, dumping something onto the ground. A thick pile of dirt. Merlin landed with a thud. Charlie willed the dirt to disappear, and Orb yelled in their ears. “Get up! We gotta run!” he cried out. Merlin scrambled to his feet and ran away from the house, moving toward the unfinished construction zones.

“What now?” he asked Charlie.

He hadn’t thought that far ahead. “Keep moving!” he cried out while he thought of a plan.

Mousifer

Mousifer’s warning came too late. He realized with a sad squeak. The knights had reached the house before he had. A stupid hawk flying overhead had slowed his progress. He saw Tomlin burst out the back door, running with a blueprint away from the house. A moment later, Charlie and Merlin jumped from the second floor and escaped the knights. Marvin the horse was calmly chewing hay outside. He exchanged a casual look with Mousifer before returning to his meal, unbothered by the chaos around them.

Mousifer looked in both directions. His instinct told him to go after Tomlin. So he did. With one last quiet encouraging squeak to cheer on Charlie and another to wish Marvin’s food was good, he turned and ran after Charlie’s friend.

Arkady

Arkady had been on the verge of catching up to Orielle when two hex knights stopped her. He paused, ducking behind a building. She wanted nothing more than to move past, but the knights spoke in hushed, heavy tones. That was odd. Sure, Aveo worked with the knights a lot, so it was only natural for them to speak with Orielle as well. But this seemed different. He didn’t know what was going on. But it seemed like she might need some help.

Arkady hurriedly moved toward the rear of the building and moved in between the buildings and the stone wall to avoid drawing attention until he reached where they were talking. He slowly crept up along the side of the house, hoping they wouldn’t notice. The knights shouldn’t from this angle, but Orielle might.

She noticed him but quickly looked away. “You’re looking for Arkady? And his friends?” She said louder than she normally would. She was warning him. “I just don’t understand why?” she asked.

The knights exchanged a look. “Sir Calda has reason to believe they’re dangerous, and a threat to the safety of the fort. We’ve been ordered to take them in for questioning immediately. It’s been reported that you’ve been spotted with this Arkady man on many occasions. We’d like to ask you a few questions, if that’s alright.”

Orielle seemed overwhelmed. “Well…”

Arkady sighed. This wasn’t her problem to face. It was his. He’d put her through enough. “Gentlemen, there’s no reason to bother her with this. If you’re looking for me, well, here I am.” He held his arms out.

The knights both reached for swords immediately, but Arkady didn’t move. “No need for that, I’ll come along willingly.”

Or he would pretend to. As soon as they stepped further out of sight of the main road, he’d strike at their weaknesses. A subtle structural weakness on the side of one knight’s helmet; the other had a weakness behind his knee. Possibly an old injury. A strike there, and then a punch to the face would incapacitate him. He’d deal with them in three strikes. In theory. A very generous theory that might not actually be the most realistic. These were hex knights, not the common bandits he was used to dealing with. Still, he’d make a good showing for Orielle.

It was as if she could sense what he was doing. She moved in between him and the knights. “This is a misunderstanding. I’ll admit I haven’t known Arkady long, but…I don’t think he would do anything to hurt someone. I don’t think he’s a danger to the fort or its people.”

She’d seen a blueprint of the fort taken from her dad’s workshop. Hearing this from the knights should have made her assume the worst, and sure, she was upset, that hadn’t changed, but she was defending him. Even she seemed surprised about it.

The knights exchanged a look.

Tomlin

When he caught up with Arkady and his sister, they were arguing with hex knights. He frowned. Merlin said the mission was important, and he couldn’t afford to let anyone else know. Even though the knights were supposed to be the good guys, he didn’t want to fail his mission. Charlie was his friend, and whatever this mission was, it would be helpful to him. He sat down in the grass and unraveled the blueprint again, looking at the location the ball had rolled over. He looked over at the tower, using it to figure out whereabouts he was on the blueprint that doubled as a map. When he was satisfied that he knew where to go, or at least had a general area, he made up his mind. If he couldn’t tell Arkady to pass on the message, he would do it himself. He hurried to the wall. His dad always left a secret way out of anything he built, and the fort was no exception. Tomlin used the secret backdoor to their home more than he used the front door. And though his dad had never shown him the secret way through the wall, he’d figured it out. He’d seen his dad’s work enough to know that much. He tapped the stones until he felt it. The latch. He triggered it, and the mechanism released. A small door popped up in the wall, and he scrambled through, quickly closing it behind him. Too quickly. Part of the blueprint got caught in the secret door. He tugged at it and felt a rip that sent him falling backwards. A moment later he thought he heard a quiet scratching on the other side, and he got up and pressed his ear to the wall. Something was squeaking on the other side. Tomlin shrugged. Probably just some animal playing or something. There were guards on the wall, but he’d snuck out before; he was good at this kind of thing. Plus, the grass was taller on this side of the fort, and it would hide him really good. He smiled. Merlin and Charlie were going to be so happy when he completed his mission. Now he just needed to find Richard.

Arkady

“Miss Orielle, please. We have our orders, and it won’t be long before the storm comes. We have to do this now so we can get back to the wall,” a knight said.

“Orielle, it’s fine. I can handle myself,” Arkady said. Even if they took him in, he could escape once the storm started. The knights would be far too distracted to worry about him. Of course, he didn’t plan to go without a fight. The only thing that had kept him from drawing his weapon was his worry about Orielle. He didn’t want her getting hurt in the fight, and of course, he wasn’t trying to kill the knights. He just needed to incapacitate them. The people in the fort were innocent, good people. He wanted as many knights around to fight when the time came as possible.

Orielle turned and started to say something to him when a frantic squeaking came shooting from the houses behind them. The knights leaned over to look past them, and Orielle looked just as confused. But Arkady recognized those squeaks. He whipped around and knelt as Mousifer hurried over to him. “Buddy, are you okay? Is Charlie okay?”

“Squeak! Squeak squeak!” He turned back and pointed a paw toward the wall, and then he spat something out he’d tucked in his mouth. He grabbed it with his paws and held it up. Arkady lifted it to inspect it. A tiny, slobbery corner of a blueprint.

Arkady had a feeling he knew exactly the one. “What’s going on?” he asked again, trying to make sense of it all.

Orielle looked shocked. “Are you talking to a rat?” she asked.

“That’s a mouse,” one knight said. Mousifer gave the man a paws up and continued trying to explain.

The knights looked at each other in disbelief.

Mousifer stood on his hind paws and pointed at Orielle and then back to the blueprint. Arkady looked back at her. “Orielle?” His heart skipped a beat when he realized what Mousifer might be trying to say. “Do you mean Tomlin?” he asked.

Mousifer shook his head vigorously. Orielle went pale. “Ark, what’s going on?” she demanded, suddenly distressed by the mention of her brother.

Mousifer turned and pointed at the wall. Arkady looked up. “Something about the wall? Tomlin and the wall what?”

Mousifer drew a line in the sand and then dashed over it. He pointed to the wall again.

“Tomlin went through a wall?” Arkady asked.

Mousifer nodded.

Orielle gasped. “Oh no. Oh no, no, no.”

Arkady looked up. “You understand that?”

“Father always builds a secret way out. Tomlin might know…if Tomlin’s outside the walls then…” She looked up at the sky. It was getting dark. Mousifer took off running and led them toward the wall. Arkady and Orielle chased after him.

“Hey!” the knights called out, giving chase as well.

They skidded to a stop at the secret entrance; a piece of the blueprint was still trapped within the doorway. Orielle wasted no time, turning on the hex knights. “You need to let Gregory know that Tomlin’s outside the walls!” she shouted.

“What?” the guards asked incredulously. “Aveo’s son? Your brother?”

“Go now!” she ordered.

The knights exchanged a look before hurrying off. At least they understood the gravity of this.

“What do we do now, Ark?” she asked. “Do we—”

She paused. Before he could answer, she paused. A drop of rain fell and landed on her arm.

Arkady looked up as more raindrops fell. The hex storm had arrived.

A serious expression crossed his face. “Orielle, if you happen to see my friends on the way to take shelter, tell them I had to do something. I’ll be back when I can.”

Orielle looked lost. “Where are you going?” she asked, taking a step toward him. “What about Tomlin?”

Arkady looked at her and put a hand on her shoulder. “I’m going to save him. You stay here and wait for me. I promise I won’t come back without him.”

Thunder cracked in the sky. Orielle sobbed. “What if he—”

“Nothing’s going to happen. Go get inside; it’s dangerous. I’m going to handle it from here.” He put his hand on her shoulder, and then, to her surprise, she shot forward and hugged him.

“Please save him, Ark. Please save him.”

A moment later he had slipped through the wall, and it took him no time at all to pick up the trail. The hex storm arrived in full force, along with the weight of what he was doing. There wasn’t any time to second-guess this. Tomlin’s life was on the line, and he wasn’t letting an innocent boy die here.
