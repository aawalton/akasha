The deeper the assassin squad pushed into the corrupted northern plains, the more the natural world seemed to actively reject their presence. The frost-covered dirt road they had been following was now completely unrecognisable. The creeping tide of black sludge had saturated the earth, turning the firm ground into a treacherous, sucking quagmire that threatened to pull their boots under with every single step. The ambient temperature had plummeted well below freezing, yet the foul-smelling necrotic fog rolling over the corrupted grass remained thick, damp, and cloying. It clung to their heavy travel cloaks like a physical weight, leaving a slick, oily residue on their leather armour.

Perberos moved through the oppressive darkness with the effortless, liquid grace of falling water. He focused his mind on the suffocating ambient dark magic, allowing it to wash over him rather than fighting it. His physical form was no longer fully solid, nor a definable shape in the gloom. He was a blur of muted colours, his edges softening and blending perfectly with the surrounding rot.

His boots did not make a single sound against the freezing mud. He did not disturb the stagnant air as he walked. He was a living ghost, entirely separated from the mortal terror that was currently gripping the human members of his squad.

Ronald led the staggered formation. The feline beastkin's jade-green eyes were heavily dilated, his slit pupils expanding to catch the faintest traces of starlight piercing the dead, rotting canopy above. He communicated entirely through sharp, precise hand signals, operating with flawless military discipline. A raised fist meant a dead halt. Two fingers pointed downward indicated a trap, a sinkhole, or unstable ground. A flat, sweeping motion of his palm meant they needed to melt into the shadows instantly.

They had been moving for nearly an hour since sending young Finn back to Ashenfall, and the tension was pulling as tight as a drawn bowstring. The silence of the plains was unnatural, heavy, and pregnant with unseen threats.

Ronald suddenly threw his hand out flat, dropping instantly into a low crouch behind the massive, shattered trunk of a fallen pine tree.

Perberos, Garth, Lyra, and Silas dropped simultaneously, pressing their bodies hard against the freezing sludge. They did not breathe. They did not shift their weight. Garth, the grizzled human tracker, stopped chewing his wad of tobacco, swallowing the bitter juices rather than risking the faint sound of spitting.

Less than twenty feet away, the thick fog parted to reveal a roaming patrol of heavily corrupted forest trolls.

There were four of them. The massive, lumbering beasts were missing huge patches of grey skin, their exposed, oversized ribs glowing with a sickly, pulsating green light. Dark, necrotic runes had been brutally carved into their remaining flesh. They dragged heavy, rusted iron chains behind them, the massive metal links clanking dull and muffled against the mud.

The stench of stagnant water, decaying flesh, and old copper washed over the hiding assassins. It was overpowering and sickening. Perberos watched the monsters lumber past with cold, calculating silver eyes. His hand rested lightly on the hilt of his curved elven blade, his thumb brushing the cool steel, but he did not draw it. The trolls were incredibly dangerous, capable of ripping a heavily armoured knight in half, but killing them would serve no strategic purpose. It would simply risk exposing their position and sounding a general alarm.

They waited in agonising silence for five long minutes, their bodies cramping from the freezing mud, until the heavy, dragging footsteps faded completely into the darkness.

Ronald gave a single nod, rising smoothly from the muck and continuing the punishing, relentless jog north.

However, the sheer density of the undead occupation meant that simple evasion was becoming impossible. The closer they got to the source of the marching, the thicker the patrols became.

Twenty minutes later, they were forced off the road entirely. Ronald spotted the glowing green abdomens of a massive pack of scuttling Crypt Weavers approaching from the north. The bone-plated spiders were moving in a wide, sweeping line, effectively cutting off the entire path.

Ronald signalled a hard right flank. The squad slid silently down a steep embankment, taking cover in a deep, freezing drainage ditch that ran parallel to the old trade route. The ditch was half-filled with stagnant, freezing black water and the rotting remains of unfortunate woodland creatures.

They waded into the freezing muck, pressing their backs flat against the slick stone walls of the trench as the massive spiders scuttled directly overhead.

The clicking of the Weavers' fused skull joints was deafening. Dust and small pebbles rained down on the assassins. Lyra closed her eyes tightly, her hands gripping her twin daggers until her knuckles turned pure white. A thick, viscous drop of glowing green necrotic venom dripped from the edge of the ditch, landing with a quiet sizzle on the stone mere inches from Silas's boot. The veteran ranger did not flinch, though a bead of cold sweat rolled down his temple. They remained submerged in the freezing filth until the chittering swarm finally passed.

Another mile north, they nearly stumbled blindly into a loose column of skeletal hounds that had been sniffing the frost for fresh tracks.

With no deep brush to hide in, Ronald pointed a sharp claw towards the crumbling, sheer stone wall of an ancient, ruined watchtower rising out of the mist to their left. It was an impossible climb for normal men, but they were the elite of the Guild.

They scaled the rough, freezing stone with their bare hands, finding microscopic handholds in the decaying mortar. They clung to the side of the tower twenty feet in the air, completely exposed to the freezing wind. Halfway up, Silas’s mud-caked boot suddenly slipped on a patch of black ice. He dropped a terrifying two feet before Garth reached out with a thick, calloused hand, grabbing the ranger by the scruff of his leather collar and pinning him silently against the stone.

Below them, a dozen skeletal hounds paused at the base of the tower. The beasts sniffed the ground, their fleshless jaws snapping at the air. The squad hung from the wall in terrified silence for ten excruciating minutes until the hounds finally lost the scent and trotted away into the fog.

They were operating with flawless synchronicity, but the physical and mental toll was immense. The human members of the squad were shivering violently, their stamina draining rapidly from the freezing temperatures and the constant, crushing adrenaline spikes.

They had just climbed down from the ruined tower and were navigating a narrow, steep-sided ravine. They used the deep shadows of the rocky limestone overhangs to mask their approach, sticking close to the walls.

That was when the frozen ground directly beneath Silas suddenly erupted.

It was a Burrowing Horror. It was a vile amalgamation of shattered bone, compacted grave dirt, and dark magic designed specifically to act as a living landmine. The creature burst from the freezing mud with terrifying, explosive speed. Its jagged, circular maw was lined with hundreds of rusted iron teeth, snapping wildly as it lunged directly at the veteran ranger's legs.

Silas was a seasoned professional. A novice would have screamed or frozen in shock. Silas did neither. He threw his entire body backwards, completely sacrificing his balance to avoid the fatal bite, landing hard on his back in the suffocating sludge.

The Horror reared up from the crater it had formed. Its jaw unhinged, its chest cavity expanding as it prepared to let out a deafening, rattling shriek that would instantly alert every undead creature within a mile radius.

It never got the chance.

Perberos did not bother reaching for his longbow. The distance was entirely too close, and the loud twang of the heavy bowstring might carry in the quiet ravine. He blurred forward, his shadow integration making him look like a wisp of dark smoke. He drew his twin curved elven blades from their sheaths in a single, fluid, completely silent motion.

The dark steel flashed beautifully in the gloom. He drove his left blade directly down into the creature's gaping, jagged maw. The enchanted steel pierced the compacted grave dirt, forcefully pinning its rusted jaw shut against its chest and violently silencing the gathering scream. Simultaneously, he swept his right blade in a brutal, perfect horizontal arc, cleanly severing the thick bundle of necrotic tethers holding the monster's fused skull to its body.

Before the heavy, detached head could hit the stone floor of the ravine and make a sound, Ronald was there. The beastkin had slid gracefully through the mud behind Perberos. He caught the horrific, snapping skull tightly against his chest, muffling the clacking teeth.

Garth moved just as fast. The grizzled tracker silently wrestled the thrashing, headless body to the ground, pinning it in the mud, and brought his heavy steel boot down in a crushing stomp, shattering its remaining magical core.

The entire brutal, frantic execution took less than three seconds.

They remained frozen, standing over the crumbling remains of the monster. Their weapons were drawn, their breathing ragged, listening intently to the howling winter wind sweeping through the ravine. They waited for the inevitable sound of approaching footsteps or the blast of a warning horn.

None came. No sudden roars echoed from the surrounding fog. The assassination had been entirely silent.

Lyra let out a slow, shaky breath, sheathing her daggers. She offered Perberos a look of profound respect as she reached down to pull Silas back to his feet. The ranger wiped a thick layer of freezing mud from his face, nodding his silent thanks to the elf.

"Too close," Ronald mouthed silently, wiping a smear of dark, foul-smelling sludge from his leather chest piece. The beastkin’s tail lashed restlessly. He pointed a sharp claw towards the steep incline leading out of the ravine, signalling that they needed to keep moving before a patrol stumbled upon the golden dust dissolving in the mud.

They climbed the rocky slope, leaving the dead Horror behind them.

The deep, rhythmic vibration they had felt over an hour ago was no longer a distant tremor. It was a constant, physical pressure grinding against their bones. The earth was literally shaking beneath their boots. The noise was a low, deafening hum of clanking iron, grinding bone, and heavy, unified marching. It sounded like an earthquake was slowly rolling across the plains.

They reached the top of the ravine, pushing carefully through a dense thicket of dead, thorny briars to emerge onto a high, overlooking ridge. The ridge was a geological anomaly on the relatively flat plains, offering a commanding, unobstructed view of the wide, sweeping valley below, where the old northern trade road cut through the landscape.

Ronald dropped flat onto his stomach, crawling the last few yards through the frost to peer over the edge. Perberos slipped silently into the shadows beside him, his silver eyes piercing the thick, swirling necrotic fog pooling in the valley below.

The elven scout had spent years fighting the horrors of the dark. He had seen brutal dungeon breaks, he had survived massive, bloody orc raids on the western borders of the forest, and he had witnessed the terrifying, coordinated assault on the forward operating base just hours ago. He thought he understood the scale of the threat facing his city.

But looking down into the valley, the cold, calculating tactical centre of his mind entirely stalled.

It was not an army. It was a moving, unstoppable ocean of death.

The entire valley floor, stretching for miles in every single direction until it faded into the dark horizon, was completely choked with a solid, undulating mass of rotting meat, rusted iron, and jagged bone. There were no gaps in their formation. There were no stragglers. It was a suffocating carpet of monsters, marching shoulder to shoulder in terrifying, unnatural unison. The sheer density of the horde made the ground look as though it were boiling.

The impossible scale of the horde defied all magical logic and previous Guild intelligence.

Thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights formed the vanguard. They marched in perfect, terrifying lockstep, their rusted halberds held perfectly upright, creating a dense, moving forest of deadly steel points. Tattered, rotting banners of long-forgotten, fallen kingdoms hung from their spears, flapping silently in the freezing wind.

Behind the knights marched endless, shuffling ranks of Crypt Ghouls and rotting infantry. Their bodies were a bruised, purple mass of decaying flesh, their glowing green eyes creating a swirling, chaotic sea of light in the darkness. They carried rusted broadswords, broken spears, and heavy iron maces, their metallic clanking creating the deafening roar the squad had heard for miles.

Towering high above the sea of lesser undead were the heavy siege assets.

Perberos counted at least two dozen Bone Goliaths. The massive titans, constructed of hundreds of fused skeletons, were dragging crude, horrifying battering rams made from the shattered, iron-banded trunks of ancient oak trees.

Hundreds of Crypt Weavers scuttled actively along the flanks of the army, acting as terrifying outriders. Their glowing green abdomens provided a sickening, pulsating illumination to the nightmare below, ensuring the mindless infantry stayed tightly within their ranks.

There were easily ten thousand monsters marching down the road, perhaps significantly more. It was a force capable of wiping Ashenfall from the map.

Garth crawled up beside them, his heavy crossbow dragging in the dirt. The grizzled tracker looked over the ridge, and his heavily scarred face instantly drained of all colour. The massive weapon slipped slightly in his trembling hands. He spat his chewing tobacco into the dirt, entirely forgetting his strict stealth discipline.

"Light preserve us," Garth whispered, his voice trembling with absolute, primal horror. He stared blankly at the endless sea of bone. "That is not a siege force. That is an extinction event. We cannot fight that. The entire Royal Army would struggle to fight that."

Lyra and Silas joined them on the ridge, their reactions perfectly mirroring the tracker's sheer terror. The lithe half-elven rogue simply stared, her cynical eyes wide and unblinking, the throwing dagger falling limply from her fingers. Silas reached under his leather armour, tightly gripping the small wooden holy symbol hanging around his neck, his lips moving in a frantic, silent prayer.

Ronald’s ears flattened completely against his skull. The beastkin’s long tail lashed furiously behind him. His pragmatic, brilliant tactical mind was struggling and failing to comprehend the impossible mathematics of the slaughter marching beneath them.

"The timber walls of Ashenfall will not slow them down for long," Ronald noted, his voice a harsh, raspy whisper that barely carried over the deafening clanking below. "They will simply walk over the ramparts on a ramp made of their own dead. Caistina and the others... they are preparing to hold a line against a tidal wave. They are all going to die."

Perberos did not speak. His luminous silver eyes flicked rapidly across the endless sea of glowing green dots, completely ignoring the sheer, paralysing terror currently gripping his squad.

He was not looking at the heavily armoured knights, and he was not looking at the towering Goliaths. He was searching the absolute centre of the massive, moving formation. He was searching for the dark, suffocating locus of magical power that had to be anchoring an army of this impossible magnitude.

He remembered the single tear slipping down Carcan’s pale cheek before he left the Guildhall. He remembered the heavy, uncompromising oath he had forced Josh to swear, binding the human Guardian to protect his sister.

Perberos was not going to let his sister face this tidal wave.

He slowly reached over his shoulder. His long, elegant fingers bypassed the standard iron broadheads in his quiver, brushing against the smooth fletching of the heavy, steel-tipped bodkins resting in the magically expanded space. His ethereal, aristocratic face hardened into a mask of pure, lethal ice.

He was not going to turn back and run. He was not going to deliver a message of doom to Ashenfall. He was going to find the general commanding this apocalypse, and he was going to put a solid steel shaft directly through its skull, even if he had to carve his way through a sea of bone to do it.
