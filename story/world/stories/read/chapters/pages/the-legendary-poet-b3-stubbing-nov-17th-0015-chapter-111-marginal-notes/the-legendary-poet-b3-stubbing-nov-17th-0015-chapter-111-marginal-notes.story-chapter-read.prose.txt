Mushin.

The word arrived cold and familiar, sliding under his ribs like an old blade finding its sheath.

Dojo old men wrapped it in incense and solemn nonsense. In practice, the body solved the fight before conscious thought caught up. No planning. No flinch. No “I should.” The cut happened because the position demanded that cut, and the mind arrived afterward to take credit like a manager after a successful project.

Genuine no-mind in the real world belonged to a tiny population. Dozens, maybe. Across the entire planet. Plenty of champions brushed the edge once and chased the footprint for the rest of their careers. Most people used the word after one good sparring session and a motivational quote.

Morpheus had watched his combat logs.

Morpheus had unlocked The Samurai after analyzing his behavior.

Then the subclass used Mushin as its peak threshold.

Option one, dramatic naming. The system liked theatrics. It called a sword a Devoted Shadow and crafted gear that Zherdev used to make furniture nervous.

Option two, actual measurement.

The second option deserved a government hearing.

Kage’s fingers tightened.

If Morpheus had taken true Mushin as the baseline for the class ceiling, the subclass was absurd. Borderline malicious. It asked players to hit timings that human consciousness usually ruined by participating.

Clean meant skilled.

Perfect meant elite.

Edge meant the body moved in the crack before thought.

Mushin meant the crack became the world.

Ordinary sword actions, even excellent ones, did not count as words. The Physical Lexicon demanded more than shape. Maybe both.

His class loved intent. It treated “what he meant” like fuel and “what the world accepted as true” like a trigger. Verses needed Titles. Verses needed syntax. Physical verses skipped Titles, but the body had to speak clearly enough for the system to read it.

A slash that damaged something was a slash.

A slash that meant [Strike] needed grammar.

A cut to a joint that reduced function was tactics.

There went the cheap exploit.
