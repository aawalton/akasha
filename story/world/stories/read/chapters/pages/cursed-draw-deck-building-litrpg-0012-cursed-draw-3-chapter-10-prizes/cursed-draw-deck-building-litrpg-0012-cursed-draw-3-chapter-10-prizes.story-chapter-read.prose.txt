“One last adventure?” Alley asked with a quizzical look at his opponent.

“That's what I said, isn’t it?” Replied the elderly bearded man. The pair of them were standing on either side of the white painted lines on the floor of the arena that marked out where the blue barrier would separate them once their Challenge began.

While Alley’s opponent for his match this morning had actually made it on time, the tournament organizers were the ones running late today. Seeing as Alley couldn’t disqualify them like they had Trine yesterday, he and the man he was meant to play had gotten talking.

The man he would be battling against today was possibly the oldest person Alley had ever laid eyes on. Well, the oldest living person, at least. His face was wrinkled, his hair was thin and receding, but his physique was surprisingly muscular, and his stature towering. His build honestly reminded Alley of his own father, who had the thick muscles of a metal worker, rather than the lithe form Alley had inherited from his mother.

“ I heard you,” Alley said. “I just don’t understand the correlation. Placing highly in a tournament this large is certainly thrilling, but I don’t know if I’d call it an adventure.”

The large old man stared down at Alley for a few seconds before shaking his head.

“You entered The Frostwing Trials without even knowing what the prizes were? Am I getting that right?”

Alley blinked a few times as he realized that actually was the case. He had considered the Ante Cards, and obviously, he’d thought about the Chips that he and Darius had wagered on themselves and Kyera making it out of the first round, but he hadn’t considered what the actual prizes for the tournament were. He assumed there were Chips and maybe an Item you could link to your Deck.

“I guess not,” Alley replied with a grin and a shrug.

The old man barked out a booming laugh that echoed across the arena, even with the noise of the crowd that was quickly filling the stands and the chatter of the other competitors killing time in the same way.

“There were posters all over the city for weeks listing the prizes for the different placings, and you never once read them. Just entered for love of the game, eh?”

Alley raised his hands up in a helpless gesture. It was a little embarrassing to admit now that he was thinking about it, but he really had just entered without a second thought simply for the enjoyment of it. Well, he admitted to himself the enjoyment, and to see where he stacked up against other players. His mother had trained him hard, and he had faith in his skills as a player, but you never truly knew until you actually played against other people. Pirates and tree-women at the edge of civilization were one thing, yet it would never be the same as matching up against a plethora of opponents with all manner of strategies.

“I only got here just before registration ended, and I don’t read Frostlander anyway.”

The old man laughed again and playfully punched Alley in the upper arm. At least, Alley thought it was playful. It was hard to tell when the muscular ancient all but knocked him over.

“Well, obviously, there is a pile of money in it for first place in both the True and Trials events. The winner of the Trials section also gets a Frostwing Manticore Cub, while our winner will get a pair of Ice-Gust Wings.” Alley puffed his cheeks and lips with breath and shook his head.

“I have no idea what those are.”

“Of course you don’t.” The old man said in an exasperated tone. “ Well, it doesn’t matter. The important thing is that the top four for both events get a map to a new world.”

That caught Alley’s attention, and he met the old man’s gaze with a questioning look of his own.

“You mean a new continent that is about to rise out of the ocean?” He asked using the more modern term for the chaotic landmasses that every country, including this one and his homeland, had begun as.

“Oh, that you know? Yes, rumor has it the new world will be in a far corner of the Jade Sea. Obviously, no one knows for sure except the highest levels of the Framers Guild, but one is coming, and the best eight of us will get to know where exactly.”

Alley was genuinely shocked. To the best of his knowledge, it had been at least a century since the last time it had happened, and it really was the chance for an adventure of a lifetime. It wouldn’t take long for people other than those with foreknowledge to start flocking to the new continent in search of treasures, land claims, and the nigh-mythical chaos lock that supposedly existed on each new world.

“Wow,” Alley said honestly with genuine surprise, before he mastered himself and shot his shit-eating grin at the old man. “Well, after I win the whole tournament, I’ll make sure to invite you to come see the new world with my group.”

The old man guffawed and hit Alley with another friendly punch. He had to repress a wince as the blow landed in the exact same spot, and his arm was already a little tender.

“I like that confidence, kiddo, but your run goes through this old man, and that's where it ends for everybody.” The pair both narrowed their eyes at each other in shows of faux aggression, before they both laughed at the friendly banter. Alley decided he liked the old man and was looking forward to their match, assuming the tournament administrators ever actually showed up.

“Hey, where are you from? If it's not rude to ask.” Alley inquired slowly. The man’s accent tickled his brain with familiarity. It wasn’t from the Final Peaks like Lorien; it certainly lacked the flare of Alley’s own Deep River country, and was nowhere near the sing-song accent of the Frostlander locals. Yet, it was still one Alley had heard before.

“Shattered Archipelago.” Replied the older man. “It's on the south-eastern edge of the Silver Sea.” The mention of the sea closest to Alley’s own home finally made where he had heard someone speaking like him before finally click. Alley had met another elderly man who sounded similar to this one when he’d fought against The Shattered Hull Pirates. Strativarius Bron, the man from the Deep Hunter’s League.

“Oh, I think I’ve met some people from there, but never knew the actual location. I’m from-”

“Deep River Country. I know”

Alley was honestly taken aback, he was surprised that the man had both recognized his accent and not called it ‘the Mucklands.’

“It’s the way you gesture with your hands every few words.” The old man added, seeing Alley’s surprise. “That and you are kind of flamboyant, which is very common with you river folk.”

Alley honestly didn’t know if he should be complimented or offended. He did talk with his hands; that much was true. He never really considered himself flamboyant, though.

Before Alley could really come to a conclusion about how to feel, a voice boomed through the arena from one of the handheld projection devices in Frostlander, followed a few seconds later by the translation in Southern.

“We apologize for the wait, but there was a minor emergency that required the attention of our organizers at the western arena.” Alley glanced over at the long table, which was now being filled by a trio of judges. He wasn’t entirely sure what they were needed for, other than simply recording who won which matches since the Dealers themselves prevented any sort of cheating…or did they? Was there some sort of concern about people attacking each other?

Beside the table stood a lanky young man who was the one speaking through the Resonance voice amplifier.

‘ Wait…Western arena? Oh, please tell me Darius didn’t turn into that demon form and kill a bunch of people.’ Alley was pretty sure he would have felt something through their link if that sort of thing had happened, but it was a concern that never went away completely. If that is what happened, Malchik was there, and if there was anyone who could stand up to the hunter’s monster form, it was the elder prince, and that was going to have to be enough. Alley had a match to play and an old man to defeat.

“You may now begin your matches.”

Alley reached out and offered his hand to shake, which the elder took.

“I challenge you.” Declared Alley.

“I accept.”

‘Alright, head in the game, Alley.’

“Vern Euchenic, Rank Eight Determination of The Stone Lord.” Alley actually already knew the name of the man’s Deck and had taken several notes about it when he’d watched him play a few days before the attack on the multi-leveled inn, and knew he was up against Earth Resonance Cards and a Relic-heavy attack.

“Alabaster Roe, Rank Ten, Crown of The Cursed King.”
