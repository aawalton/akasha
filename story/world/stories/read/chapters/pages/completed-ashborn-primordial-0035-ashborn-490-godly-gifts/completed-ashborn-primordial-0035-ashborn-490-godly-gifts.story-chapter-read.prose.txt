“Before all that,” Maiya said as they disembarked the magic carpet, which had taken them back to street level, “would you mind if we explored some of your celebrations? You have to understand—calling this a dream come true would be a massive understatement. To experience the culture of the Prime Imperium in its heyday…” She shook her head. “Even if only for historical purposes, we’d love to take part. Especially since we were lucky enough to arrive during your festival.”

Yuma hesitated only slightly before nodding. “I see no reason why we cannot spare a few hours. Come. Allow me to show you around the city.”

The goddess guided them through vibrant streets that were both intimately familiar and utterly alien to Vir and Maiya. The towering, hollow spires that Vir had once known as lifeless ruins were now packed with life and brimming with activity. Within them were entire communities, and instead of bare, empty floors, the levels were filled with workshops, Thaumaturge laboratories, and living quarters.

Vir caught glimpses of complex machinery that made no sense even to him, despite his exposure to Imperium magic. There were contraptions of silver and gold that pulsed with prana, all connected to accomplish some unknown goal.

In other spires, families laughed and shared meals on balconies overlooking the pristine streets.

The scale of it all was staggering. The number of towers alone dwarfed anything in Samar Patag, and the verticality of the city multiplied that population a hundredfold.

To think this was only one of many Imperium cities scattered across their realm. It was humbling beyond words.

Unlike Vir, however, who marveled at the Thaumaturgy and magical engineering around him, Maiya was more captivated by the people and their celebrations.

“Come on, we have to check that out!” she said, tugging at his arm. “You can stare at their machines later!”

She dragged him and the others towards an open square where dancers in gorgeous silk clothing with designs interwoven with prana performed elegant dances. They reenacted old myths, many of which were foreign to Vir and Maiya. In their universe, the Imperium were the myths. That they’d have their own never even occurred to Vir. What remained were nothing more than fragments and echoes.

Not content to view the performances, Maiya demanded they sample Imperium foods. Here again, some were familiar, others entirely foreign. Even those Vir knew of, however, were of such unbelievable quality that they may as well have been different dishes entirely. Spiced vegetables roasted on skewers inscribed with prana. Sweets dusted with crystalline sugar that melted in the mouth and delectable drinks that energized the soul when swallowed.

Everything was in another realm. More refined, perfected food than Vir or Maiya would ever know in their universe.

For those few hours, they forgot the terrible quest they’d embarked upon, returning to a simpler, happier time. Laughing, eating, and drinking as musicians filled the air with their melodic voices.

“Of course,” Yuma explained, watching them with amusement, “we are a post-scarcity civilization. Such luxuries are easy for us. If you do ever see coins being exchanged, it is only for the sake of tradition. Anything we desire can be created at will.”

Maiya’s eyes widened. “That’s… marvelous. You’ve really built the perfect society, huh? No hunger, no poverty or crime.”

The envy was palpable in her voice.

“Indeed,” Yuma replied. “Those worries are but distant memories. Yet that is not to say we are without struggles, only that they take different forms. Forms that are far more difficult to solve.”

“Such as?” Vir asked.

“Purpose,” Ashani murmured quietly. She had been silent for most of the tour, and Vir guessed she was overcome with nostalgia. He couldn’t begin to imagine what she was going through, yet his attempts to broach the topic were met only with sad smiles and assurances that everything was fine.

Yuma nodded. “Precisely. What does one do when there is nothing left to strive for? We have built a culture centered on creative expression, mastery of the self, and exploration in all its forms. But even so, not all find fulfillment. Battling stagnation when all needs are already met has proven our greatest challenge.”

Vir frowned. “I admit, I find it difficult to relate. In our world, keeping people alive and fed is already a miracle. Most would give anything for a life free of worry.”

“Of course,” Yuma said, smiling knowingly. “Many feel that way. That is, until they achieve it. Both our people value accomplishment, yet what we cherish is rarely the reality of success. It is the idea of it—the image of that sweet fruit in our minds that we crave. Too often, that image far exceeds the sweetness of the actual thing.”

“I suppose I can understand that,” Vir said. Had he not experienced the same with his own conquests in the demon realm? Was he not in more pain and suffering now, having accomplished his loftiest goals, than he’d ever been in Brij as a bullied, pranascorned boy?

Yuma turned, her expression brightening again. “Now come. I believe the gifts that await will quickly brighten your mood.”

That, Vir knew, was all but guaranteed.

The group followed the Imperium goddess down a grand avenue filled with people, floating chairs, exotic pets, and far more before arriving at a massive, rectangular structure. Compared to the elegant spires that soared to the sky, this building was squat and took up more space than three of those spires combined.

Inside, the space was every bit as vast, though much of it was filled with an array of machinery, all connected to yet another hemispherical chamber, though one far larger than the one Yuma had brought them to in the central spire. Here again, its walls were alive with pulsing inscriptions.

With a signal from Yuma to one of the many thaumaturges working in white coats—coats that very much reminded Vir of Saunak—prana flared to life, filling the inscription one after another, until the entire dome blazed with prana of every affinity. All weaving into a beautiful tapestry that was so potent, it was almost blinding even to the naked eye.

Vir’s black flames had nothing on the spectacle before them.

“Can you even believe how much there is here?” Maiya whispered as they entered the dome.

“If we hadn’t been acclimatized to Mahādi, we’d likely be dead,” Vir said dryly. “Good thing all that training paid off.”

Maiya barely heard him. Her eyes were transfixed on the arsenal arrayed before them. On the katars and daggers and spears and sets of armor. Each and every one an Artifact of the Gods.

“These are all weapons and armor we have forged to aid you on your quest. And Vir, we understand that Ashani provided you once with our pranites. As you know, pranites expire with time. Allow us to replenish your reserve, as well as bestow Maiya and your wolves with their own. As for Ashani, we might just be able to improve her prana efficiency. Among other things,” she added mysteriously. “With these, you may yet defy Fate itself.”

Vir couldn’t help but smile, and Maiya wore the same expression. Even Ashani, who'd been reserved this entire time, opened her eyes just a bit wider.

For the longest time, he’d wondered how he’d even stand a chance against an enemy that spanned universes. Janak’s decision to bring them here was beginning to make a lot more sense. Despite the risks to both their universes.

Setting aside the matter of the pranites, which had Maiya's eyes bulging in excitement, the glorious assemblage of weapons were a sight to behold. Arranged among them were a dozen chakrams, each one more exquisite than anything Vir had ever wielded.

“These chakrams,” Yuma explained, “contain built-in prana batteries. They will function regardless of the surrounding environment. I believe your current weapon lacks such a feature?”

Vir retrieved his Chakram Artifact and nodded. “It loses most of its guidance when ambient prana is scarce.”

“May I?” the goddess asked.

Vir handed over the weapon, which Yuma examined closely. “As I thought. The metals and technology used by your Imerium are crude, and understandably so, for that civilization ended over four millennia ago. Our metallurgy has advanced considerably since then. You’ll find our weapons sharper, stronger, and nearly indestructible. They never require maintenance and their prana batteries should never fail you.”

She placed one of the new chakrams in his hands. “The edges of these weapons are infused with a type of reactive prana capable of melting through nearly any substance. You’ll find that the guidance systems are vastly improved as well, containing full intelligence unlike your current weapon. They can hover around you, spinning like protective blades, and can be given commands to defend a person or attack enemies, all without your direct control.”

Vir turned the weapon over in awe. The craftsmanship was beyond anything he could have imagined, but the fact it could operate independently? “That’s… unbelievable,” he muttered.

“We hope they will serve you well,” Yuma beamed. “We’ve prepared a dozen of these weapons for your disposal. Since they can remain aloft indefinitely, you needn’t carry them. Unless, of course, you wish to keep them hidden, in which case, you may activate their active camouflage.”

Yuma took one of the Chakrams, which promptly disappeared from her hand. No matter how much Vir strained to see it—with sight or through Prana Vision—he saw only air.

Vir exhaled slowly, unable to hide his wonder. “I’ll be irrevocably spoiled by this...”

Maiya grinned beside him. “Just don’t let it go to your head, o Akh Nara, Lord of Chakrams.”

Vir chuckled. “No promises.”

He turned to Yuma and bowed his head. “Truly, I cannot thank you enough for this.”

“Ah, but this is only a taste of all we have for you. As for your katar,” Yuma said, “I’m afraid the one you wield is but a crude implement compared to what we intend to bestow upon you.”

That was hardly a surprise, considering his current katar wasn’t even an Artifact.

She gestured to a table holding two of the most beautiful weapons Vir had ever seen.

The new katars were unusual. The blades were more than one and a half times the length of his own, and the sheer size made him hesitate. He didn’t dare refuse what was undoubtedly a vast improvement over his current weapon, but…

“They seem a little too big?” Vir said, forcing the words out.

Yuma only chuckled. “This is merely their extended state. Though the katar is an ancient weapon we prize greatly, after running several million simulations, we believe its limited range might hinder you. These blades can extend or retract at will. This,” she said, tapping the blade, “is but one configuration. They can double in length, matching a greatsword, or contract into daggers even smaller than your current weapon.”

Vir blinked. “…That’s incredible.”

He felt like he was saying that a lot.

“Moreover,” Yuma continued, “they contain the same advanced metals your new chakrams possess. Self-sharpening, nearly indestructible, and capable of tearing through almost any material. But as potent as they are as simple blades, their metallurgy is not their true strength.”

She lifted one of the weapons, its intricate gold carvings practically glowing with how much prana was stuffed in them.

“The metals in these katars and in your chakrams can both store and conduct Origin prana. Their ability to do so far surpasses anything found in your universe. These weapons will not merely channel your prana arts, they will multiply their potency. Not only will you need but a sliver of the prana you once did, but you will cast offensive magic with far greater distance, force, and lethality as well.”

Vir’s head spun. The chakrams alone had been overwhelming, and the katars almost pushed him past the point of reaction.

“And now,” Yuma said with a knowing smile, “let us discuss your armor.”

“Armor?” Vir squeaked.

A mannequin suddenly appeared beside them, wearing a set of blue-white plate armor that, at first glance, looked like most steel armor sets.

Upon closer examination, however, Vir found it was made of thousands of tiny, interlocking segments. All etched with a matrix of minuscule inscriptions.

“This set will adapt to your unique situation,” Yuma explained, “changing size with your body and acting as a prana-absorption sink. In theory, this will render you nigh invulnerable to all but the deadliest creatures of your universe. It also possesses a complete self-repair function. Unless it is entirely destroyed—which, I suspect, would mean far graver issues—you will never need to mend it. This, too, acts as a prana battery, capable of absorbing your excess prana for use later on. If discharged all at once with your other weapons, your greatest strike will be amplified a hundredfold.”

“I… I truly don’t know what to say, Goddess Yuma. This exceeds even my wildest expectations. To say you’ve done us a great service would be entirely inadequate to express my gratitude. This could very well save our lives… and our universe.”

“Think nothing of it,” she replied. “Such items are trivial for us to fabricate, and we are not acting purely out of selflessness. Strengthening your universe diverts the enemy's attention from our own, and their gaze has drifted uncomfortably close of late. In aiding you, we aid ourselves.”

Vir had to wonder whether their own realm was suffering its equivalent of ash tears… or something worse.

“Quite the incredible gift,” Maiya muttered from beside Vir, her envy all but palpable.

“There is no need for jealousy, young one,” Yuma said warmly. “We have prepared an equally magnificent set for you. One custom-tailored to your magic.”

She made a sweeping gesture and the air twisted.

An entire set of armor and weaponry materialized where nothing had existed a moment before.

Unlike Vir’s heavy segmented plate armor, however, Maiya’s looked nearly identical to her handmaiden battle armor, just refined beyond comparison. The long skirt was formed of the same blue-white interlocking plates as Vir’s armor, and while it bore no gaudy adornments, the interwoven prana patterns created designs more beautiful than any Vir had ever seen.

The pattern continued up the armored sleeves and leggings. When Maiya lifted the armor, she gasped.

“This weighs less than my undergarments!”

“Indeed,” Yuma said. “It should weigh little more than a single feather. It will not restrict your motions. Quite the opposite. It will, in fact, enhance your every movement.”

“Like Vir’s armor…?” Maiya asked.

“Yes—and more. This set augments your prana, and, in your case, grants access to something far more useful.”

“And what might that be?” Maiya asked, almost fearful.

“Inter-realm subspatial storage,” Yuma said. “It will keep your orbs permanently charged and always ready to deploy. You need not even retrieve them physically, as they may fire directly from the storage. You may direct and shape the prana from within the dimensional pocket itself, allowing precise, targeted S-rank magic. Something that would otherwise be impossible for you.”

Maiya’s mouth fell open. “That means—”

“It means,” Yuma said gently, “you may finally wield S-rank magic in a focused manner against a single foe, rather than the vast, indiscriminate area it was originally designed for. You must understand that orbs, especially of the lower ranks, are toys for our children. Tools for learning and safe demonstrations of magic for the young. That you would wield them as your primary combat art is quite surprising to us.”

Maiya nearly choked. “Those orbs can destroy entire cities in our universe.”

Yuma’s brow furrowed, her smile fading. “I am sorry to hear that. Both our bodies and our buildings are so naturally reinforced that such limited power would be a minor inconvenience at best, though I do admit that several complain of the annoyance of young ones deploying A Rank magic in populated centers.”

Vir wasn’t even surprised anymore. Imperium magic went well past the realm of godly and bordered on the absurd.

“And your weapons,” Yuma continued, gesturing to her left, where yet more weapons materialized out of thin air. Daggers, short swords, long swords, spears—there was an entire arsenal awaiting Maiya, whose eyes sparkled with greed.

“As I understand it, you are proficient with a wide variety of weapons. All of these match the quality of your husband’s—each capable of storing and augmenting prana. Think of them as another form of orb, able to channel your will into specialized magical effects.”

Maiya swallowed. “This is… this is unbelievable.”

Vir was at least glad he wasn’t the only one present lacking for words.

These weapons alone were more valuable than the sovereignty of even the greatest nations.

Yet these gifts, as impressive as they were, served to show the scale of the responsibility that rested on their shoulders. Did he deserve this? Could he shoulder the expectations of an entire universe—and another one besides?

He knew the answer.

They had to.

Not just for the Garga, or the demon realm, or the human realm.

Not just for the Ash.

But for two universes, and perhaps many more. All of which now depended on his actions.

In a way, it made things simple. Vir quite simply had to succeed. There was no other option.
