"Oh shit."

That was the first thing Aodhán thought as Vaymoz's voice rolled across the rooftop, the fury in it unmistakable.

He glanced toward the cocoon of crackling lightning, then to Amoz, who had already stepped forward, his expression grave as he faced the man whose city they had just destroyed.

What could he possibly say?

"I'm sorry. We destroyed your city. We awakened your only heir without her knowledge or consent. And we're taking her back to the Storm Sect to spend the rest of her life alongside the very people responsible for all this."

None of that sounded remotely acceptable.

There was no apology large enough to bury the mountain of suffering they had unleashed upon this city. No explanation that could soften reality. No justification that wouldn't ring hollow.

But Amoz already knew that. So he didn't offer an explanation or attempt an apology.

Instead, he met Vaymoz's bloodshot gaze, held it through a long, crushing silence, then spoke in a voice stripped of all pretense.

"Yelena... is alive."

Vaymoz froze.

Then he shuddered, every trace of anger on his face vanishing beneath a wave of relief so overwhelming his knees nearly gave way. He drew a ragged breath, one trembling hand rising instinctively to his chest as though trying to steady a heart that had only just remembered how to beat.

"...Thank the heavens."

The prayer escaped him before he could stop it, hoarse enough that he barely recognized his own voice. But it didn't matter.

His daughter was alive.

Grateful tears stung his eyes as the terror that had been driving him finally loosened its grip. In that moment, nothing else mattered. Not his city. Not his anger.

Only relief.

But the emotion barely lasted a moment as his gaze swept across the ruined rooftop, searching desperately for the familiar silhouette of a stubborn, raven-haired girl and coming up empty.

He looked once. Then twice, fear rising within him with each passing second.

"Where is she?" he asked an instant later, his voice cracking with worry as the relief surging through him stuttered. His eyes settled on Amoz, his anger returning as a hundred dreadful possibilities crowded his mind at once.

"Where is my daughter?" he demanded again, his voice a growl of fury and terror. "I swear to every god that still listens—if you've done something to her—"

"We haven't harmed her," Amoz said, raising a hand to placate him.

"Then where is she?" Vaymoz shot back, taking a step forward. "Where have you taken her? What have you done?"

Amoz's jaw tightened. He glanced briefly toward Cephas before looking back at Vaymoz.

"Your daughter is alive," he repeated carefully. "She was gravely injured, and we—"

"Where. Is. She?"

Vaymoz ground out, each word striking like a hammer.

He didn't shout. He didn't need to.

He simply stood there, glaring at Amoz as though the vast gulf of power between them didn't exist. Amoz regarded him for a moment, irritation rising within him before he squashed it, and turned slowly to face the crackling cocoon.

"Your daughter is in there. Healing from her injuries."

"Bring her out," Vaymoz said immediately, stalking toward the cocoon. "We have healers of our own. Whatever you've done, they can finish it. I want my daughter out of there. Now."

His voice rolled across the rooftop, firm with authority, heavy with anger.

Yet no one moved.

The Mythics glanced uneasily toward Amoz, waiting for him to respond. Rezin and Selia looked on impassively, as though they had known this exact situation would happen. And Aodhán…

Aodhán clasped his hands behind his back, watching carefully to see how Amoz would navigate this impossible conversation.

"Did you not hear me, cultivator?" Vaymoz demanded, his eyes hardening. "I said bring me my daughter."

"I would advise you to mind your tone, mundane," Verity said coldly, unable to take the disrespect any longer. "Accord us some respect. We saved your daughter's life."

"And destroyed half my city!" Vaymoz fired back without missing a beat, his anger now on full display. "Now give her back. I want nothing to do with you people. My doctors can heal her."

"Your doctors cannot heal her," Amoz replied sternly, his words cutting through Vaymoz's anger like a blade. He took a measured step forward, his expression heavy with regret. "I'm sorry, Vaymoz, but there was only one way to save her. Her injuries were too severe."

"How severe?" Vaymoz frowned, his gaze darting first toward Verity before drifting toward the cocoon of lightning.

His eyes lingered there a beat. Then understanding struck, and the colour drained completely from his face.

"No…" He took an involuntary step backward, shaking his head in disbelief. "No…"

Amoz took another step forward. "Vaymoz—"

"No!" Vaymoz roared, his face contorting with horrified fury. "How dare you? How could you? Yelena would never have agreed to this. She hates your kind too much. Ever since she was a little girl..." His voice faltered before hardening again. "She would rather die."

His eyes locked onto Amoz's.

"How?" he demanded. "How did you convince her?"

Amoz grimaced, his expression twisting uncomfortably. And for the first time since arriving at Aodhán's door, he suddenly looked less like a Calamity, and more like a man simply carrying the weight of an unforgivable choice.

"I…" His eyes flickered briefly toward the other Calamities before returning to Vaymoz. "She was dying. There wasn't time. I had to do something to save her."

"By awakening her?" Vaymoz roared.

Amoz grimaced, but nodded. "Yes."

The sound that escaped Vaymoz was somewhere between a laugh and a sob. He stared at Amoz in utter disbelief before his face twisted with fury.

"You made that choice for her?" he asked, his voice trembling. "You decided she would spend the rest of her life as one of you?"

"I decided she would spend the rest of her life alive," Amoz replied, not expecting that he would also have to convince Vaymoz he'd made the right choice.

"She was dying," he snapped, anger finally slipping into his voice. "I had to do something, or would you have preferred that she died? Should I have stood there and watched because I feared what she might have chosen?"

The answer he received was not one he had been prepared for.

"Yes!" Vaymoz shouted, tears spilling freely down his face. "Yes! Because it was her choice to make—not yours!" His voice broke, yet he forced the words out anyway. "Her mother—my wife—was killed in front of her by cultivators. She spent fifteen years hating everything you and your kind represent. Do you think for one moment that she would rather die than become like you? Than be turned into something she's hated for more than a decade?"

Amoz's expression faltered. "I… life is important—"

"You don't care about life!" Vaymoz roared. "You and your kind slaughter each other in wars like animals. You do not care about life!" He gestured wildly around him. "Look at my city. Look at what is left of it in less than a day. All because I let you in. Is this the life you care about?"

Amoz looked down, his guilt weighing heavily on him.

"You are monsters!" Vaymoz continued, jabbing a finger forward. "All of you. Our lives mean nothing to you. We are just statistics to you all."

"If that was so," Amoz growled, fury lighting up his face. "I wouldn't have saved your daughter and wasted a fucking seed!"

"My daughter would rather die than become what you will make her with your mercy!"

"I just wanted one less death after all the destruction of today," Amoz snapped, his anger rising and his aura lashing out before he leashed it once more. Drawing a breath, he raised a hand, then slowly let it fall.

"So many have died today. It's our fault. I didn't want to add your daughter to the list."

"Well, that's just the thing, isn't it?" Vaymoz sneered, his entire body practically vibrating with anger. "It's not about you or your blasted, futile guilt. This is about my daughter, and I will not let it continue."

He spun toward the lightning cocoon before Amoz could respond, his gaze meeting Aodhán's before sliding away to glare at everyone else.

"Terminate it now!" he commanded, completely unafraid. "I will not let my daughter become that which she hates to sate your guilt. Terminate it, and take your blasted seed out of my daughter."

"We cannot do that."

Calamity Rezin replied quietly, his tone edged with a distinct sense of reproach as he glared at Amoz. "Her soul was torn in two. If we terminate her awakening now, she will die. Is that what you want?"

Vaymoz hesitated, conviction and fear warring on his face. On one hand, Yelena would rather die than live as a cultivator. But if terminating her awakening now meant death, how could he do that?

Tears stinging his eyes, he stretched a hand to the crackling cocoon. "She will hate it. She will hate herself. Is there no way to free her from this fate? Please. I will beg. Is there no other way?"

"There is none," Selia whispered, her tone sympathetic. "Ending the awakening now would waste both her life and the sacrifice made to preserve it. She will die, and we would have lost what we came for." A flicker of disappointment crossed her face. "For what little comfort it is worth... I truly am sorry."

The tears stinging Vaymoz's eyes fell then, and a sob ripped its way from his throat. He collapsed to the ground beside the cocoon, eyes tightly shut to stem his pouring tears. But they refused to stop.

A heavy silence settled over the rooftop, pressing down on everyone present as they watched him with a mix of expressions, many sympathetic, others impassive. Amoz, however, was the most burdened of all, head bowed and shoulders slumped in defeat.

It was obvious this wasn't what he had expected from Vaymoz. But more than that… it was obvious he had finally realized he had made a mistake giving the seed to Yelena.

He glanced at Aodhán, meeting his carefully blank expression, and saw what seemed to be remorse in the Calamity's eyes.

He looked away after a moment, then took another step toward Vaymoz.

"I'm sorry, old friend," he whispered, placing a hand gently on Vaymoz's shoulder. Vaymoz threw it off as though it burned him.

"I'm not your friend," he growled, turning on Amoz with undisguised hatred. "I am nothing to you. To all of you."

Amoz held his gaze for a second before drawing a weary breath.

"Then I'm sorry, Vaymoz. My people warned me against it, but I thought I was doing you a favour. That by saving her, I could somehow make up for all the lives we took today." He took in a ragged breath. "This was not the outcome I intended when I came asking for permission to search your city. I had no idea just how much your daughter hated cultivators or why she did. I am sorry."

The hatred in Vaymoz's eyes, however, did not diminish.

And after a while, Amoz sighed. "Your daughter isn't gone. She may be changed, but she isn't dead."

"No," Vaymoz swallowed, his voice faltering as a fresh wave of tears came. "She'll just awaken as something she despises, wielding powers she spent her life cursing, and bound to the very people she dreamed of killing."

His jaw clenched and his eyes hardened. "Yes, you heard me. After her mother died, hatred consumed her. She wanted revenge so badly it took years before she learned how to live with it." His voice cracked. "And now you've made her one of you. The very thing she despises."

Aodhán grimaced, and beside him, Crixis muttered a quiet curse beneath his breath.

Vaymoz turned back toward the cocoon. He tried to steady himself, but the effort lasted only a heartbeat before his shoulders sagged once more.

It was a heartbreaking sight, and after watching in silence for several moments, Aodhán found himself looking away, unable to bear it any longer.

As angry as he was—as much as he'd wanted the legacy seed for his mother—he didn't want Yelena to die either. He would have been glad for her survival, had it not required such a hefty sacrifice from him—but the truth was, even without Calamity Amoz's coercion, could he truly have stood by and watched her die?

A part of him said yes. Another said no.

Aodhán swallowed, his gaze straying to Severian, the true reason for all this, before drifting back to Vaymoz.

No one spoke as they watched him cry, mourning a daughter who still lived, yet whose life had been so irrevocably changed it was like night and day.

Several minutes passed in heavy silence, broken only by the crackle of lightning and the weight of Vaymoz's despair. The silence lasted until Vaymoz wiped his tears and looked up at Amoz, despair burning in his eyes.

"You will regret this one day," he whispered. "Yelena has always hated cultivators, and after this, she'll hate you all even more." He shook his head. "If you think you've gained an asset today, I invite you to think again. Because I assure you, you haven't."

He stood up then and simply left, shoulders slumped in dejection and resignation as he walked past Amoz without saying anything else. They all watched him as he left, climbing down a pile of debris until he was finally out of sight.

A long beat of silence stretched above the rooftop, and after a moment, Calamity Rezin spoke. "I hope you've learned your lesson, Amoz. The High Elders will not be pleased when they learn of this."

Amoz winced slightly, his shoulders seeming to slump further.

When he offered no reply, Selia gave Severian's chain a sharp tug before turning to the Mythics restraining Malverin and Emily.

"Hand them over," she said, her tone even. "I will return to the sect while the rest of you remain here until our newest special disciple awakens."

The Mythics obeyed without a word, and as she grabbed the extra chains, she turned to look at Amoz. "When I arrive in the sect, I will brief the High Elders of all that happened here. They will have reached a decision before you arrive." Her expression softened slightly. "You should prepare yourself."

Amoz nodded once. And Selia turned to leave.

Before she could, however, Aodhán spoke, his gaze fixed on Severian. "Tell the High Elders this soul cultivator has the ability to sense legacy seeds too. Perhaps, before they are even formed. He should be instrumental in finding me a new seed."

Severian looked at him, holding his gaze a brief moment before looking away.

"I will let them know." Selia nodded slowly. "And I'm sure the High Elders will certainly come up with something good for their bail. The sacrifice you have made today will not be in vain, Aodhán."

Aodhán nodded, feeling a slight sense of relief. "I appreciate that."

She gave him a slight smile, and an instant later, she vanished, teleporting straight to the Storm Sect. The tension above the rooftop did not decrease with her absence.

Rather, it grew heavier.

After a moment, though, Calamity Cephas let out a weary sigh and walked to Amoz's side.

"What is done cannot be undone," he said quietly. "It seems you and I are still more tethered to our mortal hearts than we care to admit. We are farther from ascension than either of us believed."

Amoz didn't smile at the subtle joke, and Cephas inclined his head before turning toward Aodhán.

"We will find you another seed, Special Disciple Aodhán. Whichever one of us finds it first. Us or the soul cultivator. But I give you my word—we will not stop searching until we make this right."

Aodhán nodded. "Thank you, Calamity Cephas. I'm glad to hear it."

Cephas looked away, and Aodhán frowned faintly, wondering what would happen if both the soul cultivator and Calamity Amoz began searching for soul seeds on his behalf. Could he somehow end up with two?

The thought sparked a flicker of hope, but he crushed it before it could take root, lifting his gaze as Calamity Cephas spoke once more.

"Whether born of compassion or error, the choice has been made," he said quietly, speaking mainly to Amoz. "We must all bear its consequences. Some of us more than others."

Amoz looked toward the cocoon again, his eyes lingering there before he nodded. "Then I shall begin by accepting responsibility under the Accords for everyone present."

The Mythics glanced sharply at him, their eyes wide in shock. But Amoz was resolved.

He glanced back at Aodhán, runic irises flaring slightly.

"I truly am sorry, Aodhán," he said quietly. "When enough time has passed... I hope you will find it in your heart to forgive me."

Aodhán held his gaze for a second before nodding. "I will if you find me another seed as quickly as possible."

Amoz clenched his jaw, then nodded. "I will find you another seed very soon. I swear on it."
