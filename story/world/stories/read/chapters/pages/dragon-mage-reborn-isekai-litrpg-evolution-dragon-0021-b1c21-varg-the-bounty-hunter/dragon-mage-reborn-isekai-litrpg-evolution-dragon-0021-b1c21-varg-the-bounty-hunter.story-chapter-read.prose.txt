I was able to relax better in a cozy bed opposed to camping it out in the woods. Even though I was still the size of a chicken, Nolan respected the fact that I was technically a teenage girl, and I appreciated my personal space. He got a two bedroom suite, and while simple, it was enough to satisfy us for the night.

I curled up on my bed, my dragon scales glittering faintly in the dim light of the room, and Nolan moved to his own bed. The room was quiet, peaceful too, making it a perfect environment to try out what Wilna had taught me before bed. I needed to get comfortable with my darkness, and harmonizing with my domain was the way to do it. So I closed my eyes just as Nolan’s snoring kicked in and dove right into it.

I was never good at meditation. Anxiety was my best friend, alongside Miss Stress and Mr. Pressure. The idea of shutting everything around me off to focus on nothingness was foreign to me, except this wasn’t nothingness…. It was much deeper and more involved than that.

Blackness was the void, and the void was inside of me. Darkness was my domain, and my conduit to necromancy.

It was important to coexist peacefully with my darkness. So, I breathed deeply and tried to let go of all thoughts as I drifted into the depths of my consciousness.

Static rippled in front of my closed eyes, followed by warmth and the feeling of being swallowed in. I was standing along the edge of my domain, rimming the outskirts of my inner realm. My anxieties and fears swirled around me like dusky phantoms, echoing with the hushed whispers of my past. But I knew these were only shadows of my darkness’s willpower trying to push me away.

Wilna warmed me about the pushback, and I was prepared to overcome it as she’d told me.

I allowed myself to sink further in, diving deeper into the abyss of my consciousness. The arms of darkness wrapped around me, but it wasn’t a friendly embrace. In fact, I felt like I was suddenly drowning. The waves were strong and relentless, threatening to pull me under and consume me. My heart pounded in my chest, my breath hitched in my throat. But I managed to remind myself that this was a test—a confrontation with the darkness within me.

Wilna said it wouldn’t be easy, but honestly, I think she had been sugar coating it. This power was trying to exploit my fears and any mental weaknesses to keep itself separated from me. My insecurities and traumas were being peeled in layers, and the longer I stayed here, the more terrified I started to become.

Fighting to stay here was incredibly hard. Every part of me wanted to leave this space. But instead of fighting it, I needed to accept it and understand it. To become allies with it rather than adversaries.

So instead of struggling against the waves threatening to pull me into their depths, I closed my eyes and exhaled slowly, letting go of my fear. I allowed myself to succumb, to be swept away by the darkness. I felt it surround me, fill me, and suddenly, I was hit with a cold sweat.

No, no, no, no, no! This was a bad idea, I needed to leave! But the pull was so strong that every effort I gave in escaping made me plunge deeper into it! I started to panic. Overwhelmed, I cried out, but my voice seemed to be swallowed by the darkness itself, making me feel even more isolated. I was spinning in a vortex of mental strain, pain, and distress. This felt like that one time I’d experience sleep paralysis, but ten times worse. I felt like if I stayed here much longer, that I’d die from my own fear. Just when I thought I couldn’t endure any more, I heard a voice echoing from outside of my domain, followed by a touch that made me jolt.

My eyes snapped wide open, and I found my voice again.

‘Who, what, where?!’ I blared, my head zipping left and right.

“Relax, it’s just me,” Nolan said with a cigarette hanging between his lips. “What shitstorm of a dream were you having? I found you shaking on the floor. You alright?”

I took a second to process where I was, noticing the blinds with slits of sunlight teaming through. It was morning already…. I must not have realized when I’d gone to sleep.

I thought I was meditating, not sleeping.

Somehow, I found my way onto the floor on my back.

Nolan scooped me up and plopped me on my bed, looking at me lost. He was waiting for me to respond but I was so exhausted, both mentally and physically, that I could barely string together a coherent sentence.

‘I’m fine,’ I said simply.

He blinked at me, took a long drag of his cigarette, and then exhaled in a cloud of smoke. He didn’t push for answers or explanations. Instead, he threw his things together and said, “Time to head out.”

I was still rattled from my first trip into my realm. What I thought would be a personal encounter turned out to be something too powerful for me to overcome right now.

My experience left me feeling a tad bit defeated, but I didn’t dwell on it for much longer. So what if I didn’t harmonize with my inner darkness? I couldn’t expect to get it on the first try. Next time would be different. For now, I wanted to focus on going to the café so Nolan could get his next assignment. The sooner we hit the trails, the faster I could level up.

To our surprise, our ride wasn’t tied down to the post this morning. Apparently, it was stolen, even though Nolan refused to believe it. He made a small fit about it, cursing under his breath about the audacity of the thieves. How ironic. I couldn’t blame him, though. He was possessive about his belongings and so was I. Unfortunately there was nothing we could do about it with the lack of horses on this side of town. The stables were empty.

And so, we had to continue our journey on foot. This time I traveled by his side opposed to being tucked away inside a wolf pelt. The sun was barely over the horizon, but the walk was long. Not because the café was far, but because Nolan had some errands to run.

First, he had to restock on ammo.

The town’s arms dealer, a burly man named Jok, had a place along the outskirts of Ebor. Walking into his workshop felt like stepping into a medieval armory. Rows upon rows of iron-clad shelves were stacked with everything from rifles and pistols to blades of every conceivable size. Jok himself towered over most men, his ferocious scowl only softened by a pair of twinkling blue eyes and a black beard that reached down to his barrel chest.

Then, Nolan had to trade a few magical items from his inventory.

The Enchanted Shelf—a petite, gold-eyed maiden named Esme ran the place. She was giving me clear neko vibes, her and her big sister Mesalla. If I had to guess, Esme was no older than 35. Her long silky brunette hair was braided, with the tip of the pony tail reaching her waist. She wore thin but big and round framed glasses that added to the scholarly look, giving her persona a very studious and down to business flair.

Mesalla seemed a bit distant when Nolan walked in, preoccupied with fetching products for orders, whereas Esme engaged Nolan in small talk. She said something about being particular about not touching Esomi’s shelf, which led me to believe that there may be another worker in the shop? Maybe a friend, or another sister–who knows. This was a family business after all.

Apparently, Nolan was a regular customer to both Jok and Esme. Besides the myriad of potions and magical scrolls, the shop was cluttered with other magical items like enchanted rings, amulet necklaces, and power-infused crystals of various sizes and colors. The air in the small shop was dense with magic scrolls, causing a tingling sensation on my skin. It was fascinating to learn that Esme was no regular alchemist. She specialized in creating the scrolls that humans, beastkin, goblins, and orcs used to capitalize on their mana.

After Nolan exchanged a few things for some scrolls, we were finally going to make our way to the café. I looked around and realized that we were around the plaza we’d left not too long ago. As a loyal customer, Nolan didn’t mind making that special trip to his favorite vendors.

According to him, we weren’t too far from the café shop, so I didn’t bother asking for a food break. Despite that though, Nolan was trailing, walking a little slower than usual. He was subtle about it, but the gunslinger was looking over his shoulders like we were being followed….

‘Everything alright?’ I asked, finally breaking the silence.

Nolan lifted a finger to his lips, signaling me to be quiet. The streets were crowded, yet he seemed convinced that we were being stalked. His usual casual demeanor was replaced by a vigilant anxiety, his eye flitting to every corner and alleyway we passed.

Then just as suddenly, the streets cleared. Everyone began retreating to their shops, their homes, and any place where they could take cover. The hum of life in the town got replaced by heavy silence, so thick you could cut it with a knife. It was as if the townsfolk had been alerted of an impending danger….

‘We need to move,’ Nolan said to me privately, but it was too late. From the dust came a group of three figures as they approached us, Nolan’s hand already hovering over his gun holster. The one in the middle was stupid tall! Which wouldn’t have been too bad if he wasn’t beefy, too. All three of them were wearing hooded cloaks, but I could tell the big brute in the middle was some type of bull anthro, eer, I mean, beastkin.

They stood a few meters directly across from us in the middle of the plaza, all intimidating like. Nolan, who stood by me, wasn’t about the business of leaving anymore, ready to challenge these three guys if they decided to get froggy. And knowing how terrible Nolan’s diplomatic skills were, there’s no doubt that we were about to throw hands.

The middle one pulled his hood down, revealing his mean mug underneath those two sharp bull horns. His fur was dark brown, and his eyes were solid black, in stark contrast to the golden ring that pierced his broad nostrils. His companions, dwarfed by his height, followed suit, exposing their faces—a bunny girl archer, judging by the quiver poking out from behind her, and a lizard boy, who was shorter than her, holding a long wooden scepter.

The big guy grunted. “Well, if it isn’t Mr. Black himself,” he said in a deep raspy voice. “If I didn’t know any better, I’d say you got yourself a little body guard.”

His eyes turned to me, and I straightened up real quick. For once, I wasn’t referred to as food or a pet!

Nolan snorted. “Sorry, can’t say that I recognize you from the back of any milk cartons. And that ugly mug hiding behind that giant septum ring is one face I’d never forget.”

The bull’s smile waned at his witty remark. From here, I could see the vein on his forehead beginning to bulge.

“Mighty clever tongue you got there, Black. But you won’t be sporting jokes for long. Word on the street is that there’s a 3000 gold bounty on your head! Now, you might be one slippery bastard to snatch, but they don’t call me Varg the Deadly, for nothing. We aim to collect.”

The corner of Nolan’s lips curled up in a smirk, his fingers drumming on the grip of his gun. “You and every other low-life scum, I bet. You think you’re the first who’s tried to cash me in? Trust me, it never ends well.”

The big bull snorted, sending little puffs of dust swirling into the air around him. “Hell, I’m not worried about you, Black! I’m not sweating a dirty rough-necked vagabond from O’Himer Town. You’re just like the rest of those weak-spined humans. Easily disposable. Ain’t that why you hanging around a stronger race? It’ll make no difference, the end result will remain the same. I’ll pack you up like the rest. However, that new friend you got over there has gotten me curious. I know you’re desperate, but a dragon around these parts is rare. What dungeon did you snatch that from?”
