The golem's visor flared brighter, and the corridor went red.

Reyana had exactly enough time to shout a warning before the next volley arrived.

Trish—still holding her like she was a sack of particularly interesting groceries—simply pivoted on one heel and was somewhere else by the time the beams landed.

"Put me down!" Reyana said through clenched teeth.

"But you're so much fun to carry!" Trish shrugged and let her go. Her grin had the wide, unguarded quality of a child showing off a new toy, and it had been telling Reyana—for about the last forty seconds—that she was enjoying herself rather more than the situation warranted. "Like a furious kitten with knives!"

"DON'T. EVER. PICK. ME. LIKE. THAT!"

"Focus, girlie!" Trish chirped, pointing past her shoulder. "I'd rather not have my bestie die so soon!"

Heavens above! She’s another Joe.

Reyana clicked her tongue and decided—in the absence of any better options, and with her back literally against a cracked corridor wall—that it was far easier to focus on the eight-foot murder-golem screaming "exterminate" on repeat than to unpack whatever relationship Trish thought they were building.

"EXTERMINATE THE INTRUDERS. EXTERMINATE!"

Seriously. This thing was getting more annoying with every passing second.

After another failed volley of essence beams, the golem stopped. Its visor dimmed fractionally, and an audible click-click-click of mechanical shifting rippled across its frame as waves of essence pulsed outward from its core.

“EXTERMINATE!”

From the gaps between its armor on the shoulders, emerged barrels—six that Reyana could count—each one shining up with a low, building whine.

"PHASE TWO INITIATED. THREAT LEVEL: ELEVATED. EXTERMINATION PRIORITY: ABSOLUTE."

"Oh," Reyana said quietly. "Oh, it can’t be good."

"Isn't it pretty?" Trish breathed, wide like a child at a fireworks display. She then pointed to the shoulders and the turrets. "Can you do all that in the dark?"

"No. And you can have all the golems after we kill it," Reyana said sharply. “On that note, why don’t you kill it already?”

"What's the point of keeping broken things?" Trish pouted. "Oh well. I guess that would still be better than nothing."

The barrels flared red with essence surging around them in a vortex.

Reyana gave the women one last look—who just smiled and shrugged—and dropped into her shadows before the roar of turrets started.

The world folded around her into the familiar, cold, dark, inky shadow realm, which she liked to the inbetween. But this time, even the inbetween wasn't quiet.

She felt the impact of every essence bullet like fists drumming against her skin, and the sensations rattled her connection to the skill, almost threatening to spit her out.

Hold, she growled at the darkness. Fucking hold.

She twisted through inbetween and popped out ten feet to the golem's flank, fortunately still hidden behind the lip of a broken support pillar.

The machine's sensors—whatever method of targeting it had—were locked onto Trish, who was currently sprinting along the corridor wall, then up the wall, then along the ceiling somehow, always moving faster than the rain of bullets chasing her.

Reyana pulled her longsword, and the moment her fingers closed around the leather-wrapped hilt, her aura surged, and she fed it and essence into the blade.

The cold kiss of death from her mantle found her immediately—the familiar pressure in her chest, the distant noise of a thousand ending heartbeats—but it was quieter now than it had been before she met with Jin and Rudy.

Release restrictions by twenty-five percent.

The necklace at her throat warmed.

The seals on her mantle allowed a trickle of death essence in a steady flow, which she diverted immediately into the hungry blade.

The sword drank it down greedily, its runes pulsing along its blade in muted silver.

Nae… nae… nae…

Uul… vethra… nae…

And underneath it all, she heard the voices.

She'd been hearing them since her fight with Nourma. Joe had warned her about it—the step after manifestation and aura, Rey, your mantle becomes a conversation—and what she was hearing these incomprehensible voices were her mantle’s words.

Nae… Ooooo–vael… droth… Nae…

She gritted her teeth, filtering the whispers, tuning them into background noises.

Control above everything, Rey. Control.

Her breathing evened as the silence took over. She had to act soon.

Her eyes found Trish—currently somersaulting off a ceiling strut to land crouched on top of one of the golem's spinning barrels, one hand pressed to the metal like she was asking it a question—and Reyana had zero doubts she would be fine. That woman, or whatever the hell she was, was more than capable.

Which meant the only reason Trish hadn't killed it in the first ten seconds was the same reason she hadn't killed Reyana.

It was an excellent source of entertainment.

Trish was bored, and the golem was interesting.

Reyana had briefly, desperately considered just running. Letting Trish get bored and wander off. But Trish had already demonstrated—twice—that she could grab Reyana by the back of the collar and physically drag her back into the fight, and a third demonstration seemed unnecessary.

Focus, Rey.

The golem screamed EXTERMINATE with what Reyana could only describe as genuine frustration, and from its back a dark disc unfolded. Then another. And another. Six of them in total, detaching from hidden housings along its shoulders and hovering in formation around its frame like a halo of very rude moons.

"Oh, that's new," Trish yelled over the noise. "I love it. I love it so much. Reyana, help me catch one!"

I will absolutely not help you catch one, Reyana screamed mentally.

"Reyrey! C'mon, why are you so unhelpful?"

The discs fired.

Two of them arced toward Trish, who caught both out of the air with gleeful squeaks. The Golem screamed something, and the remaining four banked sharply in unison and turned towards her.

Shoot. She'd been hoping to stay invisible.

Reyana could feel a dense amount of essence gathering in the disc, and there could only be one reason.

Reyana dropped back into the shadows, and this time she fell down, plunging through the inbetween toward the floor below. The discs passed through the empty air, embedded themselves in the corridor wall, and began to vibrate.

A high, rising whine, building toward something—

"Hmm? What's this?" Trish's voice chirped above the noise. "It even has a massage function! Look, Rey—"

The corridor went white.

The force came a half-second after the light—a concussive wave of essence—that landed through the inbetween and onto her like a hammer.

Reyana's skill destabilized mid-fall as her connection to the darkness was shredded.

She hit the floor below in a graceless heap.

"Fucking flashbangs," she hissed through her teeth.

Still, it was fortunate she hadn't been spat out while she was still between floors. That would have been a worse problem than the one she was currently having.

BOOM.

The ceiling above her exploded, and among the raining debris, Reyana pulled herself toward the wall just in time for Trish to land squarely on top of her like an inconvenient blanket.

For a full second, all she could see was Trish's face three inches from hers, grinning down.

"Get. Off."

"In a second."

"Trish."

"Shh. I'm enjoying the moment."

"TRISH. Go kill the damn golem."

"Fine, fine." Trish sprang upward off her. "You really need to learn to relax during a fight. It's all so tense with you."

Reyana hauled herself up. "I'll relax when that thing is dead, and I find Jin and others."

"That's the wrong attitude," Trish informed her. "But yeah. We do need to find the little thief."

I need to keep her away from him.

A loud, resonant gong sounded above them, and through the hole, Reyana saw the golem planting its feet in the remains of the floor as its chest plates opened, revealing… a single massive cannon.

"That's going to be a big one," Trish observed.

"Yes."

"We should probably move."

"Yes. It doesn’t look like the Goleń could move from that position, so let—"

"Nah, let's beat whatever's coming with the power of friendship." Trish pumped her fist in the air with genuine enthusiasm.

"I'll leave you to it then—"

Reyana pulled on her shadows to vacate the premises, but before the darkness could swallow her, Trish's hand closed around her wrist—gentle, the way a parent guided a child's hand, and absolutely immovable—and pulled her back.

The next thing Reyana knew, Trish had spun her around to face the hole in the ceiling, wrapped both arms around her from behind, and was very deliberately positioning her longsword in front of both of them like they were posing for a painting.

"What the fuck are you doing?!" Reyana snarled. "Let go!"

"Nuh-uh," Trish said into her ear, infuriatingly close. "I've been watching you fumble around your power like a lost kitten for the last… dunno, twenty minutes? You've been given such a beautiful gift, and yet you waste it. Waste and waste and waste it. It's painful to watch. Like watching someone try to cut bread with a spoon."

"Trish. Now is not the time—"

"No, babe. Now is exactly the time."

Reyana tried to twist free, but Trish's grip was ironclad, and above them the cannon was visibly charging… amber light racing along its barrel, the air in the corridor thickening with the pressure of gathered essence, the temperature dropping as ambient heat was sucked toward the weapon's firing chamber.

Helldiver's mark should pull me out, if this gets dicey, Reyana told herself, and forced her body to stop fighting.

She doesn’t have any ill will towards me… fuck it, what’s the worst that could happen?

She had a terrible feeling she was going to regret this.

"Good girl," Trish murmured. "Now, listen."

The voice at her ear shifted. It was still Trish, but it had undergone a small shift. For the first time, Trish was calm and serious.

"Death is inevitable."

“Even the immortals die, even the planets die, and even the sun dies.”

"Death is inevitable."

"And you are its agent, Rey. Not its victim. Not its prisoner. Its agent."

And suddenly it wasn't Trish's voice she was hearing—Or rather—it was Trish's voice, overlaid with another she hadn't heard in years… her mother’s voice.

Reyana's throat tightened.

"Stop," she whispered. "Trish, stop."

"Embrace death," Trish said, and her hands—still wrapped around Reyana's, still guiding the longsword's point toward the cannon above—moved with essence flowing through both of them in patterns that felt right in a way she hadn't experienced in over a decade. "For the sole reason of delivering it to those whose end is near.”

Her mother's voice, again, underneath Trish's: "The mantle isn't a curse, darling. It's a trust… we don't hold death chained back… we give it where it's due."

"I can't—" Reyana's voice cracked. "You don't understand, it eats me, every time I let it—"

"Because you're holding onto it," Trish whispered, and the words were definitely Trish's now. "Stop holding it. Let it pass through. You're not a cup. You're a conduit."

Above them, the cannon hit full charge.

The golem's voice boomed through the corridor: "EXTERMINATE!"

Reyana's aura surged.

She didn't do it on purpose. Her body chose her mind caught up, and suddenly the essence she'd been so carefully rationing poured out of her mantle in a steady flood.

It flowed through her… no… the death was pulled through her.

Compelled by the motions Trish was making, her dance.

The death passed through her heart, through her arms, and down the blade of the sword.

A pale blue-silver halo bloomed around her. The cold in her chest eased. The voices quieted, one by one, until there was only a single low hum beneath the rest… death's whisper, patient and attentive, waiting for her instruction.

Control above everything, Rey. Control.

She understood, in that half-second between the cannon firing and her response, what she'd been doing wrong her entire life. She'd been trying to dam the river. When the river just wanted to move through her on its way somewhere else.

The cannon fired.

A beam of compressed amber essence tore through the corridor above and slammed downward through the broken ceiling toward them.

Reyana raised her sword.

A white lotus bud formed at the blade's tip, drawn from the death flowing through her. And as the amber beam hit, the first petal bloomed.

It was slow.

That was the strangest part. The beam was still pouring down on her—she could feel the pressure of it, feel the pain her body was in—but time had gone long and patient.

The second petal bloomed.

Then a third. Then Four. Five.

"Beautiful, isn’t it?" Trish breathed behind her.

Six. Seven. Eight.

The beam was breaking against the lotus. The heat, the force, and the essence dissolved wherever it touched the white petals.

Nine.

Ten.

The lotus bloomed fully.

"Now," Trish whispered, her voice faint and distant. "Deliver its death."

Reyana stepped forward; her body moved with the sword, and the sword moved with the lotus.

She pivoted her hips. Lifted the blade overhead and stabbed the floor.

The white lotus pulsed—slow at first, then faster, then faster—and faded away just as another lotus bloomed under its feet.

All ten petals unfurled, and a pillar of white silence consumed the golem.

Once the pillar faded away, the golem dropped from the air like a puppet with cut strings.

The visor flickered once and then went dark.

The pale halo around Reyana's body retracted back into her core, gentle as breath going out. The necklace at her throat sat warm and quiet against her collarbone. And for the first time in her life—the first time in her entire goddamn life—calling on her mantle hadn't hurt.

She stared at her hands.

They weren't shaking.

Trish, who had apparently been released somewhere during the lotus bloom without Reyana noticing, was sitting cross-legged on the dead golem, applauding.

"BRAVO, see I know you had it in you!”

"…Thanks."

"You're welcome." Trish flopped backward onto the golem. "That was beautiful. We should try this again."

"Please don't."

"Well, at least I'm crying. Tears of a proud teacher."

"You taught me for thirty seconds."

"Best thirty seconds of my century."

Reyana's legs decided for her. She'd been planning to walk over with some dignity remaining, sit beside Trish like a civilized person, but somewhere between intent and execution, her body informed her that dignity was for other people, and she just dropped.

Landed on her ass on the cold stone with her sword across her lap, and every muscle from her neck to her ankles immediately started filing complaints with management.

"Vala's tits," she muttered.

" Language, girl, you won’t find a partner with that foul mouth."

"Not before I end you."

"Mm-hmm. You can try, but I’d say you are still a couple of centuries early."

They sat in silence.

Reyana studied the woman lying on the floor beside her.

She'd been trying to figure out this enigma of a being ever since they started talking, and in the span of maybe ninety minutes, Reyana could say she knew Trish somewhat.

Some of it was a relief.

With reasonable confidence—maybe sixty percent—Trish wasn't their direct enemy. The cult might have brought her into the bastion, might be using her presence as cover or tool or unpredictable wildcard, but Trish herself didn't seem to care about cult business.

She'd ignored cultist objectives. She'd helped Reyana fight a defense system the cult would presumably have wanted active.

She'd called the cultists “those bastards” among many more curses, and she'd been bored by everything except the violence, which she enjoyed regardless of which side was on the receiving end.

What Reyana was one hundred percent certain of, however, was that whatever Trish was, she wasn't human.

Or elf. Or demon. Or any of the dozen other categorized sapient races Reyana had spent her life meeting as a hunter.

"You're staring, babe."

"I'm thinking."

"Same thing in your case."

Reyana exhaled. "Trish."

"Mmm?"

"What are you?"

Trish didn't answer right away. Then she sat up and turned to look at Reyana with her chin propped on one fist.

"That's a complicated question."

"Try."

"I don't actually know."

Reyana blinked. "What?"

"I don't know what I am. Not really. My father never bothered to answer; he just threw us out into the world. Fend for ourselves, that sort of thing." Trish's grin softened.

"Why are you helping me?"

"Because you're interesting." Trish tilted her head. "And because watching someone fight her own mantle for years is depressing, and watching someone stop fighting it is the most fun I've had in a long time. I needed the entertainment."

"That's not a real answer."

"It's a real-enough answer if it helps you; you are obviously friends with that thief."

Reyana decided to push. "The voice you used. When you were guiding me."

"Hmm?"

"It sounded like my mother."

"Did it? That's fun. I was just pulling from whatever my voice needed to sound like to make you listen. Your head decided the rest. What was she like?"

"Kind and everything."

Trish flopped backward onto her elbows.

"Are you going to tell me who you are?" Reyana asked.

"Already did."

"How old are you?"

"Older than the question deserves."

"What were you doing before the cult—"

Reyana stopped.

Because Trish had gone still, and then her face lit up with a grin like that of a child finding a long-lost toy,

"Oh," Trish breathed, and the word carried a delight so pure it was almost frightening. "Oh, there you are."

“What are you?”

"Sorry, Reyrey." Trish bounced to her feet and gave Reyana a quick, almost apologetic salute. "I've got to run. The thief just popped his head up where I can finally see it, and I am not going to miss this chance."

"Trish—"

"It's been fun!" Trish said. "We should do this again sometime. Maybe. If I'm in a good mood. And if your friend doesn't get himself killed before, I can have another go at him."

Her hand came up.

The space in front of her tore open.

“I’ll probably not kill him, just so you know.” Trish winked at her and stepped into the portal.

The seam closed behind her with a soft pop.

The corridor was silent.

"Right," she said to nobody in particular. "Jin... You know what? I bet you can handle her cuz I've had enough."

Then she leaned her head back against the wall, closed her eyes, and let herself rest.

“'Cause now I want someone to find me first.”
