Our path east wound on through an increasingly chilly day. Rocky crags and pine-dotted hills rose and fell around us, each bearing early winter snowbells and scattered patches of dark moss. A crisp blue sky feathered with frosty clouds stretched in every direction. The scenery was lovely but monotonous… until, quite abruptly, it wasn’t.

I trailed behind my companions as we crested a truly mountainous hill, breath coming hard and legs aching, and caught sight of the expanse below.

What lay ahead was a deep valley that seemed to grow more green with every pace. About halfway down the hill was a very long, two-story building with a bright red roof and cheerfully painted shutters. I could just make out distant figures moving to and fro outside. A bit farther down, where the terrain leveled out, sparkling blue water peeked out through the sea of swaying pine boughs.

“Oh, Senna’s Gleam! That’s what the lake’s called, right?” Teela visibly perked up at the sight of civilization, and her voice held a wistful note. “I’ve heard it’s beautiful.”

“Yeah. Named after some runekeeper from ages ago.” Renner started down the path. “We’ll pass it tomorrow, but we’re staying at that inn tonight.

“It’s huge,” I breathed, hurrying after him. There was even a broad stable visible beside the building proper. “I’ve never seen an inn that big. I’ve never even seen a building that big!”

“It’s actually decent. Fishermen and hunters use it as a waypoint, and there’s a Watch outpost nearby. Should be plenty safe.” Renner’s expression wasn’t visible from my position, but there was a distinctly unhappy edge to his final words.

‘Should be,’ indeed. Like Fellbrook, like Snowmelt, like the inn last night, like the burned building we found a few days ago. I don’t know if we can say anywhere is safe, anymore.

“Is that the city? Down in the valley?” Royce was on his tiptoes, one hand shading his eyes as he craned and squinted below. “I see buildings.”

I glimpsed them, too. As the base of the valley, beyond more firs and around a deep bend, there seemed to be a stretch of wall. Brown rooftops and the peaks of buildings were just visible beyond. It looked like the expanse continued around a curve in the terrain.

Renner nodded. “It’s not far. Better to stop here and get a decent night’s rest, though. We can get to the livery, restock, and be back on the road north before midday tomorrow.”

No one argued, and we reached the massive building just in time for supper. We led Barley to the stable, which was dry and well-stocked, and then the four of us filed into a vast common room bright with cheerful firelight and painted wooden tables. The air hung thick with the smells of salt, sweat, and roasting meat.

A dozen unfamiliar, but not unwelcoming, faces turned upon our entrance. We were hailed with broad smiles and friendly greetings, though the expressions faltered a bit when Renner gave descriptions of the pacted from Snowmelt and asked if any of them had been seen. They had not, and he next detailed our encounter with the breathless woman.

“Blood and bones, that sounds like Fiera,” the innkeeper came forward, pressing a hand to her head. She was a sturdy woman with deep lines around her eyes and mouth, ruddy skin, and braided black hair. She wore a faded tunic and long brown skirt, both of which were dusted with fragrant spices. “Poor dear.”

“Does she have any family nearby?” I asked, setting my pack at an empty table. “Anyone in the city we could reach out to?”

The woman’s mouth tightened. “Ah, bless you for offering, but that news’ll come better from a familiar face. Henrick, you’ll let her brother know?”

An older man- Henrick, presumably- with salt-and-pepper hair and a very sharp nose nodded. He wore a leather jerkin and a grim expression. “Aye, I’ll head that way at dawn. Poor woman. And breathless, you said? Ashes.”

“You don’t sound surprised.” Renner frowned.

“We’ve had a few in Sedgewick,” the innkeeper sighed, drifting back towards the bar. “Off and on for… oh, a while now. Terrible thing, and our Watch boys have been dealing with them well enough, but… well, there’s got to be a source to such horror, of course.” A few men and women sitting at tables nearby murmured agreements. Their tones, and the disparaged looks on their faces, indicated that this was a common topic of discussion. “The Thalessians came to put a stop to it, but they haven’t had any luck yet. Not that I’ve heard, at least.”

I nearly dropped my things. “The Thalessians? Wait, there’s paladins here? In the city?”

“Aye, a whole troop.” Wood clattered as she gathered up several bowls, nodding towards a deep black kettle propped over the hearth. “Can I get you lot some supper? I’ve a nice stew warmed.”

“Yes, please!” Royce turned to me, beaming. His blue eyes were positively sparkling in the firelight. He looped an arm around my waist, drawing me towards a corner and lowering his voice as the sounds of chatter picked up around us. “Brin, that’s great! Right? You can give them… I mean, you know.” He nodded towards my pack.

It felt as thought a weight was being lifted from my shoulders. My heart swelled with the sudden, airy lightness of hope. Paladins. Here. Actual holy warriors, people who can help defend us and figure out all this mess. Who might know something about avoiding dreams or escaping pacts or… or any of it! And they’re less than a day away! “Well, yes, it… yes, that’s wonderful news! We can find them tomorrow, as soon as we get to the city! Saints, Royce, actual paladins! People who can help!”

Wood creaked just behind me. A moment later Renner growled, practically at my ear, “Help with what, exactly?”

I turned, startled by the wary edge in his voice. “With… well, with everything, of course,” I whispered, spreading both hands wide. “Ashes, with me!”

He was shaking his head. “With the shard.”

“Yes, of course, but… I mean, that’s only part of it.”

“What are you thinking, Brin?” Teela completed our little huddle, lowering her voice. She had her green brim pulled down and her cloak drawn tight, concealing every trace of her Fae features from the people nearby.

“Isn’t it obvious? I need help, and I’m not going to just find it lying by the side of the road! But if anyone in the realms can get me out of all this-”

“They won’t get you out of anything, ladyship.” Every word was harsh, dropping like a stone at my feet. “They’ll just kill you.”

I winced. “I mean, I won’t just walk up and announce it! But I’ll explain it all; that I didn’t want to, that he made me, that I haven’t hurt anyone, and surely-”

“No.”

“Renner, I listened to your story earlier! The Order marched halfway across the realms to fight, to save people, to protect-”

“There’s a lot of that story I left out.”

I floundered for a moment, gaping at him, then placed both hands on my hips. “I don’t understand. We’re going to Pinnacle. To the Order. This has always been the plan.”

“The plan,” he edged closer, voice low and lethal, “is to deliver the shard. Not march up and tell them what you are.”

“That’s been your plan!” I hissed, throwing both hands up. “That’s the whole reason you came! You wanted to turn me over.”

I regretted the words immediately. They hung between the four of us, stillness in their wake, and Royce went rigid at my side.

“Wait, what?” He looked between us, features creasing. “Turn you over? Why?” No one offered an immediate answer and, in a tone so vicious I jumped, my friend growled, “Why?”

“I had my reasons,” the smaller man snapped, gaze fixed on me.

My fingers curled over my friend’s arm. Even through my gloves, I could feel the furious strain of his bicep. “Salt, I’m sorry, I shouldn’t have said… look, that was before-”

“You had your reasons?” Royce’s handsome, carefree face was dark with anger. “What in the realms does that mean? You just said they’ll kill her!”

“And now I’m telling her to stop and think!” Renner snarled back.

“Her is right here,” I snipped, sliding my way between the two men. “And I am thinking. Saints, I’ve done nothing but think about all this! Renner, I want to tell the Order-”

“No.”

“Would you stop? It’s my decision! And Royce, look-”

“Was he going to tell them?” My friend shouldered past me, hands balled into fists. “Is that true? Salt, Brin, why are we with this prick? After he was going to get you executed?”

“Okay,” Teela hissed, stepping boldly between them. “Why don’t we all just back off for a breath, yeah?” She was met with vicious, frosty silence. “Let’s not have this conversation in the middle of a salted inn!”

That did the trick. My heart hammered as both men eased back, stark anger written across their faces. But they both seemed mollified, at least for the moment, and with another fierce glare Teela seized my hand and tugged me back to our table. She muttered something indiscernible under her breath.

The two of us took our seats. After several long, frozen moments our stone-faced companions stalked over and followed suit. I stared at my hands. Wracked my mind for a way to fix this mess. The air around me practically vibrated with tense, wary heat.

After some time the innkeeper brought over four bowls of thick brown stew. The broth was salty and spiced, rich with chunks of savory roasted meat, and each bowl had a crust of dark bread nestled on top. I ate in wooden silence, hardly tasting each bite, with my gaze fixed firmly on my meal.

“Are you all staying the night?” The innkeeper’s lilting voice made me jump. Someone made a little grunt of assent and she quirked a brow, looking between the four of us. “How many rooms?”

“Four,” I said quickly, reaching for my coinpurse and fumbling out payment. “Please. Will this cover the cost? And for the food, and our horse?”

Her brown eyes swept me from head to toe, doubtlessly taking in my embroidered tunic and fine cloak, as she accepted the glinting coin. “Aye. Rooms are upstairs, doors are numbered. You folks’ll be in four through seven.”

“Thank you.” My throat felt thick. No one else even looked up, and after another glance around the woman sidled away with a swish of her skirts.

As soon as she was out of earshot Royce took his last bite, dropped the wooden spoon down with a clatter, and rose. My heart sank as he grabbed his belongings, heading to the stairs without a word.

After a moment of fidgeting, furiously deliberating how to soothe his obvious temper, I hurried after. Unfamiliar voices chattered quietly around me as I crossed the common room, boots thudding against the wood in time with the beat of my heart.

The back of my neck prickled the whole way.
