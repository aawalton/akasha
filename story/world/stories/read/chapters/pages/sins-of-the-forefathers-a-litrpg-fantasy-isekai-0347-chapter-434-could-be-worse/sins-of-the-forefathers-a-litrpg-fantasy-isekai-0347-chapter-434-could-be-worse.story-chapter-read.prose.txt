Even though Anima had been the one to call this meeting with the ancient, elusive snake Spirit, I could tell that she was unhappy making a deal with him at all. Still, she didn’t protest at the compact I had entered into with Nehushtan, though it almost visibly pained her to hold her tongue.

In a way, I didn’t blame her. There was something…off about Nehushtan. Something about him was different from every single Spirit or proto-Spirit I had ever met. A sense that he was different in some way, and not just because he was genuinely ancient, if he pre-dated the Great Spirits. Frankly, it was probably smart to be wary of him.

I didn’t know what kind of old history there was between the two of them. Between him and the Great Spirits as a whole, apparently. Whatever it was, it must have been significant to drive Nehushtan out of the Concord and into the realm of the physical.

But that didn’t matter. None of it was relevant to our present circumstances. Maybe I would have turned the snake Spirit down, if I had known more. Or maybe I wouldn’t have. It didn’t matter. The only thing that did…

Was breaking through that shield.

Rhoscara was lost. I didn’t know what was going on in the outside world, where I was apparently in a goddamned coma. But I hoped they were smart enough to realize that fact as well.

The forces of the Unchained and the Herztalian expedition couldn’t hope to fight against not only all of the forces of the Savoy, Anguis’s dominated Rhoscaran mind-slaves, and his high-value assets.

Leonard hadn’t taken the field, but I knew for a fact he was a deadly powerful Mage. Grey may have been weakened at the time, but he had still held his own against my mentor in a duel.

And then there was Daffyd.

I…didn’t like thinking about Daffyd. I wasn’t looking forward to the agony I was likely to be in whenever I woke up. The werewolf had flat-out ripped several of my bones from my chest, and had been seconds from tearing my head from my shoulders. With Nyx dead, I was…likely the second strongest member of the expedition, and he had completely no-sold me. If it came to a battle with him again…

I’d…have to hope Alveron still had enough fight left in him, if and when we encountered the Archmage of Clan Calonawr once more.

Anima had told me that she could sort of… jump-start my connection with my body, and strengthen the connection I had to after my brief, apparent death. Nehushtan had already left by this point, not long after I had agreed to seek him out.

Now, it was just Anima and I, here at the border wall of the Concord. There was a…strange look in her eye, as she gazed down at me.

She spoke before I could ask her why she hadn’t woken me up yet.

“Do you know…” Anima said slowly. “Why have I never taken an Envoy of my own, Nathaniel?”

I blinked at the out-of-nowhere question, but decided to humor her. Time passed slowly within the vicinity of the Concord. Once I was awake, no matter my condition, I was going to need to hit the ground running. But until that point, I could spare a few seconds for the one who had done so much for me. “No, I don’t. Why is that?”

A mirthless smile stole across Anima’s lips, then. “Because it would kill them,” She said bluntly. At my raised eyebrow, she continued. “For the others, it’s a simple matter to endow a mortal with an Envoyship. The primacy of their realms is, in the end, elemental. Water, earth, sun, and moon…painting a mortal’s soul in any of these colors merely adds to the tapestry. But to inject more life into them…it’s too much. They mutate, body and soul, in an almost cancerous manner. They overflow with so much life that they cannot take it. The end result will always be a painful, nightmarish death.”

I, tactfully, refrained from asking just how she knew that. It was fairly obvious to me.

“The result is that I am less…present, upon Vereden,” Anima took a breath, closed her eyes, and allowed a peaceful smile to cross her lips. “But I am fine with this. The nature of life itself is such that it would be abhorrent to interfere too deeply. I have had no agents of my own to enact my will in this verdant world. I am marginalized, and I am at peace with that. But…somehow, when I speak to you, Nathaniel…” She opened her eyes and directed an affectionate look at me. “I cannot help but wonder if this is what it feels like, to have had an Envoy of my own.”

My own lips stretched into a smile, and it only grew when Core whispered an idea to me. Both Outer and Middle liked it.

To Anima’s surprise, I stretched out one hand towards her, from my place standing on her right palm, with my own held open before me. “Tell you what. Like you said, I can’t be your Envoy in the traditional way. I can’t accept an endowment from you. But…I could be your Envoy in spirit. A sort of…honorary Envoy, if that makes sense. We’ve already been watching out for each other, in our own way. We can just continue that and call it something official. Well, semi-official.” I chuckled.

Anima reared back as if I had stunned her, and blinked eyelids as large as I was rapidly. After a moment, though, she started to shrink, to my surprise. The entirety of the enormous figure that was Anima compressed itself rapidly, and I yelped when my footing was disrupted. I dropped slightly to float in midair where I had been standing only moments ago.

I…guess Anima had her own gravity in this borderland. But in only moments…

She had shrunk herself until she was the size of a normal, human woman. All of her otherworldly features were still present, of course, so nobody would mistake for a regular person off the streets of Blutstein.

But she was now slightly shorter than me, when only moments before she had been more than ten times that. She floated over and reached out to clasp the hand I still had outstretched before me.

Her grip was tender, her eyes slightly moist, and her smile…

It outshone her own mandala.

“I would like that very much, Nathaniel,” Anima said softly, reaching up to cup our clasped hands with her free one. “Very much indeed.”

You know what? Despite everything that had happened.

Things could be worse.

…………………………

I shot awake as if I had just been electrocuted. My eyes snapped open with a gasp, and my back arched off the ground. Anima hadn’t been kidding when she said she could force me awake. It was like fire was running through my veins, carried by thundering blood. I couldn’t see, and I couldn’t hear from the sensation.

I was consumed with something else. I immediately regretted moving at all.

Every inch of my body was in absolute agony. This was, by far, the worst pain I think I had ever experienced in my life. Which was saying something, but I distinctly remembered the feeling of my fucking arm being crushed to paste. But while my entire body was in pain, there was an area where the pain was most concentrated.

Obviously, it was around my neck and shoulders.

To my alarm, not only was I in unimaginable pain, but I wasn’t able to move my neck, my shoulders, or even my arms, for that matter. All of them felt completely immobilized in a way I had never experienced before. It was almost like most of my upper body had been paralyzed.

What only furthered my panic was the fact that I couldn’t even speak. The only noises I was able to make were some odd gurgling noises from the depths of my frozen throat.

Slowly, haltingly, I was able to concentrate on the abilities granted to my Middle and my Core lattice, and deal with…two of my issues. A wave of artificial calm rolled over me from my Middle Lattice, deadening the panic, while I completely cut my sense of pain with my Core.

With both of those solved…I was able to actually focus on the world around me.

Well.

The very little I could see, from where I lay, flat on my back and unable to move. The only thing I could move was my eyes, and…

I was able to tell very little from what I could see. I appeared to be in a tent of some kind, and not exactly a fancy one. It looked to be small, only big enough for my body to be lying on what felt like a cot of some kind, and made from canvas. It was dim and dark in here, but that wasn’t a problem with my mutated eyes. It didn’t help much, though, because the tent was entirely empty save for me.

I was alone in here, and that…didn’t bode well to me.

It was my ears that were a bigger help, in the end. There was quite a sizeable commotion happening beyond these canvas walls. Rushing, and clamoring, and shouting, and crying filled the air in a cacophony of what sounded like only slightly controlled panic. I could hear dozens, perhaps hundreds of pairs of boots stomping against stone rapidly as their owners ran to and fro. Grunts of effort, the hissing of what sounded like Chamel’s to me, and so many, many voices in various states of distress.

One thing, though, told me just where we might be.

Every noise outside of my tent was echoing, ever so slightly. As if we were underground and enclosed by stone.

I was in the Long Road, I had to be.

If I had the emotional range for it right now, in my numbed state, I would have relaxed.

That had to mean I had been recovered before Daffyd had managed to murder me. I’m…not sure how, but I would be sure to thank whoever had done it profusely.

Turns out…

They were closer than I was expecting.

My observations of the world around me had only taken a rough few seconds, but that seemed to have been more than enough time. In my physically numbed state I had woken up to, I hadn’t been able to feel anything that could have been touching me. And with my neck frozen, I hadn’t exactly been able to see everything around me.

And so, I missed Fade.

Inside my mind, I felt our bond snap into sudden alertness only moments before a great furry head, crowned by a rack of antlers, appeared in my field of vision. The fur of the normally grey Spirit Wolf was blackened slightly, both from soot and from what looked to be singeing. Normally, I would have been relieved to see him, but…

I wasn’t the only one injured.

The entire right side of Fade’s head, including his eye, was bandaged. It didn’t exactly look like a minor injury, considering there was a visible blood seeping through the white of the gauze. Not only that, but it looked like the antler just above that had been snapped in half as well. There was still a single remaining branch of it, but the rest looked to have been splintered away. That, too, had been packed with gauze.

And yet, for all that he had to be in pain as well, Fade looked ecstatic to see me.

“Nate!” I heard his voice echo through my mind. He tried to smile a lupine grin at me before the stretching of his face caused him to wince. “You’re awake! Renauld wasn’t sure you…well…if you would.”

I wanted so badly to reach up and rub his head, but my arms were still frozen in place. By now, I could tell that this had been induced in some way. Probably by the very Gnoll that Fade had just mentioned.

The fact I was being medically paralyzed did not bode well.

I couldn’t even smile at Fade, but I did my best to project one anyway. “It’ll take more than a werewolf to do me in,” I ‘said’ flatly, my emotional numbness causing the joke to fall flat. I gave up on it quickly. But even through that, I couldn’t stop a mote of concern for my Familiar from leaking into my psyche. “I see I’m not the only one who’s hurt. Were…you the one who saved me?”

Fade painfully nodded his head, unbalanced from missing half of his usual rack. “Yeah. I…can influence that transformation, just a bit. Taran taught me how, considering I’m kind of his apprentice. When that guy, Daffyd, I think? Had his claws on you, I jumped in and forced him back into his human state. Faced with Bleddyn and everyone else who ran for you when you got hurt, we drove him off. Nobody has seen him since.”

I blinked at that, pretty much the only movement I could make. He could do that? That…was actually pretty reassuring. If we could keep Daffyd in his Human state the next time we ran into him-

(Because there would be a next time.

I’d make sure of it.)

-he’d make much easier prey.

“But…how did you get hurt, then?”

Fade chuffed lightly, seeming a bit embarrassed. “Ah…he swiped at me, just before turning back. You know how it is with those ‘werewolf’ guys. The transformation both ways takes a minute or so. He…broke my antler, and got my face pretty good. He missed the eye, though!” He said, seemingly trying to reassure me. No doubt he could sense my emotional state and the growing sense of guilt I felt. “Renauld doesn’t know how to treat me, since I’m not one of you bipeds. But I’ll be fine. Daffyd missed my eye. The claw marks will scar, and I’m pretty close to shedding this rack as it is. Give me a few weeks, and I’ll be growing some new ones. But, uh…” He shifted above me for a moment, visibly uncomfortable. “You’re…probably going to be down for a while, Nate.”

I closed my eyes for a moment, did my best to sigh, and braced myself. “How bad is it?”

“Pretty bad,” Fade said bluntly, the very soul of tact. “Daffyd outright ripped out some of your bones, which messed you up pretty badly. Renauld managed to heal most of the trauma, but he’s really busy right now, from all the fighting that’s been going on. He just couldn’t spare the Mana to Heal everything that was damaged by Daffyd. So, they put you in here to rest. But, uh…there was some talk about you needing to get your bones regenerated by a Preceptor, and I couldn’t exactly tell them…”

“…that that won’t work on me,” I returned resignedly.

I had faced this problem before, after all, when I lost my left arm. As a Precursor, I couldn’t have my lost limbs and bones regenerated by a Preceptor of the Holy Gyre. Instead, I’d had to develop and have grafted to my skeletal system a custom-designed prosthesis instead.

This was…different, though.

I was completely unable to move, injured worse than I had ever been before, and likely…possibly still in the middle of a battle.

Huh.

You know what, I take my earlier thoughts back.

I’m not sure this could get any worse.
