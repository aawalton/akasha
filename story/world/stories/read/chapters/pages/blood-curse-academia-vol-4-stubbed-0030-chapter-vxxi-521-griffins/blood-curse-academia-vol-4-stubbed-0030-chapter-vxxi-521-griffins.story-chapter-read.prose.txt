Chapter V.XXI (5.21) - Griffins

“Did you see it?” Ione clutched his arm and pointed skyward. “It flew by! It actually passed us by!”

Kizu looked up but saw only blue skies marred by the occasional white poofy cloud.

“You saw….”

“A griffin, obviously!”

“I saw it,” Basil said from behind them. Unlike Ione, who was tugging on his arm like a maniac, Aoi stood to Basil’s side with a hand gracefully placed on his bicep. “Definitely a griffin.”

“That makes sense, we’re here with tickets to see a griffin race, after all,” Aoi pointed out. “Thanks again, Kizu, for the invitation.”

“Basil helped me out with my clothes the other day. I owed him for that.”

Basil turned and raised an eyebrow. “Oh, yes. That reminds me. How did meeting the parent go?”

Rather than respond, Kizu looked to Ione for a reply. His date, who still gripped his arm with both hands, was staring up at the sky, oblivious to the attention directed at her.

“Ione. What did your mother think of me?”

“Eh. She liked you fine.” Then she pointed at two mundane pigeons perched on a nearby treebranch. “That’s unusual. Normally, small birds avoid areas with griffins. Especially in a spot with so much exposed visibility to the sky. They’re likely to get snatched up as a snack midflight. The griffin riders here must have excellent control over their mounts if these birds feel safe enough to exist in such close proximity to them.”

Kizu let the conversation move on. Ione clearly didn’t want to discuss her family at the moment.

The number of pedestrians grew as they walked. The river of bodies condensed in the street as they approached a massive stone arena. The final street leading up to the entrance was lined with street vendors calling out to people passing by in an attempt to sell their wares.

At first, Kizu was doing his best to walk by and not make eye contact with any of the vendors, but then something caught his attention. Poofy, pink, and on a stick. Before he knew what he was doing, his feet instinctually redirected his pathing towards their new objective. Ione laughed as they arrived at the stall.

“Ah, do I sense a bit of a sweet tooth on you?” the vendor, a plump man with hair as pink as his product, leaned forward. “That’ll be two chits apiece.”

“Chits? I have yennies….”

The vendor leaned back and his sleazy smile slipped away. “Yennies? What am I supposed to do with that useless money? Hon’s locked down.”

“We still used yennies on Shinzou Island,” Aoi said, breaking her way through the crowd to regroup with him and Ione.

“Students at the academy?” The vendor crinkled his nose, as if taking sudden a whiff of something unseemly. “Well, too bad for you rich academy kids, you should spend more time studying local affairs at your fancy academy. Nobody in this city with any integrity will take that money of yours. Don’t know how you even afforded tickets to the race with that coinage. Disgraceful rats, thinking you can creep into our city without even considering the current feelings of those who live here. Insulting little rodents.”

Kizu started to turn away. “You know what, I don’t need—”

“Kaga Kizu! You made it!” A finely dressed man with a bowler hat and a monocle bounded over. Despite his age, the man navigated through the crowd with ease while other people stumbled over themselves to get out of his way

“Vincent? How’d you find me?” Kizu asked.

“Oh, you know, I have my ways.” Flashing a grin of white teeth, Vincent tapped his monocle. “I was on the lookout for you. Yvonne mentioned that I might have forgotten to give you proper instructions to our booth.”

“V–Vincent J. Winthropen?” The pink haired sweet seller wiped his brow with a handkerchief. “What an–an honor!”

“What’s this? Oh! It’s the unicorn hair I’ve heard so much about?”

Kizu perked up. Unicorn hair was an invaluable brewing ingredient said to have wondrous cleansing properties second to none in removing impurities. Using it as a sweet was just… unfathomable.

“Ah, pixie floss,” the vendor corrected nervously.

“Oh, yes. And not made out of real pixies. I recall someone waffling about it to me about it.” Vincent cleared his throat. “Well, regardless. My friend here demands your finest pixie floss. While you’re at it, might as well get us all some. How many is that now? Five? Maybe make it seven incase we encounter anyone else”

“At once—”

“Actually, there’s another vendor down the street,” Ione cut in. “I think it looks fluffier. Like cloud ferrets. We should get that one instead. I bet it’s far tastier.”

“Certainly!” There was a twinkle in the man’s eye that reminded Kizu of the headmaster. “If that is your wish, let us depart!”

“And who knows?” Ione said as she stepped in that direction. “Maybe the guy selling those ones won’t attempt to publicly humiliate us.”

The pink haired man fumbled for words while he attempted to offer his product for free, his red face frozen in agony and eyes locked on the back of Vincent’s head as the rich man strolled over to his competitor.

Kizu almost felt bad for him. But any such feelings evaporated as he bit into the newly acquired pixie floss. It dissolved in his mouth and left a tingling sensation. It felt like eating a slightly poisonous, deliciously sweet cloud. It disappeared long before they arrived at Vincent’s viewing box.

“Here, take mine.” Aoi passed him her stick with the puffy pink sweet. “Too sweet for me.”

While Kizu knew rationally it probably would be a bad look to rapidly inhale yet another of the sugary clouds in front of the extremely wealthy and well respected man who kindly offered him tickets, craving for sweets usually won out more arguments against rationality and this day was no different.

By the time he decimated the second pixie floss, they’d arrived at the box overlooking the arena. Kizu genuinely didn’t even remember how they’d gotten there. His ticket, which had been clasped in his hand before Aoi had handed him her pixie floss, had disappeared so he figured he must have given it to someone to be let in.

“As you can see, this viewing box rotates and rises up,” Vincent explained, gesturing at the walls of the room. We’re situated in the center of the arena but the enchantments here allow us to view out without others peering back at us. Quite handy for anonymity.”

The room was covered in lush furnishings. Kizu took a seat on a couch with Ione sitting next to him. Vincent settled into an armchair and went into further detail about the security of the spectator box and how an illusionary overlay never let outsiders know they even existed here.

“That’s excellent. Nobody explained to me beforehand that we would be in special seating,” Aoi said. “The last thing my family needs right now is someone recognizing me and attempting an assination.”

“An assination?” Vincent’s eyebrow rose. “Ahhh, I didn’t see it before. You’re the princess at the academy, aren’t you? I now see the familial resemblance.” He bowed deeply. “Forgive this humble man’s ignorance. Of course you’re concerned about assassinations after what happened to your uncle and brother.”

“Don’t do that. I prefer to stay anonymous,” Aoi said, waving away his apologies. “Also, my uncle was a piece of shit and deserved death a hundred times over.”

“I didn’t say which uncle,” Vincent pointed out.

“Both. One tried to kill me, the other murdered my mentor.”

“Speaking of that, any credence to the rumors I heard whispers of a bodyguard of yours in the city?”

Aoi sighed. “Ueno was supposed to be delivered to me months ago. But because she was an asset of the Inari family beforehand they blocked the proposal and insisted on further training. She got sent off to a camp in Tross as a means of postponing me acquiring a reliable bodyguard.”

“Oh, I know the camp you’re talking about,” Ione said. “Not a great place to be at. They employ trolls as patrolling wardens.”

“Meanwhile, these same nobles offered you a replacement bodyguard of their choosing?” Vincent guessed.

Aoi chuckled. “Of course. Which I promptly declined. After all, none of those guards offered to enter a geas with me.”

“A geas? Truly?” Vincent fiddled with his monocle, eyes wide. “What is the practice’s state of legality in Hon? It must operate under a gray area. In many respects, it is akin to willingly entering a slavery contract. Even more violating, in some respects, as it links you to their very soul. It is not a contract I would ever consider morally correct or a viable solution to any problem.”

“The woman attempted to murder me. And she approached me about the offer. I never suggested it.”

“And the legality?”

The two of them continued to talk about Hon politics and Kizu did his best to keep up with the conversation. Still, even with all his recent political theory readings, he struggled to grasp the context when they started talking about articles of certain laws and the philosophical meanings of their intent. Kizu found himself agreeing with Vincent’s opinion on the ethics of a geas.

And then the conversation further descended into a discussion of capitalism and the slavery of mankind by the governments that controlled them. Kizu followed that a bit better, as there was no longer anything like ‘Article 7.4 Section B of the Appraisal Accord of Hon Emperor Sasaki I’ being thrown around in the conversation.

“Mort would agree with you,” Kizu said, absentmindedly as he stared out at the arena. The griffins were individually doing a practice lap each. Likely a means in which for the spectators to judge their performance for placing educated bets.

His statement got a weird glance from both of the debaters.

“About people in power exerting control over others using resource management,” Kizu clarified. “He doesn’t think highly of civilization as a concept.”

“Who is Mort?” Vincent asked.

“Oh. My familiar. An owl monkey from the Hon Basin.”

“You have a familiar? Fascinating. They’ve gone quite out of fashion these last few decades. When I was a lad they used to be far more popular. That was of course back during a peace accord between Tross and Edgeland. Since Tainted are unable to bond with familiars, it was a bit of a status symbol to have one for a while. Of course, nearly all have since passed on. And the soul scarring left on my generation from their partnerships’ ending likely contributed to the next generation's wariness to form pacts.”

Kizu considered that. “Tainted can’t have familiars? I’ve never heard that before. Why?”

“That is quite the question, now isn’t it? Perhaps you’ll discover the answer someday. But for now, it looks as though the race will commence in a moment. Do you have a favorite chosen for victory?”
