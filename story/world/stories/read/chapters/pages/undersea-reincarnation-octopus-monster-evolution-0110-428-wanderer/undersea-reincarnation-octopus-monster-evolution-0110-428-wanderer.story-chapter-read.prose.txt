It was another of the pillbug-shaped ships, parked in the middle of the road. Its surroundings were quiet, and just like the one that had landed near their hiding spot, nothing crawled out of it. It was just sitting there, doing nothing, and Henry wondered how effective the plating would be against one of Maurice’s Imploding Bubbles.

Actually, how resilient were the creatures to the void of space? It’d be nice if he could just teleport them out to suffocate and freeze, but just like him, they could likely survive. In fact, they might even be better at it than he was.

Henry wondered how many of the creatures were in each, and for a brief moment, hunger gnawed in his guts as he pictured his beak piercing and cutting into their flesh, taking their strength away in droves, using all of his Avatars like a school of piranhas to feed on the infestation.

Predation of the Trickster thrummed in his soul, while his beak watered and his venom glands eagerly pulsed at the prospect of being used, but Henry caught himself and clamped down on his aura.

There was nowhere around to tell him it was leaking, but he knew enough by now that if he was feeling the predatory drive that strongly, then it must be obvious to everyone near him. In fact, a glance through the Avatars he’d left with Velistraine told him that it was enough to wake her up. She stared at him blearily, brows furrowed, then dropped back down when he apologized.

I’m gonna need to get that under control, he thought to himself as Velistraine’s snoring picked back up, and as he pulled his attention away from the cave in which she was sleeping, Henry felt… relieved. He was happy to find her well, and he’d bet her crew and the people of Thalis would be happy as well, once he managed to send words. With her safety confirmed, there was one less worry in the back of his mind, and he could focus on other matters. Keeping an eye on things, Henry continued spreading his clones about. By now, he had close to four hundred out, most of them of the Arcane kind, and he wished he could start attacking the ships as they might allow him to recover his mana faster, but between Velistraine still napping the day away and Maurice hidden in his cocoon, Henry didn’t want to rock the boat too much. He didn’t want to attract too much attention.

But that didn’t mean he couldn’t be productive.

So, as the number of his army kept swelling and as his spare eyes and limbs spread into this new environment, Henry left the nitty-gritty to his Octominds and turned his attention inwards. Specifically, toward the small sun that was the core of the Merfolk A-rank, and all the good it could do to his Aspect. Henry was going to wait for Maurice to be done, but then he realized that no matter how long the upgrade to A-rank took the crab, it wouldn’t be anywhere near as bad as fusing two already merged Aspects. In fact, the evolution would likely take less than a day, so once Henry found himself on the cusp of upgrading his Aspect, he’d just hold it in and wait.

In the meantime, he got started on drawing a thread of energy out of the core he could sense somewhere deep in his soul. To his senses, it felt like pure potential, like the power of the sun, nurturing and empowering, and immediately, he channeled it to parts of his soul that housed Unfettered Echoes.

It was like pouring a drop of water on the soil of a parched plant. The power was sucked into the soil that was his soul immediately, and thankfully, there was still plenty from where it came from. So, as Henry nourished his Aspect, hoping he had enough to see it grow and evolve, he kept his eyes on the ‘road’, and unconsciously, he glanced toward the direction Velistraine said the big door was.

Henry was already sending his Avatars in that direction, but he wasn’t really putting his back into it. He didn’t want to alert the big bad mantis to his presence. Still, he couldn’t help it.

That was likely the gateway to other worlds. Was Earth out there, through the door, or was it still somewhere here? Because all around him, in every direction he looked, Henry could see countless stars dotted the all-encompassing horizon. Millions, if not billions of suns surrounded him, and unless this was some sort of illusion or forgery of some kind, this was a full-fledged universe.

Did the System’s influence spread all through it, or was it limited to Velistraine’s world?

Might get some answers once we head back, he mused. And that might take a minute. As much as he wanted to take advantage of the slower progress of time in the hidden worlds, Henry couldn’t sense any of the Avatars he’d left there. He’d tried already, multiple times, even when he tried to filter out the noise. He got nothing.

He figured it was probably because of the System actively hiding those worlds. Most likely, getting out was much easier than getting back in. That might be even why Velistraine hadn’t wanted him to come through. But while that might be a little concerning, he wasn’t too worried. Unfettered Echoes was gobbling up the energies he was channeling to it, and eventually, it would step into the next rank, and it would level. If it ended up reaching level 10 and he still couldn’t sense his Avatars, then he’d keep pushing. After all, so long as his soul and its Aspect could be fed, why should the Aspect stop at A-rank?

Eventually, he’d regain the connection back to the Avatars he’d left behind, and then they’d go home.

Between feeding Unfettered Echoes all the power that it might need, creating his army of Avatars, and waiting for Maurice and Velistraine to come back to consciousness, time passed. Henry didn’t have a time-keeping thingy like Velistraine had, but by his account, he believed something like four hours had gone by by the time Velistraine woke up. She was in a much healthier mood, with visibly less twitching and much clearer eyes, and she proceeded to decimate a small, but still impressive percentage of his food reserves. The two chatted for a bit, then Henry got her up to date on what he was up to, at which point she left him to it, using the quiet time to meditate as well while he picked back up on healing her. More hours passed, and Henry’s swarm slowly grew and as he worked, Henry had his Lifeblood clones—of which there were fewer than twenty—craft round, small spherical chambers which he filled with telekinetically pressurized air that he then stored away. Once most of his work was done, Henry intended on creating a few more habitats, in case the first SS Henry was compromised. He could also use the bone-spheres to oxygenate the SS Henry.

Eventually, a day and a half had gone by since Maurice had gotten started on his evolution. By then, Henry had fully burned through the beastmaster’s core and most of everything else he’d been saving. But finally, he could sense the rhythmic pulsing of the Aspect throughout his soul, echoing like a heartbeat. He knew the Aspect was ready to upgrade. It might just need a little bit more juice. He just needed to get out there and hunt some void things, and with perfect timing, a wave of what Henry could only describe as mirthful curiosity suddenly surged out of Maurice’s cocoon, giving Henry an odd impulse to examine every grain of dirt, every inch of water for something he hadn’t seen before. A moment later, the construct of golden light began breaking down to reveal—well. Maurice.

The hermit crab had successfully stepped into A-rank, and Henry was sure that if he hadn’t settled the SS Henry nearly twelve miles away from the road, both Velistraine and the pillbug-shaped vessel would have detected the aura. As for Maurice…

Well. He didn’t look too different. At least, not at first. But when Henry paid closer attention, prompted by the joyful, intrigued aura suffusing the air around them, he saw the changes, and he was surprised he’d missed them. The multicolored shell, once made of scutes and having the form of a turtle shell, had changed even more, growing more into a spiral while the edges of its scutes had somehow faded away, making the shell seamlessly switch from crystal-blue turquoise to topaz to a myriad of vivid colors. As for Maurice and his exoskeleton, it was still red, but now, it had a certain shimmering quality to it, and as Henry tilted a little to the right, he saw the red change a little. There was a pattern to Maurice’s exoskeleton now, somewhat matching the Rainbow Tortoise shell he had made into his home.

Finally, turning his attention to the claws, Henry noted that they were somehow both sharper and more refined, with small grooves that’d allow him to grab onto things more easily, but Henry could tell that edge could instantly turn razor sharp, if only Maurice wished it. As for his eyes, they had also kept the same shape, two short eye-stalks that always made the crab look as if he was curiously peering over something, but there was a new luster to them. They were dark, speckled with glowing white dots, and Henry had the strong impression that they could see very well. In fact, he was pretty sure Maurice was currently struggling a little with his perception. His eyes and claws kept twitching, as every time he looked in a direction or another he twitched a little, as if blinded.

“[Ugh… too much. Just give me a second.]”

Henry chuckled, and let the crab figure it out while he prepped to carry them both back to Velistraine, which was as easy as deploying an extra clone in here. Henry knew he was approaching his limit, but he couldn’t feel bad about it. With upwards of two thousand Avatars, Henry would have plenty of help, and he’d have even more once Unfettered Echoes evolved. Though for now, he contained his excitement and cast Identify on Maurice.

Naturally, what he saw didn’t surprise him, but it still made him feel a little old.
