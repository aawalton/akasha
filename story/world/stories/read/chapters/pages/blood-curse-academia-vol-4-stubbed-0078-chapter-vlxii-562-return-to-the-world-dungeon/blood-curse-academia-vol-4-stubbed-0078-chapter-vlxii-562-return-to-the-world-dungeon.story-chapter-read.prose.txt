Chapter V.LXII (5.62) - Return to the World Dungeon

“It’s finally time?” Kizu took in his two friends beside him at the doorway to the World Dungeon.

Ione twirled a piece of chalk between her fingers. He recalled she’d been gifted it as thanks from the Hon government for her role in preventing Necro’s assault on the capital. The chalk was enchanted to never run out and it could be used on any surface. A powerful asset for a summoner.

Basil pulled on the cuffs of his sleeve. Unlike Kizu and Ione, the shapeshifter had opted for an outfit with more ruffles and frills. While it seemed a vain attempt to keep himself fashionable at a glance, Kizu knew through his spellsense that Basil had laced his clothing with a dozen different enchantments. The shapeshifter was a competent enchanter, when it suited him.

Mort, on Kizu’s shoulder, yawned and stretched. As much as Kizu wanted to keep Mort with Anata, he knew from Arclight’s countless reprimands that his familiar was an asset in battle and that they needed to be together for the greatest offensive potential. Plus, his familiar wasn’t particularly keen on the idea of getting left behind while Kizu faced danger. Not when Anata’s life had been going relatively smoothly these last few months.

The greatest downside to bringing Mort along was that it meant Kizu couldn’t use his familiar as an anchor to jump to safety on the surface. But the odds of needing to run away decreased with Mort’s presence. Still, Kizu planned to use the anchoring technique tactfully down below. It was the only way besides using a beacon that made any sort of long distance jump viable with the World Dungeon’s constantly shifting corridors and tunnels.

“Nothing left for us to do here,” Ione said. “My mounts won’t fit through this door, so let’s pass into the World Dungeon and get this delve officially started.”

Kizu looked to Basil. His friend shrugged and stepped forward. Without a key to open the sealed door, they were relying on Basil to get them through the threshold. The shapeshifter pressed a finger into the keyhole and twisted, the door unlocking with a cha-chunk as his flesh molded into the perfect key.

They all stared into the passageway. At this level and section of the World Dungeon, it took on the appearance of a lava tube. It also very rarely shifted any of its passages up here. Kizu knew through his readings that the island’s volcano was one of many that the dungeon merged with across the world. However, most were either inactive or far from civilization. Only a few in Hon were reported to have lava based biomes like the one here. Still, the precedent had given him a basic understanding of how they functioned and their common dangers.

For the first time in several months, Kizu stepped into the World Dungeon. As he passed through the doorway, a shiver went down his spine and he felt a tingle in his soul. Usually, he had no idea when Zayne was watching and listening, at that moment though, Kizu knew with absolute certainty.

The ancient soul said nothing, simply observed as Ione quickly sketched out two mounts to carry them.

“Smaller one is Basil’s,” she proclaimed, taking a step back to admire her work as the two lizard creatures crawled out of their summoning circles. They licked their eyes as they stared at the humans and Basil.

“Why not just make one big enough for all three of us?” Basil asked.

“For one thing, it decreases their mobility. For another, you get slimy when you fall asleep. And lastly, because I want to ride with the person I’m dating.”

“I get slimy?” Basil lifted up his mount’s skin flap, designed to keep them in place while it traveled across vertical surfaces. Strands of saliva-like liquid stretched between the skin flap and main body.

“Sticky and slimy are completely different,” Ione said. “Besides, even if it was slimy, it wouldn’t change my other points. I’m not going to date you.”

Basil grumbled about being separated from everyone but climbed onto his smaller mount. As if to comfort him, Mort leapt across the gap between the mounts to settle in beside Basil. Kizu suspected that Mort simply saw an opportunity to slip under that mount’s skin flap easier, but he let Basil believe the monkey wanted to ride with him for more sentimental reasons.

Ione sat in front of Kizu, as if to guide her mount. However, mere minutes into their journey, she fell asleep, leaning back on Kizu’s chest while he held the atlas out with one hand and verbally directed their mounts. Thankfully, Ione had enough control over her summons to make them listen to Kizu’s instructions even while she remained dead to the world. After learning about summoning in Conjuring F, Kizu had even more respect for the remarkable little things Ione could do with her creatures.

While the atlas wasn’t nearly as indecipherable as on his first journey, Kizu still struggled greatly. He flipped between the pages, locating both himself, their destination, and desperately attempting to keep track of everything between them. A feat made extraordinarily difficult by the constant movement of the chambers and corridors.

The upper floor was easy, as it barely shifted. The most dangerous bit of their first couple hours was when their mounts traveled vertically across a wall with a river of lava under them. Even that wasn’t too terrible, as Kizu knew how to create barriers to protect them, should the unlikely event of Ione’s summons fail to walk along the wall properly.

Other than that, they avoided the traps and Kizu purposefully did not lead them in the direction of any guarded treasure rooms. He knew from experience that Basil would chase after shiny treasure and Ione would chase after whatever guarded it.

Kizu had no doubt they’d encounter more inescapable opportunities like this in the future, so he didn’t feel a shred of guilt about avoiding the rooms.

Mort and Ione both slept as they traveled while Basil whistled, his notes echoing to different degrees as they entered larger and smaller passageways.

“What is that tune?” Kizu asked after listening to Basil whistle for an hour. It was very clearly some repetition melody and not random noise.

Basil paused his whistling and leaned back on his mount.

“Something my fathers used to sing for me when I was a child. Well… actually that’s not quite accurate. I’ve actually changed it a tiny bit. This is what they used to whistle.” Basil’s face split open in several places, his cheeks and chin collapsing inwards to form into gill-like openings. The eerie, otherworldly whistle that emanated from the holes bounced off the tunnel walls.

At first, Kizu worried that the strange noise might attract some horrific creatures. Nothing came. Minutes turned into hours as their mounts carried them forward and Basil continued to whistle, as if lost to the music. If anything, Kizu thought that maybe the noise deterred other monsters. He thought about how Anata innately attracted monsters and could control them with her glamour. Perhaps Basil’s whistling had a reverse effect. Or maybe, they simply found the noise creepy.

The temperature dropped considerably as they exited the lava tunnels and entered into more traditionally styled World Dungeon hallways. They were a combination of normal caves and pieces of an ancient stone city. In the past, they’d encountered similar structures in both Hon and in the deeper end of their previous journey. Still, it was an unnerving marvel that put him on edge. Kizu found his eyes wandering to a half-buried shopfront sticking out of the ceiling. He couldn’t read the stone sign dangling from above, but he figured it must be some sort of bakery, based on the etching of a bread loaf around the symbols.

“What civilizations do you think these came from?” Kizu asked out loud.

Neither Ione nor Basil had a response to that. However, his stowaway decided to deliver a shred of insight on the matter.

“Humanity has existed since long before my time,” Zayne said in his mind. “These are relics of eras long forgotten. A time when humans lived alongside Sekai in harmony. Before the balance collapsed.”

Kizu’s eyes widened at that implication. The world Zayne described was nothing like the one Kizu had seen in his visions.

“What happened?” he asked quietly.

Only the sticky footsteps of Ione’s summoned beast replied to his query.

Kizu intended to press the topic and not let it die. Unfortunately, between Basil and Zayne, his mind had wandered away from his current responsibility. Watching the atlas for monsters and potential traps.

While he had been distracted, the World Dungeon did not share in his lapse of attention. It had shifted. The floor under their mounts’ feet had changed from stone, to a mossy surface covered in mushrooms. There was some sort of magical veil in place, to keep unsuspecting delvers ignorant.

They penetrated the illusion and Kizu only just had a brief moment to read a note in his atlas. His vocabulary skills were still far from fluent, even with the additional childhood memories that had been blocked out by the crone, but he knew this word. One of the first he’d learned, as it was repeated throughout the atlas.

DANGER.

He read it as his mount crushed one of the mushrooms underfoot.
