We ran all the way to the mansion and burst into Juliette’s room.

She collapsed on the bed and immediately began to cry. I sat on the floor, face in my hands, shaking. I was hysterical too.

“I fucking hate this place!” she sobbed. “These monsters! Oh my God, Ramon!”

It was unreal. Someone we were laughing with just this morning... torn apart. Gone.

“We…” I began, voice trembling. “We still don’t know what happens when someone dies here. For all we know, he might just respawn somewhere. Maybe right in this town.”

“No, he won’t!” Juliette shouted. “You and Ramon with all your game bullshit! Don’t you see? This is a brutal medieval dark-age shithole of a world. Yes, with monsters—but that just makes it worse! Ramon died because we thought it was a game.”

“You’re right,” I said quietly. “We profoundly underestimated this place. And because of that... Ramon died. I... I’m so sorry.”

And I really was. Sure, Ramon pushed the monster-slaying adventure thing, but I’d been totally on board by the end. I was just as much to blame.

“Shit,” I suddenly remembered. “Juliette—your leg! That thing... the sting!”

She looked down and pulled up her pants leg, revealing a small red mark.

“It doesn’t hurt,” she said. “It’s not even swollen. I think you kicked it before it could inject poison.”

“And your head!” she suddenly remembered too. “You were bleeding—let me see.”

My hair was matted and sticky with blood. Juliette grabbed a cloth, dipped it in a water bucket, and gently cleaned the wound.

“It’s superficial,” she said. “Scalp wounds bleed a lot, but this one doesn’t look too bad. Here—hold this.” She pressed the cloth to my head. “It should stop bleeding soon.”

“Thanks,” I said, then paused. “You’re kind of amazing.”

“I just did a basic first aid course,” she said. “Nothing serious. So please, don’t get too injured.”

“Oh, cool,” I said. My brain instantly debated whether I could fake a shallow breath next time I fell over—just in case someone with first aid training happened to be nearby.

“The sting still bothers me,” I said. “Please, be aware of any changes—dizziness, nausea, anything—and tell me.”

“And then you’ll do what?” she asked, anxious. “There’s no modern medical help here. What can anyone do?”

“They have doctors here, or maybe healers. And they probably know more about the local monsters and toxins—”

“They’re NPCs. They’re like robots with five phrases in their heads. A toddler has more useful dialogue.”

“Yeah, but they still perform their in-game roles well,” I argued.

“Again with the game stuff,” she snapped. “I told you—”

“Juliette, it is a game,” I cut her off. “A horrifying one, sure, but it’s still a system. It has rules. Mechanics. Things we have to learn in order to survive. And maybe… one day… return home.”

I didn’t really believe that last part. Not completely. But I needed her to hear it. We had to stay optimistic, or we’d just spiral into despair—and that would be the end.

“Ramon was a great guy,” I said. “Let’s make sure we get strong and smart and never, ever lose another friend to this world.”

Was I… good at this? The motivational stuff? It was working on me at least—I felt a little better. I think.

She leaned closer. I noticed the tears in her eyes.

“Thank you,” she whispered. “You’re right. We can’t give up.”

Her face was really close to mine. Was I supposed to kiss her? This was either the perfect moment... or a horribly awful one. I… I think it was the latter.

Anyway, my motivation speech was officially out of steam. I didn’t know what to say next.

Wait—I had one more line.

“We’ll be alright,” I said, trying to sound wise.

“What do we do now?” she asked, wiping her tears.

“We continue,” I said. “We work. We learn. We don’t go anywhere near that forest until we know absolutely everything about each monster and go in with a super-prepared party.”

“Yes...” she nodded.

“Ramon did discover something important today,” I reminded her. “Killing monsters gives EXP. He felt it. That means we can level up.”

She nodded again.

“I’m glad at least it wasn’t for nothing,” she said. “Ramon was a cool guy. You… really think he can come back?”

I wanted to say yes. I really did. But I couldn’t lie.

“To be honest... I don’t think so. This world is harsh. But maybe. Let’s hold on to that hope.”

“If he does come back, I’ll smack his head so hard,” Juliette said. “Running into the forest like that. Getting himself killed.”

“Yeah,” I smiled faintly. “I’ll smack him too.”

We talked a while longer about Ramon—about the funny things he did in the short time we knew him. But the hour was late, and we were completely drained from this hellish day.

“I should go back to the inn,” I said, starting to stand. “It’s late.”

“Don’t make me stay here alone,” Juliette said quietly. She looked miserable. “You can sleep on that couch, okay?”

“Oh. Sure,” I said. “I wouldn’t mind the company either.”

That was true. As much as I liked the idea of sleeping in a girl’s room, my real motivation was that I was absolutely, completely terrified. I didn’t want to be alone tonight.

I lay on the couch and closed my eyes.

Immediately, I saw the scene again—Ramon, torn apart by those hellish care bear claws. I blinked hard, tears in my eyes. I rolled to face the back of the couch so Juliette wouldn’t see.

Just when I thought this world was starting to be fun—it turned to hell again. Would it always be like this?

Still... I was determined. I would succeed here. I’d become super strong, learn everything, make it so it could all be easy. Even fun.

I drifted into sleep.

In my dream, I was running through the forest again. Monsters chased me. One of those surikata scorpions caught up and stabbed my leg with its tail.

I screamed.

Then I woke up.

Pain. Real pain—in my leg.

I opened my eyes in panic—and froze in absolute horror.

Juliette was sitting beside me on the couch. Smiling.

And gently petting a Surikata Scorpion on her lap...
