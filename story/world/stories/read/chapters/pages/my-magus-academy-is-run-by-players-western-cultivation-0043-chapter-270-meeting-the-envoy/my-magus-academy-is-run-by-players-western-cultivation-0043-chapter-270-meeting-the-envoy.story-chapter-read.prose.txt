He couldn’t make sense of what these people were doing. They worked like lunatics, yet they were disciplined, methodical, and — most importantly — hardworking. The envoy found himself more intrigued than judgmental.

He had visited numerous academies where acolytes were either pampered or crushed — places where the strong flaunted their power and trampled the weak without consequence. The Arcane Radiance College was one such place. But here, things were different. The students moved with an unusual sense of unity, as if each one was contributing to some greater design. Whatever their reasons, they weren’t acting out of fear or pride; they were simply… driven.

When he had first arrived, the acolytes’ reactions had been odd. They’d stared at him, murmuring among themselves, as though he were a rare specimen in a cage. For a Magus of his rank to be met with such curious, unrestrained gazes had irritated him at first. Still, before he could say anything, an elderly Magus appeared and guided him to his current quarters.

Just then, a knock came from the door, followed by that same old Magus’s calm voice. “Sir Frederick, the headmaster is here to meet you.”

It was time to meet their leader.

Victor stepped into the guest house with measured steps. Though he was about to meet the envoy from the Arcane Radiance College, his expression remained composed. Even without the envoy showing his strength, Victor could sense that the other party was a Nexus Temporal Magus. More than that, he could feel the faint, deliberate probing of mana directed toward him.

Before him, Roland stood at the entrance to the meeting room and bowed slightly toward him. “Headmaster, Sir Frederick is waiting inside.”

“Mhm.” He nodded, pushing the door open slowly.

Inside, a tall, refined man stood with his back turned. When he turned around, Victor was greeted by silver hair that gleamed faintly under the light and a pair of sharp violet eyes that seemed to see through everything. His presence was steady and heavy, yet not exactly oppressive. From his mana signature alone, Victor could confirm that this envoy was indeed a Nexus Temporal Magus — and a veteran one at that, stronger than Alphonse. The System alert also confirmed this.

So, this is the Arcane Radiance College’s envoy, he thought. In the past, Victor would never have imagined meeting someone of this rank face-to-face. But things had changed. Now, as the headmaster of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi, he stood here not as a subordinate but as an equal, ready to negotiate on his own terms.

“Greetings, Sir Frederick,” Victor offered a respectful bow — the kind reserved for a fellow Magus of equal standing. “I am Victor Asteriscus, Headmaster of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi.”

The envoy’s gaze turned to Victor, eyes cool and assessing. There was a subtle probe in his mana, though he didn’t delve deeply and remained cordial. As expected, it glanced harmlessly off the protective layers of the Cosmic Rubik.

“Well met, Headmaster Victor,” the envoy replied with neither an overbearing nor submissive tone. “First, thank you for receiving me. I am Frederick Lightstone, the envoy dispatched by the Arcane Radiance College.” He showed Victor an envoy insignia of the Arcane Radiance College and continued, “I assume you already understand the purpose of my visit.”

“I do.” Victor, having taken a seat, gestured toward the table and the waiting chairs. “Please, have a seat.”

Frederick inclined his head and settled into the chair opposite Victor.

“I trust your impressions of this academy, from what you’ve observed so far, have been... enlightening,” Victor said evenly.

A faint smile appeared on Frederick’s lips. “Quite. Your academy is unlike any I’ve seen. Your acolytes seem intent on dismantling every conventional rule of Magus education — or perhaps reinventing them altogether.”

“Do elaborate,” Victor invited, gesturing lightly.

Frederick fell silent for a few seconds before finally replying, “I’ll admit, you’ve stirred quite the storm. In less than a year, your Sanctum has seized territory, absorbed Magus families, and owns more Elemental Adept Magi than some institutions manage to produce in a decade.”

Victor smiled faintly. “Hard work. Creative methods. A bit of madness. You’ve seen it.”

“Indeed,” Frederick agreed. “But that’s not the matter I came to discuss.”

“I’m aware.”

Then Frederick said in a serious voice, “Let me be frank. The Arcane Radiance College has no desire for conflict. But power this wild… unmoored… is dangerous.”

Victor’s gaze sharpened. “Are you suggesting that my academy should submit to the Arcane Radiance College?”

Frederick nodded. “That would be the most favorable outcome. However, it’s clear you’re not the kind of man to yield easily. Otherwise, you wouldn’t have been able to annex the Asteriscus Magus family, let alone defeat the Merlins. Therefore, we must seek a compromise.”

“My thoughts exactly.”

Submission to the Arcane Radiance College could take many forms. At its most restrictive, it meant total incorporation, reducing the Sanctum to a mere branch of the Arcane Radiance College. Naturally, Victor would never accept that. It would render everything he had built meaningless, and worse, expose the secrets he’d fought so hard to protect. This kind of scenario would be worse than an all-out war with the Arcane Radiance College.

In the less restrictive form, the Sanctum could become a vassal, and in the least restrictive form, it could simply pay tribute. These two arrangements were the most common and ones that were adopted by the majority of Magus factions within the kingdom. Even the Amethyst Ring Tower was a tributary to the Arcane Radiance College, offering regular tribute in exchange for protection and the right to govern its own territory.

Victor didn’t particularly mind the idea of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi becoming a tributary to the Arcane Radiance College. Such an arrangement would grant him a powerful umbrella of legitimacy, shielding the Sanctum from rival Magus forces within the kingdom — and from those who operated beyond any law, such as the Doomsday Cult.

As for becoming a vassal, however… that was something he had never even considered. Doing so would risk exposing too much. The presence of Arcane Radiance College’s Magi stationed within the Sanctum would inevitably uncover his secrets, and that was unacceptable.

Besides, Victor had no intention of remaining under anyone’s shadow forever. After all, his ambition was to make the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi the greatest Magus academy in the entire universe.

And all he needed… was time.

Thus, he began his negotiation with the envoy.

“Envoy Frederick,” Victor said calmly, folding his hands atop the table, “I understand the necessity of establishing order and balance within the Magus world. Power without restraint can easily spiral into chaos. However, I have no intention of turning the Sanctum into another branch of the Arcane Radiance College.”

Frederick’s expression remained neutral, though his eyes gleamed with interest. “Then you are open to the idea of a different arrangement?”

“Perhaps,” Victor replied. “If what the Arcane Radiance College seeks is cooperation rather than domination, I’m willing to discuss a tributary relationship. The Sanctum can offer resources and exchange knowledge, in return for recognition and protection under the Arcane Radiance College’s name. Such an arrangement would benefit both sides.”

After all, although the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi was currently inferior to the Arcane Radiance College, the latter would still risk losing a considerable number of Magi if they attempted to conquer his academy. Even without knowing the full extent of the Sanctum’s capabilities, they were wise enough not to take that gamble and risk becoming the second Merlin family.

“A tributary state, then. I see.” Frederick leaned back slightly, his tone thoughtful. “It’s certainly more preferable than outright defiance. But I must ask: what assurance do we have that you won’t turn that protection against us in the future?”

Victor smiled faintly. “I could make a magic oath under the Primordial One’s name that the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi will not initiate hostilities against the Arcane Radiance College, as well as any Magus organization under the Arcane Radiance College — so long as they do not strike first.”

That condition, of course, left room for interpretation. If those neighboring Magus forces chose to act aggressively, he would have every justification to retaliate and conquer them under the guise of self-defense. It was a perfect clause, wrapped in diplomacy. Not that it mattered much at the moment since his primary focus remained on the curse festering within him and the rapidly dwindling time he had left to break it.

Although the Arcane Radiance College would anticipate such a loophole, to them, the notion of abandoning territorial ambition was unthinkable. After all, relinquishing the drive to expand was akin to crippling a Magus force’s very potential to grow.

“I, for one, value stability,” Victor added, lying through his teeth. “The Magus world is dangerous enough without internal strife. Besides, the Sanctum’s growth benefits the kingdom as a whole — we’re situated in the hinterlands, near the northwestern border facing the Demornial Kingdom.”

A quiet chuckle escaped Frederick’s lips. “You’re a clever man, Headmaster Victor. You know how to wrap ambition in diplomacy.”

“Whatever you say, Sir Frederick,” Victor responded with a polite smile.

For a few moments, neither spoke again. The tension in the air was stiff, as though two predators were circling the same territory, both aware that the first to strike might lose more than they gained.

At last, Frederick nodded his head slightly. “Very well. I shall report your proposal to the Arcane Radiance College. However, we must first discuss the details of the tribute.”

“Of course,” Victor said smoothly. “What I offer will not disappoint.”

It appeared that some of the higher-ups within the Arcane Radiance College had no desire for war and only wanted to profit out of him. Yet, for some reason, Victor couldn’t shake the feeling that it was all too simple. If matters were truly this easy, it wouldn’t have been designated as a main mission, nor would the promised reward have been so generous.

So, what was actually going on?

Unable to discern the true reason, Victor turned his attention back to the negotiation. As a tribute to the Arcane Radiance College, he was prepared to offer 30 percent of the mana crystals extracted from the Sanctum’s mines. In addition, he proposed to deliver two voidstones annually.

The academy no longer depended on low-grade mana crystals as heavily as before, so parting with them was of little consequence. Besides, he already had a ton of them from the treasury inside the citadel that probably wouldn’t run out for at least a century. As for the voidstones, those were replicas, conjured through Lillie’s dreamforce. Victor had already accumulated a small mountain of them in his private storage.

Two voidstones might fetch a fortune at any grand auction, but to him, they were little more than polished pebbles. Frederick, however, didn’t know that.

The envoy’s brows lifted slightly at the offer. “Two voidstones per year, in addition to 30 percent of your mining yield?” he repeated, as if confirming he’d heard correctly.

Victor nodded. “A fair exchange for peace and recognition, wouldn’t you agree?”

Frederick squinted his eyes, studying Victor with open suspicion. “Your proposal drives quite a hard bargain… I can’t help but feel you’re hiding something.”

“Not at all,” Victor replied smoothly, wearing the sincerest expression he could muster. “The Sanctum and I merely wish to demonstrate goodwill.”

The envoy studied him for a long moment, as though trying to discern the truth behind that calm demeanor. Yet Victor held his gaze without wavering, showing neither arrogance nor fear.

At last, Frederick gave a slow nod. “Very well. I will bring your terms to the Arcane Radiance College for review.”

Victor returned the gesture with a smile. “I look forward to their response.”

Thus, with the negotiations concluded, the two men clasped hands in agreement.
