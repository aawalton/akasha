I sat there in my inner world and gathered qi into my hand.

"You need to remember to start off with the outer circle," Azure materialized next to me, "everything else builds from that foundation."

He was right.

The outer circle always came first,

As I drew it on the ground, my finger left a glowing trail of qi that slowly solidified into a perfect circle that was around twelve feet across.

Next up was the detection spiral.

This part was a lot trickier because every triangle had to be the right size and have the perfect spacing, so they could form a spiral pattern that would act like the formation's sensory network. I made sure to work slowly, carefully, and that every line was precise.

"The spacing between those triangles seems like it's a bit too tight," Azure commented. "You might want to-"

The spiral pattern suddenly destabilized, and all my carefully drawn triangles dissolved back into formless qi that dissipated into the air.

"What were you saying?"

"The spacing was too tight," Azure repeated. "Whenever the triangles are that close together, they end up interfering with each other's qi flow. You should think about it like trying to have too many conversations all at once, eventually everything becomes noise."

I nodded while studying the outer circle that was still remaining. "So, we need to space them out further apart. But if we put them too far, we will have gaps in the detection coverage."

"You should try using the golden ratio for the spiral spacing. That should end up creating a much more natural flow."

I started over and redrew the detection spiral, except this time I spaced the triangles out more carefully. The pattern held stable this time, and every triangle sat there glowing with a soft light while it connected to its neighbors.

Next, I had to create the curved channels that were supposed to distribute the power throughout the formation. This was where things started getting delicate. Every curve had to be smooth enough so it allowed the qi to flow, but it needed to be sharp enough to redirect the energy effectively.

I barely started the first channel when the formation collapsed, and all the patterns dissolved into little wisps of qi.

"What happened there?" I asked with a frown at the empty ground.

"The curve was too sharp," Azure explained. "Think about it like trying to redirect a river, if you make too sharp of a turn, you get turbulence that disrupts the flow."

I tried doing it again, and this time I made the curves a lot gentler and more gradual. The channels finally began taking their shape, and they created these paths that would allow energy to flow between the different sections of the barrier.

Then came the tricky part, the resonance chambers.

I started placing the nested octagons down at key junctions, with every one being slightly smaller than the last one so it created that funnel-like amplification effect. The first few went down pretty well, but when I added more of them, I could feel the formation's energy becoming unstable.

Before I could make any adjustments, it exploded in my face in a giant flash of white light, leaving me sitting there blinking away the spots dancing in my vision. Well, at least I had a good idea on how to create flashbangs now, although I would rather it go off when I intend it to, and not at random.

"That was too much amplification," Azure noted. "The resonance chambers started feeding back into each other and it created an exponential energy loop."

I rubbed my spiritual eyes while I waited for my vision to clear up.

"So, I need to isolate them better. Maybe if we try adding some dampening lines in between the chambers..."

My next attempt lasted a little bit longer, but it still failed when I tried to activate it. The one after that ended up developing strange harmonics that made the formation vibrate until it tore itself apart. My fourth try seemed stable until I realized the energy distribution was uneven.

Hours passed by while I kept trying, and I made small adjustments every time. Azure kept pointing out potential problems and suggested solutions, and occasionally he watched in silent concern while another attempt failed spectacularly.

Finally, after hours of failing, I sat back feeling frustrated.

"This isn't working," I muttered. "Every time we manage to fix one problem, two more of them pop up."

Azure stayed quiet for a long moment, and then he said, "What if we are approaching this wrong?"

"What do you mean by that?"

"We are trying to build this like a machine, where each part does a specific job. But formations aren't machines at all, they are a lot more like living things. Everything needs to work together naturally."

I thought about that, I remembered how the Basic Protection Barrier felt whenever it was working properly. It wasn't some collection of parts; it was a harmonious entity where every element supported the other.

"So instead of trying to make each component perfect," I said slowly, "we need to focus on how they work together?"

"You should look at natural patterns, like the way tree branches grow, or how water flows, or how crystals form. They are all based on simple rules that end up creating complex and stable systems."

I stood up and stretched out my muscles that didn't technically exist in this spiritual space but somehow managed to feel stiff anyway. "Let's take a break. Give me some time to think about all this."

When the next day came, I returned back to the practice area with fresh eyes and a new approach. Instead of starting off with the outer circle, I began with the detection spiral, and I let it grow naturally straight from the center out.

The triangles spaced themselves out almost instinctively, following the patterns you might see in a nautilus shell or a sunflower's seeds. Every single one connected to its neighbours using delicate lines that seemed like they were drawing themselves.

Next up, I added in the curved channels, but this time I didn't try to plan them out perfectly. Instead, I let them naturally follow the flow of qi, like streams finding their paths running down a mountainside.

The resonance chambers came last, and I placed them where the energy wanted to concentrate. Instead of forcing them into perfect octagons, I let them take shape according to the formation's existing patterns, which created structures that looked a lot more organic but felt more stable.

I connected everything together slowly and carefully. The outer circle ended up forming last, growing from the existing pattern instead of acting like something to contain it.

The completed formation sat there glowing with a soft and steady light. There was something different about it this time around, this real sense of rightness and natural harmony that had been missing from all my previous attempts.

"Now that," Azure said approvingly, "looks like it might work."

I smiled because I was feeling a deep satisfaction. It took dozens of attempts and a change in my approach, but I had finally managed to create something that felt alive.

"You realize that successfully creating the formation is only the very first step," Azure added, "it still needs to work as intended."

I turned my head to where Yggy had been watching us work.

"Are you ready to help test this out, buddy?"

The vine uncoiled itself quickly, it had been waiting around patiently through all my failed attempts, and it occasionally offered me encouragement in its own unique little way.

"Start at about ten percent power," I suggested. "We can work our way up from there."

Yggy immediately manifested several thorned branches that struck the barrier.

The formation responded instantly, I could literally feel the detection spiral registering the impact perfectly, and the curved channels redirected the power straight to the threatened area, while the resonance chambers amplified the defensive energy.

It worked.

The barrier held perfectly.

Yggy increased its power up and started striking from different angles in quick succession. Every time it hit, the formation instantly adapted itself, channeling the energy where it was needed while maintaining minimal power everywhere else.

When we brought it to seventy percent power, the barrier still remained stable. The resonance chambers weren't amplifying the defensive energy anymore; they were creating harmonics that ended up strengthening the structure.

Finally, Yggy unleashed a full-power strike and all of its manifested branches smashed into the barrier simultaneously. The formation flared up brilliantly, and its patterns kept shifting and flowing around like a living thing while it distributed and amplified the defensive energy.

When the bright light finally faded away, the barrier was somehow still standing there.

Yggy's branches drooped down noticeably, and I could feel a mix of pride and disappointment from it. It was proud of my achievement, but disappointed that it couldn't break through.

I reached my hand out and gently patted one of its branches. "That means it worked as intended. Besides," I added with a small smile, "we still need to test how it holds up against actual enemies anyway. And I'm sure you’ll get plenty of chances to break other people's barriers."

Yggy perked back up at that, and its branches started swaying with renewed enthusiasm.

"Well," Azure said, "I believe congratulations are in order, Master. You have successfully created your first level two formation."

"It looks like it can at least hold up against Qi Condensation Stage 6 attacks." I stared at the glowing pattern, while hardly believing it had worked.

"Yes, and now you can name it."

I sat there and considered it carefully. Names were important in formation craft; they weren't simple labels but a part of the formation's identity.

"Maybe the Adaptive Resonance Barrier?" I suggested. "No, that’s too descriptive. How about the Harmonic Shield? No, that sounds too generic."

I studied the formation while watching how its patterns shifted and flowed.

"The Symphony Shield," I said finally. "Because it isn't about all the individual parts, it’s about how they all manage to work together."

"That is a fitting name,” Azure nodded, then he smiled. “You are officially a level two formation practitioner now.”

I smiled and felt a deep sense of satisfaction. It probably didn't seem like much when compared to the achievements of true formation masters, but it was still a significant step forward for someone like me.

Looking back over at the Symphony Shield one more time, I couldn't stop myself from thinking about the Outer Sect Tournament coming up. The group stages part of it had always been a concern, because even though I trusted Wei Lin and Lin Mei, having them face off against multiple opponents who were above the 4th Stage of Qi Condensation would be challenging for them. It would be difficult for me to not worry about them but now, with this formation...

"It could buy them time," I murmured. "Even if we end up being outnumbered, the Shield could protect them long enough for us to coordinate our response together."

"The formation's ability to adapt and strengthen where it is needed would be particularly useful in group combat," Azure agreed. "It could help compensate for any gaps in your team’s defensive coverage."

I smiled, feeling a lot more confident about our chances now.

My gaze ended up drifting over to the array of runes at the center of my inner world, and then went up to where the golden fruit was still rotating slowly among the Genesis Seed's highest branches.

"We still have a few days before team training," I mused, "and another day after that before my next formation lesson with Elder Chen Yong. We might be able to finish setting up the non-elemental runes in here."

"And then we can investigate that mysterious fruit?" Azure asked.

"One thing at a time," I replied, even though I couldn't help glancing up at it again. "Let's finish the non-elemental runes first. We can worry about elemental runes and mysterious fruits after that."

The golden fruit continued its slow rotation, almost like it was patiently waiting for us to be ready. Whatever secrets it held, they were going to have to wait because now, I had real work to do.

"Back to runes then?" Azure asked.

I gave a nod, already turning my attention back to the array of partially completed runic patterns. "Back to runes. We made some good progress with these formations, so let's see if we can make similar progress here."
