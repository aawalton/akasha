I woke in a rustic space aglow with afternoon sunlight. My bed at the Deeproot was a narrow mess of gray cloth, the sole window’s drapes were drawn wide, and nearly everything else in the little bedroom was just… brown. Brown floorboards, bare wooden walls, slatted ceiling beams, an oak rocking chair in one corner, and a squat brown nightstand. The only splash of color came from a broad blue shadeblossom in a little vase atop the stand. The vase was carved from wood, shaped like a hen, and also brown.

I sat cross-legged on the bedspread for quite a while. Elbows on my knees, thoroughly lost in thought, absently twirling the shadeblossom between my fingertips.

I couldn’t help but think that it looked very sad. Normally, such a flower would be a vibrant burst of midnight-blue or royal-purple hidden amidst dark ferns. It was supposed to be a cheerful, resilient thing; velvet-soft petals unruffled by autumn air and a stem that, once powdered, could help relieve fevers.

Not this one, though. Its blue petals drooped. Its stem was wrinkled. Its center, where thin velvet met a winking golden dome, held streaks of choking black.

I stared at the dreary little flower and couldn’t quite chase the nagging thought that it reminded me of… well, me.

The seeds had been a foolish idea. Naive, even.

‘You said you missed gardening.’

My chest tightened.

They’d felt a bit like… hope. Something to cling to after watching people murdered in the marketplace; hope that even though Velidan was cruel, perhaps he was a better monster than the others. Perhaps something good could come from all this nightmare and bloodshed.

A very foolish thought, indeed. My request for mercy hadn’t been met with consideration or empathy, only a deliberate reminder of the exact nature of our… relationship. One in which he could do whatever he pleased and I was simply trapped.

I sniffled. Brushed one finger along a wilting blue petal, then gently placed the flower back in its vase.

Slowly, achingly slowly, my pulse evened out. He didn’t touch me. Didn’t hurt me. Didn’t hurt Royce this morning. The threats are all there, the… ashes, sometimes I think he enjoys frightening me. But I’m alright. I am.

Whether I would stay that way remained to be seen, I supposed. Particularly if I agreed to his… suggestion. I imagined myself scurrying along at Velidan’s heels, head ducked and eyes wide and surrounded by monsters.

Would it be a grand, extravagant party, like what I’d glimpsed inside his manor? Vast rooms filled with shimmering gowns, crystal goblets brimming with wine, mesmerizing music and graceful figures swirling along marble?

That wouldn’t be so bad. Right? Surely no one would eat me while they’re wearing fancy clothes. They’d get blood everywhere.

But perhaps it would be different. Perhaps Velidan’s gatherings were unique. Perhaps other Fae threw parties soaked in the blood and screams of weaker creatures.

What am I thinking? I couldn’t possibly agree to this. Surrounded by monsters? Even if one of them doesn’t actually kill me, I’d probably die of fright!

And even if I was safe… even if it all worked out like he said… then what? I wake up and warn everyone? And then of course I’ll have to admit how I know, and that leads to… to…

I pictured Joselia ripping my tunic off. Jareth throwing me to the ground. Other faceless, steel-plated figures lifting knives and cruel, crushing fists-

Stop. They might hear me out. They might understand, despite all the things Renner said. Joselia’s… she’s not cruel. She’s not eager to hurt people.

And even if it was as terrible as all that… it would be worth it. Wouldn’t it? If it helped defend the city? Save lives? Of course it would be worth it. Of course it would. I… I could withstand… I could be okay with… if it saved lives… I could. I think I could.

Renner won't like this idea. A sad, strangled little laugh chased that thought. Heh. He might throw a… er, ‘tantrum.’

Which, given his last few attempts to stop me from getting myself killed, would probably leave me a bit giddy in the aftermath. He’d get very close and his eyes would just smolder and his fingers would brush my skin and… er, lots of other things that didn’t bear thinking about, at the moment.

Focus, Brin. And… and speaking of tantrums… I heaved myself out of the bed with a sigh. I hope Royce is alright.

A few unfamiliar faces milled about the Deeproot common room when I made my way downstairs. The only recognizable figure was Teela, who sat with her hat pulled low and her narrow chin propped in one hand. The other was splayed out before her. A copper coin danced over and under narrow knuckles, moving almost too quickly for me to follow.

She squinted up at my approach. “How was the nap?”

I thought for a moment, opening and closing my mouth several times, before settling on, “Confusing.”

“But not… bad?” The coin glinted before disappearing beneath her palm. “Did you learn anything helpful?”

“Maybe?” I dragged a hand across my face. “Mostly I’m just… I don’t even know. Lost. Where is everyone?”

“Not sure about Royce, but everyone else is up the street. All the holy folks are back, and they’re all… comparing stories, I guess. Sounds like there were a few other, uh, messages delivered around the city.”

A chill pricked at my neck. “Oh, no…”

She lowered her lashes, thin lips twisting down. “Yeah. Ours was the… biggest. Worst. I dunno what the right word is for ‘the most people died where we were,’ but that’s the word.”

I sank into the chair beside her. “Did they all say the same thing? About barrows and Thalessians being held hostage?”

“Yep.” The word dropped like a stone. It settled between us, heavy within the quiet din of sparse afternoon chatter. “Sounds like another paladin captured someone pacted, too. They’re getting a group together to scout the northern woods, and someone’s going around to all the Watch barracks in the city to fill them in.” She was silent for a while, looking uncharacteristically solemn. Then, evenly, “Do you think should we leave?”

“Maybe.” The word scraped its way out a throat that felt suddenly very raw. I sank into the chair next to her, dropping my voice to a murmur, and relayed the pertinent details from my dream. By the time I finished we were huddled practically cheek to cheek with our elbows propped on the table and low, somber tones. “It sounds like… unless he’s lying about it all, it sounds like this could be really, really bad.”

“No salt,” she muttered. Then, after a moment of grimacing, she slapped one palm down onto the table. “Brin. I’m gonna say something no one’s said, yet, but you need to hear.”

“Oh?”

She nodded sagely. “You need a drink.”

I blinked. That had perhaps been one of the last things I’d expected to hear, at the moment. “What? It’s not even supper yet. I-”

“Need a drink. Maybe five. Seriously. We both do.”

I shook my head. “Teela, I can’t… everyone will be back in a bit, and-”

“And us having a slightly better outlook on all this salt isn’t gonna hurt anything. You and me can’t help with the stuff they’re doing right now, so we should do something for us.” She stood, hands on her hips, and fixed me with a stern look. “I asked earlier, and this place has barley ale, a keg of rum that some cane-farmer outside the city fermented, and honey mead. Which one?”

“I… well, I…”

“If you don’t choose, I’m gonna bring you one of each.”

My face grew warm. Half in very responsible uncertainty, because I’d never been one to drink much- and certainly not in the middle of the afternoon!- and half because… well, there was a little part of me, or perhaps more than just a little, that thought sitting and letting Teela cheer me up sounded like a wonderful idea.

Her eyes narrowed.

My resolve cracked. “The… the mead. Oh, but just a small cup, though! Please.”

It was not a small cup. It was also honey-hued, smelled like wildflowers, and very tasty. Sometime later, when the windows showed sunlight hazy with golden descent and a few more patrons sat scattered at tables, the room seemed just a bit brighter and the Fae-touched woman and I were once again slumped practically cheek-to-cheek.

“You know,” she yawned, tapping one finger against the rim of her second, half-empty tankard, “My mom always said to try and find the bright spot when things get dark, and there is one good thing about all this.”

“This?”

“This.” She waved a hand. “Attacks. Monsters coming. This.”

“Oh?” My voice was dry. I sipped my own second drink. Syrupy sweet warmth pooled across my tongue, earning a contented sigh. “Mm. What is it?”

“Well,” she said, very matter-of-factly, “If a bunch of monsters come rampaging through the city, the two children we’re toting around will finally have someone to fight besides each other.”

I laughed. I couldn’t help it. “Teela, they’re not that b-”

“Oh, no, they are.” Her face puckered. “They’re even worse when you’re not looking.”

“Saints, don’t say that!”

She nodded gravely. “That first morning? In Snowmelt? You should’ve seen them at breakfast.”

“Oh, no... what happened?” I took another gulp.

“Well, Royce was really worried about you. He kept saying you shouldn’t be alone and he was gonna go wake you up. Renner kept telling him to sit back down. He said we were gonna do what you asked. Royce got upset and said you’re too nice for your own good, and maybe doing what you asked was a bad idea. Then Renner said you are too nice, but that you can take care of yourself-”

I choked on a mouthful of mead, whipping my head towards her so fast it hurt. “He said that?”

“Yep!” she chirped, sipping with a little giggle. ‘So then Royce said… uh, something about how that was rich coming from the guy whose whole job was to keep you safe. Then he started grumbling about how it seemed like it was more than a one-man thing, based on how everyone had almost died. Then Renner got really mad and said you wouldn’t he in this mess in the first place if Royce hadn’t been an idiot, and of course Royce got even more mad-”

“Saints, I had no idea.” I leaned back, feeling suddenly faint. Just more fuel to this awful little fire, I suppose. And it explains why they kept sniping at each other that morning.

One bony elbow nudged my ribs. “I mean, it was kinda funny. It wasn’t hard to see what was actually going on, y’know? Men.” She snickered, red eyes glinting. “Well, anyways, that’s when captain Rell came in. The whole thing stopped there, but they looked ready to strangle each other. Heh, Lewen and I were gonna place bets.”

My chest swelled with a weary sigh. “I’m sorry, Teela. I know it’s been… awkward. Royce is usually-”

She raised both hands, palms-up, and chirped, “Oh, no, he’s great. I mean, he’s actually great. Seriously, if it wasn’t for the witchwood and how I’d be, y’know, eaten alive, I’d be making plans to move to Fellbrook.” She raised her drink in a little toast before taking a gulp.

A startled giggle burst from my mouth. I shook my head helplessly.

“I get it, though. Why he’s so moody.” She gave me a sly grin. “I bet he thought he’d swoop in, all, y’know, heroic and stuff and carry you off with those big arms. Actually, though, really, have you seen his arms?”

The giggle turned slightly hysterical. “Teela!”

“I’m just saying.” She rolled her eyes dramatically. “They’re nice arms. And I get that he’s not his normal self. And I get why. I’m actually really smart, dunno if you’ve picked up on that.”

“I definitely have.”

“Good.” She sniffed. Then, more somberly, “It’s… it is getting less funny, though. I’m worried he’s gonna snap.”

My shoulders stiffened. The mirth died. “Snap?”

Thin red lips twisted to one side. An expression of strange, unhappy knowing. “Yeah. He’s like me, y’know?”

I shifted, turning one hip to face her more fully, and looked the young woman up and down. “Are you alright, Teela?”

“Me? Oh, yeah. Feet planted, horns up. That’s what my mom always said.” Her chin lifted, head tilted towards the nearby window. Sunlight sliced across her eyes in a vivid red glint. “But Royce is like me, except without all the… experience, I guess. He keeps trying to do the thing that I do. Where everything’s bad and he can’t fix it, so he makes it his job to cheer someone else up.”

I gazed at the young woman, suddenly struck by the fact that she was several years younger than me. At the moment, it very much felt like the opposite was true.

Teela’s crooked teeth flashed in a grin. “It’s not a bad thing. But you have to… I don’t know. You can’t fall apart, and it takes practice to not fall apart, and he doesn’t have practice.”

“I don’t think either of us really do,” I murmured, taking another sip. I let sticky, smooth heat roll across my tongue and, after it slid its way down my throat and brought a fresh burst to my belly, whispered, “I… I kind of wish he wasn’t here.”

The words cracked the air. I’d been wanting to say them aloud for a while, but now that they were out… I glanced around hurriedly, feeling as though I’d done something very wrong. “I don’t mean… it’s not because I don’t want him here, I just-”

“I get it. Trust me, I get it,” the young woman sighed. She stretched back, arms flopping languidly. “I think my mom wished that about me, a lot. Towards the… well, the past few years. When people started looking to throw blame, y’know? Look at me like I was a problem. I get it.”

My vision blurred. I couldn’t imagine what being on the receiving end of such an emotion must have felt like. Especially from a parent. “Do you miss her?”

“Yeah.” She scrunched her nose. “That’s how I got my practice, y’know? When things were bad, which wasn’t a lot, but it still happened sometimes. And I’m funny and clever and all that, so I always made it my job to… you get the idea.”

My mouth curled up. “You’re very good at it.”

“One of my many talents.”

“Obviously.”

She chirped a little laugh, then clinked her cup against mine. “Royce is great. Really. That’s the best kind of friend, I think; the ones that make it their job to be there and try to help, even when they know they really can’t help. It’s just… it’s tough when you love someone and they’re hurting and you can’t fix it.”

I pictured Durst’s solemn, dark features, then blinked back the sudden spring of heat behind my eyes. “Yeah,” I rasped, sniffling. “Yeah, it is. I… I wish I knew how to help him.”

“S’not your job to help him.” She nudged my cup. I took the hint and, huffing something between a sob and a laugh, took a deep drink. She mirrored the action, then raised both bony shoulders in a shrug. “He’ll figure it out. Just keep being you.”

I sniffled. “That’s it, huh? How did you get to be so wise?”

She snickered. “Told ya, comes with experience. Now, let’s talk about something happier. Arms, right?”

I choked on the mead.

“He’s even got that cleft in his chin. And those eyes. You could just fall right into them.” She sighed dreamily, draping an arm across her belly as my skin flooded with heat. Then, while I was ungracefully wiping my chin, she grinned slyly. “Renner’s got nice eyes, too.”

My face felt like it was on fire. I ducked down, practically hiding behind the cup, and mumbled, “I… I hadn’t noticed.”

She cackled. “Oh, uh-huh. Sure, so I’m just imagining the way you two keep looking at each other.”

Fire. I’m definitely on fire. I didn’t somehow Veth myself in the middle of an inn, did I? My lips moved, the words tumbling out on their own even as I tried to hold them in, “H-how are we… um, looking at each other?”

“Well, like you want to kill each other half the time.” She snickered.

“O-oh.” I gnawed my lower lip. She wasn’t exactly wrong. Then, as the fire crept down my throat, I mumbled, “And the other half?”

“Heh. Uh… oh, you know. Like it’s hard to look away.”

I examined my mostly-empty mug. Fought a shy, slightly giddy smile. “Oh.”

She giggled again and, after a moment of studiously looking down and blushing beneath her too-sharp eyes, I offered my own sly grin. “You know, um… Jareth also has nice arms.”

She raised both hands, palms-up. “Hah! Oh, sure, yeah, but I’m not getting tangled up with that! I’m not a moron!”

“What do you mean? He seems-”

“A little too charming. I’ve learned better. Guys like that are… heh. Fun. Really fun, sometimes.” She winked. “But when it’s all over I’ll get my hopes up and he’s not the type to think about courtship and dowries.” Her mouth twisted. Not sadly, but once again with that self-assured look of knowing. Then she shook her head and drained the last of her tankard. “Besides, me and a templar? I’m literally a thief, not to mention the other stuff.” She flicked the brim of her hat.

“He…” I hesitated, unsure of how to voice the thought that- I hoped, at least- was somewhat reassuring. “He doesn’t seem to mind at all. About, well, you being-”

“No, he doesn’t. Earlier was… I didn’t expect that. Really didn’t expect that.” The hollows of her cheeks pooled with color. The back of her tunic bulged, as though her bundled tail was trying to flick straight through the cloth. “But still. The next time I fall for somebody, I’m gonna be smart about it.”

“Oh,” I said, because I didn’t quite know what else to say, and once again I was struck by the feeling that in many ways, I was much younger than my Fae-touched friend. I spent a moment in thought, wanting to ask quite a few things- namely, ‘what happened the last time you fell for somebody?’- but instead settled on taking another sip and saying very matter-of-factly, “Well, he still has very nice arms.”

Teela burst out laughing.
