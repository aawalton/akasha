It… couldn’t remember what it was, or how it had come to be. It could feel things, could hear people chatter about matters far beyond its understanding. But it couldn’t move. A cloth always covered it— heavy, suffocating —and that cloth terrified it. The symbol stitched upon it radiated a promise of punishment should it ever dare to move. And so, fear kept it obedient.

So… it did the only thing it could: it stayed still. Even when those creatures, humans, as it later learned, took it out, touched it, and spoke around it. From them, it discovered not only what they were called, but what it was called.

[Diary of Lik’Ara]

It was a diary. A diary that did not move, did not act, simply was. Remaining in such a state for so long, it learned to slip into something like sleep, a stillness so deep it drowned out every whisper of the human world. In that quiet, it drifted into another place. A hollow expanse where only it and… Him existed.

The diary didn’t know what He was. Yet He evoked the same primal dread it felt from the symbol on the sealing cloth. But unlike that symbol, His presence was… warm. Comforting. Within that formless dark, the diary finally felt home. Here, it could move freely, its was being unbound. It could sense other presences swimming somewhere far off. They were strange, lurking entities that never dared venture near Him.

The only reason He allowed the diary to approach was because He was lonely.Even in that endless void, He remained still, trapped, unmoving, just as the diary had been in the human world. The diary could feel it, that shared stagnation, that similar silent grief. He was probably asleep too, sealed in His own way.

Yet His presence shielded the diary from the prowlers in the dark. Sometimes it glimpsed them, massive shapes rippling through the gloom. Fins. Tails. Jaws. Eyes that glowed like stars. Their sounds were wrong: part shriek, part whisper, part temptation.

Some made the diary’s bindings quake with dread; others sent sparks of curiosity through its pages. Each glance it stole from them brought something new, foreign fragments of knowledge that sank deep into its being.

Before long, it began to write. To record. Symbols, patterns, spells, tiny shards of understanding harvested from those horrors in the dark. Progress was slow, but for the first time, the diary was doing something. It was alive. It was happy.

It no longer understood time. Again and again, it slipped into that sleep, just to feel that delicious trickle of new knowledge inscribing itself across its pages. That feeling… oh, that feeling. It loved it more than anything.

In the outside world, it didn’t remain as unnoticed as it wished. The humans who kept it soon began to see the strange markings that appeared across its pages, words and symbols that no one had written. They were fascinated, drawn to the mystery. But the diary wanted nothing to do with them. That knowledge was its own! It had earned it, gathered it piece by piece from the void. If those thieving humans wanted wisdom, they could find their own. They had no right to steal from it.

Luckily for the diary, they couldn’t understand any of it. The runes, the structures, the meanings, it all slipped through their primitive minds like ink through water. That pleased the diary greatly.

So, whenever it was brought out, it played dead, silent, still, and compliant beneath that cursed cloth. Yet the moment it was covered again, it would sink back into its slumber, into that endless space where it could keep learning, keep growing. It became a rhythm. It was a loop that brought it quiet joy.

Until the day it made a foolish mistake.

One of the humans, a woman, had taken it out of its seal to study it. And while she worked, the diary felt something, faint at first, but unmistakable. A warmth. Not from the void, not from that distant space where He waited, but from this very physical world.

The same warmth as His.

There was a reason the other entities in that darkness avoided Him. But curiosity, its oldest sin, won out. Before the diary realized what it was doing, it reached out with a fragment of its learned knowledge, to track that warmth.

And it discovered that it did not come from any human.

It came from a white-furred, fluffy fox.

A fox who was very unhappy about the diary’s existence.

The first meeting was uneventful. The fox noticed it, gave it a passing glance, and went on with her life. But the diary’s curiosity lingered. It wanted to know more, to learn, to understand, maybe even to befriend the fox the way it had once connected with Him.

But things quickly went downhill. The knowledge it had spent so long gathering began to betray it. For the first time, its own wisdom turned against it. Every time it tried to reach out, to make sense of the warmth surrounding the fox, something, some unseen hand, interfered, twisting the spells and symbols it invoked. That invisible hand, it realized with dread, belonged to the fox herself.

The worst part was that it couldn’t speak. Despite all the things it had learned, not once had it encountered the language of this physical plane. So it tried to make sounds, shrieks, anything that might convey its intent. But every motion, every noise, only made things worse. The fox grew angrier, her presence colder each time the diary tried.

It tried everything. And yet… nothing worked.

All it wanted was to know. Was the act of seeking knowledge such a grave sin in the fox’s eyes?

Now, everything was unraveling. Its knowledge was scattering like torn pages in a storm. Its very essence was being shredded apart. It couldn’t reach the void anymore, couldn’t find Him. Couldn’t even sleep. It was… afraid. It hadn’t felt fear in so long. But as its mind and memories dissolved into nothing, something unexpected bloomed within it… relief. A strange sense of liberation.

It just wished… it could have spoken. That it could have learned the humans’ language, even once, to explain itself.

But as its consciousness began to fade, it felt that presence again. The empty void it used to drift into. The place where He waited. The diary’s fading awareness flared back to full clarity.

What… was happening?

The broken symbols it had once recorded began to gather on their own. Words and sigils it had lost were reassembling without its will. The presence of the void was growing stronger and closer. Darkness spilled out as thousands of fragments coalesced, glowing and writhing. The air trembled. Something enormous was pushing against the barrier between worlds, trying to break through.

And then the diary understood.

The knowledge it had stolen from those creatures in the void wasn’t just inert learning, it was alive. One of those entities was using it now, clawing its way through the cracks, trying to breach into the human world.

Terror surged through the diary’s splintering consciousness as it felt the rumble, the tearing, that hunger from beyond. With the last shreds of its being, it tried to push back, to seal the breach, to take control, but it was useless.

The final thing it saw before its awareness collapsed was the fox’s face. It was calm and mildly annoyed, as she looked down at the monstrous thing forcing its way through.

And then… nothing.
