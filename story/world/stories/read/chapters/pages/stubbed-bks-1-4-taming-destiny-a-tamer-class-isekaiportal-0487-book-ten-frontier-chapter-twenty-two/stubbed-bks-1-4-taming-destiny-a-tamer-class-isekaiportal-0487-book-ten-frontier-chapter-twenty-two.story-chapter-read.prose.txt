Sarran hurries through the streets of the settlement, irritated at being delayed for such trivial reasons. The dispute between the two wood-cutters shouldn’t have been allowed to get to the point where one was throwing around accusations of attempted murder by means of falling tree in the first place. Moreover, the two remain so obstinate in their dislike of and sense of rivalry with each other that even putting them on different work details may not solve the underlying issue.

Hopefully the consequences to their pay that continued antagonism will have will be enough to prevent the issue from affecting their work quality again – Sarran has certainly made it plain to both parties that if he’s called again for the same reason, they will both face punishment duty – and halved salaries – for an elongated period of time.

In the meantime, it’s meant that Sarran hasn’t only missed dinner, but is late for the meeting the young lord has called. Hopefully Loran has been able to convey the information Markus needed.

As he quickly approaches the room in the lord’s manor set aside for meetings like this, Sarran hears the murmur of indecipherable voices which only resolve when he opens the door and breaks the privacy seal inscribed within them.

“-the future of our settlement. To that end, after you have given your reports of the current state of the settlement, I intend to lay out my objectives and then hear any comments you have on them. Ah, good evening Chief Steward,” Lord Markus greets him. There’s no accusation in his voice, or question in his eyes. Sarran appreciates the sign of trust – he’ll explain the delay afterwards. The eyes of the other lords in the room are rather more judgemental.

“My lord,” Sarran replies with a deep bow of his head, then quickly finds the seat set aside for him at the large oval table as Markus invites the first speaker to begin giving their report. He wishes he could speak to Loran and find out exactly what information may – or may not – have been conveyed.

Looking around, Sarran notes how everyone has arranged themselves. The room is big, but with fifteen people currently around the table, it doesn’t feel that way. He’s curious to see that the four other lords are each sitting with a commoner next to them – their assistants and the ones who will most likely take over their roles when they decide to leave to make their own settlements.

Sarran isn’t looking forward to that headache – needing to work out who is going, who is staying, and who can cover the essential roles that will be left with no one in them will be a challenge even for him. He’s rather hoping that the lords won’t leave all at once and they’ll wait until a trade route has been reopened with Moriax – that way they might be able to get new recruits to fill the gaps.

His eyes pause on Loran, opposite him. What’s happened? He looks…different.

It’s not immediately noticeable for anyone who doesn’t know the man well, but Sarran has spent marks every day with him for the past several months so that something has changed is immediately obvious. What, is a different question.

He looks…stronger. More solid, Sarran decides. Loran obviously feels Sarran’s gaze as his eyes flick over to catch Sarran’s while Ker Flunt drones on about the progress on various building projects. Sarran is surprised by the sharpness of Loran’s gaze – it almost feels like the man is looking inside him.

For a moment, he regrets refusing Markus’ offer of a Tame Bond. He’d just found it too much of a headache when he had it temporarily in the palace. Having so many voices at the back of his mind…he doesn’t know how Loran, Markus, or even Nicholas do it, though he suspects Nicholas’ experience is quieter. Still, if he had it, he’d be able to assuage his curiosity immediately. As it is, he’ll have to stew in his curiosity for a bit longer.

Sarran only half-listens to the reports – he knows most of the information the heads convey – they only met this morning, after all. The difference was that none of the lords were present at that one. The exception is when Lord Elarion speaks up and announces the breakthrough that they’ve had with the farming.

That meets with a round of relief-filled cheers – the precariousness of the food supply has been a weight on all of them.

“Does this mean, my lord, that the farmers will finally be able to fully supply the kitchens?” asks Kera Isolde, head of the manor kitchen.

“Not at present,” Heir Elarion replies neutrally. The disappointment in the room is palpable. “However, we will expand production as and when we are able to.”

“What is the chokepoint?” asks Lord Layton.

“In order to keep the fields in an appropriate condition for growing edible vegetation, we must depend on the efforts of the Plant Mages, and creations from the alchemists. Neither are unlimited.”

Lord Markus clears his throat.

“On that note, I was discussing with the enchanters about them developing something that could pull the mana out of the soil. If they succeed the way we’re hoping, we may find that the Plant Mages and clay balls are less needed.”

“In which case,” Heir Elarion replies with a hint of approval, “we may be able to supply the settlement’s needs sooner than expected.”

All of that is news to Sarran – good news, of course. He decides to speak to Master Mirena to find out the details – and what supplies they might need. Markus has lots of excellent ideas, but, as is common among lords, he doesn’t always consider the logistics behind bringing them into reality.

The discussion continues, again, little new to Sarran. Kera Cole is the last to give her report and takes advantage of that to ask a question Sarran isn’t surprised to hear – she’s the one who asked it in their meeting earlier that day.

“My lord, I would like to ask a question, if I may?” she asks with her characteristic poise. Sarran is unsure as to her background – the collar around her neck comes with a contract that indicates debt as a reason for her enslavement. However, her comportment is elegant, her tones are educated, and she’s capable of reading, writing, and arithmetic, all of which indicate a wealthy upbringing.

“Please go ahead Kera Cole,” Markus invites. Sarran has to admit that he finds it impressive how Markus had habituated the other lords enough to his ways of seeing the world that none of them take any exception to a slave speaking to them or sitting at the same table as if they are social equals. They certainly didn’t accept Captain Garrous as easily during the meetings on the ship. But a good deal of time has gone past, and Sarran has noticed them relaxing considerably. Perhaps Brenna Cole’s obviously educated background is also part of the reason – the way someone speaks and bears themselves often has a disproportionate effect on the way they are treated.

“Thank you, my lord,” she replies. “My query is really in two parts. First, I wished to ask about your intentions for the monetary system. I believe that the value of the salaries – generous though they are – is often underestimated due to the lack of anything to purchase. However, conversely, I have heard several people concerned that the meals you have made freely available will be ending now that the food crisis is considered over. For those wishing to save towards their freedom,” she raises a hand to touch her collar in what Sarran suspects might be an unconscious motion – though he never knows with her, “this is more than a little worrying.”

Sarran curses mentally. This was something that he wanted to raise with Markus before the meeting. However, from Markus’ lack of reaction, Sarran suspects that the question, at least, isn’t a surprise. Perhaps Loran spoke to him instead. Good – maybe he’s had some time to think about it, then.

Lord Markus nods.

“Thank you for bringing your concerns to me,” he starts. “Regarding the fear about food, I do not intend on allowing anyone in this settlement to go hungry.” Sarran notices he and Loran exchange a meaningful look. Interesting. “Therefore, I would like the manor kitchens,” he looks directly at Jule Isolde, “to consider options for a meal that is cheap to prepare, easy to store, and nutritious enough to prevent starvation if that is the only thing someone eats in a day. Can you do that?”

Kera Isolde considers the matter.

“It depends on the ingredients we have available, my lord.”

“Of course,” Markus agrees. “Perhaps discuss it with Sarran or Loran – they have a better overall view of what ingredients would be cheapest to access.”

Sarran feels Markus’ gaze land on him and he nods.

“Yes, my lord,” he agrees, already with some ideas in mind. He’ll have to coordinate with the farmers too – foodstuffs that would usually be cheapest in Moriax might not be so here due to the limits on growing them.

“You really intend to feed everyone on a permanent basis?” Heir Valence asks, sounding surprised, but not against the idea. The troubled looks on several other faces around the table indicate that others are more uncertain. “Will that not cause people to work less hard, knowing that they will eat even if they refuse to leave their homes?”

“People who haven’t eaten for days are unable to work hard,” Markus points out. “And starving people are desperate. Desperate people make bad choices. If we know that everyone who commits a crime here is doing it out of greed, and not need, it becomes far simpler to assign a punishment. However, I take your point about incentives to make going out to work worth it. Which brings me back to your point, Kera Cole.

“While a single, nutritious meal will be made available to anyone who wants it, I encourage the cooks to create other meals that will be available to anyone who wants to buy them – delicious meals with different ingredients. And the same with the other craftsmen.” Here, Markus looks at Ker Flunt. “I know that an impromptu market has already started with clothing, furniture, and various other items. I thoroughly encourage that. Please note that we will also start applying a tax on earnings.”

Sarran is unsurprised when that meets with several displeased glances. No one likes paying tax. Markus sees them too, and leans forward.

“Consider this. A meal will be made available to anyone who needs it. Healing is and will continue to be freely available to anyone in the settlement. Water is freely available, but the wells need to be checked and maintained from time to time. Similarly with the sewage system. And I intend to make education to at least a basic degree free. All of that costs money – and all of those are paid from taxes.”

Markus looks around the room and sees what Sarran does – some who are thoughtful, others who are not convinced.

“Some of you don’t seem too happy about the idea. Please speak – that’s what this meeting is for.”

In the end it’s Dorian Flunt who speaks up, though his eyes are trained on the table.

“O’ course it’s the lord’s decision but…it don’t seem fair that we have to pay for other people to eat or be healed through our sweat an’ tears.”

“This is what is troubling the rest of you?” Markus asks neutrally. Some other heads around the table nod with a greater or lesser degree of certainty. Sarran just watches, interested. None of the lords are complaining – they, no doubt, have a better understanding of just what tax is about, and benefit personally from collecting it in their territories. “Then consider what we’ve just gone through. If the healers hadn’t been working for free, just how many people right now would be dead? Or labouring under a heavy weight of debt for the removal of the parasites? And what about next time there’s an epidemic? As for maintenance of the wells and the sanitary systems, I hope that it’s easy to understand why these are important to keep in good condition.”

Several people are nodding now.

“What of the education you mentioned?” Lord Elarion asks coolly. “What do you mean by this, and to what extent do you intend to offer it?”

“Reading and writing for sure. Basic arithmetic. Perhaps some classes on sanitation and survival in the forest outside.”

Sarran’s eyebrows twitch upwards. That’s more education than many middling wealthy merchants give their children.

“Do you really think that such a degree of education is necessary for the everyday commoner?” Lord Josiah asks doubtfully.

Markus opens his mouth to respond, but just as he does, a knock falls upon the door. All heads turn towards it just as it opens and a messenger steps in.

“My lord,” the messenger greets, bowing. Then, seeing the other lords, she bobs another bow in their direction. “My lords.” Then, facing Markus again, “A sail! Entering the bay.”

Markus looks at Lord Josiah.

“Not one of mine,” the lord offers tensely. “All of them were at anchor when I came here.”

Sarran tenses – across the table, he meets eyes with Loran who, a little surprisingly, has a grim expression as if he knows what this implies as well as Sarran does.

Lord Markus nods, his expression unreadable.

“Then the meeting is adjourned for now. We’ll reconvene later.” He then looked at the other lords and then Sarran. “Let’s go and find out who this ship belongs to. Rally the guard in case they are hostile.”
