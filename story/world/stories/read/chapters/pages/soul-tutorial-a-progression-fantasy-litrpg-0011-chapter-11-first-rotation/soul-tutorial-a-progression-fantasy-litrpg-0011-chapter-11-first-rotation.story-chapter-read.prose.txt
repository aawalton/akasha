Luke tried his best not to grin; training had just started, and anything could have gone wrong. Once he had a good view of what he was looking at, he realized it looked quite fuzzy. Since it was his soul, Luke thought he would have been able to see it perfectly, but sadly, he was mistaken; it was blurry.

He had only one task at hand: he needed strength. Not just from training himself but from the various other souls in the sky. So with no more waiting, he began siphoning the power of the souls one by one, aiming for the ones weaker in light.

He wanted to save the strong ones for later and also didn’t want to risk harming himself just yet when he hadn’t had the basics down. The title had helped a little with the addition of the upgraded attribute; it was quite easy to find the source of power and take slight control of it.

He felt his soul throb once more, noticing 3 of the weaker souls in the sky had vanished, but he didn’t worry about that. It wasn’t quick, but they appeared near him, entering through his chest; Luke was clueless about how he could use any of these souls. His first use of it was all based on instinct, recognizing that he was near death in that moment.

He discovered he could store the souls in his chest alongside his own, forming a chamber. This discovery surprised him. He was amazed that he could use soul energy back when he was just shoving power into his limbs. Questioning how he even lived to see this day.

Luke suddenly remembered that this kind of power had almost blown him up and could only thank his luck that he hadn’t croaked.

So as the souls sat there, he couldn’t just allow them to do nothing while they were inside him. Luke felt them out and realized that they had come from the green slime, which was quite obvious because not only did it have a green hue, but he felt some kind of special addition held deeper inside the soul, but it felt out of reach. He hoped it wasn’t anything bad.

But shifting his focus, he tried to think of ways he could use the green souls a little more. His concentration wavered when an aching pain shot up his leg. He stood up, walking around while trying to stretch his legs.

How can I move the souls like I did back in fights? Luke thought upon realizing the souls within the chamber stayed stationary.

Luke tried copying whatever he could remember, concentrating and squinting as if it would aid his memory. Luke tried to imitate everything he recalled and prompted the three souls to proceed.

At first, nothing happened. They just sat there inside him, hovering around his own soul like useless rocks.

“Come on… move already,” he muttered, grinding his teeth.

Then one soul twitched. It wasn’t much, just a tiny shake, but it shocked him enough that he almost lost focus. He swallowed hard and forced himself to keep going. Slowly, slowly, the second soul started shifting too. And then the third. They weren’t spinning or rotating or anything cool; more like awkwardly dragging themselves around his soul in a shaky circular formation.

But he had at least accomplished something, not knowing what he could use this for. He continued to experiment with the continuous, shaky souls. It looked wrong. It felt wrong. But it was working. Luke felt a weird tug deep in his chest, as if those souls were generating some energy. The warm feeling he felt earlier came back, bubbling up from inside his gut.

It moved around in a messy, clumsy way, almost as if it didn’t know where to go. Then the energy suddenly shot downwards, rushing straight toward his stomach. Luke gulped, half scared, half excited.

“What the hell, let’s not go too fast right now.” he tried to control it, but honestly, he barely knew what he was doing. The best he could do was shove the messy soul energy in the general direction he wanted. He directed the soul energy to his right arm because that limb remained intact, unlike his left one.

The warm energy slid downward. It felt gross, making him shiver at first, like worms crawling under his skin. He felt a lot more conscious of what he was doing this time and gagged a little, but kept pushing.

When the energy finally reached his right arm, something strange happened. His arm hummed a bit. Not like a sound exactly, but a buzzing feeling under his skin. Something was alive, but it absolutely disturbed him with the sudden development.

Luke stared at his arm as if he had never seen it before; it disgusted him.

“Oh, oh, wow. That’s actually gross.” He flexed his fingers. They twitched harder than normal. The energy wasn’t stable; he could tell that right away. It jumped around inside him in random bursts, almost spilling out completely. But even though the rotation was sloppy and the control was bad, it still worked.

He actually got soul energy into his arm. He was at least happy about that, but the continuous use of him made the crawling sensation worse. Like maggots were eating his flesh from the inside out. But he quickly pushed that to the back of his mind, glancing at the pedestal near him.

For the first time since the fight, Luke felt a spark of confidence in his chest. Not because he was strong; he definitely wasn’t, realizing that. But because this proved he could get stronger. He looked back at the three shaky souls circling his own. They still wobbled as if they were confused, but they were moving. Slowly. Roughly. Occasionally bumping into each other. This made the energy a lot more chaotic. That was enough.

“If I can rotate these better, I can send more energy, and then maybe…” Luke whispered, staring at his right arm. It hummed again, calmer this time. He grinned. “Yeah. I can work with this.” Luke leaned back against the pedestal, sweat dripping into his eyes, heart thrashing. The three souls hovered awkwardly around his core, spinning as if they didn’t really want to obey him. His left arm throbbed with the hum of energy he’d forced into it, muscles twitching slightly, alive again for the first time since going insane.

He flexed his fingers, grimacing at the sharp pulse that had run through his shoulder. The stump of his left arm had twitched faintly, almost as if it had wanted to move on its own, but he had forced himself to ignore it. He hadn’t had a left arm anymore; he couldn’t have wasted energy wishing for what wasn’t there.

Every drop of soul energy had to go into what he still had. Focusing, he willed the three souls to orbit him more evenly. It had been crude, messy, and some of them had kept bumping into each other, but they had moved. The hum had spread through his chest, down to his right arm, down into his legs, and even into his spine. He could have felt the energy trying to fill the void where his right arm used to be. Not to replace it, but to become something new.

Quickly shutting it down before it had gotten to take form. He couldn’t have wasted that here, not then, closing his eyes, counting every pulse. Each time the souls had completed a rotation, he had forced a little more energy into his right arm. The hum had intensified, vibrating through his bones. His fingers had flexed, and he had felt strength returning to his muscles, crude and uneven, but alive. But with more pain than ever before, he had known he couldn’t have pushed much more into it but had to try.

“Come on, come on, work…” Luke had muttered, teeth clenched.

Sweat had poured down his face, mixing with blood that trickled out from his eyes. The pedestal had dug into his back, rough and uncomfortable, but he hadn’t cared. Nothing else had mattered except the energy swirling inside him.

He had tried something new. Instead of just rotating the souls around his core, he had imagined a line connecting the souls to his left leg. Slowly, painfully, he had forced the energy down. The hum in his chest had shifted, spreading down his torso. His left leg had twitched, then flexed.

He had gritted his teeth against the sharp sting in his muscles, but it had worked. It had actually moved.

“That’s something,” he had whispered. But he had done it. He had forced the souls to rotate faster, wobbling, jerking awkwardly, but the energy had kept flowing. The hum in his left arm and leg had pulsed like a heartbeat, and for a moment, he had felt more alive than he had in days.

He had tried sending a tiny pulse into his stump, a crude attempt to make it respond. It hadn’t moved, of course; there had been nothing to move. His stump had just been wobbling there with no muscles to support it. But the hum had clearly tried to spread out into the space. Weirdly, it hadn’t hurt.

But before he realized it, he was coming ever closer to having no more energy left. Luke’s head drooped forward as exhaustion hit him, but he forced himself to keep experimenting. He tried alternating energy between his right arm and leg, then pooling it into his chest, then sending it back into the souls.

Each attempt made the hum spike, made his muscles quiver, made him want to collapse, but he couldn’t. He had survived too far to give up then. But sadly, training had to end. The souls slowly withered away, disappearing as if they had never been there. Luke sat there absentmindedly before drifting away to rest, drenched in his own sweat and blood.
