Chapter 205 / B3-50: Take a Break

“I can smell the gears in your head smoking,” Kenda joked from behind Joe’s back, just before her strong fingers gently began to work on the knots in his shoulders.  Joe had been partially aware of her arrival at the Abaaka House apartment, but he was still pretty deeply lost in thought. “Have you been here all day … since Margen’s healing?”

“Nah,” Joe sighed.  “I ran a quick errand before starting to level [Witching Scour].  After a couple hours, I took a break with Mahq and Mojo.  Oh, they are starting a band, by the way.  On the way back, I bought a better curse wand.  After all that, then, I parked my butt here for the rest of the day.”

He stretched and looked out the window.  Night had fully fallen some time ago, but Joe wasn’t sure how long ago that was.  ‘Hey, Hawking?  What time is it, and how much progress have I made ?’

It is roughly half past 5-Bells-Dusk, or 10:36 PM using Earth’s chronometry.

Your efforts today have yielded you 5 ranks in [Witching Scour], 2 ranks in [intermediate Warding] and [Jequa's Verve], and 1 rank in [Crystal Mind], [Deaden Flesh], [Identify], and [Witchsight].

You gained the [Relentless Seeker] trait through an Achievement.

Achievement: You have labored for 8 straight hours on a puzzle.  You selected the following Trait as a reward for your actions.

[Relentless Seeker] Wow, you’re stubborn. If you fail with a non-combat, intellect-based skill more times than you have ranks in that skill within 1 hour, you gain an Insight Stack, up to a maximum of 5 stacks. You may spend any number of Insight Stacks to add 2 ranks per stack to the skill for your next attempt on the same task. All stacks are lost when you succeed or abandon the task for at least an Intermediate duration of time.

Joe felt Kenda's fingers stop as she digested his data dump, and in typical savant prioritization, she went after the skill-based fact first.

“What sort of wand?”

“I needed a curse that affects objects.  Preferably one with fey-based magic.  I found wands of [Billymuck Bane] are pretty common.  Billymucks are an oily type of boggle, you know, the gobliny-type of fey.  The spell makes objects slippery, so people use the hexes to trip or disarm opponents.  Even though it’s far less complex than the Erlkings' blight, it's still tricky as hell to unravel.  Fey curses are kind of stretchy, so they snap back together even when you sever one of the strands.  I think it’s a matter of getting the right strand at the right spot, but I still haven’t managed to do that yet.”  Joe vibrated his lips with a frustrated exhale before adding, “I think I’m getting close.”

“‘Getting close’ as in any minute now, or ‘getting close’ enough to take a break,” she asked.  “You look like you could use one.  Not to mention, you have to explain the ‘starting a band’ comment.”

Joe was torn.  He really did think he was close, but his brain felt a bit like mush.  There was a throbbing behind his eyes that no amount of healing had managed to assuage.  He had seen an option in the Healer skills that he had passed up called [Soothe], which was used to ease mental and emotional strain.  Even though it was exactly what he needed now, he knew spending attribute points for a non-life-threatening cure was not something he was ever likely to do.

Maybe a break was just what he needed.

Joe stood and turned to properly greet his girlfriend with a hug and kiss before explaining the Mahq and Mojo show.  Mojo had steadily gotten better with his penny whistle all day, to the point where he was creating repeatable melodies.

To Joe's utter amazement, Mahq had an unbelievable singing voice.  Getting the youth to speak clearly, when he wasn’t looking you in the eye, was always a challenge.  He tended to mumble his words and then get frustrated when you had to ask him to repeat himself too many times.  Yet, when singing, he was a whole different animal.  His voice had a haunting beauty that stopped the crowds on the streets.  The first time Joe heard Mahq sing, chills ran down his spine.  The young man rarely sang with actual lyrics, mostly just sounds or made-up words, but he didn’t need anything more.  When his voice rang with wonder, everyone felt it.  When it lolled with sorrow, people began to choke up; some were even moved to tears.  His joy made them dance.

Joe had planned on taking a brief lunch break and then getting back to work, but instead, he had spent almost two hours with the pair on the streets of Fort Coral.  Mojo picked a tonal mood, and Mahq brought the monkey-pup’s piping to life.  The duo wanted Joe to summon Finn to see if the great beast’s two-tone rumbling trill could be worked into their music, but Joe had to veto their request.  He wasn’t yet ready to call the big guy back, just yet.  He wanted to get a basic handle on his curse-work before he looked into the bane Devilbone Kava had laid on the chimeric breeds.

Additionally, he couldn’t have the hounds and Finn together.  Since the akhlut was more content at sea than the hounds were contained in their reliquary, Joe had sided with the ghost pack for the time being.  The spirit hounds were overjoyed to be out of the [Trisynun Ossuary]. Joe let them run around during the group’s musical meandering until it was time to head back to the apartment.  The boys headed to the Catstaff apartment, while the hounds came upstairs with Joe.

Once Joe settled himself back at his dining table, which had quickly become his workspace, Baru discerned Joe’s desire to focus.  Admonished by their pack leader, the two smaller ghostly hounds spent the rest of the afternoon alternating between people-watching out the windows and sleeping on Joe’s feet.  Baru sat at Joe’s side, unmoving.  Though, because her head was just a few inches away, the brawny girl ended up receiving the most ear scratches over the last few hours.

Now that Joe was standing and Kendell was here, all three of the Annwn Hounds were circling the pair, looking for attention. Joe scruffed a few heads quickly, before leaving it to her to apply the rest of the mushings while he packed up the table.  Thankfully, Kenda seemed to love dogs as much as Joe did, so it was not a big ask.  Even so, Joe worked quickly.  The death-blighted items were already safely ensconced in their cases, but he had plenty of tools and metal shavings to clean up before they scattered about the room.

Neither of them had eaten dinner, but it was too late for a full meal.  Both Joe and Kenda needed to stretch their legs, as did the spectral hounds.  They decided to take a walk and grab a snack as they went.  On the way out, Joe peeked in on the Catstaffs.  Berti told him that Mojo and Mahq had petered out quite a while ago.  The two were curled up together in the young druid’s room.  Joe could hear one of them snoring, but honestly, he wasn't sure if the snorer was Mahq or Mo.

Together, Joe and Kenda walked the spirit hounds down to the Seaside, where the playhouses, pubs, and eateries would be open for several more hours. On the way there, Joe asked her about her day.  It hadn’t been lost on him that she, too, had been busy until after ten o’clock at night. Kenda blurted out in an elated voice that she was going to be in charge of the initial expeditions within the Dirgeblue Vale, which is what the undead-infested valley was now calling itself.   To start with, she’d be leading teams of city guards to evaluate the difficulty rating of the twisted, blue-leaved forest, which had regrown itself overnight.

She felt sorry for Tez, who got stuck on security duty.  It fell to the archon to make sure no one attempted the dungeon before it settled into its final form and to ensure no one was claiming any of the death-cursed items that were still scattered about the valley.  Joe agreed that Kenda got the better assignment.

Edror was going to be her backup for the first few runs, but once Joe’s cooldown on [Anyone] expired, she said he could take that spot if he wanted it.  Which he did.  It sounded like the guards would be doing most of the fighting and leveling, but a chance to do some light-hearted adventuring with Kenda would be a blast.

As they walked through the Seasides entertainment avenues, numerous savory smells caught the wildness’s attention, and it set his gut rumbling in a loud protest.  It turned out he was far hungrier than he thought. While Kenda snacked on a small basket of fruit and cheese, Joe ended up devouring a dozen grilled meat sticks, mixing beast, fowl, and fish fairly evenly.

Between bites, Joe tried to describe the work he had been doing and found he needed a new vocabulary.  It was easy to talk warding with Kenda since it was very similar to her enchanting craft. Curses were completely different.  Warding and enchanting were all about building connections and formations.  Curses were more fluid and flexible.  They seeped together and formed bonds within themself.  They sought out metaphysical cracks and oozed into them to affix themselves to the target of the curse. The formations of an enchantment tended to stack up together in logical manners.  Curses were more layered.  Somewhere during the conversation, Joe realized that enchanting and warding were a bit like building blocks or math, whereas curses were more akin to painting.

“Maybe before you dive in again tonight or tomorrow, spend some time reading through the books you borrowed.  There may be a basic step that you aren’t aware of,” Kenda suggested, popping her last berry into her mouth.  The raspberry-like fruits had stained her lips, making them even more enticing.  It took Joe a second longer than it should have to process her advice.

“Good idea.  Now that I have taken a break, I can already feel my brain cells decalcifying.  A good night's sleep and some studying is probably a far better plan than just beating my head against the problem again, stubbornly hunting for something I haven’t tried yet,” he admitted.

“Brain cell?” Kenda asked, looking perplexed.

“Ahh.  Earth-thing, hon.  It doesn’t translate well to Illuminaria without a lot of explanation.”

“Hon?” she questioned again, this time with a wide smirk instead of a confused look.

“Ahh.  That kinda slipped out. You ok with it?” he stammered, not sure if they had reached the pet name stage of their relationship.

She answered by kissing his blush away and taking his hand.   They stopped to watch part of a street skit, but they could both feel the warmth growing through their intertwined fingers.  Long before the sketch concluded, the young lovers had retreated to Joe’s flower-wreathed home, seeking some time alone in each other's arms.

Or as alone as one can be with three ghostly hounds perched at the end of the bed.
