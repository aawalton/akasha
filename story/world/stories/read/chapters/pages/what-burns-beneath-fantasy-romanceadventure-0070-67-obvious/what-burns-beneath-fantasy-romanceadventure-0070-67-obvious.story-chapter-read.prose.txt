“Pet! Come back!”

I didn’t listen.

Cool grass and dense moss cushioned every step as I hurried towards the trees. Any fear I felt upon seeing the odd, ominous owl- or at least, what was appearing to be an owl- was fully swallowed by nagging curiosity.

I had a question for Horace. An important one.

Thin, scaly fingers closed over my wrist. Slip tugged with all the strength of a small child. “I’m supposed to keep you safe!”

“That’s not a mordelain,” I snipped, pulling free easily. “You said they’re big, and he-”

“Might be worse, pet!” Her croaky voice quavered. Both of her little hands fisted into the back of my hem.

I stopped, hands on my hips, and leveled a stern look at the little Fae. “I’m not a pet.”

My resolve cracked a bit upon seeing her face. Slip’s eyes were the size of saucers and she was trembling so badly the leaf-dress rasped against her skin.

I sighed. “Look, Slip, I’ve met him before. I don’t think I’m in any danger. Well. Any more than usual.”

Slip just tugged at my clothes, trying to urge me back to the dais with a little whimper.

I started to pull free, then spotted the glint of moisture on the goblin’s cheeks. My shoulders sagged.

Salt, is she really so frightened? I… ashes, all those things she said earlier. Children being flayed, stronger Fae hunting her kind… what an awful world this must be for her.

Or… saints, what if it’s not Horace she’s afraid of?

A chilling thought swelled. I knelt, covering the goblin’s knobby hands with my own and searching her face worriedly. “Slip. Your master, er, lord, whatever he is… he told you to keep me safe. So if I get hurt, will he… will something bad happen to you?”

She flinched. Naked fear flashed over her features before she looked down, placed one green knuckle in her mouth, and began gnawing on it.

“Slip?”

“It’s not as though you can run,” Horace’s pleasant, amused coo cut in. He was only just audible from our distance.

Slip squalled, eyes brimming with tears as her needle-sharp claws sank into my wrist.

I let out my own cry of pain. It grew to a scream as something tugged hard from beneath my navel, no, within my navel, pulling my insides along with it. My limbs wrenched in different directions, the sky spun and-

“Sorry, sorry, oh please don’t be dead!” A sobbing, squealing face appeared above mine.

I blinked, gasping in clean air. Cool soil cradled my back. Stars and moonlight framed Slip in an dizzying glow.

She patted at my face with scaly, shaking fingers. “Eyes, tongue, hair, skin’s still there, oh dawn that was-”

I rolled to one side, vomited onto a patch of moss, and caught sight of pristine marble framing a wall made almost entirely of glass. We appeared to be at the manor’s back end.

The goblin hopped about, fluttering both arms and flattening several flowers. “Oh, that can’t be good. Don’t worry, I’ll summon lord-”

“Please don’t!” I gagged, clutching at one thin shoulder. “Please! I’m… I’m f-fine.”

She seemed fully unconvinced.

I pushed myself upright on trembling limbs, forcing a watery smile. My stomach was thoroughly scrambled and my muscles felt like jelly. “Look, see? I’m fine. Please don’t summon… him.”

Her lips peeled back in a pained grimace. “I didn’t mean to hurt you.”

I was about to assure the little creature that she really hadn’t, but then followed her gaze. The goblin’s enormous eyes were fixed on five jagged divots in the delicate skin of my wrist. Each swelled with bright smears of blood.

“It was an accident, Slip.” I flexed my hand, wincing. The little cuts stung in the hot, sharp way of fresh wounds, but weren’t deep. “It’s alright. See? I’m fine.”

Slip gave a little moan. “Oh, but I wasn’t supposed to frighten you! I definitely wasn’t supposed to hurt you!” Spiky claws gleamed as they scraped along her scalp, tearing through coarse hair. Several black strands fluttered to the grass. “Dawn, dawn, lord Vel-”

“Doesn’t need to know,” I supplied quickly, straightening. “I won’t tell him. Promise.”

Her eyes shone with tears. “You won’t?”

“No,” I forced steel into my tone. “But only if you let me go talk to that owl.”

A guilty pit opened in my stomach when she squalled again, throwing her head back and wailing to the stars. “Pet!”

“I’m not a pet. Look, I… salt, I’m sorry.” My shoulder slumped in a heavy sigh. “I’m not trying to make things difficult for you. But I really, really don’t think he’ll hurt me. He said he wouldn’t, last time.”

Another strained wail broke from the goblin’s lips. She wrung her hands, hopping from foot to foot, and watched as I cautiously stepped away. She scampered after me a few times, whining and raking her hair, but didn’t intervene again.

I rounded the manor and crossed the garden quickly, before she could change her mind. Horace watched my approach with a slight curve to his beak. The obvious amusement grew whenever he glanced towards the shaking, diminutive creature trailing along behind me.

I marched right up to his perch, wasting no time with pleasantries. “How did you know? About his… the lord’s name.” My mouth warped into another grimace as I spat the word. “That I already knew it, I mean.”

The question had been nagging in the back of my mind. A fresh layer to a baffling mystery. My divine runes, my holy symbols of blessed, gods-given magic… and one of them was Velidan.

Not just the wicked Fae’s name, but the power behind it to drag him- quite unwillingly, from the way he’d recounted the experience and his insistence that I would never do it again- across the realms. The rune’s mere existence, and all the implications behind why a High Fae’s name was a divine invocation, was a burning question I had no way of answering.

And Horace… I remembered his words all too well. The first time we’d met, I’d asked if he knew my tormentor’s name.

‘I’m not in the habit of telling creatures what they already know.’

Spoken so casually, with that wry little smile. I’d assumed he was either jesting or mad.

Clearly, I’d been wrong.

Silver talons clicked against the willow branch. “And good evening to you as well, morsel.”

And just like that, I was hit by the reminder that I was standing face-to-face with a monster. One I’d just sounded quite petulant towards. Saints help me, I have no idea what he is or why he’s here, but this probably isn’t the time to forget my manners.

“Right.” I cleared my throat. “Good… good evening, Horace.”

“Your blood smells lovely, by the way.”

Frost crawled down my spine and silence crept around me. I tucked my bleeding wrist behind my back, heart stuttering.

“Don’t fret, little one. I’m hunting other prey tonight.”

“O-oh? That’s… that’s n-nice. Um, may I ask what you’re hunting?” I cast a worried glance towards the Slip as she took refuge beneath a dense, blossom-bedecked bush some distance away.

“Something disrespectful,” the owl hissed, wiggling a bit on his perch. He twisted his head almost fully around, facing the willow copse for a moment before turning back to me with a vicious snap of his beak.

I gulped. Right. Definitely not the time to forget my manners. “W-well, I… er, wish you all the… best. I… I’m sorry, I don’t mean to intrude, I… I just…”

The gleaming beak curved. “T’was not a foolish question.”

“I… please. There’s so much I don’t understand, and… I suppose I’m hoping you could shed some light on all this. Please. How did you know about my rune?”

“I know many things, little one. Magic always leaves a trail, and yours is brighter than most. It ripples away in so very many…” Shining golden eyes unfocused briefly. The feathers on his neck ruffled, plumping into a shadowy fluff, and he shook his head as if to clear it. “Mm. Let us speak of other things. Tonight, I’d prefer to just… be here.”

I gnawed my lip, shifting from foot to foot and glancing around uncertainly. Trying, fruitlessly, to puzzle through the bizarre statements.

Perhaps I’ve asked the wrong question. It’s one I’m curious about, of course, but… there’s wiser things to ask about.

I peered up, holding a breath. “Horace, is it possible to break a deal with a Fae?”

“Possible? Mm. I think doable is the word you’re looking for. And the answer, in your case, is no.” He twittered a little hoot, taking in my crestfallen expression, and added in a much kinder tone, “I’m sorry, little one.”

“You almost sound like you really mean that.” A miserable, mirthless laugh left my lips. It was an ugly sound, cracking the night air and falling like a stone between us.

Horace was silent for a while. Then, very softly, “I do. You… remind me of someone. A mortal I was quite fond of.” He cooed into the night air, and this time there was a hollowness to the sound. A little echo as it hung, empty silence swelling behind it… for a moment, he almost looked mournful.

I wrapped both arms around my middle. “Oh? What happened to this person?”

“She died. As mortals do.” He grew very still then, save for his eyes, which settled solemnly on me.

Once again, I shivered beneath the certainty that something very different from the owl’s lovely, glittering gold was watching me. I clasped a hand to my throat and had the strange thought that the unassuming form before me was… larger, somehow, than what I could see. Or perhaps larger wasn’t exactly the right word. Weighter.

“I’m sorry,” I murmured, trying not to tremble beneath the intensity of his gaze. “How do I remind you of this person?”

He chuckled, as if enjoying a private joke, and spent a long moment in thought before answering. “Mm. All mortals court death, but she did it so… brazenly. With such tenacity. Quite like you, little one.”

The blood drained from my face. “Saints. That’s not very reassuring, Horace.” The night air rang with his laughter, making me shiver again. “How did your friend die?”

The laughter died. “Peacefully.”

The world froze. The breeze died and, as one, every rustling plant fell silent. I glanced about, heart thrumming in the sudden stillness, as the weight of his proclamation sank into the very soil. It felt as if he was daring the realm itself to argue with sound or movement, and it dared not.

Even his countenance echoed the word. Utterly calm. Perfectly placid. Then a faint creak snapped my attention to his sharp-tipped feet, and I fell back with a gasp. The branch was bending beneath him. Dipping with a groan, threatening to snap, as though something impossibly heavy was pressing down on the wood.

Horace spread his wings, flapped once, and resettled with a little coo. The willow limb went still, the breeze picked back up, and I was left wondering if I’d imagined the entire moment. “Mm. You’re making me melancholic, mortal.”

“I… I’m sorry.” I couldn’t seem to look away from the branch. After a moment I steeled myself, shoulders back and nails making little half-moons in my palms. “I can… er, change the subject again, if you like.”

“I think that’s a wise idea.”

“Right. What does,” I nodded towards the distant, pristine manor with a grimace, “he want?”

“Oh? I thought the young lord’s desire was obvious.” Gold glinted down, shrewd and amused, and his voice took on a sing-song lilt. “Poor little thing, haven’t you figured it out?”

Irritation nipped the heels of my dread. “Well, I’ve figured some of it out,” I snapped back, aiming a scowl at the ground. “I know he’s looking for magical relics, though I don’t know why. And he apparently needs to make a portal back to the mortal realm, probably to… I don’t know, go terrorize more innocent people. Maybe he’s going to attack another town. I just don’t understand the point. What does he want?”

“At the moment, he only wants one thing.”

“Tell me, then.” After a moment I added, trying my best to sound less tart, “Please.”

His gaze settled on mine. Weighty again and twinkling in the light, brimming with mirth. “Why, he wants your heart.”

The air froze in my lungs. Silence coiled around me as I held a breath, waiting for him to… to laugh, to smile, to announce that he was jesting at my expense.

The only sound was blood rushing through my ears. I took a step back.

He’s just trying to frighten me. Or it’s a… a cruel joke. He’s bored, and this is his twisted sense of humor. Salt, or he means it literally! Maybe I'm going to be devoured or-

“Oh. Already?” Horace tilted his head back, examining the stars curiously. Lustrous feathers caught the moonlight, rippling with silver. “Time for you to leave, little one.”

I couldn’t seem to breathe. Just gaped up at him, every muscle locked into place. “W-what?”

One corner of his beak curved upwards. “Someone’s calling you, Brin. I think it’s important you answer.”

“What? Wait, n-no, don’t change the s-subje- oh!”

The world whipped sideways. Stars spun, silver-tipped willow leaves whirled, and every blade of grass beneath my bare feet slid away.

Slip cried out from her little hiding spot, rising as if to come help me. She took a step forwards, skin pallid and frightened eyes fixed on the feathered Fae.

Everything shuddered. Wind roared in my ears, and dancing within it was the owl’s quiet laughter. My last thought upon being torn away, dizzy and breathless, was that I’d never told Horace my name.
