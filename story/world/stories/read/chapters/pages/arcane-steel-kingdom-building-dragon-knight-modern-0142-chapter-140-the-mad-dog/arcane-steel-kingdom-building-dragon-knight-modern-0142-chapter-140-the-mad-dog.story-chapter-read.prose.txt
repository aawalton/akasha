The Cinderfall war council was a study in controlled panic. The stone walls of the redoubt, usually a symbol of the Hegemony's enduring strength, felt more like a tomb.

The generals and lords, men who had built their reputations on aggressive expansion and overwhelming firepower, were struggling to comprehend the new reality of the battlefield. The news from the east had finally trickled in, and it tasted like ash.

"The dwarves," Lord Tybalt of the eastern provinces sneered, his face flushed an ugly, mottled purple. He slammed his silver goblet onto the weirwood table, wine sloshing over the polished surface. "The loyal dogs of Khaz'Modan have decided to bite the hand that feeds them! After everything we’ve done for them!"

"Loyal?" Lord General Cassian scoffed, resting his hand heavily on the hilt of his sword. "They were conquered! The First King dragged them from their wretched mountains by their braided beards and took their precious flame! We civilized them. We gave them purpose! They have bowed to us for a thousand years, forging our weapons as is their duty. And now? Now they dare to close their gates when their betters demand passage?"

The sheer, breathtaking hypocrisy of the statement hung in the air, completely unnoticed by the men in the room. They truly believed that subjecting a proud nation to a millennium of forced economic servitude and cultural suppression was a favor. To the Cinderfall nobility, the dwarves weren't an allied nation; they were a utility. A resource node that had suddenly, rudely decided to malfunction.

"It is cowardice, pure and simple," Tybalt spat, pacing behind his chair. "They think they can hide behind their stone doors and their petty grievances while the real men fight their wars. They saw the metal toys this Wight boy brought to the field and got scared. Typical tunnel-rats. No honor. No stomach for a real fight."

The loss of the dwarven supply lines was a catastrophe, yet they refused to address it as a strategic failure, framing it instead as a personal insult. The Hegemony's military doctrine relied heavily on the constant influx of high-grade star-iron, adamantium plating, and alchemical mixtures produced in the deep forges of Khaz'Modan.

They had always assumed the dwarves' allegiance was a given, an unshakeable law of nature. It was an institutional blind spot bordering on mass delusion. The idea that the reclusive craftsmen would harbor any resentment, let alone mobilize and declare war alongside a boy from Aerthos, was a scenario none of their strategists had ever war-gamed.

"When we are done teaching this Wight boy a lesson," Tybalt growled, turning back to the table, "we will march east. We will rip their mountains down around them. We will drag their High King out by his beard and remind him why they kneel to the Phoenix. We will take every last forge and man them with our own people!"

King Theron Flavius listened to the bluster, his amber eyes hard and unforgiving. He recognized the bravado for what it was: fear. The lords were shouting to drown out the very real possibility that they were losing this war.

The situation was dire. If he tried to pull any more troops or resources from the interior of his kingdom to reinforce this front line, he would be leaving the established dungeons unguarded. The localized garrisons were already stretched thin. A single uncontained dungeon break in the heartland would cause more devastation than the entire Aegis Airforces bombing runs. He was boxed in.

"Drastic times," King Theron rumbled, his voice cutting through the angry murmurs, silencing the room instantly. "Call for drastic measures."

He stood and walked to the edge of the large tactical map spread across the table.

"From what we have observed," the King continued, pointing a scarred finger at the enemy lines, "the enemy relies almost exclusively on superior long-range weaponry to suppress our movements. Their turrets, their flying machines... they dominate at a distance."

He moved a cluster of red markers, representing the elite Phoenix Knights, forward on the board.

"We have been conserving our knights," Theron explained, his gaze sweeping the room. "We were holding them back, anticipating the deployment of high-tier combatants from the enemy ranks. But none have appeared."

He looked at his generals. "If we initiate a concentrated charge with our finest Tier 8 knights at the head, supported by our Tier 7, 6, and 5 combatants... we can break through with brute force."

He dragged the red markers across the map, pushing them into the blue line representing the legions automated defenses.

"Once our knights breach their front lines, their primary objective will be to destroy the long-range artillery emplacements and the rapid-fire cannons. Once those fall silent, our main infantry force will push forward and crush their remaining resistance."

The King paused, his expression darkening as he pulled a rolled piece of parchment from his belt. He unfurled it on the table. It was a remarkably accurate sketch of the Colossus-class Crawler construction sites, provided by a daring scout who had barely made it back alive.

"I do not know what these massive structures are," Theron admitted, his voice low and serious. "But our mages confirm the magical resonance is akin to a massive fortress. And knowing this boy..." He traced the outline of the spider-like legs supporting the structures. "I would not be surprised if these fortresses began to walk."

He looked at the assembly. "They have legs. I wager they are not for show. It is almost certain the boy is buying time for these monstrosities to finish construction. If they become operational, they will be significantly harder to break through than the current trench lines."

The generals nodded in grim agreement. The logic was sound. They had to strike before the enemy consolidated their position.

Lord Gravex, the man who had ordered the digging of the trenches, stepped forward from the back of the room. He hesitated for a moment, then spoke. "Permission to address the throne, Your Majesty."

Theron looked at the older man. A week ago, he would have had Gravex flogged for speaking out of turn. But the man had proven resourceful. His idea of digging deep trenches had saved thousands of lives and given the combat mages time to rest and recover their mana reserves.

"Speak," Theron commanded.

"My King, this is a truly splendid tactical maneuver," Gravex began, his voice careful. "But one fact worries me. The ships. The enemy vessels resting on the shoreline have proven to possess superior long-range firepower. Even if our knights eliminate the frontline artillery, those ships will rain fire on our main forces the moment they attempt to cross the valley."

King Theron actually smiled. It was a cold, sharp expression. He was beginning to appreciate Gravex. The man was one of the few in the room actually using his brain instead of his pride.

"I understand your misgivings, Gravex," the King said. "But worry not. I have been in regular communication with the Elven Queen."

He leaned forward, placing both hands on the table. "She has assured me her navy will depart for these shores shortly. They will reach us in five days."

A murmur of surprise rippled through the council.

Theron moved a set of green markers—representing the elven fleet—onto the map, placing them in the ocean to the west of our position. He then moved the remaining red markers of his own battered coastal fleet to the southwest.

"The enemy vessels will be forced to intercept them," Theron explained, a predatory gleam in his eye. "It is highly unlikely, even with their superior technology, that they can ignore the combined might of our naval forces."

He traced the projected naval battlelines. "If the enemy ships are dealing with two separate fleets attacking from two different vectors, they will not have the firepower to simultaneously support their ground forces. Furthermore, they cannot simply shrug off the elven navy. Those ships are fully crewed by combat mages utilizing long-range magical cannons. Unlike our ships, which rely heavily on defense, ramming, and boarding tactics, the elves can meet the enemy on a similar footing, attacking from a distance."

The King stood tall, his confidence returning. "If the enemy fleet chooses to ignore the incoming navy and continues to support their ground troops, our navy will attack their coastal supply lines while the elven fleet attacks them from behind."

The logic was brutal and sound. The plan was set in motion.

High in the air, aboard The Aegis, I watched the entire war council unfold on the holographic display in the CIC.

They were finally realizing that my army possessed a glaring weakness. Yes, my vessels and automata could combat a massive army, but they struggled in close-quarters combat against high-tier individuals. If a Tier 8 combatant managed to get close enough, they could theoretically tear a ship apart from the inside, bypassing the heavy external shielding.

Cinderfall had located my Achilles' heel. With the loss of the original Dragon Knights of Aerthos, I lacked the high-tier combatants necessary to engage their elite forces in a chaotic melee.

The only two people capable of going toe-to-toe with a Tier 8 Phoenix Knight in close combat were me and Bob. House Black’s Tier 8 mages were devastating on defense, but practically useless in this scenario; a majority of their power was lost when they moved beyond the range of their Mage Towers. It was a flaw I intended to fix with the next generation of spires, but for now, it was a limitation I had to work around.

Tes, I projected, leaning over the console. Run the numbers. What is the probability of them succeeding with this frontal assault?

[There is a 62% probability a frontal charge of this magnitude would succeed in breaching the primary defensive line,] Tes reported instantly. [However, if you were to enter the front lines and engage the Tier 8 Phoenix Knights, their entire strategy would crumble. They are not planning to deploy all of their Tier 8 combatants simultaneously. A maximum of seven to nine knights will be sent to spearhead the initial charge. You and General Goliath can easily deal with them. If General Goliath attempts to hold the line alone, the numerical advantage will allow several knights to slip past and destroy the automated turrets.]

I frowned, considering the naval threat. What is the probability my navy loses to the elven ships?

[In a head-to-head engagement, there is a 5% chance of failure,] Tes calculated. [Our vessels possess vastly superior mobility, shielding, and long-range firepower. Our ships are 65% stronger than the enemy vessels in pure combat capability. The numbers difference between them and us is staggering, but the primary reason the probability of failure is so low is the individual 'Theramore.']

The holographic display brought up a file on the old Commodore.

[He possesses a preternatural strategic mind regarding naval warfare. Under his command, the unified fleet will not lose a naval engagement.]

So the key to holding back the elven navy is giving Theramore free rein over the entire fleet, not just the 3rd Fleet?

[Correct. By utilizing vastly superior speed and hit-and-run tactics, the enemy forces will be whittled down significantly by the time they reach our shores.]

I looked over at the small, starlit blob rolling around beside the command console.

"Well, Kaelus," I said, a slow smile touching my lips. "I guess it's time for a bit of a workout. Lest you become the world's first pot-bellied dragon from all the sweets the girls keep feeding you."

Kaelus stopped rolling immediately. He sat up, looked down at his stomach, squinting his cosmic eyes thoughtfully, then looked back up at me.

But Brother, he projected, his mental voice sounding slightly defensive. The lady in your head said I am a growing dragon. I need the calories. He paused, tilting his head. But you may be right. She also said I need to do some exercise too. I guess it's time to chase some birds.

I watched him stand up and begin to stretch his wings. I couldn't help but wonder if dragons were just glorified, incredibly lethal house cats.

"Tes," I said, turning back to the comms station. "Schedule a call with Commodore Theramore. I need to brief him on the new naval strategy."

It was time to let the mad dog run loose.
