“This sucks,” Rex muttered as he made his way through the charcoal forest.

There was no way he could just accept every other person extorting him. But he couldn’t exactly take them in a fight, either. They were both stronger than him and outnumbered him.

“Well, I suppose once regrowth set in, this might actually be a decent hunting ground,” he mumbled as he walked through the burned forest.

It wasn’t just extortion; this was the only direction he was allowed to hunt in, and it was burned to the ground. Nothing but charcoal trees left standing.

Reaching the stream, he decided to follow it again. Perhaps if he went far enough upstream, he’d get back to some vegetation and find something to hunt.

Wait, the burrow is nearby, isn’t it?

He couldn’t hear anything like last time, but he assumed the goblins would have led the fire. Or burned.

I should check it out.

Scattered around the burrow, he found seven corpses burned to a crisp.

“This isn’t all my handiwork… Goblins certainly don’t have the best battle tactics, do they?”

Glancing into the burrow, he couldn’t see much. Just darkness and ash around its mouth.

“Stinks like smoke.”

Rex wasn’t sure how far the heat of the fire was likely to have reached into the burrow, but there was no guarantee it would have burned everything inside. Then again, there was a good chance the smoke might have suffocated everything within.

Would the smoke need airflow to fill the burrow, or would it just go down by itself? Rex wondered. Dammit. What if there are still goblins inside?

He remembered the warnings about how well goblins could see in the dark. There was no place more dangerous to hunt the little bastards than in their own burrows.

But what if they fled, or were all dead inside? And what if they left valuables behind?

After everything he had gone through, Rex couldn’t just walk away from a potential windfall. What if there was an artifact inside?

Where monsters were, artifacts could be found, even amongst the likes of goblins. And scoring an artifact would certainly make his debt issue sting a whole lot less.

But he couldn’t just charge down there. That was beyond stupid. Even a single goblin catching him in the dark could spell his end. If he was going to go down there, he needed a plan.

But I can’t just leave it. This is my burrow! I’m the one who cleared it, after all, assuming it is cleared…

Rex looked around, but there was nothing obvious that would help him.

Nothing. Hmm, think dammit! What would even out a fight in the dark against goblins?

Rex had already killed five, so he was feeling pretty confident. And if he had a torch, darkness wouldn’t be as much of a problem.

That light spell would sure be useful right now.

A torch wasn’t ideal. It was kind of hard to carry one while shooting off arrows. But he couldn’t think of anything else. And he couldn’t just leave a potential treasure behind.

There was a problem, though: anything and everything flammable had been burned. He didn’t have much on him, either. And it was quite the trek to find lush forest.

This is going to be effort. But there might be an artifact waiting for me down there.

It took him an hour to find unburned forest, and another two to find a good, solid branch and coat it in enough fibers and resin to keep it lit for a while.

Should be good enough. I hope.

Trusty torch in hand, he headed back to the burrow.

As expected, burned earth didn't expand far into the burrow. But the smell of smoke did. And to make matters worse, it seemed goblins weren't particularly clean. The burrow filled with trash and corpses.

“Disgusting,” Rex muttered and pulled a cloth over his face as he made his way deeper. He knew he probably wasn’t doing anything, but it made him feel better breathing in the rancid air. And the air was rancid; it was as if the goblin trash had been baked by the fire above, giving it a distinct warm stench.

“Still nothing. That’s good.”

Even with the torchlight, he was worried about goblin ambushes. But if there was anything still in the burrow, he hadn’t seen any signs of it.

Further down, he reached what he believed was the goblin hatchery. There were dozens of cracked eggs the size of a human head littered about the place, and huddled at the center of the room were about a dozen goblin corpses.

“So, it seems the smoke did get down here.”

Rex could still smell the smoke on everything, so he wasn’t particularly surprised by the sight. Although he knew goblins to be nasty little creatures that would take any chance to kill a human, the sight of them was a little sobering.

“Is this it?”

Wincing, he tried to search through the goblins for anything of value, but they had the same primitive gear he’d soon find on the others.

“Trash, all of it. And broken eggs,” Rex huffed and kicked the bones that covered the ground.

He couldn’t see anything of value. The goblin equipment was so poor he doubted it’d fetch even a few coppers on a place like Dorog.

“Not worth carrying around. Especially when I’m trying not to get in more shit. Ahh! This sucks!”

Frustrated, Rex kicked a few more piles of bones scattered about. At least they were good for a little stress relief. But as he kicked the third pile, he saw something glimmer against his torchlight.

“What’s that?”

Rex scrambled, searching through the bones he had just kicked across the cavern.

“Dammit! I hope I didn’t just lose whatever that was!”

On his knees, torch in one hand, he combed the floor in desperation. And then he spotted it, a small, green gem, no bigger than a marble.

“Wait… is that? Is that a gemstone?”

Rex’s eyes grew wide, and suddenly, he felt the faintest pulse of power emanate from the gem.

Flawed Emerald [ Rare ]

Socket this emerald into an artifact to gain its powers.

“It is a real gemstone… holy fuck! AHAHA! A real gemstone!”

Gemstones weren’t quite as rare as artifacts, at least not good ones, but they were rare. Even a weak gemstone could probably clear half his debt, if not more. Not that Rex had any intention of selling it.

Gemstones could be slotted into artifacts to boost their power. And while he had no idea what powers the green gemstone provided, he certainly wasn’t just going to give it away. It was the first real treasure he had claimed, and he was going to hang onto it.

“Okay, you were certainly worth it.”

Rex pocketed his treasure and kept looking around, hoping there was more treasure. But after over an hour of searching the burrow, he gave up. Still, it didn’t really matter. The gemstone was already more than he had expected to find.

“I wonder how goblins like this got their hands on a treasure?” Rex murmured as he thumbed the gem in his pocket and left the burrow behind.

By the time he got back outside, the sun was waning. And he needed to be back at the farmstead before he got in even more trouble with Mulvaney.

I’d better get back.

He hadn’t caught anything, but with the gemstone in his pocket, he hardly cared. The forest had been bountiful, and he couldn’t wipe the smirk from his face.

And on his way back, he sensed the auras again, and was immediately put on guard.

Oh shit. These guys want a kill, don’t they?

“You look awfully happy?” One of the hunters said, stepping out from behind a tree.

The man had a deer over his shoulders, and so the thought of him trying to hustle more from Rex annoyed him further.

“Me? I dunno about happy. I guess I’m just getting used to this forest. You know, acclimatizing, and all that.”

“Bullshit,” another hunter spat.

“Where’s our kill? You didn’t come back with nothing, did you?” The hunter said, walking toward Rex.

The others were circling around, hands creeping toward their weapons.

“He’s got something; I know it!” Another hunter sneered. “He was looking too damn happy when he was marching through here.”

“I’m inclined to agree,” said the first hunter. “Too damn happy indeed.”

Shit. If they find the gemstone, they’ll take it for sure.

Rex had to think of something. But he couldn’t fight them. They would kill him for sure.

Moments before reaching him, he heard rustling leaves and turned to see Mulvaney stepping out of the brush.

“What’s all this then?”

“Mulvaney! I didn’t see you there. I, ah–we were just helping out the newbie,” said the hunter with the deer, turning to smile at the hunt leader.

“Is that so?” Mulvaney glanced around at the hunters, clearly not buying it. “Well, you’re dismissed. I can handle him from here.”

The hunters snarled, but didn’t argue. Several of them turned to glare daggers at Rex before walking off.

“Looks like you’ve got nothing again,” Mulvaney said, stepping up toward Rex, whose smile was well and truly gone. “You’re testing me, boy. If you expect me to keep those thugs off your back, you had better come up with something tomorrow. Anything. Even a small catch will do for now. A hare, whatever. But trust me, if you come back with nothing tomorrow, there’s a good chance I'll ignore whatever is going on between you and the other hunters and let them have their way with you. Understood?”

“I understand.”

Rex nodded, but he knew he had to get out of this place. In the long run, nothing good was coming from working with Credo’s people. That much was more than obvious now.

“Alright. Get out of my face.”
