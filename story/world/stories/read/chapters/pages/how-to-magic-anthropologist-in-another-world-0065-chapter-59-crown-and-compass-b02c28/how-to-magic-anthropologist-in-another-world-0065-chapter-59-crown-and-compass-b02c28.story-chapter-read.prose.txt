I arrived at the sanctified camp, carrying a stack of nine pizza boxes, balanced carefully over one hand.

The moment I appeared, Vena noticed me. She froze.

Her eyes widened. Her composure shattered.

“Don’t tell me,” she said, her voice rising. “Those are pizza, aren’t they?”

From across the camp, Ja’a’s cyan head snapped around.

“Did you say pizza?” she called out, already halfway to her feet.

Kan blinked, looking between them. “For you two to react the same way, this ought to be good.”

“Oh, you are in for a treat,” Ja’a said, grinning ear to ear.

“Yeah,” Vena said. “Louis makes the best food, but her pizza is just the best.”

That did it.

I started handing the boxes out before they could swarm me.

One to Raik, who accepted it with noble restraint and very obvious curiosity.

One to Katar, who inspected the box, giving it a couple of sniffs like a bloodhound.

One to Kan, who looked almost suspicious.

One to Calr, who was already rubbing his hands together.

Two to Shingo.

I winked at him as I passed them over. Being two and a half meters tall and built like a living boulder meant he burned more calories than the rest of us combined. Shingo responded with a slow, solemn nod, clutching both boxes as if they were priceless treasures.

Last was Vena, who was still staring.

“You brought pizza,” she said again, as if confirming reality.

“Fresh,” I said. “Still warm.”

She inhaled sharply and clasped her hands together in a prayer. “The Holy provides.”

Despite having eaten only an hour ago, I grabbed a slice for myself and joined them, then sat on the grass as everyone opened their boxes.

The reactions were worth every step of carrying them, which, granted, weren’t many since I teleported.

Calr didn’t even wait for the steam to fade. He took one bite and froze.

Then another.

Then he laughed, a sharp, delighted sound.

“This,” he said, mouth full, “is sweeter than Chana’s ti…”

Vena shot him a look that forced him to stop mid-sentence. “Do not blaspheme.”

“Yes, ma’am,” Calr replied sarcastically. “Sorry, ma’am.”

Kan took a careful bite, chewed, then paused.

“…Okay,” she admitted. “This is good.”

Ja’a snorted. “That’s it? Just good?”

Kan took another bite, larger this time. “Fine. This is very good.”

Raik had gone quiet. He stared at his slice as it had personally challenged his worldview.

“Where,” he asked slowly, “can one buy this?”

I smiled.

“Louis’s new shop. It is not officially open yet,” I said.

Raik nodded solemnly. “When it does open, I will be a regular patron.”

Katar took two precise bites, chewed thoroughly, and swallowed.

“This is good for building muscle,” he said. “Still, I would rather eat a steak.”

He took another bite immediately.

And another.

Shingo had already finished his first pizza.

I blinked.

He lifted the empty box, looked at it, then looked at me, his expression completely neutral.

Then he opened the second box and went to work.

It was gone in record time.

Ja’a stared. I blinked.

Vena laughed, a soft sound she didn’t make often. “I believe Shingo has found his calling.”

Shingo wiped his mouth, then gave a thumbs-up. I gave him half of my own pizza as a bonus.

We relaxed after that, sitting on the grass. The tension of fighting a never-ending ghost army was completely forgotten.

“So, you know, I knew this would be good the moment Alice had the idea, the moment I tasted Louis’s cooking,” Ja’a rambled. “The girl never misses. But this? This is a new tier. If we sold these near the guild, I could triple our operating funds in a month. No, two weeks. No, wait, if we target mornings and evenings…”

Calr rolled his eyes. “What do you mean by our? Isn’t this Louis’s hard work?”

“I invested financially,” Ja’a corrected. “Plus, my sister is helping.”

Kan nudged Raik with her elbow. “See? You don’t need noble food all the time.”

Raik smiled faintly. “You have seen me eat travel rations. I am not that spoiled.”

“You eat only the most ​​austere military food or the most lavish noble food. No in-between,” Kan laughed.

“She got you there,” Ja’a grinned.

I passed around pieces of cake as dessert, to Ja’a’s delight. Then we lapsed into a natural silence.

Vena, to my surprise, began to sing in the holy tongue, a calm song about peace and perseverance. I recognized the song from the meme of the fox girl in the Great Dream, and I started to sing along whenever the chorus hit. To my double surprise, Kan joined in too. She seemed to only know the chorus as well. I guessed she did say her aunt was a Faithful.

“Flying sky high, a lasting lullaby,of peace and friendship, love and kinship,dream with me.”

Raik was looking at Kan like he was falling in love all over again.

I smiled and lay back on the grass, hands folded behind my head, looking up at the sky.

Even the weather was cooperating.

The fog was lifting, thinning into pale ribbons that drifted away on a gentle breeze, leaving the heavens clearer than they had been all evening. It was not as pristine as the sky over the Sunless Reach, nothing ever was, but it was good enough.

Good enough to see the stars for once.

The Hunter Twins stood sharp and bright, locked forever in their mirrored pursuit, and beside them rested the Crown of Laurel, its familiar curve of light calm and reassuring.

For a while, I just breathed.

The ground was cool. The air smelled faintly of ash and trampled grass. Around me, the team lounged in loose clusters, full and relaxed, voices low, the world feeling momentarily safe.

I closed my eyes.

And then something felt wrong.

I opened them again.

“What?”

I sat up abruptly, heart pounding, eyes snapping back to the western sky.

“What… is the meaning of this?” I whispered.

My voice came out thin.

“What’s wrong?” Calr asked.

I couldn’t speak. Not immediately.

I just raised my arm and pointed, finger trembling slightly, aimed at the stars.

Calr followed my gesture.

His eyes unfocused for a heartbeat, fogging over for just a second, the telltale sign of his mental power activating.

“…Ah shit,” he said quietly.

“What!” Ja’a demanded, alarm sharp in her voice.

She looked from Calr to me, then back at the sky, squinting. The others followed her gaze, confusion rippling through the group as they searched for a threat that was not there.

“Is it a monster?” Ja’a asked. “I’m not detecting anything. No hostile presence.”

“No,” I said, finally finding my voice. My throat felt dry. “It’s not that.”

I swallowed.

“The stars have shifted.”

There was a beat of silence.

Then Vena sucked in a sharp breath.

“Eep!” She covered her mouth, eyes wide. “By the Holy… the constellation of the Crown of Laurel has moved.”

“What’s the big deal?” Katar asked. “Don’t the skies shift all the time?”

“Not this one,” Vena said immediately.

“And not like this,” I added. “Stars drift slowly with seasons and latitude, but this…” I gestured again. “This constellation has completely changed position.”

“It used to be north,” I said. “Now it’s west.”

Raik frowned, his gaze still fixed upward. “What makes that constellation special?”

“It is the only constellation that appears in all seven realms,” Vena answered softly, reverently. “No matter where you stand, no matter the sky, it is always there.”

She hesitated, then continued.

“And it points toward the easiest path to reach the Holy’s main temple.”

The words settled heavily between us.

Understanding dawned slowly, then all at once.

“That means,” Calr said, his voice flat, eyes still locked on the stars, “there is a rift gate to the Mythic Realm.”

He raised his hand, pointing west.

“At the center of the Undead Dead Zone.”

Silence fell over the camp. Not the relaxed quiet from moments ago.

I exhaled slowly.

“You have a habit of jumping to conclusions,” I said at last. “That is one explanation, not the only one.”

Calr glanced at me, unimpressed. “You have a better one?”

“No,” I replied. “But before we start throwing theories, we need to check the lore. Constellations don’t just exist. They have histories, myths, and records, especially a constellation as relevant as the Crown of Laurel. We need to know if this one has ever shifted before, and under what circumstances.”

Vena nodded immediately, relief and focus returning to her expression.

“According to Holy lore,” she said, “the Crown of Laurel first appeared after the Second Holy-Unholy War.”

Everyone turned toward her.

“It did not exist before that,” she continued. “The stories say it manifested above the Lady herself as she roamed the land.”

Ja’a frowned. “Manifested how?”

“As a sign or a miracle,” Vena said simply. “The constellation is not made of stars. It is just a projection of holy will.”

“That makes sense, actually,” I nodded. “The same constellation of stars should not appear in different skies if it were made of actual stars.”

“It spread first during the Thousand Years of Peace,” Vena continued. “If you followed the constellation at night, it would always lead you to her. No matter where you started, you were guaranteed to meet the Lady eventually.”

Raik frowned slightly. “Guaranteed?”

Vena nodded. “That is what the texts say.”

Calr let out a low whistle. “That’s terrifying.”

“For the poor Lady,” Ja’a scoffed. “Imagine having no privacy.”

“After the Final Holy-Unholy War,” Vena went on, “and after the Final Reform, the Lady placed a book in the main temple. A moral guide for the faithful. Since then, the constellation no longer led directly to her.”

She gestured upward at the constellation.

“It led to the temple instead. Wherever you are in the world, the Crown of Laurel points toward it.”

“So the constellation tracks the Lady,” Kan said slowly, “or something directly tied to her authority.”

“Yes,” Vena said.

“And it used to point north to High Rock through the rift gate there?” Raik asked.

Vena nodded again. “I spent a night in High Rock, and the constellation was directly overhead.”

I rubbed my chin.

“Which means,” I said, “if the constellation has shifted west, then one of three things is true.”

I held up a finger.

“One. There is a rift gate to the Mythic Realm, and it is now the closest path to the Lady’s domain.”

Another finger.

“Two. The book has been moved.”

Vena inhaled sharply. “That would be… unprecedented.”

“And three,” I finished, “the Lady herself is currently in the Undead Dead Zone.”

No one liked that option.

Katar crossed his arms. “Why would she go there?”

“I don’t know,” I said. “But gods exploring places everyone else avoids is not exactly new behavior.”

Ja’a shook her head. “Either way, this isn’t something you guess at.”

“Exactly,” I said. “Which is why we need more data.”

They all looked at me.

“I just need to triangulate,” I continued. “Teleport to different locations and check the sky.”

No one argued.

An hour later, I returned to the camp. My mana was full, but my head throbbed faintly, as if I were mentally drained.

I dropped to one knee and spread a map on the grass.

Everyone crowded in.

I had scribbled symbols all over it, marked in two colors.

“Alright,” I said, rubbing my eyes. “Here’s what we’ve got.”

I tapped the map.

“Red stars mean that from that location, the Crown of Laurel points toward High Rock.”

Then I tapped the other marks.

“Blue stars mean the Crown of Laurel points toward the center of the Undead Dead Zone.”

Ja’a leaned closer.

I started listing them off.

“The Sunless Reach, red.” I tapped once with my finger.

“Hano, red.”

“Pickar Steppes, red.”

“Damada’s conclave, red.”

“Kurko Mountain Range, red.”

“Loumpa’s new village, red.”

“Even Ectamel, red.” I double-tapped it.

Calr frowned. “Even Ectamel?”

“Yes,” I said. “From inside the city, the constellation still behaves normally.”

I traced my finger to the edge of the map.

“The shift starts here.”

Everyone leaned in further.

“The first blue star was here, about an hour’s walk from Ectamel,” I continued. “Right where the cleared killing zone turns into meadow.”

Kan swallowed. “That close?”

I nodded.

“I jumped to multiple edge points along the Undead Dead Zone,” I said, tapping on a half circle of stars. “North, south, east. Different elevations, different distances.”

My finger circled the area.

“Every single time, the Crown of Laurel pointed inward. Always toward the same central location.”

Raik clenched his jaw. “So the constellation shift is only fixed to the Undead Dead Zone.”

“Yes,” I said quietly. “And whatever it is, it’s at the center.”

Vena looked at the map, then eyed me worriedly.

“isn’t the Undead Dead Zone more active at night? How did you check all those places?”

“I didn’t stay long,” I said awkwardly.

Vena stiffened. “That was reckless.”

“Only for seconds,” I said quickly. “I jumped in, checked the sky, compared the Crown of Laurel position with the Hunter Twins, then jumped out. I used very carefully worded wishes to return directly to the Sunless Reach each time.”

Raik grimaced. “You’re playing with fire.”

“I know,” I said. “But it was the fastest way.”

“I even checked the Bloodline Realm,” I added, trying to change the subject. “Just in case.”

They all looked at me.

“I jumped to Weavershall.”

Raik raised an eyebrow. “And?”

“The Crown of Laurel pointed straight at Hano’s rift gate,” I said.

“That was to be expected,” Vena said. “There is only one rift gate in the Bloodline Realm.”

The map lay between us, dotted with a spread of red and a tight concentration of blue.

During my explanation, Calr had gone completely still.

His eyes were fogged over, unfocused, lost deep in his mental power. He did not blink once.

Finally, he spoke.

“It is localized to here.”

He lifted his gaze from the map to the sky, tracking the Crown of Laurel once more.

“Yes,” I said. “There is something in the center that is interfering with the Crown of Laurel. It could be a rift gate to the Mythic Realm, like you said, or it could be something else.”

“No,” Calr said.

He turned his head and looked directly at me, eyes sharper than ever and slightly intimidating.

“You don’t get it.”

He paused before adding, “I figured it out.”

I frowned. “Figured out what?”

“Let me ask a question first,” he said. “Raik. Vena. I want you to answer at the same time.”

They both straightened.

Raik nodded once, and Vena mirrored him.

“What happened three thousand years ago?” Calr asked.

Raik answered immediately. “The Dravek invasion and the extinction of the ancient telepaths.”

Vena spoke at the exact same moment. “The undead invasion and the First Unholy War.”

They froze.

Then slowly turned to look at each other.

Calr exhaled.

“They’re the same event.”

Silence slammed into the camp.

I stared at him. “That’s not… possible. To get to the Mythic Realm, they would need to go through the Kindred Realm, and the Kindred Realm withstood the Dravak invasion.”

“Do they?” Calr cut in. “Need to go through the Kindred Realm, I mean.”

He stood and took a few steps closer to the map, then pointed to the blue cluster.

“Merumom,” he said. “Lord of Bones, the ancient Dravak, and Pinnacle of Death mana.”

Raik stiffened.

“He is said to have been slain by your ancestor,” Calr continued, looking straight at Raik, “Kitchi Agame, first of his name. Right here.”

His finger pressed into the center of the Undead Dead Zone.

“The blow that killed him created this place.”

Raik nodded.

“But that’s not the important part,” Calr went on. “Merumom was not just an undead Dravak. He was the one who invaded the Mythic Realm.”

Vena’s breath caught.

“Through a portal,” Calr said, “that led from here to somewhere near the Veil Kingdom.”

“That’s too much of a conjecture,” I said, frowning harder now.

“Is it?” Calr shot back.

He raised one finger.

“Why would the Silent One create rift gates in his home, the Contested Realm, to every realm except the Mythic?”

A second finger.

“How would an undead strong enough to kill gods appear out of nowhere and start ravaging the Mythic Realm?”

A third finger.

“A Dravak strong enough to do that existed. Henrietta’s final sacrifice was used to seal the rift gate, trapping Merumom in this realm.”

A fourth finger.

“There is a Temple of Compassion here. Of all places. Meaning at some point, this land was closer to the Mythic Realm than anywhere else in the Contested Realm.”

A fifth finger.

“The undead mana here is tainted with the myth of unholy, which is why Vena’s miracles become devastatingly effective.”

He raised his other hand.

A sixth finger.

Without saying a word, he lifted that index finger toward the sky and pointed at the Crown of Laurel, showing us the closest path to the Mythic Realm.

For a moment, no one spoke after that impassioned speech.

The wind passed through the camp, stirring ash from the bonfire.

I felt something click into place inside my head, gears I hadn’t even realized were loose.

“…I guess it wasn’t a baseless conjecture after all,” I said in lieu of an apology, breaking the silence.

Honestly, I couldn’t believe I had missed it.

All the hints and the timing matched. It was still a theory, yes, but after that explanation…

I wanted to believe it.

“Okay,” Ja’a said at last. “That was impressive.”

She shook her head slowly.

“I knew you were smart and liked to play dumb,” she added, “but that? I am genuinely at a loss for words.”

Calr finally relaxed, his shoulders sagging slightly, as the weight of holding that thought together had finally caught up with him.

“Now the real question,” Raik said slowly, “is what we do with that knowledge.”

“Oh,” I groaned, rubbing my face. “This is going to be a political nightmare, isn’t it?”

“Why?” Kan asked, genuinely confused. “It’s not like anyone can just clear the Undead Dead Zone for this to matter.”

Ja’a let out a short, humorless laugh. “You are thinking like a normal person. That’s the problem.”

She leaned forward, tapping the map near the blue cluster.

“Access to the Mythic Realm without going through the Kindred Realm,” she said, “is worth everything to a certain type of person. The kind of people who already command armies, artifacts, and monsters of their own.”

“Strong people,” Raik added quietly. “People who might actually stand a chance at clearing it.”

I frowned. “What is worth that much? The Mythic Realm trades with the Kindred. You can buy almost anything through them if you have enough leverage.”

Vena exhaled slowly, eyes distant.

“Immortality,” she said.

The word settled over the camp like frost.

“Bingo,” Ja’a said, grimacing. “Unicorn horn, pegasus blood, pixie wings. All alchemical ingredients can be refined into rejuvenation potions. Drink one, and your body returns to its twenties.”

“That’s the easiest version,” Vena said softly. “There are also pacts with the Fey, a nymph’s kiss, and a djinni’s wish. All of them can grant the same result, sometimes more.”

Kan stared at her. “You’re telling me people would march an army into that,” she gestured vaguely toward the Undead Dead Zone, “just to look younger?”

“To never die,” Raik corrected. “To rule longer. To outlast rivals. To become legends.”

“And they would,” Ja’a said. “They would raise elite forces, burn resources, and turn the Dead Zone into a battlefield if it meant access to that kind of power.”

Vena’s jaw tightened.

“And they would have to invade my home,” she said. “The Veil Kingdom. Any attempt to establish a foothold in the Mythic Realm from this side would require a beachhead there.”

Silence followed that.

Vena lifted her gaze, eyes sharp now, and looked directly at Raik.

“Which leaves us at an impasse,” she said calmly. “Duke Raik Agame of the Bloodline Realm, and Princess Vena Moonbearer of the Holy Veil, would not want to do the same thing with this knowledge.”

My stomach dropped.

I looked between them, my mind racing. I had never considered this. Not really. The team splitting over loot was unimaginable. This was different.

Raik didn’t respond immediately. He met Vena’s gaze evenly, his expression controlled, unreadable.

I shifted without thinking, placing myself slightly closer to Vena. Ready, spear in hand, full of star-mana. Prepared to teleport her out if things went bad.

Just in case.

Katar noticed.

He moved at the same time I did, stepping closer to Raik, positioning himself just off his shoulder. Not aggressive, but unmistakably protective.

Ready to defend him… from me.

Ah. Shit.

I realized then what that must have looked like. Two lines forming without a word spoken.

I raised my hands slowly. “Okay. No. That was not me choosing sides.”

The tension didn’t break, but it wavered.

“I trust Raik,” I said, forcing the words out clearly. “He saved my life. I am not assuming betrayal here.”

Raik finally spoke. “And I am not planning one.”

His voice was steady, but there was weight behind it.

“This knowledge,” he continued, “is dangerous precisely because of what others will do with it. My concern is containment, not conquest.”

Vena studied him for a long moment, then gave a small nod.

“Then we are not enemies,” she said. “But we are still standing on a fault line.”

Calr let out a slow breath. “Great. Ancient undead Dravak, mythic rift gate, and now inter-realm tension.”

Ja’a sighed. “I miss pizza already.”

Despite everything, a few of us huffed a weak laugh.

But the map between us hadn’t changed.

And neither had the blue cluster at its center that promised a portal to a distant land.
