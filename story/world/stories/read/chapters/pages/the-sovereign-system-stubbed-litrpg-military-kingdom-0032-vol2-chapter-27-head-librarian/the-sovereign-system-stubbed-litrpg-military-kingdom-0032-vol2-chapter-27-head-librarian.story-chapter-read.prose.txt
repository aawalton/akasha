Luke’s eyes widened, as large as saucers, when the window flashed before him.

It was the first time he’d seen someone ranked Legendary Tier—even Kayson and the others were only Epic.

But before he could fully process it, the door slammed shut behind him, yanking him out of his stupor.

Three pairs of eyes stared at him, silent yet intense.

Luke turned to face his subordinates. Their expectant expressions made it easy to guess what they wanted—but he didn’t respond right away.

They all want to come with me on this mission…? he thought, incredulous.

The problem was, he didn’t even know the full details of the mission yet. All the royal decree said was that he’d be supporting the retreat of an army from the Qin Empire.

But which army?

From what he understood, multiple campaigns were ongoing inside the Empire. He’d already taken part in one—the subjugation of Xiu Fortress—a mere few months ago.

It was likely that other armies had been given similar retreat orders for cities and fortresses farther south. Unlike his previous battle, however, he expected no interference from Lhair this far down the map.

That should’ve made things easier…

Yet unease gnawed at him.

Something about this didn’t sit right.

It felt like he was being pulled into something much larger—something that could shake the entire Marxx Kingdom to its core.

The battle for succession.

I’m accompanying the Second Prince… but what about the Crown Prince? Is he being sent on a similar campaign? Luke wondered, his brow furrowing.

“Luke… who will you be taking with you on this mission?” Victoria asked, her voice dangerously soft, her gaze sharp.

“Yeah,” Kayson added, shooting Luke a pointed wink. “Use that big brain of yours and make the right call.”

Before Luke could reply, Sebastian smoothly stepped in beside him. “I’m sorry, young Victoria, young Kayson,” he said with a bow, “but the young master has already made his decision.”

“I have?” Luke turned to his butler, brow raised.

Sebastian nodded, entirely unfazed. “Naturally. Who else could you trust more than me? Have we not shared a master-servant bond your entire life?”

Luke didn’t have a rebuttal for that. It was true.

But that didn’t mean he intended to bring Sebastian along.

“Not so fast, butler boy,” Kayson cut in, shaking his head. “If you leave the city, who’s going to be our contact with Hilda? Without you, all of Luke’s hard work could go up in smoke.”

Luke sighed. Annoying as he was, Kayson made a good point.

“I–I…” Sebastian stammered, realizing too late that he’d walked into a corner. His pained expression said it all.

“Which is why I should be the one to go,” Kayson said triumphantly, cutting off the butler’s panic with a grin of victory. He crossed his arms as if the argument was already over.

But Victoria was quick to cut in.

“I think you’re forgetting what the royal messenger said,” she said with a smirk, her tone laced with amusement. “He said Luke could bring one servant. I’m afraid that disqualifies you, boy.”

Kayson’s expression fell, his smug smile vanishing like a gust-snuffed candle.

In its place was the look of a man who had just swallowed a lemon.

“I–I—” he stammered, clearly scrambling for an excuse.

Seeing Kayson reduced to the same flustered state he’d just left Sebastian in, Luke had to suppress a laugh. It seemed Victoria was the only eligible choice to accompany him.

“She’s right, you two,” Luke said, breaking the tension with a measured tone. “I need you both to stay here and oversee our business venture while I’m gone.”

His gaze sharpened. “I don’t need to explain how important this opportunity is… do I?”

“No, young master,” Sebastian replied at once, bowing his head with solemn understanding.

Kayson, meanwhile, gave a wordless shake of the head. Though he couldn’t muster a response, his defeated look said it all—he’d accepted his fate.

Luke turned to Victoria, who had already wiped the victorious smirk from her face. He straightened his posture.

“Victoria. Will you accompany me on this mission?” he asked formally.

“Yes, young master,” she said, dipping into a graceful bow.

Luke nodded, satisfied. Victoria had proven her capability time and again. Having her with him would offer some much-needed peace of mind—provided she followed instructions.

“Now then,” he said, clapping his hands once. “While I’m away, the perfume and fragrant soap production must continue. Sebastian. Kayson. Focus.”

He crossed the room to a corner cluttered with vials, herbs, and makeshift tools—his improvised alchemy station. Though primitive, the results had been revolutionary for Ralis City's noblewomen.

For the next two hours, Luke carefully walked them through every step of the production process. Sebastian remained attentive, jotting down notes and asking pointed questions for clarity. Kayson, by contrast, wore an expression of growing boredom, his eyes drifting more than once.

Eventually, Luke dismissed Kayson with a sigh, realizing that, at least for now, only Sebastian could be trusted to carry the work forward.

When he was finally satisfied, Luke excused his butler as well.

It was late—far past his usual bedtime. Victoria bid him goodnight and retreated to her bed across the room.

Luke lay back in his own bed, eyes fixed on the ceiling.

Despite the fatigue, sleep refused to come.

His mind whirled with thoughts about the mission. The lack of concrete details gnawed at him, and the involvement of the Second Prince only made things murkier.

From all he’d heard, Prince Lyon was a notorious good-for-nothing.

So why send him at all?

They wouldn’t send the Second Prince without also sending the Crown Prince… would they? Luke wondered.

If prince Lyon returned with accolades, it could shift the tide of succession—perhaps even putting the Crown Prince at a disadvantage.

And if Luke’s theory held true, then the faction supporting Lhair likely backed the inept second prince as well. But that didn’t mean the Crown Prince’s allies would sit idle.

In fact, Luke was almost certain that Prince Leo would be sent out too—on a separate mission.

‘I need to find out more before I rendezvous with the expedition,’ Luke concluded, eyes narrowing in the dim light.

There was only one person Luke could turn to for more information about the mission—the man he had quietly coveted ever since discovering his rank.

Tristan Gale.

A Legendary-grade subordinate.

Sleep did not come easily that night.

Luke tossed and turned, haunted by the clash of steel and the screams of dying men—echoes from past battles. Thunder bombs exploding in the distance, bodies falling beneath the smoke— the memories resurfaced now that he was set to return to the battlefield.

By morning, his head felt clouded, heavy with unrest.

He rubbed the sleep from his eyes and sat up slowly. There was no remedy for this kind of exhaustion—not here. If he were still on Earth, he might’ve popped a painkiller and soldiered through the day.

Instead, he washed up, had a quick breakfast with Victoria, and made his way to the academy library.

His goal was clear: find Tristan Gale, the head librarian, and extract whatever information he could.

Even if the man knew nothing about the mission, Luke hoped to leave a good impression.

After all, how often did a Legendary subordinate appear?

Until now?

Never.

Luke climbed the library’s grand staircase, ascending level after level. Only when he reached the seventh floor did he finally encounter someone.

A lone guard stood watch, spear held upright, gaze sharp.

“The higher floors are off-limits to students without prior authorization,” the guard said coldly, looking Luke up and down like he didn’t belong.

Luke eyed the man. He hadn’t seen this guard around campus before.

Likely stationed here just for the library.

“Good morning,” Luke said politely, cupping his fists in greeting. “I’d like to request an audience with the head librarian.”

The guard frowned. His thick brows nearly met in the middle.

“The head librarian doesn’t accept visitors,” he replied flatly, making a lazy shooing motion with his free hand. “No exceptions.”

Luke’s eyebrow twitched—but he held his composure.

“I understand,” he said, bowing again. “If possible, would you kindly relay a message the next time you see him?

Please inform him that Viscount Drakon is seeking his insight.”

That got a reaction.

The guard’s expression faltered. His eyes widened ever so slightly—panic mingling with indecision—as if scrambling to reassess the situation.

“P-please wait a moment,” the man said hastily, reaching out just as Luke began turning away.

Luke allowed the corner of his mouth to twitch into a smile—barely—before schooling his features. He bowed again.

“Thank you for your consideration.”

The guard practically sprinted up the staircase, no doubt terrified of offending a noble.

A few minutes later, he returned, winded and flushed.

“The head librarian invites you to the tenth floor,” he said, bowing deeply.

Luke nodded in thanks and began his ascent, passing the guard with calm, measured steps.

Three more flights brought him to a quiet landing—and a door, left slightly ajar.

Not wanting to be rude, Luke knocked a few times and cupped his fist.

“Sorry for the intrusion, Head Librarian. Do you have time to talk?”

“Come in. Close the door behind you,” a deep, composed voice replied from within.

Luke stepped inside, quietly shutting the door as instructed.

What greeted him was a spacious, unexpectedly cozy room. Various furnishings—lounges, small tables, ornate lamps—were scattered throughout, giving the space a lived-in, almost domestic atmosphere.

If not for the towering shelves and the hundreds of books littered across every available surface, Luke might’ve thought he’d wandered into someone’s private study rather than the top floor of the library.

He moved forward slowly, careful not to gawk or seem rude.

Tristan sat by a large window, basked in the warm glow of sunlight spilling across the room. Before him was a table, and atop it, a Chinese chessboard with the pieces already set.

“Care for a game?” Tristan asked, smiling up at him.

Luke offered a wry smile. It had been some time since he last played—his victory against Kayson back in Clayton City felt like a lifetime ago. Fortunately, among the many books he’d devoured at the Royal Academy, several were advanced guides on Chinese chess strategy.

“Sure. As they say, you can learn a lot about someone by playing a game of chess,” Luke replied, quoting one of those very books.

Tristan’s smile deepened, a glint of appreciation in his eyes.

Clearly, he agreed with the sentiment.

The two sat across from each other, and the air between them shifted—growing focused, contemplative. A battlefield of black and red awaited.

Tristan played first, taking the red side. He opened with the Central Trebuchet, quickly establishing a firm foundation with his horse and elephant pieces.

His base is solid. Definitely the type who ensures his army is well-prepared before advancing, Luke thought, eyes narrowing with interest.

Luke countered with the Screen Horse Defense, a more flexible approach that opened the flanks and hinted at long-term planning.

Tristan paused, studying the board in silence.

His next sequence of moves revealed startling precision. He utilized the full potential of his pieces to construct a formidable shield around his general, adjusting his lines with expert finesse.

Each time Luke probed for weakness, Tristan retreated—never trading blows, always preserving strength.

This man would excel in a siege, Luke noted, rubbing his chin.

Against someone like this, I need to be bold.

Without hesitation, he pushed a pawn forward, exposing his trebuchet piece in what looked like a blunder.

His expression didn’t flicker.

But in truth, the move was a trap—a calculated risk designed to bait an advance. If Tristan took the bait, his flanks would collapse inward like a folding fan.
