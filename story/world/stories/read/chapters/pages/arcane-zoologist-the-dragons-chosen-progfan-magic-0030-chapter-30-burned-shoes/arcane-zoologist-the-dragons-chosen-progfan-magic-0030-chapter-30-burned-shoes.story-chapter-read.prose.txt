"Come on! You can do it, carrothead!" Tristan said as he sat lazily in between the colorful blooms of the flower meadow.

Callie exhaled, trying to concentrate on the motions Mom had shown her so many times. A small bucket of water sat in front of her, and her eyes were glued to the liquid inside.

Mom and Dad had both been secretly training her in the art of magic. As well as they could, at least. Their knowledge was severely limited. Dad could do fire magic, and he used it every day while forging blades, tools, and the like.

However, it quickly became clear that fire magic was not something Callie should attempt. Not because she couldn't get it to work. She could, and that was the problem. Each time she tried, she nearly caused a fire.

Although Callie tried to mimic the motions Dad was doing to create fire, it never helped with control. She tried to approach the fire calmly, but that resulted in no fire at all. The only time she could get it to come out was when she deliberately thought of something frustrating.

So it was agreed that fire magic would be off-limits for the time being. Callie didn't mind. She wasn't particularly interested in setting things on fire.

Water, on the other hand, refused to do anything. Maybe her motions were just… wrong.

"Your leg is trembling," Tristan noted.

Callie tried to steady her leg. Mom's movements were always so elegant. It looked like she was dancing with water, the way she made it move in the air along with her hand movements.

"Now your arms look broken," the boy pointed out.

Callie grit her teeth. She tried to correct her hand elegantly, to move it fluidly as Mom did. All she wanted was for the water to move. Just a tiny droplet in the air would have been enough. But it just refused to budge. Not even the slightest tremble in the bucket.

"Oh, I think I saw it move a little," Tristan jumped to his feet excitedly.

"Really?!"

He laughed. "Nah, that was the wind!"

"ARGH!" Callie growled, chopping the air with her fist in frustration.

Magic that had been mostly lying dormant within her suddenly exploded outward, and water in the bucket froze over in mere seconds.

"Whoa!" Tristan jumped back. "I didn't know you freeze stuff."

Callie stared at the bucket. She didn't know she could do that either. But there was no joy in finding this out. It wasn't like she had done it on purpose. It was nothing more than an accident. Another bout of uncontrolled magic.

With a loud huff, she slumped into the grass and buried her head behind her knees. "I'll never be able to control this…"

"Who cares?" Tristan said casually, walking up to the bucket and inspecting the ice within it. "You can do magic. That is so cool! I still can't believe it."

Ever since Tristan found out about Callie's magic, they have become friends. Kind of. He was still mean sometimes. A lot of the time, really, but he tried to apologize afterwards. Sort of. It was weird, to be honest, but Callie didn't have much choice in the matter.

He had found out about her magic and was forced by the chief to remain silent. Since the chief forbade Callie going anywhere alone, Tristan was often the one to remain with her since they were close in age and both apprenticed at Granny Ethel's.

Tristan was as pleased as a lizard on a sunny day. He was let in on a secret only a select few in the village knew, and that made him feel special.

Although Callie had to be grateful that nobody else had figured her out. Apparently, her mark had been glowing a little after the glauxis bowed to her. But the large arcane beast bowing before a little girl must have been what caught people's interest.

Speaking of that, Callie couldn't help but cringe at how the villagers viewed her now. While previously she had been seen as that weird kid who liked arcane beasts, that viewpoint had completely shifted for most people. They feared her.

Instead of asking what kind of mischief she was up to, they went out of their way to avoid her. Aurelia and the other village girls, especially. Callie once tried to greet them when she ran into them by accident. But the girls scattered without replying, all while glancing around fearfully. As if Callie could make arcane beasts appear out of thin air.

Callie thought she would have been happy to be rid of Aurelia and her ilk, but she actually felt a bit lonely. Perhaps that was why she felt comforted by Tristan's company, even if his tongue was as sharp as ever.

"I don't want magic…" Callie muttered dejectedly.

"Why would you say that? Magic is so cool! I wish I could do it. Maybe if you don't want it, you could give it to me?" Tristan poked Callie's arm.

She looked up. "You would just burn the village to the ground!"

"No!" He protested, then gave a mischievous, wide grin. "I would burn the whole forest to the ground. MUAHHAHA!"

Callie couldn't help but chuckle at his antics. If only things were as easy as Tristan made them out to be. But things were far from easy.

Magic was hardly the only problem on Callie's mind. Nearly two months have passed, but nobody had any clue who the egg thief was. It certainly wasn't for the lack of trying.

After the glauxis left, the chief had announced that somebody had deliberately stolen its egg. The whole village went crazy. Who could have done such a reckless thing? Obviously, many pointed to Callie at first, but it was quickly made clear that Callie found the egg.

After all, the glauxis lived in the north, so one would have travelled a considerable distance to find their nest. Callie couldn't do that. It could have only been done by somebody who traveled a lot. Which basically meant it could have been any of the hunters, merchants, or adults leaving the village to go shopping in town.

Only children and the elderly, like Granny Ethel, who didn't or couldn't travel, were excluded from suspicion. Everyone else was scrutinized. Chief Aemon questioned every hunter, checked everyone who had visited the village during the summer, and even ordered that old traps be dug up and inspected.

The first week was by far the worst. Everyone was suspicious of everyone, and they kept watching Callie at all times. Villagers had been thinking the same thing that she had. The thief would come to seek revenge against her. Unfortunately, their attention meant that she couldn't do anything.

She couldn't meet with Wiggles or Snowglow or practice any of her magic. Someone was always watching. It became so unbearable that Callie left her room only when it was absolutely necessary.

After two weeks, Dad got really angry at anyone who dared to so much as look in Callie's direction. He accompanied her whenever he could or sent Callen with her when he couldn't. Eventually, thanks to their relentless efforts (and much glaring), the villagers stopped following her and instead tried to find the thief in other ways.

However, it didn't matter what they did. There wasn't a single clue left. Even the hole where Callie and Tristan had found the egg had been covered up. If Tristan hadn't been with Callie at the time, then it was likely nobody would have even believed her that it was the place where she had found it.

"Hey, come on! You can't expect to learn magic so quickly. It takes many adults years before they can do it," Tristan said, being strangely encouraging for a change. "I still think it's cool you made this ice. How about we put it in people's shoes?"

"No! We can't do that!" Callie protested, then sighed. "It's not about magic… I can't believe the thief hasn't been found."

"Maybe they just left," Tristan shrugged. "Or maybe they never really entered the village and just hid near it."

"I suppose that could be it…" Callie nodded, although she didn't feel satisfied with this answer.

"Hey, carrothead, look there." Tristan punched her lightly in the shoulder.

"Ow! What was that for?" She grumbled but looked in the direction he was pointing to.

At the edge of the flower meadow, where the forest moved, something moved. Something small and something green with a bit of red.

"Wiggles! No!" She hissed, running towards the mireling.

Tristan ran after her. "You really need to teach it to stay away from the village. I heard just yesterday that Gerda's mom found an odd hole in the heel of her shoe. Almost as if acid had eaten through it."

"Oh no…" Callie despaired.

Wiggles hadn't been happy with all the attention Callie had been getting. In fact, he was very unhappy because he had no chance to come play with her. So in order for people not to bother her, the mireling has started burning their belongings. Mostly shoes.

Hello,  friend!

Wiggles wobbled with delight when Callie approached him, extending his tendrils in greeting.

"No, Wiggles, you can't be here! You know that!" Callie chastised, and the mireling instantly deflated.

"Heya, slimeball!"  Tristan greeted, grinning at Wiggles.

The mireling regarded him skeptically. Since Tristan was almost always with Callie, hiding Wiggles from him became basically impossible. Thankfully, the boy didn't attack him anymore, and actually came to accept the mireling's presence.

Not out of kindness. Tristan just loved that Wiggles was burning holes in everyone's trousers and shoes. He found it hilarious.

Stick boy. No like.

Callie patted Wiggles consolingly. She knew that he only wanted to play, and she would have loved to spend more time with him. Not just to play, but to understand mirelings even better. Her notes would have to be expanded, and she had a few ideas on what she could add.

If only she had a safe space to study Wiggles. But somebody was watching her at all times, and villagers being so aware of her every move, Callie couldn't do that. Especially with Wiggles burning holes in the shoes.

"Wiggles, you cannot burn people's shoes! We've talked about it," Callie said firmly.

The mireling deflated even further, resembling a puddle at this point.

They bother friend.

"They don't bother me…"

They do. Friend unhappy.

Callie didn't know what to say. Things have been quite tough lately. The fact that they would have to start harvesting crops any day now meant that Callie would be buried in work. And after that, it would be winter preparations.

"Even so, you can't burn their shoes. They don't mean anything bad. They… are just worried. Okay?"

"You totally should continue burning their shoes," Tristan cackled. "Watching them stare at the holes and wonder who did it is hilarious! Maybe I should try it too?"

"Tristan, no! Wiggles, don't listen to him. People are already on edge. If they find out you're burning their shoes, they will hurt you. I don't want you to be hurt," Callie noted, patting the mireling.

Wiggles careful. Not caught.

The mireling was completely missing the point here. A burned shoe wasn't like a stolen egg. People needed shoes for work and warmth, and shoes cost a lot of money. Thankfully, there was a shoemaker in the village, but leather cost money. Unfortunately, Callie had no clue what to do to change Wiggle's mind.

"Please, Wiggles. No more burning. I promise to find time to play with you. Okay?" Callie said.

The mireling didn't appear fully satisfied. Perhaps, he too was mischievous, much like Tristan, and actually enjoyed burning shoes.

Okay…

"Thank you!" Callie said, pulling the mireling into a quick hug.

"Aww, he agreed? Dang it!" Tristan cursed.

"That's a bad word!" Callie gasped.

Daaang it?

"Not you too, Wiggles!"

There was definitely something in common between these two.

Callie played with Wiggles for a little while before she and Tristan were forced to head back home. Days were becoming much shorter with the arrival of autumn. This also reminded Callie that she had very little time left until her survival test.

She would have only one chance to do it right if she wanted to go to the town and learn more about her magic from books. Lessons with Mister Blackthorn had been tough—he had become even stricter after the whole incident with the glauxis.

Callie wondered if perhaps he could be…

"Callie, hey!" Callen called, running towards Callen and Tristan.

Tristan disappeared almost instantly. He was afraid of Callen, perhaps even more than before, and would always slip away when Callie's big brother appeared. She didn't know why and figured it was better not to ask.

"What's up? You look happy," Callie noted the wide smile on her brother's face.

"I just got news from Mister Blackthorn. Your survival test is happening tomorrow!"
