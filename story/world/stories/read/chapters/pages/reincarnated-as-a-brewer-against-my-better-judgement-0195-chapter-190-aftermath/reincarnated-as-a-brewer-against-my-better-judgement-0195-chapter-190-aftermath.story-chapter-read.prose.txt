Alric woke up on his back, his face a scant few inches from rustic wooden panels. Glancing to his left, he saw the same. For one dreadful moment, he thought he might be in a coffin. Then he felt a warm breeze on his right; turning his head, he saw Simon.

The relief he felt seeing Simon lying there, drooling, snoring, and with a trail of snot was difficult to describe. That’s when the headache flared; he tried to bring his hand to his face and found he had to turn his arm sideways. He tried to roll onto his side and couldn’t. Alric had never found himself claustrophobic before; that was apparently because he had never unlocked the correct conditions, until now.

“Simon! Simon!” Alric shouted, trying to keep the panic out of his voice.

“Huh? Mister Alr—” Simon began.

“Simon! Get me out of this and then explain where we are!” Simon nodded and began shuffling, then fell out. This seemed to prompt a cascade of complaints.

Alric, meanwhile, was scrambling toward the rectangular opening. He couldn’t roll over, so he had to use his legs to push against the walls and crept to the opening and freedom. All Alric could see through the opening was armor, thighs, and eventually he got his head out.

“See? Told you we shouldn’t have put him in there.” Someone said just as Alric pulled his head out; he recognized the voice as one of his guards but didn’t know the name.

Alric glanced around; one smaller wooden box opened to a larger one, but Alric could just glance at the outside. “Uh? Where are we?” Alric asked, his head returning. He reached out a hand and rubbed his head. Around him, he saw his guard retinue in various states of trying to pull themselves together.

“In a wagon, Mister Alric, fancy one with a bed.” A guard said, pointing to the opening Alric was emerging from.

Alric nodded, scraping his chin against the wood. Relief and cool air felt good against his face, but then he realized the next problem. He was on his back, on what could generously be called a lumpy mattress, roughly a meter off the floor and with very limited room to maneuver.

“Uh, I have several questions. First, why am I in a wagon, and second, how do I get out?” Alric glanced. With the immediate panic subsiding, he began noticing things. His tongue was thick, and his mouth felt furry. His headache was flaring, and he had cramps in both legs. Alric closed his eyes and rubbed at them.

“You couldn’t walk, Mister Alric.” A guard explained.

“That’s not true. He could walk, just not to something.” Another guard interjected.

“Not where he needed.” Came a third with a chuckle.

Alric nodded as the memories of the party trickled back. He opened his eyes again and saw his guards offering hands. He took them gratefully, and they pulled him out and got him to his feet. Someone passed him his boots.

Around him, all his guards were still in a state of pulling themselves toward themselves. Putting on boots, tightening straps, yawning. Someone offered Alric water while he put on his boots; he almost took it before deciding dysentery wasn’t going to improve his morning.

He took a deep breath and inspected the ‘bed’ he’d slept in. It was a double mattress, albeit lumpy, placed beneath a very small cutout. He wouldn’t have climbed in there sober. After adjusting his cloak and rolling his shoulders, he finally made for the exit, determined to find tea.

The muster yard looked different in the early morning. Mist lay heavily along the river again and gathered in low parts of the field. Fires had gone out, leaving faintly smoking piles of wood. People were already trying to stoke the fires back to life. Everything beyond that looked like the aftermath of a battle fought against beer.

People were sleeping everywhere.

Some had tried. Cloaks rolled into pillows, bedrolls scattered around. The brewery carters had been vindicated completely for their foresight, Alric decided.

There was, however, abundant evidence of decisions that may have seemed brilliant several beers ago. Three shields leaned together to make a little shelter; a pair of boots stuck out from underneath. Another man slept beneath a table, his cloak spread out over the top of the table.

Further away, a spear had been planted upright. Several pairs of boots were tied to it, dangling in the breeze. Alric counted six pairs and decided that knowing the story behind the boot tree would probably be worse than not knowing.

Heading toward Berrin’s section, he kept noticing small details. Stromni, for one, was asleep, spread on the table where Alric last remembered seeing him. His snore was the most impressive so far. His hand still held his tankard. Two other men were asleep, sitting upright, backs to one another.

The most impressive example, however, was at the pole to the king's perch by the innkeeper's side. A soldier was tied to the pole, mostly through belts and rope, completely asleep and completely upright.

“Kept falling over.” An adventurer’s voice came from nearby; he was pointing at the tied soldier with a grin.

“So you tied him to a pole?” Alric asked but chuckled.

“That we did. He said he was standing watch.” The adventurer said, nodding in satisfaction at the tied-up soldier.

Alric nodded at that; he watched further along as a group headed to the beer wagon. “Is the beer finished?” Alric asked the adventurer.

He shook his head. “Three barrels, reckon each is about half full.”

So still a barrel and a half.

The adventurer he had been speaking to shuffled and finally got up. He picked up his tankard. “Well, we ain’t done. Do be excusing meself.” He said, heading for the beer wagon.

“I am,” Alric said to himself, although one of the two guards that followed him scoffed. Alric grinned at him, and he grinned back.

Finally reaching Berrin’s section, he was greeted by a welcome surprise. “Monica. How are you?” Alric said with a grin.

“I’m good. We all are.” Monica said as she moved away from what she was doing and gave Alric a brief hug. She smiled brightly, then seemed to take stock of Alric. “You need a big breakfast, a lot of tea. Let me sort you out.” She said as she returned to gather a board and load it for him. Alric didn’t argue.

Berrin’s section was the busiest. Assistants passed out boards of food and filled tankards with tea; a small group gathered around a fire.

“Can you get me a second? I need one for… Actually, sort these two out as well.” Alric said, gesturing to his two guards, “I need a board for my dwarven friend.”

Monica nodded and spoke briefly to an assistant as they began putting it all together.

“Can I ask about Berrin?” Alric asked while Monica kept piling up a board for him.

Monica nodded, “I let him sleep. I can handle breakfast and packing up easily enough. The inn is full for winter, though, Mister Alric. First time that’s happened.” Monica said with a grin as she handed over the board. Alric’s guards stepped forward and collected theirs and the extra.

“Truly? That’s good news. I’m glad for you guys.” Alric said. Monica glanced around the field, his hands on his hips, and nodded at the field with a satisfied grin.

“Are you doing this next year again?” Monica asked.

Alric shrugged. “Not sure. Anyway, I need to go feed the dwarf. We need to catch up sometime. Winter maybe?” Alric asked. Monica nodded, still smiling; it wasn’t a professional smile Alric noticed; it was a real one.

Alric took a sip of his tea, and it tasted amazing somehow. His board held a generous slab of bacon, eggs, fire-toasted bread, a bowl of porridge, sausages, and what looked like mushroom stew. Alric was excited for it all.

Heading back, he noticed something that annoyed him. Calvern was sitting on a bench; a group of mixed soldiers, brewers, and adventurers were sitting on the grass nearby, clearly telling a story of some kind.

The group in front of him looked enraptured. What annoyed Alric, however, was how well put together Calvern still looked. Alric would have expected him to have left; instead, he looked like he was holding court for the hungover. He waved at Alric when he spotted him, raising his stein to Alric. Alric lifted his board in response, trying to decide if he should hate him.

Approaching the table with Stromni was a bit clearer. Stromni had woken up and was glancing around. When he spotted Alric coming with food, his face seemed to flicker with religious fervor.

“Oof.. Lad, if ya were a lassy.” Stromni said as the guard passed him a board laden with food; they all scoffed as they settled down. Stromni didn’t say anything more for a long while; he just kept making happy dwarf noises as they all ate.

Eventually, after feeling slightly more alive, Stromni glanced around the field. “How’d we do?”

“Three barrels still.” One of Alric’s guards responded as he was too busy finding new life in a sausage and tea.

Stromni let out a long dwarven swearword that got everyone nodding. Around them, soldiers, adventurers, and brewery workers were arguing over who had failed most the previous night. People who had left were returning with fresh clothes. Two men were arguing near the boot tree over who owned which boot. The soldier tied to the post was loudly shouting to be let loose.

Alric took another sip of tea, deciding that all of it looked like someone else's problem. “How’s the distillery wall coming?” Alric asked, turning to Stromni.

“Nearly done. Just cleaning the dust, and we should be good ta go.” Stromni said, taking a deep breath. He scowled at the barrels on the beer wagon.

Alric glanced up at the weather. They had planned to keep the wash warm to distill whisky, but none of it had been tested. He knew this in theory from his previous life, but unlike beer, he hadn't made it from scratch.

In the distance, a cheer of sixteen was picked up, but unlike before, it was met with glares from other groups.

“Well, bloody dragon ain’t dead yet,” Stromni said, shifting himself forward. He picked up his tankard and made his way to the beer wagon. Alric could only watch him go, amazed.

He turned to his guards, “Any of you?” he asked, pointing to the wagon. They all shook their heads.

Alric nodded. He let out a grunt at the typical hangover pains; he knew it would pass. Wondering if there was some kind of magical cure, he turned to his guards, “What do you guys do for a hangover?”

“Bathhouse,” someone said quickly, which got a round of nods.

“Bathhouse, eh?” Alric said, considering it. “Know any good ones?”

“The one close to merchant’s row is swanky, got separate men's area.”

Alric had considered bathhouses before but hadn’t visited. Most of the bathhouses were mixed bathing, and he could make hot water easily enough, but the biggest reason was that he thought of it a lot like sitting in a jacuzzi at a concert. One person's hygiene was one thing, but when everyone was together, it just spread around.

“Think they’ll let us in?” Alric asked. He got a bunch of shocked looks from his guards before they all nodded.

“Alright, we’ll stop by headquarters and get things. Then head there. You’ll need to come in with me, of course.” Alric said, stretching.

One of the guards glanced at the barrels. “But the beer dragon, Mister Alric?”

“Stromni will get those barrels to me. Trust. Find out if any other guards want to go, oh, and let Pavise and Calvern know I’m going.” Alric said to them; they nodded, and two broke off.

Not long after, Alric had his whole guard contingent walking alongside him.
