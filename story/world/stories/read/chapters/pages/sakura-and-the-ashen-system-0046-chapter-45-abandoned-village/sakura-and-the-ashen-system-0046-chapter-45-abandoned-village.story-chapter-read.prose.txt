They climbed out of the canyon in the gray morning.

Sakura barely remembered the climb itself. Like last time, there was stone under her fingers, pain in her back and shoulders, exhaustion in her legs, and Gilbert’s commands, which were still saving Sakura from the terrain’s little betrayals. The core of the dead mage was in Gilbert’s Inventory, but Sakura still felt as if she could sense it nearby, like a painful moral debt.

When they reached the upper edge of the canyon, the wind struck their faces with cold. The sky hung low, with dark bands of cloud in the east. The weather was turning bad quickly and confidently.

Pampli met them almost at once. He came out from behind the stones, every part of his obsidian body bristling with agitation, and stopped in front of Sakura so expressively that she did not need a translator. The beast was angry. Angry, offended, and very much wanted the humans to finally admit that leaving him alone had been a terrible idea.

"Yes, you were right again," Sakura said, coming closer. "I don’t like going anywhere without you either."

Before Pampli could shove roughly into her shoulder, Sakura stepped toward him first and hugged him. It immediately made something inside her feel lighter.

Then Gilbert said, "Sakura."

She heard something in his voice at once, something that made the warmth inside her break off. Sakura let go of Pampli and turned around.

Gilbert stood a little to the side, near the stony patch where Pampli had apparently waited for them most of the time. There were deep marks on the ground, but not only from the beast’s paws. The stone had been chipped in places, dry grass had been torn out, and near one gray boulder, dark brown stains showed against the ground, like blood.

Sakura slowly came closer.

"He fought someone?"

"Looks like it."

Pampli shifted unhappily from paw to paw. Then he snorted and jerked his head sharply toward the forest.

"Who did you fight?" Sakura asked, though she already understood how stupid it was to ask that of a beast, even an intelligent one. "Can you show us somehow?"

Pampli looked at her. Sakura looked at Pampli.

"Yes, fine. I get it. Question withdrawn."

Gilbert crouched beside the bloodstains without touching them with his hands. Then he lifted his gaze to a broken branch. He examined the flattened grass at the edge of the trail. He studied the long trace leading into the forest.

"Whoever it was, he left wounded."

"A person?"

"Most likely."

"Faerd?"

Gilbert shook his head at once.

"No. I don’t sense his trail. Or any traces of magic at all."

Sakura looked toward where the trace disappeared. The forest seemed quiet. Damp twilight was already beginning to gather between the trees, even though the morning had only just begun. The air smelled of rain, wet bark, and something uneasy.

"A scout?" she asked. "A hunter? Just some traveler who was very unlucky to run into Pampli?"

"All options are bad."

"Wonderful. I love when the choice is broad, but every option is awful."

Gilbert straightened.

"If he saw Pampli and recognized him, then he may understand that an Obsidian Hierophant is somewhere nearby. If he didn’t recognize him, he could still decide he found a rare obsidian beast and bring hunters."

Sakura exhaled heavily.

"So we need to move?"

"Yes. Right now."

Pampli seemed to fully agree with them. He gave a short growl toward the forest and nervously struck his tail against the stone. Sakura settled into the saddle faster than usual. Gilbert sat behind her and took her by the waist with one hand. Sakura should have been used to it by now, but still could not quite manage it.

Meanwhile, the sky darkened even more, and somewhere far away, thunder rolled dully.

"We can’t take the trail," Gilbert said. "The tracks will be too obvious. And someone may already be on our tail."

"Then where?"

He pointed down, toward a shallow river running between the stones.

"Through the water."

Sakura looked at him over her shoulder.

"You’re suggesting we make a huge obsidian beast walk through a river?"

"I’m suggesting we not leave the footprints of a huge obsidian beast in soft ground."

"Fine. That sounds annoyingly reasonable."

Pampli clearly was not thrilled either, but he entered the water. The river was shallow here, fast, with a stony bottom. The water reached almost halfway up the beast’s legs and rushed around his black body. Pampli moved more carefully than usual, but confidently. Sakura held on to the saddle with both hands and tried not to think about how much she disliked all of this.

For the first half hour, the plan even worked. The river took their tracks and left no prints, while the rain, which had started as a fine slanting drizzle, finally blurred everything behind them. Sakura had almost begun to think that this time, things might actually be fine.

Then the rain turned into a downpour. It crashed down from above in a dense gray wall, and water drummed against the cloak, hair, and Pampli’s obsidian plates. The forest around them darkened. Within minutes, the bank blurred, the trail vanished, and the river began to rise.

"Gilbert," Sakura said, trying to shout over the noise of the water. "Tell me this is normal."

"This is not normal."

"Damn. I thought so."

Pampli snorted. The water hit him harder, dragging small branches, leaves, and mud to the side. The current was turning murky and angry. Sakura felt the beast tense with his whole body, choosing where to place his paw.

"We need to get to the bank," Gilbert said.

"Which one? I can barely see it!"

The downpour hid everything. The river was no longer a convenient road for erasing tracks. It was becoming a living force, proving more insistently with every minute that even an obsidian beast should not argue with it.

Pampli took a few more steps, and suddenly his front paw slipped off a stone. The beast dropped sharply, water slammed into his side, and Sakura clutched the saddle and cried out. Gilbert held her by the waist.

"Don’t jerk!"

"I’m just trying not to fall!"

Pampli tore his paw free, planted it higher with effort, and climbed onto a steadier patch. But the current was already hitting harder. The water was carrying branches as thick as an arm, and one of them struck Pampli’s side with a thud. The beast growled angrily.

Sakura looked around. Through the rain, a dark slope flashed on the left. There, the bank rose not too steeply, with large stones sticking out between the roots.

"There!" she shouted, pointing with her hand. "We can get out there!"

Gilbert looked that way and said shortly, "Hold on."

Pampli went across the current. Every step was hard. The water shoved him downstream, flooded his legs, struck his chest. Sakura felt the enormous body working under them, felt him dig his claws into the stones, slip, find support again. Once, the current struck hard from the side, and the whole world tilted. Sakura almost lay flat against Pampli’s neck, clinging to him as if her pathetic human weight could help.

"A little more," Gilbert said, though he sounded tense himself.

Pampli reached the bank with one final surge. He literally dragged himself out of the water, tearing into the wet earth with his claws, and heavily climbed between the roots. Sakura had only managed to exhale when a new dull impact passed behind them through the river. A broken piece of tree came down from above and swept past the place where they had been a minute ago.

Sakura looked at it, then at Gilbert.

"Apparently the river wanted to kill us now too."

"Wash us away, more likely."

"Doesn’t matter. The important thing is that it failed."

They dismounted, but did not stop right away. The downpour was still falling as if another world flood was supposed to begin, but after the river, the forest ground seemed safe, even if their shoes sank and slid in the wet mud.

Gilbert led them higher up the slope, away from the water. Pampli walked heavily and angrily, and shook himself hard a couple of times, even though he had no fur. Sakura was soaked through and had almost stopped feeling it.

After some time, the trees began to thin.

First Sakura saw a crooked fence. Then the black hollow of a window. Then a house, half hidden by bushes.

"A village?" she asked.

"Used to be a village."

They carefully entered the overgrown street. The houses stood crookedly, like old sick people. Their roofs sagged, their walls were cracked, and many houses had holes instead of windows. Bushes, grass, and young trees grew between them. Rain ran down rotten beams and dripped from collapsed roofs. On one wall, Sakura saw deep grooves, as if something enormous had once dragged claws through the wood. There was also a wall with a huge charred stain that had long since grown over with moss.

Obviously, people had once lived in this village. Then either all of them, or some of them, had died, and the survivors, if there had been any, had left this place.

Sakura fell silent. Even Pampli moved more quietly.

"Obsidian beasts?" she asked.

Gilbert examined the nearest house, then the old square, where a broken well windlass stuck up among the grass.

"Possibly. Or people. Or both. The traces are too old to tell."

"Cozy."

"We need to make camp. At least here we may find a dry place."

Gilbert said it without much confidence, because most of the roofs in front of them were full of holes and leaking terribly. Some houses had roofs that had collapsed completely. Would they really find a dry place here?

They did, eventually, but not right away. The first house that looked whole from the outside turned out to be flooded inside. The second smelled so strongly of mold that Sakura refused to go past the threshold. Something rustled under the floor in the third, and Gilbert closed the door almost immediately without explanation.

In the end, they found a small house at the edge of the village. The roof leaked in only two places, one wall leaned sideways but still held, and inside there was even an old stone stove. The floor was dirty, but not rotten. In their situation, that almost counted as luxury.

Pampli settled under the awning beside the house, too large to fit inside properly. Sakura promised him it was temporary, but from his look, she understood that he did not believe a single word of human promises. She asked him at least not to fight anyone until they came out. Fortunately, this time only a couple of walls separated them, and Sakura could watch the beast through the window. So Pampli was not too offended this time.

Their temporary shelter was cold and smelled of damp wood and past life. Gilbert quickly checked the corners, the stove, and the remains of the furniture. Then he lit a small fire with dry splinters he had found in a box under a bench and his own magical flame.

Sakura took off the wet cloak Gilbert had bargained for from Marven on the way back, sat down by the wall, and only then felt how badly she was trembling.

"Did we manage to wash away the tracks?" she asked.

"The rain, river, and mud did most of the work. If someone follows us, it will be difficult."

"Difficult doesn’t mean impossible."

"No."

"I adore your talent for keeping hope alive, but badly beaten."

Gilbert gave a faint smirk as he took several bundles out of his Inventory. And that was when Sakura remembered.

"The slime," she said.

He looked up at her.

"What?"

"The slug slime from the laboratory. You said yourself that it could be made into a medicinal base if it’s purified and stabilized. We kept putting it off, but now we at least have a roof and a fire. And a little time while we rest."

Gilbert looked at her in silence for a while.

"We have the core of a dead mage, the trail of an unknown wounded man behind us, and Lir in the Castle of Justice."

"Yes. And that is exactly why we need medicine. Who knows what else is waiting for us. It will definitely be useful."

The mage did not answer, but he took out a small sealed vessel with whitish slime inside. Even through the glass, it looked unpleasant.

"We have everything to make it, right?" Sakura asked.

"Yes. What we bought from the Free Wanderers and what I traded for from Marven before we left. It should work."

"So you were already thinking about this."

"I think about many things. Always."

"Very modest."

Gilbert spread a cloth on the floor, set the vessel on it, then placed a small metal bowl, dry powders, a fine mesh filter, a pestle, and several vials beside it. Sakura leaned closer, trying not to get in the way, but she was already insanely curious.

For one second, she thought it might look magically impressive, with glowing spell circles or something of that sort. In reality, there was only Gilbert, focused and annoyingly precise. And in his hands: tools, drops of slime, powders. There was also a murky liquid that kept turning dark, then gray, then began to smell so bad that Sakura involuntarily shifted away.

"Is this really supposed to become medicine?" she asked.

"Ideally."

Sakura watched the mixture. At some point, she noticed that the gray color was not spreading evenly, but in thin white streaks, as if the slime was resisting the powder.

"Wait," she said. "Look. Here, it isn’t mixing. Not completely."

Gilbert leaned closer, narrowed his eyes, and immediately took another vial.

"Good catch. Maybe one day you’ll start understanding alchemy."

Gilbert paused, then added, "Although I have to admit, alchemy has never been my strongest field."

He added several drops of a clear reagent. The mixture hissed, the smell became sharper, but the white streaks began to dissolve. Then Gilbert passed everything through the filter, mixed it with a thick herbal base, and warmed it for a long time over a small blue flame.

When it was over, a murky greenish-gray salve remained in the bowl. It did not inspire much confidence.

A system window flashed before Sakura’s eyes.

ALCHEMICAL BASE STABILIZED.

Item Created: Crude Healing Salve

Quality: Low

Uses: 3/3

Effect: Minor wound closure. Mild pain reduction.

Warning: Unsafe for deep internal injuries.

Sakura blinked slowly.

"Low quality."

"At least it’s not poison," Gilbert said. As if this were a great achievement.

"You know how to celebrate small victories."

He took a little of the salve and nodded toward her elbow.

"We’ll test it."

"On me?"

"You’re the closest."

"Fine..."

The salve burned her skin so sharply that Sakura hissed through her teeth and almost swore. Then the pain began to fade. The scratch on her elbow pulled closed, and the redness paled a little.

Sakura looked at the small trace of healing and suddenly felt a strange relief. After all the deadly adventures they had already survived, she had still managed to do something that did not kill.

The System before her eyes unexpectedly trembled again.

INDIVIDUAL REACTION DETECTED.

Compatibility with healing base: High

Effect on host: Enhanced

Warning: Repeated use may dull pain response and mask severe injury.

Sakura slowly raised her eyes to Gilbert, reading aloud the warning she had just received.

"Why does that not sound like good news?"

Gilbert was already looking at the window. His face had become too focused.

"Because an ordinary human body should not accept this base that well."

Sakura lowered her gaze to her elbow.

The wound did not hurt at all anymore.
