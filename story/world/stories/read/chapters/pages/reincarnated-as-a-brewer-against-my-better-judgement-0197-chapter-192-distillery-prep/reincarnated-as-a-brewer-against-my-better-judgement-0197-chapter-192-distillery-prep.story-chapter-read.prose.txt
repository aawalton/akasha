Walking with the guards, Alric was halfway to the distillery from headquarters. The party had apparently entered the stage of myth where the city was trying to decide what had happened.

His hangover thankfully had faded; however, in just one day, the rumors had become impressive. Calvern had apparently summoned a noble choir that specialized in filthy songs; Pavise had thrown three logs over the river for his caber toss; one of his guards had sworn blind he saw someone talking at length to an owl.

The owl was the oddest of the bunch. The rest, he could almost believe, but he was fairly certain he hadn’t seen an owl. Then again, there had been a lot of beer, and owls are notoriously good at blending in at parties.

Rumors involving Alric himself had turned interesting. He had arrived at the party on a dragon carrying beer barrels. Another rumor claimed he couldn’t get drunk.

Whatever, give it another week, and the dragon will have two heads, and Calvern taught it more dirty songs.

Alric glanced up as they approached the broad wall that was now the distillery. All the shutters were open. A wide grin spread on Alric’s face. The whisky-making began now. Alric stepped through the main doors, feeling giddy, and immediately heard Stromni.

“An’ this side the boilers, ya people get between a boiler en the washback be a waste o’ grain, we just gonna distill em,” Stromni said, pointing at different parts.

Captain Rein stood beside him; he was furiously rubbing his temples as Stromni pointed at various bits. Rein’s shoulders were low; he looked up as Alric approached. Alric caught the hint of a glare there.

Alric picked diplomacy, “Still hungover?”

Rein’s shoulders drooped further; he swept an arm around the building. “Calvern told me this place needs guards too.”

Alric nodded in the way of someone who had stopped listening several sentences ago. He was more interested in the new internal walls that now separated Maggie from the rest of the building.

The corner where Maggie was kept was walled off, except for the window opening through which the troughs came. There was stone dust around, but it wasn’t as bad as Alric expected.

“Mister Alric.”

“Hmm?” Alric said, glancing back at Rein.

Rein closed his eyes to sigh, “Another building, more doors, windows, and a riverside entrance?”

“I did hear you,” Alric said, looking at the walls again. “I don’t think it's an issue, though.”

Rein’s face cycled through several expressions before settling on the one Alric thought Rein had invented for him.

“Anything we make here isn’t staying here.” Alric continued, gesturing to the troughs that went to Maggie and the condenser on the other side, “Once we make something, it's going in barrels, then storage. We aren’t going to have piles of finished whisky sitting around unless we're working with it.”

Rein tilted his head but then straightened it.

“Oh.” He said, and he began looking around again, “Where are you storing it?”

“Headquarters cellar mostly, it has a magic lock. Otherwise, the brewery may take a little overflow.” Alric said with a shrug, tilting his head at Rein.

“That does make things simpler. A few guards won’t hurt, though?” Rein said, glancing at the doors.

“Aye, few at the doors, don’t need en army though,” Stromni said, pointing at the doors.

Rein nodded. He excused himself and started walking to inspect the space. He muttered something as he headed to his men and pointed at the door.

“Stromni, how are you doing?” Alric asked with a grin, “Still mad at Tarrow?”

“Good ta see ya lad, en nah,” Stromni said with a shrug, but a smug grin appeared. “If someone ‘ad ta use gods ta beat me at drinking, I can live with that.”

Both of them chuckled before Stromni pointed up at the malting section. Alric nodded and followed the dwarf up the stairs away from the washbacks.

Mara stood in the raised section with a group of people around her. Alric counted eleven: eight men and three women, judging by their dress. Speaking of dress, they were all wearing workers' clothing; many had rolled up sleeves. Mara turned just as Stromni and Alric approached.

“Right, so this is Mister Alric; he’s the boss,” Mara said, using her head to gesture to him. “Stromni is also your boss.”

“Aye, he pays ya salary.” Stromni said, pointing at Alric, “I’m here ta be the smiling one en shout at ya before ya burn yaself.” Stromni concluded, tucking his thumbs behind his wide belt.

Alric kept looking at them; many of them seemed familiar. He recognized one as a worker who tried to become a wall ornament when Alric and Rein inspected the brewery together; he was sure he had seen them either around the brewery or somewhere else. And he kept looking. The group kept looking back.

Eventually, Stromni turned to him and tilted his head wide-eyed; that was when Alric noticed.

“Right, sorry,” Alric said, shaking his head a little to clear it. “We have a lot of work to do; this place is going to get hot and steamy.” He pointed to the grain side. “We need to get grain germinating. Same as we do at the brewery. We’ll use the new pots to kiln it, and we crush it all here.”

Five of the workers nodded immediately. “These three have worked with me at the brewery; they're moving to boilers, though- better pay, Mister Alric,” Mara said, gesturing to them and giving Alric a wink.

Alric grinned, respecting the hustle. “You run boilers with Hal?” Alric asked, and three of them nodded.

That makes my life easier.

“Good, the biggest things today are heat, steam, and cleaning.” Alric began, counting them on his fingers. “We need the heat and steam for when the grain is hanging; we need to run everything with boiled water.”

One of the workers frowned at the nearest washback.

Alric followed the gaze. They needed to run everything with just hot water first. Filling them would take ages. The staves on the washbacks, however, would need to be wet to swell and make everything tight. There was an inner and outer wall for this. He wanted the inner wall sealed before he started messing with the layer between.

“You don’t need to boil for a bell yet. For today, when it's boiling, you pour. We need hot water more than the rest, and we need everything spotless.” Alric said, gesturing to the entire space.

He waited for someone to protest, or at least sigh dramatically. There was ,nothing but polite nods; he waited for a clever quip from Stromni but got nothing. It felt slightly anti-climatic.

“We need ladders,” Stromni said, pointing at the beams. Alric had protested at the wooden beams, but there just weren’t alternatives. There weren’t any close to Maggie, however.

“Sounds like a you problem,” Alric said with a grin.

“Lad, that sounded like a short joke. I’ll overlook it this time cause everything ye forget is me problem.” Stromni said with a grin, but he nodded.

Alric glanced over the germination section. It looked almost exactly like the one at the brewery, which meant Mara had it under control and he could safely ignore it for now.

“Alright, Mara, you get them wetting and hanging grain. I’ll get these boiling water.” Alric said, pointing to his team. They nodded and split off.

The raised section contained the germination section and the boilers section. In the middle section, which was lower than the germination and boilers sections, stood the two washbacks. Maggie sat on the side furthest, in a low section. Thankfully, flammable alcohol didn’t flow uphill.

Alric approached the first platform. They were placed so a barrel of water could be set on them and drain into the boilers. There was no fancy roof-lifting arrangement for these; they would use Alric’s item box primarily, but back pain was likely in people’s future. The boiler could then be tilted onto a trough that would run down to the wash backs. Alric glanced at the troughs. They were held up on stone, but they could switch the bottom trough with a chain. The chain worried Alric because it could rust. It was also expensive and had been treated with resin.

The team moved past Alric to each boiler; Alric began placing barrels of water. Each team member set up the filter and pulled the bung so the water would be filtered as it went down into the boiler. The boilers were the same arrangement as the brewery boilers. Large copper pots that took four heating stones along the bottom. Roughly three hundred liters each.

Alric began placing the heating stones and charging them. Some were harder to reach because the space was a bit tighter at the front, but it wasn’t unmanageable.

Alric was about to suggest the team do some cleaning when he saw someone approaching with a bucket. “Have ya got nouther barrel o’ clean water, Mister Alric?” he asked. The man was holding a mop and broom that Alric wondered had been magically summoned.

He nodded and set up one of the barrels of filtered water; the boiler crew got to work, sweeping and mopping their section first. They even had lye.

Alric opened his mouth, unsure what to say. The boilers made their first tick noises as they heated up.

Alric was about to check on Mara when he saw her approaching. “We need a weight and scale, Mister Alric. We don’t know how much grain.” Mara said with a shrug. Alric nodded and took out paper; he wrote that down.

Stromni, meanwhile, walked in carrying two ladders. One was very long; one of Alric’s guards outside the door helped him carry it up the stairs.

“Builder wants em back, even wanted ta charge me,” Stromni said as two of the germination crew approached Stromni and took the longer ladder.

“What did you tell him?” Alric asked as the crew set it at the wood beam. He would have preferred an A-frame ladder, at least.

Stromni shrugged, “He’s welcome ta, won’t get more work from us.”

Alric nodded; the need for an A-frame reminded him that he needed a goats-foot mechanism for the heavy crossbow. “Stromni, I’m worried you're very bored, so I do have some more work for you.”

Stromni closed his eyes and made a gesture that suggested he was already regretting every decision that had led him here. Alric grinned, produced some paper, and started sketching. The ladder was simple enough for Stromni, who dismissed it as a carpenter’s problem for when the snow came. The goatsfoot, on the other hand, was met with a deeply serious expression, unusual for Stromni.

“Dun get it lad,” Stromni said, pointing to the forks. Alric produced the heavy crossbow on the desk they were using. Stromni looked from it to Alric, wide-eyed. “Lad?”

“Alright, so I’m not completely sure on this one. See those hooks? They will go on a metal bar we put here on the crossbow, then see these hooky bits? We use them to hook the string. This part here you pull and span it that way.” Alric explained, pointing to various parts.

Stromni looked at the drawing of the crossbow for a long moment. “Ya drawing ain't making much sense ta me lad. We do this slow, I’d like ta chew ya out, but this is a proper dwarven thing ya bringing ‘ere.” Stromni said, gesturing to the crossbow.

“Alright, maybe an evening this week or something?” Alric asked. He was about to put the whole thing off till they had time, when he saw Stromni reach out a hand and stroke the stock of the crossbow.

“Aye, when we ‘ave time, lad. Let's get this whole thing going first.” Stromni said, gesturing to the distillery.

Alric turned; behind him, the germination group was wetting the grain bags, using the ladder in pairs to hang them. Each grain bag already had a net. The boiler people called out to Alric that they were ready for the first tip. Both Stromni and Alric walked to the edge to watch.

The first tilt was too fast; water ran into the wooden trough, steaming hard in the cold air. Just as it struck the first joint and changed direction, it splashed a large sheet over the side. No one was near the washbacks for this exact reason. The rest of the water ran through the trough and slashed loudly into the washback.

“Gonna need a lip there,” Stromni said, pointing.

Alric nodded; he gestured for the next boiler tip. Each trough needed more lips in a few places. The washback, for its part, began changing color as the water hit the staves. Alric and Stromni both grinned.

By the end of the day, they had a long list of small niggly issues to fix. One issue Alric could do nothing about, though, was that many of the staff, Stromni included, picked up a habit of whistling the tune from Calvern’s song.
