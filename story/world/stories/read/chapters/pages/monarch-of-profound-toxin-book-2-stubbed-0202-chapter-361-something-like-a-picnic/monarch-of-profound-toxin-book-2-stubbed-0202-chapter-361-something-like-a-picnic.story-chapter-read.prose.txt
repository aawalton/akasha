[Profound Toxin — Omnicide]

Omnicide, eh?

That didn’t sound half bad. Actually, that didn’t quite do it justice. No, it sounded absolutely insane.

Since there was no point in wasting time thinking about it, he picked it. The sense of something settling in his spirit was immediate. It was heavy, frightening, and potent. Like even a single touch could cause untold destruction.

There, in his garden lounge chair, he considered for a few minutes what to do next. In the past, the first thing he would have done after such a frankly ridiculous evolution was run out and try it out instantly. His eyes flitted around the garden, to the surrounding houses, to the extended neighborhood, to Forest at large, to the entire altered North American continent, and finally to the expanded Earth as a whole.

Yeah, it was a little too risky, after all.

After all, while the skill name was obviously interpreted and translated into words he could understand, the Omnicide was rather easily understood. The annihilation of all.

Yeah, probably shouldn’t test that here in his own garden.

But maybe just a little bit?

With a steel grip on the skill, he allowed a sliver of it to begin charging up, just to feel the energy.

Nope. No, no, no way. That could never be activated here. Not until he had perfect control, at least. Not if he could help it, at least.

If this was to be tested, he would have to find a remote world with no civilization, and preferably no life at all. There was no need to be a senseless killer all the time after all. Fortunately, remote worlds were not particularly uncommon. One of the worlds that had previously been infected with miasma would do perfectly, in fact. Maybe they could do a bit of reconnaissance as well while he was at it.

Was Sireal, the master cartographer of Eik’s Great Group of Guys home at this hour, perhaps? And was he up for a trip that, if one was generous with labels, could be called a picnic?

“Late lunch?” Fij asked as she tidied up the tools spread all over their living room table. She was lucky that her work as a refrigeration technician allowed her to bring a good amount of her work home. It mostly involved trouble shooting and light fixing of faulty components, nearly all of which were small enough to fit into her bag of holding along with the array of different tools she used.

Her bag of holding was by no means one of the larger varieties, but they were still an expensive commodity regardless of size and not a practical investment for most people. As such, hers was proof of her and her husband’s success and she carried it with pride, even if it was more or less indistinguishable from a common, unenchanted bag to any passerby on the street.

“Yeah! A late lunch sounds like exactly what I need right now,” came the reply from the corner of the room where Sireal was going through his third set of push ups of the day.

While she was by no means happy with how stubborn he was when it came to his continued work with Eik Magnasen, seeing him so full of drive and energy left her with a feeling she could describe as nothing short of admiration.

The man liked to praise her strength of mind, but she knew she could never chase a goal as high as Eik Magnasen. The fact that Sireal was apparently succeeding and now routinely performing jobs for Eik Magnasen’s frighteningly powerful and influential group of Awakened warriors, in direct contact with the highest ranked of the members, including Eik himself, only reinforced that admiration. And luckily, the missions he had been sent on since his first outing with Eik’s Great Group of Guys had been far less dangerous.

She almost didn’t mind it, if it continued like that. And they had had the A-ranked shield-man Kalavax and his wife around for dinner a couple of times, and although the enormous man had a rough demeanor, he was kind, clever, and most importantly, made sure her husband didn’t come home in a paper bag dripping with blood.

She liked him.

“Where do you want to go? That new place that opened on the same street as Sadi’s store?”

“Sounds good to me. Just give me… a moment to finish up here. And maybe catch a quick bath. Are you all done for the day?” he asked, breathing hard as he continued his exercise.

“For now,” she said and left her bag of holding on the couch. “I’ll have to do a bit more tonight, but for now, I’d like to just have a nice time with my husband.”

“And he shall happily give his wife what she craves,” he purred, coming up into the starting position and wiggling his eyebrows in an attempt at seduction.

“Oh, is that s—” A knock on the door interrupted her.

“Can you get that, honey? My shirt’s off,” Sireal asked.

“Yeah. It’s probably just one of the neighbors. There’s that street market next week, remember?”

“Oh, yeah!” he said. Then, after a couple of seconds of pause. “You think Mr. Magnasen would come if I invited him?”

“Of course not, my love. Surely, the most important man in the universe has more important things to go to,” she called into the house as she pushed the door open.

“I would actually love to,” the Worldbreaker told her with a grin before she even had a chance to stammer his name in surprise. “When does it start? My kids would love it as well. And my daughter could use some time away from her friends… especially that Philip kid. Eugh!” the X-ranker hissed with a grimace.

“M-Mr. Magnasen,” she finally stammered, taking an instinctive step back.

“Mr. Magnasen?” Sireal yelped and came running, nearly stumbling over his own feet. Red in the face, he spoke through ragged breaths, interrupted in the middle of a set. “To what do I owe the pleasure?”

“Hey, bro. Nice pecs. Been training?” Eik asked.

Suddenly remembering his nakedness, the cartographer looked down at himself. “Ah, yes, just my daily exercise routine. Push ups, squats, crunches. That kind of thing, you know. Although I’m sure I don’t have to tell you about that. You must have done so many push ups to get to where you are now!”

“Oh, uuh, yeah, sure… That’s definitely important too. Keep it up. Keep pushing up.”

“I will! What’s your max rep?”

Taken aback, Eik had to think. “Well, I’m not really sure. I don’t know, like… a billion?”

“Damn,” Sireal breathed, still catching his breath. “I can probably hit two hundred and thirty in a row on a good day.”

“Hey, yeah… Not too bad, buddy. You’ll get there.”

“Yeah, thanks…” he trailed off. “What’s your max weight?”

Eik looked around, squinting. “I guess… Well, how much do you recon the Alliance headquarters weigh?”

“Like, the main building?” the cartographer gasped, eyes bulging.

“No, like, the whole thing.”

“All of the buildings?”

“I was thinking something along the lines of the whole raised mountain with the buildings on top, actually.”

Neither Sireal nor Fij seemed capable of getting their jaws to close again. “I… I’m not sure anyone knows.”

“Okay, well, probably two or three of those.”

“Th-Th-Three?” Sireal nearly went into a coughing fit as the saliva caught in his throat.

“With personal buffs, I think I could do six of them, if I clench my teeth.”

“Fij, I’m fainting,” Sireal said as he wobbled on his feet, his hands fumbling for her shoulder for support.

“All right, honey," Fij said dismissively. "So what brings you here, Mr. Magnasen? Surely our house is not on your way home.”

“Quite right,” the Earthling said. “I was actually hoping that Sireal would accompany me on a little trip to… a place.”

Before Sireal could even take the breath to answer, Fij stepped in. “How dangerous of a place are we talking?” she asked with narrowed eyes.

“Now? Not very, I’d say.”

“Now? What about before, then?”

Eik cleared his throat. “Very.”

She did not look satisfied with that answer. “And now it’s not dangerous because…”

“Everything that was there is now not there.”

“Courtesy of…”

“Yours truly.”

“Right… I don’t like this, Sireal,” she concluded, turning to her husband.

He looked at her like a kid pleading with his mom to be allowed to go on the biggest roller coaster in the amusement park. “But Mr. Magnasen promised that he would protect me.”

Her jaw was tight as she thought quietly. “What’s the job?”

“Simple surveillance. Nothing more. It’s a world previously infested with spawn of the Lord of the Moon. It’s now been cleaned up by me. I want your husband to check a few areas there, just to make sure that there are no clues left that I haven’t been able to find myself.”

“And you can protect him?”

“Absolutely, yes,” Eik promised sincerely. “And I am capable of instantly teleporting him to a world ruled by me in its entirety. It’s made out of my ability and nothing will ever be able to hurt him there. From there, he can be sent home at the snap of a finger.”

“You made a world?” Sireal breathed.

“Not exactly. It’s more like an inheritance.”

“Worldbreaker…” the man gasped as realization struck him.

“And surveillance is all he has to do?” she questioned.

“I have something to test, actually, but I’ll send him home before that,” he assured her.

“Aw, man…” Sireal muttered with a grimace of disappointment.

Fij stepped aside. “All right, fine. You’ve never let him be hurt, so I suppose I’ll trust you.”

“Yes!” Sireal whooped and high-fived a celebrating Eik.

They were half way down the garden path when Fij called them back. “Sireal, aren’t you forgetting something?”

It took the man a couple of seconds to realize. “My shirt!”

“All right, there’s nothing here to find,” Eik concluded after they had visited more than a dozen sites where Sireal had conducted his cartographic duties. “Let’s get you home.”

Watching the man work proved clearly and conclusively that combat power was not the only way to be impressive. People glorified fighting power—perhaps rightly so, given the how it allowed single individuals to dominate families, towns, cities, civilizations, alliances, and even universes—as Eik had come to experience himself.

But damn if watching Sireal work wasn’t like watching a world-class artist perform. He could practically see the information enter the man’s brain in real time, processed quickly and precisely by a trained, expert brain.

Instantly translated visually and drawn on enormous sheets of durable paper with a combination of detail and smoothness that should have been impossible, Eik felt himself stand transfixed. The sheer scale of the work was ridiculous.

Sure, Eik could bonk stuff so hard that it vaporized like a cherry tomato under a beached cruise ship, but this was simply something else. Skill came in many shapes and sizes, after all.

“Please wait, Mr. Magnasen. I think I found something.”

“Dude…”

“I’m serious! Rather than casting a wide net, I’ve performed a depth scan. About two and a half kilometers beneath us there’s a mineral that is nothing like any other mineral I’ve encountered on this world. I don’t know how to describe it, but it’s… sinister.”

Eik stared at him. “Your wife is going to murder me, but let’s dig that shit up, bro.”
