Tristan Gale stood at the edge of the camp, eyes fixed on the line of enemy soldiers. Though they held their weapons and stood at attention, their expressions betrayed no hatred—only curiosity.

It was… strange.

To face an enemy without animosity was an experience he never thought he’d have. After decades on the battlefield, where hostility was expected and even welcomed, this quiet civility felt almost surreal.

Behind him, the four soldiers accompanying him worked with quiet urgency, setting up the parley tent. The only sounds were the rhythmic hammering of wooden stakes into the hard earth and the occasional creak of tightened rope.

Tension clung to them like fog.

Tristan could feel it in their movements—the way their shoulders tightened, the cautious glances they cast toward the enemy line. He didn’t blame them.

Parleys were promises, but promises could be broken. One moment of betrayal, one flash of steel, and the envoy could be wiped out before a single word was spoken. Not all generals honored the Confucian way.

He’d offered to help them set up, but was politely refused. They had insisted it wasn’t proper for the deputy general to engage in such menial labor—it would diminish their side’s authority in the eyes of the enemy.

While their logic wasn’t wrong, Tristan still felt stifled.

The truth was, since swearing loyalty to Luke, his body had undergone an incredible transformation. Strength surged through him like it hadn’t in decades. At fifty-something years old, he now moved with the vitality of a man in his prime.

The aches and lingering stiffness from years of war were gone, erased by something he still didn’t fully understand. Even the soreness from yesterday’s brutal eight-hour mountain ascent had vanished without a trace.

It was both miraculous and bewildering.

He found his thoughts drifting to the young man responsible for this change—a teenager he had first encountered just weeks prior, in the quiet sanctum of the academy library.

At the time, Tristan had mistaken him for a troublemaker.

Luke had seemed disrespectful, flipping rapidly through old tomes with little regard for their age or significance. Coupled with the rumors—accounts of arguments with instructors, and even a deadly duel with a scion of the Ring family—Tristan had formed a damning impression.

He believed Luke was arrogant. Entitled. A dangerous upstart who had let his recent military success go to his head.

After all, it was during the Clayton City siege that Tristan's own uncle had lost his life.

But he had been wrong.

Luke had been nothing like the rumors suggested. Calm and composed, he spoke with neither deference nor pride, his words measured, his tone steady. And yet, there was something about him that defied logic—an intangible air that made Tristan’s heart race.

A presence that felt… otherworldly.

The brief meeting had left such a deep impression that Tristan, for the first time in his career, had invited a student to his private quarters at the top of the library tower. It was a place of quiet contemplation and study, sacred to him.

And in that room, over the board of a simple chess match, Tristan had come to understand the boy just a little more.

He had been utterly defeated.

A member of the esteemed Gale family—known for producing statesmen and strategists across generations—Tristan prided himself on his intellect. But Luke had dismantled his defenses with ease, seeing patterns and moves far beyond what Tristan thought possible.

It was a humbling experience.

But even armed with this knowledge, Tristan still struggled to fully grasp the situation they now found themselves in—the blessing he had received, the brutal climb up that treacherous mountain… and Luke’s bold decision to broker their surrender.

The logic behind it was sound. Flawless, even. But what struck Tristan most was Luke’s brilliant handling of the royal decree. Any other general might have taken the decree at face value—so focused on its directive to raze all fortresses within Qin territory that they would have missed the nuance.

Yet Luke, with a few subtle reinterpretations and a shift in perspective, had found a loophole. One that not only allowed him to fulfill the king’s command, but also preserve the entirety of their remaining forces in the process.

It was a masterstroke.

Tristan turned, instinctively glancing back toward the fortress—only to find that Luke was no longer there.

He raised a brow, momentarily confused. Where did he go? Shouldn’t he be watching the negotiation unfold?

But a moment later, he chuckled quietly to himself and shook his head.

No... it’s because he trusts me.

There was no armor to mark him a general, no ostentatious display of rank—yet his sheer presence radiated authority.

He’s a frontliner, Tristan assessed instantly. The kind who earns his men’s respect with sword in hand, not through titles.

He won’t respond to flattery or flowery words… but reason? Strength of will? That, he might admire.

Unlike Luke—whose true self remained buried beneath layers of caution and mystery—this general, Tristan felt, could be read.

Some generals valued results. Others valued pride.

And Tristan suspected General Tao was the latter.

A man who would rather spill blood than suffer even a perceived slight.

Tao accepted the seat with a stoic expression, though his large frame looked almost comical behind the flimsy table. Still, if he found it uncomfortable, he gave no sign—composure etched into every line of his face.

Tristan took his own seat and offered a small, cordial smile. “If this were any other circumstance, I’d offer you some tea,” he said lightly, “but as you’re likely aware—we’re running out of time.”

Tao responded with a silent nod, his expression unmoved.

“Let us not waste words, Vice General,” he rumbled. “Tell me what it is you hope to achieve here.”

Tristan studied him. That calm, unshakable aura—it reminded him of Master Boyd, his late uncle’s old comrade. The kind of man who wouldn’t be swayed by emotion or threats. A mountain among men: immovable, but not unreasonable.

In a way, that made things easier.

“I wish to peacefully evacuate Xiandong Fortress and return to my country,” Tristan said plainly, without preamble or embellishment. “We will leave behind all weapons and provisions as a sign of goodwill—so that you have no reason to doubt our intentions.”

Tao blinked.

Just for a moment, his mask cracked. A flicker of surprise passed through his eyes—but it vanished just as quickly as it came.

“That is… quite daring,” he replied slowly, his gaze narrowing. “And what assurance do I have that you won’t simply regroup and return with greater force? What’s to stop us from cutting you down the moment your backs are turned? You’d be defenseless—without weapons or supplies.”

His tone was calm, but there was a knife beneath the words.

Tristan didn’t flinch.

“You're right, General. That is well within your power,” he said evenly, offering only a mild smile. “But you misunderstand. I’m not here to deter you with threats. I’m here to offer you a choice.”

Tao’s brow furrowed. His frown deepened.

“Where does your confidence come from, old man?” he demanded, voice rising slightly. “I could snap you in half without effort—yet here you sit, speaking as if you hold the higher ground. Do you not fear death? Do you not care for your soldiers’ lives?”

“Or perhaps,” Tao continued, his voice like rolling thunder, “you believe I’m not the kind of man who would go back on his word. Do you think me some fool who clings to intangibles like honor and integrity—at the cost of my soldiers’ lives?”

“All I ask is for safe passage from the fortress. In return, you take Xiandong without resistance and avoid a costly battle with the Qin reinforcements that are surely marching this way to reclaim it.”

With no warning, the towering general surged to his feet, startling everyone present. The soldiers behind Tristan reached for their weapons out of reflex, but of course they were unarmed.

Tristan rose with grace and cupped his fist toward the man’s back.
