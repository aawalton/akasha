The only pauses the newlyweds took in the night were to inform Lora of their intentions, to get Clay back to the hotel, and to eat. Trevor’s familiar had gotten through two more quests, bumping him up so that he was only one away from leveling. While excited, the courier knew there was nothing he could do now but spend more time with his wife.

To his surprise, there were just as many people at Lemon World during the nighttime as there were during the day. While he wasn’t expecting a drastic drop, there almost didn’t seem to be one at all. The lines were still just as long.

Like Adabelle, who was considered an offshoot of demons as a demonkin, many of them could see perfectly well in the dark. Since the majority of Aletander’s citizens weren’t beholden to the sun, there was a dichotomy in the population that naturally divided people between those who preferred the day and those who liked night.

The same thing had happened with the dwarves and kobolds who made Lucent their home, but it was interesting to see it spanning a whole city and, presumably, the country.

Since their tickets lasted until noon, Trevor and Adabelle had no trouble skipping ahead on the bigger attractions. Those were the ones that she wanted to try again the most, and for good reason. They spent the hours riding roller coasters.

As the sun began to come up, they finally exited the park for good. Their Stamina stats meant that they were tired, but not to the point of falling asleep. Regardless, they sat on a bench near Lora’s teleportation point and took a moment to relax.

“Okay, I think I’ve fully worked it out of my system now,” Adabelle said as she leaned against her husband. “As much as I’ll miss Lemon World, I’ve gotten what I wanted out of it.”

“That’s good to hear, lady,” Trevor said as he wrapped an arm around her. “It was fun running around with you.”

“It was,” she agreed before looking up at the rapidly brightening sky.

The courier followed her gaze. “We should take those Full Night Rests before Lora shows up. I’d rather not be this tired when she does, otherwise I might just fall onto our bed when we return to the hotel to freshen up. There’s no telling if I’d get back up after that.”

“Sure thing, Trevor,” Adabelle said, accepting her vial when he retrieved a pair from his inventory.

They clinked the two together before drinking them. A jolt of power went through their bodies, scraping away their fatigue in a second. Trevor shook it off, completely awake by the time the sensation ended. It wasn’t a bad feeling, but it was strange and he was glad that, unlike his wife, he could wake up normally in the morning.

Lora came to pick them up, and they were whisked away back to their suite to prepare. They had already eaten not too long ago, so all they needed to do was clean up and get changed. Clay was excited for an outing they could join their master on and pushed them both to get ready quickly.

So it wasn’t long after that Lora teleported them to their next destination. Trevor was quick to pull out his familiar’s husk so they could move freely, and he arched an eyebrow as he saw the sign they appeared next to.

“Sammie the Gamer’s Interactive Playground and Science Institute,” he read before looking at the building before them.

It, like the sign, was built in a style that someone from the past would have used to depict the future. There were curves everywhere, plenty of chrome, and the institute was inside of a giant glass dome. Purple lights followed green circuitry that seemed to be everywhere, including on the path leading up to the building.

“That’s right,” Lora replied with a grin as she gestured for them to follow. “For Sammie, it was where she kept her favorite toys and inventions. After her death, the city turned it into a museum. It’s a little cluttered now, but it’s still a marvel.”

“Trevor has been pretty excited about the games he’s expecting to find here,” Adabelle said, taking his hand. “He’s even gone so far as to use some choice phrases from Earth regarding them. Just yesterday, in fact.”

“Ah, yeah. I had to explain what a skill floor was after I brought it up,” Trevor explained with a sheepish smile.

“Well, I’m not sure what that is, but that’s okay,” Lora said as she led them through one of the holes in the dome. It was nice and brisk out due to the early morning weather, and a wave of pleasantly warm air hit them when they entered. “You’ve got the whole place to yourself as well as the caretakers of the museum today. They were quite excited to hear that an actual gamer was coming.”

“They were?” he asked, surprised.

“Yup! And I do believe you’ve met at least one of them already.”

Before they could ask who, a familiar demon opened the door of the museum. His twin horns stuck straight up, making him seem taller than he was, and his furry ears twitched. He came bearing a big smile on his face and wore what appeared to be a silver and purple flight suit with the acronym of the institute stitched above the heart.

“Trevor! And Adabelle! And Lora, too!” Stammy greeted loudly, raising his hands. “It’s an honor to have you here at the Playground! I was just saying to Dee that I couldn’t wait to see you all again. I feel like we just kept missing each other back on the ship. Did you get a chance to try that parasailing thing back on the Red Dream?”

As he spoke, the demon shook their hands one at a time and even gave Clay a smile and a wave. The familiar had been under the table during their first meeting, so they were new to him. Their introduction was quick.

“It’s good to see you Stammy, and yes,” Trevor greeted in return. “We had a lot of fun with it. We also just came away from Lemon World, so you’ve got a lot to live up to.”

“Nonsense,” he declared, clearly not worried in the slightest. “What you see here is going to make Lemon World seem like, well, an actual lemon. Especially if you’re the gamer we were told you were.”

“That would just be Trevor,” Adabelle said.

“Yeah, but if you’ve been told that I’m something great then maybe temper your expectations a bit,” the courier said with a sheepish smile.

“It’s not mine that needs to be tempered, but my kid’s,” Stammy replied with a grin. “My heyday is long past, but the Sammerson bloodline runs strong in them. They’ve been talking about getting a proper challenge ever since this whole shindig was put together.”

“The Sammerson bloodline?" Adabelle asked curiously. “As in Sammie herself? Or is that a coincidence?”

“I’ll explain more once we’re inside,” he promised before turning to Lora. “Will you be joining us today? You know it’s always better with a crowd.”

The teleporter gave him a knowing smile. “I have some business to take care of, but I should be back around noon. Since the Anderson-Katines have so graciously told me that I’m free to join them, I’ll take them up on their offer.”

“Sounds good, then. You know Boel loves it when you come by.”

“I do. Let him know that I’ll be handing him his butt this time, too,” Lora said with a grin. “Just a little later. See ya soon, friends.”

Stammy chuckled as she waved and disappeared. “Boel’s my eldest. She’s not been around very much, but he’s developed quite a crush on Asalorana, there,” he cheerily explained before gesturing for them to follow. “Come on in. The Playground is all yours for the day, and I’m excited to show you around.”

They entered the building to find a room covered in Sammie the Gamer memorabilia. It wasn’t a small one, either. A statue made of dark stone was dedicated to her in the center, depicting her with a hammer in one hand and a controller in the other. All along the walls there were paintings of the goblin innovator working on various projects.

Trevor easily spotted a few of the things they had already seen, such as Rambunctious C and the roller coasters. One of them showed her sleeping on the bill of a giant rubber duck, and it took him a moment to realize that it had wheels at the bottom. That had to have been Duck E, which was supposed to be at the garage for testing Clay’s engine, though they hadn’t seen it.

The art spanned wall to wall and had various items set up like trophies. There was a backpack with a sturdy frame around it that had long legs and arms attached, but were only solid below the elbows and knees. There were gloves and shoes at the ends. Matilsa had mentioned an invention that would allow Sammie to have longer limbs, and it seemed as though that was it.

“Welcome to the Playground,” Dee greeted as she approached, shaking their hands just like her husband had. She wore the same silver and purple flight suit of the institute. “We didn’t get to meet back on the airship. I’m Dee, Stammy’s wife. It’s a pleasure to have you, Trevor and Adabelle. And this little cutie that I didn’t see before.”

She smiled at Clay. They reached out to shake her hand as well, not wanting to be left out, and she obliged them.

“That’s Trevor’s earth elemental familiar, Clay,” Stammy explained.

“The kids are going to love them,” she said.

“This is quite the collection you have on display,” Adabelle remarked as she looked around. “Sammie the Gamer’s memory is in great hands.”

“Thanks for saying so,” Stammy said as he, too, glanced around the room. “We Sammersons have been watching over this place ever since the days of my great, great, great, great grandfather, Stammy the First. Much of Sammie’s memorabilia out there has been gathered here to honor her memory and accomplishments. There’s still quite a few pieces out there that are on loan or claimed by other families.”

“Or unmovable, like Lemon World,” Dee added.

“Right, or just national treasures like Delores and Rambunctious C, which I heard you drove, but most of it is here.”

“Yeah, we did take it out for a spin,” Trevor said with a smile. “I haven’t driven anything like that since I got here, so it was a lot of fun. And, I got to show my wife a little bit of what life was like back on Earth.”

Adabelle smiled as her tail pulled him a little closer. “You should have seen the way his face lit up when he started driving. He was having so much fun. It was good seeing him let loose like that, and it was an unforgettable experience.”

“Well, I’m happy to announce that you’ll have another here,” Stammy said as he walked towards one of the paintings. The others followed. “See, this place used to be Sammie the Gamer’s home, and my family has been taking care of it since before she passed. It’s a duty we all take seriously, and not just because it means we get to live at the Playground.”

“Though it does help,” Dee said with a smile.

“It sure does,” he agreed, putting an arm around her shoulders.

“You mention your family, so Sammerson is not in regards to the Summoned?” Adabelle asked.

“No, it is,” the man said as he gestured towards the painting they were heading for. “It all started with Stammy the First. See, Sammie the Gamer wanted an assistant. Someone who would clean up, really, but she was very picky about who could come into the Playground, even though back then it wasn't nearly as glamorous.”

They stopped in front of a picture showing the goblin Summoned and a demon that looked similar to Stammy standing side by side. She was handing him a trophy that was nearly as big as she was, almost falling over in the process, and he was accepting it with a big grin on his face.

“The way she chose was through a tournament, because of course it was,” the curator continued, gesturing towards the prize.

“Makes sense considering what she’s called,” Trevor chuckled.

Stammy laughed. “Right? He proved he had the tenacity, skill, and gumption to win over everyone else and, let me tell you, the competition was fierce. Everyone knew that working with Sammie would be a tiring gig considering her proclivity towards doing whatever popped up in her head, often ignoring several other things she had to do. At one point it got so bad that Stammy the First would have to challenge her to games in order to have her go back to projects she had left behind.”

The courier nodded. “Now, when you say games…”

“We’ll get there soon, I promise,” the curator said, grinning. “Eventually, Sammie and Stammy became thick as thieves, and she adopted him. He had no other family at the time, so he accepted. It meant he could play more, which suited them both because he was the only one who could practice enough to keep up with her.”

“I’m surprised by that,” Adabelle stated. “Did Sammie the Gamer not put a lot of points in her physical attributes?”

“Oh, she did, but she also created a headband she called the Equalizer,” Stammy explained, reaching up to tap his forehead. “It attuned to a Summoned and created a miniature enhancement zone for those who wore it afterwards. Evened the playing field, just as its name states.”

“That might be the most remarkable thing I heard about her yet,” the noblewoman said, surprised.

“Is it?” Trevor asked, frowning in thought. “Can’t the halo around Eskretet Castle do the same?”

“It can, and that’s the only thing I know of that can do such a thing until now,” Adabelle answered. She caught the Sammersons’ curious looks. “We come from Lucent, which has focused heavily on Summoned magic. There’s a massive ritual circle floating around the capital’s castle. It can be used to copy the stats, but not Powers, of a Summoned and give them to a native of this world. We use it for dueling and training, but there’s also a level of security in being able to do so if we’re ever attacked.”

“Well, it sounds like our Equalizer but on a bigger scale,” Dee mused. “Sammie made it because she became too powerful and people stopped gaming with her.”

“Just because people stopped playing her games?” the noblewoman asked incredulously.

“I understand it,” Trevor said, nodding. His wife gave him an incredulous look, and he shrugged. “Sometimes you get too good and the people around you are sore losers. They don’t want to play anymore.”

It took her a moment before she nodded. “That does make sense. I’m just surprised that she made something that advanced for something like a game.”

“Oh, we take our gaming very seriously here at the Playground,” Stammy said, and his wife nodded in agreement. “That’s why we’re glad you’re here.”

“Dad!” a young voice called from one of the hallways leading deeper into the building, and everyone turned to see the demon child waving at them.

“Jedfry, is it ready?” the curator asked.

“Yup! Got the first one all warmed up and ready for the guests,” the boy said. “Hi, guests!”

“Hello!” Trevor replied.

“Excellent, we’ll be there soon,” Stammy said, and the boy sped off. He turned to the newlyweds and clapped his hands together. “So, how about we play a game?”
