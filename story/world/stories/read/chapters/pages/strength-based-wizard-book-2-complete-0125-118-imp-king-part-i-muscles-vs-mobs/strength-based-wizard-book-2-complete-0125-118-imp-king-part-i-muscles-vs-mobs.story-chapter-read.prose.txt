Chapter 118

Imp King, Part I (Muscles vs Mobs)

POV: The Imp King

How could such a filthy little thing cause him so much trouble?

It was obscene. An insult to his chosen nature. He expected a fight, but not this. Veronica Sampietro, a mere mortal woman, was forcing him to work. Forcing him to exert himself like some common hedge sorcerer. But he was blessed by a higher power. And his will wouldn’t be denied.

The Imp King’s mind reeled. Something inside him—something carefully arranged, curated, deserved—shivered and cracked. He felt it splinter, and the anger beneath his skin boiled up through the fractures like lava through shattered glass.

His vision went red. He would rip the bitch to shreds.

“This is my [Sacrifice]!”

Above them, the night sky churned with his imps. Hundreds now. Hundreds upon hundreds, tiny winged knives cutting through the air, screeching and laughing and hunting anything that moved. He continued to bloody himself, tearing open his own flesh with deliberate cruelty, letting his Health spill out as fuel while he spammed his largest mass summoning spell again and again and again. He would enjoy watching this bitch get torn to pieces!

Blood dripped from his hands. From his chest. From his mouth where he’d bitten through his own lip in fury. It didn’t matter. Pain was a tool. His body was a temple. His Health was an offering. And offerings were meant to be spent.

Before he knew it, the swarm had become a storm.

So many imps that control became… theoretical. He could feel it slipping. Like trying to hold a river in his palms. Some were already distracted, off chasing pigeons through the empty streets as if they were wolves after fat prey. Others flitted to apartment windows and pressed their ugly faces to the glass, tapping and scratching, delighting in the terrified shapes that jerked back from curtains. A few even dove at the streetlights, biting at the glow like moths. It didn’t matter. They would all, eventually, smell the sacrificial lamb. Hunger would pull them back into orbit. The feast was here. The violence was here. His Dominion would correct itself.

His Health bar pulsed low in the corner of his mind, warning him in that dry System voice that assumed he cared about self-preservation.

You are under the effect of the [Bloodied] condition.

He pushed the notification away. He sensed the sacrifice’s attempt to push past his [Soul Hunter] mark—felt her strain against the leash like a dog trying to bolt.

Pathetic. She couldn’t move more than twenty-five yards away. There was no escape, no running or distancing herself from his Dominion. She had to keep fighting. Had to keep batting away imps, smashing and shielding and swinging like it mattered. He could practically taste the fatigue creeping into her movements. The small, barely perceptible delays in her reactions. The heavier breath, even visible through her armored form. The way her posture sagged by mere degrees.

Good. She would give up soon. He would break her. He always broke them before the end.

He pointed one bloody finger toward her like a judge delivering sentence.

“Tear her apart,” he commanded.

The imps obeyed. At least sixty more swept in at once, a thick black wave of wings and claws and shrieking mouths, collapsing on her position in a frenzy.

It should’ve been the end. A clean, screaming end.

Instead—a blue blur cut through the swarm.

A Spell, or some kind of shield? No. It was a projectile of movement so fast it made the air snap. Imps popped like overripe fruit. Clouds of crimson mist erupted in streaks as bodies were obliterated mid-flight, splattering the night with wet, glittering gore.

The Imp King blinked.

Standing before Veronica now was… a ball of blue ooze. With legs. With… long, feminine legs wrapped in fishnets. With high-heeled boots. It posed for half a heartbeat like it had arrived on a runway, not a battlefield.

The Imp King’s mind tried to categorize it and failed, insulted at the very notion it had to spend processing power on something this ridiculous.

Then the surface of the slime rippled, a bubbled like a pot beginning to simmer. A fist fired from the surface—blue slime compacted into a wrecking ball shape, crackling with purple lightning. It punched him in the chest.

The impact was a concussive detonation that turned his ribs into a suggestion and his lungs into a collapsing cave. He flew backward, body ragdolling across stone and pavement, and crashed into the ground hard enough to rattle the bones in his teeth.

His vision flashed. His Health bottomed out.

He felt his body break—felt the System’s cold accounting declare him near death, a state he had never truly inhabited before.

The feeling was new.

And terrible.

He hated it.

He lay there for a fraction of a second, stunned, blood pooling beneath him, the swarm above still screaming, still hunting, still hungry.

Then hatred took control.

POV: Our Glorious Muscle Mage

I’m flying hard, staff humming beneath my feet, cape snapping behind me like a banner that says I MAKE BAD CHOICES FAST. Public Square grows bigger below—streets emptier than usual because of the evacuation, lights still on like the city forgot to turn them off before leaving. The effect cast everything in a strange glow, shadows growing longer in the empty spaces left behind.

Above the square, massive black clouds churn.

Moving darkness, a living storm. They’re darker than the backdrop of the night sky, darker than the absence between buildings, and they writhe in a way that makes my brain itch.

I try to focus on them, and my HUD goes nuts.

Monster Identified: Imp, Level 45!

Monter Identified: Imp, Level 62!

Monster Identified: Imp, Level 24!

Monster Identified: Imp, Level 16!

Monster Identified: Imp, Level 31!

The notifications stack so fast they blur. My eyes can’t even track the numbers. Hundreds. Hundreds upon hundreds of these things, all different levels, all slightly different auras, all nipping and biting at the edges of my [Perception], all part of one roiling swarm that’s eating the sky.

“Oh, you’ve got to be kidding me,” I mutter, and my stomach clenches. This isn’t a skirmish. This is an infestation. What did Veronica get herself into?

I angle down, diving toward the square, the staff cutting through air like a blade.

That’s when I finally spot her.

“Veronica!”

A breath punches out of me—relief so sharp it almost hurts. She’s alive and moving, though clearly exhausted. She’s armored, hammer swinging, shields flaring around her like metallic sentinels, eyes locked in that fierce, unbreakable focus that makes her the fearsome badass I know.

And beside her is Jelly Boy.

He’s darting and kicking and body-slamming through the swarm like a tiny gelatinous meteor, leaving red mist in his wake. He’s doing some kind of slime-formed fist thing that crackles with energy, punching imps out of the air like he’s playing whack-a-mole with demons. I wonder when he picked up that new trick.

But even with the two of them fighting, there are still too many.

Imps flit between buildings, screaming, swarming around streetlights, slamming into windows, scratching at glass, diving at anything that moves. The square is chaos—dark wings, shrieks, a city block being treated like a buffet for these little sharp-toothed fuckers.

Something off to the side latches onto my [Perception]. My gaze catches on something that makes my blood go cold. A man—homeless, wrapped in layers, hunched near a corner of the square like he’d been trying to sleep through the end of the world—has been caught. Ten imps tear into him. Little bodies piling over him in a frenzy.

He’s screaming. Or trying to. The sound is drowned under the swarm’s screeching.

My jaw tightens. I realize that I may have come to save Veronica, but these imps pose a wider threat to the city and those don’t have the means to protect themselves.

“No.”

I trigger my stance, flexing, and cast [Raining Knuckles].

The sky answers in response to the call of my spell.

Fists hammer down over the largest concentration of flying imps near the man, raining from above like a divine tantrum. It’s brutal and fast, spectral knuckles smashing wings and bodies out of the air in wet bursts. Lower-level imps pop like balloons. Crimson mist blooms, drifting in the streetlights like fog.

The man’s attackers scatter, shrieking.

It buys him seconds.

Not safety. Seconds.

The higher-level imps spiral away from the impact zone like they’re smarter and more disciplined, and aren’t going to just sit there and passively take damage. My spell thins the swarm, but it also forces it to spread.

Fine. If they want to scatter, I’ll give them something to scatter from.

I land hard on the edge of Public Square, staff vanishing into my Inventory mid-drop, my boots hitting pavement with a solid thud. My [Acrobatics] catches the impact clean, and I’m moving before my knees even fully bend.

[Wizard’s Fist]

Silvery mist erupts around me, sparkling in the light of the nearby street lamps.

Then I push [Multiplicity] on top of it like a turbo button. Forty spectral fists bloom into existence in a ring around me, twitching with eager violence, ready to punch the sky itself if I asked them to.

I point outward toward the chaos ovehead, voice sharp.

“Clear them,” I command. “As many as you can. Don’t stop until you’re mist or they are.”

The fists launch. They rocket into the swarm in every direction—punching, grabbing, smashing imps out of the air, sweeping the square like a storm of their own.

And as my fists collide with that living black cloud, the night fills with screams, and I feel the fight finally shift.

I carve through the chaos toward Veronica and Jelly Boy, boots splashing through puddles and blood and whatever other fluids you get when you turn a demon into gore.

A handful of my fists peel off from the wider swarm-clearing pattern and follow me like loyal attack drones, knuckles still dripping silvery vapor. They slam into nearby imps that try to intercept—BAM, BAM—popping a couple midair and sending others spiraling away with wet, furious shrieks.

Jelly Boy is a blue blur ahead, legs clicking like gunfire as he heel-kicks an imp straight through a streetlight. Veronica is beside him, hammer up, shields rotating in a loose orbit. She looks steady, but I can see the strain in the tightness of her jaw and the way her shoulders sit a fraction too high.

I slide in close, fists hovering over us like a protective halo of violence.

“You guys OK?” I shout over the screeching imps.

Veronica glances at me, eyes bright, expression sharp.

“Jelly and I had this completely under control, thank you,” she says, voice dry as sandpaper, “but we appreciate you helping with clean-up duty.”

Jelly Boy vibrates and lets out an emphatic blorp, then turns and kicks another imp out of the air like he’s punting a football into hell.

“Glad Jelly made it here in time!” I yell back, and I mean it. My chest still tightens when I think about the message bubbles disappearing. “Where the hell did all these things come from? This is a massive outbreak!”

Veronica’s smile collapses into something colder.

“Some asshole,” she spits. “Jelly put him down.”

Before I can ask which asshole, the air beside us tears open.

A portal blooms, a clean line splitting open to reveal blue-white light, like someone sliced reality with a scalpel. And out step Reshma, Jerome, and Matt.

Reshma lifts a hand in a tiny wave, like she’s arriving at a dinner party and not an active demonic infestation. “Teleportation scroll,” she says lightly. “We didn’t have a car with us at the AEA Facility.”

Matt’s expression says everything else: That was an expensive Guild resource and we were absolutely not meant to spend it on this.

Jerome steps forward like the square is his courtroom.

He loosens his tie. Then undoes the top button of his dress shirt. His eyes flare. Bright amber, glowing from within, fueled by the magic pulsing through his veins like molten lightning.

He looks up at the swirling night sky full of shrieking imps and speaks like he’s addressing an undisciplined boardroom.

“Looks like we’ve got a lot of work to do.”

POV: Stephanie Wolf

“I hope you fuckers captured all of that!”

Stephanie Wolf barked it at the camera golems like they were interns who might have missed the best take of their careers. She stood in the AEA warehouse with her hands on her hips, staring up at the hunk-sized hole in the ceiling where the “Premier Team Operative” had just vanished, riding off on… was that a barbell?  Dust still drifted down like gray confetti.

The others quickly followed, summoning a portal and leaving without so much as a sentence of exposition. Nothing that can’t be cleaned up in post with some creative editing.

Her golems angled their lens-covered faces up at her, indicator lights blinking in obedient silence. One of them emitted a soft mechanical whir that, if you anthropomorphized it, sounded vaguely like yes ma’am, we did our jobs.

“It better be crisp,” she muttered, voice lowering into something almost affectionate. “If I see motion blur, I’m melting someone into scrap.”

This was going to be good.

This was raw and unscripted—the kind of story that made people forget to blink, that had viewers glued to the edges of their seats. The System Killer. Could that be true? Stephanie took in the information being buzzed directly into her ear. And now, news of a massive monster outbreak hitting the same area? This was clearly not a coincidence. What did their muscled, brainless Loverboy get himself into?

Her enchanted earpiece pinged again, a clean chime, and a voice delivered the update in clipped, efficient tones—location, activity level, security advisories, and the word that made her pupils tighten behind her sunglasses: Public Square, and exact coordinates of the sighting.

“Oh,” Stephanie breathed, and her grin sharpened. “Oh, yes.”

She didn’t fumble for a phone. She didn’t need to. She just issued a command into the air like the world was her control room.

“Touch down.”

Magic pulsed outward from her very core, and she felt it find its target and take hold.

Outside, somewhere above the building, rotors thumped to life.

She paused for half a beat as the warehouse noise filled the space again—humming lights, distant footsteps, the faint alarm chirp of an annoyed facility manager who was going to have to file an incident report titled CEILING, NEW HOLE.

And for a brief, petty second, Stephanie thought about the life before all of this.

Before the System. Before the world became interesting again. Back then, she’d been a mid-level television executive with a resume full of wins and a career full of invisible hands pressing on her shoulders. The industry had been a slow choke—old men with expensive watches telling her to wait her turn, to be “patient,” to “pay her dues,” while they handed her ideas to someone with a louder voice and a weaker vision.

She remembered the meetings. The condescension. The way people smiled while cutting her legs out from under her. Assholes. Fucking assholes!

Now? Now only power mattered. True power. Power she had.

The only ladder was the one you built yourself, and she had built hers out of levels, Skills, Spells, and sheer, ruthless timing. She could capture the stories everyone was starving for in high definition, from angles no network could afford and no agency could control. She was her own boss.

And another hit—another massive event under her banner—would make her untouchable.

Stephanie strode out of the warehouse with purpose, heels striking concrete like punctuation. The night air hit her face, thick and warm. Floodlights washed the facility grounds in harsh white. Ahead, her helicopter sat waiting like a crowned predator, sleek and ready.

The pilot wasn’t a man. It was one of her golems—humanoid but wrong in that smooth, manufactured way, faceplate reflecting light, hands already on the controls like it had been born to fly. Around the helicopter, eight drone-styled golems hovered and circled—compact, winged, bristling with cameras and stabilizers, their lenses tracking her as she approached like a school of obedient metal fish.

Stephanie climbed into the helicopter, settled into the seat, and clicked her harness with the satisfaction of a woman locking herself into destiny.

“Let’s go,” she said, voice bright with hunger.

The pilot golem responded instantly. Rotors thundered and the aircraft lifted cleanly off the ground, rising into the night as the drones swarmed around it in tight formation.

Stephanie looked out through the window at the city lights in the distance, and she could almost taste the headlines.

System Killer. Monster outbreak. Premier Team intervention.

And the only footage would belong to her.

“Oh yes,” she whispered, smiling wide behind her sunglasses. This was going to be good.

POV: Joe, Whey Wizard

The plan is simple. Which is usually how you know it’s about to go wrong.

Jerome steps into the center of the square like a lawyer walking into court with a loaded briefcase, eyes glowing amber, tie loosened, shirt collar open just enough to suggest violence. He speaks one sentence—calm, absolute—and his [Rule] washes outward like a pressure wave.

“You will stay and fight.”

The air stiffens. The edges of the fight harden into boundaries. The imps—hundreds of them—slam against an invisible limit as they try to scatter deeper into the city and fail. They ricochet off the containment like bugs hitting glass.

Jerome’s [Rule] holds them in the immediate area.

Contains the infestation.

Keeps Cleveland from becoming a demonic snow globe.

A lot of them still want Veronica, though. And now, even more turn their attention back to her location. The mark, the rage, the obsession—whatever that asshole guy did before Jelly Boy turned him into paste—has them orbiting her like she’s the brightest candle in a room full of moths.

Reshma stays near Veronica, hands glowing, keeping the Tank’s Health high, layering shields and restoration like she’s knitting survival into place. Veronica keeps swinging, jaw clenched, eyes wild, refusing to collapse even when the air is full of claws.

Jelly Boy bounces around them like a blue guardian angel in stilettos, punting imps out of the air and slapping slime-shields into existence with the manic joy of a creature who thinks this is all a game. He takes full advantage of the fact the mob of imps have all of their aggro focused on Veronica.

Meanwhile, Matt and I take the sky.

I hop back onto the Full Metal Staff, trigger the embedded [Fly], and the barbell hums as it lifts us off the ground. Wind snaps my cape. The city drops away beneath us. The imp swarm becomes a churning black ceiling of wings and teeth.

Matt joins me like it’s nothing. One second he’s on the ground, the next he’s on the staff with me, balanced perfectly, barefoot sandals gripping iron like he’s done this a thousand times. His Dexterity is obscene. Astronomical. He moves with the calm grace of someone who’s never been caught by gravity.

We cut through the air, and I start casting.

Force Blasts pop imps like gnats. Wizard’s Fists snag them mid-flight and fling them down into Jerome’s containment field like trash into a bin.

Matt just goes.

He leaps off the staff into open air, body twisting, and he deletes four imps in a blink—hands flashing, gauntlets striking, something phasing through something else. Then—before he even starts to fall—he flickers.

A short-distance space-hop. He reappears back on the staff like he never left.

It’s pretty fucking cool, I think, even as I’m sweating from concentration and adrenaline.

“Show-off,” I shout over the wind.

Matt doesn’t even look at me. “Work.”

I’m already up one level from the cleanup—System pings, minor stat bumps, the usual. I minimize it with a quick thought. I’ll deal with it later.

Fwoom, fwoom, fwoom!

A helicopter rounds us, close enough that the rotor wash buffets my cape. It’s surrounded by blinking red drone lights—eight of them, orbiting like mechanical flies.

“Wolf,” Matt snaps from over his shoulder. “She’s fast!”

“Of course she is,” I mutter.

The helicopter swings wide, drones adjusting angles, cameras pointed at us like hungry eyes. I see Stephanie’s predatory grin from the open door of the chopper. She shoots us a big thumbs up, as if saying, “This is great! Keep going!”

I sigh, and focus my attention back at the swarms of imps.

Things are going well. We’re thinning the swarm. We’re holding Jerome’s perimeter.

We’re—a pulsing sensation hits the front of my skull.

Like someone flicked my forehead from the inside.

Ping!

My HUD flashes.

SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:

You are in the presence of an interdimensional transformation!

. . .

Overwriting Game Rules

. . .

You are in the presence of an Avatar.

I blink.

“What the fuck?”

“Did you get this message?” Matt asks.

But I can’t respond to him. My [Perception] screams. My [Aura Sense] gets ripped open like a wound.

I turn my head sharply, looking down toward the ground crew.

And hovering above the pavement—above Veronica, Reshma, Jerome, Jelly Boy—is a man. Or, what was maybe once a man. He’s shirtless, thin and pale. Except his hands… his hands are wrong. His skin is jet black from fingertips to elbows, like he dipped his arms in pitch and let it crawl up his veins. Geometric black lines cover his face like a ritual mask etched into flesh. Medium-length black hair is pushed back by two long, thin black horns extending from his forehead, curving back on themselves at their points. His eyes are all crimson. No whites or pupils. Just blood-red light staring out at the world. His eyebrows are bent into a furious scowl.

I try to examine him and my HUD obliges with the worst possible answer.

[Avatar of Chernobog]

“Oh… Fuck!”

My gut turns to ice.

The Avatar moves.

The movement is instant. There’s no wind-up, or step, or even twitch to telegraph the move. Just a flicker I barely catch.

He clears the distance between himself and Veronica like distance is a joke.

He blows straight through Jelly Boy.

The slime explodes into two puddles of blue ooze, splattering across the pavement like spilled paint. The legs are left standing there, stiff and unrealizing.

“NO! Jelly!!” I scream, voice cracking raw.

Reshma dives toward the severed slime, hands glowing, ready to heal—because she can heal anything, because she’s Reshma, the Sainted Blade.

But she’s too slow.

Because the Avatar is already moving again.

He spears a hand forward. Right through Veronica’s armor.

It punctures through her armor like her plate is paper. Veronica’s eyes widen, surprise flashing across her face for the first time tonight.

Then the Avatar yanks.

He rips her straight out of her metal casing like he’s pulling a toy out of its packaging. The metal ruptures, slicing through her arms, cutting her face. The shredded armor falls to the ground like a discarded husk.

He lifts her up by the throat by one hand.

Her feet kick. Her shields swing—instinctively—but not to save herself. They shift to cover Jelly Boy’s puddled halves and Reshma, like her Tank brain can’t stop protecting even as she’s being murdered.

Reshma looks up, realizing what just happened.

It all happens so fast the world feels like it’s skipping frames.

I don’t think. I can’t.

Thinking is too slow. I just act. Consequences be damned. I MAKE BAD DECISIONS FAST. Right?

Matt’s already moving beside me, but I’m not waiting.

We hop off the staff.

The barbell snaps into my hands like it’s part of me.

I trigger [Range of Motion].

Ghostly images of myself extend ahead—ten frames of possible action in the next three seconds. One of them ends beside the Avatar. Before I can even take another breath, my arms are already in motion, powered by pure Strength and the brutal efficiency of [Muscle Mastery].

I select the final image.

Reality snaps.

I’m there.

Beside him.

The movement completes the instant the Skill places me.

The staff swings.

And takes the Avatar’s head clean off his shoulders.

It’s almost… easy.

The crimson eyes vanish mid-glare.

The horned head tumbles away.

The grip on Veronica’s throat slackens.

She drops, hitting the pavement in a limp heap.

I stagger forward, breath ragged, heart hammering, and scramble toward Jelly Boy. Blood is spraying like a fountain from the stump protruding from the shoulders of the Avatar.

Thankfully, Jelly Boy is already reconstituting—blue ooze pulling itself back together like magnetized liquid. Reshma’s hands glow as she works, calm and precise even with horror still in her eyes.

“Slime is surprisingly easy to work with,” she says, like we’re discussing first aid at a summer camp. “No bones or organs to worry about.”

I almost laugh from sheer relief.

Then my HUD pings again.

Another System notification.

The words make my blood run cold. We can’t catch a break, can we?

SYSTEM MESSAGE:

A Death Condition has been satisfied.

. . .

Game Rules being overwritten...

. . .

You are in the presence of an interdimensional transformation!

. . .

I stare at the message.

At the headless body.

At the crimson-black blood steaming on the pavement.

And I realize, with sinking dread…

Killing the Avatar might have been the first mistake.
