Back at the inn, Veronica, Gwen, and Nasha gathered in her room to discuss their next steps.

“What did you receive from the Viscount, Nasha?” Veronica asked.

Earlier, everyone involved in bringing down the Scavengers had been rewarded.

Veronica received a sum of vix. Now, she had roughly thirty thousand in travel funds. But alongside that, she also received the Viscount’s seal: a small silver signet. It represented Viscount Leopold’s words and his status; the full backing of his house. Having it meant that she now had a measure of noble backing, up to a certain degree.

He had warned her not to misuse it, however.

Apparently, he had heard of every time she used his seal while in town—including the incident with the innkeeper in the slums.

Still, it was a useful asset to have.

Gwen, on the other hand, had chosen a reward for her aunt—one year free of taxes. It was thoughtful, though Veronica felt it was a bit too modest.

Lord Tomas had apparently agreed and insisted on giving her a new staff as well.

Though maybe ‘new’ wasn’t the correct word for it.

The staff came from the Ronswick family vault, an older relic. It was made of twisted wood, thickening as it curved around a large blue gem. The material came from an elder Rosswind tree, a rare species that grew only in arid regions. It showed signs of prior use, but it would serve Gwen well until they could craft a custom one.

“Me?” Nasha said, shifting as she leaned back on her hands at the edge of the bed. “He gave me a bow from his armory. A good one. I like it. It’s in my closet for now.”

Veronica nodded. “Weapons are useful. They’ll help with where we’re going next.”

“Speaking of that,” Gwen said, cutting in. She sat near the front of the room, glancing at her reflection.

Veronica noticed the new clothes she wore: an off-the-shoulder white blouse with puffed, loose sleeves that slimmed and gathered at the wrists, paired with a black skirt trimmed in yellow. Long black tights led down into a new pair of boots to complete the look.

“Does that mean Nasha’s coming with us?” Gwen asked, glancing over her shoulder.

Veronica looked over at Nasha.

The crimson rogue was dressed more casually now—a long white sweater cinched at the waist, paired with short white bottoms.

“I heard about the end of the world straight from you,” Nasha said, looking at Veronica. “It’d be a shame to stay in Ronswick and wait for it, wouldn’t it? Besides… it sounds interesting.”

“I’m sure that’s not the only thing that’s interesting to you,” Gwen chimed with a knowing smile. What was strange was her gaze wasn’t directed at Nasha, but instead at Veronica.

“What are you talking about?” Veronica asked.

“I wanted to mention it earlier… but I thought it was just… a thing you two agreed on.”

“What?”

Gwen picked up the mirror and held it in front of herself, directly in Veronica’s gaze.

“What are you—” Veronica’s words died in her throat as her face contorted, spotting it immediately.

A red mark. Straight on her neck. One given to her from Nasha, after last night’s… pleasantries.

Veronica immediately slapped her neck, covering it up. She turned and glared toward Nasha. “Why didn’t you tell me! I’ve been walking around all day like this? That means… Hugh and the Viscount…”

“I thought you would have noticed it this morning when you washed up after your jog,” Nasha said, chuckling.

Veronica frowned. “The mirror inside the washroom is an angled standing mirror. I wasn’t looking at my neck at the time.” She lifted her palm and turned to Gwen. “Can you heal this?”

Gwen raised her hand and began casting her spell, starting the mending process of the blood vessels at the surface of her skin.

“Anyway,” Nasha chimed, a smirk still on her face. “Where are you planning to go next? I assume it’s somewhere important.”

“It is,” Veronica said. “In a few days, once everything is in order, we head west. To the Valley of Silence.”

“The Valley of Silence?” Gwen asked. “Why there?”

“There’s something I need to retrieve.”

Gwen lit up, lowering her hands, finished with her spell. “Ooh. Some kind of magical artifact? Or a powerful relic from an ancient era?”

“…yes, actually.”

“Hah! I knew it. That sounds just like you,” Gwen said.

Veronica raised a brow. “Am I that predictable?”

Gwen shrugged. “It’s what I’d do. If I went back in time, I’d grab every resource I could and make myself as strong as possible.”

“What’s in the Valley of Silence?” Nasha asked, cutting in. “That place is barren. Setting aside the monsters, just traveling through it would be difficult.”

“I know,” Veronica said. “We’ll need a guide. There should be a small town not far from the edge of the valley.”

“And what exactly are we looking for?” Gwen asked. “A weapon? A dragon egg?”

“Maybe a hidden dwarven settlement,” Nasha added, playing along.

“Ooh, that’s a good one,” Gwen said, glancing at her before turning back. “Or maybe some ancient elf tied to your bloodline who helps you unlock hidden power?”

Normally, Veronica would have sighed at them. She could almost feel the habit rising, but she didn’t follow through.

Instead, she let out a quiet laugh.

“It’s none of those,” she said. “Well… it could be. I don’t actually know.”

“You don’t know?” Gwen frowned. “What does that mean?”

“It means I know something powerful is there,” Veronica said. “But I don’t know what it is. All I know is that a year from now, someone discovers something at the center of the Valley of Silence. Something incredibly valuable.”

She paused, searching her memory.

“News spread quickly. People from across Vitian—even those from neighboring countries—all came to claim it for themselves. A year after that, a major battle broke out.”

“What kind of battle?” Nasha asked, leaning forward slightly.

Veronica’s expression tightened.

“I was a 6th Tier mage at the time. I was sent to investigate what happened. But when I arrived…” She paused. “All I found was a crater.”

“A crater?” Nasha repeated. “From an explosion?”

Veronica nodded. “Large enough to swallow most of Ronswick.”

Gwen and Nasha both went still.

“When I flew closer, there wasn’t much else to see. Nothing at the center. Just torn earth and hardened stone. From what I could tell, there had been a battle between nearly twenty people—mages around the fifth tier and other skilled fighters. All of them… wiped out in an instant.”

“How are you sure this is something we need?” Gwen asked. “That just sounds like some kind of magical bomb.”

“It’s not,” Veronica said. “Or at least, that’s not all it was. The man who found it became extremely powerful. Rumors spread, and many began hunting him down, coveting whatever fortune he had found. Fifth and sixth-tier mages, thrill-seeking challengers, renowned and infamous swordmasters, and even professional assassins. But he was able to hold them all off. Just by himself.”

She paused.

“And from what I heard… he wasn’t anything special before. Just a martial artist. Someone who had only reached Tier 3 with aura.”

“Then it has to be some kind of powerful artifact,” Gwen said.

Veronica nodded. “I don’t know exactly what it is. But I do know its name.”

“What is it?”

“The Tears of Selene.”

“Tears? Sounds like a potion or an elixir or something. Maybe holy water?” Gwen asked.

“Could be, but I don’t think so. Otherwise, the man would have taken the entire thing. There would be no purpose in hunting him down. Unless he had acquired several vials or bottles of the stuff,” Veronica replied.

Gwen tilted her head, thinking it over. “So… we’re just heading there early and grabbing it first? Should be pretty quick, right? Where do we go after?”

Nasha crossed her arms, smirking at her. “You don’t know much about the Valley of Silence, do you?”

Gwen shook her head. “No. Should I?”

“It’s not exactly a place people go to for a casual trip,” Nasha said. “Dead land. No life. Plenty of monsters roaming the place.”

Gwen’s expression stiffened. “Oh.”

She looked toward Veronica, then gave a small nod. “Well… I’ll be right behind you, then. I’ll heal you as best as I can.”

Veronica shook her head. “No.”

“No?”

“I’ll be fighting, yes,” Veronica said calmly. “But so will you.”

Gwen stared at her. “What?”

“It’ll be good training,” Veronica continued. “You won’t grow stronger by standing in the back the entire time.”

Gwen shifted slightly. “I mean… I’m a healer…”

“And you’re my student,” Veronica replied. “No student of mine is taking the pacifist route to reach Tier-10. Healer or not.”

Gwen let out a small laugh. “That’s a good one.”

Veronica simply stared at her.

The smile slowly faded from Gwen’s face.

“…you’re serious?”

Deeper beneath the earth, a chamber lay, carved from dark, polished stone that reflected the dim glow of embedded crystals along the walls. The air was cool and chilling, coming straight from vented holes around the walls and ceiling. The place smelled of damp rock with a metallic tang. Narrow passageways branched outward in several directions, leading to other rooms and chambers like veins of blood. Each sector was heavily guarded.

In one particular chamber, a long stone table sat in its center, its surface worn but smooth. Several figures gathered around it, each one covered in shadow beneath dim light.

“What do you mean we lost contact with Ronswick?” an older man demanded, voice laced with irritation.

A man knelt near the base of the table on the ground. Iron rested on his wrists, digging deep into his skin. A chained brace clasped around his neck, one so thick he couldn’t nod—but he could still bow with his body.

“T-Talenick has been captured,” he said, keeping his head lowered, his chin resting against the cold metal. “The Viscount sent his guards through the city. They’ve shut down… everything. That’s what I’ve heard.”

A low murmur passed through the room.

The elder waved a hand dismissively. “That was inevitable. Talenick is a loose end that we don’t need to worry about. His capture is insignificant.”

He leaned forward. “What about the mana crystals? As long as shipments continue, nothing changes.”

The chained man hesitated. His voice faltered.

“I… I don’t think they are,” he said. “There were rumors… a few days before Talenick was taken… the supplier disappeared.”

Silence fell.

“…disappeared?”

“That’s what I’ve heard,” he replied quickly. “No one has seen him since. And there haven’t been any shipments after that.”

The elder’s hand slammed against the table. “What?”

His voice rose, sharp. “How does a supplier just vanish? Did Talenick ruin something?”

“I-I don’t know,” the man said. “But people were talking. The Viscount’s son had been working with a merchant, trying to uncover things. That much was known. But… this wasn’t part of that.”

He swallowed, forcing the words out.

“They said… a woman got involved. She killed the supplier. Helped the Viscount’s son capture Talenick. And she took down two of your… forgers.”

The elder’s eyes narrowed. “Your?”

The man shivered. “O-our, forgers. Sir.”

“And?”

The man’s voice tightened. “From what I’ve heard… they’ve begun breaking into parts of the underground trade there, leading to mass arrests. They began purging the entire market and even… rescued and freed some of the slaves.”

For a moment, his tone shifted, tight and restrained. Almost as if he wished he were part of the rescued slaves.

The shift didn’t go unnoticed by the others.

Before the elder could reprimand him, another voice came. Much colder. Much calmer.

It came from the head of the table.

“Who is this woman?”

The room stilled at his words. Everyone sitting at the table turned to face the slave.

Varce, leader of Vermillion had spoken—and he required an answer.

The chained man bowed lower, shoulders tense and trembling. “I—I don’t know her name. Only what people have said.”

“Then say it,” Varce replied.

“She’s young. Early twenties. A D-rank adventurer. Tanned skin…” He hesitated. “…and beautiful.”

A pause lingered. The man did not dare look up; he kept his body angled to the ground, refusing to meet any of their eyes.

Varce spoke again, quieter, but much sharper.

“That could describe hundreds. Try again.”

The man trembled. “There were… other details. From what I gathered… some say she might be a dark elf. Or half-blooded. And… she has ties to the Mage Tower. So she may be a mage.”

The elder scoffed. “A young dark elf mage…” His jaw tightened. “Who the hell is she to disrupt our operations?”

He exhaled sharply. “If the mana crystals are gone, we have nothing to send to the Oprus delegation.”

“If the supply is lost… there is another path.”

Varce’s words caused everyone to look over at him.

The elder’s expression darkened. “You mean to abandon the current trade? We chose mana crystals for a reason. The profit is modest, but it is steady and keeps us out of sight.”

Varce did not waver in his response.

“If the source is truly gone, we have no choice.” His voice carried across the chamber, stoic and monotone. “We will flood the market.”

The elder’s gaze hardened. “The Kingdom will notice. There is no chance they won’t.”

“That is of no concern,” Varce said. “If we confirm there will be no further shipments, we will begin distributing the drugs.”

A quiet tension settled at the name as those around the table hesitated.

“The funds will come faster,” Varce continued. “And once it spreads, it will be too late for the Kingdom to stop it.”

He paused, looking around at those still uncertain.

“The Oprus delegation is already waiting. They require capital for their campaign. If we cannot supply mana crystals, then we will provide liquid funds.”

“This will accelerate the war substantially,” the elder said. “It undermines the current plans we have in place.”

“Then revise them,” Varce replied, his voice unyielding. “They are our largest client. If we fail to support their campaign, we lose far more than reputation. We uphold our contracts. This will be done.”

The elder frowned, then slowly exhaled, clenching his fingers into a fist.

“…then we confirm the supplier’s status in Ronswick,” he said. “And if the supply is truly gone…”

His eyes darkened, hesitating for only a moment. Even as an elder, he couldn’t disobey. “We will begin distributing Snowfell into the common cities.”

A hush fell over the room as Varce stood up. The elder stood next, then the others followed.

“Begin preparations at once. Send a scout to Ronswick and prepare a messenger for Oprus,” he said, his voice echoing as he moved through the shadows.

He stepped past the table and stopped beside the kneeling slave, resting a hand on the man’s shoulder. “As for you… you’ve done well to bring this information.”

The man gulped, the metal collar pressing uncomfortably against his neck. “T-thank you, sir.”

Leaning down slightly, he spoke softly near the man’s ear. “Earlier… that tone. You sounded almost as if you envied those freed slaves.”

A chill ran through the man’s body. “N-no! Sir! I-I am happy to serve you!”

A faint smile formed on Varce’s face, shadowed by the dim light. “Relax. I won’t have the elders punish you.”

The man’s eyes widened, still fixed on the floor. “T-thank you, sir! I—”

His words caught, refusing to come out. His hands shot up, but the heavy collar prevented him from grasping his throat. A wheezing exhale came from his throat as he struggled, fingers fumbling against the metal brace. His body convulsed, pupils rolling back into his head, until the color white took their place.

Moments later… he collapsed to the ground.

Dead within seconds.

Varce straightened, walking toward the door. He paused, glancing back. “Someone clean that mess up.”

“Yes, sir,” one of the members replied at once.

He hummed and stepped through the doorway, then paused for a brief moment.

“And… send someone to deal with that mage. Pests need to be dealt with before they grow and fester.”

He didn’t wait for a reply. As he continued down the hall, his final words came as a low murmur. “I don’t care how it’s done.”

“Yes, sir,” the elder said, watching Varce’s shadow disappear into the darkness.

His gaze drifted down to the lifeless body at the center of the room, now being carried away.

“…it will be done.”
