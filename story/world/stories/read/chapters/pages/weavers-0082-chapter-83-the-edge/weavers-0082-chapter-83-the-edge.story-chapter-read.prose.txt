Chapter 83 - The Edge

The final bar gave way with a low, protesting groan that vibrated up her arms and into her teeth. She froze instantly, every muscle locked, counting her breaths as the sound faded into the stone. The tunnels swallowed it whole.

Good.

She didn’t waste the opening.

Kana twisted sideways, shoulder first, squeezing through the narrow gap. The metal bit into her sleeve, scraping skin, but she barely felt it. The moment her boots hit the floor on the other side, she dropped into a crouch, one hand braced against the cold stone, the other hovering near the broken bars as if expecting them to snap shut again.

They didn’t.

The corridor stretched ahead narrowly, the uneven walls lit by guttering torchlight that painted the walls in sickly amber. Water dripped somewhere in the distance, each echo spaced just far enough apart to set her nerves on edge. The air smelled old, like it had been sitting still for centuries, a combination of rust, oil and other foul scents.

Kana inhaled carefully through her nose and immediately regretted it.

Okay. I’m out, she told herself. Step one accomplished. Now for step two.

She straightened slowly, resisting the urge to bolt. With panic, comes noise, and from there mistakes would follow.

Her fingers flexed unconsciously, a habit she’d picked up in an attempt to subdue her stress in the past.

Aria.

The name surfaced to the forefront of her mind.

Aria had been knocked out nearby. But unlike Kana, she was left there, free to roam around after she woke.

That alone told Kana more than any confession could have. Aric hadn’t been trying to ‘remove obstacles’, he’d been arranging them. Setting up pieces on a board and stepping back to see it all play out like he’d planned.

Kana exhaled slowly, jaw tightening.

I won’t let you. Even if Aria’s gonna end up playing into your hands, I won’t.

Coming out from the room which her cage had been kept in, she turned in a slow circle, her eyes tracing the shape of the tunnel.

She stood at a junction. Left: a long corridor sloping downward, walls dripping with moisture.

Right: a shorter passage which quickly turned off to the left.

Neither felt safe. Both felt… wrong.

Her sense of direction was shot. The cage, the blow to the head, the way the tunnels twisted in on themselves. It had all scrambled her internal sense of direction. She tried to recall how long Aric had walked, or which turns he’d made, but she was flickering in and out of consciousness the whole time. There’s no way to know where he went.

Nothing solid came back to her.

“Fantastic,” Kana muttered under her breath.

Standing still wasn’t an option. Every second she stayed put was another second Aric gained. Another second for Aria to fall for his schemes. Another second Kaelin fought above ground, blissfully unaware that the academy was rotting from the inside out.

Kana clenched her fists, then loosened them.

If I were him, she thought, I would… uhhhh. FUCK. How am I meant to do this without them. As she did so, her face scrunched up and she held her hands over her eyes, pressing into her temples.

Her gaze slid back to the right-hand passage.

I guess I’ll start there, She thought, still not entirely confident.

Kana moved, boots whispering against stone as she slipped into the darker corridor. Immediately, the ceiling dipped lower, forcing her to hunch. The walls pressed in close enough that her shoulders brushed them with each step, cold stone seeping through fabric.

She hated confined spaces. Always had, but she never let it show around Kaelin. How could she, looking weak like that wasn’t ever an option before.

The tunnel smelled worse here. Stagnant air layered with damp and decay. The torchlight thinned until it barely reached ten feet ahead, shadows pooling thick and heavy beyond that.

Argh, why did Aric have to skimp out on the torches, I can’t see a thing.

Kana slowed further, every sense stretched taut. All except her sight.

Her footsteps became careful, measured. Heel, toe. Heel, toe. She kept close to the wall, avoiding loose rubble, memorising the sound of her own movement so anything out of place would stand out.

A scrape echoed faintly through the stone.

Kana froze.

Her breath was caught in her throat as she flattened herself against the wall, breath shallow, counting again. One. Two. Three.

Nothing followed.

She swallowed and pushed on.

The tunnel curved sharply, opening into a small junction where three narrow halls branched off like veins. The ceiling here was lower still, the stone darker, older. Torch brackets lined the walls, two empty and rust-eaten, their shadows clawing at the floor. One however, held a dying flame, though still enough to see a few meters ahead.

Kana paused at the centre, turning slowly.

Her stomach twisted.

He must’ve been here recently. And if he wanted somewhere no one would interrupt him…

She shut that thought down before it could spiral.

Focus.

She listened again. This time, she caught something. It was faint, potentially distant. But there’s no harm in being careful. It sounded like fabric brushing stone. Quiet enough that she might’ve imagined it.

Her muscles tensed.

She waited. Five seconds. Ten.

Silence reclaimed the space.

Kana let out a controlled breath and chose the rightmost passage again, committing to the decision fully now. Doubt would only slow her down.

This tunnel sloped upward, the air growing marginally fresher with each step. Scratch marks marred the walls, uneven grooves that looked like something heavy had been dragged through there.

Her jaw set.

“Hold on,” she whispered, as if the stone itself might carry the promise.

The passage narrowed, forcing her sideways. The stone scraped her shoulder, sending a jolt of pain through her nerves. Ahead, a faint draft brushed her cheek. A much cooler air than the stuffy, flavourful, air which she had come to know in her short time in the tunnels.

And that moving air gave her hope. Of a way out. A way which Aric hadn’t expected, a way for her to divert his plans, and help Aria.

Hope, dangerous and unwelcome, flickered in her chest.

Behind her, deep within the tunnels, something shifted. The stone groaned softly, gears grinding, growling like wild beasts. The sound made her skin crawl.

She had to hurry.

Kana didn’t look back.

Whatever Aric was planning, whatever game he thought he was playing, she refused to be a piece for him to control as he pleases.

At the end of the tunnel was an archway. Past that, was a large chamber.

That was where the wind was coming from. Violent gusts billowing past her.

Kana stepped through the archway and stopped dead.

The tunnel in front of her felt like a lie the moment the wind hit her face.

The sharp and cold winds came, carrying salt and spray, ripping the stale underground air from her lungs. The sound followed a heartbeat later: the low, endless thunder of waves smashing against stone, so vast and violent it felt impossible that it could exist anywhere near the academy.

Kana stepped forwards.

The passage opened into a vast stone chamber carved directly into the cliff-side, its ceiling arcing high overhead. The stone here was likely older than the academy, older than the city even; smoothed by centuries of erosion as opposed to the delicate craftsmanship used to create the flagstones seen all around Erundal. Narrow openings cut through the outer wall, allowing pale daylight to spill in and illuminate dust motes spinning wildly in the air.

Set into the stone floor was a circular formation, partially obscured by dust and debris. Kana approached cautiously, boots echoing softly as she brushed away loose grit with her foot

Six indentations marked the outer ring.

Six places, evenly spaced.

At the centre of the circle was a runic symbol.

The symbol for time.

Kana’s stomach twisted. Around the circle lay dried blood. Most of it was compiled near to the six circles. Perhaps it was a surprise attack.

One trail off blood lead toward the cliff side. She looked up to follow its path. At the end of it sat an archway, cut straight out of the cliff.

And beyond it, was the ocean.

A great, expansive land, stretching endlessly toward a warped horizon. The sea churned far below, waves crashing against jagged black cliffs with manic intensity. Everything moved too fast. The water surged and recoiled in rapid cycles, foam dissolving almost the instant it formed. Birds streaked across the sky like arrows loosed from a bow. Fish flashed beneath the surface in frantic, silver blurs.

Kana’s breath slowed, almost as if her heart itself had stopped.

“This… It can’t be—” she muttered.

Erundal was landlocked, everyone knew that. Maps, history books, everything told the same story. But this went against it all, everything she had ever been taught to know and believe.

Her sense of unease heightened as she stepped closer to the edge.

The silence hit harder than before, leaving her heartbeat to resonate throughout her body.

Kana swallowed.

Almost no one ever even went near the borders of Erundal, the constant state of war in the surrounding countries left people wary to even get close in case they were captured or caught in some kind of military manoeuvre.

Of the people who did, they never came back. The people in the capital had all sorts of ideas of what could’ve happened, but they were all speculations. The vast majority of people believed every word the crown said, after all, why wouldn’t they?

The official story was always the same; they got lost, died on the front lines by chance, or even that they’d been kidnapped and interrogated by the enemy.

In the past, Kana had always bought into it, but now she felt unsure of what to believe.

She stared out at the sea, eyes narrowing.

How come there wasn’t a single person who had ever returned alive… She thought

What would happen if they had passed through the barrier. What stopped them returning? Maybe this barrier had something to do with the circle behind her.

She looked back, staring at the five spaces which were unoccupied, the symbol for time sitting in the centre.

Then her eyes darted back to the barrier.

Maybe the barrier prevented time from passing as quickly as it would otherwise whilst inside. That would mean passing through was a death sentence.

Ageing decades in seconds.

Crumbling into dust before they could utter a word.

The only other possibility was simpler, and much more likely; the crown took measures to make sure they didn’t talk.

Kana exhaled through her mouth, jaw clenched.

She crouched and picked up a small stone near the threshold, rolling it between her fingers, and tossed it forward.

The stone vanished downward almost instantly, accelerating so fast it dropped out of sight before gravity should have had time to claim it. No splash followed, or if one did, it happened too fast for her to notice.

Kana’s pulse jumped.

“…Right,” she murmured.

She glanced around, searching for anything living. A thin vine crept stubbornly from a crack in the stone, pale leaves trembling in the wind. She snapped off a single leaf and flicked it forward.

It struck something invisible and bounced back, fluttering to the ground at her feet.

Kana stared.

Organic matter couldn’t pass.

The realisation settled cold and heavy in her gut. This barrier was clearly meant to keep people in. The people in power must have a reason for this, for keeping Erundal sealed away, isolated from the natural flow of time itself.

And then another thought followed.

Aric can’t have done this.

She straightened slowly, scanning the chamber again with fresh eyes. The scale of it, the intricate symbols on the ground, the age of the stone which they were carved into—none of it matched a single mage’s work. Not even Aric, as brilliant and unstable as he was.

“No,” she said quietly. “You don’t have that kind of power.”

Which meant someone else had done this. Or a group of some sort. But no one outside of the monarchy had the power to keep people from exploring to the edges of the kingdom.

Thinking back to the circle, it had all made sense. Aric must be using it for some part of his plan.

What if he wanted me to see this. What if I’d been in the palm of his hand this whole time…

But there was no point dwelling on it. Whether she’d fallen for his trap or not, she was here, it had happened.

But the thought somehow felt worse than the alternative.

Kana straightened, heart pounding now, her thoughts racing ahead. Kaelin. Lena. The people in the capital sat still, blissfully unaware of what lurked beneath their feet.

She stared at her feet in thought, both hesitant to leave, and anxious to make her escape.

A soft sound echoed behind her.

Her gaze dropped to the far side of the chamber, and she froze.

A man stood at the entrance to the tunnel, half-shrouded in shadow. He wore a dark cloak that swallowed the light around him, its edges barely stirring in the wind. His face was obscured, either by shadow or intent.

He hadn’t been there just a moment ago.

Kana’s breath caught as instinct screamed at her to run.

The man tilted his head slightly, as if studying her.

Finally Kana managed to get some words out, “A-Aric?” she said shakily.

Surprisingly, the man replied.

“Oh no no. Aric’s busy right now. But he sent me to help out. You know, for the cause.”
