Mishki tentatively descended a vertical stone wall at a diagonal, heading toward a crumbled rampart that no longer connected to the castle. That rampart was the nearest exit from the castle into the open cavern below the Fold’s crust.

Even though he needed to scale a wall to reach it, that wasn’t a problem for him. He was a goble, after all, and navigating treacherous terrain in the dark was second nature to him. He even relished the return to natural stone beneath his fingers and the crisp subterranean air filling his lungs.

When he was close enough, he hopped off the wall and onto the stable section of the rampart. Built directly into the subterranean slopes, it dropped off steeply into the darkness.

There. Now I just have to follow this path down.

It would take him a few hours, but once the rampart leveled off, he would have to follow a winding land bridge, hop across a series of rock columns, and then pass through a few chambers to reach where the egg supposedly lay.

The quiet helped him stay calm.

All that changed when a grating sound echoed through the chasm. Mishki paused and tilted his ear, but before he could figure out what the noise was, burning black-purple ribbons of vile energy blasted upward from the depths.

With a yelp, he scrambled to flatten himself against the rampart. The purple blast was like a reverse waterfall, ascending all the way to the surface high overhead. Chunks of rock fell and slanted rays of daylight filtered through new openings.

Frightened, Mishki wedged himself into the corner where the rampart met the wall. With the purple veil of energy flailing wildly, he didn’t dare move until he knew where it settled. For what felt like minutes, he watched the undulating wave until it finally seemed to fix itself in place and the rumbling came to an end.

By the spheres. What was that?

Mishki swallowed hard when he remembered what the aerudine had said about the Devouring Coil waking. He didn’t see anything moving in the darkness, though. This might just be the first stage.

He needed to get that egg, and fast.

After smacking his legs to get them to stop shaking, he trotted down the rampart as fast as he dared.

To his dismay, new patches of the rampart had fallen away with the shaking just moments ago. The fresh gaps occasionally forced Mishki to step off the side of the rampart and climb horizontally to bypass the dropoffs.

While it wasn’t difficult, it took more time, and he kept checking the purple sheets of energy to make sure they didn’t shift again.

The next time he paused, though, wasn’t because of any dropoffs or purple explosions.

Ahead and right in his path was a colony of dreadmaw beetles. They grazed on sparse patches of gray cave moss clinging to the walls.

These weren’t like the dreadmaw beetles that existed on the normal side of the Boundary. He had encountered plenty of those when he and so many other gobles had been sent to fetch blocks of stone or bottled dirt taken from sites of power to fuel the furnace. There had been nut-hurling squirrels, flaming torch slugs, and, of course, the hard-shelled dreadmaw beetles.

But, like with several other animals Mishki had observed on this side of the Ruined Wilds, these versions were warped and highly territorial. Whether it had to do with the blight overhead, the frequent use of the furnace by the warlock, or just because it was on this side of the Boundary, Mishki didn’t know. It was probably some combination of all three things.

Whatever the case, these beetles were three times their usual size, making them altogether behemoths to a goble like him. Their hardened carapaces gleamed in the dim light, their powerful mandibles knocking chunks of stone off with each clacking munch on the gray cave moss. Even their eyes glowed with an eerie, bioluminescent sheen.

He leaned against the wall and rested with a heavy breath. Between the repulsive purple energy and a sheer drop off, his only available path would take him through beetle territory.

Mishki rapped his head against the stone wall with a few light taps before glancing back at the sun rays peeking in from above. His Night’s Favor and Shadow Slink abilities only kicked in at night and were unavailable for hours yet.

There had to be another way to get past.

As he scanned high and low, he caught sight of some pocked holes in the mountainside. Most gobles believed these were bad luck to enter, but Mishki had read about them. They were just short tunnels caused by erosion. Oxbows, he thought they were called. Sometimes they led to dead ends, but most of the time they looped back into the main cavern.

These holes were too small for the beetles to fit in, though. He might be able to find one that dipped into the mountain’s side before popping back out further along, where the beetles weren’t a problem.

Putting aside thoughts of bad luck, Mishki began scaling the wall for the nearest opening. Patches of the stone he had to climb had been loosened by the jaws of passing dreadmaws, and others were covered in clusters of brittle roots that could collapse if he put his weight on it. Instead, he decided to climb higher, over the treacherous holds, and then lower himself into a wall tunnel.

He ascended past the questionable terrain and hopped onto the ledge into the tunnel. Inside, the channel was cramped but passable. His smaller frame allowed him to slip through crevices and his progress was steady.

The tunnel spat him out onto the opposite side of the main herd of beetles, just as intended. He could walk on a narrow ledge and then get back onto the main rampart and be on his way. There were still a few beetles shuffling about at the herd’s fringe, but they were significantly smaller, and they seemed completely uninterested in him.

He was careful to avoid them, stepping lightly, but at the end of the ledge, just before reaching the rampart, a low clicking sound made him freeze.

He turned slowly.

A much larger beetle crawled onto the rampart and faced him, its enormous pincers twitching. It was a female, and her horns were smaller than the males, but she was still larger than Mishki.

She stamped a chitinous hoof. These insects could build momentum and charge fast, and Mishki did not want to provoke her. He trod lightly away, closer to the smaller beetles so he could get back into the wall tunnel.

That seemed to anger her further, though, and she swung her snout at Mishki.

Mishki dodged and nearly lost his footing on the narrow ledge. He caught himself, expecting the female beetle to lunge again, but as long as Mishki didn’t move, neither did she.

With no other options, he raised both hands, palms out, and took a wild chance as he activated Critter Taming.

“I don’t mean you any harm.”

The beetle hesitated, then bucked at Mishki once, then at the smaller beetles. She nuzzled one with her armored cheek and then bucked at Mishki again. Suddenly, Mishki understood.

Of course! She is a mother protecting her young!

“Yes, my friend, I will just walk this way. Very slowly, like this. Yes. That’s it…”

He reassured the beetle with gentle movements of his outward-facing palms. When the moment came that he would have to hop off the ledge and onto the rampart to pass her, she took a small step back to give him space.

Critter Taming is level 24!

The tension in Mishki’s shoulders eased slightly. Encouraged, he took a careful step away from them.

Between the energy explosion, the wall scaling, and now that near-fright with the beetles, his stomach growled at him. He reached into his pouch, pulling out a bit of dried gray cave moss he had gathered earlier. It worked for people as much as it did beetles, even if it wasn’t exactly his idea of tasty.

One of the young beetles scampered past his mother and clacked at Mishki’s feet.

Mishki hesitated and watched the mother, who looked on with those uncanny eyes. She didn’t seem to think he was a threat any more.

Slowly, he lowered a wad of moss and dropped it for the young beetle.

Critter Taming is level 25!

Threshold perk unlocked!Psyche BondingCreepy crawlies see much in the shadow. You can learn a thing or two from them! Go on, give it a try.

A sudden rush of information flooded Mishki’s mind. It was unlike anything he had felt before, more a flurry of images and feelings than words. The beetle gnawing at the dropped moss wasn’t just reacting to him feeding it. It was communicating.

Images flickered in Mishki’s mind.

With how the furnace skewed the levels of ambient mana in the air, it had leeched nutrients from most of the gray cave moss here. That had caused the beetles to leave their main home in search of more food. The smaller beetles were suffering from the lack of nourishment, and the larger herd had abandoned them, forcing them to survive on the outskirts.

Mishki shrank in on himself. “I’m sorry,” he said. But he had to press on.

The mother beetle clicked her mandibles. This time, though, there was something different in her mannerism. Was she… imploring him?

Mishki swallowed hard and shook his head.

She lowered her eyes to her little ones and clicked once more.

He whispered another apology before continuing toward his destination.

To his surprise, the beetles followed.

He tried to shake them off, but they remained at his heels, their incandescent eyes watching him expectantly. “I can’t give you any more food,” he said, both to them and the mother beetle. She just stared at him, and when he took another step, she nudged her children along.

He sighed and gave up. If they wanted to follow him, he couldn’t stop them.

They continued on for several junctions, scaling the walls when necessary. When they reached the section where the rampart led to flat ground and Mishki would have to head north to jump along rock columns, he stopped.

He stared in disbelief. The two nearest columns had fallen, and he wasn’t going to be able to jump the distance to the next closest one.

As he stared blankly at the wrecked path before him, the shelled insects took the momentary break to play with one another. He came out of his stupor and watched them. To them, this was probably just an exciting outing. What difference was it to them that the path forward was blocked off?

Unless…

He approached the mother and cleared his throat. “You must be quite familiar with the terrain here. You probably know the area from scouting out feeding spots.”

The mother beetle clacked in agreement, and the younglings chirped at the mention of food.

Mishki took his chance and crouched low to the floor, scratching his finger through loose dirt. “Would you happen to know a place that looks like this? It should be that way, over the rock columns, but maybe there’s another way to reach it.”

As the beetles collectively gathered around his sketch of the chamber that should hold the dragon egg, the mother stamped her feet with joy. Images of torn carapaces and glistening innards flooded Mishki’s mind.

The scene initially startled him, but he then realized the slain beetles were from a rival clan; from her viewpoint, it was a fond memory of past victories, celebrated by the satisfying crunch of broken bodies and the remains of her defeated foes’ last supper.

He grimaced, wishing he could unsee the writhing mass of legs twitching in triumph.

Critter Taming is level 26!

“Do you know how to get there from here?”

She clacked twice and nudged his shoulder, directing to a section of the flat ground. A wide purple fissure extended from the ground, and beyond it he could see plenty more, all at varied angles.

“That curves around and reaches the same place?”

All the beetles clicked in confirmation, but Mishki sagged.

Not only would he have to go out of the way to round the edge of this tear in the ground, but beyond it, three more barriers stretched across the landscape, staggered at irregular intervals like fallen branches obstructing a path. Each one forced a winding detour, requiring him to navigate around one obstacle, only to double back and weave through the next, like switchbacks on a treacherous mountain trail.

“That will take forever to reach.” He didn’t know how long he had left to secure the egg, but he doubted it would be long.

The mother beetle lowered herself beside him, mandibles clicking. Images of cavern walls blurring as she charged past them ran through his head.

Mishki blinked. “You want me to—”

The beetle clicked again. He hesitated, but there was no other way. With a steadying breath, he climbed onto her back.

The beetles charged. Their powerful legs carried them at dizzying speeds, skimming dangerously close to the edge of the shearing void. Mishki gritted his teeth and held on to the mother’s two short horns, but his body kept rising and thumping against her spine. He would be sore after this, doubtless.

They encountered collapsed rock formations, but Mishki prodded her with the best route over them, and their unison leveled his Critter Taming skill all the way to 31.

At last, they arrived.

Bathed in a soft golden glow, the egg rested in the center of a clearing.

Beyond it was a broad patch of gray cave moss. Children and mother alike chirruped excitedly and began feasting on the vegetation.

Mishki, meanwhile, stared at the egg, then at his quest screen.

Two quests.

One to hand it over. One to protect it.

His gaze hardened.

“I might need your help with something else,” he told the beetles. “This could get dangerous, but I need to protect this egg from any possible danger. If you’re willing to help, I’ll come up with a plan.”

Their unified clacks made him smile.

With a resolute nod, he accepted the quest to save the egg.
