“The small rings with spiders on them have a single-use shot of webs. So give them to people with minimal close-combat abilities. Likely Cassidy, Linda, and Judah. The coin with an eye on it can be thrown for a minor illusion. The effect is random, so I’m not sure if it will actually be effective. I’d say just throw all of them at the same time to confuse an opponent.”

“Judah, this will likely be more helpful for you than me,” Alexander said as he unzipped his fanny pack.

“This is a weapon with the ability to talk to fish. They aren’t pleasant conversationalists, but after examining it closer, it does deal additional combat damage to magical beings,” Alexander said, proffering the trident to Judah.

He held his hands before him with his fingers spread. Wiggling his fingers around, he moved his hands up and down, mirroring each other.

“Fire.”

Then he made his hands look as if he was holding an invisible ball.

“Ball.”

Cassidy mimicked his hand motion and, without uttering a word, started to levitate.

“Because I am a Wizard,” Alexander whispered.

Brian explained, “From what we have gathered, Alexander is one of maybe half a dozen people on Earth who has such a wide range of abilities. Those abilities are compounded in power compared to all the other ability users… I shouldn’t have brought you into this. You may be more important than I initially thought. I was only thinking of my mission. I’m sorry.”

“Welcome to my hallowed pool. You will be safe here while we are away, I assure you.”

The Widow’s disposition switched quickly from welcoming to urgent. “Now let us be off to kill our quarry.”

Alexander and Johnny nodded at the rest of the group before they set off.

“Good luck, you two,” Brian said with a wave.

“We are ready, Widow of the Woods,” Johnny said, patting Bubba.

“Follow,” she said, turning on her roots and gliding forward out of the dell.

They marched for an hour through thick woods and roots. Trying their best to move quickly, Alexander put his headlamp back on to make sure they had sure footing.

Johnny whispered to Alexander, “You realize which direction we are heading?”

“Yes, we are almost going straight back the direction we came from.”

“You think whatever it is followed our trail? Maybe it’s not after the Widow, but after us?”

“An unsettling thought,” Alexander muttered.

After another half hour, the Widow stopped abruptly. Alexander and Johnny caught up shortly afterward and caught their breath.

The Widow turned to the side, looking into the woods. Turning to see what had caught her attention, Alexander heard a massive crackling as the sound of twisting branches reverberated in his ears. A dark hulking shape was moving toward them, and he moved back defensively.

“This is one of mine, no need to fear, magi,” the Widow said flatly.

As the figure neared, the shape of a large tree in the form of a man strode forward.

“Is that one of those things from the Rings movies?”

“I wouldn’t know…” Alexander paused.

He recognized the being. It was markedly different, but how different can you say one tree man is from another? This one was somehow darker… its bark was leaking sap as if cracked and bleeding. The eyes were dark and haunting.

As it approached the Widow, it bowed. “Ohhh, Widoooow of the Wooood. The ancient issss twooo leagues from thisss place. Near theeee Duuusk Mire,” it bellowed.

“Half a day’s travel… we make good time, magi. We shall continue and rest within a good striking distance,” the Widow decided.

“Therrrre are sooome who have taken toooo the ancient’s service. Seeinggg opportunity.”

The tree man looked at Johnny and squinted. It started to move toward him and reach out an arm.

“Stay away from my humans. They have nothing for the likes of you,” the Widow rebuked him.

“Widow of the Wood, may I ask what this being is called?” Alexander asked.

“These are the Bark Giants. They are barely adequate servants of low intellect. They are better shepherded, as they get odd notions when unattended.” The tree man scowled as she said this.

“Is the wolf also one of your servants?” Johnny asked. “It seemed more independent.”

The Widow rolled her eyes at the mention of Big Bad.

“That one is also ancient, but a flighty beast. It serves its own best interests, but it maintains a modicum of my goodwill. So I let it roam my lands freely, as it is a blabbermouth and tattletale. It shall do no harm to you and yours.”

Alexander’s eyes were locked on the Giant, and he switched his glasses to inspect it.
