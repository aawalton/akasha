The dragon walked alone into the deeper darkness.

Zaxius had not trained in over three centuries. Not truly trained, not pushed himself to the edge of his limits and beyond. The shackles that bound his power had made such efforts pointless. But things had changed.

Noah had freed him partially in the Astral Rift, loosening the bindings enough that Zax could feel his power stirring beneath the surface, waiting for the cracks to widen. The restraints would remain in place until he shattered them completely, but they were weaker now. Vulnerable. And Death Valley was the perfect place to test exactly how vulnerable.

He just needed to find the right opponents.

Zax could feel them before he saw them: a dozen presences coiled around each other in the crater’s center, a writhing mass of shadow and skeleton that hissed and screamed at his approach.

Bone Wyrms. Ranging from juveniles barely a hundred feet long to adults that rivaled his true shackled form in size. They coiled around each other protectively, a colony that had grown fat and numerous in a place where nothing challenged them.

Zax landed at the crater’s edge and smiled.

If he was going to make progress shattering these shackles, this was a perfect place to do it. He needed the kind of pressure that would either break the chains one by one or break him.

If he failed and the wyrms overwhelmed him, at least he would fall having fought as a dragon should, not wasting away as a prisoner in his own skin.

“Come then,” he said, spreading his wings wide. Lightning crackled across his scales, illuminating the twilight with harsh blue-white flashes. “Let us see what remains of us.”

The wyrms attacked as one, jaws snapping and tails whipping, death energy pouring from their forms in concentrated blasts.

Zax met them in kind. His claws tore through the first juvenile, shredding its spectral form before it could phase into shadow. Lightning erupted from his scales in all directions, striking three more wyrms and sending them reeling. He caught a death blast on his wing, the attack burning through scales and membrane, but the pain only sharpened his focus.

This was what he had been missing. The clarity of combat, of pushing against his limits and demanding more.

A larger wyrm wrapped around his leg, trying to drag him down. Zax kicked, snapping its spine with a wet crack that echoed across the crater. Then he brought his tail around to crush another that had been flanking him, the impact turning the wyrm’s skull into powder. His jaws closed on a third, teeth punching through bone and shadow alike as he bit it in half.

But more kept coming. The Elder Wyrm had joined the assault now, its attacks coordinated with the survivors in a pattern that showed they had pack intelligence. They moved like a single organism, each wyrm covering the others’ blind spots, their death beams weaving a web of destruction that left Zax with nowhere safe to stand.

One caught him across the back with a concentrated beam of death energy, and Zax felt the wound go deep—felt his regeneration struggling against the corruption. Another struck his wing, punching a hole through the membrane that would have grounded a lesser dragon.

The shackles tightened. They sensed his strain, his need for more power, and they responded by draining him further. “No,” Zax snarled, the word emerging as a thunderclap that shattered the surrounding stone.

He reached deeper. Past the restraints, past the pain, down to the core of what he was. Lightning erupted from Zax in a storm that consumed the entire crater, no longer a guided technique, just pure uncontrolled devastating power.

Wyrms that strayed too close disintegrating under the onslaught. The death energy itself was torn apart, dispersed by the overwhelming force of his storm.

When it ended, Zax stood alone in the crater. The nest was gone. Nothing remained but scorched stone and the slowly dissipating motes of destroyed undead.

And the shackles, Zax could see them now, truly see them for the first time since they’d been placed on him. Golden chains wrapped around his soul, forged from light and law and something that felt uncomfortably like divine authority. They had been created by someone who understood dragons, how to bind them, break them into something less than what they were.

The cracks that Noah had created in the Astral Rift, widened by today’s battles, each expenditure of power a stress on bindings insufficient to contain a dragon who refused to stop fighting.

He remembered what it felt like to be whole. Remembered the days when he could have shattered this entire valley with a thought. Remembered standing beside Ryugan, laughing at the forces that thought themselves worthy of facing a True Dragon and his Outerworlder companion.

Noah was not Ryugan. He was something new that even Zax, with all his centuries of experience, did not fully understand. The ‘Devourer’ aspect that lurked within the young Voidborne was troubling. The path he walked…

But that was precisely why Zax would help push him to his limits and beyond, and clear the path for him to reach whatever waited in Death Valley’s heart.

Zax reached for that wholeness, yearned for completion again, to be himself and not this half-asleep shell. He pushed against the cracks, strained his soul to its limits and, when it felt like he would shatter under the strain, kept pushing.

The shackles fought back. They always fought back. They drew on his own power to reinforce themselves, a cruel irony that had kept him bound for three hundred years, but damage was damage. Cracks could not be healed merely by adding more power.

He could feel his regeneration accelerating, his senses expanding, his very existence becoming more than it had been.

At the end of it all, when his vision danced with darkness and he had to release the attempt or risk passing out in the middle of hostile territory, the core restraints still held. Breaking them would require more than brute force, would require the right moment, the right catalyst. But the lesser bindings, the supplementary shackles that prevented him from even approaching the core binding, those were weakened to the point where another good push would shatter them.

Zax had time now. He had cleared the corridor to the inner depths and the path ahead was open. The dragon transformed back into his human shape, weariness overtaking him for a moment. He meditated where he was, standing in the crater, surrounded by the evidence of his destruction, and considered what came next as the valley’s mana rushed into him in an eager torrent.

He could sense the other creatures in the inner depths stirring, likely woken up by the commotion he’d caused.

Zaxius grinned ferally. Let them come.
