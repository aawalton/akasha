Alex picked up the Skill Shard, and gazed at the beautiful, Pure-colored three-dimensional sigil of mana within the crystal.

He pursed his lips as he inspected it.

“You can’t Identify it, if that’s what you’re wondering,” said a voice. Clarissa was walking over.

The ‘rift items can’t be identified’ thing also applies to Skill Shards?

That’s… kind of disappointing.

His disappointment lasted about a half-second.

“At least, you can’t identify it with Identify,” Clarissa said. She held out a hand.

Alex handed it over, bemused and also a little amused she didn’t ask, just held out her palm, which—fair enough.

Clarissa glanced at it up-close.

“It’s a Skill called Reflect, Common rarity, F-rank,” she said. “There’s a version of the Skill called ‘Mana Mirror.’ Reflect’s like a very thin Mana Shield, but significantly less sturdy—except it performs exceptionally well against energy-based Skills.”

“Which it reflects?”

“Correct.”

“How can you tell?” said Alex curiously.

“Well, there are hints.” Clarissa tapped the shard. “You can guess its rarity by its aura, and the Skill tells you what it is. Focus on it with your soul senses.”

Alex did, and—

“See?”

Alex did. The Skill structure itself has this reflective quality, kind of? Like a mirror.

He considered the Skill deeply.

“Word of advice?” said Clarissa, watching him.

“Sure.”

“Sell it,” she said simply.

“Why?” He was intrigued by Reflect, but it also did kind of seem like a worse Mana Shield with a few specialized perks—it might be useful in close-range, where it’ll probably be pretty hard to get out of the way if your skill bounces right back at you.

But unless there was an excellent reason, he wasn’t keen to use two slots on similar Skills. And he wasn’t sure ‘surprise close-range reflect’ as a use case was sufficient, honestly. Even if he was kind of fascinated by the idea of reflection as an aspect of Pure mana.

“Well, besides whether the Skill actually suits you, you realize you can’t take it in,” said Clarissa. “It needs to be refurbished, like all rift-delved Skills and enchants. Which means you’ll need to take it to a Skillscribe, which adds a slight cost. Second—”

She tapped the shard again. “Mana Mirror’s a fighting Skill,” she said. “Reflect’s a Skill best used as an enchant.”

“Like, on gear?” said Alex; he knew the question was a little silly, because what else would enchants go on? Except it was clearly a Skill Shard.

“Again, Skillscribe,” Clarissa said. “All enchanting is is scribing magic into a piece of gear. The only difference between enchanting a sword to enhance ice Skills, and enchanting that sword with the ability to produce an ice arc Skill, if you pour in mana, is a huge amount of complexity.”

She cut herself off. “We don’t need to get into the complexities,” she said quickly. “Essentially, a large part of a Skillscribe’s job is transferring Skills from shards, like this, into gear.”

“Like a shield,” said Alex, understanding. “So all you’d have to do is pour in the right type of mana—Pure, in this case—and your Shield would Reflect anything coming at it. Which you’d want anyway, rather than, like, trying to reflect something with your body, if you were using it like your own Skill? Thanks for the patience, by the way.”

By his first impression of her, he kind of would’ve assumed she’d rolled her eyes at him for asking. She was much less—for lack of a better word, princess-y, than he’d first assumed.

Beckett was also much less full of himself than he’d seemed online, which—what did he even say that was that bad? Alex honestly couldn’t recall.

The common denominator is me. He winced. I could be a little less quick to assume.

“You shouldn’t expect to find a Skill shard every rift,” Clarissa said, ignoring his thanks. She turned away. “Or this kind of boss drop, actually. You two were lucky.”

She went back to the rest of the party, and after Alex collected his essence stones, he did the same.

Clarissa’s words also made him begin to wonder about how much the contents of his now pretty darn full Interspatial Ring would sell for. He didn’t want to get too excited, since the Skill Shard was still Common rarity. And he was pretty certain the boss’s glacier-like sword was enchanted as well; he’d felt a lingering signature of ice mana when he’d picked it up.

It’s just as well, if I have to make up—what is it, over a million? In a few weeks’ time, if I want a high rarity Class Card for the next rank.

Which... did put a slight dampener on things.

I guess it doesn’t have to be a few weeks? I can delay the breakthrough a bit, if I have to, but—yeah.

The Rising Stars cup is in a few days, against the Guild talents, but that doesn’t pay that that much, if I’m remembering right.

Realistically, I’m probably still going to have to find a different, really lucrative source of money, before very long.

Should be pretty easy.

A million in a few weeks.

Even if I delve as much as I can and get insanely lucky, surely just delving isn’t going to be enough?

A second.

Okay, but surely loans for this kind of thing aren’t that uncommon.

Alex didn’t have much in the way of assets or credit history, but—I am an Awakened with a pretty convincing talent card. He cringed a little thinking the words ‘convincing talent card,’ but he wasn’t above flexing it at a bank if he had to. Once he was deeper into E-rank, he assumed he’d be able to pay it back pretty quickly?

Let’s keep it as a ‘break glass in case of emergency,’ Alex decided.

He wasn’t the biggest fan of putting himself in seven-figure debt, but he supposed it wasn’t actually that risky, in his position.

And then there were Guilds. Which, at least right now, felt like the real ‘break glass in case of emergency.’

Alex wasn’t sure why he was so against the idea. Maybe it was because he was still clinging onto this his initial feeling—waking up in this new world, feeling like his future was all blue sky, and he had all the choice in the world. If he wanted to devote hours to exploring a Skill, or v-Dueling, or delving rifts, or exploring and seeing what’d changed in this new world, seeing what was out there, or simply spending hours scrolling Awakened social media, he had the luxury of freedom. After a few days, his thinking felt a little clearer; it was definitely time and freedom of choice he cared about, moreso than whatever treasure split he’d have to give up to a potential Guild.

Although he did care about treasure.

When he returned, he chatted a little with Beckett, and also Alice and Jason, which was a little awkward.

“How were the evaluations?” Alex asked.

“Pretty fucked,” Jason said with a weak smile. “And that was before we saw you two. If that’s what it takes as a Level 5—I guess my ass never had a chance.”

Clarissa seemed to want to say something at that, but her eyes flickered to Beckett and Alex, and she refrained.

“How aren’t you in a Guild?” said Alice wonderingly.

“It just isn’t my thing,” said Alex. He wasn’t sure he wanted to explain everything.

“It isn’t your thing?” Clarissa looked bemused.

Alex supposed he’d have to explain his thinking.

Clarissa listened in silence.

“I’m sympathetic to liking your independence,” said Clarissa finally. “But that’s a naive worldview. You can’t reach your full potential by yourself, and even in this rift you must’ve realized how useful it is to have teammates at your level.”

“I mean, I get that,” said Alex.

“Alex, I’ll just say it,” she said, looking him in the eyes. “You could be world class. But this idea that you can move through this world without taking on any responsibilities, and interacting with it only when you want—it’s a fantasy.”

“I’m not saying it isn’t,” Alex protested. “I get that—”

“Alex, listen.” Clarissa seemed to feel quite strongly about this, and Alex got the feeling it wasn’t entirely about him. “I’m not just talking about all the resources you’ll need, and you’ll need a lot. You’re introverted. You want to keep to yourself. Is that right?”

Alex smiled a bit. “Yeah, you got me.”

“The reality is, publicity’s unavoidable,” Clarissa said. “If you want to succeed. There’s power to a person’s image. For instance, getting selected for Tower expeditions, which comprise dozens of top talents from across the world, is a huge opportunity. And it’s very difficult to get selected—most of the slots are partitioned among elite Guilds, and if you want to enter as a freelancer, you’ll still need to win your spot in an invitation-only selections competition. Just to be invited, you’ll have to be well-known. See what I’m getting at?”

Alex was quiet as she continued.

“It’s politics, and optics, as well as actual merit that determine how high you’ll rise. That’s reality,” she said simply. “Whatever merit you have, it has to be visible. This world isn’t perfectly meritocratic. I’m sure even in your world, that wasn’t the case. If you can’t seize chances you will not be the best, because someone who has as high a talent level, who works as hard, will take the chance you’re giving up. You’re not the exception.”

She crossed her arms. “If that’s all fine with you, fine. But just realize what you’re saying.”

“Thanks for the advice,” said Alex, honestly. “I’ll think about it.”

“And I’m not just saying that because I’m—well, I’m Clarissa St. Clair,” she said. A few seconds. “I can’t tell you, if you want to join Echelon, it’s guaranteed,” she said, handing him a golden token. “But if you’re interested, you can contact us.”

“Okay.”

“I’m not saying it’s the right choice for you,” she said quickly. “If—in your old world, we’re akin to the Princeton of Guilds. But there’s others of equal class. You might like them better, and that’s fine. Just—you need to accept the reality of this world, and be realistic. That’s it. Alex, we all have to make concessions to what we want, that’s life.”

He was kind of surprised she didn’t try to sell him harder on her Guild; her attitude seemed almost indifferent to his choice.

It was also rather interesting talking to her, because she seemed the kind of person that was so self-assured when she said things, she was quite convincing; it felt like you couldn’t even argue, because what she said was simply reality. It was kind of had to parse reality from the way she spoke—he did think she was likely pretty accurate; at least, it all had the ring of truth.

“I’ll keep it all in mind,” said Alex finally. “Obviously, you understand the world much better than me. I’ll… need to think about what I want, I think. You’re right on that.“

Maybe I will need to change?

“As long as we’re clear,” she said.

“Beckett,” said Alex, turning—he was also still kind of thinking about his immediate money issues, and the wealth of treasure in his ring right now.

“What’s up?”

“I’ll need your contact info,” he said, pulling out his phone—which couldn’t connect to the internet, but could still take offline notes. “I’ll sell all this at the Delver’s Association and send you your share, which will be—pretty much as soon as we get out of here.”

“You’re sure about this?” said Beckett. A tiny part of Alex was pained at the idea, but it was rightfully Beckett’s, and he’d make it up anyway.

“I’m sure.”

“How about this?” said Beckett, grinning a bit. “Visit me in New York City sometime, after you’ve hit E-rank. I’ll guest pass you into the facilities. We’ll use my share as a wager in a duel, for all the marbles.”

“I realize what you’re doing,” said Alex, amused.

“Alex, just accept it,” said Beckett.

“If we’re in E-rank we’ll be able to afford a lot more,” said Alex. “Quintuple it.”

“Sure,” said Beckett. “Multiply it by ten if you want. As long as you have the money.”

Alex’s lips quirked. “Is that how it is?”

“Boys,” Clarissa interjected. “If you’re healed enough to talk like this, you’re healed enough to walk.”

A few minutes later, they were back in the real world, beneath a real blue sky.

Alex could faintly see a Labyrinth glittering in the far distance, running a perpendicular track to the sun’s.

Alex watched as Jason and Alice went over to their parents, who were already waiting their excitedly, in a Mercedes and a Porsche; Alex was pretty sure the two of them were both from families with Awakened in them.

“Training season starts next week for me,” said Beckett, also watching the two. He glanced at Alex. “I’ll have to prep for Interguilds—that’s this ranked talents league between the New York City Guilds.”

Alex knew about it; it was a televised thing, and decently watched, to his understanding.

Beckett licked his lips. “I guess things are starting to heat up.”

“Yeah…” Alex had his own duels against talents to think about. He did wonder if any would be at Beckett’s level.

Well—it’s all F-rank, so they could be up to Level 10, and their gear’s probably still going to be way better than mine.

“Alex,” said Beckett suddenly. “I’m going to be the best in the world.”

Alex paused.

“I’m going to get on the World Cups circuit, I’m going to clear Towers, and I’m going to be in a mission to a Labyrinth. And after that, whatever’s out there in space. I’m going to stand at the peak of the world, and I’m going to see what’s out there, at the far horizons of the world.”

Beckett gave a little grin. “Just wanted to let you know.”

“That’s your takeaway from the rift we just went through?” Alex was amused. “You just got shit-kicked, you said it.”

“Yeah, well—” Beckett flushed a little, but it didn’t wipe away his grin. “You know what’s weird, I want it even more now?  Fuck that knight! I want to run it back a thousand times, and kill it a thousand times. What I mean is—Alex, what will you be?”

Alex considered it.

He wasn’t sure he had a deep desire to reach the ‘peak,’ whatever that was.

But he did very much like magic, and he seemed quite talented at it. And he was pretty darn keen to see just how far he could push it. And he was also curious about these Towers, and Labyrinths, and the tech they held; it seemed pretty clear mankind was barely scratching the surface of all this. He mostly wanted to follow his interests, and explore.

“Claire would kill me if she heard me say that,” said Beckett.

“How about this,” said Alex, amused. So long as we’re joking. “If you’ll reach the top of this world, I’ll meet you there.”

“Deal,” said Beckett brightly, and the two shook on it—and Alex had a feeling even Beckett was feeling how ridiculous what they were saying was, even if the guy really did have ambitions like that.

A few minutes later, a limousine pulled up.

“What’s your socials, by the way?” said Beckett, tilting his head.

Alex was going to say, I’ll give you my phone, but at this point—Fuck it.

He typed it out for Beckett.

“Awesome. That’s—”

Beckett paused.

A silence.

“Your driver’s waiting,” Alex said helpfully. “We’ll talk another time.”

“You—!”

“Beckett!” It was Clarissa.

“That’s why you were like that,” said Beckett. He looked bemused. “I can’t believe you’re a real person.“

“Thanks,” said Alex.

Clarissa got out of the limousine, arms crossed.

“You’d better train,” said Beckett, laughing. “Really freaking hard, when you visit, because—I swear I’m going to virtually kill you.”

“I’m sure you’ll try.”

“You freaking—”

With that, the two bade farewell.

And while Beckett left to pack up for the glitz and glamor of the big city, Alex was taking his own ride to Stark City’s Delver’s Association.

It was time to cash in—and also finally fill out his build a bit with a proper gear spending spree.
