The spire’s proximity alarm clanged from somewhere far below, and Vir need only glance at the small army of demons approaching the building to see what it was howling about.

He broke the embrace with his family, giving Maiya a look over the top of Siya’s head. She wiped her face with the back of her hand and nodded.

“Siya?” Maiya said. “I’m really sorry to do this, but we have to go. I’m afraid the realm is in a lot of chaos right now, and Vir and I have responsibilities to attend to.”

“You are leaders,” Siya  said. “That much was obvious, even without you saying so. You are a Raja?” She looked at Vir, then shifted her gaze to Maiya. “And you, his Rajni?”

“I am,” Maiya replied. “Though I also play a governing role in the human realm.”

Siya straightened and wiped her face clean with the back of her hand. “Then go. I am not so small that I need minding. I shall await your return.”

“How about you come with us instead?”

It was not Vir who voiced the thought, but Maiya. “Wouldn’t you like to see what the world has become?” she asked the girl. “And besides… it would be awfully lonely here on this spire, yeah? Maybe I’m wrong, but… I think you’ve had enough loneliness for a lifetime, yeah?”

Vir could only shake his head in wonder. Maiya had always been better with children than he had, as fond of them as he was. Her motherly affection, however, was simply something he couldn’t hope to compete against.

Siya’s eyes bounced between them, and when she gave a nod, Vir’s respect for the girl only increased. She was so little and yet so strong. Not one tantrum thrown or petulant remark. Vir had dealt with adults less mature than this little lady.

Vir nudged Maiya toward the roof’s edge and crouched in front of Siya. “Arms around my neck. Hold on tight, okay?”

Siya complied without question, and Vir stood back up with her perched securely atop his shoulders before sweeping Maiya up his arms.

Then he stepped off the spire’s roof.

Siya’s scream split the air, her fingers white-knuckled against his armor. But when she realized their rate of descent wasn’t anywhere even close to what it ought to have been, the scream cut off and Vir felt her go still above him. When he glanced up, he found her eyes wide open, taking in the dead city before them in silence.

Vir took the opportunity to do the same, though where before Mahādi had felt like a mausoleum full of terrors, under the bright sunlight, it now looked very much like a city waiting to be reborn. To be restored to its former glory.

Who knew? Perhaps it would be. Perhaps it would become a capital of some new country, yet to be founded? Perhaps even one in which humans and demons coexisted in harmony.

They landed at the base of the spire and soon discovered that the group waiting for them was not the standard Asura detachment Vir had expected.

Cirayus stood at the front, flanked by his generals Balagra, Malik, Nayan, with Princess Ira to his right and Riyan to his left. Behind them stood Greesha and a detachment of Asura, standing on what had, until just moments ago, been the most lethal terrain in any realm.

Though Vir strove for the lightest touch possible when playing God, he’d taken the liberty of placing them all here when he transferred their souls.

Better to have the leaders in one place than scattered across a world that had just changed shape beneath their feet.

Cirayus looked at him for a long moment. “Is it true, lad? Are we saved?”

The Ravager rarely sounded like a scared child, but in that moment, that was exactly how Cirayus sounded, his quivering voice containing none of the confidence that defined the ancient demon.

A pang of pain shot through Vir as the prana core pressed against its cage, but Vir forced himself to ignore it, smiling back instead in an attempt to appear as reassuring as possible.

“We are, old friend. Our journey was long and full of peril, but it was a success.”

Cirayus exhaled through his nose, the closest the old demon ever came to visible relief. “And Janak? What of our god?”

Beside Vir, Siya clutched Maiya’s skirt.

Vir shook his head slowly.

Cirayus went quiet for a long moment, clearly taken aback. “He died well,” he said at length.

Another moment of silence passed between the group before Cirayus turned his attention to the small girl beside Maiya. “And who might this little lady be?”

“Everyone,” Vir said, taking a deep breath, “please meet Janak’s daughter, Siya.” He looked down at her. “Siya? These are my friends and the leaders of the world.”

The ramifications of Vir’s words took a moment to sink into the group. That this was the Siya, after whom the Altani had named a major city. A living survivor of the Age of Gods.

To Vir’s surprise, it wasn’t Cirayus or even Princess Ira who greeted Siya, but Greesha, who rushed forward to drop into a crouch in front of the little girl.

The terrifying old woman produced a wrapped sweet from somewhere inside her coat and held it out with a smile only grandmothers could pull off.

“It is a pleasure to meet you, young Siya. Gods know they’re not perfect, but you could’ve picked worse people to accompany.”

Siya studied it for a moment before gingerly reaching out to take the candy, smiling bashfully. “T-thank you, erm…”

“Greesha,” the seer said. “But you can call me grandma.”

“Bah,” Cirayus guffawed. “The last time someone called you that, they went mute from the terror you struck into their hearts!”

That got a laugh out of everyone, and Vir was happy to see Siya join in. Her recovery would be neither short nor painless, but he swore to do everything in his not inconsiderable power to ensure she lived the best life he could possibly give her.

Nayan, Malik, and Balagra each offered their own versions of welcome. Riyan alone said nothing, standing back from the group stroking his beard, his eyes on the girl with an expression Vir couldn’t read.

“Vir, Maiya?” Ira said. “I believe I speak for everyone when I say that what you’ve accomplished is beyond words.” She hesitated a moment, clearing her throat before adding, “though I confess I have my questions.”

“This is why I’m here,” Vir said. “Ask away.”

“How, er, did we end up in the Ash? One moment I was attending a meeting in the castle, and the next I was standing here, in the midst of Mahādi.” She gestured at the landscape around them. “And I notice a distinct absence of Ash Tears. Or lethal doses of prana.”

“There won’t be any more Tears,” Vir said. “After an entire Age, the three realms have been made one again. As for your locations, I took some liberties in bringing you here. Now that we no longer have Janak to create Ash Gates for us, I wanted the leaders of the world all in one place.”

He frowned as he said the words. He had the memories of Ash Gates clearly. Had stepped through and stabilized hundreds of them.

He understood in principle why they no longer existed—the reunification of the realms had removed the seams they’d bridged—and yet the absence felt strange to him, as though he were forgetting something crucial.

“The three realms are truly one now?” Ira asked. “I do not mean to question your words, but…”

“It is not so easily believed, I know,” Vir replied with a smile. “I assure you, we are now one realm, with a level of prana consistent throughout. What you feel here is what everyone from the Voidlands to Samar Patag will feel. Slightly above what the realms have known in the past, but not so great as to cause problems for humans or demons.”

He left out the rest. That every soul in the new realm had been transferred by his own hand. That this wasn’t a reunification of the old realms but the creation of an entirely new one, copied faithfully from the old. That detail served no one right now.

Ira’s brow furrowed. “The Pagan Order will have strong feelings about this. I fear the sudden abundance of prana in their lands will spark the dissolution of existing territorial and spiritual boundaries.”

“Undoubtedly,” Vir replied. “But it is nothing we cannot smooth over. Fear not, I do not intend to remain idle in all of this. I will help facilitate cooperation wherever I am able.”

“That is a relief, I admit,” Ira replied. “Of most pressing concern right now is for every leader of the realm to address their people. To communicate that this is not some catastrophe. That there is nothing to panic over.”

“Agreed,” Vir said. “Balagra?”

Balagra stepped forward. “We have fast attack airships standing by, Akh Nara. Though I’ll need to know how to navigate this new configuration.”

“Demon lands are to the east,” Vir said, gesturing with his arms. “Mahādi lies at the very center of the realm, with the human lands to the west. Use that as your baseline until better maps are drawn.”

“Then I shall set out immediately,” Ira said.

“As shall I.” Riyan stepped forward and lowered his head toward Vir, a gesture he offered to very few people and which Vir had never once been given. “Thank you. To think the whelps I saved that day so long ago would be the ones to save us all. I, and all of humanity, are forever in your debt.”

Vir coughed awkwardly. “I’m, er, simply glad it succeeded.”

Even if it did cost Janak everything, he thought. Even if all the lives lost to the Ash Tears would never be restored.

“I’ll arrange for the Rajas to convene at Samar Patag,” Cirayus said. “You’ll want to address them directly, I imagine?”

“As soon as possible,” Vir replied.

Maiya squeezed his arm. “I should go with Ira. Help break the news to the human realm before things spiral out of control. You’d be shocked at the human tendency to panic. It’s just…”

“You’re worried about what they might do if you are not close to me.”

Maiya nodded, biting her lip.

“From what I saw of the tapestry, it is more a matter of how closely tied you are to me in Fate. Physical interactions help, of course, but you should be safe for now.”

“We can hope,” Maiya replied gingerly. “With their attention now fully on our universe, who can really say?”

“Janak at least promised this time would be different. That the seeds they laid to rip open Ash Tears had taken thousands of years of effort. That we would be safe from them.”

“Sure,” Maiya said with a shrug. “But that doesn’t stop them from doing other things, and we both know how many agents they still have.”

“We’ll regroup as soon as we have things in hand,” Vir promised. “And I want you to take the wolves. I know how strong you are, but it would make me happy if you did.”

“Alright,” Maiya said with a small smile. “Guess that’s the best we can do.” She retrieved a communications crystal from her coat and pressed it into his palm. “Stay in touch.”

Then she crouched in front of Siya, took her hands, and looked her in the eyes. “I know this is rather sudden, but would you rather accompany Vir? Or myself?”

Siya glanced between them, before fixating on Vir’s chest. A gesture likely only he and Maiya noticed.

“You’re in good hands,” Maiya said with a smile. “He’s insufferable, but he means well.”

“Hey now!” Vir quipped. “You shouldn’t teach her bad things!”

Siya looked at Maiya seriously, but she quickly broke down. “Oh, I know,” she said, giggling. “Daddy was the same.”

Maiya kissed the top of her head before joining Ira.

He looked down at Siya, offering his hand. “Well. Shall we?”

Siya glanced at the assembled demons and at the vast landscape of a strange, new world stretching out in every direction. At the sky above Mahādi where the sun shone brightly overhead without a single particle of Ash to mar it.

“We shall,” she said, slipping her fingers into his.
