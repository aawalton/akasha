The area Lora had teleported everyone to was very similar to the one they just left. There was a beach, a road, and twin houses that were made with larger folk like minotaurs in mind. The differences were clear, though.

An overcast sky loomed above them, obscuring the sun though they could still tell that its position had changed, giving them a couple more hours of daylight as they moved eastward. The trees looked more like pine than the tropical fare of Paladona, and the ocean waves were choppy.

To Trevor’s surprise, there weren’t any boats on the water. There was nothing but an empty stretch all the way towards the horizon. The only things he could see were clouds, islands, and waves. His eyes focused on signs saying not to go swimming.

“Thanks for the teleport, Lora,” he said as he retrieved Clay’s husk and Adabelle’s magical belongings. His familiar was quick to bury themselves in the sand again.

“You’re welcome!” she replied, all smiles.

Trevor pointed towards the water. “Seems kind of empty, doesn’t it? I know the sun’s not out, but there should at least be fishing boats, right?”

Lora suddenly became serious. “There would be fishing boats, yes,” she said, dramatically lowering her voice. “If this wasn’t the most dangerous patch of ocean in the world.”

“Is that so?” he asked, arching an eyebrow. “Even more dangerous than the weird sea triangle? You know, the Sea of Strife.”

“Oh, I haven’t thought about the Sea of Strife in ages,” Adabelle said, looking over the water thoughtfully. “That’s the area between the Floating Islands, Zeeel, and Kabalari that the system actively warns people away from, right?”

“Yeah, Cindy told me all about it,” he confirmed. “Well, as much as she could. The Quest Master had to tell me itself that there needed to be enough high-leveled Summoned to even go in there. It seems like they must not have had a big enough group yet since it’s still so mysterious.”

“I honestly haven’t heard of anyone taking an expedition out there, and I certainly would have,” the noblewoman replied.

“You definitely would have been one of the first people they called,” Trevor agreed as he wrapped an arm around her shoulders and pulled her close. “I don’t think you could sword fight what might potentially be an island turtle, but I imagine you can do other things to it.”

“Definitely,” Adabelle said. “But, Trevor?”

“Yeah?”

“Lora is pouting.”

He turned to see the woman was, indeed, pouting. The serious look she had put on was nowhere to be seen. “You ruined my drama,” she complained.

“My bad,” he said with an apologetic smile. “You mentioned something about the most dangerous patch of ocean in the world?”

“I did!” she answered, snapping back immediately. “Okay, it’s no Sea of Strife, but the main cause of concern is jellyfish.”

“Jellyfish,” he repeated.

“Jellyfish,” Lora confirmed with a nod. “Specifically, Jerr’s Jellyfish, named after the man who found them. There’s whole knots of them out there, and they’re omnivores. Now, what you might have heard is, 'oh, hey, they eat both animals and vegetation.' No, that’s wrong. When they say omni, what they really mean is all. They eat everything.”

Trevor glanced at Adabelle, and she offered him a thin smile. “All joking aside, they’re an invasive species introduced to kill another invasive species, and ended up doing well enough that there’s almost no stopping them now.”

“And they eat anything,” he said incredulously.

“They’re known for taking bites out of ships both wooden and metal,” Lora said, lowering her voice like she was telling a ghost story. “It’s easiest to tell where they are when it’s dark out because they glow. First, from the tips of their long, wiggling tentacles, but then it goes all the way up to their crown of teeth.”

“Jellyfish… don’t have teeth,” Trevor stated.

“These ones do, obviously,” the teleporter said, giving him a look. “Just one of them will put a hole in your boat, but a big enough group will sink it fast and you’ll have a really bad time. The good news is that they generally take a while to digest things that aren’t meant to be eaten, like metal, so most people could get away on a life boat if they were fast enough.”

The courier didn’t miss the way that was phrased. “Could?”

Lora nodded. “Yup. Now there’s too many for that old trick to work.”

“Wow,” Trevor chuckled as he scratched the back of his neck. “That’s kind of horrifying, actually.”

“We have our own terrifying things in Lucent, you know,” Adabelle told him as she patted his arm. “I think all worlds have that side of things. While not much of a bright side, they were surprisingly helpful during the war.”

“On account of the demons having to come from another continent?” Trevor asked.

“Correct!” Lora answered. “That’s what really drove airship technology forward, though. We use them to travel back and forth between Aletander and other countries on Taele, which is the name of their continent.”

“Huh. War does breed innovation. It sucks, but a lot of changes happened in my world because of a few big events,” the courier said absently, thinking back. He shook his head to clear it before fixing his gaze on Lora. “There’s no problem now, right? Jackson had told me something about a peace treaty, I think, but it’s been a long time.”

“Oh, definitely,” the teleporter replied, waving a hand as if to banish the thought. “It's been well over two hundred years since the last time a battle had raged, and both sides are pretty well over it now. Humans come and go, demons come and go, and everyone else comes and goes. I’m not telling you that people aren’t going to look at you funny, because that can happen anywhere, but the boss and I wouldn’t send you somewhere that would actively harm you.”

“Like dungeons,” Trevor said with a dark chuckle.

“You can’t lump in dungeons with potentially volatile civilizations,” Adabelle chastised lightly. “Either way, it’s a moot point. I’m not going to let anything happen to you in either.”

“My hero,” he swooned, leaning in to kiss her on the cheek. After, he pulled away from his wife and stretched. “Alright, so. Do I have anything to drop off here before we get to the airship?”

“Right, yes,” Lora said, nodding as she started walking towards one of the buildings. “Pick up and delivery. Not much, just some things that they need over in Aletander. Like vaccines from things we can only make over here, and other medicines. While the demons have become more technologically advanced, hence the airships, we’re still ahead of them when it comes to pure magic and alchemy.”

“Alright, sounds good,” Trevor said as he and Adabelle followed.

They were led to a storage space on the side of one of the buildings and opened it to find a cool room with several boxes stacked inside. Taking out the packed talismans at Lora’s direction, he started cycling out his inventory.

“We really are going to get quite a few quests out of this” Adabelle remarked as she received the text box as well. While she wasn’t the one carrying the goods, she was the one escorting the courier.

“Only the best for Trevor and Lady Adabelle,” Lora said, trying to sound like Klar before laughing. “But yeah, the boss wanted to show off that he’ll absolutely take care of your man for you if he ever travels far away. Good accommodations, good food, good quests.”

“Yes, Klarkartar has certainly proven time and again that he’s far more generous than the other merchant princes I’ve met,” the noblewoman replied.

“You’ve met the others?” Trevor asked as Lora ushered them out of the storage space and down the road. He sent a quick pulse to Clay that they were leaving, and he felt the earth elemental begin to follow. “The most I’ve seen of them is a bunch of statues depicting the Altabrae family.”

“I’ve only met two others, though Altabrae the 5th was one of them,” Adabelle said. “Him and merchant prince Habzol. It was during a summit held in Kabalari, and I had been invited to go as Jackson’s apprentice. They were… cold, to say the least. The Isle of Horns, up until that very meeting, had been intolerant of Summoned. They thought that summoning people was destroying the fabric of reality, but it was proven that we actually make this world stronger.”

“Wait, really? We do that?”

She nodded. “It’s true. The ambient mana has risen since summonings became more popular, which makes spells and magical item creation easier. Their only other defense was a cultural one. Since Lucent was spearheading the change, they specifically pointed out that our country could lose its history and identity by switching out kings from other worlds every five years, but the rest of the nations at the summit were already on board. We’ve also gone almost a century as a thriving country since the revolution, so they’re wrong on that count, too.”

“The merchant princes are kinda like the gateway into the Isle of Horns,” Lora continued as they walked up the hill. “They’re the first officials to see the rest of the world, and they report back to the homeland. Most of them are generational title holders, but not the big boss himself. He’s made his fortune with the help of the Summoned, and has grown to become a very prominent member of minotauran society. Only a couple other princes have kinda jumped on his bandwagon, because something something generational wealth and there’s this new kid on the block, but they don’t want to change because they’re still kind of stuck in the old ways.”

“A tale as old as time,” Trevor sighed.

“A tale that transcends worlds, unfortunately,” Adabelle added as she slipped her arm around her husband’s. “It seems as though this conversation has gotten a little heavy. Could you tell us about the airship we’ll be traveling on, Lora?”

“Could I?” the teleporter asked in return. “I sure can! Just need to reach the top of this hill and I’ll tell you all about it.”

The energetic woman wore a smile as they neared the top. It hadn’t been too far, but she had clearly wanted them to see the view.

They crested the hill to see a cove far below. A large fence blocked off the water from the ocean, leaving it rather clear and jellyfish free. There was a town situated on top of the cliff that they were now on. A large, well-paved road led deeper into the mainland, and there were three docks set up high above the water.

Two airships were anchored to the cliff, and Trevor excitedly patted Adabelle’s arm when he saw them. They both had large balloons keeping them from falling into the water below. The closest one appeared to be some kind of cargo ship with propellers on either side and a wooden hull attached by ropes. Boxes and barrels were being loaded onto it. It appeared old, but it had been maintained well.

The second was painted red and gold with two large wings coming out of the bottom. Its bottom half was surrounded by glass, though it looked like there was only wood on the other side. The balloon on this one was obscured by the hull, giving it a sleeker look than its cargo counterpart, and had propellers over top and in the back. There was an open section both in front of and behind what looked like the bridge. It was also much larger than the other. Over three times the size by Trevor’s estimate.

His view of the dock was obscured, but he could see people waiting to board. Despite how big it was, it didn’t seem like it would be filled to capacity unless there were more arrivals on the way.

“The Red Dream,” Lora announced, gesturing wildly towards the airships. “This is the fastest airborne vessel this side of the Darel Ocean. It’ll get you across in just two half days and two full days' travel while allowing you to rest in quiet solitude. Your suite has everything you’ll need including someone who can fetch food and drink for you. If what you want to do is stay away from people and enjoy the boundless ocean—and each other—then that is absolutely doable.

“If that doesn’t strike your fancy or if you want to meet new folks, then feel free to hang out on the deck. As you can see, there’s a pretty prominent open-air section. That's the most popular area, and there will be live music, dancing, games, and more there as well. The point of the Red Dream is to ferry people across the ocean in style, and it does that quite well if I say so myself. Are there any questions?”

Trevor looked down as Clay emerged from the hill and shook themselves off. They clambered up his pants and shirt to sit on his shoulder. That prompted him to consider his clothes.

“Are we well dressed enough to hobnob with the other passengers?” he asked as he stroked his familiar’s chin.

“Pfft, who cares what other people think so long as you’re comfortable? You do you, boo,” Lora replied with a snort. “There’s no need for anyone to get snobby with you. I know you two have some nice duds in that inventory of yours, but just in case there’s a few choice selections in your wardrobe if you want to try something new.”

“Then I suppose the only question is, when can we board?” Adabelle asked.

Lora grinned. “As soon as we get down there. So, how about now?”

“Let’s do it.”
