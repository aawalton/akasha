Alyssa watched as her chosen tree fell, then slumped off to the side to catch her breath. Making lumber entirely by hand was... hard.

It was genuinely difficult to find trees she could work with. Not because trees were rare - she was literally in a jungle - but because pretty much any tree that stood out was some enormous old growth behemoth with a trunk thicker than she was tall. Even if nothing was quite on the scale of that one yggdrasillic tree she'd seen exactly once and somehow never found again despite the fact it was five times as big as any of its neighbors... which were themselves a hundred feet tall at least, that still didn't make them workable. And for those trees which weren't large enough to carve a house out of the trunk if she was dedicated enough, most of them were really spindly and thin and noodly and absolutely useless for the kinds of construction she was trying to do.

Oh, and because of how much foliage and everything there was, she could barely see more than ten feet in any direction unless she was in one of the rockier patches that didn't have as many trees in it. In which case she could see a whole five feet into where the trees were, because no trees just meant more vines and more shrubs. At least [Leafstep] was practically designed for traversing forests, so she could just walk along and between the various hanging vines to stay off the true forest floor.

She'd made certain to [Flamewind]-scorch most of the area immediately around the crack in the rock which led to The Geode and more generally The Underground, but it was both impractical and a bad idea to do so more indiscriminately.

But just finding a suitable tree was only step one. Now she needed to hew it into actual structural timbers and boards, such that she could turn the little section of cave she had identified into an actual away-base. She wasn't planning on doing anything too complicated or too big, certainly nothing that would require multiple trees worth of lumber, and she was immensely glad for it. She only had four Strength right now, and lumberjacking was intense work.

She'd figured out a way to tie ⟨Blowback⟩ and ⟨Tumbling⟩ into a bit of a Warrior's Stance by the end of it, and had been cleaving through the tough wood with relative ease, but that had only happened after a fairly long and exhausting process of chopping down a tree in a more mundane manner.

Once she'd recovered enough, she quickly ran back to her main camp to drink what felt like a gallon of water, then got down to the actual business of turning a felled tree into lumber.

The first step was simple enough, with her newfound tree-chopping prowess, in that she took off the branches and top of the tree where it started to bend, and then squared off each end of the log with her axe. At that point, she removed the bark in a long line from the base to the crown and marked a straight line down its length by taking a long length of twine, coating all of it in charcoal dust, fastening it at either end of the log such that it stayed under a bit of tension, and then pulling it up near the middle and allowing it to snap down.

Typically, that was done with chalk, but charcoal worked well enough.

With her line marked, Alyssa stashed the twine away and began the direct work of splitting the tree. While it was tricky to do as only one person, she used a hammer and her woodcutting axe to cut a vertical line along the base of the tree, such that if she were cutting firewood the log would have split in half. Then she repeated the process running up the tree trunk, following her marked line as she held the axe in place, tapped it in place with her hammer, and once it was no longer wobbling, driving the axe in more deeply with more solid hits. She didn't drive the axe all the way in, but instead constrained her efforts such that she was just making a long crack across the entire tree's length.

Once she was about halfway up the log, she relocated to the top and repeated the process - scoring a line across the round, then creating a crack along the charcoal-marked line - until the two ends met up in the middle.

At that point, she switched to widening the crack. She had a handful of wedges - basically small unsharpened axe heads - from Oliver, and she drove two into the endgrain, then spread the rest of them along the length of the log. She migrated up and down its length, carefully driving them in as evenly as she could. Only once she had all of them in place did she start to lean more on her magic.

She started with the wedge halfway down the log's length.

Feet planted shoulder-width apart slightly off-axis from her actual shoulders. Mana flowing from the ground beneath up into her feet, through her torso, and out her hands. A touch of ⟨Illuminate⟩ threaded into her hammer, making its handle glow and more importantly guiding other currents of mana out of her person and into her tool. Deep breath one, deep breath two. Mutter some Magespeech words about logs and trees until some of the chaotic whorls of magic spinning around in the log became more guided and stable. Then practice swing one, practice swing two, ready set and-

CRACK

The wedge fell into a massive rend running down the length of the log. It didn't fall completely in half, but it was most of the way there.

Dang that felt good.

She repeated the process at other points along the log until the entire log spit along its length, giving her two half logs to work with. From each of those half logs, Alyssa used a similar technique - driving wedges along a straight line down the entire length of the log, then driving the grain apart with a combination of steel, magic, and muscle - to create a large plank as wide as her forearm and easily a hundred feet long out of her two pieces.

Those proved light enough for Alyssa to carry, though it was still a bit of an ordeal to navigate the tangling foliage... and the board wound up being longer than the clearing she'd made, meaning trying to set it down was its own adventure, and somehow doing it a second time was just as hard as the first.

For what was left of her half logs, Alyssa repeated her initial log-splitting process of debarking along a strip, marking a line, and using her axe, wedges, and her hammer to turn a 'half' log into a quarter log. The two quartered logs were also light enough for Alyssa to carry, but they were a lot floppier than the planks had been, and she wound up breaking one in transport... that wasn't ideal, but she wasn't actually going to be using the full lengths so it was definitely fine.

The final 'half' log got broken down into more planks, and by the time Alyssa had finished doing that she was running on nothing but the base desire to get her current task done before she slept.

She technically had a prepared bed, but she sat down on a bit of soft leaf litter and was unconscious before she even strung two thoughts together.

Just as suddenly, something wet nuzzled her face, and her eyes shot open to see a mossmane sniffing her and nosing through her hair. She suppressed a tired scream, but didn't hide her general jerky wake-up well enough to not spook the enormous buffalo-like creature, and its blue, mossy mane writhed in surprise, it skittered backwards, and then lowered its head and began to charge.

Its sheer mass broke some of her so carefully cut lumber, but Alyssa only spared a single indignant thought for its disregard of her hard work, before she was far more preoccupied with not being trampled and/or gored by the creature's horns.

⟨Leaf on the Wind⟩ and ⟨Springlaunch⟩ combined such that when the territorial but tasty creature rammed into her torso, Alyssa was sent literally flying. She reoriented herself in midair, redirecting her trajectory such that she collided with a nearby tree trunk feet-first, bent her knees to absorb the impact, then to launch herself back at the beast. She led with her shoulder, and struck it with as much of a ⟨Blowback⟩ as she could muster.

It was like she hit it with a truck.

Bones crunched, the mossmane toppled over, sliding a few feet as it did and Alyssa landed on her hands and knees.

"My combat axe," she ⟨Gather⟩ed, and as soon as a weapon was in her hand she put the creature out of its misery.

Alyssa yawned, looking at the dead creature with a raised eyebrow. "You know, I don't know how much I really meant to do that, but that's what happens when you interrupt my sleep."

She rubbed her eyes to get some of the sleep out of it, her body still somewhat coming down from the immediate adrenaline high and stuck between wanting more sleep and being way too awake to possibly ever sleep for possibly the rest of her life.

That feeling lasted long just enough to get to her actual sleeping spot - a roughly bunk-sized indent in the stone not far from the inlet to The Underground that she'd collected some softer scraps and balled up her cloak to serve as a pillow after clearing out all the spiders and dust and dead bugs with some judicious [Flamewind] usage - and lay down there.

Alyssa had no idea how long she actually slept for - even back at First Tower, they didn't really have a dedicated timekeeping device, the clockwork hearts were in too much demand for that - but it felt like it had been a really long time, because she felt absolutely and totally rested. Even her mana was rejuvenated and ready, the currents of the... stone and earth around her resonating with her soul?

That didn't seem right. Her soul was Wood, Force, and Air. Being asleep underground in a tiny nook in the rock should have been practically the opposite of where those elements should abound, but she could still feel a gentle influx of mana from her surroundings, just like she'd long attributed to the abundance of all her elements in The Jungle and the world more generally.

Huh.

Alyssa got out of her little bunk, stretched, and put her hand on the stone. Dark, Earth, Shadow, and a mix of other less-prevalent elements, same as every time. Unlike further below, there wasn't much Water mana, and that officially left Earth as the only element in any amount of abundance which might have some kind of positive synergy with any of her elements, and yet... she felt it feeding her anyway.

"Well," she spoke to the darkness, "I suppose this probably counts as something weird going on, magically speaking, with landscapes and me."

She liked the idea that this connection to the world she was feeling was based in her new magic from the Jump. Sure, effectively having a crazy boost to her mana Generation wasn't as flashy as Henrietta's Inklings, but...

Alyssa entered a Warrior's Stance, truly trying to feel the mana she was pulling through her body. After a few minutes, she stopped with a sigh. Even if her mana-siphoning was actually some kind of unique magical power, she had no baseline for what a Stance should feel like without its presence. But say she did have some kind of enhanced connection to the world around her, and could pull on its mana to fuel herself, that certainly felt like the kind of thing that would help with Stances as Jacob described them a lot.

Plus there would be other stuff.

She was having trouble thinking of that other stuff, but being able to siphon mana from her surroundings definitely would have lots of applications. Maybe she could turn it into some kind of dispelling magic? That could be fun.

Then her stomach grumbled and she became aware of how parched her mouth was.

She would ponder this development more after breakfast.

Once she had food in her stomach, Alyssa got back to work on her constructions. She'd just gotten a very nosy reminder as to why having an actual shelter was important, and beyond that she needed to clear out the dust in the zero-gravity cavern, and generally clear a proper path between there, the entrance, and The Geode. That included widening the entrance to The Underground a bit more - while she and Henrietta had moved the largest boulders that had previously been blocking it out of the way as they extracted the extra-large crystal that had become the Factory traincar's Network Cache - as well as chipping out a few of the other tight squeezes, clearing out a few stalactites and stalagmites, that kind of thing.

But for now, she was still building. Making a more enclosed area wasn't crazy hard, especially since she didn't need to worry about everything being perfect. Clark's ability to tidy up any sloppy cuts was a tremendous time saver in basically every way.

But the first stage was simply drying out the lumber she had cut. She and Oliver had developed a variant of her spell to instantly dry and fire pottery into something that could instantly dry lumber. It was actually easier for her than the 'original,' mostly because being a [Ranger of Far Lands] meant having Wood affinity imbued into her soul, and that made magic with wood... well, a lot easier.

What does doing magic feel like for Oliver then, given he has Arcane? she mused. "[Ignite] fire, burn dry. Transform held wood."

Something failed, and Alyssa scowled at the piece of still-wet wood. She repeated the steps more slowly, and that failed as well. She got the impression that it failed because she did it slowly. So she cast again, this time faster. That time, she got the feeling of the spell spinning out of her grip and flying away. A nearby twig burst into flames.

She tried again, and that left a blackened handprint on the surface of the lumber.

The next time, it suddenly did work... about halfway through her casting.

If it works it works, I guess?

Alyssa moved on to the next piece, getting that one a bit faster. The third one caught half on fire. The fourth worked first try. The fifth never worked, and she moved on to the sixth in frustration. That one had its grain turn charcoal-black and the lighter parts of the wood dry perfectly.

The seventh wound up with a rune Alyssa recognized as being fire-related left everywhere her fingertips had been touching it. The eighth was much shorter than the others, being the part of a board broken off by the mossmane, and the broken edge caught on fire and burned fairly vigorously until she managed to extinguish it.

With her lumber finally prepared, Alyssa was then able to create their room in the cavern. She'd chosen a corner fairly close to the entrance but which wouldn't fully obstruct the passageway, and cut off a length of quarter-log that was about as long as the cavern was tall. She cleaned out the rock, put the post in place, and then hammered a couple pieces of wood underneath the post, holding it in place through pressure alone.

She repeated that process twice more, giving her a total of three corner posts to work with, then nailed her first boards in place. As she did the nailing, she adjusted the exact rotation of her quarter log columns to make sure their flat face was properly in line with the boards themselves, then once it was lined up properly, wedged even more pieces of wood at the bottom to hold it in that much tighter. As she then kept nailing more of the boards in place, Alyssa realized that about fifteen feet was a bit too long for an otherwise unsupported wall - the other wall was half that - and decided to make that where her door would go.

To accommodate that, she set up two more posts using what was left of her quarter logs - the cave ceiling was only about ten feet high at that point, so she'd used hardly any of her two hundred odd feet feet of posts - this time, using her existing wall as a strong guideline for where her posts should go. That did initially place one of them at an inconvenient high point, rather than in a slight divot in the floor like she preferred, but that was remedied with a few minutes with a hammer and chisel.

By the time the posts were in place, it was time to sleep. But the next morning, she finished up the walls and moved to the floor, because the cave floor was rather wet and damp and that did not make for a good storage situation. So instead, Alyssa used up most of her remaining boards to elevate the floor of their cave room just a few inches off the ground, supported by her remaining quarter-log lengths.

That still left the ceiling exposed, and that was a bit wet, but Alyssa bathed it with as much [Flamewind] as she could without risking lighting the walls on fire to dry it out. But with that completed, Alyssa felt like their base was good enough. Bare, yes, but clean, smelling of fresh-cut wood, and with two walls of wood and two of bare Jungle rock. They'd probably need to carve a hearth out and make a chimney to make it truly 'homey' in the magical sense, but for now... Home sweet home, I guess.
