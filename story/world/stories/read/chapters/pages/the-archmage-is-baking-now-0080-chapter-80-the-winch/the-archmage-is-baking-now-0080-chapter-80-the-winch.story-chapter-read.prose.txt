The town square was already filling up when she arrived. Today was the craftsmen's showcase, one of the last major exhibitions before the royal family's visit wound down. Tables had been set up in a wide arc, similar to the baker's showcase but with more space between each station to allow for the bigger, bulkier items on display.

She spotted Allen near the middle of the arc, arranging. The winch was there, perhaps the biggest item in the exhibition. On a table next to it sat the puzzle box he had mentioned, along with several other items she didn't recognize. Jane waved at him from a distance, not wanting to interrupt. He saw her and waved back as she made her way to him..

"Good luck," she said. "Everything looks good."

"Thanks. I think I'm ready. Or as ready as I'm going to be."

"Do you need help with anything?"

"No, I'm fine. This one's a bit different from your showcase. Less frantic. All I have to do is stand here and explain things when the king comes by.”

"That does sound easier."

"Stay and watch, if you have time. I'd like to have a friendly face in the crowd."

"I wouldn't miss it."

The showcase began with the usual formalities. Xand emerged from the meeting hall with his amplification device and announced the royal family's arrival. The king, queen, and prince took their places on the raised platform, looking interested but exhausted. The rapid-fire events seemed to be taking their toll on them, despite the fact that Jane had never actually seen them get bored during one.

This time, instead of the royal family sampling each exhibit directly, the amplification device was passed from craftsman to craftsman. Each one had a few minutes to explain their work. It wasn’t long before Allen’s turn came. Jane knew he didn't love speaking to crowds, but he managed it well enough. His voice carried clearly across the square as he described each item on his table.

"This is a winch system," he said, gesturing at the machine. "It was built to haul contaminated barrels out of the lake during the water crisis last year. It was used alongside the archmage's magical intervention to help clean up the contamination, and performed acceptably if not perfectly."

A murmur ran through the crowd at the mention of the dragon situation. Jane saw several people turn to look at her, and she did her best to look like she wasn't involved. Allen continued, describing the puzzle box and the precision tools, but the winch had clearly been the main attention-getter. When he finished and handed the device to the next exhibitor, a small cheer went up from the crowd.

Allen looked embarrassed by the attention, but Jane was glad for him anyway.. He had earned this. The winch really had been crucial to solving the lake problem, even if he hadn't been the one doing the magical heavy lifting. She was proud of him.

The exhibition continued for another hour. There were dozens of contributors, each one with their own specialty and their own story to tell. Jane found herself genuinely interested in most of them. She hadn't known what went into making glass or a truly precise furniture joint. Learning it was fun, even if it wasn’t directly applicable to anything she’d find herself doing.

By the time the last exhibitor finished speaking, the sun was well up and the crowd had grown to fill most of the square. The king conferred briefly with his family and the town officials, and then Xand stepped forward with the amplification device one final time.

"Citizens of Glenfall," he announced. "The royal family has made their selections for the craftsmen's showcase."

The crowd went quiet.

"In first place, for her extraordinary clockwork mechanisms…”

So Allen hadn’t won, after all. Applause erupted as the clockwork woman stepped forward to accept her recognition. Jane clapped along with everyone else. The woman's work had been remarkable, after all. She deserved the win.

"In second place, for his innovative winch design and precision toolwork, Allen!"

Jane's heart swelled as Allen stepped forward, looking pleased but uncomfortable with the attention. She pushed through the crowd toward him as the announcements continued..

"Congratulations," she said when she reached him. "Second place. That's wonderful."

"It's a good result." Allen accepted her hug. "I feel a little guilty about it, though."

"Why?"

"There are guys here who build better winches than I do." He glanced at his display. "But I got to use mine on a dragon. It feels like cheating, a bit. Like I'm getting credit for being in the right place at the right time."

Jane shook her head firmly. "That's not how it works. You built something that worked when it needed to work. You solved a real problem. That's what making things is about."

Allen was quiet for a moment, considering. Then he laughed softly.

"That's the same advice I'd give you," he said. "If you were in my position. I suppose I have to take it."

"You do." Jane hugged him again, properly this time. "I'm proud of you."

"Thank you." He held her close for a moment before stepping back. "Now, what about you? You said you were going on a trip?"

"I am." Jane pulled the letter from her pocket, now well-creased from repeated handling. "The author of my cookbook. She's a few towns over, and she's agreed to meet me. I'm leaving today."

"Today?"

"My aunt convinced me not to wait."

“She’s usually right about things." He looked at Jane and frowned slightly. I can't come with you. I have too many orders backed up, and people are going to want to talk to me after this. Unless you need me. If you need me, I'll figure something out."

Jane smiled at him. "I'm fine alone on this one. I have a feeling that's the way to go. This is something I need to do by myself, I think. It wouldn't feel right to bring an audience."

"I understand. Just find a way to let me know if you need me. Send word somehow. I'll come running."

"I'll be back in a few days. Try not to get into too much trouble while I'm gone."

"No promises." He echoed her own words from the day before, and they both laughed.

Jane left him to his admirers and made her way out of the square. She had a carriage to find.

—

The edge of town was a different world from the lake-facing center where Jane spent most of her time. The buildings were larger and more utilitarian, more in the family of barns, warehouses, and depots than small retail shops and housing. Storage structures lined the streets, and the horse traffic that was so rare in the center of the city began to become much more common.

Jane walked slowly, taking in the sights. She had been down here a few times before, mostly on errands that required her to pick up supplies that couldn't be found in the regular market. The area had always felt foreign to her, a place within her town that wasn’t truly for her. Today, though, she really did need it.

The first few warehouses she passed were clearly not what she needed. They were filled with heavy wagons meant to carry crates and barrels instead of passengers. She saw workers loading and unloading, foremen taking inventory, and that sort of thing. These were not people-moving endeavors, and she moved on from them quickly.

She continued down the street, passing wagon repair outfits and stables for horses and heavy oxen both. The street carried her onward, curving slightly as it followed the natural contour of the hill behind it. As Jane rounded the bend, she finally spotted something more promising. A smaller building stood apart from the warehouses, just a shed really. It held just enough room for one carriage and a single horse stall.

The horse in the stall was beautiful. It was fast, sturdy-looking animal with a gleaming coat. It stood still as an older man ran a brush along its flank, occasionally tossing its head as if impatient to be out and about against..

Behind the horse, parked in the shade of the building, was a carriage. It wasn’t for carrying large amounts of goods. It almost couldn’t have done that. The wheels were thin, the axles light, and the overall conveyance had as few frills as possible while still being capable of carrying passengers..

Jane approached, and the man looked up from his work. He was older than she had expected a man in this line of work to be, but his expression was friendly, and it was clear that his horse trusted him absolutely. Jane found herself biased towards doing the same.

"Good morning," he said. "Can I help you?"

"I hope so." Jane gestured at the carriage. "I'm looking for transport. I need to get to a town about two days from here. Willowbrook."

"I know it. Small place, off the main roads. Not much call to go there, usually." He patted the horse's neck. "That's close enough to walk, you know. If you needed to. Plenty of people do."

"I know. I've walked distances like that before." Jane thought of her blistered feet on the trip to Trailor, and the relief of having Dave's carriage for the return journey. "I'd rather not do it again if I can help it."

"Fair enough." The man moved to the carriage and began checking it over. "Shorter trips aren't much cheaper than longer ones, though. Most of the cost is in the preparation, not the distance."

"I understand."

He named a price. It was substantial, but not insane.

"That's a lot of money for a few days of travel," he said. "Are you sure you want to spend that much?"

"It's a good expense for me," Jane said. "I can afford it."

She wouldn’t be paying it, anyway. The king would, whether he knew it or not. As an archmage, Jane had access to an effectively unlimited budget, funded by the crown itself. The stipend appeared in her accounts regularly, far more than she could ever spend on her simple life as a baker. She used almost none of it, preferring to live on what her shop actually earned.

But the money was there for a reason, and archmages were encouraged to travel. Moving around the kingdom meant more areas subjected to advanced magical senses, which meant more chances to catch big problems before they became even bigger. A mage who stayed in one place was useful only to that place, but a mage who traveled was a protection to every town they passed. The crown funded archmage travel because it was good policy, not charity, which left Jane guilt-free about taking full advantage of her budget for once..

"Actually," Jane said, "how much would it cost to cover everything? Food, snacks, whatever you'd recommend to make the trip easier. I don't want to worry about any of the details."

The man had probably dealt with wealthy customers before, and it showed in his lack of surprise. He considered for a moment, running through some mental calculations before naming an even higher figure and a long list of conveniences it would buy.

"Do that," Jane said. "All of it."

"I’ll do that, then. When do you want to leave?"

"As soon as possible. Today, if you can manage it."

"I can manage it. My son handles most of the runs these days. But for a solo passenger on a quick trip like this, I can make an exception. I've been wanting an excuse to take this girl out for a longer walk, lately." He patted the horse again. "She gets restless if she doesn't go on trips.”

Jane watched him disappear into the building, presumably to gather some of the supplies he had mentioned. She found a spot to sit on a low wall nearby and waited. Five minutes later, the man emerged from the building carrying a large canvas bag. He loaded it into a compartment under the carriage seat, then returned for a second bag, this one softer and presumably containing the blanket and cushion he had mentioned. That went into the carriage itself.

"Give me a few minutes to hitch up. Then we can be off."

“So soon? I thought you’d gather supplies first.”

“We can get the rest of what we need on the way. Don’t worry, miss. I have it handled.”

When everything was ready, the man opened the carriage door and gestured for Jane to climb in. The interior was small, with lightly cushioned seats and a cover that could be lifted up for shelter if the weather turned. She left it down, for now.

"Comfortable?" the man asked.

"Yes. Thank you."

"Good." He closed the door and moved to the driver's seat at the front of the carriage. A moment later, Jane felt the vehicle begin to move, the motion smooth and easy as the horse started forward.
