After getting their currency exchanged at the ground floor of their hotel, Trevor and Adabelle received a pair of city passes and stepped outside onto the streets of Grafam. They were filled with people, reminding the courier of Eskretet during the Festival of Golden Lights, though the wide roads made it seem like everything was built with this kind of traffic in mind.

Demons of all shapes, sizes, and hues moved before them. Some were as big as minotaurs while others were as small as gnomes. Trevor could tell they weren’t children, either. Those still had their horns growing in, the same of which couldn’t be said for the adults. He received a few stares from those not used to seeing humans, but he was too busy looking at the city to notice much.

Grafam was lit up with neon lights on nearly every other storefront. It was almost as if he were walking through his home back on Earth again. Everything tried to grab his attention at once, and he moved slowly so that he could take in the signs.

Trevor had learned to read Demonic long before his visit thanks to the Language Fish and Klar’s collection of orbs. They were useless to the minotaur after the magic had imparted its knowledge into his mind to keep, so he had no problem passing it on.

The language of merchants was something that was prevalent back home, but hadn’t caught on here. Aletander was so large that nearly everyone on the continent spoke Demonic, even if it was something they picked up second-hand. It had been changing with more constant trade across the ocean, but it would take a long time before it became popular like it had in tourist destinations such as Paladona.

“Is that a line of cabs?” Trevor asked, more to himself than anyone.

Adabelle, however, had fantastic hearing and heard him easily over the crowd. “Cabs?”

He nodded towards a line of wracor-drawn open-air carriages. They were waiting patiently, their drivers talking with each other nearby. The only marking on the vehicles was a pair of blue horizontal lines inside a yellow circle.

Trevor slowed down to watch a couple walk up to the one in the lead and show the driver something that looked a lot like their city pass. They got in, and they were soon off. The ones in the line behind it moved forward.

“Yeah, I think it is,” the courier said. Adabelle’s tail tugged his wrist gently, and he gave her an apologetic smile. “Sorry. Cabs were wagons for hire inside a city. They’d take you where you need to go for a fee. Setting up right outside of hotels, airports, and popular bars got them more customers since they wouldn’t need to call.”

“Would you like to go, then?” she asked.

Trevor looked at the demonkin. “Take a cab through the city?”

“Yes. If the drivers know the streets well enough to take people where they need to go, then I must assume they have all sorts of knowledge about where the best sights are. We can see more while heading to the concert hall.”

“While I appreciate that you wanted me to know your motives, you had me at yes. Let’s take a cab.”

Adabelle led Trevor in the carriage’s direction. She had him by the hand and the wrist, not wanting to get separated in the crowd. Just like the couple she had seen before, the noblewoman approached the front of the line.

The man standing next to the cab was tall, muscular, and had thick horns that framed his face. His sleeve-less tunic had the same symbol over his heart that was prominent on all of the vehicles. He was in the middle of brushing down the wracor he had hitched when he noticed them coming. The driver looked, blinked, and performed a double take at Trevor.

“Good afternoon,” Adabelle greeted. She must not have liked the way the man was looking at her husband, because she stepped slightly in front of him. “Are you currently for hire?”

“Oh, yeah, I am. Hello,” the cab’s driver replied, raising his hand in greeting. “Sorry, didn’t mean to stare.”

“It’s okay,” Trevor said. He moved to stand next to Adabelle as he reached up to stroke Clay beneath their chin. The earth elemental on his shoulder leaned into the affectionate touch. “This is my familiar, and they’re not really common anywhere.”

The driver glanced at Adabelle, who smiled at her husband’s obliviousness. “They’re an earth elemental,” she explained.

“Well, that sure is something,” the driver said, scratching his neck. “Where’re ya heading?”

“We need to be at the Sephie Dahs Concert Hall by four, but that leaves us with a few hours of free time,” the noblewoman answered. “We were hoping that we might have you, or one of your fellows, take us that way. This is our first time in Aletander, so any sights you think are worth it in between would be welcome.”

“Okay, yeah,” he replied with a smile. “A silver will get you to the Arts District, which is where the Sephie Dahs is, and they’ve got a big show going on that spans quite a few blocks.”

“That sounds pretty fun,” Trevor said, turning towards Adabelle. “I think we’ve found something to do.”

“I believe so. We do have one of those city passes, though. Will coins still be necessary?”

The driver shook his head. “Nope, the pass’ll do. I’ll just have to scan it.”

“Do you have yours, dear?”

“Sure do,” the courier replied. He summoned the city pass into his hand before offering it to the driver.

He took it, but his eyes were wide. “How’d you do that?”

Trevor smiled as he pulled up his sleeve and revealed his tattoo. “Summoned. I’ve got an extradimensional space attached to me.”

“Ah,” the driver said, momentarily dumbfounded.

“Is everything all right?” Adabelle asked, looping her arm around Trevor’s.

“Yeah, sure, yup,” he replied before taking the card. The driver removed a small stone from his pocket, ran it along the pass, then handed it back after waiting a moment. “Accepted. Sorry, I think you’re going to be my first human and my first Summoned and my first elemental. It’s turning out to be a very interesting day.”

“No worries,” the courier said with a smile, not minding one bit.

The driver opened the door, and Trevor helped his wife up onto the step so she could take a seat. Clay jumped off of their master’s shoulder to lay on her lap, their golden whiskers twitching. The courier joined them as their driver stepped up onto the coach box. Some of the other cabs had passed them as they got fares, and they wasted no time getting the wracor moving.

“The name’s Sharn, by the way,” the driver said as their noble animal began plodding along. “Sorry again for the staring. You’re just already very exotic, and then you’re also a Summoned? Took me a moment to get my bearings.”

“Literally no problems, man,” Trevor replied. He glanced at Adabelle with a grin. “You hear that, love? I’m exotic.”

“Oh, you’re something, all right,” she said teasingly as she patted his leg. “I’m Adabelle. This is my husband, Trevor, and his familiar, Clay. It’s a pleasure to meet you, Sharn.”

“The pleasure’s all mine,” he replied with a wave as he merged with the traffic. It appeared as though the trolley line was meant for all other vehicles as well, because that’s what they followed. “So, what brings you to the great city of Grafam?”

“We’re on our honeymoon,” Trevor bragged as he put an arm around his wife’s shoulder. “Crossed the ocean to get here, and it was well worth it. So far, your city’s beautiful.”

Sharn sat up a little straighter at the praise. “I’m glad to hear it. There’s no place I’d rather be than here.”

“Have you traveled often?” Adabelle asked.

“Well, no, not really,” he reluctantly admitted. “Sometimes I get the occasional fare that takes me out to nearby settlements, one time as far out as Slater Port, but other than that I don’t get around a lot. Still, I know what I like, and what I like is city living.”

“It’s the opposite for me,” Trevor said as he watched the people as they went by. “Spent my whole life living in cities or, at the very least, on the outskirts of them. When I came here—the world not Aletander—I landed in a nice little place of a few thousand people, tops. It’s so quiet and peaceful there. Places like this are nice to visit but it would take a lot for me to live in one again.”

“Eh, to each their own,” Sharn replied with a shrug. “I like that it’s active here. There’s always something going on and I can make a living, you know?”

“Yeah, I hear you. I’m not knocking it or anything, I’m just saying it’s good to expand those horizons. You’ve got a pretty nice carriage here; I’m sure you could do some long distance hauls, either people or cargo.”

“That does sound nice, but unfortunately both it and Old Doog up here belong to the company, Double Dash,” he said, turning to show off the logo on his chest and giving it a tap.

“Double Dash, you say,” Trevor repeated, trying not to seem amused as he thought about a certain racing game.

“Yes, sir,” Sharn confirmed as he shifted back into his seat. “I’m working on getting my own cart and wracor, though. I should be able to go independent if I save up for a few more years. Got some obligations here in Grafam, but I just might expand my horizons, as you said.”

“Everyone should travel for the experience, even if you don’t go far,” Adabelle responded. “The next city over could be completely different from what you’re used to, and interacting with people who see things from another perspective can help open your mind in ways you’ve never thought possible before.”

“That makes a lot of sense.”

Trevor smiled as his wife dispensed wisdom. She was watching the people go by, taking in the sights. Her hand idly stroked Clay on her lap as she lost herself in this new place. It didn’t last long, though, and she turned her head when she sensed his gaze.

“You’re missing the city,” Adabelle told him.

“Nuh uh,” he said. “I can see it in your eyes.”

It took her a moment to register what he said, and an exasperated smile appeared on her face. She leaned in and kissed her husband gently, ignoring the sea of people around them. Most weren’t paying the newlyweds any mind, anyway.

“I was a little surprised to see that the cabs here weren’t motorized,” Trevor said to Sharn once his wife rested her head against his shoulder. “I was told there were cars, and I kind of figured something similar would be the norm here.”

The driver shrugged. “They’re expensive. And, yes, more expensive than taking care of wracors. There’s a lot of expertise you need to have to become a mechanic and there are only so many of them. One of the things I want when I go independent is something with a motor, though. That’s when the big fares come out. Sometimes, they even call for you specifically.”

“Alright, yeah, that makes a lot of sense,” he replied with a nod. “I imagine something like a limousine would get you a lot of interest. Might be a bit hard to drive here in the street, but sometimes that’s exactly the kind of attention-grabbing big wigs like to do.”

“What’s a limousine?” Sharn asked curiously.

Trevor chuckled. “Guess that’s not a thing here yet, huh? Alright, so it’s like this.”

As the courier explained overly long vehicles, the change between districts came rather abruptly. All it took was passing through one intersection and both Trevor and Adabelle could tell that they were in the Arts District.

The roads here were painted with the main thoroughfare a dazzling green. All adjacent streets were a different color ranging from reds to yellows to blues before cycling through again. Whereas the buildings they had seen on the way here were mostly uniform in construction and color, with a few standouts, every one in this district had its own flair.

In a way, it almost reminded Trevor of the settlement around the Grandmother Tree in the Feywood. Not because they lived inside plants, but because of how individualistic each dwelling or storefront was. He did a double take of his own as he saw a pink fairycore cottage complete with a weeping willow growing through the middle next to what could have easily been a miniature Gothic cathedral complete with bell tower.

It wasn’t just the buildings, but the civil works, too. The benches were colorful and appeared to be made of odd materials. He was pretty sure one was made completely of glass, and another was a double-decker complete with a ladder to reach the higher seating.

The sound of wind chimes and music were prevalent in the area, too, though he couldn’t tell right away due to the amount of people. A melody came over the din every so often, almost like it was haunting him. It wasn’t unpleasant, far from it, but it left him searching for the source. Adabelle caught his confused look and pointed upwards.

Every so often there would be a series of tubes aligned in a strange way on a rooftop, and Trevor frowned at them before realizing that the wind was playing them as it went through the pipes. That wasn’t something he had seen before.

It wasn’t long after that Sharn stopped Old Doog and turned around. The streets ahead of them were set up with lines of booths, each holding something inside that immediately reminded Trevor of robots. It was blocked off from everything other than foot traffic.

“This is as far as I can take you,” the driver said. “But if you head down this road, take a left on Hazelwood, and then a right on Oakridge, you’ll get to the Sephie Dahs Concert Hall. Since you’ve got some hours, I took you a little bit out of the way. Figured this would get you some optimal exploring time while still getting you where you need to go, and some of the artisanal bread places down Hazelwood are to die for. I recommend We Rise, but anyplace is good.”

“This is fine by me,” Trevor said as he started to get up. His eyes were locked onto the closest booth. “I’m especially interested in these robots.”

“You know what they are?” Sharn asked, surprised.

“Oh yeah. Robots are cool. Do they move?”

“Some will, but most are concept pieces,” he answered. “But you’ll discover that soon enough. You two have fun and enjoy your concert, alright?”

“We will, and thank you,” Adabelle replied as she cradled Clay in her arms and stepped down from the carriage.

Trevor joined her and smiled at the driver once he had his feet firmly on the ground. “Hey, Sharn.”

“Yeah?” he asked.

“Thanks for the ride,” he said before flipping a gold coin to the driver. “Good luck going independent!”

The courier knew that tipping culture wasn’t prevalent in this world, but he felt like the guy would put it to good use. With a wave, he put a hand on Adabelle’s back and started leading her towards the robots as Sharn thanked him profusely from the carriage.
