The sky tore open without warning.

High above the first layer, right above Eiyuria, space fractured like stressed glass. A seam split open the air, crackling outward. Light bent under the unstable pressure.

Two figures stepped through.

Jowlaw landed first, his feet touching down on empty air, as if there were something solid there. He adjusted his stance and stretched as he descended, arms raised above his head in an exaggerated yawn.

“Well,” he said pleasantly, before his face turned slightly sour. His feet touched the ground as his gaze swept the area. “This place could use a landscaper or two. Is this how pigs on the first layer live?”

Livira followed through the portal a second later. She, too descended, landing elegantly on her heels that touched upturned soil. Her gaze was already moving, measuring and categorizing everything she saw; noting some things, dismissing others.

They were at some kind of mansion. An estate of a noble by the looks of it. However, the entire massive front courtyard was ruined. Holes, destroyed earth, soil scattered everywhere as if an explosion had set off in the center.

Something had happened here, that much was obvious.

“She was here,” Livira said.

Jowlaw clasped his hands behind his back and leaned forward, peering toward the ruined manor with open curiosity. “Oh, absolutely. You can almost taste it. Sloppy, though. Whoever it was didn’t bother cleaning up. A good performer should really clean up after their messes.”

They both walked forward. That was when Jowlaw noticed the pit.

He crouched, tilting his head and peered over.

“Hm…?”

Two bodies lay within the shallow grave, stacked without care. One was a flesh sack in clothing—a dead human. The other was a man with completely white eyes. They had been thrown in here haphazardly, almost with disrespect and angst.

In front of the hole was a small wooden sign.

“Here lies Amberdean…” Jowlaw murmured. “Godsworn who took the children.”

He leaned back, finger under his chin. “Huh. Well ain’t that amusing.”

Livira approached, gaze flicking only once toward the human corpse before settling on the other. Her expression didn’t change, but something in her mana field shifted. She could feel the power emanating around them. It felt off. Much purer than regular mana.

“A Godsworn,” she said. “Looks like he fought our target and died.”

Jowlaw sighed. “That really ruins the symmetry of things. It should’ve been us to do everything. This would’ve been the perfect mission. We win, they always lose.”

He rose to his feet, hands on his thighs for extra support. “So. Target is gone. Godsworn guy is dead. The place is a mess. And there’s no trace’a’them.”

Livira closed her eyes for a brief moment, extending her mana field, searching for traces of mana usage. Her eyes opened as she looked over to the far right, near the edge of the town where the forest connected. There was a large pool of residual mana lingering there. A spell—a big one had been used.

“They’ve… already left the layer,” she concluded. If it was anything that large, it must’ve been some type of escape spell. That type of magic usage wouldn’t have been needed to kill such a weak Godsworn.

Jowlaw sighed dramatically. “I hate being late.”

Before either of them could say more, the air above the courtyard tightened. Pressure descended like a held breath under water.

Livira’s head snapped up first.

“Oh,” Jowlaw said lightly, following her gaze. “That’s… not ideal. They’re already here.”

A rift opened in the sky—not crude like theirs, but much more controlled and refined. Light folded inward, forming a clean aperture as two figures descended through it.

Vezavok emerged first.

His massive draconid form forced the air aside as he descended slowly, wings unfurling in a single, powerful beat that sent a shockwave rippling across the ruined courtyard. His scales glimmered like heated metal, embers drifting lazily from the edges of his wings.

Lumina followed.

She stepped out of the rift as if walking down invisible stairs. Light clung to her form in refracted layers, jewelry and artifacts catching and bending it until her silhouette seemed to shimmer, its edges hard to pin down. Her expression was calm. Focused. Nearly bored.

The rift sealed behind them without a sound.

For a moment, no one moved.

Livira straightened, her posture tightening, mana circulating just beneath her skin. Jowlaw’s playful, lazy slouch vanished. He rolled his back and stretched subtly as he shifted his weight, eyes sharp now, calculating distances and exits.

“Took you long enough,” Jowlaw said. His tone was light, but it was missing its usual jester feel. “We were starting to think you’d gotten lost back there.”

Vezavok’s slitted eyes swept past them toward the shallow grave. He spotted two bodies.

“A Godsworn corpse, and a random nobody?” he rumbled.

Unlike the dragon’s keen eyes, Lumina couldn’t see it from afar. “I’m assuming you two weren’t the cause of this.”

Jowlaw shook his head. “Nope. Not us. The place was already this fun and lively when we got here.”

Next to Livira’s belt, a small crystal rumbled. She reached down and picked it up.

Veraine’s voice came through, ethereal and echoing. “How is the target?”

Livira shook her head. “She was here. But she’s gone now. Most likely she’s already left the layer—either ascended or teleported to another layer or realm.”

Jowlaw shrugged. “Girl’s gone, boss. ’Less you can still track her down.”

“No. That is enough,” Veraine ordered through the crystal. “The evidence of her power is already sufficient. You two may retreat. I have my own plans to lure her out.”

The air rippled above them.

Light gathered around Lumina, compressing into something dense. The temperature didn’t rise. The shadows didn’t flee. Reality itself seemed to narrow, as if focusing.

Livira reacted instantly. She set the communication crystal back on her belt and began to act.

Seals bloomed around her in layered formation—hexagonal plates of interlocking runes snapping into place one after another, forming a barrier thick with authority and counterforce.

Jowlaw’s eyes widened just a fraction.

“Oh dear,” he muttered. “Liv—”

Lumina simply flicked her wrist, finger pointed as if she were cutting a slice of cake.

Light became solid. It moved with instantaneous speed. Livira’s barrier didn’t shatter. It didn’t rumble. It didn’t break.

It split.

A thin but massive beam of light cut the seals in half, along with Livira, perfectly symmetrically. A seam appeared on her body, as small as it could be. The ground below her slid and parted as a massive canyon began to form with the shifting earth.

Her body fell apart into two pieces, collapsing in opposite directions.

The world seemed to pause. Jowlaw stared at his former partner. For once, he didn’t smile.

He frowned.

“Oh dear. That’s… not good,” he said quietly. “What is a performer without their opener?”

Vezavok moved next. His wings flared, heat and force detonating in mid-sized explosions as he launched himself toward Jowlaw, claws extended, ready to pierce the jester with molten fury.

Jowlaw sighed. He looked up at the approaching draconid. His eyes seemed to lower. “I suppose this’ll be your last performance, my lady.”

The sky erupted around them.

Seals. Small seals—hundreds, if not thousands—appeared all around Lumina and Vezavok. The draconid stopped mid-charge, looking down at his body. The seals were essentially stuck to him. He waved his arm, but they wouldn’t come off.

Jowlaw looked at the two of them, covered in blinding seals.

He took a deep bow. “This’ll be the last performance of tonight, lady and gent.”

The explosion was catastrophic.

Light, heat, force, and sound tore through the sky in a rolling blast that shook Eiyuria to its foundations. Trees bent. Stone shattered. The clouds themselves split apart as the shockwave rippled outward.

When the light faded, smoke and debris churned where the courtyard had been.

Vezavok burst through it with a furious roar, scales scorched and cracked, molten lines glowing where the blast had bitten deep. He landed hard, talons gouging trenches into the earth as he steadied himself.

Lumina descended more slowly.

All of her barriers of light had gone dark. Space around her no longer refracted light, and nearly half of her jewelry was broken. She stared down at her arm, which was now partially gone, replaced by light itself. She frowned, clenching her hand. Slowly, the flesh reformed, creeping back up to her fingertips.

Vezavok roared once more, this time carrying deep force in his shout, dispersing all of the smoke.

Once it cleared, they both looked around.

Jowlaw was gone.

The only thing left behind was Livira’s body, split cleanly in two, lying where she had fallen.

Vezavok snarled. “He escaped.”

Lumina didn’t answer immediately.

Her gaze remained fixed on the empty space where Jowlaw had been.

“Come on. Let’s meet back up with Dorian. This won’t be the last time we see them. I have a feeling we’ll be fighting again. Soon.”

A rift opened above them, and they stepped through once more, leaving Eiyuria, and most of Amberdean’s estate, destroyed. The fissure in the ground from Lumina’s attack kept growing, and it would keep growing until the earth finally settled.

In another estate, distant from here, screams from a young, tied-up, and gagged teenage boy filled the guest room. Hours later, people would eventually locate Amberdean’s son. And hours later, he would learn of his father’s murder.

Minutes before the echo of the Acolyte’s power reached the Enchanted Layer.

The city screamed.

Caelus Empyria ran. Stone shattered beneath his boots as another explosion rippled through the lower districts, the shockwave hurling debris into the air like shrapnel. Towers buckled. Streets split open. Somewhere behind them, wards failed in a cascading collapse that sent arcs of wild mana tearing through entire blocks.

Virelya was already ahead of him, cloak torn, silver hair loose, her breath sharp but steady. She didn’t look back. She didn’t need to.

They couldn’t stop.

“Left!” Varus shouted.

Caelus pivoted without thinking, dragging Virelya with him as a lance of compressed force tore through the alley they’d just abandoned. The stone wall imploded, flattening everything behind them.

Near the prison, there were a group of guards trying to understand the chaos. Their eyes were wide as report after report flooded their communication crystals.

“Say that again,” one of them barked, clearly the leader. “You’re telling me the prisoners broke containment?”

A second guard shook his head violently. “Not the prisoners. The Empyrian ones. It was coordinated. Someone inside disabled the inner locks.”

“Damn it!” the captain shouted, slamming his hand along his desk. “The one time Emperor Helmutt leaves—I get the plate filled with shit! It’ll be my head on the chopping block for this!”

A third voice cut in, breathless. “Wing C’s already fallen. And—gods help us—there’s confirmation of a traitor.”

The guard captain froze. “Who?”

“…Varus.”

The name drained the color from his face.

“That’s impossible,” he said hoarsely. “He was verified. Cleared. Helmutt personally—”

A runner burst into the intersection, nearly tripping over rubble as he shouted, “Wing D’s compromised!”

Silence.

The guards stared at him.

One of them whispered, “Wing… D?”

The guard captain’s expression collapsed into horror. “No. No—activate the city-wide mana dispersion device. Now! Sound the emergency—”

There was no buildup. No warning.

The world turned white.

The detonation erased sound before it erased matter. Light swallowed streets, towers, and bodies alike as the prisoner from Wing D was released—his containment failing all at once. The explosion wasn’t fire or force, but raw, unfiltered annihilation. Buildings folded inward. Mana devices all malfunctioned. Entire districts vanished in a blink.

Hundreds of thousands were gone before they could even scream.

Caelus felt it an instant before it hit.

“Virelya—!”

He grabbed her and threw them both to the ground, turning his body instinctively, shielding her as the shockwave slammed into the city. Light burned through his vision. The air crushed the breath from his lungs. Stone and debris rained down like a storm as he clenched tightly to his wife.

The world roared.

Then—

A serene silence.

Caelus groaned as the pressure lifted. His ears rang violently as he pushed himself up, coughing dust and blood. He looked down immediately.

“Virelya—are you hurt?”

She shook her head, breath unsteady but eyes sharp. “I’m alive.”

They stared around them.

Half the capital was gone.

Where streets and towers had stood moments ago, there was only a vast, smoldering scar—glass-transformed stone, drifting ash, and a horizon permanently altered. Fires burned at the edges of the devastation, accentuating the emptiness left behind.

Caelus swallowed. “…Varus wasn’t exaggerating,” he said quietly. “That man really could level the entire city.”

Shouts echoed nearby.

Survivors—some of their loyalists, others simply people who hadn’t been in the blast radius—were scrambling to regroup. Not all of them had made it. Caelus saw bodies. Recognized armor. He realized that a few familiar faces were missing.

But there was no time to mourn.

“Move!” someone shouted. “Before they recover!”

Caelus pulled Virelya to her feet. She nodded once, already moving, her expression hardening as she took in the destruction.

They ran and didn’t stop.

Through smoke, through fire, through the corpse of an empire that had believed itself untouchable.

Behind them, the Helmutt capital burned.

And ahead of them—somewhere beyond layers, beyond prisons, beyond gods—their daughter was alive. They knew she had to be.

Several days after the attack on Amberdean’s Estate.

Far from Eiyuria, far from the scarred courtyard and the broken sky, Hazeltell City went about its days in quiet ignorance.

It was a small Fourth Layer city—stone and timber packed tightly as its foundations, buildings clustered together as if the wind itself might try to carry them away. From the outside, it looked unremarkable—a modest trade city, busy but not grand, far removed from the political centers and layered conflicts that tore the lower realms apart.

They stayed here, yet most of their time was spent somewhere else.

Enya’s sanctum no longer resembled a small, sad dungeon room.

What was once a cramped, uneven space of bone and stone had expanded into something vast and deliberate. The ceiling arched high overhead, reinforced with rib-like supports of polished ivory. Mana flowed cleanly through the walls, no longer seeping or unstable, but circulating in wide, steady channels.

At the center of the sanctum stood a chamber large enough to rival a small estate.

Enya’s workbench remained where it always had, but now it was flanked by a proper forge and a newly installed furnace, both humming faintly with heat. Shelves lined the walls, filled with tools, materials, and half-finished constructs. And resting in the center of the room—fully repaired, its massive skeletal frame reinforced and pristine—was Cinnamon Bun.

The goliath dragon lay dormant, curled as if in hibernated sleep, magical circuits etched cleanly into its bones. Behind it, on a pedestal, was Zerus’ heart. The container seemed to hum with power; whatever process it was undergoing—it was getting close.

Enya hummed as she worked.

She sat at her bench, legs swinging slightly as she leaned over a small trinket in her hands—a palm-sized bone creature, delicately carved but uneven in its joints. She turned it this way and that, frowning faintly, then adjusted one of the pieces and tried again.

It still didn’t quite work.

“…No, that won’t do,” she muttered, mostly to herself.

Across the chamber, Pell stood with his arms crossed. Mr. Bones loomed nearby, silent and still, expressionless as he always was. Elria was back in her crystal spiderling form, perched atop a metal stand on one of the tables, her multifaceted eyes focusing on Enya.

Pell didn’t say anything at first.

He just watched.

“She’s different,” he said eventually.

Elria’s legs shifted slightly. “You noticed it too.”

Pell nodded. “She’s… sharper. More confident.” He hesitated. “More decisive. She’s even telling me to do things, and its not like when she normally does it, either.”

Elria tilted her small head. “That’s one way to put it.”

“When I mentioned having her go to school here temporarily, until things settled down,” Pell continued, voice low, “she shut it down immediately. Didn’t even think about it. Just said she hated it.”

He frowned. “Back in Talo, she was eager about the idea. That was the whole reason she joined that damn tournament and got herself kidnapped.”

“Her power’s also gone up. She expanded this dungeon core that much after a few fights? Considering her normal output when fighting, it seems almost impossible,” Elria said after a moment.

Pell looked down at his skeletal hands. “I leveled up after everything. The guards. Amberdean. Even the Godsworn.” His soul-flames flickered faintly. “And I barely feel different. I know I’m a farmer and all that—but still, even with her class being a combat class—a jump this large is ludicrous. How much mana or soul-energy did she expend to teleport us away from Eiyuria to an entirely different city?”

Elria didn’t disagree. It was a ridiculous notion.

At the workbench, Enya sighed and set the trinket down again, clearly dissatisfied. She tapped it once, then nudged a joint back into place, her humming never stopping.

Pell watched her for a few seconds longer, then uncrossed his arms and walked over.

“Enya.”

She didn’t react.

He stopped a few steps behind her. “Hey. Enya.”

She turned suddenly, blinking. “Huh?”

Her expression was bright and attentive—exactly as it always was.

“Oh. Sorry. Did you need something?”

Pell searched her face. Nothing seemed different. “You feeling alright?”

She tilted her head. “Yeah. I’m fine.”

Something in his ribcage tightened. “…Can you do me a favor?” he asked.

Her brows lifted. “Favor?”

“Open your status.”

She blinked. “Status?”

Her status screen opened up, listing her skills, abilities, class, and name. “What about it?”

“Is it… normal?” he asked.

She glanced at him, and then toward it. She hummed for a moment. “Yeah. Seems normal to me.”

His soul flames tightened. “What… what does it say your name is?”
