With the fleet hopefully out of reach of the blob, repairs on the 'Armoured Justice' were initiated, and the Captains soon reconvened aboard the 'Alchemical Star'.

The euphoric celebration of their small victory had rapidly given place to concern. They had suffered very light losses, but the enemy remained undefeated.

The assembled offices stood silent around the table. Isaac, flanked by Angeline, looked around the room.

He rested his sight on Captain Amerret of the 'Armoured Justice'. “How is your ship, Captain?”

He dipped his head. “The damage is mostly superficial and does not endanger the ship's integrity. One of the aft-mounted strum cannon had lost power due to damaged mana wire, but it should be operational before I return aboard. We lost five crew during the... boarding action, but no other wounded worth mentioning; scrapes and bruises.”

He looked at the young man in command. “I must thank you, Lord Isaac. Your command helped preserve my ship. I only wish those orders hadn't needed be sent.” He glanced at the two captains who had immediately ordered their craft to gain altitude. “But it appears my idea of unit cohesion is not shared by all.”

Captain Kelcraw scoffed. “Perhaps if your idea did not include all of us being grappled by that monstrosity, it would be easier to share.”

Isaac sliced the air with his hand as Captain Amerret opened his mouth to reply, silencing him and everyone else. “This is not the time to bicker. Both approaches had merit, and I will not hear more of this while the enemy remains a threat.”

He locked eyes with all five Captains before continuing. “Conventional weaponry failed to destroy the blob. From what Lord and Lady Allwarden know, it grows stronger with each manawatt it absorbs, and we fed it quite a few mana stones as a diversion. A repeat attempt is ill-advised unless we can increase our firepower.”

He gave everyone a few beats to consider his words before continuing. “The floor is open to suggestions. If you have any ideas, voice them. Tactics, strategies, alternative weapons, please consider everything.”

The table remained silent for a while, until Captain Melville of the 'Tempestuous' spoke up. “This thing is deep in the forest, away from any population centre. We send a pair of ships back to Riverwall and amass a bigger fleet.”

Captain Sinclair rubbed his chin. “Do we have the time? The blob grows stronger the longer we leave it alone. What if it evolves beyond the reach of whatever fleet we can gather in the time it takes to do so?”

Kelcraw sneered. “Surely it can't keep growing forever. If we can have two or three bombardment ships fly out here and drop a carpet of fire bombs, we can just burn the thing.”

Amerret gave him a withering look. “Bombardment ships are kept mothballed during times of peace! It will take weeks to get them recommissioned! Even if it doesn't grow infinitely, it might still split, or, at the speed it's going now, reach Riverwall.”

Kelcraw scowled and lifted a finger toward Amerret. “Watch that tone before I-!”

“Enough!” Isaac's voice cut through the air. “Captain Kelcraw, Amerret, if you can't keep yourselves civil, you will be removed from this table. Bicker on your own time. Right now, I expect you to act as officers of the Amberfall Air Navy. Understood?”

He glared at the offending officers. Kelcraw grunted but nodded, while Amerret stood a little bit straighter. He turned toward the other captains. “Leaving for reinforcement will be our last resort. Anything else is on the table.”

Heavy silence returned to the room as heads churned through ideas, though none were voiced. Eventually, he noticed Arnelle's thoughtful look.

He addressed her. “Captain Plerigass, do you have an idea?”

Her eyebrows lifted as she looked back at him. “Hmm? Oh, well, maybe, but my employer will not like it.”

Isaac squinted and turned his head toward Cornelius. “Sir, do you know what your Captain is referring to?”

The catkin's ears went rigid as he took in the question, looked at Arnelle, then back to Isaac. “Oh, huh, no, not at all. No idea whatsoever. A complete mystery.”

Angeline's voice cut in. “Father, even Niala can tell you're withholding something.” She admonished.

His head swivelled toward his eldest daughter, looking for confirmation that it was true.

She gave him a sad smile.

His ears folded down as he sighed, then turned toward Arnelle. “Do you have any idea how much this vessel cost me?”

The dogkin captain smirked. “Relatively speaking, for you, a few weeks' salary.”

“That's still several billion bits!” He countered, tail frazzled.

Eyebrows lifted, and eyes widened at the price tag of the ship they were in.

Kelcraw cursed under his breath.

Isaac stared the catkin down. “Cornelius... what are you two talking about?”

He groaned and threw his arms up. “The combustion beam! We never got it to work past a few dozen metres, but the mixing chamber works just fine. It's... the Alchemical Star's main weapon.” He crossed his arms and pouted. “Or it would be if the stupid containment field didn't start breaking down right after leaving the cannon...”

Isaac let his head hang before looking up at Arnelle. “Explain, please.”

The dogkin grinned. “Certainly, Lord Wardenfel. As my affluent employer alluded to, this ship has another weapon. Alchemy-based, supposed to throw a super-heated liquid out to a thousand meters in a short-lived mana field. When the field hits the target, it breaks and exposes the alchemical liquid to the air.”

Her grin turned predatory. “As Mr. All Brew said, the artificers were never able to get the beam to go further than a few dozen meters, but the effect is very destructive. I don't know the details of it, but the mixture is supposedly so hot that it ignites the very air, triggering a localized firestorm.”

She looked at the silent captains staring at her and smiled. “We test-fired it at rocks. They melted.” She said with the mirth of a kid who'd found a cool frog.

Cornelius puffed his chest out as he spoke, dragging the spotlight back to him. “That's right! I, Cornelius All Brew, devised the next evolution in alchemical warfare! I call it Thermalchemy, and the substance in question, Pyressence!” He revealed, taking a dramatic pose.

Which was met with silence.

Niala's ears twitched. “Dad? Just how much heat are we talking about?”

“Ah? Hum, well... It depends.”

She furrowed her brow. “Depends on what?”

“On how much mana we can feed the mixing chamber's containment enchantment. With the ship's standard mana system, it can refine the mixture up to roughly 4,000 Celsius.” He explained with a hint of smugness.

Niala froze for an instant before speaking slowly. “...And with enough mana, how high will the enchantment hold?”

Cornelius frowned as he thought. “Hmm, the artificers calculated that it could reach at least 8,000 Celcius. Maybe more. They said there was no theoretical limit, but since the mana draw is exponential...”

Niala swallowed, eyes wide.

Captain Sinclair coughed. “Could someone explain it in practical terms? How powerful is this pyressence?”

Niala jolted out of her daze and looked at the captain. “Ah! Well... I read somewhere that a standard naval-grade strum cannon released between 1 and 2 million manajoules. A kilogram of Pyressence at 4,000 Celsius would release... huh... 20 million manajoules? Roughly?”

The captain moved his head up and down once and turned his head toward Cornelius. “And how much Pyressence is released in a single charge?”

The catkin smiled. “Ah! The mixing chamber can contain up to 500 litres of Pyressence, which is roughly 450 kilograms!”

Sinclair did some mental calculation. “So a single charge is equal to four to five thousand strum cannon shots, all at once.”

Cornelius nodded, ears pointed up. “That's correct!”

Isaac tried to come to grips with the ridiculous numbers he'd just heard and mostly failed. He looked at Arnelle. “I feel like a few dozen metres isn't a safe distance to use this weapon.”

The dogkin wave a hand. “Oh, it isn't. The tests were made with a limited amount of pyressence, just a few kilograms.”

He frowned. “Then, how do you suggest we use this weapon? It would destroy the... oh.”

She smiled. “Exactly.”

He looked around the table, seeing that everyone else had understood why Cornelius did not like the suggestion.

The only way to use this weapon was to sacrifice the ship.

They continued brainstorming for ideas for another bell, but no genius idea appeared. By the end, the discussion revolved around how exactly to use the Pyressence.

They suggested, out of respect for the All Brew, to take the time to remove the mixing chamber and drop it as a bomb. Unfortunately, the whole system had been built at the core of the ship, and to remove it would essentially require a drydock.

Cornelius openly objected to the idea, but with no true, viable alternative, Angeline ordered her father to just accept that there was no other choice.

While the catkin sulked in the corner, a plan was quickly drawn up, studied, amended, and revised a few times over.

They would only get one chance.

The final plan was to provide the mixing chamber with all the mana it needed so it could contain the Pyressence for as long as possible, to get it as hot as they could. They wouldn't even bother trying to use the firing mechanism.

The Alchemical Star would simply be rammed straight down onto the blob.

David suggested calling it the Nova manoeuvre.

Everyone groaned.

But the name stuck.

There was one issue of contention. As Cornelius had said, the mana requirement of the mixing chamber grew exponentially past 5,000 Celsius. They calculated that to get to 6,000 Celsius would require the almost entire supply of mana stones from all five ships. There was only one way to get it close to the practical limit of 10,000.

David.

Only, with the ruptures in his pathways currently venting his mana out, he was doubtful he could push enough of it out.

Niala took him aside and proposed a solution.

David narrowed his eyes. “You want to feed my mana to the mixing chamber through you.”

Niala looked him in the eyes. “Only if you agree. And only if we test beforehand. I don't want to hurt you more than you are right now.”

“Forget about me! How do you know it's not dangerous for you?!” He retorted.

Her ears flopped down. “I... don't know for sure, but I don't feel as if my ability is something that can hurt me. It's hard to describe, but when I used it, it came about as naturally as breathing does.”

She took a step forward and leaned on David's chest. “The only difference is that I was breathing in mana instead of air. But, if you don't like it, we won't do it.” She told him, burrowing her face into his shirt.

He looked at the top of her head before bringing his arms up around her and letting out a breath. “I don't know. The mana stones might be enough.”

Neither spoke for a few seconds. She rubbed her face against him before pulling it back and looking up. “But it might not be either, right? And we only get one chance.”

He looked her in the eyes. “And you want to make it count.”

She leaned her head back on him. “I don't like the alternative. If we do the Nova manoeuvre, and the blob still survives, at the rate it's growing, it might become something nobody can handle by the time we come back with more ships.”

“I might be able to handle it once I'm healed.” He suggested.

She craned her head up and gave him a disapproving look.

He held her stare for a second before conceding. “Right, stupid idea.”

She gave him a playful moue. “More like overly arrogant.”

“It's not arrogance if you can back it up.”

She squinted at him. “Stop.”

He grinned. “Why, I know a great man who once said that there was no shame in knowing once's stren-”

She planted a hand over his mouth. “If you finish that sentence, I'm creating a potion that will make your eyebrows permanently grow a hundred times faster and feeding it to you during your sleep.” She warned him.

He blinked and stared back at her as she gave him a pointed look before slowly pulling her hand away from his mouth.

He didn't finish the sentence.

David and Niala offered to remain aboard to charge the mixing chamber to the maximum, right before the ship would be tilted forward and accelerated at maximum speed toward the blob.

With the decision made, things were put into motion.

Everything that could be salvaged from the ship was loaded onto the launches and transferred to the other ships. All non-essential crew for the Nova manoeuvre went with them. The process took most of the daylight, and left the ship bare-bone, with even its rotary strum cannons cut out and carried away, then bolted onto the deck of the other ships and fed with temporary mana wires.

As the afternoon turned into evening, the only people left aboard were the Captain, a skeleton bridge crew, David, and Niala.

The two met with Isaac and Angeline on the deck, who wished them good luck and ordered them to stay safe, before boarding the last launch to leave. Two launches remained on deck to ferry away the holdouts once things were set into motion. Hopefully, they would be long gone from the ship before it even finished tilting forward.

Below, the blob quivered as mana stones were dropped from the other ships, keeping it in place.

David and Niala made their way down to the mana heart. Niala grabbed the handles, and David put his hands over hers.

They had carefully tested Niala's ability to pull mana from her husband and, apart from a bit of discomfort for David, the process was relatively painless, even in his current state.

When he had inspected his inner world while Niala had been pulling in his mana, he'd been surprised to see that the mana siphons had gained in intensity once more. They'd figured that it made a certain kind of sense; if he already had holes, then she was just sucking his mana out of them.

As long as she didn't pull so much as to begin overwhelming his system, as the white fel had done, they were mostly confident it would be safe, or at least that it wouldn't aggravate his current condition.

With a nod to each other, she planted her feet and breathed in.

David's mana surged into her, almost eager. The sheer amount made her hiccup, giving a sensation of near drowning. And yet, it was as if she was being engulfed by a divine nectar, and her body was parched.

Her pathways instinctively began drinking every drop of it. With a twist of her will, she diverted the flow, away from her, down a canal, and into the waiting mana heart.

The large gem began to glow brighter as David's mana rushed in.

On the bridge, Arnelle monitored the mixing chamber's mana levels. Once it reached saturation, she engaged the process. Pumps wheeze to life and flooded the chamber with the three-part Pyressence. The liquid began heating up as the runaway exothermic reaction took hold.

The heat gauge on the bridge started rising. With the chamber at max capacity, there was no going back now.

The ship was going to blow up.

The only questions were how big the explosion would get and how far away they'd be.

Far below, on the forest floor, the blob rippled. It had no face, no eyes, nothing that could indicate what it was doing beyond being a mass of flesh.

And yet, some of the lookouts could have sworn the thing was looking up.
