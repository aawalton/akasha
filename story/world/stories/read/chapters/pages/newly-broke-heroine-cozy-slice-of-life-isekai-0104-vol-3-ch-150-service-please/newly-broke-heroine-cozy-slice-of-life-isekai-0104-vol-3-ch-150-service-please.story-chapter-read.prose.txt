Earlier…

One intense night of effort and some minimal sleep later, Fiona and the others were ready. Except that Fiona had one last part she was nervous about:

The part where she'd be hiding in plain sight.

“Hey, you ready?” Bonnie called out, tapping her silvery wand on her palm. “Now, keep in mind, illusion spells are not pitch-perfect. But this shouldn't be a big change, so…”

“Let's just do it.” She grabbed the hem of the uniform Darla had stowed away in her dimensional kitchen, fingering the material in anticipation. She could hear the bustle of people wandering in, their curiosity piqued about the new cafe. “Let's do this before I get too anxious.”

“Anxious about what?”

“I…you’ll see.”

The words did not deter Bonnie as she got to work with a splash of dazzling lights and a tingly sensation over her body. It only took a few minutes. She closed her eyes, as if that would help ease the anxiety she felt.

It didn’t.

Bonnie let out a grumble. “Stop wiggling your nose. It’s gonna be crooked, and then I definitely won’t be able to sell this disguise."

“My nose is cute. And I can pass a crooked nose as one monster fight too many.” She let out a soft hum while keeping her eyes closed. She could hear Varith in the background, muttering about their own disguise.

“Okay. You can open your eyes now. I’m all set.”

Fiona opened them to see the kitsune peering at her, electric-blue eyes etched in curiosity. “Now, I do have to ask…why this appearance?”

“Can you pass me a mirror?”

“Here. Your nose is fine, though. I was kidding earlier.” She handed her a compact mirror from her satchel, and Fiona looked at the reflection.

Her hand went to her lips. “It…It’s me,” she whispered.

“You were already you, girl. I don’t think I’ve met anyone quite…like…” Bonnie’s voice trailed off as Fiona let out a soft laugh, and tried hard to keep from sniffling. Then the kitsune’s ears drooped, and her expression changed as she realized what exactly Fiona meant.

Staring back in the reflection was mousy brown hair, duller green eyes, and more than a fair share of freckles that hadn’t been there, the edges of her face rounder. Her figure hadn’t changed that much, but she was plainer. She didn’t feel shorter, at least. She was keeping the height difference. She swallowed uncomfortably and wiped a bit of moisture from her eye. “Sorry, Bonnie, I…I didn’t think that…”

“This is what you looked like. From before.” Bonnie nodded softly before leaning in to hug her, nuzzling gently against her cheek. “I had a feeling that might be what you were aiming for. Maybe I shouldn’t have–”

“No. I haven’t forgotten how I looked. I haven’t forgotten the old me.” She let out a shaky breath and rested her chin on Bonnie’s shoulder. “Thank you. I was worried that I’d forget someday. That everything from who I was, what I remembered, would fade to obscurity.”

Bonnie gripped the small of her back just a little tighter. “So there is a soft, sensitive side of Fiona under all that red,” she added with a faint laugh. She let go and stepped back, hand under her muzzle, while examining her face. “Goodness, I could play connect the dots with all those freckles.”

Fiona let out a snerk sound, even as tears threatened to come back up. “Sorry, that right is reserved for, uh…currently miniaturized dragons.”

A sly smile crept across Bonnie’s face. “Tell him he’s gotta share the cinnamon roll. Alright, alright, this illusion is powered by your mana, and it’s pretty low upkeep. You can wear this most of the day without issue. Illusions are always harder to do on yourself than if they’re done by someone else, yeah? Plus, it’s not going to hold up under scrutiny.”

“One-time deal,” Fiona promised. She heard Varith in the other room, then opened the door, wearing something similar to a butler coat, black and gray with gold trim and silvered buttons. He was trying his best to get one button through a slot.

“Good gods, how do men wear this? What’s the point of me even wearing this if I’m in the back? I never had to–” He looked up and stopped mid-sentence when he saw Fiona, and his mouth fell open, eye bulging. “Fi? What the…what did you do?!”

She felt her cheeks blush. “So…do you like it? I needed a disguise I could believe in. Turns out, being who I was? It’s the perfect illusion.”

Poof.

Varith disappeared in a cloud of instantly generated misty vapors that swirled around the room. Fiona and Bonnie shielded their faces when the mist blew by like a warm summer breeze. She squinted, and when the mist cleared, it was her turn to stare.

Bianca was back. Her hair was a little more frazzled than before, and her first reaction was to let out a yell of triumph. “Yes! Finally! You have no idea how long that took! I felt like a prisoner in my own head!”

“Uh…welcome back?” Bonnie wore a nervous smile as her eyes darted to Fiona. “Was this your plan?”

“Uh, no. This is one of those things that worked out unintentionally well,” she said distantly. She didn’t have long to wait as Bianca rushed over, practically melting over her. “Okay, okay, settle down, Bee. There are young kitsunes present.”

“Oh, my goodness. Bonnie, aside from a few tiny things, this is exactly what Fiona looked like. You’re a genius!” Bianca squealed and pulled in the vixen for a hug, too.

“Hey, hey, we’re still working past the ‘I went through a mad king phase,’ remember?” Bonnie protested. Bianca finally let go of them a moment later, blushing softly. “But, you’re welcome, I guess.”

“Sorry, it’s just…nostalgia,” she blurted out. “Okay, that was kinda weird. Normally, when I switch, I can switch back mentally. This felt like someone put up a partition in my brain.”

“So, are you good for now?” Bonnie asked cautiously. Fiona stared.

“She has a magical girl transformation. I want one!” she declared.

“You want a dangerously unstable personality pairing? Oh, hell no, girl, I would not wish this on anyone,” Bianca fumed. She glanced down at her uniform, wrinkling her nose. “This doesn’t fit anymore. Or, fits a little too well.”

The uniform had taken a rather exaggerated style change and was decidedly more provocative than before. Fiona found herself staring a touch too long. "How does that magic work?"

Bianca shrugged. "Hell if I know, even this many months in. Why did you even bother having me dress up? Karlin would have recognized me."

"Uh...spirit of teamwork?" Fiona proposed. Bianca tapped her foot patiently. "Okay, you have a point. We still can’t get the bracer to work. What’s up with it?” Fiona added.

Bonnie shook her head. “No idea. I can't find anything wrong with it, and I worry if she'll snap back to the tall and broody guy. So, Bianca, now that we have a plus one, have you ever waited tables before?”

“Once upon a time,” she grumbled, puffing a lock of hair out of her face, then looked down. “I wasn’t usually dressing like a fantasy waitress with gravity-defying attire. So what’s the play?”

Fiona waved everyone over. they didn't have much time before they got Karlin's attention, most likely. “Everyone, gather round. Let’s go over the plan one last time, and we have a plus one!”

The plan was simple: find the strongest scent of pyrogel in the town, then tail that contender back to their hiding spot. With the risk of a fight triggering the unstable explosive, they had to play the subtle game this time, and Fiona was convinced that Karlin himself was here.

And if he were here, this would make their job a lot easier.

“Jake, reception,” she pointed out. He slicked back his fur mane and adjusted a tie. “You see Karlin stroll up? You delay him until we get him seated. Make up a story. He will likely be brash and try to intimidate or bully you. Your guys' volunteers from the guild will cook behind the lines with Darla.”

"Won't he recognize me?" Jake asked, confused. Bonnie tapped on his muzzle and added a little grey to his fur, along with a few minor alterations. "Oh, right. Let's hope Karlin wasn't paying too much attention during the brawl in the mine. I'm worried about how effective you are with illusions, Bon-bon. Did our resident snack thief have cover of illusions and fancy tricks in the past?"

Bonnie smiled slyly. "I'm sorry, is there a question or accuasion there you want to lay out, Jake?"

"I had a sweet roll disappear right out of my hand, Bonnie." He held out his hand, then snapped his fingers. "Gone. Right in front of me. The pastry teleported out of my hand!"

"That one...was not me," she said, looking amused. "Now remember, Karlin's a bully. Act unintimidated."

“Consider me unbullied. I deal with upset kings and guild members all the time.” He filed down his nail to be less sharp.

Fiona then pointed at Doug. “You’re with me and Bianca and Bonnie, waiting on tables. We’ll take turns low-key checking customers with the scent finder. The one I really wish I had every day to locate my misplaced snacks!”

Bonnie sighed contentedly. “It detects volatiles. Not confectionery treats.”

Doug, currently disguised as a gnome of similar height and bright red hair, tapped at where his snout would be. “I hate illusions. Always a cognitive dissonance if your form is any different. I can still tap my snout and feel it. So, on the off-chance he shows up, what then? Do we take him into custody? Maybe after we soften him up a little, first.”

“Wishing bodily harm on your brother, while understandable? That’s probably not the image we should go for,” Fiona reminded him. “Jake, Rikkard has an official bounty on his head for his involvement in the theft of kingdom gold

“Correct. Though this dragon has conned plenty of people before and never seen the inside of a court room," he muttered.

“Close enough. He sent a bunch of kobolds out to raid and pillage, and made Doug his patsy. I think he’ll talk once he realizes I’m the one bringing him in.” Fiona then pointed to Darla, currently dressed in a stylish chef jacket and black slacks. “Darla? Cook like a dream.”

“Oh, I’ve got the easy job,” she purred while checking her knives for sharpness and examining the cookware. She stepped into the kitchen and the burners came to life with a quick wave of her hand, and the fire elementals went to work. She even gave them a treat of marshmallows–hey, that was her signature move!

Fiona didn’t bother to lay claim to that one. She had hungry guests to seat. She straightened her hair–wait, the illusory hair, or her real hair? She’d have to be mindful of that. She took a deep breath and stepped outside to start this effort.

A line of people had gathered outside the tent, the cavern illuminated by one of the glowing gems in the cavern ceiling, and strings of arcane lights she’d snagged from the store.

The crowd looked a little…rough. Some looked like they hadn’t even cleaned up from last night and were nursing hangovers. A few looked more alert and had taken interest in the menu drawn on a hastily erected chalkboard, with Bianca’s still-sharp artistry displaying colorful murals of food, and prices listed.

“Alright, ladies and gentle…things,” she added with a boastful smile, and clapped her hands together. “Who’s ready for Brightstone’s Travelling Cafe?!”

A muted rumbling emerged from the crowd. “I couldn’t hear you guys, because it sounds like you don’t know what you’re buying! Today’s special is brunch! You get the perks of breakfast and lunch! Seared steak, omelettes, and browned tuber hash! Oh, and some coffee to go along with it, because you guys look like you can use it!”

A wave of applause rumbled through the crowd. She had them hooked. “Alright then, fellas, let’s get you all seated, and prepare for a treat of the culinary world I doubt you’ll have twice! Darla, the ship is all yours!”

On cue, the darkling appeared, her culinary knives belted and shining, her hair tied back, and she grinned as she brought out a smaller cookset. Was she going to cook it right in front of the crowd?

Darla gave a slight bow and then wore a proud smile for the crowd. “Thank you all for coming! I know this was short notice, but I was in the neighborhood, coming back down to Underlune for a visit! You might know my father, Magnus Brightstone, from another part of Underlune with a well-known restaurant. Well, I’ve been working on my craft, too. Now, for a demonstration! Who wants Rorganth steak omelette?”

Several hands went up in the air, from still buzzed barflies to a few wearing leather duster jackets and looking more refined. Darla ignited the stove with a snap of her fingers and got to searing the steak with a mix of herbs, butter, and a tiny bit of salt, while she got to work on an omelette.

Fiona could stare all day at that tail cooking like a third, independent limb. Wait, that sounded a little off.

She cleared her head and started seating people for coffee, while a good chunk of the crowd looked on i eagerly. Darla’s hands moved like magic as she prepared the meal in record time,s, stirring, flipping the omelette without a single filling item slipping out, and the steak sizzling with a perfect sear. In a few moments, she had a plate of perfectly medium-rare steak with a side of a cheese and mushroom omelette.

The meal went to a lady with her hair in a messy bun, immensely grateful for the meal that Darla handed her with grace. “First one’s free, miss. Everyone else, grab a seat, we’ll have some refreshers and coffee out momentarily, and our lovely staff will see to your needs!”

Soon, Fiona was busy taking orders and scribbling notes, sending them to Darla in the back. Bonnie helped prepare some of the ingredients with her own enchantress tools, while Bianca and Doug kept an eye out, taking turns with the device slipped into their sleeves.

It also kept coming up empty. So far, there was no indication that the pyrogel had exchanged hands or had been handled by anyone recently.

Even though waitressing wasn’t her regular skill suite, this was still a chance to try something new. Fiona was enthused to help Darla shine in an arena in which she was truly good at. Based on the growing pool of smiles and commotion in the tent, she pondered whether they should expand the small cafe in the emporium to something a little larger.

She was shaken out of her daydreaming and smiling by a nudge from Doug, who tilted his head to one side. “Guess who showed up?”

“No way. Is it…”

“Oh, yeah.” They walked to the entryway, and Doug’s eyes narrowed as he peeked out. “Look who it is,” he growled. “Walking up like he owns the place.”

Karlin’s imposing figure could be seen strolling in from the town toward the pop-up cafe, looking smug, with his deep red scales and scarlet feathers. He had a few other people trying to keep pace with his longer stride. One of them looked like the woman Fiona had seen at the forge. Maddie? No, wait, it was Missy.

Fiona narrowed her eyes. “Looks like my nose knows, after all. I’ll refrain from hammer time until we figure out where he stashed that pyrogel. Be cool.”

“I am cool. You be cool,” he retorted, with his gnomish ears flicking in irritation. “Just keep in mind he probably has half the place under his thumb or is indebted to him.”

“Money,” Fiona said with a click of her tongue, and waved to Jake. “We’re up. Karlin’s approaching. Until we locate that stash of explosives? We don’t make a move.”

She waited for Karlin to stroll up to Jake, with their pre-thought delay plan. She put on a straight face and told herself, playing along was for Doug’s sake.

She greeted him with a beaming smile and honeyed words. She tried not to cringe at his smooth talking, which she’d heard once too many times, and led him to the VIP table with his entourage.

In the meantime, she took a sample of the air around him using the device on her wrist, all while handing out menus.

But he didn’t reach for one. “I want the prime steak and an Underlune Omelette,” he announced proudly. “My associates indicated this meal was not to be missed.”

“He’s buying,” Missy declared, giving him a scathing look. His edged smile indicated all was not well between them–something she could use for later?

She heard a ping on her relay. “We’ll bring that out shortly. I’ll bring coffee and juice right away,” she answered as she clasped the menu.

She returned to the kitchen area, where Bonnie and Darla, plus the guild members she’d bused in, were busily cooking several tables' worth. She checked the relay, which also had a readout on Bonnie’s device.

Her eyes shot wide as she saw the concentration of volatiles on or around Karlin. Worse, there was an additional alert.

The pyrogel had about two days before it would decay and set off the biggest fireworks display in the history of Underlune.
