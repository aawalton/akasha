The air around the bunker stayed still, too still. Even the faint hum of mana seemed to die near the threshold.

When Elynara finally moved from where she stood, it wasn’t her own will that broke the trance, but Aru’s motion. The automaton girl had stepped forward, closer to the entrance, her light falling over the layer of fine white sand that blanketed everything inside.

Elynara hurried after her before she even thought to.

“Wait–!”

Aru stopped just short of crossing over. Elynara caught up, heart pounding harder than the silence deserved.

Even Starpuff hadn’t moved in minutes. The little spirit hid and stayed curled under her hood, silent, its glow dim and trembling. Whether it feared the sand or the graveyard it covered, Elynara didn’t know. She couldn’t blame it either.

The place felt wrong.

Cold.

Empty in a way that wasn’t just death, but the absence of everything that should follow it.

She couldn’t even smell rot, not even faintly. No decay. No lingering hint of life at all.

Everything was dead, and the white sand was still hungry for more. Elynara could feel it pulling from everything around them. Checking on her leftover mana, more was gone than she expected from her opening up the huge bunker doors too. And…

Up close the empty skull sockets seemed to stare at her. Silent. Accusing. Haunting.

Elynara shuddered, her body growing cold. She needed a distraction, something to take her gaze away from the terrible sight. And her suddenly clammy hands from squeezing her staff too hard.

Her eyes found Aru, the girl staring ahead too.

“Should we… enter inside?” she asked quietly.

Aru startled, turning towards her. Then she tilted her head, thinking.

“Yes. But no. Not you. You will stay here.”

“What? No–”

“The white sand,” Aru said, voice firm but gentle. “It is clearly dangerous. And far more dangerous to the living than to the machines, I can tell.”

“But you’re not really just a machine anymore either! Not really! We don’t know what that could do to you…”

Aru nodded, unbothered.

“Which is why I must enter, and you stay. So when things go awry, and I return, you can heal me back up.”

Elynara’s voice cracked.

“No! And I don’t know any healing spells, you know that!”

Aru smiled softly then, turning towards her properly..

“You know enough,” she said, kneeling. Her faintly warm and soft hands took Elynara’s. “You have repaired and healed me before. Your strength made me who I am today. I can still feel your mana coursing through my core. You can do far more than you believe, Elynara.”

Elynara’s throat tightened. It took her a good moment to consider how to reply. Being relied upon by others felt so nice before, so why was it so difficult now?

“…But why enter at all then? Why push yourself beyond what’s safe? I’m sure there’s some way to neutralize it, to push it away – to go in without risk.”

“There might be,” Aru admitted. “There might be not. But my people’s last memories lie in there. In the shadows of the bunker meant to keep them safe. It did not – but it kept them, and their belongings, from the passage of time.”

Her eyes flickered to the bones laying in the darkness.

“...To a degree, at least.”

The answer left little room for argument. Not one that would lead anywhere.

Instead, Elynara looked past her to the faint, dead light beyond the door. She wanted to know too – what they had built, what they had lost. She wanted to help those who never escaped. But if entering meant risking Aru and herself both, she knew what she had to accept.

She swallowed hard.

“…Okay.”

Aru’s eyes brightened, and before Elynara could react, she was pulled into a hug – firm, soft, and unexpectedly warm.

“Thank you. Do not worry, I will do it over many trips. And return every time I feel anything wrong.”

Elynara could only nod, a muffled sound caught in her throat.

And then Aru turned, stepped into the pale dust, and disappeared into the dark.

The waiting was the worst part.

Elynara paced the length of the corridor, fingers tapping against her staff. Every echo sounded like a footstep that wasn’t hers. The heavy door loomed in front of her like the edge of a storm she couldn’t stop.

When the pacing started to make her dizzy, she sat down cross-legged beside the door and forced herself to breathe. Slow. Measured. And oh so very familiar.

Mana breathing.

The familiar rhythm she got so used to during all her training steadied her. In, gather. Out, release. The flow soothed the restless churn of her thoughts, until her pulse stopped trying to climb out of her chest.

Before long, she had fallen into a quiet pattern – shifting from simply mana breathing into gentle gathering too. Threads of light drifted toward her, settling like dust motes into her core.

It helped.

She needed it to.

The bunker was their best chance – she knew that much. Weeks of searching through the ruins had given her scraps at best. A few trinkets. Some preserved pieces. The script that wasn’t a language, and which she had found no manual for.

Some memory crystals, maybe. But no way to access the knowledge inside.

…Elynara should ask Aru about those. Maybe her new friend would know. Still…

Nothing of substance.

This place, sealed and untouched, might be the only one left that still held the truth of things. Memories from before. Knowledge otherwise lost to time.

She just needed Aru to come back. Safe.

And Elynara had a pretty good hunch that she would need a lot of mana for what’s to come.

For the first time in a long while she pushed the limits of her mana gathering, up until the edge of what was bearable, of what would let her work for a very long time.

Yet despite the immense focus it took to do, her mind just refused to fully calm down.

Elynara tried to let the slow rhythm of breathing and gathering lull her mind, and to let the emotions go, but it simply wasn’t working fast enough and well enough.Every so often her focus would break at the faint sounds she could sometimes hear from the bunker. The first time it made her twitch so hard she startled herself. Even the occasional whisper of air from the tunnels was enough to make her glance toward the door.

So she forced herself back into it again – inhale, draw in the flow, let it move through her body like a slow tide.

Exhale, let it settle into the core.

The soft hum of mana around her steadied into a faint glow. Threads of blue light drifted through the air, drawn toward her core as she guided them through practiced motions.

At least it gave her something to do.

Something to think about other than Aru vanishing into that white grave.

And yet her mind wandered anyway.She thought over the months since she had first begun training. Of the small victories and discoveries she had made.

Her thoughts returned to the very first time she could feel mana moving through her properly. To the first real spell that didn’t sputter into smoke, and the first fire she made with the sparks. The feeling of the world itself responding to her.

Of her journey after. Learning all the things about mana and magic, and practicing them out. Her mastery over fire and explosions growing. Her sharpening control over fire and man. Her senses expanding. Her magic shows, and the discovery of how the spirits work with the world. Of the water magic she learned thanks to that.

And about all the potential growth paths before her still.

It all seemed like a lot – and yet felt like so little. Once again she was here, sitting before a problem she did not understand, yet one which worried her deeply.

The Deadzone.

The weight of that silent place. The way the land seemed to pull, endlessly hungry, as though trying to drink in every drop of mana and life.

And Aru.

A miracle that shouldn’t exist, a melding of a machine with the spark of a spirit inside. A person born of both logic and spirit, walking, speaking, laughing.

Elynara smiled faintly at the thought.

She still couldn’t decide whether Celestia had guided her to the broken caretaker that day, or if it had just been luck – or mercy.

Her mana core brightened with the warmth of that thought. Even the air felt lighter, easier to breathe.

And then her thoughts turned towards the other part of that miracle: of how it happened.

Elynara could not even begin to fathom how the old spirit did it – for it had to be its doing, there were no other options. Though, if her understanding of how spirits did things was correct… It likely would have been unable to tell her regardless.

It just did what it did, willing things into working. And the world responded.

Could it respond to her too, if she tried hard enough? Even for ‘just’ water magic, it took her a lot of tries to achieve what she did.

How long would it take her to do anything of the sort?

She shook herself from the reverie. There would be time to practice more still.

With those thoughts out of mind, Elynara continued her meditation. It came easier now, the process drawing her into its embrace again.

At least until something small and fuzzy poked its head out from under her hood.

“Well, hello there,” she murmured.

Starpuff blinked, its faint green light flaring brighter as it floated free of its hiding spot. For a long moment, it hovered in front of her, curious. Then it darted straight into the small cloud of mana she had gathered.

She looked on as it bounced between the gathering mana flows and her mood lifted. Starpuff really was just so silly sometimes, and its pure joy contagious.

Smiling at its antics, Elynara prepared a ‘reward’ for it – shaped a blob of mana from her core and released it in a straight line for Starpuff.

It trilled in delight and pounced on the thing before it even fully left her control.

The sphere then wobbled, pulsing in what she recognized as the beginnings of a spell, and she panickedly closed her eyes.

“Don’t you dare–”

Too late!

The mischievous puff lit up like a tiny sun. The flare washed through the corridor, dazzling her even through her closed eyelids.

Elynara yelped, falling backward on her hands.

“Starpuff! What part of subtle do you not understand?!”

The little creature chirped, spinning smugly. Its glow faded to a softer hue as it floated near her again, utterly pleased with itself.

“You’re impossible.”

She flicked a far smaller pulse of processed mana its way.

“Here. Play with that instead.”

The puff absorbed it immediately, spinning in delighted loops. Elynara sighed, rubbing her temple, but the distraction helped. The tension in her chest eased.

Meditation came easier now and soon she was back at full mana. But before she could switch to mana breathing, she heard it.

Footsteps.

Faint. Measured. Coming from inside the bunker.

Her pulse spiked, all playfulness forgotten.

Elynara rose in a single motion, staff already in hand, mana coiling instinctively at her fingertips. Starpuff darted behind her shoulder, suddenly small again.

And when her new friend finally emerged, Elynara’s breath caught.

Aru was not looking good.

The automaton girl stepped through the entrance, her movements slow, uneven, and shivering. Her hands trembled as she carried several small items – metal tags, crystalline items, and a handful of other things Elynara could not guess the use of.

The girl’s state was worrying, and as Elynara took a step forward…

“Aru, you–”

“Stay back,” Aru said sharply. Her voice carried a distortion Elynara hadn’t heard before, a static undertone that made the words shake.

Elynara froze in place, unsure what to do.

Aru knelt down, setting the relics carefully onto the floor between them and a little to the side of the door. Then she turned and walked back toward the doorway, her steps stiff, deliberate.

Elynara realized why when Aru paused at the threshold and began to shake herself off.

The movement was violent, harsh, and more mechanical than she had come to expect from Aru so far. A shiver that sent a fine cloud scattering into the air.

Barely noticeable, a tiny haze she would not have noticed, if Aru wasn’t standing before the dark bunker entrance.

For a heartbeat, Elynara didn’t understand.

Then the light from her staff caught the particles as they fell.

The cursed white dust.

She could feel the damned powder sapping mana from the air. And the warmth from around them. The effect wasn’t anywhere near as strong as she knew from the Deadzone, but clearly still a worry.

It shimmered faintly as it settled, the tiny dots falling from Aru’s hair, from the seams of her armor, from her fingers.

And where it touched the floor, the stone and metal underneath seemed to lose just a bit of color.

Elynara’s stomach turned.

And she thanked Celestia the white sheen didn’t cover her friend more thoroughly. She doubted the girl would have survived that, not for long.

She stayed frozen for a moment too long, staring at the white dust still drifting from Aru’s hair, watching how the girl’s light flickered again – and again.

And then she felt the most worrying part. Her heightened mana senses still churning from interrupted meditation told her what was wrong.

The tiny pull, and a faint drain on everything coming from the machine girl’s body.

Aru’s mana and warmth bleeding away in slow, steady shivers.

Her heart lurched.

“Aru–”

The girl swayed.

And that was enough to break her out of indecision.

Elynara moved without thinking. She sprinted the few steps forward, grabbed Aru by the arms, and dragged her away from the threshold with far more force than she thought she had in her.

The automaton tried to brace herself, stumbling backward in confusion.

“Elynara, there is no need–”

“Yes there is!”

Her voice cracked louder than she meant.

“Enough of your protector instincts. Let me help protect you now!”

Aru blinked at her, startled into silence.

Elynara didn’t wait for her to answer. She pressed her hands to Aru’s plating, mana already flowing through her fingers in a firm, steady surge.

The girl trembled under the touch, her body greedily pulling at the warmth and mana – too greedily. Even now, with the visible white dust shaken off her surface, Aru felt like a tiny hungry void.

Not really dangerous. But draining. Constantly demanding its price be paid. Bits of supplied mana just gone in the process.

Her mana sense could hardly mana sense of what was going on, magical sight simply ending in places.

“Come on,” Elynara whispered, tightening her grip. “We’re sitting down. Now.”

She half-guided, half-pulled Aru to a clearer section of floor, near to where she sat before yet far from the bunker dust, and plopped down without ceremony.

Aru landed in front of her with a soft metallic clunk, quickly trying to rise again.

“This is unnecessary, I can–”

“Nope.”

Aru froze mid-motion.

Elynara scooted closer, wrapped her arms around the girl’s middle, and pulled her back against her chest with all the stubborn determination she had.

Aru stiffened immediately – her joints and servos locked in some flustered reflex Elynara didn’t even know she had.

“E-Elyn–”

“Sit,” Elynara said flatly. “Before you fall over instead.”

Aru obeyed.

Very slowly. And very shyly.

Elynara’s hands slid down to Aru’s core lines, her fingers settling where mana connections felt thinnest. Then she let her mana flow, fully opening her core the same way she did for spirits. And everything in this world that needed fixing.

A brief thought tugged at Elynara then.

Despite all the world problems she already knew of – at least the answer to many of them was simply… more mana. She could work with that, rely on it.

Yes, the scale of the need was immense. Ridiculously so. And yet her hope rekindled nonetheless.

Just knowing what needed fixing… Elynara could work with that.

But for now she had Aru to ‘fix’. Her idle thoughts dispersed as her focus rekindled.

The surge of mana coursed through her, bright and warm, a steady pulse that washed from her heart down her arms and straight into Aru.

The girl shivered once.

Then again, smaller.

Her body warmed beneath Elynara’s palms, softening from cold metal back into something soft and alive. Something that answered her touch.

Elynara felt her breath hitch at the sensation – but she kept the flow steady.

And already she could feel it wouldn’t be enough.

Her mana alone never would be. But it was a start, and she could always gather more. And would keep giving until her core hollowed out if she had to.

She closed her eyes and restarted her meditation mid-embrace, letting the flow settle into a rhythm, gathering more mana, letting her body breathe it in, convert, and release back to Aru. Gather and release, until it’s done.

The warmth built around them like a tiny sanctuary carved out of the cold corridor.

Aru slowly relaxed against her, her trembling easing as the mana filled the gaps starved by the dust.

“…Sorry,” she murmured, voice small, fragile in a way Elynara had never heard from the girl yet.

Elynara sighed softly.

“You really should have listened to me more.”

Aru’s head lowered.

“…Yes.”

“But,” Elynara continued, firm but gentle, “for now, just sit still. And tell me what you found in there. And what you brought back.”

Aru fidgeted – an actual physical fidget, hands twitching in her lap – but eventually she nodded.

“…I will.”

And Elynara kept her hands where they were, mana flowing, arms wrapped tight around her new friend, refusing to let her slip away into trembling again.

Every passing moment she could feel her getting better. And that made her smile in turn.

Yes, things merely needing mana to fix weren’t too bad after all.

She just needed to start small.

Aru stayed quiet for a long moment, her head bowed, hands folded neatly in her lap. Only when Elynara nudged her lightly with her chin did she finally speak.

“I… ventured quite far inside. The dust was everywhere, but the bodies were fewer there. I felt stable enough at first.”

Her voice stayed small, careful, as if she was choosing each word with precision.

Elynara narrowed her eyes, supplying the girl with a bit more mana. The mini jerk of a reaction she got from that told her everything.

“Confident, were you?”

Aru’s shoulders twitched.

“I… believed I could navigate it. It was familiar. And the danger I expected wasn’t quite so… bad.”

“Mhm. And relying on ‘knowing things you haven’t shared’ nearly got you into quite a predicament.”

Aru wilted immediately.

“Sorry…”

She tried to inch herself sideways, as if hoping she could physically slide out of Elynara’s arms without being noticed.

Elynara just hugged her tighter instead.

“Oh no you don’t. And where do you think you’re going now? You’re staying right where you are until you’re all better.”

Aru froze again, embarrassed and warm under her hold.

Elynara noted how, the more mana she supplied, the more of those tiny ‘void spots’ – as she has come to call them – disappeared. And how her friend warmed up a little more with them gone.

“And next time,” Elynara finally added, “we will both do better. So whatever secret knowledge you have that might help, do tell.”

Aru stared down at her hands, her voice only barely audible.

“I… really don’t know much. I suspect things. But I was only a protector, a caretaker. Nobody saw fit to ‘tell’ me about… such things. Not in detail. It is just too early to say any of it with certainty. I need to see the Deadzone for that.”

Elynara let out a soft breath. She didn’t press the girl more. There was little point, and Aru would tell her once she did feel confident. What’s more, she knew when pushing someone – anyone – would do more harm than good.

Instead she changed the subject, eyes drifting toward the items Aru had placed on the ground earlier.

“…Then tell me about those.”

Aru followed her gaze. The trinkets glimmered faintly in the dim light. Crystalline items. Metal tags etched with old marks. Flat rectangular pieces of opaque crystal that might have once held data. A twisted copper loop. Something that looked like a pendant frame, a gemstone set into it.

“These were among the bodies, near the entrance,” Aru said. “Items the residents carried with them. Momentos. Identification. Data cores with personal logs.” Her voice softened near the end. “These are… the few of what remained intact.”

Elynara nodded slowly.

“We will have to learn how to read the data ones someday. I also found some before…”

Aru shifted at that.

And Starpuff took that moment to pop out and hop onto her head, startling the girl. Then it hopped back – except instead of hiding in the hood this time, it settled in Elynara’s head now.

“I believe… I can. With time.” Aru admitted after a moment, a little pride – and a little fear – in her tone. “But I still need to gather more items. I saw some – in there.”

Elynara squeezed her again.

“Good.”

Aru continued after a while.

“Yet despite all the bodies, all the people inside… Far less has endured than what should have. I can just assume the white dust had done the items in. Only the more resistant pieces remain.”

Her piece said, Aru relaxed in her arms, just a little.

Enough that her trembling faded. Enough that she leaned more fully against Elynara, her fingers curling around Elynara’s hands as if afraid to let go.

“We’ll have to wrap you up properly for the next trips inside,” Elynara mused, glancing toward the doorway. “And tidy the entrance. And maybe clear the path too. I’m sure we can cobble something together from all the scrap here – and whatever I still have in my satchel.”

Aru hummed softly, a small, content sound. Her plating had warmed fully against Elynara’s palms, and her movements no longer shook.

She let her body rest against Elynara’s chest, as if the girl were an anchor she didn’t want to drift from.

Elynara squeezed her hands again, reassuring and warm.

And then–

Her stomach let out a loud, unapologetic grumble.

She froze, her cheeks coloring pink.

Aru blinked.

“…You know,” Elynara muttered, mortified, “I’m getting a little tired of my rations now.”

Aru snorted – actually snorted – before breaking into a soft laugh.

Elynara grinned, relieved beyond words.

Aru was out of her fugue now. Back to herself.

She loosened her hold on the girl only a little – just enough for Aru to shift comfortably – yet the girl made no move to leave the circle of her arms. If anything, she leaned in more, head resting lightly against Elynara’s shoulder.

The warmth felt nice, her mana replenishing steadily, and the void spots almost gone now.

Elynara let herself breathe easier.

There was a pause as they both just sat there. Peaceful and comfortable.

Then, she felt Aru fidgeting again.

“…Thank you.”

Elynara smiled faintly, her chin brushing against the girl’s hair.

“Anytime.”

She glanced at the trinkets Aru had brought, sitting in a neat pile just out of reach. Little echoes from a world long gone.

Heavy in meaning, but patient. They could wait.

For now she had more mana to gather, and a meal to get to.

The bunker waited centuries, it could wait a little more.
