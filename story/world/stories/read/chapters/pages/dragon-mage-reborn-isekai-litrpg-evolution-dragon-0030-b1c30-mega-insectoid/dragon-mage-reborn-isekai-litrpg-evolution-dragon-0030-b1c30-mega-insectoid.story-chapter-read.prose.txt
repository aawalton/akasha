I dashed toward the pile of rubble, my heart racing. The air was still thick with dust, and I coughed as grit settled into my lungs. I stopped short, inches away from the pile and waited for Nolan to come out, every second feeling like a freaking lifetime.

‘Nolan?’ I pleaded. Again, there was no answer.

Dread made my body numb as I continued to wait. The thought of losing Nolan made me hesitate to move. He was a pro at his job. Beaten by a tier three dungeon? There just was no way! And then I thought about the greater insectoids. There was complete silence on the other side. If they didn’t make it, considering the type of armor they had on, what were the chances of Nolan, a squishy smaller human, would be okay?

I’d be searching for a god damn corpse….

I roared loudly, my tiny dragon body bellowing out into the open air. It was raw and vulnerable, filled with pain and remorse. I had too much faith in him, feeling like crap for dashing out like that and leaving my partner’s side. But in my lamenting, I heard a small pop crack through the silence of falling dust, followed by a very familiar voice.

“Settle down, short stack. You’ll start another cave in with all that racket.”

A warbling noise to my right prompted me to turn around, my eyes ballooning to the sight of a portal. Nolan stepped out of the glowing rift, alive and unscathed, looking as calm and collected as ever. He brushed debris from his hair and his cloak before looking in my direction, my heart still thumping hard against my chest.

The corners of my eyes were wet, and I didn’t care how pathetic I looked. All I cared about was how selfish he was to pull a stunt like that, leaving me worried sick!

‘What the hell, Nolan!’ I retorted. That show off had me thinking he was dead!

He arched an eyebrow at me and asked, “Were you… crying?”

‘That flashy move was totally uncalled for!’ I hissed. ‘I thought you were a goner!’

“You were right, we were limited,” he reminded me. “In a tight space like that, we were cutting our defenses in half. So I thought we’d bury them instead.”

‘No, YOU thought you’d bury them instead! My plan was a lot safer!’

“It would have taken too long to put them down your way. This plan saves us time, resources, and stamina.”

‘But it was risky!’

He chuckled. “I’ve been through much worse, remember?” He smiled. “You seem to be forgetting my world is filled with all sorts of neat shit to get you out of tight situations. While this teleporter is typically used for longer distances, it can also be used to save your hide. The save is well worth the slash in durability.”

‘Asshole,’ I mumbled, trying to sound upset but failing miserably.

“Is that any way to talk to a guy who just pulled you out of the fire?”

‘You put yourself into the fire!’

He smirked. “You going soft on me?”

‘Not on your life.’ I took a breath.

He chuckled to himself. “Good. Now come on, let’s take this under-route and push forward, you know, before you start crying for me again.”

I rolled my eyes. ‘You’re not going to let this rest, are you?’

“Heh, not by a long shot.”

As we continued our run, my heart rate had finally started to calm down a bit, and I let some of my frustration fade along with it. Maybe it didn’t matter if Nolan’s methods were crazy, as long as they got the job done. I should have expected much from someone of his caliber, but he was right. I was still a noob. A noob to this body, to this world, and to all of its interesting, and dangerous, gradients.

I had to think like a dungeon raider. I was still prancing around like I was in some sort of video game, thinking about no real-world consequences until they jumped up and slapped me in the face. This wasn’t a game—this was real life for me now. And planning ahead was more vital than I was giving it credit for.

That being said, I was hoping that we could have scored some loot from those insectoids. Nolan said they were guarding the nest, as I expected, and when I popped an egg sac, I’d triggered some type of response system to the nest. That confirmed that these walls were alive somehow, because Nolan and I had been vocal the entire time up there, and yet, we didn’t stir the guards. Again, not until one of those eggies went splat.

“They are probably not dead,” he said nonchalantly as we continued to walk down the path slightly illuminated by glowing webs underneath fleshy walls. “Or maybe they don’t have loot. We haven’t exactly been lucky so far.”

‘Is that common? You know, lootless monsters in a dungeon?’

“Not really, but it does happen. For burner traps, absolutely, but those aren’t very common.”

‘Well, I’m thinking maybe those insectoids did have loot. Unlike the swarm, there were only two of them. So their primary objective wasn’t to burn our resources.’

“A dungeon core’s main objective is to annihilate intruders,” he reminded me. “So it doesn’t matter if the swarm was a resource sink or those guards were protecting a nest. When you generalize it this way, you’d stop giving yourself this mindset of categorizing monsters, which you should never do. Because then, you’ll train yourself to preemptively expect certain aspects of monsters and then become blind-sided when they don’t follow your formula.”

‘I understand.’

“Gods created dungeon cores to provide a means of entertainment and to give adventurers a chance to grow stronger in the process. But some of these cores don’t see it that way. They are like any other enemy. They adapt and change their strategies accordingly.”

‘Wait, so you’re saying that dungeons evolve?’

“They can, but not all of them do.” He looked thoughtful and added, “Most cores, especially the low-tiered ones, are hard-coded with instructions on how to deal with invaders, and they follow those instructions with minimal deviation. But your higher-tiered dungeon can learn from past encounters.”

‘So like, if we’d completed a tier five dungeon….’

“Future raiders might find themselves dealing with a different set of challenges.”

‘Not even that, future runs within the same party, right? You did say that you can repeat these dungeons, no?’

“Not a tier five. Not before a year’s past, at least.”

‘Oh, right.’ I smiled. ‘Listen, I’m still learning.’

“And that’s all right. I don’t expect you to remember all of this stuff overnight and be a dungeon raiding expert.”

‘Speaking of expert, I’d leveled up. And my skills did, too.’

“Heh, those nasty wasps were good for something, weren’t they? Like I said, don’t get comfortable expecting loot. Also, the more times you repeat a dungeon, the less loot you get from the monsters.” We finally reached the end of the tunnel, but after all of that walking, we weren’t expecting a dead-end. A large wall faced us, Nolan examining it for a moment before he brushed his fingers gently along its surface.

“A wall like this is promising,” he said. “It tells me we are going the right way.”

‘Oh? And how so?’

He continued to feel the fleshy surface before applying some light pressure on it. “For one, it’s blocking our path. Typically, these types of blockages are meant to slow us down and hinder our progress, meaning that we are making progress. Otherwise, they wouldn’t be here.”

‘So what you’re saying is that this wall is not really a wall at all.’

“Yeah, but an entrance to the next section of the dungeon. Only question is, how do we access it…”

‘Um, don’t we have a teleporter card?’

He snickered. “It’s not that easy. In dungeons, the properties of teleporters change, to avoid an easy raid. Outside, it works as intended, but here, you may only teleport to areas that you and your party had discovered already.”

‘Like I said, screw easy passes!’

“So we do this the old fashion way and find a way through this thing.”

‘I say we attack it!’ I suggested excitedly.

“I doubt it, but yeah, sure. Knock yourself out.”

On Nolan’s mark, I unleashed my toolkit on that wall. All of my skills had a turn trying to break, melt, or vaporize it. With my luck, the resistance to this wall in particular was unlike the others. This time, my flames were irrelevant. That darn thing was still standing there without as much as a scratch on it!

‘What kind of juiced up anti infiltration system is this?!’ I grunted.

“Nice try, kid.”

‘I don’t understand what makes this one so special!’

“Only explanation is that it leads to the dungeon boss, the queen herself.”

‘Crap,’ I said exhaustedly. ‘Toss me one of those magic elixirs, would you?’

“Don’t you have your own?”

‘Yeah, but you have more.’

He scoffed. Nolan loaded me up, tossing me a vial. I downed the liquid in one gulp, then belched as a tiny ball of flames escaped my toothy mouth. While it restored my mana, it did nothing for my stamina. So I leaned my stubby hand on the wall, touching it for the first time, and when I did, it moved, the surface of it pumping short waves.

‘What the?’ I gasped, immediately removing myself from the wall, where a small concave hold appeared in the middle of it.

“There you go randomly activating shit again,” Nolan teased.

‘Is that a slot?’ I said as I squinted upward.

“Seems like we must have missed something back there. We need to head back.”

‘Back to the rubble?’

“Yeah. The slot indicates we were supposed to collect some sort of key to open the doors to the dungeon master. No amount of banging this wall will break it. We need the key.”

‘Okay, let’s go.’

The trip back was a lot faster. I was anxious to get my tiny claws on that evolution stone! I was one step away from becoming a bigger, badder dragon. Understandably, I was beyond ecstatic, until I noticed a drop of bubbling green ooze fall right in front of my feet, and that was when all of my excitement turned into dread.

I was almost afraid to look up….

My terrified eyes caught those of a massive bug over our heads. It was still fairly dark in these tunnels, but I was still able to see long antennas, those huge ass pincers, and that grating mandible drooling acid ooze.

‘Ugh…. N-n-n-Nooolaaaaaaan,’ I whimpered as my head pointed upward, the size of that thing making those insectoids look small. Quickly, I [Inspect] it, realizing that it was higher leveled than the greater insectoids we’d fought earlier:

[Mega Insectoid, Level: 45]

We were cooked.
