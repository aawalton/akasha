Kyle’s mood was sour the rest of his journey back to the Courier’s Guild headquarters, where Jarberry and Suierrillax were waiting.  Adding in some unexpected teleporter delays, it took nearly seven hours for him to get back, leaving precious little time to prepare for the Tribunal.

“What more could they even want?” Kyle complained, pacing around the office.  “They said that I was able to work normally.  Now I’m barely back, and they’re already out to get me?”

Jarberry crossed his arms, giving Kyle an admonishing stare.  “Calm down, Kyle.  We don’t know exactly what they’re up to, but going in hotheaded is just going to cause problems.  Something about the timing has to be important, and we won’t know what that is until we see what they’re after.”

Suierrillax nodded her agreement.  “At this point, there are a few possibilities we need to work to exclude.  The first is that this is an immediate response to the events from the last hearing.  If that was the case, though, they could have filed an appeal right away.  It fits better to me if this was a reaction to something else.  The question is, what are they reacting to?”

[DO THEIR FILINGS INCLUDE ANY SPECIFIC LANGUAGE?  PERHAPS THERE IS SOMETHING UNIQUE WE CAN GLEAN FROM THAT.]

Jarberry pulled up the filing, looking through the lengthy document in a few glances.  “It seems to be fairly standard.  Looks like they’re making some procedural complaints and asking for some recusals from the Tribunal.  No idea why they think that would work; no Collective member states would willingly choose to step down from a panel like this.”

“Then why would that matter?” Kyle pressed.  “Why are they wasting everybody’s time with this stuff if they don’t think it will go anywhere?”

Suierrillax tapped her chin thoughtfully.  “What if the goal was just to buy time?  Their actions might make sense if we look at this through the lens of trying to keep Kyle occupied for a while?”

[WHAT WOULD THEY BE PREVENTING DR. MAYHEW FROM DOING?  RESPECTFULLY, THIS SEEMS LIKE A SIGNIFICANT RESOURCE EXPENDITURE FOR A SINGLE C GRADE THEY DID NOT EVEN KNOW WAS ALIVE UNTIL RECENTLY.]

“Hmmm…” Jarberry said, looking through the documents again.  “That’s an interesting angle.  What if it had nothing to do with our Courier at all?”

“Then if it’s not about Kyle, what could their angle be?”  Suierrillax asked, frustration clear in her voice.  “It’s bad enough everybody thought he was dead, now he can’t even come back to life without climbing a mountain of paperwork.”

“It’s exactly because he was left for dead,” Jarberry said, getting more animated.  “Whoever is behind this, they didn’t expect that there were any survivors.  There’s something going on that involved the incident on that planet, and Kyle being alive is causing a threat to it.”

The imp floated up to the center of the room, pulling up dozens of screens from his nav bracelet.  “If we assume that it actually has nothing to do with Kyle personally; and rather is focused on his circumstances, that narrows our set of possibilities.  They’re buying time to try and clean up whatever it is that they don’t want found, and somehow Kyle poses a threat to that.  Given Kyle was attacked by that criminal, and then its B Grade handler, perhaps they assumed that the issue would take care of itself.  Then, when Ghork intervened and Kyle got away, they had to scramble.  Now the question is, what do they not want found, and how are they planning on cleaning it up?”

Both Jarberry and Suierrillax’s eyes drifted to Kyle, who just shrugged.  “I have no idea.  I was pretty occupied just trying not to die.”

“That’s just classic,” Suierrillax said with a sigh.  “You’re so infuriating to people that they want you dead and gone, and you don’t have the foggiest idea as to why.”

“He really needs to work on his political thinking,” Jarberry agreed.  “It seems like any progress we made in that arena went by the wayside after a few years without our guidance.”

[I CAN CONFIRM THAT DR. MAYHEW DID NOT SPEND ANY TIME REVIEWING THOSE LESSONS DURING HIS RECOVERY AND SUBSEQUENT EMERGENCE,] C.H.A.D.D. added helpfully.

“Very funny,” Kyle said, glaring at the group clearly having fun at his expense.  “Now, what do we do about the Tribunal itself?  Even if they’re just wasting time, I’d imagine that Casiniax is going to have something to back his proposals.”

“I’d expect nothing less,” Jarberry said.  “Ultimately, waiting to see what cards he plays will help us understand their goals.  I’d guess that he’s going to pitch the Verdant Republic step away, they’ll say no thank you, and it’ll be back to business as usual.  If not, we’ll improvise.”

“We?” Kyle asked, raising an eyebrow.

“What?  Do you think Suierrillax or I would let you go represent yourself?  Sorry, kid.  We’ve gotten our approvals from the Courier’s Guild to act on your behalf.”

Kyle was about to argue on principle, but the knowledge that they’d be in the conversation with him was more comforting than anything else.  The reality remained that navigating Collective politics was a weakness of his, and certainly not one for Jarberry or Suierrillax.  Plus, he trusted them.

“Thank you,” Kyle finally said, meeting each of their eyes.

“Don’t get too ahead of yourself, Kyle,” Suierrillax said with a grin.  “My consultancy rate for legal proceedings is a lot steeper than my normal compensation.”

“Then you’d better find a way to get me out of this so I can earn enough to pay you,” Kyle shot back, feeling the smile growing on his face, too.

“That’s all well and good,” Jarberry interrupted, appearing in the space between them.  “For now, we have some studying to do.  We show up in 5 hours, and we’re not going to be unprepared.”

The interceding hours blurred by, until Kyle found himself standing alongside Jarberry and Suierrillax inside a chamber the Courier’s Guild specially prepared for such interactions.  The familiar faces appeared one by one, the grinning Casiniax being the last to show up.  The room rumbled as the dragon spoke, its eyes narrowed to slits as it looked at the skrell.

“Casiniax, I presume you have sufficient cause to call for this meeting.  The Gray Conglomerate has rarely been so… forceful, in the cases where they’ve been present in a Tribunal.”

The skrell’s grin faded, his expression looking aggrieved as he addressed the group.  “Truly, I wish it were not necessary.  However, given recent developments, we felt compelled to act.”

He turned, facing each of the Tribunal members individually, before his eyes rested on Kyle.

“I see this man is joined by his co-conspirators today.  Fitting, that they would so readily choose to identify themselves.”

Kyle felt the heat in his chest rising at the provocation, but C.H.A.D.D. urged caution through Pack Leader’s Instinct.  Taking a breath, he let the skrell continue.

“You see, it has been made clear to us that this ‘Courier,’ though I hate to use the term for someone so underhanded in his dealings, is currently in the midst of making an attempt to undermine the very nature of this Tribunal.  Why, scarcely one day ago, he returned from performing a unique favor for the Verdant Republic, and was about to embark upon another job for the Pillar, had we not interceded at this juncture.”

It was subtle, but Kyle noticed Jarberry tense slightly from where he floated.  Was that the job that D’Oillelat was talking about?

Casiniax let the silence sit for a moment after his comment, before continuing.  “It is clear, beyond a reasonable doubt, that these individuals are seeking to curry favor from the other member states in order to gain a favorable ruling.  At the least, to garner enough influence to prevent any meaningful consequences from his wanton disregard for life.  Therefore, we would move that Kyle Mayhew should be held awaiting trial by an independent magistrate.  That is the only way to ensure that a full and thorough investigation can be completed, without forever leaving doubt.”

The representative from the Verdant Republic looked about to explode in anger, though the other members seemed far more controlled.  The dragon looked at Kyle, then back to Casiniax.  “And you claim that the Gray Conglomerate has no bias in the matter, given his involvement in the disaster of Kertan Six?”

“Respectfully, I cannot say that the massive loss of life hasn’t impacted me, or the leaders I represent.”  Casiniax folded his hands behind his back, beginning to pace around his podium.  “While I am personally convinced of Kyle Mayhew’s guilt, beyond any doubt, I do recognize the need for fair and unbiased proceedings.  Even so, that only reinforces the need for prudence.  What do we lose, but a little time, to ensure that this is handled properly, and that the people of the Collective are not imperiled by his continued freedom?”And there it is, Kyle thought, catching the word.  Suierrillax’s gut was spot on.  They wanted Kyle out of play for a while, though he was still missing the “why.”  Fortunately, they would get their turn in a moment.

“And what say you in your defense, Kyle Mayhew?” the dragon asked in its rumbling tone.

“I’ll be speaking on his behalf,” Jarberry said, then shot a look toward Casiniax.  “And, given that I was so politely slandered, I figure that I can say a few things on my behalf, too.  Now, tell me, how many of you have done Guild work at C Grade or above?”

Jarberry punctuated the point by raising his diminutive hand, and when no others did the same, he nodded.  “See, that’s what I thought.  Something that the Collective holds sacred is the ‘independent’ part of ‘Collective Guild of Independent Licensure.’  It means that we can operate more neutrally, but it comes with the cost of needing quality jobs to survive.  As one of his Guild seniors, my intention was for Kyle to develop the network he’d need after his return from Kertan Six.  Then, after discovering his presumed death was an error, his handler and I acted with speed in order to get him quality work to build his reputation upon his return.”

Jarberry turned to Casiniax, his eyes icy.  “Not only has this farce of a Tribunal caused irreparable harm to this Courier’s reputation with your slanderous misrepresentations, it is also costing him a significant number of credits.”

The imp then turned to address the entirety of the Tribunal, pulling up several screens.  “I have listed here fourteen jobs that Kyle Mayhew would be qualified to take from our jobs board.  Of course, these details are privileged, and the details will be sealed within this Tribunal.  Given Kyle’s track record, it’s safe to assume that he would be able to complete these jobs within a normal timeline, and the least profitable of them would net Kyle over a million credits.”

Jarberry then looked between the phytan and the dragon.  “His path requires resources to continue.  A small look at the investment he made to the Gray Conglomerate’s Marcus Kulhavey makes that clear.  If this Tribunal thinks that Kyle needs to be locked away, then I put to you all that those responsible should need to cover his expenses and lost wages during such a time.”

“That’s ridiculous!” Casiniax exclaimed.  “There is no precedent for something of that nature.”

“Oh?” Jarberry said, a wicked smile crossing the imp’s face.  Immediately, more screens were pulled up before the Tribunal.  “We have cases here where C Grade phytans were called before a Tribunal some thousand-odd cycles ago, and they were provided both appropriate resources to nourish themselves, and an appropriate environment for their growth during the time they were under investigation.  Given your profound concern for the potential loss of life, wouldn’t you say that a million credit per-cycle payment, access to natural resources, and a nice, comfortable place to stay is a reasonable cost if you want him to wait?  Why, we could even have him tend to Kertan Six, given he was living there for years without the Gray Conglomerate having done their due diligence.”

“That is absolutely unacceptable,” Casiniax said, the faux sorrow entirely gone from his expression.

“Then in that case, why don’t we throw this whole tribunal out?” Jarberry asked, meeting the skrell’s gaze.

“Enough, you two,” the dragon rumbled, grabbing their attention.  “Casiniax, if you are unwilling to fund accommodations and lost wages, the precedent seems clear that the accused may continue their work as a member of an independent Guild.”

“Thank you,” Jarberry began, before he was interrupted by a wave of a claw.

“However, I do think that the unique circumstances of his employment do create a conflict of interest, which I recognize.  A special investigator will also be assigned, given Mr. Mayhew’s involvement with sensitive matters involving at least three member nations, and antagonism of the fourth.  Until such a time as that investigator is assigned, I would recommend that Kyle Mayhew be requested to stay within settled Collective Space.  Are there any objections?”

“None from the Gray Conglomerate,” Casiniax replied, glaring daggers at Jarberry.

“None from… the Pillar.  Mr. Mayhew, we… look forward to… your next assignment,” the phytan representative said before disappearing.

“The Verdant Republic wants to go on record saying that the aspersions cast on our character will be noted, and condemned in the strongest regard.  That said, we find this an appropriate solution, recognizing potential conflicts of interest on both sides.”

The velgian simply shrugged.  “Do what you will, I have no strong feelings.”

“Then it’s settled,” the dragon rumbled.  “The Collective will inform you when the investigator has been assigned.  Good fortune, all.”

With the Tribunal concluded, Kyle looked to Jarberry and Suierrillax, both of whom had thoughtful looks.  “What did I miss?”

“A lot, clearly,” Suierrillax said with a chuckle.  “You both worry about getting your work done.  I think there’s enough for me to work with here, and frankly Kyle, you’d just get in the way.”

Kyle was about to protest, but between Jarberry’s look and the feeling from C.H.A.D.D., he held his tongue.

“Besides,” Suierrillax continued.  “You’ve got a lot of people who want to catch up with you before you start your next job.  If I were you, I’d focus on that.”

“I still don’t have a place to stay,” Kyle muttered, looking at his road-worn clothes.

“And that’s where you’d be mistaken,” Suierrillax said with a smile, sending over a data packet.  “That’s your new apartment.  Go clean up, get some rest, and see your friends.  I’ll handle things for now, but after your next job expect that things will get busy.”
