I woke in a field of nightmares.

A sandpaper tongue licked my cheek. My eyes shot open. I was lying atop grass, blades cool and ticklish against the back of my neck, staring up into an azure sky so sharp in color it almost hurt to look at.

An orange salamander creature sat on my chest, its weight no more than a newborn kitten's. Its tongue lolled, similarly orange, hanging lazily from its mouth. In fact, nearly everything about the little thing was orange—its slick, rubbery body gleamed like liquid fire captured in a miniature balloon animal.

I stared at the thing, dazed, not entirely sure what was going on. My head throbbed faintly.

It tilted its head and observed me with wide, yellow eyes. Pupil-less and glowing, like lanterns in the daylight.

The salamander hopped on my belly as if I were its own personal waterbed, a faint trail of black, shadowy mist trailing the motion that smelled faintly sulfurous. Its tongue flopped around without restraint, occasionally smacking itself in the eye.

I blinked, the absurdity of what I was seeing slowly dawning on me. This was not a natural creature.

“What… the hell?”

My previous experience with an unnatural creature hadn’t gone so well.

I slapped the orange thing mid-jump. It tumbled off, squeaking as it bounced across the grass several feet away, trailing another streak of the mist, which dissipated into the air shortly after.

I lurched to a sitting position. My joints cracked, and aches groaned across my neck, arms, stomach, face. Basically everywhere.

“Where the hell am I…?”

I didn’t remember much. But I vaguely recalled being in a hospital, beeping instruments all around.

Instead, a field stretched around me, long grass flowing as a soft breeze danced across it. But the grass was… too green. Almost artificial. My hand rested in a small patch of ultra-bright violet pansies, their petals like velvet. A vibrant forest of trees stood in the distance with trunks a shocking burgundy and leaves glinting gold.

The colors were off. The hues too bold. As if someone had used the basic forty-pack of Crayola markers to color in the world. The sky, which I had thought was purely blue, faded to a dark gray as it neared the horizon, like a perpetual storm. And there was no sign of the sun, despite it clearly being daytime and the presence of a shadow cast at my feet. In the distance, pillars of weirdly black-colored light streaked into the air like waypoints from a video game.

This is no game.

The afterlife then? Am I dead?

Or maybe some sort of extra-lucid dream.

It has to be. The strange colors. Stranger creatures.

And staked into the ground at my feet was… a scythe.

Scythe… Why does that word seem so familiar?

The shaft stuck up from the cluster of pansies, while the blade stretched out like a taut flag in the wind.

I felt that sandpaper tongue again on my left hand, which was resting in the grass. The salamander critter stared up at me, panting like a puppy. With puppy-dog eyes too.

I pulled my hand away and shooed the creature, but it just skittered back beside me, plopping its little bum down in the flowers with a little squeak.

I rolled to my hands and knees, then stood, ignoring my insolent companion.

The ache was severe. My legs cracked like they hadn’t moved in months, pins and needles stabbing at my calves. My stomach felt like I’d eaten approximately forty Taco Bell tacos. And the tightness in my back was surreal—not to mention the pain radiating down my right arm and the throbbing in my eye.

My eye…

“Oh fuck no. No no no no no.”

I touched the skin around my right eye and eyelid. They felt normal, at least as far as I could tell without seeing them. But I remembered now.

The needle. The dark sludge. Skull-faced beings.

And the angel—if that’s what you could call him.

Everything came flooding back to me. Ending sin, saving humanity. Me, a Reaper. Whatever that means.

I pulled up my right sleeve. A thick scar snaked from wrist to elbow, weaving down into my palm and around my fingers. The skin there was tight and puckered, like a topographical map. Beneath my shirt, I discovered an even more gruesome sight: bruised, sutured, and stapled skin, more resembling roadkill than my once borderline six-pack.

It was only then that I noticed the unfamiliar clothes I had on.

A skin-tight black shirt, black denim jeans, and an orange hooded cloak with a metallic hue about my back, which trailed down to shredded ends. I immediately tried to pull the cloak off, but the tie around my neck only tightened and lines of pain surged down my back, making me gasp.

Not okay. This is not okay.

And it certainly was no dream. I’d given up on that small hope the second I saw those scars. This experience was too prolonged, too consistent. Too… real.

Heaven then? Or… Hell?

I reached into a pocket for my phone, but there was nothing there. No phone, no wallet, no keys. Zero possessions of any kind.

Of course.

I scanned the expanse before me, finding nothing notable. A hill rose to my left, but besides that there was simply emptiness. Just the field and patches of flowers and the strange trees as far as I could see.

Oh… and the monsters.

I hadn’t even noticed the fucking monsters.

I vividly recalled my intestines being gouged out by a feral lion monster not too long ago. The phantom pain threatened to bring me back to the moment, bile creeping into my throat. But I regained my composure quickly.

None were closer than a football field away, but I could see them stalking about. They were all smaller than the one that attacked me. It was hard to see from here, but they appeared to be some sort of weird human-animal hybrids. And they weren’t acting aggressive.

In fact, they were rather subdued, saddened even. Their shoulders sagged, movements heavy, almost mournful. Not hostile, just kind of there, existing.

That didn’t mean they wouldn’t attack, though.

As if on cue, something let out a heavy breath behind me. Hot air ruffled my cloak and weaved around my legs. The smell was acrid, feral. I whirled to see.

My stomach dropped.

A car-sized creature lay with closed eyes curled in a ball amid a bed of flowers that looked to be fuzzy dandelions, except they were blood-red instead of white. Its reptilian snout was only a few feet from me, with patches of green scales shining and parallel frilled spines stretching from shoulders to the end of its tail. The arms and legs were more human, muscled like a body-builder’s.

It was asleep, but how long would that last?

I instinctively looked around for a weapon so I could defend myself, then realized there was only one nearby.

You won’t take me without a fight this time.

I reached out to the scythe standing before me and grasped the shaft.

The weapon shimmered, sending a pulse of unfamiliar energy through me. It felt like static in my veins, prickling beneath my skin. Then exploded out in a gust of force. Even the grass and flowers in a circle around the scythe were pushed flat momentarily, like when a helicopter descends on a field.

I tried to retract my hand, but it wouldn’t release. A strange resonance vibrated in my veins, humming in time with my heartbeat, and the scar on my forearm tingled. My right eye pulsed.

The strange glitch flashed into my vision again.

Fuck me…
