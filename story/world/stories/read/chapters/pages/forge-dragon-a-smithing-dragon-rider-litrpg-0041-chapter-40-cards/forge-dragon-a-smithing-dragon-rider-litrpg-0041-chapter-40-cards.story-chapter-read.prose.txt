Caleb told his dad everything that had happened, this time in full. He unburdened himself of everything, even his suspicion that the attack of the village was his fault.

“No,” Kavil said, interrupting his son. “Don’t blame yourself for the evil others do. We don’t know that your actions caused the attack—though I agree it seems likely. But they could have come to us and talked. Demanded justice. But they didn’t. They attacked us like they always do and because of you they were rebuffed.”

“That sword tornado was pretty terrifying,” Grism supplied unhelpfully.

Then the implication that all the saving had been Caleb’s doing and not his own and he added, “I saved the village too you know. I helped end that storm.”

“I know you did son,” Kavil said. “But you’re not in the need of an ego boost.”

To their enhanced perception, both brothers heard as their father added “ever” to the end of the statement. All three smiled at that.

“So, introduce me to you dragon then.”

Caleb called Bog in from where he was busy digging into scrapped timbers. Many of the boards had been damaged beyond using, and Bog had spent his free time dragging them out of the burn pile in the village center to Caleb’s home where he chewed on the board until the nails were freed, swallowing the wood an iron.

“This is Bog,” Caleb said. “Bog, this is my dad, Kavil.”

They both stood looking at each other uncertain what to do. Caleb sent the feeling of familiar relations through the bond, and in return Bog sent some melancholy at the reminder that he was alone.

Not alone, Caleb sent.

And Bog’s sadness lessened.

Dragon Bond had increased to level 9.

Caleb tried sending thoughts to Bog as words again, but only got confusion back as Bog only got the vague meaning.

“Well,” Kavil said after he’d had enough awkward silence. “I never thought I’d meet a dragon let alone invite it into my home. I’ll admit the class was a strange choice, but I understand why you took it. ’m just glad you didn’t follow in your mother’s footsteps.”

Kavil turned to the dragon.

“I think I have you to thank for that,” Kavil said. “I know him well enough to say only a rare class would have enticed him from picking the Blacksmith class. Though now… the empire is taking you anyway.”

“It’s not the same,” Caleb said. “I’ll get out eventually. I’ll get leave. It won’t be like mom.”

“I don’t understand why you two did it.”

“We didn’t have a choice. Neither of us.” Caleb said. “I’m not the only one with a new class.”

Then it was Grisim’s turn to explain what had happened.

Kavil only sighed and shook his head in disappointment at the tale.

“You’re always getting into trouble,” Kavil muttered.

“We had to enlist,” Caleb said, bringing them back to the point. “If I hadn’t, they would have conscripted the whole village anyway. As it stands, we still need to provide the forces to make up for half their losses.“

“Less than that I’d say,” Kavil said. “Half the young initialized in the village already joined up. The rest stayed behind only because we’d be left helpless. But let’s put this talk behind us. We can’t undo what’s been done, but we can enjoy the time we still have together.”

Both brother’s let out a groan.

Their father had one love outside of his family, one his family universally reviled. He loved playing card games. The others of the family didn’t mind so much when they won—the only problem was that never really seemed to happen.

The next morning, Caleb and Grisim were woken by banging on the door. A auxiliary messenger had been sent to fetch them to Canus’ tent, and they rushed over as quickly as they could. They’d been given orders as to when to report to duty, but they’d been for later in the morning.

“Ah good, you’re awake,” Canus’ said when they walked into his tent.

The army had made camp near the keep with the plan to fall back to the keep if another attack arose. They’d improved the defenses of the keep during their time, digging ramparts around it with a layer of palisades.

The camp was abuzz with activity as soldiers were at work breaking everything down.

“We’re leaving,” Canus’ said. “Make your goodbyes and pack your belongings. You can have a bag of personal possessions that you will be required to carry. Caleb, I’ve sent a supply wagon to your smithy. you may fill the wagon with whatever supplies you require to ply your trade as a blacksmith. The army has a travelling forge you will get access to.”

“Leaving? Now?” Caleb asked. “What about my wife? What about the conscription?”

Canus gave a sly grin.

“The debt has been settled and your wife settled it.”
