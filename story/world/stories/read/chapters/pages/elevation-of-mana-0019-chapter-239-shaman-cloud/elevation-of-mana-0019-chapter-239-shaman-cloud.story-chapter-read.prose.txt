Whoever those people were they didn't stay long. They were here for a day or two, and there was some issue, though I couldn't understand what. I could hear the voices strained, the going back and forth, and they seemed to be speaking another language. I wondered who they were, where they came from, what they were like, and why they were here, but I had not the slightest idea.

No, whatever was happening was beyond my ability to parse or do anything about. Instead I did what I could do, paid attention to the little bubbles surrounding my skin. I still had no idea what these were for, but they came and went. So I watched them, tracked them, they seemed important. The bubbles were so pretty, so gentle, I wanted to touch them, but I simply couldn't.

As I considered this my mother eventually put me down, a small basket made of grass and stuffed with mosses so I could rest a bit. It was a simple thing, but it held me in a decent enough position, and it did smell nice, so I could accept it.

I was on my back, not my side, a good position determined by my mother, but I could still look to the side a bit. The moss was so vibrant green, the grasses that made my basket so fresh. I wanted to reach out and touch them with my small, weak hands.

So I tried, I reached and reached, trying to get to a particular spot that looked soft, looked prettier, deeper than the rest of it. My little fingers aiming for it as my mother did... I couldn't tell, but she wasn't looking. She couldn't see what I wanted.

But the spot of moss, the greener bit, it did something, it began to move, to grow. I stopped immediately, shocked at what I was seeing. The bubbles, they seemed to move around my hand as I reached, and as they did the moss grew, it responded. It wasn't a fireball or anything, not that that would be well advised, but it was something. This was magic, it was real! This wasn't just some odd, pre-historic version of my own world, but one where I had powers. That was pretty cool, not something I'd expected, but something that was quite, quite welcome.

I cheered wildly, trying to make my arms go up and down as I finally did something that changed something. Days spent unable to even see properly, to do much at all other than cry and I had actually moved something, changed something!Immediately mother was over me, looking down at my smiling face. She smiled too, seeing that I was not only alright, but happy, and said something I couldn't catch. How wonderful, how magnificent. Everyone loved a happy baby and I was truly happy. Soon more women joined her, some might be girls, they looked young, but all smiled at my smile.

Large hands came to pick me up, well, small hands, not large ones, but they were large to me, and I needed to make my displeasure at that known. A scream, as I was lifted got me put back down, and several of the women laughed and made faces as I settled down again, once more in the basket crib. One even put her finger in to play with me, which I dutifully grabbed.

While they were there I did not go back to using my newfound power. Surely that would have been great, but one could imagine how the women of this camp would react to a baby making things grow. Perhaps I would be treated as a good omen, or some upcoming king. Or one of them might smash my head against a tree as a demonic monster. I was in no mood to find out which it would be.

Weeks passed, then weeks more, then months, seasons changed. Soon I was big enough to move about a bit, big enough to run.

“Op, stop!” I heard yelled at me.

“No, run!” I replied, in my small, broken voice, running in circles around the camp.

Joyously my small feet pattered against the worn dirt, never going far, never outside the camp, just little circles around our home.

“Op, it is dangerous to run off,” my mother told me as she picked me up once more.

“Not off, just run,” I replied, my facility with the language passable at this point.

“You still need to stay near.”“Was near?”

This wasn't an odd conversation for us, and though I never used too many words it was clear I understood well enough.

Still, she carried me over to where she and the others were, not letting my little working legs touch the ground. We sat there, the women working stones onto roots and nuts, trying to get the insides out and ready to eat. Some were roasted on the fire, some weren't, but instead held in small, crude baskets.

I ran my fingers over one, made from knotted grass. The design of it wasn't pretty, it was functional, it was fast to make and sturdy enough for the job it did.

“Think they'll get any meat today?” one of the younger girls Ia, asked one of the elder women around the fire, the only truly old person here.

“Maybe, the sky is clear, and with the rain there should be tracks. If they are lucky we get meat today,” old Pa said, after considering a bit, her voice creaking with the strain of it.

Ten children were in our group, of various ages, some played nearby. Boys and girls chased each other, crude clothing of leaves or skins hanging off them. To one side a girl was letting her clothes out, undoing seams and redoing them so that they would fit her better. Beside her her mother was trading an old outfit to another mother whose child needed one.

I struggled loose from mother's grip eventually, she had to process roots at any rate, and I wasn't running now. Instead I went over to another member, the only man here.

“Ro,” I said, going near the man with the oddly bent leg. “New rock?”

He was indeed working on a rock, rubbing it repeatedly against another, harder stone.

“Yes Op, new rock. Need an ax to cut down trees.”I saw the ax he was making, and it would be... poor. It looked almost like the others we had, misshapen, not even on a handle. They were all basically sharpened rocks, that broke periodically and had to be replaced. Though all of our tools were like that, a woman using a barely shaped stone to smash nuts beside us before tossing them in a basket.

“Why do?” I asked.

“Because it's needed,” he replied with a small smile.

“Why you do?” I clarified.

“Can't run anymore. Fell on a root while running and snapped my leg, see,” he said, pointing to it. Indeed it was clearly bent. “Lived, but never was right after. I got lucky.”

“Too bad the old,” old Pa then said a word I didn't know “died, he could have fixed you.”

“What's that word?” I asked.

“One who speaks with the world, a shaman,” Ro clarified.

“What do?”“Change things we cannot change, they are different, and do different things. Call fire or sky-fire, move things, heal, or things like that. Some are even fast and strong.”

“Good?” I asked Ro.

“Could be good, could be bad,” old Pa said for him.

“Some protect, give safety, some destroy,” Ro clarified.

“Am I a shaman?” I asked, unsure how that question would be met.

“Probably not little Op, but if you find you are please fix my leg,” the man laughed as he continued to rub the stone on the other rock.

I smiled and began to put a bit of my power into his leg, not enough to fix it today, wasn't sure I even could, but enough to start the process.

“It's the shaman cloud,” one of the women said, looking up scared and pointing.

Above us I could see it, floating through the sky, a small dirigible, and it made me blink.

“I thought they left...” mother said.
