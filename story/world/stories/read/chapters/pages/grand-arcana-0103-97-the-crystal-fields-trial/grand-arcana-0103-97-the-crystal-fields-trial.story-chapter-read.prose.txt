The first thing that greeted me was cold. The second—the sight of lethal brilliance that stretched as far as the eye could see.

Crystal Fields, I thought. Shit.

Giant shards of crystal rose up from the valley floor like blades of glass. Some of them were dozens of feet high, reflecting light in various colors. Seeing them for the first time, one might be fooled into thinking that they were harmless. The truth was that they were anything but.

Looking closely, one could see bones and carcass remains scattered in the grass beneath them. Any creature unfortunate enough to walk about them for too long would quickly learn that the crystals had the ability to drain energy. They would start with aether, rendering spells and magic items useless, then move on to life force.

I looked around. I was on a mountain overlooking the valley. Behind me was an even taller mountain with a dense forest. A trickle of smoke rose up, disappearing midway to the sky, indicating the presence of a city.

Back in my previous run, I remembered doing trials in a few cities, though it was difficult to tell if they were the same one. The trial, at the time, had involved defending the city from monster invasions for a month. It had been one of the more difficult trials, though not because of the monsters, but the people themselves.

Are you just going to stand there, old man? Serp asked, his voice ringing with impatience.

“Just admiring the view,” I said as I redirected my attention back to the valley.

Without adequate protection, I wasn’t going to survive ten minutes in the fields. There was a good chance that Serp would perish shortly after. Living weapons were, in theory, indestructible, yet draining them of all their energy was bound to transform them into paperweights. On that topic. Maybe it was worth checking the valley for abandoned weapons once I found a way to survive longer.

I took a silver coin from my inventory and tucked it into the layer of soil beneath my feet.

What’s that for? Serp asked, genuinely curious.

“For good luck,” I lied. “Stay on guard. I’ll need you.”

I set off in the direction of the smoke column. Every now and again I’d glance over my shoulder, in case any other participant appeared. That was no indication that that was the case. In all likelihood, the portal transported us to different locations in the general area, and it was up to us to do the rest. After all, this was a competitive trial. Helping each other was useful, though there could only be one winner.

The closer I got to the forest, the more creatures made themselves known. They were skilled enough to remain hidden, although I could still feel their presence thanks to the Beastmaster card.

One minute in, the first encounter took place. It was more a standoff than an encounter. The beasts could feel that I wasn’t to be trifled with, so they didn’t attempt a sneak attack; rather, they grouped together to persuade me to move out of their territory.

A pack of three dozen wolves growled, walking among the trees and blocking the single path inwards. Each was the size of a small mule, with impressive claws the size of daggers. No status boxes were visible, indicating that they weren’t monsters, just local fauna. One thing I could see thanks to my Magician card was that all of them had threads of mana circulating through them.

“Can you take care of that?” I stopped.

Sure. Why though? Serp asked in his usual argumentative fashion. They won’t drop anything.

“Sure they will.” I summoned him. “They’ll drop hides we can sell later on.”

I’m starting to think you’re getting a kick out of me eating shit, the saber protested.

Before either of us could say anything else, one of the more eager wolves charged at me. He was among the smaller ones, probably trying to increase his status. Sadly, his action had just condemned the entire pack.

Ten feet from me, the animal leaped, teeth bared. Without hesitation, I performed a thrust attack. Boosted by his quick strike ability, Serp pierced the creature faster than the blink of an eye. Engaging its other skills, the serpent blade quickly expanded, devouring any flesh and blood, splintering in all directions. Extensions of the blade flew towards the nearest wolves of the pack, skewering them on the spot.

A few of the furthest wolves instantly leaped away in an attempt to flee, but it was already too late. A few seconds after the first wolf had foolishly charged at me, all that was left were pelts and bones.

I kept the pelts intact, Serp said with a note of pride. I didn’t touch the bones, though. Want to remove those? You’ll have to use the other blades.

“Good job.” I unsummoned him.

‘Skinning’ was going to be slightly annoying given the size of the blades. That only furthered my conclusion that I had to get more weapons. A knife, a bow, and potentially another blade could prove useful. For the moment, I had to work with what I had.

I slit the skin of the stomach, then shook it so the bones could drop out. The tails and skulls were a bit annoying, but with a bit of practice I got the job done in a quarter hour.

Thirty-seven pelts… they weren’t heavy, but too much to put in my inventory. Lacking any rope, I used one of them to tie around the rest, then continued along the trail.

Partially due to their scent, and partly due to the strength that I emanated, there were no further attacks in the forest. After another twenty minutes, I finally saw the first sign of civilization: wheel marks on the road. A wagon must have gone through here at some point in the past, likely when the road had been muddy.

Finally. I cast a quick water spell to wash up a bit, then some wind and fire to dry up. Experience had taught me good appearances always did wonders when non-participants were involved. A fellow participant had been lucky enough to find the Dandy card, which always created a favorable opinion of him among trial-world inhabitants. It was a pity that he abused it, ending up in a messy situation that had cost him his life.

“Unless I tell you, you’re only a sword,” I said as I continued forward.

Alright, old man. I promise to behave.

“Pup, be on guard. There are creatures that will try to sneak in after dark.”

The dream beast let out a bark in my head.

Several minutes later, the city walls became visible in the distance. The first time I had seen them, it was an almost unnatural sight. Up till then, my understanding was that jungle forts had to be made mostly of wood. Not these, though. Larger than the city of the special trial, it was made entirely of stone carried there from somewhere beyond the forest. To be honest, I had never been interested enough to find out. The key focus had been to survive and complete the trial well enough to earn a card.

Covered in vines and shrubs, the walls almost disappeared into the forest. Only the main gates were visible—they, too, were made of stone and metal. I had a faint memory that there were termites that could eat through wood as if it were paper. Thankfully, I hadn’t seen them in any of my current trials.

I stopped, paused for a few moments, then took a deep breath and continued forward. I knew that I was already being watched. Three city scouts were among the trees, bows at the ready. Most likely they weren’t going to shoot at me, but one arrow was enough to sound the alarm and designate me an enemy.

Fifty feet from the gate, I stopped. Given that it was still light, I would have expected it to be open. Unfortunately, something must have happened to require this change.

“I’m here to trade and rest!” I shouted, holding the stack of pelts high in one hand.

There was no immediate response. The fact that there were no arrows suggested that they hadn’t decided to kill me outright.

“I also have skills.”

As tempting as it was to use the Bard’s skills to convince them to move things faster, I never knew whether there wouldn’t be someone to spot the ability and consider it as an attack. I had to do things the old-fashioned way.

“Which faction are you from?” someone yelled from the walls.

Interesting choice of words. “I’m a free lance,” I shouted back. “Unpaid for the moment.”

This was starting to get suspicious. It was far too extreme for the start of a trial. Had I gone in the wrong direction?

Using the Listener card, I could hear whispers among the guards. Many of them were concerned about who I was. A few more were questioning whether I should be used as a sacrifice.

Always a fun way to start, I sighed internally.

The current trial had to be taking place prior to the survival one, I had done during my previous run. Of course, back then, I had appeared directly in the city itself. That’s where all the fighting had taken place, and that’s where the trial had ended with the place nearly overrun by monsters. It was also the first time I had heard the Crystal Fields mentioned.

“Stay there!” the voice ordered.

A section of the door moved aside, revealing an opening ten feet from the ground. Two soldiers jumped down. Neither of them appeared particularly strong, although I could tell by the way they walked that they were veterans.

I expected them to search me or ask whether I had weapons. Surprisingly, they didn’t do either.

The younger of the two took out a small white stone from the pouch hanging round his belt, then held it a few inches from my face.

“Am I supposed to do something?” I asked.

“He’s clear,” the guard said.

The older, more experienced one did the same with each of my wolf pelts, then put the stone back in his pouch.

“Freelance?” he asked, looking at me.

“Things have been slow,” I replied. “What’s going on here?”

“Worm infestation,” he said with a sigh. “Started in the mountains a few months back.”

Worm infestation? That was interesting, though not at all related to the goal of my trial.

“Nasty buggers are constantly learning new tricks. Seen that before?”

“No,” I lied. “Can’t say that I have. Is it a local creature?”

“It’s not a creature.” The guard shook his head. “Come along.”

A rope ladder was tossed from the section of the gate, letting us get back to the city. I was the first to be allowed to climb in. The younger guard followed, while the older one remained on watch.

“Careful.” A new set of guards met me on the inside, standing on a ledge of the gate. “Give me this.” He took my wolf pelts.

I didn’t protest even after he tossed them down on the ground. The man probably thought he was doing me a favor.

“Watch your step.” He rushed me further down the ledge to a makeshift staircase.

Fun, I thought as I climbed down.

As I went to retrieve my merchandise, a boy dressed in fancy clothes went up to me. He had a guard on either side of him, suggesting that he was a person of some importance. Judging by his young age and lack of crest and jewelry, I could only assume he was a page of some sort.

“Herald o’Steen,” he introduced himself. “What is your business here?”

The kid definitely needed a lesson in tact, diplomacy, and etiquette. However, I wasn’t going to be the one to give it to him, at least not today.

“Gregorius Ways,” I said, using my trial alias from the previous run. “I’m here to rest a bit.” I paused. “And hunt.”

“Hunt?” The herald arched a brow.

“You’re closest to the Crystal Fields,” I said with full confidence. “The best place to hunt creatures on either side.”

The kid turned to one of his guards, then back to me.

“You’re really going to hunt in the fields?” he asked, his recent suspicion replaced with awe.

“I’m good at hunting things.” I glanced at the wolf pelts. “A little something I got on the way here. What’s this about a worm infestation?”

“Oh, it’s—”

“Sir,” one of the guards interrupted. “I believe it might be best for his lordship to have that conversation with the man.”
