Soundlessly, the stone gate swung open, revealing a long, dim hallway.

Rowan didn’t hesitate as he strode down it, the only sound reaching his ears being the echo of his footsteps.

With a slight working of Intent, he cast [Flash], illuminating his surroundings in a soft white glow. Rowan made sure to keep the amount of mana he imbued into the spell low, not wanting to blind himself in case a threat made itself known. But as he continued walking, his eyes meticulously sweeping his surroundings, no sound reached him. Just the familiar beat of his heart and the soft patter of feet against stone.

The silence was ominous in its intensity. In response, Rowan pushed more mana into his spell. He’d expected it to pierce through the darkness with ease, but no matter how much he empowered it, only his immediate surroundings remained illuminated.

Frowning, he continued onward.

Rowan’s frown only deepened the longer he walked. The hallway didn’t end. No doors. No offshoots. Just more dark stone, more silence, more stillness. His mana churned beneath his skin, responding to the slow spike of adrenaline crawling up his spine.

Then came the heat.

It was subtle at first. Just a trickle of warmth tickling the back of his neck, like a gaze he couldn’t quite place. Rowan’s breath quickened, his hands clenching into fists.

He kept walking.

The next shift came slower, buried beneath layers of instinct and habit. A memory flashed in his mind. A fight, long past. His father’s voice, stern and disappointed.

You let your anger control you. Again.

Rowan blinked it away.

But then another surfaced. And another. And another.

A noble sneer when his back was turned. A whispered insult when they thought he couldn’t hear. A pitying look when they thought he couldn’t see.

Rowan’s jaw clenched.

His thoughts grew louder with every step, the echoes in the hallway turning to whispers in his head.

They never respected you. Never saw you. Never gave you the respect you deserved.

“Shut up,” Rowan whispered.

His feet kept moving.

The mana in his Core felt hot. Hotter than usual. Not the warmth of power ready to be shaped, but the prickling burn of something edging out of his control.

His spell flickered, growing fainter before snapping back into place.

A fresh spike of irritation lanced through Rowan’s chest.

Even his mana was defying him.

His frustrations—normally buried under layers of focus and discipline—rose to the surface like venom. Every injustice. Every failure.

The walls began to pulse, faint cracks glowing in the dim red light.

Rowan gritted his teeth, a snarl pulling at his lips.

And then, without warning, the hallway ended.

There was no threshold. No clear boundary that he’d crossed. One moment, he was walking through stone and shadow, and the next—

A sky of burning red. A field of shattered earth. A horizon trembling with movement.

Rowan came to a halt, taking it all in.

A horde of Demons, rushing to meet him. Thousands of them, big and small, sprinting across the blasted landscape with howls of fury and bloodlust. Their eyes glowed. Their claws gleamed. Their rage mirrored his own.

Rowan didn’t flinch. He could feel the fire crawling beneath his skin, see his hands trembling, and all the while, a faint urge pulled at his thoughts.

He needed to—

Rowan firmly shook his head, a low growl making its way up his throat.

What he needed to do was obvious.

He let go of the restraints he’d unknowingly put on himself, drawing deeply from his Core.

Mana burned through his channels like molten lead, heavy and thick. Even the usually calm Wind felt turbulent, zipping across his body as spells manifested.

[Tailwind] was the first spell he cast, a sudden gale blowing against his back. Rowan launched forward, feet pounding against the cracked earth as more mana raced through his channels.

Chains erupted from his back, the links blazing a fiery red.

Rowan rushed at the horde, screaming in fury.

The Demons screamed back.

This race had taken everything from him. His family, home, and future. It was time he took something of theirs in turn.

Off in the horizon, a heartbeat pulsed.

Something was there. Something important. Something Rowan needed to get to.

He reached the horde like a whirlwind of fire and fury, his chains lashing out as Wind blew at his back.

The first Demon died before it could even raise its weapon, Rowan’s spells dismembering it in barely a heartbeat. The smell of charred flesh reached him, but he paid it no mind. The same way he ignored the blood clinging to his coat.

A [Fireball] formed in his hand, hot and heavy, imbued with more mana than sense. It surged through the air as Rowan threw it, exploding against a group of Demons with the force of a Hymn, all the while, his chains never stopped their thrashing.

Rowan didn’t stop. He couldn’t. His eyes were locked on the horde, but his thoughts—if they could even be called that anymore—were consumed by the sound. That pulse. That dull thud. That fucking heartbeat that echoed across the sky, setting the tempo to his Wrath like a metronome.

He tore through the first line like a wildfire through dry grass. His chains moved in wide arcs, ripping through flesh and bone with brutal efficiency. One looped around a Demon’s leg, yanking it off its feet before Rowan slammed a [Wind Blast] through its skull.

Another beast lunged, snarling with too many teeth. Rowan drew deeply from his Core, raising his hand and releasing something that couldn’t even be called a spell. It was a raw outpouring of Intent, formed with barely any thought.

Die. Be gone. Burn.

A wave of flames washed over the lunging beast, swallowing up its cries.

On and on it went. More monsters came, and Rowan dealt with them as they did.

They were nothing.

Weak.

Distractions.

Rowan pushed harder, his muscles screaming in protest, his mana threatening to spiral out of control. But he didn’t care. The heat in his veins wasn’t just magic anymore—it was fury given form, and he wielded it like a weapon.

He was aware, distantly, that something was wrong. That this wasn’t normal. That his anger was too sharp, too potent, like a sword honed to an edge so fine it would break at the first strike.

But none of that mattered.

Not now.

Demons stood all around him, and he wouldn’t stop until he broke them.

He ducked under a barbed tail, rolled forward, and drove his hand into the gut of a howling creature, incinerating it from point-blank rage. The Demon exploded in a spray of ichor and fire, its remains blown away by an errant gust of wind.

Another step forward.

Another body in his wake.

The heartbeat pulsed again.

Rowan’s breath hitched. It was closer now. He could feel it in his chest, pounding in rhythm with his own, matching the cadence of every swing, every kill, every scream that left his throat.

He wasn’t just angry anymore. He was fury incarnate.

A massive Demon charged at him, ten feet tall and covered in scales the color of dried blood. It bellowed, raising a jagged greataxe above its head.

Rowan answered its cry with one of his own, clapping his hands with the force of a hurricane, launching a dozen [Wind Blades] that flayed skin from muscle and muscle from bone.

It stumbled once, then collapsed.

Rowan stepped around the corpse, not sparing it a glance.

Every kill fed the fire. Every spell he cast made it roar louder.

It should have been too much. His Core should have been faltering, his body giving out.

But it didn’t. Because something deeper than mana, deeper than muscles, was driving him forward. The rhythm. The beat. That distant thrum that promised something—something important, something vital—if only he could reach it.

Rowan roared, the sound animalistic and raw.

He would reach it.

He had to.

.

.

Rowan wasn’t sure how much time had passed. It could have been an hour, or it could have been a day, but the horde never grew smaller. The only thing that changed was his opponents.

The deeper into the horde he progressed, the stronger the Demons became. They grew faster, stronger, more durable. But that strange, constant beat still remained just out of reach. Just over the next hill. Just behind the next obstacle.

Rowan’s body was littered with wounds, his blood mingling with that of the Demons he’d slain. But every drop spilled only fed the fury growing in his heart. The pain he should be feeling was dull. Distant. Like a conversation overheard from behind a thick curtain.

And then, as suddenly as the Demons had appeared, they were gone.

Rowan stood alone in a barren landscape, mountains of charred corpses all around him. But why was the anger still there? Why hadn’t it disappeared along with them?

A raw, primal scream tore its way out of his throat, every muscle in his body tense, a hair’s breadth away from snapping.

More chains erupted from his back, his shoulders, his chest.

Rowan lashed out at the dead surrounding him, burning the bodies until nothing but ash remained.

Yesss, a voice whispered in his mind. Kill. Maim. Destroy.

It tried to latch onto his thoughts, using the rage coursing through his veins as a conduit. Tendrils, sharp and quick dug into him, burrowing deep in search of something.

Rowan didn’t even have time to blink as they reached his Core. And they didn’t stop there. The tendrils sank deeper—past his mana, past his mind—slithering towards something more essential.

Towards his Soul.

And for a moment, it worked.

A connection sparked—unnatural, oily, wrong. A tether formed between Rowan and something else, something old and vast and full of Wrath.

A guttural laugh echoed in his mind, full of triumph.

Yes! At last!

Rowan’s Soul pulsed.

Everything stopped.

The scorched earth beneath him fell away like shattered glass. The sky fractured into a thousand bleeding cracks.

Silence.

Rowan stood in a space of formless light and shadow, colorless and endless.

His anger—the raw fury coursing through his veins—didn’t vanish. It shifted.

His breath caught. Not from exhaustion, or pain, but from clarity.

The fog that had clouded his mind was gone. Burned away in an instant like parchment before a flame. But the fire in his chest still roared—only now, it was his. Not borrowed. Not planted. Not poisoned.

Just raw, undiluted rage.

He looked around, blinking in the sudden stillness.

Then he saw it.

Across the void, something twisted and massive pulsed like a diseased heart, black veins spreading through its flesh like cracks in obsidian, horns growing from its brow.

And it was staring at him.

Not with glee. With terror.

“No,” it whispered, stepping back like the distance between them could protect it. “This is wrong. This is not—this is not supposed to happen.”

Rowan didn’t need to move. He simply willed himself forward.

The Demon recoiled.

“You were mine. Your soul was open. Your fury should have invited me—!”

Rowan said nothing. His heartbeat echoed again, not in his ears, but in the very air around him.

This creature had tried to touch his Soul. And in doing so, Rowan’s Soul had touched it back.

“You wanted my anger?” Rowan’s voice was quiet—eerily calm.

He drew closer.

“You fed it. Pushed it. Tried to claim it.”

He drew closer still.

Rowan’s eyes burned like twin stars. Something ancient and untouchable surged beneath his skin, blazing like a cataclysm unleashed. Unyielding. Eternal.

“But this anger isn’t yours. It never was.”

He raised a hand.

“It’s MINE.”

Rowan closed his fist, and everything shattered.
