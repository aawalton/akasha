Sky Mother Sarah looked down from the top of her mountain, the city of her people traveled down the mountain, in beautiful wooden and white stone buildings. Her home was at the very top, the jewel of the angels, giving her a view of the vast fields and forests of her people’s carefully protected land.

Despite the beauty of the view, the words wouldn’t stop playing in her head, and that damnable laughter kept echoing.

“Even the Mother closest to the darkness is shielded by light! HaHAhaHaha.”

She protected the world from the darkness. She guided it into the light. Yes, she’d dirtied her hands to protect her people, but every Mother had done the same.

It was a lie. Designed to destroy her.

She couldn’t let it.

She covered her face with her hands, blocking the view of the world. Again, the words invaded her mind, threatening her sanity.

Anyone who fought the darkness as much as she did, would be tempted by that power. Would be tempted to have the power to bring their loved ones back.

No, the creature had come for her because she was the closest to the light. And if it brought her down, it meant that it could bring down the others.

That was the truth.

It must be the truth.

Elizabeth hurried through the council hall, her hair a mess, and the papers in her arms nearly blocking her vision.

Without missing a step, she moved around the two human guards standing outside Councilor Ryan’s room.

One of the guards, the one from the Human delegation called out to her, his spear lowering, but she ignored him as she shoved the door open with her hip.

Voices that had been raised, quieted as she scuttled through the room to the desk, and placed the papers down.

Councilor Ryan gave her a tight smile before turning to the man across from him.

A bone mask covered the Saint’s face, and two bird-skull earrings jingled with the tilting of his head as he studied the woman who’d barged into their arguing.

She bowed to the both of them, then stepped over to stand behind Councilor Ryan.

“What are these?” Soul asked, his voice tight.

“Reports.”

“Reports?”

“Yes.”

Soul’s eyes narrowed behind his mask, “Do not try to distract me. The Mother has made a request of you, Councilor.”

Councilor Ryan shook his head as he sat behind his desk and grabbed one of the papers. He looked it over with a quick glance, “Mother Risha has made no such request.”

“Don’t dodge out of this. My patience is running thin.”

Councilor Ryan looked up from the paper. “I do not answer to your Mother, Saint. She forsook us long ago.”

“She did no such thing. Any of you were welcome into her home-”

“And what did she do when the Alveronian King executed the entire Marako bloodline for the mistake of a child? Nothing. What did she do when entire tribes were forced into slavery for the sin of being barbarians? Nothing.”

The mana in the room thickened, drawing Elizabeth’s gaze as the particles vibrated with the intensity of Soul’s anger. “She gave the other kingdoms freedom, choice, but all were welcome into her lands, always.”

Ryan shook his head, “It doesn’t matter. I no longer answer to her, and she has no power here.”

Soul stepped forward, the mana swirling around him in a vortex. “You are her child, no matter who you claim to serve. She has power through me.”

“Calm.” Elizabeth’s voice echoed through the room, and the agitated mana in the room stilled.

Soul froze, and looked at her in shock.

Elizabeth bowed her head, “We are your allies, Saint Soul.”

“My assistant is correct, Saint Soul. We may be Mother Risha’s children, but we are still allies, friends even.” Councilor Ryan grabbed the top sheet off the pile of reports, then placed it on his desk for Soul to see.

Soul’s eyes flickered between the sheet and the Councilor.

“This lists the allocation of resources for my people, Saint. Despite being humans, every single one of my people are guaranteed food, shelter, and work. Not one child goes hungry, not one person is thought of as a slave or servant.” Councilor Ryan grabbed another sheet, and laid it next to the one he’d placed before the Saint. “This is a request from a farmer of no lineage, asking for manastone lights. Not food, not bandages, not blankets. Manastone lights. A luxury beyond any one of our imaginations. Why would I ever even think of betraying the woman who brought this to us?”

“I’m not asking you to betray-”

“You want an insider. You want the will of the Human Mother through me.” Councilor Ryan stood tall, his grizzled face who’d seen far too much death and war staring with an inner strength that filled the room. “I will not give you that.”

Soul held the Councilor’s gaze, then bowed his head. “Your will is your own, Councilor Ryan. Mother will respect your choice.”

The Saint turned, then paused, his gaze meeting Elizabeth’s.

Elizabeth held it for a split second, then looked down.

Soul left the room, the door closing softly behind him.

Councilor Ryan sagged and fell into his chair. He tapped the papers with a small smile, “How’d you know?”

“He’s been trying to get you alone for weeks. I’ve been preparing.”

Ryan’s head fell against his desk. “Remind me to give you a raise.”

“That’ll be the third time this month.” Elizabeth said as she walked up to the papers and rearranged them to the sheets that actively needed his attention.

“You deserve it.” He slowly pushed himself back into a proper sitting position, “That was terrifying. Can you ask Jenn if she can see me?”

“I already sent for her.” Elizabeth said with a smile. She finished her work, then stepped back. “He’ll approach the Drake Riders next. Do you want me to give them a warning?”

Councilor Ryan hummed, “Ask Caleb if he’d be willing to accept the offer. I want to know what the Saint is really after.”

“Of course, Councilor. Anything else?”

“No. Sun-Chaser asked for your assistance with that trade deal, didn’t she?”

“Yes, Councilor.”

“Good luck.”

“Thank you.” Elizabeth bowed her head, then left the room, the door closing behind her.
