The sun bled gold across the horizon, painting the sea in colors that still felt too peaceful to be real. From my perch atop the council building, the city spread out in the near distance, stirring to life. People meandered across a plaza, their steps slow and aimless, as if they were all waking from a long, shared nightmare.

Five days. It had been five days since the final loop, since I’d chosen this world over my own. Five days of what should have been peace, but every time I looked at that spire, I felt the ache of a thousand phantom wounds, but there was a looseness in my chest now, an absence of the rage that had been a permanent knot in my gut for years. It was a good feeling. A strange one.

Below me, through the wide-open windows of the council chamber, I could see Marina leaning over a table, her finned tail swishing with agitation as she argued a point with a handful of former Chain Breakers and city elders. They were drafting the foundations of a new order, a new set of laws. Building something better from the ashes.

A soft chord, beautiful and melancholic, strummed from behind me.

"A glorious, boring normalcy, wouldn't you say?" Punchinello sighed, his wings folded neatly behind him. He strummed again, the notes hanging in the crisp morning air. "Writing laws, organizing food distribution, debating fishing rights. It’s all terribly…civic."

I managed a small smile. "Beats fighting Void Hunters for breakfast."

"Oh, infinitely," he agreed, but his cheer didn't quite reach his eyes. His gaze drifted toward the rubble at the base of the spire, where he’d found…it. "Still… It’s hard to fully enjoy the new symphony when you know there’s still a sour note hiding in the orchestra."

A cold prickle of unease traced its way down my spine. The sword. The Blade of Collapsing Stars. We’d secured it in the armory, behind every spiritual ward we could devise, but its mere existence felt like a splinter in my mind, a promise that peace was temporary. We had no idea what it was capable of, or who it was calling.

Punchinello’s mood seemed to shift, his theatrical sigh replaced by a genuine one. He stared north, toward the distant, hazy horizon. "This has been wonderful, Zale. Truly. Building a city from the groundup is truly the act of a lifetime!” He paused. "But…my own people still live under the Frost Baroness's heel. She works them to the bone in her mines, silences any who speak out. They're not free."

The ache in my chest was instant and sharp. Over the course of the last few days, Punchinello brought me up to speed with his issues—and why he’d set out for the Stormcaller’s Melody in the first place. His people were oppressed, and the song, somehow, would help them. Whenever he talked about them, I couldn’t help but see the faces of Beachstriders in a food line, the contempt in the Tidewalkers' eyes, the memory of my own father being screwed over by men in power. The injustice of it was a familiar poison.

"Are we the only ones who were freed?" I murmured, the question hanging heavy between us.

Punchinello was silent a moment. “The answer hasn’t changed.”

I clenched my jaw. The news he’d gathered from passing merchants wasn't good. Oppression, corruption, petty wars—the rest of the world hadn't gone through a reality-shattering apocalypse. It was just…business as usual. My desire to do something, to help him, to fly to the Frostlands and shatter the Baroness's regime, was a physical pull.

But my duty was here. And my heart… my heart was torn.

I thought of Rosamae. She’d wanted to go to the Whisperwind Wilds immediately to see her sick brother, but she’d stayed. For me. For this. We’d sent the healing elixir I’d crafted with a fast-flying Skyborn courier yesterday, but I knew she was vibrating with anxiety, torn between her duty here and her love for her family. I was supposed to go with her. I wanted to go with her. But Wavehaven needed a leader, and this fragile new government needed an anchor.

The familiar tug-of-war between duty and desire twisted in my gut. Rebuilding. Or running to the side of the people I loved.

A sharp, distinct ping echoed in my mind—a direct message. My interface materialized at the edge of my vision, a single line of text from Rosamae burning with urgency.

My stomach dropped. The sunrise, the peaceful city, the debate over laws—it all evaporated in an instant, replaced by the ice-cold certainty that our five days of peace were officially over.

The message read:

Zale. Come to the armory. Immediately. It’s the sword.
