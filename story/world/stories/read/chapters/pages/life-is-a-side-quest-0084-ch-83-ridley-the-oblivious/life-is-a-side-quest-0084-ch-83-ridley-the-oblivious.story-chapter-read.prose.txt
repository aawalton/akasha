While rummaging through her closet for something to wear to the theater, Ridley found herself unsatisfied. She hadn’t managed to go clothes shopping since getting her job or her powers. She wasn’t short on coin; she just wished she had someone to go with.

She searched her memory for her last true female friend but hesitated to call any of them real friends—more like nearby space-occupying acquaintances. The realization frustrated her more than it once had; she felt she had been doing a great job making friends lately. Well, she would just have to fix that.

Clothes shopping alone felt a bit lame. Maybe she would toss out some old clothes when they moved into the new apartment and use it as an excuse. Fortunately for now, she had an amazing solution to the problem.

Ridley held out her hand with her new gold ring and said, “Disguise Self!”

She watched in the mirror as her clothes transformed into a new outfit: red Converse sneakers with patterned black laces decorated with the Akatsuki’s red clouds, faded and slightly frayed pants held up by a studded grey belt, and a white shirt featuring a stuffed lion character, Kero-chan from Cardcaptor Sakura, on the front. A complementary necklace shaped like a winged key hung around her neck. Having watched a few episodes of Attack on Titan on her new cell phone, she added one of the Survey Corps’ brown jackets with the iconic winged shield logo on the back. Finally, her feather hair clip changed into an angry symbol from manga.

Whoever had categorized her ring as merely an uncommon item was an idiot—an idiot she would gladly kiss.

Of course, the spell didn’t make any of these clothes real. They were illusory, so Ridley grabbed a real jacket from her closet, then watched in the mirror as she slipped it on and it vanished beneath the illusion.

Just then, a text notification chimed: Sam was pulling up outside. Ridley checked the mirror one last time before she leapt out the front door without a care, jumped down the stairwell, and rolled.

“Parkour!”

Ridley was experimenting to see if she could raise her base stats. Dexterity and constitution seemed the easiest to train, so she had added extra jogging to her routine and watched parkour videos online. Fortunately, her abilities included accelerated healing. She had also bought a bundle of essential oils from that Linda lady in case of emergencies. Would they work like healing potions? They sounded disgusting to drink. Maybe a healing salve or unguent? She briefly considered cutting herself to test one.

As she approached the parking lot, Ridley recognized Sam’s red Hyundai Santa Fe—a practical car, unlike the Lambo-Impala monstrosity Louis had created. She ran around to the passenger side and hopped in.

“Howdy!” Ridley greeted Sam. “Thanks for the ride.”

“No problem. I like your outfit—you look ready for a full-on convention!” Sam said, smiling at her. He wore a classic Trigun shirt featuring Vash the Stampede leaping through the air with a comically terrified expression, as though running away from someone.

“Thanks. I’ve never been to a convention before,” she replied as they headed toward the theater. They spent the drive talking about all the gear and cosplay they would need to check out when the next convention came to town.

After about ten minutes, they pulled into the theater parking lot. The giant orange and yellow ribbons of light outlining the building were impossible to miss. After finding a decent spot, they noticed other obvious Ghibli fans heading inside: someone in a likely imported Totoro onesie, and others sporting various themed backpacks, shirts, and animal ears.

As they neared the kiosk, Ridley noticed a large group gathered at the front of the theater, circling one tall figure and snapping pictures while commenting on his outfit. A few people shuffled away nervously. When Ridley realized what was happening, she smiled to herself and felt a mischievous impulse surge through her.

When they were about ten feet from the group, Ridley yelled, “Titan attack! Everyone to the Rose Gate!” Sam jumped in surprise as she ran forward. The crowd turned and parted before her as she leapt in the air; she threw a punch at the person in the center. Bru-haha turned toward her without a hint of surprise, grinned, and blocked the swing with his forearm. He stood tall and gestured dramatically with his arms.

“Luke! Join me and together we can rule the empire as father and son!” he roared.

Ridley couldn’t blame him for missing her reference, but she gave him props for playing along. They continued their mock battle for a few seconds until she tapped his ribs a bit roughly. Bru-haha collapsed dramatically to one knee and raised his hand in surrender.

“I yield! Do with me as you wish, but spare my life,” he petitioned.

“Very well! You shall buy this Ridley popcorn!”

A few people in the crowd laughed. Ridley glowed under the attention as Sam walked up.

“That was hilarious. Is this a friend of yours? That’s the best costume I’ve ever seen.”

Bru-haha smiled as he looked down at himself. He wore a black button-up shirt and distressed jeans; his hair was styled, and Ridley suspected he wore cologne. “Thanks. I enjoy dressing up as a human.”

Missing the joke entirely, Sam likely thought Bru-haha had simply misspoken. Ridley noticed Bru-haha would speak more formally at work and around Alexander but relaxed into his old self around her. They knew Bru-haha was a charismatic orc, but she liked this side of him more, so she was glad when he seemed relaxed and playful.

“Meet Bru-haha. Bru-haha, this is Sam.”

“Please, call me Bru. It’s less suspicious-sounding to humans.” Bru extended his hand.

Sam shook it, then realization hit him when the giant muscled hand grabbed his.

“Wait… HOLY SHIT! YOU’RE…”

Ridley quickly covered his mouth. “Language!” she said to cover his volume.

Sam seemed either shocked or starstruck—she couldn’t tell. She slowly removed her hand.

“He’s really an orc!” he said, looking at Ridley as if informing her.

By now more people were circling Bru, asking for pictures.

“Duh! Sam, I have to apologize. I’ve been keeping a dark secret from you. I’m not really a drug dealer. I work with Bru-haha at Congo.” Ridley clasped her hands behind her back and tilted her head, mimicking a shy girl. “I hope you still want to be friends even though I won’t be hooking you up with the ganja.”

“Are you kidding? I get to hang out and watch a movie with an orc! This is the best date crash ever!”

Date crash? Ridley decided she must have misheard.

Bru-haha called out to the small crowd. “One more picture so I can experience this Princess Mononoke everyone enjoys! Group shot!”

Ridley raised her hand. “I’ll take it!” She collected several phones, and everyone—even Sam—gathered around Bru, smiling. Some threw up the cliché peace signs. Ridley returned the phones, and the three of them headed into The Movie Tavern.

Outside in the dim evening light it had been harder to distinguish the green skin and rippling muscles, but under the bright foyer lights they were unmistakable. The desk workers and ticket takers gawked openly as Bru-haha passed through. Some seemed in denial as he ordered a giant popcorn, avoiding eye contact.

“I am happy to split a giant one if you are. I have been cleaning my hands very thoroughly since taking up residence on Earth.” He held up his hand proudly, displaying the trimmed and polished nails.

“Sure, I will grab us some sodas!” Sam offered.

“I’ll get my own, thanks,” Ridley said abruptly.

“Uh, sure. I don’t want to presume,” Sam replied awkwardly.

Now Ridley felt bad—and very self-conscious. While Sam ordered, she glanced at him a few times. It was as if she had to reassess everything she thought she knew about him. He was handsome—not over-the-top gorgeous, but by no means ugly. He was also nice and a geek. All required attributes for… for what? When he finished and looked back, Ridley turned her head away too quickly. Fortunately, she had bought her ticket on her phone, avoiding any awkward offer-to-pay situation.

They made their way to the auditorium under the stares of fellow moviegoers. They climbed the stairs looking for decent seats near the middle. These older-movie screenings weren’t insanely popular, so there were plenty of options. As they shuffled sideways toward open seats, Ridley felt something smack her butt. She whipped around and saw Sam behind her. Did he just smack her butt? If things hadn’t been awkward before, they definitely were now.

I don’t want anyone presuming, my ass!

It seemed Sam was hoping something would happen tonight. Ridley had never even kissed a boy! She didn’t want her first kiss to be with someone who went around smacking girls’ butts on a first— She heard Sam give a slight surprised yelp and turned to see him looking back at Bru-haha behind him holding his butt. Bru-haha just smiled. Ridley snickered at the thought that Bru had seen what Sam did and retaliated in kind.

They settled into their seats, and Ridley tried to relax and enjoy Princess Mononoke—a pivotal film that had undeniably influenced anime’s rise in popularity in America. The movie always moved her deeply, and she was thrilled to finally see it on the big screen.

“I’m glad you got the chance,” Sam whispered. Apparently Ridley had spoken that last thought aloud.

She supposed Sam was a pretty nice guy, but he was still down some major points. The movie opened with an ominous voice-over.

In ancient times, the land lay covered in forests, where, from ages long past, dwelt the spirits of the gods. Back then, man and beast lived in harmony, but as time went by, most of the great forests were destroyed. Those that remained were guarded by gigantic beasts...

As the film progressed, Ridley made a point of glancing at Bru-haha during the particularly gruesome scenes. To her delight, she caught him bent forward, hands over his mouth, barely containing laughter—especially during the moment Ashitaka severs a man’s arms with a single arrow. Ridley smiled up at a confused Sam before leaning over to whisper an explanation of orc humor in his ear. She wasn’t sure how he took it; his face went blank, and he almost looked like he was blushing. She supposed it might disturb the average person.

At San’s first unmasked appearance, she sucks infected blood from a giant wolf god. She turns to glare at Ashitaka downriver, whom the wolf had smelled. With her hands and face covered in blood, she wipes it casually across her cheek, smearing it further. Ridley felt a strong kinship with the character in that moment.

“Do you know what Mononoke means?” Sam leaned over and asked quietly.

“No, I just thought it was a badass name.”

“It’s Japanese for vengeful spirit or ghost. Weird, right?”

“Yeah, in more ways than one,” Ridley shuddered as she said it.

The word struck uncomfortably close to home. She was pretty sure she had a vengeful ghost hanging out in her head right now. She wasn’t sure if exorcist was a class. Maybe a cleric? She should go talk to some nuns.

About midway through the movie, Ridley sensed something off. She noticed several groups whispering throughout the auditorium. Soon she overheard someone in front of them mention a missing wallet. She caught motion to her left—Sam reaching behind his back—before his eyes went wide.

He looked at her and said, “Someone stole my wallet!” He turned to Bru-haha beside him. “Did you do it? When you smacked my butt?”

“Hey!” Ridley interjected. “You smacked my butt first, so you deserved it.”

Sam looked back at her, shocked. “I didn’t! That would be wildly inappropriate.”
