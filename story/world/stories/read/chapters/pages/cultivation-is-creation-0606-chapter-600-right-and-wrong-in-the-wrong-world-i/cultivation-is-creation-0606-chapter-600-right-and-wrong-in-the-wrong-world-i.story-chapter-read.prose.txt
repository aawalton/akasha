“Please,” Li Na gasped, scrambling backward across the black dream-stone to press herself against the far edge of the step where the void began. "Please, I didn't want to do it. I was just following orders. It was Shen Bolin who made me do it. He said it was necessary for the mission. He's my master and I owe him everything, I couldn’t refuse him. You have to understand, please."

The panic caused her spiritual pressure to flicker erratically around her body, and from the fear alone, it was possible that she was about to be afflicted by cultivation deviation. But this didn’t make me rush, instead I took my time, watching her scramble for justifications while I thought about what to do with someone who'd murdered me less than ten minutes ago. Just thinking about the regretful expression on her face while she stood over my dying body burned my chest.

"I know it was wrong," Li Na continued, her desperation growing as she realised that my silence wasn’t working in her favour. "I know I betrayed you. I’m not asking you to forgive me. You have every right to kill me for it, but I… I can still be useful. My bloodline ability can help us in the steps.”

The girl was smart. I had to give it to her. She understood that the only reason anyone in this world would keep her alive after what she had done was if she had something valuable enough to offer. But what she didn’t know was that I wasn’t from this world, and I wasn’t the kind of person that cared about practicality more than anything else. The moral aspect was just as important, if not more. I decided to turn see what Wu Kangming’s and Xie Ling’s reactions were, and what they thought.

As expected, Wu Kangming's face was completely neutral, showing nothing of what he might be thinking behind those calm eyes. As for Xie Ling, the ice princess stood slightly apart from us with her arms crossed, watching Li Na with a calculating expression.

The smart thing to do was obvious enough that I didn't need to spend much time deliberating over it, and the fact that I was even hesitating suggested I still had more of my original world's sensibilities than I'd thought.

Li Na had betrayed me, tried to kill me, succeeded in killing me actually, and then she'd stood there looking disappointed that I didn't understand why sacrificing me for her survival was the obviously correct choice.

In the cultivation world, in any world really, betrayal had exactly one appropriate response, and that was to kill her.

The reasoning was straightforward enough that even someone with no experience in this kind of situation could follow the logic without difficulty. She'd proven she couldn't be trusted the moment she transferred those fatal injuries to me instead of accepting her own death, and nothing she could say or do now would change that fact about her character.

If I let her live, if I let her join our group and continue toward the Chamber of the Sleeper King, then I'd be creating a situation where someone who'd already betrayed me once would have multiple opportunities to do it again whenever circumstances made it convenient.

That was just asking for trouble, inviting a knife in the back at the worst possible moment because I'd been too soft to make the hard but necessary decision when I had the chance.

The cultivation world didn't reward mercy or second chances or any of the other feel-good concepts that might work in safer environments where betrayal didn't carry immediate life-or-death consequences.

Here, in this palace that had killed countless cultivators over fifty thousand years, letting someone live after they'd proven themselves untrustworthy was the kind of mistake that would get you killed.

So, the answer was that I needed to kill her, but that was easier said than done because killing someone in cold blood after they'd surrendered and begged for their life felt like crossing a line that would change something fundamental about who I was.

Yes, I’d killed people before in the various worlds that I had travelled to, but those had all been life and death battles. This was different. The girl had already lost and surrendered. Executing someone like that felt different to anything before.

Maybe that hesitation made me weak, made me unsuited for the cultivation world where survival often required doing things that would be considered monstrous in other contexts.

Or maybe it meant I was still too attached to the moral frameworks from my original world, and that didn’t fit in a world where the strongest fist ruled but knowing that didn't make it easier to end Li Na's life while she cowered against the stone begging for mercy.

I guess what I could do was let her attempt the trials on her own, force her to either complete the remaining steps solo or try retreating back down the stairway through all the enemies we'd already defeated.

Either path would almost certainly kill her within hours at most, probably much sooner than that given how difficult the trials had been even with a full team of powerful cultivators working together.

The problem was that using the trial's difficulty as a proxy for my own unwillingness to make the hard decision while pretending I'd shown mercy by giving her a theoretical chance at survival felt cowardly.

The option was the one that Li Na herself had already suggested, letting her join our team as a member and using her bloodline ability to help complete the remaining trials, which from a pure utility perspective, it made sense.

The girl had killed three Peak Lucid Lawbearers at the same time with that technique, and combined with my and Wu Kangming's ability to resurrect after death, we could clear through dangerous encounters much faster than anyone else.

Li Na would use her ability to transfer fatal injuries to one of us, we'd die and come back, and the enemy would be dead without us having to risk extended combat where mistakes could pile up and lead to permanent problems.

Sure, it was cruel to allow her to repeatedly experience fatal injuries just so she could transfer them to us and kill our enemies, but it was what she was asking for because she knew that gave her a chance to survive this trial.

I knew that the injuries and pain would transfer to me and Wu Kangming, and we would feel every broken bone, ruptured organ, and severed limb, but both of us could handle it. I'd died from Li Na's transferred injuries once already and came back, and Wu Kangming had been killed by Song Wei and resurrected just as quickly.

A few more deaths to get pass the remaining steps seemed like a reasonable price to pay if it meant reaching the Celestial Dream Lotus successfully. However, I was getting ahead of myself, I wasn’t the only one who would be affected by this decision. Wu Kangming and Xie Ling deserved to be consulted, they were part of my group after all, and they had shown to be trustworthy so far.

I turned away from Li Na's terrified face and looked at Wu Kangming, making eye contact with him across the black dream-stone step.

"What do you think we should do with her?"

"We can't trust her," he said immediately. "She betrayed us, killed you without any real hesitation, and the only reason she's begging for mercy now is because she lost her protection. If the situation were different, if she'd made it to the other side of that barrier with the rest of her group, she'd be standing over there right now feeling proud of her actions."

The bluntness of his assessment was refreshing after all my own internal deliberation about moral lines and what kind of person I wanted to be, it was just a straightforward acknowledgment of the practical realities we were dealing with. And because of Wu Kangming’s experience with Wu Lihua, the sword cultivator had trust issues, which meant he approached situations like this with a lot less internal conflict than me.

"We need to kill her," he continued. "Anything else is just inviting problems later."

I nodded slowly because his logic was sound, his reasoning straightforward, and the fact that he had severe trust issues from his own experiences didn't make his conclusions wrong. If anything, his paranoia about betrayal was probably more appropriate for the cultivation world than my lingering reluctance to execute defenseless people.

I turned to Xie Ling next, curious what the woman who'd hidden her true Lucid Lawbearer cultivation level throughout this entire expedition would have to say about the situation. She’d been the one to drag Li Na across the barrier rather than leaving her with Shen Bolin and Song Wei.

That suggested she saw some value in keeping the girl alive, some reason to preserve her as a resource rather than just letting the palace separate our groups permanently. I wanted to know what that reason was.

"She can still be useful," Xie Ling said. "If she can still use that bloodline ability then it could help us complete the rest of the trials, especially now that we've lost Shen Bolin and Song Wei's combat power and experience.”

Focusing on utility and capability was exactly what I’d expected from her. She didn’t seem the type to be bogged down in moral considerations about trust or betrayal. And she wasn’t wrong about our group being weaker. As we were now, it was unlikely we’d be able to reach the Chamber of the Sleeper King without some trump card.

"I'll use it," Li Na said quickly. "I'll use my bloodline ability to clear the trials, just please don't kill me."

I glanced at Wu Kangming and Xie Ling to confirm if we were all thinking the same thing.

The look of distaste on the sword cultivator’s face told me exactly what he thought about working with someone who'd proven themselves untrustworthy, but he gave a small nod that meant he understood the practical benefits outweighed his personal feelings about the situation.

As for Xie Ling, she looked satisfied that I’d listened to her seriously.

"Fine," I said to Li Na, whose face lit up with relief. "But first, tell us everything about your bloodline technique. How it works, what limitations it has, how many times you can use it, everything relevant to whether this arrangement will actually help us or just create more problems."
