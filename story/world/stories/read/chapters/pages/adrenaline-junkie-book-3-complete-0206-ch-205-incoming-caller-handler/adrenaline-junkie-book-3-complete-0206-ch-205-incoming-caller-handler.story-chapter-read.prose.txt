Glowing beams of moonlight cast a pale shimmer across the blackened edge of the western road as Archie slowly eased his mana bike forward, as he cruised through the forest surrounding Creavenboor.

The city sat in eerie silence, save for the wind brushing through low trees and patches of tall grass flanking the path.

But Archie wasn’t looking at the gate anymore.

He was fixated on the Compass of Desire, still pinned just above his dashboard – its needle violently tugging westward, glowing so bright it looked like someone had crammed a star inside the compass casing. It didn’t jitter or flicker. It was locked on. No mistaking it now – Camilla was in this city.

“Good,” Archie muttered, eyes narrowing as he decelerated.

He’d already circled the perimeter twice, just to be sure. He wasn’t about to waste a whole day entering the city and spend Tallen’s coin on a simple mistake if she wasn’t actually here, and this place just happened to be on the way to Camilla. But now, all signs – compass, gut, and instinct – pointed straight through those looming stone gates.

Still rolling across a stretch of flat, moonlit terrain, Archie tilted his head down slightly toward his gorget. Peeking out from beneath the collar of his armor were the two tiny, gremlin-like faces of Volos and Arsenic, both semi-shielded by leather and glass goggles that perfectly fit their heads and were heavily reinforced to protect their heads when traveling at incredible speeds.

The wind wasn’t bothering them anymore, mostly because they were nodding off as a result of his slowing of speeds to properly survey the walls of Creavenboor.

Seeing their heads lolling and tiny claws lazily gripping his armor, Archie couldn’t help but chuckle to himself and slow the bike even more, scratching the top of both heads with the tips of his fingers.

The response they gave him was immediate – soft chittering, content purring, and little puffs of sleepy warmth as they pressed into the scratches like sleepy cats. In another few seconds, they disappeared with a shimmer, slipping back into his Soulspace without a sound.

He was content in that moment; Volos and Arsenic were finally sleeping – fully tuckered out, and he was zeroing on Camilla.

Just as Archie was about to pull back the throttle and shoot towards the West Gate, a sudden rumble rolled through a Spatial Storage on his arm.

He jerked the bike to a stop, boots skidding over grass and dirt.

Eyes wide, he looked down at his left arm, more confused than alarmed. “...What?” he muttered aloud.

Both Spatial Rings that he kept on his person were on his right hand – beneath his right gauntlet. That had never changed. He always kept it that way.

But now, his left arm was… buzzing?

And it wasn’t just a weird nerve twitch. It was the pulse of something inside a Spatial Storage, he could feel spatial mana within it shift in response to the rumbling. Only–it couldn’t be. He didn’t have storage assigned to that arm.

Cautiously, he twisted his left wrist and held it out forward and to the side before unclasping the buckles that bound his chestplate to his shoulder guard, and with one fluid motion, he shrugged out of the armor covering his left side.

Then he stopped.

And blinked.

There, nestled dead-center on his inner forearm, glowing faintly under moonlight like something ancient and half-awake, was a clean, black numeral: XVI

Archie stared at it in shock.

“…What the fuck is this?” he whispered.

He reached up and touched it – it was just… stuck in place and smooth.

“Great,” he muttered, dragging his hand down his face.

A tattoo.

He clenched his fist, then flattened it again, watching the muscles around the mark flex slightly under his skin.

It didn’t shift.

Archie exhaled slowly, his breath fogging faintly in the chill air. He licked two of his right hand’s fingers and pressed them to the number, trying to smear it like ink – mentally praying that it was, in fact, ink.

Nothing.

He rubbed harder, turning the skin surrounding it pink.

Still nothing.

“...Fuck,” he said again, with a deeper sigh this time.

This wasn’t just bad. This was giga-levels bad.

While Dad wouldn’t care, considering he had a few of his own, Mom would certainly lose her mind if she found out that he had a tattoo.

And Henry? Henry would kill him.

They’d promised they’d get their first tattoo together a couple of months after Henry would have his Honeymoon. Henry planned on getting a voice recording tattoo of Camilla’s cries on his bicep while he, himself, planned on getting a tree branch going around his bicep.

And now? All that planning went down the shitter with him now having one.

Because somehow, some way, he’d gotten a mysterious “XVI” inked into his skin without his consent.

The only thing that mildly eased his stress was finding out you could have a Spatial Storage tattooed directly onto your body. Still, the idea of carrying it on his skin bugged him, especially knowing that, with enough training in spatial magic, a Spatial Mage could just conjure one from thin air, bind it to their soul, and access it anywhere with a flick of their wrist.

While he himself only dabbed a bit into spatial magic, he was curious to learn how have his personal Inventory and be able to access it at any time and place without them needing to be an Accessory or object that he had to carry with him, as he would prefer to have other accessories to take up his two accessory slots instead.

"It's fine, hopefully I can still get it removed," Archie muttered, reminding himself that back on Earth, there was something called Tattoo Laser Removal, and that there was probably something similar in the Multiverse. "Or I could just cut off that part of my arm," he added under his breath, more annoyed than serious.

His fingers twitched toward the edge of his belt, already reaching through the tethered thread of mana that led to one of his Spatial Storages, when the tattooed section of his forearm suddenly began to vibrate again, a lot more violently this time.

“Right,” Archie muttered, tapping the tattoo with a flicker of mana just to see what would happen.

The response to his mana accessing it was instantaneous.

The Spatial Storage activated far more smoothly than his own Spatial Rings, and he was met with a cascade of organized compartments within the newly discovered Spatial Storage. There were rows of items, boxed and labeled, dozens of tools and sealed vials, several books with matte black covers, and at the very top, rattling as if being shaken by unseen hands, was a walkie-talkie.

What the hell? Archie thought to himself as he took out said walkie-talkie and read its blinking, bright screen:

Incoming Caller: HANDLER.

Against all better judgment, he pulled it out and brought it level with his eyes.

The screen blinked once more, just as he twisted the smaller dial on the top, it produced a short, barely audible click, even to his own Perception.

A pause.

Then, a crisp female voice cut through the static:

“Hello, Agent 16. Can you read me?”

Archie blinked.

His eyes flicked from the screen to the tattoo, back to the walkie.

Agent sixteen. XVI.

He stared into the trees for a long moment, wondering if the universe was actually screwing with him, or if this was the slow, inevitable crawl of some past life decision finally catching up to bite him in the ass.

“...Yeah,” he said slowly, pressing down on the talk button with a low click. “I read you.”

“Y-Yeah, I hear you loud and clear,” Archie added, trying to mask the uncertainty in his voice with something close to calm, as internally he was panicking.

“Okay, that’s good,” the woman replied, exhaling faintly like she’d been holding her breath. Then came the shift – one Archie caught immediately, tone sliding into surprise. “Oh. Wow. You’ve already made it to Creavenboor? That’s… that’s great news.”

Archie’s brows furrowed.

I was supposed to come here? Also – wait, does this walkie-talkie thing have a tracker on it? Does it still work while in the tattoo? Does the tattoo have a tracker on it?

No, wait, she only just saw it now, which would mean that only when I took the walkie-talkie from the tattoo’s Spatial Storage was she able to see my location. But then again, she could be faking it. I need to go with the assumption that she’s able to track my location so long as this tattoo is on me.

“Y-yeah,” he replied slowly, hedging, “come afternoon, after the explosion in Greeber, I booked it here.”

“That’s impressive, Agent Sixteen,” she said, voice sharpening just a hair, like she was zooming in on something. “To be able to cover just under five hundred and fifty kilometers in under an hour. I wasn’t aware of any Skyships leaving Greeber during that window.”

Damn, Archie blinked. Under an hour? Damn, it felt a lot shorter than… oh right, I took my time circling the city, so, yeah, I guess that sounds right.

“Uh, no,” he said, keeping his voice steady. “I drove. On my bike.”

A pause.

Then, a quiet “Oh…” from the other end.

The line went silent for a second. Archie heard soft scratching, maybe a pen against parchment or stylus over mana-paper. And then, just beneath that, muffled muttering. He couldn’t make out the words.

“…Where did you get such a bike?” the woman asked, tone still professional, but now laced with clear curiosity. “It wasn’t included in the bundle you received prior to being accepted into the I.F.O.”

I.F.O… Could that be the acronym that was mentioned in my Amnesiac Noble Questline Description? Archie mused to himself.

“No, uh,” Archie answered, reaching deep for a calm that didn’t exist. “I forged it myself.”

Another pause filled his ears as more scratching and murmurs could be heard from the other line, along with the soft creaking of someone shifting in their seat.

God, he missed his gaming chair. He really needed to find a chair that was just like it, if not better – if that was even possible.

“…We weren’t aware your smithing had advanced to the point where you could forge a vehicle capable of such speeds,” the woman finally said. “This will be noted in your file. How long ago did you forge this bike?”

Archie didn’t skip a beat. “Recently,” he lied smoothly. “I’d been building the components over time and just… finished assembling it in my free time.”

“…Noted,” the woman replied, this time without the pause. “I’ll add that as an annotation in your personal file. It seems your Profession Instructor neglected to include details of your smithing aptitude.”

Archie blinked. Profession Instructor? He didn’t even know he had one. I’m utterly fucked if she asks me their name.

There was another faint shuffling of paper on the other end, as if she were flipping pages in a thick file. He couldn’t tell if her walkie-talkie’s microphone was particularly sensitive or if she was letting him hear it on purpose. Either way, it was incredibly intimidating for someone who was winging every word that was coming out of his mouth.

“Anyway,” she said, voice smoothing out, “apologies for the small interrogation. I’ve only just been assigned as your Handler. Things tend to be chaotic when we rotate new Intelligence and Field Operatives into active deployment – especially with the change in director we had yesterday.”

Change in director? Deployment? Archie thought before coming to a realization. I.F.O. stands for Intelligence and Field Operatives, and this was definitely tied to his Class and sounded a lot more interesting compared to the other options he was presented with for a Questline.

“Nevertheless,” she continued, tone shifting to something close to formal cheer, “congratulations on the successful completion of your first I.F.O. operation. The mission with your fellow initiates to destabilize the city of Greeber and plunge it into chaos was deemed a success.”

Archie stared down at the walkie-talkie in shock. His mouth opened for a moment before closing.

…I’m sorry, what?
