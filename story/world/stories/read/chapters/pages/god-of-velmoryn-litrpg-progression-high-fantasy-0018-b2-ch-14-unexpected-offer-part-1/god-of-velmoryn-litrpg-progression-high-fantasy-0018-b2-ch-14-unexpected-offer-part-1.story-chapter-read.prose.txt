I immediately recognized the energy.. That silver aura, how could I ever forget it? It was the same divine energy I had absorbed from the Goddess’s statue I destroyed. Back then, I had acted on impulse, never stopping to consider the consequences. Yet by some stroke of fortune, it had all worked in my favor. That single act had defined everything that followed.

Now, standing before her raw energy once again, the same instinct surged within me. But this time, I refused to act on impulse.

Guidance.

[Guidance Failed!]

Using Guidance on items held by the God of Merchants (or one of their avatars) is forbidden.

“Why did you assume I’d be interested in it?” I asked, fishing for answers.

“Forgive my rudeness, Your Excellency. I had wrongly assumed an item containing one of the deceased Elf’s souls would be of value to you,” he gasped, as if disappointed in his own short-mindedness.

He began to draw the sphere back toward his tuxedo - slowly, far too slowly to be anything but bait. Yet I had no choice but to take it.

“I’d like to inspect it closely,” I said, trying to keep at least a shred of dignity. Admitting outright that I wanted to buy it would make me look like someone who changed his mind too easily. But more importantly, I wanted to separate V and the sphere.

V hesitated, though briefly. He then bowed, and the sphere drifted from his hand toward my manifestation on the throne. A bright sheen wrapped around it as his aura flared, making the silver glow swell as it crossed the space between him and the God of Velmoryn puppet.

So it’s true. This confirms that he can use his divine power inside my dominion. But how?

I had always believed my dominion to be a place where my will was absolute. Where no one, not even another god, could exert their own power unless they were vastly superior to me. Yet V was forcing me to reconsider that belief once again. If he could freely use his divine power here, then perhaps only weaker deities were bound by my domain’s restrictions. Deities who weren’t much stronger than Pintre.

This meant that I would need to be careful in the future, especially when allowing other gods entry.

Even if my manifestation was merely a puppet, it was still enough for what I intended. All I needed was to purge the God of Merchants’ divine energy from the orb and then examine it using Guidance. The earlier notification had stated that Guidance couldn’t activate while the sphere was in V’s possession. Now that it rested in my hands, I intended to test whether that limitation still applied.

[Soul Core - Unique!]

Every creature’s soul naturally contains divine energy; this is why powerful beings may yield Soul Essences upon death. The Elves, however, were an exception. Their race was not born of nature but created by the Goddess of Elves, who sacrificed a vast portion of her own divinity to give them life. As a result, each Elf became timeless, as the divinity within their souls cannot be depleted by ordinary means.

This Core is one of the final two the Goddess of Elves ever created, though she never granted them physical form. To use it, one must possess the Authority of the God of Elves…

Additional information is restricted as you lack the required Authority.

I already knew the Goddess had created the souls of Elves. What I hadn’t known was that she had birthed their entire race. I had assumed she merely inherited Elvish Authority, just as I had gained dominion over the Velmoryns.

How did she learn to shape souls? And more importantly, how did she gather so much divinity without believers?

If she truly created the Elven race, then she must have accumulated immense Divinity Points before them, when she had no believers or perhaps through another race that existed prior, one whose identity was now lost to time.

Wait. If Elves can’t reproduce without the Goddess creating their souls, how were other gods attempting to replicate that process? If I managed to learn this information so easily, how come other gods couldn’t? And how did that unknown god manage to breed Elves with demons to create the Velmoryns?

This small piece of information raised too many questions; ones I had no intention of unraveling while V stood before me. I now understood why he had offered me the Core, but understanding it only deepened my suspicion.

I already knew someone had inherited, or rather usurped, the Goddess’s Elvish Authority. Even if it was incomplete, that god would undoubtedly pay far more for this Core than I ever could. They would have both the wealth and the motivation.

So why me? Why would the God of Merchants, driven by profit above all, pass up a far richer deal? This wasn’t carelessness. A being like him would never make mistakes that large.

Which left only one possibility - letting me obtain the Core somehow served his purpose.

But how?

“I’m indeed interested in obtaining this,” I said at last, realizing the silence had stretched too long. The slight nod and sly smile V gave me confirmed he had already known my answer.

“I’m overjoyed that Your Lordship finds the item pleasing,” he said with a deep bow. “My liege has decided to lower the price as a gesture of goodwill, to show that we are truly sorry for the unfortunate mistake I made.”

I simply nodded, waiting for him to continue.

“We ask only for the value Your Lordship could extract from the Essence itself. We shall not raise its price based on its purpose,” he said, still bent at the waist. Even so, I caught the faint change in his voice and how his smile had slowly dimmed.

Lowering the price? Claiming he only wants what I could gain from using it as a Soul Essence? Could it be that the God of Merchants doesn’t even know what this truly is? V called it Essence, not Core… Is it possible Guidance is unique to me?

I still didn’t trust him. A god whose domain was trade and value not having a skill like Appraisal was far too convenient to believe.

“How much?” I asked curtly, choosing not to question why V had brought this item to me instead of the one who held the Elven Authority. If I pressed too far, I might reveal something he didn’t yet know.

“Fifteen hundred Divinity Points, Your Lordship!” V said casually, as if he weren’t asking for nearly a fourth of what I had. “The price cannot be lowered further, of course, but we will gladly accept any item of equivalent worth, or even valuable information,” he added quickly, likely mistaking my silence for hesitation.

His words, however, sparked an idea.

“I’ll buy it with Divinity Points,” I said evenly. “You still owe me around seven thousand; deduct it from that.”

I calculated that, unless I ascended in rank and lifted that limitation, it would take nearly seven months to receive what the God of Merchants already owed me. Letting that much divinity sit idle in another deity’s hands without serving me was unreasonable.

V nodded eagerly and conjured a silver contract that shimmered faintly in my realm. It stated that I had purchased the Essence which contained a deceased Elf’s soul.

Only then did I notice the inconsistency. When describing the Core, V had claimed it contained the soul of a dead Elf. Yet Guidance had shown that the soul in this Core had never gained a physical form.

That meant one of them was wrong.

And since Guidance had never once been mistaken, I knew which one to trust.

“Now that I’ve obtained the sphere,” I said, moving it beyond the mist where the Angel and Karla hovered in the air. “I must ask, do you know what it is?”

“Wh…” V stammered, his composure cracking. “It’s… Soul Essence.”

“Partly, yes, but it has one more function,” I replied, letting the words linger. Then I shifted the topic before he could recover. “What other items are you selling, V?”

He didn’t answer. His body froze mid-motion, mouth twitching as it moved unnaturally fast, too fast for speech. At this point, I was almost certain he was communicating with the God of Merchants himself. Which meant my assumption had been right; for some reason, they had failed to obtain information about the Core.

“Your Lordship,” V said after a pause, regaining control of his body, “I’ll gladly present the list of our wares… but first, we wish to know what Your Highness believes this Es… this item to be.” He bowed his head slightly, eyes fixed on my manifestation atop the throne.

“First, let’s discuss the price,” I said, suppressing a laugh. Nothing pleased me more than sensing profit, and this time, I knew I was about to gain plenty.

“Five hundr…” V began, but I made my puppet’s lips curve in displeasure.

“We cannot offer more without knowing its value, Your Lordship,” he said hastily. “We allowed Your Highness to inspect the item before deciding whether He wished to purchase it.”

“True, but information loses its value the moment it’s revealed,” I said. “Here’s my offer - there are two things I know about this item that you want to know, and I’m willing to share them for the right price. We’ll trade information for information. But only if you agree that no one will ever learn, be hinted, gestured, or informed in any way that I possess this item.”

V nodded without hesitation and conjured a silver contract. It bound him to secrecy and even had severe penalties if his own information failed to match mine in worth.

“I’ll go first,” I said, satisfied with the contract. “And know this, the first piece of information is the lesser one. You were right that the sphere contains an Elven soul… but not of a dead one. It was never given a physical form.”

V’s eyes widened, and again his mouth blurred into motion. The silent conversation didn’t last long.

“Your Lordship,” he said finally, voice trembling, “my Liege wishes to express his utmost respect. You are truly a being every merchant should aspire to become.”

I accepted the compliment with a faint nod and waited.

“Your Highness may choose between two,” V said quickly. “First - two eggs of a creature that no longer exists in this world. They are the last of their kind, a breeding pair. We cannot guarantee they’ll reproduce, but they are immune to Divinity. In the future, Your Lordship may find them… useful.”

I nodded, realizing he was hinting about my inevitable conflict with Night God, but remained silent.

“The second,” he continued, “is knowledge. We can grant Your Lordship information about the surrounding kingdoms beyond the forest You dwell in. And not merely written records. We guarantee the knowledge will include terrain, culture, and even language…”
