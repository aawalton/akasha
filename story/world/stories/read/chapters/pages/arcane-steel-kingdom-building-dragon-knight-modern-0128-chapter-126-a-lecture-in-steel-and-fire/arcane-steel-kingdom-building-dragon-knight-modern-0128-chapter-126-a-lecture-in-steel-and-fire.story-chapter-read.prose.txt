The freezing, black water of the newly formed river churned with unnatural violence, tearing at the muddy banks of the valley.

On the Cinderfall side, panic had given way to frantic, desperate industry. The vanguard captains screamed themselves hoarse, directing units of conscripts to chop down the few petrified trees that dotted the frozen landscape. They threw thick, braided ropes across the churning rapids, attempting to secure a rudimentary bridge.

The river swallowed their efforts. The current was simply too strong, snapping the thick hemp lines like rotted twine and dragging the heavy timbers downstream before they could be anchored.

"It's no use!" a mud-splattered captain yelled, pulling a shivering soldier back from the collapsing bank. "The current is pulling them straight into the kill-zones! We have to use the mages!"

It was a bitter concession. Every ounce of mana spent building a bridge was an ounce of mana not spent defending against the inevitable artillery barrage. But they had no choice. The King was coming, and if he arrived to find his vanguard staring helplessly at a puddle, heads would roll.

The combat mages were pushed to the front lines. They stood shoulder-to-shoulder, their hands glowing with raw, earth-elemental magic. Slowly, agonizingly, massive blocks of bedrock were torn from the valley floor and levitated over the rushing water.

They dropped the stones with heavy, splashing thuds, creating a series of reinforced pillars in the riverbed. They didn't build a solid dam—the water pressure would have shattered it in minutes. Instead, they laid thick, flat slabs of stone across the pillars, leaving wide, engineered gaps between them to let the raging torrent pass underneath.

They worked frantically, building three narrow footbridges for the infantry, and one massive, reinforced causeway designed to bear the weight of the heavy siege ballistae and supply carriages.

"Keep the barriers up!" the lead mage screamed, sweat pouring down his face despite the freezing air. "Do not let the shield drop for a second!"

A shimmering, golden dome of force snapped into existence over the newly formed causeway. It hummed with strained energy, a magical umbrella designed to deflect the inevitable rain of plasma fire from the aerial bombardments. The bridge was built, but crossing it was going to be a march through hell.

…

High above the clouds, far from the mud and the frantic construction, King Theron Flavius flew at the head of a burning spear.

Fifty thousand Phoenix Knights formed a V-formation behind him, their mounts setting the bruised twilight sky ablaze. It was the full, terrifying might of the Cinderfall Hegemony. Every single combat-capable knight from every corner of the kingdom had been called to battle.

Yet, looking at the glowing armada trailing behind him, Theron felt a cold knot of dread tightening in his gut.

The recent, desperate conscription efforts had swelled their ranks by five thousand, but the numbers were a lie. Those men were green. They were boys pulled from farms and workshops, hastily bonded to young, inexperienced phoenixes. They were still under training. Most of them hadn't even experienced their first death yet, and Theron knew from brutal experience that a large percentage of knights never recovered from the psychological trauma of burning to ash and returning.

Coupled with the recent losses on the coast, nearly seven thousand of his current fifty thousand knights had never seen real combat. The situation was grim.

The only thing left defending the mainland, anchoring his broken supply lines, were the nation's Mage Towers.

Cinderfall currently maintained seventeen active spires. It was a far cry from their glory days. Naturally, the legendary Maghri Vex had originated from Cinderfall, helping the First King develop the very concept of the Mage Towers. In the early days of the Hegemony, the magic woven by Vex was used to hold the newly conquered territories, providing an impenetrable anchor, while the Phoenix Knights acted as the immortal spearhead to push the lines ever outward.

Cinderfall back then had been a terrifying, perfectly balanced machine of expansion.

But pride and arrogance over the centuries had whittled down their strength. The royal family had focused heavily, almost exclusively, on the glorious, visible power of the Phoenix Knights, severely neglecting the cultivation of mages and the upkeep of the Towers.

As Theron flew toward the front, he was starting to realize just how much his family had blundered. The brutal efficiency with which the Mage Towers of House Black were currently dismantling his loyalist garrisons was a stark, bloody reminder of the power his ancestors had carelessly tossed aside.

Suddenly, a sharp, searing pull ripped through the bond with his Tier 8 phoenix.

Theron gasped, his gauntlets crushing the leather reins. A command slammed into his skull, heavy and absolute. The Mother Phoenix was speaking to him. Her will bridged the hundreds of miles from her perch atop the capital's dome, vibrating directly into his soul.

When we close on the battle zone, all riders will dismount, the progenitor’s mental voice resonated, echoing with absolute, alien authority in the minds of fifty thousand knights simultaneously. The phoenixes will shrink and perch atop your shoulders.

Theron frowned, his strategic mind instinctively rebelling against the command. A cavalry charge without the mounts?

The enemy possesses superior aerial maneuverability and suppression fire, the Mother Phoenix communicated, anticipating the objection. It makes no sense for us to provide them with a larger target.

King Theron gripped his reins tightly, swallowing his pride. He wasn't the one in charge here. Every phoenix obeyed the Mother without question. In an extinction-level event like this, the Mother Phoenix's orders entirely superseded his own. He was just a passenger now.

Bob’s boots hit the ground next to the forward command post with a heavy thud. Aquarius landed beside him, the water dragon’s massive claws sinking into the dirt.

Bob wore the uniform of a Knight of House Wight, modified specifically for him. It featured deep black tactical mesh beneath the traditional silver plating, and five polished obsidian stars lined his collar. He was the only Five-Star General. He was also the only person permitted to wear this specific uniform. It made him a double unicorn in the military hierarchy—the supreme commander of the ground forces and the last living Dragon contractor outside the main family.

The iconic silver dragon-knight blade hung strapped to his left hip. The black-hilted plasma katana rested on his right, currently powered off. His specialized Mark II-G MECH was still in transit via heavy lifter; flying ahead with Aquarius had been vastly faster.

He took a moment to run a gauntleted hand along his partner's damp, muscular neck. "Are you ready for what is to come?"

Aquarius looked down at him, his watery form shimmering under the harsh camp lights. They shall feel my retribution. Aquarius held a bitter grudge against the fire-wielders for the lives of his clanmates Cinderfall had taken four years ago. The elemental advantage was just an added bonus.

They walked into the sprawling command tent. Valen stood over a holographic table, organizing logistics and briefing a dozen commanding officers. As Bob entered, Valen snapped to attention and saluted. The officers immediately stood up from their folding chairs and struck their chests in unison.

Valen dropped his hand. A distinctly smug look crossed the young elf's face. He turned to the gathered cadets and officers.

"Sir Goliath has arrived," Valen announced smoothly. "He has faced Phoenix Knights in open combat and is best suited to brief you on handling them in the field. I know we all studied this in the Academy, but a refresher from the Supreme Commander won't hurt."

Bob cursed internally. He glared at Valen. He hated public speaking. He hated stages. He would much rather face five hundred Hegemony soldiers unarmed than stand in front of fifty admiring cadets waiting for words of wisdom. The expectation in their eyes was suffocating.

Bob reluctantly climbed onto the small raised staging area. He cleared his throat. It sounded like gravel in a tin can.

"As you all may know," Bob began, his voice a low rumble, "Phoenix Knights and their mounts can come back from the brink of death. They restore lost limbs instantly. Even a single drop of blood is enough for them to completely restore their physical body."

The commanders and officers immediately began tapping frantically on their glass-and-metal data pads with their styluses. Bob watched them, baffled. This was basic curriculum at the Aegis Legionary Academy. Why were they recording this like it was classified intelligence?

He pushed past the awkwardness and continued. "Some of you might assume the easiest way to kill them is by focusing all your fire on the knight. That is possible, but it is a prolonged and inefficient process. The number of large feathers on the phoenix's tail determines the number of times it can recover. That is where the predicament lies."

He paused, letting the silence command their attention. "Almost always, one full feather is consumed to revive the phoenix itself. However, its contracted knight can be revived or healed using only a partial use of one feather."

Two high-resolution images warped onto the interactive whiteboard behind him. Bob glanced back and saw Valen’s smug face again. The elf had prepared the slides specifically to force Bob into this presentation.

"As you can see from the images behind me," Bob said, pointing to the board. "The feather on the left is vibrant. Full of light. It has a lighter color. This signifies a fully charged feather. The one on the right is wilted and has a dimmer color. That feather has been used to revive its partner or partially heal a lost limb."

An officer in the second row raised her hand. Bob stopped. Usually, cadets waited for the end of a briefing. Before Bob could ask her to hold the question, Valen gestured encouragingly to the cadet.

"Sir," the cadet asked, standing up. "Does the used feather lead to more power being taken? If the phoenix itself is killed and requires a full revival, does the next full feather get dimmer and lose power?"

Bob looked at Valen. Valen smiled innocently. This was going to be a very long day.

"No," Bob answered. "Even a partial, wilted feather is capable of a full revival of the phoenix bird itself. That is why it is almost always pointless to focus your attacks on the knight instead of the phoenix. You will just waste their partial charges while leaving the bird fully capable of resurrecting."

The scratching of styluses grew louder.

"You determine the rank of a phoenix by counting the tail feathers," Bob continued. "One for Rank 1, up to eight for Rank 8. The Hegemony never sends any phoenix below Rank 3 to the battlefield. They consider it a waste of resources."

He brought up a new slide showing tactical formations. "Phoenixes are capable of taking a larger form to act as a mount, or shrinking to perch atop their contracted knight's shoulders."

"There is a specific scenario we need to address. When the phoenix is perched atop the rider's shoulders, it actively boosts the attack power of its knight with concentrated fire magic. Expect their destructive output to double in this formation."

He leaned forward, resting his hands on the podium. "Another tactic. A phoenix can sometimes stay behind at a safe location, giving its knight its physical feathers to carry into battle. If it gives all of its feathers to the knight, each time the knight dies, it forces a full use of the feather, not a partial one."

The room was dead silent now.

"If all the feathers are used up in this state, both the knight and the phoenix experience true death, regardless of the physical distance between them."

"Furthermore, it works both ways. If a phoenix without any feathers is killed—even if the knight is miles away holding five feathers in his pouch—both will experience permanent death."

He tapped the podium for emphasis. "That is why they never deploy that strategy. A phoenix usually leaves one or two feathers attached to itself if the knight heads into battle without them. But in that case, the phoenix cannot boost the combat capability of the knight from afar."

Bob pulled up a final diagram. "There is an alternative way for the knight to utilize higher-tier flame magic. They can actively consume the power from the severed phoenix feather they carry, draining its energy to cast spells, which causes the feather to wilt like the image behind me."

Another commander raised his hand. Valen nodded at him.

"Sir," the commander asked. "What if the knight uses up all their carried feathers, but the phoenix back at the base still has one left attached to its tail? What happens to the knight?"

Bob nodded. It was a solid tactical question.

"In that scenario, the knight remains dead," Bob explained. "They will stay dead until the phoenix physically makes its way to the corpse and revives him. There is much unknown about this specific interaction, as Cinderfall guards the secret heavily. However, our intelligence hypothesizes there is a strict time limit. If the phoenix takes too long to reach the location where the knight was killed, the revival fails, and both die."

More hands shot up in the audience. Dozens of them. The commanders kept raising questions about engagement ranges, thermal shielding, and plasma penetration rates.

Bob sighed, gripping the edges of the podium. He was definitely going to punch Valen in the jaw later.
