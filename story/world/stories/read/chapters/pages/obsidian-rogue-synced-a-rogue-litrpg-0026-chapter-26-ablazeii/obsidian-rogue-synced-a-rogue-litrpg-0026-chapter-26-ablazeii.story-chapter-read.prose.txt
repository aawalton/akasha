The words jolted Levi into action, and within a handful of seconds, he was by her side. A grimace on his face as he assessed the wound. The arrow had pierced right through her shoulder, almost as easily as the one that had pierced his arm.

In the heat of the moment, he’d forgotten that he himself was also sporting a serious injury. The only difference was the location of the hits they’d both endured. He had an arrow sticking out of his right arm. Fortunately for him, the arrow had only torn through the fleshy part of his arm. Unfortunately for him, the adrenaline that he’d gotten from the fight was starting to wane, and the pain was setting in.“I’m right here,” Levi said as he knelt beside the mage sprawled on the dirt.

His eyes roamed around the arrow that was sticking out of her left shoulder. There was no way that he would be able to remove such a thing without worsening her condition. He wasn’t an expert, but by the looks of things, the mage had at most four hours before she died.

The arrow had gone deep into her left shoulder, and if he had to hazard a guess, he’d say that the silver head of the arrow might have even been poking out from the other side. It was a pessimistic assessment, but at the moment, it was Levi's job to think the worst about what exactly was happening.

“C-Can you help?” The mage stuttered, blood pooling at the corner of her lips.

“I’m no expert, but I’ll do the best I can,” Levi nodded. “Do you have a healing potion on you?”

“N-No,” she said.

Well, this is certainly going to be a lot harder than I expected.

Without a healing potion, anything he was going to do would be a total waste of time. The arrow had gone deep, so pulling out would only make things worse. Breaking the shaft was also a waste of time, as it achieved absolutely nothing. The only other option he had was to apply pressure around the wound to reduce the blood loss.

“I need my former cloak,” Levi said, receiving an acknowledging grunt from the mage as she retrieved the item from her storage ring.

The rogue quickly grabbed the cloak and wrapped it around the shaft of the arrow sticking out of her left shoulder, and then he gently pressed upon the wound, eliciting a pained grunt from the mage.

What in the hell am I supposed to do? He mused as he continued to add pressure. What he was doing would only buy her an hour or two at most. It wouldn’t save her life or staunch the bleeding. It would only prolong her agony. He could see it in her face. The way she struggled to breathe and the amount of time it took her to blink, as though her eyelids were heavy.

He had to wonder if he’d made the wrong choice by choosing to fight because he could not imagine that the ranger did not have a solution to this problem. There was no way the man did not have a health potion on him, especially since he was not planning to trade the mage's dead body to her house.

Levi huffed softly in annoyance. Whatever healing potion or solution the ranger had, it had gone up in flames with him. Now the rogue was left with nothing more than a pile of ashes and a dying mage to deal with.

No steeds. No people. No dungeon. What am I supposed to do?

With nothing tethering them to the area, he wasn’t surprised that their steeds had bolted. The matas were long gone, spooked by the constant attacks between both sides. Leaving them with no faster way to move.

He couldn't carry the mage with him. Apart from the fact that it would aggravate her injuries, the chances of meeting anyone else on this path were slim to none, so it’d be a waste of time. Plus, there was the obvious lack of a dungeon in the surrounding area that he could've tried to take the mage to. He may have taken the chance of fighting all the monsters in the dungeon just so that she could get Cerpitals' free healing.

“D-Don’t think too much about it,” she wheezed, coughing up blood. “Running away from home as a noble always ends in some type of tragedy.”

Levi genuinely had no idea how he was supposed to respond to that, so rather than looking dumbfounded, he smiled sadly as he kept his hands pressed against his old cloak, which was wrapped around her wound.

He pushed away the pain of his own injury as he tried to comfort her. His injury wouldn't kill him, but hers…hers was fatal, and if the only thing he could offer her was the presence of a friend, then he’d do that.

Or maybe I should wing it and carry her with me. The odds of finding help are slim, but it isn’t nothing. Then again, I’d risk aggravating her injuries and making her last moments painful for a big chance at nothing?

Levi was prepared to discard his thoughts when something occurred to him. “Do you think we should cauterize the wound, that’ll stop the bleeding, right?” He asked.

It seemed like a possibility because he remembered what happened when he’d slashed at the burning ranger. His strike had gone deep, but it had also closed up within a heartbeat. The flames had been hot enough to do that. Why couldn't it work now?

The mage's eyes seemed to brighten up at his words before dimming down.”I-it might work on surface wounds, but unless I plan on burning myself to a crisp, I fear that the flames wouldn't do much for the deep wound I’ve suffered from the arrow,” She said.

Levi let out a silent curse at Xara’s words. The mage was right; the flames might’ve been enough to cauterize surface wounds without seriously harming her, but when it came to deep wounds, such as the one the arrow had dealt her, nothing less than intense flames would be able to help her.

There has to be a way, there has to be something else that I can do. I can’t let her die!

Blood coated his teeth as he bit on his bottom lip, breaking into the skin. “Where did those flames that engulfed the ranger come from?” He asked. The question had left his lips because he wanted to keep the mage talking. He couldn't let her slip; it also didn’t hurt that he was curious as to what had started such a fire.

“It was a runed fire disc.” She said proudly. “One that I enchanted myself.”

A disc, she’d been carrying a runed disc. Those things weren’t cheap in the slightest, and he couldn't imagine how much she must’ve spent to purchase a blank one to enchant, but then again, she was of nobility, and such things were cheap for them.

How expensive was it to be a mage and an enchanter?… Right, she’s an enchanter!
