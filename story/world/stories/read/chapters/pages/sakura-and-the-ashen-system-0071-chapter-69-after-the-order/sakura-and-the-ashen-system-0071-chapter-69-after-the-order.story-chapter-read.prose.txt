For several seconds after Faerd collapsed, no one moved. Sakura watched the man lying on the ground and waited for another threat, but nothing happened. The remains of the plates he had created continued crumbling into fine black grit, while Faerd himself lay motionless, face down in the wet grass.

Gilbert was the first to approach him. He crouched beside Faerd, moving his injured shoulder carefully, and touched the man’s neck with his good hand.

"Alive."

Sakura exhaled. Pampli remained beside Lir with his head lowered, growling softly at Faerd.

Sakura came closer. Her thigh reminded her of the injury with a sharp stab of pain, and she limped through the last few steps. Faerd looked even worse than he had during the fight. His clothes were torn across his back and side, blood had soaked through the fabric, and a burn from Gilbert’s lightning remained on his chest.

Lir approached too, but stopped at a distance.

"So what now?"

Gilbert straightened.

"We take him with us."

Lir raised an eyebrow.

"He just tried to kill us."

"Gilbert’s right," Sakura said.

Lir frowned.

"You too?"

"He might know who did all of this to him."

"Exactly," Gilbert continued. "Who gave the order, where they held him, and where his obsidian magic came from. If we leave him here, we lose the best source of information we’ve had this entire time."

Sakura looked at Faerd again. For a moment, Maev and Tinli appeared before her eyes. After what the water had shown her, she could not leave him here anymore. Besides, something very much like sympathy tightened in her chest. Even for this monster. Even after everything they had suffered because of him. Faerd was not responsible for what they had turned him into.

"And what if he wakes up on the way?" Lir asked carefully.

"We tie him up," Gilbert answered.

"You saw what he does to stone walls."

Gilbert said nothing.

"And rope is supposed to stop him?"

"No."

Lir took a moment to find an answer.

"Great plan."

"If he recovers enough to create that much obsidian again, neither rope nor even chains will help," the mage admitted. "But it looks like we’ve already survived the worst of it. He’s weakened by his injuries and the powder. That gives us at least a small measure of safety."

Ater finally emerged through the ruined wall of the station. Gray dust covered his clothes and hair, but the bandage over his eyes remained in place. The old man stopped beside the broken channel, listened to the water, then turned his head toward them.

"Are you planning to stand in the middle of the yard much longer?"

Gilbert looked at him.

"We’re discussing what to do with the man you helped knock out with some suspicious powder."

"Good start. Discuss it on the way."

"On the way where?"

Ater pointed toward the station. One wall had been almost completely destroyed, the door was gone, the roof had sagged on one side, and smoke was still drifting from the room. Even though Ater could not see the extent of the damage, he clearly understood it well enough.

"We’re not staying here anymore," the old man said. "Even if the building doesn’t collapse during the night, anyone could have heard that noise. I have another place."

Everyone went silent at first.

"Of course you do," Gilbert muttered.

Ater ignored him.

"An old guardhouse north of here. Less than an hour away, assuming nothing else decides to drop on our heads along the way."

Lir glanced at Faerd.

"Or unless he decides to wake up and start causing trouble..."

They found rope among Ater’s surviving belongings. They tied Faerd’s wrists and secured his arms against his waist as well. Gilbert checked the knots twice, though he was the first to admit they probably would not help much if Faerd truly tried to break free.

Pampli took more convincing. When Sakura tried to put Faerd on his back, the beast jerked away and stepped aside, growling irritably at her this time.

"Don’t even start. I don’t like him either. Deal with it."

Pampli shook his head and backed away another step.

"Pampli."

This time, Sakura said his name more firmly. The beast glared at her from under lowered brows for several seconds, then snorted in irritation and finally allowed Gilbert and Lir to lay the unconscious Faerd across his back and tie him in place.

"See? We worked it out."

They headed through the forest as the light was already beginning to fade. Ater walked first and chose the path with such confidence that Sakura once again could not understand how much his blindness actually interfered with his ability to navigate, function, or simply live. Pampli followed with Faerd on his back.

The cut on Sakura’s thigh burned with every step, and before long she was limping noticeably. Gilbert barely used his injured arm, while Lir quickly became tired again and soon walked in silence, simply trying not to fall behind. Ater alone moved as if the entire day had passed him by, with the energy of a teenager but the grace of a mature man. It was a little irritating.

Sakura glanced at Faerd from time to time. His head swayed with Pampli’s steps, but his chest continued to rise and fall steadily.

Gilbert noticed.

"He’s still breathing. Stop checking every thirty seconds."

"I’m not checking every thirty seconds..."

"Fine. Twenty."

Sakura shot him a sharp look.

"Your shoulder has a hole through it. Focus on your own problems."

That finally shut Gilbert up. For a while, they walked without speaking as the forest grew darker and damp evening mist began settling between the trees. The water was still nearby. Sakura heard it sometimes to the right, sometimes to the left, and occasionally far below the ground, but after what had happened near the station, the voices barely interfered and said nothing she could clearly understand.

Gilbert was the first to break the silence.

"What was in the powder?"

Ater did not turn around.

"Several ingredients with long, boring names that would tell you nothing."

"What an argument."

"You asked what was in it. I answered."

"Fine. How did you know it would work?"

The old man said nothing.

"You knew he specifically needed to be ‘woken up,’" Gilbert continued. "And you just happened to have a powder that helped."

"It wasn’t an accident."

"Was that supposed to reassure me?"

The old man fell silent again, but this time his face grew darker.

Sakura had noticed for a while now how Ater changed whenever one particular name came up, and she had a few questions of her own.

"Have you seen this kind of compulsion before?"

Ater slowed slightly.

"Something similar."

"Where? When?"

"A long time ago."

Gilbert sighed quietly.

"Interesting how almost all your answers come down to a few words."

"At my age, some people realize words are more useful when used sparingly."

Sakura did not let Ater steer the conversation away.

"Does all of this have something to do with Crowlis?"

He stopped. Everyone else had to stop behind him. The old man stood with his back to them for several seconds.

"Why did you decide everything has to be connected to him?"

"Because every time Crowlis comes up, you become... a little stranger."

Ater remained silent, his expression grim. Sakura continued.

"And you know too much about Hierophants. About the voices. About how magic can affect a person. You didn’t learn all of that from legends, did you?"

Ater was silent for a long time.

"No."

"What connects you to Crowlis?" Sakura pressed.

The old man let out a heavy breath and finally admitted:

"I knew Crowlis personally."

Even Gilbert went silent.

"How personally?" Sakura asked.

Ater started walking again.

"Enough."

"That explains absolutely nothing."

"And I’m not going to explain it right now."

"Why?"

The old man turned around.

"Because all of you nearly died in that fight, and now we’re carrying an insane sleeping killer with us. Under the circumstances, Crowlis isn’t going anywhere before morning."

Sakura opened her mouth, then closed it again. The old man looked as if she would not get another word out of him.

"But we’re coming back to this conversation."

"I never doubted it."

The guardhouse stood farther into the forest, almost beside an overgrown stone channel. The small square building of gray stone sat beneath a slope and was hidden by trees on three sides. Old wooden shutters covered the windows, and moss had spread across parts of the roof, but the door opened without trouble.

Inside was only one room, with narrow bunks along the walls, a rough table, a small stove, and several crates of supplies. It was obvious no one had lived here in a long time, but Ater kept the place ready in case he needed it.

"Do you lose houses often?" Gilbert asked, looking around the room.

"Often enough to keep a spare."

"I’m starting to understand your philosophy of life."

"I doubt it."

They laid Faerd on the farthest bunk. Pampli immediately moved to the other side of the room, but a minute later he came back and lay down where he could keep the man in sight.

Ater tended to Faerd first. There were so many wounds that he started with the ones still bleeding.

Gilbert watched him for a while.

"Funny."

"What?"

"That after all this, we’re treating him."

Sakura carefully lowered herself onto the edge of the next bunk.

"You were the one who suggested bringing him with us. His dead body wouldn’t be much use to us..."

Sakura sounded almost cynical, but she knew that in reality, she still felt sorry for the man. The same man who had terrified her not long ago and, apparently, still did.

"True," Gilbert replied. "I didn’t expect to hear something that pragmatic from you."

Sakura shrugged.

"At least while he’s unconscious, we have an advantage."

Ater said nothing and continued working. Once Faerd’s worst wounds had been treated, he moved on to the others. Sakura’s thigh was cleaned and tightly bandaged. When Ater pulled the bandage tight, she clenched her teeth and spent several seconds silently breathing through her nose.

Gilbert was next. The obsidian needle had passed clean through his shoulder, which Ater called lucky. Gilbert disagreed with that definition.

Lir did not need any serious treatment. He was given food and water and ordered to rest for at least an hour and not try to help anyone. He wanted to argue, but in the end simply sat down by the wall.

Sakura settled near the stove with her injured leg stretched out. A thin haze of smoke hung inside the guardhouse, but it was warm. The forest rustled beyond the walls. Pampli lay nearby with his head resting on his front paws, though he still watched Faerd from time to time.

Sakura looked over there occasionally too. Bandages covered most of his wounds, and his hands were still tied. Without the obsidian and that horrible empty stare, he looked like nothing more than an exhausted man. Sakura remembered Maev and Tinli again and turned her gaze toward the fire.

By the time Faerd finally moved, it was almost completely dark outside the shutters. Gilbert had stopped questioning Ater, Lir had dozed off by the wall, and the old man was sorting through the remaining medicines at the table.

Sakura noticed the movement first. Faerd’s fingers twitched almost imperceptibly. Pampli raised his head at the same moment.

"Gilbert."

He immediately turned toward the bunk.

Faerd opened his eyes. For several seconds, he stared at the ceiling, then slowly turned his head and noticed the unfamiliar walls and his bound hands.

Then he saw Sakura.

Faerd tensed sharply. Pampli jumped to his feet and growled. Gilbert was already standing with his good hand raised, while Lir woke at the noise and backed toward the wall.

"Faerd," Sakura said carefully.

He jerked against the ropes, and something gave a dry crunch beneath his fingers.

A thin black vein began creeping across the stone floor...
