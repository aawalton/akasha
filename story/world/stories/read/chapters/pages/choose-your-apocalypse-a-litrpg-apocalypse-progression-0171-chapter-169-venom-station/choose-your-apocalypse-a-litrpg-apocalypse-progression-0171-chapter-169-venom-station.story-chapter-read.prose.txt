<Alex, Real - Dorgon Calls for Aid, Innominate Duty Station>

Alex guided the spun-up jump-drive, feeling the guardrails Kaylee’s improvements had added. Interestingly, those very rails helped him see where he’d gone wrong in previous jumps, and that allowed him to narrow in the point of arrival even more than if he’d simply relied on the rails to begin with.

In essence, he could see what the rails were nudging and keeping in tighter parameters, and that told him exactly what mattered for accuracy. Oh, this is way more valuable than  we realized.

He sent the information through his bracelets, then put it from his mind.

Mana—tinged with gravity and other aspects—flooded out through the substructure of the Innominate, and then the ship folded like an origami square.

As a meticulously crafted, flapping dragon, they dove into nothing and emerged from the ripples that action had created as a matter of course.

The ship shattered outward from the folded perfection, the shards slotting neatly back into proper shape, leaving the Innominate at relative rest some two hundred thousand miles from the planet around which the station orbited.

Alex immediately started the ship moving, as it had begun to fall toward the planet’s surface as soon as space had stabilized around it.

As to how long this jump had taken? He had no idea. Time was relative anyway so why did it matter?

Alex blinked a few times. That’s… odd.

He sighed, placing the thoughts in the bucket of ‘jump-drive madness’ and moved on. “On an intercept course for the space station. Estimated ninety minutes before we enter a stable orbit at a safe distance. Course locked in and shifting to auto-pilot.”

Natasha sent the scans his way, and he was content with the results. There were still life signs in various seemingly sealed compartments even though they weren’t getting any responses to attempts at communication. Most of the station was empty of any large sources of magic or life energy. There was no undeath or any other known animate force in evidence either.

“Looks good for my training.”

He was about to disengage from his duty station when he focused outward, coming out of the readings and specifics that had been dominating his thoughts since the jump.

An oddity caught his vision and he stared, mouth slightly agape.

The local star was green.

More than its color, the magic radiating off of it was welcoming, like a warmth that resonated with him down to his core.

From his admittedly amateur understanding, a green star wasn’t possible with mundane physics. The magic he felt from it was likely the source. “Natasha?”

“Yeah?”

“Are you seeing that star?”

“Yes, Alex. It’s the largest thing for thousands of miles. It would be pretty hard to miss.”

He huffed a laugh. “I mean. It’s green…”

He transitioned to bridge mode and found everyone looking his way.

He blinked a few times. “That shouldn’t be possible… right? I’m feeling life magic from it.”

Natasha nodded. “It does seem to have something in it, throwing out life magic, yes. It’s one reason this star system is so full of life. Even the asteroids have monsters of various kinds spawning on their surfaces.”

Kaylee interjected. “That is the theorized source of whatever is affecting the space station. The stellar light engenders life in anything exposed to it long enough.”

Alex arched an eyebrow. “So… will the Innominate come to life if we stay here long enough?”

John chuckled. “No.” He pulled up some information before him. “It’s more likely that we get some sort of magical lichen spawning on our outer hull. It’s not a way to make living items.”

Thinking of his sword, Alex turned to Audry. “Are there any mines in the system?”

The caramel skinned woman nodded but sighed. “Yes, but sadly, all the living iron mines are fully claimed and rigorously mined. The other materials are highly regulated as well. The mine owners actually increased the rewards on this station mission, as they use the space station for shore leave for their crews, and when it went dark, the miners were less than happy.”

Alex was frowning. “How do you all know this already? Why wasn’t this a part of the briefing and the decision?”

Grant cleared his throat. “It was, Alex. You were… playing with your shoes. Even so, I asked if you were paying attention…”

Alex gave the younger man a flat look. “I wasn’t playing, Grant, I was delving into the magic they contain.” He grimaced. “But I guess I wasn’t paying as much attention as I thought I was.”

Grant shrugged. “Either way. We’re here now, and it seems like you are lacking some important information.”

When Alex looked around, he found everyone nodding. “Oh…” He cleared his throat, reflexively creating some water and swallowing. “I’ll… I’ll try to pay more attention going forward.”

Juba huffed a laugh. “I’m glad that he’s the one going onto the station.” The man took a deep breath and let it out in a rush. “But come on, Alex. I’ll give you a rundown of the station and what information we have that could be relevant. I assume you missed that as well?”

Alex scratched the back of his head. “Thank you. I’ve… I’ve been a bit focused.”

Kaylee cleared her throat. “I want everyone to send Alex a brief summary of information pertaining to their station so he has a good general overview for this system.”

He grimaced again but nodded. “Thank you.”

Audry took a step forward on the bridge. “Am I alright to deploy the collectors? The life magic in the light will make for a good haul of life aspect, even if not as much as if we could mine.” The woman hesitated for a moment. “I could push for the stellar life aspect, but we won’t even get a tenth the haul. It really is a shame that the last star was entirely mundane, or we could have gotten a good amount of daylight, given how long we were there… Still, I don’t know that we’ll be here for nearly as long as we were there.”

Alex was a bit confused, but he knew it wouldn’t go well if he flagged another thing that he’d clearly missed. I’ll just look it up in the ship’s databases.

It was funny, because his race and primary class were both life-aspected, which clearly seemed to mirror—or at least reference—something similar to these aspects.

Juba met Alex in the central cargo room, pulling up a structural diagram for the space station and helping Alex add it to his own equipment, along with the universal security keys to allow the station to recognize him and allow him access wherever he needed to go.

Aspects - GeneralDistilled magical conditioning, manifested as small crystals.

Aspects of various kinds are required for crafting and maintenance of non-System magical items and, in some cases, even System generated gear.

Alex read further, and found that it was effectively a way of conditioning materials and priming them to accept magic of various types. Alex almost wanted to call them mana crystals, but the Aspects didn’t really seem to have any mana within them directly.

In terms of cooking, they were spices rather than the core foodstuffs.

To his surprise, it seemed that his myriad repairings of his dadao with his own HP and Life had effectively been aspecting it, priming it so that when he remade it with his own Life, that power was able to take hold and make the dadao truly become the weapon it was now.

If he’d tried that from the start, it would either have failed or not succeeded nearly to the same extent. Even using his HP and Life likely wouldn’t have worked as a primer except that his race and primary class were life-aspected, meaning the health that he gained from them—the improvement to his Life—carried that same aspect, that same flavoring.

More broadly, there were different guidelines for aspecting different materials types, and many materials wouldn’t aspect well to certain magics, but all in all, aspects seemed to be a core part of what he’d been missing in regard to crafting. Not the only part by any means, but certainly a large portion.

I really have been too narrowly focused, haven’t I…
