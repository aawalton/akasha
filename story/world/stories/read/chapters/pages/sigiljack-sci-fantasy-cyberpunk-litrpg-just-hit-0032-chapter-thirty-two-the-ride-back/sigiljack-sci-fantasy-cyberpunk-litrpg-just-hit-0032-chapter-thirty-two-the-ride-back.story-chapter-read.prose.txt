The door eased shut on its own behind John, soft-closing like a whisper as it sealed. John settled into the passenger seat across from Nabe. His military droid folded itself into the rear of the car, metal knees tight to itself despite their being ample room in the backseat. The aerial drone settled in the droid's lap like a small dog.

“You stink a bit,” Nabe said, eyes on the mirrors as she pulled the luxury-sized sedan into the littered street.

“I just crawled out of a sewer,” John said, one brow up. “Kaito forget to mention that part to you?”

“Goshujin-sama shares what is prudent with me.”

“Why do you call him that? Unless I'm wrong, it basically means master,” John asked, frowning at the use of the archaic honorific. “That level of respect still a thing for your people, or is it just his thing? With him being who knows how old and all."

“You speak Japanese?” Her gaze slid to him for a beat, measuring.

“A little.”

“How?”

“Engineering papers,” he said, watching neon bleed through the windshield. “Your country's journals are too good not to try and understand and they only publish in the one language. I can read most of it. I can talk a bit if you go slow. Me writing kanji on my own is a damned lost cause though.”

Nabe nodded once. “You are a man of many contradictions.”

“Like how?”

“You taught yourself a language to read about cybernetics and design,” she said. “Yet you sell your time as violence to provide for your family. You are a ronin. Do you ever wonder if your mind would be better used elsewhere?”

He let the question sit. “I thought about it. Tried it even. It didn’t work out. I’m good at the fighting.”

“Do you enjoy it?”

“Enjoy what?”

“The killing that comes with the fighting.”

He huffed out a breath. “You asking if I’m a psychopath, Nabe?”

“I am only asking what I am asking.”

“I like the fighting part,” he said. “Liking the killing depends on the person I'm killing.”

“In what way?" she asked, her voice patient and unjudging.

“You really want an answer?”

“Yes.”

“Bad people I don’t mind taking off the board.” His jaw flexed. “Good people hurt to hurt. I don't like when that has to happen, but what can you do?"

“Do you enjoy hurting the bad people?” Nabe’s hands turned the wheel. The expensive car glided elegantly through a yellow light without a single shudder.

John studied her attractive ethnic profile. She was calm, composed. Killing seemed like a common topic for her... mostly. “Huh. I didn't see it. Not until just now”

“What do you see, John?” she asked him.

“Your eyes,” he said. “You’ve killed too, haven't you? Was it for Kaito?”

Her brows pinched, a private calculation going on behind them. “That is not my job.”

Was she trying to decide how much to tell him?

“That wasn’t my question.” he said.

“I have killed,” she said finally.

“Thought so.” He watched the set of her mouth, it twitched. “Did you enjoy it?”

Her eyes went dark, he could almost see the memories in her mind playing in her irises. “Only when they were evil men.”

“That what happened to your kid?” he asked, voice level. “That why you’re loyal to Kaito? He help you get even?”

She looked over to him. Her attention momentarily completely on him as she glared at his face. Their eyes met. Her gaze was simmering.

A horn blared. Nabe jerked the wheel and the sedan slid half a lane as a boxy truck cut by, too fast and too close. John’s hand shot to the grab handle over the black-tinted window. The car steadied in its lane once more.

“Apologies,” Nabe said, eyes back on the road.

John took a breath, steadying himself as well as he got over the near head-on collision.

“I’m sure they deserved it,” John said. “If they hurt your kid.”

Silence filled the cabin. The drone in the back hummed as it went into rest-mode. The military droid’s servos clicked softly.

“I am not the most forgiving woman,” Nabe said at last. “Perhaps not as honorable as you would deserve either.”

“That the vibe you get from me?” John asked, a small grin pushing in anyway. “You going to cancel our date because you think I’m too good for you? That's an old excuse.”

“Do you wish for me to cancel our date?” Nabe asked him, voice plain.

“Not a chance in hell," he replied.

A corner of her mouth tilted up, her eyes defrosting again. “Your youth is refreshing.”

“You’re only a few years older than me at the most, right?” John asked her.

She let a small chuckle escape.

“What?” he said. "You're not like Kaito. You're human right?"

"I am human," she replied.

"So why are you laughing?"

“Perhaps that is something I will explain later,” Nabe said. “Perhaps.”

He sighed, letting it go. “Might have to reschedule the date, anyway, though.”

“Why?”

“The Grin threatened my family,” he said. “Kaito says he'll keep them safe, that you’ll be in charge of getting the protection in place actually. I’m still not sure I can be away from them.”

“If Kaito-sama has promised this, he will keep his word.” She changed lanes cleanly. “Your family will be protected.”

“By vampires?” John said. “That's both comforting and not. And what happens when the sun is up?”

“If it is entrusted to me, they will be protected,” Nabe said, repeating her assurance and elaborating on her personal conviction to uphold it with her tone. “We have more than the blooded under our employ. The time of day will not matter.”

He filed that term away. Blooded. A term for vampires? Useful to know.

“They will be safe, John,” she said.

“You promise?” He let the weight of his emotions actually show for once, choosing to trust her with that much in the moment. “Claire is basically my kid. And her mom was like a mother to me.”

“I give you my word.” Nabe's right hand left the wheel, and came to rest steady and warm on his left thigh. Her movements had no hesitation.

He glanced down at her hand, surprised at its pleasant warmth. “You want our date that bad?” he said, letting the levity back into his tone. “Trying to make me feel better about taking a night off?”

“Perhaps,” she said, a real smile touching her eyes now. “I will be paying.”

“We can't go dutch?" he asked.

“No.” She replied. A crisp, playful answer. “I will be choosing the venue. And I would not have you waste so much of what you just earned.”

He scoffed. “You're not the first woman who refused to let me buy her things, you know.”

“Oh? Am I your type, John?” her tone became as flirting as it had so far. "Do you like a woman that pays?"

“Let’s not say all that yet," he replied.

She hummed low in her throat, amused, then eased the car into a slower avenue where the lights thinned and the city breathed a little easier.

Behind them the droid tried not to look at Nabe and John as they verbally sparred and flirted in the front seat. John noticed the awkwardness in the automaton's singular eye and metal, industrial face.

“You good back there?” he asked the seemingly semi-sapient robot.

The droid straightened its spine and shook its head in a stubborn, too strong yes. The aerial drone did not reply.

“Right,” John said, eyes lingering on the strange military droid for a moment, before facing forward again.

Nabe’s hand stayed where it was on his thigh for another second. Then she withdrew it, returned both hands to the wheel, and continued to focus on driving him home.

"Where did you find them?" Nabe asked him."On the job," John said. "Figure I can retool them to be useful."The military droid looked up at John's reflection at the mention of being 'retooled'. Its singular eye almost looked sheepishly nervous.
