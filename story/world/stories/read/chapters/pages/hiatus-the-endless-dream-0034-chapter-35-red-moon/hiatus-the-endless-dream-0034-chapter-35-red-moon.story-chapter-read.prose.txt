We stood in silence, strides away from the forest's edge, yet none of our eyes dared flick away. We held our breath and counted the seconds, waiting for something to jump out or slam against the invisible barrier that was the moonlight for this beast. The sound of static no longer existed. Darkness still wreathed the space just beyond the treeline, but its depths looked shallower, natural even as rays of moonlight broke through the canopy, giving glimpses further into its body. I recognized that pitch blackness for what it was.

A dark fog, now dissipated completely.

I tore my eyes away. Despite the urge to peer in and search for threats I knew should be lurking, I forced only the corners of my vision to engage.

"Don't look into the forest for too long," I finally got out. "I don't know what it was like when you two came here, but on our way here, it could give us hallucinations."

Both women glanced at Krevith, who nodded.

The rest averted their gaze as well, looking towards the church in the clearing.

"We have to tie ourselves together." It was the Huntress who spoke as she turned back to the ruins.

I followed her gaze and then her form as she walked back over to the decrepit ruins. The other buildings were all but dust and broken stone. How only the church survived in such pristine condition, I had no clue, but I thought back to the box I had used Peeler on. It appeared out of nowhere. I had seen the space, it wasn't, and then it was suddenly there. I looked at the church's beautiful stained glass. If an Instance Holder could create a box out of nothing, I didn't know why they couldn't do the same for an entire building. The Residents called them Gods, so I should assume that they had all the powers of one, only constrained in these Instances by what the Dream allowed.

A sobering thought, that the restrictive force of the Dream could bind even supposed Gods. Sobering still that I was grateful for that in the moment.

Maerin followed the Huntress while they looked for rope. Krevith and I stayed by the forest.

The Gladiator spoke, "Why do you find comfort in the Fiend?" I frowned before following his gaze and realizing that I indeed was absent-mindedly fiddling with the statue.

I said nothing, just pocketed the figurine.

After a silence, the towering man tried again, "You came to an agreement with Maerin?"

I was tired of questions, so I snapped one back in return, "You decided to stop being a Gladiator?"

I shot in the dark and directed it at the only thing I knew about the man. Maerin had called him Gladiator, and that's all I knew of him. I held no desire to learn more and so hoped my words would cut.

He flinched, and I knew I had been successful, but only in the cutting words as Krevith actually answered my question, "No. I simply could no longer perform my duties and so another had to take my place."

I frowned. "I wouldn't think missing an eye would be enough." I internally cursed myself for engaging further.

A pitiful chuckle deflated the man, "It did not. I just could no longer fight. Violent joy was no longer enough. My heart refused."

A familiar anger began rising in my chest, and I looked back to the forest to avoid looking at Krevith or any of the other Survivors. It was unfair. This world was unfair and sounded entirely cruel. What kind of a God forced everyone to feel primarily joy? I could see getting rid of pain, of suffering: disease, sickness, poverty, and awful people, but this wasn't that. This was slapping a bandaid on and calling it fixed.

Worse. I didn't think that was the purpose at all. Maybe if I were here in a vacuum, I could give this Instance Holder the benefit of the doubt, but I wasn't. Whatever it was, it was cruel. It had stolen me from my Instance and punished someone who only wanted to help someone they saw actively dying. My thoughts went back to Talin.

I adjusted my plans slightly. Maerin, Krevith, the Huntress, whoever Maerin decided to use the Fiend on, and Talin. That made five Survivors to look out for. I could manage that with the Fiend.

"What are you being punished for?" I asked Krevith.

"For refusing to execute someone when commanded."

I didn't even have it in me to frown. I just gave the man a tired look. "I thought this place was the execution for your people."

"It is," the man nodded, "yet I was still ordered to execute one regardless. I am only supposed to maim, not kill, our doctrine says so."

"And?" I asked, irritation rising. It wasn't directed at Krevith, not really, but he was the one in front of me.

"What else is there to say?" he questioned back.

"If that's what your doctrine says then why are you here?"

The towering Gladiator just stared up at the moon, which I noted had become even redder. "I have wondered that myself. By all teachings I should not be, yet I am."

I threw up my hands. This was stupid. "Do you think you should be here?"

He confirmed my budding theory that he was a fanatic with a nod of his head, "Yes. I do."

I sighed, but he continued before I could walk away. "I have hurt many. My soul is filled with asphalt that clings like tar. I do not believe this is punishment for me, just allowance for the Spirits to cut away the tar, and allow the good in me to combine with the good from others and be reborn." He shook his head at what I had to assume was a stray thought, "I wish for the Fiends to have the tar, but it is said even the Spirits have use for it."

"I don't see you killing yourself." It was blunt. Far too blunt, but it's the only question I could think of to get out without screaming at the man for being so zealous. I raised my voice and continued, "You're not lying belly up. If you believe in this so much why not go for everyone else's throat? Is your belief so meek as to end here?"

He stared at me, and for the first time, I saw clear frustration, or maybe distaste. "We are natural contradiction." He turned and stared off to Maerin and the Huntress, who had apparently found a length of rope and were having a short conversation of their own. Krevith continued, "Not everyone wishes to return to the river, and if my soul is already thick with tar, what is one more layer? No?"

I didn't know what to say. I hated enemies that were smart, and I hated allies with conviction. Both made things so needlessly difficult.

I wasn't religious myself, in fact, I didn't know anyone who was, so I didn't get it. Then again, maybe Krevith was just dumb. Damn it, I was getting too upset at this again.

I kicked a small rock in the direction of Maerin and the Huntress. It skipped along the cobbled street, "Yo. What's the holdup? We're on a time crunch." I didn't know what Jeren meant by the 3rd Visitor not being an ally. Chances are that we were down a Survivor, though.

The Huntress scowled. There was a distinct lack of snide remarks, and once she arrived, she spoke tersely, "It's a short rope. It's best if we cut it in half. Tie off two and two."

I looked at the rope. She was right. The length wasn't greater than ten feet, and if we tried to tie all of us with one rope, we'd just end up getting tangled on one another. "Alright. Me and Krevith. You and Maerin."

It was Maerin who, with a shake of her head, started speaking, "No. It has to be Krevith and Paltra. If Krevith decided to run off into the forest both of us would be at his mercy. Paltra can subdue him."

I side-eyed the Lady and gave her another look over. I didn't like it, but it was probably true. Krevith was a giant. He could lift me up and run off if he saw the right hallucination. I didn't know how Paltra would fight that any better, but more importantly: "You think I couldn't do the same for you?" I said, staring at the gaunt red-haired girl.

She tilted her chin up at me, "You're a Visitor. I doubt you do not have some defense."

"What happened to discretion?" I spat out, surprised at her sudden reveal to the others.

She met my gaze, and I could feel both Krevith and Paltra tense, as if they were ready to intervene or bolt. I didn't know why. I had no intention of fighting any of them, at least not yet. "They will both be discreet. They need to know what you are. They will follow my commands regardless. Your tricks just need to land on everyone else."

I was reminded of Talin again. Shit. She knew I was a Visitor, too. Maybe I should just abandon that line of planning entirely. It was hard for two to keep a secret, much less almost half the fucking people in the Instance.

"Paltra can subdue Krevith if needed?" I asked, and the tension in the air dissipated.

"She can," Maerin spoke, and I fought the urge to make a snide comment.

Instead, I just turned to the Huntress herself. "Can you?"

"Not easily, but yeah." Her straightforward answer caught me off guard.

I capitulated with a nod, and we tied ourselves off. Paltra was a Huntress and was well versed in different knots. She essentially made long manacles with the rope and connected each pair at the wrists.

We once again stood at the edge of the forest. Hesitation filled the silence. I stepped forward, letting my connected hand drag behind me to give Maerin room to move. "Let's go."

As we walked through the forest, there was a natural silence. We said nothing. Paltra lit her torch and held it ahead of Maerin and I.

Each of us was responsible for looking in a specific direction. I watched behind. Maerin took the left side, Krevith took the right, and Paltra led us forward. A part of me was relieved that my head didn't have to be on a swivel at all times, but all it took was a flinch, half breath, or stuttered shuffle from one of the others for my vision to whip around just in case.

Five minutes in.

Nothing.

Ten minutes. Nothing. There were no bird sounds or sounds of nature because the creature ate them all. There was no static because the creature wasn't showing itself for some reason I couldn't explain.

Fifteen minutes. We reached the ravine.

We broke into the clearing at a sped-up pace, each of us feeling pinpricks on our backs.

The creature—creatures possibly—inside hadn't been there. Not once did we hear static or a whisper or a vision. The unnatural black fog was never even seen. Yet, that worried me more than anything else.

If they were not here, they'd be at Jeren's group, harassing them, which meant we were heading straight towards the monsters. There was nowhere else they could be. Our two groups were the only targets in the entire Instance.

My skin began to itch as we crossed the clearing, and an icy numbness slowly crept into my nerves. The sensation of heat it gave was subtle, yet unbearable, like someone plunging their frozen fingertips into almost boiling water, lessened by factors of a hundred until it was just a dull, inescapable feeling all across my skin.

Red tinged the air now, the pale light from the moon itself having warped, as if seen through stained glass. I looked up. The moon was turning an even deeper shade of crimson.

My breaths came in more rapid bursts, and I couldn't stop thinking of the worst-case scenario. I shoved the feeling down. Later. Always later-

"Are you alright?" Maerin discreetly whispered to me out of the corner of her mouth, breaking me from my thoughts. She wasn't looking at me, intent on keeping her eyes out in the direction she was in charge of, and for a moment, I wasn't sure if she was talking to me, but she clarified at the last moment. "Dorian?"

"Yeah." I answered plainly. "Yeah, I'm alright. Thanks."

The paths on the other side of the ravine looked the same. A dark, overcast forest, but with no unnatural fog.

I finally spat out what everyone was thinking. "Where the hell is this thing?"

"Can you ask your friend if it's where he is?" It was Paltra who spoke up, "perhaps if it is there it may be best we stay split up." She took the words right out of my mouth.

I pulled out the [Communication Stone]

"Jeren, are you there?"

A few seconds passed. "Yeah. You almost here?"

"We're at the ravine, maybe ten minutes away, but the monster in the forest. The one that causes hallucinations. Has it been around there?" I asked.

"What?" There was pure confusion in his voice. "No. I don't know what you're– There was something in the looped run. It called out to the Survivors, tried to get them to kill themselves, but that's all. It's not around here why."

I didn't like the shape of this at all.

"It doesn't matter. We'll be there soon, just prime the Survivors, yeah?"

"Yeah. We're in the ship. Bottom of it, by the cells. See you in a bit."

I nodded to the rest, who had obviously heard my conversation, and we began to make our way through the winding forest trails. Everyone was still tense, and now it wasn't only me jumping at the slightest noise. We made good time, only taking seven minutes to arrive at another clearing, the shadow of a massive man o'war looming over the treeline.

We stepped out of the clearing, and the itchy, subtle feeling of frostburn I hadn't realized had left in the first place returned.

I froze. I wanted to drag Maerin back without any warning, but thought better of it. "One second everyone. Maerin. Follow me for a second."

I walked back into the forest with Maerin, and the feeling slowly vanished. I stepped out into the clearing, and the feeling grew.

I thought more. Why didn't I feel that itching in the forest? What could–

I froze. The answer was boring into me. I looked up into the sky. Into the now deep crimson moon, and paled.

Another of those winged creatures—painted by the red light, with something in its grasp—flew straight at the moon. I watched it get smaller and smaller. Then, when I could barely make it out anymore, it landed on the moon and dropped the wriggling thing in its grasp.

It jumped off and began to fly towards the spire of stone.

The moon turned a little redder.
