Chapter 136

A bird-catcher on the high ridge once told me he had lost more snares to nothing than to any fox. He would come up at first light to a cut line, a stripped bait but no track in the dust. He had learned long ago not to curse the empty air for it. A thing you cannot see, he said, is not the same as a thing that is not there. — Codex of the Long Watch, A Snarer's Observation, the island uplands

The pull had been steady since the shingle, but over the last hundred metres it had changed to a cleaner pull. Like a note that had been wavering and had now resolved.

Arthur set his boot on a root and pushed up the grade. The morning light filtered through the pines and the air smelled of resin, dry earth and, unsurprisingly, pine. Donna was ten metres ahead of the group, scanning the tree line from a habit she would never lose. Sarah and Steve came behind, picking their way along a goat track.

On the third switchback Steve put his hand up and stopped to catch his breath. As he tried to lean on the nearest tree, Arthur pulled him away and motioned at the pine trunk.

Two columns of ants, thousands of them, were working up the bark of a pine in a line so precise it looked ruled. They came down the trunk of a pine in a single busy unbroken line and each one carried a scrap of something towards the ground. Another line went up the far side of the same trunk carrying nothing.

"Myrmidons," Steve said.

He was looking at the ants with the faraway expression that meant a lecture was coming.

"Go on then, you'll only sulk if we don't ask," Donna said, her eyes still scanning the top of the hill.

"Aegina. This island, right here." Steve crouched by the trunk to take a closer look at the ants. "The story goes that a plague swept through the island and emptied it. The king was a man called Aeacus, and he stood in a grove much like this one with nobody left to rule. He watched a colony of ants working up an oak, tireless, in their thousands, labouring to a common goal without complaint."

Steve tipped his head to the sky. "So he prayed to Zeus. ‘Fill my city the way you filled this tree’. And Zeus did, he took the ants and raised them up into a people. A new nation, made overnight from insects. The Myrmidons, from myrmex, the Greek word for ant. They were tireless and obedient and became Achilles's soldiers at Troy."

"What happened to the fishermen?" Donna asked.

“Hmmm?”

“These stories never talk about what happened to the common folk, do they? The fishermen, the cobblers.”

Steve opened his mouth to answer, but they had started moving again somewhere during the lecture. The path turned at the top of the switchback, and the temple came out of the trees.

It stood on the crown of the hill in the full light of the morning, twenty-five Doric columns of honey-coloured stone that looked almost white where the sun struck them. The columns still stood in a broken rank along the near side. Below and beyond the temple the Saronic Gulf shimmered a brilliant blue-green.

Arthur stood staring at the canvas before him. The temple had faint blue lines running through the columns as well and those combined with the incessant pull behind his eyes told him they had reached their destination.

“The Temple of Aphaia,” Steve said motioning at the temple. “The original was built in five hundred and seventy BC, gutted by fire and rebuilt around five hundred BC. There were sculptures here depicting the Trojan wars and highlighting the exploits of local Aeginetan heroes Ajax and Telamon.”

"I've been here before, on a school trip, when I was twelve. It feels different now," Sarah said quietly.

“You used to go on an awful lot of school trips,” Donna said.

“My history teacher, Mr Panagiotis, did not think pictures and videos were instructional enough.”

“And right he was. No substitute for fieldwork,” Steve replied, nodding.

They decided to move their gawking closer to the temple. Steve pulled out his ledger as they reached the temple.

"The second hand waits where the line carries through," he read from the ledger. “The centre of the platform, I think. Where the Ether lines terminate."

“We all know what’s going to happen next. Stow the fieldpack somewhere safe and check your gear before Arthur touches anything,” Donna said, taking out her axe.

They did as Donna suggested, stowing their packs and non-essentials away from the temple, and each of them went about double-checking their gear. Once Donna was satisfied she gave a nod and they climbed into the temple.

Unlike Sounion, they didn’t have to look for the marker here. The faint geometric Ether lines running through the temple all terminated at a small raised piece of polished marble at the centre of the temple.

Arthur stepped up onto the platform and the others fanned, Donna wide to his right with Ravener held in a ready grip, Steve a few paces behind her with the focus ready and Sarah as always stood a pace behind Arthur's left shoulder. He crouched at polished marble with the familiar Seeder spiral etched on it and laid his palm on it.

A lock that had been waiting for him gave way and Ether-blue light washed outward from his hand across the marble in a brief pulse and was gone.

Then the ground unfolded. Arthur had no other word for it. A stretch of the stylobate three metres to his right lifted. What had been bare tawny marble a heartbeat ago poured upwards from the surface of the platform with a boneless ease that turned Arthur's stomach. It had no fixed shape and it was the colour of the stone it had been lying on. And between one moment and the next it was something very large and alive.

Then the chromatophores raced across its skin in a wave and it became the colour of the pine behind it, and then the pine behind it was all there was.

"Where did it—" Steve started and looked around.

The stylobate was empty. The pines were just pines. The morning light was clean and bright and there was nothing on the platform except four people and twenty-five columns.

It is still there. It has just stopped emitting the signal you hunt by. Your eyes are the wrong instrument. Archie said in their heads.

"Yes, thank you, Archie. Really helpful," Arthur said.

He scanned the tree line, then the columns. Nothing. The shield was up, the maul was in his right hand and his heart hammered. Every shadow on the marble could be the creature that had risen from the stone.

Something hit him from the left.

Hit was the wrong word though. It was more of a grip. A mass of cold wet flesh wiggled around his shield and wrapped around his shield arm and squeezed. He felt suction on his arm, a cold adhesion pulling at the skin beneath. A fucking octopus. He swung the maul at where he thought the guardian was. Apart from the pull on his forearm, he was still staring at air and columns.

The maul made contact but he could tell that the force was wasted, spread across a body that flowed past him. The grip released and Arthur staggered back.

The marble was empty again.

Steve had sighted using Arthur's position, and the rod tip flickered heat over frost. A column of disturbed air leapt across the platform and hit wood. Pine bark steamed and cracked.

“It’s gone,” he said.

Donna had the same idea Steve did of gauging the location of the guardian based on Arthur’s arm that it had gripped. Just as Convergence leapt she burned Threshold Drive and crossed six metres in the time it took Arthur to turn his head. Ravener came up for a body that was not where she had thought it was. There was nothing to cut.

She spun, searching. The Drive was burning seconds she would not get back. Marble, columns, pines, morning light and no guardian.

Except the guardian was there, just not where she was looking. It attacked from below.

It came from under the platform edge and a limb Arthur could not properly see wrapped Donna from shoulder to hip. It lifted her off her feet and squeezed. Her Drive-quick body could do nothing about a grip that had closed before her eyes reported it was there.

The Ravener was pinned against Donna’s side by a grip that was tightening. She screamed.

Arthur was running towards her. He could see Donna, but the thing holding her was shifting colour, one moment it was her vest, then the stone beneath, then the pine behind, pulling itself back into invisibility with Donna still in its grip.

The only place he could be sure of a guardian limb was around Donna’s pinned arm so he crashed into it with the shield. The grip on Donna did not loosen. It began, unhurriedly, to pour off the edge of the platform and take Donna with it.

It was Sarah who saved them. She had moved up close enough that a second limb could have taken her too. Her eyes were unfocused and she did what she had done in the dark under Kastella. The creature had no wish to be seen but could do nothing about being alive, and Field Triage read the living.

"Steve, I see it. The mass is below her, underneath the platform edge. It's holding from the left, clinging to the underside of the stone. Steve, two metres past Arthur's hip at knee height. Trust me. Now."

That’s the thing about being in life and death situations repeatedly. One creates bonds that are more than friendship or family. The surety that the person next to you will always have your back.

Steve fired blind off her word, his hands shaking. He might be terrified of hitting Donna but his brain was still firing. He chose ice, not fire. It hit.

Frost spread over the chromatophore skin in patterns that could not be shed. And where there was nothing around Donna a moment before suddenly there was a something. A glitter of ice hanging in the air over bare stone.

The creature was cold-blooded under all its cleverness and its body seized as the temperature dropped. Donna dropped out of a slackening grip. She hit the marble, rolled and came up dragging air in wheezing pulls.

Arthur had made his own decision. The shield was useless against something that could just flow around it. The maul was ineffective against something that had no bone to shatter. He dropped the shield and his maul. It was time to test the Messer.

And as Stavros had said, the Messer was a blade shaped for a man who hit things. Arthur struck the frost-rimed outline where Sarah called the mass thickest, and the edge bit through skin and found something underneath to sever. That, unfortunately, broke one of his theories. He was hoping that once he made the creature bleed, they would be able to track the blood and would be done with this soon enough. Yet the Messer came away wet but not red or black. What coated the messer was a transparent substance slightly thicker than water.

“The blood is transparent,” he shouted, swinging the sword again and finding empty air, now that the frost had melted.

“That’s rather unfortunate,” Steve said, having hoped to track the creature using blood as well.

“Sarah?”

“I don’t see it. It’s beyond the field.”

Arthur pulled back closer to Sarah. Donna’s breathing had settled and she was looking around fruitlessly. Steve kept aiming his rod one way and then the other.

“It is coming again, from behind me,” Sarah said.

Arthur pulled her back and switched positions just as Sarah said. “It’s gone again.”

It was another thirty seconds before Sarah saw the guardian coming up behind Donna, but just as Donna turned to swing her axe, it pulled back.

“I can’t keep the Triage running forever,” said Sarah.

“Is it reading our movements? Twice cannot be a coincidence,” Arthur said.

It is a node guardian. Why are you surprised that it can think and plan? Archie added unhelpfully.

Steve was chewing his lips. “I might have something.”

“Something is good. I’ll take something,” Arthur said.

“My concussion cone modified to something like a cone of cold. The range would be rubbish though. Much easier to push air than frost.”

“It’s a 5th level spell, init. Wouldn’t do if it was easy.”

“Arthur, are you seriously thinking of D&D right now?” Donna said.

“It’s a coping mechanism. Steve, so what’s the plan.”

“We bait it closer. Sarah calls the direction but we don't move. When it is close enough, I give it a face full of frost and you two give it a face full of steel.”

“Better than anything I can think of.” Donna shrugged

They moved in closer, each facing a different direction, waiting for the guardian to show itself. After another thirty seconds, Sarah squeezed Steve’s hand.

“Ten metres, coming towards Arthur.”

“Arthur, take a knee on Sarah’s mark,” Steve said.

“Seven metres. It is directly in front of Arthur but it is moving low. Five. Three. Now.”

Arthur dropped to his knee just as Steve turned and unleashed a cone of white frost into the air over his head. The cone travelled the three metres and covered what was air a moment before in a thin coating of frost.

Arthur exploded forward leading with the Messer just as Donna triggered Threshold Drive. The frost had stunned the guardian for a moment and in that window the sword and the axe both found their mark. Viscous water coated Arthur's hands as the sword slashed at the guardian. Donna had already put two strikes into what looked like an appendage joint.

Then the guardian broke off the stun and started flowing backwards. Arthur and Donna kept pace, Donna her usual economical self and Arthur swinging the Messer like a hammer. They got a few solid blows in before the guardian managed to drop off the platform and move into the open space beyond. Without the column shadows in the temple, the sun had an immediate effect and the frost on the chromatophore skin melted away.

This was bad, since in their mad rush to put it down they had moved beyond Sarah's Field Triage range and now they were on open ground, with dirt and rocks and pines.

“Back, we need to get back. Too much open ground,” Donna said, dropping out of Threshold Drive, a small trickle of blood flowing down her nose.

Arthur managed to take a step back and then found himself staring at the open sky. One of the tentacles had gripped his ankle and toppled him over. What Donna did next was smart and crazy. She recognised the only advantage they had: they knew the tentacle was gripping Arthur’s ankle while he still held the Messer.

So while Arthur sat up and brought the Messer down where the tentacle gripping his ankle would have been, Donna triggered Threshold Drive for the third time and for the next few seconds her axe was moving all around them in a tight circle, inches from Arthur. An axe head moving two inches from your limbs, at a speed the eye catches only in stops and starts, is terrifying. It is also a superb deterrent to any other tentacle reaching in.

The messer had hit its mark and Arthur’s ankle was free; he scrambled back carefully as Donna moved in front of him. By the time he had found his feet, Donna had dropped out of Threshold Drive and was running beside him. They scrambled up the temple platform.

The first thing Arthur felt was a stream of warm Ether flowing through him. He saw another strand leap from Sarah and attach itself to Donna who was running on the last of her reserves.

“Don’t think we will not talk about what you two just did,” Sarah said through gritted teeth.

“Do you see it?” Arthur asked, trying to change the subject.

“No,”

A few seconds later Arthur felt the healing cut off as Sarah focused all her attention on Donna, who was still bleeding from her nose.

“I’m sure it is hurt pretty bad. We need one more shot,” Arthur said.

Suddenly Sarah’s head jerked up and she screamed. “Above.” She had been concentrating on stabilising Donna’s Nadi-Matrix and had missed the guardian ambushing them from the top of the pillars.

Arthur was standing rigid in the middle of the platform with his arms at his sides while Steve and Donna looked about frantically for some sign of the guardian.

Then Sarah gasped. “It is around Arthur. All around him.”

“What do you mean all around him?” Steve asked.

“I can see its matrix all around Arthur. It must have enveloped him, like children putting a bedsheet over themselves to imitate ghosts.”

“That’s a stupid move. I could peel it away one layer at a time, if I know it is around Arthur,” Donna said, moving towards Arthur.

“Except he will suffocate much before that,” Sarah said.

Arthur’s arms were pinned to his sides and the veins on his neck stood out against the skin. His eyes were wide but there was no sound coming from him. He was trying to move his wrist which still held the Messer but it barely moved an inch.

Donna and Steve stood helpless as the guardian started to drag itself back while Arthur fought for breath covered in a transparent shroud.

Sarah did the only thing she could, she raised her hand and a Nadi-Matrix disrupting jolt leapt at the guardian. The disruption was better when used with touch but she couldn’t risk getting near and getting trapped herself. It would have to be enough.

Both Arthur and the guardian spasmed. But Arthur had been a guinea pig for Sarah before while the guardian was experiencing a foreign Ether overpowering its Nadi-Matrix for the first time. Arthur regained muscle control first and the shroud around him was now slack enough for him to move his arm.

He did not try to throw it off and scramble to safety. He was now within the guard and couldn’t miss even if he tried. The Messer went straight up and kept going. It tore through the guardian from the inside and came out from the top. By the time the creature had regained muscle control it had two feet of steel sticking out through it from the inside. It tried to flow away, more concerned with the steel than the man, but Arthur was too close and the guardian too injured. He clutched at the insides with his left hand and his fist closed on something wet and slippery, he ignored the bile rising in his throat. With the grip on the guardian he pulled the sword down and thrust it back up again.

The shroud around him went slack, Arthur threw it over his shoulder and slumped to his knees, gratefully gulping in lungfuls of air. It was done. What lay on the stylobate in the full morning light was a creature after all. Soft and grey and terribly simple with no more tricks left in it.
