The next morning, Luke awoke from a restful sleep. Unlike the night before, he didn’t have to worry about Victoria curled up beside him. That peace of mind allowed him to fully relax and drift into a deep, undisturbed slumber.

As he stretched, his eyes landed on Victoria at the far end of the room. She was getting ready, her silhouette framed by the golden morning light filtering through the window. For a brief moment, the sight stole his breath, leaving him in a daze.

He watched in silence as she plucked a flower from a nearby vase, crushed it gently in her palm, and dabbed the petals on her wrists and neck.

Luke froze.

His mind raced.

“Victoria, what are you doing?” he asked, his tone sharper than he intended.

Thankfully, she responded without concern. “It’s jasmine. I quite like the scent on my skin.”

Luke frowned, deep in thought. His brows drew together as an idea began to form.

“I think I know how we can make some money,” he said, his voice suddenly serious.

He had already considered a few options. With his knowledge of Earth and his Scholarly Pursuit skill, Luke knew he could eventually create innovations that might revolutionize this world. But those ideas required time, resources, and a society willing to accept change—none of which he had in abundance.

Yet watching Victoria apply the crushed flower to her skin had sparked something different.

He didn’t need to engineer a miracle. He needed something simpler. Something immediate.

A grin spread across his face, lighting up his features.

“What is it?” Victoria asked, intrigued.

“You’ve just given me a brilliant idea,” he said, leaping to his feet. “Let’s grab some breakfast. I’ll tell you everything later.”

He nearly skipped as he got dressed, energized by his epiphany.

Perfume doesn’t exist here—at least not like the ones I remember from Earth, he thought gleefully. And it’s not just perfume. Proper makeup and cosmetics don’t exist either.

Even in this world, what woman wouldn’t want to smell nice or enhance her beauty to turn heads? This could be huge.

***

For Luke, the day’s lectures felt endless.

Thankfully, the etiquette instructor still hadn’t been replaced, giving him an unexpected break—an entire hour to explore the library again.

Armed with his ability to absorb the contents of books almost instantly, Luke wandered the fifth floor, grazing through the shelves. He’d learned from experience not to overdo it—last time, reading two thousand books had nearly made him faint. This time, he capped it at a thousand.

With that, he’d completed about thirty percent of the fifth floor. At this rate, he could finish the entire library in record time.

Once lectures ended, Luke walked to the academy gates alongside Victoria.

What he saw waiting there made him stop in his tracks.

A full escort stood assembled—at least ten guards in polished armor, spears in hand, forming a protective ring around a man dressed in a crimson robe lined with golden trim.

Luke didn’t recognize him, but the deference shown by the guards made it clear: this man held significant power.

Immediately, Luke straightened his posture and schooled his expression.

He had no idea who the man was, but one thing was certain—this wasn’t a casual visit.

“You must be Luke Drakon,” said the man in the golden robe, his tone surprisingly friendly. He had a fair complexion, a clean-shaven face, and eyes that gleamed with intelligence. While one wouldn’t call him handsome, he wasn’t exactly plain either.

“That is me, my lord,” Luke replied, cupping his fist in a respectful gesture.

“You are in the presence of Crown Prince Leo Marxx,” a nearby guard bellowed. “You shall address him as Your Highness.”

Luke flinched and lowered his head further. “Forgive me, Your Highness,” he said quickly. “I’ve only just arrived in Ralis City and haven’t quite found my bearings yet.”

The prince let out a light laugh and clapped Luke on the shoulder. “All is forgiven. Now come—my father has been anxious about your safety,” he said with a warm smile.

“Thank you, Your Highness.”

Now that Luke knew the man’s identity, confusion set in. He had met King Julius before, but the person before him bore little resemblance—apart from the eyes.

Even his demeanor is a stark contrast to the king. Could he be an illegitimate son? Luke wondered.

But he quickly dismissed the thought. From what he’d read, the king’s concubines were kept deep within the inner palace, surrounded by eunuchs. The odds of another man planting his seed there were virtually nonexistent.

“I heard you had a bit of trouble during your last trip?” Leo asked, glancing briefly at Luke before shifting his eyes to Victoria behind him.

“Yes, Your Highness,” Luke answered evenly. “Thankfully, the royal alchemist and I managed to escape our attackers.”

The prince chuckled, the sound light and pleasant. “Escape? I heard you killed five men in the morning and another ten on the way back. No need for modesty, Viscount,” he teased.

“I was merely protecting my life, Your Highness,” Luke replied without embellishment.

“Of course. Even at a glance, I can tell you’re a capable man,” Leo said, his eyes narrowing slightly as he studied Luke.

Feeling that penetrating gaze, Luke tensed.

Thank the gods I got the Sovereign’s Mask last night, he thought. Being scrutinized by someone of this status is never a good thing.

“You interest me, Luke Drakon,” the prince continued unabashed. “For a young man of just seventeen to have accomplished so much already… It’s a sign you’re destined for greater things.”

Luke pushed down his discomfort and replied with polite modesty, “You flatter me, Your Highness. I’ve only done what was necessary to survive.”

Suddenly, the prince halted, bringing the entire escort to a stop. He turned toward Luke, his confident smile returning.

“I could use someone like you, Viscount. Allow me to offer—albeit prematurely—a place within my service once you graduate.”

Before Luke could respond, the prince added, “Of course, I don’t expect an answer now. Even as crown prince, I’m forbidden from recruiting students directly from the Royal Academy. Still…” He paused, his gaze sharp. “I believe in your potential.”

He wants to recruit me into the war for succession? Is he insane?! Luke reeled inwardly.

To join the war for succession meant choosing a side—and dealing with the consequences. If that side lost, the prince could be executed, and all of his supporters stripped of their titles, wealth, and influence.

This was why most noble families remained neutral until the final stages of succession. Of course, that didn’t mean they weren’t quietly backing a candidate from the shadows.

But this didn’t apply to Luke, who was a viscount in name only. He held no real power. He didn’t even have a coin to his name. So why would the crown prince want him on his side?

Luke cupped his fist and bowed politely. “I thank you for your consideration, Your Highness,” he replied with practiced respect.

Thankfully, the crown prince said nothing more for the remainder of the trip to the palace.

When they arrived at the gates, Luke bid him farewell and left as calmly—and quickly—as he could. Internally, he was screaming.

Fuck… Things just got complicated, he cursed inwardly.

He was already on thin ice because of the gunpowder recipe. Now that the crown prince had shown open interest in him, he’d likely drawn the attention of the other princes—and their allies.

“Why do you look so stressed, Luke? Do you need a massage?” Victoria asked sweetly, brushing her hand along his arm.

Luke let out a sigh and shook his head. “The last thing I want is to be caught up in politics,” he muttered, weariness creeping into his voice. “We’ll need to reassess our plans soon.”

“It’ll be fine,” she replied, voice warm and confident. “We’ll follow whatever decision you make.”

Her words eased something in his chest, and for a moment, his mood lifted. On the bright side, he still had the remainder of his two years at the Academy. Technically, he was safe—at least for now.

This should give me time. Time to assess the situation and prepare.

I’ll need contingencies. If the worst comes to pass, I’ll abandon the Marxx Kingdom entirely, Luke thought, his eyes narrowing.

“Viscount, it’s good to see you again,” came a voice from the doorway. Blake Thompson, the royal alchemist, stepped forward with a smile. “May I ask who your companion is?”

“Blake, this is my servant, Victoria. She accompanied me today for… protection,” Luke replied, giving the man a pointed look.

“O-Of course,” Blake stammered. “Please, come in. We’ve prepared another batch of the ingredients for the gunpowder, but decided to wait until you were present before proceeding.”

Luke nodded. “Good, good. But before that, I have a few ingredients I’d like you to hand over—if you don’t mind.”

Blake raised a brow. “Are they for the gunpowder, Viscount?”

“No, nothing that urgent. Just a few common materials for a separate project I’m working on,” Luke said casually, waving it off.

“I see. That should be no problem. Please write them down, and I’ll have my assistant gather them for you before you leave.”

Score! Luke cheered inwardly. Now I won’t even have to spend a single coin to make the first batch of products.

“Excellent,” he said aloud, clapping his hands together. “Let’s get to it.”

***

Later that night, Luke and Victoria were shown to a guest room within the palace and served an array of dishes straight from the royal kitchens. Despite being visitors, the hospitality extended to them was generous—something Luke truly appreciated.

The room was spacious, furnished with a dining table and a small lounge area. However, there was only one bed. A large window opened to the night sky, letting the moonlight filter in and stretch long shadows across the stone floor.

“I’m starving,” Luke muttered as he took a seat at the table. Without another word, he dove into the roasted chicken and array of sides with visible gusto.

Today had been productive. Not only had he finalized the inferior gunpowder recipe, but he’d also conveniently left out one key detail—the proper storage method.

He chuckled to himself, imagining the assistants’ panic when they tried to ignite it and failed. It might’ve seemed cruel, but Luke had his reasons. Creating doubt among the alchemists would make them hesitant to tamper with the formula. And any delay on their part only bought Luke more time to stay ahead.

“You’re in a good mood,” Victoria said, smiling across the table. She ate with poise and elegance, a stark contrast to his borderline barbaric enthusiasm.

“I’m having dinner with a beautiful woman. Of course I’m in a good mood,” Luke replied, laughing lightly.

Ah shit, where did that come from? he thought, blinking in confusion.

But then he saw her blush—and his mind blanked.

Victoria turned her gaze away, cheeks flushed. She quickly changed the subject. “What were those materials you asked the alchemist for?”

“Ah—those are for our next venture,” Luke said, regaining his composure. “You actually inspired me this morning, when you used the jasmine flower on your skin.”

Her eyes lit up with curiosity. “What is it?”

“It’s called perfume,” he explained. “Basically, I’ll infuse pleasant scents into oil or alcohol and sell them to noblewomen to wear. It’s subtle, elegant, and completely revolutionary. If it catches on, we could make a fortune.”

Victoria gasped softly, then suddenly leapt forward and grabbed his still-greasy hands, her eyes locked onto his with excitement. “Will you make some for me?” she asked, her voice almost pleading.

“O-Of course…”
