Kyle swore as he narrowly avoided another thick spear of stone, only to find the rock beneath his feet jut up to restrain his ankles.  He used Cultivate to break the stony restraints with spears of bamboo, but it cost him a precious moment.  The hodag closed the distance between them, and one of its massive front paws came swiping down to crush him.  Kyle’s counterattack would need time that he simply didn’t have right now.

Dodging toward the creature would be the easiest way to avoid the paw swipe, but with Storm Shelter and Aura Force still needing time before they could be used, he had no reliable method to avoid being crushed by a follow-up body slam.  Dodging backward gave the hodag more opportunities to continue its assault, while blocking was entirely out of the question.  Covered in stony armor, the descending claw had enough power to smash him flat.

So, Kyle jumped backward.  At the same time, he extended the pointed end of the staff in his right hand toward the hodag.  Willing the wood to release some of the seeds stored within, he activated Cultivate, creating a thick barrier of bark and dozens of fast-growing bamboo spears.  The spears struck the hodag’s stony armor, barely penetrating, though managing to push Kyle back to get some distance.  Using his staff like a pole vaulter, he pressed the pointed end into the ground to avoid more grasping stone, creating even more distance between himself and the rampaging hodag.

This was truly one of the worst matchups for him.  It had extremely high durability, limiting the effectiveness of Aura Force and requiring some sustained focus with Parasitic Resonance.  At the same time, the monster had incredible physical strength, befitting its size.  It made trading blows directly a non-starter, as even with Kyle’s impressive durability he knew that he’d take grievous injuries from direct hits.  On top of all that, its use of stone both prevented Kyle from easily connecting with Parasitic Resonance, while also exploiting one of his greatest weaknesses: physical restraints.

Fighting the hodag was like waging war against the land itself.  The hodag’s mastery of its skill made the area around it a truly living domain, small-scale stone manipulation constantly threatening his footwork and balance, while larger expressions threatened to engulf and smother him under a wave of manipulated rock.  The inorganic material was nigh immune to Parasitic Resonance, preventing him from disrupting the mana being used to manipulate it.  He needed a change of scenery, and fast.

“C.H.A.D.D., highlight where all the trees fell!”

The drone obliged, while flying Kyle toward the area of the battlefield with the largest concentration of fallen trees from his earlier aerial bombardment.  As he approached, he could feel his dormant energy within, the trees lying dormant among the shattered stone.  Feeling the connection despite their distance, he activated Cultivate.  Limbs and roots exploded outward from the fallen trees, digging into the craggy gray rock while also climbing toward the clear sky above.

Vines erupted out from the shoulder of Model: Shield, wrapping around the nearest protruding branches and pulling him close in a large arc.  All the while, the hodag was hot on his tail.  It let out another loud roar, and fired a cannonball-sized chunk of stone directly at him.  With his trajectory defined by the vines and branches, Kyle raised Model: Shield to absorb the blow.  The plates of wood creaked under the strain, even as the shock-absorbing vines beneath stopped much of the impact before knocking the projectile away.  Still, he felt the impact reverberate throughout his body as he felt the uneven tree bark beneath his feet.  With the hodag still pursuing him, he finally had an opportunity to fight back.

The ground let out ominous cracks as the tree roots dug deeply into he stone, the branches of the swiftly-growing trees reaching out toward the charging hodag.  It didn’t slow for a moment, clearly expecting to overpower the defenses.  That is, until they started glowing with golden light.

Kyle felt his mana draining as he activated Infuse, flooding the trees with energy from the World Tree Seed as well as the concept of Immutability.  The wood suddenly turned as strong as any steel, gouging deep furrows into the hodag’s natural stone armor as it crashed into the wooden barricade at full force.  It roared in pain and thrashed about, managing to snap off some of the tree branches still impaling the armor.

Kyle tasted blood in his mouth as he let out a primal roar, pushing Cultivate and Infuse harder than he ever had before.  Glowing golden roots erupted from the ground, wrapping around the hodag’s legs, holding it while Cultivate reached for seeds outside of its normal range; within the hodag’s armor itself.

Before unlocking Aspect of the Tree: Branch, Kyle doubted he would have been able to influence these seeds without a direct connection via Parasitic Resonance.  Now, he could faintly feel them lying dormant within the mana-infused stone.  The connection was tenuous, as if he was trying to move through molasses, but it finally formed.

While Kyle was doing this, the hodag was busy trying to extricate itself from the binding roots, each major movement tearing out chunks of stone along with the torn roots.  Its physical power was nothing to be scoffed at, but this was its first major tactical error.  The deep roots already broke through most of the thick layers of stone, and were helping to hold the stony ground together.  Between that and the excessive stone manipulation the hodag used, the land was already growing unstable.  The moment the roots were removed, a geyser of egg-smelling steam erupted from the spot the hodag’s paw had been.  The ground cracked, then fell into a pool of sizzling sulfuric water, dragging the hodag into the depths below.

Kyle knew the scalding water wouldn’t be enough to kill the creature on its own, so with a final push of effort, he forcibly expanded the seeds embedded within the hodag’s natural armor.  Many were simply crushed, but more than a few, particularly the bamboo and razorpine seeds, managed to explode outward, causing deep cracks and fissures within the creature’s armor.

It wasn’t enough to remove all the thick layers of stone, and Kyle didn’t expect it to be.  Instead, the cracks allowed more and more of the boiling-hot liquid to enter the stony armor and drag the hodag farther down into the depths.  Despite the beast’s resilience, being dragged to the bottom of the liquid would be a death sentence if it couldn’t escape.  With its heavy armor weighing it down, it had only one option.

As Kyle expected, he soon saw an unarmored paw emerge from the newly-formed sinkhole, grasping at the cracked ground as a horned head broke the surface, letting out a spluttering roar.  Kyle was ready for it.  Tendrils of Parasitic Resonance were already reaching out, and his superior Willpower suppressed the monster’s own as he established dominance of its mana network.  Then, with all the remaining power he could muster, Kyle twisted, allowing Phaegos to flood the creature with conceptual Death.  He felt the backlash to his own mana network in the form of familiar numbing cold, and he accepted it.

The hodag simply looked at Kyle for a moment with wide eyes, then collapsed as something new happened.  Kyle felt invigorated as a stream of energy flooded into his own network, alleviating the burden slightly while all of the deathly energy remained isolated in the hodag’s body.  He felt a smug sense of satisfaction from Phaegos, and realized that the entity was getting better at its use of the concept.

“C.H.A.D.D., can you guide me to whichever of the trees is on the most stable ground?” Kyle asked, withdrawing Model: Shield back into the World Tree Seed and deactivating Genesis Engine.

[CERTAINLY, DR. MAYHEW.]

C.H.A.D.D. highlighted a tree trunk off in the distance, and Kyle walked toward it gingerly.  The last thing he wanted in his weakened and exhausted state would be to take a sulfur bath.  Not that he thought it would be particularly dangerous for him, but that it would potentially ruin or damage the scrolls he was supposed to deliver, an outcome that could lead to him failing his job.

[THAT WAS AN IMPRESSIVE FIGHT, DR. MAYHEW.]

“Something tells me it won’t be the last,” Kyle said with a sigh, channeling Adaptive Regeneration along with the concept of Adaptability.  The backlash from using the Death concept was slightly less pronounced than before, but he still needed to work on adapting his body and mana network to handle it better.  Whether he liked it or not, it was a concept that Phaegos was working on incorporating with Parasitic Resonance, and felt was tied to the Progenitor’s path.  Building up his resistance was a paramount objective.

Settling in to meditate a bit once the worst of the damage was healed, he let out a slow breath when he saw the results from fighting the hodag.

He knew he’d gained some levels in his Unbroken Survivor class during some of the other hodag fights, but he felt that the massive gains in the World Tree Tender had come in his most recent encounter.  It made sense, given how much Kyle had to lean on the skills provided by the class in order to emerge victorious.

Giving a quick look at his free attributes, he split them between Endurance, Willpower, and Intelligence.  Those were the attributes most core to his path, and with mid-tier C Grade just around the corner, he wanted to ensure he was prepared.  Seven more levels in Unbroken Survivor, and one more in World Tree Tender.

Once he made the final leap, he would have several more big decisions to make.  At Level 60 and Level 61 in the Unbroken Survivor, he would have skill upgrades to make, and then he’d have the World Tree Tender class evolution and new skill that it provided.

[DR. MAYHEW, ARE YOU READY TO CONTINUE?]

The drone’s question brought Kyle out of this thoughts, and he nodded as he stood.  “I think so.  We still have a lot of ground to cover.”

With all the deliveries, and likely hodag nests between him and the completion of his job, Kyle knew he’d be making those decisions sooner than later.  And he was looking forward to it.
