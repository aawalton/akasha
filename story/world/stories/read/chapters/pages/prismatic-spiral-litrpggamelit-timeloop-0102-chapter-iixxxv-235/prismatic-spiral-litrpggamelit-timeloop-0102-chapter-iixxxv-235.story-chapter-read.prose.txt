Chapter II.XXXV (2.35)

Mitsuko and the Sage of Dilation spent the next few hours sitting in the ice cavern, chatting with one another. Not about the Prismatic Loop or temporal spellcraft. Instead, they simply swapped anecdotes about their respective childhoods and journeys throughout their lives. Perhaps there were more efficient uses of their time but neither of them cared to pursue them. Mitsuko had spent the last few months constantly training, learning about temporal spells, and desperately attempting to uncover secrets. It felt so nice to just chat with someone who really got it. Sterling was… well, Sterling. Her friend, certainly. Not the same as chatting with another girl about life. Verna resonated with Mitsuko in a way she hadn’t felt in ages. Someone cut from the same cloth.

Verna had been crafted from the very heart of Porcelain Island in an attempt for Hrim to harness the power of the archipelago's Titan. Much to his chagrin and frustration, her father discovered he had no talent for temporal spellcraft. His exploration of Porcelain Island and discovery of this place had been a result of him attempting to turn back time on his own body. To return his health to a state pre-accident. Instead, he found this chamber deep within the caverns.

With golem crafting capabilities, Hrim had carved out a child from the ice and instilled in it his most powerful temporal artifact. One that resonated with the Titan’s essence and brought Verna to life.

“He was a terrible father,” Verna confessed as she tapped ice over the frozen clock where her heart should be. “But, I suppose in fairness, I should also admit that it wasn’t like I was a normal child either.”

In return for the sage’s stories, Mitsuko told about her own life back in the Hon Basin. She told Verna about how her mother had died at her father’s hand and the sculpt queen listened in stoic silence, an anger dancing in her eyes.

“You should kill him,” Verna said cooly once Mitsuko finished explaining how she’d gotten word of the murder in a letter.

Mitsuko sighed and rolled her eyes. “I’m not going to kill my pa.”

“Why not? He has been prospering off murder. From the sounds of it, only you and your pals Kizu and Holly know this even occurred. Nothing will change until you force the trajectory. He will remain unpunished. He has a new wife, right? And a new kid? As is, they stand at risk of dying just the same way your mother did. They deserve better just as you deserved better.”

“He was drunk when he killed her.”

“Did he stop drinking after?”

Mitsuko remained silent. It had been the opposite. He’d drowned himself in cheap beer.

“Would you kill your father in my situation?”

“Yes. I was a tool in a quest to discover immortality. I despised my father nearly as much as I loved him. If he ever actively gave me an excuse to want him dead, I would have done my best to do so.

“If you hated him, doesn’t that imply you’d want him dead?”

“No,” Verna replied. “He saw me as an object. When it was proven that I served no purpose in his schemes for immorality, he lost interest. Negligence isn’t in the same category as murdering your wife.”

Mitsuko opened her mouth to argue the point when some fell from above. A small bundle of fur slapped into the floor. It twitched.

Verna approached and prodded it with a crystalized icy foot. A worm-like tail flopped over and a leg stretched out from the creature.

“Ow,” Sterling moaned. “Verna, please stop jabbing me. I am in great pain. And Mitsuko… a hand please?”

Mitsuko cast Mend on the sack of fur and flesh. Her intent kept it from shooting back up the hole and it instead popped back into form, revealing the body of a brown field mouse. Sterling bent his little legs and flicked his long tail across the ice.

“I didn’t realize mice could live on this island,” Mitsuko commented.

“They cannot,” Sterling replied. He strode forward in his mouse body. “Not for any substantial amount of time, at least. The island’s natural chill every night kills most organic life forms. There are precious few native creatures on this island. Unfortunately, I was extremely limited as Hrim’s taint touched a great number of them. I am not fond of birds and I refuse to be relegated to the form of an insect or other small arthropod. This creature was my compromise. I found him on board The Terror.”

“That explains why it took so long for you to revive,” Verna said. “We’ve been waiting for hours now.”

“This is the second mouse body I have inhabited since your father killed me. The first was consumed by a glacial annelid.”

“You mean an ice worm? Like the one I fought earlier?” Mitsuko asked, intrigued. “Are they a common species here?”

“Same species,” Sterling confirmed. “However, the size of your earlier opponent was an abnormality. They rarely survive long enough to expand beyond the length of your leg.”

Verna poked him again with her foot. “This reminds me. I need to decide what creature to inhabit!” She sounded delighted by the prospect. “Any suggestions? I don’t want to inhabit a gull or cat, since Juniper and Pilroth already staked their claim on those.”

“Actually, I do have a suggestion,” Mitsuko said. “I investigated a menagerie on an early loop in preparation for more sages.”

“A menagerie?” Verna’s eyebrows raised. “Why not mention it to Juniper when you met? As far as I am aware, she inhabits nothing more than a common gull.”

“Because she was busy being an ass.”

Verna chuckled. “Fair enough. Anything interesting?”

“I found a friendly little blue crab—”

“Hold up.” Verna raised a hand. “Gotta stop you right there. Can’t do a crab.”

“Why—”

“I can’t tell you. Not without revealing Prismatic Spiral information. Just trust me. I’m not a crab host kind of girl. What else was there?”

“Clams,” Sterling offered. “I took note of mollusks from all reaches of the world. It was quite an exhaustive and impressive collection.”

Verna and Mitsuko both offered the brown mouse withering glares.

“There were several colorful birds,” Mitsuko recalled, thinking back. Then it clicked. “And a turtle!”

“A turtle?” Verna asked, clearly confused.

“Not just any type of turtle.” Mitsuko raised a finger in triumph. “This species is from the far northern reaches of Tross. And their shells are made entirely of ice.”

“Hmm. Thematically appropriate. Though I’m afraid I’ll lack agency in the body of a turtle. They’re not known for their agile natures.”

“Actually, that concern is unfounded in this instance,” Sterling interjected. “Unless their biology has altered significantly since my time, this particular breed of tortoise is quite a bit quicker than its mundane cousins. As they age and grow in size, they slow down considerably, but at the stage of life the tortoise in question is currently at, you’ll be capable of as much free movement as a vole.” He paused, then added an amendment. “So long as the terrain is flat.”

Mitsuko stared at the sage. “Why do you know so much about a turtle?”

“Tortoise,” Sterling corrected. “Unlike a turtle, the creature in question dwells primarily on land. Their shell shape and feet are quite a bit different from one another as well.”

“Of course the shell is different from other turtles,” Mitsuko replied. “It’s literally made of ice.”

“An interesting fact about that ice is that this species of tortoise creates its own shell, excreting fluids through its skin and freezing the liquid with natural magic. So the ice is not simply water, as you might assume, but instead a fluid more akin to sweat.”

“You’re really selling me on this turtle,” Verna replied dryly, though she was smiling.

“Tortoise,” Sterling corrected. “And, as I said before, this particular breed has significantly more agility than you might first assume. The bottom of their shells is not only slick, but also designed to slide over rough ground. They propel themselves forward with their legs and slide across the ground. Not unlike how Mitsuko was navigating in the low hanging ceiling caverns of this cave network.”

“Interesting.” Verna tapped her chin with a finger. “I’ll consider the offer.”

A silence stretched between them.

“So… I freed the sage. Now what?” Mitsuko asked.

—

Mitsuko lunged and threw herself forward as the ice worm burst out of the ice in an attempt to gobble up Sterling. She caught the creature, her fingers jabbing into its mouth and forced her weight down on it, pinning it to the icy floor. It was only the size of her arm but it bucked and squirmed with enough force to rival a donkey’s kick. Resolute, Mitsuko wrestled the creature and did her best to ignore the pain as it grinded her fingers between its rows of teeth, shredding off layers of skin and flesh. Thankfully, her bones were too thick for the creature to crunch without a better angle.

With Hrim’s influence fading as the loop went on, she could now cast spells on the wild creatures of Porcelain Island, though with more difficulty than what she experienced with normal animals and people. The spells would slip off and not activate quite as intended without her full focus and intent. However, Mitsuko deemed the extra challenge as good practice.

She cast Deteriorate on the rowdy worm. With so much focus on the spell, the monster nearly slipped out of her grip as it jerked. She steadied both her mind and body.

The worm hissed, steam rising up from where her bare skin pressed against it. A minute later, it fell to the ground, trisected, both of her hands having seared through its icy body. Unlike some other worms found in the wild, this species couldn’t survive after getting chopped in pieces.

Mitsuko flicked her wet hands and turned her attention to the annoyed sage in a mouse body. She nodded along as she pretended to listen to him complain about being used as bait.

She picked up the mouse and they started on their way back to the ice chamber at the heart of the island.

Without a good reason to return to The Terror, Mitsuko had spent this back half of the loop in the ice caverns, training under both Sterling and Verna’s tutelage. Unfortunately, she needed to wait until the loop ended before she’d gain access to Verna’s Dilation spell, so she mostly experimented with Deteriorate and Mend. The ice caverns offered a very unique training ground for those two spells, as they were more susceptible to changes in time than other landscapes. Deteriorating the walls caused them to melt while Mending them froze them over again. It made for a fun setting to practice her magic.

As the week continued, Mitsuko found more and more caverns submerged. She’d been  on her way back from practicing Mending large ponds back into ice when she’d noticed the ice worm. It was a rare opportunity. She didn’t regret for a second using Sterling as bait.

Mitsuko slid down the hole in the throne. Her ankle snapped and she only just caught herself from sprawling on the icy floor. With a shake of her foot, she Mended her broken ankle back into place with ease.

“—unorthodox and rude behavior,” Sterling ranted from her pocket. “I used to be respected. Throngs of thousands bowed and offered their bodies in subligation for the mere opportunity to spare me the inconvenience of a stroll through the mud. Of course, I’d never stoop so low as to accept such an offer, but the point is that is how much respect my presence inspired!”

“And now you’re a scrawny rodent riding around in a half-dressed woman’s jacket pocket,” Verna replied, skipping over greetings in favor of mocking Sterling.

“No need to say it like that. I’m only missing a shirt,” Mitsuko protested. “The jacket completely covers me up.”

She’d never found the massive worm she’d slain during her fight with Hrim. As a result, she’d never recovered the ashes of her old shirt and, of course, nothing else down in these caverns wore shirts.

“Well, you’ll be fully dressed soon enough,” Verna said. “You have about a minute until the loop concludes.”

“You’re not supposed to reveal information like that,” Sterling said in a huff.

“Nah, you’re not supposed to reveal info like that. I’m at no risk of being obliterated and banished for the remaining loops. The other sages would never do that. Everyone loves me.”

“Any parting advice?” Mitsuko asked the sage.

Verna pursed her lips as she considered for several seconds. “Nope. Can’t think of anything. Catch you in the next—”
