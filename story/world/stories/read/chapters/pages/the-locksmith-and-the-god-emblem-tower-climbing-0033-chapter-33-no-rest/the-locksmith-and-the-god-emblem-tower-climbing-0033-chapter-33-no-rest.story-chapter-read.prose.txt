"She did what!?" Daniel said while audibly growling. "And you got hit by her without even striking back? How weak are you?"

"Okay, dad, that's enough..." Brun held back her dad, sounding somewhat disappointed. "She wronged all of us, but I'm sure you're the one who got most affected, Harry. I deeply apologize for not seeing it earlier. I mean, now thinking back, the circumstances in which she appeared were strange enough. I just... I had so much in my head that I..."

Harry didn't answer, but instead gave her an apologetic smile and sighed deeply. They were all reunited, except for Prism. It had been a couple hours since Harry and Goldman fell unconscious in the sand. The pirate, even though sustaining the most damage, healed way faster than Harry. Although he wasn't sure, the young thief blamed this on his physique and athletic disposition. Yes, the Locksmith was athletic in his own way, being nimble and such. He wasn't, however, a force of resistance like Goldman.

The group explained that they went after the hatch once it became clear they were no longer in control of the situation. Perhaps never were. Vela tried to go back, but after losing grip of Petrikov and almost dying several times, he thought if maybe they weren't already on the next floor. Seeing Harry here made Vela and the rest of the group a bit uncomfortable. Not because they didn't want him, but rather that they left him for dead.

Specially Madeline, who fought hard not to stare at the young thief. Being the one who knew Harry the most out of all of them, she felt even more responsible. Although they were 'opponents' in the outside, this didn't apply totally here. I'm sorry. She said to herself, unable to direct the words to the man.

"It's okay, Brun. It wasn't your fault. It wasn't anyone's fault." Harry replied. "I also got too... over my head and didn't saw her for who she was."

They were surrounded by trees and vegetation, yet it all felt artificial. There wasn't anyone with botanical knowledge in the group, but they all saw the different greens and smelled the different scents; the whole place was borrowed. A few palm trees clashed against regular pine ones, which made the whole place feel asymmetrical and inconsistent. On the side, Manes used his Emblem to grab a coconut.

"Um, I wouldn't eat that." Goldman said, his voice now hoarse. "We don't even know where it comes from."

"Of course we do." Manes replied with a uncharacteristic grin. "It comes from the palm trees."

Goldman let it go and they continued their discussion. The lighthouse still loomed on the distance, its light periodically shining through their heads, almost as if it could hear them. Apparently, everyone had already used their C-Coins for the loop, so they began to formulate a plan to escape the floor.

"Does anyone know where the hatch is?" Daniel asked, looking around.

"Not yet." Harry said. "But maybe it is probably in the center of the floor, just like last time."

"And I'm positive it will be on that lighthouse over there." Brun pointed. "What we don't know, however, is that if it'll be on the top of it, or directly below the island."

"But why wasn't the hatch below the mountain as well?" Madeline asked.

"Because there wasn't proper space for the hatch to properly exist." The old woman replied. "That's my guess, at least. This floor, for example, doesn't have such a verticality element to it, unlike the last one, who was based entirely on climbing the mountain. There is a logic to these floors, and the hatches are normally around that critical point. Thankfully, all of these points have been concentrated in the center. Either way, I can use my Emblem when we're near to see if we're right. In the meantime, we can explore."

The ground them rumbled suddenly as the waves all around the floating island got agitated. The vortex, converging on the center, where the island stood, was spinning faster as a shadow could be seen underneath the waters.

"What was that?" Manes was the first to ask, clearly terrified. "Did anyone see that?"

"...Yeah, we did." Madeline replied, getting near Harry. "Looks like we got company."

"W-What do you mean company?" The boy replied. "Like, um, with the birds and shit?"

"Relax, kid." Goldman gave him a smile and patted on his back. "She was only joking."

"No, I'm serious, we literally got company." She pointed towards the sea, exactly where the shadow stood. "Take a look at that."

Far away, a group of small creatures formed a shadow for an instant. Although it was obvious to see that the shadow was composed of hundreds, maybe even thousands of little critters.

"...Has anyone seen any Meta around?" Harry then asked, an idea forming on his mind.

Everyone then, after shaking their heads, came to the same conclusion; that thing could be a Meta. They could adapt and evolve inside a given loop, and although the previous floor didn't give too much room for development, this one was brimming with life all around. Even the trees glowed if you looked at it for too long. Huh. Funny.

The group, led by Goldman, decided to find a more comfortable spot near the forest and get enough rest for the next stage. Although Goldman was in much better shape than Harry, he still wasn't ready for any kind of fight, especially against Emblem users.

They had to stay alert, as Prism's group certainly knew they were on the island. They had no idea what Emblems Prism's group possessed, so they didn't want to risk their chances. They spent the next two hours recovering their energy.

The Locksmith had only mentioned to the group that Prism might be with a group, but omitted the conversation they had a loop before the climb. Vela, however, knew.

At one point, Harry went out to look for some fruit or coconuts. As he moved away from the group, he heard a branch breaking behind him and quickly turned around, his reflexes on high alert.

"...Ah. It's you." He then gave a chuckle and relaxed. "Fuck. Don't scare me like that, Maddy."

Maddy? She grinned.

"Like you could actually do something." The young woman approached him. "Look at yourself; I've seen better Holksworth folk in better shape than you."

"Wait, so you still remember!?" He smiled as well. "I thought I was the only one left."

"I wish I didn't, to be honest..." Madeline said. "But holy shit! How could anyone forget that? Why would you throw an orgy right after coming from a fucking funeral? Like, I know it was a banker, which meant he only had half of his soul left, but by the gods..."

"It was the first time I actually regretted trying to steal something from a noble." Harry confessed. "Really. Of all the times and all the fights, that was definitely the worst one. The images of Ms. Bellingham and, oh God..."

Madeline burst into laughter at his reaction. Harry had never seen her... like this. Laughing. Having fun. He realized then that he had never truly known the woman. He knew the thief, but not Madeline.

"...That was the first robbery we met at, wasn't it?" Harry said, continuing to walk, Madeline following him. "How comical..."

"It's a good thing it wasn't the last," she said, more relaxed. "I'd hate to look at your face and only remember that moment."

Harry just chuckled lightly and kept walking. He gazed at the trees and the world around him. The Locksmith marveled at how someone could fit all of that onto a single floor. He thought about how the Logos Tower could have saved thousands from hunger and misery if applied to the community. Instead, it became a prison and exile for the most hated and loved, which certainly said a lot about human nature. He then looked at Madeline beside him and, as he was about to ask what she wanted with him, she overruled him.

"I'm sorry," Madeline began. She sighed deeply. "Really. As soon as all those stones started falling, I looked back and didn't see anything anymore. I thought Petrikov and you were right behind me, but I was wrong. If I'd been a little more focused and attentive, I would have realized that—"

We've already witnessed a whole orgy of geriatric people and pretentious nobles, so I guess a near-death experience doesn't count for much. Wouldn't you agree?"

Madeline, surprised by the response, opened her mouth to say something, but quickly closed it and simply laughed. "You idiot..." And then she lightly punched his shoulder.

"Although I should've noticed Prism's true intentions." Harry said, a new layer of melancholy in his voice. "Maybe Petrikov would still be alive."

"She used you, Harry. Just like she used all of us. I mean, yeah, if you were thinking straight you could've predicted it, but..."

"...What's that suppose to mean?" He asked, dumbfounded. They resumed their walk.

"Oh, Harry." She said with a malicious smile. "A girl knows a thing or two. I could sense you turning rock solid every time she accidentally touched you—"

"You know what? Shut up, Maddy." He raised a finger to her and looked the other way. "Y-You don't know what you're talking about. For your information, I... I got laid pretty often?"

"Is this a question?..." She raised her hands and said with a grin. "Didn't peg you for such an insecure guy, Harry boy. Actually, thinking about it now, I think I remember that day at the Holksworth a little clearer now... You clearly wanted in."

"Oh, shut the fuck up..." He bumped her to the side, Madeline laughing to herself. "That's the meanest thing you ever said to me."

"Yeah, yeah..." She replied, looking down. "And I'm sorry. For everything, I mean."

Harry then took a glance at her and saw a faint smile rapidly escaping her face.

"I know."
