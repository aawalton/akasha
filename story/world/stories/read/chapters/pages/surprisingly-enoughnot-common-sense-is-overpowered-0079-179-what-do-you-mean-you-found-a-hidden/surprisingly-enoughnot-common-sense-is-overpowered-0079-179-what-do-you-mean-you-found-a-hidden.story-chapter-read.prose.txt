“Okay, you stay on watch.” Ning crouched beside Fenghuang and lowered his voice. “The Crystal-Eyed Hound usually spends more than an hour hunting, but who knows if today will be different. If you see anything, give me a signal, and I’ll come straight back.”

Fenghuang stared at him for a few seconds before giving a small nod.

“Good.” Ning smiled and rubbed his head. “You really are the best.”

Fenghuang no longer bothered resisting.

He had hated it in the beginning. Every attempt to touch his feathers had been met with an angry peck or a burst of flame, but after living with Ning for so long, he had apparently gotten used to it.

Ning could even feel through their bond that the bird was enjoying himself.

Not that Fenghuang would ever admit it.

Was it cheating to use Pure Eyes to figure out exactly where Fenghuang liked being scratched?

Maybe.

Was it effective?

Absolutely.

“So,” Ning whispered as he stood, “Operation Take-the-Shit-and-Go officially begins.”

Fenghuang stared at him.

“…Right. Stealth mission mindset.”

The white bird spread his wings and flew into the trees, soon disappearing among the branches.

Ning activated the Turtle Breathing Art. His heartbeat slowed, his breathing became shallow, and the spiritual qi around him settled until barely any leaked from his body.

He headed toward the cave.

Thick roots and broken stone covered most of the entrance, making it difficult to spot from a distance. Ning could still see faint traces of spiritual qi around the opening with Pure Eyes, along with several old claw marks on the nearby rocks.

He slipped inside.

The temperature dropped quickly as he went deeper, though the cold didn't bother him much. The smell was another matter.

Wet stone, old blood, and rotting meat mixed into something particularly unpleasant.

Cultivators really romanticize exploration too much.

Ning continued deeper without touching anything.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound was a late-stage Qi Condensation spiritual beast, roughly resembling an oversized black wolf with crystalline growths along its neck and spine. It was strong enough to tear through low-grade defensive artifacts, but its senses were what Ning was actually worried about. Its eyes were extremely sensitive to spiritual light and qi fluctuations, making sneaking into its territory difficult even for cultivators skilled at hiding themselves.

It was also extremely territorial. Once it decided someone was an intruder, getting chased for several kilometers was entirely possible.

Ning had no intention of fighting it if he could avoid it.

He was here for the treasure.

Pure Eyes had consumed an unreasonable amount of spiritual liquid over the years, and its appetite only seemed to grow with every improvement. Ning still bought suitable resources whenever he found them at a decent price, but relying entirely on the market was becoming expensive.

So he had started looking for places where such resources formed naturally.

That was how he learned about the Shining Crystal Spiritual Liquid.

Si Sihua had helped with that. Working in the Mission Hall meant she heard all kinds of rumors and reports, including plenty that most disciples would ignore.

One of those reports came from an outer sect disciple who had killed a low-tier boar in this region and found several drops of strange crystal-like liquid on its body.

The story itself wasn't much, but Ning had looked into it anyway. Once he found traces of a Crystal-Eyed Hound living nearby, the possibility became much more interesting.

There was a good chance those few drops had come from its territory.

So Ning had waited, observed the beast's hunting habits, found its cave, and chosen a time when it was away.

Now came the important part.

Looting.

A mandatory skill for any respectable cultivator.

The cave stretched much farther underground than Ning had expected. Crystals grew from the walls in clusters, giving off enough pale blue light for him to see without trouble. Water dripped from the ceiling and ran through narrow cracks in the stone.

It was actually quite beautiful.

Then Ning spotted half a deer rotting beside the wall.

Never mind.

He kept going until the passage opened into a larger cavern.

There, near the center, was a wide depression in the stone filled with silver-blue liquid.

Ning's eyes lit up.

“Shining Crystal Spiritual Liquid…”

And there was a lot of it.

He approached the basin and crouched beside it.

Shining Crystal Spiritual Liquid was a First-Tier high-grade treasure that formed slowly in crystal-rich caves. Moon-attributed spiritual qi mixed with mineral essence over decades until small amounts of liquid began to gather.

Ancient texts called it Earth marrow reflecting moonlight.

It had several uses, but Ning only cared about one.

It was extremely useful for refining the eyes and sharpening perception, which made it highly compatible with Pure Eyes.

This was exactly what he had been looking for.

Ning examined the liquid more closely. It was almost transparent, with a faint silver-blue tint, and he couldn't see much impurity even with Pure Eyes.

Better yet, the basin was nearly half full.

No wonder the Crystal-Eyed Hound had chosen this place as its den.

The beast had probably been drinking from the basin for years. Considering how sensitive the species' eyes already were, Ning didn't particularly want to know how good this one's eyesight had become.

The creature probably noticed most intruders long before they ever realized it existed.

And now, Ning was about to rob the creature blind. Pun entirely intended.

Ning quickly removed several jade bottles from his storage bag, then carefully started to take the resource.

His movements were extremely precise.

The jade bottles tilted gently as he transferred the liquid with painstaking care, making absolutely certain not to waste even a single drop. Even the spiritual mist rising from the surface was carefully sealed within the containers.

One bottle after another.

It was at that moment that Ning heard a distant roar and felt the ground shake violently.

“Hmm?”

...

At the same time, outside the cave, Fenghuang had already run into trouble.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound came crashing through the forest, knocking aside branches and smaller trees as it rushed back toward its cave. Fenghuang had been slightly careless while keeping watch; the hound's enhanced eyesight had spotted him circling above the area before he noticed it approaching, leaving him with little time to warn Ning.

Craw!

Fenghuang climbed higher as the beast rushed beneath him. Ning was still inside the cave, so leaving now would only lead the hound straight back to him. Since he had already been discovered, Fenghuang decided to keep its attention on himself for as long as possible.

The hound leaped from the ground with enough force to crack the soil beneath its paws, its claws passing just beneath Fenghuang's tail. Fenghuang folded one wing and turned sharply over its body, then spat a stream of white flame onto its shoulder as he passed.

The hound landed and immediately twisted around. A patch of black fur had been burned away, but the wound underneath was shallow, which was enough for Fenghuang to understand the difference between them. He wasn't going to overpower a late-stage Qi Condensation beast, so he gained some distance and continued circling through the trees, relying on his speed to keep the hound occupied.

Fortunately, Ning had spent much of the past year making him practice exactly that.

Their flight training had started normally enough, with Fenghuang flying overhead while Ning watched from below and pointed out problems with his turns, acceleration, and use of qi. That lasted until Ning decided that watching from the ground wasn't giving Fenghuang enough pressure.

The next day, he had arrived with a bow.

“You keep dodging upward,” Ning had said. “That works against beasts on the ground, but what happens when someone has a bow?”

Fenghuang had looked down at him, then at the bow in his hands.

“Exactly. Start flying.”

Ning used blunt practice arrows and had made it clear from the beginning that Fenghuang could stop whenever he wanted. Fenghuang naturally refused, and what followed had been several months of increasingly unpleasant training.

At first, Ning simply shot at him while he flew. Once Fenghuang became comfortable avoiding those arrows, Ning began aiming where he expected him to go, forcing Fenghuang to break his own flight patterns. Later, Ning started using Shadow Steps while shooting, changing positions between shots and attacking from angles Fenghuang couldn't easily see.

Many feathers had been lost during that period, and Fenghuang had developed a particular dislike for the expression Ning made whenever he picked up a bow and said he had thought of a new training method.

The results, however, were difficult to argue with.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound leaped again, but Fenghuang dropped beneath its claws and climbed behind it before releasing three small clusters of white flame. They scattered among the surrounding trees while the hound ignored them and continued its pursuit.

It regretted that decision a moment later when one of the flames exploded beside its hind leg.

The hound stumbled, and before it could recover, another patch of white flame erupted behind it. Fenghuang used the opening to climb higher while the beast spun around, searching for whatever had attacked it.

This particular trick had also come from training with Ning.

Originally, Fenghuang's flames either struck something and exploded or simply dispersed after leaving his control. Ning had wondered whether he could keep a small amount of qi attached to them and delay the explosion, which had led to several weeks of experimentation.

Learning the technique had been unpleasant. Most of Fenghuang's early attempts either dispersed before he could activate them or exploded far sooner than intended, with one particularly memorable failure blowing him out of the sky and into a river while Ning sat on the bank recording the result.

Fenghuang still remembered Ning laughing that day.

Now, several patches of white flame remained scattered between the trees below him, and when the Crystal-Eyed Hound charged beneath him again, Fenghuang waited until it passed one before triggering it.

White fire burst against the beast's flank. The hound snarled and changed direction, only for another patch to explode near its front legs, forcing it to pull back.

The flames themselves weren't doing much damage, but Fenghuang didn't need them to. Every patch left behind gave the hound another place it had to avoid, making it harder to chase him freely and buying Ning more time inside the cave.

The hound seemed to realize this as well.

The crystals around its eyes brightened, and Fenghuang had barely noticed the change when a thin beam of blue light passed beneath his wing and punched through a tree behind him. Another followed almost immediately, forcing him to roll aside. The beam missed his body, though several white feathers burned away as it passed.

Fenghuang began weaving through the trees while watching the crystals around the beast's eyes. Whenever their glow intensified, he changed direction, sometimes climbing and sometimes dropping between the branches to make himself harder to follow.

For a while, it worked.

Then Fenghuang turned left and saw the hound move before he had even completed the turn. A beam passed directly through the space he had been heading toward, forcing him to pull back sharply.

Fenghuang finally understood why the Crystal-Eyed Hound had noticed him so easily in the first place. The beast wasn't only following his current position. Its eyes were sensitive enough to notice the small changes in his posture and qi that came before each movement, allowing it to anticipate where he was likely to go.

He immediately changed the way he flew, breaking his rhythm with sudden drops, incomplete turns, and abrupt bursts of speed. The hound continued following him, and another beam passed close enough to singe the feathers along his side.

Craw!

Fenghuang cried out irritably and climbed above the trees.

If continuing to dodge wasn't enough, he would have to force the hound to defend itself.

White flame spread across his wings as he flew upward, though instead of releasing it immediately, Fenghuang began drawing the fire toward his beak. Ning had made him practice this as well, forcing him to hold his flames together and compress them into a smaller area rather than allowing the fire to spread freely.

It required considerably more control, but the result was simple enough: the same amount of flame concentrated into a smaller area burned much hotter.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound crouched below as the white flame around Fenghuang's beak grew brighter.

Fenghuang released it.

A narrow stream of white fire shot downward, and the hound immediately tried to move aside. Before it could escape, several of the delayed flames scattered around it erupted at once, cutting off the easiest paths and forcing it to hesitate.

That brief delay was enough for the concentrated flame to strike its chest.

The hound was thrown backward, crashing through several trees before hitting the ground and rolling across the dirt. Burned fur and blood marked the path behind it, while Fenghuang remained above the battlefield and struggled to steady his breathing after using so much qi at once.

For several seconds, the hound didn't move.

Then Fenghuang heard a low growl from below.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound slowly pushed itself back onto its feet. Its chest was badly burned, blood ran down one of its front legs, and part of the crystalline growth near its face had melted under the heat, but the injuries hadn't put it down.

Craw...

Fenghuang had hoped that attack would accomplish a little more.

The hound raised its head, and the crystals around its eyes began glowing again. Before Fenghuang could decide whether to attack or retreat, the beast opened its jaws and released a howl that shook the surrounding forest.

The sound struck Fenghuang hard enough to blur his vision. His wings lost their rhythm, and he dropped several feet before managing to steady himself.

By then, the hound was already in the air.

Fenghuang tried to turn away, but the beast adjusted the angle of its attack as he moved. Its claw followed his dodge, confirming that the hound had read his movement before he could complete it.

There wasn't enough time to escape completely, so Fenghuang twisted his body and tried to keep the attack away from anything vital.

The crystal-covered claw tore across his wing.

Craw!

White feathers scattered through the air as Fenghuang spun away, blood running along the underside of his injured wing. He managed to remain airborne, but every flap sent pain through his body and made it harder to maintain his balance.

The hound landed and immediately jumped after him again.

Fenghuang forced white flame around himself, hoping to keep the beast away long enough to recover, but before the hound could reach him, an arrow tore through the trees and buried itself in its shoulder.

The impact knocked the beast sideways.

Fenghuang recognized the qi on the arrow immediately, and a moment later Ning appeared beneath him and caught him before his injured wing finally gave out.

“You were supposed to warn me, not fight it by yourself.”

Craw.

“I know there wasn't time.”

Ning quickly checked the injured wing before feeding Fenghuang a healing pill. The cut was deep and would need proper treatment later, but nothing seemed to be broken.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound roared behind them.

“Yeah, yeah. I'm leaving.”

Ning tucked Fenghuang against his side and activated Shadow Steps, disappearing between the trees before the hound could attack again. He had already gotten what he came for, and there was no reason to fight a late-stage beast when Fenghuang was injured and they could simply leave.

During his earlier investigation, Ning had roughly marked the edge of the hound's territory and expected it to stop chasing once they crossed it.

It didn't.

Ning crossed the marker and continued for another hundred meters, but the hound remained behind him, smashing through the undergrowth without showing any intention of turning around.

“Seriously?”

A beam of blue light passed beside Ning's head, and he ducked beneath it without slowing down.

Apparently, stealing half the contents of its cave had made the beast considerably less concerned about its usual territorial boundaries.

Fair enough.

Ning continued running for a while longer before realizing that this was becoming more dangerous than simply dealing with the hound. Fenghuang was injured, the beast could follow their trail, and running farther into the mountains increased the chance of attracting something even stronger.

He changed direction and landed on the branch of a large tree, where he carefully placed Fenghuang against the trunk.

“Rest here for a while. I'll deal with it.”

Craw.

“Yes, I know this was supposed to be a quick trip.”

Another roar sounded below, and Ning took out his bow as the Crystal-Eyed Hound appeared between the trees a few breaths later. The burns across its chest were still bleeding, but the crystals around its eyes remained bright as it stared up at him.

Ning placed an arrow on the string and drew.

Yes, Ning had already reached Unity in archery.

After comprehending Unity through the spear, crossing the same threshold in archery became far easier. In fact, his natural talent with the bow had always surpassed his spear talent from the very beginning.

And honestly, what were arrows if not tiny spears flying very fast?

Ning released the string.

The hound moved almost at the same moment, allowing the arrow to pass beside its shoulder. Ning immediately drew another and fired, only for the beast to shift away before the arrow had crossed half the distance between them.

So Fenghuang hadn't simply been having trouble keeping up with its speed.

Ning activated Pure Eyes and fired again while paying close attention to the beast.

The hound dodged again.

Ning remained on the branch and fired another arrow with nearly the same timing. The hound avoided that one as well, moving almost as soon as his fingers began releasing the string.

After several shots, Ning understood what was happening.

The Crystal-Eyed Hound was simply very good at reading the information available to it. His posture, the angle of the bow, the movement of his fingers, and even the gathering of qi before an attack gave the beast enough information to begin reacting before the arrow was released.

That made things much easier.

Ning fired again, and the hound moved left.

He repeated the shot from almost the same position, keeping his draw and release as similar as possible. The hound moved left again, reacting even earlier this time.

Ning continued.

After several arrows, the beast had clearly begun recognizing the pattern. Its weight shifted toward the left before Ning had even finished drawing the next arrow.

This time, Ning changed his aim at the last moment and fired toward the other side.

The hound had already committed to its movement.

The arrow struck its hind leg, causing it to stumble as Ning immediately reached for another.

He didn't give the beast time to settle. The next arrow passed high enough to force it down, while another targeted the injured leg and pushed it sideways. Ning continued controlling where it could safely move, watching each response through Pure Eyes and gradually narrowing the directions available to it.

Then he gathered spiraling qi around the next arrow.

The hound saw the change immediately and prepared to dodge.

Ning released.

The arrow passed wide.

The hound still reacted, jumping toward the opening Ning had deliberately left for it.

By then, Ning had already drawn the next arrow.

The string snapped forward, and before the Crystal-Eyed Hound could correct its movement, the arrow pierced through one of its crystal-covered eyes.

Its body carried forward for several steps before its legs finally gave out. The hound crashed through a patch of bushes and struck the ground heavily, where it remained motionless.

Ning kept the bow raised for several breaths to make sure it wasn't getting back up again. Once he was certain, he lowered it and let out a sigh.

“Well, that was troublesome.”

Fenghuang gave a weak cry from the branch.

Ning looked over at him. “What? You fought it first. I'm allowed to complain too.”

Craw.

“Fine, fine. You were very impressive.”

That seemed to satisfy him.

Ning jumped down beside the Crystal-Eyed Hound and stored the corpse away. A late-stage Qi Condensation beast was worth far too much to leave behind, especially one that had spent years consuming Shining Crystal Spiritual Liquid.

Roar

Hearing a distant sound quickly nearing, Ning had no chance of respite. He picked up Fenghuang and ran away.

[Wind Waking Arrow: Spirit Rank Technique]

[An archery technique focused on control and trajectory manipulation. Instead of forcing arrows through the air, the user synchronizes with surrounding wind currents, allowing arrows to travel more silently and unpredictably.

At higher mastery, arrows can curve mid-flight, bypass obstacles, and strike from unnatural angles.

According to sect rumor, the creator invented this technique after failing to shoot the same spirit bird for three years straight before concluding that "straight lines were the true enemy."]

[Lightning Cry Arrow: Spirit Rank Technique]

[A lightning-attributed archery technique centered around explosive speed and destructive impact. Lightning qi erupts along the arrow during release, drastically increasing acceleration and penetration power.

Upon impact, the compressed lightning detonates violently, disrupting spiritual defenses and temporarily paralyzing targets. Advanced users can manipulate the timing of the lightning burst itself, creating delayed explosions or chained strikes.]

“Hmm… these two techniques would fit my arsenal the best right now,” Ning muttered thoughtfully.

What was he doing, one may ask? Window shopping.

Ever since entering the inner sect, Ning had developed the habit of regularly visiting the Scripture Pavilion to browse through techniques, manuals, and strange cultivation arts. Now that he had access to Spirit-ranked techniques, he treated it almost like research, reading through their descriptions and analyzing which ones might fit his future cultivation path.

Of course, there was one major issue: he was broke.

That meant Ning had to be extremely careful with his choices. Every Spirit-ranked art cost an absurd amount of spirit stones, and choosing poorly would basically cripple his finances for the foreseeable future.

Fortunately, Li Houran had already helped narrow down the direction of his spear cultivation, leaving Ning to figure out his archery on his own. Among all the Spirit-ranked archery techniques currently available to him, Wind Waking Arrow and Lightning Cry Arrow seemed to fit him the best, especially since he needed stronger techniques capable of properly carrying intent.

“Hmm…” Ning rubbed his chin. “Wind Waking Arrow would synergize really well with Turtle Breathing Art and my stealth-oriented fighting style.”

Then he looked at the other manual.

“But Lightning Cry Arrow…” Ning considered it for a moment. “That one would significantly strengthen my long-range offensive capabilities.”

That was currently the bigger issue. As beasts and cultivators became stronger, his ordinary arrows were gradually starting to lose their lethality. Just like during the outer sect competition, his archery was excellent for suppression, interference, and positioning, but lacked the decisive killing power needed against tougher opponents.

He needed a proper one-shot technique capable of ending a fight quickly, and Lightning Cry Arrow specialized in exactly that.

Ning sighed.

Choosing techniques really was difficult. It felt strangely similar to choosing a favorite waifu; there were simply too many excellent options, yet he could only commit seriously to one.

“…That analogy still needs work,” Ning muttered.

He sighed dramatically. “But alas… the hardest choices require the strongest wills.”

At the moment, the biggest weakness in his archery was its lack of explosive finishing power, and Lightning Cry Arrow could compensate for that perfectly. Lightning techniques were already known for their destructive power among spell cultivators, so learning one through archery might also give him some understanding of lightning principles.

That could become useful much later, especially once heavenly tribulations entered the picture. Every cultivator feared thunder tribulation, and understanding lightning beforehand certainly couldn't hurt.

Wind Waking Arrow was still useful enough that Ning had no intention of giving up on it entirely. He would simply postpone learning it until later. Once he reached Foundation Establishment and had more resources available, he could always come back for it.

Having made his decision, Ning continued browsing through the other manuals. Spirit-ranked techniques possessed far more diverse effects than Mortal-ranked arts, so reading through all the bizarre techniques available in the inner sect was quite interesting by itself.

While browsing, Ning eventually noticed a familiar figure.

There were already quite a few disciples on the second floor of the Scripture Pavilion, yet the section where this particular person stood was almost completely empty. Ning grew curious and quickly discovered why.

Xiao Fan was standing there, staring intensely at a technique manual in his hands.

“So, what are you doing?” Ning walked over casually. “You’re glaring at that book so hard you might burn a hole through it.”

“E-eep!” Xiao Fan yelped at the sudden voice and immediately threw a punch toward Ning out of instinct.

“Wait, wait! It’s me!” Ning barely dodged the attack.

“N-Ning?!” Xiao Fan finally recognized him and relaxed. “Heavens, don’t you have any presence at all? You nearly scared me to death!”

“You were the one too enamored with the technique. I just walked over here,” Ning said. “Also, eep, huh? I really didn’t expect a manly guy like you to make such a girly sound when startled.”

Xiao Fan’s expression darkened.

“You will forget that immediately,” he declared in a tone that sounded suspiciously like a threat. “Seriously, you need to wear a bell or something. You walk around without making any sound at all.”

Must be because of my mastered Turtle Breathing Art and Shadow Steps, Ning mused. Once techniques reached mastery, they naturally became ingrained into the body like instinct.

He wisely changed the topic before Xiao Fan decided to throw another punch. “So anyway, what were you looking at so seriously?”

Xiao Fan lifted the technique manual in his hands and pulled out a thin sheet hidden within the cover.

“I found this inside the manual,” Xiao Fan explained quietly. “It was hidden between the inner layers. I only noticed because the texture felt slightly different. Otherwise, I never would have discovered it.”

“Of course you did,” Ning said immediately, already understanding the situation.

Classic hidden-manual trope. Honestly, he wasn't even surprised anymore.

“So,” Ning asked curiously as he leaned closer, “what’s written on the sheet?”

“Let’s have a look.”

Both of them leaned closer to the jade sheet spread across the table as faint spiritual light flowed across its surface and revealed the recorded text.

[Food Qi Nourishment Method: In this world, everything is illusory. Swords are external objects. Spears are external objects. Merits and wealth are also external objects, all of which may disappear one day.

Only what I eat is truly mine.

Thus, I care more about the quality of my food than anything else.]

“…This senior sounds extremely committed to eating,” Xiao Fan muttered.

Ning nodded solemnly. “A respectable mindset.”

The technique continued afterward, detailing a method of refining and nourishing the body through food. The user gradually strengthened their digestion, absorption, and nutrient conversion efficiency, allowing more of what they consumed to be properly utilized by the body.

Simply put, it was a cultivation technique entirely dedicated to becoming absurdly efficient at eating.

The more Ning read, the brighter his eyes became. “This is actually pretty good.”

Xiao Fan glanced at him strangely. “Really?”

“Yes. This is what I’d classify as a utility technique.”

“Utility technique?”

“I've been sorting techniques into a few categories,” Ning explained. “Attack, defense, movement, healing, storage, investigation, and then everything useful that doesn't fit neatly into the first six.”

Xiao Fan counted them. “Seven?”

“More or less. It's just something I use when deciding what I'm missing.” Ning tapped the Food Qi Nourishment Method. “I'd put this in the last one. It won't help much in a fight, but being able to absorb more from everything you eat is useful pretty much forever. With all seven aspects covered, a cultivator should at least be prepared for most situations.”

“Seven aspects, huh?” Xiao Fan muttered thoughtfully. “Never really heard anyone categorize things like that before.”

“Well, I summarized it myself, so it would be weird if you had.” Ning shrugged. “The idea itself is pretty useful, though. Most cultivators specialize too heavily in one area and then suddenly die because they neglected another.”

Xiao Fan blinked. “That sounds oddly specific.”

“It usually is.” Ning nodded sagely. “Sword cultivators love offense so much they sometimes forget defense exists. Alchemists stack healing and utility, then collapse when someone punches them in the face. Formation masters spend twelve hours preparing a battlefield and die instantly if someone drags them outside it.”

“…You sound like you’ve personally met all these people.”

“Well, I’ve read enough books to understand the general pattern,” Ning replied calmly.

He returned his attention to the Food Qi Nourishment Method. What interested him most was its focus on improving digestion and reducing the amount of spiritual energy wasted during absorption.

That alone gave the technique plenty of practical value. Cultivators consumed pills, spiritual plants, beast meat, and medicinal dishes throughout their lives, so even a modest improvement in how much their bodies could absorb would add up considerably over time.

“This thing has a lot of hidden value. Did you know that when consuming spiritual dishes, our bodies can’t fully absorb all the nutrients?” Ning explained. “Especially with meat from higher-tier beasts. A large portion of the qi is lost after consumption. Some dissipates naturally, some remains unabsorbed, and some simply becomes waste because the body can’t refine it efficiently enough.”

“I see.” Xiao Fan nodded thoughtfully.

“Of course, how useful it is also depends on what you're eating. It’s not like we can casually eat high-level beast meat every day, right?”

High-level beast meat was extremely expensive, which meant the only practical way to obtain it regularly was through hunting, and hunting powerful beasts in the wilderness naturally came with its own dangers.

“H-Haha… right.” Xiao Fan laughed awkwardly.

His mind immediately went to the absurd amount of peak Qi Condensation boar meat currently sitting inside his storage bag.

This technique truly could not have appeared at a better time.

Seeing Xiao Fan’s expression, Ning could roughly guess what the protagonist was thinking. He casually took out a jade bottle and tossed it toward him.

“Here, take this.”

“Hm?” Xiao Fan caught it instinctively.

“This contains Shining Crystal Spiritual Liquid compatible with the Pure Eyes Technique.” Ning smiled slightly. “Consider it payment for letting me study the technique. After all, you’re the one who found it.”

“There’s no need for that.” Xiao Fan immediately tried to refuse. “It’s not like I bought the technique or discovered it through effort. I just found it hidden inside the manual. Besides, you already explained a lot of useful things to me.”

“You should still take it,” Ning said, waving off his refusal. “You finding this technique in the first place was probably due to your luck, and I'm benefiting from it afterward. I’m a fair guy, so naturally I should pay properly.”

“B-But—”

“Okay, okay. If you really want to repay me, then help look for the next part of the Pure Eyes Technique someday.”

Ning had recently been reading about karma and related concepts while trying to better comprehend his Soul Ferrying Scripture, and what had happened just now was almost certainly another manifestation of Xiao Fan's ridiculous protagonist luck. Some senior cultivator had probably hidden this technique away for a destined inheritor or something equally cliché.

From what Ning understood of karma so far, repaying Xiao Fan for the benefit was the safest choice. It also gave him another reason to remember Ning if the later parts of Pure Eyes ever happened to fall into his hands.

“If you say so.” Xiao Fan finally accepted the jade bottle. “Though honestly, even if you hadn’t given me this, I still would’ve shared it with you if I found anything else.”

“Just take it already, would you?” Ning waved him off. “Being this wishy-washy doesn’t fit your image at all. Wait… actually, considering that earlier girly scream—”

Xiao Fan’s expression immediately darkened, and Ning corrected himself before he could say anything.

“Right. My mistake. Nothing happened earlier. Absolutely nothing.”

Xiao Fan stared at him for several seconds before snorting. “…Good.”

He carefully stored the jade bottle away.

Ning watched him and felt strangely satisfied. “Dropping exposition on the protagonist, giving him useful information, handing over rewards, and setting up a future fetch quest...” He paused. “Truly, I'm playing the supporting character role perfectly.”
