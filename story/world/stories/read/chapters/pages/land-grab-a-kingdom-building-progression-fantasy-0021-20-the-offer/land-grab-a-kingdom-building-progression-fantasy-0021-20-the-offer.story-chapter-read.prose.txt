Anarvia’s words hung in the air.

“I’m ready to let you go free.”

Tavian’s heart beat in his chest. Anarvia was afraid of something.

“We don’t have to fight,” the man said, interrupting the silence. “I’d rather not lose good men that I don’t need to lose.”

Tavian crossed his arms, but he was listening.

Anarvia continued. “Your ship is still in the harbor. Take what or who you want, and leave the Isles. Never come back. We’ll forget that all this ever happened. I’ve food and mercy enough, even for the soldiers who backed you against me.”

He could escape from here. He could take Braun and Calli. Erond and Cormath. Other friends. They could go to Endara, and live their lives in peace. The thought of abandoning all this chaos and fighting had a certain appeal to it.

Tavian eyed the man. What was weighing on his mind? What pressure was he feeling that he was willing to make this deal?

It was a good deal, wasn’t it?

It certainly gave him information. Lord Anarvia wanted Tavian gone. What benefit did that give him? The Conqueror’s Core?

“The offer is more generous than I expected,” Tavian admitted. He waited for the man to demand the Conqueror’s Core in exchange.

Anarvia rose, extending his hand and smiling warmly. “Take my hand,” he said. “Take my offer.”

“It’s strange, though,” Tavian said.

Anarvia’s smile faltered, just for an instant.

“If the situation is as dire for me as you’ve made it seem, you wouldn’t have to offer me so much.”

“Consider it a bit of generosity,” Anarvia said.

“I think you’re afraid of something.”

Anarvia dropped his hand, a flicker of emotion on his face. He glanced back at his daughter.

Was he looking for reassurance? For… permission?

“I’m not afraid of you,” Lord Anarvia said. “But you test my patience. I did not come to be insulted for my generous offer.”

“Your offer is tempting,” Tavian confessed. “But I cannot seriously consider it. You have proven yourself to be too strong a manipulator, too deceitful a foe. As soon as I leave the security of my kingdom, you will kill me.”

“My word means nothing to you?”

“No, and you have done that to yourself. You will struggle to convince the other houses to join with you now. How will you rule the isles, without Calenor’s money bags, or Donath’s granaries?”

Was this Anarvia’s problem? So long as Tavian still lived, the other Houses refused to submit to the man? If only he could contact and coordinate with the Houses.

Lord Anarvia shook his head, looking quite somber. “The other Houses support me,” he said. “They have all along. They saw the decline and failures of your father, and encouraged me to take action.”

“Even Brask supports you?” Tavian asked. “I would have thought that your heavy handed collection of import taxes would have created animosity between you and their trade vessels.”

“Even Brask,” Anarvia said. “The removal of the king creates new opportunities for all of us, you see, and Brask was anxious to claim Verosian real estate along the coast.”

“Perhaps. Lady Calenor, Lord Donath, and Lord Brask are clever and will play the odds. But I do not believe that Lady Tiren would ever support plans for a coup.”

“Tiren?” Anarvia scoffed. “They have no awareness of politics, not with their noses in books all day, sequestered in their precious temple. They will serve whatever king rules.”

“You’re afraid of angering the other Houses by coercing her,” Tavian observed.

Ilara leaned over to her father, whispering something to him through her bandages.

“Here, perhaps, we might have a solution to the problem,” Anarvia said. “If you feel you cannot trust me, then perhaps you will trust her?”

“Lady Tiren?”

“Yes. If she will escort you to your waiting ship, would that soothe your distrust of me?”

The offer was intriguing. Tavian was not sure if Lady Tiren and her priests would be willing to do such a thing. Surely if Anarvia tried to cut through House Tiren to kill Tavian, the other houses would turn against him. The Tirens were beloved by all.

But the assurance was insufficient.

“I appreciate the willingness to accommodate my paranoia,” Tavian said. “But the old adage rings true; only let your friends patch your hull.”

“You fear sabotage? My honor is offended,” Lord Anarvia said sarcastically. He leaned back in his seat. He gave a slight shake of his head.

Ilara gave a slight nod.

Tavian noted the exchange with interest.

“Fine then,” Lord Anarvia said, scratching his chin. “What if I send Ilara with you?”

Tavian scoffed. “As if,” he said. “You think I’ll feel safer with your assassin at my side?”

“My daughter,” Anarvia said, indignantly. “Whom I love, and find very useful to me. She will see you safely across the ocean to Endara. Take her as a prisoner, if you don’t trust her. I would never sink a ship bearing my own kin.”

Tavian raised an eyebrow. What was he playing at here?

He took a deep breath. He wondered if Ishtoran was giving him an out. Perhaps he had made a mistake in setting up the Conqueror’s Core here. Maybe he should have listened to his father and fled to the southern stronghold.

But more importantly… What did Anarvia know? Why was he so desperate to get Tavian out of Tavirost Fortress?

“You’ve given me a lot to think about,” Tavian said.

“Please, take your time to consider. I’ll return at… noon?” Lord Anarvia scanned the sky. That gave Tavian about an hour.

“Fine,” Tavian said. He turned and walked back into the Core Courtyard. As the Life Gate closed behind him, Braun caught his arm.

“What did he want?”

“I don’t know,” Tavian said. “I mean, I know what he said to me. But there’s something more going on, and we need to figure it out before he comes back. Calli! Calli!”

He found his sister with the open chest of gems. Judging by the sweat on her face and the puddle of discolored earth and stone at her feet, she’d been trying to separate miasma from Essence.

“I know there’s a way to isolate the specific elements,” she muttered as Tavian approached.

“Calli,” he said, waving his hand in front of her face.

“Huh? What?”

“How hard would it be for you to disable this Core?”

That caught Calli’s attention. “Shutting down a Core? That’s one of the most challenging things a Coresmith can do. I mean deactivating it properly so that it still works when you reactivate it. I know how to do it in theory, but in practice… I can’t make any guarantees.”

“Maybe that’s it,” Tavian said. “Maybe he’s afraid that we’ll damage the Core somehow. He wants it for himself.”

“What are you talking about?” Calli asked.

“We need to have a meeting. And we need to do it quickly, or else we’re going to be fully encircled early this afternoon.”

A short time later Tavian stood in a circle, with Braun, Calli, Erond—though he still sat on the ground, nursing his broken leg—and Cormath.

Tavian explained the offer that Lord Anarvia had just made to him.

“It seems like a reasonable offer to me,” Cormath said, with a shrug. “We could get away, start fresh somewhere else.”

“That’s not the point,” Tavian said. “The point is, why would Anarvia make an offer like that?”

Erond nodded slowly. “You’re right,” he said. “If Anarvia is being honest with you, then why make the offer? If he really has the upper hand he claims that he does, then he can just march in here and take what he wants.”

“I can think of two things,” Tavian said. “He’s afraid we’ll damage the Core, or it will be ruined in the fighting. Or there’s something going on in the miasmic dungeon, that we’re getting in the way of.”

“Why would he care about the Core?” Calli asked. “He can’t know that there’s anything special about it, can he?”

“Besides, wasn’t he just firing explosive bolts at it the other day?” Braun added.

“And another thing,” Erond said. “If we destroyed it on the way out; his gambit would be for naught. He hasn’t made any effort to deter that kind of action.”

“And he hasn’t even mentioned the Core to me. So maybe it’s not that,” Tavian conceded. “What about my other theory?”

Everyone shrugged. “Maybe,” Braun said.

“I think we’re overlooking something here,” Cormath said. “The offer. Do we need to know what Anarvia wants, as long as it’s forcing him to make a good offer?” He gestured to Erond. “More people are going to die if we stay here. Prolonging the fighting will only cause more damage and destruction to this land that we love.”

“And leave Tar Corinath under Anarvian rule?” Tavian asked, derisively. “What about justice? What about standing up for one’s self?”

“What’s the alternative?” Cormath asked. “Civil war until there isn’t a city left? I love this land,” Cormath said. “And I’ll be sorry to see it in Anarvia’s hands. But maybe this is an opportunity worth considering.”

Tavian pressed his palms to his temples. “I just wish I had all the information,” he said. “What could be down there?”

“Tavian,” Braun said. “We know he’s going to be back here in an hour. This could be our chance to decapitate them. Kill Anarvia and Ilara; that could turn the tide for us.”

“No,” Tavian said, shaking his head. “I was counting mages. You couldn’t see from where you were, but he had more waiting in the wings. I think it turns into a bloody waste of a battle.”

Braun shrugged. “Think about it.”

“I don’t have time.”

“There’s something else that’s bothering me,” Braun said. “Ilara already tried to kill you once. If you take her with us, she’ll try killing you again, won’t she.”

“I don’t know,” Tavian said, “But that one’s easy. We act as though we’re bringing her aboard the ship, so that we can trust that Anarvia hasn’t sabotaged it. And then we leave her behind when we embark.”

“Ah,” Braun nodded, seeing the logic.

“Where is Jarden?” Tavian asked.

Braun led Tavian to where Jarden was tied up. The man had recovered consciousness, and the swelling from his spider bites had reduced a bit.

“What are you not telling me?” Tavian demanded. “What’s in the dungeon that you haven’t told me about?”

Jarden cowered. “My lord, have I not proved my trustworthiness? The injuries I have born on your behalf; and we made it safely through the tunnels—”

“I still think you’re holding out on me,” Tavian said. “Anarvia is interested in the dungeons and I want to know why.”

“Anarvia knows nothing about them,” Jarden protested. “His men are blind and lost without my help.”

“Are you certain?”

“He knows nothing of the floors beneath.”

“What do you know?”

“Miasma,” Jarden said with a shrug. “It is dangerous, and volatile. I do not go beneath.”

Tavian ground his teeth. For the first time, Tavian felt certain the man was lying to him. But he wasn’t going to get to the bottom of this before the noon deadline. He walked away from the others.

Cormath was right. He should at least consider the offer made by Anarvia. It was dangerous, to be sure, but either choice was dangerous. He looked at his sister. Could he ask her to die here, for him? For his dream? And Braun, and Erond, and all the others?

And taking up Anarvia on the chance to escape didn’t mean that Tavian had to abandon the Corinath Isles forever. He could sail that boat right around to the southern stronghold. Calli could deactivate the Core, they could bring it with them. It would take time to work out the details of the Tiren escort, so Calli would have time to figure it out.

It would give everyone an opportunity to make a real choice for themselves. Keep fighting with Tavian, or make a new life somewhere else. They deserved that. And then Tavian would know he could utterly trust anyone who stuck with him.

With the power of a Conqueror’s Core and the might of the southern stronghold, Anarvia would be forced to negotiate with Tavian. Yet, if Tavian did this, he’d be admitting he’d read the situation wrong. His father had been right all along.

The sun hung in the sky, directly to the north. Tavian stood in front of the Life Gate, waiting for Lord Anarvia to resume his seat. Ilara did not return with her father. Instead, Lady Tiren joined him.

A short, silver haired woman, she wore the traditional robes of the Temple of Ishtoran. House Tiren were not priests themselves, but they managed and oversaw the temporal needs of the priesthood.

Lady Tiren lifted her chin in acknowledgement as her eyes met Tavian’s.

Tavian suppressed his surprise at seeing her there. Lord Anarvia must be truly desperate to get him to leave. But why? It had to be something to do with the miasmic dungeon.

“You came,” Anarvia said. “I thought you might have succumbed to your stubbornness.”

“Lady Tiren,” Tavian said, ignoring Lord Anarvia and bowing to the woman.

“You see I am a man of my word,” Anarvia said. “I have brought you the promised escort.”

“How many more women will you bring to try and lure me out of my stronghold?” Tavian asked. “They increase in beauty.”

A smile touched Lady Tiren’s lips. She did not speak.

Lord Anarvia laughed.

“Ah, is that all it would take? Alas, we could have resolved this so much sooner. Such a shame that we had this misunderstanding.”

“Perhaps,” Tavian said. He watched every movement of Lady Tiren. How had Lord Anarvia convinced her to intervene? What was she doing here? Did she really support the coup?

Anarvia leaned back into his chair. “Then let us finish this unpleasant business. I’ve promised you a ship, an escort, a hostage, even gold. What else do you desire?” He looked relaxed. Too relaxed, like it was all an act.

Lord Anarvia’s eyes flicked to Tiren, then to the fortress, before making their way down to the ground.

The ground.

He wasn’t looking at Tavian. He was looking past him; through him.

Something beneath the keep.

“No, I’m sorry,” Tavian said. “The answer is no. I’m sorry, but I cannot take you up on the offer.”

“Don’t make me change my mind, boy,” Anarvia snapped, the relaxed mask cracking slightly. “See reason!”

“You don’t care about the crown, the fortress, or even my life. You just want me out of the way, because you’re afraid.”

Anarvia’s knuckles turned white around the stem of his wine glass.

“Go, Tavian, just leave, Tavian. We’re bending over backwards for you.”

“You’re worried about what I might find.”

“Oh,” Anarvia said, sounding genuinely disappointed as he tried to regain his composure. “You’re insane. How unfortunate. My daughter was looking forward to traveling with you. She does like you, you know, and regrets your most recent encounter.”

“Enough with the lies,” Tavian said. “I’m not listening any more. You’ll have to kill us all to get Tavirost Keep.”

“This is your pride, speaking,” Anarvia growled. “Be reasonable.”

“There’s something hidden beneath the fortress,” Tavian said.

The shock in Anarvia’s eyes gave him away. He’d hit the nail on the head.

“I’m going to have it,” Tavian said.

“You don’t know what you’re talking about,” Anarvia protested. “If it’s war you want, it’s war you’ll have!”

“Any man who you send I will kill,” Tavian said, his voice dropping to an intense whisper. “And then, I’ll kill you too. Just like I killed Anax. You know he begged me for his life in the end?”

Lord Anarvia sprang to his feet, his wine glass crashing to the ground as his face twisted with pure rage. “You’re a liar,” he roared.

Tavian held the man’s gaze for a heartbeat.

“See you in the void,” Tavian hissed.
