When the system came, Don Damon was having an average day.

It was Friday evening, and he had just finished a hard week of work at his uncle’s construction firm. The job paid the bills and kept him in shape, but it was a tough living. Sometimes he wondered if his parents had been right to push him into going to college— a notion he had rejected when it had been an option. It wasn’t that Don didn’t have the brain for it, but he hated the idea of sitting in a cubicle every day and having to play games to climb the corporate ladder.

The guys at work were not the friendliest bunch, but at least they were honest and direct, something Don appreciated.

However, his aching back was starting to disagree. The walking helped, which is why immediately after coming back home he took Baxter, his german shepard for a walk. Baxter huffed around excitedly, going from bush to bush, sniffing around and marking his territory over previous dogs.

“That’s a good boy, Bax,” Don said as he watched the dog go. “Show them who’s boss around here.”

While Baxter did his dog stuff, Don breathed out and tried to touch his toes. His back crunched, which wasn’t ideal, but it did make him feel a bit more at ease.

“Please, Don,” a shrill voice of an elderly lady said. “Can’t you keep him on a leash? He’s so big!

Mrs. Terry was walking her chihuahua in slippers and a fluffy pink jacket, frowning at Baxter with a cigarette in her mouth. Her chihuahua, Miss Penelope was an almost hairless little demon, who shivered constantly and barked like a rabid demon from hell anytime anything living came close. It was smoking the cigarette just as much as Mrs. Terry, for the old lady’s idea of a dog walk was carrying the little chi.

No wonder the little thing is so pissed all the time.

Baxter rounded close and sniffed at Mrs. Terry’s feet curiously, tail wagging. Mrs. Terry responded by raising her frantically barking dog higher in the air.

“He’s a gentle giant, Mrs. Terry,” Don said and tried on a smile.

He hated Mrs. Terry and her stupid dog, but as far as he understood the unwritten rules of the unwritten book on “How To Behave With Other Humans”, smiling and hiding his true feelings was ideal. Not because Don had anything to gain from this old bat of a woman, but because she could definitely make it her mission to make things more miserable for Don to spice up her boring retirement.

Because Mrs. Terry looked like she was about to kick the curious Baxter, Don called him. “Come boy. Let’s go this way. There’s squirrels in the park for you to chase.”

Baxter, well trained as he was, immediately followed, tongue hanging happily from his mouth.

“Good boy,” Don said and petted the German shepard’s head.

The park was behind a crest of a little hill. Baxter ran straight up, but Don took his time. His heart was hammering fast. That notion made him frown. He had a birth defect in his heart. Made it inefficient or something, Don wasn’t sure on the details. He wouldn’t last forever on the construction gig, but Don wasn’t sure what else he was supposed to do.

He had wanted to join the army. That made sense. Becoming a soldier. Defending the weak and society. Naive thoughts, Don knew, but it beat working as a drone to make some corpo rich.

But after the medical examination, the army didn't want him. Since then, Don floated around until his uncle bailed him out. But now he had a back problem in addition to a heart problem. And the money certainly wasn’t worth screwing his body over.

“I don’t really get the point… You work to live, but live for what?”

For Baxter? Sure, Don loved the dog. Family? Estranged to the point of coming to visit on christmas. Friends? Coworkers, bar runs, poker nights. It was good fun, but Don wasn’t fulfilled.

“Maybe I should go back to college and learn to play the social games…”

The thought literally caused him physical pain. A lurch in his stomach that tasted like despair. If there was one thing Don was good at, it was listening to his instincts. Going to college wasn’t going to make him happy. But his instincts told him his life was at a dead end, and that’s a harrowing feeling.

Don crested the hill and watched Baxter jump around in the tall grass. He sat on a wooden bench under a maple tree and scrolled his phone. Bad news, annoying ads, fake success of his old school mates on social media. Or maybe it was real success, either way it didn’t make Don feel better. But when he looked at Baxter, he managed a smile.

“Things aren’t so bad. Sure my back’s busted, but I can change my circumstances. I just need to figure out what I want to do.”

But at that moment the system came. In an instant, like someone had flipped a switch, the sky turned purple. The orange soft glow of the evening sun was blocked by thick purple clouds. A deep rumble started in the sky, a heavy and unnatural thunder, as the clouds gathered.

[We welcome you to the Multiverse, Planet Earth. Please find a secure location. You are about to enter a new reality. Your civilization as you know it has ended. This is not a hallucination or a dream. Every human on Earth is receiving this message. You are about to enter the Tutorial Phase of your integration.]

“W-what?” Don stammered out. The text was floating in a rectangular white box in front of him. He touched it and his fingers passed through. Baxter came up to him and made a nervous whine.

“It’s—it’s okay, Bax. C’mere.”

The dog came up closer and pushed against Don’s knee. Don scratched the dog idly as he tried to understand what he was reading. There were no other people around. He was alone.

[We are beginning phase one. Within ten minutes we are demolishing all buildings on earth. We will use the material to repurpose. Please remove yourself from any building to continue existence. You will be teleported to your designated tutorial area. Additionally, we are initiating the Monster Evolution phase. Please remove yourself from the vicinity of any animals.]

The purple clouds roiled and suddenly lightning started striking down. Red flashes without sound. They started striking randomly around the surrounding area. One of them almost hit them, surging down just at the root of the hill. Suddenly Don heard a terrible screech below the hill.

Miss Penelope was standing over Mrs. Terry’s bleeding body. The little chihuahua had changed. The mouth had enlarged into a maw of jagged teeth. It had grown in size, at least double. The skin was turning into hardened black leather. The eyes were bulging and violent. It chomped down on Mrs. Terry’s throat. The old woman let out one last strangled scream that was cut short.

Baxter started barking, and that was it.

Miss Penelope stopped its feast, and turned the monstrous face towards them. It snarled and started bounding up the hill. The lightning was raining down everywhere the eye could see.

Don took a few steps back. Baxter continued barking, but Don grabbed the back of his neck, trying to calm the dog down. Baxter whined as Miss Penelope bounded at them.

[Would you like to select a class now?]

Don got distracted by the system prompt and let go of Baxter. The dog immediately left Don’s side to meet the rabid monster-dog.

They clashed and Baxter was immediately toppled. Miss Penelope bit into his throat and blood splattered everywhere. Before Don even knew what he was doing, he was charging down the hill. He sprinted and kicked the chihuahua off his dog, sending the little demon flying off into some bush.

Don knelt by his dog, who was whining in pain.

“Hang on, Bax!” Don said and pressed his hands on the puncture wounds. The warm blood spilled on his fingers. Baxter was looking at his eyes. Don realized he was crying when he looked back. Baxter licked his face, and bravely stayed in the fight. But soon enough the light in his eyes was starting to fade.

But then the red lightning struck them. Maybe it was meant for Baxter, but Don caught it. They both did. It didn’t immediately blast Don into smithereens, but it did something. Something wrong.

It pierced the very core of Don, and he immediately felt something was wrong. It felt like his blood was boiling, like his innards were bubbling and his bones seemed to fracture into jelly. Immediately Don fell to the ground like a wet sack. He convulsed and lost his sight and hearing. There was only immense, overwhelming pain. It seemed to last forever, and Don screamed, but he could not hear it. Then the system spoke again:

[Anomaly detected. Rearranging parameters…]
