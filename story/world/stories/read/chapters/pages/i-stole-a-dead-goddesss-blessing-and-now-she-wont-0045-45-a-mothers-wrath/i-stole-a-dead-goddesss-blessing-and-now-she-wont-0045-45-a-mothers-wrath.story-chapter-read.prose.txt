Anadis climbed the hill toward home, muscles burning under the strain of carrying what had to be a… Actually, he didn’t want to start guessing a girl’s weight. Not now, not ever.

He looked up into the white glare of the twin suns, blinding and far from setting. This would have felt normal before, and it still did. But now that he remembered his previous life, he couldn’t help comparing it to Earth. The twenty-four-hour days back on Earth had felt so short compared to Gaelora’s. Here, the days were long, too long, possibly half again as long as he remembered from Earth, and he would bet every possession he had—not that he owned much—that they were twice as hot in summer and twice as cold in winter.

Sweat dripped from his damp curls despite the chilly air, stinging his eyes as he tightened his grip on the girl he carried on his back. It wasn’t that she weighed much. It was his body that ached all over, and his palms were still raw and blistered from using Soul Sever. His feet were even worse—he guessed he had at least four blisters on each one, burning with every step.

“You’re walking like you’re about to fall off the hill,” Hel said, flickering into view. Her wings were dim and jittery.

“I’m fine,” he pushed out.

Her expression was troubled. “You’re not. You look like—”

“Hel… I know,” he warned her weakly.

Her brows furrowed, her eyes sharpened into a glare. She pressed her lips into a thin line and flew away.

It had taken them nearly two hours to make their way through the forest and circle around to the gate. Why hadn’t Anadis simply gone straight to the guards? Because with Soul Sight, he had spotted the Emanant bastard waiting there. Beneath his tunic, Anadis had seen the unmistakable uniform of the Blessen Division. There was no way they could pass through the gate or enter the town without that man finding them.

Relief washed over him as the gate to the Kalonis villa finally came into sight. Almost there. Almost home. Just a couple more steps and he could lay her down somewhere safe. He could rest. He could—he didn’t even know anymore. Think? Breathe? Collapse?

Nora was out on the house terrace, swaying in a rocking chair. She spotted him and started toward him with that light, teasing stride she always took whenever she saw him.

Great. Exactly who I don’t have the strength to deal with.

However, as she drew nearer, Nora’s blue eyes widened in shock. Her teasing expression froze instantly, probably the result of noticing his condition and the girl’s on his back. She ran, curls bouncing, then skidded to a stop, eyes darting from his face… to the girl.

“Oh gods, what… Anadis, what did you—who is that? Wait, is that—” she stammered.

“Nora, move,” he said flatly.

Hesitantly, she did.

Anadis wasn’t angry, just tired. Bone-deep tired. He lacked the strength to say anything, let alone explain to anyone. A bed… food… He stumbled, but she grabbed him by the elbow before his knees met the ground.

“Hey! Hey, don’t collapse on me. You’re bleeding!”

“It’s not mine…”

She crossed her arms and stared him down. “The blood leaking out of your nose isn’t yours?”

He didn’t answer. Was he still bleeding from there? Just how hard had that man slammed him? “I need to get her inside.”

Nora’s face tightened, and her voice dropped. “Alright. Lean on me.”

They made it inside just as his legs threatened to give out. Anadis laid the girl on the sofa and slouched down beside her, head spinning. Nora immediately called for Mother and Grandma. Both rushed down the hall.

“Anadis! Where in the gods’ name did you sneak off to? I said you were grounded—” Mother’s voice cut off as soon as she reached the common room. Her trembling hands clasped over her mouth. “Oh, mighty gods… all this blood—whose blood is this?!”

“Child…” Grandma whispered, her eyes wide. “What happened?”

“Mom, Grandma, we have no time! Please bring any mending supplies we have. This girl has lost a lot of blood,” Nora urged. They nodded, snapping into motion. That was when Aelia wandered into the hallway, yawning and rubbing her eyes, still in her sleeping gown. She really needed to get her sleeping schedule right.

“Aelia!” Nora’s shout made her flinch awake. “Go to the twins’ room. Albus should have some shard potions left on his nightstand.” Aelia hesitated, frozen in the mayhem, and blinked for a second. “Go!” Nora repeated, louder.

Aelia spun around and sprinted off, her blonde hair billowing behind her.

“Water…” Anadis gasped, his tongue parched.

“Right away,” Nora rushed off toward the kitchen. Not even fifteen seconds passed before she popped back in, holding a jar of what Anadis thought was life itself. He opened his mouth, and she slowly poured the liquid—fresh and delicious. At that point, the throbbing in his head dulled to a faint pulse.

Mother and Grandma rushed back with a roll of bandages, clean cloths, and even alcohol to clean the girl’s wound. Anadis winced and moved aside, letting them get to work.

Hel had morphed into her light form and darted inside his core.

“Be glad you made it back after an encounter with an Emanant,” she said into his head.

He didn’t answer.

“I-I only found one shard elixir in his room,” Aelia said between huffs as she arrived. “No potions. I tried to ask Albus, but I can’t find him. I couldn’t find Sevran either.”

Nora looked at Anadis, a single predicament clear on her face. Shard elixirs were far more potent than potions, but they only worked at full dose. If split, it would be no better than drinking sour-tasting water. That flask held the full essence of a Grullshard. They were expensive. Anadis just hoped Albus wouldn’t mind them using it to save her.

He won’t.

“Give it to her,” Anadis gestured toward the sofa. “I’m just drained. I’ll be fine.”

Aelia nodded and uncapped the small bottle. The viscous, glittery liquid clung to the glass. Its color was dark enough that one might easily think it poisonous. It wasn’t, of course. Nora poured Anadis another cup of water as Aelia carefully poured the elixir down the girl’s throat.

A few minutes later, the girl had been bandaged up, blood wiped from her arm and face. The elixir had worked its magic. Within minutes, her skin regained a vivid tanned tone, her breathing steadied, and her body temperature mellowed.

With a sigh of relief, Anadis allowed himself to relax for a second before a shadow loomed over him. He looked up to see a very angry Marcia, brows tugged together, hands on her hips as she tapped her sole against the marble floor.

“You,” she said sternly. “Have a lot to explain, young man.”

He gulped.

Great. I survived an Emanant only to face something worse—my mother.

Anadis stood with his hands clasped before him, staring at Mother as she completed what had to be her tenth loop around her room. She ran a hand through her hair and bit her lip while doing so. He fiddled with the hem of his ripped pajamas.

One would assume that after being Blessed, regaining past-life memories, and fighting Grulls, he’d stop fearing the woman. He thought wrong. Actually, no—he didn’t fear her, at least not the way one might fear a Grull. No… what frightened him was knowing how she would react after learning about the danger he had been in, and so soon after he had almost died to an Unhallowed. He didn’t want to make her worry. That was a kind of dread even grown men would regret.

Mother stopped pacing and stared at him. “So you didn’t sneak away?”

He nodded, gaze lowered. Anadis had already tried to explain that he hadn’t escaped, that it was because of a Boon that he was transported away. She could ask Sevran and even Albus, but apparently they weren’t in the house right now.

“You’re telling me you were transported into the forest without knowing, wearing your sleeping tunic, and there you found a girl being attacked by another Blessen. You stepped in, somehow fought him off, and escaped, bringing her here?” She paused, a sharp huff escaping her nose. “Am I getting all of this right?”

“Emanant,” he murmured.

“What?”

“It was an Emanant. You said Blessen. Technically, Blessen is an umbrella term—”

She stomped on the floor so loudly that it made him shudder.

Looking up with one eye, he caught a glimpse of her flaring nostrils, grinding jaw, and bone-white fists at her sides. Mother snatched a stuffed teddy bear from her bed—it was Nora’s from when she was little—and savagely tore into it, cotton spilling out.

Ow. Sorry, Nora.

Hel had been hovering beside her but decided to fly away quietly. “Oh, she is angry…” she chuckled, bemused.

“Tell me about it,” he agreed in a whisper.

“What did you say?!” Mother roared.

“N-nothing, Mom—Mother.”

The ripped teddy bear hit the wall with a dull thud as Mother hurled it after a frustrated sigh. Then she sat on the edge of the bed and gestured for Anadis to do the same. He hesitated, but she patted the bed once, hard, her eyes sharp.

I’d better go…

When was the last time he’d seen her this angry? Probably never. She clearly wasn’t putting up with any nonsense today. As he sat beside her, she rested a hand on his head. He flinched, expecting the worst, but she pulled him closer and slowly threaded her fingers through his hair. Her touch was soft and gentle, like a mother’s should be.

“Anadis, I just want you to know that I’m not angry at you,” she said, her voice softening as she lowered her hand to cup his cheek.

“Really?” He raised a brow. “You aren’t?”

“Never mind. I am.” She pinched his cheek sharp enough to make him wince. Blessen or not, it still stung.

“But you have to understand how this looks from my end. You chose not to trust your parents before, and that hurt you, which in turn hurt us. Hurt me. And now you’ve run into danger alone for the second time.” Her voice trembled as she asked, “I don’t want you to get hurt, son. So why don’t you trust us?”

Hearing her say that felt like a dagger to his heart. Of course he trusted her—she was a splendid mother who had shown nothing but love all his life. And yet, despite it all, he couldn’t help but hurt her, the same way he once had as Leander…

Shaking his head, Anadis thought, No. She has been nothing but loving toward me. She was his mother—his only mother. He couldn’t repeat the same mistakes and hurt her this way.

“I’m sorry, Mom. I just… when I first escaped four days ago, I wasn’t thinking clearly. I thought that after just becoming a Blessen, you wouldn’t let me try exploring what I could do alone.”

“Of course I wouldn’t. No mother would. Look at what happened to you, Aelia, and your friend Sevran.”

“He isn’t my friend,” he muttered, barely audible. She tilted her head, confused. “Don’t worry, Mom. Just trust me. I trust you and Dad more than anyone. I was just acting like a dumb teenager. Don’t I get to act like that at least once in my life?”

“I suppose, but—”

“I was just a bit jealous that Albus and Aelia could use their Blessings so much earlier than me. I wanted to…” The apology was real, even if what he said wasn’t entirely true. Although there was a bit of truth to it. And so was the part of him that knew he would make the same choice again. “Prove myself. I’m sorry.”

She blinked at him, then her face softened. “Well, you are your father’s son, and that man was always hard to control,” she said, kissing him on the forehead.

“I doubt it,” Anadis said, one eyebrow raised as he snickered. “You seem to have a pretty tight leash on him now.”

Mother stifled a laugh. “Well, I said hard, not impossible,” she exhaled, her breath carrying a hint of amusement. “Now, you’re still grounded. I want you to come straight home from the Learning Hall tomorrow and every day for the next month. I’ll be counting the minutes. And if you’re late—” she pressed his nose.

“Of course, Mother. Thank you for not getting too angry at me,” he said and hugged her.

She hugged him back. “Well, at the end of the day, you did save a life.” Then her eyes narrowed, her tone shifting somberly. “That girl… are you sure she’s not dangerous?”

“I know she isn’t dangerous,” Anadis said confidently, though he knew why she had to ask the question.

She nodded thoughtfully. “You do know what she is… and I’m not talking about her being a Blessen.” She searched his face.

“I know, but still I believe she’s not dangerous.” he held her gaze, refusing to blink, a resolute certainty settling in his chest. A slight smile tugged at the corners of her lips.

“I understand, my love.” She brushed his cheek with her thumb. “Anadis, one more thing.”

“Yes, Mother.”

“Don’t lie to me again… please.”
