Amanda gritted her teeth as she threw herself to the side, narrowly avoiding a javelin of ice larger than her entire body that went screaming past her. The last time she’d tried to block such a shot with her shield the lich had surprised her by revealing that it could start freezing her armor solid if she so much as touched a speck of the ice it conjured. She’d been forced to superheat her body for a moment to free herself, and doing so had given the lich an opening where it had nearly ripped off her head.

At this point, Amanda and the lich attacking Furora had been locked in a tense battle for what felt like an entire day, but what was probably closer to only about fifteen minutes or so. As powerful as she was, the lich had proven to be more than her match, and the two of them had been locked into something of a stalemate. Of course, with everything going on, the stalemate unfortunately favored the lich. While neither of them could seem to finish the other off, the lich hadn’t stopped raining death and destruction down on the city below for even a moment. Amanda tried not to think about how many hundreds of lives were being lost each minute that she failed to destroy the lich, and she shouted as she slashed her sword and sent out another arc of searing light.

“How many times must I tell you, that won’t work against me,” the lich chuckled. The dark gray flames danced humorously in its skull as shadows stretched up from the cloud and consumed her Light Slash without issue. “Most people think light vanquishes darkness, but it is the other way around. Darkness is everywhere. It is the natural order of things. Soon, your light will be extinguished, and then I can go back to focusing on my fun.”

Amanda didn’t bother replying to the lich’s provocations, her mind whirling as she tried to figure out a way to put the monster down for good. The two of them were battling atop the giant storm cloud hovering over the city of Furora. The lich seemed capable of walking along its own skill, presumably from an Air element or the like, and Amanda had her unique physical make up to rely on. Ever since entering the Stage of Power, her Light element had altered her body. Most people were only aware of the fact that she glowed when she wasn’t focusing on keeping the light contained, but only her Order were aware of the other benefit her unique body gave her.

If she wanted, she could weigh as little as the very light that filled her body.

Deciding to switch tactics, Amanda risked one of her blended skills. Raising her sword over her head, she triggered Blinding Flame, the skill she’d gained from her Light and Fire element merging. Her sword flared brighter than ever before, and everything the light touched instantly erupted in a roaring inferno. The clouds around her feet, the chunks of ice hovering around the lich, and even the lich itself.

“What a cute skill,” the lich laughed, seemingly unbothered by the fact that the front half of its body was engulfed in flame. A coating of ice manifested over its body, quenching the flames as it melted and dripped off. “What else—”

The lich swore when Amanda flashed forward with Light Step, teleporting directly in front of it as she tried to sever its head from its body. She knew they were supposed to keep the liches alive for Asher to come and take care of with his unique skill, but this undead abomination was slaughtering innocent lives with every second it stayed active. Her Order’s entire purpose was to destroy monsters such as these, and she refused to let it continue bringing death and destruction when she could do something to stop it.

Unfortunately, while blinding the lich with Blinding Flame had given her an opening, the blended skill’s cost proved to be her downfall.

A dark blade of ice filled with veins of shadow erupted out of the cloud beneath their feet, blocking Amanda’s gleaming sword in its tracks. The blades pressed against one another for the barest of seconds as she and the lich stood face-to-face. But before she could cut through its skill, her sword shattered, the metal having had too much strain placed upon it from Blinding Flame. Amanda leapt back in time to dodge the lich’s ice-coated hand that grabbed for her, but she wasn’t able to dodge the razor-sharp tendrils of shadow that stretched out of the cloud and sliced across the joints in her armor. The gashes weren’t too deep, but they would hinder her movements until she got the chance to heal them.

“That should have ripped your arms clean off,” the lich snapped, confusing her by sending a fire-filled glare down at the cloud beneath their feet for some reason. “That pest is still trying to shoot me down…?”

Amanda didn’t know what the lich was talking about, and she didn’t care. Taking advantage of its momentary distraction, she focused with her Life element, healing up the damage her limbs had suffered while she got back to her feet. She’d be able to restore her sword to its former glory once the battle was over, but she had the feeling the lich wasn’t planning on giving her the few minutes she’d need in order to do that right now.

Amanda had already tried and failed to vanquish the lich with her most powerful skill. While the skill that had earned her the moniker Falling Sun was devastatingly powerful, its largest counter was a quick, nimble opponent who could simply get out of the way before it landed. By now, she was well aware of the elements owned by the lich before her. Ice, Shadow, and either Air or Cloud. Ice gave it a surprisingly powerful defense, and Shadow and Air lent it a ton of mobility. She was struggling to both pin the lich down and crack through its frigid foundation, a task that was going to be all the more difficult now that she was down her sword.

She would have called for backup ages ago, if the lich hadn’t knocked her sending stone out of her grasp with an astonishingly well-placed shot that had nearly ripped her ear off.

“Why do you struggle so?” the lich asked, tilting its bony head to the side as the fires in its sockets flickered. “Not just you, but the pitiful humans running around below, desperately trying to save their lives as if they were anything of value. I still remember what it was like to be alive, you know. It was horrible. To be weak… threatened by anything and everyone… forced to suffer at the hands of those stronger than you… Things would be different now that I’m the strongest one around, but back then? I would have simply let myself be crushed and accepted a gracious end to my existence.”

Why do liches like to talk so much? Maybe the sound of their own voice is all they have left to help them feel human?

Not willing to throw in the towel just yet, Amanda rushed forward once more, making a desperate gamble as she triggered another of her blended skills. Breathing life into her hands, she hurled the six living balls of fire up into the sky, each one quickly taking on the form of a large bird as they soared around and began cawing angrily. She wouldn’t be able to tap into any of her Life element skills for a while, but hopefully the tradeoff would be worth it.

The six birds shot forward, leaving small trails of fire in their wake as they dove for the lich. Clicking its tongue, the lich lazily waved a hand, spinning the dense balls of ice orbiting around its body and easily blocking the first two birds as they crashed into the ice. Mentally controlling the remaining four, Amanda had them circle the lich, searching for an opening while she used Light Step to blur into place and tried to punch the undead in the face.

The crack of a shadow whip was all that met her advance, and she was sent flying back, bouncing atop the cloud before she rolled to a stop. Coughing out a mouthful of blood, she gritted her teeth at the confirmation that the whip hadn’t just broken one of her ribs, but forced the shard of bone into her lung as well.

“I’m not like those other so-called members of the Council of Death,” the lich laughed, striking down another one of her birds with a well-placed icicle. “Half of them we only added over the years with the intention of increasing the number of phylacteries at our disposal. Our core group? Those of us smart enough to keep our locations a secret from the others, even from that prying Zygrul who I always suspected was up to no good? We’re in a whole league of our own.”

Amanda grimaced as she pushed herself back to her feet. She hadn’t been pushed this far onto the back foot since taking down one of those speech-capable undead in the Deadlands that seemed to pop up every now and again. Even so. Despite the fact that her sword lay in pieces. Despite having locked herself out of her Life skills for the time being. Despite her current injuries.

She wasn’t going down without a fight.

“You will die here, monster,” she said, standing up straight as her body began glowing a brilliant gold. As conviction and belief flooded through her, so did the warmth that came from her paladin class, slowly healing her injuries and filling her weary muscles with fresh strength. So long as the undead threatened the innocent people of this world, a paladin’s job was never done. “You may have lived for thousands of years before this. But today is the day of your demise. I can promise you that.”

“Oh ho, I do love when humans get all dramatic in their final moments,” the lich chuckled, shooting down the last of her flaming birds with a few flicks of its fingers. “Do you have any idea how many paladins I’ve killed over the years? How many clerics have fallen by my hands? I’ve genuinely lost count. You will die like all the others, and in another thousand years, I won’t even remember your face, let alone your resolve. You tried. And you failed. Take solace in the fact that you are not the first, and that you won’t be the last. Now, I think we’re done here. Do yourself a favor and—”

A dense sheet of ice erupted out of the lich’s decrepit skin without warning, forming something akin to a suit of frozen armor in a split second. Amanda had just enough time to wonder what was going on when a crossbow bolt the size of her forearm screamed down from the heavens, punching deep into the lich’s chest and rocking the undead back a few steps. Before she could even spot who had joined the fray, she received her second surprise in as many seconds.

In the form of Asher flickering into existence directly in front of the lich, separating the undead’s head from its body in a brilliant blue slash.

“Asher?!” Amanda shouted, her eyes wide as Asher immediately fell through the cloud and plummeted to the ground below. Before she could figure out what was going on, she received her third surprise, as an ancient woman who looked older than her own great-grandmother fell from the sky up above, holding a giant crossbow in her hands and cackling like a madwoman.

“Take that, you decaying sack of crap!” the elderly woman shouted. She fired another bolt point-blank into the lich’s unmoving body as she fell straight through the cloud layer after Asher, cackling all the while.

Amanda simply stood there, staring in utter confusion at the dead lich lying before her with two crossbow bolts sticking out of its chest, and the two human-shaped holes in the cloud on either side of it. Already the giant, dark cloud beneath her feet was beginning to dissipate from the edges in as the ice stopped falling, but she barely even noticed. Slowly, she took in a deep breath, before letting it out.

“…What in the nine hells was that?!”
