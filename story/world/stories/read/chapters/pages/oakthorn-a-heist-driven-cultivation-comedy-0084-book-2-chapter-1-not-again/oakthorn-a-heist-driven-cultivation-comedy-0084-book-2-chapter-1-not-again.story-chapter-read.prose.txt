If the gods really did have a plan for everyone, Cade’s apparently involved dying of allergies in a town that was basically one giant middle finger to his sinuses.

The rural nightmare of Dogwood City sprawled beneath a canopy of white-flowering trees, their branches weaving a network of botanical torture that rained death petals on unsuspecting victims—or at least on one very specific victim trying not to sneeze his cover straight to hell.

To everyone else, it was probably charming. Quaint, even.

To Cade Stormhollow, it was cruel and unusual punishment.

The evening market bustled with farmers hauling carts of spring vegetables, traders arguing over prices, and children weaving between stalls stealing samples when vendors weren’t looking. The thief and his crew moved through the crowd in staggered groups so as to reduce their chances of getting recognized. But even this precaution would not last, if the previous two villages they’d visited were any indication.

“I swear,” Orro muttered, watching Cade fidget with the mask the thief had ‘borrowed’, “if you stretch out the enchanted filters, I will end you myself and save the Lifekeepers the trouble.”

Cade would have replied with something wonderfully witty, but another sneeze was building. He clenched his teeth, tears streaming down his face behind the enchanted black mask.

They’d been on the run for three days straight, barely sleeping, and each of them taking turns keeping watch. The bounty on their heads had grown high enough to tempt even honest folk, and these persistent Lifekeepers had eyes everywhere.

“Cadey. Cadey. Cadey,” Chubbs whispered loud enough for everyone in a ten-yard radius to hear.

“One moment, Chubbs,” Cade replied distractedly.

The recently reincarnated goddess of Creation, forged into the body of a toddler that barely came up to his hips, tugged on the hem of his tunic with a strength to rival Orro’s. Cade jolted to a stop as Chubbs yanked again. Hard.

“Cadey!” Chubbs whined.

He smiled in faux embarrassment at curious onlookers before kneeling down to be at eye-level with the tiny deity.

“Yes?” he asked with all the patience he could muster, which wasn’t much.

“I saw a butterfly.” Chubbs smiled brightly enough to compete with the sun.

“She saw a butterfly,” Elena repeated with a barely contained scoff. “That was worth the wait, everyone.”

“Hey, butterflies are amazing,” Evie answered defensively. “They tell the stars secret stuff all the time!”

Cade glanced at the siren of Fate walking up behind Chubbs. She held her staff with a carved satyr affixed to the top like it was some mighty weapon of the Progenitors. But in her dainty hands, the gnarled monstrosity was little more than comical. Behind her, Nora Lancaster, Evie’s paladin and their local Dreadcaller, wandered about with the same inconspicuousness as a dragon at a gala.

“Cadey, can we get breakfast?” Chubbs pleaded, her grip on the thief’s tunic unwavering. Her golden eyes were wide with hope, and something in his chest tightened. “I smell honey cakes.”

“Not yet, little one,” Rayka answered before Cade could. She kept her hood low, scanning the crowd quickly. To Cade, she said, “We need supplies first. Real supplies.”

They needed more than supplies.

They needed a real plan, preferably one that didn’t end with them dead or captured. The power thrumming through Cade’s core—the piece of goddess that had changed everything—felt like a beacon painting a target on his back. Every day brought them closer to Destruction, and every day the weight of what that meant for Chubbs grew heavier.

A flash of white and green in the crowd sent ice through Cade’s veins. He casually turned toward a vendor’s stall, pretending to examine jars of preserved fruits while watching the reflection in the glass. Two Lifekeepers moved through the market, their pristine cloaks in blinding opposition to the earthier tones of the townsfolk. They moved with purpose, showing something to the merchants—probably sketches of Cade’s crew.

“Three more by the well,” Orro breathed, not turning his head. “They’re boxing us in.”

Cade’s fingers brushed the hilts of his daggers hidden beneath his cloak. The Lifekeepers were proving themselves to be full of surprises. In the previous village they’d absconded from, Cade and the crew had discovered that the Elysian cult possessed enchanted visors designed to see through glamours and disguises.

One wrong move, one slip of Orro’s mask, and it would all be over.

“Don’t even think about it,” Cade warned, reading his best friend’s body language. “We’re not fighting our way out of a crowded market. I refuse to die in a town named after my greatest nemesis.”

Orro exchanged a sidelong glance at him. “I thought that Fenwick prick was your nemesis.”

“No,” Cade said with a disappointed shake of his head. “Dogwood pollen has been, and will always be, the personification of evil in this land. It is my humble duty to eradicate it once and for all.” The thief leaned in close. “For the children.”

He gestured conspiratorially at Chubbs.

The assassin rolled his eyes. “I don’t think an ancient goddess who merely reincarnated as a child counts.”

The pair of them glanced back at the toddler goddess. She was crouched on the rough-hewn cobblestone, putting tiny pebbles in her mouth before spitting them out again. This cycle repeated several times just in the brief seconds they watched her from a few feet away.

“I win,” Cade concluded with a squeeze of Orro’s shoulders.

Another gust of wind showered them with white petals, and Cade felt his nose begin to twitch ominously.

Not now. Please, any god that’s listening, not now—

The nearest Lifekeeper turned, their mask’s crystals beginning to glow with detection magic.

Cade’s hand shot out, grabbing a passing farmer’s cart and yanking it between them and the Lifekeeper just as the detection spell fired. The beam of light scattered across jars of preserves, casting rainbow fractals through the evening air. The farmer cursed, but Cade was already moving, shepherding their group deeper into the market while trying not to breathe too deeply.

Who knew allergies could actually save your life?

“Split up?” Cade suggested, his voice tight with tension.

“And leave you with my mask? Not a chance.” Orro steered them toward an alley choked with flowering vines. Because of course it was.

“Chubbs stays with me,” Cade wheezed.

One small downside to having a silver-ranked core was that all of his senses were heightened. Which made his current predicament truly miserable.

The little girl’s hand found his, squeezing with complete trust that made his chest ache. She didn’t even look scared—if anything, she seemed to be enjoying their evening sprint through town.

“Look!” she whispered excitedly. “Those guards have the shiniest armor I’ve ever seen!”

“Those aren’t guards, sweetie,” Nora muttered, pulling the girl’s makeshift hood lower. “Those are people trying to kill us.”

“Oh.” Chubbs considered this. “They’re not very good at it.”

A laugh burst out of Cade before he could stop it, quickly devolving into a series of sneezes that had Orro looking murderous. “If you get snot in my mask, I swear by all the—”

“Incoming!” Jer’s voice rang out as he burst from a side street, Gav right behind him. A small white form—Bunny—clung to Jer’s shoulder, looking simultaneously proud and guilty. “We may have a slight problem.”

“Define ‘slight,’“ Cade demanded between sneezes. They were getting worse.

“Bunny tried to rob that bakery we passed earlier,” Gav explained, barely containing his grin despite the situation.

“He was charging three silvers for a single loaf!” Jer defended Bunny whose tiny dragon form puffed up with far too much indignation. “That’s basically highway robbery!”

“So you committed an actual robbery?” Nora asked, pinching the bridge of her nose.

“More like attempted culinary justice,” Jer sniffed.

The sound of armored boots and angry shouting grew closer, mixing with the measured approach of Lifekeeper forces.

Perfect. Now they had both the town guard and magical zealots on their tail.

Two more Lifekeepers emerged ahead of them, their white cloaks and enchanted visors transforming their appearance into angry ghosts with bejeweled eyes. The crystals in their masks pulsed with building detection magic, and Cade could feel the weight of their focus zeroing in on his core. On the Remnant that made him such valuable prey.

“Hard left,” he ordered, already moving. “Through the baker’s.”

“Not that one!” Jer squeaked. “Different baker!”

They burst through another shop’s door, bells jingling with offensive cheerfulness. The smell of fresh bread and honey almost masked the ever-present dogwood scent. Almost.

“Sorry about this!” Cade called to the startled baker as they charged through his shop. He flipped a silver coin onto the counter. “For the damages!”

“What damages?” the baker asked.

Orro’s answer was to flip the nearest rack of cooling bread into the doorway just as the Lifekeepers reached it. Their pristine white cloaks were suddenly significantly less pristine.

“Those damages,” Jer clarified as they escaped through the back door.

“YOU!” the baker bellowed, fist raised in outrage.

They emerged into a narrow service alley lined with—because the universe clearly had a vendetta against Cade’s sinuses—more flowering trees. Their white petals drifted like snow, beautiful and deadly. To his allergies, anyway.

“This is ridiculous,” Cade wheezed through Orro’s mask. “Who needs this many trees? What, did they get a bulk discount on botanical warfare?”

“Less complaining, more running,” Nora suggested, but there was tension in her voice. More Lifekeepers were converging on their position—she could feel it, same as him.

The net was tightening.

A crystal-enhanced spell blast sizzled past Cade’s ear, close enough that he felt his hair crisp. He pulled Chubbs closer, shielding her with his body while scanning for options. The alley opened onto what looked like a main street, but he could hear the metallic click of Lifekeeper boots approaching from that direction too.

“Plan?” Orro asked, his normally unflappable demeanor showing cracks of concern.

“We could try apologizing to the baker?” Jer suggested while he scratched underneath Bunny’s chin.

Cade felt another sneeze building, pressure mounting behind his eyes. The street ahead was filling with white cloaks. The alley behind them echoed with approaching footsteps. Above them, the dogwood branches swayed mockingly in the breeze, showering them with more ammunition for his allergies.

Above them...

“Oh no,” Nora said, recognizing his expression. “Cade, don’t you dare—”

“Everyone grab a branch,” he ordered, already reaching up. “We’re going up.”

Cade hoisted Chubbs onto his back and launched upward, grateful that thieving had given him excellent upper body strength. The dogwood branches weren’t exactly thick, but they’d have to do. White petals exploded around them as they disturbed the canopy, and Cade’s eyes watered behind Orro’s mask. If he survived this, he was burning every dogwood tree in existence.

A crystal-enhanced spell shattered the branch just as Nora grabbed it. Cade’s heart lurched, but she was already moving, using the falling branch’s momentum to swing herself higher. Show-off.

“Um, slight problem,” Jer called out, struggling to climb while Bunny clung to him like a terrified scarf. The little dragon’s eyes were squeezed shut, his whole body trembling. Another spell blast splintered bark near Jer’s head.

“Incoming!” Rayka’s warning came just as six Lifekeepers rounded both ends of the alley. Their mask crystals hummed with building power, ready to fire.

Cade didn’t think. He just moved. “Chubbs, hold tight!”

He launched himself between branches, one arm wrapped backward around Chubbs while his other hand caught limbs just long enough to redirect their momentum. Each landing sent fresh waves of pollen into the air. His sinuses were going to murder him if the Lifekeepers didn’t get to him first.

“They’re in the trees!” A guard’s shout carried from the street. “Get the archers!”

“Oh, that’s just perfect,” Orro muttered, surprisingly agile despite his complaints. “Cade, if an arrow ruins my mask—”

“You really have a weird fixation with this mask, friend!” Cade’s voice came out nasally as another sneeze built. The pressure behind his eyes was reaching critical levels.

“My parents gave that to me just before they disowned me!” Orro roared behind him.

“So much to unpack there, buddy,” Cade yelled back, ducking under a clothesline laden with white sheets.

A volley of arrows hissed through the branches. Evie yelped as one sliced through her cloak, pinning it to a trunk. She yanked free just as spell-fire lit up the spot she’d occupied.

They needed a distraction. Something big enough to—

The sneeze hit like an explosion.

The force of it ripped Orro’s mask clean off Cade’s face, sending it spinning through the air in a graceful arc.

For one horrible moment, everything seemed to move in slow motion. The mask tumbled end over end. Orro’s expression shifted from annoyance to horror as scalding magma trailed behind the mask, lava sizzling in over a dozen locations beneath their vantage.

Below them, every single Lifekeeper dove for cover even as their visors confirmed the Destruction magic that had just exited out of Cade’s nostrils.

“Found you,” one of them breathed, voice full of zealous triumph.

“No shit,” Cade breathed, reeling from his explosive sneeze.

“Stormhollow,” Orro growled, his voice laced with enough venom to kill a god. “You just burned a family heirloom that was over ten generations old. I am going to kill you now.”

The air crackled with gathering power. Dozens of spells, all preparing to fire at once. Chubbs’ arms tightened around Cade’s neck, and he felt her face press into his shoulder.

Trust. Complete, absolute trust that he wouldn’t let them fall.

He really hoped she wasn’t about to regret that.

The Son of Ruin reached into his core and pulled. Scorching winds formed in tight spirals around his hands and forearms, his twin bracers warming under his magic’s direction. Before a single Lifekeeper could finish their spells, he struck. He punched his fists forward and Destruction magic crashed into the line of Elysian warriors. The concussive blast reverberated through the town with the power of a thunderclap.

“Go!” Cade commanded, and his crew didn’t waste the opportunity.

They moved like a single organism from the dwindling rooftops and through Dogwood’s winding streets, each member of their crew covering the others’ weaknesses.

Orro practically melted between shadows, materializing just long enough to redirect pursuing Lifekeepers into dead ends. Jer kept Bunny secure while maintaining their makeshift squad’s formation, flipping over a stationary cart in his path. And Rayka and Elena ‘reacquired’ some essential supplies as they fled.

“Left!” Cade shouted. “The tannery district has lower roofs—”

Crystal-enhanced spells shattered the cobblestones ahead, cutting off their path. Cade spun, Chubbs clinging to his back as he assessed their options. The goddess-fragment in his core pulsed with destructive potential, but something else stirred beneath it—a different resonance that felt almost... alive.

No time to dwell on it.

Three Lifekeepers emerged from the side streets, visors glimmering menacingly. Before they could fire, Nora stepped forward. Cade witnessed the air around her darken as she reached forward with a clawed gauntlet. With a twist of her wrist, shadows lurking beneath the cobblestones snapped toward their pursuers. Their screams echoed off the walls as they collapsed, trapped in personal nightmares.

“That won’t hold them long,” she warned, already moving.

“Don’t sell yourself short,” Cade said with a grimace at the writhing Elysians. “They don’t look like they’ll be getting up soon.”

“There’s more,” Orro grunted darkly. He pointed at a group of ten soldiers closing in on their position.

The assassin, still radiating murderous intent over his mask, proved why he’d earned his reputation. He flowed through the Lifekeepers’ formations like water through stones, his daggers finding gaps in their defenses that shouldn’t have existed. Each strike disabled without killing, yet left their pursuers groaning on the ground.

But the Lifekeepers were nothing if not prepared.

Crystal light bloomed across the rooftops ahead as more white-cloaked figures emerged from hidden positions. They’d anticipated this route. Planned for it.

“They’re herding us,” Cade realized, spotting more teams moving to cut off their alternate paths.

Their options narrowed with each turn. Dogwood’s outer wall loomed closer, but the gates were clearly visible now—and clearly guarded by dozens of crystal-enhanced zealots. Every side street sprouted white cloaks. Gav bulled through several of their ranks, but they just kept coming.

They skidded to a stop in what had probably been a lovely town square before their arrival. Now it was just another corner of their shrinking cage. Ancient stone walls rose on three sides, too high even for their acrobatics. Lifekeepers filled every street and alley leading in, their masks gleaming with captured starlight.

“Cade?” Chubbs whispered against his shoulder, her complete trust somehow heavier than the weight of their situation.

The head Lifekeeper stepped forward, triumph written in every line of their posture. “An impressive display,” they said, genuine respect coloring their zealotry. “But surely you didn’t think we came unprepared for the Destroyer’s vessel?”

“Destroyer? Is that really the title they gave you, Cade?” Elena asked with a quirked eyebrow.

“It’s not like they put it to a vote or something,” Cade answered with a resigned shrug.

Behind the Lifekeeper who’d addressed, a scrawnier wood elf raised his hand. “Actually, we did. Destroyer won out over ‘Scourge Of All That Is Good And Sacred’.”

“The Destroyer,” the head Lifekeeper emphasized through gritted teeth, “shall face justice for his crimes. Him and his acolytes of evil.”

“I’m more of an intern, actually,” Gav muttered with a grin.

“ENOUGH!” the Lifekeeper yelled.

Spell-light crystalized around them, dozens of magic circles activating in perfect synchronization. The air hummed with contained power. One wrong move would trigger an avalanche of destructive force.

Cade felt his crew shift into defensive positions around him and Chubbs. Orro’s cold rage, Nora’s calculating focus, Rayka’s coiled tension, Jer’s steady presence with Bunny’s claws digging trenches in his shoulder—all of them were ready to fight their way out or go down trying.

The foreign magic stirred again in Cade’s core, discordant against his Destruction power like two songs playing in clashing keys. His next breath tasted of steel and starlight and something ancient that had no name.

The head Lifekeeper raised his hand, crystals blazing. “It’s time you died, Cade Stormhollow.”

“We might have to postpone that, little Lifekeeper,” came a new voice from someone perched atop the high wall.

Everyone glanced up as one at the newcomer. Nora cursed while Evie waved at the fresh arrival.

Cade’s stomach churned, though whether it was due to relief or the ominous presence of this person, he couldn’t say.

“You see, I have need for this Son of Ruin, but I really do want to find out if he can get out of this situation on his own. Nevermind, Lifekeeper. Go at it,” Astrid Clover said from her perch with a wave of her hand. Her undead draugr eyes glimmered with mirth.

“Have fun, Stormhollow.”
