Chapter V.LVIII (5.58) - Bloodspawn Weakness

The next few days fell into a pattern for Kizu. Since he couldn’t spend blood on spellwork as freely any longer, he spent more time hunched over his cauldron, preparing for the delve into the World Dungeon via potions, rather than practicing direct magical improvements. He also did his best to uncover any and all information about Blood Lords. Which, he quickly realized, was extraordinarily sparse. Unlike Dragons, which had an entire mythology formed around them and even a dead religion worshipping them, Blood Lords were nearly never mentioned more than in passing as dangerous threats. Which made sense, as Otochi was the only one in the world, as far as Kizu knew.

Mentions of bloodspawn were rare, but significantly more frequent and less cryptic about their powers. They were a widespread threat across the World Dungeon, which either meant that Otochi had spent thousands of years creating tens of thousands of minions to send out to the far reaches of the world, or that bloodspawn had another way to increase their numbers outside of Otochi’s influence. He found one note about their numbers increasing due to a ritual that involved drinking blood. Though if the blood in question belonged to another bloodspawn, a Blood Lord’s, or another entity entirely remained ambiguous in the text.

Kizu recalled that the first bloodspawn he’d killed had mentioned something about exile. So there were communities outside of Otochi’s direct power, at the very least.

As he’d already discovered, the bloodspawn had the ability to drink a mage’s blood and use the blood’s affinities as their own. Kizu assumed this must mean it likewise came with an understanding of how to use those spells, because he couldn’t imagine a bloodspawn figuring out something like complex spatial magic that took Kizu months of constant trial and error to secure a grasp on. If so, it was a devastatingly powerful ability at their disposal. He shuddered to think what they’d be able to do if they somehow got their hands on some of the professors’ blood.

Their weaknesses listed were exactly as he remembered. Fire and sunlight were the two keys to vanquishing bloodspawn quickly. Of course, with enough effort, he could brute force a death out of them through more mundane means as well. Beheading, for example, seemed a viable option mentioned in a few texts. But bloodspawn were hardier than a normal mage or human. Well, that was assuming they had access to blood. Without a steady flow of nutrients, they weakened significantly. Their bones became brittle and their muscles atrophied. They could survive for decades without blood, but they’d feel every second of the time that passed.

He wondered how Anata’s blood came into play with these abilities. Sojan found the taste repugnant, an opinion he shared at the slightest provocation. It must not be the same for her kin, though, or they wouldn’t have been using her as a blood bag for all these years. Did it grant them a better understanding and command over divination spells? Or did it simply satisfy their hunger? When Kizu used the blood, it enhanced his senses and granted him energy. Maybe it was similar for the bloodspawn.

Kizu noticed a footnote about how they used a magical leech to store blood for later use. He flipped to that page and was fascinated to find that this listed a boggy area of the World Dungeon where the leeches were harvested. Bloodspawn were known to create leech farms in these stretches of the dungeon. The notes on terrain reminded him of Stina and Aoi’s fight the other day.

“What are you reading?”

His attention torn away from his book, Kizu looked up to see Sene looking down at him. Ione’s twin was distinct from Kizu’s girlfriend in how immaculately her hair was brushed and the perfectly presentable uniform in comparison to Ione’s laxer sense of style. Sene’s facial features were also always wildly different from her sister’s. The current pursed lips and scrutinization of Kizu’s book were a dead give away of the girl’s identity. The tone of those four words spoken to him was a hint as well. The fact that she spoke to Kizu in the library was yet another clear sign.

Since classes were over for the semester, the library’s occupation had dipped down to single digit numbers. After how chaotic they’d become during finals, Kizu found the silence here an extremely welcome reprieve.

“Just a book about monsters,” Kizu replied, handing the tome to her. Being cagy and evasive about responding would only create suspicion. Plus, there was nothing wrong about him studying monsters in his free time.

Sene flipped through the pages, frowning. “Why are you studying magical leeches? Oh, this has to do with Ione, doesn’t it? Is this some harebrained scheme to fetch her new magical creatures? Please do not tell me she’s conjured a ghastly fascination with the evil parasites. Our mother will not be pleased if we return home and spend summer vacation with a home full of experimental magical leeches.”

Kizu tugged the book out of her grasp.

“No. It has nothing to do with Ione. Am I not allowed to study in my free time?”

“Studying is admirable; however, did you forget what we are meant to be doing today? You should be preparing for our next venture.”

Kizu groaned. “You mean the water fountains? Sene, we’ve already configured and done everything in our power to make the water supply as widespread and accessible as possible. We even rebuilt that entire fountain by the headmaster’s office. What more can we do?”

Sene harrumphed loudly and fell into the seat adjacent to him. “If you’d been paying attention in our last Student Council meeting, you would know that the next step is that we need to make water available for all of those spectating and attending the tournament. It’s an open air venue, directly impacted by the sun above. A breeding ground for sun based illnesses. While, yes, the stands are covered by shade, it does not fully protect everyone from the potential danger imposed. Hydration can resolve and prevent a myriad of issues.”

Kizu’s eyes wandered as Sene spent the next ten minutes explaining the exact dangers of over exposure to the sun and the benefits of drinking water.

They were in the Living Library, so a massive tree grew in the center of the space, blocking off the view of the rest of the room. He watched as a student used the tree’s enchantment. The girl peeled back a bit of its bark, wrote down what she wanted with a stylus, and replaced the bark on the tree. After a few seconds, the roots lit up with an eerie blue color and led her to a bookshelf out of sight.

Kizu had used that tree's enchantment to find his current tome. It was a handy system and only one of the many uses of the tree. That section of the trunk was used for gathering information from throughout the library, but the tree also supplied answers to questions about its nature and the branches were known to shift and rustle, despite being locked in a windless room.

Staring up at the skylight that stretched across the majority of the ceiling, Kizu wondered if the tree would continue to grow beyond this room’s capacity, or if it was at its maximum size already. It was hard to predict uniquely magical trees like this, especially when he was ignorant of any precedent set by others of similar natures.

“Do you think the bark could be applied for a divination potion?” Kizu muttered, staring at the tree. “Or maybe something to enhance the amount of information you perceive?”

“What?” Sene paused her rambling to scowl at him. “Are you not listening to me? Ugh. Extraordinarily disrespectful behavior. How does my sister put up with you? Actually, she probably doesn’t even notice because she’s busy being disrespectful to you in return. It cancels itself out. Anyway, while I am not a botanist nor a member of the Gardening Club, I can tell you that under no circumstances are we permitted to damage or experiment with academy property like that tree. There are specific gardens where we are permitted to harvest ingredients for brewing, the library is not one of them.”

Kizu sighed and sat back in the plush seat. He shouldn’t have asked that question out loud. He decided that, rather than let Sene get worked up about him vandalizing academy property, he would change the subject again.

“I saw your fight yesterday. You did well. Any idea why they matched you up with that other student? She didn’t seem to pose much of a challenge to you.”

“Of course she didn’t. Arina has it in her mind that we are rivals, simply because she holds the position of second best in a few subjects. The gap between first and second was very much on display in our duel, so I wish she’d wipe away these nonsensical notions of a rivalry. Though, perhaps if she was on the Student Council I could rely on her to assist me with projects more than my current group. That gnome, for example, is utterly useless. You know he had the gall to laugh at me when I proposed we assemble for two meetings a week until the end of the semester, rather than the scheduled one?”

Kizu had hoped the whiplash of three rapid subject changes would knock Sene off her game and she’d stop scolding him like a child. He supposed that somehow getting her to rant about Faible was a more realistic outcome. In hindsight, he was surprised the two of them hadn’t fought yet in the duels. The drama fueled matches hadn’t ended with only their first fights. Somehow, the brackets continued to pit them against more students with personal grudges.

Not that Kizu’s second or third matches had been particularly interesting. He had intended to end them fast, with a simple jump to his opponents’ skulls. Both times, they actually dodged his first strike, and he either used his enchanted ring to slam an ice blade down on their neck, or he kicked them with his monster foot with enough force to break bones and toss them out of the arena grounds. That was enough to remove the competition. Not quite as quickly as he would have liked, but the strategy had used very little of his blood supply so he didn’t mind the few seconds extra of combat.

Sene and Ione had similarly easy fights. Kizu assumed that they were pitting the twins against people they’d unknowingly offended in the last three years. Despite their vast differences, both girls had a knack for unknowingly upsetting other students. Kizu’s mind paused as a realization struck him.

Just like the Ione, he hadn’t known his last two competitors. He dismissed the thought before it could take root. Surely, that couldn’t mean he was in the same situation. He liked to think he had a bit more awareness than that.
