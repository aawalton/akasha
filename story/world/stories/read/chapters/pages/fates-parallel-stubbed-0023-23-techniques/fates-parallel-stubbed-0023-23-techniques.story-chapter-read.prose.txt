Though the lesson had already ended, Jia and Eui were too excited about their new techniques and stayed behind for some extracurricular training. Takeda, amused by their enthusiasm, chose to join them and offer some pointers.

Lee Jia absorbed the information within the jade slip. The manual contained forms all the way through the fourth stage of ki cultivation. Lee Jia wasn’t really certain how many there were, come to think of it, but the ultimate forms of the technique were completely beyond her comprehension. She only understood that apparently they had been added to the technique by Ienaga herself, with the original master having never made it past the third stage.

The first few forms could be done in the first stage, however. Step one was to realign the flow of ki within her body to strengthen her affinity for the lightning element. Thanks to her sparring—and, strangely enough, her calligraphy practice—she had some experience adjusting the flow of her ki already. Meditating according to the manual's instructions, Lee Jia soon had her ki flowing through her body in jagged lightning arcs, rather than the natural mimicry of her blood vessels.

With that done, Lee Jia was eager to try out the first forms of the technique. She took a stance and began to practice, getting her body used to the new movements. Unlike the slow, flowing motions of the basic bujutsu techniques, the Lightning God Transformation’s forms were fast, punchy, and dynamic. To an outside observer, Lee Jia appeared to be locked in an intense, fast-paced battle with an invisible opponent.

Takeda shook her head and smiled wryly as she watched Lee Jia in action.

“I know you’re excited and all, but I wouldn’t expect much from your first day. It’ll probably take you a few weeks at least just to get the ki alignment right, never mind actually—”

Bzap

Takeda’s words were cut off as Lee Jia completed the first form by throwing a spear-hand, causing a small spark to flicker from her fingertips. It didn’t seem like much, but Lee Jia knew it was meant to be applied with direct contact—preferably to a pressure point where the lightning ki would wreak havoc on an opponent’s meridians.

Takeda stared at the display with a flat expression and dead eyes, grumbling something vitriolic about talented people exploding. She often found it annoying whenever Lee Jia picked up new skills quickly, but was always happy for her in the end. Takeda turned her gaze to An Eui and noted with grim and slightly guilty satisfaction that she at least was struggling.

Eui had been able to realign her ki just as easily as Jia had. After all, since they had begun the practice of regular joint cultivation, Jia’s practice was Eui’s and vice versa. Unfortunately, after that she’d encountered nothing but problems.

The Six Arms of Asura, true to its name, had precisely six forms. Of those, only the first could be practiced in the first stage. Unlike Jia’s technique, which was a fully comprehensive fighting style right out of the box, the first ‘arm’ of Eui’s technique was strictly supplementary to basic bujutsu. Specifically, it was a weapon technique.

According to the manual, destruction ki was like a wild animal which had to be tamed. It was necessary, therefore, to practice on external elements to the point of perfect mastery before even considering turning its power inward and channeling it through one’s own body.

The first form had only three steps—First one had to imbue a weapon with their ki without allowing it to ravage their own body in the process. Second, maintain control of the ki empowering the weapon to prevent it from destroying the object. Third, once the weapon was fully imbued, release control of the ki, preferably at a safe remove and hidden behind a solid object or a particularly loathsome person.

Eui grimaced down at the half-disintegrated knife and the nasty black veins that had spread painfully up to her wrist. She hadn’t made it past step one. Not only was the technique extremely difficult even with the manual’s help, but failure was costly and painful. The knife was destroyed and her hand was badly injured from just one attempt.

Eui just sighed before picking up the remains of the knife with her good hand and trying again.

Despite her injured arms, Eui was all but carrying her half-conscious friend home. While Eui had been foolishly attempting to continue practicing with injured hands—a mistake she wouldn’t soon make again, as it only made the technique even harder—Jia had gotten overconfident and challenged Takeda to a full contact spar. Takeda had taken maybe a little bit too much pleasure in humbling Jia, and now the two of them were limping home, having both been thoroughly reminded of their place.

“I probably could have won if I used spells.”

Jia grumbled sourly, having mostly recovered by the time they made it back home. Eui smiled archly at Jia.

“You know she has those too now, right? And it would be a huge waste to use talismans on a friendly bout like that.”

Jia sighed with a frown. Of course she knew all that. She had been measuring herself against the Takeda she’d sparred against in preparation for her duel last month, but she’d forgotten that Takeda hadn’t been idle all that time—she’d been training just as hard.

“You’re right, but it still sucks to lose. Do you want to meditate before we eat? I’ll be starving by the time we’re done, but feeling the food digesting in someone else’s stomach is gross.”

Jia grimaced and shook her head at the thought. Eui glanced self-consciously down at her injured hands. She tried flexing them a bit and winced as the pain shot through them along the black veins that had reached all the way up to her elbows.

“I don’t know if that’s a good idea…”

Jia gingerly took Eui’s hands in her own, and regarded them with a sympathetic expression.

“Didn’t Ienaga just tell you not to push yourself too hard? Don’t worry about it, we’re both beat up right now, and if we’re going to cultivate together, that’s going to mean that sometimes we share our pain and our failures.”

Eui nodded, a bit misty-eyed, but said nothing else for fear her voice would break. When the two began their now-familiar meditation routine, there was a slight difference. Jia hadn’t really realized it, but after realigning her ki towards lightning, it had taken on a restless and agitated nature. For a moment, she felt a flash of pain that nearly broke her concentration as she felt the cold and destructive potential of Eui’s ki.

As their ki combined, however, the cold and restless feelings seemed to cancel each other out, leaving only a pleasant warmth that spread throughout both of them. The warmth was unexpectedly soothing and they hardly even noticed their injuries as they settled into comfortable meditation.

“As you’ve begun to practice elemental body techniques, I feel it is only appropriate that you begin training similarly practical applications of qi.”

As usual, Elder Qin made them wait in the pavilion for a while before appearing out of nowhere and beginning the lesson as if he’d been there the whole time. Jia and Eui were used to it by now, and the view there was quite lovely, so it wasn’t an unpleasant wait.

“You’ve already completed the first stage of the foundation building technique and mastered qi bursts and qi shielding, so in truth there is nothing left but practical techniques left until you’ve built up enough qi to break through to the qi condensing stage.”

Jia and Eui nodded. Jia had already demonstrated her mastery of the qi burst in their team sparring match against Satou and Kasai. The qi shielding technique was very similar to the mana shield spell she already knew, except that it was permanent and automatic. The tradeoff was that it was significantly weaker, albeit more energy efficient.

Once she had learned qi shielding, Jia understood why opening her duel against Yan Zhihao with the lightning bolt would not have worked. No doubt he’d be injured, but a significant portion of the lightning bolt’s effect would have been absorbed by his qi shield. It would have left her out of mana without taking Yan out of the fight.

Lee Jia looked up and met Elder Qin’s cold gaze expectantly. He stroked his long beard as he spoke.

“You may begin at any time.”

Lee Jia furrowed her brows in confusion.

“What? That’s it? You’re not going to give us some advice? Some kind of lesson? An actual technique to practice!?”

Elder Qin stared down his nose at her condescendingly.

“Why would I? I doubt you want for manuals after acquiring the Yan boy’s spatial ring. They contain all you need to know. If you have questions, ask them.”

Lee Jia huffed angrily and pulled out the assortment of manuals she had taken from Yan Zhihao. She was a little bit miffed that she wasn’t getting a personalized recommendation as she had from Ienaga, but she supposed that Qin had his own way of doing things.

Jia and Eui puzzled over the manuals for a while. They were all interesting, especially flight even if she wouldn’t be able to practice that one yet. Paralyzed by indecision, Lee Jia looked up at Elder Qin.

“Do you have any suggestions for which we should learn first?”

“Tranquility of the Verdant Marsh for both of you.”

Lee Jia stared wide-eyed at Elder Qin. He hadn’t even glanced at the manuals and he’d answered instantly. Did he already know which ones she had? What was she thinking, of course he did. He’d probably known all along and knew which one to suggest a week ago. Lee Jia shook off the frustration.

“Why that one? And is there some way we could figure out for ourselves which techniques are best suited to us?”

For a brief moment, the corner of Elder Qin’s mouth quirked up in a rare smile.

“At last, a good question. I was beginning to lose hope. Very well, heed my words carefully.”

Jia and Eui prepared themselves for a lecture. Knowing Qin, he’d planned the upcoming speech already and had simply been waiting for them to request it of him. He could be such an infuriating teacher.

“What you have here is the complete houtian training regimen of the Awakening Dragon sect, one of the great sects. It is, in theory, completely comprehensive and could be learned by anyone to produce an individual cultivator worthy of the great sects. For you, however, there are two faults.”

Lee Jia raised her hand slightly to indicate that she had a question. She had quickly learned that unlike Ienaga or Hwang, Elder Qin did not appreciate being interrupted. He nodded for her to proceed.

“What does ‘houtian’ mean? And I’ve been meaning to ask, but what are the great sects?”

“A sect is an association of cultivators dedicated to their practice. In theory, the sect exists simply to pass techniques on to new generations of disciples, and keep the practice alive. In reality, the sects of Qin are deeply political and fiercely competitive entities. The great sects are those which have the recognition and support of the Empire. While they are subordinate to the imperial family, they control most of the land in the Empire.

“As for houtian, it is a word which is used to represent the lower stages of cultivation. The upper stages of cultivation are called xiantian. The gap between a mortal and a houtian cultivator is significant, and grows exponentially greater with each stage, but the gap between houtian and xiantian is as great as that between heaven and earth.”

Jia and Eui nodded to indicate their understanding.

“As I was saying, there are two problems with you cultivating in the Awakening Dragon method. Both problems share the same root. The cultivation method was crafted over the course of centuries and passed down by the Yan clan and their disciples. Perhaps you can guess why this presents a problem to you.”

Jia considered it for a moment.

“Is it because they would hunt me down for stealing their method?”

Elder Qin scoffed.

“Not at all. These techniques are not all common—some are even rare—but only the Awakening of the Dragon’s Heart is secret, and even then, it’s not a closely guarded secret. Any inner disciple of the Awakening Dragon learns it as a matter of course.

“No, the first problem is that despite my recommendations, you have chosen to walk the path of body and mind cultivation. This will disrupt your elemental balance, which would be harmful to your future cultivation. While focusing on a single element is practical for short term benefits and early cultivation, balance is of utmost importance in the long term.”

Lee Jia remembered Magus Hwang saying the same thing in his earliest lessons.

“Is there a way to fix that imbalance?”

Elder Qin frowned at the interjection. Lee Jia had forgotten herself and spoken out of turn, but he answered her anyway.

“Some of my so-called ‘contemporaries’ within my homeland would tell you it’s simple. Miss Lee cultivates lightning within her body, and so she should simply forgo the metal techniques. Likewise, the destructive yin energy within Miss An is strong enough that she has no need to train in the element of water.”

Lee Jia blinked in confusion. Magus Hwang’s lessons on mana theory were dense and boring, but she still tried to pay attention and that didn’t quite match up to her limited understanding of the elements.

“I can see from your expression that you understand it isn’t quite so simple. Indeed, such ‘experts’ would leave your cultivation crippled, and your friend dead within a decade. Though her ill-fate is likely to lead her there, regardless.”

Eui scowled.

“I’m right here, you know.”

As he often did, Elder Qin ignored Eui’s protest, continuing as if she wasn’t there.

“There is a reason it took the Yan family centuries to perfect their cultivation method. It is because their understanding of the elements was flawed, requiring significant trial and error to correct their misapprehensions. I’m not a scholar, but as a cultivation expert I would be a fool to reject the reality before me. Unfortunately, while the scholars of my country agree that modern mana theory is superior to the traditional wuxing, it’s adoption among the sects has been politicised.

“In any case, I regret to admit that redressing that imbalance is beyond my knowledge. You’d be better served discussing it with Magus Hwang. Besides which, we have yet to discuss the second problem, which is far simpler and yet infinitely more difficult. Like most noble clans within the Empire, the Yan family is patrilineal. Their ancestral techniques are best suited to their scions. In other words, men.”

Lee Jia narrowed her eyes suspiciously. This was blatant discrimination!

“What does that have to do with it? Are you going to tell me that we’re unsuited for these techniques because we’re women?”

“Yes.”

Elder Qin answered without hesitation. Lee Jia was too flabbergasted to respond, and Elder Qin continued, ignoring her indignation.

“I am not saying your gender renders you incapable, nor is this merely a matter of cultural gender roles—though I admit that it is no coincidence that the disciples present here from my homeland are all men.”

They were? Lee Jia hadn’t really noticed, but now that he mentioned it she had yet to meet any women from Qin.

“What matters here is the alignment of the soul. This is not quite the same as one’s elemental affinity, which is a result of natural corruption accumulated within the body. Soul alignment is a natural property of the soul, which has a great impact on the manifestation of one’s qi. In the vast majority of the population, the souls of men are yang-aligned, while women are yin-aligned. Perhaps one in a thousand have their alignment reversed, and one in ten thousand have unaligned, balanced souls.

“While soul alignment has no effect on elemental balance, it has an enormous impact on the effect of qi techniques. As you are both women, you can expect the methods detailed within those manuals to produce unexpected, potentially even unpleasant results. The instructions for opening the meridians will be safe to follow, but the practical applications of the qi you channel through them will most likely fail.”

Lee Jia bit her lip in frustration. Nothing about cultivation was ever simple. Her eyes widened and her head jerked up as she came to a realization.

“Wait! What would have happened if I’d just tried to learn one of these techniques on my own?”

Elder Qin sneered down at her in the way that he always did when she asked what he thought was a foolish question.

“Then your failure would have been a valuable lesson. I’m not without compassion, however, and I do have my pride as an instructor. While I will not provide you with replacement techniques, I can suggest alternatives.”

Lee Jia’s ears perked up at Elder Qin’s words. As always he held his true intentions back until the last moment.

“First, there is the possibility of physical intervention. The soul is shaped in part by the body, and there are rites which can change one’s alignment. I should warn you that they are permanent, unpleasant, and there is a possibility that you are already too old. The Eunuchs within the imperial palace are one such example, albeit in reverse of your predicament.”

Lee Jia grimaced and shuddered. There was no way she would even consider going through whatever that was. She wished she could unhear it.

“The other option is to modify the techniques to suit you, instead of modifying yourselves to suit the techniques. My only recommendation is that you discuss the matter with Magus Hwang. Unlike me, he is a scholar. A well renowned one, at that. There should be nobody better qualified than he to assist you in the matter.”

Lee Jia thanked Elder Qin through gritted teeth. As much as she had learned some valuable information in this lesson, she really wished that he had led with that.
