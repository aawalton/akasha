One moment, Tara was standing at the top of the airship's ramp, hand outstretched and ready to pull her friends aboard. The next, a surge of divine power welling up from the center of the plateau, completely overwhelming her magical senses. Her eyes widened in panic.

"Hurry!"

Maybe if she'd had mana, she could have done something. But she was out of magic, and her friends were out of time. The tek'kalla mage watched helplessly as the explosion of darkness tore through the plateau, toppling buildings and sending giant cracks cascading across the breadth of the massive rock formation. The last thing she saw was a trio of familiar bubble shields burst into existence around her three friends, and then they were consumed by the ravenous gloom.

"No!"

The wall of darkness slammed into the airship like a tidal wave, sending it yawing hard to the side. A penetrating chill pierced Tara's chest and she was flung back inside the craft, slamming against the opposite bulkhead with bone rattling force. An icy grip squeezed her heart, growing tighter and tighter, and frost began crystalizing on the interior of the passenger compartment, creeping across her suit with terrifying speed. Then Seris engaged the ship's barriers. The force field flickered on with a snap-hiss, pushing the vortex of dark energy away. It broke around their vessel like a boulder parting a torrent of water, and the suffocating sensation eased.

"Hang on!"

Tara's stomach lurched as the vessel dropped, and she barely managed to grab a handhold before her body was pulled towards the ceiling. Naomi seized her by the arm just as her grip began to slip, pulling her into one of the seats while the ship rocked and bucked from a series of heavy impacts.

"We have to go back for them!" Tara gasped, pulling one of the straps across her waist. "They're still out there!"

The maelstrom of dark essence was ebbing. Tara could see out through the still-open rear ramp, and she watched intently as the world outside slowly resolved back into focus. The plateau came back into view as Seris righted the ship, getting her back under control. Her heart dropped. Of the courtyard, cathedral, and the cliff they'd just stood upon, nothing was left. That entire quarter of the plateau had sheared away, fracturing into a massive rockslide that left nothing but mounds of shattered stone piled on the wasteland surface far below.

"They're gone." Seris's voice was tight and controlled.

What? No. They couldn't be. They'd been right there!

"But!" Naomi's hand squeeze Tara's arm tightly. "We can't--"

"There's nothing more we can do."

The ship rocked again, and this time Tara saw streaks of fire flash past the stern of the ship. The demons had finally arrived, and they weren't here to talk.

"Get us out of here." Tara said, punching in the command to close the ramp on the panel beside her seat. She heard her own voice, flat and emotionless, as if it were someone else that was speaking.

"But…" Naomi's eyes were filled with unshed tears. She squeezed them shut, pressing her head back against the bulkhead. On the other side of the compartment, a battered and bloody Fenrin slumped in his seat.

None of them exchanged another word as Seris engaged the main drive, sending them soaring across the sky faster than any demon could pursue.

Jade woke to the worst headache of her life. Her temples throbbed like they were being used as the anvil to someone else's hammer, and a great weight rested heavily on her chest. The world was dark and cold, and it was hard to move.

What's… what's going on?

Where was she? How had she ended up here? She struggled to recall, but everything felt hazy. Slowly, the pounding in her head receded, and her thoughts began to clear. A grinding, clattering sound from somewhere nearby caught her attention, and she managed to turn her head in that direction. There! A shaft of crimson light split the darkness, illuminating… rocks?

It all came back to her in a dizzying torrent. The desperate battle, their rushed escape, and the final explosion of divine power that had consumed them.

Luis! Siora!

Jade extended her senses, ignoring the spike of pain that act produced in her head. She spread her awareness, sweeping the area for any familiar presences, but there was nothing. She could only detect the dark flames of demonic spirits, and even they were distant. Next, she struggled to shift her limbs beneath the crushing embrace of the rocks burying her. Leveraging her enhanced strength, Jade managed to shift into a somewhat more stable position, but the weight pressing down on her was immense. She'd been clinging to Siora when they fell, but there was no sign of the elf now.

No…

But try as she might, Jade couldn't deceive herself. She'd felt the moment Luis's spirit had been extinguished, and Siora's hadn't lasted much longer. It wasn't much of a surprise, they'd been caught in a vortex of uncontrolled divine power. A dangerous proposition at the best of times, and the demon lord's demise had come when they were exhausted and vulnerable. The only surprising thing was that Jade herself had somehow survived.

No, not somehow. Luis had protected them. He'd thrown them both away from the blast, using the last of his strength to conjure a protective barrier around them. That her friend's last act had been one of heroic self-sacrifice came as no surprise to Jade, and that only made the piercing pain in her heart bite all the deeper.

Luis…

Tears prickled at the corners of her eyes. Luis had been with her since the very start, and Siora had joined almost as quickly. Over these past six months they'd grown to be family, guiding and protecting each other as they journeyed through the Labyrinth. Of all the people to perish in such a desolate, unforgiving land, why did it have to be two of the kindest, most cheerful people she'd ever met?

It wasn't fair. None of this was. Yes, they'd won, defeated Vastrozun, and presumably stopped the demon invasion. Because of them, the goddess could close the portals, and the cosmos would be safe again. But here, lying among the corpses of her friends, it just didn't feel worth it.

"I hope the others got away…"

That faint glimmer of hope sustained her. Naomi, Tara, and the others. They'd been on the ship. Maybe they'd been able to escape. But even if they had, Jade wouldn't find out. She was done. Both physically and emotionally. Not an ounce of fight remained in her spirit. Heart heavy with the burden of regret, she closed her eyes and waited for the end.

…

…

…

But… no… Something about this didn't sit right with her. Luis had given everything to give her a chance, and she was going to repay that by, what, giving up? As much as she longed to succumb to the numbness of the beyond, doing so would be an insult to the memory of her beloved companion.

"Damnit!"Jade clenched her teeth and squeezed her eyes shut, tears now streaming freely down her face in the claustrophobic, sooty darkness. No. No freaking way. Screw that! If she wanted to call herself his friend, then she had to keep going. Ira's pained face flashed through her mind, an image of the angel's final moments. That's right. There was something else that she still had to do, too.

With a mighty heave, Jade shoved the stone slab off her torso. A torrent of dust and pebbles slid down into the gap, battering her head and causing the other stones surrounding her to shift alarmingly, but she somehow managed to scramble free from the hole before more stones fell to bury her again. It was a feat made possible only by her superhuman strength, and even then, if she'd been buried any deeper, she would've been done for.

How am I alive?

Jade paused, looking up at the sheer cliff extending hundreds of feet above her. She stood atop a mountain of rubble, pieces of buildings mixed with the fractured remnants of the ground upon which they'd stood. Of her friends' bodies, there was no sign. They were probably buried somewhere beneath her, crushed by the mountain of rubble. The thought made her sick, and Jade had to force herself to focus on her own situation.

Everything hurt, from the tips of her wings to the ends of her fingers, but it was more of a dull, sore pain than the sharp, piercing ones she expected. Amazingly, her body appeared to be mostly functional, albeit extremely stiff. Then, she noticed the blinking icon in the corner of her vision. It was a buff icon, a plus sign enclosed in a small bottle. The Labyrinth interface's symbol for regeneration. That's right, Siora had created a regenerative elixir for her after her wing had been damaged in that battle in the demon camp next to the portal. Its long-lasting effect was the reason why she hadn't been able to drink any potions during her time here. Had it been at work the entire time she'd been unconcious, slowly repairing injury after injury before she could succumb to them?

"Siora…"

Another pang of guilt struck her like a knife to the chest. So, she owed her life to both of her fallen friends. Distant shapes fluttered past high overhead. More demons? Were they searching the plateau? Jade doubted it would take them long to send someone to check the rubble down here. She had to go. But to where?

Jade…

A distant voice rang in the back of her mind. Was that…?

"Lady Elaria?"

Yes. This time the goddess was clearer. I've been searching for you. You and your friends achieved something incredible today, but Vastrozun's defeat destabilized the dimensional connection between our worlds. It's degrading faster than I thought.

"How long do I have?"

Distant portals were less affected, but all the ones you could easily reach are gone. I'll have to open a new one for you. It won't stay open for long.

That didn't sound good. Jade began clambering down the pile of fallen rocks and debris, making her way back to the flat ground of the wasteland.

"Tell me what I have to do."

As it turned out, the dimensional disturbance prevented the goddess from opening a portal anywhere within a few miles of the demon lord's demise. Jade had to make her way into the wasteland far enough for the disruptive effect of the power that had dispersed upon his death to be more manageable. After explaining that, the goddess directed Jade's attention towards the horizon.

There. I've begun the process of growing a link between worlds. You must hurry, it won't remain stable for long. This world has already begun drifting back into isolation.

Jade didn't need to be told twice. She took off at a jog, the fastest pace her sore and tired body could handle, running towards a twinkle of blue light that had appeared in the distance.

"Are… the rest of my friends okay? The ones… the ones who…"

The question was a waste of breath, but she had to know. Thump, thump, thump. Her feet pounded a steady beat on the hard packed dirt and soot.

Yes. Their vessel returned to the Labyrinth a few hours ago. I’m… sorry about the other two. I wish I could have done more.

Jade let out a long and steady breath of relief, only to cough halfway through it. When she took her hand away, a few drops of blood speckled her palm. Not fully healed, then. Not even close. Jade checked her interface again. It still wasn't safe to take a potion, the regeneration icon was still there. She'd just have to push through.

They gave it their all. I can't give up now.

Jade stumbled on a shallow slope, kicking up a cloud of sand and soot. Damnit, her legs were just too heavy. She forced herself forward, exhaustion pulling at her limbs like lead weights. Focus! Breathe in, breathe out. Breathe in, breathe out. Step after step she continued, doggedly planting one foot in front of the other. Until--

Whoosh!

A jet of fire slammed into the ground nearby, burning a line of crackling glass into the sand. The sudden attack caught her completely off guard. Jade glanced over her shoulder and was alarmed to see a trio of large, winged demons flapping their way towards her, slicing through the sky like giant birds of prey. But wait, why were they attacking her?! She was still in her succubus form, shouldn't they at least try to talk to her first?!

Except, she wasn't a demon. Not really. Not anymore. Even to her own senses, Jade's aura felt different. It had absorbed enough Light and Growth essence from the angels she'd drained that her spirit now shone with a luster no demon aura possessed. That alone set her apart, now, and her pursuers didn't seem inclined to give her a chance to explain the oddity.

Another blast of fire arced towards her and this time she had to dodge to the side to avoid it. Up ahead, she could see the portal finish forming. It resolved into the shape of a silver archway filled with flowing mist, just large enough for her to fit through.

Hurry! The goddess's voice echoed in her mind.

"No shit!" Jade gasped between ragged breaths. "I'm… trying!"

Jade took to the air, flying fast and low over the scarred landscape. Her mana had somewhat recovered while she was unconcious, but even a short flight caused it to drop at an alarming speed. One hundred yards. Eighty. Sixty!

"Aahh!"

One of the fireballs connected. It hit her square in the back, right between the shoulder blades. With her armor's defenses destroyed, the only thing protecting her were her own innate resistances and fortitude. She fell to the ground as hot pain flashed over her back, sliding to a stop less than fifty yards from the glimmering portal. She scrambled back to her feet and stumbled forward, one leg dragging behind her. She was so close! But the demons were faster. They were closing in too quickly, and she was in no state to fight. Her mind raced, looking for a solution.

No, no… Yes! That!

Jade plunged her hand into her belt pouch. Not her dimensional bag, but the other one. Due to its potential to be just as dangerous to her as any of her opponents Jade had only dared use the Bag of Death once before, when Malice had backed her into a corner in the second floor corespace. She was there again now, out of options and out of time.

Her hand disappeared into the void within the bag just as another pair of flame jets bracketed her. Jade ducked, sliding down a small hill and breaking their line of sight for a moment.

Please. Please please please!

It was odd, part of her realized, how desperately she now wanted to live so soon after she'd been wishing for it all to end. Then her thoughts were interrupted by something cold and clammy squeezing onto her arm.

"Gyaah!"

Jade shrieked, whipping her hand free from the necromantic bag and reflexively tossing it to the ground behind her. She ran with renewed vigor as a large, shadowy form rose from the mouth of the discarded item, taking the shape of a spectral being shrouded in a dark, misty cloak. She caught the name "Grave Wraith" in one of the notifications that flashed by before she minimized them, concentrating on making good her escape. She had no idea if she had control over the undead monster or not and didn't much care. What mattered was, as her pursuers crested the hill behind her, they ran straight into it.

Jade didn't look back. Not at the inhuman shrieks emanating behind her, nor when she took another glancing blow by a second beam of magical fire. Pain, exhaustion, fear, none of that had stopped Luis, so she refused to let them stop her. Jade arrived at the portal on trembling legs, leaving a line of blood staining the sand in her wake. There she paused for half a heartbeat, leaning on the archway for support as the sounds of conflict rang out behind her.

"Thank you." She whispered, closing her eyes. "Goodbye."

Then, she collapsed through the portal.
