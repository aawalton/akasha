The afternoon, the day before the party, Alric headed for the muster yard. With him were six of his personal guard, Rein, and two of his guards.

Pavise had sent a message saying most of the setup was done and what arrangements they needed to collect the beer. Alric had simply put the beer in his item box and left. He had no real opinions on the field; he assumed a few wagons around the edge, a shortage of tables, and whatever the innkeepers needed.

He mostly wanted to see the field where they’d be drinking.

Passing through the gate was also unusual. It was one thing for bridge guards to know him, but the night watch greeted him by name as well.

They followed the road running along the wall; the field finally came into view, and Alric slowed.

The muster yard was massive. Far bigger than Alric expected, it stretched along the river outside the walls, mostly flat ground. There was even someone cantering a horse around the edge. Alric had expected at most something the size of a football field; this was probably big enough to house five.

Everywhere Alric looked were old hitching posts, dug-out fire pits, outhouses, cleared areas, and a pole that may have been a maypole or a place for a huge tent. Alric was glancing around when Rein pointed down. Following his gaze down to the river, Alric saw a collection of wagons. At least ten of them.

They stood off around a cordoned area; cloth or canvas had been stretched over several of them. Many tables ran in long rows in places, and stacks of benches were nearby. Great piles of firewood were scattered around the edges.

There were people everywhere.

People carrying tables, hammering stakes, dragging wood, pointing at things, someone shouting at someone else; at this distance, they couldn’t even hear them. Among them, Pavise could be seen nodding to someone; they couldn’t hear someone shouting but could recognize the huge guild master.

They turned off the road to approach Pavise. Alric still glanced around.

“Looks like they dug out new latrines,” Rein said, pointing to the side.

Alric followed his gaze and felt a swell of respect and gratitude. It was one thing to drink the night away with three hundredish people. Drinking with three hundredish people who didn’t have good access to toilets was something Alric would treasure his ignorance of, forever.

“Looks like they thought of everything,” Alric said, nodding approvingly.

“Well, they're better campers than soldiers, I think.” Rein said, gesturing to the adventurers, “Soldiers get somewhere and start looking around for someone to feed them. Maybe they pitch a tent first if they think someone is watching.” Rein grinned when he said it; one of Alric’s guards scoffed.

Pavise had heard that comment; he turned with a wide grin. Holding out a hand to Rein as they approached. Rein shook it.

“Some respect, just when I thought the day couldn’t get better.” Pavise said, holding the grin, “Pavise.”

Rein looked up at the big man, released his hand, and said, “Captain Rein, border watch, your people are good at digging holes.” Rein said, pointing to the latrines with a smug grin.

Pavise laughed; the pair made small talk, but Alric wasn’t listening. Up close, it became obvious how much work and thought had gone into this. The wagons were neatly lined up. Benches had large gaps in places to give people room to move. Alric was impressed.

Rein walked away without explanation, pointing to a brazier. “That needs moving.” He said to a nearby adventurer.

“Eh? Why?” The adventurer asked, approaching the firewood.

“Cause if the wind blows from the city, gonna set that canvas ablaze,” Rein explained, pointing to it. The adventurer just nodded and lifted the brazier.

The adventurer just stared at Rein holding the brazier. Rein pointed to something else and wandered off, pointing to various things and shouting at various people.

Alric and Pavise just watched. “He's enjoying himself, you think?” Pavise asked, tilting his head at Rein.

"It certainly looks that way," Alric said, watching as Rein discovered new things to be offended by and pointed at them with professional annoyance.

Alric turned to survey the innkeepers, each apparently issued a wagon as standard. Denner was directing people around a cooking trench, a pig hanging from one of the posts like a warning to other pigs. Berrin’s territory was further down, marked by several large pots and a long fire trench with a large spit over it.

“You were smart about this. Everyone can get to the food without crowding the middle,” Alric said, nodding approvingly and pointing.

“They arranged it that way, actually. I was gonna put them in circles, but they refused.” Pavise said, shrugging and drawing circles in the air.

Alric nodded at that. A wagon being pulled by horses came approaching toward the innkeepers' section. This wagon was uncovered; it had three large iron pots. The pots were big enough to bathe in, should the party take a strange turn. Two men walked alongside, their hands reaching for the pots when the wagon hit a rut. Alric opened his mouth, then glanced toward the wagons again. Several spits were being assembled.

“Uh, Pavise, isn’t this a bit excessive?” Alric said, pointing to the wagons.

“Hmm? You mean the food prep? Definitely.” He said, setting off toward the wagons. Alric waited for more explanation. Pavise glanced at him as they walked. “People are going to be drinking the whole day tomorrow. Most will come hungry because they know the food will be good. Then we snack all day, maybe eat properly come sunset, then snack all night.” Pavise explained, gesturing to the wagon they were approaching.

Alric raised a brow as he glanced back across the field. He had been thinking of this as an evening thing. People arrived, ate, drank as much as they could, stumbled off somewhere, and slept somewhere regrettable. Pavise had planned it as a whole day of questionable decisions.

Well, there is free beer and a lot of it, day drinking is one way.

“That makes sense, I’ll let my people know to be here earlier,” Alric said, still looking at the wagons being unpacked while they walked.

Pavise nodded; he followed Alric’s gaze. “My people got a bit carried away on the meat when they found out about this party. I think most of the guild went out.” He said, he cleared his throat, avoiding eye contact, “We also bought a lot of pigs. The farmer had a good amount ready.” Pavise pointed to a nearby wagon; five pigs were currently hanging on the far side of it.

Alric was about to protest what he considered a waste before deciding it was the adventurers' time and money that would be filling a field with roast pork and not his problem.

“Well, I think breakfast may actually be the best time for the inns,” Alric said, glancing at the deep pots. Pavise gave Alric a confused look, so Alric continued, “Everyone is going to be drunk. You give someone a lot of beer and a good piece of pork- what are you gonna remember? Probably the beer.” Alric said, gesturing toward the field and river, “But when you regret everything? That hot bread, eggs, something greasy, tea? People remember that.”

Pavise’s eyebrows rose. “You’re how old?” he asked, but a nearby shuffling from the innkeepers got both of their attention.

Three innkeepers or their staff were all staring at Alric wide-eyed; the first looked at the other two, the second looked at his cooking area, and the third turned and called for someone.

Pavise just smiled, the previous point forgotten, “You seem to have that effect on people Alric.” He said, pointing to the innkeepers who were all moving around much faster. Alric heard one shout to another to find eggs.

Alric shrugged; it was pointless to deny it. They moved toward the center of the field; Rein rejoined them, grinning around at everything.

Alric, meanwhile, was looking at the sheer volume of weapons everywhere: bows leaned against tables, swords carried everywhere, a battle axe leaned against a bench, and a set of spears was spread out on a nearby table.

“Is fighting going to be an issue?” Alric asked, generally pointing to the evidence everywhere.

Pavise and Rein glanced at each other before they glanced at Alric.

“What?” Alric asked as they both gave him confused looks.

“Drawing steel surrounded by what, three-hundred-and-fifty-armed people?” Rein asked, his eyebrows up.

Pavise nodded, “No one is that stupid.”

“Even drunk?” Alric asked, glancing at the pair.

“Especially drunk,” Pavise said. He waved a hand at the weapons. “Someone draws a weapon, everyone near you does the same. Some people will fight with fists, maybe wrestle. Steel is something else.”

Rein nodded along, “It’s the same with my boys; fists are fists. If any of mine drew steel, my own boys would put him down.” Rein explained, glancing at the nearby weapons.

Pavise nodded. He wiped something off the edge of his lip. “Do you think we need some rules, though?” Pavise said, glancing at Rein.

“No weapons in games, no riding at all, anyone who wants to fight can go wrestle on the river's edge.” He said, glancing around, mentioning the river, which got a wince from both Alric and Pavise.

“Works for me,” Pavise said, gesturing to a nearby bench, but nobody sat down.

“Games?” Alric asked, but Rein was glaring at something; he left abruptly before he began shouting at people carrying benches that might block a route.

Pavise just watched. “Mind leaving him here?” Pavise asked, pointing to Rein.

Alric considered that for a moment. “Don’t think he’d follow me if I asked.” Alric glanced around. “Where do you want the beer?”

“That wagon over there without wheels. I’ll put tables around it, the innkeepers are giving us servers.” Pavise said, pointing.

“Alright, let's do that quickly then,” Alric said, walking to it.

Pavise glanced around before catching up. “The dragon is here?”

Dragon? Stromni called it that, has he been invovled?

“It’s in my item box,” Alric said, pointing to the wagon, he decided he'd speak to Stromni later. Pavise almost tripped however; Alric stopped so he could recover. “My items box is big; let's drop off the barrels.” Pavise opened his mouth but said nothing; he just pointed to the wagon.

Alric nodded. Approaching the wagon, he saw the sense in it; the slightly raised platform should make it easy for anyone to pour. Alric placed the first barrel, watching the ground shift slightly beneath the wagon. He placed a second opposite. He continued this way, checking the ground before placing all eight barrels, the bungs pointing outward, except for the last three, which he set into the racks that had been set up.

“Dragon’s here!” Someone shouted from behind him. Alric turned around; first he noticed Pavise staring at the barrels wide-eyed before a cheer went out.

Pavise shook his head slightly before he approached the wagon; he began shuffling the barrels slightly. Cheers were resounding from around.

Someone approached and began to clap; others nearby picked up the sound. Glancing around, Alric saw all the nearby adventurers and some of the innkeepers clapping. He grinned widely.

“We’ll tap them tomorrow morning.” Pavise said, appearing next to him, “I knew it was a lot, but the beer dragon is, wow.” Pavise stared at the barrels.

The beer wagon was in the center of everything. As the clapping died down, Alric watched two adventurers drag a table into place; two others nearby shouted about whether it was straight. Rein was at the edge, pointing at a wagon and probably ruining someone’s afternoon by making them move it.

“I owe you a big one for this,” Pavise said, taking in the atmosphere with the widest grin Alric had seen on him.

Alric was about to protest about how he didn’t want to waste beer and wanted the barrels before he decided having the big guy owe him a favor wasn’t so bad.
