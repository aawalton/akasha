Ezra turned slowly as he took in the person who was speaking. The thin man bore a tattoo on his forearm, one that Ezra knew well. It was a simple dagger with the haft pointed towards his hand, which stretched from his wrist to, Ezra supposed, the thug’s elbow, though his coat had only been folded up to mid forearm. His face was clean but unshaven, his long dark hair pulled back into a tail and tied with a leather thong. His clothes were sturdy and well-suited to the winter, though patched and worn, and on his hip hung a thin, small stick, about the thickness of a thumb and hung down to his knee. Behind the speaker stood two more thugs, one a woman with a shorn head and the other a brutish man with shaggy blonde hair and heavily scarred fists.

“What the—”

“Silence, Ezra,” the Madam silenced his accusation before he could say anything. “Welcome back, Master Nicholas. Would you like to purchase lunch?”

“You know I love your food,” he said, smiling, “but I’m here for business, and not yours. Please, give me the good news so I can just go and tell Knifefingers. He’ll give you the rates you  need to charge, and maybe supply a couple girls, but he’ll expect your waitresses to do most of the work. After all, they’re young and pretty, and have been teasing the men around here. This angry boy here too, I know there’ll be men and women who would prefer him over one of your girls.”

“You—”

“I will not tell you to be silent again, Ezra,” the Madam’s voice cut him off a second time. “This is my home and my business, and I will not be a wilting flower to be spoken for, neither by an excitable young man nor an outcast beastkin. The answer I once gave is the answer I repeat: The Dragon’s Toenail is not a brothel nor will it ever be. If Knifefingers wishes to enter business with me, we can discuss terms, but he cannot speak and expect me to fall in line.”

The leader of the three thugs, Nicholas, sighed and hung his head in disappointment at that. “I really wish you’d reconsidered. Maxen, Freya, you know what to do.”

Ezra didn’t try to hold himself back as the large man, Maxen, walked forward into the room and grabbed one of the benches. He was nearly as tall as Boyd, but broader, and he took the bench in both arms and threw it towards the bench. It smashed through the legs of one table, and he hurried towards the Madam with a face that warned of evil while Freya walked towards the fire.

The fire was a less pressing concern than the Madam who was in clear and present danger, and Ezre threw himself at Maxen. With a roar, he lunged forward with his cane still in hand. The giant thug turned with surprisingly quick steps as Ezra came closer, his face looking even more excited to beat the shit out of a man instead of whatever he was going to do to the Madam. Screams filled the air as the other people in the bar fled from the attack, but he didn’t pay any attention to them. It felt like the battlefield, but less organized.

Maxen raised his left hand to take Ezra’s first hit, and the soldier obliged the thug. The thick handle of his cane smashed into Maxen’s wrist, and the huge man seemed surprised by the sound of his own bones breaking. He sagged but roared as he threw himself at Ezra with his right hand. The meaty fist caught Ezra in the waist and he groaned as he was thrown back. Even so, it couldn’t compare to being thrown by a bugbear, and he kept his feet.

Ezra fought to find an opening against Maxen as the giant attacked, but the next three probing strikes he landed on the blonde man did nothing. Instead, he constantly lost ground as Maxen charged forward. He continued to curse leaving his spear and shield behind, but as it was, he was significantly stronger than he once was. Finally, Maxen threw a massive hook that Ezra sidestepped and then closed in. With both hands on the bottom of his cane, Ezra smashed his weapon into the giant’s jaw.

The crack of wood on bone echoed through the room, and Maxen fell, unconscious. His body thumped to the ground as Ezra tried to catch his breath while looking for the other two Blades.

Nicholas still stood in the doorway, though his eyebrows rose as his subordinate fell. Freya was at the fire, and had pulled a burning log free with a boker, throwing it beside the wall of the inn. The flames didn’t immediately catch on the building, but it was only a matter of time. Ezra didn’t think as he drew on his inner world and the wind rushed through his body.

Don’t overdraw it! Jormun’s voice, silent until then, thundered in Ezra’s head, and he heeded the command. If he exhausted himself right now then he’d just be killed while being unable to move. Instead, Ezra maintained a tight control on the aura as Freya pulled a forearm-length dagger from her belt.

“C’mon kid,” she said as she sunk into a fighter’s crouch. “Don’t wanna cut your face too bad.”

Instead of simply throwing himself into a panicked overhand swing, Ezra  circled around until nothing stood between him and Freya. When there was a clear path, she smirked.

“I wouldn’t run straight at someone with a knife, if I was you.”

He said nothing, noting that Nicholas, behind her, seemed to be considering something as he grabbed the handle of the stick on his waist. Then, when the other thug seemed to be willing to leave his companion to the fight alone, Ezra pushed the aura to his feet and made the leap that he’d slowly gained proficiency with over the past week and a half. Instead of having to run through the several meters that separated them, Ezra jumped and closed the distance faster than the knife fighter was prepared. She tensed and raised her blade, but was too slow. The cane crashed into the top of her head, and, just like Maxen, she fell in an unconscious lump after the single hit.

“There we go, fuckers,” Ezra laughed as he looked at Nicholas. He’d never fought since ascending to become an Adept, and the difference in power between him and these others was addicting. He hadn’t even needed to actively use aura against the giant!

“You’re an interesting one,” Nicholas said as he drew his weapon. Ezra’s confidence bled away as he saw rocklike aura crawl up the stick until, instead of an unintimidating stick, the thug held a stone weapon that would absolutely break Ezra’s cane the moment it made solid contact with it.

“What’s an Adept doing working for a gang?” Ezra asked, stepping away from Freya, just in case she regained consciousness and interfered.

“Getting paid,” Nicholas shrugged, the same gentle, regretful look on his face as when he’d asked the Madam to force her waitresses into becoming prostitutes. “How about you? A young Adept like you? Lots of money to be made doing things much more profitable than getting in the way of a gang that can crush you.”

“Protecting my home sound like a good enough reason?” Ezra spat.

“Ahhh,” Nicholas nodded knowingly. “I’m sorry to hear it. Look, you beat my two kids here. Fair and square. I’ll count it as the price the Blades had to pay to do what the boss tells us. If you look in the corner there, you’ll see that the building’s done for.”

He gestured to where Freya had tossed the still-burning log, and the fragile paneling there had already caught flame. It was spreading quickly, and Ezra knew he couldn’t do anything about it, not with another Adept here and himself only able to use one more aural attack. He knew nothing about how long Nicholas could keep going, and if he ran out first, he’d be dead.

“I’ve got another five guys outside,” Nicholas continued, “to make sure that the fire doesn’t spread too far. I don’t like killing kids, and, as additional payment for you, we won’t take anyone. The lesson’s been learned on both sides, okay?”

Ezra swallowed a couple times as Nicholas then finished, “And if you’re not alright with that, then I guess my mercy’s run out.”

As he said it, rock crawled up his arms all the way to his elbows. His head was still exposed, so—

“Ezra, come here.”

The Madam’s voice, something he’d either been ignoring or hadn’t heard until now, summoned him before he could make a decision. When Ezra glanced her way, she gestured for him to come to her. Keeping an eye on Nicholas as he did so, he approached.

“Go out the back door,” she said, her voice calm. “We’ll leave them to this, and be on our way.”

Even as he started to open his mouth to protest, the Madam’s eyes flashed, and the decision was made for him.

“Much appreciated,” Nicholas called out from the room that continued to burn. “I’m sure you can work something out with Knifefingers about a new business plan.”

“I’m sure,” she replied, and then, walking briskly with Ezra’s hand in hers, she walked into the kitchen and then through a hall to the back entrance of the inn.

“But it’s The—”

“Ezra, thank you for saving me from them. Now, kindly shut up and trust me. You haven’t been here for months, and things are changing. Despite a small hiccup, the plans have been made and now it is merely time to execute them.”

Knowing that she wouldn’t give him any more information than that until she was good and ready, Ezra followed her out, and saw there all of the staff of The Dragon’s Toenail.

“What took you so long?” Matilda demanded. “I knew we needed to tell Ezra, but he never sent letters and we never talked about what we were doing!”

“It’s fine,” the Madam said. “He saved me from a deviation in the plan. Now, it’s time for us to go north. There’s no need to delay. Everyone, travel together. Our destination has all of our things already, so there’s nothing to worry about. We simply need to leave the Blades’ territory before they realize we’re not just going to ground.”

“What is going on here?” Ezra asked, looking at a dozen faces that, despite their home burning down behind them, seemed to be in good spirits.

“We knew that Knifefingers is getting ideas of how important he is,” Tilly sighed, glaring at him. “His thugs have been around, and people all over the south side have been shoved out or made to start paying him.”

“Yes, Tilly, we had a plan,” the Madam said, looking at Ezra. “One that you very nearly ruined, though that plan had no contingencies for them attempting to take a prisoner. For that, I must thank you and thank Inkosuza the Watcher for seeing this coming and sending you in time. Now, would you like to see our new home?”
