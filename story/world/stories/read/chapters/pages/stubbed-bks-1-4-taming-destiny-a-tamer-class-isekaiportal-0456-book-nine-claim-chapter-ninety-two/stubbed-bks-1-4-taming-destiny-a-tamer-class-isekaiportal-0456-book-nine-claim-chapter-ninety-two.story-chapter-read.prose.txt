Elarion, would you like some help? Layton asks politely. When they have such an easy way of communicating, simply assuming that his ally is in trouble would be rude.

I’d appreciate it, the Great House heir replies a little stiffly, clearly not all that happy to admit it.

Then this is what I was thinking, Layton starts, then continues by explaining what he had in mind.

Elarion makes some suggestions, as does Josiah – once he’s worked out what they intend – but within a flame, they have the outline of a plan. Just in time – the Tier three Josiah and Layton have been battling has almost broken free of the layers of ice and plant vines they’ve been trying to use to trap it.

“Lay on more vines and ice!” Layton calls, focusing on bringing the temperature down as far as possible even as his cousin concentrates on communicating the orders to all the captains who in turn communicate with the ironshields in charge of the shieldwalls.

They’re fighting a losing battle – Layton is fully aware of that – but delay while their forces move into position is essential. He darts a look over at Elarion’s forces – the farm beast’s bellows have whipped the beasts around them into a frenzy, and they’re attacking Elarion’s people with even less self-preservation than the parasite-ridden beasts are known for.

Josiah, now! he tells his cousin, deciding that this is a good moment to shift to the next stage. The two of them hurry to meet together, Josiah summoning the water and controlling the air currents while Layton manipulates the temperature of it to evaporate and then condense it in an efficient fashion. While the cloud expands larger and larger, Layton finds his eyes drawn to Elarion’s battle, anxiously hoping that they won’t be too late.

Elarion’s forces just don’t have the right composition for the battle they’re currently facing. Almost all the beasts on the left flank have deserted their positions and are surging to attack the artillery unit.

The mages are trying to take up the shortfall of the limited numbers of melee Classers, but many of their more powerful spells are designed to be used at a distance. Layton winces as one magical shell lands too close to the shieldwall. The explosion tears apart beasts and sends the defenders reeling alike.

As for Elarion’s powerful ice-plant guardian, Layton is incredibly impressed with its power and versatility. Watching Markus transform Tier threes from enemies to allies in a moment is mind-boggling at times, but seeing Elarion grow a whole tree in flickers that’s capable of both creating spears that emerge from the ground in unexpected places and sending out volleys of ice shards, seemingly without limit, is even more awe-inspiring.

However, it can’t be everywhere at once, and its ice-spears are increasingly unpredictable because of the Tier three apparently interfering with its root network. Even at this distance Layton can see Elarion’s face is drawn tight with the strain. The Great House heir even confessed that his own attacks are significantly weakened while holding the connection with the guardian – and the connection is necessary unless they wish to risk the guardian accidentally attacking their own.

All of that would be challenging enough. But to add to the complications are the Tier threes themselves – the massive beast that uses a sound-based attack to disorient its foes before it stamps all over them, the smaller farm-beast whose charges risk breaking through the shieldwall at every strike, and the elusive earth-magic-using beast that flows in and out of the ground as if it’s water, striking and then gone.

Layton can only hope that their plan will work. At least, if nothing else, their forces can bolster Elarion’s own.

“It’s ready,” Josiah half-shouts to be heard over the racket of battle. Layton blinks, pulling his attention back to immediate concerns – at least the kind of temperature manipulation he was doing there is something he could do in his sleep.

“Then let’s go,” Layton agrees. Adding just a little more cold, he releases the fog bank entirely to Josiah’s control, then takes off on fleet feet.

His cousin presses the fog with carefully-controlled wind currents towards the bulk of the beasts threatening Elarion’s forces – and where the two biggest Tier threes are too.

Running ahead of the Tier three that’s almost free of the ice and vines that have been keeping it entrapped, Layton slaps it in the face with a wave of burning heat, and then follows it up with a wave of just as burning cold.

The beast snorts, fixing its unfocused eyes on Layton and tossing its head threateningly.

“Come and get me!” Layton challenges, slapping it once again with heat and then freezing cold. The beast bellows, the sound much higher-pitched than the massive beast attacking Elarion, and without any of its ability to affect the beasts surrounding it.

Still, his provocation has done its job. The beast pulls free of all their attempts to entrap it and charges straight for Layton, its antlers lowered threateningly, several of the tines already dripping with blood.

Layton turns and flees as fast as he can, a thrill of what might be either fright or excitement frissoning down his spine.

The Tier three is fast. Too fast. Layton activates his only movement Skill – it draws on his mana and infuses him with warmth, allowing his legs and arms to move faster. The heat grows as he keeps it active – if he holds it for too long, it will cook him from the inside out. However, he also grows faster the warmer he becomes so it is a delicate balance.

Even with Heat Step active, he’s barely faster than the Tier three who’s charging at full speed towards him by this point. Layton doesn’t dare look at anything but the rocky ground beneath his feet – if he places a foot wrong, he’s done for. Though he’s confident his cousin and the rest of their forces are pursuing them, the chances of them being in a position to save him from being ripped to pieces and trampled if he falls are slim.

His path is simple: he just mustn’t fall.

And so he runs, despite the enraged snorting that only grows louder as the Tier three, impossibly, increases in speed and fury the longer its prey evades it.

Finally, Layton feels the wisps of freezing fog against his face. As hot as he’s running, they don’t interfere with him in the slightest. However, they do present something far more relieving – the opportunity to leave this mad race.

Deactivating Heat Transfer, Layton throws himself into a roll down the hill, activating his Stealth Skill as he does so.

The uncontrolled tumble is more than a little painful, but Layton’s armour prevents him from earning anything more than bruises.

He crashes to a halt when he slams into a rock. His head still spinning from the rapid descent – and the faster stop – he listens hard for the sound of pursuit.

Nothing. No pounding hooves rapidly approaching him, ready to trample all over him.

He breathes out the breath he’d unconsciously held.

He credits his Journeyman-level Stealth Skill for the relief – without it, surely the sound of him bouncing off every rock on the way down would have kept the Tier three’s attention.

However, now he is focusing on more than just his immediate situation, he becomes aware of a cacophony even worse than what had previously been sounding. Bellows, cries, screams, explosions…. Layton needs to know what is going on.

Pushing himself to his feet and necking a health potion – perhaps a waste since he had no truly threatening wounds, but the bruises were painful, and limited his movement to an extent – Layton hurries back up the hill.

Just as he crests the top, the freezing fog bank he and Josiah created is blown away – whether by one of their own or one of the magic-using beasts, he doesn’t know. Revealed is a truly satisfying scene.

The Tier three ulan that he and Josiah’s forces had been fighting has driven a bloody swathe through the beasts that had been attacking Elarion’s forces. Layton notes with gleeful smugness that he can still bait an enemy in the perfect direction, even when he’s running for his life.

Because not only has the ulan completely avoided any of Elarion’s forces, but it’s come to a dead stop – with its antlers buried in the farm-beast’s side.

Layton takes a moment to bask in the sense of accomplishment – and relief – that sweeps over him at the observation. I did it.

The impact site is where a good portion of the cacophony is coming from – the ulan is bellowing and tossing its head, trying to free itself while the other Tier three is bellowing and attempting to attack the beast that just struck it.

Unfortunately, with the fog blown away, it seems that the parasite within each has identified that the other is an ally. However, the brief confusion has given Elarion, Layton’s, and Josiah’s combined forces the chance to turn the tide.

A volley of ice, exploding magical grenades, and other powerful area-of-effect spells land right on top of the ulan and the beast it’s still entangled with. Layton looks away as the light becomes too bright to be comfortable.

When he looks back, he sees what looks far more like a mass of blood and bone than living creatures.

Yet, they are alive. At least for now. If the number of other attacks landing on them in quick succession are anything to go by, they won’t be for long. Layton wonders briefly whether they should focus on pinning the beasts down for Markus to deal with, or killing them directly. He guesses it depends on whichever uses fewer resources – their potions are not limitless, nor are their armaments. And Layton himself is already beginning to feel fatigued. He also feels more than a little useless in his current position.

Where do you need me? Layton asks in the Alliance Bond.

We need to deal with that underground beast, Elarion tells him briskly. With it off the battlefield, we will be able to more easily confine the biggest Tier three. Any ideas?

I can probably track it, Layton muses. If it is warm-blooded.

Do so, Elarion orders. And let me know what you find.

Layton sighs a little at the other man’s heavy-handedness, but he doesn't protest – he knows well enough that the middle of a battle isn’t the place for such.

Instead, he finds a nearby boulder that should keep him out of the way of any beasts passing by, jumps to sit on top of it, checks his Stealth is still active, and then closes his eyes.

He doesn’t exactly have a Skill for tracking body heat, but the nature of his Class-cornerstone Temperature Control Skill means that he has to be aware of the temperatures around him. Now at Master, he’s able to focus on specific temperature auras even at a distance from himself.

Finding the Tier three digger is the biggest challenge. There are so many other warm bodies that it is almost impossible to separate one from the other. But the longer Layton spends searching, the more he’s able to tell apart those heat signatures above the ground – and those below.

I have it, he reports to Elarion, keeping his eyes closed, his mind focusing on the warm mass travelling easily through the cool of the earth. I can cool the area around it – or heat it.

Heating and cooling beings directly is challenging – he could do it to a Tier one, maybe even a Tier two, but he doubts he’d succeed with a Tier three.

Neither, Elarion responds. Tell me when it’s likely to emerge from the earth – and where.

Understood, Layton agrees, and keeps a close eye on it. He sees how it swims through the earth, interfering with the bursts of coolness that must be Elarion’s attempts to send out attacks through the roots of his guardian tree.

Finally, he sees it making for the surface.

It’s coming up – to the left of the ulan and other beast, he warns Elarion.

The other doesn’t respond with words, but Layton knows he’s aware.

Opening his eyes as the underground digger emerges, Layton sees a mass of attacks descend on the area all at once. An ice volley from Elarion’s guardian is the least of it – several magical grenades, magical shells, fireballs, force-explosions, and more strike the hapless Tier three all at once.
