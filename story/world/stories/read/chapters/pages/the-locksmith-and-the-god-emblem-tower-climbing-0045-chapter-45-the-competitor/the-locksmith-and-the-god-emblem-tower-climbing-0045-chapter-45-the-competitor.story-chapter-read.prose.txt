Grasping his wrist carefully, Harry, even knowing it wouldn't work, tried to free himself from his handcuffs while following the line. Behind him, Vela and Manes followed, and in front of him, the back of a gentle giant stood, metal sheets covering his body.

The Locksmith wondered if it would be wise to use his Emblem to escape at this moment, but quickly dismissed the thought. Goldman and the others seemed to have reached the same conclusion; they first needed to understand how this floor worked. After the first contact, they were captured by the loop natives and were now following the same one-eyed man.

They walked between the edges of the coliseum. Harry could hear the cries of pain and the cheers of the crowd every time someone broke a bone. I hope I don't have to participate in this... But he had a feeling he wouldn't be able to avoid the fight.

"...You can only take the Locksmith." Daniel argued, at the front of the line. "He's the only one of value here. I tell you the truth!"

"Hey!" Harry yelled, throwing his head to the side in an attempt to spot him. "What the hell is this, Daniel?"

"It's-It's just a joke... hahaha..." He laughed awkwardly when he realized Harry had heard. "Of course I lied... Your value is almost negative."

Of course... Harry ignored him while trying to understand where they were going. After a few more minutes of walking under the roof of the coliseum's edges, they began to descend the stairs. The Locksmith looked to the side, confused. The guards, frowning, simply pointed and pushed Harry until he went down.

"Okay, okay!" he exclaimed, hurrying to follow Goldman, who was a few steps below.

As he ventured into the darkness, a flood of questions inundated his mind. How could his brother be in the Tower? Besides, the commander was also there... And yet he arrived before me in the Tower's timeline. How long has Matthews been waiting for me? And if it were anyone else, Harry would feel embarrassed to have such egotistical thoughts. Since it was the general, however, the Locksmith knew that was the likely reason.

Suddenly, a sea of ​​lights enveloped the group. They were now in a large room, vaguely resembling the aesthetics of a courtroom. A trial beneath the Colosseum? The young thief added inwardly as he looked at the upturned noses of the jurors and the unnecessarily pompous haircut of the supposed judge.

"...What a scoundrel!" one of the girls said, looking down with disgust. "Surely he must work with his hands...!"

Under normal circumstances, Harry would have ignored the comment, but something about the intonation and the way the phrase was delivered... It was almost as if it came from a noble. But it couldn't be; nobles wouldn't have a reason to come to the Tower... Would they?

The Locksmith looked closely at the girl who seemed to be mocking his position as a prisoner. What caught the young thief's attention, however, was the lack of other noble mannerisms. Furthermore, things like the dirt under her fingernails and her sandy hair betrayed this supposed royal confidence. Her clothes? They looked like they'd come straight from the earth. Certainly, like everyone else in the loop, she still wore her noble clothes, but her attitude no longer matched the rest of her appearance. Or perhaps it matched too well.

"Don't be offended by little things," Vela said as he pushed Harry forward. "Now let's go, Mr. Locksmith."

Positioned in the middle of the circle in a row, the group looked up. In the privileged spot, a female figure scrutinized them with her steady, sharp gaze. After excruciatingly long seconds, she spoke.

"New competitors?" She seemed to question the group's appearance to one of the guards.

"...No, ma'am. They are, um... That's the Locksmith." And Harry could perceive a slight change in the judge's gaze in the group. He didn't know if that would be a good thing, however.

"Harry Bockart." She declared. "Step forward and pay for your sins."

Reluctantly, the young thief followed her orders while trying to appear determined.

"Well, I must say I'm surprised to find that you actually managed to get this far. It's good to put a face to the name, you know." She said, laughing to herself.

Okay... That's more casual. I don't think I'm going to die.

"Um..." Harry searched for words while giving a nonchalant smile. "Your Honor, could I ask you the reason for... well, the reason we're still alive? And how do you know my name?"

"...Has it been that long?" She replied, almost as if offended. "Seriously, you don't remember me, dear?"

The young thief then felt embarrassed under the gaze of his group members, especially Madeline's. "...Did we meet in some bar? I'm sorry, I'm bad with names."

"Great Salesville." The judge said succinctly, as if that were answer enough. For Harry, however, it was. "Do you remember that name?"

Shit... Harry added inwardly as a tide of good memories washed over him. He remembered all the robberies he committed at his family's mansion and how he brought nothing but humiliation to Madam Jules, the family heiress. Well, if you took the blonde out of her hair and made it less curly, and maybe added some red lipstick and a good bath, she'd even look like Jules...

"...Madam Jules?" Harry said with an embarrassed smile.

"Don't you dare call me that!" She stood up. "For you, I am the judge. In this place, you only call me Your Honor! Understood?"

...Are we roleplaying now?

"Y-Yes... Your Honor..." The young thief said, suppressing a laugh. "And well, if I may ask: What are you doing here, Ju—Your Honor!? As far as I know, nobles don't seem to be the kind of clientele the Tower likes to house. Perhaps you've found some value in illegal activity after all?"

"Not at all!" Jules humphed. "This place is our salvation! It's where we're hiding until the world recovers again, isn't it, folks!?"

The nobles around the room seemed to agree. Harry took the opportunity to look back only to see the confused faces of Madeline and Daniel, who seemed to be analyzing each other's clothing with his gaze perpetually fixed in a frown.

"Excuse me, but salvation? The Tower is a prison, Your Honor. I don't understand."

"And you're not supposed to!" She retorted, sitting back down in her seat. "What we need to deal with now is the sentencing of your group, and especially yours."

"The Tower of Logos is our sentence!" Harry said, raising his voice. "I mean, that's why we were sent here, isn't it? Besides, we're not in New Cavebrook anymore. To tell the truth, we're nowhere! There's no jurisdiction to apply to in a place that ignores even the laws of time itself! Tell me, Jules, where are these orders coming from?"

"From me." A hoarse, grainy voice came from the shadows. Beneath Madam Jules's seat, a sturdy man with leather goggles, a cloth mask over his mouth, and a hood took a step forward. His clothes reminded Harry of the nomads and adventurers he occasionally met on his journeys across the continent. Not a single patch of skin was showing on the man's body, who wore black gloves and a dark green scarf.

Despite wanting to forget where he knew that voice from, Harry couldn't. Suppressing the urge to take a step back or even run away, he raised his head to see the approaching man.

"...Matthews," Harry said. "Long time no see."

The man did not answer.

"Since the general has been a great help from the beginning," the judge continued. "Besides providing us with great entertainment in the games, we give him some control over certain things. The general is excellent at controlling prisoners and ensuring the safety of our little piece of paradise. I'm sure it will be no different with you... Therefore, we have promised him total control and ownership over Harry Bockart and any members who were with him when he arrived. In my view, it would be easier to just kill you all, since he wasted a lot of time on you when we were still on the old continent. From that point of view, I think you can understand; he wants to play a little. In any case, he is all yours, general."

Matthews shuddered when the woman emphasized the waste time searching for the Locksmith, but did not complain.

The air in the room seemed to thicken as soon as she finished speaking. Her pomp, along with her presence, had been devoured by Matthews, who was approaching Harry. Despite this, the man had neither disrespected nor interrupted the woman while she spoke. According to the judge, he was under control, and that was something they had promised the man, but everyone there knew that general could kill them all before he could complain of a headache.

Does he still serve them? Harry thought to himself, still surprised by his brother's appearance, but even more incredulous at his actions. Well, we can fix this.

The young thief then took a step forward, causing everyone to hold their breath. In front of Harry, Matthews stood like a rock in the middle of the ocean. Unyielding. Permanent. Almost as if he were part of the scenery. Despite being the older brother, Harry looked like a dried plum compared to his younger brother's physique, who had two Emblems. At least he didn't mention that we're related... I doubt anything good would come of this.

Matthews clenched his fists. This was what he had always hoped for. This was the moment he had been fighting for his whole life. He looked at Harry and saw his relaxed smile, just like when they were little. His hair was long, reaching his shoulders. He always said he wanted to grow it long, but Mum never let him. Guess it's another thing he still doesn't care about, huh? He saw his pretentious suit and black boots. His black cloak gave him a roguish air, but still made him charming. Even his scruffy beard made him look like a low-life merchant. Still, he was his brother. He recognized him in his voice and his gaze. Still a jerk.

The Locksmith took another step forward, almost touching Matthews.

Should I attack him from the right? Or should I— Matthews' thoughts were erased by what had just happened. Around his cold, dense bones, he felt a presence gripping him. It was a different kind of warmth. He looked down and saw his brother's hair brushing against his chest.

"You didn't have to come for me, little guy." Hearing this, Matthews suppressed a sudden urge to rip Harry's ears off right there. Instead, he sighed deeply and remained silent.

"You haven't changed one bit," Matthews said.

"Thank you!"

"...It isn't a compliment. It's sad."

"...Harry," Goldman was the first to ask. "What's going on here? Who is this guy?"

The Locksmith then detached himself from his brother and turned to his group, seeing the perplexed looks on each of their faces. Putting an arm around Matthews' shoulders, Harry pointed to the figure with the other.

"I thought the resemblance was obvious," Harry said, smiling. "This is Matthews, my little brother."
