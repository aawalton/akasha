Brace yourselves for a chapter-length dump on my thoughts & proposed solutions XDFundamentally, I feel there are two primary issues:1) The Fateweavers fall flat as the series' primary antagonist and2) The ending could use more polish and explanation. Specifically wrt Vir's plan, how he ends up being able to defend his universe from the Fateweavers, Tia's characterization, and some other things.Re: the Fateweavers,

From well before I wrote chapter 1, I had the Fateweavers planned in my head as an unknowable foe whose alien-ness and near-omniscience instilled the same sort of fear one has with a magnitude 9 earthquake, or being trapped in a burning building or seeing a tornado approaching you.

It doesn't care about you. It's not good or evil. But it's entirely unavoidable, and you know you will die. There is no escaping it.

Fundamentally, Fateweavers leverage the butterfly effect. Ensure a person is in the right place at the right time, and 20 years later, that event has shaped their life view such that they act in a certain way as to completely change the outcome of a war that forever alters the face of the realm.

In my head, this is absolutely terrifying. Precisely because it has no agenda, it's more terrifying than any big bad could ever be. Or that was the hope. I think that has not quite landed.

Writing an enemy this complicated has not been easy. I've spent many hours pondering how they would function. What they would actually impact, and how that plays out through the story. It would have been so much easier to write some cosmic big bad readers hate. Someone Vir could satisfyingly punch or cut to death.

I didn't because that didn't feel like it fit the story, and because such an antagonist is just not that interesting to me. I'd rather shoot for the stars and come short than to play it safe & do what everyone else has done, because that's where the joy of the art lies for me.

On a related note, one issue that has plagued Ashborn since the beginning is the subtlety of the foreshadowing. Examples:- Fate has been spelled with a capital F from the prologue of b1 in reference to the extreme importance the concept plays in the series

- In b1 when Vir ventures below Daha to the Imperium outpost where Janak's avatar speaks with him, the avatar mentions the Fateweavers. (Iirc he calls them they)

- In b2, Maiya meets his Avatar after venturing deep into the sewers of Sai, where he expands upon this, telling her of the existence of beings who control Fate.

- As part of her ascension as the Blessed Chosen, Maiya realizes just how much they affect fate. (This is B2, still)

- In B3, Ashani's introduction to Vir announces herself as a Fateweaver agent (obscured by Old English as it was, I'm guessing few really understood the ramification of that)

- In B4 & B5, we see Fateweaver strategies foiled by Janak's actions, only to be countered by yet more Fateweaver reactions. All of this happens in the background.- In those same books, Vir delves into Janak's primordial chambers and continues to uncover the nature of this mysterious enemy. At least, so far as anyone knows.

There are many such examples. Basically, the Fateweavers have been present in the story since the beginning, and their actions have impacted it all along, and yet those events were a) so long ago and b) too subtle and so indirectly impactful that they were easily forgotten. Which makes this ending seem random & out of left field rather than something planned from the very beginning, which is tragic because it was.Ideally I'd go back and update those early book references, but the ship's sailed there, sadly.

Alright, that's my braindump. If I'm completely honest, juggling this many plot threads and weaving them into a cohesive ending is anything but simple. The story inevitably diverges from the initial outline, forcing updates & revisions, and at the end of the day, I'm only human. Did I create this problem? Absolutely, but I don't regret trying to tackle a hard problem and perhaps coming up short.
