I sprinted through the train cars, using [Spacefold] to bypass entire sections when I could. The interior was a mass of shards of corpses, broken windows, and dried blood caked across the walls from whatever carnage had happened here a year ago.

I tried to ignore it as best I could. The level of realism the Cataclysm went to to copy the real world was nauseating at times. At least, I sincerely hoped it was a copy.

I arrived at the front car to find pretty much exactly what I’d feared.

The tunnel ahead was packed with shamblers. Not a handful here and there, but nearly a hundred, just milling about.

Just our luck that they’d somehow managed to congregate in this particular section of the metro. The train’s headlights illuminated their rotting bodies as they turned toward us, screaming for blood.

“Yeah, I see them,” I said, moving to the shattered front window. “How fast are we going?”

“Forty kilometers per hour,” Aerion called back from the conductor’s compartment. “Any faster and we risk derailing if we hit too many at once. Any slower, and…”

“The horde behind us catches up. I know.”

We were threading a needle here, trying to go just fast enough to stay ahead of the zombies we’d attracted behind us, but slow enough not to derail from the ones ahead.

I cracked my neck. Time to earn my keep.

I [Launched] out my flying swords through a broken smaller window just to the side of the main windshield, letting them do their thing.

And then I pulled out the chain whip. The weapon practically vibrated with anticipation, which honestly scared me more than I cared to admit. After its feeding frenzy at the station, the thing seemed to have developed a taste for zombie flesh. I could almost feel its hunger come through our connection.

“Well,” I muttered. “There’s plenty of food ahead.”

The thing seemed to wriggle in excitement, but I really hoped that was just me reading into things.

I let it extend to its full thirty-foot length, thankful I’d made the weapon as long as I had. The moment the first link made contact with a zombie, [Buzzsaw] activated.

Like before, the hypersonic vibration turned every edge into a spinning blade of death. The chain carved through the zombie horde like a lawnmower, spewing geysers of chopped gore everywhere.

The whip joined the blades in doing their own thing without any input from me, seeking out targets according to whatever intelligence it had thanks to the dozens of souls embedded in the links.

It would strike a zombie’s neck, sever the head, then curve mid-strike to bisect the one next to it before coiling back to crush a third’s skull.

It was a literal machine of death, but there were just too many enemies.

For every zombie the whip killed, there were three more that appeared out of the darkness. Thankfully, whatever chunks the whip did chop up were small enough for the train to handle.

It plowed through the bodies with a sickening crunch, like a car running over roadkill.

Needless to say, the windshield was painted black with zombie ink. Thankfully, the designers had thought to install windshield wipers on the lead car, or we’d have been flying blind. Why subway trains needed wipers, I didn’t know, but whoever came up with that idea deserved a medal.

“We’re slowing down!” Aerion shouted. “Too many bodies on the tracks!”

I watched grimly as the speedometer dropped to thirty kilometers per hour, then twenty-five.

[Soulwoven Voidsteel Alloy Chain Whip of the Damned]’s ability [Buzzsaw] has leveled up! [Buzzsaw] is now Foundation-5.

The whip was getting stronger, sure, as were my flying blades, but that was of little consolation. We were down to twenty kilometers per hour and still slowing. Unfortunately, Aural Siege Bolts were out, considering how there wasn’t enough space to make it safe. Even if there was, they’d also take out the tracks ahead of us, not to mention the walls and ceiling of the tunnel too, dooming our plan.

“Greg,” Yashas’s voice crackled through the train’s intercom. “I’m starting the sweep train now. Two minutes until it reaches your position. You need to maintain speed or the timing will not work.”

“Easy for you to say…” I muttered. “Sorry, Aer, but we’re gonna have to lose the windshield. Duck if you have to.”

“Understood,” Aerion replied, already crouching, no doubt anticipating what I was about to try.

I extended my other hand, launching Soulwoven shards from my inventory. The bullets punched through the windshield like it wasn’t even there, pummeling zombie skulls with varying degrees of accuracy. Manually aiming at moving zombies while moving ourselves wasn’t exactly the easiest thing in the world, but that didn’t matter too much.

The instant after I fired them, I recalled them to my inventory and fired again. Between the whip and the shards, I was creating a killing field, but it really did feel like trying to empty an ocean with a bucket.

Fifteen kilometers per hour.

The zombies behind us were catching up. I could see them in the rear camera feed that Aerion had pulled up on a monitor—a solid wall of undead sprinting after the train. Some even managed to get on, which we’d have to deal with later.

“Come on, come on,” I muttered, firing my shards as fast as I possibly could, thanking Cosmo that the normal Essence constraints didn’t apply to my powerset. I’d have run dry long ago with how much I was abusing my abilities.

“Thirty seconds!” Yashas announced.

It was funny. Half a minute was practically nothing in everyday life, but right now? It might as well have been ten hours.

The tunnel ahead was still packed. The whip was carving a path, but not fast enough.

Fast enough?

An idea hit me, and it was so obvious I didn’t know why I hadn’t thought about it before. Well, I knew why.

It was a question of trust.

My whip… didn’t need physical contact. I’d been hanging onto the thing for dear life, but it really didn’t need me to. My flying blades were already out there, slicing and dicing on their own.

I grabbed the radio and punched the button to transmit. “Yashas, slow the train behind us down. I’ve got an idea.”

“Are you certain?” Yashas asked.

“Positive. I’m gonna slow us down so the zombies ahead aren’t a problem, and I’ll deal with the ones following behind us, too.”

“I’m unsure how, but alright,” Yashas responded. “Slowing your tail to ten kilometers an hour. Is that sufficient?”

I glanced at Aerion, who nodded.

“Perfect.”

“Aerion?” I said, setting the radio down. “Slow us to ten.”

Her hands were already dialing in the changes. “What will you do?”

“I’m heading to the back to cull our tails. If we can’t go faster, we just need to thin out the ones behind us so we can go slower.”

Aerion’s eyes lit up in understanding, and she smirked. “I suppose I should stop being surprised at your capacity for creating crazy, last-minute schemes.”

“Hey, it’s me!” I said, planting a kiss on her lips… right as a piece of zombie gore flew through the broken windshield, splattering us both.

I folded space on my way to the rear of the train, taking out the not-inconsiderable number of zombies who’d managed to hop on along the way. Light of the Fearless came out for the first time in a while, and it felt good to be chopping bad guys up with my own hands again.

Upon reaching the rear, what I found wasn’t so much a horde, but rather a wall of zombies who were trying to clamber onto the train, with many failing and falling off by the wayside.

Ten kilometers an hour wasn’t exactly fast, though, so there were many more to replace them.

I extended an arm and rapid-launched my bullets, cycling them back into my inventory in a continuous loop.

I’d gotten faster, too, resembling the output an old hand-crank Gatling gun might have. At least, when I wasn’t bothering to aim. With so many targets, I hardly needed to.

Slowly, I beat back the tide. It was easier now, with the train running away from the pursuers rather than at them. I’d never win this way, but I didn’t need to.

“Ten seconds!” Aerion called out over the train’s PA system. “Yashas is accelerating the other train!”

“Yeah, I can see that,” I muttered, already turning to bolt back to Aerion’s position.

I heard the other train barreling down the tunnel behind us before I saw its lights. It was moving at full speed.

“We’re at the marker!” I heard Yashas’s voice announce as I used [Spacefold] to get to the lead car. “Get out now!”

I didn’t even stop as I touched Aerion, pulling her into my inventory the moment she slammed on the brakes, folding space again out the front windshield onto the tracks ahead.

I recalled my flying swords and my whip, which to my alarm actually resisted, not wanting to stop feeding. Luckily, I managed to grab a hold of its handle, allowing me to suck it into my inventory with a sigh of relief.

I’d have to deal with that later. I had no use for weapons I couldn’t control.

I folded space again, jumping onto the platform of Nihombashi Station, bringing Aerion back out just as the train slammed to a stop a few dozen feet behind.

For a brief second, that was it.

Then the sweeper train collided with the one we’d just been in. Even at the other end, far away from the actual impact, the sound was apocalyptic.

I’d never known metal could scream, but in that moment, that was exactly what it sounded like as train cars crumpled in the tunnel beyond, the wet crunch of countless zombies being pulverized between two multi-ton trains only adding to the fucked-upness.

The impact sent shockwaves through the entire station, and the platform vibrated beneath our feet as the collision created a pressure wave that exploded outward from the tunnel.

I pulled Aerion down behind a concrete support pillar as metal debris flew from the tunnel entrance like a grenade.

The air quickly filled with a noxious cloud of iron and ozone, the stench so overwhelming that even my enhanced stats couldn’t keep me from gagging.

“Holy shit,” I breathed.

When the dust finally settled, the tunnel was silent except for the hiss of ruptured hydraulics and the sounds of twisted metal.

Where there had been thousands of zombies, there was now just… well, I didn’t want to imagine what. A gruesome smear of organic matter, probably.

The silence was soon broken by Yashas’s voice over the station’s PA system. “Outstanding work, you two. My conservative estimate is around four thousand zombies eliminated or diverted from the Asakusa approach. You should be clear to head north for the next phase.”

Aerion leaned against me, breathing hard. Both her armor and her face were splattered with gore, and I imagined I didn’t look all that different. “That was insane.”

“But effective!” I corrected, though my hands were shaking slightly from the adrenaline dump.

She laughed. “I suppose both can be true.”

After taking a few moments to collect ourselves, we began the arduous process of repeating the harrowing feat two more times over the next several hours.

The second run went smoother. We’d refined the timing, and I’d gotten better at anticipating the whip’s… tendencies. And though I managed to recall it every time, it was getting harder and harder as it tried to avoid my attempts to grab it.

It hadn’t actively attacked me or Aerion yet, but this couldn’t go on. I’d have to deal with that, and soon.

Luckily, its continued leveling meant it was better and better able to operate autonomously.

Sadly, the same could not be said for my Voidsteel Alloy Shards. While their ability, [Frag Out], was exceedingly effective as a frag grenade, against zombies, they didn’t do a whole lot.

Not to mention activating the ability destroyed the shard in the process. Which was why the ability sat at F-0.

Luckily, both the whip and the flying swords compensated, making me level to C-8, with [Buzzsaw] hitting F-9 by the end of that run and my Soulwoven Mythril Shortswords’ [Blade Projection] abilities hitting D-5. They’d gained length, width, and sharpness on their rank up, cutting through zombies like lasers when they spun.

After that second run, both Aerion and I were feeling optimistic about finishing the last two.

The third, though, was anything but easy.

The zombie density increased exponentially the further north we went. We’d known that, of course, but these weren’t random shamblers wandering the tunnels. These were coordinated hordes following the authority of a commander.

One of the ones we’d dealt with at Tokyo Tower.

I supposed it was just a matter of time, but that made everything harder.

Aerion had to slow the train to a crawl, and for a few terrifying minutes, I thought we might actually get overwhelmed.

Then [Buzzsaw] ranked up and all hell broke loose.

The whip’s vibrations became so intense that they created a visible distortion in the air. Zombies no longer even had to touch the chain to get cut. Just getting close sent them exploding into a fine mist of gore.

Moreover, it began to work together with the flying blades, carving through the zombies like a squad—a squad of flying weapons. And it might just have been me, but it felt like the other blades diverted and attacked where it wanted them to.

Despite my reservations with that weapon, it was the only reason we made it through the third run.

By the time we’d crashed the trains, I was exhausted, and both Aerion and I were covered head to toe in zombie residue that had somehow gotten everywhere.

But we’d done it.

“How are we looking?” I said, collapsing onto a bench at Ueno Station. My legs felt like jelly from all that running around, and my arms ached from wielding Light of the Fearless. My Grace suit had gotten us through, but the lack of Vigor really showed.

Still, I’d picked up another level, ascending to C-9, while [Buzzsaw] was now D-2 and [Blade Projection] hit D-7.

“Quite solid,” Yashas reported through the station PA. “The majority of the route to Asakusa is clear. With one more run, you’ll get there. I’m just sorry to say it won’t be tonight.”

“Ah, fuck,” I muttered, glancing at a clock on a nearby wall.

“Greg, Aerion,” Yashas said, his voice sounding serious. “You need to find shelter outside immediately. Once full darkness falls, the zombies will enter their aggressive phase. You do not want to be in the tunnels when that happens.”

He was right, and we both knew it. During the day, the shamblers were slow and had stunted senses. Fighting the upgraded versions in the depths of the subway would be suicide, even with all our recent upgrades.

I brought out a piece of paper and a pen from my inventory and scribbled on it, holding it up to the camera.

It read: Is Akiba clear?

“Akihabara?” Yashas asked. “Yes, it was one of the stations on the route you cleared earlier.”

A smile spread across my face despite my exhaustion. “Good. Very good.”

Aerion gave me a look that oozed suspicion. “Why are you smiling like that?”

“Well, for one, because we need to get topside anyway, but I want to take a little excursion,” I said, exiting my Grace suit for my Vigor build, if only to mask the aches and pains. “Call it a hunch. Er, after showers, of course,” I added, seeing Aerion’s unamused glare.
