Kroult stumbled as he exited the rift, falling to his knees beside Asher as the others stared at him. Other than the soulsteel short sword currently lodged deep within the undead’s chest, he wore nothing more than some ragged shorts. That also meant the hundreds of deep puncture wounds inflicted all over his body from the spike-filled coffin he had been trapped within were quite visible.

“Realms…” Moxy frowned, looking more serious than Asher had ever seen her as she stared at the half-fallen lich. “I actually feel bad for this thing.”

“He is still a person,” Rulfar muttered, peering curiously at the undead as he sniffed the air. His Bear element had a powerful Scent skill that allowed the bloodhound to focus on and learn more from smells than anyone else, and he grimaced as he shook his head. “...Though he is in desperate need of a shower.”

“I have a simple cantrip to deal with the smell,” the lich croaked, causing all of them to flinch at the unexpected voice. Kroult sounded as though he were on his deathbed, his words just as shaky and feeble as the green fires dancing in his eye sockets. “It was part of how I passed as a human during my travels. Back before… before I was trapped.”

Despite not actually needing to breathe, Kroult took one long, shaky breath, tilting his head down to stare at the soulsteel sword lodged in his chest. Touching a finger gently against the blade, he sighed. “When you freed me from my prison, I felt a flame of vengeance ignite within my soul that I thought had died out centuries ago. But just as quickly as it sparked, it burned out once more. While the fire rages within me, the fuel is simply not there. I… I am tired. So, very tired…”

Asher frowned as he observed Kroult in his current state. He’d hoped they may have uncovered a secret weapon to use against the Council of Death and maybe even Zygrul that the lich hadn’t expected them to utilize. But listening to Kroult speak… Asher realized Kroult wasn’t some sort of magical, secret weapon. He was just an old man who had lost the will to fight at some point during his centuries trapped within the earth.

“How many people have you killed?” Samantha asked, her voice hard as she stared the lich down. Her expression hadn’t shifted in the slightest in response to the lich’s words, and she held her bow and arrow in hand, as if prepared to shoot the undead between the eyes the moment she heard an answer she didn’t like.

“By my own hand… forty-eight,” Kroult said, the fires in his eye sockets dimming to little more than embers as if he were closing them. “Some I killed because they attacked me first. Others because they attempted to go back on deals we had struck. A few were in order to save the lives of others. I do not regret taking any of their lives. I would do it again.”

Hell, assuming he’s telling the truth, I’ve killed more people than that… Asher thought, shaking his head at the realization.

“And not by your hand?” Samantha asked, her eyes narrowing as she picked up on the addition to his answer. “How many people then?”

“I do not know,” Kroult sighed. “My life was spent traveling the lands as a wandering hedge wizard. Few are able to achieve the class and learn to harness their tritessence in this way, but I found a talented master who apparently saw something within me. I spent my days granting people temporary skills and assisting where I could in exchange for food and shelter. Who is to say the Woodsman I granted a boost of strength to didn’t instead use that power to behead his enemy rather than fell a large tree he had been working on. Or the young Seamstress I enchanted a needle for to increase her control over it did not then drive it into another’s heart. I did not watch over what was done with my boons. I simply traded them to survive.”

“Why did you become a lich in the first place?” Rulfar asked, looking curiously at the undead. “Assuming you’re telling the truth, that kind of life story doesn’t sound like one that ends with lichdom.”

“It was never my intention,” Kroult admitted, getting surprised looks out of all of them. “My master was the one who secretly feared death. He never let it show, and over the years, he worked to come up with a way to stave off death’s creeping embrace. While I aided townsfolk with my cantrips and earned us safe passage across the land, he focused on his own project, unbeknownst to me. Eventually, after growing too old, he tried to enact the ritual and become a lich. I… I stopped him. And unintentionally took his place.”

“That can happen?” Moxy asked, leaning back on her log and grinning as if she were watching a play being acted out for her own personal entertainment. “Didn’t see that coming! How did you stop him?”

“I have answered your questions because I am too tired to fight, and I appreciate being freed from my prison. But I do not wish to discuss my master any further,” Kroult said, laying a hand on the soulsteel sword in his chest as he bowed his head. “Please. Just kill me and get this over with. Once I have reformed at my phylactery, I can work on destroying it and putting my soul to rest for good. I am tired and wish to rest.”

“That won’t work,” Asher said as he watched Samantha, noting how she looked like she was struggling with her own thoughts as she stared at the bowed lich before them. “Zygrul took all the phylacteries that had been put into its hands to create the element of Eternity and has fled to the void realm. I don’t know why it hasn’t just destroyed them itself, but I’m pretty certain if you or any of the other liches perish you’ll manifest in the void. And I doubt you’ll survive long. Unless you want to be trapped in an eternal loop of reforming just to be destroyed again, I wouldn’t recommend dying.”

Of course, Asher was leaving out a rather important piece of information. If he were the one to kill Kroult, the lich would in fact die for good. Sever the Thread was the perfect skill for hunting liches. The element of Eternity that the Council had spent so long getting to form had ironically become the strongest weapon to use against them.

“The void?” Kroult repeated, the flames in his eyes flickering back to life as he turned to look up at Asher with a grimace. “You speak the truth? How is Zygrul able to survive in there?”

“Zygrul has a Void element,” Rulfar admitted, causing the green flames of Kroult’s eyes to flare brightly in what Asher figured had to be shock.

“I don’t understand,” Samantha said, getting to her feet as she stood before the kneeling lich. “Even if you hadn’t intended to become a lich, you still joined the Council of Death. Why?”

“My master had found a way to contact them. I believe they were the key to him perfecting the ritual he’d been working on all those years,” Kroult explained, his voice hesitant as if he didn’t know what to believe anymore after hearing that the Void element wasn’t just real, but was in the very capable hands of a deadly lich. “One of them was waiting for the ritual to complete. When I emerged instead of him… they did not care. They told me I would have to become a member in his stead and informed me they would be in touch when I was needed. After that, not much changed. I disguised myself and continued wandering the lands for a few decades, able to focus more on my magic as I did not need to worry about such things as eating or sleeping any longer. Then one day, they returned. They claimed they needed my phylactery, only… I did not wish to give it to them. I assume you can figure out what happened from there.”

“Wait, you don’t even know why they wanted your phylactery?” Asher asked.

“No. My phylactery is my master’s spell book. The very book responsible for allowing me to become anything more than a common street beggar. It was precious to me and I continued adding to it after my transformation, meaning it contained my entire life’s work, before and after my death. When they came for it, I refused to hand it over. Or at least, I tried to refuse.” Kroult sighed, shaking his head as he looked between the four of them. “...I do not know what to do. If what you claim about my phylactery being in the void is true, I cannot die, lest I be trapped within an even more horrifying situation than the one you rescued me from. Yet at the same time, I am weary and do not wish to go on any further. Death is a blessing I had not even intentionally tried to avoid in the first place, and I wish to be welcomed into its cold embrace.”

“Can we have a team meeting?” Samantha asked, her face still a mixture of conflicting emotions as she looked pleadingly toward Asher. “Please?”

“That sounds like a good idea,” Asher said, motioning for the others to follow him. “Kroult, stay there and don’t try to take that sword out of your chest. We’ll be right back.”

Leaving the lich kneeling before the fire, the four of them stepped only a few feet away. They didn’t have to go far thanks to Spatial Lock’s ability to stop sound from escaping its barrier, and the moment the skill was put into place, Moxy whistled.

“Realms, someone needs to take this guy out for a night on the town and get him drunk or something. He’s depressing as hell!”

“Moxy,” Samantha said, giving the warrior a serious look as she shook her head. “This is no joke. We need to figure out what to do about Kroult, and I think we need to all be in agreement. Asher, I’m assuming based on the fact you didn’t tell him about Sever the Thread that you still want to use him?”

“Yeah,” Asher admitted sheepishly, now feeling a bit worse about the idea after hearing how much the lich just wanted to embrace his long-overdue death. “It might not be all that fair to him, but we need every advantage we can get, and this is the first time Zygrul’s ever made a mistake. Did you guys catch the discrepancy between the notes Zygrul left us and Kroult’s story? I’m pretty certain the lich who was there to greet Kroult when he turned didn’t even bother informing the rest of the Council that it was Kroult who became a lich instead of his master. They were probably just trying to add to their number in preparation for the plan to manifest the element of Eternity.”

“More phylacteries means less time needed for the element to manifest,” Rulfar nodded slowly. “Zygrul must have figured Kroult’s master, a man with a soul twisted enough to cast off his humanity to escape death, would attack us the moment he was free and we’d kill him. Or that we’d kill him before he even got the chance to do anything. Zygrul didn’t realize it wasn’t Kroult’s master within that tomb but Kroult himself.”

“So we’re finally one step ahead of that damn lich,” Moxy said, scratching her chin thoughtfully as she stared at the undead’s back. “Can’t say I ever expected to fight alongside a zombie, but I’m willing to try anything once. You think he can still put that strength boost on people like he talked about?”

“I know you don’t like the idea of keeping him around, but this is an opportunity we can’t just pass up,” Asher said, taking Samantha’s hands in his own and giving her a reassuring smile. “I promise, it’s not forever. But we need every advantage we can get if we want to take the Council down. And it sounds like Kroult really isn’t a bad guy, all things considered.”

“I know,” Samantha said, the fight leaving her eyes as she sighed and deflated slightly. “We need to verify what he’s saying is true, but assuming it is, I can’t even imagine what he’s gone through. Losing his master, accidentally becoming an undead monster, and then being trapped in that coffin for six hundred years… honestly I just feel sorry for him.”

“I think you should be straight with him,” Moxy suggested. “We’re not going to be able to hide your Sever the Thread skill. The moment he sees us kill another lich, he’s going to know what’s up. Keep it simple. He helps us take down the people who sealed him away for a few centuries, we help him die. Seems like a fair trade to me.”

“I can’t believe I’m saying this, but I agree with Moxy,” Rulfar said, ignoring the warrior’s giant grin as he peered at Kroult’s back. “It’s a win-win for all of us. Our team covers a lot of different angles, but having an actual wizard with us would be massive. Even if he is only a hedge wizard.”

“I guess that settles it,” Asher said, gazing at the back of who would hopefully be their new temporary teammate if he agreed to their demands. “Kroult helps us for a bit, and then I kill him. Time to give what might very well be the strangest job offer in the entire world.”
