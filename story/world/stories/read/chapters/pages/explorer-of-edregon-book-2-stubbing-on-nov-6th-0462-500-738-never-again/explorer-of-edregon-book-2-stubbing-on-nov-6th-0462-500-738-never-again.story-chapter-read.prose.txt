“Damn it, Ovord!” Vin shouted into the void, nervously counting down the seconds until the minute was up. Having relinquished control of his body to Alka dozens of times in the past, he thought he’d known what to expect when triggering the active portion of his title. But unlike with Alka, where he was still able to watch out of his own eyes and witness what was going on, when he’d triggered his title this time around he’d instantly found himself trapped within his own mana pool. Rather than seeing and hearing what was going on with the neilans, Vin witnessed nothing but darkness.

It’s alright. Ovord is smart. He wouldn’t go on a killing spree just because he could, right? He didn’t seem like that kind of guy. Common Ally might not be able to tell me his race, but I got the sense that his people valued their word quite highly. He swore he’d help.

Even with all that running through his mind, Vin continued shouting threats into the void, mad mainly at himself for trusting in the soul mage. After besting Ovord and promising to try to get him a new body, the two of them had been getting along swimmingly. Ovord had been helping him with his magic and offering him excellent advice, and Vin had stupidly started to trust the mage, even after their lengthy battle over his own body. If he’d somehow managed to find a way to make the one-minute timer permanent…

Thankfully, Vin didn’t have to follow that train of thought, as almost without warning he snapped back into control of his body. Blinking, Vin looked around the room, taking in the three neilans sitting calmly across the table from him. The chairs were in slightly different positions, as though everyone had stood up and sat back down again for some reason, but other than that, everything appeared normal.

“Is everything okay?” the lead neilan asked, giving him a look of genuine concern. “You look troubled.”

“I’m… fine…” Vin said slowly, almost more confused at the fact that one of the neilans of all people was asking about his health. These were the people who had scoffed when informed that their actions had wiped out an entire race of people, doing little more than shrugging and stating that it didn’t matter because they were going to die sooner or later anyway. “…Are you three alright?”

“We are better than ever,” the leader smiled, and the two neilans behind him nodded in earnest. “Back to the matter at hand. We would love the chance to assist your people. There is no need to worry about the ritualistic contract we spoke of earlier. The three of us will personally see to it that what occurred with your previous wave of missing Earthers does not occur again.”

“Oh. Okay. That’s good,” Vin said, still reeling from finding everything not just alive and well, but strangely on board with helping all of a sudden. “Could you three work out the details with Theodore and the others? I need to have a quick chat with… myself.”

“Of course,” the neilan said, placing a fist against his chest in an oddly familiar gesture that Vin couldn’t quite place before taking his leave. As soon as the door was shut behind him, Vin wasted no time before slipping back into Introspection.

“What did you do?” he demanded, crossing his arms as he stared at the innocent-looking soul mage standing before him.

“Exactly what I told you I would,” Ovord said, his face an expressionless mask. “You required their help, and they were refusing to do so. Not only that, but they were contemplating taking advantage of your desperation to set up a grand-scale ritual to wipe your people from the face of Edregon.”

“They were?” Vin asked, startled by the news. He’d been prepared to tear into Ovord for what he’d done, but the knowledge that the neilans had been secretly thinking about killing everyone within Terra simply because they were annoyed took the wind right out of his sails. “I knew the neilans were bad people, but that… Doesn’t that seem a bit extreme?”

“I could see it in their souls,” Ovord said, his face turning dark for a moment before he collected himself. “I did as I told you I would. Yet in order to do so, I was forced to shunt you into your own mana pool, even if only for a minute. For that, you have my sincerest apologies. As your vassal, I will try to ensure that does not happen again.”

“You’re not my god damn vassal,” Vin grunted, shaking his head as Ovord got down on one knee. But when the man bent his head and placed a hand against his chest like he had when first offering him a soul bond, Vin’s eyes narrowed. “…You did something to the neilans, didn’t you? I mean, the complete one-eighty change in personality was obvious enough, but they just flashed me your same mark of subservience I’ve seen you use before. Ovord, what did you do?”

“I will not lie. I tweaked their soul like a gardener would care for a dying plant,” Ovord said, causing Vin’s breath to catch. “As you saw with your own eyes, they are healthy and hale. I did not harm them, and I even gave them a choice.”

“Was the other choice death?” Vin drawled, grimacing when his newfound vassal nodded his head. “Ovord… Look, I know you came from a world filled with stuff like soul bonds being a semi-common thing, and I can’t believe I even have to say this. But messing with a soul is not okay. That’s not even close to okay. You can’t do something like that again.”

“You would prefer I allowed them to go through with their plan to take advantage of your need for help to destroy Terra and everyone in it because your existence annoys them?” Ovord asked, keeping his face aimed at the ground so that Vin couldn’t see his expression. “I merely want clarification for how I should handle such events in the future.”

“I’ve had to put up with Scule for seven months now, these kinds of games won’t work on me,” Vin stated bluntly. “If it ever happens again, inform me, and we’ll figure it out together. End of discussion. Also, if you ever shunt me into my own mana pool like that again, you can kiss getting a new body goodbye.”

“I understand,” Ovord said. “What is to be my punishment?”

Rolling his eyes, Vin walked over and flicked the mage on the forehead. That actually seemed to get a reaction out of him, as Ovord blinked and looked up at him in surprise, his silver eyes confused.

“Your punishment is I’m annoyed at you,” Vin said, holding out a hand. “Don’t get me wrong, I still have some serious problems with what you did, but I understand things worked differently on your world. However, now you know. This was your one, Ovord. You don’t get a second. And maybe I’ll make you share a conversation about the importance of free will with Myers later, or something along those lines.”

Slowly, Ovord reached up and took Vin’s hand, accepting the help as he was pulled to his feet. The soul mage had a confusing expression on his face that Vin didn’t understand, before it vanished and he smiled at him.

“I would welcome such stimulating conversation. From what I have heard of Myers thus far, she sounds quite intelligent.”

“Oh, she is. Probably the smartest person in all of Terra, if I’m being honest,” Vin admitted. “Though our Strategist Mei is also up there. And as annoying as he is, you can’t rule Golrim out.”

“I would like to meet all three of them one day, if that is acceptable,” Ovord said.

“Yeah, that’s not gonna happen. Even after we get you a body, I’m not letting you within the same fragment as Golrim if I can help it.”

Chiding done, Vin exited Introspection, surprised to find a frowning Theodore sitting next to him.

“What happened?” Theodore asked, throwing a nervous glance at the door, as if ensuring it was sealed. “Tom and Vanessa both think you’re just some sort of master negotiator who's been hiding his skills all this time, but I know you better than that. What did you do to them?”

“It’s not that I don’t trust you, Theodore, but this is something only the council and my team knows about,” Vin admitted, running his hands down his face as he sighed. “Do you really want to know?”

To his credit, Theodore took a moment to think about it before nodding.

“I’m your second in command for the Foreign Branch. If you’ve got some giant secret, I think it’s only fair I know what it is.”

“Fair enough,” Vin said, making a snap decision. Without warning, he triggered Human Vessel again, taking a step back into his own mind as Ovord suddenly found himself in control of his body again. The soul mage blinked using Vin’s eyes, clearly caught off guard when he realized he was already running the show again.

“Go ahead and introduce yourself,” Vin told him, kicking back in his own head. “Consider it part of your punishment.”

Theodore ended up taking the news that Vin had a former Arch Mage of the Soul palling around within his core surprisingly well, which Vin supposed he shouldn’t have been all that surprised by. The Spy had known him back when he had Alka literally possessing him half the time, so this probably didn’t seem all that different. Ovord even managed to downplay what he’d done to the neilans to get them to change their tune, which Vin was grateful for. He didn’t think the soul mage would make a habit of running around performing minor soul surgery on people, but he still didn’t want anyone freaking out about the possibility.

Once Theodore was pacified, Vin returned to Terra, thoroughly exhausted after the day’s events. All he wanted to do was crash in his apartment and check in on Lumel with Dream Walk.

Which naturally, was when the clocktower exploded.

Vin was a good half-block away when the blast rang out, and he jerked out of his tired stupor, staring in shock as the building started to collapse. Without wasting any time, he triggered his new belt, leaping off the ground and flying directly at the crumbling tower. A quick cast of Sense Soul confirmed there were two people at the top of the crumbling tower, and eight more walking along below it, all of whom were at risk of being crushed by debris. Deciding the eight below took priority, Vin cast Slow Fall, his mana flowing out of him as the twisted remains of Terra’s devastating cannon and pieces of the clocktower itself started falling much more slowly.

Thankfully, he wasn’t the only person around to help. One of Abby’s guards ran forward, shouting at people and grabbing the hands of two crafters, yanking them to safety before they could be crushed. The guard managed to clear the area, and Vin let his spell end, allowing the debris to crash down. The other two souls remained up at the top of the tower, and Vin shot over to them. A cast of Create Air cleared the dust, and he frowned while he took in the guilty faces.

“Myers? Yughe?!”

Myers and the town’s newest Artificer, the goblin they had picked up alongside Quill and the minotaur, were coughing and rubbing dust out of their eyes. Once she was able to see again, Myers blinked as she took him in.

“Vin. You can fly now?”

“Are the two of you okay?” he asked, touching down on the edge of what remained of the top of the clock tower. “What happened? I saw the remains of the giant cannon.”

“We were improving upon its design!” Yughe said helpfully, grinning up at him. “Then we improved a little too far.”

“I told you the metal wasn’t sturdy enough for such a high-yield round,” Myers frowned, shooting an exasperated look at the goblin. “You could have killed us.”

“Bah, why have a town Healer if you don’t put him to work?” Yughe shrugged. “Besides, the cannon needed to be redone from the ground up to utilize our new design. This one was a wash anyway. The next one will be better in every way!”

Vin looked from one inventor to the other, slowly shaking his head before sighing. He’d figured weird stuff would happen once the two of them met, but he’d hoped they’d save any sort of deadly explosion for at least the second or third month of them working together. “Look, I have had a rather tiring day, and I just want to get some sleep. Do the two of you need help getting down from here?”

“That would be nice. Thank you,” Myers said, giving him a small smile. “Sorry about this, Vin, but we appreciate the help. Want to talk about your day? Though I suppose you have Lumel for that.”

“What are you doing? We have no time for talking!” Yughe argued, prodding the side of Myers’ leg. “Now is the time for building!”

“I’d say now is the time for rebuilding, personally,” Vin countered, glancing down at the remains of the top of the clocktower. Casting Slow Fall on them, he instructed them to jump. Once they were on the ground, he turned toward the goblin. As tired as he was, he figured he should still probably ask. “Yughe, I can see you’re growing comfortable with the town just fine. How are Quill and Noctis?”

“Noctis is putting his strength to work helping your supply team, and Quill has gone back to his people,” she said, smiling wistfully at the thought. “Frank and Shia were able to repair his wings for him, and he said he had some things he needed to work out with Telin. He also said he’d be back.”

“Huh. Sounds like Terra’s going to have two fliers then,” Vin muttered, wondering how the avisul and the divigel would get along. “Alright. And with that, I have fulfilled my duties as Terra’s head of the Foreign Branch. If anyone needs me, I’ll be in my bed. Please don’t need me.”
