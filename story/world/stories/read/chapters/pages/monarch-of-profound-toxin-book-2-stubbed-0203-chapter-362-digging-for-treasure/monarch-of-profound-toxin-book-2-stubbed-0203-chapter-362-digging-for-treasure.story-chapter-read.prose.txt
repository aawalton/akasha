Chapter 362

“Y-You want to… dig it up?” Sireal stammered with clear trepidation. “Perhaps you didn’t hear me properly before, but I believe I did use the word sinister, no?”

“No, you definitely did.”

“Okay, good, so… why are you trying to dig up something that’s sinister?”

“To see what it is, naturally.”

The cartographer’s hands moved around as if he was juggling. “I already told you what it is, though. It’s a mineral of some kind. Case closed!”

“Yeah, exactly. ‘Of some kind’. I want to know of what kind, and specifically if it’s of the Lord of the Moon kind.”

Sireal’s voice was very small when he spoke. “Can’t we just say that it’s not and call it a day?”

“Would you prefer to let a hostile Worldbreaker keep a potential card up his sleeve when we clash with him in the future?”

“I… No, I definitely don’t want that,” the nervous man said with a sigh.

“That’s what I thought, so let’s get to work. My aura can cover you and shred to pieces any influence from the Lord of the Moon that might be in it. Trust me, I’ve checked recently. You will be one hundred percent safe, just like how I’m shielding you from the spiritual pressure in the atmosphere here.”

“Then why can’t you just sense the mineral and its properties here where it’s, you know, safe?”

“I would if I could, man. If it’s two and a half kilometers, then it’s well within a distance I should have been able to sense, which leads me to believe that there is some kind of veil on or around it which obscures it even from me. And the fact that it must be that powerful is yet another reason why I think it’s not just some interesting rock.”

“But I found it, and I’m way weaker.”

“And you’re also not using the same type of sense to find it. This is an ancient beast that lived in its prime before any of our species had even evolved—before the natural development of any of these abilities we rely on now—so I would not be particularly surprised if the Lord of the Moon simply wasn’t able to take into account your specific flavor of tracking.”

Sireal nodded to himself, stars of pride glittering in his eyes.

“The Lord of the Moon is probably much more similar to me in that aspect, and uses his keen perception and extremely potent aura to sense the world around him. Your metaphorical feelers probably just bypassed his concealment due to sheer incompatibility, if I had to guess.”

“Thank you, Mr. Magnasen!” the man said, beaming. “Thank you for that.”

“You’re thanking me?” Eik chuckled. “I should be thanking you, mate! In fact,” he said and fished something out of his bag of holding. Twenty chromatic pills plopped into the cartographer’s waiting hands. “have some of these. I have so many of them, they’re practically coming out of my ass at this point. They’re for your power level too, so you can take them all yourself or sell them. I don’t mind either way.”

Sireal’s eyes were ready to pop out of their sockets and join the Legendary Mystery Medicine heaping in his palms. “Gh— Wha…”

“And for good measure,” Eik added and slapped three crispy muffins down on top of the pile of pills. “Now, let’s get this damned thing out of the ground, shall we?”

“Y-Yeah, of course!” Sireal muttered, not looking up as he began stuffing them into his own pack, which was completely ordinary, one by one. When it was done, he sat down and watched Eik.

“I need your help,” the Worldbreaker told him.

“Mine?”

“I can’t tell where it is, I can dig down there easily, but I need some direction or I’ll destroy it by accident.”

Ten careful minutes later, and Eik finally started to sense the strange mineral deep in the earth. The hole they were standing at the bottom of was… well, two and a half kilometers deep. If not for Eik ramming glowing staves of crystal into the walls of the hole at regular intervals as they had descended, Sireal would have been blind down here.

“All right, you go up,” Eik said and grew a pillar of crystal under the cartographer’s feet and sent him up to the surface. “I’ll bring it up with me. Give me a second.”

Carefully, Eik dug the final few meters down and finally made contact with the mineral. What he unearthed was no crude chunk of metal and certainly not some rock. It hissed as he pulled it free of the ground which mainly consisted of a greenish, grainy clay-like substance. A rank, piercing stench assaulted his nostrils, only a layer of aura allowing him to keep down his lunch.

Despite having just been pulled out of a wad of dirty clay, the rod of pitch black metal appeared to be polished to a mirror-like sheen. Rectangular in shape, neatly carved, intricate runic script ran down each of its four sides.

To Eik, at least, it was utterly unintelligible. Not to mention that fact that it appeared to basically roll down the sides of the rod, the characters ever-changing. The closest thing he could think of would be those digital information boards one might have been able to find on, for example, train stations on old Earth, where it went through all the upcoming stations as the letters slid sideways.

They weren’t exactly the same, but the rod exuded the same sinister energy as the effigy had done. The energy signature just wasn’t quite as similar to the Lord of the Moon.

But that didn’t mean there was any doubt regarding its origins. This mineral was definitely planted here by the enemy Worldbreaker.

So, what the hell was its purpose? The effigies were placed within the miasma in order to absorb and store the empowering substance until it could be transported to and given to the Lord of the Moon to bring him closer to the level of his peak. Or, at least, that was the best theory they had been able to come up with given the limited evidence they had access to.

Pinching the rod between two fingers of gooey, semi-solid Profound Toxin, Eik ascended on a second pillar identical to the one he had sent Sireal back to the surface on. The hand facsimile was extended a couple of meters from his body, the rod held at double arm’s length, just in case.

He wasn’t about to touch the thing directly. It might stick him with a tracker or something like that. Any contact with the unknown object was a risk in and of itself, obviously, but they couldn’t just let it be now that it had been found.

It measured a little over three meters in length with a width roughly equal to a broom handle. Sireal had already identified it as a kind of metal, but it was polished to such an extreme degree that Eik would most likely have mistaken it for something like a slab of obsidian.

“Can you tell what it’s for?” Eik asked Sireal when he reemerged.

Sireal looked at him incredulously. “Of course I can’t. I survey the earth and draw maps. I’m not a geologist. They might be moderately adjacent disciplines, but I’m really not particularly knowledgeable about something like this. I know enough about metals, stone, wood, and soil to advice my clients about the best way to get rid of them when clearing land for a road, a field, or any such thing, but that’s it.”

“Damn, yeah, okay. I’ll have to get a hold of those guys that helped me with the effigy before. Maybe they can figure it out. Speaking of that, why didn’t we find these rods earlier?”

Sireal shrugged. “It was deep. It takes focus and energy to look this deeply, and it narrows the breadth of a search considerably. Not to toot my on horn, but even most high-rank trackers wouldn’t find this. They specialize in living signatures. Their nets pass right over something like metal and stone.”

“You think you can find more?”

“If there are more, I can find them. But if they’re very infrequently placed, it might take longer than is acceptable. I have some colleagues I could refer you to if you’d like more hands.”

“Please.”

***

In the end, Eik managed to hire and bring with him another seven professional cartographers and surveyors within two hours. As it turns out, offering twenty times their normal rates would make most people drop their current work in a heartbeat to run to a distant, hostile environment to look for metal rods in the ground.

They searched for five, grueling hours of constant vigilance. And the fruits of their labor? One more rod. Utterly identical dimensions, shape, appearance, and flowing script.

All right, that was good enough for now.

“Thank you so much, everyone,” Eik told the gathered specialists. “I think we’ve more or less established that they’re quite rare, so I will hand the two samples we have over to some people who might be able to get me some answers.”

Nods all around.

“And now, as Sireal is already aware of, I have something to do on my own, so I will send you all back first. Again, thank you so much.”

Two minutes later, the crew was heading back through the fracture to Gimleh, Eik having to practically punt Sireal through the gate. He was begging to be allowed to see Eik’s experimentation.

Eik refused on the basis of preferring the man to not become a pool of biological slop on the ground.

When he was finally alone and the fracture had faded properly, Eik took a deep breath.

There, deep inside his spirit, Omnicide sat and eagerly awaited its moment to break out. It was… potent as all hell. Maybe the most powerful potential power he had ever felt inside himself.

To begin with, Death Star was an absolutely ridiculous ability. Out of all of his skills, that was probably the most pure destruction he had. Most others had some kind of utility to them as well.

But with Omnicide, he just couldn’t tell. They had spent hours searching for more buried rods of metal, but Eik had barely been able to focus on such an important task, because he just couldn’t stop thinking about his new skill.

With a heart that was racing away with excited anticipation, Eik took mental hold of Omnicide and let it course through him in an immediate and violent release.
