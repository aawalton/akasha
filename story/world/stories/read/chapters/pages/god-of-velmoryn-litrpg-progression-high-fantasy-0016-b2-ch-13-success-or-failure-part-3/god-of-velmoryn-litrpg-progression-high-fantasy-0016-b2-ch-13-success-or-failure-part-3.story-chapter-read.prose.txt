“Our kin has united into one tribe, and we seek to trade with you,” Aria explained shortly.

“If it is our potions you seek, that can be arranged,” the Velthwyn leader replied, its cracked and rough lips curving faintly. “But tell me… was it for this alone that you wove such a spell?”

Aria hesitated for a moment, glancing briefly at Avenor before nodding. “No. I require a drop of nature’s essence.”

Avenor turned to her, confusion plain on his face. “A drop of what?”

“We do not trade it,” the Velthwyn replied, the words low and rasping. “You must have known this already. Else, you would not have willingly shortened your life.”

“You shortened your life? When?” Avenor burst out, disbelief in his voice.

“The formless magic,” Aria explained. “It draws on a mage’s lifeforce instead of their mind. That’s why only Elves used it… and the Velthwyn.”

“Why only them…” Avenor muttered, his brow furrowing before realization dawned. “Because Elves are immortal. Their lives can’t be shortened. How much did you sacrifice?”

“That is not for you to know,” Aria said, voice sharpening, though the irritation in her eyes faded as quickly as it came. “Not much. I may not be immortal like Elves or those who share their blood, but Velmoryns live long enough - more than a millennium, if we’re careful.”

“Poor children,” the Velthwyn leader murmured, shaking its head slowly. “Your lives are not long enough to forget the passing of time, yet not brief enough to cherish it.”

“Know this,” its golden eyes were fixed on Aria, voice low. “We granted you audience not for the price you paid, but for the sincerity with which you paid it.”

“So you’ll give her what she came for? The… erm… nature’s essence?” Avenor asked. “She sacrificed part of her life, isn’t that enough to earn an exception?”

“We asked for no such offering,” the Velthwyn replied. “Yet the burden falls upon us, as though we had demanded it.”

“No,” Aria said sharply, before Avenor could speak again. “I did not perform the spell to pressure you, nor to invoke guilt. The cost is mine alone. I merely wished to show the depth of my intent.”

Her eyes met Avenor’s, warning not to speak on her behalf, and then she turned back to the Velthwyn.

“I was also asked to inform you that our kin have united under our Lord, the God of Velmoryn. He will not only rule over us, but over the entire forest…”

“We are aware,” the leader said. “We have felt the forest shake with new breath. We have seen the tharuuns marked by your God and the change that followed. Yet if you come to claim our blood in His name, we must question your truth for none among you carry the scent of divine breath.”

Aria said nothing, her silence alone confirming that she had indeed tried to use my name to gain what she wanted.

“Let’s talk about the potions first,” Avenor said finally, realizing she had no intention of speaking. “Rather than just purchasing them, we’d prefer to learn how they’re made. Would that be possible?”

The leader fell quiet. It weighed the request carefully before giving a slow nod.

“Knowledge is meant to be shared,” it said at last. “We shall teach the children of darkness and light the art of brewing potions that do no harm. Yet,” its gaze drifted toward Avenor, voice lowering to a slow rumble. “We too would ask something in return… grant us passage to the Godly Tree within your burrow.”

Avenor blinked, caught off guard. Then the corner of his mouth twitched, a faint smirk betraying the thought already forming.

“In exchange for the…” he began, but Aria cut in before he could finish.

“We would need the council’s approval,” she said, ignoring the glare Avenor shot her. “But I believe no one would oppose. Still, the Divine Tree only obeys Father’s will, and it only listens to the Priestess. You should know, it can be dangerous to approach without bearing His mark.”

“No Velthwyn fears a tree, nor would one ever raise harm against it,” the leader said sharply, the sudden firmness in its voice strange compared to the earlier monotonous and rough tone. “Even should it be a Godly being.”

Aria nodded and got ready to press her original request again, to try one more time for the Velthwyn’s golden blood, but then my attention was drawn away as a blue notification appeared before me.

[A Message]

Request for the Almighty God of Velmoryn:

I, the humble avatar of the God of Merchants, V, seek permission to enter Your Divine Kingdom. Should this time prove inconvenient, I shall withdraw and return when it better suits Your Highness’s will.

More like the avatar of the god of bootlicking.

I’d seen flattery before when I was a human. I had seen it both in movies and in real life, but nothing compared to what V was doing. Every workplace I’d ever been part of had its fair share of people trying to climb the ladder with fake smiles and exaggerated praise, but none were as exhausting or as blatantly performed as this.

Still, I’d gladly let him in.

The moment I resolved myself, white divine power flared in front of the throne within my dominion, and V appeared. Though this time, he wasn’t wearing the Velmoryn disguise or the leather armor, instead, he had decided to show his true face.

“I’ve been counting the days until I was granted the honor of basking once more in your Lordship’s divine presence!” he proclaimed as he bowed so low he could almost touch his boots with his head.

I made my manifestation on the throne nod slightly and then said, “You look different.”

“Allow me to explain this foolish mistake through a story that may, perhaps, amuse your Highness or at least ease whatever anger my blunder has caused,” he said, performing another deep bow, nearly sweeping the floor with his hands. “When I returned to my liege and confessed that I had dared to appear before You in disguise meant for one of those low, witless pretenders who call themselves gods, my liege was furious! He told me that no guise could deceive a being as mighty as the God of Velmoryn.”

Is this some kind of act? A calculated display of submission to make his clients drop their guard?

“What about your clothes?” I asked, curious why he was wearing a suit or rather a black tuxedo with tails so long they brushed the floor, almost like a cloak.

“I’ve grown rather fond of the attire of a fragile but resilient race known as humans,” V said smoothly, his vertical-slit eyes gleaming with amusement. “If your Lordship disapproves, I shall cast it aside this instant and even offer another gift to atone for such insolence!”

“Another?” I asked, deciding to take the bait.

“Of course!” V spread his arms theatrically. “My liege would never allow one as lowly as me to offend the God of Velmoryn and not even offer a gift worthy of Your divinity!”

I was curious to know what he had brought, but asking felt undignified. Better to wait and see what he thought was worthy of my attention.

“But before that,” he continued, lowering his head again, “I must first deliver what we owe you.”

With a graceful motion of his hand, a dark box appeared in my realm. It was black as the void and even in my realm, I was unable to see through it.

[Warning: You’ve earned 999 Divinity Points!]

“Unfortunately, your Lordship,” V said as the box dissolved, releasing divine energy that shimmered briefly before merging into my realm. “The limitations placed upon You because of Your current dwelling prevent us from delivering the full amount. We have earned 15,729 Divinity Points in total, half of which, of course, rightfully belongs to You.”

“What do you mean by my location?” I asked, unable to hide my irritation. The thought that I could have gained more than seven thousand Divinity Points, only to be restricted by some unseen rule, was infuriating.

“Your Highness,” V began carefully, his tone almost apologetic, “information of that nature… is rarely free.”

He gave a small, uncomfortable shrug, eyes flicking toward the lower corner of my throne room as he mumbled something too fast to catch. Then, just as quickly, his expression brightened.

“But!” he continued, his voice suddenly smooth again. “Since this visit is no ordinary one, after all, we come not only to apologize, but as Your new business partners, we shall share this information at no cost.”

He paused, clearly expecting gratitude. I gave him none. My manifestation simply raised an eyebrow, waiting.

“The Gods,” he said at last, “do not dwell in mortal realms. Or rather, those who are truly worthy of the title do not.” He hesitated, his tone shifting quickly as if afraid I’d take offense. “Every deity, of course, begins in the mortal world. But as they grow in strength, when they reach what we call the Fourth Stage of Godhood, they ascend to inhabit the divine plane where all true gods reside.”

He scratched his chin, claws scraping against pale scales.

“I cannot tell Your Lordship more about that higher plane,” he said finally. “But I can tell all about Your current dwelling.” His tone turned sly, like a merchant trying to distract a buyer from noticing the cheapness of what they were being sold. “Before reaching the Fourth Stage, all deities remain in the mortal realm. They are protected by absolute laws - such as no ascended god may harm them or their followers without issuing a direct warning, and even then, only by paying a heavy price. Yet those protections come with limitations. One, in particular, being that no ascended god may grant a lower deity more than a tenth of that deity’s threshold of Divinity Points per month.”

So there really are many gods… and everything I’ve gone through is just part of the same process the others endured. It looks like I’ll have to increase my Rank to gain my seven thousand Divinity Points.

“How many stages are there?” I asked. “And what are they?”

“The stages differ for each god,” V replied smoothly. “But there are eight in total.”

Before I could press him for more, his grin widened - sharp, gleaming teeth flashing as he reached into the air.

“That reminds me, your Lordship. We happened to acquire something that may interest You.”

A silver sphere appeared in his hand, radiant with a familiar energy that made the air around it tremble faintly.
