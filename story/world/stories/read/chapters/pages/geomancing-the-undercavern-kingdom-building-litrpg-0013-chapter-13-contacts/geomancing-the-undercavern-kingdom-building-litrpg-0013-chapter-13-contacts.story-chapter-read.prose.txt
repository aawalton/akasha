Commander Gorn and the other officers followed Carmack around as he added additional defenses to the spire. In seconds, through geomancy, he formed parapets and arrowslits along walls and within defensive corridors built into the outer edges of the spire itself. He also improved the external walls built around the bridges and added turret towers on either side of them.

The officers had been asked to follow along and take notes as they walked. Carmack wanted them to know about every little sneaky hole and the angles from which they could effectively defend.

“These defenses—they rival even Lydrus,” one of the sergeants in the group said, pressing up against an arrowslit to peer through it.

“Yes, the defensive capabilities have surely come a long way, but we still lack many things. For example, we don't even have arrow shafts to fletch new arrows and lack timber,” Captain Cella said. “There’s not much our archers can do without arrows.”

“Yes, that’s on my to-do list. Unfortunately, it'll have to wait. There's no workable means of satisfying our lumber needs until trade can be established with a surface city,” Carmack said.

“A surface city?” The two lieutenants looked at one another.

“Yes. Once we have the manpower, I intend to open a trade route. Unfortunately, it’ll be a dangerous undertaking, and we can't afford to lose men to the tunnels right now.”

“So, how do you suppose we fortify the walls?” Cella crossed his arms smugly. “My men need equipment if they’re expected to fight.”

“We have several plans forming, Captain. For now, you’ll have to make do with strong walls and turrets.”

“Strong walls, that’s it?” Captain Cella scoffed. “You expect me and my men to defend against whatever is out there with nothing but some walls?”

“Hush now, Captain,” Commander Gorn wrapped an arm around his shoulder. “We’ve been training the men for this. Put on a brave face and show them the courage you’re known for.”

Captain Cella reddened. Commander Gorn had turned his mockery of the hero’s preparations around on him, attempting to make him look like a coward.

“That’s not–”

“Hey, you don’t want the men to hear you speaking like that. They might get the wrong impression,” Gorn pressed a finger to his lips.

Cella ground his teeth but held his words as he noticed the amusement playing on the lips of the sergeants and lieutenants in attendance. They looked like they were holding back a storm of laughter.

“Okay, well, you’ve all seen what we have to defend ourselves with. I’ll leave it at that for now. I’m very busy,” Carmack said and walked off, leaving the officers on the wallwalk.

“Bahahah!” Echoed through the chamber at his rear as Carmack entered the spire, curling his lips into a grin. “Seems our fearless captain has gotten cold feet. Baha.”

Carmack appreciated Gorn stepping to counter the troublesome captain, but he knew the man wasn’t entirely wrong. He had been so caught up with all the tasks required to get the settlement moving in the right direction that he hadn’t considered alternative defensive mechanisms to keep invaders out. Pacing through the halls, he realized it was about time he paid the alchemy station a visit.

Hidden away at the back of the market floor, Carmack spotted the sign marking the entrance. The moment he stepped into the store, the scent of interweaving spices, herbs, and so much more assaulted his senses. Dangling from wires strung from the ceiling were all kinds of drying herbs, and dozens of oversized mortars and pestles were lined against the far wall. Several shelves were lined with potions of all colors, handwritten labels indicating what they were, and several dreadlock-wearing and cloudy-minded shamanic-looking people wandered about.

“Welcome to the Alchemist’s and Herbalist’s Corner,” an old man turned to Carmack as he slowly checked potions and rearranged the display. “See Sunflower or me at the counter if you need anything specific.”

“Do you by chance have combat and specifically defensive potions for sale?”

“We have the basics. Healing, antidote, endurance, and strength potions. Are you after some?”

“I’m looking to procure potions for the guard. The kind that would assist them in defending this place.”

“Well, most guardsmen already have at least a couple of potions. They come and buy them with their own coin. Better that than dead.”

“Oh, I see. So if I brought them a few vials of what you have in stock, it’s unlikely to change much.”

“I’d imagine so,” the old man nodded.

It seems I'll need to get a little more creative to boost morale and reinforce the defenses.

“Is it possible to make anything special for me?”

Though, as far as I’m aware, there are no combat uses yet. However, these tunnels are teeming with mushrooms we’ve never seen before. If you could collect samples, we could test them and determine what they might be good for. Who knows, we might strike gold.”

“With honor, hero. I’ll pass the message along.”

Carmack returned to the barracks, which were just another room carved into the lowest currently occupied floor, with a large, crystal-lit space for training.

Target and training manikins were set up across the space. Gorn stood by about twenty or so guardsmen with Aphexia by their side, looking characteristically unimpressed.

There was no doubt the men were disciplined, which he had to thank Commander Gorn for. The men he had met in the caverns when first arriving on this world had looked ragtag in comparison. But their movements were stiff and poorly rehearsed.

On several occasions, Carmack noticed guardsmen missing easily parried strikes and blocks and were punished by the practice weapons slamming into them. It was clear even to Carmack who relied mostly on magic that these men were using moves they had only recently learned.

She knows battle tactics… that’s beyond simple adventuring. I’d wager she has fought alongside an army before.
