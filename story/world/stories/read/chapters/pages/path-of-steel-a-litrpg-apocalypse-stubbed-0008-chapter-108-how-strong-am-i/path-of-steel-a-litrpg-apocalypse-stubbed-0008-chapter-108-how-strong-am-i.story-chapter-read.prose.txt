A level 20 shadow materialized before Gary. Its aura field was wider than before, its technique more solid, and its stats no doubt higher. Gary was trying to conserve his energy, but he had no difficulty in taking care of this phantom.

Then came a Level 22. It resisted for a short while.

Then came two level 15s. Their movements were sluggish compared to Gary’s. He got behind one and speared it to the chest, then cleaved the other’s head off with a horizontal slash.

Two level 17s came next. The difficulty spikes were getting smaller, which Gary assumed was meant to provide a more fine-grained evaluation of the Birth of Aura disciples’ strength.

On the first step, which was meant for Energy Harnessing disciples, the spikes were massive because no one really cared.

This was the second step’s sixth wave. After the level 17s came two level 19s, followed by level 22s which actually gave Gary a bit of trouble. The last wave contained a single phantom, looking significantly more real than the previous ones. Through the swirling shadows that made up its body, Gary could see the hints of cyan robes and a silver-headed spear.

It didn’t seem like a massive spike from the two level 22 shadows that came before, since this phantom was still at the Birth of Aura realm, but that couldn’t be farther from the truth. This phantom possessed a vibration. It felt simple, maybe a plain vibration of the Spear, but it gave it a strength worlds apart from the previous phantoms. Gary didn’t even need to face it to know—he could sense its strength from afar. When it charged, it arrived with a blinding thrust.

Spear Phantom, Level 25 (Elite)

There’s more to life than levels, genius. Go touch grass.

The shadow unleashed a blitzkrieg offensive on Gary, who found himself challenged for the first time since entering the Steps of Power. Its skill was leagues above the other shadows, and its vibration made its every move stronger. This shadow alone was as strong as the four-armed knight he’d faced in the inheritance.

Thrust met thrust in a heated exchange, then two spears crossed. To pre-System humans, or even to Energy Harnessing cultivators, their speed would be impossible to follow. Gary vibrated his aura to the frequency of black lightning, watching it transform from pure aura into actual black lightning. How exactly this worked still baffled him, but now was not the time for considerations.

“Black Thrust!” Gary yelled, unleashing a massive thrust that blew the opponent back. He thunderstepped, letting the move’s burst of lightning assault the opponent at the same time that he unleashed his own attacks.

“Nail Gun!”

His spear thrust so rapidly it seemed like a dozen spears rather than one. Afterimages blurred with the real thing. The phantom tried to defend, but every parry sent it further off-balance, and the lightning burst at the end of Thunder Step had slowed its legs. A thrust finally pushed its spear aside, letting Gary thrust for the center of its chest. It connected, but not before the shadow twisted its body to ram the butt of its spear into his temple.

Gary went flying. The shadow pursued, bleeding darkness, and it struck at Gary with its own storm of attacks. Feints blended in with thrusts and slashes. Gary thunderstepped again, arriving behind the shadow. Its chest injury made it slower to turn—Gary’s spear ran through its throat, killing it.

Gary panted as the phantom dissipated. His head was still ringing from the strike he’d taken to the temple, and his legs were slightly charred from the two thundersteps. However, even as he stood there, he could feel himself recovering far faster than normal.

He thought to the seed inside him, that little red orb which had anchored crimson tentacles into Gary’s soul. Its identity and origins remained a huge mystery, but the power it offered him was undeniable. It had given him so much vitality—the stat affecting endurance, durability, and regeneration.

He just hoped it wasn’t something evil.

“That was a strong shadow…” he thought aloud. When he’d looked over the rankings, he’d seen that half the Birth of Aura disciples had been stopped at this wave. Now, he knew why.

Of course, while this phantom had been as strong as the four-armed knight, the true difficulty wasn’t even close. The four-armed knight had been preceded by sixty-four level 17 knights, and thirty-two more before that. By the time the four-armed knight appeared, Gary had been almost dead already. In comparison, the trip to this elite phantom was a walk in the park. The hit he’d taken to the temple had been a lucky shot.

But, this level wasn’t the end. Not even close.

There was a small delay at the end of each step, giving the cultivator a few breaths of time to rest. By the time he was teleported to the third step, Gary had mostly recovered. The mists were blue around him now. He immediately fell into a battle stance.

Two level 25 phantoms materialized before him. They weren’t elites, but they did possess a vibration—both used the same Vibration of the Spear. They split up and charged him from both sides.

Gary’s black lightning revved. His spear flew wild, intercepting strikes at speeds he never even considered possible. He became a maelstrom of violence. The shadows were fierce enough to challenge him, but not to overwhelm him. The level of difficulty was just right.

Just right to feel good.

Gary laughed as he span. He fell into the zone. He parried and dodged, enhancing his every movement with black lightning. Whenever the shadows tried to parry his thrusts, they were blown back as if struck from a cannon. The second shadow was always there to cover while the first recovered, but Gary simply pressed on. He was accelerating. As he fell into a rhythm, the battle belonged to him. He was pushing them back.

“Nail Gun!” he roared, stabbing the shadows until they went flying. He pursued one, flashing over it and stabbing down. “Thunder Step! Black Thrust!”

It raised its spear to block. Gary’s spear fell like divine judgement, smashing through its defense and thrusting it into the ground. The shadow bounced, a hole pierced clean through its waist. It dissipated. The second shadow landed a slash to Gary’s shoulder in the meantime, sending him flying, but he span mid-air and slid to his feet.

The spear’s blade had only cracked the bone despite arriving at almost full force. Even as the shadow charged, Gary could already sense his wound regenerating. He cracked a grin.

“Black Thrust!” he yelled, laughing, and the shadow was soon dead.

The second wave arrived immediately. Three level 25 shadows. Gary sank into combat, no longer conserving any energy. They pressured him from all sides. Facing three opponents was drastically different than two.

He finally managed to slay one shadow. He paid for it with a deep cut to the thigh, which spilled darkness onto the stone floor—he, too, was a shadow body here. With only two shadows left, it was as manageable as the previous wave, and he slowly but surely eked out an advantage.

The shadows were eventually gone, and Gary stood by himself in the middle of the step, panting and bleeding heavily. His massive vitality patched him up, but these weren’t wounds he could easily regenerate. Moreover, his aura reserves were running low. Even with his sky-high vitality, he was exhausted.

He’d defeated the second wave of the third step. That put him in the top ten or so Birth of Aura disciples, which was a massive accomplishment. However, Gary had an overwhelmingly powerful vibration, a legendary spear which enhanced said vibration, and a titanic vitality which counteracted the backlash of his vibration and helped greatly in protracted combat. He had no idea how ten people had reached the same level without either of those three.

I guess everyone has their own advantages. Sui Duang must have drunk Muscle Sap instead of milk when he was a baby.

Clearing the next wave would place in the top five disciples. Gary had no idea what to expect. As the wave formed, however, he could only chuckle in disbelief.

There were three shadows again, all Level 25, but one was an elite.

They fell on Gary. The simple Level 25s were fierce but manageable. Adding an elite on top made the assault overwhelming.

As the shadows flanked the already injured and exhausted Gary, he could only lament his own weakness. He did his best. Black lightning erupted in a storm. “Omnidirectional Nail Gun!” he roared, fighting to keep them at bay, but his defense collapsed as surely as snow melts in the spring.

Spears found his body, inflicting first surface wounds, then more serious ones. His 79 points in Vitality couldn’t protect him from the attacks of level 25 enemies. Gary thunderstepped from place to place, using the bursts of speed to flee their encirclement, but every escape was temporary.

Eventually, the simple shadows pushed away his spear, while the elite put its weapon through Gary’s leg, sealing his mobility. The pain was searing. Gary took advantage of the gap to stab a shadow in the heart, dissipating it. He tried to fight the remaining two, but with his leg gone, it was impossible.

The elite pierced Gary’s throat.

The mists spat him out, in his real body this time, sending him rolling over the rock. He took a moment to orient himself. Cheers rang out all around him. He turned his head to find dozens of cultivators screaming his name.

“What the hell?” he muttered.

“They started gathering when you reached the third step. We’re famous!” Goldie exclaimed, emerging from his spear. “Hey everybody! I helped too!” she yelled, waving her tail.

Gary stood, and the crowd cheered again. He raised a fist. They cheered even harder.

“I could get used to this,” he muttered.

“I’ll be rooting for you at the tournament, Gary Steel!” a person shouted.

“Me too! The families have been bullying us for a while—you need to teach them a lesson!” another cried out.

“You guys know my name?” Gary asked as he heard footsteps behind him.

“You got the Golden Bolt, which had been sitting on the Hold for decades, and you had two impressive duels. You publicly argued with Sui Duang. Of course they know your name—you’re one of the most gossiped people in the clan,” Deacon Song said, carrying a light smile. “Don’t let it get to your head. Every powerful cultivator gets supporters. Real strength lies within.”

“Yes, senior,” Gary said, unable to hide his grin.

The ranking stele brightened on cue. A name shot up the rankings to appear in the eleventh place—Gary Steel, third step, second wave!

“You did well,” the deacon said. “You just returned from the expedition. I imagine your strength will rise a little more until the tournament, but I’d advise hiding it from now on. If you want to practice in the steps, try to lose one or two waves before your limit. You don’t want your opponents in the tournament preparing against you.”

“You are wise, Deacon Song.”

“That’s a load of crap! Don’t listen to him, Gary. You should impress everyone and show them you’re the strongest!” Goldie said.

Gary laughed as he petted her little head. “You could have whispered that, Goldie. Deacon Song is a good man. We don’t want to offend him.”

“As if I’d hold my tongue for a mere Core Formation cultivator!”

“But you’d do it for me?” Gary asked.

Goldie pouted. “Fine. Sorry, Deacon Song. I shouldn’t have called you out.”

The deacon scratched his head. “Spirits are always eccentric. I appreciate the apology.” He looked towards the formation control panel, where a small line of people waited. “I must return to my post. I wish you all the best, Gary Steel. What are you going to do now?”

Gary smiled wide. “I’ll go into town for a bit. I have a thing going on. Then, I’m going to get much, much stronger. You’ve seen nothing yet, Deacon Song!”

“Gary Steel! Gary Steel!” the small crowd cheered. Gary thanked them and took off. Opening his chat, he saw he’d received replies to his previous messages.

It was time to meet some friends.
