Bandarrr hated boats.  He detested vast stretches of open water.  And he very much loathed his current situation, since he was on a boat in the middle of the sea.  As a member of the extremely dense (physically, not mentally) Lithari people, his buoyancy ranged somewhere between wishful thinking and nonexistence.

If he fell off the boat, he would sink like a rock.  He very much did not want that.

It was just his rotten luck that the Delve would have an entire Floor dedicated to sailing around on fragile wooden ships.  He had expected to deal with water at some point, and he had even purchased a few one-time use items from the System Shop that would help him get unstuck if he found himself underwater, but even these had their limits.  And he had no idea how deep the water was, because the captain wouldn’t give him an intelligible answer.  The woman leading the ship would only respond with nonsense words like “Mark Twain!” when asked for a reading on the depth.  In exasperation, he’d dropped the ship’s anchor from the bow and watched as the cable paid out.  The water was deep.

Despite his misgivings, the Lithari had so far encountered nothing that would endanger his boat.  He had explored a few islands that he’d come across but found nothing of great interest.  The islands were small, ridiculously so.  Without any reason to stick around other than the fact that it was dry lands, Bandarrr gingerly returned to his ship in the small rowboat that was meant to ferry the crew to and from land.

He had just taken his place on the deck when he spotted a flying object heading straight toward him.  It appeared to be damaged since it left a great cloud of smoke in its wake.  Whatever it was, it was fast.  The sailors, spotting the incoming object, began to panic.

“Oi, calm down, you spineless, pathetic lot,” the Lithari yelled as he extruded a projectile from his body and prepared to throw it.  Bandarrr’s Squire rushed forward and began to shove big chunks of what looked like modeling clay against the Lithari’s rocky skin, replacing his lost mass almost instantly.  The Knight paid no attention to his Squire’s actions, restoring lost mass was the whole point of bringing one of his first-generation buds along with him.

Bandarrr used several Skills to enhance his projectile, including Harden, Seek Target, and Friction Reduction.  The rounded, dense stone left his arm with a crack so loud that the nearby sailors took damage from the shockwave.

The incoming object dodged, proving that it was no mere projectile as it corrected its course.  Luckily for Bandarrr, his Seek Target Skill allowed for a few degrees of correction, preventing him from missing entirely.  A few feathers erupted from the spot where the stone made contact.

“It’s a bird!?” the Lithari exclaimed, confused, as he reassessed the situation.  In truth, this was good news.  Bird-type foes tended to be highly mobile but lacked the power to penetrate powerful defenses.  Their light bones were weak against the type of crushing attacks that Bandarrr favored as well.

Heartened by the discovery, Bandarrr began to prepare another pair of projectiles, planning to force his onrushing foe into a trap.  The Lithari was no stranger to fighting foes that wanted to keep their distance from his powerful limbs and had developed a number of strategies to take down nimble assailants.  A mere tenth of a second separated the pair of stone spheres that Bandarrr loosed next.

The bird dodged both of them.

His next attempt involved charging three spheres with mana to create rudimentary grenades.  These were ignored.  Then four spheres joined by cunningly woven organo-metallic wire failed to snare as the bird simply melted the chains.  Next, the five spheres were dodged with a grace that left Bandarrr stunned.  A moment later, the six spheres he launched slammed into a mana shield that the bird somehow placed far enough away that it did not impede its own flight.

Bandarrr prepared to launch seven spheres, wishing for a miracle as he did, but his time had run out.  The insanely tough bird, smoking like a tire fire covered in peat moss, slammed straight into him.  His rocky exterior cracked under the assault.  Without hesitation, the rocky being used his ultimate trump card.

He hated to do it, since the Skill came with painful side effects and a period of debilitation after, but Bandarrr had no choice.  It was clear that he was in over his head, and he was fighting for his life.

He mentally disabled all the failsafes that limited the output from his Adamant Core.

Power flooded throughout his body as metal-aligned mana reinforced every inch of his frame.  His rocky skin took on a metallic sheen, and his insides grew denser and tougher.  This effect was further magnified by something that he referred to as the Concept of Mass.  Pure mana took on a density of its own, causing the Lithari’s weight to skyrocket.  The ship’s wooden deck groaned beneath him as if complaining about the sudden increase in the amount of weight it had to support.

But that wasn’t all.

The very air around him began to solidify, passing directly from gas to solid in a process called deposition.  The thickness was more than a physical barrier, it was a conceptual one as well.  Passing through the air took a considerable amount of willpower even if one possessed the strength necessary to force the hardened air molecules aside.

It didn’t affect the crazy bird at all.

Catching a hold of one of the cracks in Bandarrr’s torso, the smoldering avian used his powerful legs to pull in either direction as it literally stood upright at a ninety-degree angle on the Lithari’s chest.  The shriek of tortured metal assaulted Bandarrr’s ear holes as he tried to grapple his opponent.  Without bothering to look up, the bird deflected the Lithari’s grab as it continued to widen the long gash in Bandarrr’s torso.

The Lithari was in full panic now.  His flesh was being torn asunder by a strength that no creature under level 100 should possess, let alone one that was as agile and adroit as this avian.  Even his metallic skeleton, which had been painfully reforged in the trials of Mons Enduring, deformed under the immense pressure.  Even though his mana sense was not that highly developed, Bandarrr could feel the terrifying power radiating from a spot in the avian’s breast with a roar that was almost audible.

The Lithari had no time to process just how the avian was overwhelming him, however.  With a supreme effort, the bird heaved one last time before giving a squawk of triumph.  It had uncovered one of Bandarrr’s two weak points: his Core.

Nooooooo!  Bandarrr’s Squire shouted through their mental link as he sought to force his way through the thick air surrounding his Knight.  But it was too late.  The avian’s beak flashed forward, cracking the crystalline organ.  Bandarrr’s existence was swallowed up into a single point of white hot suffering as he felt something deep within his very essence crack.  But with the pain came another unpleasant feeling.  It was more than just pain, more than just humiliation or knowledge that he had failed.  It was hopelessness.

He had never stood a chance.

Another peck and the agony intensified.  More things inside Bandarrr’s body broke beyond repair.  More negative emotions overwhelmed him.

His Adamant Core spluttered off for a moment before firing up again.  But it was unstable, and the gravity field it generated misaligned.  Bandarrr found himself falling off the edge of the ship.

His last conscious thought was “Not the water, please not the water.”

Charlemagne was rather miffed as he stared down into the blue-green water.   The rocky creature’s hard, crunchy center had smelled amazing, but he hadn’t gotten more than a tiny little taste before it fell straight into the ocean.  Its Squire had dutifully jumped in right after it, too, meaning that the rooster hadn’t gotten a chance to eat it, either.  He could still sense the creature far below, but its life force was quickly fading and Charlemagne was in no mood to jump in the water after it.

After all, chickens didn’t panic flap when dropped into deep water for no reason.  They were ill-suited for swimming and their feathers quickly became waterlogged.  And since Charlemagne’s awakened predator instincts were largely neutral when it came to water, his reluctance to jump in overcame his desire to eat the other Knight’s Core.  Barely.

But the rooster had some happy news waiting for him.  He had completely forgotten that the Squiggles had some messages for him, so he took a moment to review them.

Error: Insufficient Divinity

Scanning

Partial Template Application Possible

Reattempting to Apply Template: “Charlemagne” (partial)

Success

Species Designation (R. Gallus Viscountus) Removed

DNA from a variety of sources was incorporated into your new template, which has increased the benefits listed at the time of absorption.  Further beneficial mutations due to the ambient radiation have been preserved.  Further beneficial changes from Skills and natural treasures have been preserved.

Congratulations, you have successfully evolved your species far beyond what would ordinarily be genetically possible.  All of your Attributes will receive a permanent twenty-five percent bonus.  This bonus is applied before any other bonuses, meaning that all Achievement bonuses will further multiply these bonus attributes.

Charlemagne absorbed the information with pleasure.  He was especially happy to learn that his newest species transformation had provided such a powerful benefit.  The last time his species upgraded, he didn’t think that his gains were quite that impressive.

Having read the messages from the Squiggles, the rooster was at a loss as to what to do.  He really wanted to figure out a way to reach the other Knight, which was somehow still alive despite having reached the bottom of the ocean.  But it was deep down, far deeper than any rooster had ever gone before.  And he was getting further away with every second.  If he wanted to get his beak on that delicious Core, he would need to act quickly.

It took a few minutes of serious thinking, but the rooster finally realized something important.  He had people to think for him.  He could just make them figure it out.

Charlemagne swiftly grabbed the ship’s captain and flew off with her, flying straight as an arrow back to his own ship.  As he approached it, he realized that something about the vessel looked different, but he did not have the mental bandwidth to puzzle out that particular issue.  He dropped to the deck and released the captain, who stumbled around until Charlemagne’s ship’s captain stomped over and put her to work polishing a railing.

“Bawk,” he said in the meantime, ignoring the interaction between the current and former captains going on next to him.  “Bawk bawk!”

Phasima, who had been teaching Phatagin how to play some sort of game that involved moving tiny mana constructs around on a flat surface, jumped up.  The game vanished into motes of mana that she quickly reabsorbed.

“Yeah, I can help with that, I think.  How deep’s the ocean here?”

“No bottom at 200 fathoms,” the captain answered calmly before turning and yelling something unprintable at one of the sailors, who had managed to tangle up some of the rigging lines.

“Oooof, that’s deep,” she said.  “But you can still sense the creature?  And it’s alive at the bottom of the sea?”

“Bawk,” the rooster confirmed.

“All right, this sounds like a pretty fun challenge.  I’ve never been to the bottom of an ocean before, at least not without getting into the water.  How do you suggest we get started?”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne demanded.

“Well, you’re the one who wants to get down there so badly,” the former Empress of the Forest shouted.  “I swear if you hadn’t somehow grown ridiculously stronger than you already were, I’d toss you overboard!”

The rooster squawked in indignation.

“Wait, is the reason that you need my help that you just don’t want to get wet?  What, are you afraid that you’ll mess up your feathers?  Or that you’ll start smoking?”

Phasima broke out in a cackle that was definitely not very regal, and it took a long time for her to settle down enough to speak again.

“All right, let’s get going before something else happens,” she agreed when she’d finally calmed down.  “At the very least, I’ll get some good mana control practice out of this.”
