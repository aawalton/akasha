“This is where they did it,” Tlazo told me evenly, as I wandered the chamber I had found him in. I felt like I was in a daze as I slowly traced the impossibly tiny etched runes in the chamber with one finger. I still couldn’t understand them, but now I was much more motivated to do so. After all…

“This is where the gods ascended.”

Tlazo hadn’t been being literal when he had told me that this complex was where the gods were ‘born’. More…metaphorical. I had been somewhat taken aback by his words, there in the long abandoned dining hall when he had told me that, because I knew for a fact that wasn’t true. Tlazo knew very little about the existence of the Netherim, though I had been astonished that little wasn’t nothing.

The Lich apparently knew that there had been a race of people that had existed in the heyday of Vereden, and that the gods originated from that people. But he didn’t know what they called themselves, and how they had ended.

He didn’t know who they had been.

After all, the Netherim thieves who had stolen their power had not all been from a single place. Travers had been vague and unhelpful on this when I had pushed him. But what he had told me was that all of them had been from different Netherim bunkers. Hidden places from all over the Vereden-that-was, in that primordial age. A cross-continent conspiracy born from a lust for power so strong that it broke more than one planet, in the end.

Instead, all he had learned was that this place, this mysterious stone complex hidden within the walls of an underground caldera…had been where the Netherim usurpers had ascended into Divinity.

It was a false Divinity, stolen from the System in some way. Their strength and authority was conditional in some ways, and from what I had learned, flawed in others. But it had granted them enough strength and pure mystical might to conquer the entire connected planetary network and rule over it as deities for millennia.

And it had all happened here.

In this very chamber. On that very altar.

No wonder I thought this place felt strange. Such an undertaking had likely scarred the fabric of reality to such an extent that it had bled for who knows how long in the aftermath. And still, in time period of somewhere over five thousand years, it still left me with a vague feeling of unease just from being inside of it.

Tlazo knew very little of that. The knowledge I had gained about the distant past of Vereden and the true identity of the gods as nothing more than thieves, was something I had only obtained because of my status as a Precursor. The only surviving record of the Netherim could only be found within their own ruins, and those were inaccessible to all but Precursors.

Because of that, I had been incredibly curious about how he had discovered that knowledge at all.

Turns out, I was wrong. There were still records about the Netherim outside the bunkers.

Just not physical ones.

“He told me,” Tlazo said heavily, deliberately turning his gaze down to the floor beneath us.

For a single, stunned moment, I didn’t understand what he meant. And then I remembered just what was below us.

“That thing is still alive?!” I hissed, tensing up and preparing to sprint for the bottom floor of the complex.

Where Sylvia and Fade were resting…not far from the apparently not so quiescent corpse of that mole thing.

To my irritation, Tlazo waved away my concern and coughed a quite literally dusty laugh. “The sage is only ‘alive’ in the sense that I am alive, Hart,” He said, amused. “But you needn’t worry for the lives you’ve brought to this place. He is, in his own way, quite benevolent. The Great Sage Equal to Death has had…quite a long time, to muse on failures past. It’s left him very agreeable.”

I paused, completely thrown off of my anxious worry.

Great Sage Equal to what?

I didn’t realize I had said that out loud before Tlazo smirked at me. “Why don’t we go downstairs anyway? I’m sure if you asked politely enough, the sage would be more than willing to explain matters clearer than I could, anyway.”

I…okay. Whatever. I wanted to check up on Sylvia and Fade anyway. My love had been doing fine, but Fade had been struggling a little in such a Death Aether-filled environment.

I firmed my back and nodded at the amused Lich, pivoting on one heel and making for the entrance into the lower floor.

Follow closely by the perfectly even steps of a desiccated corpse wearing a funeral shroud as a toga.

…………………………..

I was understandably relieved to discover Sylvia and Fade had been just fine in the hour since I had left them alone. In fact, I was a bit surprised to discover just how fine.

The two of them had been napping until I had jumped down to the lower floor. Fade had been nuzzled into the crook of Sylvia’s neck, cradled within her arms. I almost regretted waking them, from just how peaceful two of the most important people in my life had looked. It was rare enough for Sylvia to get any sleep at all, with the double whammy of her high Level and being a Sculpted.

Alas, the sound of my impact on the stone floor was enough for Fade’s ears to twitch, followed by the rest of the Spirit Wolf right to wakefulness. Sylvia wasn’t far behind.

Both of them were…quite surprised to see who and what floated down behind me. While Fade tensed up immediately, crouching low and baring his fags at the Lich, Sylvia merely blinked rapidly. Like me, I think she almost instantly recognized the only friendly Lich she had ever met, back in the caverns under Elderwyck. “Tlazocuauhtli? What…how are you here?”

Of course, she had remembered his whole name instantly and pronounced it perfectly. I had needed a dedicated memory dive session a few months ago to remember the complicated word, and I still hadn’t remembered how to say it.

I engraved her pronunciation into my memory.

To my consternation, Tlazo veritably beamed at Sylvia in greeting.

Well.

As much as a desiccated zombie lurking in the dark could, at least.

“Dear girl, how good it is to see you again. And looking so well, too!” He said jovially, striding up to her and ignoring the growling Fade as he did. Sylvia was unresisting as he helped her to her feet, actually patting some dust off her shoulder as he did. “I must say, Cultivation seems to be agreeing with you! My my, I’ve never seen such an interesting physical evolution.”

Somehow, someway…I felt one of my eyebrows twitch. That was a markedly different reaction than what I had gotten.

I suppose we all had our favorites.

“Ah…thank you.” Sylvia blinked once more, looking decidedly lost for a moment. I didn’t miss the subtle pleading look she shot me over the Lich’s shoulder. “Lady Honoka agrees. She believes that if this continues, I’ll be able to-”

Oddly, Sylvia cut herself off mid-sentence, as if she had just realized what she was saying. To my confusion, the pleading look she was directing at me turned much more panicked in nature.

Emulating the increasingly confused Fade, I tilted my head at her with a raised brow.

At the same time, Sylvia looked away from me with an uncharacteristic blush, Tlazo followed her gaze with an amused look on his leathery features. “Oh, I can guess what she believes,” He chuckled. “I was married to the woman for centuries, after all. We even operated a Healing practice together for some time. But I digress.”

Almost instantly, Sylvia sobered up as she utilized the same training I had received to suppress her emotions. “Yes…what’s going on? Did you find him up there, Nathan?”

“Yeah, Nathan,” Fade directed my way, a tad snidely. “Did you find this bag of bones up there?”

“I see you’re feeling better,” I mentally told my familiar, while I spoke aloud. “Yes, he’s…apparently going to help give us some answers down here.”

“I have no further answers for you, children,” Tlazo said amiably, casually walking away. “But I do applaud your sense of curiosity, and so I will beseech someone else on your behalf. I hope the Great Sage can help you on your path to understanding, as he did me.”

As Sylvia mouthed the words ‘Great Sage’ to me in ever deepening confusion, all I could do was shrug my shoulders at her.

We would find out soon just what this was all about.

Sylvia, Fade, and I lagged behind Tlazo as he approached the dais with all of the oddly posed corpses upon it. To my surprise, before he stepped on leathery foot upon the raised stone, he actually paused and bowed toward the large, seated skeleton in the center. The bow was long, and deep, and lasted for more than a second. It was only at some unseen signal that the rose and stepped foot upon the dais.

Nothing changed that I could verifiably sense. There were no shifts in the Aether, no sense of awareness or stirring in either physical or mystical airs…and yet.

Yet for some reason, I felt a chill run down my spine, for no reason I could divine.

Tlazo approached the massive mole skeleton and stood on his tip-toes in order to whisper into the side of the skull where an ear would be. I couldn’t hear him from where I stood off of the edge of the dais, which itself was odd. Neither could Fade, apparently, when I looked down at him with an inquisitive look. He just shook his furry head with a disturbed look on his face.

Finally, after some time, Tlazo relaxed back down and turned to face us. “You may approach,” He said calmly. “The Great Sage has agreed to speak with you.”

I reached out for Sylvia’s hand, only to find that it was already questing for mine. As one, we stepped foot on the dais, with Fade at our side…

And the world…

Changed.

Absolute. Isolation.

I had thought I knew what an Aetheric dead zone was. I had made them before, either for rituals or for surgical procedures. But I suppose there was a difference between a dead zone…

And a zone of death.

There was nothing upon the dais. Nothing in the air, nothing in the Aether. I’m pretty sure that if we were in the Concord itself, it would be great zone of nothing as well.

This was more than just scrubbing the residual Aether of elemental and mystical imbalances.

This was the removal of Aether itself in an area.

I hadn’t even known that was possible.

Entirely involuntarily, I felt my hands spasm, and I couldn’t help but look down in shock. I didn’t even have muscles or nerves right now that could react like that.

How had that…

I was knocked out of my shock by Tlazo speaking once more. “Approach, and know no fear. The sage offers nothing but understanding.”

I looked up at Tlazo almost suspiciously then, and found him watching our frozen trio with a knowing look on his dead features.

You know, you’re sounding an awful lot like a priest there, Rafael. I exchanged equally trepidatious looks with my companions and did as we were bid. What other choice did we have?

When at last we stood before the enormous skeleton of some…some creature, I locked eyes with the empty sockets, just waiting for them to flare up once more as Tlazo’s did. I was so sure that whatever this ‘Great Sage’ was, it had to be a Lich of some kind. It was the only thing that made sense to me. And if it was, then those sockets would light up with blue flames, and I’m sure the damn thing would start jawing at me.

Only…that’s not what happened.

Tlazo cleared his throat, in an apparent attempt to dislodge dust or something. “Behold!” He cried in the most dramatic tone he had ever spoken in. “You stand before the Father of Necromancy! It was he, in ages past, who gathered unto him two upon seven disciples! Onto them he bestowed all his knowledge of death and its endless cycles! Onto those blessed mortals he kindly crafted Spells and Arts, and all manner of rituals upon which one could guide the essences of the end! He, who looked upon those afflicted with mortality, and felt compassion! He, who sought to bolster our fragile spirts, with a more kindly eternity. He, who-”

As if from a great distance, I then heard a voice. There was no source for it, or at least one I could discern. It came from no particular direction, and I don’t…I don’t think I was even hearing it with my ears. Not only that, but this didn’t quite feel similar to the soul speech I had grown accustomed to, from existences that were larger than my own. This was something more….

More primal. More essential to reality.

‘I believe that’s enough, Rafael,’ I heard faintly. ‘Our guests appear to understand well enough.’

I looked up into the sockets of the enormous skull before me, and saw…

Nothing.

‘Greetings to you, young ones,’  The voice said in a kindly manner. It was wispy, and in a way, faint.

But it wasn’t coming from the skeleton.

‘I require no titles, and yet…once, in a long, fraught, bygone era, I was known by those things Rafael announced,’ The voice chuckled airily, before a note of almost mischief entered into it. ‘But before that…I was known by a different title. Perhaps even one you might recognize?’

‘Once, long ago…I was known as the Great Spirit of Death.’

Immediately, my mouth fell open in disbelief, while I heard an almost panicked whine come from Fade.

What?

‘But you may call me…

Hermus.’
