It was quiet. Silent. Still as the dead, as everybody stared up at Eik where he stood, hands supporting him as he leaned in over the lectern.

Jaws hung slack and eyes were as wide as saucers as brains worked through the effort of processing what their ears had just told them had been said out loud.

Eik didn’t bother filling the void with empty blabber as he might have been inclined to do under more normal circumstances. As a matter of fact, he was perfectly happy to simply let his words sink in slowly and surely. There was no room for misunderstanding or deflection in this matter, so the longer it stewed the better.

Gradually growing to a cacophony of whispered voices, hushed conversations broke out, filled with tension, apprehension, and disbelief.

In some of the best seats sat the most important of the Gohkamorian leaders, trembling with fury and indignation.

Eik could feel their auras begin to run amok, and some of them were incredibly powerful Awakened. At least a handful of the Gohkamorians officially present, numbering a little more than two dozen in total, were solidly within S-rank, with two or three rivaling both Andihar and Gul.

The pressure was strangling the weaker people around them as anger firmly gripped their minds, skills charging up with little to no regard for others.

The ruthless side of Eik almost celebrated this potentially deadly development. If Gohkamorian generals and high-ranking politicians became responsible for the loss of innocent lives in the middle of Gimleh, it would strongly reinforce Eik’s argument for intervention against them.

But, in the end, he couldn’t bring himself to let that happen. Not when it was so easy to suppress.

Like a lake’s worth of water dumped on a single ember, his aura devoured theirs hungrily, leaving absolutely no trace of them having ever existed. With Eik’s immaculate aura control, the violent passage of his Ak’ki went unnoticed by the struggling crowd, the haze of blue-tinted energy instead bathing them thoroughly in a soothing wave that seemed to revitalize them to an even healthier and more sprightly condition than they had been in before being harassed by the Gohkamorian outbursts.

“Preposterous!” roared the largest of the Gohkamorians—the same man who had spoken up once earlier—spittle flying from his mouth like a garden sprinkler. “Gohkamoria will not stand for such a farce! Surrender ourselves and let you annihilate us? I have never heard anything more absurd!”

Well, this was about how Eik had expected the first few minutes of this thing to go. Lots of anger, lots of disbelief, and lots of twisting of words. And, funnily enough, that was also how he expected the end to be. All the way up until he got what he asked for.

Gohkamoria was used to bullying those they stepped on into submission, but unfortunately for them, they were going to find Eik considerably less receptive to such tactics. No, this time they would see what it was like to be stepped on.

Eik was very serious.

“And nonetheless, those are the terms we of Earth have decided on,” Eik said calmly. He could not afford to be the one to lose his cool in this. “Although, you have somewhat misconstrued my words. Our demands are not for the entirety of the Gohkamorian population to surrender. We are only asking for those responsible for the invasion, harassment, suppression, and massacre of nearly all humans of Earth.”

“What?” The enormous man’s voice boomed through the park.

“There is only one scenario in which all of your citizens will suffer and perish,” Eik clarified. “And that is if you decide that your own life is more important and worthy of preservation than those of your people. Only a fraction of all Gohkamorians will pay for your crimes if you simply make it so. We will spare all others.”

“You can’t do that!”

“I think you will find that our terms are exceedingly favorable,” Eik pointed out before his voice dropped into something closer to a whisper, which was only carried to the ears of all listeners, both those seated and those watching from afar, by the strength and breadth of his aura. Despite his attempt to keep a neutral tone, ice cold malice still managed to creep in. “If you take even a moment to consider what you have done to us, it will become clear just how benevolent we are.”

“Just as we have done until now, we vehemently maintain that the accusation you have made against us has yet to be proven factual and real! We are innocent of the crimes you have described!”

“We have already submitted proof which has been widely accepted as real and sufficient. All that’s left is to punish you for it.”

“Maybe so, but a court of law has yet to make a judgment.”

“I don’t care.”

“What?” the giant Gohkamorian spluttered. “You have to care!” Obvious by his voice, anger was beginning to give way to desperation and fear.

At this point, the eyes of many of the Gohkamorians had made a few rounds of the other people present who were from different civilizations, and there wasn’t much sympathy to find there. Although they might well be among the most severe cases, Earth wasn’t exactly the first world that had been mistreated by the Gohkamorians one way or the other.

By nature, Gohkamorians were ill-tempered and warlike and that seldom translated well into amicable relationships or business partnerships with other races. There were outliers among the brutish aliens, of course, and Eik had even met a few of them himself, in fact, but it wasn’t particularly common.

“And why, pray tell, do I have to care?” Eik asked, leaning over the lectern on his elbows.

“Because…” the Gohkamorian leader began and hesitated. “Because your world, and in extension you, is a member of the Nidafjeld Alliance! And as a world enjoying the benefits of membership, you are required to abide by the laws of the Alliance.”

“But you never did, did you?”

“Again, that has never been proven in an official capacity!”

Eik stared, eyes cold and unfeeling, as the Gohkamorian leader continued to frantically defend his position against a man who just didn’t seem to care. “Once more, I fear you are operating under a dire misconception, my good man,” he said, a lilt of humor coming into his voice, even if it barely showed in his expression.

Eik thought the Gohkamorian representative might well have ripped out his own hair if he had had any. Several times, his eyes flicked around, seemingly unable to believe that nobody was calling out this blatant disregard for proper conduct. The large man forced a strained smile onto his lips. “And what might that misconception be?”

“That I, and in extension Earth, have to abide by anything,” he said. In addition to the official Gohkamorian delegation in the front seats, many common Gohkamorians were in attendance further back, naturally interested in this matter that concerned them and their world. Eik’s gaze swept across them, counting more than a thousand scattered here and there. Nearly all of them looked afraid.

“I know I have been calling this a request, but I will gladly call it an ultimatum if that will make the reality of it penetrate that thick, stubborn skull of yours. This will happen because I say it will.” It was a straightforward statement that carried with it the weight of the universe.

“This is abuse! You’re practically pretending to be a god!” The fury was back—the only sense of a defense he could muster.

In an instant, Eik was in front of him, the S-ranked giant incapable of even sensing that anything was happening before Eik's face was centimeters from his. Undiluted rage warped Eik’s face into an ugly visage that he was glad his children weren’t in attendance to see.

A transparent mask of ethereal blue shimmered into existence in front of his face, unsettling patterns swirling in and out of visibility on its surface.

In that same moment, a figure of pure blue rose high into the air. It didn’t stop growing until the crown upon its titanic head reached a height of over four hundred meters. Although Eik himself was forced to put his head back to look up at the Gohkamorian, the situation was very much reversed when the avatar of the Monarch stared down at the alien, its deep hood obscuring all features but the glittering glow of its eyes.

Eik’s voice was distorted almost beyond recognition.

“To you, Gohkamorian, I might as well be,” he hissed, the words traveling to every ear within a couple of kilometers.

“O-Okay, please! If we could please have some order,” the man dispatched by the judicial authority shouted. He sounded utterly devoid of confidence in his own ability to rein in an S-ranker and an X-ranker. His eyes kept flitting up at the aura Monarch, but unlike the Gohkamorian down below, he wasn’t feeling any of the hostile pressure.

It felt more like it had when Eik had been named Elixir King. Like a warm embrace. It was the only reason he even dared to raise his voice.

And, again, before the S-ranked Gohkamorian could realize what was happening, Eik reappeared on the stage behind the lectern, the avatar of the Monarch dissipating into motes of azure.

“I apologize,” he said earnestly, voice still traveling far as he bowed to both the crowd and the judicial representative. “My emotions took control for a moment there.”

“Ah, no, that’s…” the man trailed off, unsure of how to react. “that’s… you know…”

The large Gohkamorian’s face was frozen in terror, his muscles paralyzed to the point where he hadn’t even been able to fall to his knees. The rest of them had skipped the knees entirely and fallen straight on their asses.

Addressing everybody present, Eik spoke again. “I am aware of how Earth’s demands may look at first glance. Ruthless revenge, to hell with rule and law. No?” he said with an easy smile. “And you know what? I can’t deny that that is one part of it. Out of my whole family, only my father wasn’t killed by monsters bred by Gohkamoria. I don’t know of a single friend who survived. Humanity is a fraction of what it was. We’re a broken people. So do I want to exterminate every last one of them?” he asked rhetorically. Spectators swallowed as he drew out the moment.

“A part of me says yes. A part of me thinks that, now that I am a supreme being, why shouldn’t I have every right to exercise my will to the fullest? As I have so often heard in the Unified Mass, the strong trample the weak, no? And I am strong,” he said with a sigh and pointed down at the Gohkamorians. “And they are no—”

“Only you!” the Gohkamorian suddenly roared. Seeing his chance of survival dwindle to zero regardless of the outcome, he must have been lashing out like a cornered animal. What else could he really do? “Only you are strong! The rest of Earth is still fresh! No matter how strong you are, a single man can’t protect an entire planet! If you continue this, it’s open war! You think we don’t know about your friends? Your family? Your children?” the man snarled. “It will only take a moment of carelessness!”

Like flames, bright, blue aura blazed from Eik’s eyes as True Toxic Harmony triggered along with all other personal buffs, the reinforced stage shattering from the sheer output of potential energy.

Abruptly, a leviathan world serpent blocked out the sky as it hung from the heavens. Eik really did sound like a wrathful god as he spoke.

“So you will be the first to fall.”
