The doors of Prator’s mansion were guarded by two men, wearing some kind of old fashioned armor. Elion had never seen anything like it, and wondered if it had been made pre-cataclysm. The guards admitted Bortim and Elion.

Bortim crossed the hall and knocked on a door.

“Enter!” Prator’s voice called.

“Stay here,” Bortim said to Elion. He entered the room.

Elion listened at the door.

“Welcome Bortim,” Prator said. “Arnolfo and I were just completing our agreement. I am glad you are here to bind it. The agreed upon sum—”

A clatter of gemstones being dumped out of a bag punctuated Prator’s words. Elion could hear a soft scraping noise, and men’s voices muttering numbers as the stones were grouped and counted.

A heavily accented voice spoke. Elion guessed it was Arnolfo. “A pleasure doing business with you,” he said. “These are some of my finest creations, and I hope you use them well.”

“If I had my way, I’d never have to use them at all,” Prator said. “But things being the way they are, with an army gathering on our doorstep, well, I’d rather be safe than sorry.”

“Are we ready to proceed?” Bortim asked. “Excellent, please join hands and we will complete the contract.”

A clap of hands joining, and a few muttered words from Bortim sufficed for the ritual. Elion wondered about the Red Judge’s role in the contract.

Elion looked at the door. Nothing preventing him from leaving here. The guards probably would not stop him if he tried.

Footsteps approached the door. Elion backed away, then deposited himself in a plush leather seat against the wall nearby. He kept his hood up, shading his face.

The door opened.

“A pleasure doing business with you,” Prator said, leading Arnolfo, into the room. Bortim followed. Elion kept his head bowed, and only saw their legs as they walked past. Prator escorted Arnolfo to the door, exchanged pleasantries, and showed the man out.

As the door closed, Elion saw the man mount one of the arachnatronics out front.

Prator waited for the door to close fully behind him, then flashed a bright smile at Elion. Elion straightened in his seat, meeting the man’s gaze.

“My spy,” Prator said. “What have you discovered for me?”

Elion grimaced at the characterization of himself as a spy.

“Who is that woman?” he asked. “What is wrong with her?”

Prator looked disappointed. “She hasn’t said anything to you?”

“She said a lot of things,” Elion said. “Like ‘tip tap the treasure chest,’ or, ‘we both control stars on my face.’ I don’t know what any of it means,” Elion said.

“Maybe she’s still warming up to you,” Prator said. “I know you’ll get something out of her.”

“Maybe,” Elion agreed. “As long as we’re working together, I’d like to ask you a question.”

“Of course. What is it?”

“What did Dorian do? I mean, I know he was once an Aurelian, but is no longer. I heard that he betrayed his oaths. What does that mean?”

“An excellent question. You are a thoughtful student, Elion.” Prator massaged his beard, pondering his answer.

“You recall the oaths you made at the Altar?”

“I do,” Elion said. “Stand as a shield, defend the truth, save my friends.”

“That’s not quite right,” Prator said. “The precise phrasing of the oaths matter. Do you know them?”

Elion recalled the words spoken at the Altar. The experience had been a blur, but the words stuck with him, impressed upon his mind with the power of Aurelia.

“I will stand as a shield to your people. I will wield my blade in defense of the truth. I will fight to save my friends.”

“And my enemies,” Prator added, finishing the oath. “People try to leave that portion off sometimes, but its important.”

Elion nodded, eager for instruction and clarification.

“The oaths bind every Aurelian, and are connected to your power and abilities. Texts have been written about the specific phrasing of the oaths and the concepts contained within. Serious disagreements have lead to schisms within the ascendency, but that is not important here. The important thing for you to know is that violating the oaths leads to a withdrawal of your abilities.”

Elion thought about the oaths, and wondered how Prator’s behavior aligned with them. The man lied, betrayed, and enslaved. Elion felt like these things were polar opposite to the values espoused in the oaths.

Elion himself had done things since swearing the oaths which he felt were in violation.

Prator read Elion’s expression. “You don’t believe that I am honoring my oaths,” Prator said.

“I don’t,” Elion agreed.

“Then I’m lucky that you aren’t the judge,” Prator said. “Life is complicated, and sometimes it is challenging to know the correct course of action. Especially when choices must be made, when the oaths appear to conflict.”

“Who is the judge, then?” Elion asked.

“Aurelia,” Prator said. “And, so far as I have been able to understand, she does not expect a perfect foreknowledge from us. She merely askes for our honest effort.”

“That’s pretty… squishy,” Elion said. “A lot of bad things have been done by people who claimed to mean well.”

“A lot of books, as I said, for you to read about this conversation. But, to answer your question, there does seem to be one, unequivocal thing which Aurelia frowns upon. Abandoning your oaths, like Dorian.”

“You mean…” Elion turned these words over in his mind. “You mean giving up on them?”

“Yes, something like that. Walking away from them. Forgetting them. No longer considering them as you—”

A sharp knock at the door interrupted him.

“Take him,” Prator hissed at Bortim, but the door burst open, and Venya strode into the room. Her heels clicked across the stone floor, legs flashing, black hair bouncing. Elion’s breath caught, and he shifted slightly, allowing his hood to fall over his face.

“What do you think you’re doing here?” Venya demanded. “Holding out on me?”

Elion gripped the armrest of the seat. Venya was not looking in his direction.

Prator laughed. “To what do I owe the pleasure of your visit?” he asked. “Not that I mind your beautiful visage gracing these halls.”

“For days I’ve been sending my people into your caves. They’ve been wandering about aimlessly in the darkness, and I only now learn that you have spend the last year mapping every tunnel?”

“Still having trouble locating your fugitive?” Prator asked.

“Don’t mock me,” Venya snapped. “We know he’s in your town somewhere. Or beneath it.”

“You have some kind of Warlock tracking spell?” Prator asked.

“Yes, something like that.”

“But it only tracks in two dimensions?”

“That’s an oversight, which is being remedied as we speak.” Elion shifted in his seat, allowing his hood to rise slightly. He caught a better view of Venya. She stood with her back toward him, her curls jostling as she gestured angrily.

“Right now I need your maps. You should be glad I’m bothering to ask.”

Prator chuckled nervously. “Of course,” he said. “Though you’ll have to use them here. If you would share more information with me, I could be more helpful. If I had known you were trying to search the tunnels—”

“Stop being intentionally obtuse,” Venya said. “Give me your map. We’ll flush the boy out and then leave you to your squalor.”

Prator nodded to Bortim, who scuttled up the stairs. Venya paced the room, pushing her hands through her hair. She drew closer to Elion. He kept his eyes down, his hood low.

“Step over to this reading device,” Prator said. “We’ll observe the maps here.”

Venya walked away from Elion again.

“This is an ancient Artefin projection reader,” Prator said. “We salvaged it here. It may even be pre-cataclysm.”

“I didn’t come here for idle chat, Prator,” Venya said.

“I’m merely telling you this for your information. If you would like a copy of the maps, I can provide that. But unless you have your own reader, they won’t be of much use to you.”

“If I need to take your maps, I’ll take your reader too,” Venya spat.

Prator groaned. “Readers are really quite valuable, and rare. If you transport it you risk damaging the equipment, and—”

“Don’t worry about it. We’ll view the maps here. But if your guards try to refuse entrance to me or my warlocks again, I’ll—”

Her threat was cut off by Bortim returning. In his hands he carried a metal box, about the size of a hard drive. He crossed the room to the projection reader and slid the box into a receptacle. The device powered up, projecting a three dimensional teal wireframe of the tunnels beneath Kairn Tol.

By manipulating knobs on the device, Prator scrolled around and gave Venya a brief tour of the tunnels. Venya’s anger melted away as he demonstrated the controls.

“The analogue controls may be somewhat antiquated for your tastes,” Prator explained, “But I prefer them. They’re more tactile, and easier to fix.”

“You’ll grant access to these maps for my warlocks,” Venya asserted. “We’re closing in on the boy, and with this, we’ll be able to get him. We’ve been tracking his movements, triangulating his route through the tunnels. I’ll send my technician over to see if we can match his route to a path underground.”

“Of course,” Prator said. “Anything you need.”

“You have been quite accommodating.” Venya pressed her index finger against his chest. “And I regret my stay here has been so frantic. I should have liked to… relax a little more.”

“You are welcome here any time,” Prator said, flashing his winning smile at the energetic woman.

“A skyskimmer is coming for us.” Venya said. “I’ve heard rumors of an army gathering?”

“As have I,” Prator said. “They seem to have come down out of Upprifer. My outriders haven’t been able to determine if there’s any connection to the explosion near Aterfel.”

“We don’t want to get involved in any local squabbles,” Venya said. “As soon as we find the boy, we’ll be gone. You just keep the Thrandicite flowing; don’t get distracted by the fighting. Zelian knows we pay you enough.”

Prator placed a warm hand on Venya’s shoulder, turning her toward the door. “Of course.” He slid his hand down to the small of her back, leading her away from the projection reader.

“There’s also the matter of the girl,” Venya said, stopping. “We thought he’d be with her, but he seems to have abandoned her to Botim’s slavers.”

Elion’s pulse quickened at the mention of Keyla.

“We prefer the term ‘owned.’”

“She was involved in the attack on your person,” Venya said. “We’ll deal with her, and let you know anything we discover.”

“Thank you.” Prator opened the door, and Venya strode out into the night.
