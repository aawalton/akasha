I will go with him, Goldie sent in a simple, resolute tone.

“It is not safe,” Rosslyn said. “Please, the faster you give this up, the sooner Frederick can receive care.”

The conversation would have been marginally easier for her if she could make eye contact with Goldie.

Do not hold your breath, the spider sent. I will never give up. Transport Frederick, and cease worrying about me.

“If you are discovered—”

You are not easily capable of discovering where I am, Goldie replied. The Empire’s minions will be less so.

The spider had shrunk down while Carolien was out of the cottage and concealed herself somewhere on Frederick’s person, and now Goldie was refusing to be removed. It probably would have been easy enough to forcefully remove her—she was just a little spider, at the end of the day, and mana could only enhance her physical strength so much—if only Rosslyn and Carolien could figure out where she was.

They had quickly patted Frederick down over his clothing already, with no trace of the spider. Even if they strip-searched him, it was no guarantee that she would not sneak back onto his body immediately after. The mystic beasts were very good at concealing themselves.

Rosslyn glared at Adon, who showed no reaction. He had remained almost silent this whole time, passively refusing to help them. She knew he quietly agreed with Goldie’s choice. If he had wanted to, he could have located her and stopped her from doing this. He also likely knew exactly where she was already, since he had been the only one present when she hid herself.

“Would Frederick truly want you to do this?” the Queen asked finally, exasperated. “Risking both your lives does not make him safer.”

Don’t try to throw what Frederick would want in my face, Goldie replied, her tone sharp. I’m not doing what anyone else would want today. I am making decisions for myself. Frederick, Adon, and you are the only people I have left who matter to me. I will not let Frederick be taken away and thrown into the lion’s den alone. I understand why you want to send him to get medical help, and I won’t try to stop that. But I won’t leave his side either. When he has been treated, if the Empire doesn’t let him go, you might be glad that I stayed with him. Otherwise, they will have no reason to think I’m anything more than a normal bug.

Rosslyn growled quietly to herself. Goldie might be right. The problem was that other possibility, that the Empire would find her and destroy her, or do even worse.

She was going to attempt to argue again, when there was a knock at the door.

Sergeant Angus poked his head in. “Your Majesty, my neighbor has loaned me his plow horse for the mission. I believe everything is prepared.”

The Queen nodded. “Thank you, Sergeant.” She turned back to Frederick’s unconscious body. “Very well, Goldie. I wish you a safe journey. I cannot stop you. At least this plan is less reckless than what Adon had proposed.”

Perhaps the Empire would not search the Dessian’s body for signs of additional creatures. They would have little reason to expect the Kingdom to stupidly commit one of its only remaining strategic assets to such a nigh suicidal mission.

Angus stepped forward, moving to where Frederick lay, correctly taking this cue to come and collect the body.

As he stooped to pick the young lord up, however, a noise broke the stillness of the hamlet air. At first, it was just small enough to make the Sergeant pause and cause Rosslyn to turn her head.

Then it became impossible for anyone to miss it.

The sound was the slowly growing, steadily advancing noise of hoofbeats.

“The Empire has discovered us,” Rosslyn said, groaning quietly.

“You can still hide, Your Majesty,” Sergeant Angus began. “The forest has many secret places.”

Rosslyn, we can do the same thing we did to fly out of the dungeon to escape, Adon sent. I’ll assume a form large enough to carry you, and you fuel me with enough mana to keep my wings strong and steady.

That seemed like a more realistic plan to the Queen, and she was just about to say so, when Carolien spoke up.

“Are those horses coming from the right direction?” she asked, raising an eyebrow.

Rosslyn frowned. She had almost lost track of the relative directions of the respective landmarks, because they were indoors, and she wasn’t entirely certain she could tell the direction the sound of hoofbeats originated from anyway.

But perhaps the Royal Family was finally due for a little good luck.

“Adon, could you please go outside invisibly, and tell us what you see?” she asked in a quiet, tight voice.

Do not hope, she told herself. Not yet.

Her heart clung to whatever twig of driftwood it could find to keep afloat in the sea of troubles that assailed her, despite her admonitions.

Maybe that was not Imperial cavalry slowly drawing nearer outside. Perhaps those were the horses of a friendly army. The Kingdom had called upon its allies to defend it, early on in the invasion. Perhaps, at long last, some power had answered the call.

I’ll be right back, Adon sent.

The butterfly seemed to simply vanish from where he had been perched a moment before, effortlessly affirming that sending him was the right choice.

As long as he is not dragging an unconscious human with him, it would be very difficult to know he is around, let alone to capture him.

The mission to save Frederick would have required speaking to the Empire’s representatives, letting them know what was wrong with the young lord, so that Adon escorting the Dessian still would not have made sense in Rosslyn’s mind, but he was certainly an ideal scout.

A tense minute passed inside the cottage. Everyone looked at everyone else.

They were all wondering the same things: Do we need to hide, or should we be running? If we hunker down here, will the Empire come looking? Did someone betray us? What will Adon see?

I’m back, Adon sent. He reappeared in almost the same place he had vanished from, just a different side of the table he had perched on previously.

“Is it the Empire?” Rosslyn asked.

I don’t think so, he replied immediately. Unless they’ve disguised themselves. The symbol on their banner is an eagle.

“Parmonia!” exclaimed both Carolien and Rosslyn at the same moment.

The stepmother and stepdaughter looked at each other, then let out a shared, nervous laugh.

“They came through,” Rosslyn said after a moment. She took a step away from Frederick.

And you used to call the people of that country boors, she thought but did not say, recalling how Carolien had often spoken of Parmonia and its leadership.

“What does this mean?” asked Sergeant Angus, breaking the odd tension that had settled in the room.

“First, it means a reprieve for you, Sergeant,” Rosslyn said. “Now you will remain a very brave and loyal but very retired Sergeant, not someone whose fate I worry over at night. Parmonia has brought its cavalry to our aid. They may have brought gifted healers as well. Even if they have not, they will have messengers who can approach the Empire’s lines with official protection from the Kingdom of Parmonia. The Empire will not treat such a person roughly, since they are not currently at war with Parmonia, and since they do not act contemptuously of a strong power.”

The Queen left unspoken what the Empire’s treatment of Claustria said about them, and she walked outside without further delay.

In the distance, she could see the eagle banners.

Acting in a less than regal manner, she started running. Frederick was still on borrowed time. Every minute could make the difference.

She felt it when Adon caught up with her.

I’ve got your back if you need it, he sent.

Rosslyn gave him a warm feeling back in return. She was getting the hang of his use of Telepathy and understanding how she could shape her own thoughts to communicate with him effectively.

Thank you, she thought. I will certainly be all right, but thank you.

They drew so close that she could suddenly see the nearest horse, far ahead of the men holding the banners, ten feet away through a few trees.

“Halt!” Rosslyn called. “Horsemen of Parmonia, halt!”

There was a sudden commotion at the front of the advancing group as the cavalry officers nearest to the Queen’s voice tried to stop and passed the word back.

“Who goes there?” called a loud voice from among the cavalry. “Who commands the Winged Knights of Parmonia to halt?”

That was Rosslyn’s cue.

She stepped out from within the trees.

“The new Queen of Claustria,” she said in a loud, clear voice, addressing the various figures who towered over her on horseback. “I am pleased and relieved to see that our stalwart friends have come to our aid in our hour of direst need.” She stopped in the middle of what she was planning to say as she noticed a different set of banners in the back, mixed in with the eagle banners.

That is the symbol of the Goddess, she thought.

The image was that of a woman embracing the planet. There were variations where the woman was cradling the planet to her womb, or where the planet was a baby bump as if the Goddess was about to give birth to all of life.

But only the High Church could use that symbol in its official business. No nation would dare to appropriate it for its own purposes.

Who exactly is traveling with this group of cavalry?

The men were well-equipped, she now saw, covered in thick layers of shining plate armor and armed to the teeth with multiple weapons to each knight. Many of the Winged Knights had actual metal wings affixed to their armor or their horses’ armor, to do with their magical specialty, and it seemed as if the entirety of that famous group was here, along with additional cavalry that lacked their specific training.

The large number of knights—Rosslyn could not come close to counting or estimating their number, both because of how many there were and because of the forest blocking her view—had all stopped in their tracks as she spoke, and now the Queen saw a single man dismount from a horse near the back.

The figure was tall and regal, and he matched the portraiture she had seen of the new King of Parmonia, Janus. His thick, droopy mustache, broad shoulders, and salt and pepper hair were all immediately recognizable.

As he approached, she steeled herself just to be able to talk to a fellow ruler effectively. She had never done this as a head of state before, and though she would normally be calm in such a situation, the stakes had never been this high in any conversation she’d ever had.

If she said the wrong thing, King Janus might just take his knights and march them back to Parmonia, leaving her and Frederick to fend for themselves.

You’ve got this, Adon sent. You’re not like me!

That almost made Rosslyn laugh, which left her feeling a little better than she had expected by the time the figure of the King loomed over her.

“What, pray tell, is the Queen of Claustria doing in the middle of nowhere?” asked a heavily accented voice, not without sympathy. “For that matter, Your Majesty, what has become of the King? The man I signed a pact of alliance with?”

Rosslyn swallowed. That was a question she had hoped to answer later.

“We have suffered a terrible loss,” she said. “I will explain it in fuller detail later, but my father, the late King, is dead. The Empire has seized the capital. More urgently, the heir to the Dessian throne is with us, and he is suffering severely from poisoning the Empire’s assassins afflicted. We need help. Do you have healers? His older brother, William, already died of the same poison.”
