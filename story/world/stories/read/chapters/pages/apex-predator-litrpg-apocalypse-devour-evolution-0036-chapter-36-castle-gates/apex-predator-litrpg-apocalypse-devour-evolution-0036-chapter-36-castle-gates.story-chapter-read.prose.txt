Don took meticulous care with the setup for his nap. He backtracked several platforms and leveraged his Stealth and Camouflage to make sure nobody saw him enter a windowless hut by the cavern wall. He started gathering around spears and other items he could use to bar the door. It wasn’t foolproof, but he figured it was literally the safest he could be. The only enemies here were the Grimoids. And he could kill them even if they attacked him en masse, surprise as an advantage or not.

If he exited the dungeon, for one he wasn’t sure he could return. And second, the outside was unpredictable. And third, it would waste time, which Don found categorically unacceptable. He would sleep as much as his body needed right here right now and then he would continue.

So he barred the door and set himself up in a little corner. He closed his eyes and listened, but everything was quiet. Only the drums beat steadily in the upper cavern. Nothing moved outside. Don would trust his Survival Instinct to wake him up if danger came. But right now he needed rest. Within minutes Don drifted into a deep slumber.

*

When Don woke he was feeling amazing. He had no idea how long he had slept, but it didn’t really matter. Now he could keep going for all day or even days again. It was really amazing. Back in the old world with his regular nine to five and all kinds of phone addictions and bad diets and what have you, Don’s sleep had been kind of shitty. He would spend too much time in bed watching nonsense and wonder why he was feeling groggy the next morning.

Now everything was clear and serene. Don felt his body. No back pain. Absolutely none. That was such a boon. Sure, there were no more mobile games and hot showers, but overall this new reality was a win.

He had no pains, aches, or stiffness what-so-ever. His body felt amazing. Fast, powerful, flexible, ready. Ready to hunt. Don didn’t want to waste another second. He just kicked open the shack door, blasting his barricade into an explosion of wooden chunks and he started making his way to the shanty castle.

Everything looked just the same. The dungeon was an underground cavern with no sunlight. Only the smoky braziers and torches lit it. The drums still beat steadily.

Don moved close to the castle gates with careful stealth. There were two of the yellow painted larger Grimoids guarding it, equipped with chain vest and long spear. As far as Grimoid standards went, that was basically a whole ass armory.

Both of the guards were the level 12 Hobgoblin variant. Don really wanted to see if he could take two of them on.

This would be the highest level monster he had faced. But he could use a challenge. Ever since the bears, he hadn’t been really tested. He had a feeling this castle would be a great opportunity to push his skills even further.

Because it wasn’t just the levels. The System governed a large part of his progress. But there were other things. Confidence, experience, his ability to enforce his will. Don had learned that the System really rewarded going the extra mile. Pushing through pain and discomfort and staying in the fight. If Don didn’t keep pushing, he would stagnate. And he would not let himself do that.

Jacques and Blazeclaw awaited. And if he failed that particular test, Mal’asha would eat him.

Yup… I think I have my external and internal motivation pretty spelled out. Time to kill stuff.

Don edged closer to the castle, staying low behind the railing of a wide rope bridge that led straight to the front gate. The two hobgoblins hadn’t moved. They weren’t bored, or distracted, or squabbling like the regular Grimoids. They stood still, silent, scanning the darkness with their yellow eyes. Waiting.

Don crept within thirty feet. Then stood.

No use testing stealth against guards that weren’t going anywhere. Besides, he wanted a fight.

He walked forward, casual, claws flicked out.

The hobgoblins saw him immediately. They didn’t shriek or charge. They simply shifted stances and raised their spears into a high ready. Not like monsters. Like trained soldiers.

Don didn’t stop.

The first hobgoblin moved. No noise. Just speed. It lunged forward with its spear and kept its distance, testing his range.

Don batted the spearhead aside with Telekinesis and closed the gap. He slashed low—aimed for the legs—but the creature jumped back and circled left. It didn’t overcommit. Didn’t flinch.

The second one came in from the right.

This one didn’t lunge. It swept the spear in a fast horizontal slash, trying to trip him.

Don jumped, but it was bait.

The first hobgoblin was already moving back in. Its spear shot forward—Don twisted mid-air and the blow sliced across his ribs. It didn’t go deep, but it went clean.

[Health at 89%]

Don grunted, but the pain was manageable. He landed on one foot, crouched, and bolted toward the first attacker. The second hobgoblin followed close, weapon ready.

They’re trying to herd me.

Don faked a turn, then shot backward with quick steps and caught the second hobgoblin flat-footed. He grabbed the spear shaft with one hand, anchored it in place with Telekinesis, and yanked the creature forward. It stumbled, and Don drove his knee into its gut.

The thing didn’t go down.

Don followed with a slash to the neck, but the hobgoblin raised its shield at the last second. Don’s claws screeched off the metal. They bit into it and sliced solid chunks off the shield, but it held.

Damn. These guys are tough.

The first hobgoblin was already behind him. Don dove sideways and rolled. The spear sliced the air above his back.

He came up grinning.

“Alright,” he muttered. “This is more like it.”

Don rushed forward and swung low. This time his claws bit flesh. The first hobgoblin hissed and struck back, fast. Don barely avoided a spear through the gut. He redirected it with Telekinesis and moved inside the creature’s guard.

He slashed again.

The hobgoblin twisted with him, using its body weight to smother the motion. It was fighting like someone who’d sparred before. No wasted movement. Brutal efficiency.

The second hobgoblin wasn’t far. Don heard its footsteps and spun, throwing a blowpipe dart behind him with his mind. It wasn’t fast enough to kill, but it staggered the creature.

He used the moment.

Don drove both claws into the side of the first hobgoblin. It screamed and thrashed, but he pulled his arms apart and ripped something loose.

[You have slain [Grimoid Hobgoblin – lvl 12] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 41 System Points earned]

Don turned to face the other one. It had recovered. Its face was twisted with rage now. It threw down its spear and drew a pair of curved blades from its back. Scimitars, well made too. And most importantly…

Fast ones.

The hobgoblin flourished his blades and swung them around in tight arcs around his body. He was clearly skilled with the blades.

Don didn’t wait.

He sprinted in, faked high, then dropped into a slide. The hobgoblin struck down, but Don was already past its legs. He spun, caught one foot with Telekinesis, and tripped it mid-pivot.

He didn’t go for the kill. Not yet.

Don grabbed the Grimoid by the leg with his hand and hurled it against the castle gate with all of his psychic force combined with his powerful physique.

It smashed into the wood, cracking it loudly, and dropped to the floor.

Don was on it before it could recover. He planted a knee on its chest and grabbed its wrists before it could slash. The curved blades trembled in its grip.

“Bad day for you,” Don said.

The hobgoblin struggled, but it was on its back, and Don was stronger. Through his hands he routed his psychic energy into his prey. Don watched the hobgoblin’s ugly face, as it got more groggy and drowsy. The eyelids drooped and mouth hung slack within a minute.

Don let go of the creature’s wrists. It didn’t try to get up. It was just lying on the ground in a psychic energy induced stupor.

Don Devoured it.

Don stood and rolled his shoulders.

Blood dripped from his claws. His cut side was knitting whole again from the power of the life force he had just drained. His adrenaline was high. Not panic. Not fear. Just focus. Pure predatory intent.

He liked these new enemies. They filled him with thrill. He wanted to fight more.

They were fast. Disciplined. Coordinated. But he had the edge in power and speed. If there had been more of them, it might have gone another way.

And I bet there are more inside.

His more cerebral part kept repeating a mantra to his more primal self.

“Be careful, be careful, be careful.”

But the thrill was overwhelming. Don wanted to see if he could take on three, or maybe even four of these. He wanted to push himself.

Fine. We’ll be careful if there’s more than five.

A blood drunk grin on his face, Don stepped over the bodies, pushed open the wooden gates, and walked into the castle.

“Alright,” he said. “Let’s see what else you’ve got.”
