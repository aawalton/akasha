Another coordinated volley of destructive radiance from the elves bloomed, and two more giants collapsed in a heap. Both sides began to adjust. The giants suddenly broke away from their occupation with the elite groups to come stomping for the elven pockets. Magic began to spawn deeper in the ranks. Fireballs reigned down in the direction of the elves and were blocked.

“Prioritize protecting the Radiancers at all costs,” Dax shot into the army mental gestalt. “Elites, delay those giants by any means necessary. Radiancers, you need to fall back and fire, fall back and fire, if you want to live through this.”

Samantha had the vegetation conjured up by Redberry & Ilk set on fire and otherwise left, as they needed to turn their focus to helping with the much more imminent threat posed by the giants. Thankfully, they weren’t at all lined up to properly converge on the new threat to them, as the elves were much in the same direction, while the giants were spread out to cause their army-wide carnage.

The nearest giants went down to Zadkiel and Gelmak as they coordinated, tripping them up with their comparatively tiny forms, but still nearly equal raw strength. Another minotaur had some sort of super-strong lariat with which he wrapped a moving giant’s leg, thereafter getting the aid of his brothers to heave-ho the giant to the ground with a crash. Redberry & Ilk came in and entangled a few more.

All the while, the Radiancers were blasting the giant undead, retreating, blasting more, and so on. The magic of the Dominion wizards came down on them, but the Counter Crew was sacrificing the greater mass to keep all damage away from the fragile elven specialists.

That includes Samantha, using all her actions to interrupt blooming spells or block them out of the air. She zipped around as needed. With the 1-minute cooldown on [Reflect Force], she decided to start using that, too.

Some of the attacks were fast, arcing physical ‘fire bombs’, essentially, the likes of which needed physical spells of one or another kind to block, rather than being capable of getting directly counterspelled. They could also be hit with a dispel, but were resistant out of the range of all but the strongest, such as her. Judicious and watchful eyes were needed to organize it in real-time. The ever-watching Resemblants collated such info for them, flagging spells and members toward priorities in split-second coordination. Samantha was one of the last lines of defense, essentially, always holding one action in reserve against any failures by others, or against anything that was sneaky or too fast.

Sometimes, it was a near thing, and sometimes they fully failed, and spells exploded in the midst of soldiers. If all was still working efficiently, protections and wound negations would keep them from harm. But over time, the Dominion began to concentrate fire and overwhelm, creating casualties. Who they really wanted to protect stayed protected, but others died, and how much longer would it hold out? It was ramping.

There was an advantage, though. In increasing pockets, the Dominion’s sources were becoming clear as they had to expose their locations to focus and intensify their firepower so much.

We got too defensive against the giants, and they’re aiming to punish us. We need a counteroffensive, or this will get worse. They’re forcing this. Time to return the favor with a goddess who moves and counters like lightning!

Samantha began with a [Reflect Force] on one of the nastier of those arcing fireballs, from one of the bigger groups of magi. As they then quite predictably pivoted to block their own attack coming back at them, Samantha charged the group like a rocket. In their midst, she unleashed a storm of annihilating magic, even as she directed [Conjure] with an independent additional layer of cutting blades, and more layers of confusing and bewildering illusions.

Along with her, the phantasm of Ralli wreaked havoc with more maximized AoE elemental fireballs stacked on top, and the phantasm of Ozzy conjured a mechanical giant of her own that entered the field with a smashing double fist that blew zombies and wizards in every direction.

This was not all, however — it couldn’t be, not against wizards blooming with contingency-triggered protection magic. Mirrors spun out and released trapped and waiting reservist Servitors, the likes of which were high-level, yet seldom seen in battle: Constance, Winter, Rochefort, Raphael, Uriel, Metatron, Camael, on and on they spawned, unleashing a decidedly pent-up aggression and ingenuity in service to their goddess.

All hands on deck for this battle.

“I’ve been saving this for a dreary day!” boomed Constance, in the shapeshifted form of a terrifying, many-eyed, many-tentacled thing covered in poisonous spines. It was apparently the perfect follow-up on wizards who had dropped their guards and defenses, as she killed many in a short span. “This one will have to do!”

The Angels on tap were less stylish but no less determined, dropping light blasts, bombs, or just chopping with their swords, as suited the strategy. Their cold precision proved quite deadly.

Some war wizards persisted and struggled on doggedly, erupting with protections and slinging offensive blasts; others simply fled while they could. A rare few managed to dismiss a Servitor. Far more died, though, to the vast layers of AoE obliteration. Samantha lined up three strong sages to spend an SP on and finish with an actual [Spellfyr: Unleash]. Their spirits slipped away into the distance. Sadly, Samantha could not spare the time to sever their souls permanently, not in this situation. She had to keep moving and striking.

So she did, making herself the Avatar of Destruction on the battlefield.

Two more wizard groups were torn apart with them all together. By then, nearly every reservist Servitor was gone, her phantasms had been banished multiple times, and even Samantha took licks from eating so much of their magic. Armor was blasted away and remade. Magic scorched her as she gritted her teeth and flew right through it, storms of blades licked at her exposed limbs and bled her, and Entropic blasts grazed her, even meant as assassinating finishers from behind.

Once, that last thing. After she dodged, she turned at the would-be wizard assassin, her armor disintegrated temporarily, and wisps of dark magic played at her mostly bare, still-perfect skin, with the damaged shreds of her Battle Dress still clinging to her. She smiled. Briefly, she saw terror in his eyes.

Then she turned him into dust.

Whatever they thought of themselves, none of those she faced truly endangered her, not in her way of thinking about it, though it was perhaps a nearish thing a few times. Major wounds from their elites. An ultimate child’s play level of risk to her. How much worse had she been through already?

It came down to the ridiculous advantage of her ghostly allies, as usual: Resemblants, ever there with supporting buffs, stacking her with wound negations, temporary boosts, and immediate healing. Trying to whittle her down now would take a literal army — if you refused to send your best.

But that’s what they were doing. Trying to drain resources, uncaring of losses, and refusing to duel her properly behind their ivory tower. Perhaps they thought they were really stressing her; expending her precious resources. It would’ve been true, not very long ago. But she was too strong for that, now. She’d expended almost nothing that she didn’t have in spades. A bit of SP, and the cooldown effect on the Battle Dress, that was all.

She found herself extremely not impressed with the war wizards of Matrazere, as she slaughtered them with impunity. Even for a First Act, it felt anticlimactic. Perhaps it shouldn’t have been so surprising. She was forged in a wildfire, while they were grown in tepid pools indoors. She’d charged the inner sanctum of a lich, while they played with toys of torture in their safe room.

After the last wizard of the group dropped like a sack, head removed by searing light-lasers, her eyes scanned the field in front of her, seeking more targets…

“Your Majesty,” came the admonishing voice of Dax directly in her mind, “with all due respect, don’t you dare push the envelope practically by yourself after that. Fall back. I wish I could make that an order. Suffice to say you’ve done what was needed.”

Samantha chuckled as she replied, “That’s almost sweet talk from you.”

“I’d sing ya a bloody lullaby as sweet as ya please, if it keeps ya from doing something stupid here.”

“Deal! I’ll expect my payout when this is all over.”

“Wha-?! I-.... damn you, Samantha.” She disconnected in a huff.

Smiling smugly to herself, Samantha took a breath as she turned to her remaining three: Constance, Raphael, and Metatron. Her eyes briefly cast for threats, too, but there was nothing but a few mewling zombie heads nearby. Their AoE left the ground scorched and smoking.

She smiled grimly. “Back in your holes, then, boys and girls. I should probably keep a Mazed reserve, even reduced to only you three.”

Constance, shrinking as she returned to a normal size, struck a pose, cocking her hip and grinning Cheshire. “I’m just glad I got in on the fun for once, Queen Bee Beautiful!”

“I’d recommend stowing a few more,” Raphael said, flicking a bit of gore off his armored shoulder. “Additional support layers, especially. Sirens and Cat Siths.”

Samantha nodded. “Good idea. Jeeves can get on that then.”

She quickly Mazed them away, even as an AoE fireball exploded around her. Feeling the heat on her skin and ignoring it, she zipped away and cloaked herself in illusions, then invisibility, headed back for the actual lines of her army after her daring feats of advanced destruction. She knew she’d given plenty of relief to the Counter Crew, who’d been keeping the enemy from wiping out their precious Radiancers. They’d been trying to block too many ramping things at once in a small area, on top of dealing with the damned giants.

It was time to get repairs, recharge, and check the fallout.
