“Oh my god,” cooed Samar as she scooped up Kim Jong-un from his pillow and hugged him tightly. “He is the most adorable thing ever!”

“I’m not sure he can breathe though,” I pointed out, since the scorpion suricata was clearly struggling.

“Wow!” Samar suddenly said. “I just saw, like, a picture in my mind! Of me putting him back on the pillow!”

“That’s how the system translates his fur-vibration language,” I explained. “Basically, he politely told you to put him back.”

“Oh, sorry little guy!” She gently set him down. Then she asked, bright-eyed: “Can he understand Arabic? I mean… if I talk in Arabic?”

“Yes. The system translates your Arabic into whatever he can understand. Just keep it simple—short words, easy concepts. And touch him if you want feedback, so you’ll know if he really got it.”

Samar crouched near him, speaking slowly: “What is your favorite food?” She gasped after a moment. “Oh my god, he likes… fruit cake! This is insane, I’m actually talking with a kitty scorpion thing!”

“Yep, it’s pretty amazing,” I said. “But we need to put him in the bag now and meet Juliette and Hyun-woo at the market.”

“He really doesn’t like the bag,” Samar observed, watching his vibrations. “Do we have to?”

“I don’t think NPCs will react well if they see a monster in town,” I said. “Guards attack monsters on sight. Worst case, they decide we’re monster collaborators or some shit and throw us in jail.”

“Oh right,” she nodded. “They actually threw you in jail once, didn’t they? Was that also for something silly?”

“Uhhh…” I rubbed the back of my neck. “Not… really silly.” My cheeks burned.

Before she could push, I added quickly, “But you’re right—we should test this. There’s a forester NPC cabin near the forest edge, away from the main town. If we show him Kim Jong Un from a safe distance, we’ll see how he reacts. Safer that way.”

Samar’s face lit up. “Ohhh, like an experiment. Without risking ourselves too much. Smart.”

“Exactly. There are tons of things about NPCs I’ve wanted to test, but I’ve been too cautious. Today, we test.”

We met Juliette and Hyun-woo at the market. They’d bought food and a few pieces of cloth—looked like they were planning a picnic. I explained my NPC experiment idea, and Hyun-woo perked up.

“I too have things I want to test,” Hyun-woo admitted, rubbing his neck. “But… I’m so used to being polite and respectful. It’s hard.”

“And that’s a good instinct here,” I said. “But we need experiments. What did you have in mind?”

“Well,” he hesitated, “if you stand and block their path, they don’t push you. They’ll only attack if you’re aggressive. So… what happens if they can’t pass? What will they do?”

“I already told you,” Samar interrupted, “they’re robots. They don’t mind. You can’t inconvenience them.”

“Mm, not sure it’s that simple,” Hyun-woo murmured. I agreed.

The forester’s cabin was a small wooden shack, much closer to the treeline than to the town buildings. Perfect for our test—if things went badly, we could dash into the forest with Kim Jong Un.

First of all, we got into the cabin, with Kim Jong Un hiding in the bag.

Inside, the forester NPC greeted us warmly: “Ah, travelers. Not often I get company out here in the wilds.”

“It’s not really wilds, though,” I remarked dryly.

He ignored me and went on in his Skyrim-ish tone: “These woods provide all a man needs… if he knows where to look.”

I tried pushing conversation. “So, do you sell stuff?”

“I’ve got pelts, dried meat, and a few good arrows—take your pick.”

“Do you have cake?” Samar asked.

“Cake? This is a forester’s cabin, not a Jarl’s banquet hall.”

“Oh, that’s too bad.” She pouted.

Then he went silent, just standing there.

So I carefully let Kim Jong-un out of the bag. We all stepped back, ready to run.

The moment the NPC saw him, his entire demeanor shifted. His gaze locked on the suricata, body tense, shoulders squared. He didn’t draw a weapon, but he looked ready.

We froze. One minute. Two minutes. Eventually, he relaxed—face softening, posture returning to neutral.

“What’s your name?” I asked, trying to keep the conversation casual.

“Name’s Elrik. Means ‘oak’ in the old tongue. Suits me fine.” He answered, but his gaze stayed on Kim Jong-un, cautious.

Another minute, and he looked completely normal again.

“Step closer,” Hyun-woo suggested. I did—and Elrik instantly snapped back to alert mode. I stepped back. He relaxed again.

“Interesting,” Hyun-woo muttered. “It’s like he has a distress variable. Proximity to a monster raises it, but time without hostile action lowers it.”

“Maybe he thinks it’s tamed,” Juliette said. “What if Kim Jong-un isn’t near you?”

I placed Kim on the counter and we all stepped to the far side of the room. Elrik’s eyes tracked him, but when the suricata sat still, the NPC eventually relaxed.

“That’s huge,” I said. “We could get NPCs used to our monsters. Maybe even bring trained care bears into town.”

“Now I want to do the blocking experiment!” Samar said.

“It’s really best if you don’t,” warned Hyun-woo. “It’s dangerous—he might pull a knife.”

“It’ll be fine,” she brushed him off, already slipping behind the counter.

I quickly lifted Kim Jong-un, not wanting to add stress. Samar then pushed Elrik, very slowly, toward the end of the counter. He… let her push him.

“Hey, stop that,” I said. This wasn’t the plan. Samar wasn’t being careful at all!But Elrik let her push him all the way to the end of the counter, trapping him completely. She opened her hand to block him.

So far, nothing happened. He didn’t look alert or anything. Did NPCs react differently to a fourteen-year-old girl? Like humans do? I doubted it.

I walked to the other side of the counter. “Hey Elrik, I want to buy something. Come here.”He didn’t answer, but he started shifting in place, as if trying to walk along the counter. But he couldn’t push past Samar.

He kept moving in place, more and more.

Then he stopped and his face hardened—same alert look he’d had when he saw Kim Jong-un.“His expression changed!” Juliette warned. “Samar, out of there!”

Samar, wisely, darted aside. Elrik calmed again after a tense moment.

“Well,” I exhaled. “That’s enough testing. Let’s not push our luck.”

I gave Elrik a little wave. “Thanks, man. Next time I’ll buy some pelts, promise.”

“Make sure you have cake next time!” Samar chirped as we turned for the door.

We were already leaving when Elrik suddenly spoke again:

“If you’re heading out, I could use help with something important.”

A quest bait. Classic. Normally I ignored these, but we’d already agreed to start testing quests.

I looked at the others. Hyun-woo gave me a small nod. “Sure,” I said. “What can we help with?”

“It’s my daughter, Lori,” Elrik began. “She went missing two nights past. Off to gather herbs in the forest. Never came back. She was headed northeast, toward the old mines. I fear something’s happened.”

“Near the old mines…?” I thought. “You mean the hill with the big lone tree and all the flowers?”

“That’s the place,” he repeated flatly. “She was headed northeast, toward the old mines.”

I turned to the group. “I know where that is. It’s close, not deep in the forest. Very safe.” Probably.

Hyun-woo sighed, but nodded. “Alright. Let’s do it.”

“Don’t worry, sir,” I told Elrik in my most heroic voice. “We’ll bring your daughter back.” I thought I sounded very cool.

And so, our party of four brave adventurers—plus one possibly brave scorpion suricata—set off on our first official quest. And our first official picnic.
