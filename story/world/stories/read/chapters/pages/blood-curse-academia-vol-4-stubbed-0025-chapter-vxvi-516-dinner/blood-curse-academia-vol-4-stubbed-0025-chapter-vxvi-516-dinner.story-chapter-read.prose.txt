Chapter V.XVI (5.16) - Dinner

Unlike Kizu’s previous experience at this restaurant, back in his first semester on a date with Emilia, it was now largely vacant. A few well-dressed adults chatted quietly but were all far enough away from their reserved table that Kizu couldn’t hear a word of what they were saying. Which suited him perfectly. He had enough distractions between Ione, her mother, and Mort.

Kajima Mikoto wore a traditional kimono, though the colors were darker than most women’s styles. Unlike her daughters, Mikoto’s bangs were split, long black hair falling evenly to either side of her face. Like an adult version of Sene, she kept a barrier of seriousness up at all times and her black eyes seemed to calculate as they took everything in. Nothing appeared to escape her notice and her stoic expressions left Kizu with absolutely no idea what she thought of him. In short, the woman terrified him.

“Your family deals in the eastern silk trade?” she asked. If she approved or disapproved of his family’s business, her dry tone gave nothing away.

“Yes, ma’am,” Kizu replied. His mouth was parched but he resisted the urge to take yet another swallow of his half-drained cup of water.

“No further comment on it?” she pressed. “Are you insecure about their position in the Hon hierarchy?”

Kizu glanced at Ione, desperate for help. But his girlfriend was busy folding a napkin into a bird.

“Honestly, I spent a lot of time away from my family,” Kizu said. “I don’t know very much about the silk trade personally. My younger brother, Finn, would know more about the subject.”

Mikoto nodded slowly. A slight frown touched the edges of her lips. “Sene mentioned this. A witch’s apprentice. Then I suppose that implies your hair is permanent and not a cosmetic feature.”

“Yes. There was a brewing accident when I was a child.”

“You have his mentor locked up in the high security vault,” Ione said, unhelpfully.

“A witch in high security.” Now a true frown formed on Ione’s mother’s face. “It’s true we had someone of that description. A nameless entity known only as the crone.”

“H—had?” Kizu stuttered.

A server stopped by to take their order, interrupting their conversation. Kizu, who hadn’t even glanced at the menu in front of him until that moment, panicked and ordered a baked potato, the first thing he saw listed when he glanced down. Not exactly a sophisticated meal. It was likely on the menu to give children something to munch on while their parents ate expensive cuts of meat. Thankfully, Ione also ordered the potato, sparing Kizu from death by embarrassment alone.

After the server departed, Mikoto steepled her hands in front of her and leaned forward, eyes locked on Kizu’s face. “Your mentor disappeared from her cell the day of the Dragon assault on Shinzou Island.”

“Maybe she didn’t want to miss Kizu’s Parent’s Day performance,” Ione suggested. “Too bad she never made it. Another witch on stage would have made things infinitely more interesting.”

“Another witch?” Mikoto turned her attention to her daughter.

“Yeah. We have one in the club. But she’s on holiday right now visiting her coven in Tross for the solstice.”

“A student witch. Interesting.” Her attention returned to Kizu. “Do you know where your master disappeared to? I imagine if the borders hadn’t suddenly closed you would have already been interrogated by authorities from Hon. Any hints on safehouses or theories on how she might have slipped out of the high security unit she was confined within?”

“Ugh. Mother, stop bothering Kizu.” Ione flopped onto the back of her seat and stared up at the chandelier, as if exhausted. “Ask him about his favorite color or animal or something. You know, normal smalltalk?”

That sounded like smalltalk for a four-year-old. But the mention of animals reminded Kizu to refocus his attention on Mort. His familiar remained crouched up on a curtain rod in the hallway, watching Charles’ door. The monkey was camouflaged with illusions cast and maintained through Kizu. Over in the common room, Basil had promised to hang out and watch Charles so as to not let the other student slip away and hide the token somewhere else.

“Sorry.” Kizu shook his head, returning to the conversation in front of him. “I didn’t catch that. My mind went blank for a second.”

“Your witch upbringing clearly accounts for your place among the Student Council alongside my daughters. I imagine your knowledge of brewing eclipses even that of most modern professionals in the field. Do you have any other notable hobbies or interests?” If Mikoto thought his blunder strange, her face gave nothing away as she repeated the question.

Kizu glanced over to Ione to see if she’d give him a hint as to what her mother wanted from him, but she’d returned to creating a napkin menagerie, with newly acquired material from beneath his cutlery.

“I spend a lot of time studying,” Kizu answered, deciding that was a safe and honest route to take the conversation. “My education was a bit lopsided growing up so I’m doing my best to increase my proficiency in all subjects. But outside that I’ve been learning spatial spellcraft.”

Mikoto’s brows rose ever so slightly. On her constant stony expression that tiny gesture screamed ‘impressed’ and ‘interested.’ “Have you experimented at all with warding or extending spatial areas?”

“Um. A bit. I’ve studied the concept and have notes on the theoretical material. I even recorded the schematics of a spatially altered room. But so far all I’ve managed is to break down the wards. Not replicate them.”

“So the spatial ring you wore to our prison over the break was not a creation of your own? Shame. That would have been truly remarkable.”

Kizu blinked. “My…ring.” He raised his hand. “You know about it?”

“I keep detailed records of all confiscated objects that enter my prison.”

“Mother, stop being weird.” Ione pulled on the wings of one of her napkin birds and flattened out to a blank square. “I said to talk about your favorite colors. Not try to recruit him to help you with your prison and accuse him of smuggling.”

Kizu opened his mouth to assure the woman that he hadn’t taken offense at all and actually really enjoyed the topic of spatial spellcraft, but it was at that moment that Mort finally spotted a red-scaled Tainted boy sauntering down the dorm hallway.

Splitting his focus, Kizu watched as the boy practically skipped through the hall. He entered his dorm while Ione simultaneously argued with her mother. Mort leaped down from his hiding place and attempted to slip into the room before the door closed. Just as he reached the threshold, the wooden door slid closed and the lock clicked.

While the woman was unflappable when speaking to Kizu, that stoicism cracked when speaking to her daughter. Ione likely knew exactly how to get under her mother’s skin. Kizu prepared a few words to smooth out the conversation, but then Mort pressed his little hands against the wooden barrier between himself and his objective.

The monkey pulled on Kizu’s blood reserves and jumped.

It was a risky move. Jumping always created a popping noise. But because of Mort’s size the noise would, at the very least, be far quieter than when Kizu jumped.

“What’s that?” Charles’ eyes were wide as he scanned the room for the sudden noise. But Kizu had already assisted Mort by using his familiar as a conduit for magic and bathing him in illusionary shadows. Thankfully the room was a mess with plenty of shadows to hide in. Kizu recalled when he broke into Aoi’s pristinely clean room. Here piles of dirty clothes, discarded textbooks, and an accumulation of clutter all acted as a shield for Mort. The monkey darted behind a painting propped up against the wall. It seemed that the back hooks had broken and not been fixed yet.

“Kaga Kizu? Is that you?” Charles asked. He raised a hand and placed it over his heart, clearly checking to make certain the token remained in his jacket pocket. “I’m not giving it back. You’ll have to fight me.”

Mort didn’t respond. Though Kizu did feel a bit of excitement through the bond at the prospect of fighting the Tainted boy.

“When did you get so violent?” Kizu muttered.

“What was that?” Ione asked. “Did you just call me violent?”

“What? No!” Kizu quickly corrected himself.

“Kaga Kizu, you look rather pale,” Mikoto said. “Are you well? Is meeting with me taking a toll on your health?”

“Sorry. Just forgot to breathe for a second. Ione takes my breath away.”

There was a moment of silence and Kizu thought he might die of embarrassment. The food arrived, saving him before he felt forced to follow up on that statement.

Kizu stared down at his potato. Steam wafted up from the crispy brown skin. He raised a knife and pierced it. The quantity of steam increased tenfold as the fluffy white insides of the tuber were exposed to open air. The chefs had also used some sort of magical trickery to insert the fillings inside the potatoes. Green onions, cheese, butter, and small bits of meat. The meal might have been designed for children, but it was still made with class.

“Passable.” Mikoto nodded her head in approval after swallowing her first bite of steak.

“Nice spot, Kizu.” Ione elbowed him in the side. “You just got my mother to admit the food is tasty.”

“Ah, yeah. Basil actually suggested it to me last semester.” At Mikoto’s glance, he hurried to clarify. “Basil is my roommate and the ward of the headmaster. I grew up in a bog so I go to him for advice regarding, um, civilized things.”

“Now that I am aware of the woman who raised you, I admit I am surprised by how civilized and well-mannered you remained,” Mikoto commented. “Your parents must have left a strong impression on you as a child before the witch stole you away.”

“My sister spent most of the time raising me when I was a kid,” Kizu amended. He wasn’t about to give his parents credit for Anna’s accomplishments. “And I think most of my… less civilized memories are locked behind a mental barrier.”

“Mental magic. Interesting. I don’t suppose you were the one to place those restrictions on yourself? I could use a mind mage under my employ. Doubly so if your expertise with spatial magic is half as competent as you imply.”

“I am not dating a mind mage,” Ione said, exasperated. “And Kizu’s a pro at space magic. He’s always hopping about and creating barriers. He trained under Wakino. But regardless, stop trying to hire my boyfriend.”

“Does he not aspire for gainful employment after graduation? He already implied that his brother is a better successor for his family business. And an individual whom both of my daughters have spoken highly of the talent of is a rarity.”

Ione’s face darkened. “What did Sene say?”

“Only positive attributes. She commended his work ethic and—”

Kizu shoveled a big mouthful of potatoes in his mouth and let his focus bounced back to Mort as the monkey watched Charles from the shadows. After his initial scare from Mort’s entrance, the Tainted boy had calmed down and had spent the last ten minutes fiddling with his scrying orb.

But now he finally moved. He tossed his uniform jacket onto the back of a chair and started rummaging through his rucksack. He pulled out a yennie purse and counted his coins. Good. This meant he likely intended to go out and celebrate tonight. Kizu had been worried that Charles might go to dinner in the cafeteria and wear his uniform. But, from the looks of it, the Tainted boy was hitting town. You didn’t need yennies for anything on campus.

Sure enough, Charles removed his academy uniform and started putting on normal civilian clothes. A permanent smirk seemed glued to the boy’s face as he got ready. He was muttering something about girls.

Kizu swallowed and took another bite of his potato before anyone could ask him a question. Beside him, Ione continued to bicker with her mother about overreach and control. It was weird seeing Ione so vocally agitated. But Kizu didn’t see a means in which to smooth the confrontation, so he continued as a silent spectator with a mouth full of food.

When Charles lifted a shirt over his face, Mort took advantage of the moment of obscured vision and scampered across the room. Now under the chair with the jacket. Mort grabbed ahold of its sleeve.

Kizu tried to silently instruct the monkey to wait until Charles left the room to loot the jacket. He was immediately rebutted by Mort’s point of view. The owl monkey saw no point in waiting and leaving more things to chance.

Siphoning a generous amount of blood from Kizu, Mort jumped.

A split second later, the monkey appeared in the restaurant with a pop. An academy issued jacket flopped to the floor beside him.
