Wind whipped against my face so hard that it left me dizzy, and I couldn’t catch my breath. No matter how hard I tried to inhale, it just felt like the oxygen passed my lips without actually entering my lungs, and my eyes were streaming. Tear flowed freely from my eyes as I plummeted, making my vision blurry, but I could still see the city rushing to meet me at what should have been a terrifying pace.

The fear didn’t quite reach me, though. It should have. I knew with utter certainty that I would die, despite the staff still clutched in my hand. There was probably some spell or something that would help, something that would make me fly or turn the ground into a giant pillow that I would catch my fall, but I didn’t know it, and it was too late for me to learn.

But that was fine. I had come to peace with what was happening. Although I didn’t remember much about the people who were hunting me, the ones who had cornered me on the scrap of land outside the maze, I had no doubt that it was better to jump than it was to stay with them.

Blinking hard, I took one last look at the city beneath me. My gaze wandered through the slums huddled in the shadows beneath the maze before moving on to the Academic district, taking in the vast campuses that I once dreamt of attending. I stared up at them, the towers stretching up far above the factories and cramped streets I lived amongst, and imagined being one of the chosen.

And, when I was selected to enter the maze, I thought I’d actually gotten my chance. I was scared, obviously, but I was also foolish enough to actually feel hopeful. My memories were still hazy. I couldn’t quite see them all, but my sister’s face, too gaunt from a lifetime of not enough food, loomed in my mind.

I felt my heart break. Tears streamed from my eyes, not only caused by the rushing wind. It was the sudden awareness of how much pain she would experience. If the maze wasn’t being played on the crappy television that was propped on a shelf in the corner of the factory she worked at, someone would go find her. They’d tell her what happened, and I could already see her reaction.

My eyes shut instinctively, and my face screwed up as I tried to push the image from my mind. I didn’t want to see it. I couldn’t. It caused agony to grip my chest, the sensation more painful than any of the times I’d been shot. I felt as though someone had plunged their hand into my body and ripped my heart out, laughing as they did, and nothing made the pain go away.

I pulled back, trying desperately to get away from it, and darkness engulfed me, but I still didn’t open my eyes. I didn’t want to, didn’t trust myself to do it. I was scared that if I did, I might find myself back in the world, and then I’d need to deal with the emotional pain again.

Either that or I’d have to deal with the physical pain. I’d been falling for a while. I wasn’t sure how much time had actually passed. It was moving weirdly. Somehow, it felt like it had simultaneously been a few seconds and a lifetime since I’d jumped, but surely I had to hit the ground soon. I couldn’t just fall forever.

That thought sent a wave of panic through me that was so strong it managed to break through the calmness I’d felt ever since I’d stood at the exit of the maze and looked out over the city. Fear thrashed within me, making my arms shoot out, searching for something to grab hold of, but I found nothing. The only thing around me was the endless rushing of air as I continued to drop.

Both of my hands were empty, though. I was certain I’d been holding the staff the last time I’d checked, and I couldn’t remember letting go. It could have been ripped from my grasp at some point without me noticing, but I hoped it was a sign that I was no longer truly in that world, that I’d pulled away from it and returned to the void within my mind.

Relief washed over me, but it was short-lived. Too soon, I realised it didn’t matter. I was still at least somewhat in the other world. The emotions continued to grip me, and I could feel the wind against my face, so that meant I might be connected enough to the world to know when I hit the ground. The pain might still reach me.

How much would it hurt, I couldn’t help but wonder. Although when I first started travelling between realities, or whatever it was that I was doing, I hadn’t really been able to feel anything, that had changed. Anything that happened in the world I was in felt just as real as if it had happened in my world, and that terrified me.

All I could hope was that my brain wouldn’t even have the chance to register the pain before it was splattered against the street or wherever it was that I landed. That would be ideal. A quick, painless death. Much better than if I’d stayed in the maze.

I slammed into the ground hard, and all air was forced from my lungs. I was paralysed by pain, unable to move as it danced through me. My head spun, and every part of my body was alight with agony. I could hear my pulse beating in my ears. It sounded strange and wrong, but I wasn’t sure why. I couldn’t form thoughts, couldn’t work out what was happening. All I could do was lie there, face down on the warm pavement.

An amount of time passed. There was no way to know how much. It felt like I drifted in and out of consciousness for a while as the world turned nauseatingly around me, and my hearing faded in and out. The vague, distant need to get up pulled at me, but I batted it away clumsily. I didn’t have the energy to do it, to do anything.

I was alive. That was one of the thoughts I grappled with as my head slowly cleared. Somehow, I must have survived the fall, but that wasn’t right. It shouldn’t have been possible. I didn’t know exactly how high up the maze was, but I knew it was far above the towers, and a drop from that height should have killed me, but it didn’t.

Why?

The fact that I was still alive should have been a relief. I should have been happy about it, but all I felt was suspicion and anxiety. I was worried that it was some kind of trick, that they’d cast a spell on me when I’d jumped so they could catch me and punish me for trying to escape.

Or perhaps they did it long ago. Maybe they cast the spell on all of us long before we even set foot in the maze, just in case someone managed to get out. It would make it easier for them to track us down if it happened, and I assumed they were probably already on their way. In fact, they could be drawing near already.

That thought made my body tense subconsciously, and a spasm of pain flared in my shoulder, racing down my arm. It was so strong that it forced a gasp from my lips, which only further hurt my already aching chest.

I was injured. That much was obvious. I’d survived the fall, but something was wrong. I had done some damage to myself, but it didn’t matter. I’d already been lying there for too long, and I couldn’t just remain face down on the street until I recovered. That would probably take a while, and I could be caught at any second. I needed to get up and work out where I was in the city, so I could get moving. I wasn’t sure where I’d go, but I knew I had to figure it out.

But the thought of lifting my head and looking around was too scary. I was worried that I’d see guardians lining the street with their staffs or whatever other weapons they had, I still couldn’t remember, pointing directly at me. It was cowardly, weak, but I kept my head down and listened as hard as I could, hoping that I’d hear something that would tell me where I was and if I were surrounded.

Confusion and anxiety pulled at me. Regardless of where in the city I’d landed, I should have been able to hear something. There should have been the footsteps, hoofs striking the cobbled streets, voices, or the wind blowing through the buildings, but I heard nothing, and that was wrong. There were people everywhere in the city. No matter where I was, I should have heard them.

Did the impact make me deaf? Was that even possible? I had no idea, but I was scared that I’d hit the ground so hard that I’d given myself brain damage, that my hearing was permanently gone. It was the only explanation I could think of for why everything was so quiet, but if that was the case, surely it would hurt more.

Most of my pain was already starting to fade. I could still feel it pulsing in my shoulder, in time with my heartbeat, and there was a burning in my shin, but the rest had begun to grow more distant. It was bothering me less and less, and I wasn’t sure what to think of that, but if it were bad enough to have caused brain damage, I assumed it would have been agonising.

Curiosity and fear rose within me, urging me to look around and work out what was happening, but I hesitated, resisting for as long as I could before finally giving in. Slowly, I took as deep a breath as I could with my chest still burning and lifted my head.

My eyes immediately found broken glass twinkling mere centimetres from me, and fear slashed through me. Had I landed on some? My face could have been shredded beyond recognition, or maybe that was what the pain in my shoulder was from. A shard of glass might have been embedded deep within the joint, severing the nerves and arteries. I could bleed out within just a few minutes, if not less.

Were there even any arteries in my arm, a voice at the back of my head asked, but I wasn’t sure. I knew there was one in the neck and leg, so I assumed there had to be one in the arm too. It made sense, and I needed to check.

Carefully, I glanced around, checking to make sure there were no pieces of broken glass beneath me, before placing my hands on the ground and trying to push myself upright. My left arm collapsed as soon as I put any weight on it, but I caught myself and sat up, my eyes immediately flicking to my injured shoulder.

No glass stuck out of it, but I refused to let myself feel better yet. I pulled my jacket aside, making sure no blood was seeping into my shirt below, but there was nothing. With a relieved sigh, I continued my search, making sure there were no visible injuries on the rest of my body, but I saw none.

I sat back on my heels, allowing myself a moment to breathe before even attempting to get to my feet, but confusion washed over me before I could. My gaze returned to my body, my eyebrows pulling together as I scanned it slowly.

The white jumpsuit I’d been wearing before was gone. It had been replaced with what looked like more normal clothes, ones someone would wear in my reality. I was in a t-shirt and jeans that had dust or something smeared on the thigh. There was a backpack slung over my shoulders, the strap digging in painfully on my bad shoulder, and I was even wearing a leather jacket.

I didn’t need to look in a mirror to know it was far cooler than anything I’d ever worn in reality. I wasn’t the kind of person who could pull off a leather jacket. It would just make me look awkward or like I was in fancy dress, and I’d probably only wear it once before thrusting it deep into the back of my wardrobe to be lost forever.

But the one I wore was different. The black material was old. Deep creases, that bordered on cracks, had formed at the elbows, and it felt like it had been moulded to my body from years of use, and that was wear. I wasn’t certain, but I doubted leather jackets were really a thing in the world with the maze. I didn’t remember ever seeing anyone wearing one, and if they did exist, they were probably horrifically expensive. There was no way I could have afforded one, so where had it come from?

A belated realisation dawned on me, and I rose to my feet. The weight of my backpack pulling on my shoulder brought tears to my eyes, making my eyes water as I looked around. I swallowed hard as I took in the car that was directly in front of me. The wheels were missing. It had been propped up on cinder blocks, and the windows smashed, which explained the broken glass that I’d narrowly avoided.

My gaze remained on the car for a few seconds, my mind spinning, before moving on to the next. Another car was parked, or maybe abandoned, directly behind the first. In fact, I realised as I glanced down the street, there were loads. They lined both sides of the wide road, and most of them seemed to be missing their wheels and had broken windows. I could see the glass glittering on the pavement around me, and some of the cars even had their bonnets popped.

Anything of value must have been stolen years ago. There was no point in wasting my time searching through them when I should have been focusing on getting out of the city, I realised. Any time I was in a place as big as the one I’d found myself in, it made me antsy. Especially when I knew there were still other people in it.

I started to take a step before freezing as I caught up with my thoughts. My gaze darted around the buildings, taking them in, and I felt my eyes widen as fear trickled into my heart. I was in another world. I hadn’t even noticed it happening, and it wasn’t my decision to enter. I knew nothing about it, but just like in the last world, something was keeping me there. Curiosity burned within me, and I knew I should have turned my back on it, tried to return to my reality, but I couldn’t bring myself to do it.

Stepping back instinctively, I moved into the shadow of the building that loomed above me and looked around again. My mind spun as I tried to remember anything about the world, and what had happened to me. I’d hit the ground hard, but from where I was standing, I couldn’t see where I’d come from. I had no recollection of whether I’d jumped, been pushed, or lost my footing.

The lack of memories was starting to annoy me. It had happened in the last world as well, and I was getting fed up with it. I felt like I’d always been able to access them pretty quickly when I went into a new world before, and I didn’t know what had changed, but I didn’t like it. I hoped it was just a coincidence, though. Perhaps it wouldn’t last forever.

It seemed to be late afternoon, maybe moving towards dusk, I decided as I looked around, trying to rustle up any information that I could. The sky was beginning to darken, but there were no lights on in the building on the other side of the street. Judging by the state of it, it didn’t have any electricity.

The tall, once-magnificent skyscraper seemed abandoned, and the huge windows along the ground floor were shattered. Some still hung in their frames, the surface a network of cracks and jagged-looking shards, but others were missing completely, and the revolving doors weren’t doing much better. Only one of the golden frames still held any glass.

It must have been standing empty for a while, I realised as I peered at the dull and tarnished metal. Back in the day, before all of that happened, it was probably a hotel or an office building, and they most likely had someone polish the metal every single day to make sure it gleamed.

My breath caught in my chest. What had happened, though? I had no idea, but it was clear that something had caused it to fall into such a state of disarray. That wasn’t the kind of thing that just happened randomly.

But it wasn’t just that one building. I tore my eyes away from the tower in front of me, looking down the street. Giant skyscrapers surrounded me. There were a few smaller, less grand buildings too, but even they must have cost a fortune, and as far as I could see, every single one was just as dark and deserted. Weeds had started to force their way through the cracks in the road, and vines crept across the edge of some of the buildings.

The city was abandoned. That was the only explanation, and I racked my brain, trying to recall what had happened. There must have been something. It took some kind of event, a bomb or war, and time to cause that level of ruin. The version of me that lived in the world must have been aware of it. There was no way that I could have existed there without knowing, but the answers remained stubbornly out of my reach.

Irritation that verged on anger burned within me, and the urge to move pulled at me again. I couldn’t just stay there forever. It wasn’t safe. I didn’t have a solid base that I could fortify and set up traps to keep others out, and I hadn’t had that in years. I couldn’t recall when exactly I’d had that kind of security, but it felt like a lifetime ago.

But that meant I needed to remain on the move. That was the next best option. If I kept going and never stayed anywhere for long, it would be harder for anyone to track me. That would help keep me alive, so despite the pain in my shoulder, I needed to get going.

I didn’t want to, though. I could feel the need pulling at me, and I knew it wasn’t coming from me. It came from the version of me who’d live in that world before, a leftover emotion or instinct, and the desire to remain exactly where I was tempted me. Perhaps if I did that, I’d remember more. Maybe the inevitable panic and fear that I could feel bubbling up within me would cause them to reveal themselves to me.

It was tempting, far too tempting. I longed to give in and allow myself to do it, to stubbornly refuse to move until I got what I wanted, but I couldn’t. I wasn’t cruel enough, and the only person who would be suffering if I did that was me. It would just make my life harder and maybe even bring it to an end quicker. I’d already survived seventeen years, and that wasn’t enough for me.

My heart leapt. Seventeen. In that world, I was seventeen. That knowledge had just come to me, and it had felt effortless, so perhaps that was the problem. Maybe I was just trying too hard, pushing myself too much to immediately know everything about the world I’d just entered, and I needed to let it happen more organically. I needed to listen to my body and do as it wanted.

I wasn’t sure whether that would work, but it was worth a try, I decided. I hitched my backpack higher on my shoulders, gritting my teeth against the pain it sent through my left arm, and looked around one more time before beginning to walk.
