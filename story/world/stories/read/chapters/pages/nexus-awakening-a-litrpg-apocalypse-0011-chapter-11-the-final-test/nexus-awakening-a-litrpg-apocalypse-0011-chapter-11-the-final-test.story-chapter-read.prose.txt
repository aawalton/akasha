Chapter 11: The Final Test

"The Resonance Flux doesn't just move through all living things, it surrounds us. You can reach through it to sense others, and even some objects."

It was one of many lectures Vash delivered to me as we fought. Her voice never wavered, never showed strain even as she danced around me, her blade a blur of twilight. These lessons were never given during moments of rest or quiet contemplation, always during combat, forcing me to divide my attention between her words and her attacks.

She won every bout, and I paid for it in pain, each lesson burned into me, each fight remembered. My body was a canvas of bruises and burns, a living record of my education. The healing potions she administered afterward erased the physical evidence but not the memory of how each injury was received, the mistake that had led to it, the lesson it contained.

The training room echoed with the distinctive sound of our blades clashing, crystalline chimes that resonated in the air long after the contact ended. Vash moved with that inhuman grace that still unsettled me, her perfect features never showing exertion or concern. I, on the other hand, was drenched in sweat, my movements growing increasingly desperate as fatigue set in.

"Try this now," she stated as a ten, a heavenly slash, came striking toward my head. The overhead vertical cut descended with frightening speed, the purple blade humming as it split the air. I blocked, my white resonance blade clashing with hers in a spark of crushing light. The impact jarred my arms, sending vibrations up to my shoulders. Even a successful block against Vash left me hurting.

Vash insisted on every one of my attempts to use Flux being done under the pressure of combat, and often while I was in pain. Pain brought a crystalline rage, the world shifting grayscale, and power flooded into me. This had become my rhythm, my daily reality: fight, hurt, rage, repeat. The cycle was brutal but effective. Each day I lasted a little longer, landed a few more strikes, understood a little more about the Flux that flowed through and around me.

A Flux surge, one of my abilities. It was what made me stronger, faster, and more durable. Though at the initiate stage, it was a small boost, miniscule compared to the tide of power Vash could call on. When it activated, I could feel the difference immediately—muscles responding more quickly, senses sharpening, pain receding to a distant concern. But it never lasted long enough, never gave me enough of an edge to truly challenge my master.

Instead of a surge, I focused on that power, the sweet cocktail of wrath and pain, on Vash herself. Reaching through the Flux as she had instructed, trying to sense her, to gauge her strength. This was a different application, requiring concentration rather than raw emotion.

A vague sensation fed into my mind. An unknowable power, but one I couldn't gauge. It was like looking at a distant mountain during the birth of dawn on a cloudy day, but with the clutching, insidious hands of night's final embrace hanging on. The image came to me unbidden, a poetic approximation of something beyond normal perception. I could sense Vash through the Flux, could feel her presence, her power, but couldn't measure or quantify it.

The Flux returned a notification to me, one I didn't need, but one that put the feeling into tangible words.

???

Vash was beyond me. Her Flux Level was more than I could grasp in that moment. A vastness not yet within my reach. The single line of question marks mocked my attempt, a digital representation of my inadequacy. I knew she was powerful, obviously so, but this experience drove home just how vast the gulf between us truly was.

My momentary distraction cost me. The duel, if one could call it that, ended with Vash smashing an elbow into my face. Blood erupted from my nose, spattering the training room floor in crimson droplets. Never did my master shy away from causing me pain if she could. In fact, she seemed to take a professional satisfaction in it, as if each injury was a teaching tool carefully applied.

I staggered back, one hand reflexively rising to my face. The hot metallic taste of blood filled my mouth, my vision swimming from the impact. Vash stood calmly, her blade deactivated, watching my reaction with clinical detachment.

"Tomorrow will come your final... test aboard this vessel. Then we will land on Crucible, where you will enter the Initiate Games."

The statement cut through the pain of my broken nose, focusing my attention immediately. After weeks of training, of being beaten and rebuilt, we were finally approaching whatever next stage Vash had planned. The mysterious Initiate Games she occasionally referenced but never explained.

I rubbed at my jaw, my white blade vanishing as I let go of the Flux within me. The weapon disappeared not with a dramatic flash but a gradual fading, like mist dissipating in morning sunlight. Without its glow, the training room seemed darker, the overhead lights suddenly insufficient.

"You still haven't told me anything about these games," I stated, trying to keep accusation from my tone. Questions often earned additional punishment, but this seemed important enough to risk it.

"You do not need to know just yet. Concentrate on the problem in front of you, initiate. Tomorrow you face Lt. Zorin."

My jaw fell open, the pain of my nose momentarily forgotten. The lieutenant who had escorted me to my quarters that first day, who had held Elisa during my blade manifestation test. The man I had nearly killed with my newly awakened powers.

"What? Why? He'll crush me!"

Fear and disbelief mingled in my voice. Zorin was a full Nyxen lieutenant, not a new initiate like me. He had been training for years, decades perhaps, while I had barely scratched the surface of Flux abilities.

Yes, I had used the Flux to nearly kill Zorin, but that had been born of a wrath I hadn't been able to call on since. The first breath of my bloodline, as I would later learn, stirring to life. A moment of perfect clarity and purpose that I couldn't replicate at will, not yet. That had been when Elisa was threatened, when my rage had a clear, immediate focus. This would be different, controlled, observed.

"Perhaps. Or perhaps not. But it is necessary. The Nyxen have... rules. A Nyxen Lord or Lady may only have one apprentice."

She spoke the word "rules" as if it were distasteful, a concession to a system she would prefer to ignore. I had seen enough of Nyxen behavior to understand that they valued power over protocol, strength over structure. Whatever rules existed were likely enforced through might rather than mutual agreement.

"But I'm not an apprentice, I am an initiate."

The distinction seemed important to me, though I wasn't entirely clear on the hierarchy. From what I had gathered, initiates were the lowest tier, those just beginning their journey. Apprentices were chosen, sponsored by a specific Lord or Lady for personal training. My status felt ambiguous, somewhere between the two.

Vash shrugged, the gesture elegant despite its dismissiveness.

"Yes, but should you survive the Initiate Games, it will become a problem that will need to be corrected. In the Nyxen, power is all that matters. Tonight you will address your advancement in the way I have shown you. Tomorrow you will either defeat Zorin, or you will die."

Her voice remained conversational, as if discussing the weather rather than my potential death. There was no cruelty in it, just a matter-of-fact acceptance of how things worked in her world. A world I was now part of, whether I wanted to be or not.

"He's likely levels ahead of me!" I protested, grasping for any argument that might change this decree.

Vash shook her head, a hint of contempt flickering across her perfect features.

"Not as far as you might think. Zorin is a lieutenant, but that is because he is my official apprentice, not because he is powerful. He is weak. Cut the weakness out, Thorne. Like removing cancer. If you cannot, then it is not worth investing in you further."

The revelation was unexpected. Zorin, the stern-faced man who had shown such disdain for me, was Vash's current apprentice. And she spoke of him with complete disregard, as if he were a failed experiment she was ready to discard. I wondered if she had spoken of previous apprentices the same way, if this was a regular occurrence in her pursuit of worthy students.

She strode out of the training room without further comment, leaving me alone with my thoughts and the drying blood on my face. The door sealed behind her with a soft hiss, the sound somehow final, ominous.

I stood there for several minutes, processing what I had learned. Tomorrow I would fight for my life against a man who had every reason to hate me. A man I had humiliated and nearly killed. A man who would surely be coming for my blood with everything he had.

And my master, the woman who had claimed ownership of me, had casually set these events in motion with no more concern than one might show when scheduling a routine meeting.

That night, after some time with Elisa, and her falling asleep, I sat cross legged on the floor of my quarters. The room was dimly lit, just enough to see by. The soft hum of the ship's engines provided a backdrop to my meditation, a constant reminder of our journey through the void toward Crucible.

Elisa's gentle breathing from the bed was a counterpoint to my own measured inhalations. She had adapted to our new reality with remarkable resilience, learning about the Flux from her caretaker Lyra, asking endless questions about the ship and the strange people who inhabited it. She still missed her mother and brother, still had nightmares that left her crying and calling their names, but the raw wound of loss was beginning to scab over, at least for her.

For me, it remained fresh, fueling the anger that powered my connection to the Flux. I kept the memories close, cultivated them, used them as Vash had taught me. Pain and rage were tools, weapons, sources of strength in this new reality.

It was time to address my advancement. I closed my eyes, focusing inward, reaching for that warm center where the Flux resided within me. The status screen appeared in my mind, glowing blue text against a backdrop of darkness.

[STATUS] Name: Varus Thorne Age: 31 Flux Level (FL): 1 Race: Human (Awakened) Faction: Nyxen (Initiate)

[PROGRESSION] Flux Experience (FEXP): 200

Meditate on your path to advance Path: Unnamed/Unrefined

[TEMPERING] Mind: Awakened Initiate Body: Awakened Initiate Spirit: Awakened Initiate Bloodline: Nexus Bloodline

[ATTRIBUTES] Resonance: 15 Projection: 12 Containment: 3 Perception: 4 Attunement: 4

[ASPECT ABILITIES] Vital: None Primal: Flux Surge (Initiate)

Flux surges within you in response to your anger, granting you enhanced strength and speed. Deepen your connection to the Primal Aspect to enhance the strength of this ability. Flux: Flux Sense (Initiate)

Your awareness of life, and objects connected to Resonance Flux. Can currently sense the strength of other initiate tier life forms and objects. Deepen your connection with the Flux on a neutral level to increase the power of this ability. Void: Consume Flux (Initiate)

You can concentrate, tapping into the void aspect of the Flux and consume an initiate level Flux ability. Echo: None

[RESOURCES] Credits: None

The numbers had grown familiar over the weeks of training, a quantification of my progress, my potential. Two hundred experience points accumulated through countless hours of pain and effort. My attributes still low compared to what they could be, what they would need to be for me to survive what lay ahead.

Meditation on the Flux was something all Flux users needed to do. At the time, I only knew how the Nyxen did it. In the Nyxen, they believed in the Primal and Void aspects of the Flux. Passion, shadow, pain, anger, hunger, and more were the core of these two aspects of the Flux. While other factions focused on different aspects, the Nyxen cultivated these darker, more visceral connections.

There were more aspects than these, of course. The Resonance Flux was a reflection of all things that made up life, death, and all of reality. Each aspect represented different elements of existence, different approaches to harnessing the universal energy that bound everything together.

I had seen hints of this in my ability list—Vital, Primal, Flux, Void, Echo. Different facets of the same underlying power, each with its own applications and philosophy. The Nyxen favored Primal and Void, but even they recognized the existence and utility of other aspects.

Have you ever tried to know your pain? To court your anger like a lover in a provocative outfit and an intoxicating scent? To study the shadows, to understand the hunger that gnaws away at your stomach?

You're welcome to try it. I imagine you don't dwell on it for very long. You may notice the shadows, you may give into your anger, perhaps you fear your pain, but you don't know them.

Not as I would come to know them. Not as the Nyxen do.

Most people experience these sensations briefly, acknowledge them, and move on. The Nyxen approach was different, more intimate, more consuming. They didn't just feel these emotions and sensations, they inhabited them, explored them, mastered them. Pain wasn't something to be avoided but understood. Anger wasn't a flash of emotion but a blade to be honed. Hunger wasn't just a bodily need but a metaphor for ambition, for desire, for growth.

I reflected on my training. Every sting of pain as Vash beat me, playing a symphony of pain across my flesh. Every beat of rage within my heart, like a drum with no beat, a storm that followed no path. Each moment of suffering had been a lesson, each flash of anger a glimpse of potential power.

The hunger that crawled in my belly, that drove me forward to consume power. The shadows that danced with the light, like a clumsy teenager at prom with his date for the first time. Awkward, uncertain, but full of possibility. These feelings, these experiences, these aspects of existence that most people tried to avoid or minimize, were the very things the Nyxen cultivated and channeled.

As I delved deeper into these reflections, allowing myself to fully experience and acknowledge these aspects of myself, I felt something shift within me. A deepening of connection, a strengthening of resolve. The warm center where the Flux resided seemed to pulse in response, expanding, integrating more fully with my being.

You have advanced to Flux Level 5. You have 4 attribute points. Assign them now.

The notification appeared in my mind, confirming what I had felt. Advancement, progress, growth. Four levels gained in one meditation session, far more than I had expected. The weeks of training, of pain and struggle, had accumulated more experience than I realized. The blue text pulsed gently, waiting for my decision.

Vash had told me that attribute points were important, as they would shape my first steps down my path, the one I would eventually name when I thought of one suitable. When something resonated with me through the Flux. The distribution of these points would influence my strengths, my specialties, the type of Flux user I would become.

I considered my options carefully. Resonance determined my overall connection to the Flux, the foundation upon which everything else was built. Projection affected my ability to manifest and extend Flux energy externally, crucial for offense. Containment governed control and defense. Perception related to sensing and awareness. Attunement determined my affinity with specific aspects.

For tomorrow's fight, for survival, I needed raw power and the ability to project it effectively. Defense could come later. Awareness was secondary to action right now.

I put two into resonance, and two into projection.

The decision made, I felt the attributes shift, strengthen. It wasn't a dramatic change, more like a subtle recalibration. The connection to the Flux deepened slightly, the ability to project it externally sharpened.

I wouldn't say something profound happened, but I did think my connection with the Resonance Flux was deeper. Like turning the volume up on the television a click or two. Or perhaps adding subtitles to see the words. It wasn't a lot, but it was there. A foundation to build upon, a beginning rather than an end.

I just hoped it was enough.

Tomorrow would bring the greatest challenge I had faced since my awakening. A formal duel against a trained Nyxen lieutenant who had every reason to want me dead. A test not just of my growing abilities but of my will to survive, to continue on this path I had not chosen but now had to walk.

As I opened my eyes, ending the meditation, my gaze fell on Elisa's sleeping form. Her small face peaceful in the dim light, innocent despite everything she had witnessed. She was why I was here, why I endured Vash's brutality, why I embraced pain and rage as tools rather than fleeing from them. She was why I would face Zorin tomorrow and why I would win, no matter the cost.

I rose silently from the floor, moving to stand beside her bed. Gently, careful not to wake her, I brushed a strand of hair from her forehead. In that moment, I made a silent vow. I would survive tomorrow. I would advance in the Flux. I would become whatever I needed to become to ensure her safety, her future.

Even if that meant becoming something I once would have feared.
