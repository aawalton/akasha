Up high in the sky, near the clouds, two mages were situated, looking down at the world below. Fire and chaos spread across the land like a disease. It was the end of days—and it was only getting worse.

“Armentes, how are you holding up?” Veronica asked.

The man rolled his shoulders, trying to stifle an ache out of them. “Been better. Been worse,” he replied. “I still have enough mana to eliminate another warlord or a few commanders.”

Veronica nodded. “I’ll deal with the demons that are flooding in from Faumr’s Pass if you can handle the east side.”

Two separate beacons of light flashed in the distance: one marked Faumr’s Pass, the other marked the eastern plains.

Voices sounded from beside them, transmitting directly into their ears.

“I’ve placed the markers for you to see. We have some mages under Duke Pelotema coming as reinforcements in roughly an hour,” Parek said.

Veronica looked over at Armentes. He was also staring into the distance. He too had a close-aide in the communications division.

“Right. I’ll see you back in Annesheim after this is all over,” Armentes said with a nod.

Before she could respond, he flew off into the horizon.

Veronica looked back at her marker and surged toward it.

An hour passed by as Parek had said, and the reinforcements came from the south.

Beneath Veronica were several large craters, all scorched black or burning with lingering embers. Ravines cracked the earth, snaking up to a kilometer away. The battle had proved far more difficult than she had imagined.

“We’ll handle things from here, Exalted Mage Veronica.”

The voice came from the man that was leading a small group of four other mages. Each one was a powerhouse in Vitian—all 8th tiers, while their leader was a 9th-tier.

Veronica nodded and quickly flew away. Her mission there was done.

“Parek. Where’s the next mission?” Veronica asked.

Silence answered her.

Veronica continued flying, wind rushing past her barrier as the ruined land blurred beneath her. For a moment, she assumed the connection had simply faltered. With this much demonic energy spread across Vitian, disruptions were not uncommon.

“Parek?” she asked again.

Her eyes narrowed. There was still no answer.

That was too odd. Parek was always there. Even with several battles happening at once, his magic was always connected to her. That was the advantage of being a high-tier Path of Revelation mage. He should have been able to respond to her at any moment.

Unless he had purposely disconnected the link. Or perhaps something was keeping him too occupied to speak.

A cold feeling settled in her chest.

Veronica lifted her hand, eyes dropping to the communication artifact ring on her finger. The metal band glowed faintly as she poured mana into it, forcing the connection to search beyond the usual network.

“Come on,” she muttered.

The ring pulsed once. Then again. For several long moments, there was only a quiet static, a faint whisper of magic.

Then the connection clicked.

A voice came through, but it wasn’t Parek.

“H-hello? Who is this? And please don’t talk too loud,” said the voice.

Veronica paused midair.

It was a young voice. A teenage boy, perhaps. Someone whispering as if he were hiding from bandits.

Veronica furrowed her brow. “Who is this?” she said quietly, as instructed.

“M-my name is Jallen. I-I need your help. Please. Send someone over here.”

Her expression tightened further.

“What happened? Where is Parek or any of the other Revelation mages?”

“There are demons,” he said. “Demons are rampaging all around Fort Mannow. They’re everywhere. Please, wherever you are, tell someone to send us help.”

For a brief moment, the world stilled around her.

Fort Mannow was where the refugees she had rescued a week ago had gone. It was where that little girl she saved was residing.

And more importantly, it was where Parek was operating.

Veronica resumed her flight, moving faster than before and burning a larger amount of mana. “Where is everyone? What’s the situation?”

“They’re all outside,” he replied. “All of the mages and soldiers are trying to hold back the demons. But there are too many. It’s looking really bad out here. Please send someone to help us.”

“Don’t worry,” Veronica said. “I’m on my way. Just keep hiding wherever you are and stay quiet.”

Veronica blurred through the air, flying as fast as she could toward the fort. There should have been a powerful shield generator there, but if a demon could destroy it, then it was no easy opponent. At least, not for the forces stationed there.

“Veronica,” a feminine voice called out.

“What is it?” she asked.

“Huh?” came the voice from her communication ring.

“You said my name,” she replied.

“I-I don’t even know your name,” said the boy.

Veronica narrowed her eyes.

That was right. She hadn’t actually given the boy her name. Who just called her?

She stopped in the air, looking around. But there was nothing there. Just empty sky and blackened earth beneath.

Was she imagining it?

She hummed before turning back toward Fort Mannow.

It took her roughly an hour of continuous flying before she reached Fort Mannow.

When she arrived, everything was chaos. The entire fort had been overrun. Demons slipped through every open entrance now that the barrier was broken.

The shield generator had collapsed near the center of the courtyard, its metal frame twisted and half-melted. Blue light still flickered weakly from the broken crystal inside, but it wouldn’t work anymore without repair. The dome that should have protected the fort was gone.

Below, people were screaming.

Soldiers fought along the walls and throughout the courtyard, trying to hold back the demons flooding through the broken gate. Mages stood on raised platforms, casting spells as quickly as they could, but there were too many enemies. For every demon burned or cut down, two more seemed to force their way inside.

Bodies covered the ground.

Soldiers in broken armor. Mages with shattered staves. Villagers who had never even held a weapon. Some lay near overturned wagons. Others had fallen beside the tents where they had been sleeping, probably only hours before. Veronica saw blood on the stone, on the walls, and across the canvas roofs of the refugee shelters.

Her mana rings screamed.

She dropped down from the sky like a falling star.

A demon centurion raised its head just in time to see her descend. Its mouth opened in a shriek, but the sound was drowned out as Veronica’s mana began leaking from the ground beneath it. A burst of concentrated light exploded upward, creating a beacon that reached the clouds.

Dozens more beacons of light followed, all erupting beneath the legs of other demons rampaging through the fort.

She followed the spell with another.

“Fourth Armoury: Children of Gungnir,” she said.

Dozens upon dozens of spell circles appeared in the air behind her. Each one burst, sending a spear of condensed, concentrated light forward. They streaked across the battlefield, each seeking out a demon to impale and destroy.

“The Exalted Mage!” someone shouted.

“She’s here! Everyone! Push forward!” another commanded.

Their forces began to push back against the demons now that Veronica had cleared out a significant portion of them.

As a soldier ran by her, she flew beside him and stopped him. “You. Do you know where Parek is?” she asked.

“Yes, Exalted Mage,” he said, pointing east with his finger. “He should be stationed over there.”

She nodded and flew over.

It didn’t take her long to spot him.

Parek stood near the broken gate, one hand pressed against a wound along his ribs, the other raised toward the battlefield. Several small spell circles floated around him, each one spinning with intricate patterns that shifted too quickly to follow.

A Path of Revelation mage.

The combat strength of those mages didn’t rely on force. Their strength lay in finding weaknesses. Critical joints between armor, nerve clusters—places where a single strike could cripple something far stronger than him.

A demon lunged at him.

Parek’s eyes narrowed as he focused. “Left lung. Lower spine. Tendon.”

Three beams of light struck in rapid succession. The demon staggered, collapsed to one knee, and was immediately finished by a nearby soldier.

Veronica flew toward him, blasting apart two demons as she crossed the courtyard.

“Parek!”

He looked over, relief flashing across his face. “You came.”

“You cut the connection.”

“I had to,” he said, voice strained. “The communication room was breached. I sealed one of the villager boys inside with a communication array just in case, then came out here to help the soldiers.”

“The boy contacted me.”

“Good.” Parek turned and fired another spell. A demon’s eye burst apart, and a nearby soldier drove a spear through its throat. “Then he’s still alive.”

Veronica raised both hands. A circle of light formed beneath an entire cluster of demons near the gate.

The ground detonated. Several demons were thrown into the air, their bodies blown apart by the force of the spell, limbs mixed together with spraying dirt.

“Where are the rest of the villagers?” Veronica asked. “There’s too few here.”

Parek’s expression tightened. “North riverbed. We tried to evacuate them when the shield fell. Some made it through the northern passage, but demons broke around the side and chased after them.”

Veronica’s heart dropped. “The refugees?”

“Yes,” Parek said. “A lot of them.”

Veronica flew up and looked north.

Past the fort walls, beyond the broken road, she could see movement. Small figures running through the dark riverbed below. And behind them, shadows.

Demons.

“Go,” Parek said. “We’ll hold the fort. Help the retreating villagers.”

Veronica hesitated for half a breath, looking down at the blood running down his side.

Parek noticed her gaze. “We still have a few blooming mages. Don’t waste time worrying about me. Go.”

She clenched her teeth, but didn’t waste anymore time. She launched herself higher into the air and flew north.

The northern riverbed stretched below the fort, wide and mostly dry, with only a shallow stream cutting through the center. Hundreds of villagers had fled into it, stumbling over stones and mud as they tried to escape. Some carried children. Some dragged the injured behind them.

Others… they had simply given up.

The demons had already reached them.

A few soldiers ran alongside the villagers, trying their best to protect them. However, several centurions simply charged through, tearing people apart, destroying mana-powered wagons and slaughtering those hiding inside.

Screams filled the riverbed. Sharp and desperate wails. People begging for help as the snarls of demons roared alongside them.

Veronica’s eyes burned violet.

She raised one hand.

A dozen beams of light shot downward, each one striking a demon through the skull or chest. Bodies collapsed into the mud. Another spell followed immediately, a wave of fire sweeping across the riverbank and forcing several demons away from the fleeing villagers.

“Run!” Veronica shouted. “Keep moving north!”

Some looked up at her. For a moment, hope appeared on their faces.

Then a roar shook the riverbed.

Veronica turned toward the sound.

A demon warlord stood near the center of the dry river, towering over the others. Its body was massive, covered in blackened plates of armor grown directly from its flesh. Horns curled from its head, and its mouth stretched too wide as it stared up at her.

It smiled at her.

Veronica clicked her tongue. “Of course.”

The warlord bent its legs and leapt.

Veronica flew higher as its clawed hand swept past her feet. She twisted midair, forming three spell circles at once. The warlord landed against the riverbank, crushing stone beneath its weight before launching itself at her again.

Despite not having wings, it was quick.

Veronica raised a barrier just as its fist struck.

The impact sent her flying backward through the air. However, her barrier didn’t crack or even shudder. She stabilized herself, teeth clenched, and immediately countered with a spear of light that pierced into the warlord’s shoulder and exploded the ground behind it.

It barely flinched, despite the dirt and debris showering it from the explosion.

The warlord opened its mouth, and black flame gathered between its teeth.

Veronica’s mana rings spun faster.

She couldn’t afford to let it fire into the villagers.

A large spell circle formed in front of her palm, its rings twisting and locking into place. Light condensed at the center, growing brighter with every passing second.

She aimed her spell directly at the demon’s chest. A simple warlord wouldn’t have enough power to contend with an 8th-tier spell.

But before she unleashed it, she heard something.

A scream—a young scream. One familiar to her.

Veronica turned, eyes drawn to where the cry had come from.

On the far side of the riverbed, a young girl was running across the stones, tears streaking through the dirt on her face. Her hair was tangled, her small hands clutching the front of her torn dress as she stumbled forward.

Behind her, a thin, long-limbed demon crawled across the ground on all fours. Its claws dug into the mud as it rushed after her, gaining with every second.

“H-help! Help me please!” the girl screamed.

Veronica froze.

That was the girl she had saved. The sister of the boy she had let die.

“Run!” Veronica shouted.

She canceled her spell. Mana surged through her body as she flew toward the girl, diving as quickly as she could. The world blurred around her, wind screaming against her barrier. Veronica held her hand out, forming a compressed beam of light before her palm.

The demon chasing the girl was right behind her.

Its clawed hand thrust forward.

“Don’t you dare!” Veronica shouted.

She fired her spell at the demon. It pierced straight through its head, killing it instantly before digging a deep hole that stretched for nearly a kilometer into the earth.

But Veronica was too late. She fired her spell a fraction too slow.

She saw it. The demon’s clawed hand—it had pierced straight through the girl’s body. As the demon’s body collapsed, so did the girl.

The girl turned her head to look at Veronica in the air. But her gaze wasn’t focused. It was simply the girl’s body jerking forward.

Veronica landed so hard the riverbed cracked beneath her feet, disrupting the stream of water. She stopped several dozen meters away.

Her body froze. She couldn’t take a step forward.

She saw enough to know. The girl’s body lay flat on the ground, head lying flat on her ear.

“No…” she muttered. She couldn’t even find the strength to clench her fingers. “No…”

Go away. I don’t like you.

The girl’s words echoed in her mind.

H-help! Help me, please!

A torrent of black flames washed over her from behind. Her mana barrier protected her, but the attack covered the entire riverbed, burning the trees and drying the stream in front of her. The demon warlord’s flame swallowed her vision. As the attack faded, the air continued to twist from the mirage of heat.

And in front of her were two charred corpses.

One of the demon.

The other—of the little girl. One whom she was never even able to learn the name of.

That was the second mistake of The Fall.

Veronica’s fingers twitched, clenching hard, despite the stone beneath her veins. Her hand cracked, losing a large shard of flesh that dropped to the ground.

“You fucking demons…”

Veronica.

“I’ll fucking kill you…”

Veronica!

It was that same feminine voice she heard earlier.

—She ignored it.

She turned around to face the demon warlord that had just burned down the riverbed.

A tear streaked down her face. Her eyes burned violet as her hair lifted. Further fissures cracked down her arm as she forced power out of her mana core.

“I’LL FUCKING KILL ALL OF YOU!!” she shouted, baring her teeth.

[Administering high-voltage neural shock.]

Veronica’s eyes flew open.

Her body snapped up, heaving heavily, coughing and trying to catch her breath as if she had just drowned. Tears and sweat streaked down her face relentlessly.

All she heard were muted sounds around her, her senses completely distorted.

[Sympathetic nervous system activity exceeding safe thresholds. Increasing inhibitory neurotransmitter release. Amplyifying Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid activity to suppress central nervous activity, reduce heart rate, and lower blood pressure. Administering synthetic analgesic. ]

After a few moments, Veronica’s body calmed considerably. The tension around her body eased, and her breathing became steadier.

Finally, she got hold of her surroundings.

All around her was a large cloud of black mist, blocking out the sky and everything beyond a few meters of her vision. Next to her, she spotted Gwen, Nasha, and Dusk, all unconscious and lying on the ground.

Veronica placed a hand over her heart, clutching at it.

“What… what happened?” she asked.

[You fell under a mental trance, encasing you in a nightmare in a coma-like state. I have been working to free you from this state as soon as possible.]

She took slow, deep breaths.

“Free me?” she repeated.

She paused for a moment, thinking about it. “I… so that was you. You were the person calling my name in that nightmare.”

[That is correct.]

She took a few seconds to compose herself. Seeing that they were still inside the dark mist, the demon clearly had no intention of harming or killing them yet. None of the others looked as if they had injuries either.

Veronica looked down at her palm, flexing her fingers a few times.

“If only that really was a fake nightmare…”
