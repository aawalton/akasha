I stepped through the portal, and found myself in the same room as before. Whimpers echoed down the tunnels as Strange raced away from the room.

Complete darkness filled the space, and I vanished, following him.

Lenna appeared on Strange’s map, along with others. Way too many others. They clustered behind her like she was a teacher.

I followed the tunnels along the twists and turns until light washed over the area.

Non-clan members all huddled behind Lenna. Fireflies twisted all around her, concentrated in front, facing the waterfall.

The crash of water didn’t cover the whimpers from the people on the floor.

Something moved beyond the water.

Strange padded close enough that the red dot appeared on the map.

A snout punched through the curtain of water and growled in Lenna's direction.

[Snapper, Barometric Siphon, Sedge-Stranger, Level 225, Moss Bright, Predator, Unknown.]

“This area is claimed by me, you need to leave!” Lenna's voice filled the cavern opening, echoing in the stone cavern.

The snake-like creature shook its head back and forth, hissing. “The Great Ones have said the destroyers are fair game.”

“These aren’t destroyers. They have rejected those of the Clan,” she said, not moving from her position. The light in front of her flared brighter, making part of me shiver.

I stepped out of the shadows and slowly walked around the mess of people, keeping a good amount of distance between us. A group of five kids huddled closest to Lenna. One small hand wrapped around her ankle. None of them would look at Snapper.

His presence flared out from the opening, making the beings whimper.

I frowned and touched on my own aura, trying to direct it at Snapper. The Sedge-Stranger recoiled. It jerked back out of sight behind the water, and the red dot on my map fled.

That pleased me.

Lenna didn’t even turn, but her shoulders dipped down a little. The people huddled behind her on the floor all relaxed a little.

“You survived,” she whispered.

“Barley,” I said, trying to sound lighthearted, and failing as I approached.

Strange chirped, darting closer and nudging her ankle. “I delivered the quest!”

“You did good,” she said with a grin, though her eyes didn’t leave the waterfall. “I knew you could do it.”

At least one of us knew something about his little quest.

I continued approaching from the side until I stood a little in front of her, and away from those on the floor. Several of them sat up a little straighter as the snake thing fled.

“Why does it not surprise me that you are ranked number 1?” she asked, shaking her head.

“Believe it or not, I didn’t want anything to do with it.” I rubbed the back of my neck and swallowed, thinking of the notification about my shadows being hungry. But I didn’t know how to ask her about it.

“Can you claim this area from me?” she suddenly asked, her voice coming out in a rush. The fireflies in front of her all shivered.

“Sure, why not?” I chuckled. It wasn’t like it mattered at this point. Unless someone appeared out of nowhere to fight, I should be fine.

[You have gained a territory.]

[Your ranking is the same: 1.]

I didn’t know how rankings were calculated, or how I possibly sat in the first position. There were much higher leveled creatures on this planet, like that tree thing, that could eat me whole.

None of this made any sense to me. Reflecting for a moment, though, a lot of things hadn’t made sense. Rolling with it seemed like the only real option.

The light around Lenna went out, then she sat down on the rock floor. The kids inched away from her.

I gave her a look, but she just smiled.

“You can guard the gate,” she said, motioning to the waterfall. “I need a break. I made it far enough.”

“If anything comes nearby, I’ll scare it away,” I said with a shrug. “Dawn has to be close.”

“Very close,” she said, leaning back on her arms. All of the others started whispering to one another at that, while the kids joined the other adults. “We don’t have long now.”

I opened my mouth to speak, but stopped as the notifications rolled in.

[The leadership contest for this world is now closed. Rankings will be finalized.]

[Current Standing: 5 territories.]

[Current Ranking: 1.]

[The top five finalized rankings will meet with the Great Ones. You will be summoned.]

Everyone else in the cavern suddenly exploded with noise, and a few people started crying.

“We lived!” cried one person.

The guy who had led us to the portal stood up and bowed toward Lenna. “Without your help, we would be gone.”

The others quickly followed, and Lenna scrambled to her feet.

I stepped back, closer to the waterfall.

She smiled at them and nodded. “You should be safe now, though you might want to get some rest.”

They all took the hint and funneled back into the tunnels until it was only Lenna, Strange and I in the front cavern.

I immediately relaxed.

“So, what happened?” my voice trailed off, as Lenna’s face went completely blank. I stepped closer, and reached out to touch her, but then she suddenly shuddered. A soft light coated her eyes.

“Woah, are you okay?” I asked.

She held up her hand until the light went out. She swallowed hard. “Yeah, I just leveled… a lot.”

[Lenna de La Dengu, Pathfinder, Firefly Shield, Level 148, Friend]

She steadied for a second, then slumped to the floor. I moved and caught her before she hit the rock, and sat down beside her.

Strange chirped a few times, but stayed hidden in the shadows.

“And you argued with me about leveling,” I muttered, as I helped her relax onto the rock, holding her steady.

“I just need to recover…” She grimaced, and held her head with both hands.

I went to grab my healing crystal, but paused. The healing crystal, soarstone, and storage crystal necklace were gone. The heavy weight had lessened, and I hadn’t noticed. Dust coated the inside of my top, and the lone remaining inventory crystal.

That cold feeling rose again.

My shadows had eaten most of my crystal necklace. Thankfully, they hadn’t eaten my inventory crystal, but who knew what would have happened with that.

“Well…” Now I felt a little useless. “We can just rest here together.”

Strange appeared, then climbed into my lap. “Alpha is strong, protect us.”

I chuckled and patted him on the head lightly. He wasn’t wrong about how strong I’d become. So strong that Lakeside felt weird. Too weird.

I didn’t know how Noseen did it, flying around on a much lower-ranked world.

This place felt better, as long as I avoided those weird pockets with less mana, and the ones with too much mana. If we could fix that, maybe this would be a great place to live.

“Your shadow moves now,” whispered Lenna.

I froze, hand still stretched out above Strange.

“It’s good!” chirped Strange. “Hides me better.”

“What…” I coughed a little. “What do you mean?”

She pointed.

My shadow stretched out behind me, away from the light that still hovered around Lenna. Yet, it didn’t have clearly defined limits. Instead, it swayed and shimmered, almost like smoke.

“That’s new,” I mumbled, thinking of the skill. It wasn’t super defined.

Lenna let out a huff. “You need to be prepared, and know what you want when they call you.”

“You mean the summons?”

“Yes, the stars will call, and you will need to answer.” She stared at me for a long moment, and I let my hand fall.

“I’ll figure that out,” I said with a sharp nod.

“Do.” She returned my nod, then glanced around. “I want to feel the sky.”

Strange jumped off my lap and padded closer to the waterfall.

“Let me allocate my free stats,” I added quickly, as I stood. I spread my free stats across my various weaker areas, but again the rush wasn’t like before. Instead, I almost didn’t notice it, and probably wouldn’t have if I hadn’t been paying attention. It was nothing like the core I ate.

I studied my stat sheet for a moment.

My eyes kept going back to Devouring Shadows and Shadow Walking. Both had the same vibe to them, almost like the text in front of me had a drop shadow added.

One thought crossed my mind. Can I merge those two skills?

I didn’t need to, but something inside me wanted to.

[You have merged Devouring Shadows into Shadow Walking - IV. Your skill has evolved into Umbral Stride. Umbral Stride: You no longer merely slip into the dark. You become wrapped in a living hungry void. While merged with any shadow, your form, sound, thermal footprint and mana signature drop to zero. Your shadows actively hunt and consume any ambient energy that would give away your presence.]

[You walk your path.]

The second notification flared inside my mind, and my title rattled around in my head. Hungry Shadow. Everything inside me burned just a little brighter for a moment, then I stood back in the cavern next to Lenna, who stared up at me.

#

Lenna, in the cavern…

Alex stood and I moved to follow, but her shadow suddenly vanished. The dark blob behind her just disappeared, making me freeze. Strange padded closer to me and shivered. While we both knew Alex would never hurt us, that didn’t mean that her power wasn’t… hungry.

Her presence flared through the cavern so strongly that I braced myself on the ground with my hands, then it was gone. Her shadow returned, somehow even darker than before.

Strange chirped twice, then darted right for it. As soon as he touched it, he turned into the same type of substance, flattening out until just an outline remained. Then even that merged in.

I swallowed hard.

The whispers that had congratulated me on my levels and earned growth for walking my path, lacked the same emphasis as what surrounded Alex.

I wanted that.

That strength to follow my own path, and help those I wanted.

Alex blinked, and glanced at me.

I quickly climbed to my feet with a grin and asked, “Are you ready to go?”

It took a moment, but she smiled. “Yeah, though I think I’ll be flying again to get to the jungle above. We need to get back to the portal that we arrived here from.”

That cold area didn’t excite me, but trekking through the jungle was also a pain. Hopefully, the trip wouldn’t be too long.

“We can use the tunnel to get above if you want,” I suggested.

Alex blushed. “I forgot about that. I’d rather not get soaked.”

I laughed a bit, and motioned for her to follow me. The tunnel to get back topside wasn’t far. The twists and turns didn’t take long. Though, in the darkness Alex completely faded away. Her form remained caught barely in the light my silver orb provided, but was nearly perfectly transparent.

I resisted asking about it. Everything inside me screamed that she walked her path with confidence.

We all needed that.

By the time we reached the hidden door, I wanted to run ahead and get outside. Instead, I opened it carefully. Soft sunlight filled the space, but before I could step outside, Alex went first.

Her outline filled in, and her eyes studied the trees like they might move. The jungle went quiet. Not silent, but quiet, like it knew something dangerous approached.

A faint whisper drifted down from the heavens, making me frown. Normally, I couldn’t hear anything like that during the day.

Something big was coming. Very big.
