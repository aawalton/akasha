Azran gazed at what used to be the maze of narrow corridors.

Where twisting passages had once coiled tight as a hangman’s noose, forcing those who attempted to pass through to commit to countless blind turns and dead ends, there now sprawled a single immense chamber. The adventurers’ explosives had torn through its dividing walls with indiscriminate enthusiasm, collapsing what careful malice had built into an uninterrupted expanse of fractured rock.

Nine months ago, he had walked through these passages.

Thirty-nine weeks.

Two hundred and seventy-six days, to be exact.

He had been here with Lahmia, traversing the maze together as they headed for the Core Room to steal the Core that lay waiting at the dungeon’s heart. Today, he found himself retracing those same steps, but with a different company, and for a different purpose.

“There was once a maze in this place,” Cedric said, oblivious to the fact that the man standing beside him was not an ordinary adventurer, but a Dungeon Reaver who had been here before. “But people have used explosives to blast through the walls to clear the way. As a result of their reckless destruction, the old staircase leading to the third floor has collapsed, buried under the rubble. At the same time, it revealed a new area, the desert where we’re heading now, spreading out beyond where the maze’s outer wall used to stand.”

Azran snorted. “You really think all of that happened by chance? That it was caused by the adventurers? No, of course not. The old route was destroyed because the dungeon wants it destroyed, and the desert was discovered because the dungeon wants it discovered.”

Cedric slowly nodded. “You’re right. This dungeon is a strange one. It’s very different from the others. So I wouldn’t be surprised if that really is what happened here.”

Azran took in the flat, stretched-out cave before him, his gaze sweeping across the jagged stumps of masonry protruding here and there like broken teeth, the last stubborn reminders that this place had once been something more than just a hollow emptiness.

“So that way is the dead end, huh?”

“Yes. As I said, the staircase is no more. At the end of the path, you’ll find a chamber. An arena of some sort. People said sometimes a Cyclops and a chest of gold would spawn there, waiting for those brave enough to seek them out. An optional challenge, I suppose, for whoever wanted one.”

Azran knew that room. More than anyone here, more than Cedric could ever imagine. He had stood in it. He had fought in it. With Lahmia, against the Crocodilian. Two of them had gone in, and only one had come out.

For the briefest moment, he wanted nothing more than to go back. To that place, to where he had last seen her.

Not that it would accomplish a thing, of course. He knew perfectly well what happened to those who perished in a dungeon. Her body had been stripped bare, then thrown unceremoniously into the disposal pit along with all the others who had failed to survive their own ambitions. So, if he really wanted to find her, the pit was where he would have to look. There he would find her broken bones, scattered among countless strangers.

He shook his head and let out a deep sigh. “Let’s go,” he said. “We should pick up the pace.”

“You’re right.” Cedric nodded. “If we’re taking a different route from them, then there’s no reason for us to hang about anymore.”

With that, they left the maze that was no longer a maze, and stepped into the desert of the dungeon’s second floor.

Cedric walked at the front of the column, the gnoll Noi’ri close behind him. Next was Fiora, the auburn-haired girl had always been with the young warrior whenever Azran saw him, and after her came the blond mage, Lucian. Azran himself took the rear, keeping an eye on everyone’s backs.

Above them, Renee, an aeromancer who had her hair tied in two large buns atop her head, circled in slow, sweeping arcs. She wasn’t a member of Cedric’s party, actually, but a friend of Quinn and Fiora who had offered to join this competition to help them out. Now she flew overhead, carrying her iron-tipped staff at her side as she scanned the surroundings to warn them of any danger before it could ever get near.

Their trek through the desert had been remarkably free of incident so far, however. Other than the merciless sun above and the scorched sand below, there were no obstacles whatsoever on their path. Nothing challenged them, nothing threatened them, nothing even tried to slow them down. And that didn’t sit right with Azran even one bit. In his experience, a dungeon that made its passage look easy was a dungeon hiding something far worse just out of sight. The last time he had been here, he and Lahmia hadn’t seen so much as a rat until they walked into that arena with the Crocodilian waiting inside.

He had voiced his concern to the others, and to their credit, they had taken his warning seriously. Noi’ri, especially. The gnoll had the look of someone who had learned caution the hard way, and didn’t need telling twice.

Renee, on the other hand, puzzled him a great deal.

Word was that her party had once ventured inside the complex surrounding that gigantic building, and the dungeon had come down on them hard. They barely managed to get out with their skins on—much like he had after Lahmia’s death—but two people who had joined their expedition weren’t so lucky. Given all that, he had expected her to be more on edge. But no. For some reason, she carried on as if the utter lack of monsters along their path were the most normal thing in the world. And since she served as the party’s scout, the one whose vigilance the rest of them depended on the most, that attitude troubled him deeply.

Still, despite the unease that kept gnawing at his mind, nothing came of it. Perhaps the dungeon was saving its surprises for the edge of the desert, or the third floor.

“Hey!” Fiora suddenly shouted. “What’s that?”

Everyone turned at once, following her outstretched arm to where she was pointing, and they saw... a flying boulder?

Yes, it was. A big-ass rock, tumbling end over end through the sky with terrifying momentum. Azran could tell that much despite the distance. And it flew past them and continued on toward the gigantic building looming beyond the dunes.

“What the hell is that?” Lucian asked.

“Ekon told me when his party went into the complex, they got shot at by a ballista,” Cedric said. “Is that right, Renee? ...Renee? Something wrong?”

Azran glanced up at the aeromancer, who hovered just above them, and found a completely different woman. Her previous confidence was gone. She now stared at that boulder without blinking, tracking it across the sky as all the color had drained clean out of her face.

What? Did she have a phobia of boulders or something?

“...Yes.” Finally, she swallowed. Hard. “You remember it right. Ballista bolts, yes. In fact, there was one in particular that kept chasing me relentlessly, and it could’ve killed me if Mandragora’s vines hadn’t lashed out and stopped it in time.”

“That sounds dangerous,” Fiora said.

“I imagine that boulder is the same sort of thing,” Cedric said. “Maybe the ballista isn’t the only weapon this dungeon has at its disposal. Maybe it has a catapult as well.”

“Catapult?” Azran snorted. “Considering the sheer size of that damn thing, it must be a freaking trebuchet.”

“So you’re saying,” Lucian said, “that thing just now was being aimed at the Iskora team?”

“Must be,” Cedric said. “And it does seem to have come from the far side of the desert, exactly as Ekon described it. We’re on the right track. Let’s get moving.”

Azran wasn’t quite sure whether this whole venture was a good idea, or if it was some elaborate trap being laid out for them. But Cedric had made up his mind, so there was nothing to be done but follow along and be ready to catch him when things went wrong.

Yes, this time around, he was not going to escape alone.

And so they stepped into the other half of the desert, the half that offered no ruins, no shade, nothing but sand stretching flat in every direction. Thankfully, they didn’t have to suffer it for very much time.

From up high, Renee found the boulder’s origin quick enough: a great maw gouged into the desert floor, she called down. After that, Azran and the young aeromancer hauled the rest there rather than let them trudge across the murderous stretch of sand on foot, so it didn’t take long before they stood at the lip of the thing.

The girl’s words proved true. There it was—a massive hole gaping before their eyes. And this, apparently, was the road down to the third floor.

“So,” Lucian said, “what—”

A thunderous BOOM cut him off, as though the world suddenly cleared its throat. The ground shuddered under their feet like something buried deep down there was trying to get free.

“What the hell was that?”

“Boulder has found its mark, by the sound of it,” Azran said with a shrug.

“Whatever it hit,” Fiora said, “I’m glad it wasn’t us.”

Those Iskora bastards were on the receiving end of a proper pounding, Azran supposed. The question was who was the one doing the pounding. If the boulder was indeed coming up from below, then it stood to reason the dungeon itself was behind the attack. But that boy Quinn’s theory, which Cedric believed wholeheartedly, was that it was the dark spirit thing hunting down those men for reasons of its own.

Not that it mattered a damn to him either way. All he wanted was to have the job done and get out of this cursed place as fast as possible.

And that was when the second BOOM came, this one somehow worse than the first.

The whole desert seemed to tremble.

And Fiora, who had been standing a bit too close to the edge of the chasm, was thrown into the dark.

Her friends screamed her name in panic, as though shouting at the air could help her somehow. Lucky for the girl, Azran had better reflexes than the children he was travelling with, and didn’t waste breath when there was action to be taken. He blinked to her side mid-fall, closed a hand around her shoulder, and blinked them both back to his original spot.

“Eh? What... what—?”

Fiora looked around in utter confusion. The whole thing had come and gone too fast for her to manage a proper scream herself.

While the two young lads went stumbling over to check her for breaks and bruises she clearly didn’t have, Azran cast a wary glance toward the great building that dominated the heart of the desert, from where, no doubt, the booms had come.

What the hell was going on there? He had not the faintest idea.

What he did know was that they had overstayed their welcome in this spot already. If it had been up to him, he would have sent all of them running for the entrance without looking back. But that was not an option. So the only sensible thing left to do was drop into that hole, get down to the third floor, and be done with this whole business.
