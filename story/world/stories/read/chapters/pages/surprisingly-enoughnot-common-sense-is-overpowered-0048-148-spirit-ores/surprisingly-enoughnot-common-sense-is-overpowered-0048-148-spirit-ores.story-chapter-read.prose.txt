Running into Xiao Fan so soon had been unexpected, especially after Ning had personally witnessed the strange, trouble-attracting aura that seemed to cling to him.

Still, Ning adjusted his mindset quickly.

There was no point worrying about something that had already happened. He would just deal with things as they came and try to benefit where he could.

Besides, Ning remembered enough transmigrator novels to know how these things usually went. The harder someone tried to avoid the plot, the more creatively the plot found a way to drag them back in.

So why bother?

If he couldn't reliably avoid it, he might as well go with the flow.

Maintaining a good mindset was important for cultivation anyway. Inner demons were a real thing in this world, and Ning had no intention of developing one again because he spent every waking moment worrying about protagonist-related disasters.

Over time, he'd gotten rather good at throwing unnecessary worries out of his head.

"Gambling?" Sun Jing blinked. "You mean spirit ores?"

"Yes." Ning nodded, then turned to Xiao Fan. "What do you think?"

In this world, when cultivators talked about gambling, spirit ores were usually what they meant.

Xiao Fan stopped walking.

"Spirit ores?" He frowned slightly. "You mean the kind used for gambling? I don't know much about them."

Sun Jing nearly stumbled.

"How is that possible?" he blurted out. "These things exist in almost every major city. I thought someone from the Xiao Clan would frequent these pavilions. Young masters like us usually come here all the time."

Ning watched Sun Jing's expression shift between confusion and disbelief.

He was a little surprised himself.

Then again, wasn't this also a common protagonist trait?

For some reason, protagonists could know obscure ancient secrets lost for ten thousand years while somehow missing things every random passerby on the street knew.

Of course, Xiao Fan had a perfectly reasonable excuse.

"It's not that I don't know what they are," Xiao Fan said, a trace of distance entering his voice. "I've simply never paid them much attention."

He paused before adding, "Back then, I was focused on other things."

His thoughts drifted back to the Xiao Clan.

The first few years of his life had been peaceful, even happy, while his parents were still around. After they disappeared, however, everything changed.

From then on, most of his attention had been consumed by two things: repairing his damaged spiritual root and searching for clues about his parents' disappearance. Compared to that, something like gambling on spirit ores had never seemed important.

Seeing the brief sadness cross Xiao Fan's face, Sun Jing seemed to remember the rumors surrounding him as well, particularly the disappearance of the Xiao Clan's heir—the man who should have been Xiao Fan's father.

Realizing he'd touched a sore spot, Sun Jing quickly changed the subject.

"Anyway, spirit ores are interesting because almost anything can be inside them."

This world had existed for an immeasurably long time. Across that vast stretch of history, countless treasures had been lost, buried beneath the earth, or simply forgotten.

Some had belonged to cultivators who died in places no one ever found. A few had enough time to establish inheritance sites and wait for some destined successor to arrive. Others simply died and left everything behind.

Then there were ancient sects.

No sect, no matter how powerful, could last forever. When one disappeared, its techniques, artifacts, medicines, and countless other possessions didn't necessarily disappear with it.

Xiao Fan listened quietly, arms crossed.

"And you're telling me," he said slowly, "none of that simply rots away?"

"Not exactly," Ning replied. "Some of those things eventually become sealed inside stone-like shells. Those are what we call spirit ores."

Ning had also read theories claiming that the phenomenon was somehow the world's way of preserving its own heritage.

He wasn't sure how seriously to take that.

Then again, he was currently standing beside someone who fit the definition of a protagonist disturbingly well.

Ning thought back to the recoverable spirit stone he'd obtained from Xiao Fan, followed almost immediately by trouble that should have fallen on Xiao Fan instead.

Maybe fate really did exist.

While Ning was thinking, Sun Jing nodded enthusiastically and continued, "Spirit ores usually appear in places with dense spiritual energy, especially spirit stone mines. The higher the grade of the mine, the greater the chance of finding spirit ores, and usually the better the things that can come out of them."

Xiao Fan let out a short laugh. "So the world hoards its own junk and sells it back to cultivators."

Sun Jing hesitated.

"...When you put it that way, yes."

"I see." Xiao Fan nodded. "Then where are we going?"

Ning also looked toward Sun Jing. This was his first time in the inner city, so he was just as curious.

"You've asked the right person." Sun Jing patted his chest confidently. "Just follow me."

Wherever money could be made, someone would inevitably build a business around it.

The Treasure Abundant Pavilion was one such establishment, specializing in buying and selling spirit ores.

"If I remember correctly," Sun Jing said brightly, "a new batch should've arrived by now."

As they walked, he eagerly began recounting his previous experiences.

Breaking open a spirit ore didn't guarantee anything good.

Some radiated dense spiritual energy yet contained nothing more than fragments of ordinary spirit stones. Others were considerably worse.

There were stories of cultivators breaking open spirit ores and getting struck by unknown curses. Others had managed to release sealed magical beasts that immediately attacked the person who had freed them.

Even gambling rocks were not safe in this world.

On the other hand, some spirit ores looked completely ordinary yet contained valuable artifacts, rare materials, or even spirit artifacts.

Those could make someone rich overnight.

"But I'm an old hand at this," Sun Jing added confidently. "In recent years, I've never really lost too much."

Ning quietly tuned out the bragging.

Sun Jing might have been wealthy and born a young master, but he wasn't arrogant in the slightest. If anything, he was a chatterbox, completely different from the cold and aloof young masters that seemed so popular these days.

"So?" Ning turned to Xiao Fan. "What do you think?"

"Sure." Xiao Fan nodded calmly. "I don't mind."

"Then it's settled." Ning gestured forward.

...

Before long, the three of them arrived at the spirit ore shop, accompanied by Sun Jing's nonstop chatter.

"This is it, the Treasure Abundant Pavilion!"

Unfortunately, despite it still being early in the morning, the place was already packed. Cultivators crowded around the entrance, forming a sea of heads that almost completely blocked the pavilion from view.

"Let's go!" Sun Jing said eagerly.

"Eh? But there are so many people." Ning looked at the crowd. "Don't we need to line up?"

"Relax." Sun Jing waved it off. "Most of them are just here to watch the excitement."

That wasn't particularly surprising. If there was one thing cultivators seemed to love, it was a spectacle. At the slightest hint that something interesting was happening, people would gather around to watch.

Ning followed Sun Jing deeper into the crowd, and it didn't take long before he began hearing the reason so many people had gathered.

"What shitty luck! I shattered ten low-grade spirit ores, and only one had anything inside. It was just a lump of low-grade spirit stone!"

"You're still luckier than that guy over there. He cracked five middle-grade ores and got nothing but rocks."

"Hey, did you hear? Someone earlier shattered a top-grade ore and found a hive of Red-Winged Wasps inside."

"Seriously? How unlucky can you get?"

Ning silently offered a moment of prayer for that unfortunate soul before continuing after Sun Jing, who glanced back at him with a puzzled expression.

By the time they finally reached the front of the pavilion, the noise of the crowd had grown even louder.

Countless stones of every shape and size were laid out before them. Some were no larger than a fist, others were as large as a human head, while a few massive ones towered over an adult.

Xiao Fan stared at the rocks for a moment. "Those... are spirit ores?"

"That's right," Sun Jing replied.

"Really?" Xiao Fan frowned. "They just look like normal rocks. Mm... no, I can feel spiritual energy coming from them, but it's very faint."

As he focused more closely, he seemed to sense something else as well, a subtle power hidden beneath the surface.

Sun Jing smiled. "Don't just look at their shape. Look at the patterns on the surface. That's one of the easiest ways to distinguish them from ordinary stones."

Ning leaned closer and compared them with what he'd read before. Sure enough, every spirit ore was covered in strange, intricate markings, and no two patterns were exactly the same.

"The clearer and more complex the patterns, the better the ore tends to be," Sun Jing continued. "Though that doesn't necessarily mean there's something valuable inside."

Spirit ores were generally divided into four grades: Low, Middle, High, and Top.

The Treasure Abundant Pavilion priced them accordingly. A low-grade spirit ore cost around ten spirit stones, while a middle-grade one usually went for thirty. High-grade ores started around a hundred, and top-grade ores could easily reach three hundred spirit stones or more depending on their size and appearance.

Higher-grade ores generally had a better chance of containing something valuable, but there were never any guarantees. Someone could spend hundreds of spirit stones on a promising top-grade ore only to crack it open and discover something nearly worthless.

Or, apparently, a nest of angry wasps.

Spending three hundred spirit stones only to be attacked by whatever happened to be living inside your rock was certainly an interesting way to gamble.

Just as Sun Jing finished explaining, a man approached them. Judging by his clothes and the way the nearby workers treated him, Ning guessed he was the shopkeeper.

"Well, if it isn't Young Master Sun." Shopkeeper Feng greeted him with a flattering smile. "It's been a while. Are you here to try your luck again?"

"It has indeed been a while, Shopkeeper Feng." Sun Jing stepped forward. "I'll be picking a few spirit ores today, as usual."

"Then you've come at the right time. You've been our customer for many years, so we've reserved a fine spirit ore especially for you. While I can't promise some priceless treasure, I can at least ensure you won't suffer a loss."

The two spoke with obvious familiarity, causing Ning to raise an eyebrow.

Earlier, he'd assumed Sun Jing's confidence was mostly bragging, but apparently not. Judging by the way Shopkeeper Feng treated him, Sun Jing really was an old customer here.

As expected of someone who had lived in the inner city before entering the sect.

"Haha, then I'll have to thank you, Shopkeeper Feng." Sun Jing grinned. "Don't worry. I'll play my part properly, just like before."

"That's a relief." Shopkeeper Feng nodded. "Come, let's head inside."

He personally led them deeper into the pavilion. As they walked, Sun Jing seemed to notice the shopkeeper's sour expression.

"What's wrong, Shopkeeper Feng? You don't look too happy."

Shopkeeper Feng sighed. "Business hasn't been good today. You must've heard the crowd outside. Nothing but complaints and hardly any cheers. No one's gotten anything particularly worthwhile from the ores today, and after watching so many people lose money, the others became hesitant to buy anything. Now most of them are just standing around watching."

His expression brightened slightly as he looked at Sun Jing.

"But now that Young Master Sun is here, perhaps we can turn things around. I've prepared something especially for you."

Sun Jing waved a hand modestly. "You place too much faith in me. Still, I'll do my best not to disappoint."

The two continued talking as they headed farther inside.

Ning was about to follow when he noticed Xiao Fan had stopped paying attention to them. His gaze kept lingering on the nearby spirit ores, as though something among them had caught his attention.

Ning nudged him lightly. "Did you notice something?"

"No." Xiao Fan shook his head. "Just... a feeling."

With that, he wandered toward another group of spirit ores.

Ning watched him go with narrowed eyes.

A feeling from the protagonist was rarely just a feeling.

Cultivators occasionally experienced strange impulses or intuitions. From what Ning had read, cultivation gradually brought someone closer to heaven and earth, and sometimes that connection produced feelings they couldn't properly explain.

Most of the time, such whims probably meant nothing, but Xiao Fan was hardly most people.

Ning looked around at the nearby spirit ores and decided there was no harm in trying it himself. He concentrated for several seconds, attempting to sense anything unusual among them, before his attention settled on one particularly suspicious-looking rock.

Nothing.

He stared at it a little longer before giving up.

Apparently, protagonist intuition was not contagious.

Shaking his head, Ning went after the others and soon reached the private room Sun Jing and Shopkeeper Feng had entered earlier. He pushed open the door without thinking much of it.

Then he saw the sword in Shopkeeper Feng's hand.

Its blade was pointed directly at Xiao Fan.

Ning had been gone for one minute.

What the hell happened?

...

A minute earlier...

Shopkeeper Feng took out a spirit ore and placed it in front of Sun Jing.

"Young Master Sun, this is the one we've prepared for you. It looks like an ordinary low-grade spirit ore, but something has already been placed inside. I don't know exactly what it is since my manager handled that part, but he assured me it's worth roughly as much as a decent artifact."

Sun Jing picked up the ore and examined it. "Haha, I trust you. So, same as usual?"

Shopkeeper Feng gave him a tired look. They had already done this several times before, but Sun Jing still insisted on confirming everything whenever he came.

With a sigh, he took the ore back. "I'll mix this among the ordinary low-grade ores. You buy several and make sure this is one of them. Once you shatter it and find what's inside, act surprised and make enough noise for the crowd to notice."

The plan was simple. Business had been poor all morning because everyone had watched one person after another lose money. If Sun Jing suddenly obtained something valuable from a cheap spirit ore, some of the crowd would regain their confidence and start buying again.

Sun Jing nodded seriously. "Understood. I'll make sure they notice."

Behind them, Xiao Fan was becoming increasingly uncomfortable.

He had followed the two inside without thinking much of it and hadn't expected them to start discussing something like this right in front of him. For a moment, he genuinely considered quietly walking back out and pretending he'd heard nothing.

Unfortunately, that option disappeared the moment he understood what they were planning.

A planted win to make the crowd believe their own chances were better than they actually were.

Xiao Fan frowned. Sun Jing had helped him before, and he considered the man a friend, but that didn't mean he could simply ignore something he thought was wrong.

"Sun Jing, Shopkeeper Feng." He stepped forward. "Isn't this cheating the customers?"

"What nonsense are you spouting? This isn't cheating, it's—"

Shopkeeper Feng stopped halfway through his response and finally turned around.

For several seconds, he simply stared at Xiao Fan.

His expression slowly changed.

"Who are you?"

Xiao Fan stared back with almost equal disbelief. He had been standing there the entire time. How had this man only just noticed him?

Sun Jing seemed to realize the problem at the same moment and hurriedly introduced Xiao Fan as his friend. Unfortunately, explaining that Xiao Fan had been with them from the beginning only made Shopkeeper Feng's expression worse.

The man looked from Xiao Fan to Sun Jing and back again.

"You brought someone else in here and didn't tell me?"

"I thought you saw him," Sun Jing replied awkwardly.

Shopkeeper Feng clearly had not.

His face went pale as he glanced toward the closed door. If word got out that the Treasure Abundant Pavilion was planting rewards inside spirit ores to encourage customers to gamble, his manager would skin him alive.

Panic quickly overtook whatever remained of his composure, and his hand moved toward his storage pouch.

Sun Jing's expression changed immediately. "Shopkeeper Feng, don't do anything stupid. We can talk about this."

Unfortunately, Shopkeeper Feng apparently considered drawing a sword part of the talking process.

Xiao Fan's expression darkened as the blade pointed in his direction, while Sun Jing hurriedly moved between them and tried to convince the increasingly panicked shopkeeper that threatening a Pure Qi Sect disciple was perhaps not the best solution to his current problem.

That was when the door creaked open.

Ning stepped inside.

"What happened? I was gone for barely a minute..."

His words trailed off as he took in the room.

Shopkeeper Feng was holding a sword, Xiao Fan looked ready to punch him, and Sun Jing stood between them with the expression of a man regretting several recent decisions.

Before Ning could ask for an explanation, Shopkeeper Feng noticed the new witness and panicked even further.

"There's another one?!"

That proved to be the wrong decision.

Xiao Fan's remaining patience disappeared immediately. "You old man! I was already tolerating you. Now you're pointing a sword at us?"

Ning's mouth twitched.

Wonderful.

Sun Jing hurriedly stepped between everyone again before the situation could become any worse, then quickly explained what had happened.

Ning understood almost immediately.

So the pavilion was planting a winning ore to encourage the crowd outside to start gambling again.

Shady, certainly, but not particularly surprising. Casinos in his previous life had spent centuries finding ways to convince gamblers that they had a chance of beating the house. Apparently, changing worlds hadn't changed business practices very much.

The bigger problem was the situation in front of him.

Xiao Fan clearly wasn't going to accept the arrangement quietly, while Shopkeeper Feng looked one bad sentence away from doing something extremely stupid. If they actually fought, the commotion would draw the attention of the entire pavilion. Then the manager would get involved, perhaps some guards, and knowing Xiao Fan's luck, Ning wouldn't be surprised if the building somehow exploded before lunch.

Better to stop things here.

"Shopkeeper Feng, put the sword down first. None of us came here looking for trouble."

Perhaps because Ning was considerably calmer than everyone else in the room, Shopkeeper Feng actually listened. The sword lowered slightly, though he still looked nervous.

"Xiao Fan and I aren't going to spread what we heard," Ning continued. "Since all you need is for the customers outside to see someone win, we can help Sun Jing finish this without making things worse."

"Ning!" Xiao Fan stared at him in disbelief.

Ning glanced at the sword still in Shopkeeper Feng's hand. "I'm agreeing to the option that doesn't end with us fighting the shopkeeper inside his own pavilion."

Xiao Fan followed his gaze and reluctantly fell silent.

Ning turned back to Shopkeeper Feng. "Besides, if Sun Jing goes out there alone and immediately gets something valuable, it might look suspicious. Let the three of us choose ores and open them together. It'll draw more attention, and when Sun Jing gets something valuable, there'll already be a crowd watching."

Shopkeeper Feng considered it for a moment before slowly nodding. "That could work."

"Good. Then give Xiao Fan and me two middle-grade spirit ores each, and we'll play along."

Shopkeeper Feng's expression immediately changed. "Why do I have to give you anything?"

Ning smiled. "We're helping you resolve a rather delicate situation."

The room fell silent for a moment.

Surely two spirit ores were a reasonable consultation fee.

Sun Jing quickly stepped in before Shopkeeper Feng could object further, agreeing that the three of them opening ores together would attract far more attention than him acting alone. By now, Shopkeeper Feng had also calmed down enough to realize that trying to silence three Pure Qi Sect disciples inside his own shop was probably a worse idea than simply trusting them.

After a moment of hesitation, he finally put the sword back into his storage pouch. With the immediate danger gone, Sun Jing spent the next few moments convincing Xiao Fan that refusing wouldn't stop the pavilion from doing the same thing later. At least this way, nobody would get hurt, and they could keep the situation from becoming worse.

Xiao Fan still looked unhappy.

Then Sun Jing mentioned the free middle-grade spirit ore.

"...We're getting those for free?"

Ning looked at him.

Xiao Fan coughed. "I still don't approve. But since Brother Sun has helped me before, I'll give him face this once."

Sun Jing immediately brightened and threw an arm around his shoulder. "Haha! We're brothers. Of course we help each other."

Xiao Fan gave him a long look but ultimately didn't argue.

Ning watched the exchange with some amusement. Apparently, moral objections became slightly more flexible once free things entered the equation.

Not that Ning was judging.

He was getting a free spirit ore too.

"Fine." Shopkeeper Feng rubbed his forehead as though he'd suddenly aged several years. "Choose your ores and let's finish this quickly. We've already spent too long in here."

For once, everyone agreed.
