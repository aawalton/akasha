The ruined city stretched endlessly around the industrial complex that had been designated as their fortress. Lee Hyunwoo stood on the rooftop of a half-collapsed warehouse, surveying the surrounding perimeter. Rusted cranes loomed over the nearby blocks, their long arms frozen above the district like skeletal giants. Below, barricades of scrap metal, concrete slabs, and overturned trucks had already been arranged into layered defensive lines.

Under his direction, the army moved with practiced efficiency. Divine warriors patrolled the outer perimeter while others reinforced choke points along the factory entrances. Watch posts had been established atop the taller buildings, granting clear sightlines across the surrounding streets. Compared to the chaotic conditions that had plagued many factions in his previous life, this formation was already far more organized, and it was precisely what he had intended.

“This area is much better than I expected,” Kim Taegon remarked as he stepped beside him, folding his arms. “Good line of sight, solid walls, and plenty of scrap to reinforce defenses.”

Hyunwoo nodded slightly. “The terrain does favor us. Industrial complexes are naturally built for logistics and storage. That means thicker structures and multiple internal layers. It’s much harder for monsters to break through quickly.”

More importantly, it was predictable. Unlike open terrain, every street surrounding the complex funneled approaching enemies into narrow avenues. With the right positioning, even a smaller force could hold out against overwhelming numbers. This place was also the reason why they could prevail in Hyunwoo’s past life.

Park Sooyeon approached from behind, her staff resting lightly in her hand. “The statue has been secured,” she reported calmly. “There’s a barrier surrounding it, just like the archangel described.”

Hyunwoo turned slightly toward her. “Good.”

A blue holographic screen hovered before his eyes.

[Party Formation – Active]

Several dozen divine warriors had already been integrated into temporary parties under his command. The skill allowed him to coordinate smaller units efficiently, distributing buffs and improving battlefield awareness among allied members. It was the upgraded version of the Party Creation skill, which could only form a single party of five. Party Formation, however, allowed him to establish several independent parties.

It was one of the reasons Aurelion’s faction had become far stronger than in his previous life. Of course, organization alone wasn’t enough.

Hyunwoo’s eyes turned toward the distant skyline. Somewhere beyond the ruined city blocks were the other two factions.

“The Goddess of Imagination…” he murmured quietly.

In his previous life, her faction had collapsed early. Their divine warriors had lacked unity, and internal conflicts had weakened them long before the real battle even began. Their supposed leader—Graham—had been incompetent. But Hyunwoo still frowned slightly. Even with his regression, one rule remained absolute in Divine Will: never underestimate unknown variables. After all, it had been precisely such variables that had led to his downfall in his previous life.

That was why he had gone to great lengths to acquire the Lie Detection skill. The only way to maintain control over the battlefield was to distinguish truth from deception. With that ability, he could anticipate potential threats and deal with them before they got out of hand. If there were traitors or infiltrators among their ranks, he would be able to root them out quickly.

Furthermore, to ensure that Istellia’s divine warrior army collapsed just as it had in his previous life, he had already dispatched a special party to sabotage them. He believed that they would be more than enough to deal with Istellia’s side.

Still, the true threat of the first stage wasn’t this.

He turned back toward his companions. “Taegon. Elena. Ethan.”

The three of them stepped forward immediately.

“Monsters will attack soon,” Hyunwoo said calmly. “But they’re not the real threat. The other factions are. More specifically, the one under the God of Deceit.”

If he remembered correctly, Velkhar’s faction would not make any major moves during the early phase of the first stage. However, since Hyunwoo was aiming for first place, he knew they would eventually attempt to destroy his fortress. Before that could happen, he intended to seize the initiative and strike first so they could maintain momentum.

Taegon cracked his knuckles. “So what’s the plan?”

Hyunwoo looked toward the distant streets before answering, “First, we hold the fortress and secure the surrounding area by clearing the thrall camps. Once the battlefield stabilizes… we begin hunting the other two factions.”

“Now that sounds like a plan.” Taegon’s grin widened slightly at those words, as if already eager for the coming battle.

Elena leaned against the rooftop railing, the crimson accents of her armor catching the dull gray light of the ruined sky. “A bit aggressive, I guess, but it makes sense. If we sit here waiting, the other factions will eventually come to us.”

Ethan, standing beside her in polished knight armor, nodded in agreement. “Clearing the thrall camps will also reduce the monster pressure on our fortress. It’s a logical first step.”

Hyunwoo inclined his head slightly. “Precisely.”

The thralls were more than just servants of the eldritch creatures that infested the city; they were catalysts. Wherever thralls gathered, monsters, particularly the zombies and flying octopuses, inevitably followed. Eliminating those camps early would not only weaken the incoming monster waves but also secure valuable territory around their base. More importantly, it would buy them time.

Hyunwoo’s gaze drifted toward the ruined streets again. In his previous life, Aurelion’s faction had been forced onto the defensive almost immediately. They had wasted precious time reacting to the battlefield instead of dictating it.

This time would be different.

But he didn’t allow himself to grow complacent. Even with the memories of the previous timeline, Hyunwoo knew better than to act recklessly. Velkhar’s divine warriors were cunning and unpredictable. Charging straight at them without preparation would only play into their hands. He would make sure every variable was accounted for and that nothing went wrong.

Just as he was steadying his thoughts, hurried footsteps echoed up the stairwell.

“Sir Hyunwoo!” a divine warrior shouted as he burst onto the rooftop, breathing heavily. “The thralls—!”

Hyunwoo turned sharply. “What about them?”

“They’re attacking us!”

“What?”

The thrall camp was a grotesque sight.

Rows of crude tents made from torn tarps and rotting fabric filled the ruined plaza between collapsed apartment blocks. Rusted vehicles had been dragged into circles to form crude boundaries, and in the center of each cluster stood grotesque totems carved from bone, scrap metal, and blackened stone.

Divine warriors weren’t the only ones preparing for war. Dozens—no, hundreds—of thralls shuffled through the camp. These thralls looked different from the ones in the second scenario, and they were clearly much more powerful. Many of them knelt before the totems, whispering incoherent prayers while pressing their foreheads against the ground.

[Abyssal Thrall – Lv.26]

[Abyssal Acolyte – Lv.53]

[Abyssal Brute – Lv.56]

[Abyssal Devotee – Lv.82]

A thrall whose body has fully adapted to the corruption of the eldritch entity it worships. Its physical strength and resilience far exceed that of ordinary Abyssal Thralls, and it can channel unstable abyssal energy through its body to enhance attacks.

[Abyssal War Acolyte – Lv.124]

An advanced priest who has survived numerous rituals and mutations. Its body has partially transcended normal limits, allowing it to wield powerful abyssal magic capable of corrupting the battlefield itself. These thralls act as battlefield coordinators for lesser servants, directing hordes of monsters and thralls in the name of their unseen master.

[Abyssal Juggernaut – Lv.130]

A heavily mutated thrall that has abandoned all traces of its former humanity. Towering in size and covered in hardened abyssal growths, it possesses monstrous strength and durability capable of smashing through fortified defenses. Though it cannot wield magic, its sheer physical power and relentless aggression make it one of the most dangerous frontline creatures among the thrall ranks.

Watching them from the shadows of a broken building was me. Or rather… not me. At the moment, I looked exactly like one of them.

The Mask of a Thousand Races had reshaped my appearance perfectly. My skin had taken on the sickly gray hue of the thralls, while a faint parasitic growth coiled around the side of my neck like a half-grown tentacle. To any observer, I was nothing more than another servant of the eldritch parasites.

“Disgusting,” I muttered quietly.

Still, the disguise worked flawlessly. None of the thralls paid me the slightest bit of attention as I walked deeper into the encampment. Also, I had unsummoned both Ember and Solana beforehand.

Before long, I reached the largest totem standing at the center of the plaza. Unlike the smaller ones scattered throughout the camp, this statue was nearly four meters tall. Its form resembled a twisted humanoid figure with dozens of tentacles branching from its head like a grotesque crown.

More importantly, I could sense a dense concentration of belief, devotion, and fanatical worship—faith—accumulated within the structure. Even thicker than any statue I had encountered elsewhere.

Without hesitation, I stepped closer, carefully mimicking the posture of a worshiper as I approached. Lowering my head slightly to maintain the disguise, I placed my hand against the cold surface of the stone.

Absorb Plausibility.

You have activated Absorb Plausibility.

The moment the skill was activated, invisible threads of energy began flowing into my body. The faith contained within the statue reacted violently. Compared to the faith I had absorbed in the past, this one was far more corrupted, saturated with the warped devotion of countless thralls. It struggled to resist, as if trying to lash back at the one stealing it.

“Hello there, old friend.”

However, Absorb Plausibility easily suppressed the resistance. Unlike the first time I had absorbed corrupted faith back during the second scenario, the power no longer had any chance to overwhelm me. The tainted faith was quickly stripped of its corruption and converted.

You have absorbed the power of faith contained in the statue.

You have gained 519 Plausibility.

Not too bad, I thought. If the faith had been slightly purer, the amount would have been much larger.

Still, I could barely suppress a grin. There were several more statues like this scattered throughout the other camps. If I could absorb them all, my plausibility reserves would increase rapidly. Of course, that wasn’t the only reason I was here.

I stepped away from the statue and glanced toward the largest cluster of thralls. Time to start the real show. Raising my voice slightly, I mimicked the distorted tone the thralls used when chanting. “The fortress…!”

Several heads snapped toward me.

“The enemies of the Great One gather there!” I shouted, pointing toward the distant industrial complex. “Their defenses are weak! The offering must be claimed!”

The effect was immediate as they reacted like a hive suddenly stirred into motion. Dozens of the more intelligent thralls began screeching in manic devotion.

“THE OFFERING!”

“THE FLESH!”

“KILL THE BLASPHEMERS!”

Within seconds, the entire camp erupted into chaos. Hundreds of thralls surged toward the streets leading to the industrial complex. As they moved, they gathered thralls from the other nearby camps along the way, swelling their numbers into the thousands. Since they were going to attack eventually anyway, riling them up proved far easier than expected. I didn’t even need to use Grant Plausibility or call upon Thalos through Fabled Vessel.

With the thralls gone, I calmly walked toward the next statue in one of the nearby camps. It was time to collect the accumulated faith.
