“Oh, man. How long was I out for?” Rex groaned and stretched. He had been so tired that he had slept right atop a rock, and he was feeling it. “I really need a portable bed or something.”

He was far too young to be complaining about a bad back.

“Okay, levels. Let’s take a look.” Rex climbed out of his hiding spot, got comfortable, and opened his System.

“Spells. What to pick—Ack!”

Rex jumped out of his skin, bouncing up onto his feet in one smooth motion as he spotted the blue blob rolling toward him. It had only been an inch away from his hand, and he rubbed it reflexively as he remembered how it dissolved things.

“Okay, no damage. That’s something. Why did you follow me, you persistent little—shit!”

He quickly checked his belongings, feeling for his ring and reaching around for his new bow.

“They’re still there. You know,” he said, pointing at the blob. “You eat my new bow, and I swear, I will kill you. Exploding fire be damned! How long have you been here?... Wait, have you been here while I was sleeping?”

There was no answer, but a moment later, the blob jiggled in a way that suspiciously resembled a nod.

“Really? And you didn’t eat me or my bow? Look, this doesn’t make us even for earlier. You still owe me! But…” Rex trailed off, trying to decide how he actually felt.

He’d seen the blob in action. If it had found him asleep, it wouldn’t have had any trouble dealing with him, so then, why hadn’t it? Why had it spared him? Or the artifact that it clearly wanted.

“You’re gonna need to give me a little more than that.” Rex crossed his arms. “Why’d you follow me, and why are you… I dunno, being friendly? Well, for a thief, at least.”

The blob jiggled.

“Of course you can’t talk,” Rex sighed.

It was one thing to pull a yes or no answer out of it, assuming its little jiggle had even been a nod, and Rex wasn’t just seeing what he wanted to see. And another entirely to get it to answer questions.

Rex’s stomach rumbled, distracting him from the blob and reminding him that it’d been over a day since he had eaten anything.

“Oh, I left those damn bush turkeys back at the battle sight, didn’t I?”

He couldn’t go back, either. It was too dangerous. He would have to go hunting, and that was best done deeper in the forest, as more hunters were likely to come looking for him.

“Look, thanks for not eating me. But you’re still a thief. I’m going to go this way, and you, I dunno, do whatever you do. Okay?”

Rex started walking, glanced over his shoulder, and spotted the blob rolling after him.

This isn’t going to work, is it? I might as well do my leveling now. It doesn’t seem like he has any intention of trying to eat me. Then I can figure out my next move.

He brought up his System screen and eyed the options again.

Right, I guess [ Weightless ] is probably the best, all things considered. Or maybe I just get the darts. I have to think about survival, after all.

As he tried to make a decision, the blob jiggled again.

“What do you want? Haven’t you done enough damage already?”

But when he tried to refocus on his System screen, an idea struck him.

“Hey… little guy. I wonder…”

Rex grinned wildly as his gaze settled on the blob. It might have been a monster, but it wasn’t trying to kill him, and he remembered that monsters were already connected to the System, without needing to challenge the Shrine.

“If you really want to make it up to—”

He was cut short. He had sent a party invitation, not expecting it to work. But even as he spoke, the blob accepted.

“You’re eager.”

Mana Slime [ 12 ] has joined your party

“Wait, twelve? You’re level twelve? That’s outrageous! I’m only level five, and I killed a group of Freedmen hunters! What’d you do to get your level?”

The faceless slime stared back.

He had to fight back his jealousy. His plan had actually worked, and it changed everything. A monster had actually joined his party despite all odds.

This slime was actually able to join my party, and if he keeps following me around, then…

Rex had been drawn to [ Transference ] the moment he saw the spell, but without a party, and with slavers hunting him down, he had dismissed it. But if the slime was going to keep following him.

“Wait, stay still. I need to try something.”

He reached out and used [ Harden ] on the slime, and to his surprise, a spellcircle rippled across the monster’s surface.

“So it does work. And you didn’t attack me, either. Interesting. Okay, perhaps you’re not so bad. Look, here’s the deal. You work with me. You help me kill the people hunting me, and I’ll let you eat the less valuable artifacts we get, deal?”

The mana slime jiggled excitedly. And while Rex didn’t want to part with any artifacts, first and foremost, he needed to survive. Once he was safe, he could negotiate with the monster or come up with another plan.

Putting my trust in this little guy is a massive risk, but if it works…

Rex had already taken a pretty sizable risk when choosing his first spell, and that had turned out amazingly for him, so he was feeling pretty confident when he selected [ Transference ].

“Okay, you stand right there and don’t move.”

He used his new spell, [ Transference ], on the slime, linked it with a mana thread, and started walking.

“No. Don’t follow. Just stay where you are. I’m not leaving you. You’re far too valuable for that now.”

Once he had created some distance between them, he activated the spell, and in an instant, he was standing exactly where the slime had been.

“It worked! Did you see that?”

The slime came rolling out from some bush.

“That was amazing, wasn’t it? Damn! That could be seriously useful. Okay, okay. Calm down. Don’t fuel your aura. You’ve still got a spell upgrade and Feat to pick,” Rex murmured to himself as he took a breath.

The slime wiggled.

“I suppose if we’re working together now, you need a name. Hmm… How about… Slimer!”

Slimer remained motionless.

“I know it’s not very creative, but unless you can come up with something better? Right, I didn’t think so. Now, moving on. Spell upgrade time.”

Rex knew enough about upgrades to know that they essentially just made the Spell or Skill better. But without details on specific Spells and Skills, it was hard to predict. One spell might do more damage, while another might cost less mana or gain an extra effect.

Because of this, Rex was quick to select [ Corrosive Touch ]. In all likelihood, it would do more damage. He didn’t know how [ Harden ] could be improved, and he hadn’t used [ Transference ] enough to know if it needed improvement.

Of course, he could have waited. Tested his new spell out more. But there was always a chance that the hunters would attack again. Besides, if he survived, he’d gain more upgrades at higher levels.

The moment he made his decision, he walked over to a tree and marked it with [ Rune Scribe ].

“Okay, better stand back for this one just in case.”

The slime followed Rex as he took cover behind a larger tree and activated the spell.

Suffice to say, he was not disappointed. The area of effect for [ Corrosive Touch ] had significantly increased, and the spell melted straight through the trunk, bringing the entire tree down.

He winced as it slammed down against the forest floor and then looked around.

“That was louder than I would have liked. We’d better finish up and get moving.”

Flicking through his System, he quickly moved on to selecting his Specialist Feat.

Sharpshooter [ Basic ]

The Sharpshooter specializes in taking opponents out from afar. This Specialist Feat significantly improves the distance that the user can see.

Trapper  [ Basic ]

The Trapper specializes in traps to take out their prey. This Specialist Feat allows the user to add certain Feat and Attribute bonuses to their traps.

Ambusher  [ Basic ]

The Ambusher specializes in luring their enemies into favorable conditions. This Specialist Feat reduces the user’s aura presence and provides a minor camouflaging effect.

“Damn, not bad,” Rex muttered as he looked over the options, thinking back on fights he had been in.

Specialist Feats were a little special in the sense that they were far more malleable than most aspects of a Class, since the options provided were adapted based on how one fought when gaining levels.

He couldn’t complain about the options. All of the Specialist Feats sounded pretty damn good. For example, an archer, being able to see great distances, would come in handy, especially with his new bow and [ Powershot ].

“Yeah, tempting…”

Moving on, Trapper sounded good. Who knew what deadly traps he could make by combining Feats and Attributes with them? However, it also gave him an idea…

If I had used Corrosive Touch on the spikes in my spike trap, I might have saved a lot of effort.

It was an interesting Feat, but his Class’s unique take on casting spells meant that he could likely achieve something similar without the Feat. Heck, inscribing spells on his traps would probably be even more effective, since he didn’t really have any Feats that would work with a trap yet.

Finally, he got to Ambusher. In truth, it was hard to stop himself from immediately selecting it. Was it the strongest of the Feats? He wasn’t sure. But stuck in a dense forest and hunted, it seemed like a no-brainer. Ultimately, there was little point in planning for a future he might never see if he got killed.

The aura reduction alone is worth taking the Feat. It could easily mean the difference between getting killed out here and not.

Taking one last glance over the options, Rex confirmed his selection, purchasing Ambusher, and looked up at the little, wiggling slime.

“I’m done. And we had better get moving. I don’t know how far away that tree would have been heard, or if anyone would think anything of it, but I’d rather not find out. Besides, I’m starving.”

It was time to go hunting, and the slime seemed to roll along the forest floor with extra vigor.

He or she sure seems excited at the mention of food, Rex thought, convincing himself that the slime really did understand most of what he said.

Lavrian

Bile slugs were relatively weak, but nasty to deal with for non-casters, which made hunting them rather profitable. The problem was that the little beasts had a tendency to explode with acidic bile when killed.

For young Lavrian, though, that was perfect.

A nest of bile slugs had sprung up around a rural Gate used for trading between a few small outposts and Tartarus. Passing through it, they had found themselves on a distant and poor outworld with a decisive lack of spellcasters on hand to deal with such problems.

Good contracts were not easy to come by for newbies. Everyone wanted to hunt weak monsters. Their deaths meant levels, and the easier, the better. In fact, newbie parties often even went as far as bidding on contracts like goblin slaying.

So, to gain a profitable contract, and an easy one at that, was ideal. And since Lavrian was the entire reason his party was able to take the contract, he had become the group’s de facto leader.

From afar, he threw fist-sized bolts of lightning at the slugs, making them explode with crackling arcs of energy. When one-shotted, the slugs were easy to kill. But they were surprisingly fast when enraged, which caused issues for ranged combatants like archers. If your arrow didn’t kill instantly, you could easily end up with an exploding slug in your face.

As Lavrian dispatched the slugs, three others now stood around him, reduced to guard duty.

“Argh! This sucks. He’s getting all the experience while we just stand around!”

“Don’t let us stop you. Feel free to go get some kills for yourself,” quipped a young girl armed with daggers.

The barrel-shaped boy sighed. Despite holding a tower shield and dressed like an armadillo, he was not too keen to take his chances with the slugs.

“Right, I thought so,” she added as he went silent.

“There we go. That does it,” Lavrian said proudly as he blasted the last of them away and brushed off his hands. “Another two levels to boot. You guys really need to work on your form. At this rate, I might leave you behind. It would be troublesome to have to find another group.”

“Raagh!” The armored boy huffed.

“Oh, calm down.” Lavrian rolled his eyes.

“It’s fine, Davard. We’ll be more useful on the next contract,” the girl said, calming her armored companion. “Besides, we’ve earned some coin. We can buy some gear to help us.”

“About that,” Lavrian said. “Since I did all the work, I’ll be taking a larger cut. It’s only fair.”

“That wasn’t what we agreed!” She hissed through gritted teeth.

“Oh? I don’t remember agreeing to dragging around dead weight, either.”

Davard, their armored companion, tightened his grip around his mace.

“You had better calm down before I take offense,” Lavrian said.

The girl placed a hand on her companion’s shoulder. “Relax. We don’t need the trouble, and it’s not easy to come by casters looking for groups.”

“Good,” Lavrian nodded as the boy lowered his weapon. “Now, let’s get a move on.”

“What about the loot? Shouldn’t we check them for artifacts?”

“No. These are nothing but hatchlings,” said their healer, who had remained quiet throughout the argument. “They haven’t lived long enough to form artifacts. And even dead, their bile can cause nasty burns. I would be careful about touching them if I were you.”

“Haha. Hear that? Our healer can’t handle a little acid. You lot really would be useless without me,” Lavrian said. “Their shells are also worth a few coppers each. It would be a shame to leave them.”

“He is right about that,” the girl said.

“So, we just have to burn our hands and hope your healing is good enough to deal with the consequences?" Davard said.

“That, or I take most of the contract reward since I did all the killing,” Lavrian said.

The others looked at each other, and then over to the bile that was hissing as it bubbled on the ground.

“I suppose it’ll be fine,” the healer said. “I am full of mana. Just be careful. One shell at a time.”

“And don’t take too long,” Lavrian said. “I want to get this contract handed in. I don’t entirely trust this Credo fellow. He came off too eager, back at the tavern.”
