Chapter II.XL (2.40)

An albino barn owl sat perched on the mast of a skiff, red eyes carefully watching Mitsuko’s approach down the purple wooden dock. Mitsuko, of course, knew the bird on sight. There were very few white barn owls in the world. The odds of another happening to be here now seemed astronomically low. A moment later, Fuku confirmed the bird’s identity.

“Rou will, obviously, accompany us on our journey,” Fuku explained. “I instructed him to fetch our captain.” She paused, then lifted an empty hand, palm open.

“Yoouch!” There was a thud from within the small ship. “Whaaas that? Blimey. Sculpt spittle on the back of the eye.”

A disheveled head poked up from behind the gunwale. The man gaped at them, something crusty and yellow coated his short beard. He rubbed his bloodshot eyes. Mitsuko noted a bit of a sway in his stance that had nothing to do with the placid bay. That alone was enough to reveal his condition.

“Please don’t tell me this is who we’re trusting to get us to Amber Island,” Mitsuko pleaded.

“Captain Hamish is the only person currently on the island with both the knowledge and clearance to access the wizards’ island,” Fuku said. “I need but a moment and I will clear his head of intoxication.”

“Witch! Don’t!” Hamish ducked back behind his skiff’s gunwales. Unfortunately for him, the physical barrier of wood did nothing to protect him from Fuku’s spellcraft.

Mitsuko noted Fuku softly muttering something to herself as she waved her hands. Some sort of witch curse, likely designed to amplify the spell. Hexes and curses usually involved a verbal component that was unnecessary in most other fields of magic outside of rituals.

“Noooo,” Hamish bemoaned. “What have you done to me, witch? You’ve stolen everything.”

“He’s no longer drunk. Let us board and depart from this island.”

Mitsuko shared a look with Verna, who’d poked her head out of the bag. They both shrugged and stepped on board the skiff.

The small boat shifted under their weight. She examined what exactly they were taking to the sea on. It was by far the least sea worthy vessel that she’d been on since the Prismatic Spiral’s start. Even the lifeboat she’d drifted on had been in better condition.

The faded name at the bow read ‘Mag’s Flam’ with a large space between the lettering. Mitsuko assumed it was supposed to say Mage’s Flame. The wood across the deck and gunwales had been replaced so many times as to make the original coloring and material unknown. Mitsuko was no shipwright, but she felt confident that it wasn’t standard practice to have different types of wood on the same hull. It looked as if he’d taken from trees across the archipelago for the replacements as she spotted gray, yellow, purple, and green wood. All gritty and with barnacles growing on more planks of wood than not. With such a hodgepodge amalgamation of materials, Mitsuko doubted even her Mend spell could revert this to a decent condition. Where would she even begin?

“Who’s this?” Hamish asked from his slouched position on the floor. He gestured at Mitsuko with an empty bottle clutched in his hands.

“My name’s Mitsuko. I need to get over to Amber Island.”

“I told the witch yesterday. We should wait until the dome thing goes away. Too many dangerous unknowns.” He frowned. “Was it yesterday? Time’s tricky without day and night. All seems like a blur.”

“Cut off from the tides, the sea is calmer than ever,” Mitsuko said. “Forget ‘dangerous unknowns,’ no offense, but I doubt the Mage’s Flame is likely to make the journey any other time”

“I do take offense,” Hamish huffed. “Mage’s Flame is a beauty. You simply don’t understand her.” He patted the deck softly, as if comforting an offended child.

“We will be fine to travel,” Fuku said. “Take us wherever my companion desires. It has been sworn to me that our souls will remain unmolested so long as we comply with her demands.”

Hamish stared at Mitsuko.

“I’m not going around threatening to molest people’s souls,” Mitsuko clarified. “Fuku is repeating the advice of an outside source, someone I do not know and do not care to know.”

“Judging by his face, he either doesn’t believe you or doesn’t understand you,” Verna said.

Mitsuko sighed. “Can you just deliver me to Amber Island? You can leave the moment I step foot on the island. Stay on Amber, return here, go all the way to Verdant, for all I care. I just need a ride.”

“Hm. What’s in it for me? I mean, I’m contracted to bring her to the island.” This time Hamish pointed the empty bottle at Fuku. “Not you.”

“I will not crush your mind and force your flesh into a puppeted thrall,” Fuku offered.

Hamish edged away from the woman. He rubbed his large, sunburned nose and muttered something suspiciously similar to a swear.

“Fine. Let’s get off this blasted island. But, I promise you this, you clear my mind of alcohol again and I’ll drown myself in sea water instead. You’ll be left stranded at sea and die.”

Mitsuko, having been left stranded at sea multiple times now, did not feel particularly concerned about this threat. Not to mention, not once in all these loops had she even ever actually died from being stranded at sea. Even on the leviathan corpse, she’d been set up to survive the rest of the loop before the chance arrival of Zeaqu.

Hamish rubbed his fingers together in a weak attempt at a snap and the ropes mooring them lazily slithered onto the deck. They looped around in a sloppy coil and went still. Then he rolled onto his feet and stumbled his way over to the wheel. His little skiff jerked and wobbled, then slowly started to make its way out of the port.

A Sailor’s Guild member chased them down the dock, yelling about all ships being detained. Hamish yelled back about it being ‘wizard business’ and the guild member instantly retreated.

“Are you a wizard?” Mitsuko asked.

“Yeah. Can’t you tell?” Hamish didn’t even bother looking at her. He spun the ship’s wheel and redirected them.

“Marine navigation is his specialty,” Fuku said. “He knows more about the local currents than any mortal soul.”

“Doubt there are many immortal ones better either,” Hamish muttered.

“Do you have a rainbow sleeve?” Mitsuko asked, curious. “How many colors?”

“Dropped mine in the sea years ago. They won’t give me another. Hardly matters anyway since they all know who I am.” He eyed a cupboard precariously nailed onto the gunwale by the wheel. Reaching for it, he brought out a bottle of golden brown liquid. Mitsuko spotted an actual stream of drool dribbling down his chin. One hand still on the wheel, he uncorked the bottle with his teeth.

As Mitsuko settled in for the journey, she couldn’t help but reflect on how Hamish reminded her a bit of Gina from her first loop. It was probably just the alcoholism connection. As she watched him finish one bottle and start on the second, she wondered if she should check back in with the gossip. Ever since the woman had sold her out to her father and ex, Mitsuko had avoided her, but she was still a prime source of information in regards to the island’s goings-on.

Then again, did Mitsuko really need more city gossip? She was at a stage in the loops where she knew how to get everywhere she needed to be except for Azure Island.

“Maybe she’d know a local crust I could interrogate,” Mitsuko muttered to herself.

“Who?” Fuku cocked her head. “What need to you have for crusts?”

Mitsuko blinked. She hadn’t thought her murmuring would be heard over the lap of water against the skiff. But, of course, she hadn’t taken into account Fuku’s familiar bond. Those with familiars often took on traits of those animals. Owls happened to have excellent hearing.

”It’s nothing.” Mitsuko quickly thought up an excuse. “When I arrived on the archipelago, my ship was attacked by a kraken. I want to question some crusts as to how they’ve somehow allowed a kraken to nest so near their home waters. They’re supposed to produce the most elite sea monster slayers in the world.”

“Excellent deflection,” Verna said from beside her. “Not a lie, but almost certainly not truthfully what you were dwelling on.”

Fuku nodded slowly. “I agree, that is odd. I will assist in cracking open the minds of any crusts we encounter. I haven’t had the opportunity to experiment on their species so it will take perhaps a few attempts. Such an experience will serve as an interesting exercise.”

“I… appreciate the offer.” Mitsuko wondered about the ethics of destroying someone’s mind in a timeloop. Normally it would be out of the question, a fate worse than death. Here though, where every action was undone at the end of the week, it could be a genuinely good method of gathering information. Something to consider and debate internally at another time.

While the skiff sailed on, Hamish would occasionally check a golden compass he kept in his jacket pocket and adjust the small ship’s course accordingly. Mitsuko assumed that the compass was directing them towards Amber Island, though when she questioned Hamish about it, the seafaring wizard waved her away like a bothersome mosquito.

It quickly became apparent that this would be the most drab of all her journeys. Fuku kept staring at her with her oversized eyes, which meant Mitsuko had no ability to practice her spells. She simply didn’t trust the witch with the secret of her temporal magic. Without the ability to chat openly with Verna, Mitsuko was left with only two social options. The drunk man at the skiff’s helm, or the white-haired witch who held the power to deconstruct her entire brain on a whim.

Suffice it to say, the following hours were spent silently staring at the empty sea, lost in thought rather than conversation.
