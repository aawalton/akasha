In the dream, I’m crouched in the shadows of a wall, watching as the tournament grounds are erected before me.

It’s the middle of the night, and the air is cool and quiet. The voices of the mages drift easily across the open field of grass as they work their magic. A lattice of trees are sprouting from the ground, roots and branches groaning as they twist themselves into a forest in a matter of minutes. Some sort of hedge maze, perhaps?

No, that doesn’t seem like Lorata. What would a labyrinth have to do with her domain? Light, intelligence, foresight…

The trees’ branches are weaving together, covering the tunnels with tightly bound roofs. Perhaps one is meant to maneuver it in the dark? That still doesn’t seem right.

A twig snaps to my left and I whirl around, heart in my throat. They wouldn’t disqualify me for snooping, would they? It’s not my fault they were so obvious about where they’d be setting up the tournament grounds. They can’t have expected students to just wait around, twiddling their thumbs.

But no, it’s just another student—another competitor. I recognize him from alchemy class, but I never bothered to learn his name. No one’s bothered to learn mine.

The boy snorts. “And I thought they said no one could ever sneak up on you.”

I glare at him. “Who says that?”

He shrugs, crouching down behind me. “Everyone.”

“Shut up.” I turn my attention back to the trees. “They’re going to hear.”

“As if they don’t know we’re already here,” he says, though he does lower his whisper.

“Why would they…” I trail off as I realize what he’s getting at. Foresight. Is that it? Does Lorata want us to prepare by scoping out the fields in advance, learning as much as we can prior to the tournament? I wonder how many other competitors are also hiding nearby.

Hopefully not many.

More students passed that first round than anticipated. The hidden message in Lorata’s letters had been embarrassingly easy to reveal, however; a simple Light spell resulted in the letters rearranging into the location of the next stage of the tournament. I spent more time than I’d like to admit searching for a second, deeper message, convinced it couldn’t be so simple.

The second test had been more engaging, at least. It had included a series of logic puzzles to deduce the ingredients needed to combine into a potion. What that potion did, and if it was meant to be drunk, or topically applied, or used on something else entirely, was also part of the test. More than one student ended up poisoning themselves during this stage of the tourament—in various and spectacular ways. In fact, the potion itself was incomplete without a final component: a drop of holy water from one of Lorata’s shrines.

After that it was safe to drink, and the resulting spell summoned illusions only apparent to the one who consumed it. The brew forced its drinker to face their deepest fears and, if overcome, rewarded them with the location and date of the final test. This, of course, had been trivially easy for me to surmount; fears are hardly scary when you know they’re not real.

Even so, there were nearly fifty students ready to tackle Lorata’s final task. She initially said whoever completed the final test first would be her champion, but I’m skeptical of that claim. Lorata doesn’t do anything fast: she does it thorough.

So what is she intending with these woven tunnels of trees? Trees are more Lord Nellida’s domain, anyway.

Light, wit, foresight…

My undesirable companion, who I’d managed to ignore quite successfully for the last few minutes, lets out an annoyed breath.

“Why are you even doing this?” he hisses.

I cock an eyebrow at him. “Same reason you are, I’d bet. Or do you not remember the point of this whole tournament?”

He shakes his head. “But you can’t actually think she’d want you. Even if you did win.”

My lips peel back in a sneer, but I clench my teeth shut before I can snap back. It’s not anything I haven’t heard a hundred times before.

“Guess we’ll just have to wait and see.” I try to ignore him, focusing on the trees. Maybe it will require a combination of the previous two tests. The letter and the potion—

“You’re an insult to Lorata,” he whispers, and I go rigid. “She’s everything you’re not. Just being in her tournament is an affront to her light.”

Heat rises up my chest, and I clench my fists, trying to stop myself from doing something I’ll regret. I’ve gotten this far—I’m not about to let my temper get the best of me.

“You’re going to ruin this for all of us,” he continues, his voice growing lower and angrier. “You being one of the finalists will make the rest of us look bad. What if she doesn’t think we’re worthy of her time? If someone like you could make it this far, what does that say about the rest of us?”

I exhale my anger. He’s just bitter. He feels threatened by me. No different from the other students, offended they could lose to some sickly lowborn.

Whatever. I won’t have to deal with them for much longer—not when I win this. Not when I become Lorata’s champion.

“You’re a shadow to her light,” he hisses, his words worming into my mind no matter how hard I try to block them out. “Unworthy. How could you ever serve as her hands? Her eyes? No. I’ll make sure you don’t.”

“What—”

I’m halfway turned back toward him when he lunges at me. There’s a blade in his hand. I react on instinct, the shadows of the wall rising between us, but his weapon cuts right through and plunges into my side. I gasp in shock, in pain, in fury at the injustice of it all. How can this be happening? Why now, here? When I’m so close?

No.

Hot tears sting my eyes as I kick out, blasting the boy back like he was rammed by a boar. I stagger to my feet, hand pressed to the hot agony melting into my side.

“No,” I snarl, limping after him as I wrap the night’s shadows around him. “You don’t get to decide what I can do. Who I am. What I am worthy of.”

I gasp as I pull the knife from my side. My hand shakes, and I want to return his blade the same way it was given to me. I want justice. I want to feel vindicated. I want to expel all my anger and shame and frustration in one final blow.

Instead, I sob, dropping the knife as I sink back to my knees. This isn’t what Lorata would want. This isn’t who I am. I’m not a killer.

The boy opens his mouth to respond. Then he freezes. And then he dissolves into the ground.

“What?” I croak.

The knife disappears as well, melting out of existence. Then the wound in my side. Even the pain vanishes. I sit there, panting and bewildered, wondering if I’ve lost my mind.

“Well done,” a woman’s voice says. Lorata’s voice. Yet the lawn is empty—I’m sure I’m alone. “You faced your fears admirably. I am happy to invite you to the final round of my test.”

An odd fog lifts from my mind—a fog I hadn’t even noticed until this moment.

The potion. It had still been in effect. But it’s been days since I took it. At least, I think it’s been days.

I fall back against the cool night grass, breathing hard. Tears are still streaming from my eyes, but I don’t bother wiping them away. I laugh, and it turns into a sob.

“Are you crying?” Álvaro asks in disbelief.

I groan, sitting up as I hastily scrub the front of my shirt against my face. “Can you blame me? First thing I see is your ugly face.”

Cyros barks out a laugh.

I stretch my arms next, wincing at the stiffness in my limbs. Hard packed dirt isn’t exactly my favorite surface I’ve slept on since arriving to this world. Sure beats hospital beds, though.

“So uncalled for,” Álvaro says, faking affront. “Here I was, just trying to make sure you were okay!”

“Just a stupid dream,” I say, stifling a yawn. Really stupid. I can’t believe I’m having dreams about wanting to be a champion. My subconscious has betrayed me.

Rubbing the crust—and sand—out of my eyes, I blink blearily around at my surroundings.

It’s just about sunset, the dunes stained red on the horizon. The spelled shade-cover Cyros had set up before we went to bed has since been torn down, and the first couple of stars are starting to wink into existence overhead. We’ll be traveling during the night while we head for the wyrm’s den; the daytime heat is brutal outside of the city’s cover.

“Come on, get up,” Álvaro urges. “I’m starving!”

I crawl out of my bedroll—more designed to keep sand out rather than warmth in—and summon my Apothecary at the edge of our makeshift camp.

“I’m not your personal chef,” I say, popping my neck as I survey our supplies.

“I mean, you kind of are,” Cyros says. “Technically speaking.”

I try to glare at him, but it ends up as more of a squint.

“It is literally your Role,” Álvaro helpfully adds.

“Oh, shut up,” I grumble, grabbing a pot and some grain. Even if I didn’t have my Chef Role, I was planning to make food for them—they’re really bad at cooking.

My daily Role Requirement was already satisfied back when we ate dinner at dawn, so I don’t get a notification when I start prepping food now. Even so, I find myself Checking the Role out of habit; better safe than sorry.

We’ve only been on the road for one day and the Moonfall mesa is already out of sight. Hopefully that means Widengra’s champions will still be searching for us around the city, but I won’t be counting on it. They tracked me and Cyros down somehow, so there stands a chance they can do it again. This time, we’ll just have to be ready for them.

Cyros and Álvaro are chatting when I sit down with them and offer up the skillet of roasted veggies and rice.

“And no one has magic?” Cyros is asking Álvaro in awe. He grabs a bowl, barely glancing my way as he stays laser-focused on his current conversation.

“‘Thanks for the breakfast, Sal,’” I say. “Hey, no problem. It’s the least I could do.”

Álvaro grabs a bowl, too. “Nope. Well, not like people can do here. There was stuff like fortune telling and hypnosis.”

I roll my eyes. “None of that was real, you know.”

“Yes it was!” Álvaro insists. “My abuelita totally did tarot card readings.”

“What’s that?” Cyros asks.

Álvaro shovels a spoonful of fried rice into his mouth, and I wrinkle my nose when he doesn’t even finish chewing before replying to Cyros. “It was like this deck of cards that you could use to tell your fortune. She tried to teach me, once, but I wasn’t any good.”

Cyros is utterly encaptivated. “That sounds amazing! We don’t have types of fortune telling magic here. Well, aside from Lorata—it’s said she can see the future. But, you know: gods. What else was different about your world?”

“Our world was boring,” I grumble, digging into my own bowl. “Why are you asking about it, anyway?”

He shrugs. “Because I like to learn. And it’s not like you’ve been forthcoming.”

“Really?” Álvaro tips his bowl back and scrapes the last bits directly into his mouth. “Why not?” He holds the bowl out for more.

“Because it’s not safe.” I snatch the bowl from him. “And how did you finish that already? I literally just gave it to you.”

“Technically, you gave it to him one minute ago,” Cyros says. He lifts an impressed eyebrow at Álvaro. “Which is still fast, to be fair.”

“It’s been a while since I’ve had anything homecooked.” Álvaro waves off the food questions. “But why would it be dangerous? I mean, aside from the two homicidal champions who are after you.”

I respond with a flat look.

“But it’s not like telling him would make him a target,” Álvaro points out. “Like, he’s here whether or not you fill him in. The only difference is that he can be better prepared if he’s in the loop.”

Cyros gestures to both of us. “That’s what I’ve been saying!”

“Guess it doesn’t really matter, anymore.” I fill up Álvaro’s bowl with a second serving and then shove back in his direction. “Cat’s out of the bag now.”

“Oh!” Álvaro happily accepts the fried rice. “Speaking of cats. What was that badass claw thing you used the other day?”

I glare at him.

“It’s a gift from the gods,” Cyros replies automatically, but even as the words leave his mouth, he pauses with a faint frown. “Or, that’s what I thought. You got that from Shirasil, didn’t you?” His eyes widen. “The Tournament. That’s when you became his champion.”

“That’s sick,” Álvaro says. “Hey, you think he’d make me a champion, too? I’d love a magic weapon like that.”

“What about that spear I got you?” Cyros asks.

The weapon is laying nearby, and Álvaro glances its way with a forlorn look. “You know that’s not the same as giant magic green claws.”

“I’d hand it over if I could,” I grumble, finally given a moment to dig into my own breakfast.

“I don’t get it,” Álvaro says. “Why do you hate the gods so much? I mean, yeah, it seems like Widengra is a jerk, but what’s Shirasil done? He must like you if he made you a champion.”

My stomach clenches, and I focus on swallowing down the knot of rice that’s moving slowly down my throat.

“Not now,” Cyros says gently, giving Álvaro a significant look.

Álvaro glances between us in confusion, then must seem to put something together. “Uh, yeah, sure thing. At any rate, if you’ve got a card like that up your sleeve, you should play it more. I mean, you don’t have to like the guys who gave it to you to make use of a tool, right?”

I regard him warily. His reasoning makes an annoying amount of sense. “Maybe,” I grumble. “We’ll see. In the meantime, I’m going to start practicing with the smaller variety.” I gesture to the sheathed claws on my belt. “Increase my Proficiency with them.”

Álvaro perks up. “Good idea! I should practice with my spear, too. I’m only at Level 1 Proficiency. Maybe we can spar while on the move!”

“That sounds like a total waste of energy,” Cyros says. “And water.”

“No way,” Álvaro disagrees before I can do the same. “We need to get better with these weapons before we reach the wyrm’s den. And we have plenty of water, right? You guys did the math.”

“We will be moving at night,” I point out to Cyros. “That will help conserve water.”

Cyros just shakes his head with a sigh, going back to his breakfast. “I don’t think I could stop you two if I wanted to. Just don’t hurt each other—I’d rather this mission not take any longer than necessary.”

Álvaro grins at me. “Sounds like a yes!”

I snort at his enthusiasm. “Alright, fine. Let me eat and get everything cleaned up, first.”

“I’ll help!” Álvaro volunteers. He hops up to start sorting through the dirty dishes and utensils I’d used to make breakfast. “You got soap or something?”

In spite of myself, the guy is starting to grow on me. He’s just too earnest for ulterior motives.

And hey, maybe I can use sparing as an incentive to get him to clean up after future meals, too.
