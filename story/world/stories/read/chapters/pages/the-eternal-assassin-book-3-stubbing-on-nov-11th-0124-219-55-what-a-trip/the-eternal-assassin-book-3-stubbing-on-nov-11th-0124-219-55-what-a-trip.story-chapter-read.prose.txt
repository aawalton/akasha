Asher’s journey through dimensions was honestly far less eventful than he’d been expecting. He knew from his hours of research into the different realms that nearly all travel between realms technically involved dipping into the void for a mere fraction of a second. But just like the books had claimed, that portion of his journey must have happened so fast he didn’t even notice.

No, he was primarily distracted by the shaking, spinning violet bubble surrounding him.

Asher was hurled through space in what looked like some sort of magical hamster ball. He had no doubt the tritessence making up the sphere around him was protecting him from all sorts of crazy forces, but he wasn’t exactly about to thank Richard for its protection. Asher himself was bounced around and jostled every which way, and by the time he figured out how to keep himself stable, the trip was already over. With one final, violet flash, the ball vanished, dropping Asher to his hands and knees on a grassy plain.

“Richard...” Asher muttered weakly, desperately trying to keep his lunch down as the world continued spinning around him. Even with Complete Resilience helping him combat nausea, that trip through the realms had been like a roller coaster on steroids. “I’m gonna… stab you… so hard…”

Eventually, he looked up, and all his thoughts about precisely how he was going to get back at the court wizard were forgotten as his mind raced to make sense of what it was seeing.

He was currently on his hands and knees in some sort of empty, grass plain, but that only went so far. A few miles away was what appeared to be a castle, complete with eight large towers that stretched up into the sky. More curious, however, was the fact that the grassy plain seemed to simply stop and change into a dark, rocky terrain. As if a young God had drawn a line between the two regions and demanded that nobody else cross over onto their side. The very sunlight itself changed to night, and the peaceful, sunny sky surrounding him transformed into a stormy, heavy rain, complete with lightning cracking down every so often.

And if that wasn’t weird enough, that was only the beginning.

The more Asher looked at the distant castle, the more he realized the minor details seemed to be constantly shifting and changing. After turning to look up at the lightning-filled clouds, when he returned his gaze to the castle, suddenly, it had only six towers instead of eight. After closing and rubbing his eyes to try and make sure he wasn’t seeing things, he opened them and jumped as he discovered he was no longer looking at a castle at all, and everything else had changed as well. Other than the small patch of grass he currently knelt on, the plain itself had somehow been replaced with endless sand in all directions. The distant castle was no more, but now there was a massive herd of some sort of camel with three humps wandering just beyond the ridge that hadn’t existed moments ago.

Any time he turned his focus away from anything, whatever he’d been looking at moments ago threatened to change completely. It didn’t always happen, but it occurred frequently enough that Asher couldn’t help but feel as though he’d gone insane. The world itself didn’t make sense anymore, and Asher immediately knew where Richard had thrown him.

“The wild realm,” he muttered, realizing in an instant this was both good and bad.

The wild realm was the only major realm known to be even less stable than the material realm. From what he’d read about, it was a land of utter chaos where just about anything could happen at any moment. The books he’d read discussed changing landscapes, creatures appearing out of thin air, and even elements spawning in places that were entirely irrelevant to the element itself. Asher knew the wild realm was regarded as one of the favorite realms for those with the means of traveling between realms to go to, as the payouts could be massive if they were lucky.

Though on the other hand, the dangers were innumerable and unpredictable.

Asher immediately got to his feet, shoving the Signal of Notification Richard had given him into his Personal Rift before pulling out his mithral dagger and a steel one as he eyed up the distant camels. None of the creatures looked like they wanted to eat his liver or anything, but there was no telling when it came to the wild realm.

He’d read accounts of tiny, harmless animals morphing into monstrous beasts the moment someone turned their back on them, so he wasn’t planning on taking any chances if he could help it.

“God damn it, Richard,” Asher sighed, already starting to sweat from the intense heat of the surrounding desert. Using Comprehend to skim through some of the books he’d Incorporated earlier, he went back over the rules of the wild realm that famous realm wanderers had spoken of.

Rule number 1: Never enter the wild realm on your own. The realm itself was so unstable and volatile that unless someone was actively watching it, it was liable to change at a moment’s notice.

Rule number 2: Don’t enter the realm without an emergency escape already prepared. Horrifying monsters or deadly terrain could appear at any moment, so a quick get away was necessary for survival.

Rule number 3: Bring a teleportation specialist with you. Not only are they essential for escaping danger, any location of interest was liable to vanish or change before it was reached on foot.

Asher mentally closed the book in his head, letting out an even greater sigh as he realized he’d already broken all three of the recommended rules for anyone daring to visit the wild realm. Granted, he hadn’t even known he’d be coming here in the first place, so he couldn’t really fault himself for that.

I am going to kill Richard when I get back to the material realm…

Realizing he’d forgotten something obvious, Asher focused on his Recall, curious if he could just teleport home all on his own. Unfortunately, he quickly realized that wasn’t going to work. While he could still sense his Eternal Marks, albeit vaguely, almost like they were deep underwater, he could tell that he couldn’t quite form the usual connection he whipped up in an instant between himself and his marks with Recall.

Regardless of what happened, he was stuck here until Richard got his tritessence back.

“It’s only an hour,” he muttered, flinching as he felt something cold brush against him from behind. Spinning around, he blinked as he found himself facing the edge of a monstrous blizzard. Even more alarming were the ten-story-tall snow golems slowly lumbering around in the distance, each step sending a booming quake through the hot sand at his feet.

…How much trouble would killing the court wizard actually get me in?

Before he could draw the distant attention of the golems, Asher closed his eyes tightly, slowly counting to five. Willingly taking his eyes off dangerous threats went against everything he believed in, but here in the wild realm, it was the quickest way to escape from a dangerous situation.

Or make it worse, he realized as he opened his eyes only to find he’d somehow been transported immediately before the closest golem.

The monstrous golem roared, rearing back an icy fist as it swung down at him. By reflex, Asher tried to slip into the astral, intending to let the giant fist the size of his car phase right through him.

Only to discover Astral Dip didn’t work in the wild realm.

In the split second before he was splattered by a few tons of ice and snow, Asher leapt into his own Personal Rift, dodging the attack by millimeters as he suddenly found himself safe within his rift. Panting at the close call, he mentally smacked himself for trying to rely on Astral Dip. Just like the realm of ages, the astral realm mirrored the material realm. Those three realms were all tightly connected, and separate from the other major and minor realms. That meant Astral Dip only functioned on the material realm, where hopping over between the two was a quick thing.

The skill wasn’t nearly strong enough to transport him through the void that now separated him from the astral realm.

Alright, I'm stranded in the wild realm for the next hour, or until Richard actually gets around to bringing me back. Knowing that prick, he might leave me here for a bit longer, just because he can. I don’t have Astral Dip to rely on, which means things are going to be a little bit trickier. The good news is that Personal Rift still works, even if it’s a rather costly choice.

Asher winced as he took stock of his current energy reserves and confirmed his fears. Opening his Personal Rift on the material realm barely took any energy. Opening it in the astral realm, on the other hand, took a rather noticeable chunk, almost like it was harder for him to pry the skill open.

Opening it on the wild realm took an even bigger chunk of his energy, and he was losing a non-insignificant amount with every second he continued hiding out within his rift.

There goes just hiding out within my rift until Richard gets around to bringing me back… I suppose at the very least, I’ll probably survive, just based on the fact that Donvath didn’t barge in and stop all this from happening.

But even as Asher had the thought, he realized that might not actually be the case. He already knew there were plenty of things that messed with Donvath’s ability to see into the future, and he was willing to bet the other realms of existence fell into that category. The void was famous for consuming just about everything, and seeing as it separated the material realm and the wild realm, Asher feared it would prevent Donvath from seeing anything related to what was currently happening.

First thing’s first, I can’t keep burning energy just to hide in here. Already, Asher had lost a good quarter of his energy simply from dodging that snow golem’s attack, and feeling it continue to drain away didn’t exactly fill him with hope. Preparing himself to be on the receiving end of a giant icy fist once more, Asher took a deep breath and leapt out of his rift.

Landing in a shallow lagoon that went up to his knees.

Asher spun around, doing his best to keep everything within eyesight all at once. It seemed major changes, like the entire terrain and world shifting around him, were only at risk of happening when he looked away for at least a couple of seconds. So long as he kept his eyes peeled and slowly turned in a circle, the wild realm kept the constant changes to a minimum, such as changing the exact appearance of the nearby trees or the temperature of the water gently lapping at his legs. As he spun, he took in his new surroundings.

The lagoon was filled with large, dark trees growing straight out of the water, their roots reaching out and making up the very ground beneath his feet. Each one looked to be at least a few hundred years old, and Asher couldn’t help but briefly admire the gnarled wood. There were schools of tiny fish with far too many fins darting around in the shallow water, the light reflecting off their shimmering scales as they darted away whenever he moved his feet. All in all, the lagoon was a massive step up from the roasting desert or the golem-infested snowstorm, and Asher decided he’d be fine with simply chilling here until Richard pulled him back to the material realm.

Which naturally was when one of the long roots beneath his feet snapped up, wrapping itself around his ankle and flipping him upside down as it yanked him into the air.
