“Good!” Illadrox roared, swiping his axe at me, forcing me back. “Fight harder! Make your carcasses worth dining upon.”

Eryn sent five arrows into the monsters surrounding us. Thunder rolled across the plain as each lightning bolt chained between multiple monsters, bringing down an entire flank.

Coldhide rose and we attacked from two sides.

The variant’s axe lit up with blue light, and it struck in a wide arc, the light extending far beyond the edge, the way Veskar’s had. Coldhide took the ability across the chest, and it cut deep and wide through fur and flesh. I ducked sideways, but the end caught my pauldron and spun me around.

Counter triggered, wrenching my arm to hammer Roq into Illadrox’s side. It seemed to do nothing but tickle him, and I stopped Counter. Normally, the blows from it would be devastating and worth surrendering control of my arm for a moment. But in this battle, I couldn’t afford the cost.

I turned back and caught the next blow on Roq, but before I could counter, Illadrox turned and struck Coldhide, opening a deep gash in the bear’s neck, and then swung for me again.

I’d seen Rowan fight like this with Battle Rhythm, once he got it going. The longer he fought and the more hits he got in, the faster he struck. If this was a similar ability, Illadrox would eventually overwhelm us.

The axe rang off Roq, cut Coldhide, and came back at me before I’d recovered from the first block. But it was a feint, and the stump struck the side of my helmet. Coldhide snapped at the variant’s leg, but Illadrox kicked the bear beneath the jaw and buried his axe in the bear’s side, where it stuck on something.

Illadrox couldn’t swing while Coldhide hung from the axe. The bear screamed in pain, and my legs barely held me, but the opening was enough. I triggered Rupture and swung Roq into the leg I’d already wounded, spike first.

The tip of Roq’s spike sank into the cut I’d already opened.

“Severance!”

“Done.”

I released all the mana pent up inside him. It spread below the hide, damaging flesh and muscles beneath.

The leg buckled.

“Hit yourself in the face with your axe!” Roq roared, a mouth opening in the hammer’s flat surface.

Severance didn’t work, and Illadrox didn’t hit himself in the face. But I hadn’t expected that either. I just wanted to see where the connection from Illadrox went. Hundreds of lines ran from Illadrox into the army. One ran the other way, stretching due east from Illadrox toward the mind giving the orders. It seemed Cageheart was farther east still.

Then Illadrox clubbed me in the head with his stump and I stumbled away.

A Firebolt burned a Slicer’s throat out before it could reach Eryn, dropping it among the ice.

Then Mara shouted over the battle.

“Strike me!”

Illadrox ignored her call, and I barely blocked a strike from him, but the other monsters nearby all turned from us and charged, throwing themselves at her.

Mara buried her axe in a Bial’s eye, lopping the top of its head clean off, while arrows thudded into the monsters surrounding her.

An Earth Spike erupted beneath a Kruhaggoth. That had to be a weak spot in their armor because the spike pierced straight through, impaling the beast.

“Got the variant!?” Mara called.

“Of course!” Roq replied before I could. “It’s nearly dead!”

“We’ll hold the rest!” she called, weaving her axe around her in large sweeps.

I wanted to tell her to get over here and help kill him, but barely blocked an axe strike with Roq.

Before I could recover, Illadrox struck Coldhide, then my chest, cutting a new line in the Panoply. I lost track of the axe after that, until it crashed against my helmet and knocked me down.

Coldhide lunged, ignoring his own wounds, but Illadrox chopped into the bear’s shoulder again, worsening the wound and knocking him back.

The variant turned back to me before Coldhide landed, but instead of striking me, he lifted the axe before his face, blocking three arrows.

That bought me time to drag myself upright on shaky legs.

“Tell Mara we need help!”

“Mara, I might have exaggerated how close to defeated the winged beast is,” Roq said begrudgingly.

“For rift’s sake!” she called.

Monsters surrounded her five deep as she and the deserters killed them as fast as possible.

I barely blocked an axe strike with Roq, and then gripped Illadrox’s wrist, but he ripped free and struck at my leg, cutting armor and flesh open. The stump hit the front of my helmet, blood splattering through my broken visor, and drove me back down.

Blood Forge moved from my chest to my thigh. If I couldn’t move, I couldn’t fight.

“The knee! Somebody make this winged bastard kneel before the king!” Roq roared.

Eryn shot at Illadrox, but hit his cheek, the arrow piercing through and into his mouth. He snapped the shaft with his teeth and spat it away as he swung the axe for my neck.

An Aegis Gate appeared between us, and the axe cracked into the shield, destroying it. But it had stopped the blow long enough for me to trigger Overload.

Yellow lightning snapped from my pauldrons. Illadrox didn’t even twitch.

Then Arclight Blinked in behind the variant and sank her fangs into his knee and twisted.

Illadrox’s leg folded, sending him down on one knee. He chopped for Arclight’s back, but Coldhide body-slammed him, driving him to the ground.

Illadrox ripped his leg from Arclight’s mouth and shoved the bear away with his good leg. Then he rolled onto his back.

Before he could rise or strike, I activated Resonance and lengthened Roq’s haft and increased the size of his head, shoving my hammer onto the variant’s chest. It did no damage, but that wasn’t my plan.

“King’s Anvil!”

“Fifteen seconds!”

Illadrox tried to sit up, but couldn’t move, and his eyes went wide.

“Now render judgement upon this foul beast!” Roq said.

Arclight attacked the variant’s legs, biting and ripping, while Coldhide threw itself onto the axe arm, accepting another cut to trap it against the ground.

Illadrox heaved, but Roq didn’t shift.

I let go and stood, trying to think of how to end him.

“Let this one open the path,” Snarky said, and turned into her Primal Form with a puff of smoke. She slithered over the ice, between dead monsters, and looked down at Illadrox’s head.

“Venom Ray!”

Green acid struck Illadrox across the eyes and face. He screamed and thrashed, his horns scraping furrows through the ground.

“Ten seconds!” Roq said.

I dropped to a knee beside his head and channeled Mass into both hands. A Kybadur spike formed in my left, thicker than my thumb and broad-capped for striking. A short hammer formed in my right, little more than a head and handle.

Illadrox jerked his head from side to side. Snarky slithered around his neck and one horn, tightening until his head stopped moving.

“Six!”

I stabbed the spike into Illadrox’s left eye, the acid having burned away the eyelid, and struck the cap with the Kybadur hammer.

The spike sank partway in.

Illadrox’s stump punched into my side, denting the Panoply and nearly tearing the spike from my hand.

I struck the cap again, driving the spike deeper.

“Three!”

The little hammer glowed golden as I activated Smash and struck the cap with everything I had.

The spike sank to its broad cap, disappearing into Illadrox’s skull, and he went still.

Energy rushed through me as I leveled.

King’s Anvil ended. I grabbed Roq’s haft and canceled Resonance, shrinking him to normal.

“Told you I’d kill him,” Roq said.

“We killed him.”

“I was on top.”

I swiped Illadrox and its axe into my spatial and hurried to Eryn. A Quillhound volley arced toward us, and I covered her with my body as quills skipped around us and several snapped against the back of my Panoply.

Blood ran from cuts across her body and one side of her face, left by Glacier’s Shatter. But she’d live.

Arclight had Blinked to Lan and covered her, while Snarky slithered over.

“Get to the wall!” I shouted and hung Roq on my belt, lifting Eryn into my arms. She didn’t protest, which scared me more than the blood.

My wounded thigh folded with the first step, but I just shifted Eryn higher and kept moving, letting Blood Forge do its thing.

Arclight rolled onto her side with her back to Lan, and then Snarky slithered beneath the two of them, before going up and over.

When Arclight stood, Lan was bound to her back by Snarky, and the lightning cat padded over to Nabeeh. “Fire mage. In,” she said, opening her jaws.

“By the robes,” Nabeeh said. “Not an arm.”

Coldhide stood on three legs over Knut, the fourth hanging limp.

“Can you carry him!?” I called.

The bear growled, put its good front paw beneath Knut, and then rose onto its hind legs, throwing the northerner over one shoulder and lumbering toward the walls. If not for the monsters around us and how close my friend was to dying, it would have been a hilarious sight.

Mara led the deserters as they closed around us.

Arclight ran with Nabeeh held carefully between her teeth, and despite limping on her wounded side, kept pace.

“Faster!” Mara called. The deserters’ mana had to be running low, and their abilities would be on cooldown. We were in no state to keep fighting.

The city wall rose before us, and the faces crowding the battlements took shape as we neared.

Bows were lifted and mages stood at the ready, hands raised.

As soon as we came within range, arrows were loosed and the mages spent mana like wine at a wedding.

Explosions rang out all around us, and we ran through clouds of dirt. Walls of water rushed by on both sides, lightning thundered down around us, and stones shot into the monsters.

The barrage cleared our path, and the monsters fell behind us.

Mara led us to the base of Hillock’s wall where she shouted, “Change back or there won’t be room!”

Arclight lowered Nabeeh to the ground, then lay flat and dissolved into smoke, depositing Lan safely on the ground. I sank to my knees so Eryn could grab her bow. Snarky loosened her coils and disappeared into smoke, leaving her staff form in Eryn’s hands. Eryn placed her in Lan’s arms.

Coldhide dropped onto all fours, letting Knut fall off its shoulder and to the ground.

“Careful!” Eryn snapped at it.

“He brags of being a tank and the strongest wall,” the bear said as it disappeared into smoke and flowed back around Knut, reforming as Glacier.

“If he dies from being placed on rocky ground, he deserves the forever rest.”

“See to Knut first!” I called, and Dole dropped to his knees next to Knut, his hands glowing golden.

“Earth Wall!” Phil called as everyone gathered close, and the ground lifted beneath us.

The slab was barely large enough for everyone, and Hillock’s wall still rose high above us. Arrows and spells passed by overhead. A Loper Blinked onto our rising platform, but Mara kicked it in the chest, sending it flying off the wall.

Hands reached down from the battlements to pull us up as soon as we were within reach.

They took Lan first, then Knut, and Nabeeh. I raised Eryn as high as I could, and hands caught her, pulling her from my arms. With Mara standing at the edge of the platform, quills bouncing from her armor, I climbed onto the wall, helped by the defenders, and rolled to the ground, settling next to Eryn.

For the first time since falling from the sky, there was a wall between us and the monsters.

I took my wife’s hand and let my eyes close, leaving Roq to work Blood Forge, and Mara to take command.
