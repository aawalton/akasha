Luke and Hannah shared a glance, but both kept their expressions neutral."How long ago was this?" he asked. "When you saw that explosion, I mean?"

"Time loses its meaning after a while, but I would say upwards of two hundred years."

Nick spoke before Luke could ask follow-up questions, "You've been here for two hundred years? What have you been doing all that time?"

"Research. And I have honed my craft," she said, gesturing to the army of undead, "and pitted them against the demons and their underlings who have taken over this world."

"Undead combating unholy," Clementine whispered to herself, as if she couldn't wrap her head around the concept.

"Like Simean," Ray said, appearing right next to Luke.

Luke put a hand up to cover his mouth as he turned to whisper, "Good thing you didn't jump out and kill her."

"Geez, Luke," Ray said, mock-hurt in his voice. "I know how to read a room."

"Considering your combined strength, I did not see a need to intervene against that particular demon. My scouts tell me you felled it. Good work. It has escaped the clutches of my underlings for a long time."

"So you got stuck here and then fought against demons. What happened to all the people? Why did the demons show up in the first place?" Hannah asked.

Leminal sighed. "My neck will soon pain me if I have to swivel my head back and forth to address you in turn. Once a world is doomed, others encroach upon it. It is not just the void that is looking to lay claim to systems. Surely, you know that much?"

"Don't call me Shirley," Ray muttered.

"What doomed the world?" Luke asked, dreading the answer.

"I did."

He didn't know if that should make him breathe a sigh of relief or feel more afraid of the Singer than he was. Luke settled on relief. At least it hadn't been his fault.

"The cataclysmic weapon intrigued me. So, having been left behind along with a few of my fellow Singers, we set out to recreate it with the help of the Lilineese."

"Well, shit," Luke said.

She raised an eyebrow. "Sorry?"

"Nevermind. So you built something like it, and that destroyed everything?"

Nick turned to Luke. "Are these really the most important questions right now?"

"Yes, they are."

"The energy from that event was not a simple matter to replicate, especially here in this primitive world, but I have made progress on that front, yes. While we were unable to make it as explosive as what we saw on that day, we have harnessed the same sort of energy."

Luke swallowed hard. "You have?"

She nodded. "We named it death energy. Many questions remain unanswered, however, and my fellow Singers are long dead after exposure to the energy. Tell me, do you originate from the same system as the Integrated who produced that blast?"

It was Curtis's turn to lean over and whisper to Luke. "This is the last time you enter the portal first."

Not sure if he wanted to answer that question, Luke posed another one of his own. "Is that what killed the Lilineese?"

Leminal gave him a long look, as if weighing her options, followed by a curt nod. "Unfortunately, a test subject escaped and spread the energy to the guards who caught him, and from those two, it found new victims. It didn't take long after that."

"It's..." Hannah began, the words failing her.

Ariel jumped in, dry-washing her hands. "Contagious?"

"Do not worry yourselves. The death energy has long since ceased its activity in this world. Other than one much-needed display of power to keep the demons at bay, I have seen no need for its use. Once I return to my people, we shall refine the process so it is safe for us Singers. Have the system bridges appeared yet?"

"System bridges?" Nick asked.

A look of confusion swept across Leminal's face, but she did not elaborate. "No matter. It is only a matter of time. Once I spotted the portal opening, I knew that my time in this wasteland of a world had come to an end. From your world, I will be able to travel to my home at some point to unite with the Singers. Until then, I will explore and build relations with your elders, warlords, and guild masters. Diplomacy will be of the utmost importance in the years to come. As luck would have it, you speak the same language as the Lilineese. Perhaps you were bred from the same stock at some point."

"You want to come back with us," Luke said. Again, it was not a question. This female singer wanted to leave this place and come to Earth. It wasn't unexpected, of course. Being stuck in a place like this would not be a fun time, and since the years seemed to flow at a different pace, it would be like a prison lost to time. A nightmare.

Ray: "You still think I shouldn't have jumped out and killed her?"

Hannah: "If she's created some sort of radioactive virus-magic... Luke, we can't let that get to Earth."

Curtis: "I think she should join our guild."

Luke: "Godammit, Curtis. Stop making Ray sound like the sensible one."

Luke: "I'm not sure what to do here, but I agree with Hannah. That radioactivity-magic will wipe Earth in the blink of an eye. Painful death for everyone and everything. We can't even be sure of her intentions."

Hannah: "So what do we do?"

Ray: "We kill her."

Luke: "If it comes to that,.. I think Ray is right."

Nick broke the silence. "Of course you'll return to Earth with us. As you say, we have to stick together against the void."

Was Luke imagining things, or did she just relax her shoulders a little? That could mean a lot of things, but he couldn't help but feel suspicious.

"That is well," Leminal said, her gaze shifting from Nick to Luke. "Earth, is it? Being escorted by earthlings will alleviate local fears, I'm sure."

She stood then, showing her full height, more than a head taller even than Nick. "Let us be off. I expect there will be no issue with me bringing my entourage?"

"The skeletons?" Nick asked, giving the massed army a sceptical look over his shoulder before shaking his head. "Perhaps you'll scale it back a little? Also, we cannot return just yet."

"Who is The Conqueror?" Ariel asked.

Clementine found her voice again in that moment. "This leader of undead shall not trespass upon the land of the Lord!"

"This is not the time for that sort of talk, Clem," Nick said.

"But."

"No buts. Not now!" Nick said, raising his voice.

Clementine gave him a death glare. She didn't reply, but it was clear this was not something she would just drop.

Leminal studied their expressions, and when the matter sounded settled, at least for the moment, she smiled for the first time, showing too-white, wide teeth. "A quest. Of course, you received a quest upon entering this forsaken place. If you received a mission to engage that... thing, you must be high-leveled indeed." She gave each of them a long look, shaking her head a little or nodding. When she settled on Luke, she spoke again. "Interesting. A healer with such a high level, even compared to the rest of your group. Interesting indeed. Your class is not unknown to me. We must find time to speak, just the two of us. I have questions."

"So do I," Luke said. "Who is The Conqueror and where can we find... it? Him?"

"Is it another demon?" Nick asked.

"He is, and he is not," Leminal said after a moment of consideration, during which she took a few deliberate steps forward, her wide hips swaying, to stand right in front of the group with her arms at her sides. When she wasn't covering herself. It was clear that the robe she wore was more like a thin shift, because it didn't cover much, and what it did cover was a little too translucent for comfort. Once she was satisfied that she had their attention, she continued. "The Conqueror is of the same world as the demons, but he takes many forms."

Clementine drew in a sharp breath, but, for once, didn't interrupt with a long and loud tirade.

"He is not the ruler of their realm, but as far as I have been able to gather, he is one of their lords. Almost a king in his own right."

"And he's here?" Nick asked.

She stiffened, and her eyes flashed to the door the group had entered through, before she calmed herself. "He resides in this structure, yes."

"And he just allows you free rein of the place?" Luke asked.

Her eyes flashed with anger. "No one allows me a thing, Lifeweaver. I come and go as I please."

"You're allies, then?"

"No," she said, as if the mere question filled her with disgust. "Never. We have a truce, of sorts."

"Care to elaborate?" Nick asked, showing his diplomacy skills. Luke wanted to shout at her to explain herself, but a command like that wouldn't land well, he figured.

"The demons move into worlds in their never-ending quest to gather souls as a way of strengthening themselves. Without a constant influx, their strength withers. When they arrived, I had been here for a long while. As part of my research, I captured the souls of those lost to the death energy as a matter of course. It is part of my class. Without it, The Conqueror and his cohort found themselves locked in here, unable to leave, much like myself."

Ray scratched at his faint stubble. "Bet they liked that."

"They did not. It is a miracle I survived the battles that followed, but the army I amassed during my many years of solitude withstood their assault long enough for the fighting to come to a standstill."

Curtis glanced at a skeleton to their right. "The skellies?"

"Skeletons, yes. After altering a vial of my death mana to affect only their ilk, The Conqueror himself retreated, not willing to risk injury. This gave me some room to breathe. For many years now, we've held to an uneasy peace while the demons gather their strength, feeding on a trickle of cultists to regain what they have lost."

"With a weapon like that, why haven't you finished them off?" Hannah asked.

Leminal turned her back on them, walking toward the throne. She made an offhand gesture as she replied, "I am a careful Singer by nature. Going into his seat of power would be a poor decision. Even if I managed to end his existence, that would have disrupted The Greater System's motivation."

"What do you mean?"

Luke understood. "Without the demons, there would have been no reason for another portal to open up. You wouldn't have been able to leave."

The look she gave in reply told him everything he needed to hear.
