The Coalition’s stronghold was impressive, expansive, and efficient. While Edge spotted a handful of uncored individuals, most of the people he saw were warriors—a mix of late stage-twos and early stage-threes, judging by the mana emanating from their cores.

They waved to Shield as he passed, but no one followed as he led the duo deeper into the base. Apparently, the Coalition felt the man was capable with dealing with Sakura and Edge if they decided to cause trouble, and after observing the scion for hours, he was forced to agree.

Every move Brennen made displayed an impeccable awareness of his surroundings, the confidence of someone who knew he could handle any obstacle placed in his path, and the reflexes of a fighter who had battled with his life on the line countless times before.

While he found Shield fascinating, most of Edge’s attention was devoted to the fortress and its staff. This might be his only chance to obtain a deeper insight into the faction’s operations in Lakelands, and every scrap of information he gleaned would prove valuable during his deliberations with King.

It was clear by now that the Coalition was wealthy beyond measure. Everyone was outfitted with high-end weapons and armor. Even the uncored staff wore equipment that was superior to anything he owned, except for Opulence and his runic gear. Combined with all the top-grade magitech defenses, this place was a true military installation.

Even without Corpse and Shield, they could wipe Puppet Town off the map with a fraction of the forces contained within these walls, and I’m sure they have other strongholds scattered across the globe. There might be more elite heavy hitters living in World’s End, but the Coalition’s combined might is far greater than any settlement populated by former tourists, or jailbirds for that matter.

Everything he saw reinforced the fact that he couldn’t afford to make enemies of these people. They must be interested in his potential rather than his present ability, which made him hopeful he could string them along, buy some more time, and learn something valuable along the way.

“This is as far as I go,” Shield said as they approached a spiraling staircase. “Take these to the fourth floor and let King’s assistant know you’ve arrived. He will guide you to the conference room and provide any refreshments you require. I’ll meet you here on your way out.”

Edge hadn’t been expected to be left alone, but no one hurried them or challenged their presence as they climbed the stairs, taking advantage of the opportunity to examine as much of the facility and its workings as possible without making a bad impression.

The duo stopped at each floor along the way, passing by teams of cored crafters, researchers, and warriors who seemed like they were part of an army rather than the hunters and gladiators he was used to. He was dead certain he was being observed. That this was some manner of evaluation before the negotiations began. When he looked at Sakura, she nodded, having arrived at the same conclusion.

He memorized every detail and every word until they reached the fourth floor and made their way through a spacious lobby with a reception desk at the far end. It was obvious that the guards were expecting them. They waved the duo in without challenging their presence.

“Do you think this is meant to be a gesture of trust, or a sign that we’re no threat to them whatsoever?”

“Probably a bit of both,” Sakura replied, “along with an opportunity to impress us with their power and wealth. I’m sure they’re analyzing our behavior and building a profile of our psychology. I assume they’re listening right now, trying to get a grasp of where we’re coming from, and which tactics will be most effective to achieve a favorable resolution.”

While they crossed the lobby, he tried to identify an alternate escape route if they needed to beat a hasty retreat, although he didn’t care for their odds. They came to a stop in front a well-dressed man standing behind the desk, who had been patiently awaiting their arrival.

“Edge Vasher and Sakura Lunaris, I presume?” He nodded by way of reply. It wasn’t encouraging that they knew who Sakura was, including her full name, but he adhered to the role he had chosen to play, taking the lead while she followed two steps behind.

“Right this way. I hope your journey was pleasant. King has been looking forward to meeting you.”

They were led into another wing of the facility, passing through an administrative complex that had room for hundreds of employees but was relatively empty right now. There were a few dozen people going about their business—all cored from the looks of it—but it was obvious this base had held a considerably larger staff at one time.

When the man led them into an empty office on the highest floor of the building, Edge put the pieces of the together. “You’re not an administrative assistant. I presume you are the man known as King.”

“Indeed, well played. You figured it out before I had time to close the door and give you a pointed stare.”

The man stepped behind the most elegant desk Edge had ever seen, motioning for the scions to take a seat in a pair of comfortable armchairs. They looked at each other and did as King had bidden. Whatever is going on, we don’t seem to be walking into a trap.

While he waited for the man to explain what was going on, he took a closer look, trying to get a better sense of the person they’d come here to meet. King had fair skin, round features, and sea green eyes. He wasn’t fat or thin—fit but lacking the musculature of a hunter or warrior. Edge couldn’t sense any mana coming from him at all. Given his position, that made the man seem more dangerous, not less. His gaze was alert and he took their measure in turn before speaking.

“I hope you will forgive my little deception. I like to get a sense of peoples’ character before they know I’m in charge and put their masks on. My name is Olandro. I am the head of the Coalition’s Problem-Resolution Squadron, and the executive officer of our interests in the northwestern frontier. Or I was before Ord’s surface was scrambled, at any rate.

“Now my reach is limited to the free city and surrounding biomes, but change is in the air. We have developed workarounds to the Red Shield lockdown. I hope to have our communication and transportation networks reestablished before the year is through, once we finish building infrastructure that doesn’t rely on teleportation, Guide messages, and magitech vehicles.

“On the subject of apologies, I am genuinely sorry about the way my invitation was delivered to your door. Attacking your settlement was not part of my orders, and violent coercion is an explicit violation of our long-standing policy. I know that won’t mean much after lives were lost but allow me to explain how such an unfortunate development came to pass.

“Several members my team bear Unique cores. They, along with most of the scions I’ve met over the years, have developed… peculiarities from their exposure to the fallen Lords of Ord. Corpse was a sociopath before binding her core, and in recent years, she has come to view death as an alternative to life rather than its end. A preferable state from her perspective. I do my best to reign in her destructive impulses, but my control is limited at the best of times and next to nonexistent when she is in the field for long stretches at a time.

“I have punished her for her actions to the extent that I am able, and I swear to keep her far away from your home going forward. Rest assured, no harm will come to Puppet Town regardless of whether you accept my offer. I want you to join the Coalition because it is in our mutual interest and believe you will do so of your own free will. As a gesture of goodwill, I have left a shipment for you in the free city that should be of great benefit to your settlement. It is waiting in the Association vault and keyed to your Guide.”

This speech was exactly what Edge was hoping to hear.

He didn’t believe a word.

Good people had died under King’s watch. Whether he covertly condoned Corpse’s actions or merely failed to stop them, he was responsible too. All the apologies in the world wouldn’t bring Momo back, and everything King said was so smooth and genuine that it paradoxically raised Edge’s guard instead of lowering it. The scions must be even more important than I thought to go through all this trouble.

He did his best not to let his skepticism cloud his judgement—to give the Coalition the benefit of the doubt for the duration of this conversation. He would listen to everything the man had to say with an open mind and weigh his options carefully before coming to a decision. That way, if King could infer Edge’s intentions, he would believe everything was proceeding according to plan.

Regardless of the details, he was certain of one thing. If King thought there was no chance that Edge would join his faction, neither he nor Sakura would leave this place alive. Fortunately, he hadn’t come to a decision despite his fervent desire to find a way out of this dilemma, which meant he didn’t have to lie or pretend to be considering an offer he had already chosen to decline.

One way or another, he hoped to remain on good terms with the Coalition going forward. His instincts were screaming that these people were dangerous. Sakura believed Corpse was well into stage four and Edge sensed Shield was near the peak of stage three. Given their Unique abilities and the resources at their disposal, they were out of his league for the time being.

That kaiju was merely a test to her, or maybe an experiment or game. Who knows what she’s capable of when she’s going all out?

And this was only the group working out of World’s End, a mere fraction of the faction’s might. If Edge acted out of anger and burned any bridges, he could be signing a death warrant for all his friends.

Besides, while I owe them some payback, the Wardens are even more dangerous. With the Ancients lurking in the shadows and an invasion from the monsters’ dimension drawing closer with every passing day, I need to pick my battles carefully going forward.

“What exactly are you proposing?” Edge said at last.

King smiled. “Allow me to provide an overview of who we are and what we’re attempting to achieve. Then I’ll answer any questions you have and make a concrete offer.” With that, the man pressed a button on a magitech device that projected an image onto the far wall.

“As I’m sure you’re aware, Prison World is one of the most profitable ventures in human history. An entertainment complex comprised of tens of thousands of feeds that have voracious viewers on every inhabited world. On top of that, there are countless recaps, games, fictional works, and fan clubs using the feeds as source material, letting the System reap untold riches.

“On that subject, the planetary AI is unimaginably powerful, but it is bonded to the world core and limited in many ways. To extend its reach beyond the atmosphere, handle day-to-day affairs, and deal with problems that its magic isn’t well-suited to solve, the System employes two allied factions—the Prison World Administration and the martial organization known as the Wardens.

“Since Prison World needs an influx of fresh convicts and puppet tourists from myriad worlds to sustain itself, much of their power is concentrated on other planets. They are immensely wealthy and challenging them would be impossible for any one faction, which is why dozens of interested parties have come together to form a meta faction known colloquially as the Collation.

“We have been planning to supersede the Wardens and Prison World Administration for decades, consolidating our power as we sow the seeds of insurrection. However, the anomaly changed everything in the blink of an eye. With the Red Shield severing all contact with the greater galaxy, roughly seventy percent of our opponents’ pieces are unable to reach the board. Additionally, while the lockdown is in effect, it is difficult for the Wardens to amass their strength and respond to threats in a timely manner, leaving their captains vulnerable like never before.

“In short, this is an unprecedented opportunity to eliminate their elites, capture their infrastructure, and consolidate our control of the planet. Even with these advantages, they are a superior force, but with each scion we recruit, that gap shrinks and our odds of success grow. If we can defeat General Strieger and the Captains of the Twelve Divisions before the lockdown ends, the System will accept us as a replacement. If we don’t impede the profitability of the feeds, it won’t intercede on their behalf.

“While your opinion of the Coalition is understandably low, consider the alternative. The Wardens are brutal, amoral, and led by captains who make Corpse seem cuddly by comparison. They intended to wipe out every potential threat over the coming days, then start fresh when the broadcasts resume. That means every scion, every faction, and every hunter above stage two will be dead before the year is through unless we prevail.”

That was a ton of information, much of which was new to Edge. Even if King was keeping secrets and distorting key facts, most of it rang true. However, the man had made no mention of the lords’ tragic past, the origin of monsters, the Ancients, or the natives, which made Edge wonder how many variables were missing from this equation.

“I’m listening,” he said. “What are you offering, what do I need to do in return, and how much autonomy would I have going forward if I accept your terms?”

“Excellent questions. I will answer each in turn. We will help you reach your full potential, growing as fast as possible with access to the best gear, skills, and information this planet can provide. Our elites will take you deep into the frontier to cycle up at unprecedented rate, provide expert instruction in magic and martial arts, and secure the resources required to make the most of your unique magic.

“Additionally, we will provide wealth and protection to your friends and family off world, and to everyone living in Puppet Town. I don’t need to tell you how dangerous Ord is, particularly the uncharted frontier. That being said, this world is home to horrors the likes of which you cannot imagine—from class-SS criminals to stage-seven kaiju to monsters that could wipe your settlement off the map without breaking a sweat.

“You have done well to come this far on your own, but one day, a threat will arrive that you cannot defeat alone, and on that day, we will be there to fight at your side. In the meantime, we can provide Puppet Town with all the materials, manpower, and data it needs to thrive. Even more critically, by replacing the Wardens, we will forestall the purge they plan to implement and find a way to share the profits from the feed in an equitable manner. If we manage to win this war and retain control of the planet when the Red Shield comes down, your rewards will immeasurable.

“As for what we require. For now, I only want you to grow strong enough to participate in the battles brewing on the horizon. Cycle up, rank up your skills, and evolve faster than ever before. If you can help us recruit new members, or better yet, locate additional scions, so much the better. I am happy to provide a bonus for every success in those areas. In the future, I will require your participation on critical missions and eventually help take out the Wardens’ elites. Outside of that, I will not dictate your behavior in any way.

“Joining us will be dangerous, but not for your people in Puppet Town. The Wardens have no knowledge of your affiliation, and we will use codenames and countermeasures to conceal your identity. In short, joining the Coalition is the best option on the table—for yourselves, for your people, and for Ord as a whole. The Wardens day in the sun is over. It’s time for someone new to control the fortunes generated by the feed and the incredible resources created by Ord’s magic, which we will use not only to enrich ourselves, but invest in projects that will benefit all of humanity.”

Edge had to admit this pitch was insanely persuasive. King’s argument was airtight. There weren’t any better options, and it would be foolish to let this opportunity slip through his fingers. If he hadn’t decided to confer with Trapper’s crew before making a decision, he would have agreed to an alliance then and there.

“We’ve given you good reason not to trust us, and I won’t blame you if you decide to go your own way. All I ask is that you don’t decline right away. Return to the free city and ask anyone how we treat our neighbors. While we can be brutal to our enemies, we are stalwart allies and have no desire to harm innocents. Once have determined that our intentions are honorable, I ask you consider how much we can accomplish by working together as we enter this new era. How much safer your people will be with our forces at your side.

“Besides, what are your alternatives? You can go it alone, scavenging for scraps while praying Ord’s true horrors pass you by. But that will end in inevitable ruin. Every scion has a target on their back, and you cannot hide forever. However you feel about the Coalition, the Wardens are worse. Half their captains are psychopaths, and the rest are almost as bad. The Prison World Administration doesn’t care about anything beyond what the System wants, dealing with problematic prisoners, and maximizing the profitability of the feeds.

“Once the captains find you, and they will find you in the end, there will be no recourse. They will take you by force and break you until you beg to serve. You will have no freedom, no compensation—only the brutal indifference of faction that has reigned unchecked for centuries. We can protect you from the Wardens, but not until I know our courses are aligned. Until then, I recommend you do everything in your power to stay off their radar.

“They have multiple means of sussing out scions. If you ever run into a core sniffer stage three or above, run for your life and don’t look back. When you return to the free city, pick up a skill that masks the status of your core. Your companion’s mana control is exquisite, but I knew you were a scion the moment you set foot into World’s End.

“In fact, while I know a great deal about you, Edge—courtesy of the records you submitted before purchasing your puppet and simucasting to Ord—I know nothing about this young woman other than her name and prisoner number. Her records are priority sealed, which means the administration was paid to remain silent about one embarrassing matter or another. I assume she is a scion too?”

Sakura didn’t say a word or return his gaze, protecting herself from truth-detection and social-deduction skills. Edge realized King was fishing for information. Despite his claims, he might not know which of them was a scion after all, and since he hadn’t mentioned it, King probably didn’t know about Skill-Eater either.

When neither of them replied, the man smiled as if the question had been rhetorical. Edge decided that he had learned enough for now. It was time to make their escape before they gave anything else away.

“You’ve given me a great deal to contemplate, and your offer is tempting. I’m inclined to accept your proposal. All I ask is enough time to consider your terms, consult with my people, and look into a few matters. Give me a month to prove that you’re bargaining in good faith.”

“Of course. That’s only fair under the circumstances. However, we don’t plan to remain in this area much longer. I’m afraid two weeks is the most I can spare.”

“Agreed,” Edge replied. “I will return with my answer by then.”

They rose to their feet, and everyone shook hands. “Make your way down to the lobby and Shield will meet you there. You are welcome to tour the facility along the way, as long as you stay out of restricted areas.”

When the hunters were out of earshot, Shield opened the door he’d been standing behind in case his services were required.

He turned to the head of the Coalition’s Problem-Resolution Squadron and asked, “Are you really going to let them go if they don’t agree to join us?”

King looked offended at the suggestion.

“Of course not. I can spare two weeks to earn some goodwill and give my threats time to sink in, but if they don’t join the Coalition, we will kill them both and seal the scion’s core until we find a compatible bearer. Edge was telling the truth the entire time, so perhaps we can salvage this stinking pile of monster shit Corpse flung onto my desk. If Death-Walking wasn’t so picky about his bearers, I would have eliminated her long ago and found someone who isn’t a bloodthirsty sociopath to be his scion.”

“Can you kill her? She is the most dangerous person I’ve ever met.”

“Not in a direct exchange of strength, but you should know by now that there are countless ways to bring about someone’s end. I’ll do whatever it takes to win the war, no matter how many bodies I must burry along the way.”
