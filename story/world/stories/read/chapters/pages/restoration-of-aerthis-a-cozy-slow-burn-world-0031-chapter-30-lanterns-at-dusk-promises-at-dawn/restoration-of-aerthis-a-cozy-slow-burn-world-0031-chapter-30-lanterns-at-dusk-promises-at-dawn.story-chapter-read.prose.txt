The old stone archway came into view just as the sun slipped low behind the church’s spire, casting golden light across the gravel path.

Elynara stepped through first, staff tapping lightly against the worn stones as the familiar air of home settled around her shoulders.

Mina followed half a pace behind, her satchel bouncing against her side. Pufflet trotted ahead of them, nose twitching, ears perked for trouble – or snacks.

Probably both.

They barely made it past the garden steps before Father Alaric rounded the corner in mid-stride, robes gathered in one hand as if hurrying toward something.

He stopped as soon as he saw them.

“Ah,” he breathed, voice already bright. “There you are.”

The sun framed him in soft gold, catching the edges of his sleeves and the silver in his hair. His face, however, was nothing but warmth. He stepped forward with arms open, expression light as breath.

“Welcome home.”

The words were simple, but they wrapped around Elynara like roots sinking into soft soil. Her step eased before she even knew it, tension fading without permission. Mina, on the other hand, instinctively slowed, her shoulder angling slightly away.

It didn’t help.

Father Alaric reached her first and folded her gently into a side hug with the ease of someone used to getting away with such things. It wasn’t tight, simply a gesture meant to bring comfort and safety.

Mina squeaked in protest, cheeks already pink.

Elynara laughed softly and stepped up beside them.

“You’ll be pleased to know she has finally done it, and that training was a success.”

Alaric raised an eyebrow, but the smile on his face only grew.

“She has awakened her Mana Sense,” Elynara added proudly, folding her arms and puffing her chest just enough to be ridiculous. “And not by accident, either.”

Mina made a strangled noise and tried to squirm free, but Alaric only chuckled, not letting her slip away just yet.

“A fine achievement,” he said, tone gentler now. “A now rare and noble path.”

He rested one hand atop Mina’s hair, ruffling it with careful fingers.

“The world needs more with eyes open to the unseen. I’m proud of you, little one.”

Mina looked like she wanted to melt into the floorboards. Her shoulders hunched defensively, but she didn’t pull away. The soft curl of a smile found its way to her lips despite her best efforts.

Elynara grinned from the side, watching every flicker of hesitation soften.

Pufflet bounded up a moment later and yipped approval with perfect timing. Mina bolted for the hallway with a muttered, “I’m getting water.”

She made it three steps before Alaric snagged her by the shoulder and pulled her in again for one last ruffle.

Elynara snorted, hand over her mouth.

“Little lightning bolt,” Alaric murmured, amused.

He let her go that time, and she vanished toward the dorm hallway in a flurry of flustered footsteps.

The amusement dimmed a little as he turned back.

“We’re running light here. Three of the sisters left yesterday to help care for the injured. There’s still food and shelter, but things may be slower for a time.”

Elynara’s smile faded. Her fingers tightened on the strap of her satchel.

“I really want to help, but the guild is stretched thin too. Alicia’s got me handling all the odd jobs and…” She stopped herself, jaw shifting. “I’ll stop by when I can. Just... maybe not every day.”

Before she could say more, Mina’s voice piped up behind them.

“I can help.”

Both turned.

Mina stood just inside the hallway now, her fists curled tight at her sides. She looked like she wanted to take the words back already, but her chin stayed up.

“I know where everything is,” she continued. “And Pufflet can help carry things. And I’m not… small.”

The last part was barely above a whisper.

Elynara blinked. She hadn’t expected that. And Pufflet didn’t look like it could carry anything, but its tail wagged at the mention anyhow.

Alaric, however, smiled like it was the most natural thing in the world.

“Then we’re doubly lucky today.”

He crouched slightly and laid a hand on her head again, gentler this time. Mina didn’t flinch, just straightened a little, growing into the praise.

Pufflet barked again, clearly excited. He spun a little circle on the tile, tail wagging.

Elynara didn’t say anything at first.

She looked between the two of them, from Alaric’s calm pride to Mina’s nervous determination, and couldn’t help it.

Her smile bloomed all on its own.

Alaric caught her eye and gave a small understanding nod.

But before she could reply, one of the younger children spotted them first.

There was a sharp gasp from near the stairs, followed by the slap of sandals on stone as three small shapes sprinted toward the courtyard, already yelling.

Pufflet let out a high-pitched bark and spun in place, tail a blur, unsure whether to run or defend.

The noise drew more. Another child peeked from a window, then two more came tumbling down the hall. Laughter spilled through the corridor like water breaking a dam.

Mina was swept into the chaos before she had time to react.

One of the younger girls latched onto her arm. Another started asking questions too fast to answer. Pufflet tried catching all of them at once, barking in gleeful confusion as he slid across the polished floor.

Elynara raised her hands trying to fend the wild herd off.

“Alright, alright! I brought souvenirs! Sort of!”

That got their attention, for a brief instant at least. Curious eyes turned toward her as she backed up a few steps into the courtyard.

She took a breath.

The spells she wove didn’t need much. Water and fire, balanced in the air. She coaxed the mana together slowly, shaping the mist first. The cool droplets spinning outward in a fine cloud that kissed the edges of the courtyard stones.

A few glimmers even caught the last of the sunlight, turning gold at the fringe.

Then came the flick.

Two tiny lights sparked within the haze, each one catching fire at its core and blooming into a slow-floating butterfly of red-orange glow. They drifted gently on the mist, wings pulsing as they turned lazily through the air.

The children gasped as one. One of the youngest reached out a hand and let out a squeal when a droplet popped against his nose.

That was all it took.

Laughter erupted like a chorus of bells, and suddenly hands were reaching skyward, bodies twirling through the haze.

Even Father Alaric leaned quietly against the doorway with his arms folded and a smile tucked into the lines of his face.

Mina stood off to the side, half-hidden by a column, her arms crossed tight but her eyes bright with open wonder. The edge of her mouth twitched, trying to hide her smile, but Elynara caught it anyway.

Pufflet launched himself into the center of the mist, barking at the floating lights, tail wagging furiously as he skidded into a puddle and flopped belly-first onto the stones. He yipped in surprise, scrambled up, and did it again.

Elynara let the spell hang a little longer, then let the mana slip gently back. The mist thinned. The lights blinked out one by one.

She clapped her hands once.

“Okay! Show’s over. Food time. Then I’ve got work to do.”

A chorus of groans met her announcement.

“Nooo!”

“Again!”

“Just one more–!”

“Food,” she said with mock sternness, already turning toward the doors. “You don’t want Sister Lyra coming after me, do you?”

That worked.

A few of the kids shuddered theatrically, then bolted down the hall. The rest followed in staggered formation, Pufflet included. Who was now proudly parading his victory against the glowing butterflies.

Elynara ducked into her bedroom long enough to drop off her pack.

By the time she made it back downstairs, someone had already placed a bowl of thick stew in her hands without asking.

The warmth curled through her fingers and up her arms, earthy and rich with root vegetables and herbs she vaguely remembered helping harvest.

Mina was already passing out flatbread and fending off one of the older boys trying to sneak a second portion.

Elynara took her seat with a contented sigh. Even Starpuff hovered quietly, orbiting a ceiling beam without bouncing off anything or anyone. That alone was rare.

The food didn’t last long.

Once finished, she handed the bowl off, wiped her hands, and stood again with a soft stretch. A few of the kids waved lazily from the benches. Mina gave her a solemn nod like she was heading off to battle. Pufflet didn’t even look up, far too busy licking something off the bottom of a now-empty bowl.

She stepped out into the evening light, the last pinks of sunset fading along the edges of the sky.

The streets she took to the guild were quieter now, with only a few people out.

The guild hall greeted her with quiet as expected.

Marcus was reading something that looked upside down, and Alicia was nowhere in sight. Without a word, Marcus tapped the small stack of requests on the desk.

A slip of paper on top read “Light Requests” in Alicia’s handwriting, underlined twice.

Elynara thumbed through and picked two.

The first was a delivery, a small parcel to the weaver’s shop near the east fence. The second asked for bramble clearing near the south wall, where a few thorns had overgrown a field path.

She managed to get both done before full nightfall.

And after logging them quietly, she left a short note for Alicia, and stepped back into the cool dark. It was a bit more chilly now, with few lights shining through the passing windows.

Her legs ached, and her eyes burned more than expected.

But tomorrow would come soon enough.

And for now, she was going home to relax.

The next three days passed in a steady blur.

Elynara’s mornings began before the light fully settled, her hands already moving through the familiar cycle of drawing, pushing, and letting go. Mana coiled through her limbs in lazy circuits, not as sharp as it had been the week before, but steadier. She stopped each time after getting some results in, rubbed her face, and sighed.

There wasn’t time to savor her progress.

Her first stop was always the guild.

Alicia waited with pencil in hand and sleeves already rolled. The moment Elynara stepped inside, the next slip was pushed across the counter.

“Snack?” Alicia asked more than once, voice deceptively casual as she set down a roll or a cup of water or fruit juice beside the requests.

Sometimes Elynara took it. Other times she just gave a nod and walked right back out.

There were deliveries to run, fences to check, a leaking roof near the carpenter’s shed. She inspected a root cellar that had started collapsing inward, then carried three crates of smoked fish for an elderly merchant who kept calling her “Fire Queen” and thanked her with pickled onions.

It wasn’t glamorous. But it helped.

Even Marcus had vanished.

The second morning, Alicia muttered something about him “doing his part” while flipping through a logbook, then handed her another job without missing a beat.

From what little she overheard, Marcus had taken up extermination requests that needed being done. Axe in hand, coat slung over one shoulder, and a familiar grumble trailing behind him as he left town.

Her curiosity satisfied, Elynara picked up her next task.

By the second day, her rhythm was ironclad. Report in. Drop the last slip. Grab a fresh bun. Down half a cup of water. Take the next job.

Sweep the square. Deliver the letter. Help with moving merchandise. Smile. Repeat.

At least her magic helped.

Water spells cleared mud off carts before it dried into cement. A simple flare of fire took care of the thick brambles that had overtaken an old shed path. She shaped mana faster now, even tired. Her hands moved before her thoughts caught up.

The warmth in her chest at the smiles she earned kept her going longer than anything Alicia handed over.

Back at the church, Mina worked with quiet focus. She folded sheets, ladled stew, guided the younger kids to bed with the firmness of someone who knew they would just get up again in five minutes. Pufflet followed her loyally, carrying small baskets and occasionally barking at stools that weren’t where they should be.

Knowing Mina was giving her all made it easier.

Elynara pushed harder because of it.

Starpuff had no such concerns.

It flitted through windows, zipped past rooftops, and occasionally slammed into her forehead at the worst possible time. Once, she nearly dropped a bucket of kindling from the jolt.

The spirals it carved in the air were tighter now, and less chaotic. Elynara started noticing the shapes. Some looping helixes, nested arcs, glowing figure-eights left in its wake while pretending to be aimless.

Though she didn’t have time to investigate.

People still talked about the mines, now relief was more evident than fear. The worst had passed, with no more deaths or missing.

One child said their cousin sent a letter from the village. Another swore their uncle saw something glowing in the deep and came back with a sketch. Even Josh mentioned someone finding ore veins down there, hidden behind the cave-in.

Elynara only caught bits of it.

Always between jobs. Always between steps.

She would nod, listen, then keep moving.

And on that day the blinking dot and +1 to Fitness, bringing the total to 9, told her all that walking and working did wonders for her body.

Or so it seemed.

Elynara felt the difference, however small, but was just too busy and tired to properly celebrate.

Not while her arms still ached and her breath came shallow at the end of the day.

By the third day, her legs burned, and her voice cracked when she tried to banter with Alicia. Her sleeves clung to her arms, sweat tucked behind her ears, and even the satchel at her hip felt heavier than it should have.

When she pushed open the guild door that afternoon, hair damp and sticking to her brow, she expected another stack of errands.

Instead, she found two unfamiliar adventurers already at the counter.

Alicia stood behind it with her arms crossed and her lips pressed into a line that only almost resembled disapproval.

“Finally,” she muttered, though her shoulders sagged with visible relief.

The taller of the pair was saying something about the caves, though Elynara didn’t really catch what.

They didn’t sound worried though, so that was good!

Marcus came in an hour later, boots scuffed and axe still strapped to his back. His usual scowl didn’t last. He clapped the shorter adventurer on the back hard enough to make the man stumble two steps forward.

“You live?” Marcus asked.

“Barely,” came the wheezed reply.

“Ha! Good enough.”

From that moment on, things eased.

The requests didn’t vanish, but fewer tasks landed in her hands directly. Deliveries passed to newer feet. Fences were fixed before she arrived. Even Alicia stopped pressing snacks into her palms like edible bandages.

Elynara still had plenty to do, but the avalanche was gone.

So she let herself sit.

The chair creaked beneath her as she sank into it, elbows on the table, cup of juice already halfway gone before she remembered to taste it. Tart, a little sweet, and cold enough to bite gently at the back of her throat.

Her staff leaned beside her, balanced just so. Starpuff zipped overhead in a tight corkscrew loop, spiraling into a pop of green light before reappearing mid-hover on her shoulder.

She flicked its side with two fingers.

“Show-off.”

The sprite trilled, smug.

The noise in the hall felt right again. Footsteps. Papers being shuffled. Adventurers arguing. The world had resumed its usual wobble.

She let it wash over her.

Training hadn’t stopped, even when it probably should have. Her mornings were still spent on mana cycling, though now it felt like climbing a hill with a rock under her foot. Focus scattered too fast. Fatigue dragged everything down.

But the progress ticked forward. Slow, but sure.

She had hit 130 mana capacity by that morning, and even if it didn’t feel like much in the moment, it was.

Elynara smiled at the small progress.

The door creaked behind her. Familiar footsteps echoed once on the wooden floor.

Mina walked in with her sleeves rolled and dust on her cheek, face lit up like she had just won a personal war against the storage room. She waved once, then beelined for Alicia with a folded note in hand.

Elynara watched her go, sipping again, and happy her friend was getting more independent now.

Later, she returned to the chapel and found Pufflet curled in Mina’s lap, one ear twitching as he snored faintly. One of the smaller children was curled up next to him, their head resting on Mina’s knee.

Elynara pulled her boots off in silence and sat nearby, just listening to the lull of quiet breath and distant kitchen clatter.

She smiled to herself, gaze trailing across the beams above. Starpuff floated into view again and nudged her cheek, once.

This time, she didn’t flick it away, only raised a brow at it.

The sprite rotated lazily in place, forming a loose circle with its light. The motion was tighter than before, more focused. It wasn’t looping randomly for fun, but repeatedly drawing a shape?

A letter?

No… It was a symbol?

“Where would you learn that?” she murmured.

Starpuff wiggled in place. Then did it again.

Elynara chuckled, too tired to investigate further.

“Later.”

She leaned back in her chair, let her shoulders fall, and just sat there for a while.

People were still talking about the village, but normalcy was already mostly back. The worst had passed.

And Oltenar, just like its people, was learning how to breathe again.

So was Elynara.

She was tired, yes. But lighter.

Starpuff hovered quietly near her cheek, unusually still. It made one slow circle around her head, then nudged her ear once with a soft pulse.

Elynara smiled without looking.

Tomorrow would bring more.

But for tonight, she was content to sit in the quiet, listening to the far-off sounds of laughter and spoons clinking gently against bowls.

Inside, quiet voices filtered through the open doors, and she got up to check them out.

She barely entered before the ambush began.

“She’s back!” one of the boys yelled, and then there were hands tugging at her sleeves, voices overlapping as they flooded the hall.

“Did you bring magic again?”

“You promised more glowy things!”

“I bet I can catch one this time!”

“Miss Elynara, you’re late!”

“Can you teach Pufflet how to fly?”

Mina’s yelp of surprise came from behind her as she too was grabbed, laughter bubbling out of her as the whole group surged forward.

Elynara raised both hands in mock surrender.

“I am unarmed and exhausted!”

That changed nothing.

Within moments, it became a game. Half the children dragged them by the wrists and elbows through the hallway and into the courtyard again, the rest darting ahead, circling like giggling scouts as if preparing the terrain.

Pufflet woke up mid-lurch, let out a confused bark, and flung himself into the chase with blind enthusiasm. He zoomed past Elynara’s feet, skidded on the stone, then tried again.

Starpuff chose chaos, of course. The sprite zipped into the air with renewed energy, trailing sparks as it darted between heads and bonked the slowest runners with precision strikes.

Elynara stumbled after the crowd, laughing despite herself.

“Mercy! I’m too tired for this!”

“No mercy!” one boy shouted triumphantly. “You’re captured!”

Mina, red-faced but grinning, was pulled along beside her, trying and failing to keep her balance as three smaller kids pushed from behind.

“I don’t think this is a rescue mission anymore,” she gasped.

“They’ve turned against us!” Elynara managed between laughs.

The wild run spiraled into a full loop around the main garden. Shoes slapped on stone, Pufflet barked himself hoarse, and even Starpuff began slowing its orbits, drifting closer to Elynara’s shoulder with increasingly exaggerated swoops.

Finally, the group herded them back toward the chapel steps.

“You’re our prisoners now!” declared the self-appointed leader, arms crossed, chest puffed.

“I demand a fair trial,” Elynara said, slumping onto the top step with theatrical flair. “And snacks.”

“No trial!” someone else yelled. “We decide your fate!”

Mina flopped beside her, legs folded awkwardly, chest rising and falling with fast breaths. She gave Elynara a helpless look, lips twitching.

“Do you think they’ll go easy on us?” she asked.

“They already made Pufflet a judge,” Elynara whispered back. “We’re doomed.”

Indeed, the tiny animal had taken its place atop the lowest step and was sniffing the air with self-importance, tail swaying as it looked between the gathered crowd.

Then came the creak of the main door.

Father Alaric stepped out with a long-suffering smile already forming beneath his brow. He took in the scene of two exhausted girls on the steps, surrounded by children in full performance mode, and raised a questioning eyebrow.

Elynara groaned and covered her face with one hand.

“Evening,” he said mildly.

The children turned.

“She’s guilty of not showing us magic!” one shouted.

“And of being slow!” another added.

Mina giggled into her sleeve.

“Harsh crimes,” Alaric mused, folding his hands. “What sentence shall we impose?”

The children huddled for a moment, whispering furiously.

Then, in one loud voice:

“Butterflies later!”

Elynara peeked through her fingers.

“That’s it?”

“For now!” said the lead negotiator.

“I’ll take it,” Elynara said.

Mina leaned over, her shoulder bumping lightly into hers.

“We got off easy.”

“Don’t jinx it,” Elynara murmured.

The children scattered soon after, pleased with their victory. A few skipped toward the dining hall. Others raced back inside for evening chores. Pufflet padded after them, triumphant.

Alaric gave the two girls a quiet nod and returned inside.

Elynara leaned back against the stone wall, stretching her legs. Mina remained beside her, arms wrapped around her knees, hair loose now, cheeks still flushed from the chase.

“I don’t know where they get the energy,” the girl said.

“Secret child mana pool,” Elynara replied. “Untapped and dangerous.”

Mina chuckled.

They sat there for a moment longer, the air cooling gently around them, Starpuff circling once overhead before nestling onto Elynara’s shoulder again with a soft hum.

Things were looking up.

And she would sleep well tonight!
