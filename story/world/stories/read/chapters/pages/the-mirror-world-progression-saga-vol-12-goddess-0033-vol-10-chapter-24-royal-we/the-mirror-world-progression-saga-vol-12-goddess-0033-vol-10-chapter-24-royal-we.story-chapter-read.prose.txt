Crow studied Samantha as the silence hung in the common room of the one-time inn. It was obvious things were getting to her, and hardly any surprise. They all had something internal about themselves to struggle with. The ‘high concepts’ they represented couldn’t even be properly understood by their meager brains. Who could know that the building block ideals of reality could be so terrifying?

“I’ll deal with the head as soon as I can,” Crow said finally.

Samantha nodded and shrugged. “No rush. Did you find anything from those corpses you had dug up and such?”

“Many things. Bits and pieces of useful information hidden in a pool of too much mere, collective curiosity.”

“Like what?”

“Do you want the bits and pieces, the pool, or both?”

Samantha grinned lopsidedly. “Just the pertinent. A summary of the rest, if context is needed.”

“Perhaps. Suffice to say, I learned about the assassinating item — some of its history, as it was handed down from father to son. Dry details about other old assassinations. Sadly, I have no means to reach the son, who certainly was the source of what killed Agatha. One thing is clear: they served others primarily. At one time, on a mercenary basis, but eventually, they came to serve only the Witchhunters. Fanatically, effectively religiously, from what appeared to be leveraged uses of Geased oaths. Brainwashing and mind control.”

Samantha grimaced. “Figures. So, we have another enemy to worry about. Not that I expected anything less.”

“Not you. That sounded like a ‘royal we.’ Absolutely not.”

“Pardon?”

“Let this side handle it, Samantha. That’s no different than every vision you’ve seen of Calrenazzod’s future, is it? You deal with the west, and the Borderlanders deal with the east.”

Samantha raised her brows and held up her hands. “Fine, fine! No, that-that’s what I meant! I’m not worming into your territory, buddy!”

Crow rolled his eyes. “It’s not for my sense of territory, oh all-knowing supreme deity — it’s to let you focus. To not have you overwhelmed when it isn’t even necessary.”

Samantha took a breath, and whatever would have come out of her mouth became a sigh. “I… understand. Yes. No ‘royal we’... I just meant it in the sense of the greater team, of which there are many different groups doing many different things semi-independently.” She raised an eyebrow at him with a bit of leftover sass. “Is that satisfactory for you?”

“Very. Now then, back to the intel… Mostly, we have complete confirmation of it being someone prominent in the Witchhunters. This person even came to the location we found disintegrated and seemed to coordinate operations there. Sadly, I don’t know who, and they’re gone. But the townsfolk knew some things when we had one of your Sirens dig-”

“Did you have people mind-controlled without even a by-your-leave?!”

Crow gave her a deadpan glare. “She gently charmed men after getting them drunk. Do you really think it’s that difficult?”

Samantha frowned. “Ugh. Fine… go on.”

“I’m somewhat surprised the Witchhunters didn’t deal with them to some degree. Perhaps they looked down on common folk, figuring them too ignorant to see the signs. But if there’s anything they do well, it's to observe what happens in their own areas and gossip about it. In any case, they didn’t know much, just saw the blatant indications of a guest in that old estate, and ‘intrigue afoot.’ Conspiracies abounded about it. None dared to use the word ‘Witchhunters,’ mind, but they made enough indirect insinuations to know they had inklings of the connections.”

Samantha was nodding slowly, then froze. “Wait… didn’t you get a name, then? Can’t you use that?”

Crow felt his face scowl up terribly. He scoffed and waved his hand. “I got one, but it didn’t bloody work.” He left it at that, eyeing Samantha.

It took a moment of her eyes searching his face before they widened. “He had a hidden True Name!”

Crow grimaced even deeper as he nodded. “I even knew his family name, absolutely thanks to the dead dug up. But each of them, when I leveraged their remains to summon them, gave up unique names. That is who they are. And I simply… do not have his. I haven’t uncovered any means to get it, either.”

“Damn.” Samantha sat back in her chair and sighed. “Seems you found quite an enemy. They strike at our ankles and then vanish when we turn around. So, what’s next?”

“Tracking down the threads I can. They will certainly have agents in Mot Mekess. I… perhaps have enough information to dig them out, though it's quite hostile territory now, and probably soon to be under siege, besides. I can also attempt to go deeper in Dominion territory to track down the agent Lord Doran knew. Viranna. She would have no reason to think herself compromised. Doran swore she was ‘long gone,’ which I’m inclined to believe based on the general caution this group operates with. But good agents don’t simply disintegrate. They go somewhere else. We’ll see.”

Samantha nodded. “I should go. Get some sleep before you deal with this.” She tapped the sack with a lich’s head in it.

Crow nodded. “I’ll try.” Not that it will work, and I won’t waste time if it doesn’t.

Samantha frowned at him but didn’t press. She appeared to have a sudden thought and said hesitantly, “If… If you get the chance? Not required, but… if you do, ask the Consolidator about his custom spells. And his staff.”

Crow raised an eyebrow. “Anything noteworthy to point out?”

“A lot. I’m not sure what was staff-born and what wasn’t. Even if it was, I think he crafted that thing. And I dunno if some things were… corrupted, by his lichdom. But, okay, spells… Dirge of the Pitiless King, Time. Enforcing Proper Distance. That was a strong forcefield that expanded out, pushing people absolutely. The Night’s Decrepit March, a ridiculous protection spell that turned mana into transmutative, regenerating shadow armor. With his staff… he used a strong spirit-striker, a spell that fired like a bazillion deadly green beams, and something that made a massive number of sludge orbs.”

“Hmm. That’s a lot.”

“Yeah! I guess… focus on what he was most jazzed about in life, maybe. And I want to know about the staff. Ooh! It actually might be locked. Maybe he’d know how to get into it. Re-activate it and so on. It was made out of gold, with a book at the top.”

“As you wish. I’ll do what I can.”

“Right. Thanks. I’ll see you, [De-]... Crow!” Samantha didn’t get up. Embarrassed by her slip, she turned away and summoned a mirror, staring into it, slowly dematerializing into it until she was gone, and the mirror was darkened over. It remained hovering there for a few more moments, undarkening as Samantha’s Avatar shunted elsewhere, and then vanished.

Am I [Death]? Not really. A slow transformation. He isn’t trying. There’s plenty of time left.

Crow stared at the space she’d been for a while longer, then turned his gaze down to what waited on the table. He pulled the lich’s head out of the sack to have a look at it.

It was a dark-gray undead thing covered in a thin but tough layer of preserved and quite-dead flesh, giving off the odors of decay, embalming fluid, and alchemy. Empty pits, decayed lips, but his nose and ears were well-preserved. No hair, and an indentation that suggested a hat had been on top for aeons. He still had bejeweled earrings in his ears, mildly enchanted for preservation. One had a little gold chain connecting from the bottom to the top.

Crow couldn’t help but smile.

The Consolidator. A fine addition to the collection, I’d say.

Gelmak Por had been feeling pretty down. He’d missed the battles in Rivermount, and though he’d been told what he was doing was important — acting as the champion marine for a sailing, exploratory expedition — his feelings on the matter were in conflict. He’d fought, yes, but not at the magnitude and spiritual rightness of a war for independence alongside his brothers.

Nonetheless, he was obedient and didn’t complain. That was the way of a warrior-servant to the gods. To heed Little Sister was to be smiled upon by Father Mountain and the Zedwarorra, and thus, by all of minotaurkind. There was no other thing he would do — never again. So he had been told, and so he had accepted, as he must, according to his oaths.

The crew was great. Very appreciative and praising of his might, especially after wiping out two Naugite pirate vessels and damaging another, with his mighty axe, Chopper of Many Sizes. After that third damaged vessel, they stopped coming. ‘Word had gotten out,’ according to the captain. Certainly, they were an odd duck in Naugite waters, flying the rippling, glorious flag of the Celestial Horizon. A reputation was inevitable.

They had hoped to find another place Samantha and Azure could make a spiritual anchor to, so they had gone island to island in search of old temples, ruins, and caves. He was needed to slay the often unknown, dangerous threats that cropped up, and therein to keep the spellcasters and crew alive.

But it was a frustrating go. They’d found very little and kept sailing on to chase rumors and legends, often to find nothing but rocky coastlines, sandbars, and endless, endless ocean.

Finally, they had a breakthrough as a pirate ship carefully communicated ‘peace’ across the distance, got a satisfactory response, and they ultimately met on a sandy shore. The Naugite captain was very friendly and smiling with his rotted black teeth, which seemed odd for a pirate who wore a necklace of shriveled ears around his neck. But all he wanted was coins. Instead of murdering and stealing, he was offering services as a guide, informant, and trader. It was nice to see such civilized behavior from villains!

Indeed, all but the guide services were traded for coin, and the most precious thing of all was acquired: local maps, stamped with the ‘northern seal’ for authenticity. The haggling went on endlessly, and neither party was smiling by the end. Gelmak Por was worried it would turn into a fight at one point, but just then, there was a curt agreement.

The maps had cost such a great deal of coin that the captain muttered constantly about ‘thievin’ Naugites,’ and the ‘waste of coin.’ Silly. It wasn’t theft, and weren’t the maps priceless to them, essentially? But Gelmak Por said nothing. The captain was often seemingly grumpy and an open complainer. Apparently, this was a right of captains compared to the crew, as he’d seen it multiple times.

They had to pick a destination or route with the maps. During the conversation before, the captain had asked about ‘treasure’ a lot, and the pirate had directed him to two spots that they discussed at length, showing clear interest and intrigue. A third was a temple on an island far south that was claimed to be utterly looted, with aught left of it but curses, its beach little more than an overnight camp area with some coconut trees nearby. The Samanthian captain had seemed dismissive and asked about everything other than it after that.

‘Cursed Old Temple’ was the name on the map. And it was to this that they sailed hard and fast under enchantments — strange! The captain had acted completely uninterested! But he’d obviously changed his mind, or gotten more info from the Samanthian realm, because the temple discovery had lit a fire in his heart, despite all the complaining.

Gelmak Por hoped it was ‘the one.’ He did not want to miss Matrazere’s epic battle to come, too. Surely Fate was not so cruel as that?
