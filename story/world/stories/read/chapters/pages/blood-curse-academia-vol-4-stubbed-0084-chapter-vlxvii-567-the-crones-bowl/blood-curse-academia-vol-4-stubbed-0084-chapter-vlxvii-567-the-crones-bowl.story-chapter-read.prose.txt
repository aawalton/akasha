Chapter V.LXVII (5.67) - The Crone’s Bowl

The first thing Kizu did in preparation for his escape was locate Mort. His familiar remained asleep and blissfully unaware of their predicament. Apparently, exiting the dreamscape didn’t necessarily awaken them all simultaneously like Kizu had assumed.

As much as he was loath to wake up the monkey, he’d rather face the grumpy primate’s irritation than continue sitting in his box of a prison cage.

Some slight soul prodding jolted Mort to attention. As expected, the monkey immediately shot back with a wave of vexation through their bond at being rudely awoken. But the emotion was surprisingly short-lived. It was quickly replaced by confusion and fear.

When Kizu’s doppelgänger had mentioned Mort being in the next room over, he’d neglected to mention that the ‘room’ in question had no doors. Or lights. And was the size of a large tome.

He’d essentially sealed Mort away in a stone tomb.

Kizu did his best to calm down his familiar, explaining their predicament through their bond in flashes of memory. Mort’s immediate instinct was to use Kizu as an anchor to jump to his side, freeing himself. Somehow, Kizu managed to talk him out of it. If his doppelgänger could track Mort’s position through their familiar bond, then he no doubt would know about his escape immediately.

Both Kizu and Mort shared a supreme dislike for being trapped in the dark, unable to see their surroundings. It was the result of the comfort brought by their nocturnal vision. Not being capable of seeing something in the dark after so long was unnerving. Still, Kizu held Mort back for the time being.

Kumiho remained on the floor outside of his cage. Without legs to run, the mangled Kitsune woman wasn’t capable of anything more than squirming on the stone. Even that much required copious amounts of energy she didn’t currently have to spare.

Kizu barely considered himself a novice at medical spellcraft. Countless times in his lectures, Taroe had harped on about how medics should never heal species other than those they specifically studied and trained on. Kizu knew that first hand, his old bum leg a result of a Tainted girl not recalling the specifics of his human biology. And yet… he couldn’t let Kumiho continue on like she was.

He knelt, pressed against the bars, and extended a hand to brush against her matted fur. He channeled the most basic healing spell into her, simply smoothing out bruises and surface level cuts. He didn’t trust himself to do more than that. Still, that little bit of healing caused her to sigh in relief.

“My back… left ankle,” she gasped out.

It wasn’t an ankle, just a stub. But Kizu immediately saw what she was referencing. A bit of metal was woven into her haunch. He recognized it as an antimagic enchantment designed to keep her helpless. He doubted she even had the ability to assume her humanoid form with it on.

It raised the question, why hadn’t he been secured with antimagic? It seemed like a massive oversight. Unless his doppelgänger wanted him to attempt escape. Then again, without his atlas he might as well be trapped down here. He had no means of truly escaping the World Dungeon. Leaving Otochi’s lair would result in Kizu dying to either the countless threats or starving to death long before ever escaping to the surface.

“I’m not skilled enough to remove this,” he said after examining the metal. If it wasn’t buried in her flesh, he might be capable of stripping the enchantment. But her blood soaking it made things trickier. Anything inside a living body was a million times more difficult to work on.

Kumiho whimpered.

“I’m not trapped by antimagic,” Kizu said. “I could jump out of the cage and carry you out of here. Do you have a way to communicate with the World Dungeon? It’s your progenitor, right? Will it help us?”

She shook her head. “Not in my… current state.”

Kizu took a few minutes to silently consider both Kumiho and their predicament. She was in terrible shape. She didn’t even have eyes or legs. It made his old bum leg look luxurious in comparison. Not even Taroe would be able to patch her together.

“My potions have been stolen, along with my equipment. If I can regain access to that, then I’m confident I’ll come out on top in a fight against my doppelgänger.”

“And… without?”

Kizu considered, playing out a few scenarios in his head, then shook his head. “Too many uncertainties. I don’t know what, exactly, he can do. What spells can he cast? I’m limited in my blood supply, but so is he. But as it is, he does have my spatial storage ring. Given time, he’ll doubtlessly figure out my sorting system and start rummaging through my more dangerous potions. That will put me at a severe disadvantage."

“His spells… you… don’t know?”

Kizu blinked. The Kitsune was correct to question him. After all, now that he considered it, he did actually know what hexes were in his doppelgänger’s arsenal. They were sorted away with his sealed memories growing up with the crone. But that would require him to spend time scanning them. Time he was reluctant to sacrifice.

Next, he studied his cage. He realized that Kumiho might have been placed in front of him intentionally, to keep him from breaking free with elemental fire. Melting through the bars would fry the Kitsune woman. Instead, he tried a blade of force, but it left a mere nick on the reinforced bars.

Rather than jump out of the cage, Kizu pressed his hands against the bars and phased himself through the metal. It was a technique he’d been practicing over the last few months, combining it with his excessive anatomical studies for Rejuvenation and Restoration F. While it cost him a great deal more in concentration and blood, it lacked one significant drawback that came with jumping. Beacons.

Kizu vividly recalled the last time he’d jumped while near Otochi’s lair. It had resulted in him getting sealed away in a box for a month. Now far more experienced with spatial spells, he could defy the beacon’s pull, but it would still alter his trajectory a bit. And even just half a meter off from his intended destination was dangerous in close quarters like this.

Fully phased and out of his cage, Kizu got to work weaving together an illusion over himself. He figured that if his doppelgänger acted as an opposite to his own skills, then he should be capable of casting hexes and summons. Both of which were terrifying magic to face against, but it left him utterly inept to spells in branches like enhancement magic. That meant he wouldn’t be capable of spellsense. And, even if he had an artifact or potion to help him around that weakness, Kizu also retained his necklace which the doppelgänger had foolishly left on his person.

Honestly, Kizu didn’t know what his other self was thinking. He left Kizu capable of casting spells and with half of his gear still on him. Sure, his rings, bag, and atlas were missing. But his enchanted clothing and necklace were both extremely valuable tools that the doppelgänger surely knew about.

He melded into the shadows and crept through the passageway, leaving Kumiho behind. Instead of going in the direction his doppelgänger had gestured in when referencing his friends, Kizu instead crept after his other self. That casual reveal of Ione and Basil’s location might very well have been a trap. The problem with his doppelgänger was that he was an entirely unreliable source of information. He could just be carelessly throwing out information, or he could equally as likely be setting up some complex trap. His broken sanity created a dangerous unpredictability to his words.

Once in the next room over, Kizu quickly took in the layout. It seemed to be some sort of meeting chamber with a few chairs surrounding a large wooden table. When he spotted the bands of metal on the armrests of one of the chairs, he reevaluated his assessment. This was an interrogation chamber. Which made sense, seeing as how close he currently was to the dungeon cages.

Kizu set a hand on one of the spare chairs off to the side and quickly set a divination enchantment in it. It was simple and easily detectable. But if his other self lacked access to spellsense, it should be a viable method of spying. Of course, he needed a scrying conduit, but he felt confident he could find something without difficulty.

The next room immediately validated that confidence. He stepped into a well-worn kitchen. The pots and pans that hung from hooks on the wall were rusted and untrustworthy for food preparation, but would be more than adequate to serve as holders of small pools of water.

As he reached for a battered pot, something else caught his eye. Next to a lump of cheese and brown mush on the counter, was a familiar wooden bowl.

Abandoning the battered cookware, he instead stepped over to the bowl. Little symbols were etched into the rim. A leaf, an insect, a mouse, an owl, an ocelot, a human, then it looped back to the leaf.

He’d forgotten he’d packed the crone’s old scrying bowl. For some reason, his doppelgänger had unpacked it and set it on the counter here. Kizu gripped the edge, happy to find a far more suitable object for scrying. However, he found it heavier than it had any right to be.

His eyes widened in surprise. Despite the flawlessly transparent interior, there was liquid inside the bowl. His mind quickly ran through the possibilities. Not a potion of invisibility, but rather, an invisible potion. While he’d never personally brewed anything that matched the description, he knew of several possible ingredients that might cause similar effects. The fact they were rare World Dungeon materials was an issue rendered moot by his current location.

Kizu ripped his hand back as he mentally settled on the most likely concoction. The jerking movement wobbled the bowl and a tiny amount of the invisible contents splashed out. Kizu only knew because he felt sudden and immediate pain from his leg where the liquid made contact.

Luckily,  the droplets landed on his monster leg, not his human flesh. It soaked through his pant leg as if the fabric didn’t even exist and splattered against the scales of his leg.

Extraordinarily lucky. The aching burn that the drops of potion left behind was nothing compared to the potential hole seared in his very soul. He was more than familiar with several of the ingredients used, as he’d crafted similar, albeit less potent and far more delicate, brews for use against Chiame a few months ago. If it touched his human skin, Zayne’s soul protection would have been far too little to save him from a truly disastrous fate. He would have been rendered a soulless shell, like the Dragon Sojan puppeteered.

“Damn. I thought I’d get you with that one.” Kizu heard his own voice and whirled on the noise and faced the door of what he’d assumed to be a pantry.

There he stood, a decrepit mirror image of himself, flanked by two summoned magical beasts. On his right, was a massive, seven-headed hydra, each with a distinct color, their necks bent and knotted to fit in the room’s tight space. On his left, stood a beagle-like dog with floppy, oversized ears and a tongue that sagged to the side of its mouth. Kizu genuinely wasn’t certain which of the two to exercise more caution around.

“So, you’ve caught me,” Kizu said. He held the bowl aloft, suspended in a barrier to keep it safely contained. “What was your plan?”

“I was hoping you’d accidentally spill that on yourself, so I could then enter your soulless husk.”

“And you didn’t drench me in that stuff while I slept because?”

“The reason I wish to commandeer your body to begin with,” his doppelgänger snapped back. “Otochi layered restrictions on this when he constructed it. I’m not able to remove your soul.”

“I see.” Kizu paused. “You left the necklace to lure me into a false sense of security?”

“You know me better than anyone else.”

Kizu frowned. That was unfortunately true. Especially after unlocking the memories of his past. He doubted a soul alive understood this miserable abomination better than he did. However, he also doubted the reverse was true. He liked to think he’d changed a lot in this last year.

“So, we’re going to fight now?” Kizu guessed, looking between the two summons in the room. At least one was doubtlessly designed for violence. One of the hydra’s many heads snapped the air. The beagle remained a dangerous enigma. “You’ll strong-arm me into a position where I am forced to purge my soul?”

His doppelgänger raised a finger to his smiling lips. “Shhh. My next move is a secret. Don’t go sharing it with the world.”

Kizu’s monster leg flexed in anticipation and he prepared for a battle.
