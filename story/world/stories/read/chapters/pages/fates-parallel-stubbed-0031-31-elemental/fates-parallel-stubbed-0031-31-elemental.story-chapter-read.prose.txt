As the two groups continued to approach the mountain, the number of beasts they encountered actually began to decrease, despite the steadily growing mana density. Still, they had plenty of encounters, and Lee Jia got the opportunity to see Yan’s team in battle—their strategy was completely different from her team’s.

Where her team focused on tying down foes to allow their heaviest hitters to attack unimpeded, Yan’s team was all heavy hitters. Using blitz strategies and overwhelming offense, most of their fights ended in an instant. It was visually impressive—and Yan certainly seemed to enjoy smugly lording it over Lee Jia that his battles went so much more smoothly than hers—but Lee Jia wasn’t particularly impressed.

First, it relied on their numbers—Yan’s team was half again as large as hers and all of them specialized in offense. Their battle potential would drop exponentially as they took injuries. Second, it was weak to ambush—without their alpha strike significantly reducing the enemy numbers, their defense wouldn’t be likely to hold up. Finally, they lacked stamina—even after relatively short engagements, they would need to spend time meditating to recover their energy.

It all reminded Lee Jia of her duel with Yan Zhihao. She had used his own aggression against him to wear him out through attrition, and then taken advantage of his weakness. His team fought as a group the same way he did in a duel, with all the same weaknesses.

One thing that did impress her was their use of magic. While Yan Zhihao stubbornly stuck to only spiritual arts, the rest of the students from Qin seemed to have taken well to arcane practice. Though they were only in the mana gathering stage, they had apparently discovered a way to use their qi to empower their talismans and create impressive elemental barriers. Lee Jia resolved herself to learn how to copy that technique when she got the chance.

Following the flow of the leylines, they were soon brought to the base of the mountain and even began to ascend when things became eerily quiet. Not only was there no longer any sign of the magical beasts that had been plaguing the wilderness until now, there was no sign of any wildlife. Everyone was on edge as they slowly hiked up the mountain slopes, keeping a wary eye out for any signs of danger.

Eventually, they stopped in front of the mouth of a cave where the mana had grown so dense as to be nearly overwhelming to Lee Jia’s mana sense. Unlike the leylines that had led them here, the mana pouring out of the cave was unbalanced. Lee Jia didn’t immediately recognize the element, but Guan Yi did.

“Metal element. This is almost certainly the source of the disruption.”

Indeed, while the leylines seemed to want to converge elsewhere—most likely the peak of the mountain—whatever was in the cave was disrupting the balance and causing the leylines to twist in strange unnatural whorls. As the two groups approached the cave, Lee Jia noticed Eui growing increasingly agitated.

“What’s wrong?”

Lee Jia’s whisper was so low as to be nearly sub-vocal. There were other half-spirits present, after all. Eui scowled and shook her head before whispering back.

“I don’t know. Something feels really wrong, here. This thing”—she tapped the center of her chest—“feels like it’s pulling me towards that cave, but every other fibre of my being is screaming to stay away.”

Lee Jia frowned. She had also been feeling that familiar buzzing sense of danger, but the behavior of Eui’s newfound mana core was even more disturbing. Before she could reply, they were interrupted by Yan Zhihao.

“Well what are we just standing around for? Let’s investigate!”

Without another word, he headed into the cave and was quickly followed by his entourage. Lee Jia hesitated for a moment. She wasn’t sure whether it was a good idea to follow him, but she didn’t want to let Yan Zhihao plunder whatever was in there on his own. After receiving a nod of confirmation from Eui, she made up her mind.

“Alright, we’re going too. Stay vigilant, we don’t know what’s in there.”

The inside of the cave was dark, but between her night vision and her mana sense, Lee Jia had no trouble finding her way. After a while, though, Dae used a talisman that created a little ball of floating light that followed above his head, revealing the unnaturally smooth walls of the narrow passage that they were in.

“The mouth of the cave looked natural, but this seems to have been purposefully constructed.”

Xin nodded in agreement with Dae, running his hand along the walls as he inspected them closer.

“Perhaps it’s the abandoned grotto of some ancient cultivator. The mana gathering formation could have inadvertently activated some dormant formation in here.”

As they followed the path, Lee Jia found herself quickly losing her sense of space. Though there was only a single pathway to follow it was long and winding, sometimes sloped downward, sometimes sloped upward. If there had been any branching pathways, she knew she would have gotten lost by now.

After hours traversing the strange labyrinth, the passageway began to open up and a source of light could be seen in the distance. Briefly, Lee Jia wondered if they had gone through to the other side of the mountain, but she was soon proven wrong. As they approached the source of the light, the temperature in the cave began to rise dramatically until they entered a wide cavern and found the source of both the light and the heat.

A massive roaring flame in the rough shape of a human—if a human was twelve feet tall and made of fire—stood in the center of the cavern. Scattered throughout the cavern were the bones of various magical beasts, charred black where they hadn’t been completely burned to ash. Behind it was some kind of strange altar surrounded by a glowing magical circle. Sticking out of the center of the altar was a sword, with chains running from its pommel to the anchor points of the magic circle.

“An elemental!? But why fire? I thought you said this place was flooded with metal qi?”

Yan’s voice was excited, but cautious as he stared up at the intimidating creature. It had yet to react to their presence. Dae pointed at the altar behind it nervously.

“Look closer—those aren’t chains. They look like vines of some kind. They’re dried and withered now, but the fact that they’ve withstood the heat at all must mean they’re imbued with an extremely powerful wood element. The fire elemental’s presence must be overwhelming the water aspect of the wood...”

“...and the remaining elemental composition resembles metal!”

Xin finished for him and Dae nodded in confirmation. Lee Jia furrowed her brows in confusion.

“Don’t elementals form where a certain element is unnaturally dense? How is it here?”

The group went silent for a moment before Eunae pointed out the bones.

“I can’t say for certain, but elementals are intelligent. Perhaps it formed elsewhere and was led here by the disturbance, it seems to be using the mana density to lure in magical beasts to consume.”

“So then what’s stopping it from consuming us?”

A dozen angry glares fell on Eui as she tempted fate and she grinned sheepishly.

“Uh...sorry.”

Despite Eui’s provocation, the elemental remained still and silent and Lee Jia let out a sigh of relief. As she started to consider how they should proceed, she noticed Yan Zhihao eyeing the sword greedily. Feeling a terrible premonition, Lee Jia tried to call out to stop him, but she was too late.

“I fear no flame! That treasure is mine!”

Yan Zhihao ran forward into the cavern and as he crossed the threshold, two brilliant stars lit up on the elemental’s ‘head’ where it’s eyes would be and fixated on Yan before sweeping over the rest of the group. The eyes lit up with a blinding light for a moment and a wall of flames erupted behind them, cutting off their retreat.

“Ancestors damn you, Yan! You’re going to get us all killed!”

Lee Jia shrieked angrily as she got into a battle position. Clearly they had no choice but to fight now, and hesitation could mean death. Yan Zhihao swiped with his sword, sending a wave of qi that instantly severed one of the creature’s arms. Unperturbed, the creature swept what remained of its arms towards Yan and launched a torrent of flame as one of his allies hastily cast an earthen barrier to protect him.

Yan’s smug expression quickly turned ashen as the barrier was quickly melted away into molten rock. A strange, inhuman voice echoed throughout the cavern.

“Intruders! All shall be consumed by my flame!”

Everyone froze for a bare instant before Dae’s voice cried out urgently.

“Xiantian! Elementals can’t speak until the fourth stage!”

“Can it be reasoned with!?”

Lee Jia’s panicked question was answered by an explosion of flame that instantly incinerated two of Yan’s teammates alive. Her blood felt like ice as she watched them disintegrate without even leaving behind a corpse. Damn Yan Zhihao! So much for the snake swallowing the elephant. Never mind defeating the elemental, it would be a miracle if any of them made it out of this cave alive.

“Dae, do something about that wall of fire! Everyone else, focus on protecting him. We’re retreating!”

Lee Jia tried to analyze the fight as her team moved into position, not entirely sure how to defend against the indomitable fire elemental. Despite its provocative words, the Elemental wasn’t especially aggressive, throwing out attacks casually and mostly ignoring anyone who wasn’t attacking it.

It made no moves to defend itself even as Yan’s sword art cut huge chunks out of it, and it was immediately obvious why—the creature’s body was entirely immaterial. Every severed limb would simply dissipate into mana, only to be instantly reabsorbed and regenerated. An elemental was made of pure mana given sentience, and its body was only a manifestation of that mana.

They had discussed the possibility of encountering an elemental before, and Dae had told her that they were nearly completely impervious to physical attacks. Between that and their intelligence, an elemental was many orders of magnitude more dangerous than a magical beast of the same strength.

The only way to harm them was to use techniques that targeted the mind, or to find and destroy the elemental core. Except, this was a xiantian elemental—there was no way it would allow its core to be damaged so easily.

Yan was clearly trying to do just that, however—striking with his sword art over and over in different places. Yet no matter where he struck the creature, it made no attempt to defend. The only reason Yan Zhihao himself hadn’t fallen was because of his own flame aura, and his team’s valiant efforts in raising barrier after barrier to defend him.  Unlike a mindless beast, the elemental quickly caught on and started taking opportunities to pick off Yan’s allies one by one.

“Dae, how long is that going to take!?”

“I don’t know! I’m trying to use a mana suppressing formation, but this barrier is very powerful. Never mind when, I’m not certain if I’ll break through it.”

Dae’s panicked response did not fill Lee Jia with the confidence she had been hoping for. They needed more time! Something!

“Eui...”

Jia hesitated before asking Eui to help distract the elemental. She was their heaviest hitter, but could Jia bear to put Eui in that position again so soon? Unfortunately, Eui knew Jia’s thoughts almost as well as she did—and she never backed down from a challenge.

Before Jia could call her back, Eui charged forward and brandished her knives—replenished from Jia’s storage ring. Without hesitation, she imbued a knife with destructive ki and hurled it straight at the elemental’s head. It passed straight through harmlessly and exploded in the wall behind it. Eui scowled and then froze and stared down at her hand in astonishment. It was completely uninjured!

At last, it seemed like she had mastered the first form of the Six Arms of Asura. It didn’t even bother her that the technique seemed to be useless against the fire elemental as she began throwing knives one after the other, experimenting with the timing in an attempt to get them to explode near the elemental. It ignored most of her attacks entirely, only occasionally leaning out of the way of an explosion until something very strange happened.

After the last knife Eui threw, the elemental moved almost as soon as it left her hand, throwing itself in the way and intercepting the knife with a fiery ‘hand’. Instead of passing through harmlessly, the knife was immediately destroyed, causing it to detonate prematurely. This time, the fire mana didn’t dissipate—it was consumed entirely by the explosion and the damaged limb did not reform.

Lee Jia was shocked and excited—they had finally damaged it! Her excitement quickly turned to horror as the burning stars of the elementals eyes focused directly on Eui. Oh no! Jia and Eui’s faces went pale as the elemental moved from its place for the first time since the fight had begun, striding purposefully towards Eui.

“Oh, fuck!”

As Eui turned to run, Jia’s mind worked furiously to try to figure out a way to save her. She had enraged the elemental—no that wasn’t right, they didn’t have emotions. The elemental suddenly determined that Eui was a threat, but why? It had simply been ignoring or dodging her attacks before, so what changed with the last one?

Lee Jia replayed the scene in her head. The elemental had moved to intercept Eui’s attack, but why? She tried to calculate the trajectory of Eui’s attack, estimating where it would land—her eyes fell on the altar. The elemental had stood in front of it the entire time, and had even taken its only injury so far to protect it. Did it really value the mana lure that much?

Another possibility formed in her mind. The chain-like vines tying the sword down to the altar made Lee Jia think of some kind of seal. The presence of the fire elemental had clearly weakened it, but it hadn’t broken. With the amount of mana empowering the formation, surely the fire elemental would have benefited enormously from consuming it—yet it hadn’t. Could it be that it considered whatever was sealed to be an even greater danger?

“Yan—”

Lee Jia hesitated for just a moment. If she was right, they might be throwing themselves into the fire to escape the smoke. What was she saying—were they not already engulfed!?

“Yan, the sword!”

Yan Zhihao’s eyes widened as Lee Jia pointed at the undefended altar. With a nod, he dashed towards it. The elemental had nearly caught up to Eui and stopped just short of bringing a flaming fist down to immolate her. It’s eyes flashed as it whipped around to point a flaming hand at Yan, but it stopped before launching a torrent of flame—the altar was in its line of fire!

Yan Zhihao had no such compunctions and gleefully unleashed his own flaming breath across the altar. The weakened seal couldn’t withstand such a direct attack, and the formation quickly shattered. The elemental flew towards Yan at full speed, ignoring everything else as he reached out for the sword to pull it free from the altar. The elemental reached him at the same moment that he pulled the sword out, and Lee Jia was blinded by a brilliant flash of light.

As her vision returned and she tried to blink the white spots out of her eyes, she was baffled by the scene before her. Between Yan Zhihao and the fire elemental a man had appeared from out of nowhere.

He was nearly seven feet tall and shirtless, with a handsome face and a lean musculature—not as brawny as Guan Yi, yet he still managed to make Yan look scrawny. His skin was unnaturally pale and his short hair was an unusual dark purple color. A single black horn curved from his forehead to a sharp point, and the surface of a smooth red orb was visible in the center of his chest—disturbingly reminiscent of the core that Eui had formed.

The man’s arm was extended outward, penetrating the chest of the elemental which had gone completely still. With a cold expression, the man tore his hand free and the elemental’s entire body vanished in an instant. He held up the perfect quality grade four mana core—a priceless treasure—and regarded it with glowing red eyes.

For several seconds, the entire cavern was still and silent—nobody dared make a move. Then the strange man raised the core up—

“You were supposed to free me, not use me like a worm on a hook you stupid, useless motherfucker! Rrraagh!”

—and shattered it on the floor of the cavern with an angry shout.
