Chapter 116

"The requirements have gone up," Nikola said.

Riley let her hand stay where it was on the timber of the silo for a moment longer. Then she took it away and turned to face him.

"Go on."

"The tower will finish soon. All new building upgrades will require much more resources."

"How much more?"

"On the tower, probably three times the last cost."

"Three times?!?"

"Level 10 is a milestone level and when you select the next build the requirements are shown based on the current standing. They will change if we change the build."

"How?"

"Research is one example, if we complete research to reduce the resource cost of building, but the we are in the early stages of that research so the impact will be minimal until we reach the later stages."

Landryn's shoulders tensed up, like she was bracing for impact.

Nikola just waited.

"And the timer," Riley said.

"The timer will sit idle with another upgrade, assuming you choose a short upgrade, days?. Not because there is nothing to build. Because we cannot afford to start what needs building next."

He paused, while Riley's eyes toggled back and forth like she was reading her thoughts.

"Go on."

"There is a second matter. We are behind on research."

She looked at him.

"How far behind?"

"Enough that some of the upgrades we would want to start next are not yet available. There are thresholds. Certain research levels open certain building upgrades and certain troop tiers. We have not been meeting the thresholds. The tower's next step is one of them. The barracks will be the one after that."

"So even if we had the resources."

"We could not spend them on what needs to be built. Not yet."

She did not answer right away.

She felt it in the pit of her stomach, the problem that has been in the back of her mind was finally being pushed to the front and she couldn't ignore it anymore. She felt she was now playing a game of wack a mole, chasing instead of planning ahead.

She turned back to the silo. She put her palm on the timber banding again. The wood was cool under her hand. The cavern went up into dark somewhere above her that the lamps did not reach, and the faint sharp smell of the saltpetre wall was in the back of her nose, and she stood with her hand on the silo and let the two things Nikola had just said settle in her chest.

He was right. Of course he was right. She had known this. She had known it the way you know a bill is coming and you do not open the envelope. All the way back at home, in the game, the balance was three-legged and everyone who played long enough learned it the same way. Buildings. Troops. Research. Skip any one of them and the other two would carry you for a while, and then one day they would not, and you would find yourself sitting on a fat barracks and a full silo and no way to upgrade anything because your alchemy level was where it had been three weeks ago.

Research was the leg I let go slack, she thought.

The truth was that this had never been lost on her. It had only been deferred. Back home the whole game was a careful balance of resource management and timing, and the players who did well did well because they had figured out how to bend the timing to their advantage. There were hacks. You could starve your soldiers by keeping food deliveries from gathering timed to just before you would start a timer, this way the food would go to your builds instead of into their bellies. She saw the faces of the soldiers here and the idea of starving them, starving Valrik, was something that she just couldn't live with.

Research was the other one. Research was expensive. Research was slow. But at the end of the day, tier four soldiers were coming. Even if she could defeat the Clawborn level 4's, the threat of other kingdoms power would be a threat and she would not only need to hit those higher levels, but to build thousands of those higher-level troops just to survive. There was a reason the Clawborn and the like where running these massive mines, slaves and raiders, they need resources to keep adding higher level soldiers.

She took her hand off the silo.

She turned back to Nikola and Landryn, and she looked past them at the cavern, at the sorting tables strung out along the north wall, at the chain of oil lamps running back toward the mouth of the mine, at the pale bloom on the saltpetre wall and the second silo she had put a hand on a moment ago. The cavern was not small. The cavern went back further than the lamps went. All of it was hers. All of it was going to have to work harder than it was already working.

Feed the machine, she thought.

That was the whole of it. Buildings, troops, research. Feed all three. The machine was a machine and it did not care that the person feeding it was tired.

Landryn had shifted his weight. He glanced at Nikola. Nikola glanced back. Neither of them said anything. They were waiting on her, and they had gone through enough of these silences to know they were meant to wait through this one too.

She looked down at the black stone of the cavern floor.

Back home when players had hit this same wall the answer was always the same. You banded up. You joined an alliance. You picked a strong player and you fed them resources you had, and in return they carried the alliance when the fighting came. The weaker players sent resources up the chain to keep the strong player's troops moving, and when the strong player opened a rally the weaker players sent their own troops into it. The rally hit harder because there were more soldiers in it. When it came back into the hospital the wounded were spread across three or four players accounts instead of one, and the whole alliance was ready for the next fight in half the time a solo player would have needed.

She did not have an alliance. There was no chat channel she could open to a group of strangers who would send her resources because they were on her team. She had what she had. She had the Hoshin. She had the mine. She had Landryn's crews and Nikola's silos and Thorne out beyond the tree line hunting Level Two monsters with Zelgra.

But the Clawborn had shown her the other version of the same solution. They were not banding up. They were taking. Whatever they needed to feed their requirements they were pulling out of the communities they overran, and they had been doing it long enough because it was effective. Months and years of army-building did not run on virtue. It ran on other people's grain. Other people's stone. Other people's backs.

Slavery was not on the table. She said it to herself flatly and then she said it a second time because she wanted to be sure she meant it. Slavery was not on the table. Whatever the Clawborn's answer to the requirements was, it was not her answer.

The King in the South must have his own answer. Taxes, maybe. She did not know what a tax was worth in this world but she could guess that a kingdom that had held the south for as long as his had would have a rhythm of collection worked out and a schedule to it. The mainland was bigger again. The mainland's builds must be astronomical and their requirements with them, and their resource lines had to be running somewhere. Every large power in this world was solving this same problem in some way. She was going to have to solve it too.

She could do a lot. There was no shortage of things she could do, but time was not on her side.

Resources were something she could answer, at least for now.

She looked up.

Both of them were looking back at her already.

"We are preparing to hit the Clawborn resource depot," she said. "Preparations are underway. While we are gone I need the two of you to work on a few things."

Nikola inclined his head.

"Nikola. Once the tower timer clears, I want another four silos down here. Not up in the yard. Down here. Then one of each of the resource buildings, also down here. Farm. Sawmill. Foundry. All four resource sets, mirrored underground."

"Understood."

"How long."

"Give me the numbers on the raid and I will give you the numbers on the build. The silo I know. Four silos we can begin within the hour if the timer opens as expected. The resource buildings I will a little longer.”

"That's fine. Start leveling them up once they are built."

"Yes, Warden."

She turned to Landryn.

"Landryn. The clearing north of the main gate. Outside the wall. I need it measured."

"Measured for what."

"For how many soldiers it will hold. Standing formation. Rally formation. Both counts if you can get me both."

"I can get you both."

"After that I need you to draw something out for me. Top priority. Ahead of the west push, ahead of the saltpetre bed, ahead of anything else you have on your board. I want construction started on it before I get back."

Landryn nodded slowly, watching her.

"I can pull Nikola in on the measuring. A few of the soldiers can help. We'll have it inside the day." He waited a beat. "What are we building, Warden."

She looked at him.

"A catapult."

Landryn's face hardened with confusion, then he nodded.

Nikola did not react at all, the instructions were clear.

Riley turned back toward the mouth of the mine and was about to leave.

"Warden?"
