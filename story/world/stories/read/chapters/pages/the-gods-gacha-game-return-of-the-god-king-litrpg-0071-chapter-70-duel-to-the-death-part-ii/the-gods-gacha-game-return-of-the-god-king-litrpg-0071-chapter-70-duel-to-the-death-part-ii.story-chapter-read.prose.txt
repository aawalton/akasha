I didn’t give him time to recover and spammed the skill. “Windstorm! Windstorm!”

The air howled around us as the repeated waves of pressure kicked up dirt and debris, forcing Graham to brace himself behind his greatsword. His coat whipped violently in the gale, and he looked increasingly unsettled and more injured.

[Desperate Willpower Lv.8] has activated.

The relentless wind kept battering him, grinding against his defenses. Shallow cuts continued to open across his arms, legs, and torso—one after another.

These wounds were nothing individually, but even a thousand paper cuts could bleed a man dry.

But still, I wasn’t done yet.

“Grant Plausibility.”

You have used Grant Plausibility.

Air currents are now amplified three times around the caster’s position.

Movement speed increased by 30% within the affected zone.

I didn’t bother to correct him. Let him chase shadows and false labels while I turned the field into my weapon.

“Windstorm!” I shouted again and swung my blade, unleashing another slicing burst that screamed toward him.

This time, it struck him head-on. The blast hit with enough force to stagger him and forced him to his knees. His expression twisted in frustration as blood trickled down the side of his face.

“Damn it!” But somehow, he forced himself up, gritting his teeth as he raised his greatsword through the roaring gale. “Blade Echo!”

Suddenly, three phantom slashes erupted from the ground beneath him, tearing through the lingering wind and hurtling toward me like spectral guillotines, disrupting the storm and turning my own domain against me.

But I had already seen his attack and moved away. Thanks to the wind-enhanced zone and my bolstered stats, the phantom slashes missed entirely, cutting nothing but air. They carved through where I’d been an instant earlier, trailing behind like angry echoes chasing a ghost. I sidestepped the last with ease, my body gliding like it weighed nothing.

“Wind Rush!” I shouted, closing the distance in a blink.

Before his phantom slashes faded, I was already behind him. He whirled around just in time to catch the glint of my blade.

“Heavy Slash!”

The impact landed cleanly against his side, tearing open a wide gash. Graham flinched, gritting his teeth as blood splattered across the arena floor. However, I didn’t stop with just one attack. I kept attacking, one strike after the other. Quick, deliberate slashes carved shallow lines across his coat and arms, whittling him down piece by piece.

Graham snarled and swung his greatsword broadly, but I was already gone, circling him like a predator teasing its prey. I stayed just outside his reach, attacking between his movements, never giving him a chance to recover. While my attacks weren’t lethal on their own, they added up. Cut after cut. Wound after wound.

It was only a matter of time before he bled out.

He must have felt the humiliation seeping in—the way I was treating him not as a threat, but as meat. His expression twisted with rage and shame, so contorted with fury that I was certain it would leave a permanent frown line across his face.

“ENOUGH!!” An ear-deafening roar erupted from Graham’s mouth, causing me to wince.

You have been afflicted with Lion’s Roar.

You are stricken with fear.

All stats have been temporarily reduced by 20%.

Movement speed decreased by 50%.

Your body has momentarily frozen up and is unable to move.

What? A debuff skill?!

Across from me, Graham stood on his feet while blood was dripping down his torn armor. And yet, his presence hadn’t diminished in the slightest. His eyes burned—not just with fury, but with a cold, calculated hunger.

“Thought you could humiliate me, didn’t you?” he growled, stepping ever closer. “I’ve killed many stronger opponents than you in the scenarios.”

One Who Conquered the Impossible’s effect has activated.

All your reduced stats have returned to normal.

You are no longer frozen in place.

I barely had time to register the pain before Graham attacked again, this time driving a brutal kick straight into my chest.

“Gahh—!”

It felt like a good few seconds passed before gravity reclaimed me and I hit the ground hard, face-first. My sword, torn from my grip, clattered across the dirt and stabbed into the ground a few meters away.

Fuck! It hurt so much!

My limbs trembled as I tried to push myself up while feeling sweet liquid on my throat. My thoughts were hazy, like smoke curling around the edges of a fire about to go out.

Graham’s heavy footsteps approached, each one sending vibrations through the ground.

“If only… I can restrain him…” I muttered, my voice barely coming out.

Then I came to realize something as I clenched the dirt on the ground.

You have used Grant Plausibility.

The ground churned beneath him like a quicksand made of stone. And even Graham—wounded, bleeding, and drained from our clash—couldn’t escape it.

The terrain dragged him downward, inch by inch, anchoring his legs deeper into the earth. The strength left in his body was being sapped away with every heartbeat until he was completely trapped.

I coughed hard, blood flecking the dirt around me as I pushed myself up to my knees. My arms trembled, but my eyes remained locked on my sword, still embedded in the earth a few meters away. Now’s my chance…

I wrapped my fingers around the hilt and tore the blade free from the earth. The wind whistled around me again, as if sensing my resolve.

I turned back to Graham, who was still struggling to free himself, and walked toward him. He glared at me with a mix of fury and desperation, his gaze sharp enough to cut, as if willing me to die through sheer hatred.

Flinch I did not.
