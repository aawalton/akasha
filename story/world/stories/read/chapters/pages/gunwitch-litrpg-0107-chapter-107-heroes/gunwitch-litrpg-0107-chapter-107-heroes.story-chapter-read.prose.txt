Vasil was still looking at the trees when he finally found his words. "I was a fool," he said. "I thought that by serving the King, by stabilizing the realm, I was making a safe world for her. “I gave them everything,” he whispered. “Every waking hour. My loyalty. My counsel. My years. I thought I was part of something... something grand. I should have spent more time with her. “They called me Vasil the Wise,” he spat as if the title disgusted him now. “They should have called me Vasil the Fool. Vasil the Blind. Vasil, who traded his daughter for a hollow title. I didn’t even know. I did not know her class was unsanctioned. I rarely saw her. I wasn’t even there when she chose her class. I am sure she told me at some point, but it didn’t even register. I just told her ‘that’s nice, dear’ and went back to my duties."

He exhaled slowly. “You ask me what she was like? How she lived? I am ashamed to say I do not know. Or at least not as much as a father should know. I was too busy with politics to realize my own daughter had just signed her death warrant.”

I looked at him, seeing the weight of that guilt pushing down on him. It wasn't that he didn't love her; it was that he had prioritized the “important” work of a kingdom over the “ordinary” life of his daughter. And now, the kingdom was gone, his title was gone, and Zosia was gone.

I wonder if she’d chosen the class hoping he’d finally notice her, pay attention to her.

“So they told me. Apparently she had chosen [Krviodavtsa]. A class that allows you to heal others with your own blood. How she got offered that class, or who offered her that class I do not know. They did not know. She would not tell them.”

A small, sad smile appeared on his lips. “Apparently she was quite defiant, even in the end. They told me she was joking and insulting them right up until…”

“So, you see, Hecate,” he said, meeting my eyes. “In a lot of ways you remind me of her. I know you are not her. But when I saw you, I just thought, maybe, I’d been given a second chance. To help you help the people of Silesia. I see the good in you. I see your spark. I stared back at Vasil. He was standing there with tears on his face, finally admitting that he’d been blind to a daughter who was literally bleeding for her people.
