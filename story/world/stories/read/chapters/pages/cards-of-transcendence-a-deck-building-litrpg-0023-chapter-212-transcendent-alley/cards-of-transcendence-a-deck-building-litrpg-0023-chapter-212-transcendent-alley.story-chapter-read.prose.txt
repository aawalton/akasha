Flynn followed Nyx down the street commonly known as Transcendent Alley in Khalreth. Towering buildings and temples rose up on each side of the wide brick road, each with one or more large statues out front depicting some divinity or patron saint. Flynn wasn’t familiar with most of them as religion wasn’t common in the wastelands. The only temple in his hometown of Aramath had been a tiny little structure dedicated to the patron saint of traveling.

Of course, he had learned that the Transcendent Council was more than just a religious organization. Several common organizations that he’d never thought of as being religious or governmental had a presence along the road and presumably on the mythical council.

The streets were full of people wearing robes of various colors who appeared to be devotees of one temple or another. There were also well-dressed folks who walked with an air of self-importance, much like the Guildmaster of the Adventurers Guild had. In addition to the people that seemed to belong to one of the organizations, there were people that appeared to be there to see something they thought was important or miraculous in some way.

Flynn paused in front of one of the buildings and studied the large statue of a man holding a set of scales. He wore a medallion around his neck that had the symbol of the Merchants Guild on it. The building behind the statue was a more practical than some of the fancy temples, but it was still obviously a building of import.

He shook his head and took a few quick steps to catch up with Nyx. “So is the Merchant’s Guild a guild or religion? What about the Adventurer’s Guild? Or the Mercenary Guild?”

“Is there a difference?” she asked, a sour look on her face. “Whatever you call them, they have rules and tenets that you must follow to be a member. Those rules usually focus on doing what’s best for that particular organization, often to the detriment of everyone else.”

Flynn thought her take was more than a little cynical, but wasn’t sure if he could disagree. He had witnessed some level of contempt between the Adventurer’s Guild and Mercenary Guild, even though they both ultimately worked for roughly the same thing.

“I guess I just thought that religions were led by gods while guilds were controlled by people.” The idea of gods was foreign to Flynn. Sure, he’d heard legends of powerful beings that did impossible things, but nothing in his personal experiences pointed to the existence of supernatural forces that could or would affect his life. And the idea that there was a god that controlled the buying and selling of merchandise seemed absurd.

He knew better than to voice his skepticism in the open, because it was obvious that most of the people around them very much believed in the existence of powerful beings that could either help of hurt them at a whim. Flynn had nearly run into more than one person who had dropped to their knees to offer prayers or beg for something from a gilded statue.

How can anyone believe these idols contain a powerful god? He wondered. Surely a god capable of miraculous things wouldn’t trap themselves inside a statue.

Flynn wasn’t completely dismissing the idea of gods, but it seemed to him that a real god would be more interested in the wellbeing of all humanity and not concern themselves with something like the price of merchandise.

He didn’t miss the fact that the largest building, by far, belonged to the Church of Knowledge.

They continued down Transcendent Alley, passing ten large temples and cathedrals, five on each side, until the road dead ended in front of two small buildings. One was a squat building made out of black stone. There were no visible windows or doors, but stairs led down below the building. There was no statue in front of the building, but the entire area seemed to be shrouded in unnatural shadows. A shiver ran down Flynn’s spine as he studied the Temple of the Deeper Realm which looked like it was dedicated to something nefarious. No one was kneeling, bowing, or offering prayers, but he had noticed a couple black robed people descend into whatever was below the building as they approached.

The other building was made of chipped and cracked grey stone. There was a roughly life sized statue of a man in front of the building, off to the side. The statue had been beheaded at some point and both arms were broken off above the elbows. An empty scabbard hung at the man’s waist, but there was no sign of a sword. It had probably been in the hand of one of the broken arms.

This guy definitely isn’t a popular fellow, Flynn thought, realizing that the chips and cracks in the building were from acts of vandalism like the desecration of the statue.

The idea that an all-powerful god would allow this kind of destruction to their temple didn’t do much to convince Flynn that gods were real.

“The Storm Temple,” Nyx announced. “Otherwise known as the Temple of the Fallen God.”

That was a story that Flynn had heard before. The story of a god who had renounced his godhood and fled. Or been cast out. Or lost whatever gave gods their power. It depended on the story. The one common element of all the stories was that there were once twelve gods and now there were only eleven. Whether this God of the Storms would return also varied by story.

Of course, Flynn had always thought the story was a fairy tale. The same with the Council of Transcendence, though it had become abundantly clear that many people did believe.

The fact that a god could lose their power also went against the idea of an all-powerful, immortal being, as far as he was concerned.

It all seemed too convenient—too contrived—to Flynn.

His musings of unbelief were interrupted by Nyx. “You stay here. I’m going to send a message to Khalor.”

“In there?” Flynn asked incredulously. “Isn’t there a messenger service we can use or something?”

She frowned and cocked her head to the side. “Do you know where he’s at to send a message?”

“Well, no, but…”

“Exactly,” she said with an emphatic nod. “Don’t ask me how it works, but the people inside can get a message to him.”

“There are still people in there?”

“Of course. Just because the Stormbreaker isn’t active doesn’t mean he doesn’t still have a following.”

Stormbreaker? Flynn wondered but didn’t ask, concluding it must be this God of Storms’ name.

“Alright then,” he said. “Go send the message and let’s get out of here.”

People had started to take notice of them, and Flynn didn’t think he wanted to be associated with either of the temples at the end of Pilgram’s Row. Or any of the temples and guilds, for that matter.

That didn’t mean he wasn’t curious.

He waited five whole seconds after Nyx went inside before he creeped toward the door to the dilapidated temple. The door was hanging on damaged hinges and hadn’t closed all the way, so Flynn cautiously peered inside.

It took a moment for his eyes to adjust to the darkness inside. When they did, he saw the room on the other side of the door was relatively empty. Candles lined the walls on both sides of the room, some lit, but most were extinguished. There was a large, arched double door in the center of the back wall and a pair of regular sized doors, one on each side.

Nyx stood in front of the wall of candles on the right side of the room, her back to him. She reached down and picked up one of the already lit candles and used it to light a large, central candle. There was a puff of smoke as the taller candle lit, and the room noticeably brightened. The extra light showed an expression on Nyx’s face that Flynn had never seen before. The normal sarcastic smile and twinkle in her eye was gone, replaced by a very solemn, maybe even sad, look.

The blue-haired woman pulled a something from her pocket and held it out over the flame. Flynn saw that it was a folded piece of paper as it caught fire and was consumed in a flash.

The bright flash of light caused him to step back in surprise, and Flynn realized he wasn’t alone.

“So, what do we have here?” a sarcastic voice asked.

Flynn spun, his hand going to the blade strapped to his waist. His hand wrapped around the hilt, but he didn’t draw the weapon as he took in the group of people arrayed before him.

A man no more than a few years older than himself stood at the center of a dozen black robed men and women. The man had short blond hair and had a sneer on his face that seemed to be his natural expression. His robes looked a lot like those wore by Jerome, the priest from the Church of Knowledge, but pretty much everyone he’d seen was wearing similar garments.

“What are you doing here, boy? I don’t think even the fallen would take someone like you.”

Flynn knew he looked pretty rough. They had spent weeks traveling and had fought thousands of monsters along the way. His clothing, not to mention his skin and hair, was covered in dirt and grime.

That was no excuse to be rude, though.

His hand tightened around the hilt of his sword. Flynn had no idea what card tattoos might be hidden under the man’s robes, but he wasn’t about to back down from a bully.

The man held his arms out to the side and raised his eyebrows. “You sure you want to do this, boy?”

Flynn clenched his teeth. The way the man said boy jabbed into his mind, obliterating any thought of backing down, even though he knew it was foolish to try and fight over a dozen people that he knew nothing about.

The door to the Temple of the Storms slammed open and Flynn could see a familiar blue glow from Nyx’s Thunder Thrower out of the corner of his eye.

He did a double take.

Nyx’s gun had been destroyed.

Where did she get that?

Before he could ask, she growled at the group of robed people. “So, is this how acolytes of the Church of Knowledge greet newcomers to the city now?” Venom dripped from her voice, and Flynn could sense that Nyx wanted to fight as bad as he did.

He started to pull his sword free, but the atmosphere suddenly changed. The near-irrational desire to fight suddenly dissipated and Flynn could think clearly again and realized how precarious their position truly was. Fighting more than a dozen people over a simple insult was crazy.

What was I thinking?

He glanced Nyx and saw that she still glared at group, her magical gun remained pointed at the man who was the apparent leader of the group.

“My apologies, Nyx,” the man said, nodding his head. “I didn’t know that you were back in town, and I didn’t know you had brought a friend.”

The way the man threw out the word friend made Flynn doubt that his attitude would have been any different if he had known.

“Why don’t you scurry back to your rat hole before I show you what a real storm looks like!” She waved her weapon at the man, shooing him away.

Flynn could see the man’s jaw clench, but he nodded curtly then spun around and walked away. His entourage turned in unison and followed him.

What the hell just happened?! Flynn wondered, but when he turned to Nyx to ask, he could tell she was in no mood to talk about it.

“Let’s find Throm and get off the streets before they come back with more people.”
