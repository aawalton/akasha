Silence, rain, and stillness.

Jack was the first to move. She trundled up to Hollow and started disentangling her shield from the saddle, barely six feet away from Kalsin and already far enough in the stormy midnight downpour to reduce her to a mere silhouette.

Pike and Crux began cursing all at once, Fals rushed over to Littleguy to help with a strap around his back, and Shoreline just barely stopped Wardoll from running off into the night with a steadying grip on her arm—but it was Ringtail who spoke first.

Ringtail who remained next to Kalsin and yanked him down by the collar to speak into his ears as the horns and the thunder threatened to blow out their eardrums. “The Veiled! In the middle of the night?"

Kalsin only knew what he’d been told about the invaders’ attack patterns. “It’s possible! But…” He glanced aside—at Cyanfish, animatedly pointing at something in the distance; at Littleguy; and at the empty space beside him that still ached, where Crawl should have been. “They shouldn’t. Not in the same place. Not so soon after being beaten back so decisively.”

Ringtail held up a finger. “One call. Right? I only heard a single call from our lookout.”

Kalsin curtly nodded. He pried her wet fingers from his collar, not ungently, and straightened up.

That was it, then. The agents knew their seb hordozó was here, and they knew the only way they could hope to catch him was by rousing the entire army to attention. Whether it was desertion or heresy that got Kalsin killed, well; it didn’t matter to the Civil House.

He would be hunted from this point forward, no matter what. But not everyone had to share his fate.

Kalsin strode up to his nearest mate, boots squelching in the mud, and clapped Dweller on the back. “One horn, ladies and gents!” he shouted over the rain. “And it’s not a Veiled that triggered the alarm, that’s for certain. We can barely see farther than we can spit in this weather. How the hell could they spot those sneaky buggers?”

“Dunno,” Dweller muttered and wrangled water from her shirt, too low for anybody else to hear. “With their peepers, probably?”

Kalsin continued around the circle, smacking Fals on the shoulder. “The same people who hounded me before joining the army—folk funded by the créme of the Republic—are springing their trap right as we speak!” He hesitated before reaching Littleguy, and instead strode to stand in the middle. The soaked men around him listened, but didn’t pause in their hurried preparations. “See, when the collector of Remallon lay dying at my feet, he wasn’t shy about what the Civil House was going to do to a particular silver-eyed individual. Or anyone around him, for that matter. Killed, cut, butchered; those are the only realistic futures for heretics like us.”

Kalsin paused, letting thunder roll across the land before continuing. “But you, friends, you’ve still got a choice.” He felt more than heard Jack return to his side, leading both their bahten behind her. “You haven’t done anything incriminating yet. Leave. Go back to your huts and to your lives.” He reached up to tap the scar at his jaw. “These little things… they're not pretty, and they’re a painful reminder of some awful shite you went through. But they are not worth throwing your lives away for.”

The wind howled.

Nobody replied immediately. Nobody refuted his words.

“Can’t we just, I don’t know… just delay this whole theatre?” Wardoll asked with a vague gesture. “Let’s say we all go home today and leave on the morrow instead. Same place, same time. No worries about stupid alarms and being spotted, then. No worries about fellow countrymen causing us a headache.”

“Neeh, not quite on the mark,” Fals drawled. “If this mess is as big as it sounds, half the guardsmen in Újtavak will be flooding the borderlands within a few days—long before we can reach the ocean between the two defense lines. So that plan is shot.” She nodded eastward, where—presumably—the older boys were even now forming their defensive lines along the wall. “In the meantime, the alert’s meant to keep us boxed in. Wouldn’t surprise me if a horn blows every twelve hours just to keep that wall nice and stacked.”

“As if!” Pike let out a low laugh, pulling a glove over his hand. “As if they expect us to flee into the Eastern Expanse,” he said.

His grin slowly evaporated when nobody answered. “We’re not fleeing into the Eastern Expanse, right?”

Yes. No. Maybe. What Fals was saying made perfect sense. Kalsin knew the destination they needed to flee to, but not how they were going to get there.

“That may be our only realistic option left,” Kalsin said. “Again: please, Pike. Crux, Shoreline, everyone. Consider if this is what you really want. Consider if—”

Ringtail elbowed him in the side, hard. “Nuh-uh! No takebacks from here on out, fellas.” She hitched her pack higher on her back and flung out an arm firmly eastward. “Ghost Kalsin, your majesty and first of his name, needs your help and your undying service in the trying journey ahead of us. So let us stop wasting time, and, you know… fucking scram already!”

Crux grunted, tossing over something long and metallic to Quiescent. “Agreed,” he said, standing and kicking his chest shut.

“Conditions are favorable, too,” Shoreline pointed up at the dark sky. “We’ve got a storm to cover our necks.”

It appeared that the consensus was unanimous. His mates gathered around him, soaked from head to toe in the evening and itching to move out. Even Wardoll and Littleguy stood ready, neither of which Kalsin had expected to truly consider desertion for someone like him.

“Wait, just a— Let’s take a moment and…” Kalsin started, his mind wrestling with too many things at once. The storm raged around them. The vague outline of a plan wanted to form in his head. Wind blew in his ears. Ringtail bickered about something to Jack at his side. Low sounds of shouting echoed from the darkness as soldiers manned the wall. Water trickled into his eyes. He shut them, thinking, overwhelmed, thinking, how should they proceed, where should they go, how do they slip past the soldiers, where was his sister…

“Where is Cinder?” Kalsin said, opening his eyes. He pinned Jack with his gaze once more, and this time, there was no—absolutely none, zero give in his voice. “Why did my sister not come with you tonight?” The question carried a harshness that felt like the wrong answer could bring down the walls of a fortress. A question where the wrong answer could cause irreparable damage.

Jack just looked down at him, rain dripping down her face. She put a hand on the side of his neck, then brushed up to his cheek. A small, sad smile. But she mouthed ‘I love you’ all the same.

No. No, no, no, she couldn’t be… They needed to go back. Back to the Clenn camp, she was there, she had to be there, there was no way anything could’ve happened to her and that she was hurt and that they’d need to leave her behind. To hell with this, to hell with everything. He turned, because no reality existed where he would just walk away from her, no rationale that went beyond—

“Kalsin!”

The shout came not from the circle of soldiers around him, but from the howling night toward the east. Blonde hair plastered to her face, leathers tugged and tossed by the wind, Cinder stepped out from the dark.

Kalsin ran to her.

“Come on, hurry them up! We need to—”

Cinder’s urgent words came to a halt as Kalsin rushed into her, embracing her in the rain.

“I, I thought…” he breathed, face buried in the crook of her neck. “When you didn’t show. Gods, I worried that something went wrong.”

He could hear Cinder’s breath hitch. She gently pried them apart with her one good arm, and she glanced at the people watching them before looking up at Kalsin with a pained expression. Her mouth opened silently, unsure what to say. “There’s too many… I tried to be careful, I tried, believe me that I never wanted you to find out like this.”

Kalsin laughed quietly, once. His hand still gripped his sister’s forearm as if to confirm she was real. ”Find out what, Cinder? You’re fine. You’re here. We’re back together now, just like the old days.”

He heard cautious steps drawing near from behind, and once again he saw Cinder glance at the onlookers with a cringe. “Yes. No. I…” she bit her lips, eyes pleading. She slowly reached up and touched a cold finger against the scar between his eyes. His first scar, the one he’d taken so so long ago. “Oh, Kalsin. I’m so, so sorry. Other people can’t— they could never… Because I’m…”

Cinder stopped herself. She stepped back and took a deep, shuddering breath. “Later. I swear to you, Kalsin—I will explain everything later. But we need to run. I scouted ahead, and the gap is closing, and we need to go now.”

Kalsin wiped rain from his eyes and, after a moment of hesitation, nodded. His sister was right. There was no more time for reunions. If they wanted to be free from the Republic, they had to escape the noose slowly tightening around their necks.

Cinder took an abrupt step back the moment Ringtail crept up to Kalsin’s side. “Um,” she said, slicking a wet strand of dark hair from her eyes. “So uh. Who are you talking to?” There was some badly hidden concern in her eyes as she looked at Kalsin.

But before Kalsin could answer, his sister was already running into the darkness, half turned and urging them to follow. Lightning flashed and thunder drowned out something Cyanfish was saying, and for a moment he could make out the tiny silhouettes crowding the earthen walls in the distance.

“Cinder. My troublemaker of a sister,” Kalsin muttered as he took Sap’s reins from Jack and pointed ahead. “She’s not usually this rude, but… circumstances. Come on, fellas. She’s been my intel for years. If she’s leading the way, then that’s damn well our best option.”

He saw Shoreline draw up a questioning brow. He saw Fals and Littleguy exchange an incredulous glance. He saw Jack’s dark scowl at everyone as she took her place at his shoulder, warning them to silence. She needn't have bothered, because Kalsin didn’t give time for his mates to put up an argument.

Boots squelching in the mud, he hurried after his sister’s faint outline leading them into the dark.

After a long moment, his company followed.
