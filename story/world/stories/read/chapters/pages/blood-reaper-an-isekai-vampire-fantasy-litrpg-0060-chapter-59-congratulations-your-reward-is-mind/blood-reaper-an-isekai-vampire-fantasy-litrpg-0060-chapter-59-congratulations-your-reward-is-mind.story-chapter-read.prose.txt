Raw and unyielding agony threatened to swallow Ace whole.

Through the white-hot misery of the acid burns, Ace felt the alpha's massive life essence flooding into him. It surged through his veins with a raw, primal power that dwarfed anything he'd absorbed from the smaller ones. His body convulsed as it struggled to process the overwhelming influx, his cells regenerating and dying in chaotic cycles. The sensation of his skin knitting itself together competed with the persistent sizzle of acid still burrowing into his bones.

Beneath the layers of pain, he registered a disorienting euphoria as the alpha's power restructured something fundamental within him. Minor wounds sealed themselves shut with unnatural speed, only to dissolve again as the acid burned deeper into him. The dueling sensations—healing and destruction, all at once—sent conflicting signals through his body until pain and pleasure became indistinguishable.

His vision went red, and not from anger—from blood.

His blood.

Ace collapsed to his knees. His arms shook dangerously as he tried to hold himself upright, but he succumbed to the pain and fell forward onto the stone. His world tilted, reality blurring at the edges as he came dangerously close to losing consciousness.

His plan had worked. The team was safe, the threat eliminated—but at a cost.

He rolled onto his back, barely able to breathe, and stared up at the sky. Crimson clouds rolled past through breaks in the canopy. The leaves swayed with a surreal sense of peace as his body ate itself alive.

“Oh, my!” the System said cheerfully from somewhere far above him. “He’s not looking too good, is he?”

"Ace!"

Tara's voice pierced the fog of agony, seeming to come from miles away though he knew she must be closer. The rhythmic slam of her boots on the stone thundered through his skull, each impact sending fresh waves of pain radiating from his temples. His vision swam in and out of focus, catching only glimpses of her blurry form—a flash of blonde hair, the dark silhouette of her figure, the determined set of her jaw—nothing concrete enough to latch onto as consciousness threatened to slip away.

"Stay with me," she ordered, suddenly next to him.

"Trying," he slurred, the word barely recognizable even to his own ears. His tongue felt swollen, uncooperative, as if it belonged to someone else.

Ice-cold hands pressed against his ravaged chest, and he flinched in surprise. Her fingers moved with practiced efficiency, probing and assessing his wounds with the methodical touch of a professional. Each touch sent electric jolts of pain through his veins, but beneath it lay something else—a cooling sensation that spread outward from her fingertips as she channeled her magic.

The healing magic flowed into him like ice water through sun-baked earth—a blessed relief that battled against the acid's continuing destruction. Where her power touched him, the burning subsided, replaced by an intense tingling as dead tissue sloughed away and new cells rebuilt themselves at supernatural speed. He could feel individual muscle fibers reconnecting, the strange crawling sensation of skin reforming over raw bone, the deep ache of muscle and cartilage restructuring themselves.

"Breathe," Tara commanded, her palm now pressed against his sternum.

He sucked in a greedy breath, but the expected surge of relief didn’t hit him. His lungs expanded painfully, drawing in air that tasted like copper and smoke. Each shallow breath sent fresh waves of agony through his chest where his ribs were still knitting themselves back together.

Through half-closed eyes, he watched as crimson light pulsed from Tara's hands. Her magic illuminated her face from below with an otherworldly radiance. Sweat beaded on her forehead as she worked, her expression locked in fierce concentration. The veins in her forearms stood out in stark relief as she pushed her power to its limits, and her face paled.

Her own reserves were clearly depleting with each passing second.

“Don’t—” Ace grimaced as another flash of pain shot through him. “Don’t give me all of it.”

“Shut up,” she said softly, almost tenderly. “We’re nearly there. The acid is neutralized, but you've lost a lot of blood."

The hunger seized him without warning. One moment, he fought through the haze of pain; the next, his entire being narrowed to a single, overwhelming need that burned through his veins like fire. His fangs extended and sliced open his bottom lip. The coppery taste of his own blood triggered something feral within him.

His vision sharpened, and colors faded as the world transformed into heat signatures. And there, mere inches away, pulsed the most enticing beacon of all—Tara's throat, where her carotid artery hammered like a siren’s song.

Calling him toward her.

Inviting him to have a taste.

Ace's body tensed. His fingers curled into claws. The System's notifications in the corner of his eye blurred into meaningless symbols as his conscious thought retreated behind a single, overwhelming directive.

Feed.

Consume.

Kill.

As she leaned over him, he reached for her throat. It was instant. Uncontrolled. Hungry. His hand grasped her neck and yanked her forward. Her enticing scent filled his nostrils—sweat, fear, and beneath it all, the iron-rich promise of salvation. Of survival.

His mouth opened.

His fangs rested against her skin.

All he had to do was bite.

“Ace,” she said gently.

Tenderly.

“Stay with me,” she ordered.

Something in her voice—the familiar and commanding tone, perhaps, or simply the vibrations of her vocal chords that hummed through him—penetrated the red fog clouding his mind.

He snapped out of it, then. His fangs still pressed against her neck, and his hand still gripped her throat. She went still under his touch, and she swallowed hard. The movement in triggered his hunger again, but this time, he tamped it down before it could consume him.

Barely, but he still did it.

The beast howled inside him, demanding that he relinquish control and give it the blood it needed to survive. His body trembled with the sheer effort of his restraint. Every instinct screamed at him to bite, to drain, to consume her until nothing remained.

No.

Her voice.

Focus on her voice.

“Ace,” she said again, quieter this time.

He forced himself to breathe, and though his lungs no longer required air, it helped calm his racing thoughts.

A ragged inhale.

A shuddering exhale.

Repeat.

His grip on her neck tightened to the point where she gasped in pain, but he couldn't release her. If he let go, he would rip her throat out.

Though he still hadn’t let her go, she pressed her palms against his chest once again. Her healing magic continued its work, trickling through his system like cool water across burning coals. The agony that had consumed him gradually receded, transforming first into bearable pain, then into the dull throb of recovery.

With each passing second, the bloodlust loosened its hold on him—though it remained coiled inside, ready to strike again at the slightest provocation.

Bit by bit, Ace's vision slowly returned to normal. The red haze faded, but the hunger remained. It was a constant companion that waited patiently for his control to slip.

He refused to let it win.

Ace refused to hurt her, no matter the cost to himself.

His eyes shut, and when he tried to open them again, they wouldn’t obey. His body went limp, and he fell back to the ground. She let out a sigh of relief, soft as the breeze through the canopy. The hunger flared within him yet again, but this time, he was too weak to do anything about it.

Though he clung to consciousness, he lost control of everything else.

His life was in Tara’s hands, now. He could only hope he hadn’t pushed his body too far.

Time stretched and compressed. Reality fragmented into disconnected sensations. Pain ebbed and flowed. Voices drifted through the void. System notifications appeared and vanished before him like ghosts.

Steadily, slowly, breath by breath, his head cleared. He had no idea how long he laid there, chest heaving, his skull on the brink of shattering from the raw agony of it all. Tara’s magic seeped slowly into his bloodstream, carrying with it the soft rush of soothing calm that settled his surging dread.

When the worst of it finally subsided, he opened his eyes.

Sunlight blinded him. He winced in agony as a fresh surge of pain shot through his head and down his spine. Ace grimaced and rolled onto his side. He held his face in his hands, and it took a moment to register that he had been able to move at all.

What a relief.

Carefully, he sat upright. Tara held his back, giving him support he no longer needed, but he didn’t mind. She had gone above and beyond for him, and he wasn’t about to be an ungrateful asshole about it.

As his vision cleared, he squinted up to find Marcus and Olivia gathered around him. The System had disappeared, and the others watched him with pale and panicked faces. The forest clearing was a battlefield of dissolving slug corpses and acid pools with Ace at the center.

"Jesus, boss," Marcus whispered, his usual joking demeanor stripped away. His face was ashen, his eyes darting between Ace's wounds and the carnage surrounding them. "You look like you went swimming in a vat of industrial waste."

"Thanks," Ace said dryly through his blood-flecked lips. He tried to stand but found Tara's firm hand on his shoulder.

"Don't move," she ordered. "Your muscles are still reforming in some places."

He groaned and laid back down.

“Go,” he said, his voice almost inaudible. “Find any survivors. Kill them. Before they can regroup."

Olivia stood a few paces back, her dancer's posture rigid with tension. The Blood Dancer's eyes scanned the treeline methodically, and she rested her makeshift staff on one shoulder.

"There are four clusters left," she said quietly. Her usually elegant voice had a raw edge to it. "It looks like they didn’t go far.”

“Go get ‘em,” Ace said, his voice weaker than he had intended.

"I'm not leaving you," Tara snapped at Ace.

She pressed her palms against his back, and a fresh wave of cooling energy snaked through the worst of the wounds that hadn't yet been tended.

"We need to secure the area," Olivia insisted. “Tara, we could really use your help.”

"She's right," Marcus said. He caught Ace’s eye, and there was something new there—respect, perhaps, or a deeper understanding of what Ace was capable of. "You just focus on not dying. Again."

"I'd rather not make it a habit," Ace rasped, and a smile pulled painfully at the edge of his acid-burned mouth.

"I can track them," Olivia offered. "The acid leaves distinctive traces in the underbrush."

“Well, yeah,” Marcus said. “It burns everything it touches. Not exactly rocket science.”

Olivia cast the man an annoyed sidelong glare, but she didn’t otherwise speak.

"Point taken," Marcus said quietly. "Olivia and I can handle this. You just keep him alive, Tara."

The healer simply nodded, too focused on her work to say anything.

"Don't do anything stupid while we're gone," Marcus added with a wry grin. "One near-death experience per day is your limit."

"Noted," Ace replied weakly.

"We'll be back soon," Olivia promised, already moving toward the treeline with predatory grace.

Marcus gave a final nod. With that, the three of them disappeared into the forest and left Tara alone with Ace's broken body. Her healing abilities slowly closed the worst of his wounds and neutralized the remaining acid. As his body healed, so did his armor—though more slowly than he would’ve liked.

But he'd pushed himself far beyond safe limits. His Soul Meter had flatlined completely, and his HP hovered at critical levels.

"Help me up," he rasped, his throat raw from acid exposure.

Tara slipped her arm beneath his shoulders, her fingers digging into the still-tattered remains of his chestplate as she carefully lifted him to his feet. His body responded with a chorus of protests—his muscles trembling with fatigue, his bones creaking like ancient hinges, his newly regenerated skin stretching uncomfortably across his body. The world tilted and swayed around him, its colors bleeding together as his vision blurred and refocused over and over.

The simple act of standing left him dizzy.

When he finally steadied himself, he stared down at his feet until the world stopped spinning. It felt like ages before his head finally began to clear.

Fuck.

He wasn’t going to do that again.

Something shifted in the shadows at the edge of his vision. His eyes darted toward it, fueled by whatever new and unstable situational awareness he had absorbed from the alpha and its minions.

Something lurked at the edge of the treeline, its presence betrayed only by a subtle shifting of shadows in the underbrush. Eight crimson eyes glinted like smoldering embers in the darkness, tracking every one of his movements with cold, calculated intelligence. The creature's scorpion-like silhouette stretched nearly six feet in length, and its segmented exoskeleton absorbed what little light penetrated the canopy. Three barbed tails arched high above its back, their hooked stingers dripping fluid that sizzled against the forest floor and released tendrils of acrid vapor into the still air. The beast remained perfectly poised between stillness and action, a living weapon waiting for the perfect moment to strike.

Nope.

He was not in the mood.

Ace didn't hesitate. He reached for the Crimson Sword at his feet, his muscles screaming in protest as he hefted the blade.

"Ace, don't—" Tara began.

The sword left his hand with surprising accuracy despite his weakened state, spinning through the air before impaling the creature directly in the center of its clustered eyes. The scorpion shrieked, and the high-pitched scream sent pebbles skittering across the forest floor. It curled inward, its legs twitching as it died.

The Crimson Sword dissolved into shadow just after impact, returning to his Abyssal Armory as the creature collapsed.

Without a word, Ace slumped back against Tara. System notifications appeared in the corner of his fractured vision—level-up indicators, evolution updates, attribute changes. He ignored them all, too exhausted to care about the numbers.

All that mattered was that they had survived.

“Grinding might be off the table,” Ace said with a chuckle.

“No kidding,” Tara agreed with a glance around at the carnage. “Do me a favor, will you?”

“What’s that?”

“Never fucking do that again,” she ordered with a sidelong glare.

He grinned weakly and nodded. “Yes ma’am.”
