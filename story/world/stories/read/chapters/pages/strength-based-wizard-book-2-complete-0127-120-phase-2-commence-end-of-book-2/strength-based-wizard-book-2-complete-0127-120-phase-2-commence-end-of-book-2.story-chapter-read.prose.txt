Chapter 120

Phase 2 Commence

My body is a world of unimaginable pain. The pain I feel is hard to describe. It’s on a cellular level, like every nerve in me has been peeled open and scrubbed with salt and fire. My skin feels too tight, and I’m pretty sure I’m one gigantic bruise of a human being. My bones feel soft. My organs feel like they were shaken in a jar and poured back in wrong. There a soft, blinking flash in the bottom corner of my HUD.

Health: 0.

Stamina: 0.

That last spell didn’t just take everything I had. It destroyed me, multiple times over. I can feel the ghost of it—the way my vitality burned down to nothing, leaving my body a lifeless husk. Only for Reshma’s magic to stop me on the needle’s edge of true destruction, and rebuild me in an instant.

Over and over and over. Together, we recycled my body several times, pumping all of its energies into [Wizard’s Fist]. She had been right. Even if I used all of the vitality in my body, it wouldn’t have been enough. I needed her.

Reshma.

I can’t feel her now, though I had quickly lost all feeling in my body after the second cycle. Now, the pain was likely drowning out her touch.

I can’t hear her, either. But I’m pretty sure my ear drums are pure blisters at this point, and my ears are caked full of dry blood.

“We did it,” I whisper.  My lips move on their own, the words coming out as a cracked whisper through blood and ash. “Th… thank you.”

My knees buckle.

The crater around me wobbles and swims, the world blurring at the edges like my eyes are filled with sand. The floodlights from the drones and emergency crews descending from helicopters hovering overhead turn everything into harsh white shapes. Somewhere in the distance, sirens wail.

My HUD is a screaming mess—notifications stacked on notifications, text scrolling so fast it looks like static. I struggle, but minimize the messages, closing out of my interface. It takes almost more effort than I’m able to expend at the moment.

None of it matters. Not right now.

God damn, everything hurts.

I collapse to my knees.

The motion sends a fresh spike through my spine, and my vision whites out for a second. I sway forward like I’m going to faceplant into rubble and gore.

Hands grab my arm and chest, stopping me from falling face first into the ground.

“Bzzztt!...”

“Jelly?” I ask, the word slurring in my mouth. I spit. Or, at least try to. My mouth is still coated in blood.

I think I see the slime, out of the corner of my eye. He’s at my side, vibrating frantically, each vibration accompanied by a small, nervous bubbling sound.

The hands holding me are strong, firm.

“Easy,” a voice says. “You’re alright, Joseph.”

Jerome.

My head lolls slightly. I can’t lift it properly. My neck feels like it’s made of wet rope.

Jerome’s hand stays locked on me, anchoring me in place.

“Medivac is here,” he says. “Stay with me.”

I try to breathe. The air feels thick. Every inhale scrapes through my core like it’s hollowing me out. I manage to form one word.

“Jerome,” I whisper.

It comes out like a question and a thank you and an apology all at once.

Jerome squeezes my arm, just once.

It’s not a [Rule], but a command all the same: Stay.

But the darkness is already pouring in around the edges of my vision, warm and heavy, like a blanket being pulled over my face.

I don’t fight.

I can’t.

And unconsciousness seeps in and takes me like the tide.

I wake up to a sterile white light. My eyes crack open and immediately regret it. My skull feels like it’s been filled with wet sand and then shaken. My tongue is dry, but no longer coated in blood. My body is heavy in that weird way where you aren’t sure if you’re tired or if gravity just got stronger. I feel like I’ve slept for a week and also not at all.

My nose, which I’m pretty sure was broken by face first impact with concrete during the end of our first against the Avatar, is surprisingly greeted by the smells of a hospital—antiseptic, plastic, faint metal. I realize I’m laying in a bed, head slightly elevated.

I try to move and something tugs in my arm. An IV. I turn my head—slowly, like my neck is made of rusted hinges—and see bags hanging beside the bed. The liquid inside them is a glowing green, like radioactive Gatorade. A healing potion solution?

Other tubes run from my body to glass containers on a stand, where crystals float in suspension, hovering in the middle of the containers. They glow with clean white light, pulsing gently, steady as a heartbeat.

I swallow and it feels like swallowing gravel. Goodness, I would kill for a sip of water.

Someone shifts beside me.

A hand extends towards me, holding one of those handled hospital cups with the thick bendy straw in it. It’s Jerome. He’s seated in a, looking like he’s been awake for forty hours. Maybe he has been. He’s still wearing his ruined dress shirt, so clearly he hasn’t had time to switch into a new suit. Labonte stands beside him, arms crossed, eyes bright, face practically vibrating with contained excitement. The old man’s head looks like an unchewed piece of classic bubble gum.

“Thanks,” I muster, taking the cup. I place my chapped lips around the straw and take a cautious sip. The ice cold water is heavenly, washing down my dry throat and providing instant relief. I take another sip.

Jerome’s gaze locks onto mine.

“You’re awake,” he says. “And doing well, per the doctor who just stepped out.”

“Am I?” I croak.

Jerome’s mouth twitches. “Well, how do you feel?”

I stare at him for a second, then manage the only honest answer.

“Worse than you, apparently.”

Labonte snorts out a laugh.

Jerome doesn’t take the bait. “My mana channels will take a couple of weeks to fully recover,” he says. “But I got pretty lucky, all things considered.”

I blink, trying to process that, then remember the world outside this bed.

“Veronica?” I ask, voice rough.

Jerome nods. “In another room. Already fully recovered.”

Relief hits me so hard it’s almost nauseating.

Then the silence comes. The kind of silence that isn’t empty. No, it the silence that’s packed with something nobody wants to say first.

My mouth goes dry again.

“Matt…” I whisper. “And Reshma?”

Jerome’s eyes don’t flinch.

He shakes his head once.

And the world shifts.

It’s like my brain refuses to accept it for a full second—like it’s waiting for him to add but they’ll be okay or but we got them stable or but they’re in surgery. But he doesn’t.

The truth settles over me.

Matt isn’t all that surprising. I watched him splatter across the side of a building, after all. Still, I held out hope that Reshma and other powerful healers may have been able to get to him and pull off a miracle.

But Reshma…

She had burned herself away to fuel my spell. I can see it now when I close my eyes—golden light streaming from her body, motes lifting like fireflies and disappearing into me. Her hands on my back. Her voice telling me not to look back.

My chest tightens. Guilt rises in my throat like bile. Grief follows, deep and heavy.

Reshma had given everything. Everything.

If I was stronger, I could’ve been the one to make the sacrifice.

But I wasn’t. I was too weak. And so she had to do it.

My eyes sting. I blink hard, but it doesn’t help.

Labonte steps forward into the silence, smile wide across his pink face. I can’t stand to see his smile in this moment.

“They will be remembered as heroes. True heroes. But,” he says brightly, “I must say congratulations, Joseph.”

I stare at him, not understanding.

He presses on, voice full of pride. “You saved the day. And you put Harvest Guild on the map!”

I frown, confused even through the haze. “What?”

Labonte spreads his hands as if presenting a product launch. “The entire fight has been playing on every news channel, streaming service, and across social media. For hours now. And it’s only the raw footage, too. Apparently Wolf will be releasing the edited material in a couple of days.”

He grins wide enough to be indecent. “Muscle Mage saves the day! That’s what they’re saying!”

I flinch at the phrase like it’s a slap.

Hearing it like that—like a headline, like a meme—makes my skin crawl. I don’t feel like a hero. I feel like a burned-out battery that happened to explode at the right time.

Labonte’s eyes sparkle. “Everyone knows your name now.”

I swallow, throat tight.

Labonte leans closer, voice lowering like he’s sharing a financial tip.

“And now,” he says, “you can opt out of the Game and rest on your laurels as a wealthy, powerful man. An ending fit for a hero, I say.”

My eyebrows knit.

“Opt out?” I repeat.

Labonte blinks, then looks at Jerome like Jerome is supposed to translate.

Jerome sighs quietly and stands.

“He’s been unconscious, Mr. Labonte,” Jerome says, tone gentle but firm. He turns to me. “Joseph. You should check your System notifications.”

I stare at him, heart starting to beat faster again. I take another sip of my water.

Jerome reaches out and touches Labonte’s shoulder, guiding him toward the door.

“We’ll leave you to rest,” he says.

Labonte practically bounces, still giddy. “Another congratulations!” he exclaims, as if we’re at a graduation party and not in a hospital after two people died saving my life.

He does a little excited cheer—an actual little yes!—as Jerome ushers him out.

The door closes. The room quiets, save for the steady beep of the machine monitoring my heart rate, and the hum of the healing crystals.

Sighing, I place down my cup of water on the bedside tray, and settle into the elevated bed. I summon my System interface. The interface slides into view like it’s been waiting patiently for me to wake up so it can share all of its excited news with me. A cluster of unread messages blinks in the corner of my vision, bright and insistent. Dozens of them.

I mentally select the unread messages, and patiently sit there as one after another opens.

Ping!

You have defeated True Avatar of Chernobog, Level 175!

Partial credit awarded to… Jelly Boy!

. . .

Jelly Boy left battle prior to completion. Partial credit voided.

Partial credit awarded to… Jerome Williams!

Partial credit awarded to… Matthew Bruck!

. . .

Matthew Bruck fell in battle. Partial credit voided.

Partial credit awarded to… Reshma Murmu!

Reshma Murmur fell in battle. Partial credit voices.

Level 30 increased to… Level 40!

Level Forty. That number sits in my head like a funeral bell’s tone. Heavy and significant. I should be excited. It’s the most levels I’ve ever climbed in a single instance. But it doesn’t feel like a trophy. It feels like a scar. I close out the System Message and move on to the next series of windows cluttering my interface.

Ping!

[Bludgeoning Resistance] upgraded from Tier 2 to Tier 3!

[Piercing Resistance] upgraded from Tier 2 to Tier 3!

[Slashing Resistance] upgraded from Tier 2 to Tier 3!

NEW SKILL: You have gained [Fire Resistance] (Passive)!

[Fire Resistance] is currently Tier 2.

NEW SKILL: You have gained [Psionic Resistance] (Passive)!

[Psionic Resistance] is currently Beginner.

I still feel numb. I close out these windows without another thought.

Ping!

20 Stat Points Currently Unallocated. Assign Stat Points?

FLEXIBLE CASTING: You have 1 Modified Spellcasting available. Please select one Spell to see available options.

Right. I was probably due for another [Flexible Casting] trait upgrade. I don’t touch it yet. My brain is too foggy to make permanent decisions. I’ll need to carefully consider what I do with it.

As for my unallocated stat points? I dump them all into Strength, less afraid at wrecking my body thanks to my decently high Constitution, and the use of [Muscle Mastery].

Ping!

QUEST UPDATE: THE NETHER APPROACHES

You have successfully defeated the True Avatar of Chernobog and prevented the Dimensional Breach!

Reward: ‘Remember the Fallen’ (x1), New Ability: [Cast Fist]

Remember the Fallen. The words punch me in the gut. It’s like the System knows where the knife is and decided to twist it even deeper.

Surprisingly, it’s apparently an item. When I close out of the window, I feel something settle into my Inventory, like an additional mental weight that immediately fades into the background.

Ping!

New Ability Gained!

[Cast Fist] (Grandmaster)

Description: Sometimes spellcasting isn’t enough! You are no stranger to getting up close and personal. When your spellcasting fails you, you can solve problems instead with violence and stubbornness. Tackle all obstacles head-on and fist-first! You may combine [No Pain, No Gain] and [Muscle Mastery] into a single, all-out punch. This Ability may be applied once per day.

Note: Per the rules of [No Pain, No Gain], use of this Ability comes with a high cost of 75% of your Maximum Stamina and 60% of your Maximum Health.

Note: You are incapable of consuming any Health or Stamina potion for 15 minutes after applying this Ability.

Note: [Cast Fist] may be stored within your body for up to twenty-four hours after applying this Ability.

A single, all-out punch? I stare at the words for a moment longer, then glance at my bandaged arms, the IV tubes, the glowing crystals.

“Yeah,” I mutter, voice hoarse. “That sounds like me.”

The System keeps blinking politely, waiting for me to finish reacting. There’s one notification left. I close out the Ability description, and let the System usher in the final message.

Ping!

Congratulations!

You have successfully completed Phase 1 of the God Game!

It is with great enthusiasm that I invite you to the next stage of the God Game.

. . .

Continue?

“Er… Sure,” I mumble, before mentally selecting yes.

. . .

STAGE ONE: TUTORIAL – CLASS SELECTION (COMPLETE)

STAGE TWO: THE BLESSED FEW

In this second phase of the God Game, Participants will endeavor to complete three Objectives in order to gain access to the third and final stage of this divine contest.

Before proceeding, all Participants will have the option to [Opt-in] or [Opt-out] of the God Game. All Participants shall be provided five days to make a selection. If a Participant fails to make a selection, they will automatically be Opted out of the God Game.

[OPT-IN]: If a Participant selects [Opt-in], the Participant will proceed to Stage Two of the God Game. All Objectives and Consequences of Stage Two shall apply.

[OPT-OUT]: If a Participant selects [Opt-out], the Participant will be eliminated from the God Game.

Elimination Type: Removal.

All Participants eliminated in this manner will have their Levels permanently locked. This effect will only impact System User Levels, and will not have any impact on Skill or Ability levels or tiers.

All Participants eliminated in this manner will no longer be able to access Gates.

OBJECTIVES OF STAGE TWO:

1: Receive a [Blessing] from a God.

Note: Gods will have one month from the commencement of this phase of the God Game to select their champions and bestow a [Blessing]. If a Participant is selected by more than one God, the Participant may select which [Blessing] to receive.

My skin prickles. I don’t like the sound of this objective. Particularly because it sounds like it’s largely out of our hands. I think back to the arrival of the System, and my meeting with that man with the fox-like smile. Yeah, I don’t like the sound of this at all.

2: Obtain a Silver or Gold Gate Ticket.

Again, no further details on how one obtains a Silver or Gold Gate Ticket.

3: Use the Silver or Gold Gate Ticket to obtain the Divine Tear.

Note: The Divine Tear is a Mythical Item. There are only 10 Divine Tears available. A Divine Tear may be shared by a Party. A Party may not control more than a single Divine Tear.

Only ten, I think. Only ten Parties will advance to the third and final phase of the System’s game.

It’s a terrifying thought.

CONSEQUENCES FOR FAILING TO COMPLETE OBJECTIVES:

Elimination from the God Game

Elimination Type: Culling

TIME REAMINING IN STAGE TWO: 59 Days, 18 Hours, 14 Minutes.

Thank you for participating in this Cycle’s Game. May Creation watch over all Participants and bring them success!

And suddenly the “merciful” opt-out makes a different kind of sense. If the second phase of the Game is a slaughterhouse, then the off-ramp becomes extremely tempting. Why wouldn’t everyone take the [Opt-out] option?

I stare at the words until my eyes blur. It’s all too much for me right now. I close out of the window, and lay my head back, letting my eyes close.

Then, my [Perception] flares. A soft, specific ping in my mind—someone entering my room. I open my eyes and the hospital room comes back into full focus.

The door opens.

Mom and Liv step inside.

Liv is holding Jelly Boy in her arms like a stuffed animal with opinions on this week’s episode of Summer House. The slime is quiet, though—strangely quiet—his little body tucked against her chest, only vibrating faintly like he knows he should behave. Perhaps he’s feeling bummed about missing out on the experience from defeating the Avatar?

My mom looks like an absolute wreck. Her hair is pulled back like she did it with shaking hands. Her face is pale, and the lines around her mouth and eyes seem deeper than I’ve ever noticed before. Her eyes are red-rimmed, swollen, like she’s been crying for hours and then crying about the fact she’s been crying.

Of course, she probably didn’t like seeing her son in a life-and-death situation. And I bet seeing me in a hospital bed, hooked up to all these machines didn’t help any. I swallow hard, throat suddenly tight, and force my voice to work.

“Hey,” I say, trying to sound normal. “Hey, you guys.”

Liv steps closer first, hugging Jelly Boy tighter. She’s trying to smile, but I can tell immediately something is tense and off in her face. Her jaw is tight. Her eyes don’t quite settle. She’s holding herself together with discipline, and that alone scares me because Liv is usually the one who can hold herself together.

“Hi,” she says, voice steady but too careful. “How’re you feeling?”

“I’m… not bad,” I say, attempting a weak grin. “Which is a pretty big win lately.”

Jelly Boy lets out a small, sympathetic blorp, like he agrees.

Mom comes up behind Liv, hands clasped in front of her like she doesn’t know what to do with them. She looks at me and her mouth trembles.

I feel my chest squeeze.

“Mom,” I say gently. “Hey. Hey hey hey.” I reach a hand toward her, IV tugging slightly. “I’m okay. I’m okay.”

She takes one more step and her eyes fill completely.

Her lips part and a sound comes out, half breath, half sob, as if her body forgot how to speak and remembered only how to break.

I realize something is off. Something in the back of my mind notices that something is wrong. Something… No, someone, is missing. My heart skips—hard—like it stumbles over its own beat. I choke on air.

“Joseph,” she chokes, voice cracking. “It’s your father.”

A cold stone sinks into the pit of my stomach. The words hit me like the floor dropping out from under my hospital cot.

And suddenly the God Game and all of the System bullshit falls away like it never mattered. Because something so ordinary cuts deeper than any demon ever could.
