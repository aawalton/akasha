The Mages blinked, then bowed slightly and opened the gate. Rylan let out a sigh as he walked through them, circulating his mana out of habit. He was now in the family’s garden. The red and orange flowers fluttered in the breeze under the moonlight, creating the illusion that the garden was on fire. It was a welcome change from the fires of war in Roland’s memories. For a few seconds, he stopped in place, merely looking at the garden.

Calm down.

Keeping one’s cool was one of the most important traits of a swordsman. One needed to always be capable of precise judgement and choices. Rylan started to take the deep, rhythmic breaths that Roland’s memories had taught him. Serenity cleared the haze created by rage.

I can’t give Evenon anything he asks for.

It wasn’t just because he didn’t have access to something worth 1,000 gold coins or because he didn’t want to ruin thirty more people. It was a matter of principle. He couldn’t listen to the demands of a gang boss used to destroying others’ lives for his benefit. He wasn’t a hero of legend or a white knight in shining armor; Roland had killed far too many for that, while Rylan had made too many mistakes. However, Evenon stood right in the path to regain the trust of his family.

For his own goals, he would get the staff, one way or another, even if it meant dismantling everything Evenon had built. Since he didn’t want to comply with the man’s demands, he would need to explore his second plan.

In front of the entrance to the house proper was a frowning Sarah. As he approached her, she eyed him up and down. Rylan flashed a bitter smile.

“This was necessary.”

She said nothing, merely staring at him. Had he gone back to square one in her mind? With a sigh, he walked past her. She followed him inside.

“I need a bath.”

“Yes, young master.”

They walked in silence for a while.

“Are you disappointed, Sarah?”

“It is not my place to judge your actions, my lord.”

“I don’t care about that. Answer me.”

She hesitated.

“…I thought you were making honest progress, but now I’m afraid I was mistaken.”

“I see. Do you want me to explain why I did what I did?”

“No.”

Rylan didn’t try to defend himself further. He looked at the full moon through the corridor’s windows.

“The moon is beautiful tonight, isn’t it?”

“Yes, young master.”

They walked to his room.

Rylan woke up to the sound of birds chirping outside. He sat up with a sigh.

I used to never need to sleep.

Roland’s Aura could sustain him for years without true sleep. Some rest was enough. As Rylan, he spent most of the time high as a kite, including the effects of stimulant drugs, so he only slept five hours per night. From Roland’s perspective, it was still an enormous amount of time.

He wasn’t particularly fond of sleeping. It was one of the states where one was most defenseless. Swordsmen routinely underwent training as apprentices to keep at least a sliver of awareness even when sleeping; it was incredibly difficult and straining to truly achieve such a state. It was different from being a light sleeper, as it meant being aware of one’s surroundings even when deeply asleep. Many outright failed and grew stronger without this trait. Roland had been one of the few who had succeeded. However, Rylan hadn’t been able to mimic this trait yet.

I’m getting closer, though.

So long as he specifically trained it a bit every night, he should be able to achieve it in the near future. As usual, he didn’t bother putting on a shirt and walked to the middle of the room. The first training session of the day officially started when he woke up. Without wasting any time, he started doing push-ups. Once his arms got tired, he switched to another exercise. The main goal was to develop his whole body without too much strain, especially considering that he would train with the soldiers soon afterward.

Timing his resting windows and the intensity of his movements, Rylan continued. It was only after some time that he stopped and stood up, letting out a breath and wiping the sweat on his forehead.

“Status Window.”

A translucent blue screen appeared in front of him.

Status Window

Level: 8

Novice Mage; Wastrel; Good-for-Nothing; Reincarnator.

His stats had increased a bit. Improving oneself without leveling up was a slow process, but Roland’s experience allowed him to train exactly what he needed at each point in time. His progression speed could only be described as “frightening,” but he still wasn’t satisfied. There was a long way before he reached Roland’s level. He stared at his Trait with distaste.

Still unchanged.

Traits weren’t mere adjectives or titles. They were representative of one’s inner self. Rylan’s entire existence was summarized as “Weak-willed.” This Trait, in turn, affected his results. Whenever he depended on his willpower, this Trait would undermine his efforts. That was why others strived to get rid of negative Traits; for instance, Rylan could manage to acquire the “Hard-working” Trait after some time keeping up with the training, which would in turn help him keep working hard. Negative Traits were insidious poison, while positive ones could grant one a sizable boost and create a positive feedback loop.

So why hasn’t it changed?

He had acquired Roland’s memories and been fundamentally changed by the experience, beyond even the memories. He couldn’t be described as the same person he was before, so why was his Trait still the same?

My past life’s Trait was Complete Prodigy.

To think he would be stuck with a “Weak-willed” Trait. He sighed. This needed to change. He would work himself to the bone if necessary. He looked at his other stats, rubbing his chin. The best course of action would be to improve his stats through exercise and training before spending Free Points on them. The higher his stats before he started leveling up, the more Points he would save. At the same time, leveling up would grant him stats based on his Class.

“Since my Class is a Mage, leveling up won’t change my physical stats except for Endurance… It’s safe to start hunting and saving up the Free Points.”

As a swordsman, he depended mostly on his physical stats, so leveling up wouldn’t hinder him. At the same time, he needed to grasp his abilities in true combat. His main goal was to train his body for a month. It was likely the most amount of time he had bought himself before Evenon got suspicious.

Evenon will probably realize something is wrong by then.

Since the man had kept the staff instead of simply reselling it, Rylan could assume that the staff would remain in Evenon’s possession. However, if it were moved somewhere else, he would be stuck at square one again. It would be best to get the heirloom back as fast as possible. To do that, he needed power. It was different from the power he had acquired as Roland. There was purpose.

Rylan let out a breath and grabbed the sword that had spent the night by his side on the bed. It slid against its scabbard as he pulled it out. He walked to the middle of the room once more, holding the weapon’s hilt firmly, then ran his fingers on the flat of the blade.

I need to know if this body can bear the Stormcaller Sword Style.

It was the most flawless beginner swordsmanship style that Roland had created by joining hundreds of different styles, but it had the sole drawback of being extremely demanding on the body. It was imperative to see if his current self could withstand it. If not, he would need to turn to other, weaker styles.

He got into the Stormcaller Sword Style’s most ideal stance. Then, he started. Slashes flowed seamlessly into stabs. Even though the space within the bedroom was limited, Rylan utilized it perfectly as he pictured an enemy swordsman. It wasn’t difficult, but that was a matter of course. Roland had fought and trained for too long for him to struggle with coming up with an enemy’s actions.

He kept a close watch on his body’s condition as the seconds ticked by. It took less than fifteen seconds for him to feel like he was approaching his limit. Ten seconds after that, his entire body shook. His muscles screamed at him to stop and his heart galloped in his chest. Sweat flowed down his body like rain. However, he kept perfect control of his breathing. After a few more seconds, he stopped and sighed. Without hesitation, he sat on the ground, laying the sword on his lap.

Goddamnit.

Roland’s days as a novice had been harsh and unforgiving, but he hadn’t felt as hopeless as Rylan’s current self. To think that he wasn’t able to bear even thirty seconds of using the Stormcaller Sword Style. It was honestly laughable. Rylan let out another breath and stroked the sword’s blade as if he were touching a lover.

Still, it doesn’t mean all is lost.

There were countless sword styles in Roland’s memories. Even if he couldn’t properly utilize the Stormcaller Sword Style, he could rely on other, simpler styles. They would do the job while he strengthened his body and tried to adapt the Stormcaller Sword Style to his current self. Finally, he looked at the System notifications floating in front of him with a smile.

[Strength has increased by 1.]

[Body has increased by 1.]

[Endurance has increased by 1.]

No amount of training was wasted. So long as he could keep this up, he would keep making progress.

He stood up, dried his body, and got dressed. This time, the clothes were more proper for training. He had asked Sarah to deliver them to his room. He looked through the window as he put the sword in the scabbard on his way. It was still dark outside. He had woken up before sunrise.

She must already be waiting for me outside.

He made sure he was ready and opened the door. As expected, Sarah was sitting on a stool next to the bedroom’s door. She stood up with an indifferent expression.

“Good morning, young master.”

He flashed her a bitter smile. She had almost gone back to her old ways of treating him after his arrival last night.

“…Good morning, Sarah.”

He gestured for her to come in. She grabbed the wand on her waist and drew a few symbols in the air. The drawn shape glowed. It meant that soon, someone would come and deliver his breakfast. She then entered the bedroom as he pointed at the chair.

“Feel free to have a seat.”

She sat down while he grabbed the stool, brought it inside, and then sat on the edge of the bed. Together, they waited. Rylan broke the silence with a question.

“Have you reported back to my father yet?”

“He asked me to only do so after watching you for a while, my lord.”

He lay on the bed, his arms spread out.

“What are you going to say?”

She hesitated. He immediately waved at her.

“No, never mind. That wasn’t an order.”

Silence reigned once more. After a few seconds, she spoke.

“…You’re different, young master. But I am not sure to what extent just yet.”

He nodded, closing his eyes.

“That’s fine. I’ll show you. I’ll show everyone.”

At that moment, a knock on the door resounded. Sarah walked up to the door and opened it, then came back inside holding a tray of food. She put it on the desk as Rylan stood up.

Looks right.

The cooks were doing a good job of keeping up with his diet. He smiled and pointed at a smaller dish, near the edge of the tray.

“That’s yours.”

For a brief moment, he thought that he’d seen the shadow of a smile cross Sarah’s face. Then, it disappeared, as if it were never there. Rylan blinked. With a smile of his own, he brought the stool to his side and sat down on the chair. She sat down on the stool. They ate in silence. Soon, both of them stood up. It was time to head to the soldiers’ training grounds.

Rylan walked while relentlessly circulating his mana. He preferred to focus on it during the afternoon or evening, but that didn’t mean that he wouldn’t train it in the morning. Soon, the duo reached their destination. The soldiers already there stood up and bowed, speaking.

“Good morning, young master.”

“It’s good to see you here again, my lord.”

Rylan smiled and nodded at them.

“Good morning.”

He looked at Jack’s approaching figure as he let Sarah walk off to sit on the usual bench. The soldier greeted him and he responded. Then, he spoke.

Jack seemed to let out a breath of relief.

Truthfully, Rylan didn’t have much experience leading a group either. Roland had trained and fought alone. That was why the target choice would be crucial for this endeavor; he needed an enemy that he could defend the others against if the need arose. They couldn’t use Aura, nor did they know how to use mana to strengthen themselves. It was still too early for him to teach them knowledge that important. As such, they needed a weak opponent.

“Don’t worry. You guys will be fine.”

Rylan tried to go for as long as possible without needing to resort to his mana empowerment. It was the way he preferred to train. As usual, he was one of the first to need to stop. Jack took it as a signal to move on. The group went back to the training grounds and started weapons training. First came training with spears. The soldiers completed it faithfully under Rylan’s watchful eyes, but there was an atmosphere of unrest. It was as if they were waiting for something. Jack spoke.
