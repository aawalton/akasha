A few days earlier…

Glados had a plan. And by plan, she had a gambit. One that she figured had almost no chance of working, but with Aegortin eager to get rid of her, despite all her tireless work, she reached one conclusion:

They’d burn with the rest.

Which was why she was currently sitting in a secure room within the Adventurer’s Guild, waiting patiently with that irritating nullifier bracelet on her wrist. Soon enough, her request for legal counsel would be here.

On the surface, it was all an effort to write down a proposal for a plea deal, with an admission of guilt to get a reduced sentence. In exchange, she would name co-conspirators, and any detailed information she could give of Aegortin’s obsessions with conquest, and their growing internal strife.

Their ships sailing up the coast were efforts to move the military far away from a growing dissent. The rot could no longer be contained. And, as the leadership likely surmised, the military would be unwilling to keep harming its own citizens.

Playing the angle of a triple agent would be enough to keep her from being walked up to a gallows. Treason was the only crime left on the books where lethal punishment was allowed, and solved her short-term problem.

And, would lead to her long-term solution: find that Aurelium that Karlin had conveniently stolen back, somewhere in Underlune, just as planned.

The door opened, and a woman with all the markings of a river folk with long, sleek brown fur, a corporate-looking button vest and blouse, and dress pants walked in the door. A large beaver tail trailed behind her, and she cautiously closed the door and walked to the table with a single wooden chair opposite Glados. She set down a stack of papers clipped together and other folders of more material.

“Glados Hennaway?” The woman asked and smiled politely, her prominent beaver teeth on display. She spoke with a hint of an accent from the Bardathi river plains. Interesting.

Glados played it loose. “Rachel. A pleasure, as always. Is this room warded against prying eyes and ears?”

The riverkin glanced around, and a shimmer of light in the form of a halo wrapped around her head, if for just a moment. She peered around again, then nodded. “No one is listening. My detection ability isn't perfect, but…I do owe you a favor.”

“Good. Please give me the situation as it stands. Will Rikkard hear out a plea deal?”

“Rikkard isn't the court, and you know that. He has gone out of his way to avoid sticking his nose in it. Barry is still under house arrest, and he's trying to claim you duped him and put a spell on him. Which you probably did.”

“Minor compulsion to nudge him in a few mostly innocent ways. But who's counting?” Glados leaned in, talking in a whisper. “My only play is getting out of here and taking the unaccounted Aurelium. Then kill Swiftheart and assume the mantle of a deity.”

“Yeah, that plan won't work. Because it's insane,” Rachel pointed out. She tapped a set of papers on the table, with angry red letters written over the printed text. “They'll reject your plea. You need to give them more. Give me a sob story, give me threats of coercion, brainwashing, anything! Or give up the gold, if you know where it is. That alone might save your neck!”

“I need the gold for other purposes." She didn't explain why, as it was one less risk.

Rachel clicked her teeth tightly. “You do know where it is, don't you? Wait, who am I kidding? Why would I help you? Glados, you will forever have a target on your back if you do this. Tell me why you need this gold so badly. And I doubt it's just money."

“I have a theory on what is needed to concentrate the essence of the Aurelium back to its true form: that of the fallen Feo’thari.” She rubbed at the nullifying bracer. But she could still hear the echo of a recurring dream.

No. A nightmare ever-recurring. One she couldn’t get rid of, ever since this damn bracer was placed on her.

The Outsiders will return…

Claim your divinity. Claim it from the goddess who abandoned her world.

By any means.

Rachel sighed before leaning in, fingers steepled together. “I need something to give them. The gold is the one that will cause the least fuss and is immediately helpful.”

How is Karlin getting the reserves at the bottom of the central treasury? A break-in would not be possible. Or…

Glados frowned, while another thought came to mind. Karlin's latest shipments. Pyrogel and its derivative products. He’d begun shipping them through his less-than-reliable partner, the disgraced Santino, before his scheme fell apart, and Varith was deposed.

Then, the idea hit her.

It was insane. No one would ever expect it. Which meant no one would see it coming but her. “Get me a one-on-one meeting with our mutual associate. I think he can help me get out of here. I have a plan.”

“And the gold?”

“Underlune. Technically speaking, I know where the gold is now. And better yet…I know where it's going,” she said with a smug smile. “Get me the otter’s lawyer.”

Rachel raised a brow. “If you’re suggesting what I think you are…you know they’ll figure it out, then the whole city will be after you. Presumably, he may not be willing to help, considering the gamble he’ll be taking. You’ll buy yourself maybe–”

“Just get him inside, and I’ll handle the rest of the persuasion,” she said calmly, rubbing her hands together gently.

Finally. My big break.

“Let me get this straight. You want me to take your place.”

“Les, you’ve done this service for many.” Glados sat across from the otterfolk, with slicked back hair and a dark velvet vest and collared shirt. “You can do this one for me. For favors owed.”

“No ‘favor’ is worth putting myself in jail for you, my dear,” he said with a resigned sigh. “You know they could still, in theory, execute you by burning you at the stake, or hanging you? Treason is probably the only crime in the books of Fiefdala that carries capital punishment; the rest are all permanent prison stays or rehabilitation programs.”

She scoffed at that. “Really, with the amount of classes and abilities that offer fire protection, they still use that one? How barbaric.”

“I said in the books, it's not practical,” he snapped. “Look, I can assume a form for about five minutes. Even yours. You’re not fooling anyone with that suppressor bracer on your wrist, and unless you plan on cutting off your wrist–which even I would be impressed by–you’re not getting out of here.”

She folded her arms, looking at him with a raised brow. “Did you find the thing I was looking for?”

“You mean, Swiftheart’s whereabouts?” he scoffed and pulled out a folder from his satchel, and flipped through pages. “At the shop, obviously. Though I’m led to believe she is currently some kind of ‘favor’ for my client’s father. Someone I barely have any influence on, mind you.”

“Skip to the point. Where is she?”

“Underlune. Likely looking for a chance to find the gold, and give Karlin what he deserves: a beating with that comically large hammer of hers.” He leaned back in his chair, letting out a sound of delight. “Oh, the coin I’d pay to see that play out.”

Glados ignored his posturing. She knew her timing window was exceedingly limited. That dragon conman had a secure fallback place not that far from the outskirts of Fiefdala, or just under it, as it were. But she didn’t need to know where the dragon was–she just needed to pinpoint the spot where his likely heist was taking place.

All she had to do was map out a spot underneath the treasury, and she was more or less there. “Les, you do this for me, and I can guarantee you that you never see a jail cell for more than a day of minor inconvenience. Then, when I’m all done, you’ll be pushed to the top of everyone’s client list. The rich ones,” she added with a slight pursing of her lips.

“You still have that nullifier on your wrist. That will be a problem. And I can’t use mine unless I take it off, which will set off red flags.”

She pulled gently at a lock of hair, and a few silvery threads of arcane wiring wound around her hand gently, then showed him a single vial of alchemical flash fluid she’d managed to grab from one of the guards when one detainee in the Guild caused a well-timed commotion. “One blast of magical disruption, isolated to this room. We play the old switcheroo, sixty seconds of blackout. What they will see is one lawyer leaving the room, and one prisoner transported back to their cell, same as the people walking in. Easy enough.”

“Except you’ll have about twelve hours before my ability wears off and people will, you know, notice. Even then, though I know your mannerisms enough, I can still fail the sniff test if people ask me too many questions.”

He tapped the bracer with one claw. "These can’t be that easily fooled…can they?”

“I had time to study it. I learned enough. You think I got this far with just pretty looks?” She said snidely, narrowing her eyes. “These bracers prevent a user from using their abilities. It has no impact on negating external magical sources. Which means after it reactivates in sixty seconds–about how long this little magical disruption will last–any polymorph effect on me will persist. It does not affect abilities previously used. It only prevents activating new ones. A slight oversight, but not one many people know about.”

The sleek-looking lawyer tapped a claw patiently on the table. “I’m going to say you assaulted me and switched us involuntarily. For when someone inevitably asks when the effect wears off.” She frowned, but waited for him to continue. “Assuming there’s gold left out of this insane idea of yours…I want twenty percent. Santino would murder me if I negotiated for any less. And favorable business opportunities.”

“Ten percent.”

“Twenty.”

She pursed her lips. He was not budging on this one. “Fifteen.”

“Twenty.”

She rolled her eyes; if this went well, likely all the Aurelium would be consumed. “Fine. Twenty.”

“Now extend your hand. I presume we will get one shot at this. You’ll also need my clothes to play the role, because those don’t transform, either."

She sighed softly. There was a chance the room was being surveilled, but that was a risk she’d have to take, regardless. “Turn when I’m not decent, and I’ll strangle you with my bare hands.”

He let out a huff. “I have standards to adhere to. Prison garb is not how I planned my day, either. But in this instance, I’m willing to take a slight risk for a substantial return on investment.”

One awkward moment later, Glados was wearing the sleek lawyer’s suit. He was wearing a prison uniform that looked rather uncomfortable. “I should make this twenty-five percent,” he grumbled. “Remember, if this doesn’t work, I’ll say I was coerced under duress.”

“It will work. On my go.” She took the small cylinder and threaded the wire to deliver a short-term magical dispel, and wired it to the bracer. “From three…two…”

“...One.”

Triggering the small amount of magic dispelling material wasn’t as dramatic flash of energy as one might expect. Still, the mild blast of light left her temporarily seeing bright spots in her vision.

More importantly, the bracers identifying them were dead. She clicked hers open without a single warning chirp going off. Les did the same, rubbing at the discomfort of the bracer. “Fifty seconds. Activating my form swap. Guess this one won’t be a secret I’m keeping much longer

Thirty seconds later, she looked like a furry freak who dabbed far too much oil to slick back his fur, and a matching outfit. Meanwhile, her radiant self stared back at her. “I am filled with such self-loathing,” he muttered. “Fifteen seconds by the way.”

They switched bracers, and he sat in her position at the table. She tapped on the relay in the room, clearing her throat. All natural. I’ve had them fooled for years. I can fool them for another few minutes. “I’ve finished with my client. I’ll need more time to draft up some paperwork regarding the terms of the plea deal, along with information regarding certain concessions and sensitive information that will draw the ire of…many interested parties.”

Glados heard the bracer beep and came back to life with a soft green glow. No warning went off, and no magical drain occurred. The same could be said of Les, serving as her body double. No warnings went off, and no magic instantly dispelled.

It worked. All because of a single oversight in what should have been a well-executed design.

The relay clicked nearby. “We’ll be there shortly. Please remind your client that the patience of the crown’s justice department–and the court–is exceedingly limited.”

She clicked the relay button, smiling evilly. “I will.”

Ten minutes later, she signed out of the Guild, briefcase in hand.

As long as Les didn’t screw this up, she had twelve hours to figure out where Karlin was pulling off his heist to top all heists in Fiefdala.

Luckily, she knew where to start.
