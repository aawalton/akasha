Korvakh, our mysterious guide, gestured silently towards the innocuous stairs in the center of the Heartspire. There was something oddly…mundane about their appearance. Everything about the reality of the Heartspire was contrary to the rest of Karag Korvak, from what we had seen of it.

There was a lot I had expected, once we finally managed to break into the heart of Korvak control. I’d expected all of the depredations of these maddwarves to be turned up to eleven. I’d expected something similar to a full-on charnel house, where their excesses were on full display. Corpses strewn around without care, blood thick enough to wade through, flesh adorning every last surface like it did the pulsating spires of the Hold proper.

I hadn’t expected something almost clinical. Stark, even. It was clean in here, to the point that I think someone was deliberately maintaining it. It was also strangely barren, both materially and structurally. Literally, I think the only space within the spire itself was the bare hallway from the entrance that Bleddyn and I had destroyed, and the gigantic hollow that stretched all the way up to the distant point of the tower, upon which what I believed to be Nehushtan was impaled.

And right in the middle of it all were the stairs.

“Please, follow me,” Korvakh said quietly. All the same, their voice echoed loudly throughout the entire interior of the Heartspire. The acoustics in here were insane, reflecting back at us any sound, even our footsteps, a hundredfold. I almost wanted to wince at just those three words repeating over and over.

Follow…follow…

Follow.

My gaze flickered over to my fellow commanders, each of whom had marched with me at the head of our column. I could tell they were also uncomfortable, but I was grateful they were still choosing to follow me.

They trusted my judgment, and my judgment was telling me that this was still our best chance of fulfilling our goals.

I nodded to our guide and did as he bade.

For now.

And we stepped foot onto the staircase.

………………………………

We descended far, and we descended long, following in the footsteps of Korvakh the Steward. The Dwarf seemingly felt no fear in presenting his back to us, as he, it, they, proceeded down the surprisingly wide stairs. They were more than large enough to accommodate the entirety of our forces as we descended along them, shoulder to shoulder. Oddly, there was even a smooth, flattened section along the inner edge of the staircase that led downwards.

I had to assume it was there to act as a ramp, because the possibility of the Korvoks, of all people, having wheelchair accessibility was beyond farcical.

The stairs themselves weren’t quite a true spiral, as they extended for dozens of feet in a straight line, before taking a sharp left at the landing, and continuing with another left. We had been descending them for…quite a while now, actually. Nearly twenty minutes alone had passed in complete silence, stepping ever downward into depth with the only light provided coming from our combined Skills and Spells. If it wasn’t for those, it would have been dark enough down here that we wouldn’t even have been able to see our hands in front of our faces. Beyond the reach of our abilities, the darkness that stretched below us was all-encompassing, even smothering in its totality. Even when we dared to look over the side of the stairs and over the railing, nobody could see either the bottom or the destination.

Fade had whispered to me over our bond as well that he couldn’t really sense anything down here.

We weren’t even walking at a slow pace, either. There was something about the atmosphere down here that was…not quite oppressive, so much as aware. The Aether from the syringe that was the Heatspire far above was ever-present down this shaft, lending an almost energetic humidity to the air.

The result was like we had stepped into the throat of some impossibly large giant, whose breath we felt surging with every beat of the distant, impaled form of Nehushtan’s heart.

A pump…and the Aether swelled, and then ebbed. Over and over again.

We had to be miles below even the blood lake at this point, from our pace. At least, I hoped so. I couldn’t even imagine how much blood would have been needed to fill a depression this deep. It would be something more akin to an ocean than a lake at that point.

But like all things, our journey came to an end. The stairs abruptly terminated into a wide landing, the width of the entire shaft we had all been led down. There was nothing down here…

Other than the massive steel door that dominated the far wall of the landing.

My eyes narrowed at the sight of it, and I was incredibly tempted to ascribe it immediately. After all, I had seen my fair share of huge, circular metal doors before. Both of which had opened only at my touch. And yet…

“Nate, is that…?” I heard Renauld whisper off to my right, the words tinged with doubt. He had good reason. Of everyone present, Renauld was the only person who had been with me during our trials within the depths of Mt. Goryuen. He would recognize both the similarities of what was before us…

As well as the differences.

I slowly shook my head. “No,” I murmured. “I don’t think so.”

This was different from the entrances into the Netherim bunkers I had seen in the path. Those had been incredibly distinct, both in architectural style and in just how neglected they had been. Both of those doors had been neglected, corroded either by the passage of time or by the malice of a curse.

This was not. This door was superficially similar in concept to the portals to the Netherim bunkers, but only that. It had the same basic shape, but it was wrought in a distinctly Dwarven style, not all that dissimilar to what I had seen in Karag Varla, in fact.

I…it almost looked like…

“This isn’t Netherim at all…”

“Interesting,” A calm voice, I hadn’t heard in nearly half an hour, spoke up. Many eyes, mine included, flickered over in that direction to find that Korvakh’s concealed head had turned to look over at me. Their head was tilted in visible interest. “You are well read indeed, honored guest. No, this door is not one of those relics. Rather…it was ordered forged well after their height by none other than Yorgun itself. May I ask how you came to know of the forebears?”

I blinked slowly at the near-friendly tone that the mysterious Dwarf was taking with me. Out of the corner of my eye, I could see that Sylvia and more than a few others were just as confused as I was.

Hadn’t we just been at near all-out war with these people? Why…why was this person being so friendly?

“I…” I started, stopped, reconsidered things for a moment…and told an abbreviated portion of the truth. “I’ve been in a few bunkers.”

Korvakh leaned forward in deepening delight. “Really now. It’s incredibly surprising that those ancient sites even still exist. Frankly, I wouldn’t have thought so. Why, we-” They cut themselves off abruptly.

This time, I was the one staring at them in ill-concealed interest.

Whoever this Korvakh was, they’d just nearly let something slip they hadn’t meant to.

A moment later, they continued as if nothing had happened at all. “In any case, there’s no sense in wasting any more time out here. You’re eagerly awaited within. Please, allow me.” They bowed and then approached the door. As they did, runes previously hidden upon the surface of the darkened iron by the gloom around us lit up in a dim orange light.

That…made me more inclined to believe our guide. Netherim

Without a care about the ominous spectacle, Korvakh placed their hand on the center of the door. The runes flashed brightly upon the contact, blinding more than one person around us with their brilliance…

And then the entire door rolled smoothly off to the left, on a track I hadn’t even noticed. That certainly wasn’t Netherim engineering, not from my experience.

But I wasn’t paying an ounce of attention to something like hidden door mechanisms.

Instead, I was taking in what was on the other side of the door itself. If I didn’t know any better…

I would have thought we were back in Karag Varla

Not Karag Korvok.

The weathered stone of the staircase we had just been led down had been darkened almost from the weight of Aether stolen from Nehushtan.

But on the other side, the stone abruptly transformed into the pure white marble that had been so evident throughout Karag Varla. It tiled the floor in almost impossibly neat squares that stretched out in the distance.

That distance seemed to be some kind of entry hall. An incredibly extravagant one at that, lined by tall, shining white marble pillars, ornamented with a copious amount of gold. The shining metal was truly everywhere. It lined every fluted channel running up and down the columns, dusted every separation between the tiles, and shone through in intricate geometric patterns behind the walls. But those same patterns were mildly obscured because gentle waterfalls fell from high slights cut into the smooth walls of the hall.

But this being Karag Korvok, it wasn’t pure, sparkling water that fell from those outlets. Not like it had in the distant cavern of Varla.

No, it was instead blood. Thin blood, of a kind that trickled down in a manner similar to water. But still blood nonetheless, nearly physically assaulting us with the smell of iron as it collected in gutter pools that lay along the edges of the entryway.

The dichotomy of the blood that fountained right from the very walls, festooned with copious amounts of gold, with the gentle shimmer of strangely flowing blood…

I had to admit, there was something morbidly beautiful about it.

“Welcome, honored guests,” Korvakh said softly. “To the Inner Heart. Please, follow me. I shall be happy to lead you to the domain of Just Ul-Korveth.”

The Steward of the Heartspire stepped over the dividing line between the tower itself and into the ‘Inner Heart’, and the rest of us remained frozen for a moment.

This was just…not what I think any of us had been expecting. This was far cleaner than any of us had been expecting from the Korvoks. Yes, there was a tangible scent of iron from the blood that even now fell gently along the walls, but beyond that, the air was clean. There was an almost recycled quality to it, as if the atmosphere within was controlled in a truly artificial manner. Not only that, but the air that was wafting over us was cool, far more so than the breathing humidity of Karag Korvok proper. This place was actively being maintained in this near-perfect state, whatever it was meant to be. The spray from the falling blood didn’t even seem to reach the surrounding walkway, leaving only a surface of pure ivory marble and brilliant gold to stretch out into the distance.

It was ridiculous, but I was abruptly aware that every single member of our column was absolutely soaked in blood. It caked every single inch of our bodies, alternatively dried into clumps and clung to skin and hair like oil. I would be tracking blood in there like mud on clean kitchen floors.

I didn’t actually care, of course, considering who and what this place belonged to. But the thought still crossed my mind.

The entryway itself didn’t seem terribly long, not like the one into the Heartspire itself had been. But the far exit of the hall seemed to be hidden from view by some kind of curtain.

We had no way of knowing just what we were stepping into, the moment we were beyond it. For all I knew, this was all an elaborate setup for a trap.

A really elaborate setup.

But I wasn’t inclined to give up yet.

Instead, I took a step into the hall of the Inner Heart, and inexplicably shivered. The colder air combined with the blood all over my body really was…unpleasant. I shrugged it off, though, and took another step inside.

And as I did, the rest of my people wordlessly followed me.

The squelch of our footsteps as we trudged down the bizarrely elegant entryway combined with the soft trickle of waterfalls as we followed Korvakh down the length. It didn’t take long before we reached the curtain. As we neared it, I heard a low murmur of what sounded like conversation reach my ears. It was calm, almost lazy to my ears, sounding like nothing more than idle chatter. It put me in mind of the polite luncheons I had attended at Kyronkar in my function as both Grey’s apprentice and later as the Marshall of the Order of the Polaris Reach.

But as Korvakh drew back the curtain…

I wasn’t quite ready for the scene that greeted my eyes.
