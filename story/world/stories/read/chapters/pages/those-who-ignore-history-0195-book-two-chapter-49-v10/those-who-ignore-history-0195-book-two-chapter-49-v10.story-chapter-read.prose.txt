This sucks.

No, really. Deeply. Profoundly. Catastrophically sucks.

Tea-Pot going off on his own and getting separated? That’s practically a seasonal event at this point. If there’s a fork in reality, fate itself will grease one side and send him skidding off it headfirst. That part doesn’t even spike my blood pressure anymore. I just adjust the mental checklist and start counting how many disasters I need to prepare for instead of if.

But Ten not being here?

That’s different.

That looks bad on me. Real bad.

Not just because I’m technically her warden and the official paperwork probably has my name under something like IF THIS GOES WRONG, BLAME THIS ONE, but because I’ve started relying on her in ways I try real hard not to rely on anyone. She’s the one who goes first. Always. Short, yes. Compact, absolutely. But that girl hits the world like a siege engine that learned how to smile.

Fierce doesn’t even begin to cover it.

She could thrash anyone in any of the blocks. I’ve seen it. I’ve seen a man twice her height hit the ground so fast the dust hadn’t even decided where to land yet. Come to think of it, that’s probably why she had her own block in the first place. That and the dragon-eater thing. The administration never likes it when the inmates start stacking too many skulls.

I roll my shoulders and keep moving through the glowing underbrush, boots sinking slightly into soft, humid earth with each step. The forest doesn’t feel hostile. That’s the problem. Hostile environments announce themselves. You smell rot. You hear too many legs moving at once. You get that itch between the shoulders that means a dozen somethings are lining up for a clean shot at your spine.

This place is quiet in the wrong way.

Not empty. Just… paused.

Bioluminescent vines curl around the massive trunks like veins of slow-moving fire. Insects hum softly in the canopy like drifting embers that learned how to buzz. Every now and then I swear I catch movement between the trees, but when I turn, there’s nothing. No displacement. No mana turbulence. No sound of retreat.

It’s like the forest is deciding whether I’m worth the effort.

“Yeah,” I mutter under my breath, fingers brushing the edge of a mine disk at my belt. “You and fate both. Get in line.”

I really, really hate being alone.

Not because I’m scared. Fear is easy. Fear is measurable. You feel it, you fight through it, or you die. Clean equation. Loneliness is harder. Loneliness sits in your chest and makes you think about things you’ve spent years building traps over.

Like Ten.

I snort quietly. She’d hate that I was worrying. She’d call it inefficient. Or worse, she’d grin and say it was cute. The kind of grin that makes you rethink all your life choices in under half a second. I can practically hear her now.

Yeah. That’s the problem. I trust her completely. Which means I trust her to survive almost anything. Which means the only thing that really scares me is the one percent. The things even she can’t punch into submission.

I set a trip-mine cluster along a root the size of a fallen tower and keep moving, mapping paths in my head as I go. If Fractal’s fighting illusions, if Fallias is lost in poetic misery somewhere, if Cordelia is trying to outthink a forest that listens, then someone needs to control the ground.

That’s what I do.

I don’t win fights. I decide where they happen.

Still, the longer I walk, the more wrong it feels that I haven’t heard Ten’s voice cutting through the trees. No complaints. No tactical adjustments screamed at me like insults. No sudden impact sounds as she tests the environment by running into it at full speed.

The quiet stretches.

And then I hear it.

A sound that doesn’t belong.

It’s subtle. A soft, hollow knock. Wood on wood. Slow. Measured. Not the chaotic rhythm of animals. Not the layered noise of machines. This is deliberate. Repetitive. Testing.

I vanish into a crouch behind a glowing fern without thinking, breath slowing, senses stretching outward along old habits drilled into my bones. The sound comes again. Closer now.

Knock.

Pause.

Knock.

Something is walking on roots with wooden feet.

“Of course it is,” I whisper. “Why wouldn’t it be?”

I shift to the side and peer through the foliage. Between the massive trees, something tall moves in silhouette. Too straight. Too rigid in its balance. Its joints bend wrong for flesh. The glow of the forest paints it in gold and teal, but the color doesn’t sink in. It reflects off it.

Wooden.

Not carved like a statue. Grown. Rings spiral across its torso like the inside of a tree turned outward. Branch-limbs end in blunt, split shapes, not quite hands. Not quite weapons.

A simulacrum.

One of many, if the forest has any imagination at all.

It stops.

Its head, if that’s what the knotted mass atop its shoulders is, tilts.

And then it turns directly toward my hiding place.

“Yep,” I mutter. “That tracks.”

It moves.

Fast.

Roots explode upward as it surges forward, weight cracking the ground with every step. I roll aside just before a branch-fist smashes through the fern and into the earth hard enough to leave a crater. The shockwave rattles my teeth.

I plant a mine against the trunk beside me and leap backward.

“Let’s dance, ugly.”

The simulacrum lunges again. I flick the trigger.

Salt and fire detonate in a concussive roar that tears half its torso into splintered debris. The blast lights the forest in violent white for a heartbeat, bioluminescence overwhelmed by honest-to-gods destruction.

The construct staggers.

And then it just… keeps coming.

The torn wood knits itself back together with creeping vines and glowing sap. Not full regeneration. Slower than a Hydra’s. But steady.

“Great,” I grumble. “The environment has healing.”

It swings. I duck. The air screams where my head was a moment before. I slide under another blow and rake a blade across its knee joint. The edge bites deep, sap spilling like liquid light, but it doesn’t fall.

Something changes in the forest then.

The silence breaks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

From three different directions.

I freeze for half a second longer than I should.

And then I grin.

“Oh, this is definitely a problem.”

The underbrush rustles as more wooden figures step into view, their forms growing out of shadows and glowing vines. Not identical. Some thicker. Some leaner. All of them wrong.

I back into a narrow corridor between two massive roots, hands already moving, mines slipping free like coins for a toll I’ve paid a hundred times. My heartbeat steadies as the terror sharpens into focus.

This is where I’m useful.

“This is why you don’t leave the trap guy alone,” I mutter as the first of them charges.

The forest erupts in controlled chaos.

Explosions ripple through wood and light. Salt flares sear through creeping growth. Triggers snap in rapid succession as I kite, redirect, herd, and mislead. The simulacra are strong. Relentless. But they’re predictable. They rush. They strike. They do not think.

That’s their mistake.

Still, as splinters fly and glowing sap rains down like molten stars, one thought keeps punching through everything else.

Ten should be here.

She’d be laughing. She’d be taking bets on how many I could take before I ran out of toys. She’d be calling me slow while I bled and still somehow winning.

The absence burns sharper than any wound.

As another simulacrum raises its branch-arm to strike, I slam a detonator into its chest and kick off its torso as it explodes backward into a storm of glowing debris.

“Hold on,” I breathe into the humid, violent dark. “All of you. Just hold on.”

The isolation of this forest reminded me of those years.

Those long. Long. Years.

Alone.

Behind bars that weren’t made of iron, but rules. Numbers. Commands. A living sentence burned into my skin in the shape of a crest that never cooled, never slept, never forgave. Killing by directive. Culling by designation. Being a prisoner inside a body that was trained to move before it was ever allowed to feel.

It reminds me of things I’m not supposed to remember.

And things I’m not allowed to say out loud.

Even when I want to.

I let out a slow breath through my nose. The forest exhales back at me—humid, electric, thick with mana that crackles faintly with every heartbeat. Even my thoughts feel slower here, muffled beneath the constant, restrained shock of power cycling through the crest on my arm. Iron-rich blood becomes a conduit; every pulse carries voltage.

Alone.

Empty.

There are many words for this forest. Beautiful. Alien. Silent. Oppressive. But none of them quite fit. It feels…structured. Like a room built too large for anything meant to live in it.

The light is wrong—suspended between day and night. Bioluminescent veins glow through bark and leaves like exposed nerves. Every step hums faintly beneath my boots, the ground answering my weight with soft, electrical complaint.

My hands stay loose at my sides.

My legs stay ready.

Then the forest tightens.

Not sound. Not movement.

Pressure.

A shift in mana density sharp enough to prickle across my scalp. I pivot on instinct, weight dropping as something tall and fast erupts from the undergrowth—wooden body, jointed wrong, arms shaped like warped polearms. A wooden simulacrum.

I slide inside its reach and snap a front kick into its knee.

The joint detonates in a burst of glowing splinters. The construct lurches.

Before it can compensate, I spin and drive a roundhouse kick through its torso. The impact booms through the clearing as bark collapses inward, light spilling from the fracture like exposed plasma. It stumbles back—but does not fall.

Roots surge across the cavity, knitting it back together.

Regeneration.

“Figures,” I mutter.

It swings again. I drop my center and slip underneath, catching its arm at the elbow and shoulder in a tight judo lock, pivoting hard on my foot as I use its mass against it. The throw is clean. The simulacrum slams into the glowing earth with enough force to crater the soil.

It still moves.

The forest answers with company.

The ground trembles—once, twice—then multiple wooden bodies rise from the undergrowth like soldiers extruding from the earth itself. Different shapes. Same intent.

Overwhelm.

Good.

I shift my stance.

The first charge comes from my right. I step inside its swing and knee it upward through the core. The second reaches for my back—I hook its leg with my heel and sweep, riding it down and slamming my elbow into the joint where neck would be. The third lunges high—

I kick off the second’s chest and vault, twisting mid-air to drive both heels into the third’s head in a double drop strike that explodes it into cascading light.

They recover fast.

Too fast.

Vines lash out from the forest floor and coil around my ankle mid-step. I go down for half a second—

Half a second is all they need.

A wooden shoulder tackles me sideways through a thicket of glowing ferns and into a crystal-veined tree trunk. The impact rattles my skull. Pain flares hot across my ribs.

Good.

Pain keeps timing honest.

I roll through the hit and come up low, snapping a lightning-fast side kick into the attacker’s knee. Another kick breaks its hip. A third caves in its chest. I don’t give it time to regenerate—I spin, hook its neck with my shin, and wrench backward in a takedown that tears the head clean off.

More close in.

They adapt.

They keep distance now.

Smart.

The mark on my arm pulses hotter.

No.

Not yet.

One steps in with a long, bladed branch aimed at my midsection. I redirect it with a parry of my shin, pivot around the limb, and drive my heel into the base of its skull. As it pitches forward, I clamp onto its arm and execute a violent shoulder throw that slams it into two of its allies like a living battering ram.

Wood shatters.

Light spills.

They re-form.

Relentless.

Efficient.

Unemotional.

I’ve fought enemies like this before.

Soldiers grown instead of trained.

Tools instead of fighters.

Sweat slicks down my neck. The static in the air thickens until every movement leaves afterimages of pale lightning tracing my path. My heartbeat grows louder in my ears—each pulse answering the hum beneath the forest floor.

They herd me back.

Step by step.

Toward a natural choke point between massive roots. I recognize the tactic too late. Something slams down from above—an overhead strike like a falling tree.

It catches my shoulder.

Bone screams.

I hit the ground hard, breath tearing out of me as impact rattles through my spine. Shapes loom in the glowing haze. Wooden feet plant around me.

For the first time since the ambush began, doubt flickers.

V isn’t here.

Fractal isn’t here.

Alex—

No.

I plant one foot flat against the chest of the nearest construct and thrust upward, launching myself backward into a kip-up that shakes the roots with my landing. I spin immediately into a sweeping hook kick that decapitates one at the knee. Another closes high—

I catch its arm, step inside its stance, and slam my hip against its center of mass, executing a full rotational throw that drives it headfirst into the glowing soil with enough force to crater stone.

They close tighter.

The mark burns.

Demanding.

Promising.

Efficiency. Finality. An end to resistance.

I grit my teeth hard enough to taste blood.

Not yet.

One breaks through my guard and tackles my legs from the side. I go down—but I take it with me, wrapping my thighs around its torso and rotating with violent precision. The joint shear is audible even through the forest hum. I release and heel-kick off its chest to regain distance.

Another strikes from behind.

Too slow.

I pivot on one foot and drive a back kick straight through its sternum. The impact sends a shockwave through three others behind it.

They hesitate.

Just enough.

“Fine,” I whisper.

The restraint cracks.

Mana surges—but not as a blast.

It floods my legs.

The world sharpens.

Every micro-shift in weight becomes a fulcrum of devastation. My kicks hit with the authority of collapsing towers. Each step liquefies the glowing soil beneath me. I don’t rage—I accelerate.

A straight kick erases one from chest through spine.

A spinning heel shatters another into radiant dust mid-regrowth.

I slide beneath a high strike and drive an upward knee so hard the simulacrum lifts off the ground before exploding into failing light.

Wood can regenerate.

It cannot regenerate fast enough.

The forest lights pulse wildly as construct after construct falls apart under disciplined, brutal precision. No wasted motion. No hesitation. Only rotation, leverage, and annihilation.

The last one tries to flee.

It gets exactly three steps before I launch forward and drive my heel through its back, pinning it to the glowing earth as its mana binding collapses in a silent burst.

Stillness returns.

Not quiet.

Heavier.

I stand in the middle of broken light and torn roots, chest rising and falling, the mark dimming slowly back into its restrained burn.

The forest watches.

I wipe blood from the corner of my mouth with the back of my hand and exhale once, long and steady.

“…I really hate places like this.”

Because they don’t test my strength.

They test my control.

And they remind me how easily I could stop pretending I still have any.
