The puppeteer’s attention shunted aside the sound of the wind and waves, as if Henry’s senses had suddenly been isolated and in their place, a soft hum slipped through, sounding almost like static. It wasn’t hard to guess what would happen next. And while Henry had hoped there might be a little bit of hope of delaying the violence and getting the creature to back off, what it did next made it hard to consider that.

It came into his spirit like a silent song. Like some sort of invisible tug inviting him to let go. To just rest and let it deal with all of his troubles for him. It would take care of him and make him stronger. He just had to let…go. Let the thin, smooth threads he could sense—but not see—slink under his defenses and embed themselves into his soul—

Henry flared Dispelling Pulse, empowering it with the essence of Thaumaturgy and shredded the insidious lines. He didn’t sense much of a resistance right then and there, but thanks to the multiple Octominds, which he immediately pulled to himself, he had the time to sense some residue left along the path they tried to take, like an oily thumb print on glass, which he assigned one Octomind to focus on scrubbing off.

Attempts of control might be easy to fend off at first, but it could get tougher as time goes on, he theorized before he spoke telepathically to the creature. “[This isn’t your territory to hunt. Leave.]”

Just for its attempt to hi-jack his body, Henry was tempted to immediately go on the offensive. But he needed to buy time. Through the clones still on the island—which he didn’t have that many of—he was trying to locate Velistraine and Attal, but he could provide that process only so much attention. There was still a damn powerful A-rank in front of him, and Henry had no intention of having his body taken over by a juiced-up parasite.

The creature, still eyeless and mostly immobile, did not respond in words. But it did respond by lashing out with more of its threads. This time, it put a lot more effort behind the attempt, enough that the damn things were visible to the naked eye, like thin, barely visible tendrils of light reaching out from it toward the crew. As for what it thought of his message…

“It’s like you stole food out of its mouth,” Maurice chimed in, the air vibrating around him as the crab created a concept-empowered dispelling zone all around him. It made the threads fizzle out before they could even get further than ten feet from the creature and to Henry, it seemed that Maurice had done it through a smooth combination of a Domain and Dispelling Pulse, somehow managing to spare him from its effect with what looked like pretty deft control.

Maurice hovered next to him, legs crossed as he mimicked the gesture of a pincer with his fingers. “What did you say to it?”

“I told it that it’s not welcome here,” Henry simply said, and he had to admit, its response was clear enough. From the parasite’s point of view, if it wasn’t being let in peacefully, it would take control by force and with that in mind, Henry put one of his Octominds in control of the clone nearest to Attal’s lab, and tasked it with getting them here as soon as possible. He even had it start channeling so he could teleport one of them to him as soon as they were in sight. As for the rest, of his Octominds, they would support him while he gave his full attention to this thing.

Henry just wished he could drop the flesh-suit and get into a more comfortable form, but he didn’t want the whole island to know the truth.

With Maurice next to him, Henry flew up and away, just in time to dodge another few threads as he hoped to lure the parasite after him and just as he flew past, he pointed with a hand and shot a gray-looking Toxic Ray at it.

The painful toxin of the Lionfish should get it to pay attention to him. If it reached it. Henry had no expectation of doing much damage with a C-Rank ability, but if he got through, it’d give the sailors a chance of getting away instead of sitting duck on the ship.

“Get the ship out of here,” he shouted as the gray, palm-wide ray bounced off a smooth, pearl-like shield. He didn’t even look back at the ship, sensing another attempt at hi-jacking his body. Instead, Henry switched gears and with the same hand, a different flavor of Toxic Ray shot out. This one was purple, and it almost looked as if it was on fire.

Henry had been looking for a chance to use the corroding, mana-eating toxin of the Stinglance Tyrant. The ray slammed into the shield, but just like he expected, in its current form and potency, it just bounced harmlessly off. The gap in rank between the abilities was too stark. Trickster’s Toxic Concoctions was still only C-rank, so it could contend that well against the parasite.

But Henry had some new tools to bring to bear, and thankfully, the parasite seemed like it didn’t mind allowing the ship to slink away, levitating closer to Henry and Maurice as the two led it back to sea. Maybe it was because Henry was taking them toward the bulk of the starfish, which he assumed the creature could weaponize to great effect. Maybe it was because it wanted to gets its string into him instead of the others, considering how fast it had managed to take control of the weaker sailors. But Henry could make do with this. He’d spare the others a certain death, and he’d likely be able to bring the fight underwater, where he could unleash everything he had at his disposal without revealing it to the humans on the island.

In the meantime, Henry started blasting and this time, he didn’t intend for it to just be a distraction. He didn’t want this fight to last overly long, so the next Toxic Ray got almost everything he could give it.

Holding both palms out, Henry activated Rupture Casting. Mana gushed out of his chest and into the skill nexus he created in front of his palms, making the air glow with an intense, almost golden brown. Deep, hungry red-essence seeped into it next, mixing in with the prodigious amount of mana already raging within giving them a crimson luster as Predation pushed the skill to the next level, almost dyeing Henry’s surroundings in its eerie light.

Then he let the first one go.

If the normal Toxic Rays were around four inches wide, then this one was more than double. Also, normal Toxic Rays didn’t howl into existence like this one, blaring with an almost mechanical whine as it tore through the air. It crossed the air separating it from its target instantly, like a hungry, barbed tongue reaching for a particularly juicy piece, lighting up the air around them like a cursed thunderbolt before it slammed and sizzled against the pearly shield.

Neither Henry, Maurice, or the parasite had been expecting the Toxic Ray to hit that hard. The impact had melted a hole in the shield, and the hole was widening visibly as the toxin-addled meltdown of corrupted and flaking magic ran down its edge.

The parasite did not like that, and if it had been shocked, the shock was quickly replaced by affront. Though before it could do much, a second empowered ray blasted its shell, immediately tearing down what it had re-cast.

Then Henry shot a third. Then a fourth and by now, Maurice was lobbing his own dark bubbles at it. Henry still had plenty of Predation essence stocked up, and he could keep up with the mana consumption for a while longer. Though ideally, he’d score a couple of good hits and inflict it with Veilwatch’s Mark. That ought to keep him in the fight for longer. As for the strain of using Rupture Casting… well. Between the skill itself now firmly part of his soul official and having his spirit tempered by yet another skill, he could go on for a while still.

Then he finally scored a hit. The ray pierced through the weakened shell, and clipped the pale parasite, rocking it a few inches back and scarring into its flesh.

For a moment, Henry felt hope. But in the next second, it was dashed away as the pale, spotted injury was healed, wiped away as if it had been mere dust.

Henry swallowed hard, and he hoped his Velistraine-retrieving Octomind was close to its target.

A short distance away, renewed shouts came off the ship, and through a clone’s eyes, Henry saw the massive limb of the starfish break out of the surface, rising in the air like a lurching tower threatening to fall at any moment. Even under Henry’s and Maurice’s continuous onslaught, the parasite was not feeling any pressure, and as Henry tried to spare the attention to get the ship and its crew out of its way, a fresh batch of the puppeteer’s threads sprouted from its root-like limbs.

Instantly, Henry could tell these were different.

In a way, he almost felt like they were already part of him, which was extremely unnerving, to say the least, and it soon became clear why.

The Puppeteer was using Aspects.

The sound of shattered wood and splashing water filled the air, sending debris and water spraying up as if an explosion had gone off. The starfish limb had crushed the ship utterly, and as a knot tightened in his guts, Henry hoped the crew had made it out, but he couldn’t spare his attention on anything but the creature in front of him. Henry put all of his energy in rebuffing the parasite’s new assault, and when he made it out of that exchange, he spared an Octomind and a few clones to go look for survivors while he urged the clones he had near the seabed to come back up quickly.

He hoped the Octomind-controlled clone was about to find Velistraine and Attal soon. Because Henry’s strength didn’t lie in ranged-combat, and he really didn’t want to get close to this thing.
