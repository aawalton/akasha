I drop off the final crate of miscellaneous items in the compound’s warehouse. While none of it was the water crystals everyone is so desperately looking for, maybe something will come of the various monster parts, mineral samples, and chunks of coral. The last Fracture was in a coral reef, with a particularly large shark as the boss.

Stepping around tall stacks of crates and in one case, a shipping container—they go for cheap, these days—I come face to face with very likely the most valuable item in the warehouse. The dented, melted, and rent body of my doppelganger. The identical helmet is cold and dark, though it was once filled with a weak golden light.

Though Morgan’s been extremely busy with the arcology project, she did spend some time attempting to study it, though only after I basically begged her to. I am likely the most durable object on the planet. We have no idea what my armor is made of, though if we could produce more of the material, we could save so many lives.

Though the thought of people wearing what is effectively my nearest approximation of flesh leaves me feeling a little disturbed. When I first brought it back from the Fracture in Amiens, I was all for the endless self-sacrifice. Everything for the cause, as it were. A little discomfort is well worth saving a life was my thinking.

Now? Well, Morgan was right, I guess. I don’t need to sacrifice everything. It’s still difficult to draw that line though, but I think I can make an exception. Besides, given how many toes I’ve stepped on, it’s probably for the best we never truly figure out what I’m made of. Maybe it’s a little selfish, but I’d rather not have some government—or corporation—figure out how to kill me.

It sounds arrogant to proclaim how important I am to the defense of not just the United States, but the world as well. But it is true. I often do things no one can do. Take on tasks that would break others if they tried to bear them. I can’t do everything, but I’m unmatched in things I can do. I suppose that goes for all of the Ascended, really.

I hear a door open, and a few moments later a familiar voice comes from behind me.

“Penny for your thoughts?” Morgan asks, silently floating.

“You were right,” I admit. “I didn’t need to—and probably shouldn’t have—brought this back,” I say, gesturing to the suit of armor.

“We could always get rid of it,” Morgan offers. “Just bring it into a Fracture and dump it. There’s plenty of them that form out here, don’t even need to bring it into the city for everyone to gawk at.”

“It feels like a mistake to throw away something so… important. Consequential. Especially since we’re trying to understand the Fractures.”

“I understand where you’re coming from, but I doubt anything good will come of understanding you. There’s a lot of people who want to kill you, for a variety of reasons.”

“But the things my doppelganger did could be earth-shaking. It controlled the Fracture. It ripped it apart. Think of what we could do with that!”

Morgan shudders. “I’d rather not, actually. We’ve got enough holes in the fabric of reality as it is. I don’t think we need it anymore.”

“Let’s just leave it for now. We can always throw it away later,” I say, turning away from it.

With my complete 360 degree vision, I can see the concerned look Morgan shoots at my back.

“Hey, Mach, could I get your help with something before you go?” Morgan asks once we return to the gallery.

“Sure.”

She pulls up the arcology design, and explains what she needs. Earlier today we had actually received some data on mithril from Guan Yu. China’s own laboratories had managed to fast track some material science studies on it. It means I can actually put some of my mechanical engineering knowledge to use. Mostly in Finite Element Analysis models to simulate how the building will react under different loading conditions. FEA can be very computationally expensive, but fortunately for me I have computational capacity in spades.

Several hours into the process I receive a call from the good General over the secure line I created for the Council to contact each other. He has something for us, and is calling for us to convene once again.

Estrella is the last of us to arrive, having flown in rather than portal. I’m sure she could find someone to do it for her, given her position as an Ascended. To be honest, I think she just likes to fly, and I can’t really blame her. Flying at hypersonic speeds does sound enjoyable.

Even Pythia is here, though she leans a bit more heavily on her cane than last time.

“Thank you for coming,” Guan Yu says. “I know it was very abrupt, but it has been awhile since we’ve checked in, and I have some updates regarding the project.”

Warden pulls a crate out of a black void that appears at his feet and sets it on the ground. He flicks open the lid, and inside are a dozen blue crystals. The crate itself is half full of water, which rapidly begins to freeze in the frigid Arctic air. Warden snaps the case closed after we all get a look.

“We managed to retrieve several water crystals from a coastal Fracture. I’d like to offer a portion of them to you, Sorceress le Fay,” the General says. He coughs. “If, of course, you’d be willing to provide your ritual for growing additional crystals? I advocated for this to be a gift rather than an exchange, but my superiors demanded something in return.”

“No problem at all. The ritual had been completed and works. It could use some improvements, of course,” she says, grimacing. “It’s not cheap or easy to run, but it does work. I’ll have the details sent to you once I get back home.”

I store the crate of water crystals away, and the mood among us all is much better than the last time we met.

“Just one more piece,” Morgan says.

“If we can find it. If it even exists,” Singularity says.

“It’ll exist. The real problem will be actually building the arcologies once we do find it,” Morgan says.

“How could you possibly know that?” Singularity asks. His orange-ringed pitch-black eyes stare at us both. “What do you know that we don’t?”

Morgan shrugs. “Not much. Just that this is magic. We all keep asking what magic is capable of doing when we should be asking what it isn’t capable of doing. We can make sunlight with lightbulbs and electricity. Why wouldn’t magic be capable of doing that? Hell, it made a giant flower with buds that make plants grow faster. Want to tell me why that exists?”

She points at Estrella hovering overhead.

“For fuck’s sake, it made Estrella into a literal living star! I think a crystal that glows is a few steps down from that.”

“I think we get your point, Sorceress le Fay,” General Guan Yu says diplomatically. “As the resident expert on magic, I see no reason to doubt your claim. Do you have any suggestions as to where we should look for these sun crystals?”

“Sunny, hot places, most likely,” Morgan says. “We all know that Fractures tend to replicate their environment and tend to have both related threats and resources. So, I imagine you’ll find them in Fractures that form in deserts.”

“Parts of the Gobi Desert lie inside China. I will send out aerial patrols to look for Fractures. If we find anything, I will let you know. Does anyone else have any updates?”

“Monster attacks on the west coast of India have dramatically increased,” Singularity says grimly. “We aren’t sure why or how. We may be missing more Fractures under water, or maybe some very powerful ones recently ruptured.”

“They’re not just focused on Mumbai either,” Adrenaline adds. His pilot’s mask dangles from a strap, dancing in the Arctic wind. “We’ve started to see them attacking up and down along the entire coast.”

“Maybe they’re running from something?” Warden offers. “The Arabian Sea is large and deep.”

“I’m more worried about this ‘King of Monsters’ Pythia mentioned,” Singularity says with a nod towards the elderly Greek oracle. “Perhaps they’re being driven or even commanded.”

Guan Yu narrows his eyes. “I don’t suppose you’ve been able to help support the freedom fighters inside the Empire?”

“No,” Singularity says with a grimace. “We have fourteen hundred miles of coastline to defend. We’re stretched too thinly as it is. I am sorry, we cannot afford to assist.”

“It’s enemy action, I have no doubt about it.” The General strokes his long beard thoughtfully. “India is one of the few relatively close countries who would have the ability to seriously fund the proxies they now suffer from. There is another actor at play here, trying to stay hidden. Estrella, Zheng Fei, Galahad, did you find this unknown Ascended in France?”

Zheng Fei, who claimed the title of The Honest from Pythia’s prophecy, shakes his head. “No. Every time we thought we got close they managed to slip away. We aren’t even sure what they look like. We’re hunting them based on their Aura alone. Whoever they are, they’re a master at hiding it.”

“It’s hard to imagine an Ascended being so good at hiding. We’re all just so… much,” I say.

“We are also nearly limitless,” Guan Yu says with a shrug. “To say that hiding is impossible is a foolish endeavor. Morgan herself did just say that we should assume everything is possible until proven otherwise.”

“To have so many in the wind is a liability,” Warden says firmly. “We need to find all of them. Do we even know how many Ascended there are?”

“Twenty-four,” Pythia says. “There are twenty-four Bright Lights. Everyone else I can see clearly.”

“There is something we need to prepare for,” I say, and everyone turns to me. “What if the sun crystal we need is in the territory of the Empire? What if they find one? I’m sure they’ll want it just as much as we do, even if they can’t use it.”

“If for no other reason than we want it,” Morgan mutters. “Bastards, the fuckin’ lot of them.”

“Then we shall have to find another source,” Guan Yu says grimly. “We cannot afford to engage in combat with them. I am certain for something so important as the sun crystal Berhane will commit his Vassals. Possibly even himself. We cannot afford direct confrontation.”

“What if we don’t have time to find another source?” I ask with heat in my voice. “We are running low on time as it is.”

“We cannot afford to engage in confrontation with them. We have decided this, as a group,” he says more firmly.

“You would doom the world to starvation because you’re too afraid to fight someone who could fight back?!”

Anger clouds his face, and he marches towards me. I step forward too, ice build up breaking off with a cacophony that sounds like laughter.

“You accuse me of cowardice,” Guan Yu seethes. “Yet you are the one too scared to face facts. We cannot fight them. Not because we cannot win but because of what it would cost. You want to save lives but to fight Berhane and his Vassals would cost more than you would save!”

“They are evil! Objectively, truly, deeply, evil!”

“The world is full of evil,” Guan Yu growls. “It always has been. You want to steer the course of history? Learn to deal with it. Every leader must learn to make compromises.”

“We can find a way to stop them without getting anyone else hurt!”

“No, we can’t.”

“Have we even bothered to try?!”

I look around at all of us, some of the most powerful people in the world, individuals capable of even forcing the largest and most powerful nations in the world to listen if not act.

“Have we even bothered to try to stop the Empire? We have so much power and people are counting us on to do something!

“None of you could possibly understand just how many people send me messages begging for me to do something, anything! How many people are so desperate for salvation they send a message to man on the other side of the world in the slight hope I might say yes!”

Everyone looks uncomfortable, and Galahad thumps her chest.

“Hear, hear. Something is better than nothing. I do not think we could possibly make things worse than they already are. Khalid scooped out a city with a strike. He razed Mogadishu. We cannot let this continue.”

General Guan Yu clenches his jaw, and the world feels sharp. The ice beneath our feet creeks and groans.

“So what’s your plan, Machina?” He spits through clenched teeth. “Are you going to put every evil in the world to the sword? Are you going to kill Berhane and his Vassals?”

“If I have to,” I growl. I don’t want to be a killer. But if killing was ever justifiable, I think it’d be over this. Over the deaths of hundreds of thousands by their hands—and millions indirectly—and many more to come. Not all monsters come from Fractures, but they’re monsters all the same.

Do not negotiate with evil, destroy it.

The Council breaks up shortly after Guan Yu and I’s heated argument. Galahad surprisingly follows us, joining us in Morgan’s gallery. She looks around with faint curiosity at the dozens of prototypes, experiments, and abandoned ideas.

“Hm, so this is where the magic is made,” she mutters before turning her attention to me.

“Machina, we are clearly of one mind. The Empire must be stopped. Letting them continue as they are will be much worse than fighting them.”

“When do we actually attack? Now? The world is on the brink and is relying on us to keep them fed,” I say with a sigh. “What do you think, Morgan?”

She runs a hand through her hair.

“I…I don’t know. We’re so very close to being able to build the Arcologies. Southern Mining is pulling more and more titanium rich sand out of Georgia’s coastal Fractures. The crystal growth ritual is effectively complete, and I managed to get the power cost for the growth arrays to a manageable level. The last thing we need is the sun crystals.”

“Damn it,” I snarl. “I hate this. Having to choose between who to save and who to let die!”

“You can’t save everyone,” Galahad says mournfully. “All we can do is to try to save as many as we can.”

All three of us are silent, thinking about what to do.

“I think we should finish the towers first,” I say. “Billions of people are on the verge of starvation, and if that happens, there won’t be anyone left to fight the Empire.”

Galahad grimly nods. “We have the support of nations—for now. Once the famine starts to set in, the proxy war will dry up and they won’t be off balance anymore. Not to mention all the nations of the world will fight over the food that is left. Civilization will burn—more than it already is. Between the three of us, I think we could take Berhane and his Vassals without question.”

Morgan looks a little uncomfortable and slightly ashamed.

“I…I am not a warrior,” she says looking away. “I don’t want to fight.”

“I’ll help in other ways, of course,” she quickly adds. “I just don’t want to fight.”

Galahad and I share a look. “Alright,” I tell Morgan. “It’s fine.”

“It is?”

I shrug. “Not everyone is a fighter. I won’t blame you for that, and I won’t force you into it.”

“Do you think we can take on all three of them at once?” Galahad asks gruffly. “No Ascended has ever fought another. We don’t know how a match up like that would go with just the two of us.”

“When the time comes, we’ll make it work,” I say with conviction I’m not sure I actually feel.

“Aye,” she says, nodding firmly. “Failure is not an option.”

“We need to get a message to the Party of Heroes. Maybe they can help?” I ask. “They haven’t contacted me, and they don’t seem to have phones or anything else I could connect to.”

“I’ve never been to Africa,” Galahad says, and Morgan mumbles the same.

A woman fades into existence, and all of us jump. My shock lasts for mere nanoseconds, and my sword forms in my hand before she fully appears with a muffled pop.

She’s a youngish woman, clearly of Indian descent. She wears what can only be described as adventurer’s gear. She wears thick, well worn clothes suited for tromping through heavy forests and solid boots to keep her from slipping. A backpack is strapped tight to her back, with bedroll, tent, and all. A heavy machete rests in sheath along one hip, the only weapon she visibly has.

Morgan backs away, floating up. Magic crackles along her arms and through her brush in her hand, conjured in a flash. Galahad wields her double-headed battleaxe in two hands, ready to attack.

She gives us a jaunty wave. “Hello!”

“Who are you?” Galahad asks cautiously, and the stranger eyes our weapons with a sense of nervousness, though it’s well hidden. It’s difficult to truly hide body language from an Ascended, and me especially with my extremely high-resolution sensor suite.

“Aarana Chand!” She says cheerfully. She looks around. “Where am I?”

“How did you get here?” I ask. She shrugs.

“I just go places,” she says. “I don’t really get to decide where exactly if I haven’t been there yet. I wanted to go somewhere interesting that I haven’t been before. I guess you three are doing something interesting!”

Galahad, Morgan, and I all share a look.

“You just… go places?” I ask slowly. “No need for a portal?”

“Yep!” She says cheerfully. “Now, if you don’t mind, I’ll have a little bit of a look-around! I love seeing new places and finding cool things! Any suggestions?”

“The Flat Irons are that way,” I say, pointing to the west. “They’re pretty.”

“Thanks!” She turns to leave.

“Wait!” I call out, and she turns around.

“You said you like looking for things. Could you help us find something?”

She perks up. “Sure! What are you looking for?”

I quickly explain the hydroponic arcologies and the missing sun crystal. She nods along, and when I’m done, smiles.

“Sure, that sounds fun! Any ideas? Wait, never mind, don’t tell me. I’ll figure it out. It’ll be an adventure!”

She vanishes as quickly as she appeared, fading from view rather than teleporting. With a whispering breeze, she completely disappears. Her Aura, now gone, was one that could only belong to an Ascended. It spoke of wide open spaces, new sights and the joy of an endless journey.

“What was that? Who was that?” Morgan asks incredulously.

“I have absolutely no idea,” I reply.

With the interruption evidently over, we continue to plan for our eventual conflict with the Empire. Even if it’s just myself and Galahad from the Council.

Trying to get a message to the Party of Heroes is proving to be extremely difficult. Normally I handle all of the communication between Ascended. I use various satellites as relays and make sure everything is heavily encrypted using methods even I barely understand beyond an instinctual level.

All of the Ascended currently a part of the Council have a phone or something else I can connect to relay the messages. Unfortunately, the Party of Heroes either were unable to get their hands on them—extremely unlikely—or they’re intentionally eschewing them. Possibly to evade being tracked by the Empire. As new of a nation as they are, they have enough money and power to get their hands on ways to track a phone.

I personally defend Council Members’ devices from any tracking or spying method. When it comes to cyberspace, I am nearly unmatched. The only other person I know who holds a candle to me is someone who works for the Conglomerate in Korea, and I’m still faster and better than they are.

Africa itself is in more or less in total chaos, still reeling from monster attacks and the tidal wave that is the Empire. While the proxy forces are already biting at their tyrannical heels, they still have strength aplenty. Whatever friends Galahad may have had on that continent are either unavailable, busy, or more likely, dead.

Galahad is pretty cagey about her past and why she’d have friends there, which is uncharacteristic for her. She didn’t even really explain who these friends were. I don’t push. We all have things we’d rather forget or move on from.

In the end, we decide that Morgan will try to figure out a messaging spell of some kind. It’s a long shot, but we live in an era of long shots and Hail Marys. At the very least we decide that the Empire’s days are numbered, and we’ll make sure the count is low.

We just have to save the rest of the world first.

Damn Berhane and his hounds for making us choose who to let die.
