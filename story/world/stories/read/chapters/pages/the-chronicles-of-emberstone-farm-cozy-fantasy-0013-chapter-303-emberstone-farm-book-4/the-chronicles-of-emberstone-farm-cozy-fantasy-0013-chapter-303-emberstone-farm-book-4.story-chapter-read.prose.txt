In this world, something called “qi” or spirit energy was said to be everywhere. I couldn’t sense it myself, but certain special people, like clan members or cultivators, could. Though spirit water was a very grand name, it wasn’t that rare. Water taken from deep in the ground was quite often infused with earth energy. The difference was that water taken from the wells inside my farm was infused with a lot more qi than those outside.

“I think they will be,” said Lari. “Teacher, may I dip my tools in the wells after we dig them?”

“Maybe the well water gives elemental powers to whatever you dip in it,” said Lari.

We chatted a bit more about it over the meal, and Mo requested that we finish planting the [Tea] before starting the quest, pointing out that it looked like the quest might take a long time. She had a point, so I agreed.

Once we were ready, the four of us, with Eggy in tow, walked to an open field north of the river and south of the [Greenhouses]. The nanny took a break since we didn’t need her to watch Eggy.

We all put on our new Emberstone Farm hats, and Kharli taught my son how to whistle and make bird sounds using a blade of grass held between the thumbs. I tried it myself and found that the sounds I made were less like a whistle and more like an annoying screech. And so we spent the entire time walking while making weird squawking sounds.

As we neared the field, we put aside our leaf whistles, and I looked around. The morning light cast a lovely golden light over the farm and the forest behind it. The grass was still damp from the morning dew, and a light wind moved through the open space, bending the new shoots and sending ripples across the green. The meadow was full of color, with patches of sheep grass, bluegrass, and needlegrass scattered throughout the field. Purple windflowers and yellow primroses dotted the landscape. The sky above was pale blue, with a few wispy clouds catching the sun near the horizon. The air was pleasantly cool.

“What a beautiful place! I almost regret clearing these out and replacing them with crops.” I plucked a primrose. “Maybe we should pick a different field?”

“It is very pretty,” said Mo.

“We could go to tomorrow’s field instead,” said Kharli.

We walked down the rows of plants, me on the left, and Eggy on the right, and I matched his pace as we watered the plants, merrily singing Frère Jacques together. It was his current favorite tune.

The difference was that regular plants and animals contained impure qi, while spirit plants and animals had purer qi. Eating spirit food made people healthier, stronger, and, with daily consumption, even helped retain or restore youth.

A small group of [Cows] was grazing on tender spring grass in the nearby pasture. Their bodies were solid and heavy, their hides marked with patches of black and white. Some had their heads down, moving slowly through the grass, their jaws working steadily. Others stood still, occasionally lifting their heads to look around before returning to graze.

“If you spook them, they could run away or even attack you.”

Kharli, Mo, and I hung back, letting Lari take the lead. He showed Eggy how to cautiously approach the nearest [Cow] by moving to its line of sight. He gave it time to notice them before walking near enough to pet its side and neck with slow, sure strokes of his hand.

I bought the [Sweet Heart Gauge] from the Cash Shop and activated it. The effect was even more exciting than I expected. Petting or talking to farm animals in Adventure Incarnate made them happy and slightly increased the quality of their products. It wasn’t really necessary; however, it was a firm belief in this world that it was good for clan members to have lots of contact with animals during their childhood. It was supposed to help their qi grow and boost their immunity to common diseases.
