Elder Oltak stared up at the marble building as his apprentice worked on the finishing touches of her enchantments.

Elder Mo, the recently advanced High Goblin Architect, drew Oltak’s gaze with a whistle.

Oltak walked over to the Architect, the bracelets on his wrists twisting with the mana he’d stuffed into them.

“She done yet?” Elder Mo asked.

Oltak looked back to his apprentice. She was covered in even more artifacts than he was, the circlet on her head and the staff in her hands glowing as she used her mana for another series of enchantments. During the course of this project, she’d evolved into a High Goblin Enchanter, and they’d had to redo a majority of the enchantments they’d been surrounding his academy with. “Almost, Elder Mo.”

Elder Mo nodded.

Oltak looked up at the building. It was the combination of the best and brightest of the Empire. Every race had contributed in some way. Rare crystals mined by the Under Goblins powered the enchantments that ran throughout the building. Spiders and Orcs hauled the materials, then used their webs in the construction. Alexandra, the human, had described the necessities of an Academy, and brought her own people for the designs. Sun-Chaser, and a strange kobold named World-Keeper had contributed their own magics to several of the rooms. Goblins had been the glue for every step of the project, and at the end, the Scaled had joined them in constructing the larger towers, the largest of which a dragon could land on.

It could not just be called a building anymore. It was a castle, with several offshoots that stood tall, as the entire hillside had been commandeered for construction. All of it, white marble that sparkled in the sun.

The two goblins waited in silence, admiring the building they’d spent the last several months constructing.

The enchantress stood up, and stretched, finished with her work.

Oltak turned to the other Elder, and let him know that she was done.

Elder Mo grinned with excitement as he turned to the building and held his hands up. The building moved under his command, marble twisting to cover the enchantments Oltak’s apprentice had finished.

Then it stopped, and Elder Mo lowered his hand as he stared at something in front of him.

Oltak was about to ask what had distracted the other goblin when a notification appeared in front of him.

Academy Of Monsters

An academy designed to teach the arcane arts belonging to the monstrous races.

The Kobolds bring their magic of Transformation.

The High Goblins bring the art of Runes

The Under Goblins bring their Channeling

The Orcs bring their Shamanism

The Scaled bring their Pacts and Bonds

The Humans bring their Sorcery

The Spiders bring their Strings

As a reward for this crowning achievement, the Academy has gained the blessings of the System.

Oltak stared at the screen, then looked to his apprentice, who stared slack-jawed at her own screen.

He smiled wide, it was only right that the jewel of Risha’s Empire would be recognized by the system itself.

Sarah blinked, and turned away from the tree that stood in the center of her home in the spirit realm.

A woman she had not spoken to in a long time, stood there, armor covering her form.

“It has been a long time.” The Sky Mother said, a slight frown on her face, “What brings you here, Eydis?”

The Mother Of Dwarves bowed her head the smallest amount before directing her gaze to Sarah’s eyes. The golden glint that was so bright in the Dwarf’s gaze was dulled in a strange way that Sarah couldn’t place. “I’ve a proposal, Sarah.”

“You seem different, Eydis.”

“I fought a Demon, and he injured me.”

“A demon capable of harming you?” Sarah stepped closer, “I thought I killed all those capable of it.”

“This one was old. Older than I am.”

Sarah’s eyes widened, then narrowed. She walked closer to the dwarf, studying the dents and scratches in armor that had never before been pierced, “What do you need, Eydis?”

“We have a common enemy, and I would like your help in killing her.” The Dwarf’s eyes followed the angel as the rest of her body stood still.

Sarah’s face twisted in hatred as she spat the next words. “A truce was called. You know why.”

“You’re going to let that Monster walk free? Already, her children are capable of fighting and killing yours.”

Sarah opened her mouth, then paused, her expression going blank as she studied the dwarf’s eyes. “Who are you?”

“I am-”

“Do not lie to me. You are not Mother Eydis.”

The Dwarf closed her mouth, and didn’t speak for a long moment. “Does it matter? Our enemies are the same. I will strike her in the depths, and you will take the skies from her.”

Sarah’s eyes narrowed, and her hand extended, gathering silver light to it, “I will kill her, but I do not need a parasite’s help. You will release Mother Eydis.”

The Dwarf’s face twisted as a tall dark spirit spread out above her, “Then there is only one path.”

The shadows twisted, then shot into Sarah.

Sarah’s wings flared, and a sword appeared in her hand, only to be shattered in the next moment.

The darkness invaded her, corrupting her mind, twisting her body. Her vision faded as her wings darkened and she fell to the floor, her mind leaving her. Slowly, she gave into the madness.

With it, no Monster could resist her. She could kill Risha, and the Orc who slaughtered her child.

She wouldn’t have to listen to the others. She would be more powerful, beyond their touch.

She could bring them back. She could see her lover’s eyes again.

All of it could be hers.

A wave of light filled the room, throwing the Dwarven Mother back, her body rolling across the floor.

Sarah screamed as the light filled her, pushing the darkness away. A matching scream echoed from the Dwarven Mother.

Another wave of light passed through the room as Sarah pushed herself to her feet. “Parasite. I will kill you, even if another Mother’s blood must dirty my hands.” Sarah called a silver spear to her, and drew it back, before throwing it into the armor of the downed dwarf.

The spear deflected into the wall as the Dwarven Mother slowly pushed herself to her feet. The darkness surrounding the dwarf pressed against the light that radiated from Sarah’s tree. A mad laugh echoed from the dwarf, filling the room. “Even the Mother closest to the darkness is shielded by light! HaHAhaHaha. You will not stop me! I will take them all!”

The creature that had taken over the Dwarven Mother snarled at Sarah, then disappeared.

Sarah attempted to trap it in her home, twisting the spirit realm.

She failed, and coughed blood, the spirit of the parasite eclipsing her own.

The light in the room flooded into Sarah as she fell to her knees, her wings softening the fall. The parasite had left her with pain, but the light of the tree pushed it away and out of her.

Sarah slowly pushed herself to her feet and pulled another spear to her as something approached her home.

A dragon filled her home, fangs bared, and claws sharp.

“You’re late, Iris.” Sarah said quietly.

The dragon disappeared as a woman with blue skin hurried to Sarah. Sarah collapsed into Iris’s arms. “It came here?”

“It thought it could deceive me.” Sarah said quietly.

“It failed.” Iris said, gently lowering her to the floor.

Sarah laughed, then slowly lost the humor as tears fell from her eyes. “I was tempted, Iris. For a moment, it had me.” She cried, her stomach twisting from her own weakness.
