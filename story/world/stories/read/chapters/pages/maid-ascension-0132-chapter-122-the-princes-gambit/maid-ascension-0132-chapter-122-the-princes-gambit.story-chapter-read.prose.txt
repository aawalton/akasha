From behind the veil, Lira watched Kavard watch Luxia dance with Lord Duval Sollaris Junior.

The girl was perfectly polite, nothing like the enthusiasm bordering on impropriety she had shown with Aric. Still, young Sollaris looked thoroughly smitten all the same.

Lira decided she should probably warn Aric that Kavard had arrived, especially once she noticed Sir Estophon wasn't with him.

She slipped out from behind the curtain and made her way toward where Aric and Deren stood, both of them having forgone the second dance. As she got closer, she caught the tail end of their conversation.

"So you really plan on going to the Holtary Mountains to find Almara?" Deren asked.

"Yes, next month, probably."

"Why next month?"

"I'm waiting for Kavard and Sir Estophon to leave for the countryside first," said Aric.

"Good thinking, that," Deren said. "There is no telling what he would get up to if he heard you weren't around."

"Yeah. He's been acting strange lately."

Lira decided that was her cue. She stepped close enough to touch both men on the shoulder and lowered her voice.

"He's here," she said. "Prince Kavard."

"What?" Deren turned sharply, his hand going instinctively to his belt, where his sword should have been but wasn't. Neither of them had come armed since both were in formal dress.

Before Lira could say anything more, the herald's staff struck the floor at the base of the mezzanine, twice, sharp enough to cut through the last bars of the dance.

"His Royal Highness," the herald called, "Crown Prince Kavard, son of Argon, First of His Name, Heir to the Throne of Haltar!"

The music stumbled and died. Every dancing pair on the floor stopped where they stood, turning toward the stairs.

Kavard came down slowly, one hand trailing along the banister as if he wasn't in any particular hurry. Why would he? It was the privilege of the people gathered here to wait for him. His suit was completely violet, accented with gold thread. Two royal guards followed a step behind him. None of them were Sir Estophon.

Lira saw Deren and Aric both go completely still at the sight of him.

Kavard reached the floor and spread both hands, an apologetic smile already in place.

"Forgive the intrusion," he said, his voice pitched to carry across the whole hall. "I was supposed to be occupied elsewhere this evening... a matter of state, I'm sure you understand, but when my plans fell through, I found I couldn't bear to miss one of the season's finest debuts."

"Baroness Valis." He inclined his head toward Lady Dalia, who curtsied so quickly and gracefully in response to his attention. "Your daughter's presentation, I was told, has been anticipated by most of the noble households in the capital."

He crossed to Luxia and took her hand, bowing over it with a courtliness that looked, from a distance, entirely genuine.

"Lady Luxia. You do the gown more credit than it does you." He lifted her wrist to his lips, and then glanced at the small ivory card dangling from it, and let his mouth curve into a chagrined smile. "Full already. I've arrived too late to even beg a place on the list."

"Your Highness." Lord James Evergreen stepped forward; he was standing to the left of Luxia, waiting for his turn to dance with her. "I would be honored to yield my dance to you."

He bowed low.

"Would you?" Kavard's smile widened, as if anyone could deny the crown prince what he wanted. "That's very good of you, Evergreen. I'll remember it."

Evergreen bowed again and gracefully stepped back into the crowd of watchers.

Lira didn't like any of it. Especially not the timing of this visit.

Kavard turned to the musicians and lifted two fingers. The next song began as the first prince led Luxia out onto the floor.

Lira turned, taking the opportunity to slip back through the curtain into the dim servants' room, where the noise of the ballroom dulled to a murmur and the servants' own hushed chatter rose to a hubbub. Though no one on the other side of the veil could see her lips move, she lifted a hand to her mouth anyway and whispered.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

Her hearing sharpened and narrowed at once. The sound of people around her and the music fell away, and the shuffle of dancing feet disappeared. Only one private conversation remained.

"I notice my brother Aric has been rather attentive to you lately," Kavard was saying.

"You think?" Luxia smiled, guileless. "I can hardly believe my luck, my lord."

"I heard the two of you had been dancing together even before tonight."

"We have, my lord. Only a few times for practice." Luxia blushed.

"You like him, don't you?"

Lira saw the shape of his eyes, cold and devoid of kindness. Luxia, still turning through the steps of the dance, saw only a prince smiling at her.

She nodded, shy and pleased with herself.

"Are you aware," Kavard said, as lightly as if he were remarking on the weather, "that he keeps a mistress?"

Luxia's feet stopped moving. For one full beat she simply stood there, the dance forgotten around her, before Kavard's arm turned her body back into the rhythm, his grip firm enough that she had no real choice but to follow.

"I take it from your face that you didn't know."

"That can't be true." Her voice had gone thin and unsteady. "Who?"

"One of the staff. The new maid who runs the library. With the orange hair and freckles, you would know her if you saw her."

Luxia went rigid a second time, and this time Kavard didn't bother forcing her back into step. The melody was already winding down toward its close, giving him the perfect excuse to let her go. Luxia didn't wait for the final note. She turned, and half ran for the dressing room, eyes already brimming with tears.

Baroness Dalia, who had been watching her daughter dance from the edge of the floor the entire time, was moving before the girl had gone three steps.

Lira stood very still behind the curtain and forced herself to think.

Why her? Why would Kavard drag Lira specifically into this? Out of every lie he could have told, why this?

It didn't take long to land on an answer: this had nothing to do with Lira, and little enough to do even with Luxia. Aric was the real target. Kavard had decided, rightly or wrongly, that his brother was interested in the noble girl, and he was petty enough to try to ruin it for him, as punishment for the crime of choosing to bring the deaths of the maids to their father instead of quietly burying it the way Kavard would have preferred. In Kavard's world, everything bad that had happened to him was now Aric's fault. Lira and Luxia right now were just collateral.

The safe thing to do was nothing. She could wait out the rest of the ball, let the lie sit, correct it later if the chance ever came. By any measure, it was the safer path.

It didn't sit right with her, though.

Through the curtain, she watched Aric and Deren finally approach Kavard, the three of them talking in clipped voices; she used the distraction to slip out of the servants' room and cross toward the dressing chambers instead.

She opened the door to find Luxia sobbing against her mother's shoulder. The moment the girl saw who had come in, her whole face changed; her wet eyes narrowed into an angry look that didn't suit her angelic face.

"How could you?" Luxia's voice cracked on it. "I trusted you."

"Luxia. I need you to listen to me. This matters more than you know."

"I am not listening to a word you say." She turned her face back into her mother's shoulder.

Lira weighed her options for a heartbeat; what she was about to say could risk everything she had built until now. She owed Luxia nothing...

No, that wasn't true; she owed her Salvek's death.

"You can choose to trust a man who raped three maids, got them pregnant, and then had them and their babies murdered to keep it quiet," Lira said, her voice sharp, "or you can trust me. Those are your two choices tonight."

Luxia lifted her head slowly, confusion breaking through the tears. Beside her, her mother's eyes had gone cold.

"That is a very serious accusation to level at the Crown Prince."

"Ask your son," Lira said. "He has seen the graves."

"Deren?" Luxia's voice wavered, caught between her grief and not wanting to believe Kavard's words were true. Lira's claim gave her a path where Aric could still be hers, the same Aric of her dreams and not a womanizing rake.

"Mara. Amelia. Carry." Lira said the names slowly, the way Deren had said them in front of a crowded arena. "Remember those names. Deren named all three when he killed Salvek, claiming them partly avenged."

Both women went quiet; of course they remembered that.

"Whatever Kavard told you tonight, whatever it was meant to do to you, understand that it came from a liar trying to hurt his own brother through you," Lira said. "Don't hand him the victory."

Something in Luxia's face steadied. "I won't," she said, and reached up and wiped her eyes, smearing the face powder further across her face. "Oh, look at the state of me."

"Sit still," Lira said, already moving. "I'll have you fixed in a minute."

Dalia watched them both while Lira worked, silent for a long moment before she finally spoke.

"How does a library caretaker come to know all this, when even I was never told?"

Lira kept her hands steady on the powder brush and gave her the truth, at least partly. "Some of Lord Atropos's old ledgers ended up in my library after his death. I was the one who noticed the maids' records didn't add up, and I brought it to Celene."

She hesitated, making sure to let the hesitation show on her face.

"No one outside Celene knows I'm the one who found it. I would like to keep it that way, my lady. I don't want to end up the next one buried in a shallow grave."

Dalia held her gaze for a long moment before she gave a single nod.

"I will not let him hurt you," Luxia said, and for the first time all evening her voice held nothing shy about it.

"Thank you," Lira said, smiling at the girl.

A few minutes later, Luxia swept back out onto the floor looking every bit as radiant as she had before, and picked her dance back up with Sir Liam without missing a beat. From the safety of the dressing room doorway, Lira watched Kavard's frown deepen as the girl he had expected to find broken met his eyes challengingly across the hall, her chin up and her face resolute.

He didn't stay for the rest of the ball. The moment he saw Luxia laughing on Aric's arm during the next set, he turned and left without ceremony, his guards trailing after him.

Lira let herself smile at that, satisfied; seeing Kavard defeated was worth a little risk. Hopefully she wouldn't regret her decision tonight.
