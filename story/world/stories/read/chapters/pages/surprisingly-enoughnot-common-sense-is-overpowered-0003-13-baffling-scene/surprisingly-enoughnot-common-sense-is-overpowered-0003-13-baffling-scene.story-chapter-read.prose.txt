Ning stood before the mirror, studying his reflection. It wasn't out of vanity or self-admiration, though he had to admit this body was quite easy on the eyes. No, he was here for a small experiment.

"Panel."

A translucent blue panel materialized before him, but he didn't spare it a glance. His attention remained fixed on the mirror.

Reflected there was an unassuming, good-looking young man with pale skin and warm blue eyes.

Ning let out a sigh of relief.

The panel wasn't reflected.

Most people would've simply assumed that no one else could see it. After all, if it really was his cheat, that seemed like the obvious conclusion. Ning, however, wasn't the sort of person to leave something important to an assumption. There were already too many unknowns surrounding his transmigration and the mysterious panel, so verifying even the smallest details was simply common sense.

Just imagining himself casually summoning a floating blue screen in public, only to discover that everyone around him could see it, was enough to give him a headache.

"Well, that's one less thing to worry about," Ning muttered with a faint smile.

With that concern put to rest, he straightened his robes and walked toward the cave entrance.

Today marked the first true step of his journey.

"Alright. Let's see what being a cultivator is actually like."

...

Ning raised a hand to shield his eyes as warm sunlight poured over him. After spending an entire week inside a cave, even ordinary daylight felt uncomfortably bright.

He blinked several times until his vision adjusted.

You know, locking teenagers inside caves for a week sounds very questionable when you stop and think about it.

Fortunately, the sect wasn't completely unreasonable.

Before sealing him inside, they had given him a fasting pill.

It was a remarkable little thing, capable of sustaining the body for an entire month without food or water. In his previous world, a medicine like this would've rewritten human history overnight. Here, however, it was little more than a common convenience that every cultivator took for granted.

As Ning made his way down the mountain path, he quickly discovered his first genuine problem since arriving in this world.

He had absolutely no idea where he was going.

No one had thought to give the new disciples a tour before locking them inside caves to cultivate. In hindsight, perhaps that should've been expected.

His gaze swept across the winding paths until it settled on a young man dressed in grey robes, sweeping fallen leaves nearby.

"Let's see..." Ning recalled the elder's explanation during the entrance ceremony. "Black robes are for inner disciples, white for outer disciples, green for peripheral disciples, and grey for servants."

Perfect. It was worth going over the memories relentlessly.

Ning had spent part of the past week practicing the unfamiliar language, speaking aloud to himself until the words came naturally and he no longer worried about sounding strange. He'd also tried to familiarize himself with the local customs and organize everything he knew about the sect, going over the information repeatedly until he felt reasonably confident.

Now, at least, that preparation was proving useful.

Ning walked over to the young man. "Excuse me."

The servant jerked so violently that the broom nearly slipped from his hands. He spun around, his face paling the instant he saw Ning's green robes.

"T-this servant greets Lord Cultivator!" he stammered, lowering his head. "H-how may this servant be of service?"

Ning blinked.

So scared? I knew this was common, but isn't that a bit of an overreaction?

The grey-robed servants were mortals accepted by the sect to handle the countless mundane chores that cultivators couldn't be bothered with—cleaning courtyards, washing robes, preparing meals, carrying supplies, and whatever else needed doing. They possessed no spiritual roots and, for almost all of them, no future on the path of cultivation.

It sounded cruel by modern standards, but here, such a hierarchy was considered perfectly normal.

Judging by how the young man was trembling simply because Ning had spoken to him, though, this probably wasn't his first unpleasant encounter with a peripheral disciple. Some people, regardless of the world, seemed to believe that possessing even a little authority entitled them to throw their weight around.

Ning deliberately softened his voice. "I'm looking for the General Affairs Hall. Could you point me in the right direction?"

The servant hurriedly nodded. "Y-yes! It's... it's in the center of the outer sect. Please follow this road until you reach the main plaza, then head east."

Ning glanced down the path before nodding. "Thank you."

The servant froze. "I-it's my pleasure, Lord Cultivator."

By the time Ning glanced back, the young man was already hurrying away, repeatedly looking over his shoulder as though half-expecting Ning to call him back.

Ning couldn't help shaking his head.

Being polite had cost him nothing, and he wasn't so arrogant that treating someone with basic courtesy somehow wounded his pride. Besides, this was a strange world. The possibility of this servant becoming some hidden super-expert was low, but it wasn't zero.

More importantly, Ji Ning's memories contained no shortage of stories about people making mortal enemies over the smallest slights. Ning had no intention of becoming one of them simply because he'd been too proud to say please and thank you.

Making enemies for no reason was just a bad survival strategy.

With that thought, Ning leisurely made his way toward the General Affairs Hall.

Before he could reach the entrance, however, he noticed a small crowd gathered nearby.

Ning slowed his steps.

He still knew very little about how cultivators actually behaved outside of Ji Ning's memories. Watching them interact firsthand seemed like a good opportunity to learn more.

With that thought in mind, he quietly made his way toward the edge of the crowd.

"Xiao Fan!" a tall youth shouted, pointing an accusing finger at the young man before him. "Why haven't you paid Brother Xiao Hong's protection fee? Are you looking down on Brother Xiao Hong?"

"Protection fee? Just say it outright. You're trying to extort me!" Xiao Fan retorted, clenching his fists.

The tall youth's face twisted with anger. "Looks like the last beating wasn't enough to teach you your place!"

He lunged forward without another word, driving a heavy kick straight into Xiao Fan's stomach.

Bang!

Xiao Fan staggered back several steps, clutching his abdomen as pain flashed across his face. Even so, he refused to lower his head. Instead, he slowly straightened and glared at the group with unwavering determination.

"You may be stronger than me today," he declared through gritted teeth, "but don't bully the young and poor! One day, I'll make every one of you repay this a hundredfold!"

The surroundings fell silent.

Standing at the edge of the crowd, Ning stared at Xiao Fan.

Protection fees. Arrogant lackeys. A defiant underdog telling everyone not to bully the young and poor...

Ning's eyes lingered on Xiao Fan for a few seconds.

He had only been in this world for a week, yet somehow, this was the most familiar thing he'd seen since arriving.
