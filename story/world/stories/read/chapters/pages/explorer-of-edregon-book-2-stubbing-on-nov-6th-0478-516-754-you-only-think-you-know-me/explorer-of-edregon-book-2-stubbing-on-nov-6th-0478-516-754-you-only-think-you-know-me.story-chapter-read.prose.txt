“Allow me to apologize for the tense greeting,” the Goddess of Benevolence said, her smile magically calming Scule down as she turned to look at them all. “Ovord, are you alright?”

“That actually went better than some of the conversations I have had with Contracts in the past,” the soul mage said, brushing off his suit as he straightened up. Contracts’ chains that had been binding him had vanished right alongside the God, freeing him from his prison. “I don’t believe you and I have met, however. Sympathy?”

“Benevolence,” the Goddess smiled, and Ovord nodded.

“That makes more sense. You seem more specific.”

“I’m sorry… How in the world do you know the Gods like this?” Vin asked, staring at Ovord in an entirely new light. “And what exactly does being a Candidate entail?”

Ovord looked like he wanted to answer, before thinking better of it. Instead, he turned and raised an eyebrow toward Benevolence in an unspoken question.

“Yes, I will permit you to share whatever you feel is necessary with them,” Benevolence said, earning a gasp of surprise from the other two Gods.

“Hold on,” Loyalty said, frowning for the first time since her arrival. Her robe turned a dangerous, dark red, and she held up a hand toward Ovord to give him pause. “Benevolence, we only agreed to help you spare these mortals’ lives. Revealing details about the Candidates to mortals who have not even hit their third prestige classes… This is going a bit far, don’t you think?”

“What is the point of creating an entirely new world if we are still bound by the rules of the old?” Benevolence argued, slowly shaking her head and causing her drifting hair to bob about around her. “Keeping people in the dark is not a kindness. They should know the truth, even if it scares them.”

“I for one sort of like being ignorant and not scared,” Scule called out unhelpfully from the side, earning a glare from the rest of them.

“The rest of us would prefer the truth,” Shia said, staring defiantly at the God of Devotion who had protected her from Contracts. “My master always stressed that all knowledge was power, regardless of how useful it was. I want to know what Ovord has to say.”

“I do as well,” Lumel nodded. Walking over to Vin, she took his hand in her own. “This is obviously important, and I don’t want it to be another secret that only Vin knows but can’t share with us.”

Reginald squeaked out his agreement, glaring at Scule and causing the petian to sigh as he raised his hand.

“Okay, I take it back, I want to know too! Sheesh!”

“I can’t say I think this is the right idea… but I will not interfere,” Devotion said. Shaking his head, he turned and walked into the void, vanishing the moment he set foot into the darkness. “Good luck, young ones.”

“If they weren’t so loyal to one another, I’d put a stop to this,” Loyalty said, bending down and patting Reginald lovingly atop the head with a finger. After a pause, she turned and nodded toward Scule, reaching out and resting a finger upon his head as well. “You did well borrowing the power of my divine boon from your companion to save his life. I am proud of you.”

With that, her body ignited and she vanished in a burst of flame. Scule simply stood there, staring up with wide eyes at the spot where the Goddess had once stood.

“How long are you able to hold us here?” Ovord asked, before spotting the unspoken question in Vin’s eyes. “This void is a special place usually only accessible by the Gods. It is the only place certain bindings built into the System itself can be ignored, such as those that prevent higher prestiges from sharing information about what they are experiencing with lower prestiges.”

“On my own, not very long,” Benevolence admitted. “This single moment has already stretched on for some time. At best you have a couple of minutes.”

“Then I will endeavor to speak quickly,” Ovord said. Clearing his throat, he turned to address all of them. “As you already know, I am Ovord Soulseeker, former Arch Mage of the Soul. What you did not know before today is that prior to being ambushed by my peers and trapped within that orb, I was a Candidate to become a God. And not just any God. I was a Candidate to the God of Contracts specifically.”

“What?!” all of them other than Alka gasped. Even Reginald squeaked in shock, but the Slayer looked largely unimpressed.

“So what, is it like some sort of apprenticeship thing?”

“No,” Ovord said, shaking his head. “The title of Candidate is bestowed upon any and all who manage to reach level 80. A feat that is not nearly as simple as it sounds, I assure you. Given enough time, anyone can manage to reach level 60. It is at the third prestige where the mortals and the potential divine truly start to separate.”

“Though that is not the case any longer,” Benevolence chimed in, causing Ovord to blink in surprise. “As we have gone from a universe filled with thousands of different worlds to only one, our selection of potential Candidates is far more limited than it once was. To try and combat this, we altered the new System in an attempt to make any potential Candidates more pure than they once were. We believed this would make them even stronger down the line. Far fewer people will be able to reach level 60, but those who do should be able to keep growing faster than ever.”

“The requirements one needs to meet in order to advance to the next prestige,” Lumel muttered, nodding along as if it all made sense.

“I did not realize that was something that had been changed,” Ovord admitted, watching Benevolence carefully. “Are the tiers the same?”

“Yes. The structure of increasing one’s power remains identical to how it once was,” she nodded. “No changes there.”

“Good. As you all are already within your second prestige, you already know about class abilities,” Ovord continued. “These are cleverly disguised methods of the first real step toward eventual divinity that mortals experience. Gifts of mana itself solidified via the System. Think of it as akin to spoon feeding eventual Gods.”

“So what does the third prestige bring?” Shia asked, eagerly hanging onto Ovord’s every word.

“It is difficult to explain without having experienced it for yourself,” Ovord admitted. “The best way to describe it would be a conceptual power fueled via an additional resource. This can be internal or external, mind you.”

Ovord paused, looking around the void and clearly noting that he’d lost them. His lips pressed thin and he sighed.

“Think of it as a power beyond one’s own mana that they can harness to do impossible things. Even non-mages are capable of doing so. Leveling at this stage will involve the collection and subsequent use of this resource, as doing so brings one closer to the eventual God they could potentially become.”

“Wait… Vin, you mentioned how you thought the ranker king was utilizing his own honor like some sort of resource, didn’t you?” Alka pointed out, scratching her head as she thought. “Is it like that?”

“I do not know of whom you speak, but presumably, yes,” Ovord nodded. “Honor is just one example. There is truly no limit to what the resource could potentially be at this point. Mine was souls.”

Everyone stared at him, and Ovord rolled his eyes.

“As in souls changed or touched. I did not collect them like some sort of twisted vampire.”

“You said you were a Candidate of the God of Contracts specifically,” Vin said, feeling like he could practically sense the seconds ticking by. He knew they didn’t have much time left, and he was desperate for answers before Benevolence had to let them go. “What did you mean by that? Like you would be his replacement?”

“As a master soul mage, I was responsible for the creation of countless soul bonds throughout my life,” Ovord explained. “Soul bonds are nothing more than a carefully constructed contract, which made me an appropriate match for the God of Contracts.”

“This is difficult to explain as not even we Gods fully understand it, but becoming a God is not actually the final step of the journey,” Benevolence said with a small smile. “Eventually, after enough millennia have passed, even Gods tend to grow weary. But before they are allowed to pass on, they must find a suitable Candidate to take their place, to prevent a vacuum of power from occurring. However, not all Candidates are specifically chosen for such a feat. Many ascend to Godhood and become entirely brand-new Gods, taking on new tasks for themselves and sharing the burden of a few others.”

“Wait, the God of Contracts… one of the strongest Gods in all of existence… wants to retire?” Scule asked, looking blown away by the revelation.

“Functionally, yes,” Ovord confirmed. “I was not his only Candidate, mind you. There are countless things that can go wrong between someone reaching level 80 and achieving Godhood, as my own experience would attest to. I do not know what happened to his other Candidates seeing as he is still serving as a God, but I can only assume they failed him one way or another.”

“It is an unfortunate truth that the God of Contracts is tired, and that fatigue is clearly influencing him,” Benevolence said. Her smile and floating hair drooped in sync as she sighed. “But he cannot help what he is. When most Gods reach this point, they are more flexible. They find a Candidate who fits their role well enough, and then trust the rest of the Gods to take over the slack and make it work while they pass on. As you all no doubt saw, Contracts is far too strict for anything like that. His replacement must be perfect, and thus he has refused to step down, despite his ever-growing fatigue. Two of his Candidates did actually end up reaching Godhood over the last few centuries, but they did not meet his satisfaction. Instead, they became new Gods, granting us the God of Promises and the Goddess of Fealty respectively. The other Candidates he’d been waiting on died one way or another. I probably shouldn’t say this, but Ovord was one of his biggest hopes.”

“I was?” Ovord asked, looking surprised at the admittance. “He never acted like it. Our few conversations often ended with him scolding me for missing certain minor things in my soul bonds. The excitement of having drawn a God’s attention on myself quickly grew old.”

“Wait, where do divine classes fall into all this?” Shia asked with a frown. “My master has to be one of these Candidates, right? I never asked him for his level, but he has to have hit that point.”

“Your master and divine classes are two separate things,” Benevolence explained. “Think of divine classes as gifts we used to give to people we thought had promise from a very young age. We’d lend them our power to give them an edge, but we would then revoke the divine power when they reached level 60, to allow them to come into their own. Your master…” Benevolence sighed, looking like she was choosing her words carefully. “…He’s what we call a nonconformist. He figured out the entire purpose of the System early on and decided he had no interest in becoming a God. He probably would have already ascended if he so chose.”

“Wait, it’s not just you hit level 100 and bam, Godhood?” Scule asked.

“Not necessarily, though that is what is most common. Level 80 is the only hard requirement that has to be met,” Benevolence admitted. “After that point, there are a number of factors that influence when and how one ascends, most of them relying entirely upon the person in question. Though I could count on both hands the number of people I’ve witnessed over my incredibly long life who have managed to ascend before level 90. It is beyond rare.”

The void surrounding them began to shake and flicker, and Benevolence gave them all a sad smile.

“While I wish I could answer all of your questions, I am afraid we will have to stop there for now. And while I may have managed to surprise Contracts this time by grabbing the aid of Loyalty and Devotion, Contracts will be ready for such a trick next time. I’m sorry to say this is most likely the last bit of information you will get from me for some time. Before you leave, you must promise me one thing. Contracts was not wrong about the danger of you messing with the new System,” Benevolence said, staring hard at Vin specifically as if she knew his answer was the one that mattered most. “Promise me you will not attempt to touch upon the nucleus of the System ever again. Should you try to repeat your actions here today, I believe Contracts will kill you. Assuming you don’t blow yourself and the entire fragment you’re standing on up.”

“I promise,” Vin nodded, more than happy to agree to such terms.

“Good,” the Goddess said. Her smile returned in full force as she gave them all a friendly wave before turning toward Alka. “With that, let me be the first to give you the greeting you should have received today instead of all this insanity.

“Welcome back.”
