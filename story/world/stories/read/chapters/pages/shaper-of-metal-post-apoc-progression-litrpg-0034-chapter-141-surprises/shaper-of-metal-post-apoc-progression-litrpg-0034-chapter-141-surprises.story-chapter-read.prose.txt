Jack flew like the wind on his metal platform, his hands tightly gripped on the railing — he might be able to do this type of shit himself, but he wasn’t used to it by any means.

I need to get there, though.

The platform took him relatively in the middle between the edge and the center of the wide area, and up into an opening hatch. After passing through multiple body lengths of metal like a vertical corridor, he found himself unceremoniously smack in the middle of his new home. The platform opened up somewhat like a direct elevator within a small cylindrical tower, near a spiral staircase going up, which was probably an exit.

His mouth dropped open at the size of the area — there were larger houses out there, but this was a full-sized condo at least, far bigger than he could’ve dreamed. The design was clearly architecturally custom, a bit fancy yet classy, but not overboard for his tastes, with white wallpaper prevalent, combined with golden-brown, wooden accents, and a bit of thoughtful asymmetry to avoid blandness. There was a massive living room with two sitting areas. One was a full circle that could seat a preposterous number of people, another was separated and faced a vidscreen wall, cultivating a cozy feel.

At first, Jack thought he’d been somehow teleported elsewhere, because a full third of the place, mostly wrapped around the living room, appeared to look through windows directly out on a mostly dark, mountainous Antarctic horizon. Quickly, he realized it was an ‘outside’, massive holographic display, but the deception was truly convincing, and he thought it was a live feed going. There was even a false balcony! It had seating!

Ignoring everything else temporarily, Jack ran over to it, sliding a glass door aside to walk out.

“Oh my goddess,” Jack muttered, shaking his head in disbelief, because he felt wind, heat, and a change in the air, as if someone had hyper-analyzed how to simulate fresh air and then tediously reproduced it with hidden vents and holographic tricks.

Laughing in delight as he leaned over the balcony railing and marveled at the feeling it gave, he sobered a bit when he realized why this sort of thing was here.

I’m encased in her steel womb, protected on all sides. This is probably where she prefers us to be. Meanwhile, some of us can never feasibly have a home out there. There are barriers, regardless. Memoria has every reason to make these facilities as pleasant as possible.

The view, and the whole cultivated experience, was certainly real enough and desirable enough to convince yourself that it was. Jack supposed that was therapeutic.

Walking back in, leaving the ‘door’ open, Jack took a self-tour through his new pad, oohing and ahhing at the grandeur and the fanciness of the brand-new, grinning continuously, feeling like he now lived in the luxury suite of a top-tier old-world hotel — something he mostly only knew about from movies. New Babylon had some, both on the seamier side and the fancy, whether for niche functions or as getaways, but they weren’t everywhere, like in the legacy Earth, where everyone was traveling all over the world all the time.

There were places like this where high-rankers could stay in fashion abroad, but he’d certainly never been. All his quarters had been utilitarian, made for sleep and getting in and out of in 8-12 hours' time, depending on schedule mandates.

All of Jack’s stuff was already inside the condo, as promised, though some of it was crammed into a tucked-away, large storage area like a room unto itself. The message was clear: if you want the ‘feng shui’ ruined by your personal trash, you’ll have to commit that sin yourself.

Honestly, it really is too beautiful and balanced to screw with willy-nilly. I might need to consult the Memorial Daughters if I want to incorporate some of my shit… pictures, for example.

When he checked the closet, he was at first somewhat confused to see another ANP blue longcoat, but then he realized it was the Jackette, newly updated in color to the official blue.

Jack laughed as he pulled it out. “Well, look at you! Lookin’ sharp, sweetheart.”

The Jackette suddenly did something to the effect of ‘blushing’ bashfully, curling an arm up to its lapels. This made Jack jump back in surprise, as the Jackette continued floating in the air.

I didn’t do that.

Mini chimed. “Memoria wanted to surprise you — probably specifically to see that look on your face right now. She updated the Jackette, as promised. This is version 1.1, optimized as much as possible for travel abroad. If you’re able, she wants more testing and input for a final update before you leave. In any case, because she couldn’t resist, it includes some rudimentary autonomy, particularly personality-wise. It will currently shut off somewhere outside of her range, going dormant, but she’s working on that for the next version, depending on your input.”

Jack continued staring at the Jackette. It waved at him emphatically. He gulped. I asked for this, didn’t I? “Okay then. Uh, hi.”

The Jackette did a grand shrug, as if to admonish him, because, well, of course they were already acquainted.

Jack rubbed the back of his head. “So, uh, I guess I can just wear this thing from now on, for the most part?”

“That’s right, Jack,” Mini replied. “The PACCs are history, and your undercover days might be over for a while. Considering she’ll be wrapped around you, aren’t you glad you made it a girl?”

The Jackette did a floppy, tossed-hands motion that signaled exasperation.

Jack just shook his head and sighed — ironically, this put him in sync with the Jackette’s apparent feelings on the matter. “I believe the term would be ‘female,’ but I’m also not going there. Is there some reason it doesn’t have a voice?”

“Can you guess? In addition to creative license, you generally are supposed to direct the Jackette with your powers. The autonomy is for flavor and secondary use when she happens to be separated from you.”

“Mmn. I guess…”

“She may be silent, but she’s mighty.”

The Jackette flexed its ‘biceps,’ which shaped themselves and ballooned out comically.

Jack squinted his eyes, not sure how amused he was by all of this yet, though Memoria either was or would be in stitches, he was certain.

With popcorn ready, no less.

He proceeded to take off his normal jacket, somewhat reluctant to be potentially retiring the thing from active service, yet feeling a contrasting happiness that the original ‘prize’ would remain hypothetically pristine.

He hung it up in the closet and began untightening his dress uniform. Glancing at the Jackette, he commanded, “Go wait for me in the living room. Enjoy the view, maybe? Not sure if that applies.”

It saluted him with a sleeve and flew off, thoughtfully closing the bedroom door behind it.

Internally, Jack asked, “What’s next?”

Mini replied, “Hang tight, eat, relax a bit. You’re on light standby; nothing crazy without unforeseen emergencies. You’re good for at least thirty minutes, but be ready to respond and move in fifteen minutes at any given time after that.”

“Roger that.”

Jack changed out of his dress uniform and went into relaxation mode, moving to the kitchen to try out the slick new amenities. It was stocked, stacked, and had room to spare, with a prodigious marble-topped island also with storage space and hooks to hang stuff on. It was significantly nicer than his former quarters in Everywhere Hall.

I still can’t believe this place is mine. I may not even have time for it to sink in before I leave it for a while.

He cooked up some egg, sausage, and cheese sandwiches and ate on the balcony where the Jackette was hanging out, which was apparently ‘gazing’ off at the horizon.

“So, you like the view, huh?” Jack asked as he ate.

The Jackette vigorously nodded its lapels.

“Me, too. I look forward to traveling again.”

The Jackette did an emphatic cheer of sorts, pumping its sleeves high in the air.

Jack chuckled and ate in silence for a while. Finally, he asked, “So, are you a she?” It shrugged. “Something else?” It shrugged. “Mmn. Oddly, the ambiguity probably works better.”

The Jackette dusted its sleeves together.

“Yeah, point taken.”

Jack was sitting in front of the massive viewscreen watching random channels with the Jackette, when a notice came in, communicated via Mini, but with the feel of being sent to many in some form. <Agent Ferro, you are called to attend a briefing in fifteen minutes at Tower Room A-7. Your Commons interface will bring you there when ready. Please refrain from querying other agents about this or discussing it, as the briefing is for a smaller, highly specialized task force and mission.>

“Here we go, then,” Jack muttered as he hopped up quickly and made for the shower.

After dressing in a standard uniform, Jack made for the platform, with the Jackette settling around him along the way. He was soon zooming down and across the Commons zone for one of the towers, and near the top, to an area blocked by many dark windows. When he exited inside by an opening alcove, he noticed two others coming out of separate alcoves, indicating a very large number could enter the place at the same time, and quickly.

The room inside was circular and screamed ‘war room’ — there was a huge circular table of many comfy-looking executive seats, each with a viewscreen popping up from the table in front of it, and the center area was dominated by a large holographic projector, currently showing a clock countdown above the bronze-colored mushhushshu symbol of the Exemplars. Jack stared at it with new appreciation.

I’m really one of them. It’s legit my symbol, now, because I’m completely representing it as a full member.

The windows were not just blacked from the outside for this meeting — thick locking panels that must normally retract were blocking them from the inside too, in what made for an environment that felt like it was entirely sealed off and ‘everything-proof.’

Jack was early but saw a collective of familiar faces already present and standing around, including Lighthouse, Highfive, Délight, Sato, Conflux, Bermuda… and, sitting down like only two were so far, Ira. She was sitting next to InSite.

Ira smiled and waved at him enthusiastically. He smiled and nodded back.

Is this ‘it’? The gathering for going abroad? Now I’m suddenly anxious to see Bo among them.

Despite that thought, Jack moved to greet everyone amiably. A smiling Lighthouse called, “How’s those new quarters, newbie?!” to which Jack and several others made appreciative expressions and noises.

Jack made his way to shake hands with Sato, asking, “So, here we are, do you think? That journey you mentioned.”

Sato nodded seriously. “I do.” He glanced at the others briefly, those deductive eyes working. He spoke almost in a whisper, “Quite a ragtag crew so far, but there’s a lot of versatility. I’m seeing the sort of specialists that scream of cramming too much in one can, at least for a small team.” His eyes shifted over to Ira, but he said, “I’m surprised Mother would risk InSite outside the core, especially with the shit going on. And Délight?” His eyebrows raised significantly.

Jack nodded slowly, pursing his lips. Délight’s social engineering was deeply beneficial at home. But Jack had some ideas about why she might be included, even beyond the fact that she had a buff with a massive radius now, that Sato probably didn’t know about yet.

He also doesn’t know just how important this mission is. These people… frag me, they’re in for one hell of an awakening today, depending on how much is going to be shared.

Highfive walked up and shook hands with Sato, introducing himself, a little bit too obviously carrying himself like a fresh-faced, awkward noob. “Agent Highfive, here! Don’t think I’ve had the pleasure.”

Sato nodded neutrally up at the big man. “Sato. Welcome to the Agentus, Rook. Can’t say this looks like a gentle first mission, so get ready to get tossed into the deep end like no one ever has before.”

With a statement like that, he really is psychic…

Highfive looked dumbfounded. “How did you know?”

Sato glanced at Jack briefly, his expression grave, his poker face absolute. “If I told you that, I’d have to kill ya, and seeing your size, kid, I’d rather not make a carcass out of you, to trouble the help that would have to drag you out.”

Highfive blanched. “I, uh-... Sorry, sir.” He cleared his throat and looked away.

Sato nodded gravely, and then turned his head sideways, smiled, and reached a hand to clap Highfive on the arm. “I’m joking, kid! Ha. Just a little rookie hazing. I’m an old bastard set in my ways, sue me.”

Wide-eyed relief overtook the big man. Hands going to his hips, he exhaled a held-in breath. “Phew! Man, you had me going, sir!”

“No need for sir. Agent, if anything. I’m actually not even a full ANP, if you can believe it. Just a rather high-ranked Exemplar.”

“No way! You’re kidding me, again.”

“No, actually. You might not be aware, but…”

Jack slid away from the convo, certain Sato was about to give the lovable doofus a rundown on the low-powered ‘Quirked’ people, as he had given to Jack once.

He approached Ira and InSite sitting and chatting, to which they both cut off and directed different sorts of smiles at him.

“The jacket looks great!” Ira said. “Did you like your new quarters?!”

InSite’s grin was more of a knowing smirk as he studied Jack judiciously.

Jack had trouble not constantly glancing at the staring InSite, but he replied to Ira, “Everything is great! So far, it all makes me feel like a million lux, and it’s difficult to believe, and wow, is the holographic design on point, not to mention the architectural taste and — excuse me, Agent InSite, but what’s with that look, dude?! Spit it out!”

His emphasis and his eye contact seemed to suggest he knew a lot more than most already. Ira’s widened eyes and pursed lips as she side-eyed the man largely told the rest.

And then Neex communicated through their bond, too. <Yes, Jack, he knows. Memoria gave him a preliminary rundown after his suspicions. It’s difficult to hide from this one. He also, uh, kinda got me talking about you, and so he knows that aspect, too. Not sure how he could tell, but when he asked me directly if we were ‘romantic,’ I just blurted out that we were boyfriend-girlfriend. Aside from that, children have to annoy their mothers more than the other way around, don’t you think? To say nothing of siblings. Sato, I kinda wish you’d tell me, but the exclusive feeling of being in the ‘as knowledgeable and observant as Sato club’ is just too good.

An agent even older than the others, Jetset, showed up, grinning in his usual cocky way and holding up his hands as if to forestall a stampede. “People, people, if you want autographs? After the show, okay? Okay.”

Keep the faith, eh? There’s more truth with us than with them, even if we’re scraping it off the bottom of the barrel. If a sum’bitch like me can say it, you better take it to the bank.”

“I appreciate it.” And he did, little that he expected it from the arrogant, jaded veteran.

Ira sent a warm hug through the bond, which he accepted, though he felt guilty about it. His dad was the one ‘detained’ by him, after all.

At least he’s safe. That’s what counts, damn it.

One of them, a young brunette woman who looked haggard and bone weary, made Jack do a double take, because he thought he recognized her, though he couldn’t quite place her, even as her eyes swept over his, smiling in recognition and waving awkwardly.
