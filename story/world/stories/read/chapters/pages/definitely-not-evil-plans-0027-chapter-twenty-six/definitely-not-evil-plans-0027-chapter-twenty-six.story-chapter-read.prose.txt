Friday November, 3rd

Greg’s denial came out Friday morning. I was eating breakfast (which was a word that for me meant, ‘black coffee and maybe a protein bar’) when my phone buzzed with a news alert.

Greg Harrison Jr of Harrison & Sons Design Claims Confession Under Duress.

I clicked through the article with a sinking feeling. Greg had hired a crisis management firm to clear his name. His statements were polished, practiced and full of righteous indignation. The confession was forced upon him by a hacker. The hacker group was holding his family and companies secure documents hostage in exchange for a specific statement and incriminating documentation without context to be sent to various news groups.

Shit. Shit. Shit.

The article noted that forensic experts were examining the servers at H&S to look for signs of entry. The documents in the leak were apparently not truly damning without context, and the context was being skewed. Greg was putting the blame on hackers who take advantage of businesses only to throw whole companies to the wolves without concern for the employees they claimed to be fighting for.

Greg also was claiming that he was willing to send out this email to mitigate damage to his employees lives and that he was doing everything in his power to ensure that no employee, current or past would be negatively impacted by this.

The reporter made sure to note at the end of the article that no response had been received from anyone the reporters reached out to and that no one named in the files had come forward at all. It also mentioned that Greg would be pursuing legal action towards anyone contributing to this libel.

Of course they hadn’t. They’d signed NDAs, they'd been threatened, and they’d taken money and tried to move forward with their lives. And now they were sure to not, he was practically threatening anyone who was thinking of speaking up.

I threw my phone across the room. It bounced off of the couch and landed with a thud on the floor. The screen appeared to be cracked but at this point I didn’t care. I stared for a long moment breathing hard as my vision darkened and the screeching began to ramp up in my ears. The longer I sat there the more I could feel the code pulsing around me.

He was going to get away with it. He was going to hire enough people to muddy the waters and create discourse that he was going to come out looking like a victim and a savior to his plummeting company. Then once people forgot, he’d go back to doing whatever he wanted to whomever he wanted to again.

Unless.

I walked across the room and picked up my phone. My finger tracing the crack along the screen. I could see the places in the code which showed the cracked glass and as my finger slid across the surface I began knitting it back together.

I could stop him. I could find more evidence, I could try to scare him again, I could do something he couldn’t explain away. I could burn his entire world to the ground so completely that there was nothing left to salvage. I had the power to do so.

But… I’d promised Kate. I took a breath and sighed. No more revenge.

I was in the middle of assembling a bookshelf that afternoon when my phone rang. Kate’s name and photo filled my screen.

“Hey!” I said cheerfully. “You ready for our night out on the town?”

“You did it.” Her voice was angry. “You did all of it. However you got into Daniel’s stuff, however you edited that slideshow. Now, you’re threatening people?”

My stomach dropped. “Kate? Slow down. What are you–”

“No.” She cut me off and took a shaky breath. “Don’t lie to me. I just got off the phone with my cousin who works at channel 7. She was worried considering some of the names on the list, Sam. You know, the list of names that wasn’t publicized.

“Kate...”

“There were names, Sam. Names they were trying to verify before releasing anything. One of those names was Jessica Hudson.”

I closed my eyes. This was not good. My slip up from yesterday was coming back to bite me.

“Jessica,” Kate continued. “Who you mentioned at the coffee shop with such conviction before you walked it back. You knew Jessica had been hurt. How did you know? Did she tell you? Have you talked to her?”

“No, Kate, I haven’t talked to Jessica. I–”

“And you know what else was weird. Your name wasn’t anywhere on that list.”

“Why would my name be on the list? He never assaulted me.”

“That’s the whole of it. Nowhere in his confession letter did it mention him assaulting anyone. All documentation, even implying such, never made it to the news without verifying. So how did you know? How did you know about Jessica and him using his position to assault women?”

I didn’t have an answer. I probably should have said that I’d read it in the comments. I should have come up with any excuse but I couldn’t bring myself to even speak.

“I trusted you,” She spat at me. “I believed you when you said you were letting this go. I defended you to myself over and over again. I told myself that I was being paranoid, that my headaches were causing me to overthink, that you would never lie to me again.”

“Kate,” I begged. “Please listen to me. I was trying to help. Greg is a monster Kate, like a serial killer level monster. You have only seen a fraction of what he keeps on his home laptop.  He deserves–”

“I don’t care what he deserved. I care that you lied to me. I care that you shut me out and that you’re doing something illegal that I can’t even explain. I care that you drug me into it, that you’re dragging all of those poor women back into it. Women who may have moved on with their lives, now having to see their tormentor on every news channel. My job is on the line, and for all I know, you’ll be in a federal prison before Thanksgiving.”

“Kate, please.”

“No. I’m done, Sam.” Her voice broke. “I can’t keep doing this. I can’t keep wondering what’s real and what isn’t. I can’t keep worrying that you’re getting involved with dangerous  criminals and what that could mean for you, or me or anyone else.”

She let out a choking sob.

“I need you to leave me alone Sam. Just stay away from me.”

“Kate.”

The line went dead. I called back but it went straight to voicemail. I called back, same thing.

Me: Please just let me explain.

The message never marked as delivered.

So, I sat on the floor of my apartment, surrounded by bookshelf parts. Unlike this shelf, unlike my phone, I didn’t know if I would be able to be put back together.
