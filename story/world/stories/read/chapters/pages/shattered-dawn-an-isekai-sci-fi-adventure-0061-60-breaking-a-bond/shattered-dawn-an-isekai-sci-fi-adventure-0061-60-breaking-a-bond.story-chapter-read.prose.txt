Lunch was terrible. It consisted of two flat, grainy disks, with a tangy spread that tasted like raspberries and anchovies. Elion gaged but forced it down. He couldn’t smell anything other than the pervasive stench of the acid pits, so that helped.

Alven, the guard, stood in one corner, shotgun leaned against his shoulder. He wore a wide brimmed hat, which he pulled down low over his eyes.

Keyla left half her food untouched.

They sat at a long table in Boff’s bond house, along with the other 35-40 ‘owned’ that Boff had care of.

“Owned is a stupid term,” Elion said to Keyla. “Why not just say ‘slaves.’ That’s what we are.”

Keyla wasn’t paying attention to him. She was staring down the row of dirty men and women at a gigantic man.

“That’s Ryfer,” she said. “He looks a bit different, but I’m pretty sure it’s him. He’s from Aterfel. One of the people who left with Prator.”

Elion took a closer look at the man. Totally bald, with a low brow, his massive shoulders rolled and flexed as he ate. He held his food daintily in sausage-thick fingers, nibbling at the edges of it. His face was dark, his cheeks sunken.

“He’s changed a lot,” Keyla said. “But that has to be him. Some of the other people look familiar too, but I don’t remember names.”

More people filed into the room. One man in particular caught Elion’s attention. The man’s gaze was unfocused and dazed, his eyes dark. He shuffled into the room and plopped down into a chair, but didn’t partake of the food.

His movements were jerky and rough, like he was a marionette controlled by a novice puppeteer. His face was permanently locked into a mask of deep sadness.

“We should talk to Ryfer,” Elion said, looking back at the man Keyla pointed out. “He might know what happened to your mother.”

“Oh, I definitely know them. That’s Remy and Hol,” Keyla said. “Both from Aterfel too.”

Elion looked and saw a woman and a man. “Hol? Isn’t that Brynna’s son?” He remembered the kind old woman who’d fed him stew in her home, made of freshly picked mushrooms. He felt a stab of pain in his back, where she’d stabbed him with a carving knife after being infected.

Recalling the crack of her head as he slammed Brynna down onto a rock, Elion grimaced. But she got up and ran off after that. She could be okay. I wonder if Kasm figured out how to heal infected.

Elion inspected the man’s face, and found features that reminded him of the kind old woman; the shape of his eyes, ridge of his nose, and the way he rubbed at the skin behind his ear.

“Where are the rest of them?” Elion wondered. “I thought you said a few dozen people left with Prator.”

“Probably not all here,” Keyla said. “I saw people heading in to other structures around the pit last night. My guess is these are mostly people running the refinery. I would guess probably half these people are from Aterfel.”

“You don’t think any of them would be willingly working for Prator?” Elion asked.

Keyla frowned and shrugged.

Boff entered the room at that moment, chains jangling around his neck and arms. “Everyone stay seated, and be quiet. I’m going to give out your assignments for this afternoon.”

Boff addressed various people, giving them specific instructions. It seemed like overkill, since most of the people were already doing the jobs assigned to them. Maybe Boff’s just worried they’ll forget what they were supposed to be doing. Or maybe he’s just trying to strengthen the compulsion of the bondhold.

Keyla and Elion were assigned to lidding again, along with Yaret. Elion groaned, flexing his aching forearms. The people with more complicated tasks in the refinery were given more specific instructions.

Honestly this seems like a terrible way to manage things. The refinery workers clearly know more about the job than Boff does. They could be sabotaging things without him ever knowing.

A group of people appeared to be working on some kind of new pump system, that would allow the refinery to pump acid up directly from the pools, rather than relying on the barrel-elevator system.

From the assignments given to everyone, Elion gathered that this group of people generally had technical skills. A few of them, like Ryfer—and Elion, for that matter—were given grunt work assignments: moving things around, lifting things, hitting things.

Elion wondered what it would take for him to get an easier job. No, that’s the wrong kind of thinking. I need to get away from this place. Remember Liora. Get to Zev.

Elion’s eyes wandered back over to the older man with the sad face. He still hadn’t touched his food, but sat slumped in his chair, limbs dangling uncaringly by his sides.

Boff approached the man hesitantly, holding his clipboard like a protective barrier. He made eye contact with Alven, the guard.

“Roban, your assignment—”

The man came to life, barking loudly and snatching at his food. He snarled, shaking his head as he tore into a piece of bread, worrying it like a dog.

Boff flinched back. “—Your assignment.”

Roban howled.

“Roban,” Boff said, “Be quiet! I order you to be quiet!” But Boff was already slipping away from the man as Alven approached.

Roban did not obey. He slammed his fists down on the table, rising to his feet with a shout.

Alven griped his shotgun.

“Roban,” Boff said again, moving so that a table stood between him and the raging man. “Sit down and be quiet.”

“I’m not gonna!” Roban said. “You can’t torture me anymore! I’m… I’m… I’m leaving!”

Alven reached forward to grab Roban, but the owned man moved faster. He spun, grabbing his chair and smashing Alven across the face. Alven staggered back and Roban threw the chair into his gut, like a WWE move.

All the owned sitting at the tables just watched.

Boff blustered. “Somebody… Someone…” He searched through the bracelets and necklaces he wore until he found the dog collar, a leather strap with a charm on it.

Roban approached Boff, eyes wild.

“This is your Bondhold!” Boff shouted. “I hold it and command you to lie down on the floor!”

Roban cackled. “I don’t know what that is!” he shouted, leaping gleefully into the air. He shoved Boff aside with both hands, and ran for the door.

Boff collected himself and scampered across the room.

He picked up Alven’s shotgun, unclipping it from the strap. Alven, unconsious and bleeding from a gash on his cheek, did not protest.

Boff ran after Roban.

None of the owned in the hall were eating. All eyes were fixed on the grimy window overlooking the elevator and the stairs down into the pit.

Roban ran past the window, arms waving in the air and shouting meaningless gibberish.

A moment later Boff appeared. He raised the shotgun and took aim, framed perfectly by the window.

He fired.

The buck of the shotgun blast sent the small man stumbling backward. He fired again, then lowered the weapon.

Boff pursed his lips and shook his head, then turned and walked out of view. A moment later he re-entered the room.

“Alright everyone, no time to waste. Time to get to work.”

Boff selected two strong men to help him with Roban’s body. Elion, Keyla, and Yaret followed them out to the staircase.

Roban’s body lay strewn across the grated walkway, blood dripping down onto the stairs below. Part of his head and skull were missing, and a massive ragged hole gaped just below his right shoulder. Elion looked away when he realized he was staring at the man’s brains.

The two owned collected Roban’s body and made way for them to hike down to the base of the refinery.

“What happened to him?” Elion asked as they walked. “Why did he flip like that?”

“He’s been cracking for a while now,” Yaret said. “If you’re desperate enough, you can force yourself to forget the bond. I don’t really know how it works, but I’ve heard it described as having your memories ripped out of your mind.

"Roban’s been acting erratic for a few days now, like he doesn’t understand Boff’s orders. When I saw him walk into the room this morning, I wondered if he’d broken down last night. Will to live is an interesting thing, you know? It seems to me like it’s largely driven by the connections we have to the people and things around us. Take those away and what do you have left?”

They reached the bottom of the steps and Elion glanced back up to where Roban’s body had laid. “So breaking the bond drives people mad?”

“In some cases,” Yaret said. “Or it removes their will to live. Strange how that works. My daughter died years ago, but somehow my memories of her keep me going. I don’t know what I’d do without them.”

Elion pursed his lips, rubbing at his neck through the rubbery suit as they reached their work area. The more he learned about this place and these bonds, the more he hated it.

They needed to get away from here.
