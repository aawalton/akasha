Loria was the capital city of the Sarmovia Kingdom. The slums of the capital was where Oswald spent most of his life. Of course, the boy rarely ever went out of the slums, let alone out of the city.

But now, for the first time ever, Oswald was actually going far outside of Loria's walls. The journey to Volstarn would take weeks, a daunting stretch of time that would’ve been unbearable on foot. He was more than grateful that Selene had secured a wagon for the trip.

The covered wagon swayed gently with each bump and dip in the path, its canvas roof offering shade from the late morning sun. A faint breeze slipped through the open flaps, bringing relief to the passengers inside.

It had been roughly a week since Oswald and Selene left the capital city. The road stretched long ahead, and though he didn’t mind traveling, there was something about wagon rides that always made time drag. Across from him, Selene sat with her usual composed posture.

Vivi was curled up nearby, eyes closed. The occasional twitch of her tail was the only sign she was even aware of the world outside her nap.

Then, Selene spoke. "Oswald, do you finally wish to share more about your childhood?"

He sighed, leaning back against the wooden frame. "You still care about that?"

Selene didn’t so much as blink. "I have been trying to get you to talk about it for days now. And frankly, we do not have much else to do besides converse to pass the time."

Oswald huffed, rubbing the back of his neck. "Ain't like I was dodgin’ the question or nothin’. I told you already—I didn’t really have much of a childhood." He let his hands drop into his lap, gaze distant. "Had to start stealin’ young. Then mugging." His jaw tightened slightly. "Then… well, you can imagine the rest."

Selene studied him, silent for a moment. Then, instead of dropping the subject, she asked, "How did you learn to read?"

That caught him off guard. He blinked, then let out a short laugh. "What, you surprised?"

"Frankly, yes," she admitted, arching a brow. "Given your circumstances, I did not expect literacy to be among your skills."

Oswald smirked. "Well, there was this old guy—Old Man Devin. Used to run a little tavern in the slums. I liked hangin’ around there ‘cause he’d slip me free food every now and then." His smile faltered slightly. "One day, he just… started teachin’ me. Said if I was gonna get by, I oughta at least know how to read what I was stealin’."

Selene’s expression softened. "And where is he now?"

Oswald’s smirk faded entirely. He stared at the wooden floorboards of the wagon. "Long gone."

Selene’s voice was quiet. "I am sorry."

Oswald snorted, shaking his head. "Don’t be. The old man wouldn’t want me mopin’ over him. Probably kick my ass for it if he could."

"Do you believe he is in a better place now?"

"Him? Maybe. He wasn't the preachin' type, but he had more faith than most. Paid his tithes to a temple once a year, more than anyone from the slums ever did."

A brief silence settled between them, broken only by the steady roll of the wagon wheels. Then, out of nowhere, Selene spoke again. "Do you believe in the existence of gods?"

"What’s with the random question?"

"I simply wish to hear your thoughts."

Oswald shrugged. "I dunno if there’s gods out there or not. Never really thought much about it. But if gods are real… I got a lot of questions for them."

Selene tilted her head slightly. "What kind of questions?"

"Like… why’d my life have to start out so shitty? Or why do some people live even worse than me?"

"Depending on who you ask, some priests would tell you that it is your fault. That you must have done something terrible in a previous life. Or perhaps you unknowingly offended the gods in some way." Selene explained.

Oswald let out a dry chuckle, shaking his head. Right. Like the gods waste their time keepin’ tabs on gutter trash. "If the gods really care that much about some nobody like me, I’d actually be impressed. What about you?"

"I do believe in the existence of gods. But that does not mean I worship them."

"What’s that supposed to mean?"

"It means that no gods deserve worship, because when humanity needed them most, they ran away and hid themselves like cowards."

Oswald blinked, his breath catching for half a second. What? That was not the answer he was expecting. Not from someone like her. "How can you… say that kinda stuff? Just like that?"

Selene’s gaze flicked toward him, but she did not answer immediately. Instead, she leaned back slightly, arms resting over her lap. "I had wise teachers, ones who granted me great knowledge. And I would like to pass some of that knowledge on to you."

"There was a time, mankind thrived in ways you would not believe. The gods were with them then, and under their guidance, they lived in unfathomable prosperity. It is the reason why many scholars and mages today yearn to find artefacts from the ancient era, when mankind was at its most powerful. That was, until the Sundering happened."

"The… what now?"

"The Sundering, it was a calamity unlike any before it. It was in that time, when mankind's faith was tested most, that the gods retreated, never to be seen by mortal eyes again."

Oswald's frown deepened. "What exactly happened?"

Selene's lips curved into a faint, knowing smile, one that didn’t quite reach her eyes. "I have said enough for now, but in time, I promise that I will tell you more."

Oswald exhaled sharply through his nose, rolling his shoulders in frustration. Of course she’d leave it at that.

Yet, before he could press the issue, the wagon lurched violently, to the point that it sent him pitching forward. He caught himself against the wooden frame, tensing as the wheels skidded across the dirt road. The horses up front whinnied sharply, hooves stamping in protest.

Vivi startled awake with a sharp hiss, eyes snapping open as she bristled, fur standing on end. Oswald’s hand shot out, gripping the wagon’s wooden frame to steady himself. “What the—?”

Selene had already braced herself, one hand resting against her knee while the other hovered near her sword. Her gaze turned toward the front of the wagon. “It appears that trouble has decided to find us.”

Oswald shot her a look. “What kinda trouble?”

“Bandits, roughly a dozen of them."

Oswald’s fingers twitched. Sure enough, a voice called out from outside the wagon.

“All right, listen up! Everybody out nice an’ slow! No funny business, an’ maybe ya get to keep your skins!”

Another voice, gruffer than the first, barked out. “Don’t make us repeat ourselves! Step out an’ drop yer goods, or we’re gonna have a problem!”

That last voice was very recognizable to Oswald. It was a voice he hated hearing for the longest time. Jorven?

Selene’s eyes flicked toward him. “Oswald, are you alright?”

“Yeah, I’m fine. But… mind if I handle this one myself?” Oswald asked.

“And why is that?”

“Pretty sure these guys are some old acquaintances of mine.”

At that, Selene chuckled lightly. “Is that so? Well then, by all means. Good luck.”

"Thanks, I'll be quick," Oswald said, hopping down from the wagon.

The midday sun hit him full force, and he squinted against the glare, lifting a hand slightly to shield his eyes. His gaze swept over the bandits, a ragtag bunch draped in mismatched leathers, their thick boots caked in dirt from a lifetime spent on roads that never led anywhere good.

And just as expected, every last one of them bore a black hound tattoo inked somewhere on their bodies. Figured I'd see them again eventually.

But it wasn’t the ink that held his attention. It was the man standing at the front of the group.

Greasy, thinning hair clung to a narrow, ratlike face, beady eyes darting between him and the wagon with both fury and disbelief. Ah, Jorven, how I didn't miss ya.

The rest of the Black Hounds stood tense, but Jorven? He was livid.

His face twisted, veins bulging at his temple as he stomped forward. "I've been lookin’ for you, Black Eye. You ruined everythin’, you little rat!"

Oswald could barely hold back his laughter. Jorven used to be a pretty big deal back when he was still working for the Black Hounds. The man rose up from a grunt to Pack Leader thanks to a natural talent for backstabbing and scheming.

However, now, Jorven was demoted back to being a grunt. Evidently, the bosses must not like him much anymore, since he's clearly been assigned work outside of Loria.

“From the looks of it, you got demoted. What happened? You were runnin’ a whole pack, an’ now you’re just another mutt.”

Jorven’s face twisted into an ugly scowl. “Shut your damn mouth. Ever since you pulled that little stunt at the warehouse, the big bosses chewed me out for it. Called me a dipshit for not takin’ out one boy.”

“Ain’t my problem. And as you like to say, I got bigger plans on the horizon now.”

"Y’know what, Black Eye? I could let all the mess you made slide. Water under the bridge, yeah? Long as you come along nice ‘n quiet."

Before Oswald could answer, a heavy hand shoved Jorven aside. “Piss off, Jorven,” growled a much larger man, easily a head taller than the rest. “You ain’t all that no more.”

Jorven stumbled, nearly losing his footing. His face burned red with humiliation, but he didn’t dare talk back.

The bigger man turned his gaze toward Oswald, looking him over with a sneer. “Ain’t often we get to settle debts, Black Eye. So I’ll be generous. You either beg for forgiveness and come with us… or you die.”

“Funny, cause last I checked, the Black Hounds still owe me a good chunk of change." Oswald said.

The man’s brows rose before he barked out a short laugh. “That so?” He turned toward his men. “You hear that, boys? He thinks we owe him!”

Several of the other Black Hound Bandits chuckled, but the bigger man’s amusement didn’t last long. His smirk twisted into a scowl as he raised a hand. “Y’know what? I’ll pay ya in arrows. Shoot him.”

The moment the command left his lips, several of the thugs raised their bows. Oswald started running.

His body twisted fluidly, sidestepping the first arrow as it whistled past his ear. His boots scuffed against the dirt as he pivoted, dropping low as another arrow sliced the air where his chest had been moments ago. A flick of his heel sent him darting to the side, dodging the next volley.

The bandits barely had time to register what happened. None of them could believe what they saw.

Oswald cracked his knuckles as he smirked. “Gotta say, y’all really need to work on your aim.”

The thugs stood frozen, their faces draining of color as if the blood had been scared clean out of them. The bigger one, the bastard who had barked the order to fire, clenched his fists. “What the fuck are you?”

Oswald let the silence stretch just long enough for the tension to sink deep into their bones before speaking again. "I'm just a guy, nothin' else to it."

He unsheathed his sword, and a wave of fear rippled through the gang, their grips tightening on their weapons. Oswald stepped forward, his hold on the sword firm yet loose.

"I’ll tell ya one thing, though. What’s about to happen next? That’s just me blowin’ off some steam."

In the blink of an eye, Oswald lunged. The first thug barely had time to flinch before Oswald drove the hilt of his sword straight into his gut. A strangled choke ripped from the man’s mouth as his body folded in on itself, but before he could collapse, Oswald twisted and rammed his knee up into his chin.

Bone snapped with a sickening crunch, and the thug hit the ground like a sack of bricks, dead before he even landed. Then, a sharp glint flashed at the edge of his vision. Oswald sidestepped just as a knife tore through the air where he’d been standing.

The thug holding it stumbled forward, off-balance, and that was all the opening Oswald needed. He caught the bastard’s wrist in an iron grip. When he twisted, the man screamed.

His weapon clattered uselessly to the dirt as he cradled his broken arm. Then, Oswald slid his sword straight through the thug’s chest, killing the man almost immediately.

Oswald wrenched his blade free, the steel slick with blood, droplets trailing in the air before hitting the dirt. The lifeless body crumpled at his feet, the dull glaze of death already settling over the thug’s vacant eyes. Ten more to go.

A guttural snarl tore through the air. "Bastard!"

Oswald turned his head, seeing the biggest of the lot gripping the handle of his axe. His bloodshot eyes locked onto Oswald with animalistic fury, lips peeling back over yellowed teeth.

The axe lifted high as the thug attempted a brutal swing aimed to cleave Oswald in two. But the weapon never met its mark.

Oswald lunged first, kicking the thug’s shin, the impact sending a brutal shock through flesh and bone. A sickening crack rang out as the man’s leg buckled.

As the thug lost his balance, Oswald swung his sword in a perfect arc, slicing clean through skin, muscle, and rib. The edge bit deep, splitting the thug’s chest wide open in a mess of crimson. Blood sprayed, painting the ground in a violent burst.

A ragged, choking gasp rasped from the man’s lips, his body convulsing once before his legs gave out beneath him. He collapsed to the earth, twitching in the dirt before stilling completely.

Oswald barely had time to straighten before he heard Jorven screaming. "Quit standin’ ‘round, kill ‘im, you idiots!"

There were only nine thugs remaining, Jorven included. Before meeting Selene, Oswald would have struggled and would have had to use his Animus. Not anymore.

One foolish thug reached him, swinging a rusted short sword in a wild, clumsy arc. Oswald sidestepped, letting the blade slice through empty air, and drove his knee into the man’s gut. The thug let out a choked wheeze, doubling over just in time for Oswald to bring his sword down hard. The blade carved through his back, cutting deep into his spine. The man soon collapsed.

Another came from the left, swinging a dagger towards Oswald’s ribs. He caught the thug’s wrist mid-swing and twisted sharply.

Bone cracked as the dagger tumbled from his useless fingers. Oswald shoved the broken arm aside and plunged his sword into the thug’s chest, feeling the shudder of flesh giving way before he wrenched it free. Blood gurgled from the man’s mouth as he staggered backward, knees buckling before he hit the ground.

A club wielding thug came next, aiming for Oswald’s skull. Too slow.

Oswald dashed and cut through the thug’s thigh with a vicious slash. A deep, jagged wound split muscle and tendon apart, sending the man crashing forward onto his knees.

Before he could cry out, Oswald grabbed a fistful of his hair and yanked his head back, exposing his throat. One clean slice, and the man was dead.

The others hesitated now. Oswald saw it in their stances, they were almost at their breaking point.

Jorven’s voice rang out again, this time much more frantic, nearly unhinged. "Keep fightin’! He ain't gonna stop till we're all dead! You want that?"

Two of them took the words to heart, rushing him at the same time: one with an axe, the other wielding a spear. The axe came first, hacking toward Oswald’s shoulder.

He ducked low, feeling the air ripple as the weapon missed its mark. Then, he kicked out, slamming a boot into the axeman’s knee. The thug screamed, toppling sideways. Oswald ended it quickly, his sword stabbing through the side of his neck.

The spearman thrust forward, trying to catch Oswald off-balance. He pivoted, twisting out of the weapon’s path, then grabbed the shaft with his free hand.

He yanked hard, pulling the thug forward, off balance. The instant their bodies collided, Oswald drove his sword up beneath the thug’s ribs. The man's eyes went wide before Oswald shoved him off his blade.

Only five left now. But one of them turned and tried to run away.

Not on my watch. Oswald grabbed a fallen spear nearby, reared back, and hurled it. The weapon spun through the air before sinking deep into the runner’s back. The thug pitched forward mid-step, hitting the dirt face-first.

The last four spread out, trying to surround him. Oswald smiled as he felt a pleasant tingling sensation at the back of his head. Come get some.

One lunged with a machete, Oswald parried, shoving the attack wide before stepping in, slamming his pommel into the thug’s face. The man reeled, blood spraying from his broken nose, just in time for Oswald to carve his sword across his throat.

The second one made his move, swinging a rusted longsword in a wide arc. Oswald twisted his wrist, forcing the thug’s weapon down before shifting his grip and driving his blade clean through the man’s chest.

Oswald ripped his sword from the dying thug’s chest. Only two left now.

However, he heard the sound of boots pounding against the dirt as Jorven and the last remaining Black Hound thug turned tail, desperation fueling their sprint. It was a futile effort.

The wind howled past Oswald's ears as he closed the distance. The last thug, slower than Jorven, barely had time to glance back before cold steel carved through his spine. A gurgled cry slipped from his lips as he collapsed, blood spilling into the dust.

Jorven skidded to a halt, hands raised. "W-Wait! Just wait, yeah?"

He shuffled back a step, nearly tripping over himself. "You ain't gotta do this, Black Eye! Just let me go, yeah? I’ll disappear! You’ll never see me again!"

Oswald wiped the blood from his blade slowly. "If I let you go, the Black Hounds are just gonna come after me even harder. At least if you die here, the bosses’ll have to figure out what happened first."

Jorven’s breath hitched. He dropped to his knees, palms pressed together like a man praying for salvation. "Please, Oswald! I’ll do anythin’! Just lemme live, yeah?"

Oswald tilted his head, a slow grin creeping onto his lips. "Alright."

Jorven blinked. "Yeah?"

"I’ll let you go. On one condition."

"Yeah, yeah, anythin’! Just name it!"

"All the money you got. Every last coin."

Jorven hesitated, just for a fraction of a second. Then, as if flipping a switch, he forced a grin onto his rat-like face, trying too hard to look convincing. “A-A’right! You can have it!"

Oswald raised his sword slightly, gesturing toward the pouch at Jorven’s belt. “Hand it over.”

Jorven swallowed hard, nodding rapidly. His hands fumbled at his waist, unfastening the small pouch of coins. The leather jingled softly as he lifted it, holding it out in offering.

Oswald didn’t take it. Instead, he watched and waited.

A bead of sweat rolled down Jorven's temple, trailing past his cheek. His eyes darted downward, it was too obvious. There it is.

Oswald saw it before the bastard even moved. Jorven lunged.

His other hand shot for his waist, yanking free a hidden dagger as he went for the kill. But Oswald didn’t flinch.

Steel met flesh before Jorven’s attack could even reach him. Oswald’s sword tore through his chest in one clean slash.

Blood sprayed across the dirt. A strangled, gasping noise ripped from Jorven’s throat as his dagger tumbled from his fingers, clattering uselessly onto the ground.

He staggered, hands flying to his chest as if he could somehow hold himself together, as if he could push back what was spilling out of him.

Oswald stepped closer, watching him struggle. "You really thought I wouldn’t see that comin’?"

Jorven’s mouth moved, but no sound followed. Only a choked, wheezing rattle.

Oswald tilted his head slightly, letting the moment stretch, letting Jorven feel every agonizing second of what came next. Only after he was satisfied, did he drive his sword straight through the bastard’s heart.

Jorven jerked violently, seizing around the blade. His fingers twitched a few times until the light in his eyes went out.

[Congratulations, you have earned 12 Souls!]

Oswald wiped the last traces of blood from his sword before sheathing it. Afterwards, he took Jorven's coin pouch and started walking back to the wagon. Part of him considered looting the bodies of the other thugs, but he dismissed the thought.

With Selene helping him, it's not like he needed that much money anyway. And besides, he didn't feel like digging through blood soaked corpses today.

The wagon stood where he left it. Oswald pulled himself up, slipping into the dim interior, and the first thing he saw was Vivi.

The cat was curled up on a pile of cloth, twitching slightly in her sleep. How'd she sleep through all that?

Selene sat across from him, her sword resting at her side. As soon as he stepped in, her eyes flicked up. "Did you have fun?"

Oswald smiled, dropping onto the seat opposite her. "This might make me sound like a piece of shit, but yeah, I did."

The wagon jolted slightly as it rolled forward. Oswald let the movement settle into his bones, stretching his legs out and resting his arm against the side of the wagon.

Across from him, Selene watched him with that same unreadable look. Then, unexpectedly, the corners of her lips curled into a small, knowing smile. "Having witnessed what you did, most people would respond in one of two ways."

"The first would claim that your actions were excessive, perhaps even cruel. The second would insist that you were entirely justified, that you merely cleansed the world of criminals who brought their fate upon themselves. Now, what do you think my opinion is?"

Oswald let out a quiet chuckle. "I’m hopin’ it’s the latter."

Selene’s smile widened just a fraction before she answered. "It’s not."

"So you think I did a bad thing?"

"No. Anything can be justified, with the right cause, even the worst atrocities can become noble. And just the same, anything can be vilified, turned monstrous in the eyes of the right people."

"Right, I get that, but what's your point?"

"I don’t particularly care that you killed those thugs, Oswald. They were just men who made the mistake of standing in your way. And all Animus Hosts are prone to bloodshed anyway."

“Gotta say, I wasn’t expectin’ you to think that way.”

Selene rested her hands lightly in her lap. “I am a pragmatic person, Oswald.” Her voice carried no weight of judgment, no moral deliberation—just simple fact. “At the end of the day, killing is an intrinsic part of the Animus. It is woven into its very nature.”

Oswald frowned slightly, the words digging at him more than he expected. “Why’s that, though? You told me before that the Animus was invented by someone. But why’d they make it so... twisted?”

“You are mistaken about something, Oswald. The Animus is neither good nor evil, is merely a tool shaped by the intent of those who wield it.”

“Yeah, but that still don’t answer why it’s the way it is.”

“Because when the Animus was created, mankind was forced to fight against enemies far beyond its capabilities.”

“What kind of enemies?”

“Ones you will encounter in Volstarn.”
