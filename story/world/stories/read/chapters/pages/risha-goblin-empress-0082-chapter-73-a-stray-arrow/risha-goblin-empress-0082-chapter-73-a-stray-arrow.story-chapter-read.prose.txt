Risha growled, as she placed pressure on the bleeding stomach of the Jackal kobold. [Mother’s Care] was healing him, but slowly, she couldn’t tell if it would be enough to save his life.

Thankfully, they were still close to camp, and Lolo would return soon with Tya.

The Jackal’s eyes fluttered, closing.

“Stay awake!”

The Jackal listened, his eyes hardening under her commanding gaze.

Moments later, Lolo led Tya into the clearing, moving quickly for her age. Behind them, followed Glo and his warriors.

Immediately, a healing light much stronger than Risha’s healing surrounded the Jackal, pouring into him.

Risha stepped back as the healer took her spot next to the wounded kobold.

Glo stood over the dead assassin as one of his assistants examined the body and the separated head.

“Find the scouts watching this area.” Glo’s voice held an edge to it that Risha rarely heard.

One of the lieutenants nodded, then went running into the forest.

Glo turned to her, then frowned, “You’re bleeding.”

Risha looked down at the puncture wound in her leg, only now starting to feel the pain of it. “He surprised me.”

Glo glanced at where Tya still stood over the severely injured kobold, then stepped closer to Risha and picked her up.

Risha yelped in surprise, then laughed despite the seriousness of the situation.

Glo hurried back to the camp with her in his arms, honing in on the healing tent quickly. One of the healers there immediately saw to her, healing the wound in her leg.

“What happened?”

Risha glanced away from the healer, meeting the other goblin’s worried eyes.

“He attacked with the power of the spark behind him.” Her eyes narrowed as she looked down at her hands, “If I’d had my swords with me, he wouldn’t have done as much damage.”

“What was the kobold and Lolo doing there?”

Risha looked up, “I thought you had The Jackal watching me.”

Glo shook his head.

“That would be me.”

The both of them turned to see Elder Kulos. The older goblin cleared his throat, “Since the assassination attempt with the Shades, I’ve tried to keep someone with you at all times. Mist-Prowler has taken care of more than a few attacks without your notice.”

“Your secret order.” Glo said, some annoyance in his voice.

“The Grand Mother has given me approval.”

Risha had?

“I do not like your craftiness.” Glo bared his teeth.

“All is for the Grand Mother.” Kulos did not respond to the aggression, his eyes staying serious. “And tonight, it may have saved her life.”

That quieted Glo, and the younger goblin looked away from Elder Kulos in annoyance.

“Will he live?” Risha asked.

They both turned to her, and Elder Kulos hesitated for a second before giving a small nod, “Tya has taken him to her tent, but he still lives.”

Risha let out a breath, “That’s good. Glo, have someone bring my swords to me. I shouldn’t be walking without them.”

Glo bowed, then walked over to one of his lieutenants who’d waited some ways away.

The healer finished with her leg, then bowed to her and left, leaving her and Kulos alone for a brief moment.

“Do you have someone you can send deep into enemy territory?” Risha asked.

Elder Kulos paused, looking at her, then nodded.

“I need them to search for something. And no one else can know.”

“What?”

Risha paused, “Dragons.”

Her Spymaster’s eyes widened, before he quickly hid the expression. “Are they real?”

Risha nodded.

Before anything else could be said, Glo returned to them. Risha was not opposed to telling her general about the dragons, but something about the way Grand Mother Iris had spoken to her told her that knowledge of the dragon’s roost was not common knowledge, and that the woman wished to keep it that way.

Risha would respect her friend’s privacy.

Her swords were soon brought to her, and she attached them to her belt with practiced motions.

“How soon can we move out, Glo?” Risha asked as she cinched her belt..

“Within the hour.”

Risha stood up, and turned towards the endless swamp. “Let’s continue our march.”

The two goblins bowed. Glo turned to his Lieutenants and began shouting orders, while Elder Kulos stepped out of the healer tent.

Risha spotted Kliks watching from the ceiling, and held out her hand for the spiderling to join her.

The spiderling dropped onto her arm.

“Did I worry you, Kliks?” Risha asked softly.

The spiderling nodded “I can’t leave you alone.”

Risha laughed, then stepped out of her tent. She had her own preparations to make.

***

The army moved through the swamps. The Saplings marched at the front, expanding the roots of the forest so that they weren’t forced to trudge through too much murky water.

Risha rode Kliks with her Spirits, and the Elders riding with her. Above them, the two Draconic Riders flew through the sky, several drakes with purple scales following them.

Occasionally, a skirmish could be seen as enemy drakes attempted to harass them, or scouts encountered each other.

Reports soon reached Risha of another army marching towards them from deep in the marshlands, but no one had gotten close enough to confirm their numbers.

On the second day of marching, a strange sight caught Risha’s eyes.

Elder Oltak stood tall on top of a purple-scaled serpent that made its way through the marshland, having befriended the beast.

“Elder.” Risha called to the Mage, who turned to her with a self satisfied expression. “Who do you travel with?”

“This is Eskel.” The Elder kneeled down to pat the creature’s scales. “He has offered to have me ride with him today.”

“Oh?” Risha tilted her head, then looked at the serpent, “I am glad you have befriended my people, Eskel.”

The serpent bowed its head, “The purple goblin has been welcoming. Kinder than many.”

“Oltak has experience making peace with his enemies. It is something Risha admires.” Risha smiled.

“Your kindness far surpasses mine, Grand Mother.” Elder Oltak bowed his head.

“How has the organization of mages been going, Elder?” Risha asked.

“There are troubles, but with Glo and Sun-Chaser’s cooperation, it’s been smoother than I feared. Another goblin evolved from shaman to mage with the latest battle, and her support makes all the difference.”

“I’m glad to hear it.” Risha smiled.

***

On the evening of the third day, an arrow took the life of Elder Lya, one of the first goblins Risha evolved, and an Elder that had supported her since the beginning.

She’d been with a scouting group that had been ambushed, and had used her shamanic magic to take down several scaled beasts with her.

When Risha heard the news, all she could see was the image of the old goblin when she’d been a Mother, saving small food scraps for a small Risha in an already hungry village.

Risha stumbled into her tent in a daze, then cried.

They were winning, and even so, her people were dying, with nothing she could do to save all of them.

She knew that she couldn’t hold every death close to her heart, but this one hurt all the deeper.

The next morning, Risha rode ahead despite the worry of Glo. She found an enemy group of scouts, then with her spiderlings, she killed them. It was a slaughter, her swords slicing through the enemy without stop, as even their best warriors couldn’t draw blood from her.

It wasn’t enough to heal her grief, but the adrenaline of battle distracted her as she once again reveled in her power.

She was not weak.

The conversation of the Ancient Goblin Shaman with the chained Spark went through her mind as she killed her enemies.

“I do not fear you-”

“You should, Spark. Even like this, I hold the power to remind you of your youth.”

The Spark had felt fear then. And Risha too, would remind this Spark that it was facing the wrath of a mother who had seen her children tortured and killed.

So far, the spark’s warriors had put Risha on the back foot, but she’d faced worse evils, and she had grown past them. She would grow past this one too.

More power was within her reach, and she knew how to grab it. It was time to seek out the Ancient Shaman, and time to bind yet another spirit to her.

That night, Risha cleaned her bloody swords, then entered her home in the spirit world.

She grabbed the spirits of her swords off the wall, walked through her garden of broken blades, now covered in flowers, and approached her gate. This time, she left the Council, and entered the spirit world.

A void filled with stars expanded in front of her, but she remembered well the path the shaman had shown her through the stars.

Elly’s star shone bright in the cosmos, and she entered the realm easily. The field of flowers led to the singular cottage.

She walked through the flowers, wondering at what they were, and eventually arrived at the doors of the cottage.

She opened the door without knocking, and there, she found the goblin she was looking for.

He stood watching the empty fireplace with a lost look in his eyes, but eventually, he turned to her, his eyes regaining a light that had been gone for a moment.

He smiled wide, “Little Sister, you’re back.”

She placed her hands on her chainswords, Igni flaring within her. “I’m ready.”
