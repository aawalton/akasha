“Going off of how they don’t have frills yet, they’re not Mithril tier,” Gabriel said, appearing in front of the party. The fact that he was not just calling out instructions, but actually about to involve himself with this conflict, was enough of a reminder to the party that mana eaters were known to be deadly dangerous. “However, there’s more of them than you can safely deal with, if we count the second pack that’s only a kilometer behind these ones. I would stay here with you, but I don’t know if there are any higher tier ones there, and if there is, just its aura would be enough to weaken you all and get you into too much danger. I’m going to deal with the ones back there, then I’ll be back as fast as I can. You have, at most, thirty seconds before these first ones get here. Do not let them bite you, especially Skandr and Benedict.”

Then, as a further testament to the old man’s worry, he didn’t leave without making a sound, but instead left a crater as he sprinted off in the direction of the monsters. The hissing, screeching calls of the mana eaters became shrill, seemingly terrified, as he made his way through the pack of beasts. Evidently, he’d made a few attacks himself before he was off to deal with this unknown threat.

“Together,” Astrid said, standing only a meter away from Felix. “Muti, you’re not off on scouting or assassination duty until we better understand what these things are capable of. All of you, if you have something to say about what you can or cannot do, call it out.”

There was no time for additional instruction or planning, as the first of the pack of mana eaters appeared. As they pushed through the dense, corrupted foliage, Astrid’s eyes narrowed. The creatures had no eyes, only a cavernous mouth at the front of their heads. Each one had six legs, along with two sinuous tails that curled almost like ribbons behind them. Their mouths were full of needle-like teeth, and though the monsters were only the height of a rather large wolf, they somehow communicated a great weight with how they crashed through everything in their path.

Their bodies were squat, almost crocodilian, but their mouths didn’t open like a creature with a jaw. Instead, the circle at the front of their long heads simply expanded and inhaled anything in its path. The monsters didn’t have scales or fur, only gray skin that hung loosely off their bodies in folds. Several of them seemed to have eaten more than others, as their skin was tighter on them and their torsos more bulbous. A few looked emaciated, as if they’d never eaten, but those ones made small detours toward the trees before continuing  towards the Wanderers. Just as the fight began, Astrid recognized that the name “mana eater” was very much literal. The mana all around them sucked toward the mass of fleshy creatures, the skinniest of the beasts growing ever so slightly larger as they drained the life from the trees nearby.

Astrid activated Body Surge, ready to face the beasts, and didn’t feel the familiar swell in her attributes.

“I can’t use my burst Skill,” she said, her voice tight.

“You can use it, but it’s weaker,” Skandr corrected. Astrid didn’t care to figure out why he knew that, but trusted him. She lowered her stance to prepare to fight against the beasts. The first threw itself at her, and she raised her shield. Its claws scraped deep into her shield and almost knocked her back a couple steps.

By habit, she drew on Indomitability to strengthen her stance, but it didn’t work. She swung her hammer down at the creature, trying to kill it straight away, pushing Smite into the hammer. Her mana reacted sluggishly, moving, but not delivering even nearly the force of a blow that she anticipated.

Her hammer smacked into the side of the monster’s mouth, knocking it away just in time for two more to take its place.

“My shadows do not bother them,” Muti said, her voice tight. Her blades sliced down at the neck of one, cutting into the beast, but its saggy flesh turned her blow to the side before she could cut its muscle below.

Astrid shoved her shield forward to divert one of the mana eaters while her hammer cracked down on the second. The weapon landed solidly, breaking bone, and the creature wailed in response. It stepped back just long enough to rip a massive chunk out of a tree, becoming able to walk much better thereafter.

“I can’t get anything to work,” Benedict reported, panic obvious in his tone as Astrid tried to deal a crippling blow. “Nothing will activate.”

“Keep your head,” Astrid growled as her stomach twisted. At first, she thought it was a reaction to the news from Benedict, but she swiftly realized that it was an effect of the aura from the monsters. The mana around her, instead of how it usually flowed naturally toward her to heal and help recover, was non-responsive. Yes, the mana eaters were reducing the ambient concentration of mana, but what was already there was also somehow stiff. It didn’t flow to fuel her passive Skills or recovery. Even as she desperately fought to regain control over the fight, three more mana eaters came forward.

“There are eight in total,” Muti reported as Skandr cursed sulfurously.

“I can barely get anything to respond. I’ll stick to simple spells.”

As soon as he said it, he twisted his hand and sent out a gout of deep red lightning. Either he was trusting the rest of the party, or he, much more likely, was finding it easier to fuel his spells with health or stamina instead of mana. With Warlock’s Constitution fueling it, the bolt of lightning crashed into two of the mana eaters, knocking them away. Astrid started to cheer, seeing the deep wounds each of the monsters had sustained, but then the mana that made up the lightning seemed to break down and flow in a vortex into the beasts' mouths.

As they lived up to their names, the mana eaters' bodies recovered from nearly crippled so simply wounded. The longer they had to recover, the closer they’d grow to their peak while inhibiting the Wanderers’ ability to recover.

At Astrid’s side, Felix seemed to push more of his mana into activating Defender’s Avatar, and he doubled in size. Then, with a growl, he smashed his axe down twice on the nearest mana eater. It rolled around, reducing the strike to something far less potentially deadly. Even so, he chopped one of its legs off and left it screaming their head-achingly high-pitched wail of pain.

“Kill it,” Astrid said as she stepped forward and tried to do as she commanded. She forced more mana into her Smite, giving it the deathly attribute Spectral Graviton carried. As her hammer landed in the wound, the mana ripped into the beast’s body, and it seemed to wither even more. Maybe it was only the mana that entered its mouth that would help it recover? Even so, the creature wasn’t finished off. It twisted and caught a bite on her right forearm before she could reposition herself.

The Champion ripped her hand back as fast as she could, feeling the monster’s fangs try to rip through her defenses. Fortunately, her gauntlets and bracer were thick enough to keep the beast’s venomous fangs from reaching her skin. Even so, the monster’s natural weapons managed to cut surprisingly deeply into her high Steel tier armor.

“Don’t let them get close. Most defenses won’t hold up,” Astrid grunted.

Felix roared in agreement as four mana eaters closed in on him. One went to each arm while the other two went for his legs. Astrid and Muti tried to support him, but one managed to get past all of their defenses and latched onto his knee. His roar became a scream as the mana eater made a horrendous schlorping noise.

Every one of his Skills was immediately canceled, and he shrunk down as a consequence of taking a single attack. Felix swung at the creature three times, his attacks growing weaker. Muti screamed in challenge as she kicked off one mana eater and threw herself at Felix. Both of her blades flashed and landed on the beast’s neck. The first, the thinner midnight mithril blade, cut through the skin, and her second finally chopped deep into the monster’s spine. Its bite went limp, and it fell from Felix’s bloody leg as Muti adjusted her stance and chopped a second time.

Mana eater slain. 4,720 experience gained, split among party.

Astrid tried to feel the same exaltation that Muti screamed to the heavens, but the fact that another seven of the mana eaters were swarming them and the Wanderers' frontliner was heavily wounded, if not out of the fight, was a worry.

There was nothing to do but continue to fight, and the next dozen seconds became a desperate, bloody standstill.

After what Felix experienced, Astrid was far more careful to keep the monsters away from them, but with him fighting at only half of his original strength and Benedict, as well as Quick Recovery, unable to heal him, Astrid and Muti both took a series of small bites. The first four that landed on Astrid were repelled by her armor, but the fifth, sixth, and seventh all managed to break skin. The wounds they sustained were negligible, something that she wouldn’t have noticed if they were from any other creature. Instead, they were mind-breakingly painful. The mana in Astrid’s body revolted against her, and the small amounts of mana she rallied in support of herself that she had managed to give bled away as her mana fought against her control. Instead, every part of her body screamed in protest against her.

There were too many of the beasts and not enough people to fight them individually. Felix valiantly occupied two, but he couldn’t manage any more than that. Muti had landed several blows on the ones in front of her, each cut slowing the beasts. That allowed her to only be struck several times, but she didn’t try to hide the pain cadence to her breathing.

“We need to kill more, and fast,” Skandr said from behind. His breathing was raspy, a series of small spells flowing continuously from his hands. The lightning darted around the mana eaters, striking them in the flanks, while gusts of wind lifted their feet from the ground for brief moments. Even so, with the disruption to the ambient mana, he struggled to achieve any of his strongest workings.

“Got any good ideas?” Astrid asked, shoving her shield forward into one as she lifted her right foot and stomped it onto the back of another. Her efforts didn’t manage to push either of them very far away, and the one under her foot rose up and destabilized her stance further. She stumbled back, threatening to fall to the ground, as the others seemed to sense weakness. Three more piled at her, and, desperate, she pushed every bit of mana she could summon into Body Surge.

Only maybe half of the Skill’s intended effect appeared, but that needed to be enough. She stumbled back, but the boost to her Alacrity kept her from going down. With a roar, Astrid turned and smashed her hammer into the broadside of one of the beasts. The strike sent it tumbling into one of its companions, both going down in a thrashing heap of limbs. Astrid took a hit to her hip, but she twisted, keeping the thing from piercing her defenses. Then, raising her hammer again, she let it fall.

The notification that flashed in her eyes as her hammer pulped the creature was divine. The two that she sent flying were back now, but the one she’d hit had lost the use of two of its legs. Several dozen claws struck at her, each one digging into the deep manasteel that made her armor. Even so, she had enough left in her, barely, to raise her hammer and smash it down a second time. This one caught the spine of one of the mana eaters, just behind its front legs. That left it with only two functioning legs instead of six, and Astrid kicked it away while its companion seized the opening that came from there.

It tried to go to Skandr, but Astrid threw herself forward and tackled it. They went down in a tumble of limbs, and its mouth caught her left shoulder. She screamed in agony as venom that felt like liquid fire burned into her. The mana in her body, near her heart, revolted against her. With every heartbeat, her body betrayed her, but Astrid had access to the underside of the creature.

A part of her noted two kill notifications appearing as was locked into the struggle, but this was her fight. Nothing else mattered right now, and she shoved the creature’s head up with her weakening left arm. Body Surge had been deactivated by the creature’s bite, and she lacked the strength to hurl it away as she wanted to. However, she still had enough strength to force enough space to start raining punches on the creature with her right hand.

In agony and without mana, Astrid still had a whole lot of pent-up rage. Her right fist was deadly and fell with meaty thuds against the base of the creature’s skull. The flesh tried to twist away under her hits, but her fist flashed as fast as Skandr’s lightning. After the fourth hit, Astrid seemed to strike a nerve cluster on the beast, and it twitched, going stiff. She didn’t care. She wasn’t done.

In a blink of an eye, four more punches struck the same spot, the metal of her gauntlet finally shredding through the beast’s flesh. Blood and viscera flew up, her fists going so fast that the blood spray only served to coat her arm up to the elbow. In less than a second, her fist had fallen so many times and so hard that her knuckles brushed the creature’s spine.

With a scream, Astrid jabbed her hand in, grabbed the creature’s spine, and started whipping the mana eater around by its spine. Bone crunched, and just a split second later, the creature’s spine was broken.

Astrid stood, letting the corpse fall from blood-soaked, aching fingers. Even so, she was more than ready to grab her hammer and look for the next monster to kill. Three remained alive, one tied up by Muti, the other two trying to get around Felix. All of them bore scars from the fight, but Astrid could see that they continued to devour the mana that surrounded the battlefield. The more mana they consumed, the more energetic and healthy they became.

“Come on, don’t let up!”

Astrid’s command was followed by her own charge into the monster that was about to catch Felix’s foot in its mouth. Instead, it received Astrid’s foot in its throat, which was more than enough to send it flipping backward a half dozen meters.

“Keep that one,” Astrid grunted as she pursued the one she had just struck. Her body screamed in pain, the venom coursing from everywhere she’d been bitten. She had no idea how she would be able to recover from this, but one thing was for sure—she could only begin to recover once all these bastards were dead.

In a few short seconds, Astrid was able to cripple and then kill her mana eater, just in time to see the other two kill notifications flashing in her vision.

Mana eater slain. 4,640 experience gained, split among party.

Mana eater slain. 4,720 experience gained, split among party.

Mana eater slain. 4,560 experience gained, split among party.

Astrid felt her own blood coursing down her left arm and all down the left side of her chest. Somehow, it felt cold, and she shivered.

“Report,” she commanded, the word burning in her throat. “Skandr, Benedict, you first.”

“Useless, but healthy,” Benedict said. His self-loathing was obvious in the set of his shoulders and his tone.

“I’m rough, but will be fine with a little rest,” Skandr added. “I used Warlock’s Constitution to get those spells moving, and you all know what the cost of that is.”

“I need rest,” Felix said concisely.

Muti didn’t answer verbally, but it was obvious that she felt the same.

Astrid started to open her mouth to say something, but was cut off by the appearance of Gabriel. He rushed into their field of vision, glancing over the different party members, his expression largely hidden behind his helmet, but still visibly relaxing as he saw that everybody was still conscious.

“Collect the bodies,” he said, his voice tight but his expression approving. “Let’s get out of here.”

Astrid couldn’t help but agree with the sentiment, and as she looked at the rest of her party, she could see that they agreed. After all, it was time for them to return to Neverwood anyway, to see how Olafson was coming along on their equipment upgrades, and also to receive the answer about what they could do with regards to evolving here in the Verdant Sanctuary.

And then, it would be time to go to war.
