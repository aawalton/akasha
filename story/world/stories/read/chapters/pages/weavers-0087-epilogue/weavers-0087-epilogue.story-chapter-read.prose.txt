The boat cut quietly through the water.

Built decades ago, made from worn wood, darkened by salt and time, creaking softly with each slow rise and fall of the tide. The sea stretched endlessly in every direction, a vast, shifting expanse of muted grey and steel, reflecting a sky heavy with low, unmoving clouds.

The shoreline came into view gradually.

At first, it was nothing more than a dark line on the horizon. Then, as the boat drifted closer, details emerged. Jagged cliffs, pale stone rising sharply from the water’s edge, their surfaces carved by centuries of wind and wave. Sparse vegetation clung to the rock in stubborn patches, twisted and low, shaped by an environment that offered little in return.

There were no docks, nor any recent signs of life.

The hull scraped softly against the shallows as the boat came to a halt. Water lapped gently at the sides, a steady, rhythmic sound that seemed too calm for a place so utterly still.

They disembarked without ceremony.

Their boots met the course sand. No footprints marked the shore but their own. No debris washed in from distant lands. It was as though the world itself had forgotten this place existed.

Ahead, a narrow path wound upward through the cliffs, and they followed it.

The air changed as they climbed. The scent of salt faded as they left the ocean behind, the sweet smell of nature replacing it.

At the top, the land opened. And beyond it, the remains of a city lay barren.

It stretched across the horizon, vast and silent, its scale immediately apparent even in ruin. Structures rose in uneven clusters, their forms unlike anything they had seen before: sleek lines carved from pale, unfamiliar materials, surfaces etched with patterns too precise to have been shaped by hand alone.

Or perhaps shaped by hands far more advanced than theirs.

Towering spires leaned at unnatural angles, some collapsed entirely, others frozen mid-fall as though time itself had faltered in the act of destroying them. Wide roads cut through the city in straight, deliberate lines, though they were cracked now, broken apart by time and neglect. Strange frameworks of metal and stone intertwined between buildings, remnants of systems no longer functioning, their purpose lost.

There were no bodies, nor any signs of recent decay.

They moved forward slowly, stepping into the outskirts of the city. Their footsteps echoed faintly against the hollow structures, the sound carrying farther than it should in the stillness.

Windows—if that is what they were—stood empty, their surfaces long since shattered or removed. Interiors lay exposed, filled with dust and fragments of unfamiliar furniture. Some buildings had partially collapsed inward, leaving jagged openings that revealed rooms frozen in abandonment.

It was different to what they were used to.

Kaelin’s gaze lingered on the details; the precision in the architecture, the unnatural smoothness of certain surfaces, the way even in ruin, the city held a kind of order, a nod to the expert craftsmanship, and the artistry that went into it.

It didn’t feel ancient.

Not in the way that the ruins she’d seen before did. Though perhaps that was due to how much more advanced they were than the city of Luneah.

A faint wind moved through the streets, stirring loose fragments of debris. It carried a low, hollow sound as it passed through broken structures, almost like a distant echo of what had once filled this place.

Life.

Time had moved differently here, that much was clear.

In the fifty years since Aric—Ezra had been here. Since Kaelin had in his memories, while Aric had been holed up in Erundal, two hundred—maybe even three hundred had passed out here.

The city had endured so long. None of them could fathom the things which had to have occurred to bring down such a magnificent place. But everything has its time.

The scale of it stretched far beyond what they could see. The city continued inward, its centre obscured by distance and layers of ruined architecture rising in uneven tiers.

They walked further in.

Past fractured roads.

Past collapsed structures.

Past remnants of something once impossibly advanced.

Until they stopped.

At the edge of what had once been a central expanse.

Before them, the city opened fully.

Towers reached toward the sky, their silhouettes jagged against the dull light filtering through the clouds. The remains of immense structures stood half-intact, their designs intricate and alien, their purpose unknowable. In the distance, what might have once been the heart of the civilisation loomed—larger, more complex than anything surrounding it.

It was impossible to take in all at once.

They stood there, nothing against the great expanse of the city which stood before them.

The five of them stood there in front of the city for a while.

How small they seemed.

But how far they had come.

End.
