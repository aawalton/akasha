Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. followed Angela into the small office, taking note of the privacy wards in place around it.  Despite the building’s small size, it was still well-enchanted.

Taking notice of Kyle looking around, Angela gave a bitter smile.  “A far cry from our former glory, I’m afraid.”

He knew the best response would be an empathetic one, but the burning question in his mind came out instead.  “How did you manage to sell the whole building?”

“We didn’t sell the building, we moved it as part of our consolidation effort.  We’re selling the planet, the shipyard, and most of our remaining vessels.  This is simply a requirement to call this area a shipyard.”

“I see,” Kyle said.  “When does the sale go through?”

“In a few weeks.  Your expedition won’t be returning here, you will be coming back to the Corthus family estates.  Similarly, the vessel you’ll be taking is one that I own privately, and will remain as our family’s primary transport.”

Not much of a fall from glory if you have a whole estate, a massive office building, and a private spaceship, Kyle thought.  Verbally, he turned to the matter at hand.  “What do you want to discuss about Benjamin.”

Angela clasped her hands behind her back, then turned to look out the window toward the ships.  “Context may be important, but to cut to the chase, I want you to find a place for him within this new settlement of yours.”

Kyle blinked, taken off guard by the request.  “Won’t he be living in the family estate?”

“He is certainly welcome to, of course.  But I know him well enough to know that he’ll find it stifling in short order.  All he’s ever known has been the freedom to travel, with security and support provided for him.  With our resources becoming as limited as they are, I will not be able to continue to give him free reign in that regard.”

“All due respect, but you all still have vastly more resources at your disposal than I do.  Why can’t he just get a place on the Hub?”

“He already has a residence there, which he despises.  I am not asking for special treatment, other than to leave an opportunity for him.  We also have other skilled workers, including some D Grades that will need new employment after we cease operations.  We could also send them, which would more than offset any inconvenience caused by keeping space for Benjamin.”

And there’s the carrot.  He expected Angela already suspected that they were in need of talented manpower, and she was offering it for a favor.  However, Kyle also spotted a hidden danger.  If former Corthian Mining awakened started showing up, especially D Grades, management could get complicated quickly.  The newcomers would all have experience with each other, and some would be more powerful than Joseph and the others.  They would need to get covenants in place to protect the existing leadership, and have a means to monitor and enforce regulations.

“It’s a generous offer,” Kyle said carefully.  “As you know, this isn’t just my decision to make.  Let me connect with my business partner, and we can work on specifics.”

“Naturally.  Just be advised, the longer you wait, the more likely my D Grades are to find opportunities elsewhere.  Doors are easy to open for people with their skills.”

“I’ll keep that in mind,” Kyle said.  “Is there anything else we need to discuss.”

Angela’s eyes grew cold as steel as she met Kyle’s.  When she spoke, her voice cut through the air like a blade.  “Yes.  Kill the bastard responsible for my nephew’s death.  And make him suffer.”

Kyle was taken aback by the harshness of her voice, and she continued before he could respond.  “If I’d have known everything that little pissant was going to take from me, I would have left him to die with your backwater planet.”

She turned again to look out the window, her voice cracking.  “I never imagined that he would die there.  I always thought that he’d have what it took to escape, to hold on until help arrived, even in the worst case scenarios.  And then what?  My insurance policy?  It was worthless.  I didn’t care about the colony.  The whole planet could burn.  I just wanted the people responsible dead.  And they got away.  Both of them got away.  I threw away everything I built to make them pay for killing that little boy.  And it was for nothing.”

The steel was back in her voice now, and she took a deep breath to compose herself.  The temperature in the room had risen to uncomfortable levels as waves of heat rolled out from her, the air and mana in the room seeming to be drawn toward the older woman as her power spiked.  Even that small lapse in control made Kyle appreciate exactly how powerful she was.  He’d known that Angela Corthus was a high-tier C Grade, and this reinforced the gap between them.

Once she regained control of her aura, Angela seemed to slump a bit.  “Sorry about that, Kyle.  I lost myself for a moment.  But make no mistake; Victor DeRosa deserves to suffer for everything he’s done.  To your people and mine.  And once he’s dead, and my family is settled, I can use what resources I have left to track down the other one.”

Kyle nodded.  “They’ll both answer for what they’ve done.”

“Good.  Then we’re in agreement.  Now, I’ve gotten notice that some of the others have arrived.  We’d best not keep them waiting.”

Angela turned on her heel, walking toward the door with her hands once again clasped behind her back.  There was no sign of the lapse in control; no bloodlust in the woman’s aura as she strode out to meet the other waiting expedition members.  She was cold, dispassionate, and ruthless.  Only Kyle knew the bubbling rage that seethed beneath, roiling magma waiting to erupt.  His mind went unbidden to the dossier Marcus shared with him.  Angela was exactly the type of opponent he would struggle against, and he was very glad they were on the same side in the matter of DeRosa.

He was also glad for her presence because it would prevent any impromptu sparring sessions.  Skippy and Tillienne were among the crew waiting outside, and both of the other C Grades looked at Kyle with borderline hungry expressions.  He’d promised Skippy a good match once Storm Shelter had been upgraded, and Tillienne had been trying to set up time to really test some of her new skills.  There simply weren’t that many opportunities for C Grades to really test their mettle, and as he’d learned from Arhades during their joint hunt, Kyle made for a premium punching bag.

Another familiar face was Randolph, the doctor who had accompanied Corthian Mining’s expedition to Earth.  The man still gave off an early D Grade aura, though Kyle knew his medical expertise was top-notch.  The lights from the shipyard gleamed off the man’s bald head, and he did a double-take as he recognized Kyle.  The old healer gave Kyle a curt nod, which Kyle returned.

Angela looked over the group, then frowned.  When she spoke, all the idle chatter ceased immediately.  “I believe we’re waiting on a few more people, though they may have since changed their minds.  Regardless, we will wait another thirty minutes, then begin boarding.  Feel free to mingle amongst yourselves until then.”

As she stepped aside to check her nav bracelet, Kyle walked up to join the others.  Randolph was the first to walk up and greet Kyle, extending his hand.  Kyle took it, noting that the crushing Strength he’d encountered back in D Grade was no longer overwhelming to him.

“Stars above, boy, what happened to you since we last met?  You’re like an entirely different person.”

“He almost died a bunch, for starters,” Skippy interjected as he walked over.  “Angela said we have thirty minutes, do you think that would be enough time for a quick match?  You should be able to get patched up by the time we get to our destination.”

“Stop it, Skippy,” Tillienne said with an exasperated sigh.  Turning to Kyle and Randolph, she gave a slight bow, accentuating her gentle curves as her dark eyes met Kyle’s.  “And it’s been too long, Kyle.  You deserve a vacation from your gardening project now and again.”

“Great to see you both, too,” Kyle said, ignoring the request to fight.  “Also, Tillienne, meet Randolph.  Randolph, Tillienne.”

“It’s good to meet you.  Never seen so many C Grades in one place before,” the bald Healer grumbled.  “Just a fair warning, patching you three up will take a lot more out of me than the others.  So try not to all get grievously injured at once.”

“Hear that Skippy?  No sparring,” Kyle said with a grin.  “We’d both be too beat up to recover.”

“You can take care of healing yourself,” the skrell shot back.

“We can all celebrate by beating each other up after DeRosa is dealt with,” Tillienne admonished.  “Speaking of, do you have a plan of attack in mind?”

That question drew the interest of several onlookers, and the group went quiet as their eyes settled on Kyle.  He took a deep breath, sharing what he and C.H.A.D.D. put together.

“There are a lot of variables we have to consider, particularly as we think through our approach to the moon.  He’s had a long time to fortify and entrench himself, which gives him a distinct advantage.  That said, he’s also had to protect a massive surface area.  No matter how many preparations he’s made, DeRosa can’t muster his full power everywhere at once.  That means our best strategy is to have a focused strike team move to neutralize him, while a second group defends the extraction point.  Skippy, Tillienne, and I are best suited to be the tip of the spear, while my ask of all of you will be to ensure our exit is protected.”

Randolph cleared his throat.  “That seems… simple, Kyle.”

Kyle shrugged.  “The scans of the moon gave us some rough ideas of where to go, but there are still a lot of unknowns.  Simple means we can adapt on as we go.”

Randolph shrugged as the other D Grades began to talk amongst themselves, and were only interrupted by a flash of the teleporter.  Three more figures appeared, all people Kyle knew well.  Garth was the first to step off, wearing his usual combat armor and long, curved sword strapped to his back.  Behind him was Chester Drake, the foreman from Corthian Mining’s first journey to Earth.  The man was in his fine battle robes, and Kyle knew his skills at earth and stone manipulation would be extremely valuable.  Last was somebody Kyle hadn’t expected to see, Captain Rochelle.  The woman’s silver hair was pulled back, as usual, and two sheathed daggers were strapped to one hip.  She also had what looked like a small arsenal of throwing knives attached to belts and bandoliers that wrapped around her.

She flashed Kyle a smile as they walked up, but before anybody else could talk, Angela made her way back to the front of the group.

“Excellent.  Now that everybody is here, let’s be clear on what is going to happen.  You will be taking a private shuttle to the moon where we believe the Mycomancer has nested.  Scans indicate numerous fungal creations, similar to what we faced on Kertan Six.

“We have identified what we believe to be the best point for entry, and as Kyle mentioned, our C Grades will take point in the incursion.  Your goal is simple; destroy the Mycomancer and any of his creations that get close, protect the shuttle, and extract yourselves once it is safe to do so.  Should that be accomplished, you will all receive full pay.  If the shuttle is destroyed, its cost will be taken out of the group’s pay.  Should you fail and require Collective intervention, there will be no compensation, as all of my remaining resources will be tied up in fines and restitution.  Is that clear?”

“Yes, ma’am!” the group responded, though Kyle, Tillienne, and Skippy merely nodded.

“Excellent.  Then board the shuttle and prepare for departure.  Your trip will only take several days, thanks to the relays set up by the Collective.  Strike swift, strike hard, and I await news of your success.”

With that, the shuttle’s doors opened, and the D Grades began walking in.  Kyle sighed and followed suit.  This was a chapter he was ready to close.
