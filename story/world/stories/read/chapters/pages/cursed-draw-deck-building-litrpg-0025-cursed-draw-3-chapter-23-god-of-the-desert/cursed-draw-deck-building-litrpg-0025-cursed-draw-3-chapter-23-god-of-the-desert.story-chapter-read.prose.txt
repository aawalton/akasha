‘Don’t slouch!’ Soren reminded himself as he pulled the final spear from his chest and tossed it aside, before turning to the collected Dunerunner forces that looked upon him with horror. It had taken weeks of disappointment and failure to pin down the alliance of nomadic tribes who had done their utmost to only fight his expeditionary forces in brief hit-and-run skirmishes or when they could bring the more traditional armies of the Shimurg Country to bear.

Soren still didn’t understand how the desert raiders had managed to forge an alliance with their ancestral enemy against him, but forge it they had, and it had turned what should have been a relatively simple conquest into a brutal and bloody affair. It was enough that the Lord of Creeds had come to forgive his ‘brother’ Savat for his utter failure to bring the nomads to heel as he had been commanded. Soren had even begun to wish that he had not banished the general to the north to assist with Gwenia’s journey.

None of that mattered now, not when Soren had managed to finally bring the four grand-elders of the richest and most powerful desert tribes to the field of battle. Well, it wasn’t really a field, and it hadn’t really been a battle either. The Dunerunners might be a scattered breed that migrated from oasis to oasis throughout their sand-covered country, but they had retained certain building techniques that had created the miraculous roads that crisscrossed the dunes, and the stunning cities of stone and glass beneath them.

The ancient cities beneath the country of dunes were things of incredible beauty and cultural significance to the nomads. The tribes didn’t have the kind of populations to live in them anymore, and hadn’t for centuries. Yet they weren’t abandoned ruins either, so much as hidden monuments the Dunerunners took great pains to keep in pristine condition. Soren had a great deal of respect for the efforts the tribes made to maintain the sandstone cities in their grand caverns beneath the desert. Human beings were almost always transient and temporary things. Architecture, on the other hand, could stand for countless centuries with a little bit of care. In the Walking God’s mind, a culture’s buildings were one of the truest expressions of a culture’s identity. That was why it had hurt him so much to pillage and destroy the hidden cities, especially since they were such treasure troves of powerful Cards and Linked Items. It hadn’t stopped him from ordering his warriors to descend beneath the sands and take everything they could find of value before shattering the palaces, temples, and streets of the ancient cities, of course. Not when he knew the Dunerunner tribes cared so very much about their ancestral homes, and besides this, foray into the deserts of the Dune Country had cost a toll in blood; even The Final Peaks would take years to recover from. So Soren fully intended to recover whatever he could get his divine hands on, no matter what he had to destroy.

The hit-and-run tactics of the desert nomads had bled Soren’s warriors every step of the way, but his plan had eventually succeeded in forcing the Dunerunners to meet him head to head lest the forces from the Final Peaks continue to pillage their culture.

In all the sand-covered land, there was only a single above-ground city, which they called Karvani, and nowhere was more important to the Dunerunners. While not a true capital or seat of government to the nomads, Karvani was treated as both neutral territory and holy ground where their tribes met upon thrice a year.

After sweeping aside the meager defense at several oases thanks to the overwhelming power of Soren’s Cards, his army which was still tens of thousands strong, had carved a path towards Karvani, raiding each underground site along the way. Having seen what Soren had done to their other sites of significance, the tribesfolk had no choice but to meet him in force.

In the end, Soren wasn’t actually trying to wipe these people out, but force them to accept that he was so superior that they had no choice but to bend the knee. So he had brought his army to the edge of Karvani. While he wouldn’t admit it to anyone, the city had taken Soren’s breath away when he first saw it. The walls weren’t the most impressive of defensive features, but they were a multi-league-long mural carved with the full history of the Country of Dunes writ large in images carved upon the orange and brown walls surrounding Karvani. Beyond the circular walls was a city of towers so tall and grand it made much of the Final peaks' architecture, which had always been a point of pride, look banal in comparison.

Over the weeks in which Soren’s forces had battled their way towards the city, he had devoted hours to meditation so that his god could come up with a plan to force the surrender of the Dunerunners without actually wiping them out. The whole point of coming down here was to bring the scattered nation into the fold so that he could get closer to reaching his next rank up. Soren didn’t quite understand why he needed a different culture under his command to continue growing, but need them he did.

The god inside Soren’s Dealer had turned its ludicrously powerful information-gathering abilities towards understanding the customs of the soon-to-be-conquered tribesfolk, and had informed Soren that they had a tradition of having their leaders fight when a true battle would cost both tribes too much.

It was the perfect opportunity now that the four most powerful tribes had gathered in defense of Karvani, Soren had strode out in front of the assembled lines of his army and demanded that all four grand-elders face him at once.

The gathered tribes who were manning the walls of their sacred city had decried him as arrogant and foolish, yet even from the desert floor far beneath the ramparts, he could tell they were relieved that they were being given a potential way to avoid the forthcoming siege. Not that it would be a true siege of course, as neither side could afford to play the long game. Soren would be forced to simply shatter the walls of the city himself and command his warriors to assault.

The traditions of the Dunerunners were a little different from the rest of the worlds when it came to these ritualized duels. They didn't expect people to make use of the Challenge ability built into a Dealer, and in fact didn't even require everyone to use the same sort of Deck when they fought. If a True-Deck wielder challenged a Trials Deck to a duel and they agreed, it wasn't even considered unfair.

The ornate iron lattice of a gate swung open, and one by one they had emerged, the four so-called grand-elders. Each came bearing a spear in one hand and a banner declaring their tribe in the other.

First was the blue flag of Ilarion, borne by elder Preda. Next emerged elder Scava with his white and gold banner of tribe Lancu. Third was the deep green of Florea held by elder Emanoil, and finally the deep crimson of tribe Atanasie in the hands of elder Leontin. Each man bore a beard and was older than Soren, though he didn’t really think they lived up to their title as ‘elders’. Obviously, it was just the name given to the leaders of the Dunerunner tribes.

They had each declared themselves and planted their banners in the sand before the gate. Each man was dressed in loose robes reminiscent of Soren's own, and they practically glowed with Resonant power. That was no surprise given that the four men represented two Rank seven True-Elementals, one Rank six  Greater-Elemental, and a Rank seven Post-Human.

What followed was the most challenging fifteen minutes of Soren’s life up till that point. For years, Soren had lamented the lack of pain and difficulty in his life, as injury would almost instantly be healed by the flow of Life Essence from his Vials back into his body the moment he took any kind of injury. On that day, the Walking-God was forced to reassess. Impaled countless times, hammered by Spells, and Creatures of immense destructive potential. Still, in the end, destiny remained true, and the Lord of Creeds stood victorious above the four corpses of the slain elders, and the sands of the Dune Country turned to glass around him for fifty feet in every direction. His robes were torn and utterly covered in blood.

Soren was glad that his Vials only displayed themselves to other people during a Challenge, lest both assembled armies see that of his two hundred and fifty-six, only four remained full. The god hadn’t felt anywhere near this mortal since he was a child. Thankfully, he had enough Life Essence left that his wounds healed without emitting the steam of someone running on complete empty, but it had been a close thing, and even he was breathing hard and covered in sweat.

Soren would never admit it, but he knew in his heart that had he not drawn and played the False God Savat had secured for him, he and not the four elders might have been the one to die here in the shadow of Karvani.
