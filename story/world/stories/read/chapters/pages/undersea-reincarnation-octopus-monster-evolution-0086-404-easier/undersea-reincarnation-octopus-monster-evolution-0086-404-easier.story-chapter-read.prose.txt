Thanks to the wrap of healing energies Henry had cocooned himself with, his eyes shot open within hours. The aches weren’t all gone, of course. The backlash from forcing a fusion on two already fused Aspects for days came with a price. But that price felt a lot milder right about now.

Too mild, even.

For a moment, Henry thought he’d slept a whole day. As much as the essence of the new Aspect could be a powerful amplifier, it couldn’t get him back on his feet that fast… could it? But then Maurice confirmed that yes, Henry had only dozed off for approximately three hours, which had Henry stare at the crab for nearly five seconds before the reality sunk in.

Life Surge could accelerate soul recovery. Then Lifeweaving amplified that. With those two in mind, Henry had expected his recovery from the strain to be anything between two and five days. There was also the fact that Henry had recently upped his soul’s resilience with Depth-Tempered Soul, which should make it so he recovered faster rather than slower. So two days. That had been his expectation. Two days of rest so things to be recovered enough for him to exert his abilities a little. As for the bits that hadn’t meshed so well in his Aspect? Henry had no clues how long those would take to go away. He expected them to iron themselves out by the time he reached level 10 in the Aspect.

But the essence of the new Aspect saw his expectations, then laughed at them.

Even though I spent days working on this fusion, after the nap it feels as if I just got done finishing an easy one, Henry thought. He recalled perfectly the effort that’d gone into his other Aspect fusions and how long it had taken him to recover back then, which really hadn’t been that long. So the contrast was definitely apparent, and Henry found himself considering how else he could exploit this prodigious recovery. At first, he considered another Aspect fusion, but he immediately shoved that aside. Aside from the fact that he didn’t want to be cooped up for another week, he was really happy with what he had, and he didn’t want to force another fusion. Three Aspects was a good number.

But the skills. I could organize things a little more. Maybe create some new combinations, Henry thought. He’d recently felt his shortcomings when it came to ranged combat, and that would be something to address and work on.

Nodding to himself, he decided to start with that. And when he wasn’t working on that, he’d look into infusing his body with the cores, see if he could snatch a few more stat points. For now, Henry wanted to stretch his legs and find a belligerent monster to get some target practice in.

With a thought, the scene in front of him changed as he looked through a nearby avatar. Or clone.

Henry paused. Which term should he use? He got used to calling them clones. And avatar sounded so… serious.

Eh. This isn’t worth stressing about, he shrugged. Interestingly, while Henry looked through the eyes of this one at the waves crashing on the cliff-side, he could feel and see through all of the nearby clones, all fifteen of them. Even his own body. Before, he had to consciously flip the switch as it were, or he could have his Octomind experience everything, then they would feed it to him as they were more seamlessly integrated with his mind. But now, it was as if all of his Avatars—at least he ones near him—were extensions of the same consciousness.

Henry might need some time get used to it. It wasn’t really overwhelming, and he knew he could handle the perception of a couple dozen more, but when he wanted to rest or meditate, it was still a lot easier to limit the input. It was a lot less distracting, that way. But now that he was paying attention to his surroundings, the information flooded his mind, and Henry found it disturbingly easy to parse.

I’m pretty sure this would have fried my brain a few months back, but here were are, Henry thought as he immediately cataloged the nearby creatures and their threat levels, while Maurice tried to communicate to his new pets that he would be leaving.

“[There isn’t much to hunt here,]” Henry said after a few seconds. “[It’s mostly low to mid B-rank birds. We’ll need to head inland, and we could head in the direction of the orb. As for A-ranks, Find the Path says there’s an A-rank thaaat… huh?]”

“[What?]” Maurice asked, looking up to find Henry—and the one avatar still in the cove—looking up at the bare stone wall. Henry wasn’t really suddenly interested in rock formations. Instead, he was staring at the golden path that would lead them to the orb located on the Reach. Which… he could swear had moved a little since he had last looked at it when they’d first arrived here.

“[I’m almost sure the orb has moved,]” Henry said after a beat. “[Or we did? No we didn’t. It moved. It’s just a couple degrees off, but… yeah. Depending on how far it is, it could have traveled pretty far.]”

It was too bad that Find the Path couldn’t give him an exact distance from his quarry, especially with something preprogrammed like the divination magic of the set. Maurice stared at him for a few seconds.“[Does it mean someone has it? Or something ate it?]”

“[Could be either of those,]” Henry allowed, looking up at the sky, then at the high clouds. His gaze narrowed for a second, then he shook his head. For a moment, Henry considered floating islands, but what were the odds of that? The orb was most likely in the guts of something big. Or in someone’s bag. Which might complicate things a little. But what was the point of worrying about that right now?

“[I guess we’ll find out soon,]” Henry said. No need to stress about the answer until they reached their target.“[Also, just in case we come face to face with the locals, let’s be careful when it comes to appearance alright?]”

From what they’d learned from Attal and from the asking around in the guilds of the Emerald Isles, this would be where most of humanity’s A-ranks were. Exploring, leveling, and harvesting cores to bring back to the rest of the world. They might be trigger-happy when it came to monsters, so there was no need to risk a hostile encounter when a more diplomatic one was on the table. Not that Henry planned on sticking around people much anyway. Plus, he wanted to work on his ranged attacks, so he didn’t need his usual size and arms.

Quickly enough, Henry and Maurice were ready. With their human disguises back on, the duo floated up in the air, lifted by Henry’s Telekinesis, which felt as easy as it was to breathe. He lifted them higher and higher, and he didn’t miss the anxiety radiating off of Maurice, who just let himself be carried up while peering down at the cave where he’d spent the last two weeks.

Henry felt a twinge of regret, at that. “They’ll be fine, buddy. You can come say hi whenever you want. With the new clones… that’s gonna be easy.”

That was underselling it a little. Maurice looked up, eyes glimmering with hope. “You don’t think we’ll get too far?”

The innocence of the crab pinched on Henry’s heart and twisted. “No, it’ll be easy. My network is much more robust now, and my range got really extended with the fusion. And again… I’m sorry I left you alone for so long. Do you wanna go see Ash and Fabian? Or go pester Attal? I’m pretty sure I could swing that last one in minutes.”

Henry had wanted it to be a surprise, but the guilt was eating him up. Maurice’s face lit up, but then the crab got a hold of himself. “Ah. Uh. Maybe after a quick fight? It’s been two weeks. We gotta get ready for… you know. But really? You can reach First Landing? From here? What about Thalis?”

“First Landing will only take me minutes. Thalis… I lost contact when we traveled here, but with the boost I just got? I’m pretty sure I can re-establish it in a few hours. Especially if I use Unfettered Echoes,” Henry explained. He could see it. With the new Aspects, he could have his clones travel at the speed of sound to reconnect the Verdant Grove. Henry was still wrapping his head around the changes. “Honestly Maurice? I’m just as shocked as you. I’m still listing the changes, but it’s pretty intense. And this was with Aspects having regressed to level 7. I can’t imagine what it’s gonna be like at level 10. Or in A-rank. But yes, you’re right,” Henry continued. “We need to work hard to earn our breaks. And I think I see our first target. Or at least, where it should be. But give me a second to make sure I’m ready.”

Maurice used the opportunity to go and hang with the crabs some more, while Henry looked through the facts. He had already reached out with his awareness and took stock of the current status of his network, and the contrast was like night and day. Because even though Henry had lost some levels on the Aspect, Unfettered Echoes at Level 7 was outperforming Dancing Shadows by a significant margin.

For starters, the numbers Henry could field. Henry had noticed the change as soon as the cornerstone upgrade and its effects propagated to his network. Having reached level 10, Dancing Shadows had allowed him to upkeep nearly eight hundred clones, with each having a maximum range of three miles and a half.

At level 7? Unfettered Echoes was already handling nine hundred and fifty clones. Henry had been summoning them since he’d woken up, fifty of them at a time, and it had taken him a while to finally feel the limit. As for the range? The Inkblood kind, which was supposed to have the weaker range, could handle a maximum range of nearly four miles. The Arcane kind?

Henry was still actively trying to measure that.

So far, even the inner-range—which was the limit where the clone would start losing the connection, until the connection was lost at the maximum range—was upwards of four miles. Henry wasn’t sure yet what the maximum range of the Arcane Avatars was. He just knew that, for starters, he might be able to reestablish the connection to First Landing in minutes. Thalis would take a few hours, and Henry believed that he wouldn’t even need to use all of his bandwidth for that.

As for the clones he’d given away or lost contact with, he couldn’t really sense them from this far, unfortunately, but Henry had a feeling that he’d soon get there, especially if the growth of his range continued to accelerate this fast. The description of The Trickster’s Dancing Constellation had undersold the range each clone could reach, and Henry was still making tweaks. As he progressively changed most of the avatar-relays to Arcane Avatars, he had to space them out significantly as there was a lot of overlap. Once he fixed the inefficiencies, things should get better.

And that would be the sort of tasks he didn’t need to take care of by himself. So Henry assigned an Octomind to it, then left the subminds to work while he and Maurice went hunting.

Following the trail drawn by Find the Path only took minutes, and it led the two toward a craggy, jagged rock-filled bleached pit just past the cliff that faced the water. It almost looked as if it had been a stalagmite-filled cave whose roof had been ripped off. Henry scanned the grounds for a few moments, unsure of what it was exactly that lived here, when one of the stalagmites moved. As if it had twitched. It took Henry a second to understand what he was looking at, and when a pair of bulbous, yellow eyes suddenly appeared out of nowhere, the image became crystal clear. Especially when a blue, bulbous tongue peeked out of the gray stone for a fraction of a second before shooting up at him, reaching out for an easy meal and as he saw the attack coming, Henry could only smile.

He did like it when he got attacked first. It made things much easier.
