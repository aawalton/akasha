"The what?" Karlsson the Adept asked as he approached. "Alas, I am but a humble servant."

Spellweaves formed around the cultist's shoulders. Luke sprang into action with the use of Weavestep, bringing the annoying bastard close enough for threads to reach him. After cutting through the spellweaves, there wasn't much more to the fight. Walking off toward the village, heading down the path to where the man who'd just lost his son indicated he'd find the village, Luke pocketed Karlsson's monster core. Not a meta-heart. Despite Guide's explanation, he had more than a little difficulty wrapping his head around the inner workings of dungeons. At least he knew the trickle of experience he was getting helped The Greater System to some small extent. If that experience was a measure of how much, though, they didn't value it all that high, at least not compared to dealing with void-breaks. Now that had been some serious experience gain. After this, he would just go ahead and leave the dungeons for others, except for maybe on some special occasions.

Luke: "They've been like that for the better part of a decade and a half."

The village was uninspiring. Log houses. A mud street or two. Chickens running around and a dog barking somewhere. People were screaming. Skeletons were skeletoning. A couple of cultists carried a young woman between them, dragging her toward the forest. Knowing it was all some elaborate show had dealt a blow to his enthusiasm for dungeons, and that hadn't been at an all-time high before Guide's reveals. Yes, it was void creatures, and yes, they needed to be dealt with, but...

Luke sighed as he ran up to the two cultists and wrecked them before they even noticed him. A cluster of skeletons running from house to house fell to pieces in the mud. The young woman was maybe nineteen and bled from a gash on the left side of her head.

Liesen. Farmer. Level 9.

Not Integrated. Even if he was unsure about what the woman even was, Luke healed her. Seeing a wound like that and not tending to it was against everything he stood for. He still believed in healing those who needed it. That hadn't changed. Ignoring her thanks, he set off again, heading deeper into the village. More cultists appeared. They were a good mix of women and men, young and old. Luke spared none of them, and the fights were without challenge. Each one transpired much like the ones before it. The cultist would be surprised someone challenged him, her, or them, and attempt to form spellweaves. When those spellweaves fell apart, so did Luke's opponents. Boring.

Skeletons were plentiful enough, and a few more advanced sorts made their appearance. They wielded crossbows, suits of armor, and a couple even cast spells. That helped them little since Luke just ignored them all and leapt from cultist to cultist. When killing one cultist meant dropping all his or her skeletons in one go, it didn't make sense to even engage the monsters.

A spell came out of nowhere and struck Luke's back. It did no damage at all, but his threads fell apart. Producing new ones didn't work. Whirling, he saw the offending cultist on the second floor of a two-story building. The little weasel had hidden up there in ambush. Losing control of his mana had brought Luke to a stop, and now monsters and cultists were closing in from every side. There weren't all that many of them left at that point, but enough to be worrisome. One of the remaining cultists formed a fireball and lobbed it Luke's way as spear-wielding skeletons closed in, their jaws rattling with silent glee. Boring.

Threads of mana reappeared, this time with an orange glow. The searing pain was still there, but it was a mute thing, not worth paying attention to. When untinted mana no longer worked, that didn't mean Luke was out of options. Infernal mana was available as well, and if The Cult of the Undead thought he'd put them through the wringer before, they had another thing coming for them. Slicing through the fireball with a thread, Luke raised the hood of his robe again. The skeletons came to a stop, then stood there shuffling, as if unsure of themselves. They would not advance, but they didn't flee either. He wondered if that meant whoever controlled them was stronger than the one back at the farm. It didn't matter much either way. Another spell soared down from overhead, but Luke grabbed hold of the nearest cultist and used him, an older man, as a shield. When the spell hit, the cultist imploded. Seeing a person pulled in on himself before bursting was... gross.

It didn't take long after that to clean up the remaining cultists. A single one escaped because Luke let her go. The curse that left Luke unable to use mana timed out and drained away on its own, allowing him to stop relying on infernal mana. To his great disappointment, the quest did not complete once he had rid the village of all living cultists.

Luke rubbed his temples as he leaned against the wall of a small building, muttering, "Damn it."

"Thank you, great adventurer!" a bald man with a great big beard streaked with gray said as he walked up. "You've saved us all!"

"Please just tell me where to go. It would be faster if I didn't have to wait."

More people were gathering now. Quite the crowd. He wondered where they'd hidden away when he was out single-handedly fighting off the cultists. A lot of them cheered, but it was a half-hearted one. Tears streaked many faces in the crowd.

"Unfortunately, we do not know the location of their dastardly lair!" the one Luke guessed was the mayor cried.

"The forest!" another man supplied. Real helpful.

"Fine," Luke grunted. "I'll find it myself."

Rather than deal with the townsfolk, Luke extracted himself from the crowd and walked off, glad to see no one was following him. The cultist woman he'd let off the hook was still running through the forest somewhere ahead of Luke. He sensed her echo like a burning beacon. Weaver's Perception was an excellent tool for tracking. The difficult part had been healing the cultist woman using infernal mana. He wasn't sure who that had hurt more, her or himself, and the result hadn't been what you might call a healing success, but she could still run. Quite the trooper, that one.

Rather than follow her through the thick underbrush and risk being spotted, Luke sat down on a stump and waited. She'd get there soon enough.

Two damn hours! What kind of location scout puts their secret, evil lair that far from the place they're harvesting for recruits? Stupid cults! More than a little irritated, Luke reached through the limited weave of the dungeon, located her echo thread, and weavestepped through.

She was on her knees, hunched forward with her arms reaching forward on the ground, right in front of a wide wicker chair with a man in it. The prostrate cultist was letting out a low, prolonged moan of despair while what could only be a ritual circle glowed in a forest clearing right behind her. Even with Luke having killed a decent number of these bastards by now, it didn't look like it had made a dent in their numbers. Hundreds of them filled the half-cleared forest, all of them in the ceremonial-looking robes any self-respecting ritualist couldn't do without.

"Greetings," the man in the chair said. "This wretch," he pointed at the woman, "was just telling me of your exploits in the village."

Magan. Master Ritualist. Level 48.

"Hello," Luke said.

Now, here was a proper challenge. Perhaps it wasn't right to hope for a more difficult time, but there was just nothing interesting about this dungeon. It felt like he'd done this before. Perhaps not this exact scenario, but ritualists? Raising the dead? Meh.

Magan rose to his feet, drew in a deep breath as everyone else in the forest seemed to hold theirs, raised a finger as if to point something out, then turned, pushed the chair aside, and fled. Everyone else stood there, frozen, with Luke wondering what the hell had just happened.

"Kill him!" Magan called out.

Luke lashed out with a thread to divert a knife-wielding cultist, who tripped and fell over the still kneeling young woman, then weavestepped past them both as he set off running after the group's fleeing leader. That was not a turn of events he had anticipated.

Magan was quick on his feet, and Luke couldn't help but get bogged down with assholes throwing themselves in his path. Leaping over their heads helped some, but the gap kept growing, and it seemed everyone in that jolly bunch knew the fireball spell, because they kept trying to form it, forcing Luke to cut through spellweave after spellweave or risk being burned to a crisp. One of those spells hitting him wouldn't be the end of the world, he was sure, but he hadn't yet suffered much in the way of burns, if you didn't count radiation ones, and he hoped to keep it that way for as long as possible.

In the end, Luke had to, well... end almost a third of the cultists before they would leave him alone. By that time, Magan was nothing but a small speck in the distance, and almost out of the forest. The robe hung in tatters around Luke, who'd been forced to use Weaver's Renewal on his right arm twice, once because a ritualist cast some curse to possess it or something, because it kept hitting Luke in the face, and once because that same cultist later cast another spell that made the flesh melt off the bone, leaving nothing but a skeletal limb. That hurt. A lot. Luke wasn't sure if that counted as burning, but he had made sure to pay the cultist back in full, and would get great pleasure in feeding his monster core to Smudge later. Stupid assholes.

Once free of their grasping hands and annoying spells, Luke propelled himself forward again and again, launching himself from the ground and off the trunks of trees to gain momentum and catch up. When the trees gave way to farmland, he realized where Magan was heading.

"The portal," Luke groaned.

What. A. Pain.

Speeding up even more, doubling the thread-pushes off the ground with momentum-carrying weavesteps, Luke only touched the ground on occasion, and felt like he was flying over the swaying stalks of wheat. Wind whipped against his face, and tears formed in the corners of his eyes, but he didn't blink. If he did, he might miss Magan, whom he was catching up to. Even so, the Master Ritualist had gained himself an impressive head start by throwing his minions at Luke as he fled, and the portal grew ever closer. Another couple of seconds, and the bastard might have been able to cross over to Earth.

Luke, still pushing to gain more speed, wondered what that part of the whole experience was about. If the monsters and most other beings populating dungeons were beings of the void draped in a second-skin of some long-lost system, why were some of those creatures compelled to find their way to Earth? Well, maybe the why of it wasn't such a difficult concept to conceive of. The void wanted to, well, eat Earth. That idea still being present in their makeup wasn't too far-fetched to imagine. The better question, then, was why they didn't turn back into void creatures and start munching on the weave. Those questions would have to wait. There were no answers to be had before Luke cornered Guide again, and he was about to crash right into Magan mere steps from the portal.

This was going to hurt.
