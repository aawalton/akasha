Arrack scowled as the bastard dragonkin freed himself from his spear.

Then he froze.

The room had shifted.

From one fraction of a second to the next, something had changed. His mind worked as fast as it ever had, pushing into overdrive to try and comprehend what had happened.

The time dilation field…

Even as an A Grade, Arrack had never been the fastest thinker. He had pushed the majority of his resources toward strengthening his offensive spells, and it had paid off. Why think through a complicated plan when he could simply hit his enemy with a telekinetic-reinforced strike and put them out of their misery?

The room flickered, from one fraction of a second to the next. He perceived Xavier moving, stuck inside that time dilation field of his.

He’s shrinking. Dying, Locked Down for an eternity. Even off the spear he’s still in my clutches.

Then something shifted. Arrack’s spell was cancelled out, a notification flashing into his vision.

Hundreds of Xaviers popped into the room. Arrack’s face was stuck in a scowl, but at the sight of this he began the long journey toward widening his eyes.

Then, one of the drones touched him.

The room flickered again, and everyone in it was moving at normal speed—or rather, he was encapsulated by the time dilation field’s time stream once more.

Xavier and his hundreds of drones were still there, an abundance of black wings flapping, all echoing off the walls, creating a cacophony of sound.

“What—” Arrack cut himself off, fear making his heart flutter.

His cores. His energy channels. Even his System notifications—all were blocked.

Locked Down.

He felt the blood rush from his face.

Xavier Collins—the real Xavier Collins, not one of the dragonkin’s drones—stood three feet from Arrack, wearing a ruthless smile. His Storage Rings were back on his fingers, his soul bound weapon was strapped to his hip.

Palini’s corpse was gone, a patch of dried blood on the stone floor denoting where it had been.

And standing on either side of the dragonkin was a pair of B Grade twins: Dybal and Lydad. They glared daggers at Arrack.

“It took me some time to figure out how to get the Locked Down spell to work on you,” Xavier said. “I’d tell you how I got it to work, but those are exactly the kind of trade secrets you were trying to torture out of me.”

Arrack sprang forward. He slammed into something solid—an invisible wall he couldn’t detect without his spiritual sense. A wall strong enough to stop him. He stumbled back, touched a finger to his lips. Blood.

He drew upon all his power. His fist crashed into the invisible wall—the bastard had taken Malina, his double-edged spear, the soul bound weapon his great-great-grandfather had gifted him when he’d been A Grade.

I failed you, Ancestor. Your plan should have worked.

The bones in his hand crumpled against the hard surface of the barrier, opening the skin at his knuckles.

“You can’t trust them,” Arrack spat, nodding at Dybal and Lydad. “They’re criminals, the lot of them. That’s how they ended up here in the first place.”

Xavier stepped to one side; his drones and the others around him followed suit, all parting for a powerful-looking, broad-shouldered man in black armour.

“We both know that isn’t true, Arri,” Striker said in that cool, deep voice of his.

Arrack stared open-mouthed. “Striker?”

Arrack’s gaze slid from Striker to Xavier, then to all the other agents in the room. “You’re really gong to leave with this man? You’re all going to get yourself killed!” He raised his chin. “Capture him! Kill him if you have to! I will personally ensure your freedom if you do.” His voice was strong, convincing. Hearing it made him stand straighter. “More than that—you’ll be rewarded for your loyalty!”

He let his words sit with the crowd and looked back at Striker, trying to communicate with the man with his eyes. They’d fought together long enough. They didn’t need Communication Stones to know what the other was thinking.

Except there was no surge from the crowd.

And the look on Striker’s face told him everything he needed to know.

“Why?” Arrack said.

Striker’s face turned hard. “Why do you think?”

Several more Denizens came forward. Teeri. Lyan. Ver. Zannic.

A Grade memory mages, every single one of them. The most powerful the base had to offer.

And they could only be here for one thing.

Arrack told himself he wouldn’t struggle. Wouldn’t scream.

Wouldn’t beg.

He’d fight, tooth and nail, until the last.

Except he knew he couldn’t win, so what was the point? He didn’t understand how any of this was possible. Didn’t understand how Xavier had freed himself from the Locked Down spell, or even from this room.

He cast spells when he should have been Locked Down. The runes in the air…

He’d heard of the man’s strange abilities in all the briefings he’d received, but those spell patterns of his should have taken a stylus and many hours to draw up—yet they’d materialised instantly.

He needs to know about this. He needs to know about all of this.

He brought his hand to his chest. Thousands of years he’d lived in this body.

And now, he was going to have to start over from scratch.

But maybe I haven’t failed you, great-great-grandfather.

Arrack’s energy channels were blocked, but he didn’t need them for what came next. A series of runes were etched into the bones of his sternum. He remembered, vividly, waking from the surgery that had put them there.

Sometimes he still felt a dull ache.

He ran his index finger across his chest in a series of small movements the runes would recognise. Something inserted deep inside his body—a peak A Grade item—shifted.

The Dead Man’s Switch.

It activated.

All the energy from all his cores shot outward in a massive wave, incinerating his body, his mind, everything he had worked on for so long, leaving nothing behind—

Except his soul.

He didn’t stop to look down at his body. Didn’t stop to look at the explosion that would break through that infernal invisible barrier. If he stopped, his spirit might get caught. Everyone who’d read up on Xavier Collins knew he’d been a Soul Reaper before he became… whatever the hell he was now.

And so all that was left of Arrack fled—or rather was called—back to the inner sectors, where the soul mages of his great-great-grandfather’s empire would find a suitable body to rehost his spirit.

Then he could tell Jhanku all he knew.

~

The explosion ripped forth from Arrack’s cores.

Xavier saw the shift in the man’s internal energies, then a flash of light, but there hadn’t been anything he could do to stop it.

The man’s body disintegrated—everything his mind held was gone.

His last resort.

Though Xavier couldn’t stop the explosion, he could contain it. He had his own, separate time dilation field around him that was different to the one that encapsulated the rest of the people in the room. A safety precaution, one he was glad he’d taken.

He shifted the time dilation field encapsulating everyone but him, forcing it to move as slow as the drone that held it could manage, freezing the explosion where it was.

Xavier stepped over to the barrier he’d set up around the man and looked through it. Sinking into his Immortal Soul lens, he saw just how powerful the explosion was.

All of an A Grade’s cores. All that stored energy. Far more energy than Xavier possessed. Every bit of it expended as a destructive force so powerful it disintegrated the A Grade’s body from within.

I didn’t know you had it in you.

The force would be enough to destroy the barrier. The base.

And whatever planet this base was built on. I’ll have to get them out—all of them.

He blinked. Frowned. Something niggling at him.

The flash of light after Arrack’s internal energies shifted… it was too early to be part of the explosion.

With everyone else in the room frozen outside the field, Xavier retreated into his mind and replayed the explosion—Arrack looking defeated, touching his chest, the strange movements his finger had made that Xavier had thought was some nervous tick.

Then: boom.

Devastating power erupting from the A Grade’s cores.

Only… that flash of light came a fraction of a second before those cores had erupted and before Xavier had reversed the stream of the time dilation field.

Reviewing the memory in incredibly slow motion, Xavier watched as Arrack’s soul was pulled away.

It didn’t just drift away, it was yanked. So fast…

Xavier opened his mouth. He’d been about to ask Roln what this might mean, but the mental construct was still huddling, injured, in one of the rooms of his mind. Besides, Xavier already knew.

Arrack’s soul escaped.

Xavier curled his fingers into a fist, nails digging into his palm. A wound opened and closed as he clenched.

He’d intended to give the man a true death.

Xavier shifted his own time dilation field so it would grow to encompass Striker. Xavier had been careful—very careful—when attempting to convert that man. He’d spent months inside his time dilation field, trapping Striker, an A Grade, inside a barrier identical to the one Arrack was inside, and learning how to use a similar spell to Locked Down that he’d created on the fly on someone with more raw power than himself. It shouldn’t have worked with such a power difference—not for more than a second or so.

But he’d made it work.

He’d manipulated the raw Celestial Energy around him using his Immortal Soul lens and his Living Soul as the lever, depleting his soul reserve to the edge of soul sickness then awaiting its renewal.

It was an agonisingly time-consuming endeavour, but time was one commodity Xavier had in abundance.

He’d secured the base, along with the cooperation of all its agents and staff—even the Denizens who’d been standing next to Arrack in the Social Rooms had cooperated, seeing a true possibility for escape with Arrack effectively neutralised.

Striker, to Xavier’s surprise, had been the most grateful for his freedom.

Though Xavier could erase contracts, such a skill wasn’t available to those he’d freed. To ensure he wouldn’t be betrayed a second time, he’d signed truth contracts with every single one of the Denizens he’d freed, truth contracts that went both ways.

He’d explained that he couldn’t erase his own contract without erasing theirs alongside it.

That precaution had helped develop mutual trust.

I managed to free you all after all. He glanced at the blood stain on the floor. No… not all of you.

He touched his main Storage Ring. He would deliver Palini’s body back to his family. It was the least he could do, and it was nowhere near enough.

Striker blinked, staring at the contained explosion where Arrack should have been. He glanced around at the frozen Denizens, then looked at Xavier. “What happened?”

“Did you know about this?”

“Know about what?”

Xavier held his hand out. A glowing memory orb appeared. “Watch.”

Striker took the orb and closed his eyes, replaying the moment from Xavier’s perspective. “Arrack’s soul. Bastard had a Dead Man’s Switch with a Soul Escape.” He opened his eyes. “I didn’t know.”

Xavier stared at the far wall, in the direction the soul had fled. “He’s going to be rehosted, isn’t he?”

“Yes. Then Jhanku will know what happened here. In detail. And while I still don’t rightly know how you managed…” Striker gestured to the room. “All this, Jhanku might. After all the stories about you, he’ll assume you survived the explosion. He’ll assume we all did.”

“It doesn’t matter.”

“What?”

Xavier locked eyes with the man. “Jhanku already had his sights on me. You’re not the first agents of his I’ve encountered, though I didn’t expect such a base of operations so far from his domain.”

“His influence reaches farther than you’d imagine.”

“And one day mine will reach farther than anyone’s ever has. When the time comes, I’ll deal with Jhanku.” Xavier dropped his gaze, allowing himself to fully stare at the dark stain on the floor. “He’ll pay the price for what happened here today.”

“I don’t know that he’s the only one who’ll pay,” Striker muttered.
