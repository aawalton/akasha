Koré

The halls of House Dulmont were brimming with activity even this late at night. She hurried past guards escorting enemy prisoners: mages, soldiers, and even spirits captured during the fighting. Many of their people were weary and exhausted, but time was crucial now. In the morning, the fighting would continue, and father needed any scrap of information he could get to turn things in their favor.

She held up the small hand mirror she usually kept in her pocket to quickly inspect herself. A mess. Those were the words that came to mind. She looked like a mess. She pulled light-purple hair into a side ponytail that draped her left shoulder. Her lab coat was wrinkled and unwashed, both problems that could luckily be addressed with magic. She did so with a wave of her hand. The eye bags were more out of her control; they’d fade away with a few nights’ rest. That wasn’t happening anytime soon. She only hoped she’d composed herself enough to at least look presentable.

She didn’t want to worry Alader.

Many lab-assistants, researchers, and guards stopped to acknowledge her as she walked by. She nodded politely, though she wished tonight of all nights they could skip the formalities. In times like this, the reminder of her noble status made her…uncomfortable. These men and women, mages and soldiers, died in the name of her household. They died in the name of causes she rarely agreed with. Nevertheless, it was beyond her control.

At least for now.

The familiar sound of Tetra’s heels clacked from a room up ahead.

Koré smiled.

“Is that my dear sister I sense nearby?” Tetra called out moments before she emerged from the doorway of her own lab. Though calling them torture chambers might be a bit more appropriate. Tetra was as beautiful as ever. She had long, light-purple hair and mana-blue eyes that caught the attention of everyone she passed. Koré had never been all that concerned with her own looks, but from time to time she felt a quiet envy when she looked at Tetra. Her sister was the kind of person who was beautiful no matter what situation she was facing. She’d seen Tetra cut men down on the battlefield, spraying blood in her wake; she’d seen her sister pale from sickness and bedridden, and even now, after hours of interrogating an enemy soldier, Tetra was beauty incarnate.

She’d have more suitors if not for the fact that she was…well, some might say evil. Koré preferred to refer to Tetra as morally challenged. “It is,” Kore responded.

Tetra caught her in a hug before Koré could react to the obvious blood splatter on her sister’s dress.

It left quite a stain on her white lab coat. Koré frowned at that and looked up to notice Tetra’s subtle smile.

“Oops! I’ll clean that while you tend to my prisoner.” Tetra playfully leaned forward to inspect the lab coat. Koré turned to stare inside Tetra’s ‘lab.’ A man was strapped to a standing table. To an untrained eye, he’d look perfectly healthy, aside from the pained look on his face. But Koré’s mana senses painted a different picture. She was especially sensitive to the flow of mana, and his gave off the impression of a putrid, dark purple, rather than the calm, serene blue that most mana took the form of.

Mana Rot. The specialty of House Dulmont. At least it was the path everyone else in her family walked. She held up a hand without stepping into the room. “Mana Cleanse!” Her authority purged the impurity from his body. He sucked in a deep breath, and relief flooded his face. He looked like a man who had surfaced from a deep ocean after almost running out of air, or someone who had taken shelter from the scorching sun in the midst of summer.

Tetra smirked. “Excellent. He’ll last quite a while longer now.”

Koré frowned. She should’ve known better than to believe Tetra would take a break from her work. “Who is he?”

“One of the Tower family’s mercenaries. As usual, they send lapdogs and hired hands instead of their own.”

Tetra’s words were brave but foolish. House Dulmont might be minor nobles, but the Tower family consisted of extraordinary mages with powerful techniques. The Queen of Towers, her grandson Aren, who now took the title Lord of Gold, and of course their rising star, Susanna Tower, who likely wasn’t far from a title of her own. Especially if the rumors of her receiving personal training from her grandmother were true. If any one of them took to the battlefield, House Dulmont would suffer greatly.

But they wouldn’t. Why would they need to? The Tower family was…well, the Tower family. They were essentially royalty. Her father’s obsession with revenge was a minor nuisance at best. And it would continue to be, unless Koré’s research paid off.

Tetra clapped her hands together. “Well, back to it, dear sister. Shall we eat breakfast in the morning? I’m sure we’ll both work until sunrise, and I’d like to look forward to something.”

Koré stared at the prisoner. “Sometimes I think you enjoy your work too much, Tetra.”

“I’m a hard worker, and I live for praise. But I can only work as hard as I do because of your talents, little Ko.”

The nickname reminded Koré of happier times. Before they’d entered a world of research subjects, prisoners, and war. “Yes. I’d like to get breakfast later,” she said.

Tetra smiled and approached her prisoner. “Alright, ready for round twelve?”

Koré reached her lab eventually and found Alader waiting for her.

“My lady, I’d wondered what was keeping you away from the lab,” he said without looking up. His voice was deep, as was customary of a dreadbeast. A cuff was already affixed to his wrist; it painlessly removed a small sample of blood and teleported it to a mana-device not far away. He stared at it, making sure it was working properly.

Unlike some of his kin, Alader preferred to cover his upper body. She’d had a special tunic made just for him from the pelt of a large mana-beast she’d purchased. He was broad-shouldered and muscular, even by dreadbeasts standards. His horns jutted outward and upward. Each one extending several feet away from his face before spiraling towards the ceiling. His traits were buffalo-like in nature, but there had always been something more human about him than most dreadbeasts she’d interacted with. It wasn’t just his looks, but his interests. Alader liked human music and literature. He even enjoyed their work together.

He sat in the center of her lab on a medical table designed specifically to support the weight of dreadbeasts. An array of instruments and tools was on neat display on the table beside him. The rest of the room was carefully arranged for her mana-medical research. Cabinets for storage, mana devices for analyzing samples, and runes of cleansing and calming.

“I’m so sorry, Alader. Tetra needed my help, and everything is…chaotic tonight.

He looked up at her. “I understand. You look lovely today—” His face hardened. He jumped up from the bed and took a step toward her, gently raising a hand. “What happened? Are you okay?” Urgency filled his voice.

She shook her head and followed his gaze.

The bloodstain on her lab coat. Of course Tetra hadn’t held up her end of the bargain. Koré had forgotten all about it, so much for not worrying Alader. “There’s no need to worry. Tetra hugged me.”

The dreadbeast scowled and waved a hand. “I wish you’d let me escort you again. I worry about you being around her without me. She’s…unhinged.”

“And still my sister. Be nice!” Despite her words, Koré couldn’t help but smile. Alader’s concern was endearing. He was the closest thing she had to a close friend these days. She approached the mana device and pushed her mana through it. It didn’t take long to analyze the blood sample. The metallic cylindrical device lit up as a variety of runes glowed on its surface and some even within, though she couldn’t see them. The results came through her System interface, which was completely integrated with her lab.

Mana Resistance Rate - 58%

She raised a brow at that.

She’d had reservations about testing their family technique on the dreadbeasts. They were technically slaves. Or they had been. Her father purchased them hoping to bolster their family’s forces, but Koré had thrown a fit over it. Ironically, it had been Tetra who offered a compromise. Koré needed test subjects for the project father had assigned her, and they could give the dreadbeasts an opportunity to work off the money her father had spent this way. That way, at least, they’d eventually be freed.

It still wasn’t to her liking, but Alader and a few others had happily agreed. At first, some of the dreadbeasts still had been tasked with menial labor on her father’s behalf. But Alader and the other volunteers had contributed so much to her efforts that she had managed to get them relative autonomy. They couldn’t leave, not until father considered their debt paid off, but she was closer than ever to finishing her work. Work that would pay their debt and then some.

58%.

Her serum was working. It wouldn’t be long now. At 70%, she’d have a prototype effective enough to make others take them seriously. A potion that could undo lingering magical effects in place of a healer. Others had attempted it in the past, but most of those projects met untimely ends. It turned out powerful mages weren’t fond of easily accessible remedies for their most dangerous and potent spells. But three members of her family were lord-class mages, including herself, and their status as nobles allowed them to mask their personal affairs better than most independent researchers. Once she reached her target number, her family could use it to seek favor from the Council of Gods. The rulers of the world rewarded those who contributed to its progress. They weren’t as malignant as the gods of the past, at least, if the rumors were to be believed.

“Well, how is it?” Alader asked, snapping her out of her thoughts.

“58!” She grinned.

Alader stepped forward and lifted her in the air in a hug. It was amazing how gentle he could be despite his size and strength.

“It’s improving faster and faster. We could finish in months at this rate,” he said. Every day he seemed more like a research partner than a volunteer test subject. It helped that dreadbeasts had some natural resistance to magic already. It made them a perfect fit for her work.

“Then father will free your people,” she said. Koré smiled, though the thought selfishly made her sad. She’d come to appreciate Alader’s company quite a lot. She’d miss him when he left.

He sat her down and smiled. “Yes, I suppose so.” Alader’s head suddenly shot up. He looked at the ceiling, and then his gaze trailed toward the door. He stepped in front of her protectively.

“What is it?” she asked.

He seemed on edge.

“We’re under attack,” he said.

“What?” Alarm filled her voice. “What do you mean? That’s not possible. Our guards—”

“I don’t know. But I sensed something approach. Something powerful. It flew overhead, but it’s here now.”

She took a deep breath. “Wait here. I need to warn father and Tetra!” If the three lord-class mages of House Dulmont worked together, they should be able to repel almost any attack on their home territory.

“I’m coming with you,” Alader said. His tone left no room for refusal. Admittedly, she appreciated it. This facility was part of the Dulmont Estate. They should be untouchable here. If something had really penetrated their defenses, well, it would make her feel better to have Alader at her side.

He went on ahead before she could protest and pushed the door open. He leaned his head to push one horn out in front of the other to avoid getting stuck in the doorframe.

Death greeted them.

The guards in the hallway had been replaced by corpses. Koré ran forward and checked the pulse of the closest one. Nothing. His skin was cold to the touch, and some bizarre mixture of blood and green liquid had formed a puddle in front of his mouth.

She frowned. Alader had only noticed moments ago. How was it possible that someone had been dead outside the door long enough to grow cold?

She stepped away and examined the others. One’s skin was patchy; another looked as if his body had been drained of blood. A third guard’s hair had all fallen out, and he now had an empty pit where his eyes had once been.

“What happened?” Alader asked, a grim expression on his face.

“It’s like…it’s like they were all infected by something different. All at once,” she said. She placed a hand on the corpse closest to her.

“Mana Cleanse,” she whispered. The power of House Dulmont was one that corrupted the mana within someone, killing it, like rotten flesh. It caused immense pain and could leave magicians magically crippled if not treated. It was a power she’d refused. She’d developed her own technique instead, a variant that cleansed impurities rather than introduced them. She’d wanted to help rather than hurt, but of course her father had still weaponized her.

The power to heal meant he and Tetra could use their magic more carelessly. After all, they could always call her to fix their broken victims and repeat the cycle anew. She frowned at the thought while she waited for her spell to work.

The impurity in the corpse burnt away.

She sensed something else strange. “It’s highly contagious, but it’s not affecting me. The really odd thing is that they all have different symptoms. It doesn’t make any sense.”

Alader reached down and placed a hand on one of the other corpses. He waited a moment, then raised a brow at her. Her mana senses swept over him. “Nothing. Either you’re immune to whatever this is, or it’s not something transmissible between species. Good. Let’s proceed.”

It looked as if a wave of death traveled ahead of them. No matter how hard they ran, it seemed impossible to get ahead of it. The sense of dread only grew as she hurried to find her sister.

Please be okay Tetra.

Alader sensed the fight before she did. “Up ahead!”

He charged ahead of her into Tetra’s room. The prisoner was torn in half at the waist; it looked like one of Tetra’s techniques had killed him. Perhaps it was collateral damage. But Koré’s eyes didn’t linger on the dead man for long. She focused on the gaping hole in the wall leading outside.

Outside, her sister clutched her heart, wearing a pained expression. Before her stood a being in the shape of a man made from a green liquid. The creature radiated death and sickness. “What is that?” Koré asked.

The being turned and focused on Alader. “You’re free now. Leave this place. I will handle the cleanup.”

Alader shook his head. “What? What are you talking about?”

Tetra glanced over, and her eyes locked on Koré. “Where is father? Why hasn’t he brought reinforcements yet?”

The green man tilted his head. “Are you referring to the Lord Class mage in the throne room? He was corrupted through and through. But do not despair. He is cleansed now, and soon you shall be as well.”

Tetra snarled and held up a hand. “Mana Rot Corrosion!”

A blast of purple mana filled the air around the creature.

When it dissipated, the green humanoid creature was unharmed. A barrier glimmered around him.

Koré stepped outside and took her place beside Tetra. Alader followed her, glaring at their opponent.

The green man frowned. “You misunderstand. I am here to free your people,” he said again toward Alader. “And I have no quarrel with you, young lady. You are not corrupted. I do not sense anything to cut away within you. Please step aside. I don’t wish to harm you by accident while performing my work.”

Koré’s face hardened. Tetra might not be the best person in the world, but she was Koré’s sister. Maybe her father really was dead, or maybe the being before them was lying. But one way or another, she would not let him do as he pleased.

The Green Man

He wouldn’t harm the dreadbeast before him. Already he’d sent a portion of his being to escort the others trapped here away. This one was confused, but he could guide it to the others once he finished with the mage. His eyes turned to the new woman. The woman he had the feeling he wasn’t supposed to kill. Despite that, he couldn’t afford to simply ignore her. If she was going to get in his way, then he would deal with her. The Green Man had no choice but to eliminate them both and set the last dreadbeast free. He held up a palm and created another fireball in his hand. His orders forbade him from infecting them; his instructions were clear. He was to deal with lord-class mages personally. That was exactly what he intended to do.
