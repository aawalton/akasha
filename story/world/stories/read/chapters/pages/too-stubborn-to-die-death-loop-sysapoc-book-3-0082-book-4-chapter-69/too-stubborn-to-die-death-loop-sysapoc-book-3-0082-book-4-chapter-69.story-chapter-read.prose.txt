“I don’t report to you, Death God! If you have problems, take it up with your fellow gods. But I have been instructed to provide passage to the boss. He wasn’t exactly sure what would happen if he didn’t hold them back, but since the quest objective said that they had to beat the boss before they were overwhelmed, he had a pretty good feeling that the undead could break into the boss chamber if left alone.

He met the horde head-on, a wide smile bending his face as his fists slammed into rotting flesh and bone. He trusted his team to deal with the objective more than anybody, and now he had the chance to break minions of that damned Death God.

But as he smashed through them, he noticed something. Each time they died, a burst of the corrupted aether poured out, and it kind of felt invigorating.

“Damn, that shit is like a Red Bull!”

Aaron shook his head. He was getting hopped up on corrupted aether, and his eyes went wide as he crashed into the horde with ever-increasing vigor.

At first, he’d liked the feeling. But he was getting jittery, and kind of anxious, like he was headed for a caffeine overload, but he had a seemingly endless sea of undead still to carve a path through.

“Not good. I’m gonna die of a heart attack at this rate!”

The problem was, it was really fun. Skeletons exploded under his fists, and zombies were torn apart, and the aether bursts pumped excitement straight into his veins.

It was too much. He needed a second, feeling like his chest was about to explode, and he used a particularly powerful [ Bombardier Fist ] to blow dozens of the undead away and get some breathing room.

“Shit. shit. Shit. What am I going to do? What am I going to do?” Aaron was speaking like a chipmunk, words tumbling out of his mouth in a jumble.

The corrupted aether might not have been hurting him, at least not yet, but the buildup was certainly getting to him. He was starting to feel like he might explode if it kept up.

He could feel like a separate pool of energy within him, and he knew he needed to figure out how to release the valve.

“Wait! Fuck that’s what’s going on!”

[ Reap ] was working the entire time passively. Every time he killed an undead, the racial Skill was syphoning energy from his kills. But unlike normal kills, the only energy that could be stolen from the undead was the corrupted aether.

And it was something else.

“I do not need undead crack in my life!” Aaron roared, but there wasn’t much he could do. The horde was already atop him again, and if he ran, or stopped fighting, they might go after his teammates and head to the boss room.

He tried cycling the aether through his core, and expelling the energy from his pores, but it wasn’t anywhere near effective enough. It did, however, give him a nice deathly aura of corrupted aether.

“What am I gonna do?”

His mind was racing a million minutes a second, and he tried to think of anything as he threw himself into a dizzying array of punches and kicks. With his usual stubbornness charged forward, pressing himself against the horde, attacking wildly as he tried to come up with a plan.

Aaron’s fists and kicks were shooting every which way, somehow getting faster and faster as the corrupted aether built up. Soon even the undead horde was falling back as he scored kills at stupendous speed. It was incredible. He turned into a blur of hopped-up destruction, brutalizing the undead forces.

But he seriously needed a release. And he was starting to get very worried. Screw heart attack. What if he exploded and then revived using the corrupted aether? Would he turn into an undead? He didn’t want to find out.

Aaron closed his eyes, but his mind was soaring, making concentrating incredibly hard. But he had to focus. To concentrate on his aether as he fought back the horde. It was like his third or fourth, or maybe fifth eye by that point had opened up, and he felt like he could read the undead minds, let alone their movements.

It was like he was a part of them. A matrix of expanded thought stretching out, and encapsulating all of the dead within his understanding.
