Albert hated seeing unattended cups around. The Infinite Archive was currently experiencing what the administration had described as a multiversal containment emergency, but someone had nevertheless left a half empty porcelain teacup on the edge of a mahogany reading table in the East Wing. Albert considered both problems as equally serious.

He stepped up to the table, his polished black leather shoes making absolutely no sound against the marble floor. He was a lean man with hair greying sharply at the temples, his posture perfectly straight. His immaculate black suit bore the silver insignia of the Chief Custodian. His white gloves were spotless. His black bow tie was perfectly straight. He picked up the teacup, wiped a faint ring of condensation from the polished mahogany with a cloth, and placed the offending porcelain onto his rolling cart.

Around him, the Infinite Archive was alive. It was a staggering, deafening symphony of existence. Thousands of patrons and staff moved through the endless marble galleries. Above them, floating catalogue indicators zipped through the air, guiding scholars toward shelves that stretched impossibly high into the vaulted ceilings.

The portal arches at the end of the grand hall flashed in a constant, rhythmic sequence whoosh, crack, gurgle, chime!! spitting out dignitaries, researchers, and field archivists from a hundred different realities and worlds. Automatic shelving tracks clicked and whirred, shuttling requested volumes through pneumatic glass tubes. The enormous Intake Hall buzzed with the overlapping arguments of intake processors categorizing the daily shipments of over thousand worlds.

"Morning, Albert," a passing senior indexer called out, balancing a stack of levitating scrolls.

"Good morning, Madam," Albert replied with a polite nod.

"Is the eastern stairwell still closed?"

"It is," Albert confirmed.

"Reality problem?" she asked, adjusting her glasses.

"Wet floor," Albert said.

She paused. "Also in reality a problem."

Albert nodded sympathetically and continued pushing his cart.

For all the alarms, the Archive still looked as though it were functioning exactly as it was meant to. It was a bustling, chaotic, magnificent workplace that just happened to contain the collected knowledge of the multiverse. But beneath the noise, Albert could feel a faint vibration in the marble.

INSTITUTIONAL ALERT TERMINAL PROGNOSTIC RECORD INCIDENT

Containment status: PARTIAL

Staff access restrictions expanded.

The blue system text flared in the air near the ceiling. Most of the patrons didn't even look up. The Archive dealt with hazards constantly.

A few minutes later, another alert appeared.

CITATION CASCADE DETECTED

Automatic relationship inference: SUSPENDED

Albert dismissed the notice with a wave of his gloved hand. He wasn't authorized to know what a Citation Cascade was, nor did he possess the clearance to understand the Terminal Prognostic Record Incident. He was just turning toward the logistical transit corridors when a third alert materialized.

EXTERNAL SPECIALIST CONSULTATION

Consultant: MORZATH THE UNDYING

Field: SOUL-STATE CONTINUITY

Access class: CATASTROPHE

Sponsoring authority: KEEPER #4,091

Albert considered the classification unnecessarily dramatic.Then something screamed from Consultation Chamber Nine.

Albert reconsidered. But the East Reading Room still had broken glass on the floor near aisle four, and that fell entirely within his jurisdiction. He navigated his cart through the grand intersection connecting the East and North wings, turning into the transit corridors. The noise of the public galleries faded, replaced by the heavy, echoing thuds of sealed iron containers being loaded onto track bound pallets. The transfer station was a hive of frantic activity, flooded with harsh white route lamps and shouting junior staff.

Albert stopped for a moment observing the chaos unfolding,Standing beside the transit rails was Keeper #4,091. The Keeper looked terrible. His administrative robes were torn at the hem and hands were stained with black ink with dark circles beneath his eyes. He looked as though he had not slept in a week, yet he was moving with a frantic energy.

Standing beside him, looking exactly as composed and immaculate as Albert felt a professional ought to look, was the External Closure Consultant. The Curator held a leather bound clipboard, supervising a line of junior staff physically hauling black iron boxes toward the transfer platform.

"Batch three hundred twelve is ready," the Curator said, his voice crisp and sharp.

"Then move it," Keeper #4,091 replied, rubbing his temples.

"The western route is degrading," the Curator warned.

"Use the north."

"The north route crosses Citation."

Keeper #4,091 stopped rubbing his temples. His hand dropped to his side. "Then do not use the north."

The Keeper turned, spotting Albert waiting patiently with his cleaning cart.

"Chief Custodian," the Curator said, acknowledging him with a slight nod.

"Gentlemen," Albert replied. He looked at the heavy iron boxes. "Are we relocating the Restricted Collection?"

"Only the terminal hazards," Keeper #4,091 said dryly. "We are establishing an external boundary. Curator Annex Seven requires an independent guardian. Someone whose operational mandate exists outside the ordinary cataloguing structure."

"Mabel," the Curator suggested immediately while the Keeper nodded in agreement. Albert looked up. Mabel was currently assigned to the grand Public Intake hall. She was one of the Archive's most effective deterrents against unauthorized entries.

A heavy thud echoed down the transit corridor. Mabel appeared, fully in the silver and blue insignia of the Infinite Archive, her pale, stone-like hide gleaming. She was magnificent, an enormous predator radiating absolute authority.

"You requested my presence, Keeper," Mabel rumbled, her voice vibrating in Albert's chest.

EMERGENCY SECURITY REASSIGNMENT

Employee: MABEL

Position: Senior Access Guardian

From: PUBLIC INTAKE To: EXTERNAL CUSTODY ANNEX 7

Duration: UNTIL EMERGENCY CLOSURE TRANSFER COMPLETE

Mabel read the glowing blue text that materialized before her. She looked at the iron boxes, then down at Keeper #4,091.

"How long?" Mabel asked.

"Just a few hours, I think" Keeper #4,091 said, his voice tight. "Just until the transfer is complete."

"Understood," Mabel said.

Albert stepped forward slightly. "I'll see that Public Intake is covered in your absence, Senior Access Guardian."

Mabel looked down at the Chief Custodian. "I expect my post back, Albert."

"Naturally," Albert replied.

Mabel turned her massive body and began walking down the transit tunnel, following the polished steel rails into the brightly lit transfer corridor.

Keeper #4,091 turned back to the staging area. He grabbed a stack of sealed administrative files and threw them onto the lower shelf of a passing logistics trolley.

Albert sighed. He parked his cleaning cart, walked over to the trolley, and picked up the files. He realigned the edges precisely, moved them to the upper secured shelf, and locked the retaining bar in place.

"Keeper," Albert said.

Keeper #4,091 turned, looking completely frayed.

Albert tapped the locked retaining bar. "Emergency Closure is not Records Disposal."

The Keeper stared at him for a second. "Albert, reality may end before lunch."

"Then there is very little reason to make the filing worse beforehand," Albert said calmly.

Keeper #4,091 blinked. And then, despite the exhaustion, despite the terminal hazard alerts flashing in the air above them, the man laughed. It was a short, sharp bark of genuine amusement.

"What would we do without you, Albert?" the Keeper asked, shaking his head.

"Have a significantly untidier apocalypse, sir," Albert replied.

The floor suddenly dropped out from under them.The marble simply ceased to obey gravity for a fraction of a second. Albert's shoes left the floor. The iron boxes rose an inch into the air.Then gravity slammed back into place.The sound of shattering glass echoed from the public galleries. A deafening, metallic groan tore through the architecture of the Archive.

Albert looked toward the end of the transit corridor. The tall, arched windows normally displayed a serene, magical projection of a sunlit courtyard. Now, the leftmost window showed a blazing, impossible noon. The middle window showed a screaming vortex of blood-red clouds. The right window showed nothing but absolute, lightless void.

To his left, a row of heavy wooden supply shelves violently vibrated and then duplicated themselves, phasing in and out of each other with a sickening crunch of overlapping matter. The brass lettering on a nearby directional sign simply fell off the wood, the concepts no longer clinging to the metal.

REALITY INTEGRITY DEGRADING EVACUATION - NONESSENTIAL PERSONNEL

The blue text burned in the air. The distant noise of the Archive changed. The productive hum of a thousand conversations shattered into the unmistakable roar of panic.

"Curator," Keeper #4,091 snapped, his exhaustion vanishing behind absolute, terrifying focus. "Seal the boundary. Now."

"Understood," the Curator said, already moving toward the transfer platform.

Keeper #4,091 looked at Albert. "Evacuate the custodial staff."

"I am already doing so, sir," Albert said, pulling a heavy brass ring of keys from his belt.

He didn't run though, Running caused panic and led to mistakes. He walked swiftly, his mind rapidly sorting through the Infinite Archive's vast geography. He knew the floorplans and the maintenance routes by heart. He knew which service stairs bypassed the magical elevators, and which structural corridors relied on physical architecture rather than spatial enchantments.

He directed a dozen panicking junior indexers down a spiral stone staircase, locked a violently vibrating spatial door before it could open onto a vacuum, and manually triggered the fire shutters in the West Wing. His uniform lapel chimed.

"Chief Custodian," a frantic voice crackled through the administrative enchantment. "Four junior archivists are unaccounted for in the East Section."

Albert checked his mental map. The East Section was currently buckling under a spatial anomaly. "Where exactly?"

"Sub level three, near the topographical maps. But you can't reach them. The East service corridor has failed."

"Then they cannot use the East service corridor," Albert said calmly.

"Albert, that is what failure means. The geometry is gone."

"There is a manual maintenance access beneath it," Albert replied.

He turned down a narrow, unmarked hallway, pulled a specific brass key from his ring, and unlocked a low wooden door that most staff didn't know existed.

The maintenance access was cramped and smelly. The Archive was shuddering violently now, shaking dust from the stone ceiling. Albert moved quickly, navigating by memory. He emerged through a floor grate into Sub level three.

The room looked a disaster. The massive topographical map cabinets had been thrown across the room. The far wall was simply gone, replaced by a shifting fracture in reality that smelled of diesel. The floor was tilted at a 20 degree angle. Huddled behind an overturned oak table were four junior archivists, clutching their heads as the room shook.

"Come along," Albert said, stepping over a pile of spilled parchment.

One of the juniors, a young man with a bleeding scrape on his forehead, looked up in absolute terror. "We're going to die."

"Not in the reading room," Albert said firmly.

"What?"

"It would create paperwork," Albert told him, pulling the young man to his feet. "Now, please follow me."

A violent shudder threw them sideways. One of the massive, ceiling height map cabinets began to tip, threatening to crush the only exit.

Albert lunged. He kicked the brake off a trolley and rammed it beneath the tipping edge of the cabinet, using it as an improvised brace. The metal shrieked under the weight, but it held.

"Go," Albert ordered, bracing his shoulder against the trolley to keep it steady. "Through the floor grate. The maintenance shaft connects directly to the Intake evacuation route."

The juniors scrambled past him, dropping into the dark access tunnel one by one.

The Archive took another massive hit. The sound was like a god tearing a mountain in half.

The floor beneath Albert buckled upward and the improvised brace snapped.

Albert tried to dive backward, but the tilted floor offered no purchase. An enormous, reinforced marble load beam above the doorway cracked, separated from the ceiling, and fell. Albert hit the ground. The beam slammed down onto the lower half of his body, crushing him against the stone floor.

The pain was so profound it momentarily blinded him. He couldn't breathe or move. He lay pinned in the dust, gasping for air that wouldn't come.

From the open grate, the young archivist's head popped back up. "Chief!"

"Do not come back in here," Albert wheezed, his voice straining.

"But…"

"That is an order not a request…."

The young man hesitated, tears cutting through the dust on his face. Then he nodded, and disappeared into the dark.

Albert let his head fall back against the cracked stone floor.

He looked down. His left leg was somewhere beneath several tons of masonry.Then he looked at his torso. His pristine white gloves were ruined. A massive pool of dark red blood was spreading rapidly across his immaculate black uniform, soaking into the fabric and pooling on the marble floor.

Albert stared at the stain. He had spent quite a few years keeping the Infinite Archive clean. It seemed profoundly, deeply unfair that his final contribution to the facility would be a mess.He was dying. The realization came with cold certainty, the damage was too catastrophic. His vision was already beginning to blur at the edges.

Footsteps echoed sharply over the shifting stone. Keeper #4,091 dropped to his knees beside him. The Keeper's hands hovered over the massive marble block, recognizing instantly that he could not lift it, and that removing it would likely kill Albert faster.

"Albert," the Keeper said, his voice cracking.

"Keeper," Albert whispered.

"Don't move."

Albert looked down at the several tons of stone pinning him to the floor. "I had not intended to, sir."

A blue system panel flared violently beside them.

EMPLOYEE BIOLOGICAL SURVIVAL PROBABILITY: 0.4%

Catastrophic vascular trauma: CONFIRMED

Systemic organ failure: IMMINENT

Conventional restoration: UNAVAILABLE

Keeper #4,091 stared at the blue text. His jaw tightened.

"No," the Keeper said.

A surviving senior archivist slid into the room behind him, looking horrified at the scene. "Keeper, the evacuation-"

"No," Keeper #4,091 repeated, his voice dropping into a register of command. He didn't look at the archivist but kept his eyes on Albert. "Where is Morzath?"

The senior archivist froze. "The lich?"

"Yes."

"Consultation Chamber Nine. Keeper, his personal portal is already open and wants permission to depart"

"Close it."

"It leads to an active siege he has been planning"

Keeper #4,091 finally looked up. "Then someone else can lose the war for 10 minutes. Get him."

The archivist didn't argue as she had never seen Keeper like this before. She turned and ran.

Albert struggled to keep his eyes open. The pain was fading, replaced by a suffocating numbness that was creeping up his chest. The edges of the room were turning black.

Time slipped.

A voice, dry as desert wind and echoing, spoke from behind Keeper #4,091.

"You summoned me at an extremely inconvenient moment."

Albert forced his eyes to focus.

Dark, ancient robes pooled on the cracked floor. The skeletal face of a lich peered down at him, the eye-sockets glowing with purple light.

Morzath knelt gracefully beside Albert. The lich tilted his skull, examining the crushed lower half of Albert's body, the spreading pool of blood, and the damage.

A long, silent pause stretched over the ruined reading room.

"Oh," Morzath said simply. "That is considerably worse than I was told."

Albert forced his remaining breath into his throat. He looked up at the terrifying undead entity.

"There is blood on the floor," Albert whispered.

Morzath looked down at the massive, pooling stain and looked back at Albert's face.

"Yes," Morzath agreed mildly.

"It should be cleaned."

Morzath stared at the dying custodian for a long, quiet moment. The purple lights in his eye sockets flickered.

The lich turned his skull toward Keeper #4,091.

"I like him."
