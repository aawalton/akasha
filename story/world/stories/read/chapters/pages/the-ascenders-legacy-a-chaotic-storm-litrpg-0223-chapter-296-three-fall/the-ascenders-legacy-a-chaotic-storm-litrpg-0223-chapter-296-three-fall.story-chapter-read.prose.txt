General Lucas Deaton was ripping an army of Mythic cultists to shreds when the black tower shattered, reality screaming as it collapsed in on itself to reveal a yawning void and a floating black box the size of a mansion.

He recognized Matharantha's handiwork immediately and was just about to smile when his gaze landed on the furious visage of three figures—each one radiating power on a level he recognized intimately.

Calamities.

His eyes widened in terror, and without hesitation he acted—not to attack, but to flee, or at the very least put some distance between himself and them.

Unfortunately for him, he didn't get very far before the world ground to a sudden, absolute stop—space solidifying around him like an immovable mountain. He hung suspended in the air, residual essence from the tower's destruction solidifying around him like a halo of death. He couldn't move. He couldn't even breathe.

Time was frozen completely.

Panicking, he fought against the halted time, willpower exploding out of him in defiance, but it had absolutely no effect.

Time didn't ease, didn't move, and General Lucas cursed himself for underestimating the cultists once again. Despite all they had seen starting with the Ziggurat, they hadn't considered the fact that the cultists had Calamities of their own.

And though it seemed quite illogical now, it had been perfectly reasonable at the time.

Every Calamity in Lutia was bound by oath to their reigning ruler to prevent history from repeating itself in the form of conspiracy or usurping. And so, with such oaths in place, it was practically impossible for Calamities to be cultists.

Unless, of course, they had somehow lived the last seven to eight hundred years outside of Lutia. In such a long time, any Calamity should have died or ascended.

General Lucas hadn't accounted for the cultists somehow finding a way to extend the lifespan of their Calamities over a period of nearly a millennium. None of the other ambassadors had even considered the possibility, and now they were doomed.

One Calamity was enough to put a stop to this entire war with minimal effort.

With three, they could simply wipe the Alliance out of existence before any of them could do anything about it.

And as if his thoughts had triggered a response, General Lucas felt time begin to rewind around him. His body jerked backward—not from any force, but because he was moving in reverse.

The forward thrust he'd just executed unwound itself. His arm, which had been extended to direct crimson essence toward the frontlines, pulled back to his side. He tried to resist. Failed. His muscles weren't obeying him—they were obeying time.

Around him, the entire battlefield moved backward. Soldiers who'd been charging forward now ran in reverse, their steps perfectly mirroring their advance but inverted. Swords that had been swinging down pulled back up. Skills that had been launched flew backward toward their casters, essence streaming in reverse through the air like film rewinding.

An explosion that had just detonated imploded—flames sucking inward, debris flying back together, the blast compressing into the skill that had caused it, which then flew backward into a cultist's hand.

Lucas tried to speak. His mouth moved—backward. Words he'd spoken seconds ago pulled themselves out of the air and back into his throat, syllables reversing. His colors—the enhancements he'd painted across his troops—weren't just fading. They were being unapplied in reverse.

Blood that had splattered on snow lifted and flowed upward, returning to the wounds it had come from. And ahead of him—where the black tower had just shattered into nothing, where the floating box had been exposed—the tower was rebuilding itself.

Not magically. Not through any construction technique.

It was simply unhappening.

Fragments of black stone that had been scattered across the battlefield flew backward through the air, slotting perfectly into place. The shattered walls reformed. The collapsed sections rose. Reality folded in reverse as Matharantha's destruction was slowly erased from existence.

And then, just as General Lucas gave up any hope of survival, the reversal stopped—an explosion of willpower erupting out of the still-exposed black box so densely its mere appearance sent thousands of Mythics to their knees. Even Lucas trembled, muscles straining under the weight of power that had just been unleashed from the black box.

It was as if someone had grabbed time itself and refused to let it unwind further.

And the only person that could do that was another Calamity.

The three Calamities turned to glance at the black box in shock, their eyes widening in abject terror, and an instant later, reality collapsed all around them as they teleported, time shattering into individual fragments of past, present, and future.

There was another Calamity inside that black box—one strong enough to defy three others alone—and it seemed they weren't on the side of the cultists. Lucas, however, had worked with enough Calamities to know that didn't mean they were on the side of the Alliance either.

And if Calamities were about to begin a battle right above them, the last thing he wanted was to remain underneath it.

"RETREAT!" He bellowed immediately, urging every member of the Alliance to flee for their lives.

They didn't need to be told twice.

Everyone fled immediately, both Alliance and Cultists, their hatred for each other temporarily forgotten. In the face of a potential war between Calamities, their little spat no longer mattered.

What mattered was survival, but even as they fled, they knew without doubt that they wouldn't get far enough. How could they, whether Mythics, Champions, or ordinary soldiers, escape the wrath of a single Calamity, let alone four?

And true to their thoughts, the first explosion of power from the Calamities wiped out nearly a hundred thousand soldiers in an instant—Alliance members and cultists crushed to bloody paste regardless of their tier or strength.

And the battle hadn't even begun.

***

Inside the black box—within the amphitheater where Jethro, his elders, and the handlers scrambled in panic—High Elder Zarxan Al'ruhn sensed the first wave of death and scowled in annoyance.

He had come into Lutia with a simple plan—get into the order and find Az’marthon’s legacy.

The war was of no concern to him. The Alliance and the cultists meant little to him.

He had one aim and had crafted a flawless path toward it: leashing his aura and power so tightly that he'd become practically invisible to anyone searching. He had slipped into Lutia unnoticed and had further proceeded into the Ziggurat alongside the Alliance, disguised as nothing more than a common Mythic.

Everything had been unfolding with precision. But, as ever, Lutia delighted in ruining perfection.

The last thing he wished was to draw the gaze of the three pitiful excuses for Calamities who governed this decaying dump. He had never encountered Calamities so diminished before, but now as they appeared before him, his actions having drawn attention to his presence, their appearance made the reason obvious.

Beneath the ornate awakened armor they postured in, they were little more than ancient corpses—skin shriveled and joints creaking. Weak, bony, tired, and scared.

The first thing they did the moment they arrived was to teleport the boy he'd been questioning and his guards out of the amphitheater. An inconvenience, but Zarxan wasn't too bothered.

If he couldn't get information from the boy, then he would get it from the Calamities themselves.

So, he folded his arms behind his back, his stance unthreatening as he turned to face the three Calamities squarely. "Forgive the disruption—" he began, but the female Calamity cut him off sharply, her voice a rasp that hinted at her frailty.

"Name yourself, foreigner," she demanded. "And speak—by what means did you get in here?"

"I came in with the Alliance," Zarxan replied calmly, trying not to take offense at how rudely she had cut him off. "As for who I am, I am Zarxan Al'ruhn, and I have absolutely nothing to do with this war. I care not for what is happening here, nor am I interested in whatever petty outcome it may find. My purpose here is solitary." His gaze hardened slightly, and a hint of his aura leaked out. "There is a storm transmigrant in your possession. A youth named Aodhán Brystion." A faint edge entered his tone. "You will tell me where he is."

The three Calamities tensed within their fancy armor, runic irises dilating in caution and fear.

The Watchers were scared.

After sacrificing the bulk of their powers and advancement to extend their lifespan, they had been weakened far more than any Calamity should be. And in the presence of a man like Zarxan—one only eleven steps away from ascension—that weakness couldn't have been clearer.

Nevertheless, the Watchers had no intentions of backing down.

Aodhán Brystion was an incredible asset, one whom, if properly linked and harvested, could push Jethro into an entirely new realm of power. He would become completely unstoppable then—a machine of creation and destruction that lived up to the legacy of Sárán Beithir.

Zarxan's presence here, whether he was interested in the war or not, was a hindrance to that plan.

And for that, he had to be eliminated.

Despite their obvious frailty, the Watchers were certain that they could take him. There were three of them, after all. Alone, perhaps they would fall. But together? Together, they had no choice but to believe they could prevail. Even if only one of them survived the battle, it would be a victory nonetheless.

Their expressions hardened in determination, and Zarxan sighed.

Lutians. Ever fools.

The karma watcher moved first. Not with mere threads. But with judgment.

Karmic essence erupted from him in blinding torrents—golden chains of accumulated consequence reaching out to indict Zarxan with the crimes of his past, to weigh him with debt, with the burden of every action he'd taken across four centuries.

Simultaneously, the fate watcher warped causality itself, fate bending around Zarxan like chains—every possible future collapsing into one: his death. Not probability. Not chance. But certainty imposed on reality.

The time watcher moved last, and this time, time didn't just stop—it fractured in an attempt to pull Zarxan forward through multiple seconds of his own existence, to a point where he was already bleeding and defeated.

Three concepts. Three fundamental forces of existence. All converging on a single point.

Zarxan stood in the center of it all.

And laughed.

Not in mockery or cruelty. But in genuine amusement at the audacity of the Watchers trying to bind him in their weakened states. It wasn't even a challenge.

And with a smile, he met their combined attacks with one of his own.

A single beam of primordial lightning—the concept of electricity distilled to its fundamental truth: change incarnate.

All four attacks clashed, and the sky shattered like glass—not metaphorically, but literally. Cracks spread across the firmament, jagged fractures racing outward from the point of impact. Through those cracks, chaos bled—raw, unfiltered primordial essence pouring into reality like water through a broken dam.

A myriad of origin planes flickered in the sky as they breached the veil of reality and began raining down on the armies below.

Space screamed, reality folded, and the earth itself groaned as if begging for them to stop.

But the Calamities had only just begun and would soon remind the world why it was forbidden for Calamities to fight. Because even their weakest skills could rend the world asunder.

***

Jethro stumbled into his living quarters alongside a host of elders, heart pounding loudly in his ears. He had always respected the Watchers for their tremendous power, but feeling the true might of a Calamity for the first time, Jethro couldn't help but worry.

He turned to the first elder with wide eyes. "Will they be alright?"

Elder Connor nodded, not quite as worked up as he was, though still very concerned. "They'll be fine, Fated. There are three of them. Even in their states, a single Calamity could never overwhelm them."

But the elder was wrong, and the world proved it a moment later as a sound like the sky itself splitting cut through the air, a shockwave of power ripping through the chamber despite its distance from the tower.

Jethro paled and glanced out the window, eyes widening as he saw chaos bleeding into earth from a crack that ran the entire length of the order. His heart stuttered, and terror simmered in his veins.

He believed in the Watchers and had looked up to them for months, but right now, in this moment, he wasn't sure how much he believed in their strength.

He had to do something.

Breath hitching, he glanced around the room in search of Jian. When he didn't find her, he barked, "Find me the death transmigrant and bring her to me. Search the whole building. Now!"

Elders scattered in obedience, and a few seconds later, Jian's defiant screams reached his ears. Jethro's lips curled downward in a grimace. He liked Jian. But he liked his life more, and right now, the most important thing he could do was to keep it.

The screams intensified, explosions erupting as Jian tried to fight back, but against Mythics, her strength was futile. She was dragged through the entrance a moment later, a desperate, frightened mess.

"You bastard," she cried, tears running down her face as she was thrown at his feet. "I hate you and all that you stand for. This wasn't what you promised me. This isn't the freedom and power you promised me!"

Jethro's grimace deepened, but before he could respond, another shockwave hit, blasting the windows apart. Fissures ran across the walls. The earth groaned, and the entire building shook.

"We cannot stay here any longer, Fated," Isobá spoke in concern. "Say the word and I will kill her swiftly."

"No, no, no," Jian cried out, shaking her head even as she tried to crawl away. But Jethro couldn't let that happen. This woman had slowly become a weakness, and the earlier he ended her existence, the better for him.

For half a heartbeat, guilt flickered in his heart, but survival crushed it.

Gaze hardening, he whispered, "Don't worry, Isobá. Some things I have to do myself."

Jian paled even further, eyes wide as she prepared to let out a futile scream.

The sound never made it past her throat.

Faster than she could even blink, Jethro swiped a hand, burning an entire seal to create a searing blade of light that split her cleanly in two.

Her body had barely collapsed to the ground before the strength of her spirit began draining into Jethro, filling him. Strengthening him.

"By Sárán!" Jethro groaned in pleasure as the weight upon his spirit subsided even further, becoming nearly negligible. He smiled then, relishing his newfound strength.

But as the last of Jian's spiritual strength drained away, a creature screeched in the distance, feeling a searing heat in its core as the tether between it and its master was severed.

The Skalk responded to the searing pain with a tornado of death blades that quickly grew into a phenomenon no different from a natural disaster, wrecking homes and slicing cultists to pieces.

In the last few hours since it had been unleashed, the Skalk had caused a maelstrom of despair, and as its last link to humanity was cut off, it began absorbing all the despair it had caused as well as that which the war itself had generated.

It needed no energy or cultivation technique. All it needed was despair.

And as it absorbed the infernal energy, its size ballooned outward, becoming double and then triple its original size. But that wasn't the greatest change it experienced.

The greatest change was its core, which seemed to be collapsing and rebuilding itself every two seconds, despair and chaos pushing its evolution and advancement forward.

Tier 45 → Tier 51 → Tier 54…

The unnatural evolution continued like that over a minute in real time, and with each tier it gained, the more visceral its thirst for vengeance became until its body was practically ablaze with death and power.

When the transformation finally stopped at tier 71, the Skalk screeched again, maddened by rage as it rushed toward the point of its master's death like a comet.

A desire for vengeance and despair boiling in its bony heart.

***

Duke Solaris Valerion ducked behind a barrier of solar essence as another shockwave hit, leveling thousands of soldiers to dust in an instant even as the sky split open and half a dozen origin planes breached reality at once.

Screams echoed all around him as people fled for their lives, both Alliance soldiers and cultists, the war all but forgotten in the face of imminent death.

But not Solaris.

No, Solaris stood, eyes wide as he watched four Calamities clash in the air above him, the floating box all but destroyed from their initial attack. Despite his power and status, this was the first time he was seeing Calamities clash so openly in the sky.

And it was wondrous.

The power they wielded, the way reality itself groaned and churned like a construct barely holding on to its form. The way the origin planes constantly breached the veil of reality, with darkness dripping, water pouring, fire burning, and light searing.

It was the most beautiful thing Solaris had ever seen, and despite the shouts and screams from dying people all around him, he refused to move from his spot.

They clashed against the fourth Calamity once again, fate, karma, and time struggling to leash the man, but the storm stood against them all—a mere element functioning at a level so cosmic and divine, Solaris could have wept at the sight of it.

But despite the uneven odds, the Watchers soon began to lag, their bodies unable to sustain the prolonged battle.

Their speed, once a blur, soon became easier to see and follow.

That was the beginning of their end.

The time watcher fell first, sliced cleanly in half by a sword of pure electromagnetic energy. The air itself didn't flash—it folded, severing existence along magnetic field lines to erase the watcher at a subatomic level.

The karma watcher didn't even get a chance to mourn the death of his friend before his existence too was ended, this time by a convergence of light frequencies to a singular point. Color collapsed into destructive resonance—red bleeding into violet and violet into white until the spectrum broke entirely.

Light imploded.

And when it faded, nothing remained of the watcher but an afterimage burnt into reality like a divine scar, humming faintly with residual radiation.

And then there were two.

Zarxan and the fate watcher.

She tried to flee—space rippling behind her—but she didn't get far before a hum rolled across the air, so low it barely qualified as sound. The earth vibrated in response, and reality tore open in multiple places—chaos and nothingness pouring into reality from the beyond.

The sound caught up with the watcher, and she faltered, eyes widening as the hum deepened and every atom in her body began to oscillate violently out of sync—and then she simply came apart, her core exploding in a wash of fate energy so turbulent it stained the shattered sky gold and drenched the earth below in a soup of bubbling fates.

For a moment there was silence, fear and awe gripping people by their throats.

And then Zarxan turned, his expression muted as if he hadn't just murdered three Calamities in less than five minutes. His gaze landed on Solaris, and he grimaced.

"Carry on with your war, Lutians," he said softly. "I truly didn't mean to intrude."
