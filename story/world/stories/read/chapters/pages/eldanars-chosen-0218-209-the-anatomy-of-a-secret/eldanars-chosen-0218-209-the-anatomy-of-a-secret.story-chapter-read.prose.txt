The wooden doors of the Ashenfall Guildhall swung shut behind them, cutting off the pale morning light and the sounds of the oblivious city outside. Inside, the atmosphere was suffocatingly heavy. The grand hall, typically a chaotic centre of boasting and bartering, resembled a makeshift military hospital crossed with a morgue.

Exhausted adventurers slumped over the long wooden tables. Some were nursing crude bandages wrapped around bleeding limbs, while others simply stared blankly at the floorboards, entirely traumatised by the sheer scale of the undead horde they had just fled. The air smelled strongly of stale ale, sweat, and the sharp, metallic tang of blood.

Josh led his party through the subdued crowd, his heavy steel plate armour clanking dully with every step. He found an isolated table tucked away in a shadowed corner, far from the roaring hearth in the centre of the room. He unstrapped his heavy steel shield and let it crash onto the floorboards before heavily dropping his massive frame onto a wooden bench.

Brett slid into the seat beside him, immediately pulling his thick fur-lined travel cloak tightly around his shoulders. Despite the ambient warmth of the crowded hall, the elemental mage was shivering faintly.

Perberos and Carcan took the seats opposite the humans. The elven twins moved with their usual liquid grace, though the dark circles under their luminous silver eyes betrayed their deep magical exhaustion. Carcan rested her amber-topped wooden staff against the table, her pale green robes pristine despite the horrific conditions of their retreat.

For a long moment, nobody spoke. The revelation in the alleyway hung heavily over the table.

Perberos leaned forward, resting his forearms on the scarred wood. He studied Josh and Brett with a look of intense, quiet scrutiny.

"I genuinely do not understand," the elven ranger began softly, his voice barely carrying over the low murmur of the hall. "How can you two possess such highly developed combat classes and have absolutely no concept of a body evolution? It is a fundamental aspect of high-tier magical theory. Every novice is taught about it during their initial academy training."

Brett pushed a pair of non-existent spectacles up his nose, a nervous habit he always fell back on when cornered.

"Our magical education was basically non-existent, Perberos," Brett lied smoothly, though his voice was rough from the cold. "We were essentially drafted upon arrival here and thrown straight into basic combat training. We learned how to swing swords and cast basic spells to survive. Nobody ever sat us down and explained the biological mechanics of high-level progression."

Carcan offered a small, empathetic smile, her aristocratic features softening.

"It is incredibly rare," Carcan explained, keeping her melodic voice hushed. "As Ronald stated, a physical evolution of the mana core does not typically trigger until a fighter has absorbed decades of combat experience, usually around level forty. It is the system permanently altering your physical vessel to better channel your specific affinity."

She turned her gaze to Josh. "Your skin, your bone density, and your muscle fibres have clearly taken on an aspect of metal, Josh. It is the only logical explanation for how you are able to withstand the crushing physical blows you take on the frontline. Furthermore, your ability to generate and expel raw static electricity would permanently damage the nervous system of a normal human. Your body has evolved to ground the current, turning you into a living conductor."

Josh rubbed a dirt-caked gauntlet over his jaw, feeling the thick, dark red stubble there. He thought about his 'Stormforged Adamantite' skill. He had assumed it was just a flashy magical barrier. He had never considered that his actual bones and organs were actively changing.

"And Brett," Carcan continued, turning her silver eyes to the shivering mage. "Your evolution is perhaps even more extreme. You have cast aside a traditional magical focus entirely. Your 'Scorched Veins' skill means your blood vessels are acting as the conduit for living flame."

"I do run a bit hot these days," Brett muttered, rubbing his long, elegant fingers together.

"It is more than that," Carcan insisted gently. "You manipulate raw thermal energy with terrifying efficiency. A standard fire mage commands the element. You are becoming the element. Your magical output, your raw damage potential, is likely vastly superior to any other elemental mage at your current level. In fact, I would wager your fire burns hotter than mages ten levels your senior."

A heavy silence fell over the table once more.

Perberos drummed his fingers soundlessly against the wood. "Having one abnormally gifted fighter in a squad is highly unusual. Having four of us cross this threshold before level thirty is a statistical impossibility. There is something fundamentally different about this group. The system is reacting to us in an unprecedented manner."

Across the table, Brett and Josh locked eyes.

A silent, incredibly heavy conversation passed between the two best friends. It was a look filled with a shared, isolating knowledge. They knew exactly why their growth was breaking the fundamental rules of the world. They were not from Eldamar. They were anomalies, pulled from a completely different universe and forced into this brutal system. Their rapid levelling, their unique skill combinations, and now this physical evolution, it all stemmed from their unnatural presence in this world.

Josh felt a deep, twisting knot of guilt form in his stomach. He looked at Carcan’s kind face, and then at Perberos’s stoic, loyal expression. He thought of Bhel, currently lying in the temple medical ward, who had nearly died holding the line for them.

These people were his family here. They had bled together, starved together, and saved each other's lives multiple times. He desperately wanted to tell them the truth. He wanted to explain the world of concrete and electricity he and Brett had come from. But a dark, suffocating fear held his tongue. What if they thought he was a monster? What if they thought the humans were somehow responsible for the portals, or a threat to the natural order of their world? Josh could face a charging Bone Goliath without flinching, but the thought of losing the friendship and trust of the twins terrified him completely.

Josh gave Brett a microscopic shake of his head. Not here. Not now.

Brett swallowed hard and nodded slightly, wrapping his cloak tighter around himself.

Before Perberos could question their silent exchange, the heavy wooden doors at the front of the Guildhall slammed open, striking the stone walls with a resounding crack.

The low murmur of the exhausted adventurers instantly died away.

Three figures marched purposefully onto the raised wooden platform at the front of the room. Caistina Tharistrar stood tall, her sapphire-blue robes commanding immediate respect. Her pristine white staff glowed with a faint, calming light. Beside her stood Ronald, the feline beastkin looking incredibly tense, his slit-pupiled eyes scanning the room with predatory sharpness.

But it was the third figure that truly silenced the crowd.

Rochelle, the head clerk of the Ashenfall guild, stepped forward. The older elf was notorious for her stern, uncompromising demeanour. Her greying hair was pulled back into a severe, tight bun, and she carried a thick leather ledger under her arm. If the administrative head of the guild was stepping away from her paperwork to address the fighters directly, the situation was truly dire.

Rochelle did not wait for absolute silence. She simply began to speak, her sharp, authoritative voice cutting through the damp air of the hall.

"By now, the rumours have circulated," Rochelle announced, her tone entirely devoid of warmth. "The northern perimeter has completely collapsed. A unified undead horde of unprecedented size is currently marching on Ashenfall. We estimate they will reach the outer walls within days."

A few nervous murmurs rippled through the crowd, but Rochelle silenced them with a harsh glare.

"A priority distress missive has been sent to the capital," Rochelle continued, tapping her ledger. "However, the Crown’s forces are heavily deployed already. We have absolutely no time frame for when, or if, Royal military support will arrive. Therefore, the survival of this city rests entirely on the people in this room."

Rochelle paused, letting the heavy reality of her words sink in.

"I have just concluded a meeting with the city council," she stated coldly. "By decree of the local magistrate, a state of emergency has been declared. Every registered adventurer currently within the city limits is hereby conscripted for the mandatory defence of Ashenfall."

A heavy fighter near the front stood up, his face pale. "What if we refuse? We are independent contractors, not soldiers."

Rochelle fixed the man with a look of pure ice. "If you refuse to fight, you are in direct breach of your Guild contract under the survival clause. You will be immediately stripped of your rank, your gear will be confiscated, and you will be exiled from the city gates to take your chances with the horde. There are no exceptions."

The man slowly sat back down, completely cowed.

"You will need to be at your absolute best," Rochelle continued, her tone softening just a fraction. "The Guild armoury and the local blacksmiths have been opened. You are encouraged to re-equip immediately. Repair your armour, restock your potions, and acquire whatever gear you need to survive."

She tapped her ledger again, a sharp smile touching her lips. "If you cannot afford the upgrades, the Guild will cover the cost with a promissory note. The funds will simply be subtracted from your future bounties. Assuming you survive to claim them, of course."

Even at the end of the world, Josh thought grimly, the Guild was still focused on their profit margins.

Ronald stepped forward, replacing Rochelle at the edge of the platform. The beastkin did not raise his voice, but his words carried a dangerous, quiet intensity.

"We are flying blind," Ronald stated plainly. "We know they are coming, but we do not know their marching speed, their exact numbers, or their siege capabilities. Later today, I will be leaving the city to scout the northern tree line. I intend to establish a vigil and track their progress."

Ronald scanned the room, his jade-green eyes locking directly onto the shadowed corner where Josh and his party sat. The beastkin held eye contact with Perberos for a long, deliberate moment.

"I cannot do this alone," Ronald added. "Anyone with advanced stealth, tracking, or scouting capabilities is welcome to join me. We need eyes in the dark to give the city guard an accurate timeline before the attack hits the walls."

Finally, Caistina stepped forward, her amethyst eyes filled with a deep, sorrowful weight.

"The logistics of the city are changing immediately," the elven mage announced, her melodic voice echoing through the silent hall. "The city guard will begin moving the civilian refugees from the outer camps into the city proper within the hour. It is going to be incredibly cramped, and resources will be stretched thin. Every civilian of fighting age will be armed in some capacity."

Caistina gripped her white staff tightly. "When the horde arrives, there will be no reserve forces. Everyone will be on the walls. Rest now, adventurers. Gather your strength and prepare your gear. You will receive your specific defensive assignments from the clerks shortly. May the light guide our blades."

With that final, ominous blessing, the three leaders turned and walked back through the heavy doors behind the platform, leaving the hall in a state of stunned, terrified silence.

The reality of the situation had finally set in. There was no running. There was no secondary retreat. They were going to fight a hopeless battle on the walls of Ashenfall, and many of them were going to die.

Perberos slowly stood up from the table. The elven ranger calmly adjusted the leather straps of his armour and checked the string of his longbow. He looked down at Josh, Brett, and Carcan, his pale silver eyes entirely calm and unreadable.

"Well," Perberos said quietly, his voice perfectly level. "I guess I have my assignment."

Without another word, the elven scout turned and melted seamlessly into the crowded, chaotic hall, heading towards the front to find Ronald and volunteer for the deadly scouting mission.
