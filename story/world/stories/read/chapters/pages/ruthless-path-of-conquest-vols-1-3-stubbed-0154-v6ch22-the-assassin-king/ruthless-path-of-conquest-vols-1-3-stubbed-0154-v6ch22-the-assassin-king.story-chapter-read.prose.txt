James and Zhang Chāo continued to work the room a bit, but in between hobnobbing with global powers, James saw where the figures he had seen with the Assassin King had gone.

They seemed to have a significant group they belonged to, comprising dozens of people. Most of them were entourage rather than Rulers, but James sensed the auras of at least a dozen actual Rulers among them.

When did I piss off a group of Rulers that large? he thought wearily. I only started throwing verbal bombs at the international community when I got here.

His apparent enemies looked like they were dressed for the desert, in long flowing robes and similar clothing that covered every inch of vulnerable skin.

“Do you know who those are?” James asked. He gestured by pretending to yawn and stretch in the group’s direction.

The Heavenly King made as if to crack his neck and twisted slightly to look at them. Then he shook his head.

“I’m afraid not, at least on sight,” Zhang Chāo said. “If we find out what faction they represent, I’ll tell you whatever I can about them.”

“Yeah, thanks,” James said. “Let’s corner the Assassin King for now.”

He took one last look at the group. They were too far away—almost the other side of the room—for him to make out every detail of what they were wearing and what they looked like. But on this final glance, he noticed they all wore some form of necklace with a golden symbol on it. He squinted. He could almost make out the details.

What is that…?

“James.” Zhang Chāo’s voice pulled him away from what he was looking for. That symbol was definitely familiar, but he couldn’t place it without another second or two to think about it.

“Yes?”

“The Assassin King. He’s in between conversation partners now.” Zhang Chāo pointed with an elbow. “We could corner him quickly and make it seem like just a normal chat among Rulers. Probably better to keep this as low-key as we can for now, given that violence is not possible in this room.”

James nodded. “Thank you for paying attention.”

Zhang Chāo looked at him curiously. “Do those others look familiar to you or something? You were staring for a while.”

“No. You would think I’d know the people who want to kill me, but I’m afraid not.”

“Perils of being strong,” Zhang Chāo replied.

Maybe that’s it. I kind of doubt that’s the only thing behind this. Organizing an assassination is a dangerous thing to do, especially in a world where most Rulers, including me, are people of mass destruction. You wouldn’t undertake that lightly.

He allowed Zhang Chāo to lead him toward the Assassin King. Showing that he had some skills to go with his Job title—not just System-granted Skills, but something beyond that—the man noticed them and reacted as soon as they made a line straight for him.

He did something with his cape, tried to hide behind it—and then it fell back down to its regular position, leaving the Assassin King standing there looking perplexed and slightly embarrassed

For a moment, he forgot that everyone’s Skills were disabled, and he tried to pull some kind of disappearing act, James noted. That could be a useful Skill. I really wish I could get a more selective version of Pillage.

Zhang Chāo smiled proudly in James’s peripheral vision.

Yes, yes, James thought. Now I’m glad that Han Jianguo has his Skill cancelling thing active. But you had no way of knowing this would happen, so don’t get a big head about it.

With his escape Skill having failed, the Assassin King just stood there with a grim expression and waited for the two men to reach him.

“Mr. Banner,” said Zhang Chāo as he and James got within a few feet of the Assassin King. “Or should I say Mr. Richards? Mr. Xavier?”

“Oh, you know a few of my names,” the Assassin King said, donning a friendly smile. “Your intelligence network lives up to its reputation!”

“Perhaps not,” Zhang Chāo said, giving the man a thin smile in return. “No one seems to know which one of those names is the real one.”

“None of those,” James said.

And the Assassin King is apparently a big Marvel fan, if I didn’t mistake the pattern there.

The Assassin King looked pleased with the simple deduction. His smile broadened.

“For this interaction, why don’t you just call me Mr. Chen?”

I don’t know which Marvel character that is, and now he’s getting a little too blatant with it. The Assassin King wasn’t of Chinese descent. Even if he had been, many Chinese living in the West had changed their surnames following the Sino-American War. So adopting a Chinese surname for this conversation just seemed provocative.

For his part, Zhang Chāo looked annoyed but said nothing. His body language was quietly contained, as if he just wanted to be a fly on the wall for the chat.

“I hear you’ve been hired to kill me,” James said, starting out blunt and threatening. “I feel like I should impress upon you what a mistake it would be to accept such a job. Even more of a mistake to attempt to complete it. Your loved ones will wish you hadn’t.” He gave the Assassin King his most vicious smile.

But “Mr. Chen” looked unperturbed.

“Truly, until one has been challenged by the Fisher Emperor, one has not lived,” he said. “Now I can say I have been threatened by the very best. I should ask—” and here his smile turned cold and cruel—“was that last a threat to harm my loved ones or me?”

“Whichever frightens you more,” James replied. “I wouldn’t be doing myself or you any favors if I pretended I was above going after the people you care about. I’m not.”

“You’re a vicious man, and despite being a family man yourself!” As he said this, the Assassin King’s good humored facade returned. “I like killing vicious men. The best kind of prey. I should tell you, I have no stake in your conflict. To me, this is not a personal matter at all. Other than the challenge aspect. Have to climb Mount Everest because it’s there, as they say. As for the religious thing, I was an agnostic before the System, and I haven’t been blessed by a god or touched by an angel or something since the System, so I really have no dog in this fight.”

The religious thing?

James and Zhang Chāo exchanged a glance.

“Why exactly do they want him dead?” Zhang Chāo asked.

For the first time, “Mr. Chen” looked genuinely surprised. He swiveled his head back and forth, trying to interpret both James and Zhang Chāo’s expressions, before he finally seemed to come to a conclusion.

“Oh? You genuinely don’t know. The two of you! Between the two of you, you couldn’t figure it out. Mr. Robard, you really don’t know why they want you dead? Wow.” He shook his head, looked bemused, and then frowned. “Well, if you don’t know, the clients probably wouldn’t want me to tell you. That is surprising, though.” He gave James an approving nod. “You’re quite a guy to have people trying to kill you and not even remember what you did to make that happen.”

“So I hear,” James said. “It’s a lot more trouble than it’s worth, being ‘quite a guy,’ I can tell you.”

The Assassin King nodded. “I believe it.”

“Listen, you’re doing this for money, right?” James asked.

“System Credits, yes. An exorbitant number of them.”

“Well, tell me how many, and why don’t we see if I can outbid the people who want me dead. I’m not exactly impoverished myself, and I’ll have even more money flowing in after this Summit. Trade deals and such. I can almost guarantee that I want to be alive more than they want to be dead. I’m sure there’s some number we would both find agreeable, and then we don’t have to go toe to toe. Maybe even a number that would cause you to go after your existing client?”

“With respect, Mr. Robard, with the sort of work I do, my reliability in getting a job done once contracted and especially my confidentiality are core to my business,” the Assassin King said, his voice cool . “For heads of state and powerful and influential men such as yourself, more important even than tangible results is knowing whom to trust. I never breach a contract of this sort once I’ve been engaged in it. I know you would want me to treat you in the same honorable way if you ever retained my services.” He bowed his head to Zhang Chāo and James in turn. “I wish you both a good day.” He started to pivot away from the two Rulers.

“Wait,” James said.

The Assassin King stiffened. He seemed to hear the steel in James’s voice.

It was the angriest James had felt in a while. He could keep it from showing through on his face, but he couldn’t keep it out of his tone completely.

This man is going to try and kill me, and he’s amused by the whole thing. I’m just sport to him. An agreed upon target. A mountain to climb…

“I know I made a remark about loved ones before,” James said. “Regarding that subject, I want you to know, if you come after me, and you harm anyone I care about, even by mistake, just because you missed me or something…”

“Yes, yes, you’ll do terrible things to me and the ones I love in return.” The Assassin King was trying to play it off as if it was a non-threat.

“I won’t be very specific about it either,” James continued. “I’ll go to your territory, and I’ll leave nothing but scorched earth. I’ll violate any and all norms that might get established. I’ll make you an object lesson for the rest of the Earth. People will whisper your name—or your stupid goddamn aliases—as a warning to others. Don’t fuck with the Fisher Emperor, or you’ll end up like Mr. Chen and his family and his friends and anyone who knew him.”

“That’s assuming you survive such an encounter,” said the Assassin King.

“I always survive,” James said. “Doesn’t seem to make a difference who I’m fighting. That’s the trouble you have to look forward to if you pursue this. No matter what you do, you’ll end up having to deal with me later on.” He spoke the words with absolute certainty in his voice.

And James felt that certainty. He’d been shown visions of the future once before, and he was becoming more and more sure that they were real representations of possible events. Ways that he and the people he cared for might die. But there was no future where this Assassin King killed him. James didn’t believe that could happen in a million years.

The exchange seemed to rattle “Mr. Chen” a little bit. At least there was a small change in his expression, and he changed the conversation slightly.

“Listen, if you want to nullify my contract, all you have to do is go after my clients,” he said frankly. “You’re probably stronger than them, if you live up to your reputation. It’s obvious that you know who it is that I’m dealing with, even if you didn’t expect them to come after you. Rather than worrying about dodging my blades or even threatening my poor loved ones, you could just kill off the ones who hired me. Then there will be no one left to pay me, and the job will be canceled.”

“You throw them to a wolf so easily,” Zhang Chāo observed.

“Because this is nothing personal!” the Assassin King repeated insistently. “It’s just a job for me. Maybe an opportunity to burnish my reputation as well, but mainly, it would have looked bad for me to turn down this work. Like I was afraid to piss off the wrong target.”

“You should fear me,” James said. “You clearly know me by reputation.”

“In my line of work, fear of a potential target is a liability I can’t afford.”

The Assassin King turned and strode purposely away, breaking off the conversation.

“You’d better come after me very slowly if you don’t want to die!” James called after him.

Give me time to kill your clients, he thought.

But the Assassin King did not show any acknowledgement in his posture or movements that James had said anything. He was truly resolute. That was James’s interpretation. Even if it killed him, he wouldn’t abandon this contract—perhaps couldn’t. Whether he could or would slow-walk it, James didn’t know.

The last outburst had drawn curious eyes to James, Zhang Chāo, and the Assassin King, but all three men ignored the attention for the moment.

“Well, that was far from constructive,” Zhang Chāo murmured.

“I disagree,” James said. “He told us what we have to do to make him go away. One thing I know is that avoiding someone trying to kill you is a much more unpleasant task than just killing someone else off. Sometimes the best defense is a good offense.”

“So now we deal with the true enemy.”

James nodded. “One way or another, we have to figure out how to do that.”
