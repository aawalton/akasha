[WE ARE NEARLY AT THE SPACE STATION, DR. MAYHEW.]

Kyle yawned as he sat up, still feeling a bone-weary exhaustion from the constant struggle against the draining concept.  The fatigue was as much mental as physical, and he understood that it came from the near-constant applications of the different concepts he’d leveraged.  His body was pushed as it constantly felt the infused energies coursing through it, just as his mind was strained from trying to control the different forces all at once.

Idly, he wondered how long it would take him to get truly comfortable leveraging the concepts he’d been training.  Heart of Creation’s ability to forcibly empower his skills improved alongside his conceptual understanding, so the more time he invested in the effort, the better off he’d be.  To an extent, Kyle viewed his study of concepts to be similar to his practice with Jarberry’s tomes.  It was a necessary part of shoring up his foundations, and would be the key to helping him achieve strong evolutions for his World Tree Tender class, and eventual B Grade evolution.

Even discounting any changes offered by the World Tree Seed, Kyle’s lifespan was now measured in centuries, at least.  His leveling pace had been tremendous so far, and with the compatibility between his classes so high, he wasn’t likely to hit many of the bottlenecks that other C Grades did along their journey.  In many ways, Kyle’s biggest risk was leveling up too fast, and getting stuck with weaker foundations or class upgrades for the World Tree Tender class.

With a quick round of meditation, Kyle entered his Center and checked his levels.  Level 34, and Level 56, he mused.  Kyle hadn’t expected so many levels to be gained inside his World Tree Tender class; however the constant process of healing and recovering his organs as well as purging the detrimental energy from the World Tree Seed was something very tightly aligned with his path.  He quickly allocated his free attributes, adding primarily to Willpower while also shoring up Dexterity a bit.  Afterward, he took a look at his attributes one more time.

KYLE MAYHEW.  UNION SYMBIOTE. UNBROKEN SURVIVOR.  LEVEL 34 (C)

SECOND CLASS: WORLD TREE TENDER.  LEVEL 56 (E)

PATH SYNERGY: 97%

DEVOURER’S ANATOMY – ENHANCED CARAPACE (FERRIC AUGMENTATION), AURIC PERCEPTION (SYNAPTIC BARBS), EXOSKELETAL STRENGTH (MUSCULAR COMPRESSION), CEASELESS VITALITY (TENACIOUS VIGOR), INSTINCTIVE INTELLIGENCE (PACK LEADER’S INSTINCT), COVERT DEXTERITY (REFLEXIVE EVASION), UNBREAKABLE WILL (HEART OF CREATION)

VITALITY: 3111

ENDURANCE: 4010

STRENGTH: 1205

DEXTERITY: 2222

WILLPOWER: 5105

INTELLIGENCE: 3813

PERCEPTION: 3036

FREE ATTRIBUTES: 0

At his current pace, the next milestone would be unlocking his final E Grade skill for the second class.  Then he would likely hit Level 75 in it, followed by needing to bring his Unbroken Survivor class up to Level 60 for its next skill upgrade and progress to mid-tier C Grade.

How long it would take, he wasn’t entirely sure.  What he did know is that after this nonsense with the Tribunal was done, and he had a chance to petition for the Earthling refugees, was that he could use a return to some normalcy.  At least, as close to normalcy as he could get.  With Arhades working on an invitation to the Alabaster Courts, and Duroc’s special Courier Assignment, he was confident in the fact that there would be plenty for him to do.  Something he was frankly looking forward to.  Bureaucracy was never something he’d been great at navigating, and it was more exhausting to him than any enemy he’d encountered so far.

By now, the shuttle was fully docked, and after dropping off the probe with a curt “thank-you” from Roole, he made his way back to the hangar to begin the journey back to the Hub.  As he did, he called Suierrillax on his nav bracelet to get up to speed on his impending meeting with the investigator.

“I have to say, Kyle, you should have gotten to C Grade sooner.  If I’d have known how much better the pay would be, I’d have jumped ship to a C Grade Courier years ago,” Suierrillax said in greeting, causing him to smile.

“Sorry to have taken so long,” Kyle joked back.  “Any pushback on having to leave this job early?”

“None at all.  I still have no idea who the investigator is, but they were expressly clear that you had time to finish the entire job before they would meet you.  It was the Pillar’s governing council that decided it was best to put the job on pause to get this business sorted.  After seeing the last meeting of your Tribunal, I’d expect that owes to them being accused of collusion by Casiniax.  Not exactly the kind of thing they want to have hanging over their heads.  Wait, do phytans have heads?  Maybe hanging over their algae?”

“I’m not entirely sure, but I’ll leave that investigation to you,” Kyle replied.  “In any event, I’m glad to hear that I’ll get to finish this job.  Jarberry really knew what he was doing when he got it lined up for me.”

“What about it worked so well?”  Suierrilalx asked, her tone perking up.

“The environments here were really challenging, and they gave me opportunities for a lot of growth.  I’ve never been places like these before, and they pushed me pretty hard.  At the same time, because a big part of the challenge was environmental, I could tackle them at my pace without worrying about something actively trying to kill me.”  Kyle left out the part about the death-attuned mana and Phaegos nearly doing exactly that, more than anything else to avoid a well-deserved lecture on recklessness.

“I’ll keep an eye out for similar jobs,” Suierrillax said, and Kyle could tell she was taking notes.  “C Grade jobs are a lot fewer than the standard D Grade work, but I’ll keep an eye out for any that you might find to be particularly helpful.  I’m also finally getting a meeting with Jarberry’s Guild Liaison, and we’ll do our best to keep an eye out for things beneficial to either of you.”

“Jarberry has a Guild Liaison?” Kyle asked, a little bewildered.

“Of course he does.  Why do you ask?”

“He just strikes me as somebody who gets it all figured out himself.  I figured if he had a Guild Liaison, they would have been more involved in a lot of the stuff we’ve done.”

Suierrillax let out a soft chuckle before replying.  “Kyle, no offense, but you’re an interesting pet project of Jarberry’s.  I’m not saying he doesn’t like you, or anything like that, but his involvement with both of us is very much of a personal nature to him, and has nothing at all to do with his work as a Courier.  Is it really that surprising that his Guild Liaison hasn’t been involved with us up to this point?”

“When you put it that way, it isn’t surprising at all,” Kyle admitted.  “So, any idea how this meeting with the investigator is going to look?”

“Not really,” Suierrillax said.  “You’ll actually be meeting them here in the Courier’s Guild offices, in one of the private meeting rooms.  Rumor has it that the Guildmaster himself was pretty insistent on that.”

“That seems odd,” Kyle said.  “Any idea why?”

“Only speculation, but I get the sense that there’s an increasing lack of trust toward this whole  Tribunal process.  Casiniax overplayed his hand a bit with some of the accusations, and both Jarberry and the Guild are choosing to stand behind you.  If Casiniax or the Gray Conglomerate did anything shady with the appointment of this Investigator, the Guildmaster wants to have some assurances of fair play.  And, since you’re a member in good standing, they’re being more insistent than usual.  A good thing, in my opinion.”

“Any other news about the auction of Kertan Six?  Or any oddities there?”

“It sounds like there were a couple of anomalies when they brought the planet closer to the Hub proper, but nothing that I’ve got any details on, nor anything that it sounds like the Collective is eager to investigate.  The whole system is still under watch, and there haven’t been any whispers of foul play.”

“That’s good at least.  No news is probably good news.  Any idea what the investigator is going to want to talk to me about?”

“Nope.  You’re going to have to figure that part out yourself, because it’ll just be the two of you in the meeting.”

Kyle pushed down a welling feeling of anxiousness at that idea.

[AS A POINT OF FACT, MS. SUIERRILLAX, HE WILL NOT BE ALONE.  I WILL BE JOINING HIM IN THE MEETING.]

“No disrespect intended, C.H.A.D.D.,” Suierrillax corrected herself with a chuckle.

They talked a while longer about plans after the Tribunal’s end, and the possibilities of finding a sponsor to give Earth’s refugees a place to be on Kertan Six when it was all said and done.

“Marcus is working on that front,” Suierrillax said.  “Our communication is necessarily limited, but don’t stress yourself too much on that side of things.  You’ve got plenty on your plate right now as it is.”

Finally, the shuttle arrived at its relay station, and communication was cut short as Kyle began the series of jumps to take him back to the Hub proper.  Once there, he saw the confirmation that he was expecting.

Mr. Mayhew,

Your presence has been requested at the Courier’s Guild Headquarters.  A private room has been set aside for you to meet with a neutral investigator, who is responsible for making a recommendation to the Collective about appropriate resolution to your Tribunal.  Please make your way to the designated area.  Be warned; all other uses of Collective teleporters will be documented, and may impact the outcome of your Tribunal.

Sincerely,

The Collective Bureau of Citizenship and Domestic Affairs

Seeing no reason to delay, Kyle had his nav bracelet chart a course back to the Courier’s Guild, arriving at the familiar halls in a matter of a few quick jumps.  Once there, he was directed toward a teleporter he’d never used before, where he was taken to a hallway outside an ornate door.  Without even having to activate Identify, Kyle could see the mana coursing through the layered runic formations, though he could only recognize a small portion of them.  This was clearly a room designed for high-grade meetings, showing both affluence and power.  The Guildmaster is really sending a message, Kyle thought.

The moment he took a step toward the door, it opened, revealing a cozy-looking room inside.  There were several large, well-cushioned chairs, a large table that appeared to be carved from a single piece of wood, and a hearth that crackled in the corner, giving the darkly-painted room a feeling of comfort.  Along one of the walls was a wide assortment of snacks and drinks, though Kyle’s eyes were drawn to a large painting of the Hub that dominated the opposite wall.  Or rather, to the figure standing in front of it.

It was an orc, easily a meter taller than Duroc, and Kyle could see long, curly white hair jutting out from underneath a stylish top hat.  The porcine features were more boar-like, though this orc was wearing elegant finery.  Runic inscriptions tastefully decorated the well-fitted dark jacket, and the matching trousers had similar embroidery down the side of each leg and around the cuffs, revealing powerful hooved feet.

The orc turned to Kyle, raising a three-fingered hand to adjust the golden monocle on his right eye.

“You must be Kyle Mayhew,” he rumbled in a deep basso voice.  Without even appearing to move, a plate of food appeared in his hand, followed by one appearing in Kyle’s.

“Come, sit,” he said, gesturing to one of the chairs.  “My name is Iberious, and I will be conducting the interview today.  And let me just say, Kyle, it seems like you have been particularly busy of late.”

Kyle didn’t move, feeling a cold trickle of sweat running down his back.  To some, it may have been comforting to see such a cheery fellow conducting the Tribunal’s investigation.  But, despite activating Identify and focusing on Auric Perception, Kyle couldn’t feel a thing from the boisterous orc.  With Jarberry as a notable exception, the only other time that was the case was when he faced Zierlan.  An A Grade.
