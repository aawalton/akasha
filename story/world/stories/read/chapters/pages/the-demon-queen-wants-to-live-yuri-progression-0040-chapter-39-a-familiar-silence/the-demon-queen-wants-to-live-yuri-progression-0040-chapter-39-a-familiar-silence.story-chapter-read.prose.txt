Ren crept down the hallway like she was sneaking past a hydra. Frankly, as far as she was concerned, that was not too far off. She pressed her back against the wall and held a finger to her lips. Edda stood three paces behind her, with her arms folded. Few could ever tell what Edda was thinking. Ren was one of the few. Right now, Edda was annoyed.

"Why are we sneaking?" Edda asked. Ren had asked her to whisper.

"Shh!" Ren hissed as she looked around. There was no-one else in the hallway as far as she could see. She crept forward another step, inching closer. The door to their room was right there, and it was closed. She turned to Edda and leaned in.

"Yesterday," Ren whispered, her voice coming out at roughly the volume most people used for regular conversation, "I opened that door. The latch was back! Don't know how but…anyway that's not important. I opened the door and…and…and they were-" She pressed her hands together, interlocked her fingers, and made a gesture that left nothing to interpretation.

Edda's eyes widened. She looked at the door, then back at Ren. "What? Really?"

"I didn't see them do that but…but they were sleeping together, trust me! They were all snuggled up and stuff so…." Ren nodded gravely. "I'm sure about it. They are like that."

They both stared at the door, which remained closed. A wisp of flame crawled up Ren's forearm and died at the elbow. Ren swallowed as she inched forward again. She raised her fist and knocked, so lightly it barely made a sound.

Ren squeezed her eyes shut. "Hey I'm coming in! Better not say I didn't warn y-"

The door swung open on its own the moment her hand brushed it. Ren opened her eyes and found the room...empty. Ren threw her hands in the air. "Are you kidding me?! I was being so careful and they aren't even- where the hell did they go?"

"Maybe the resonance chamber?" Edda offered. "What if...they stayed overnight? Master Aldric did say he gave them access."

Ren was already shaking her head. "No way does anyone stay in a resonance chamber overnight. That's insane. You'd have to be as crazy as a half-" She paused. Edda was looking at her. Ren sighed and they started walking.

It was early. It was so early that all of the students who walked past them looked half-asleep. Ren would have been half-asleep too, if the adrenaline hadn't woken her right up.

"So how's the core? Does it still...?"

"I can still feel it inside me." Edda flexed her hand. "It's still kinda sore too and…oddly heavy. Mana never felt like this."

Ren nodded. That all made sense to her. "Y'know that soreness goes away eventually, just takes a couple weeks sometimes. Sometimes it happens right away. It's pretty rare for someone to never stop noticing, you know. Don't worry about it, you'll be fine soon." She glanced inside her own core, at the churning flames. Those flames were always begging to be let out. Often they let themselves out. Ren supposed she was one of the ones who'd never stop noticing.

The two of them walked in silence for a few turns. "What about Corin? How's- how's he doing?"

"He's holed up in his room," Edda said. "I tried to get him to come out but…."

That answered her question, though Ren wished it didn't. Neither of them said anything after that.

They passed a group of students as they turned a corner. Four humans, and all of them with Lines. Their conversations dropped as Ren and Edda approached. They picked back up as they walked past.

"…more of them every year…"

"…the hell are we all even in the same wing? Makes me feel sick…"

Ren was no stranger to whispers like these. Everyone with their blood heard them plenty. A year ago, she'd have turned and done something. Now she kept her hands in her pockets and kept walking.

"...and that blue-haired freak. At least she looks good walking away-"

Ren stopped. Her Sigil flared without her meaning to. Heat rolled off her skin in a visible shimmer, enough that the air around her started to hiss. She turned.

The humans had noticed and two of them stepped back. One had the decency to look afraid. The fourth just stared. He had his chain raised, and he was daring her. A Line, daring someone with a Sigil. If Ren had been human, they wouldn't dare. What, did they think they were safe? Ren was going to-

Edda's hand pressed down on her shoulder.

"Ren, it's fine. Let's just go. I'm fine." It took two seconds for Ren to control herself. Then she whirled and kept walking. A demonblood striking a human would lead to issues, always. Ren had been dragged to the Archon's office enough times that she knew his face better than any of her instructors'.

They finally reached the resonance chambers, and the doors were heavy enough that Ren had to both use Essence and lean her weight into them. That was odd. They were supposed to open with a lot less effort, and open smoothly when they did. Now they opened with a loud groan. Something was wrong.

The chamber was dimmer than Ren had ever seen it, and she had been here dozens of times. Hell, the entire place was almost dark! Then the strangest thing of all: the air was thin, so thin there was hardly a difference between this room and the hall outside.

Ren saw the both of them near the far wall. Ash was asleep, propped against the crystal with her head tilted to one side. Her breathing was slow and even from what Ren could tell. The half-demon was face down on the ground and there was blood on the floor beneath her. It had dried to a dark brown around her face. It was coming out of her nose, her mouth, maybe even her ears. She wasn't breathing, or if she was, Ren couldn't see it from here. Her stomach dropped.

"Oh shit-" Ren crossed the chamber in four running strides. She grabbed the half-demon by the shoulders and shook her as hard as she could. "Hey! Hey, wake up! What the hell happened!"

Edda moved towards Ash. Ren shook the half-demon harder. There was a horrible second where the body stayed limp. Then Lysanthia groaned and her eyes opened. One second they were unfocused, the next they were furious. How the hell were they so angry so quickly? Lysanthia looked at Ren's hands on her shoulders. Then up at Ren's face. "Why," the half-demon said, "are your hands on me? Do you perchance wish to lose them? That can be arranged."

Ren fell back on her butt and let out a very deep breath. Honestly, it was a miracle her soul didn't leave right with it. "Yeah. She's fine. Scared the shit out of me but she's perfectly fine if she can still talk like that," she muttered to herself.

Ren glanced towards Edda. Ash was awake now too. The blonde woman blinked, and immediately looked across the room. She wasn't looking at Edda, standing right in front of her. Not at Ren, in the direction of her gaze. At Lysanthia. There was a brief pause.

"Did you do it?" Ash asked.

The half-demon wiped dried blood with the back of her hand and nodded. They both looked at each other, but it felt like they'd done more than just look. To Ren it felt like a conversation had happened right in front of her and she'd completely missed it. Yep. Those two were definitely together together. And with the thought came images that might have condemned her to the deepest depths of hell.

"So," Ren said quickly, clapping her hands on her very warm cheeks. "You-You've both really made your Essence Cores or what? Can't believe you actually slept here though, that's crazy…." She looked around. "I guess that wouldn't be too hard anymore."

They both nodded. Ren stared at them. It was so stupid she immediately wanted to call them both liars. Even if the entire point of finding them had been to take them to the measuring hall just in case. Oh well, she would choose to believe them. One always trusted their brothers and sisters…a little.

"Great then. Absolutely wonderful even," Ren said, clapping her hands. "Breakfast first. Then the measuring chamber. We have time." Ren stood. "Just uh…try not to bleed on the porridge."

The measuring hall was not what Ren had expected. This was strange, since Ren had been here plenty of times. Every other time she'd been here, the place was always at least half-empty. Today it was not empty at all. Today, it was hard to even find room to stand.

Tiered stone seating lined three of the walls, and those seats were full. Ren saw other students and faculty, even some she recognized. She spotted at least four Crests in the crowd, and those bastards didn't do anything but train. Ilyasviel wasn't here, thank the Divine. A small mercy.

"What the hell," Ren muttered at the entrance. "Are they giving away Essence Stones or what?"

"I told you already. I thought you were listening." Edda sighed. "It's because of Earl Wulfram's daughter, Alisa," Edda said. "She's getting measured today too. I guess that must have gotten around. Didn't think there'd be this much of a crowd over it though. I don't think it's because of your new friends."

"They're not my friends!" Ren protested.

"We spent all morning looking for them." Edda pointed.

"Because I was ordered to!"

Blessedly, it didn't seem like either Lysanthia or Ash had heard Edda. Or perhaps they hadn't cared? They stood a few paces away from everyone else, or perhaps everyone had stepped away from them.

Ren's gaze went away from them, to the far end of the room. There, as it always did, stood the Arbiter.

Ren had seen it many times, and she always found herself looking at it for a second or two too long. She suspected that would never change.

The crystal pillar rose from the stone floor. It was a pure white, and so tall it almost reached the ceiling. In its current state, the pillar held a dim light that pulsed in a steady pattern. Almost like breathing.

It would not be like that during the testing. When someone placed their hand on the Arbiter and poured in Essence, the crystal reacted to their touch.

It filled from the base upward. The concept behind the measurement was very simple. The crystal slowly filled up, and how much it filled up showed the raw amount of Essence a person's core could hold. How clear and bright the light was showed the efficiency of the core itself, how much of a person's mana core had been carved into an Essence Core.

The Inkers had finer ways of measuring the crystal, ones that tracked down to percentages. Ren didn't know them and didn't care. This thing was simple, and she liked simple.

A tall and brilliantly filled crystal was the dream. Something that everyone could just dream of. A short and murky one was most people's reality.

The Archon was present. How the hell had she not noticed him before? He stood at the rear of the hall with his hands clasped, his dark robes blending into the shadow of the upper tier. Now that really was odd. Ren had seen so many of these, and she was sure she'd never seen the Archon attend one himself. Ren shook her head, turned back to the Arbiter.

Instructor Voss stood beside the Arbiter with a flat tablet. The man was thin enough that a stiff wind might have done him in. Ren had never liked the man, and he had never liked her, or any other demonblood, or any other elf, or human or….Okay, maybe he just didn't like anyone. The man called out loudly, and the whispered conversations stopped.

"Everyone with an Essence Core to be assessed, come stand in front of the crystal. I will not ask again."

Ren looked to Edda. She was already walking forward. Ren grabbed her arm, making her stop. "Good luck," she said.

Edda looked at her and nodded, and then she was stepping forward. Ash and Lysanthia had already moved forward. The Inker waited until there were a dozen people standing in a row a few feet away from the Arbiter. He waited a second more, and then he nodded. Ren walked over and took one of the very few empty seats. Then, the testing began.

The first students went up. They were, as expected, really nothing special. Most times, the Arbiter filled to the lower quarter. The white light it gave off was never particularly bright. One boy managed a reasonably bright fill that reached a third of the crystal. It was a decent enough showing, Ren supposed.

"Edda Eryn."

Ren straightened in her seat. Edda stepped away from the line and walked to the Arbiter. She placed her hand on the crystal. The people around her began to whisper and snicker.

"...that demonblood huh? I don't know why they even..."

"...seriously blue hair? I mean they don't even try..."

Ren pointedly blocked them out. Her fists were clenched. Show them. Edda's expression didn't change at all. It was blank, or close to blank, as it always was. Ren didn't know what hers looked like.

The crystal slowly filled. The light began from the floor and rose upwards. It stopped at the one-third mark, which was a respectable result for just about anyone. The crowd gasped anyway. They gasped because of the light. The light was pure and clear. Everyone in the crowd murmured.

The Inker stared at his crystal, then at his tablet, then back at the crystal. Ren wished she could make out his expression properly from here. "The efficiency is…notable," the Inker said finally, his voice carrying.

Ren bit the inside of her cheek to keep from grinning. Then she grinned anyway, because why the hell not? That's my girl! It took all of Ren's self-control not to yell it out loud.

Edda returned to the line, blank as ever. Or almost as blank as ever. Ren was maybe the only person alive who could see the slight grin on her face.

"Alisa Wulfram."

The earl's daughter stepped forward. Ren recognized her immediately, and would have even if she hadn't stepped forward. There was just something about the way a noble brat walked that stood out even from these other humans. It was something about the way they always managed to walk while looking down on the world. This girl had golden hair, done in careful locks. Her Academy grey fit like it had been tailored, which it probably had. She was pretty. Judging from all the murmurs and looks from the boys, maybe she was more than pretty. Whatever.

Ren hadn't seen her at the resonance chamber yesterday. It wasn't a surprise. When you were rich enough, you had your own. Most of the noble brats came to the Academy, Essence Core in hand. Some of them even had Lines. Just thinking about it pissed her off.

Alisa Wulfram approached the Arbiter and placed her hand on the crystal.

The light climbed from the floor again, and it was soon past the lower third. It kept climbing until it passed the halfway mark. It still kept climbing, and in seconds it was past where Ren's own reading had reached two years ago. It reached the last quarter of the crystal and climbed no further. That was impressive enough, but then there was the type of light. The light was clean and strong. It was almost too bright to look upon. Ren had never seen the crystal look like that.

There was silence for two seconds. Then the silence broke into thunderous applause. "The capacity and the efficiency are both…" the Inker called out. "Remarkable!"

Ren could even see some members of the faculty leaning forward now. Ren's first and last instinct was to dismiss this. There were plenty of reasons to. Between Essence Hearts and Beast Cores, the girl might have had a thousand advantages Ren never had. Those were more than enough to make Ren want to dismiss her.

Except there was a small part of her that had watched enough noble brats touch that crystal and not amount to anything. It still pissed her off.

Lena was just going to love this Alisa. God damnit.

"Ash? Next."

Ren leaned forward. Ash walked to the Arbiter with an easy stride and placed her hand on the crystal.

The seated crowd hadn't stopped talking since Alisa. It seemed they weren't about to stop for this 'Ash'. Ren suspected most just didn't know she was one of the Consecrated students yet. The crystal hummed. The lower third filled. There was always a process. It started from the bottom, and slowly crept up. It did not creep up. The lower third filled, all at once.

From there it just kept going. In one instant it went past the halfway mark. In the next it was past where Alisa's testing had sent it. In the third, it hit the very top of the pillar. Within moments, the whole pillar was blazing so bright it was hard to even look at. The Archon took a step forward towards the pillar. He hadn't so much as moved for anyone else, and Ren wished she could see the look on his face.

And the entire room was just silent. It had been for some time. Seconds passed. Three. Five. Then someone clapped from somewhere far to Ren's side. Then someone else clapped, this time from somewhere far to Ren's left. Then more people were clapping, until just about everyone Ren saw was applauding, even many of the Inkers. Master Voss stood next to the crystal with the tablet limp at his side. The cheering was even louder than it had been for Alisa Wulfram.

Ash pulled her hand away and walked back to the line. Ash wasn't even smiling, as if whatever had just happened was supposed to be normal. Ren's mouth was hanging open. She closed it. For some reason, Ren looked at Lysanthia. The half-demon was smiling. It was the first time Ren had seen that face do anything but frown.

Okay, that freaking settles it! Ren thought. They are together together. Fuck why didn't I knock AHHH!

Inker Voss regained himself and called out the last name in a loud voice. "Finally, we have Lysanthia."

Then it was quiet again. It had been quiet before, but the silence now felt different, somehow. In a way that Ren could not have explained. Every eye in the hall was fixed on the half-demon. Ren still couldn't believe that there was another one in the Academy. Even worse, this one walked around calling herself Lysanthia, and actually seemed to believe it. Thank the Divine Ilyasviel wasn't here.

Ren held her breath. Something on Lysanthia's face changed as she looked out at the crowd. Ren was sure of it. Then she stepped forward and placed her hand on the crystal.

The Arbiter screamed. A crystal should not be able to scream, but Ren simply had no other word for it. For Ash the crystal had shot up as it filled, however fast. That did not happen this time. One moment the crystal was breathing. The next, there was simply white.

Ren threw her arm over her eyes. The light was so bright it almost blinded her with her eyes mostly closed. It leaked through the gaps between her fingers. And then there was the sound. The Arbiter was still screaming. Everyone around her was flinching and pulling back. Ren barely opened her eyes enough to see.

She heard and saw the crystal crack. A thin line raced from the crown downward. Then another. Then a dozen more, branching across the surface of the crystal. Light poured out through those cracks and onto the floor. There was much of it. Enough that it flooded everything until there were no shadows left in the hall at all. Then the massive crystal simply shattered in a massive explosion of crystal.

And the pieces of that crystal all hung in the air for one impossible moment, as if all the laws of the world simply did not apply to them. Finally, they fell. The sound they made was almost delicate. Almost beautiful.

They looked like so many drops of crystal rain. Where the Arbiter had stood, there was now nothing.

Nobody moved. Ren was pretty sure nobody even breathed. The hall was frozen silent. Every face Ren could see wore the same expression. Fear. Everyone who had stood close to the front of the Arbiter had fallen to the ground. Even the Earl's daughter. Even the Inker. Everyone but Ash. No applause broke the silence this time.

Ash had stepped forward, pressing her body between Lysanthia and the rest of the room. She was shielding the half-demon from the rest of the hall. It was a reflex Ren could not understand at all. Why would anyone shield the person who had just done that? At some point, Ren realized that she had forgotten to breathe herself.

Ren looked at Lysanthia. The half-demon stood with her hand still extended to where the crystal had been. Shards glittered around her bare feet. The two Lines on her arms pulsed. Ren saw the red and green, and for a moment, Ren thought she saw Black and White instead. Just for a moment.

Ren had struggled to find a word for what Ash was. The word for Lysanthia came unbidden: Divine.

Ren waited for the smile. For the imperious tilt of the chin. Perhaps for the gloating. That was how people with power reacted, and after a display like that, anyone would gloat. Ren certainly would have. Lysanthia did none of those things.

Instead, she lowered her hand and stared at the empty space. The half-demon paused and looked back at the room. Her gaze trailed over everyone, lingered a second longer on Ren, then moved past. If forced to guess, Ren might have called that strange expression sadness.

Which made no sense at all.
