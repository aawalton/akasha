Ryland double-checked that Viren was still safe and well protected before he focused on the approaching warriors riding comets. Because Viren was right. There were indeed invaders using burning meteors as mounts as they reached the location of their invasion.

Although, this wasn’t a full-on invasion yet. Ryland had seen one before, and those tended to be far more overwhelming.

“Do you feel that?” Ryland asked.

Viren frowned for a minute or so before his eyes widened. The pressure landing on them became a lot more acute and pressing. “Is this…?”

“An atmosphere of sorts,” Ryland explained. “The invaders are bringing in a bit of the atmosphere, much like what we have with [Oneself World].”

It was true. The invaders couldn’t cross the vast distance within the cosmos from their world to Abarosk just on their meteors. They needed to breathe, after all. Even forgean bodies needed air. As such, their “mounts” were Inscribed with a spell circle that wasn’t dissimilar from [Oneself World], just with a few amplification Glyphs that ensured the combined atmosphere was greater than the sum of its parts.

Stronger together, as was the way with most raiders.

“Sir,” Viren said. He was admirably trying not to shake in fear. “They’re not exactly amenable to a peaceful resolution, are they? Even with you present?”

“If they’re anything like the last time I faced them,” Ryland said. “Then I’m afraid not.”

As proof, the first of the Astral Invaders arrived with burning determination.

Viren’s first impression was correct. In essence, they really were forgeans with rocky-metallic bodies standing atop mostly circular comets blistering with fiery energy, each forgean holding chains like reins used to control the comet’s trajectory.

“Prepare yourself,” Ryland said. “They’re about to attack.”

Their strategy wasn’t surprising in the least. The very first invader at the forefront of the formation blasted out a tremendous fireball. It was easily big enough to engulf the entirety of Arcoryx Academy or the Rhellian Institute in flames. As it was, it struck Ryland’s location and buried him and Viren in a sea of roaring, overwhelming inferno.

The sound was so loud that Ryland barely even heard Viren’s startled scream. He confirmed the boy remained unharmed, however, and focused on the rest of the battle.

“Charge!” one of the invaders screamed in a croaky, grinding language.

“He’s distracted!” someone else yelled from far behind. The leader of the assault party was conducting the entire attack from the rear of the meteor-riding warband. Ryland was blessed to possess the powers of [Omniscience] to be able to hear their every spoken word. “Keep going.”

Unfortunately for them, a simple distraction of this sort was nothing. Several meteors bearing raiders shot past, the clink of their metallic bodies against their armour barely audible over the rush of their motion, before they reached their target. Abarosk.

Or rather, they met the barrier Ryland had placed before the planet.

[Eternal Bastion] was as irrepressible as ever. Several meteors shot past Ryland’s location before crashing into his barrier, exploding and bursting apart upon impact.

“Halt!” the warband leader screeched. “The planet is barricaded! Revert charge to third formation!”

His words came in a little too late to prevent more deaths. By that point, the smoke and flames physically occluding Ryland’s and Viren’s views were clearing away, revealing the true devastation that Ryland had visited upon them.

“Unbelievable!” Viren said as he waved away the flames and smoke in front of him. He was gawking at the flaming spots on the translucent barrier of [Eternal Bastion], where the smoke and conflagration were quickly spreading out and dissipating due to the lack of an atmosphere. “They’re killing themselves!”

The grim reality of warfare was undoubtedly something Viren had no experience in. Hmm. Ryland had focused on why fighting was necessary for them, but he hadn’t quite educated the boy on the deadly devastation that could arise in armed conflict. A failure on his part.

“And now they want to kill you instead, sir,” Viren said, with slight incredulity.

Ryland supposed it was rather incredible that the Astral Raiders thought they could do much against someone who had so thoroughly blocked the main thrust of their assault.

“Attack!” the leader said. “Circle and surround the Realmbreaker. He’s old now. We can kill him!”

“Sir, I can hear them!” Viren said. “Is this your spell too? And they know you!”

Ryland nodded. “[Omniscience], yes.”

He didn’t speak further because the attackers were righting themselves into the correct formation before launching their renewed assault on Ryland himself. Viren was too busy staring at how the invaders’ formation was changing and shifting.

Right that moment, the invaders were circling high above them, like burning orbs forming a hellish circle.

Then they broke apart in multiple different directions.

Ryland nodded. He was impressed. It almost looked like they had learned from their last, fatal encounter—well, fatal for their predecessors—and were creating specific formations and strategies to counter the various threats he presented.

As was evidenced by the multiple directions they spread apart, with each raider now pelting Ryland with beaming spells.

Viren flinched as the first of the crimson rays fired by the invaders reached them. Of course, they remained unharmed. Going by that initial enormous fireball, and Ryland’s own meetings with them before, he was quite certain there was no one among them higher than Primordial-ranked. As such, Ryland’s barriers would remain standing strong.

Still, Ryland wasn’t about to just let them attack willy-nilly. “[Concave Nadir].”

The spell took effect behind them. A translucent bowl erupted into being out of nowhere, like wispy clouds forming a pointed cup big enough to cradle Rhel’s artificial moon. The lasers fired by the raiders were all twisted and bent towards the very centre of that bowl, where they were forced to gather in greater and greater concentration.

“[Temporal Reflection],” Ryland incanted next.

The gathered stream of lasers now went back the exact way they had assaulted him. Since the invaders were still firing their beams, all Ryland’s spell did was set off a series of explosions detonating through their pocket of the cosmos.

“Don’t falter!” the forgean leader cried out. “Don’t give up. The Realmbreaker is not infallible!”

Their assault resumed. The coordination they maintained between all the different groups of raiders was actually impressive. Ryland caught Viren staring at how the invaders were combining their magic to raise their potency far higher than Primordial-ranked.

Swirling missiles of fiery magic shot through the cosmos next, blasting out from the raiders as they shot past in one column. Farther off, several of the raiders were slowing down and forming something like a tunnel, through which lone invaders were rushing through with their meteor mounts, enhanced by the spell circles formed by the outer meteors.

Ryland didn’t want to remain on the backfoot in this encounter, of course. So, he decided to counterattack. His magic came alive as his manasoul danced with relentless power.

The best way to deal with pesky mosquitoes such as these raiders was by swatting them away.

“[Dragongod’s Dimensional Claw],” Ryland incanted.

A rift cracked open in the atmosphere above Abarosk. An enormous rift. Golden cracks split space and time to rip open a tear in the universe, through which that gigantic undead draconic claw burst through.

Just as it had against the Archdrakes on Vyrd, Ryland had the enormous claw slash through his enemies. Crimson power wreathed its every talon, shooting out of every missing patch of skin and flesh, oozing out of the holes in the undead Dragongod’s bones. It smashed in with devastating effect.

The raiders screamed as one as their formation was broken and they were sent scattering in every direction, desperate to survive the attack that had come from nowhere.

Ryland didn’t recall using this specific spell against them before. That was good. It was clear the raiders had done their homework, studying him and how he had dealt with their predecessors, before coming up with strategies to tackle him based on the information they had.

Unfortunately for them, their information was limited.

“[Rewind],” Ryland incanted next.

Ryland didn’t have too many spells for this exact scenario. Blasts of energy that could chase down a large number of targets and take them down were unfortunately not in his arsenal, even though he prided himself on maintaining a repertoire of spells that could deal with practically any situation.

That said, there was an easy solution to that. All Ryland had to do was use the raiders’ own magic against them. So, he just reverted time using [Rewind] and recreated those crimson beams.

They shot across the space of the cosmos between them, creating a crisscross of destruction that caught several of the runaway raiders unawares.

But they weren’t pinpoint accurate. Several beams just missed wildly. Some just hit that enormous claw of the undead Dragongod, fizzing away to nothing. It was a good thing Ryland had an answer to improving their accuracy, however.

“[Endless Evolution],” Ryland incanted. “[Sovereign’s Crown].”

With a burst of energy around his skull, Ryland partially changed bodies. He brought on the skull of an ancient Kurodarr, the nerves, blood vessels, and other biological matter of his current body seamlessly melding with that of the new head. All thanks to [Endless Evolution] and the power of his Soul Glyph of Genesis.

The Kurodarr head he had pulled on was capable of far higher concentration and perception than nearly any other creature he knew of. This immediately allowed him to locate and note down exactly what was going on.

He noted down where each beam went shooting. He pinpointed just where each of the invaders were escaping.

He figured out just how much he needed to adjust his own spells.

“[Translocate],” Ryland cast.

A slew of rifts cracked across the cosmos before them, eating up over half of the beams created by [Rewind]. They crashed out of several other rifts he had created with the last spell. Essentially, this was course correction. Their trajectories had been fixed and their targeting greatly improved.

The invaders began falling by the masses.

Screams rang out with greater intensity. Spells began failing all over the battlefield in the cosmos. It was all falling apart. The raiders were dying, their entire formation broken, with even their leader no longer calling out commands or issuing any further orders.

“I believe their numbers have been sufficiently reduced,” Ryland said.

He was speaking calmly, observing the chaos unfold dispassionately as his magic refused to abate since he didn’t consider the situation done by any means. Ryland didn’t look down to see how Viren was reacting. This devastation was no doubt a harsh sight for any child to bear.

“[Repulsion Plane],” Ryland incanted.

In the distance, several rifts shattered open, quickly joining together to form one enormous rift. One that spewed a deep crimson energy, a miasma that was a hue somewhere between wine and blood, a wall of irrepressible power that immediately began pushing upon the invaders trying to escape.

Because Ryland had easily decided that those who were willing to cause untold devastation and conduct horrific pillaging didn’t deserve any mercy.

[Dragongod’s Dimensional Claw] had already faded, with the enormous claw having sunk back into the enormous rift it had burst out of. With [Rewind]’s effect now fading as well, there was a lot of open space now left for Ryland to cast more magic into.

“[Primordial Deadsea]!” Ryland incanted.

The sky split apart. Where one enormous rift was already throwing down a terrific amount of magic pounding on the would-be escapees, there was now a proper trap for them to fall into. Ryland had placed an ocean of roiling liquid energy in a pocket carved out of the cosmos itself.

It wasn’t long before the screams started again. The last of the raiders were physically pushed into the enormous vat of deadly ocean. Their splashes were immediately accompanied by horrific sizzling, their screams cutting off abruptly as death claimed them.

Ryland generally avoided calling upon the Deadsea of Realm 20 unless he intended to break through defences and walls that were far too thick. However, in certain situations such as his current one, he had no other choice. Letting the Astral Raiders retreat just so they could regroup and attack again wasn’t something he’d stand for.

As such, annihilation was the only answer he considered correct.

Ryland closed the rifts once the last raider was dealt with. It was almost strange. For a while, the entire cosmos around them had been lit up with violent magic and the cries of battle and death and desperate struggle.

Now, there was nothing. No blindingly bright magic. No deadly intent or endless determination. No warriors riding comets aimed for isolated, innocent people.

[ Achievement Recognized: Cosmic Defender

You’ve defended a whole world against Astral Raiders.

Rank up from Empyrean [29] to Empyrean [31].

Reward: 50 Evolution Prints. ]

Hmm. Evolution Prints. He’d need to make use of those at some point or another.

“Are you alright, Viren?” Ryland asked.

Viren had a complicated look on his face. One that confessed that he was struggling to absorb everything that had happened. It almost seemed like he was wishing everything had been a dream of sorts. A nightmare he had been forced to witness. Ryland felt sorry for dragging him to the battle, although there was a certain steeliness in him.

After all, he had seen what had happened when the Living Mana Incursion had managed to infiltrate Arcoryx City’s borders and barriers.

“I think I’ll be alright, sir,” Viren eventually said. Notably, he hadn’t admitted he was fine now.

He just would be fine. Eventually.

“Would you like to return to Abarosk for the time being, then?” Ryland asked. “The Ivail Trade Guild would be honoured to host you for a time.”

“Aren’t you coming, sir?”

Ryland looked away, eyes focusing on the distant end of the cosmos. “No, not quite yet. This business still feels unfinished. As such, I intend to end it for good.”
