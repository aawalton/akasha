Zero days until the tournament,Fifteen days until the invasion.

The birds chirped. The sun rays fell almost horizontal on Gary’s bed, evidence of the recent dawn, and for the first time in weeks, he felt no grogginess at all. From the second he woke up, he was ready to go.

Gary jumped off the bed. “Good morning, Cog!” he exclaimed.

On the other bed, the metallic lifeform grumbled something and covered his head with the pillow.

“How can you be sleepy? Today’s the tournament! Let’s go!”

“Easy for you to say. You know you’ll win,” Cog said, sitting up. He stretched his arms, making eerie creaking sounds alongside the hissing of steam.

“Don’t be like that. You trained so hard for today. Taste the fruit of your efforts!”

“Fruit is inefficient sustenance for metallic lifeforms.”

“Cog, I swear to God I’ll drag you to the Shores of Glory if I have to.”

He laughed. “Fine, you tyrant. I’m ready. Let’s go!”

They exited the cabin alongside many men from the surrounding ones. The air was electric. Everyone was rearing to go. They’d spent almost four months in the clan by now. Most new disciples had broken through, but few had experienced real combat in this new realm. How strong were they? Who was the strongest? Could they challenge the previous generation of disciples, who participated in the same tournament?

A falcon shot off the ground, drawing a wide curve in the air. It screeched to the sky.

More people joined them as they entered the mountain path. They were the women and gender-neutral species from the nearby batches of cabins. “Alicia!” Gary yelled, spotting her in the crowd. She rushed over.

“This is so exciting!” she said. “I can’t believe I’m in the Heaven Reaching tournament. I watched it every year! I dreamt of participating!”

“Was it impressive?” Cog asked.

“Yeah! Especially during the finals. Those cultivators were so powerful!”

Gary gave a warm smile. “I’m really proud of you guys. You said you’d work hard every day, and you really did. How does it feel?”

“The training itself sucked ass,” Cog said. “But now, I feel great! I’m so much stronger. I can’t wait to see how well I’ll do.”

“Same for me, but…” Alicia hesitated.

“What?” Gary asked.

“Well… I never really cared about victory before, but now I do. I’ve worked so hard that I’ll be really upset if I lose. And I might lose. I don’t know, that scares me.”

Gary smiled wider. “That’s exactly how it should feel.”

“Hey, Gary!” A trio of people approached them. They were beaming with excitement.

“Hey guys,” he replied.

“We really can’t thank you enough,” Helen said. Pop Ear and Caramel nodded. They were the three people Gary had talked to outside the Valley of Broken Spears. He’d referred them to Bob the Cultist to buy Muscle Sap.

“Do you feel stronger?” Gary asked with a meaningful smile.

“We’re so much stronger. The families have reigned long enough—they won’t know what hit them,” Pop Ear said, clenching his fist.

“We can only take care of the weak ones, though. We’ll count on you for the finalists,” Caramel said.

Gary raised his jaw. “You got it.”

As the three walked ahead, he stared at their backs for a moment, then sent a message to Chloe.

Gary: I met the three people I referred to Bob. They seem happy. We’re doing the right thing. I’m so fucking glad.

Chloe: Me too! Thanks to those guys, we sold half our reserve!

Gary: How many drops did they buy!?

Chloe: Not just them, silly. Didn’t you know? They told their friends. Your clan is crawling with our customers.

Gary looked up. Suddenly, the pumped-up faces around him carried new meaning. People banged their fists together and grinned with battlelust. They really were ready to go.

“Oh,” he said.

Chloe: Remember to meet us after the day is done. Bring Alicia and Cog!

Gary: You got it.

Chloe: We’ll be cheering for you. Go get them, tiger!

“—thousands of people! That’s so many. Did I fix my hair?” Cog said.

“But you don’t have any,” Alicia said.

“Oh! You’re right!”

She grumbled something, fixing her own hair, and Gary smiled as he followed them. The large group walked through the now-familiar paths to reach the front of the mountain. The letters “Heaven Reaching” were still carved onto the mountainside, exuding a grandeur that only seemed to grow as Gary got stronger. A large horse lay sleeping on the foot of the mountain—the clan’s guardian, the Gate Horse.

Gary looked up at the clan mountain. It was usually a hub of activity like an ant’s nest, but today, it was empty. All he saw was more groups of disciples pouring down the mountainside.

They’d all been instructed to depart on sunrise.

As one, the many disciples flew over the stone, into the cliff’s edge, then jumped. Gary felt the exhilarating rush of wind as he free-fell. They landed with heavy thuds, like a series of comets on the already messy forest floor, then broke into a jog towards the Shores of Glory.

The cliff-jumping was Gary’s favorite part of the clan. There was no better start to any excursion. Maybe it was on purpose. Everyone was grinning, exhilarated. Some people roared and shadow-battled while running.

Everyone present had participated in a clan selection, either the last one with Gary or the one before that. They’d all run through this forest. Now, they ran the opposite way, filled with anticipation in place of despair. Their steps thundered on grass. Little animals ran to hide. They passed through the clearing where they’d undertaken the first two trials of the clan selection, then stepped onto the wider gravel road that led to the city.

Soon, they approached the northern gates. There was no line. The guards let them through, and two hundred spear disciples swarmed the streets with energy unconstrained. People were waiting on the sides—they cheered.

“Here they come!” someone yelled.

“Fight hard, disciples!” another person cheered. Gary saw a father holding his little son over the heads of the crowd. He waved. The boy laughed. Young men stood on rooftops, watching the disciples’ entrance, and Gary saw their eyes filled with awe, saw their clenched fists. Saw their fire. Many of those could be cultivators. Many wanted it.

He understood. If he saw this sight from the sides, he, too, would have wanted to participate.

They ran through the main street leading directly from the gate to the Empyrean Steps, the ninety-three-story tall skyscraper in the middle of the city. There was more crowd around. They stood on the side of the street, balconies, rooftops, climbed up on anything that was conceivably climbable. A guy dangled from a brick roof’s underside. He cheered the loudest.

“GO GO, HEAVEN REACHING!”

Many disciples raised their spears in greeting. Gary did, too. He saw Feng Shen jumping over the crowd and hovering for a moment. “Today is the day, Heaven Reaching disciples. For glory!” he yelled.

“FOR GLORY!” many disciples roared. The crowd exploded with cheers.

They approached the Empyrean Steps, which hovered larger the closer they got. It was tremendously high—maybe half a mile. Airships of all makes and kinds hovered next to the tower’s edifices, which served more as docks than balconies. The sun spread its shadow all the way to the city’s western gate.

The massive plaza in front of the tower was absolutely packed. People yelled and cheered. Many held paper or wooden spears made especially for the occasion. The cheers were deafening.

“GO GO, HEAVEN REACHING!”

Gary felt the sound washing over him. A deep instinct surfaced. He was a gladiator. This was where he was meant to be. This moment, this instant, held more worth than his entire life so far.

This was real. He existed.

The arena was at the Empyrean Steps’ ground floor. They ran through a massive, arched entrance leading to a long corridor. Iron gates closed it off usually, but they were all open.

Bright light came from the end of the corridor. As Gary approached it and the first people stepped through, an even greater round of cheers arrived. The moment he stepped into the light, he was blinded, then found himself in a massive arena surrounded by thousands of standing, clapping, cheering people. The sound and light washed over him. The energy was unbelievable. Gary lost his breath, and suddenly his pride came unbidden, borne of his endless hard work. He raised his spear and yelled. The crowd replied in kind.

“WELCOME, HEAVEN REACHING DISCIPLES!” an authoritative voice boomed over the arena. Gary looked for its source.

Sand covered the flat arena’s floor. It was only a inch thick with stone underneath. Around the sand stood ten feet high walls, leading to stepped audience seats which could easily house thousands. All were filled, except a segment meant for the disciples themselves. A hundred feet from the floor, the ceiling was packed with light, blazing straight down to illuminate the arena. It was blinding.

All around the arena flew flags bearing the Heaven Reaching insignia—a simple, wooden spear pointing up. There were also flags of the Shores of Glory, bearing a crown over a blue wavy line.

The voice had come from a small platform on the far end of the arena, where a Heaven Reaching Elder stood above the disciples. Gary recognized their rank from their signature brown robes, but also because he knew the person—it was Elder Feng, a middle-aged-looking woman who’d once helped him get the Duel of Honor against Rat Wei.

The familiar presence grounded Gary. He’d half-expected to see Elder Sui up there.

Elder Feng was flanked by four deacons, who stood with their backs straight, hands clasped behind their backs, and eyes looking straight ahead. Gary didn’t recognize any of them.

“Today marks the Heaven Reaching clan’s twenty-thousand seven hundred and eleventh initiation tournament!” Elder Feng announced. Her voice was excited but dignified, carrying enough boom to reach even the farthest spectators clearly. They cheered.

“To those of you who bought tickets early, you’re in luck! Two clan selections coexist in this tournament. A hundred and ninety-three junior disciples, including drafters from the planet Earth, are ready to demonstrate their hard-earned skills for you! We would also like to thank you for accommodating our sudden change of schedule, due to the unforeseen invasion quest.”

Many voices came from the crowd, blending together into something unrecognizable. Gary looked around for familiar faces. Alicia and Cog should be here, having snatched two tickets from people who couldn’t adapt to the new schedule, but he couldn’t find them among thousands.

“The rules will be the same as in previous years,” Elder Feng continued. “Each battle will be a duel to incapacitation or surrender. Crippling or killing a fellow disciple on purpose will be grounds for immediate elimination from the tournament and disciplinary action from the clan. For the first nine days, each disciple will fight a randomly chosen opponent once per day. The pairings of each day will be posted the day before. Whoever accumulates two losses is eliminated. This will continue until the strongest sixteen disciples remain, who’ll earn significant rewards and move into the tournament’s second phase: the finals.”

“What’s the reward?” a person shouted from the audience. His voice was particularly loud, piercing over the clamor to be heard.

“To anyone who reaches the top sixteen, the clan will award ten drops of Muscle Sap and one thousand aura stones!” Elder Feng announced, leading to exclamations of wonder from the audience and disciples alike. Gary himself could only scratch his head. Compared to the profits of their little operation, this reward was nothing. Then again, the rewards should rise exponentially after that. He’d need to do better than top sixteen to earn anything important. That had been his goal anyway.

He stared at the back of Feng Shen, who stood in front of the crowd. He was suddenly pumped. He clenched his fists.

“We got this!” Goldie whispered, coiled around his arm.

“Not yet,” Gary admitted, “but even the first phase lasts nine days. That’s nine days of training. If I can master everything Master Colorful teaches me, who knows—maybe we’ll stand a chance!”

“Do you want to win, Gary?” she asked.

“Yeah. I really fucking want it.”

“Yay! I just hope Sui Duang doesn’t kill you in the meantime!”

Gary had managed to forget about that. He looked around, spotting Sui Duang, who was already staring at him. They exchanged heated glares. The two of them had agreed on a Duel of Honor during the tournament. The chances of meeting in the first phase were low, but they’d go up by the day, especially if both reached the top sixteen. Then again, low wasn’t zero. Gary could be called to fight for his life on any given day—and he didn’t know whether he could beat Sui Duang.

If they didn’t meet during the tournament, they’d still duel after.

“If we happen to meet, don’t blame me for not holding back!” Cog said.

“That’s my line!” Alicia said.

“If we meet, I’ll let you win,” Gary joked.

“Not if I let you first!” Cog said.

“Disciples, please move to your seats so we can announce the first pairing.” She took a deep breath, letting her voice boom far and wide. “Let the Heaven Reaching tournament begin!”
