Renner produced a rolled strip of vellum and lay it flat on the bed. Gloved hands braced the sides down and he frowned at the iron-hued, long-dried ink. “Rell brought fresh supplies this morning. I’ve been looking at this, and we have two options.”

I examined the stark swirls and lines curiously. It appeared to be a detailed map of the area; its top portion ended at an etching of Pinnacle, and the lower segment stopped just south of Fellbrook. Snowmelt lay to the left, surrounded by forests and mountains, and a few other towns and cities lay scattered on the right.

The realm was much bigger than all that, of course, but this map seemed to be focused on the southwestern portion of everything. Inns, Watch outposts, and landscape features along every road were well-detailed.

Teela peered over his shoulder. “I’m gonna guess ‘straight north’ is option number one?”

He nodded. “Nine days to Pinnacle that way. Could get rough. Lewen said the snows start any day now, this close to the mountains, and things can get nasty pretty quick.”

I reached over, tapping a settlement northeast of Snowmelt. “Is this the other option?”

Another nod. “Sedgewick Vale. I’ve passed through a few times. It’s a small city, but it has a livery. An actual one.” One finger traced the route, which led through forested terrain, a lake, and into a valley. “It’ll take a few days to get there, but horses will make up for the difference afterwards. There’s inns along the way, and on the off-chance they’re destroyed we’ll be in the woods. Better odds of finding shelter.”

“Wouldn’t being near the mountains have better shelter, though?” Royce piped in, leaning in from behind me. “Caves, rock cover, that sort of thing?”

Renner shook his head. “Only if we find them. Gambling that we’ll be near a cave come nightfall is a bad idea.”

“Burned inns are rare though, surely,” I offered. No one answered and I added, sounding very small, “Right?”

Renner frowned. He seemed to be lost in thought.

Teela puffed her cheeks. “Yeah. Yes. Normally, I mean. But…” she cut her eyes skyward, nose wrinkling, “Salt, a lot of things from the past few days are supposed to be rare. Runekeepers, magic, hags, Fae showing up in Fellbrook, that awful fog the other-”

“Point taken.” I winced.

The young woman shifted back, clearing her throat, and added unhappily, “I’ll also point out that the last time we decided to go out of our way for horses things didn’t, uh, go so well.”

“What happened?” Royce looked between the three of us.

I drew my knees up, fingers tangling in the twisted tip of my braid, and sighed. “We came here.” I pondered that statement for a moment, then added softly, “And… and it was awful, but… it’s a good thing we did.”

Renner and Teela both looked at me as though I’d grown a second head.

My face grew warm, but I forged on. “Think about it. Isn’t Snowmelt better off? Because we came, I mean. Baltha’s dead because of us. She can’t hurt anyone else. The town is… maybe not perfectly safe, especially not with the pacted running loose, but… we helped this place. We probably saved a lot of lives by coming here.”

Renner snorted, giving his head a little shake. “Told you I was a damn hero.”

“That’s one word for you,” Teela snickered.

“Oh? I can’t wait to hear the rest.” He smirked at her. “Handsome, right? Rakish?”

She rolled her eyes. “I was thinking smug.”

“You say that like it’s a bad thing.”

“Full-of-himself.”

“Who wouldn’t want to be full of-”

I bit down on a grin and, before the pair could fully devolve into bickering, chimed, “I think Sedgewick Vale sounds like the best option. Much better than walking for nine days on the open road with snow on the way.”

No one protested. Renner re-rolled the map, then affixed it to a loop on the backside of his belt.

“That settles it, then?” Royce stretched and shot me an inquiring look. “Breakfast for you and then… back on the road?”

I nodded, and my three friends began to file out. Renner was halfway to the door when, on impulse, I jumped up and caught the thick, heavy fabric of his cloak.

“Could I… do you have a moment?”

He cocked an eyebrow, then closed the door and turned to face me. Muffled voices and footsteps echoed from beyond as Royce and Teela paused, spoke quietly, and trailed down the hallway.

I spent a moment piecing together what I wanted to say, and he spent a moment looking me up and down. Jaw tight, eyes hard, tracing every line of my body with an intensity that nearly made me blush. It was an expression I was starting to become familiar with.

He’s not… he’s not flirting. He’s not ogling me. Saints, he’s looking for damage. My stomach roiled.

“I’m alright. Really.” I drew a deep breath, fingers twisting together. “That’s actually what I… Renner, look, everyone’s been so worried about me. And I get it, it’s not a bad thing, I’m touched by how much you all care. But… I don’t know. It seems like someone should be worried about you.”

He blinked at that. “Me?”

I stepped closer, voice softening. “Well… you went through everything that I did. Mostly. You were taken prisoner, you killed someone, you were hurt, you nearly died-”

He was shaking his head. “Wasn’t the first time, ladyship. For any of those things.”

“That doesn’t mean-”

“Look, I won’t pretend we didn’t have a salted awful night.” His voice was calm, if a bit rough. “But I’m not losing sleep over any of it, if that’s what you’re getting at.”

My mouth twisted to one side as I examined his face. Hair unkempt, eyes dark and shadowed. I wasn’t sure I believed the claim. “No? Because it’s okay if you are, or if you want to talk about… well, any of it. All of it. I know I’m not exactly a… a pillar of strength and comfort and things like that, but… if you need anything, I’m here.”

“Oh?” The look he gave me was positively shameless. “Well, if you’re offering comfort, I’ve got a few ideas.”

Heat flooded my face. “I’m being serious!”

He chuckled.

My shoulders sagged in a heavy breath. “Look, if you’re really alright, then… well, then I’m glad. I just want you to know… if there’s anything I can do, please let me know.”

One brow rose. “Anything, huh?”

The blush crept down my neck. “You know what I mean.”

His lips twitched. Then, more thoughtfully, “Hm. Actually, yeah. There is something you can do.”

“Oh?”

“Lewen left your things outside the door. Get dressed and I’ll… I have something for you.”

He left without further explanation, and after a moment of wondering I gathered my belongings. I left the spare nightgown neatly folded atop the bed and dressed in my long blue tunic and dark, soft trousers. After re-securing my cloak and lacing my boots I opened the door.

Renner leaned against the hallway wall, one hand behind his head and the other holding a dark leather belt. A sheathed shortsword and black-hilted dagger were both affixed to the strap. He stepped into the room, closed the door, and handed me the belt without comment.

“You bought me a sword?”

The leather went snug across my hips. The shortsword sheath tapped lightly against my left thigh and the dagger’s hilt settled against my abdomen. Both were an unfamiliar, but not uncomfortable, weight.

“Yeah. It’s light, and the balance is good. Not bad craftsmanship, and you’re gonna practice with it later.”

I blinked at him. “I am? I mean… sure, but-”

“You said you wanted to do something, right? Help me sleep a little easier?” Copper eyes narrowed. “Draw the knife.”

My brows rose at that, but I didn’t argue. My fingers closed over sturdy, polished wood. Metal hissed as I slid the blade free. It flickered gold in the room’s warm glow.

“You had a dagger just like that when Thom grabbed you.”

My throat grew thick. I stepped back, heart pounding unevenly at the too-recent memory.

Renner drew close. Very slowly, as if he was afraid I’d bolt. Gloved fingers closed around my wrist. “The next time,” every word was low and measured, making a shiver run down my spine, “Someone has you pinned, you draw a weapon and fight back. Aim right here.”

His grip tightened, leading my hand up until the dagger’s edge was flush against his throat. Wicked steel pressed up, indenting the skin beneath his jaw.

“Saints, don’t-” I pulled down, but he held fast. The knife didn’t budge.

“Right here. Got it?”

I gulped, nodding, and he drew our hands down. Dark leather creaked as the blade went horizontal between us, pressing into his jerkin. Right over his heart.

“Push in.”

“What? No! Are you insane?!”

He scoffed. “No, just proving a point. Push.”

I shook my head. “I don’t want to hurt you.”

“You won’t.

“Renner.”

“I’ll wait. I’ve got time. I don’t even mind.” Familiar mirth danced across his features and he smirked, leaning closer. “You actually smell nice, for once.”

An indignant growl bubbled up the back of my throat. “Wish I could say the same for you.” He actually does smell… really nice. Like fresh pine needles and last night’s soap and… mm.

Husky laughter sent warm breath across my skin. Another shiver followed the sensation. “Go on. Try to stab me.”

I fidgeted but, with little other option, did as he asked. Gently, terrified of actually breaking through his protection. The knife met resistance and, after a moment of pressing, his fingers tightened.

“That’s why you don’t aim for the chest. Even if I wasn’t wearing armor, there’s too much bone to fight through. You don’t have the muscle for it. You might get lucky and slide through the ribs, but don’t count on it.”

“Right. No stabbing people in the chest. Got it.”

His lips twitched. Then our hands went up, and the blade’s tip met his shoulder.

“Right here. Collarbone meets the shoulder, and there’s a nice open space beneath. Aim for that, and most men’ll drop their weapon.”

I nodded.

He searched my eyes for a moment. Then his grip lowered, drawing mine with it, until-

“Renner!” Heat scorched my face as I tried to twist back. The dagger, and our hands, were approaching somewhere far too intimate.

He chuckled, holding fast. “If you can’t reach the throat, this is your next best bet.”

“I know that! Let go!”

“Not there, ladyship. Look.”

My cheeks burned, but I did as he asked.

Past limbs twined very closely, the blade was pressed length-wise along the inside of his thigh. He tilted it a bit, making the steel flicker within our shadows. “Most armor has a gap here, for the seam. If there’s armor, stab the gap. Cloth, you can slice. Either way, you’ll do some damage. Might even kill them, but the important thing is you’ll be able to run. Or get enough distance to use magic.”

“R-right. I get the… salt, the idea.” My skin felt far too warm. I cleared my throat, flicking my gaze back to his. “Um, you can let go now.”

“You sure?” His voice turned husky and quavered a bit, as if he was holding back laughter. One leather-clad thumb slid down, circling along the delicate skin of my inner wrist. The sensation sent a little thrill up my arm. “You’re getting awful red. Something on your mind?”

I narrowed my eyes. “I have a knife, you know.”

“Uh-huh. I’m shaking in my boots.”

“And I can set you on fire.”

He snickered and released me, stepping back.

My heart fluttered. A little pang stabbed through my belly at the realization that, for a moment, I hadn’t quite wanted him to let go. I swallowed tightly and the traitorous heat slid down my neck.

I took a moment to steady myself, willing the blush to fade and studiously avoiding his eyes. Salt. Get it together, Brin! He flirts all the time, with practically every woman he meets, it doesn’t mean… He’s trying to be nice, not… self-defense lessons are not something to get worked up about! He just doesn’t want to have to come to the rescue every time I’m in danger. And he’s right, I need to be ready to defend myself in case… if I need to. Again.

The thought was sobering. I sheathed the dagger, sighing. “You make it sound so easy. Hurting… hurting someone. Killing someone.”

He went very still. “Easy? Nah. Simple, though.”

“Simple. It… it shouldn’t be. Thom was… ashes, he was… h-horrible, but he was still a person,” I whispered, blinking back tears as I envisioned the man’s face and all the horror that came alongside it. Pungent, burnt flesh, ragged cries of rage, weight crushing my ribs. Hot, metallic blood flooding my tongue as the world rang and spun.

And even still, even knowing the aftermath… could I have done it differently? Unsheathed the dagger the moment he’d grabbed me? Plunged it into him? I wasn’t sure. “Nothing about him dying is simple. He had friends. People who loved him and will miss him and-”

“You have those things, too. Right? Durst. Your farmboy out there.” He nodded towards the hallway, lips curling into a quick grimace before settling back into a thin, flat line. “They don’t want a world without you in it.”

My mouth went dry. Didn’t I have almost the same exact thought yesterday? On the balcony with… with him. About Durst. About how I can’t just die and leave him all alone. Not for anything.

Renner stepped closer, head dipping down. One knuckle caught the underside of my chin, tilting up until our gazes met. His was fierce, simmering with something I couldn’t name, and the sight made my chest tighten. “The next time someone’s trying to kill you, you kill them first. Got it?”

“I will. I’ll try to, at least. I… I’m sorry you had to.”

He searched my eyes for a moment. Then, low and even, “I’m not.”

And then he strode out of the room, leaving me alone. Staring after him with a heart beating entirely too fast.
