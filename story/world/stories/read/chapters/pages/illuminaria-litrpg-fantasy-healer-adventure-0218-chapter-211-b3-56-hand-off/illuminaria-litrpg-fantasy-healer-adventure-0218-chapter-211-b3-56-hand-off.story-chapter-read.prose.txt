Chapter 211 / B3-56: Hand Off

While Joe and Yuk were happy to watch the ship take shape, Mojo eventually ran out of ‘sit still and watch’.

“Okay, Mo,” Joe sighed after the shipwrights levered in the last beam of the first floor. The carpenters still had to frame out the top level, but Joe knew the shug pup had had enough of the builders’ platform, especially since he was not allowed to go say hi and hang out with the construction crew. “Let’s go.”

They had been sitting on a makeshift bench that they had put together out of scrap wood: a pair of beam trimmings and a short section of plank with a deep flaw running through it. Since the scraps had been weathered to that driftwood gray, Joe was almost certain no one would care what became of them. So instead of just abandoning the old slab, he decided to use it. A [Dual Cast] of his signature spell lifted up the board, swinging Mojo and a hefty portion of Yuk toward their rowboat.

Through [Parasitic Bond], Joe felt Yuk’s surprise, but his raggedy friend was not the one Joe was trying to please. His true target screeched with excitement, scampering to the front of the plank. Mo gripped tightly with his four monkey-like paws, leaning way forward and ruffing happily.

‘Hang on, Bud,’ Joe sent to Yuk before giving the shaggy goofball a real ride. Joe projected his intent, and the [Casting Claws] drove the board through the air in huge circles. Joe nudged the flight pattern a bit here and there, adding suggestions, but by this point, the rare-ranked skill had enough autonomy to handle the idea of what Joe wanted without him having to consciously control every movement.

Joe had the amber paws zoom the board forward, skimming the waters of Coral Bay, much to Mo’s delight and Yuk’s annoyance.

‘JOE! Come ON! We just rebuilt, and you’re shaving us apart with this wave-riding.’

‘You’ll be fine. A third of you is still riding on my shoulders. I’ll boost your stamina on shore, and you’ll be back to your full skittering armada in no time at all.’

‘Fine. Just give us some warning next time, huh?’ Yuk huffed in Joe’s head before their mental voice perked up. ‘Mojo really is having a blast. Okay. We’re gonna cut our connection to the bugs on the board. Feel free to give Mo a crazy ride.’

Smiling, Joe did just that. No longer having to worry about dislodging Yuk, Joe and the paws ramped it up. The plank shot skyward and came down spinning. Just before plunging into the bay, it shot sideways, throwing a stream of seawater high into the air behind it. Keeping to the water’s surface, Joe powered the board around the massive construction platform three times before sending it up into the sky again.

Mo was filling the air with joyous screeches. At the same time, Joe was completely distracting everyone around them. The shipwrights had all stopped working and were watching the spectacle. Some looked nervous; others were laughing.

Joe knew just how strong Mojo’s grip was after weeks of prying an endless number of monkey-prizes from the little man’s hands. He knew Mojo was unlikely to come off that board. Even if he did, Mo’s [Slipstream] skill would glide him down safely. The shug pup also wasn’t as densely boned and muscled as a true ape. His lighter build was a leftover from his previous avian form. While he was not a great swimmer, Mo could dog-paddle well enough that Joe wasn’t worried about him taking a dunking.

Even so, Joe realized that the foreman was starting to look a tad annoyed and decided to move the show back to shore. He hopped into the dory with the remainder of Yuk and rowed them away, letting the paws drive Mo around on their own, though in simpler patterns since Joe couldn’t watch closely while rowing.

Back on the Warder’s Beach, Joe gave his pup a few more loop-de-loops before bringing the plank back to the shore. The wooden leftover hadn't even landed before Joe found himself fully tackled by a hyper-happy hug.

“Mo! Mo! Mo. You’re screeching in my ear,” Joe shouted, hugging the little man back. “You liked that? It did look like fun.”

The pup jumped off, grabbed Joe’s hand, and dragged him toward the plank.

“You want me to try it? I don’t think it works that way. I don’t think I can lift myself with my paws,” Joe admitted, going on a gut feeling. “I’m the point the paws anchor to.”

Mojo was not to be deterred by Joe’s logic. He pulled again, and Joe followed.

“Okay, but this is not going to work,” he sighed as he doused the retreating Yuk with [Effereous Endurance] so they could rebuild.

‘But it’s going to be fun to watch,’ Yuk sent, their amusement obvious.

Joe stepped onto the gray board and positioned his [Casting Claws]. He placed one on the front right side of the plank and the other at the back left corner. He then tried to lift. Nothing. It was like bending over and trying to pick up his feet with his hands while he was standing. Joe could feel the hands exerting pressure, but as Joe had said, he was the anchor; the paws didn’t understand how to lift their own anchor.

He was about to say ‘I told you so’ to Mo, but he didn't want to give up that easily. He had over forty ranks in [Casting Claws], which was almost enough to lift his own body weight with just a single paw. With a few more ranks, he could try planting one paw against the ground and having the other lift the board like a serving tray. For now, though, neither paw could carry him alone. Even as he worked out the math of this, he realized he was making the process overly complicated.

Instead, he decided to go the willpower route. This was his signature spell, after all. If he wanted it to lift him up, then he would make it happen.

Except it just didn’t work. Joe set his whole focus on his intent, yet try as he might, he couldn’t lift the board with himself on it. He added [Crystal Mind] and [Jequa’s Verve] to the mix, and still no dice. He futilely tried [Steadfasting] himself to the board, followed by inscribing a LeafFall ward on the plank. Nope and nope.

‘You know there are dozens of levitation and flying spells, Joe. If you really want to get airborne, there are easier ways to do it than this.’

‘I know, Yuk, but one, I don’t have any open points for a new skill. Two, I’m trying to be careful about how many new skills I take. Kenda has warned me that taking dozens of common skills instead of developing a few higher-ranked ones is a bad move. Three, I looked at flying items, and they are expensive as hell. I didn’t think this was going to work, but I really was hoping it would.’

‘Yeah, seems like everybody wants to be able to fly. It is pretty awesome, even if we are not very good at it. We mostly hover and swoop rather than really fly.’

Joe gave it close to another hour before calling it quits. His best attempt had been an explosive moment of movement that spun the board like a top, hurling Joe onto his ass. Even though he was feeling a bit abashed at his tumble, there was that sliver of time when the [Casting Claws] had acted independently of his footing. While he wasn’t able to repeat it again, having done it once gave him hope that he might someday manage it properly. The idea of being able to Silver Surfer his way around was too cool not to pursue.

Feeling mentally exhausted after the attempts at self-flight, Joe knew he wasn’t in the right headspace to go back to curse-breaking, so he decided to hang out on the beach for a while longer, giving Mojo another round of skyboarding. Their play on the sandy strand began to gather a crowd. Since Joe was terrible at saying no to kids, they stayed even longer, giving the kids much gentler rides, while Joe treated some minor injuries among those watching the fun.

The next few days were more of the same. Joe spent a chunk of each day watching the Shellter’s construction, which was rapidly approaching completion. The Bracey family had gifted the project with some amazing windows that were now in place. Myllo was finishing up the navigation mechanics. Moving-in day was right around the corner.

After ship-watch, Joe spent the rest of each day practicing his ward-work, working on curse-breaking, and hanging out with his friends. The countdown on [Anyone] was almost up, and Joe found himself having mixed feelings about that. He knew that once he didn't have to be careful about gaining experience, he would throw himself back into active duty: patrolling the DirgeBlue Vale with Kenda, taking quests with the guild, and holding more clinic sessions. It felt like his spring break was almost over.

The biggest surprise of those last couple of lazy days came when Joe answered the door to find Dorian’s grim-faced hunter standing on the threshold. Nallorakk handed Joe an embossed envelope before spinning on his heel and marching off soundlessly, without uttering a word. The only sound Joe heard at all was the cry of the bugbear’s falcon.

Joe cracked the wax seal and withdrew a note written on heavy, cream-colored paper. Itread:

Joe,

I have received confirmation that our claims will be heard at the next Lughnasadh Moot. Oddly enough, it will be the first true Lughnasadh Moot in a century. Due to the absence of the Fey Kings, Imbolc, the turning of Winter to Spring, and Lughnasadh, the turning of Summer to Autumn, have devolved into three days of festivals instead of governance. Hopefully, the Oak King will be awake and present. I do not like our odds should we have to face the Queens with only his champion in attendance.

An additional reason for this missive is that I understand your [Anyone] will become available to you tomorrow. Before you adopt another trait, I ask that you visit me. I have an old marker from an impatient ally that I would be willing to pass on to you. If you accept, I believe it will greatly strengthen our position against the Queens and make it easier for me to name you my second once your work with the chimeras is complete.

To accept my invitation, place this card back inside the envelope, and I will expect you tomorrow morning at 5-Bells-Dawn at the Barrbarrow Trading House. If you have already committed the trait elsewhere, simply tear the card in half, and we will consider the matter closed.

Dorian Barrburrow

“How the hells did he know?” Kenda breathed in his ear as she read the note over Joe’s shoulder. “Nobody should be that well-informed.”

Not sure what was riling her up, Joe turned, kissed her on the cheek, and stepped back to throw the skill-savant a puzzled look. “How did he know what? That I was waiting for the cooldown on [Anyone]?” Joe guessed.

“Exactly,” she exclaimed. “He knows you’re a changeling, so knowing about [Anyone] is not a surprise, but the specific date of it becoming available again … Somehow that man seems to know every secret in Fort Coral. Nobody at the guild would have told him about the exact day, and thanks to [Noone], you are almost impossible to scry. So how did he know the date?”

“I wasn’t particularly careful when discussing it with you, Wen, and Tez. Maybe someone overheard us.”

“Or he was spying on us. It’s a given that the man is ridiculously well-informed; nobody gets THAT rich without good intel. Yet, this is not the first time he has revealed some really specific knowledge that he could not have come by without disregarding normal social boundaries. He seems to have an endless supply of divination, as well as ears and eyes everywhere.”

Kenda shrugged. “I guess I shouldn’t be surprised he’s watching you. The two of you are going to face the royals of the Feylands together. Of course, he is doing as much research on you as he can. Still, I wish the guild’s information network were a tenth as good as his.”

“Would you be willing to come with me? If I’m going to be offered a trait, I’d like to make sure it’s not a dud or a trap.”

“Oh, it won’t be a dud,” she remarked, wrapping her arms around his neck. “But it could be a trap. I’ll be there.”

Joe slipped the card back into the envelope and flicked it onto the dining room table, freeing his hands to pull her close.
