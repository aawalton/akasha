Saorex moved toward Sakura on his long spider legs so silently that it only made things worse. Sakura instinctively tightened her grip on the tiny key in her palm.

She could feel the silence between them growing more and more tense. In the end, she was the one who broke first. Maybe that was exactly what Saorex had been waiting for.

“I did everything you asked of me,” Sakura said, trying to keep her voice even. “I made it to the center and found this.”

Sakura wisely decided not to mention yet that she most likely had not finished in time. First, she needed to understand whether she had failed at everything else.

She opened her hand.

A tiny key lay on her skin, its edges pressing faintly into her palm.

Saorex stopped very close. His black eyes slowly dropped to her hand and fixed on the key. He looked at it for a long time. Longer than Sakura would have liked. She was already starting to feel stupid standing there with her hand held out.

“A very interes-sting choice,” Saorex said at last.

Sakura blinked.

“Is that the item that was needed?”

Saorex said nothing. He simply looked away, turned around, and walked back into the depths of the cave.

Sakura stared after him.

“That did not sound like a confident ‘yes,’” she muttered under her breath, but hurried after him anyway.

The tunnel they walked through was dimly lit by the same crystal-white veins in the rock. After the web chasm, that light almost felt cozy, though Sakura already suspected that in Saorex’s caves, anything that looked even remotely cozy should immediately be treated as suspicious. She followed him, feeling the cold stone under her bare feet, and grew more nervous with every step because she still had not gotten an answer.

Saorex was staying silent on purpose. Sakura could feel it.

He seemed to enjoy dragging it out and making her guess. And he was annoyingly good at it.

When they finally returned to the hall full of human junk, Pampli noticed Sakura first. He immediately jerked toward her and let out a low, almost catlike rumble of joy. The tension left his body so quickly, as if all that time he had been waiting for her to come back out of the darkness alive and unharmed.

“Yeah, I’m glad to see you too,” Sakura breathed, absentmindedly stroking his neck.

Pampli bumped his muzzle into her shoulder hard enough that he nearly knocked her over, and Sakura smiled without meaning to, but almost at once she shifted her attention back to Saorex. He was already heading toward the very place where the old music box lay tangled in the web among all the other objects.

Sakura followed him.

Gilbert rose from where he had been sitting and now watched in silence with his usual wary calm. He asked nothing, but it was obvious from his eyes that he wanted to know how this would end too.

Saorex stopped beside the box and lifted it very carefully into his hands. The long dark limbs behind his back rose slightly, then stilled again. He looked focused.

Sakura stepped closer without taking her eyes off him.

“So it was this after all?” she asked quietly.

Saorex did not answer this time either. Instead, he slowly reached out his hand toward her palm. Sakura placed the key into his fingers without a word.

He looked at the engraved note, turned the key slightly between his fingers, then inserted it into the tiny lock on the side of the box.

At first, the box jammed. Something clicked inside it, then clicked again, as though the mechanism itself was faulty. Sakura had already started thinking this was about to end in yet another unpleasant lesson, but suddenly the box shuddered, the lid lifted just a little, and the cave filled with music.

It was quiet, but startlingly pure.

Not cheerful and not sad. The notes spread through the cave softly, as if they were touching every stone and every strand of web. Even Pampli froze, no longer breathing so loudly. Gilbert did not move. Sakura herself stood just as still, listening as the melody filled the dark space. There was something springlike and kind in it, something that stood out sharply against the grim picture of this world.

When the box played to the end and went quiet, Saorex exhaled.

Sakura looked at him and finally dared to ask.

“Did I pass the trial?” she asked. “Was this the right item?”

Saorex carefully closed the lid of the music box and only then looked at her. His gaze was still as black and heavy as ever, but now there was something new in it. Not softness, of course. Saorex probably would not have known what that even looked like. More like the faintest shade of thoughtful interest.

“You are too impatient,” he said. “And too s-sharp at times. That is a bad combination for s-someone who wants-s to live long.”

Sakura had already opened her mouth, about to argue, but he continued without giving her a chance to cut in.

“But I will allow you and the beas-st to s-stay in my caves for a while.”

Sakura blinked.

“Really?”

“I never lie,” Saorex finished calmly.

Sakura let out a breath, as if only now realizing how tense she had been this entire time. But the relief did not last long.

“Do not interrupt,” Saorex said, even though she had not said anything yet. “You still managed to avoid pointles-s cruelty in the web. You did not s-start breaking, tearing, and killing s-small creatures just because they were in your way. You chos-se to observe, think, and find another path. In this-s world, that is-s very rare.”

Sakura swallowed.

One of Saorex’s rear legs shifted slightly.

“I do not tolerate people who bring pointles-s cruelty. Those, I kill.”

The words were spoken as evenly as if he were merely informing her that the cave was cool. That was exactly why they made her uneasy.

Sakura nodded slowly, very clearly aware that what she was feeling right now was deep and perfectly sincere relief that she had not ended up in that category.

Pampli, as if sensing the change in tone, gave a low snort and moved closer to her.

“I see,” Sakura said.

Saorex did not react.

Still not quite daring, Sakura asked again anyway.

“And the item? Did I guess right?”

Saorex shrugged so lightly, as if the question hardly mattered at all.

“I did not know which item was-s suppos-sed to end up in your hands-s,” he said. “And I was-s not suppos-sed to know. What mattered was-s not my choice, but yours-s.”

Sakura blinked in confusion.

“So you did not know either?”

“I wanted to see what you would bring back from there. By what means-s. And why. You managed to surpris-se me.”

He lowered his gaze slightly to the music box.

“I am glad I heard its melody. It is-s truly magical.”

Sakura let out a breath that was almost openly relieved now. It seemed she really had passed. It seemed everything had turned out fine. It seemed...

“But you did not finish in time,” Saorex said sharply.

Sakura froze, her mouth falling open a little.

“What?”

“You completely failed your timer.”

Something inside her dropped hard.

“So... I failed after all?”

Saorex looked at her as though the very fact of her horror was mildly exhausting.

“Time,” he said slowly, “is s-something ephemeral. Not always important. And far from always-s the main meas-sure of a result. The s-same goes for numbers-s, which people are so us-sed to relying on.”

For several seconds Sakura simply stared at him, trying to understand whether he was mocking her or whether he really thought that way. As always, there was no reading it from Saorex’s face.

Then he slowly swept his gaze over everyone present and spoke louder.

“I officially declare: you may use my caves-s. No more than three days-s. But under no circum-s-stances are you to destroy anything here. And do not kill anyone.”

He put special emphasis on the last words, turning his head toward Pampli.

Sakura followed his gaze and saw that Pampli was very intently watching one particularly careless little spider that was skittering over a nearby stone. The beast had the exact expression of someone who had already decided that right now he was finally getting proper compensation for emotional damage.

“No,” Sakura said faster than Pampli could do anything.

Pampli looked at her with displeasure, then at Saorex, and sat down with an expression that suggested the world had once again been deeply unfair to his hunting ambitions.

“Good,” said Saorex.

Sakura nodded.

“We won’t destroy anything. And we won’t touch anyone.”

Only after that did Gilbert finally come over to her.

He stopped beside her and glanced briefly at her bare feet.

“Well done,” he said. “I never doubted you’d make it.”

Sakura slowly turned her head toward him.

“You’re lying.”

Gilbert did not even try to look offended.

“Only a little.”

Sakura rolled her eyes.

After that, the tension finally began to fade for real. Saorex, showing not the slightest desire to continue the conversation, led them to a separate chamber deeper inside the cave. It turned out to be small, but spacious enough, with a flat stone floor, soft diffuse light from the glowing veins in the walls, and several dense bundles of webbing that, on closer inspection, turned out to be something like blankets.

At first, Sakura stared at them distrustfully.

“These are safe, right?” she asked.

“If you don’t start chewing on them or setting them on fire,” Gilbert replied, “then yes.”

Sakura was far too tired to argue.

The webbing turned out to be unexpectedly warm. Not sticky at all. Quite the opposite, dry and soft, like a very dense fabric.

The moment Sakura lay down, her body immediately thanked her for the decision. Only then did she finally feel just how tired she had been.

Pampli settled down beside her, let out a heavy breath, and fell asleep almost right away. Gilbert lay down a little farther off.

Sakura closed her eyes without trying to hold herself back anymore. And almost instantly sank into sleep.

When she was woken up, nothing in the cave had changed. The light still lay softly over the stone.

“Time to go,” Gilbert said.

Sakura opened her eyes with effort.

“Where?”

“To deal with important matters.”

She pushed herself up on one elbow and immediately grimaced.

“Important matters right after waking up are some kind of crime.”

“But at least you had time to snore.”

Sakura sat up sharply.

“I do not snore.”

Gilbert looked at her with such a straight face that it was exactly what gave him away.

“Of course not.”

“Actually, that was Pampli snoring,” Sakura said, pointing at the beast.

Pampli lifted his head with a deeply offended look, as though not only had he not been snoring, but he did not even understand what kind of absurd accusation this was.

Gilbert let out a quiet laugh.

“A very convenient excuse.”

“That’s not an excuse. It’s the truth.”

Sakura rolled her eyes again, but the sleepy heaviness inside her had already begun to clear a little. She stood up, adjusted her cloak, and only then noticed that Saorex was not in the room.

“Where is he?”

“Gone,” Gilbert replied. “Which is very much in character for him.”

They left the chamber and started down the cave corridors. Sakura was still not fully awake, so at first she simply walked beside him in silence, trying not to stumble barefoot over the uneven stone. Then she finally asked:

“Tell me more about him. About Saorex. What kind of creature is he, exactly? And what level is he?”

Gilbert took the question calmly, as if he had been expecting something like it.

“A very ancient and very powerful creature,” he said. “Long ago, the Arachnoborn ruled the forests of Veyr. Not like kings in the human sense, more like ancient masters of their own territories. Then came humans and greed. As usual.”

Sakura frowned as she listened.

“They were captured because of their weaving,” Gilbert continued. “The Arachnoborn could create more than simple fabric. Their masterwork weaving could be built into gear as reinforcement. Clothes, cloaks, gloves, belts, even armor lining made from their silk increased resistance to blows, spread impact better, held up longer, and literally raised a person’s chances of surviving a fight.”

Sakura stayed silent for a long moment.

“So they were just... captured for clothes?”

“For profit,” Gilbert corrected. “In Veyr, almost everything is done for profit. Especially when it comes to those who can be declared not people, but resources.”

Sakura clenched her teeth.

“And Saorex?”

“I got lucky and helped him get out. During a rebellion. The mages were the ones who started it. It was crushed quickly, of course. But in the chaos, a few prisoners were pulled out. Saorex was one of them. We both survived.”

Sakura glanced sideways at him.

“You never told me that.”

“I just did.”

She accepted that in silence. In this world, it seemed that every second person had a story about being broken, used, or nearly killed. And every third one seemed to have all three at once.

“So what level is he?” she asked at last.

Gilbert did not answer right away.

“One hundred ninety.”

Sakura nearly tripped.

“How much?!”

“One hundred ninety.”

“That is... a lot.”

“Exactly.”

Sakura involuntarily thought of her own second level and felt a sharp stab of embarrassment. Next to Saorex, her level looked not just small. It looked pitiful.

“And mine is only twenty,” Gilbert said.

Sakura snorted.

Gilbert’s mouth twitched in the faintest hint of amusement.

“There used to be Arachnoborn who reached level three hundred,” he added. “At least, people said there were. Maybe some still exist somewhere. I’ve never seen them.”

Sakura spent a while digesting that in silence. Once again, the world of Veyr had turned out broader and more dangerous than she wanted it to be.

At last, they entered another chamber, and Sakura stopped at once.

The entire space was filled with enormous sheets of webbing. They stretched from wall to wall, hung in layers, and formed complicated, almost geometric patterns. Crystal light shimmered between them, and all of it together looked like the workshop of some inhuman craft.

Saorex himself stood among those vast woven sheets.

“At las-st,” he said, without turning around right away. “I am tired of waiting.”

“Perfect time to get Sakura some proper clothes,” Gilbert said dryly.

Sakura did not have time to answer, because several small spiderlings were already hurrying toward her.

She tensed without meaning to, but with surprise realized that she no longer felt that old animal terror. Caution, yes. Disgust too, a little. But not the same icy fear as before.

The spiderlings quickly climbed onto her legs, then her pants, then her sleeves, and Sakura still went rigid.

“What are they doing?” she asked, trying very hard not to move.

“Taking meas-surements,” Saorex answered.

Sakura blinked.

“Oh...”

“They aren’t just measuring the length of your arms and legs,” Gilbert explained, and for the first time in a long while there was a spark of real interest in his voice. “They’re reading where the fabric needs to hold firmer, where it needs to give, and how the weight should be distributed.”

“I have never felt so professionally analyzed in my life,” Sakura muttered.

Not that she remembered enough of her past life to swear to it. Still, something told her this was probably true.

Meanwhile, the spiderlings really were working with brisk efficiency. One ran quickly along the line of her shoulders and chest, another across her waist, and a third went down one leg. As they moved, each spiderling left a thin silver thread on the fabric. The threads faded almost immediately, but apparently that was enough for Saorex.

When they were done, they all sprang off Sakura at once and ran back to Saorex.

“And now what?” she asked.

Saorex extended his hands toward one of the sheets of web. Several thick threads trembled at once and pulled taut. The spiderlings darted between them, bringing thinner strands now, not white but in different shades: gray, silver, smoky black. Somewhere deep in the lattice, dim knots of light began to glow.

Sakura watched in silence as he worked.

It looked nothing like ordinary sewing. It looked more like magic. Saorex did not cut or trim. He shifted his spider limbs across the stretched sheets, touched certain knots, pulled threads from one frame to another, and they began weaving themselves into a new pattern. The spiderlings brought fine fibers, worked them into the structure, then ran off for more. Sometimes one knot flared a little brighter, and then the whole construct seemed to rearrange itself, changing its tension and density.

The weaving slowly began to take the shape of clothing. First Sakura made out something like a fitted upper layer. Then pants. Then several dense, wide strips that, she guessed, were meant to protect the forearms and lower legs. Last of all, Saorex assembled light, flexible boots from layered silk that looked soft, but not fragile.

All of it hung in the air for only a few seconds, and then the threads drew tight, the light in the knots flared and went out.

Done.

Saorex removed the set from the web frame and tossed it toward Sakura. She reached out, already thinking that she did not want to hold the clothes in her hands and that it would be better to put them straight into the Inventory.

The set vanished before her fingers touched it. For a second Sakura just stared at the empty air. Right. So intention mattered too.

“You may change now,” said Saorex. “And do not complain if something fits too tightly. Gear is not meant to flatter. It is meant to s-save.”

Sakura did not argue.

She had to find a secluded spot to take the set out of her Inventory and change.

When she returned already dressed, the first thing she felt was that the fabric really did seem to know where it should hold tighter and where it should not get in the way of movement. The upper layer fit closely across her shoulders and chest without restricting her breathing. The sleeves ended in reinforced cuffs. The pants were lighter than her old ones and did not catch when she moved. The boots wrapped softly around her feet, but the soles felt far more reliable than her previous shoes. Over all of it was a short dark vest-like layer with dense weaving across the key sections.

Sakura took a few steps, bent one arm, then leaned forward.

“This is... comfortable,” she admitted, a little surprised.

System light immediately flashed before her eyes.

NEW EQUIPMENT SET ACQUIRED. SET: ARACHNOBORN SILK. QUALITY: RARE.

SET BONUSES: +1 STRENGTH +2 ENDURANCE +2 DEFENSE

MODERATE RESISTANCE TO CUTS AND BITES MINOR REDUCTION OF BALANCE LOSS ON UNSTABLE SURFACES CHANCE OF SURVIVAL: 13%
