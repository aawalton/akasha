We all have an origin story. That which drives our purpose. Sometimes, we let circumstance influence that narrative and drown us in the dark. We lose our way and believe the evil that surrounds us is all that exists. But the true light is always there, even in the blackest of shadows. You only have to look.

* * *

I rolled over and groaned, pulling the sleeping bag tighter around myself as the obnoxious chirping of a hundred crickets assaulted my ears. My back killed. My left arm felt like I’d been sleeping on a rock, which if I thought about it, I probably had. My bladder was about ten seconds from exploding.

And the hangover I’d have in the morning was already beginning to throb in my skull.

Fuuuuck.

I moaned, making barely human noises that I probably would have sprinted my ass away from if I heard them in the middle of a dark forest like this one.

I didn’t hate camping, but it was never my top choice of destination. My own brother should’ve known that. Of course… he wasn't my real brother, so there was that small sympathy.

Sure, the hiking had been fun. The mountainous landscapes, incredible. The air, sharp and pine-scented, unlike the city smog I'd gotten used to. Hanging around the campfire, drinking and singing at the top of our lungs because no one else in the world could hear us… That part was pretty solid.

For a little while, it actually felt like this entire event had been for me, and not Derek’s excuse to become “a man of the wild.”

Not anymore.

I didn’t even remember crawling into the tent. The miniature battery-powered lantern hanging overhead was still on, casting a harsh white glare that stabbed at my headache. I reached up blindly and squeezed the button to turn it off.

I dreaded getting up and walking, not sure I could hold my bladder if I moved an inch. But I couldn’t very well pee right there in my tent.

The sleeping bag’s zipper fought against me like it’d be the end of the world if I escaped its enclosure. The cheap nylon rasped against itself as I tugged and cursed under my breath. Eventually, I got out, threw the stupid thing into a ball in the corner, and exited the barely-big-enough tent to a scene reminiscent of my college partying days.

Crushed beer cans and empty bottles littered the ground, clinking faintly when I accidentally kicked one. The sour tang of stale alcohol and smoke still clung to the air. From nearby trees hung a half-assed paper banner that read Congrats Miles! with a picture of a ball and chain next to it.

Nearby, the remnants of a ground-level beer-pong game stood unfinished, and a deck of cards was scattered around the still-flickering campfire.

They couldn’t even put out the fire. Not a hazard at all.

I picked up five cards, undiagnosed ADHD in action, then thought better of it and tossed them into the dancing embers. The edges curled back turning black. A hole burned through the face of the jack of spades.

A chilling wind blew across my back, far too cold for the summer. I rubbed my arms, feeling goosebumps. Another gust and the fire instantly extinguished like a kid blowing out birthday candles, the warm orange glow vanishing and leaving behind only the faint scent of ash.

Weird…

Luckily, the light of the near-full moon was enough to still see. Its pale reflection painted long shadows across the campsite. I picked up another card. It was cold to the touch, frost coating the face of the jack of spades.

That’s… not normal.

I rubbed a thumb across the card, smudging beads of water on the surface. Not frost. Water. Just dew forming. My stupid drunk mind was playing tricks on me. I tossed it on the ground with the rest.

I passed by the three other tents, which were set up around the site, each zipped up and dark. A faint chorus of snores leaked from one of them. I turned to look at my own tent, much smaller than the others, with a garland of bras and panties wrapped around it.

God, my friends are idiots. Obviously, Ben had orchestrated all this. Immature, perpetually single Ben… Before I go back to bed, all of that is going on his tent. Then we’ll see who’s laughing in the morning.

I pulled out my phone and turned on the flashlight to see better. Something in my reflection on the glossy screen caught my eye.

Is that…?

No… No!

I switched the phone camera to selfie mode. College flashbacks were about to become reality.

Fuck me.

Fuck me.

Sure enough, a big, bold penis had been drawn on my forehead.

I’m gonna to kill him…

After I finish peeing.

I squinted closer at the screen. Something large moved in the trees behind me. A branch creaked, swaying, but when I turned to look, nothing stood out beyond shifting shadows and the smell of damp earth.

Right. Pee. Now.

I stumbled over to the pond we’d swam in the previous day after pitching our tents. The moon reflected, magnificent in the glossy, dark water. Thin clouds veiled the celestial light, which highlighted the wisp-like mist hovering over the pond. Tall grass at the edge of the water wavered in a gentle breeze, brushing together with a faint rustle.

It would have been a peaceful moment, if not for the endless chirping of crickets piercing my headache-rattled ears.

If I’d had any say in my own bachelor party, I definitely would have chosen a location where I could actually pee in a toilet, rather than behind a bush or into a pond. Why couldn’t we just stay home and play video games or rent a house by the beach like I’d suggested? Volleyball, corn hole, kayaking on the ocean.

Those all sounded far nicer right about now.

This algae-ridden pond didn’t give me the same feeling as the ocean. The salty breeze, the rolling thunder of waves. Something about that brought peace.

I imagined myself looking out into the vastness of that blue expanse. It always provided perspective. Gentle waves, given time, can break through stone. The ocean should’ve made me feel small, but it didn’t. It had always given me hope that the ripples from my tiny life would matter someday.

To someone.

And they did now. To Avery.

If Derek ever got married, I was one hundred percent going to commandeer his bachelor party and make it my own. Assuming he was willing to give that responsibility to his adopted brother. Of course, that also assumed he ever got married. And the chances of that were… less than slim.

He hadn’t had a steady girlfriend in years, instead opting to bring his little one-week stands to family events or on double-dates with Avery and me. Like that Easter dinner he brought the girl dressed up as a cowgirl with half her plaid shirt unbuttoned—bra-less, of course. Avery kept kicking me under the table even though I tried to keep my eyes anywhere but there, as she stared herself, mouth agape. It would have been fine if this girl didn’t also talk as if she were about to bring us all to a rodeo. Or if she’d stopped her rambling long enough that evening to take a single breath.

I chuckled at the memory. At least Derek was good for entertainment. And memories, I supposed. Wasn’t that what life came down to in the end, anyway?

I sighed. Who was I kidding? Despite all his faults, Derek had always been there for me. There was a reason I made him my best man. I'd never connected with my adoptive parents, even after all these years. I could always feel them judging me for my past. Afraid of me. But Derek. He'd accepted me from the start. A partner in crime, a confidant, a friend.

I made it to the pond and glanced back at the tents that held my slumbering compatriots now. And the chaos they'd left behind around the campsite. I couldn't help but chuckle.

My friends.

I turned to pee, but right about the time I finished unzipping my fly things got weird.

The crickets stopped.

I stuck a finger in my ear, thinking I must have gone abruptly deaf.

But the tall grasses at the edge of the water stopped moving too. Crystals of frost formed along the long blades, crackling faintly. The few ripples in the pond vanished, leaving it flat as glass. Ice spread outward with a brittle sound, forming in a semi-circle around me until the entire pond was frozen.

The wintery wind swirled again, carrying with it the faint metallic tang of snow.

Despite that, the leaves on the trees no longer rustled. Even a nearby bat, silhouetted by the moon, hung in the air mid-flap, utterly motionless, like something painted against the sky.

I squeezed my hands over my temples.

How much did I drink again?

That’s when I heard the roar.

Not like a lion or a bear. This was far too guttural. Far too resonant. Like Godzilla had come to life. The sound shook my ribs and seemed to vibrate in the marrow of my bones. It was everywhere, yet nowhere, all at once.

I turned frantically, not knowing which direction to look.

The beast lumbered from the shadow of nearby trees, their trunks warping around its body rather than impeding its movement. It was lion-like in shape, despite the otherworldly roar, but far more massive, towering up to the lower branches of the pines.

Flesh dripped from the beast, its face all but gone. The stench of rot rolled across the clearing in a wave, gagging me. Its mane was mangled and matted in knots. Bones jutted out from ragged patches of fur. Exposed muscle and sinew pulsed around its maw as it roared again. Spittle and blood spewed toward me, sizzling as it hit the frozen pond.

It was precisely that moment my bladder decided to finally relieve itself.
