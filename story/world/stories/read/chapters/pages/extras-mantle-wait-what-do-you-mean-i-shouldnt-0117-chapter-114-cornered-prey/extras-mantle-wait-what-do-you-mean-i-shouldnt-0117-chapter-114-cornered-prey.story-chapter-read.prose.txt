Elenor had made a mistake, and she was paying for it at a dead sprint.

The corridors of the thirty-first floor’s lower wing blurred past her in a wash of grey stone and flickering lights, her boots hammering the floor in a rhythm that matched the pounding behind her eyes.

The gash in her shoulder wept freely now, a hot slick running the length of her arm, and the taste of copper sat heavy on her tongue.

"Goddamnit," she hissed, tipping back one of the healing potions she'd looted off the cultist she'd put down in the last corridor.

The stuff tasted like someone had liquefied rotten meat and added a single crushed mint leaf as an afterthought. She nearly gagged and only the confirmation of her [Observe] skill telling her the contents were a legitimate full-grade restorative kept her from spitting the whole mouthful across the stone floor.

“Shitty taste, shitty people!” A tingle of warmth spread through her chest as the potion began its work, too slow for what she needed, but at least the bleeding in her shoulder eased from a steady flow to a sluggish ooze.

Small mercy. She'd take it. Only Valla knows how much she needs them.

There was no doubt in her mind that behind her, the cultists were closing in, and she knew—knew with the certainty—that she could not take all three of them head-on, and that was if there were actually just three of them.

The two Highmortals she'd killed had been just cannon fodder. Those fights, if you could even call them fights, had cost her barely any essence to finish. A thrust through the throat for one. A crystal detonation to the chest for the other.

Quick, clean, and efficient, just like the way she liked to handle her tasks, but the overmortals were a different breed entirely.

Three of them had come at her out of nowhere. The first had lunged with a spear wreathed in dark essence so thick it left a trail of decay on the stone behind it, and although Elenor had sidestepped the thrust and driven her shortsword up through the gap between his collarbone and his neck guard, the man had just kept coming. He'd wrenched himself off her blade like it was a minor inconvenience as the flesh mended itself shut before her eyes.

That was when the other two had hit her with their auras.

Three overmortal auras slamming down on her at once. The weight of it had nearly buckled her knees.

She didn’t know if it was luck, fate, or just their schemes that the one with the strongest aura didn’t attack her. He just stood with his arms folded and watched.

She couldn’t fight this battle, so she did the only thing that would give her a chance.

She made a decision to retreat.

She ran, throwing down her last essence disruption vial—the glass shattering with a sharp crack, a cloud of chaotic essence blooming outward to scramble their senses for a precious few seconds—and she kept running, because stopping meant dying and Elenor was not going to die today, or any day soon if she had anything left to say about it.

"Should have trusted that person," she spat between ragged breaths, banking left at a junction she recognized from her patrol routes. "The golem would have been a massive help, right now! Damn it. Damn it, damn it—"

She almost went down as the pressure slammed into her again, all three auras bearing down at once like invisible hands shoving at her back. They were closing in steadily.

They're herding me.

The realization hit with a sick lurch as she banked hard around another corner, her wounded shoulder clipping the wall in a burst of pain that whited out her vision for a fraction of a second. She stumbled, caught herself on the opposite wall with her sword hand, and pushed off without breaking stride.

Elenor had walked these halls enough times during her guard shifts to have the layout committed to memory, and her skill sharpened that recall into a three-dimensional overlay she could read even at a dead run.

The path she was on funneled toward the agricultural reserve wing. Toward the Seed Vault—which, if she remembered correctly, had been her destination anyway before the ambush—but the three behind her were pushing her there faster than she wanted, cutting off side corridors she might have used to double back or lose them and every time she'd angled toward an alternate route, a pulse of dark essence had sealed the passage ahead or a burst of killing intent had redirected her forward.

They wanted her going forward. Toward what?

I'll die standing before I walk into whatever this is.

Her free hand closed around the crystals in the belt pouch at her hip. She was running low—only five left of the nine she'd started with. It would be a long time before her mantle recovered from the stress she'd put it under, before she could manifest more. But the crystal warmed in her grip as she fed it a trickle of essence to prime it, and that warmth was enough. It meant she wasn't empty yet.

The wound in her shoulder was getting worse despite the potion. The blood had gone slick enough that her grip on the shortsword's hilt kept threatening to slip, and she had to consciously tighten her fingers every few strides to keep the weapon from sliding out of her hand entirely.

Her breathing had gone ragged in a way she didn't like—not from exhaustion, her stamina was fine, the adrenaline and her overmortal stats saw to that—but from the steady drain of essence she was burning to keep her perception field active and to fight off whatever curses the bastards kept trying to layer onto her. She could feel them like oily tendrils at the edge of her awareness, probing for a gap in her defenses, and every one she burned away cost her another sliver of what she had left.

Forty meters back. Gaining, but slowly.

She had time. Not much. But enough.

The corridor ahead opened into a wider space—a junction hub, she remembered—where multiple hallways converged beneath a high ceiling lined with dormant UV light arrays from the agricultural operations.

Grey stone walls, industrial grating underfoot where the stone gave way in places to drainage channels, and the faint chemical smell of nutrient solution long since dried to a residue.

Elenor burst into the hub and spun, planting her feet and raising her sword.

If they wanted to run her into a corner, she'd make them pay for the privilege.

Essence surged through her core as she began building power, pulling from her dwindling reserves with a focused intensity that made the crystal in her off-hand pulse in response. The perception field around her sharpened, the contours of the room snapping into clear detail, every sealed door and overhead pipe and cracked floor tile catalogued in an instant by the skill humming behind her eyes.

Come on, then. Come and get it.

And within the next few seconds, instead of the overmortals she'd been bracing for, two figures appeared at the corridor entrance.

High mortals. Both of them.

Elenor's mystic eyes flared alongside her [Observe].

Cultists, both of them.

The telltale signs of corruption threading through their essence channels were unmistakable once you knew what to look for, a dark chaotic web woven into their otherwise ordinary signatures.

The one on the left carried a shortsword and the other had a spear, held loose but balanced.

Neither of them looked particularly elite. Middling essence density, standard physical conditioning, no notable equipment beyond the blades themselves.

Elenor frowned. How were there so many cultists in the Bastion? And just how were so many of them down here, deployed just for her?

This wasn't a chance encounter; this was a hunt… no, the fact that the overmortals had sent these two ahead instead of closing the gap themselves told her she was one of the primary objectives. Not just the objective.

Fucking hell, This was getting too heated for her liking.

"Damn it," she muttered, and let go of two crystals.

They floated free of her fingers, rising to orbit her at shoulder height. Her hands came up, fingers moving through the gestures of a spell.

The one on the left charged first, closing the distance with a yell that echoed off the high ceiling. "Get the bitch!"

"Ye, brother!" The spearman roared and broke into a sprint behind him.

The spell completed. Both crystals snapped toward Elenor's wrists, flattening and reshaping as they made contact—crystals flowing like mercury across her forearms to form thin, angular bracers that locked into place over her guard—and thin lines of red essence bled outward from the bracers, racing along her arms, her shoulders, down her torso.

Speed, strength, durability. Hope that’s enough.

The shortsword cultist was already on her, coming in low with a sweeping cut aimed at her legs. “I’ll get ya!”

“No, you won’t,” Elenor vaulted the strike, her enhanced speed turning the motion into a blur, as she landed with one boot on the flat of his blade. The metal shrieked under her weight, and before the man could wrench his weapon free, she kicked off it into a spinning slash that caught him across the face. Her boot’s edge opened him from jaw to temple in a spray of red and a sound like tearing fabric.

He went down screaming, both hands clutching at the ruin of his cheek, and in the same motion Elenor twisted to track the spear she'd seen leave the other cultist's hand. The weapon hissed past her head. Way too close for a throw she'd only half-seen coming.

She was off-balance and still in the air when the spearman produced two knives and came at her in a scissoring cross-cut, his face twisted with fury. "You bitch! No one hurts ma’brother!"

Elenor bit her lip and threw her shortsword. The blade tumbled end over end, and the cultist deflected it with a reflexive bat of his left knife.

And that half-second of redirection was all she needed to land and reset her footing as she pulled another blade from the sheath at her lower back in a single fluid motion.

"Nothing saving you today from our wrath!" the man snarled, closing the remaining distance in two hard strides.

Both knives came at her in a converging cross—left high, right low—and Elenor brought her new sword up flat to catch them both. The triple impact jarred up her arms hard enough to make her wounded shoulder shriek, and for a heartbeat, her grip nearly failed.

Not yet. Not yet.

She grunted through her teeth, braced, and angled the flat of her blade in a sharp deflection that sent both knives sliding off to her left with a screech of steel.

In the same motion, she let one bracer dissolve, the crystal reforming as it dropped from her off-hand into the gap between her fingers. She drove it point-first into the cultist's sternum before he could recover his guard.

A mental yank. The crystal detonated on contact.

The man's ribcage caved inward with a wet, crunching sound, and he was launched backward into the corridor wall.

A fist-sized hole where his chest had been.

He wasn't getting up from that.

Elenor staggered back a step, chest heaving, and clapped her hands together. The motion triggered her retrieval skill, and a spike of agony drove itself through her heart. Her knees almost buckled, and for a terrible second, she was sure she was about to pass out standing up.

Hold. Hold it together.

She forced herself through it, and the crystal fragment pulled itself free from the dead man's ruined chest. It traveled back to her, reforming into a smaller crystal that settled into orbit at her side, diminished but functional.

“Fuck, that hurts… Argh,” She grunted and turned to look for the first cultist and found him trying to rise, one hand still pressed to the gaping wound she'd opened across his face.

Dark essence flickered around his fingers, a sloppy, desperate attempt to heal.

“I said no, you don’t,” Elenor crossed the distance in two strides. She brought her boot down on his weapon hand, pinning the shortsword to the floor with a crunch of small bones, and drove her blade through the back of his neck.

He went still.

Elenor pulled her sword free and straightened, scanning the hub. Two more Highmortals dead. That should have given her confidence—and it might have, on any other day, in any other circumstance—except the overmortals hadn't followed them in.

And she knew her body was shutting down.

She could feel it in the tremor running through her sword arm, in the shallow cadence of her breathing, in the way her perception field kept flickering at the edges like a candle guttering in a draft.

She needed rest, needed time to let her mantle recover, but she was going to get neither.

Elenor turned toward the corridor entrance.

It was still empty.

Did they give up? No, it isn’t possible. Then where—

The essence surge came from above.

BOOM

Her [Hypermind Core] and mystic eyes screamed the warning a fraction of a second before the ceiling caved in. Elenor barely threw herself sideways, rolling across the hub floor as a column of dark violet essence punched through the stone overhead and cratered the spot where she'd been standing.

Debris rained down, and through the billowing cloud of it, she saw him drop through the hole, landing in a crouch that cracked the floor tiles beneath his boots.

The one with the strongest aura.

He was older than the others. Dull grey hair hung long and loose around his face, and his eyes—when he raised his head to look at her—were solid black. Not the flickering, partial corruption she'd seen in the others. Full. Complete darkness with a violet storm in it.

"Tch." The man clicked his tongue and hissed through his teeth, straightening from his crouch with the unhurried ease of someone who didn't consider her a threat. "Two more of the Great One's will carriers are dead."

Elenor spat blood to one side, dragged the back of her wrist across her mouth, and gave the man a bloody sneer. "Yeah, I'm about to send a lot more his way."

The words came out steadier than she felt. Good. Let him hear confidence, even if what was behind it was held together with spite and fading adrenaline.

The man looked at her. Those black eyes moved across her body with the detached assessment of a butcher sizing up a carcass.

"Cocky, are we?" A smile split his scarred face, showing teeth that were too white for the rest of him. "Killing these children… You think you have what it takes to stand against His will?"

He raised both hands and balled them into fists.

Two more overmortals appeared behind him, stepping out of the shadows with their heads lowered and their eyes locked on Elenor.

They were the ones who attacked her earlier. Now that she had a chance to observe them carefully, she saw that one of the two was clearly a woman.

She was the one with the spear, and Elenor saw that half her face was tattooed in sigils. The other Overmortal was a man carrying a warhammer. Neither of them spoke.

She was deciding on what to do when three auras descended at once. The leader's aura was on a different level, which she dreadfully recognized as the threshold of ORDER IV.

The combined force drove down on her like the ceiling had tried to, invisible and immense. Her knees buckled. Her vision narrowed. The stone under her boots cracked in hairline fractures as the pressure found its way through her defenses and pressed her body toward the floor.

Elenor's knee hit the ground.

No.

She pushed back up, flaring her own aura. It was thin—she knew it was thin, knew they could feel how thin it was—but she forced it outward anyway, a barrier of defiance that said she was still standing, still fighting, still refusing.

The pressure didn't lessen, but it didn't drive her down again either, and she locked her legs beneath her and held.

The leader's smile widened, and she was certain the bastard was enjoying this.

"Good, good!" He barked it like a laugh. "What's the fun of a hunt if the prey just rolls over and dies?"

Dark violet essence surged from the man, coiling around his figure in thick ropes that wound and compressed and hardened as they went. Layer upon layer of the stuff wrapped itself around him, solidifying into intricate armor that covered him from throat to boot.

"Don't die too fast," he said, and his voice carried that same pleased edge, that same hunter's anticipation. "I want to see how well my pups do, first."

He raised one clawed gauntlet and pointed at Elenor.

The two overmortals behind him charged.

All five crystals floated around Elenor, orbiting faster and faster as she poured everything she had left into them—every scrap of essence, every ounce of will, every last breath of aura she could scrape from her failing reserves—and the crystals drank it down.

She was going to die here. And if this was where the road ended, she was going to leave marks on every single one of them deep enough that the scars would still ache come morning.

So come on, then.

She raised her blade, set her feet, and let the crystals scream.

I'm right here.
