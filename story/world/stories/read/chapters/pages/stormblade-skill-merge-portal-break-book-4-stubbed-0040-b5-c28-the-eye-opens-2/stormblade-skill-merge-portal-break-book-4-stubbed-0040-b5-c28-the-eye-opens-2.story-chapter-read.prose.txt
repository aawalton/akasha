The Placid King fled across the desolation.

Ellen followed.

Her Voidwalker form fed off Kade like a vampire, leeching Mana from him in torrents. She had no direct connection to the surging wall of wind and lightning, to the echoing bursts of thunder, that swirled around the portal world they shared. But she didn’t need it. Voidwalker was incredibly Mana-hungry. Every spell she cast felt like it cost exponentially more than the last.

But the Eye of the Storm kept Mana flowing into Kade’s core, and her Dual Skill Progression with him kept it moving into hers. It wasn’t perfect. She couldn’t go quite all-out with Voidwalker running. But it was close; if they’d had time to rank up Shadowstorm Battery, she might be able to.

Still…

As Ellen’s purple-and-black body strode across the flooded, broken Void Garden, she rained spells down on the Placid King. They couldn’t let up. Not for a second.

She’d fought relentless, powerful enemies. Enemies like Bob. He was the same way—convinced that he was unstoppable, smarter than everyone else in the room, and four steps ahead. Right now, the Placid King was on the back foot, just like Bob had been when Kade and Jessie had put their suit brief on his desk. But if she gave the Paragon time to recover, he’d turn it back on them in a heartbeat.

Shadow Puppeteer ripped the Placid King’s shadow away from him for the fourth—or maybe fifth—time. It assailed the monster, half a dozen ethereal tentacles trying to wrestle the gigantic octopus to the ground. It failed. Echoing Black grabbed at the Paragon’s magic, leaving a coating of darkness across the battlefield that slowly faded but did nothing to stop his retreat. A massive Abyssal Storm raged, fed by the link between the Eye, Kade, and Ellen. It tore at the Placid King.

Not enough.

He moved impossibly swiftly for how much damage he’d taken, seeming almost to coast on the salty water that still poured in from his portal world. If he escaped, Ellen and Kade couldn’t pursue him. Not into his own world; the one thing their plan had absolutely agreed on was that an assault on an SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon’s portal world was the fastest possible way to die.

She had to stop him. Right here. Right now.

The Shadow Cell closed around the Placid King. Black Hole opened right in front of him. Ellen gritted her teeth and kept on casting. Casting. Casting.

And the spells found purchase.

The massive monster slowed, then stopped. Its eyes flicked back, and jets of hypercompressed water ripped across the battlefield—only to stop as Ellen dropped another Echoing Black to cut the spell off. It worked—partially.

Enough.

Another Abyssal Storm. Then another. Arcane Resonator echoed the effects, and the storm of green-black lightning and shadow tore into the Placid King.

In front of her, between the thrashing tentacles, Kade cast his own spells.

Rolling Thunder.

Cloudburst.

Windfall.

I burned through almost every charge I’d accumulated in less than a second. The silence effect layered with Ellen’s, shutting down the watery beams cutting into my armor and slicing apart my muscles. Stamina poured into the wounds, and I gritted my teeth when it wasn’t enough. The snare helped pin the monstrous Paragon in place.

And the Rolling Thunder started a familiar combo.

A combo that fed the Eye of the Storm overhead, pouring dark clouds and crackling electricity into it. That, in turn, fed into Brendan’s Storm Sigil. And that led into Hand of the Thunder God.

A Hand of the Thunder God that I doubled with another Rolling Thunder, consuming the last Lightning Charge.

The two full-strength Hands of the Thunder God ripped out, one after another, boring into the Placid King’s massive, bulbous body. They scoured a path, and Ellen’s Abyssal Storm poured into the gap. Green-black lightning mixed with the blue-white of my own spell. Steam rose from the wound as minimum-strength Hands—still empowered by the Eye of the Storm, but missing the strength of my full combo—hammered in one after another.

The Placid King literally shook from the sheer impact.

So did my portal world. It roiled below my feet, like Earth had when the God of Thunder entered through the portal to the Crone’s world. I braced myself, but kept casting. So did Ellen. Magic poured through the air, battering the Placid King’s defenses.

And through the storm, deep inside the cavernous gap our magic carved into the Placid King’s body, hovered a core.

It was dark. Still. The air seemed to bend around it, and no lightning touched its borders. It almost seemed inviolable—like it was impossible for our magic to damage it. Mana poured out of it. So did Stamina, and a third form of energy. Health. Even through our strongest spells, the Placid King was healing.

I couldn’t let that happen.

The Placid King wasn’t the God of Thunder. He was massive, yes. And he’d proved himself a threat to me, no matter where I was. The God of Thunder was a combat monster, though. And he had—or at least, he’d had in the past—a shred of honor and dignity. He’d protected the people I cared about. Yes, he’d also left them to die in Yalerox’s world, but he’d delivered Ellen back to Earth, then left before he could do permanent damage.

The Paragon of the Stillwave Path wouldn’t show the same restraint.

I couldn’t, either.

Massive tentacles slammed down around me. I shifted stances to Torrentborne. My feet hit a tendril, sinking into the burning, twitching flesh. Another slammed into me, but I pushed off just as it touched my shoulder. Dodged a third. More rained down. Jets of water sliced through the air in front of me.

Stormrunner. It was the only ability I had the charges for. I accelerated and raced toward the core even as Ellen kept hammering the air around me with spells. My hair stood on end, and my connection with my spells flickered in and out as I pushed through her Echoing Black.

The Placid King slammed another tentacle down in front of me.

Thunderlord. I launched into a volley of lunges, then mixed Stormfall into the dance. It punched through the flesh, right between two eyes, even as they shot beams of water that scored smooth cuts across my scalp and shattered thigh armor. The Placid King screamed, but it was unintelligible.

Stormfall activated a second later, and chunks of gore splattered around me as the shockwave pulped a massive chunk of the Placid King’s limb. I pushed through it, hacking away with my rapier like it was a damned machete. The battle trance was fully on me, and by the time I finished, the limb flopped on flaps of flesh behind me.

I sprinted through the massive cephalopod’s guts. The stink of saltwater and acrid, burning flesh hung around me, but I kept pushing even as the walls Ellen and I had carved with our barrage of magic closed in under the weight of the Placid King’s Health.

My sword’s tip reached out. It tapped the core.

No explosion. No shock wave. Energy didn’t pour out from the impact site in a steaming, smoking torrent. The blade simply slid off the dark, still core as energy poured from it.

“You are nothing! You can’t! You can’t!” The Placid King boomed. Tentacles hammered down toward me. Ellen’s magic shredded them, but the sheer weight would still be crushing.

Then the Storm’s Blade caught on something. It dug into it. Stormfall activated again, and the explosion rippled across the flesh already covering the Placid King’s core.

The massive Paragon screamed. Its flesh seemed to boil around me as huge quantities of Health, Stamina, and Mana surged out from the impact zone. Lightning filled the gap, pouring into the Stillwave’s core. Tentacles thrashed, the one I’d carved through shredding itself in a fountain of gore that erupted a hundred feet high.

It took almost a minute. I spent that entire time dodging flailing tentacles as the Placid King’s aura hammered away at me and he screamed ever-weaker-sounding curses at me, Ellen, Tanat, and everyone we knew—mentioning them all by name. I shivered; the Placid King had known enough about me to have made this much, much worse.

But eventually, he died, and I waded free from the watery blood and gore, sword hand wrapped around the Storm’s Blade’s grip like a life-line and off-hand holding the Placid King’s core.

It was…shockingly small.

Dark blue, almost black, it seemed to swirl with an almost tranquil motion. That was a lie, though. Down below the emotionless, relaxing exterior, a storm boiled.

The same storm that boiled inside the Crone, the God of Thunder, and any other SS-Ranked monster. A turbulence brought on by pressure. A pressure that could only be relieved by ascension—or by death.

Ellen and I were covered in gore.

The Placid King’s five and a half remaining tentacles loomed around us like a massive framework of muscle, flesh, and suction cups. His eyes stared sightlessly in every direction. Cleaning the body would be a nightmare, even with our ability to control the portal world.

We’d won, though.

Somehow, against all odds, we’d won.

I let the last of my Stamina fade away, and the pain hit me all at once. My back was one massive scab. My hand had been mangled, and blood poured from a water-beam cut that had scored through my skin and into my skull. I shivered—that one could have been lethal. My leg was a mess of blood and pain, and I dropped to a knee, one hand down in the Placid King’s gore, as Ellen sprinted toward me.

She wasn’t in much better shape. Her stomach had a hole clean through it—the pain had to be unbelievable, and her hands were pressed to the wound, but couldn’t quite cover it. Still, her S-Rank body was functioning despite her injury, or the half-dozen lesser ones. Her robes were tattered and shredded, exposing bloody thighs and a savaged sternum that her delver’s healing was slowly putting back together.

Another minute of fighting, and neither of us would have survived.

And then there was the stink. The Placid King’s body had regenerated even through our shadow and lightning barrage, but now, that was done. The smell of burning flesh permeated the air around us. It mixed with a dead fish stink as the Placid King’s portal closed and the water began to drain to nothing. Ellen reeked of fear, adrenaline, and pain.

I pulled her in close anyway, holding her tight enough to get a gasp of pain, then letting up. “Sorry. I’m just…”

“You didn’t think we’d win?” Ellen asked. Her eyebrow raised questioningly.

“No. Honestly, no. We got incredibly lucky, and it won’t work again. Not against—“

“Kade, shut up for a second.”

I did. Ellen and I stood there, holding each other, in the corpse of an SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon. One of the most powerful beings in this universe, and we’d killed it and taken its core.

Its core.

It hung in my hand, radiating cold and pressure. Even as I watched, it slowly shifted. The calmness faded, replaced with a weight that could only be from its massive rank. This…this was going to be a problem. The God of Thunder would know we had it. So would the Crone—and so would any number of monsters who could benefit or ascend by consuming the sheer energy inside of it.

But right now, we’d won.

“You have something in your hair,” I said after almost thirty seconds.

“Oh?” Ellen asked.

I reached out and pulled a chunk of Placid King out of her long battle pig-tails. Then I dropped it. It hit the calf-deep water around us with a splash. “Yeah.”

Ellen stared at it. Then she started to laugh.

I did, too.

The Placid King was dead. We’d killed an SS-Plus-Ranked monster. At S-Rank. That would have consequences—most of which I couldn’t even predict. And then there were our statuses.
