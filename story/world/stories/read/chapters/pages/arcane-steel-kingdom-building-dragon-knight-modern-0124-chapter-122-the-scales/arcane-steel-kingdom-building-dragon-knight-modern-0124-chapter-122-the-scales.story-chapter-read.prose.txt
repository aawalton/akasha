The fragile peace of the morning in the gazebo lasted exactly as long as it took for Kaelus to finish his breakfast.

My brother had an appetite that honestly defied logic. He usually ate in the specialized dragon habitation zone—a pocket dimension where the ship's massive kitchens sent over specially prepared, dragon-sized meals for Cygnus, Kaelus, and the few royal guards who had come aboard The Aegis with their king. This morning, though, after already putting away what looked like a small mountain of roasted meat, he still came waddling over to our table looking for dessert.

He shrank himself down to his favorite cat-sized form, his starlit scales catching the light as he padded across the gazebo’s polished weirwood floor.

Lyra spotted him right away.

"Eggy!" she shrieked. The sound practically rattled the crystal panes around us.

She scrambled out of her chair, hit the floor running, and scooped the Dragon Prince up into one of her signature bone-crushing hugs. Kaelus’s little legs dangled limply while she squeezed him against her chest, that familiar look of long-suffering patience settling over his scaly face.

I watched the color drain completely from Ela’s face.

She had been taking small, hesitant bites of her Cake, finally starting to relax. Now, she was frozen, her sapphire eyes wide with stark terror. Her hands hovered over her plate, the food forgotten. The words she tried to form caught in her throat, leaving her gasping for air.

It made perfect sense. The only time Ela had ever seen Kaelus was in the freezing mud of Oakhaven. She had seen a thirty-meter titan descend from the sky and vaporize thousands of heavily armored soldiers in a single breath.

To her, Kaelus was an entity of destruction.

Watching an apex predator being manhandled like a cheap plush toy by a five-year-old girl was a jarring sight. If a stranger had tried to hug Kaelus like that, my brother would have bitten their head off and complained about the taste.

But when it came to Lyra, the cosmic terror had a blind spot a mile wide.

I stood up, moving slowly so as not to startle Ela further, and walked around the table. I knelt beside her chair, keeping my voice low and steady.

"It's okay," I said, gently patting the top of her silver head. "Kaelus won't hurt her. He actually enjoys it."

Brother, I do not enjoy it, Kaelus projected into my mind, his mental voice thick with wounded dignity. I simply find it... acceptable. And it makes her happy. Do not spread slander about my preferences.

I ignored his mental grumbling.

"Ela," I asked softly, trying to draw her attention away from the terrifying display of draconic tolerance. "Do you like dragons?"

Ela swallowed hard, her eyes darting from Kaelus back to me. "Yes," she whispered, her voice barely audible. "I love dragons. Nanny Lue told me stories... she said my father was a Dragon Knight."

She snapped her mouth shut instantly, her small hands flying up to cover her lips. Panic flared in her eyes. "I mean... my other father," she corrected frantically, looking toward the head of the table where my parents sat.

She was struggling. Her young mind was trying to wrap around the concept of this new family, terrified that mentioning her biological father would upset the giant, scarred man who had just taken her in.

My father set down his coffee cup. The stern, unyielding mask of the Duke melted away, replaced by a gentle smile.

"You don't have to call me 'Dad' until you feel completely comfortable, Eleanore," he said, his voice a warm, rumbling baritone. "And you should never be afraid to speak of your father. Orian was my younger brother. And he was one of the finest Dragon Knights to ever ride the skies of Aerthos."

Ela’s eyes lit up. The fear melted away, replaced by a sudden, fierce hunger for stories. The puzzle pieces were finally clicking together in her head; these strangers really were her blood.

"Really?" she breathed, leaning forward in her chair. "Can you tell me stories about him? And about the Dragon Knights?"

"I would be honored to," my father nodded, a glint of pride in his stormy grey eyes. "You know, I am also a Dragon Knight."

Ela actually bounced in her seat at that. "Really?! Nanny Lue told me all the Dragon Knights disappeared when the fire came! Are there any others left?"

"Yes, there are," my father said, resting a heavy hand on my shoulder instead. "There’s a huge gruff knight you’ll meet soon enough. And your older brother here..." He gave me a proud little squeeze. "...has also achieved resonance. He’s a full-fledged Dragon Knight, bonded with Kaelus."

Ela turned to look at me, her expression shifting from cautious gratitude to outright star-struck admiration.

I suppressed a sigh. I had rained food from the heavens, descended from the stratosphere to vaporize an army, and carried her across an entire continent. But apparently all of that meant nothing next to the title of 'Dragon Knight'. The inner workings of a little girl's mind were a mystery even Tes couldn’t decrypt.

I glanced across the table at Nyxia, who was currently watching the exchange with a bemused expression. Scratch that, I thought wryly. I'll never understand women of any age.

Meanwhile, Lyra had dragged Kaelus down to the thick rug in the center of the room and was aggressively rubbing his belly. The dragon lay on his back, his four paws sticking straight up in the air, looking thoroughly humiliated.

Lyra looked up, her face flushed with exertion. She patted the empty spot on the rug next to her. "Ela! Come help! He likes it behind the front legs!"

Ela hesitated, looking at the dragon, then up at me, a silent request for permission.

Kaelus? I asked mentally.

She smells like Lyra, he replied, a begrudging sigh echoing in my skull. It is acceptable. Tell her to watch the claws.

I nodded to Ela. "Go ahead. He won't bite."

Ela slipped out of her chair and approached the dragon with the caution of a bomb disposal expert. She knelt on the rug, her hands trembling as she reached out. She gently placed her fingers on the starlit scales of his belly.

Kaelus let out a soft, rattling purr. I could swear I saw his back foot start to twitch as Ela found the right spot.

I left them to their bonding, offering a brief farewell to my parents. The domestic peace was vital, but the war outside the pocket dimension was still waiting.

I headed back toward my private workshop, the heavy weirwood doors closing silently behind me. I wasn't surprised to hear the rhythmic click of heels following me down the obsidian corridor.

Nyxia fell into step beside me, her arms crossed over her chest. The Ice Queen mask was back in place, but her eyes betrayed a burning curiosity.

"So," she began, her voice crisp and demanding. "Let me get this straight. In the middle of the night, you snuck out of our quarters to rescue a long-lost family member from an enemy force of a hundred thousand soldiers. And you did this halfway across the globe?"

"Yes. Kinda," I said, not breaking stride.

"And this is a typical morning for you?"

"Generally speaking, yes."

We reached the workshop. I pressed my palm to the scanner, and the door clicked open. I walked straight to the central holographic table, bringing the dormant systems back online.

Nyxia followed me in, standing near the edge of the blue light cast by the displays. "What is it you are working on that has you so distracted you can only manage one-word answers?" she asked, her tone shifting from accusatory to genuinely inquisitive. "What am I looking at here? Why does this fortress look like it has spider legs?"

I didn't look up, my hands already moving to expand a complex, three-dimensional schematic rotating above the table.

"I call them Crawlers," I explained, manipulating a cross-section to show the internal layout. "They are 3 kilometers in diameter. They are meant to move alongside the front lines. Unlike the Vindicator-class supercarriers like The Vengeance and The Retribution, which rely on speed and vertical dominance, this is a mobile fortress. It sacrifices mobility for significantly greater weapon capacity and defensive shielding."

I pulled up a comparison chart. "These move slowly. They use those articulated legs to navigate rough terrain, or they can use built-in gravitic engines to hover for short, tactical repositioning. They can even hover over water, though that redirects power away from the primary kinetic shields. They are designed to be the anchor of the front lines."

I tapped the center of the schematic, highlighting a massive, glowing core.

"They are equipped with fully automated Forges. While the Vindicators specialize in paradropping and rapid aerial combat, these specialize in sustained land deployment. They house internal factories creating a continuous, unbroken loop. They recycle damaged automata recovered from the field, melt them down, and produce fresh units directly onto the battlefield. It is an impenetrable, mobile wall meant to spawn an unending tide of MECHs."

I looked at her, finally meeting her gaze. "The official designation is the Colossus-class. They are my solution to holding the newly conquered lands and consolidating our power. They will utilize the two mature dungeon cores General Goliath is currently extracting from the Dominion. Eventually, I intend to produce three of them."

Nyxia stared at the rotating hologram. She stood there for a long, silent minute, her crimson eyes tracking the intricate lines of the mana conduits and the heavy armor plating.

"First of all," she finally said, her voice hushed, "how can I possibly be upset about not receiving a dungeon core as a wedding gift if you are planning on building this? It makes a standard Mage Tower seam obsolete."

She stepped closer to the table, her brow furrowing. "And secondly... what are these specific runes? This matrix here. It's almost akin to a traditional Mage Tower's core stabilization logic. Did you invent your own Mage Tower designs?"

She looked up at me, her expression dead serious. "Do you realize how impressive that is? In the history of the world, there are very few original designs. First, the ones gifted by the dragons in Dragon Valley. It shames me to admit it, but the famed Mage Towers of House Black are merely highly modified versions of those ancient blueprints. Then, there are the designs created by Maghri Vex, the genius mage who fought beside the founding king of Cinderfall."

She took a sharp breath, her eyes returning to the hologram. "What you have engineered here is something entirely new. These are partial Mage Towers that operate without a mage to guide them."

She looked at me as if I were some kind of arcane anomaly. I decided it was best to keep it a secret that I had drafted the foundational logic for this core design when I was five years old, while playing with wooden blocks. It might break her brain completely.

"Which reminds me," I said, abruptly changing the subject. "I have something for you."

I turned away from the table and walked over to a secure drawer built into the workbench. I rummaged through the tools and spare parts until I found what I was looking for.

I walked back to Nyxia and held out the small black velvet ring box.

I opened it.

Resting on the dark fabric was the ring. The band was a simple, sleek circle of dark metal, but the real star was the gemstone — a flawless diamond with a deep, internal blue shine that seemed to swallow and reflect light in ways that shouldn’t be possible.

I rubbed the back of my neck, feeling the awkwardness hit me all at once. “You know how dragons produce veins of minerals and gems around their nesting sites? Well, the band is forged from the very first vein of blue platinum Kaelus ever made in the habitation zone. And since he’s a Star-Born dragon, he also creates unique gems. That diamond right there is the first one he ever produced. It’s actually a natural spatial storage gem.”

Nyxia stared at the ring, her breath catching.

“And,” I continued, rushing a little to hide my nerves, “I’ve been working on a side project with nuclear reactors. Basically, I’ve been trying to make something that works like a pseudo dungeon core, but way weaker. This ring is the prototype. I managed to shrink a miniaturized version down and stuff it into a spatial pocket dimension, so the power output is even lower — right now it’s only running at about fifty-seven percent efficiency compared to a full-fledged reactor.”

I pointed at the glowing blue diamond. “But unlike the magical crystals most mages use for high-tier spells — those one-time things that burn out after a single cast — this doesn’t run out. It’s self-sustaining. It’ll give you a constant, stable mana reserve for your research.”

I was about to start explaining the fail-safes I’d built into the containment field when Nyxia cut me off.

She closed the distance in one quick step and threw her arms around my neck, pulling me into a fierce, tight hug.

The sudden contact completely wiped the technical talk from my brain. I stood there stiffly for a second, my hands hovering awkwardly in the air, not quite knowing what to do with them.

She pulled back, her cheeks flushed, and practically snatched the ring box from my hands. She plucked the ring from the velvet and slid it onto her finger. It fit perfectly. She held her hand up to the blue light of the holograms, a brilliant, unrestrained smile breaking across her face as the diamond caught the light.

I watched her admire the jewelry, a profound sense of confusion settling over me.

Women will always elude me, I thought to myself. I hadn't even finished explaining the most important parts about the structural integrity and the safety protocols.
