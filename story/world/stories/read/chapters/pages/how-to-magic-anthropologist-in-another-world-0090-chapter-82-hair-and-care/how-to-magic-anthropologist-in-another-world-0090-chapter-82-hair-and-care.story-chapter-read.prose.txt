After the battle, everyone agreed they deserved a short rest. I was currently sitting on one of our folding camp chairs while Kan stood behind me with a pair of scissors.

"Hold still," she muttered.

"Sorry," I apologized for the third time.

I fidgeted slightly every time I saw another strand of hair hit the ground. The last lightning strike from the elemental had done more than leave me in agony. The ends of my hair had been completely ruined. Every strand past shoulder length had become dry, brittle, and curled in unnatural directions. Some tips had even fused together into little blackened clumps that discharged static electricity.

Kan carefully lifted another lock between her fingers before snipping away several centimeters, and more burnt hair drifted toward the stone floor.

"It smells awful," she commented. "You're lucky it didn't burn all the way to your scalp."

"I guess I am."

Around us, the rest of the team was enjoying a well-earned break.

The rooftop offered one of the most spectacular views I had seen in either world. Endless plateaus, interrupted by a few mountains, stretched beneath the eternal night sky of the Sunless Reach, their peaks illuminated by countless stars. Every few minutes, another shooting star crossed the heavens, leaving glowing trails behind before fading into darkness.

Shingo stood near the edge, quietly enjoying the cool mountain air.

Katar sat on the stone railing, his legs dangling over the ten-story building, silently watching the landscape below.

Raik had spread out a rough map on the ground, trying to determine the fastest route back to Hano.

Vena knelt near the edge of the rooftop with her hands folded in prayer, probably thanking the Holy Lady for helping us survive the battle.

Meanwhile, Calr and Ja'a were examining the ancient telescope mounted near the center of the roof.

The enormous bronze instrument was almost as fascinating as the tower itself. Intricate gears surrounded its rotating base while dozens of engraved runes covered its frame. Calr carefully inspected one of the engraved rings.

Ja'a pushed lightly against one of the wheels, and the telescope shifted position.

"It still moves," she gasped.

"It shouldn't," Calr said, inspecting the gears for signs of lubrication and finding none.

"It really shouldn't," Ja'a nodded.

"You two sound disappointed," I laughed.

"We're impressed," Ja'a corrected. "Things this old are supposed to be broken."

Kan snipped another strand.

"So..."

I glanced upward.

"So?"

"Do you really plan on living here?" She gestured around the rooftop with her scissors. "In this tower."

"Yes!" I smiled. "Assuming it's actually possible."

"It would certainly be unique," Raik commented without looking up from his map.

"I just don't know who I have to ask about ownership." I shrugged. "Maybe the chief of the nearby village? Or perhaps someone in Hano?"

"The Freelancer Guild," Raik answered immediately.

"Really?" I blinked.

He nodded.

"This is wilderness territory." He tapped the map. "Nobody officially owns it. Ruins, monster nests, abandoned mines, things like that all fall under Guild jurisdiction. If someone wants to claim or develop one, they normally register it through them."

That made things considerably easier.

"Perfect," I grinned. "I'll have Nada handle the paperwork."

Ja'a looked around the rooftop again.

"The place is far too... ruin-y... for my taste."

She nudged a loose stone with her boot.

"I mean..." She pointed toward one of the broken ledges.

"I plan to restore it."

I gestured toward the tower beneath us.

"Repair the walls, replace the broken windows, and add furniture."

"You make it sound cheap," said Ja'a.

"What are a few gold coins compared to living in a three-thousand-year-old observatory?"

"Well, when you put it that way..." Ja'a laughed. "I can't argue against it, can I?"

Kan trimmed my bangs a little shorter.

"What happens if another Sky Elemental decides this is its home?" she asked.

I pointed toward Vena without even turning around.

"I plan on having the entire peak sanctified," I said.

"That could work." The cleric opened one eye.

"You think?" asked Calr.

She nodded thoughtfully.

"I doubt a blessing would repel elementals the same way it repels undead." She looked toward the sky. "But holy ground would discourage many hostile creatures from lingering. Even if it doesn't stop them entirely, it might encourage them to nest somewhere else."

"Good enough for me."

A few more careful snips echoed behind me.

"There." Kan stepped back. "Finished."

I reached into my Bag of Holding and pulled out a mirror.

The moment I saw my reflection, I winced.

"You don't like it?" Kan asked.

"I mean..." I hesitated before noticing Kan's slightly dejected look.

I quickly shook my head.

"No, no. You did an excellent job."

"It's just..." I turned my head from side to side.

"I really don't like having short hair..." I sighed.

"It suits you, though," Vena said, smiling at me.

"It makes me look like I'm about to ask to speak to the manager."

For a second, nobody reacted.

Then Ja'a burst into laughter.

"I don't know why..." She pointed at me. "...but somehow I completely see it."

"You know," she added, "you can always have it magically regrown."

"That's expensive," Kan immediately said, shaking her head.

"The woman who wants to spend gold restoring an ancient tower isn't going to worry about paying someone silver to grow her hair," Ja'a scoffed.

"Exactly." I gave her a cheerful grin. "If I start running out of money, I can always pick up my shovel and wish for more."

Before the conversation could drift further, Raik folded his map shut.

"Before you start teleporting all over the continent again..." He looked directly at me. "We should decide what we're doing next."

"Aren't we just heading home?" Kan asked.

"Yes," Raik nodded. "But it's still a day and a half back to Hano." He looked around the group. "Do we take jobs along the way?"

"Or do we head straight back?"

"I can solve that," I said, raising my hand.

Everyone looked toward me.

"We're in the Sunless Reach." I pointed toward another shooting star crossing the heavens. "My Star mana is practically unlimited here."

Realization slowly spread across their faces.

"I can teleport everyone back."

Calr rubbed his chin.

"What about the wagon and the draft animals?" he asked. "We rented them from Hano."

"We'll just pay someone to return them," I shrugged. "Bobby's son would probably do it for a handful of bronze."

"Honestly..." Ja'a said, throwing herself lazily into her chaise lounge. "I've had enough riding."

"I vote for teleportation." She looked hopefully toward Raik.

The others nodded one after another until only Raik remained.

"Fine..." he finally sighed.

A grin spread across my face.

"So who's going first?" I asked. "Do I take you all to the Freelancer Guild?"

Most of the team nodded.

"Take me to the Holy Temple first," Vena said, shaking her head.

She looked toward the distant horizon where Hano lay beyond the mountains.

"It is long past time I informed Sana about the portal to the Mythic Realm."

"I will go last," said Calr. "I want to explore this tower some more."

"Okay then," I said. "Let's start with Vena first!"

“I wish I would be back here in five minutes,” I said, banking a return teleport wish.

I stood, stretched my shoulders, then wrapped an arm around Vena.

"Ready?"

She smiled and nodded.

I waited until another shooting star crossed the eternal sky, letting fresh Star mana pour into my soul before speaking again.

"I wish we were next to the Holy Temple entrance."

The world folded around us.

One heartbeat later, we were standing outside the familiar white stone walls of the Holy Temple. A handful of worshippers climbing the front steps froze in surprise when we appeared from nowhere.

Vena blinked at the shift from night to day, then looked around with a smile that immediately softened her features.

"It feels good to be back," she said.

"It has been a while," I nodded.

"Yeah."

"Tell everyone I said hi," I said. "I'll visit once I finish taking care of a few things."

"I will."

I started stepping backward before remembering something.

"Oh, and don't forget. I'll be stealing you away again soon enough so you can sanctify the tower."

Vena considered it for a moment.

"Let's do it the day after tomorrow."

"That works for me," I nodded.

"Fireday at noon," she smiled. "That will give me enough time to report to Sana and visit Louis and the orphanage."

"Fair enough."

She held up a finger before I could leave.

"There is one more thing."

"Yes?"

"You need to find seven objects for the sanctification ritual."

"What kind of objects?"

"Something small and symbolic," she explained. "Anything you believe is related to your home."

I frowned.

"Do they need to be Holy symbols?"

"Not necessarily." She shook her head. "Since it is your home, they need to be significant to you. Maybe a family heirloom, if you have one."

That sounded surprisingly flexible.

"Anything that tells the Holy Lady, 'This is my home,' should do," she added.

I rubbed my chin thoughtfully.

"So as long as I can explain why each object matters..."

"It will work."

"I like that."

Vena smiled warmly.

"I thought you would."

"Okay then," I said. "My five minutes are up. I'll see you in two days."

"Travel safely."

I waved one last time before the banked wish activated. The Temple vanished, and I was back on top of the tower beneath the starry night sky.

"Welcome back," Kan said.

I smiled back at her before asking, "Okay, who is next?"

"Me, me! I am next!" Ja'a shouted.

I couldn't help laughing.

"I had a feeling you'd volunteer."

A few moments later, another shooting star passed overhead.

"I wish I would be back here in five minutes."

Once the return wish settled into place, I placed a hand on Ja'a's shoulder.

"I wish we were standing outside the Freelancer Guild."

The rooftop disappeared again.

Over the next half hour, I repeated the teleportation routine.

Ja'a went first. She immediately ran toward a nearby freelancer and started asking about the latest gossip.

Then Raik went inside the main hall, probably to make his report.

After him came Katar, who simply nodded once before walking away toward the portal hub.

Shingo followed and gave me a quiet smile before heading toward the dorms.

Calr decided to remain behind for a little longer.

"Can you come back for me in an hour or two?" he said without taking his eyes off the observatory walls. His fingers traced another line of ancient Telepath script.

"Sure."

That left Kan. We teleported together and stepped into the busy main hall of the Freelancer Guild. We spotted Raik speaking with Nada near the reception desk. The dark-skinned girl had actually come out of her office to greet him personally.

Several freelancers and guild employees were gathered nearby, listening with obvious interest.

"Everything appears to be in order," Nada said as she finished skimming through multiple bounty reports.

"You even exceeded expectations on several assignments."

She stacked the completed paperwork neatly before smiling.

"I'll be forwarding a copy to Grand General Okar informing him about the end of the challenge."

Raik nodded.

"But that's just a formality. As far as the Freelancer Guild is concerned..."

She extended her hand.

"Congratulations, Sergeant Raik Agame."

The surrounding guild hall immediately erupted into whispers.

"Agame?"

"Already?"

"I heard they killed a Commander-ranked troll."

"They spent a week in a Dead Zone."

"No wonder."

I glanced toward Kan before gently nudging her shoulder.

"Go."

She looked at me in confusion.

"What?"

"Stand beside him."

"But..."

"He'd want you there."

She hesitated for only a second before gathering her courage and walking across the guild hall.

Several people immediately noticed.

"Isn't that the traitor's daughter?"

"I heard she's on his team."

"Seriously?"

"Why her?"

"I would've killed for a chance to join an Agame-led squad."

Kan heard them.

I could tell from the way her shoulders stiffened. Even so, she continued walking until she stood beside Raik.

He gave her a small nod, a quiet acknowledgment that she belonged there.

For some reason, I liked that more than any grandstanding.

Nada invited both of them into her office to finish the promotion paperwork.

For a moment, I considered following them. I still needed to ask about claiming the observatory, and it would have been nice to catch up with Nada as well.

I shook my head. She would be buried under reports for hours after everything that had happened with us, especially the cult encounter.

The paperwork for my tower could wait another day.

Besides... I looked down at myself. I was still dirty from days of riding, climbing mountains, fighting elementals, and getting electrocuted. A hot bath suddenly sounded like heaven.

After that… I smiled to myself.

I needed to find a place where I could get my hair fixed.

After thirty minutes of soaking in the guild baths, I dressed in fresh clothes and summoned my spear.

Instead of asking for a hair salon recommendation, walking across half the city, booking an appointment, or, God forbid, waiting in line, I decided to abuse my teleportation perks again.

I took a bit of my reserve Star mana before making my wish.

"I wish I would teleport inside a place where I can immediately get my hair fixed."

The familiar sensation washed over me, and a heartbeat later, the guild vanished.

I found myself standing inside a surprisingly elegant salon.

Shelves lined the walls, filled with colorful bottles, fragrant oils, combs carved from polished bone, and dozens of strange hair accessories I couldn't even identify. Comfortable seating faced a large mirror, and an empty waiting area looked more like a noble's sitting room.

The room smelled pleasantly of lavender, citrus, and something floral I couldn't quite place.

Two women stared at me in complete surprise.

One had vibrant green and yellow wings, marking her as a Valkyren Dreamer. Her plumage reminded me of a tropical parrot, the bright colors almost glowing beneath the enchanted lightstones hanging from the ceiling.

The second woman possessed deep azure-blue hair, dark enough to border on indigo. Judging by the color alone, she was almost certainly a Soulit like Ja'a, although her shade of blue was considerably darker.

Both of them blinked.

I smiled awkwardly.

"...Are you open for business?"

The Valkyren recovered first.

"We are."

"Please have a seat," the Soulit added with practiced professionalism, already gesturing toward one of the padded salon chairs.

I happily sat down.

The Valkyren tilted her head.

"...How did you know?"

"Know what?"

"That we had an opening."

I frowned.

"I didn't."

She exchanged a confused look with her coworker.

"A group of five noblewomen canceled their appointment barely ten minutes ago."

"Really?"

"Yeah." She nodded. "The party they were going to attend got canceled."

"We've been wondering if we should close for the day," added the Soulit woman.

"I guess I got lucky then," I laughed.

"Lucky?" the Dreamer woman asked.

"I teleported here randomly."

She blinked.

"Randomly?"

"Yep, randomly." I grinned.

Both women stared at me for several long seconds.

"Oh!" I almost forgot.

"I'm Alice."

"Mimi," said the Valkyren with a cheerful smile.

"And I'm La'a," said the Soulit.

"So..." Mimi walked behind my chair while La'a picked up a brush.

"What happened to your hair?" the Valkyren asked.

I sighed dramatically.

"It got zapped by a Sky Elemental," I said, chagrined.

"Ah! So you're a freelancer then," Mimi said. "I figured as much when you appeared from thin air."

La'a gently gathered my damaged hair into her hands before slowly brushing through it.

"It looks mostly fine," she said. Then she lifted a strand and examined it. "Ah. It was freshly cut."

I nodded.

"It used to be much longer. My teammate cut off the worst of the damage."

She examined another strand, then started massaging my scalp.

"The roots are perfectly healthy," she said. "You've been taking good care of your hair."

"I've been using an oil from the Freelancer bathhouse." I almost moaned from the scalp massage.

"Oh? Which one?"

"They said it was made from nymph hair."

"That's an excellent choice." La'a immediately nodded approvingly.

"A little expensive," Mimi added, "but absolutely worth the money."

La'a continued massaging me while Mimi opened several bottles, smelling each one before selecting two pale green potions.

"You are lucky your hair is healthy," she explained. "It means we can restore it more easily."

"What style were you hoping for?" La'a asked.

"I usually wear it long." I pointed to the middle of my back. "Either loose or in a ponytail."

"So no dramatic makeover."

"No." I laughed. "I just want it long and healthy again."

"That we can do."

While La'a resumed carefully brushing every strand, Mimi began applying one of the potions to the damaged ends.

A pleasant warmth spread through my hair.

"It tingles."

"That's normal."

"What exactly does it do?"

"It softens the burnt fibers enough that we can trim them cleanly."

A second potion was applied to my scalp.

"This one encourages growth," explained Mimi.

Over the next hour, the appointment somehow evolved into something much larger.

While my hair was soaking in magical liquid, Mimi convinced me to let her care for my nails. Spear fighting had done quite a number on them, and my hands needed a lot of care.

"I understand that, as a freelancer, you don't want to lose your calluses," said Mimi, "but you can't go around with chipped nails either."

La'a insisted on giving me a proper facial treatment after noticing how dry my skin had become from traveling through mountains, marshes, and sleeping near campfires.

"You don't wear makeup, do you?" she observed while gently applying moisturizer.

"I don't."

"I thought so." She smiled. "You don't need it. You're pretty as you are."

"And your skin is also healthy." She gently touched my cheek. "But a proper moisturizer would help keep it smooth."

"And it would reduce scarring," added Mimi.

"I'll take your word for it," I said.

"I'll even recommend something that suits your complexion," she said.

"Are you Kindred?" she asked.

"Does it matter?" I asked.

"A bit. It's recommended for Kindred people to switch formulas every once in a while so they don't adapt to the effects," explained La'a.

"Or worse, become dependent on them," added Mimi.

"I think I'm one-sixth Kindred," I said.

"You should be fine then."

They started making recommendations about everything. A mouthwash potion that cleaned teeth more effectively. A gentler body soap for someone who spent half her life camping outdoors. Multiple shampoos, each suited for different climates.

Even a different menstrual suppression potion.

"The one you're using completely blocks your cycle," La'a explained.

"It works." I shrugged.

"It does," she nodded, "but there are better alchemical mixtures now."

She opened a cabinet and showed me a small bottle.

"This one stops the bleeding and the cramps while allowing the rest of your body's cycle to continue normally."

"It is less stressful on the body in the long term," added Mimi.

"That actually sounds better," I said.

"You have to be careful, though," La'a said, shaking the small bottle. "Unlike the one you're taking, this one doesn't prevent pregnancy. It just stops the bleeding."

"No worries there," I chuckled in embarrassment, my cheeks turning slightly red. "I'm not sexually active."

"Why not?" asked Mimi. "You're so pretty it would be easy for you to find someone."

"You aren't Holy, are you?" asked La'a.

"No, I'm not." I shook my head. "Just busy studying, that's all."

By the time they finished, nearly three appointments' worth of time had passed.

My hair once again reached just below my shoulders, soft and healthy, although still a little shorter than I preferred. Still, it was the experts' recommendation that I not magically grow it too much. My nails looked neat for probably the first time since arriving in this world. My face felt impossibly clean, and they had even talked me into removing my body hair with painless alchemical wax.

I looked into the mirror.

"...Okay." I smiled. "I really like this."

"We're glad," Mimi said proudly.

La'a handed me a neatly written shopping list containing every product they had recommended. Apparently, they received a commission if they sent in new clients.

"You don't need everything," said Mimi.

"But they're worth considering," finished La'a.

I folded the paper and slipped it into my Bag of Holding.

"I'll probably end up buying most of it." I shrugged. "It's not like I'm worried about money."

When it came time to pay, the girls gave me an invoice for twenty silver, mostly because of all the magical potions they had used.

"You can keep the change," I said as I placed a full gold coin on the counter.

Both women stared.

"...Miss."

"That's too much."

"Don't worry about it," I said. "You girls have been so sweet."

"Plus," I added, "you fixed my hair."

Mimi looked at La'a before slowly accepting the coin.

"Thank you."

"So..." La'a asked hopefully.

"Would you like to book another appointment?"

I thought about it for a moment.

"How often should someone with long hair come in?"

"Every couple of months."

"Then..." I smiled. "Let's make it two months."

La'a happily opened her appointment book.

"I think we can arrange that."
