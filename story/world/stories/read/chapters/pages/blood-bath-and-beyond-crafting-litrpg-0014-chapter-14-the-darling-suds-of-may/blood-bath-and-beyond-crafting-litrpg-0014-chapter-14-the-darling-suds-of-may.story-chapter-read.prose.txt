To make proper, good quality soap I needed supplies, scales, running water…but I was fairly sure I could make a very rustic soap with what I had available. The main ingredients would be optimism, confidence, tallow, and lye… with a sprinkling of mania. It would not be fancy, but it should lather. Most importantly, it should clean. I should be able to trade it for things in town too, especially if it was magical!

I got out my trusty-done-nothing-to-arouse-my-suspicion-yet-bucket and filled it with the pieces of fatty deer that I had saved. The bucket went over the low coals of the fire to render down. This was a slow process, but one that needed me to keep an eye on it. So, I watched and daydreamed.

My magical soap will be amazing, everyone will love it. It will be MAGICAL!! I will trade it for potatoes, cooking implements, clothes! Chocolate. Do they have chocolate in this world? I really hope so. Foooood. Ooh, a blanket. Maybe a mattress. A house with a roof! Okay probably getting ahead of myself there.

I eyed the bubbling fat.

All my magic so far had been spells. This felt like it would be a little different. Was it enough that I was a [Witch]? Would the fact that I was a [Witch] make any soap I made inherently magical? Or did the ingredients need to be magical? Were there words I needed to say?

And what magic could I do with it? A healing soap was what I needed, I thought ruefully, looking down at my battered body. Was it possible? The way my stat sheet was phrased made me think I had to either invent or discover recipes. Well, I had some ideas there.

While the fat rendered down, I set about gathering herbs that had the healing tag: devil’s flight, comfrey, some elderberry flowers. As I worked, outside, I heard the enraged roar of the unicorn. Who or what it was attacking now? It would be interesting to see what soap made from insane unicorns did. Would it scrub away dementia? Maybe it would make people crazy? Or give non-virgins a rash?

I chortled to myself for a bit, poking at the gently bubbling fat.

The deer fat had started to smell gross (and also kinda tasty), and I saw it was ready - brown crackling floating in a bath of clear liquid. I strained everything through some rags I had boiled earlier and set it aside to cool.

Voila! I had made tallow.

[Deer tallow. Edible. Quality: poor.]

“Well fuck you too,” I muttered. “Like to see you do better without even a ladle.”

No particular attributes, which was a shame. Never mind, it was tallow. Tallow was what I needed. Now I needed lye. Sodium hydroxide.

The basic process of saponification occurred when lye was added to fat, anything else was an extra step to give it properties in additional to cleaning. I had, on Earth, made soap with activated charcoal to cleanse people’s pores (this came out an attractive dark grey), beer to nourish hair (smelt like hops after the alcohol had burned off), oatmeal to exfoliate skin (it worked but looked gross, like cold lumpy porridge) etc. I had a feeling here my additives had the potential to be much more exciting.

There was also scent to consider, although the scent of this soap was likely going to turn out as ‘meaty fatty smell’ no matter what I added to it. Well, that was just leaving room for improvement!

Now, how shall I go about incorporating the healing herbs into the soap? They would go brown if I just added them on top, although, aesthetics were not going to win here. No matter what I did these would be crude bars. Oh, I know! I would make the lye from their ashes - that way the essence of the plant was baked into the soap’s chemistry. And I could sprinkle some on top as well.

I poked at my wood ashes, separating out the big chunks from the fluff. Lye needed to be treated with respect. It was extremely alkaline. Normally I would use safety gear to handle it. If I got it on my skin it would interact with my natural oils and do its darned best to make me into soap. I would rather not be human soap, so I would do my best not to let that happen. I had enough injuries to be going on with I don’t need to add lye burns to the list.

As I worked, I pondered the potential use of lye as a weapon, but decided, at the moment, I was more likely to injure myself with it than anyone else. It might come in handy if my ruin was besieged but until then, I would keep it for soap.

Working as carefully as I could, I burnt the healing herbs to ash and combined them with white wood ash from the fire. After scrubbing out the fatty bucket, I set the ashes inside with some water to rest.

The air was growing colder, so I built up the fire once more, and took a nap while the shadows crept across the ruin, and the moons spun overhead.

When I woke the lye was ready.

I found a fat stick to use as a paddle, and carefully, oh so carefully added some of the lye to the heated fat (eyeballing the amount since I didn’t have access to anything remotely resembling a scale).

If I had got the quantity of lye massively wrong, I would end up with a nasty lye slurry with bits of fat floating in it. I poured, and mixed and watched the batter with an eagle eye for signs it was heating too fast. Oh, how I missed electricity. Having to do this without an electric blender was hard work, even with extra muscle.

A few sweaty, hot, intense, frazzled hours later, and my first batch of soap was ready! Dum, dum dummmmm!

It looked okay. Was it okay? I rolled it into fat little balls and set them on a slab to cure.

They were, as expected, highly unattractive. A bit too caustic, from the redness of my hands, although the PH would come down as they cured (I hoped). They were a little soft. They were rather wonky. Some of the rust had come off the bucket (which was now scoured clean) and was embedded within. I managed to poke those bits off with another stick.

But was it magical?

[Soap, cleansing. Quality: very poor.]

No. No it was not.

I stared at it in frustration, swallowing a scream. Making soap in the woods was a fucking achievement! It was amazing that I had made it at all! GAH! I was a [Soap Witch], damnit! Where was my notification? Where was my XP!

My screams bounced off the walls, and somewhere out in the woods some birds took flight.

A pair of concerned stony faces peeked over the roof edge.

“It’s fine,” I said, with a weary wave. “I’m fine. Just a…technical hitch.” The faces retracted without comment. I glared at my soaps. ‘Very poor’ quality - fair enough, but it was just… soap. What was I doing wrong?

“This sucks,” I said. “How the hell am I supposed to know what to do?”

[Oh, if only you had started in a nice castle specifically kitted out for your exact archetype! But oh nooooo we couldn’t be having that could we! We didn’t fancy it! We didn’t want to be evil! Oh no! Boo hoo. Well, you can’t say I didn’t try to convince you not to switch your class, you know.]

“You mean the castle full of abominations and under siege by a horde of angry peasants and bloodthirsty knights? That castle?”

The silence was huffy.

“Come on, give me a hint,” I said, bringing my fist down on top of one my squishy, imperfect horribly unmagical soaps, squashing it flat.

[I am afraid I cannot ‘give you a hint’. All I can advise is that you seek information.]

“Fine,” I said. “Fine. I’ll do that.”

I breathed deeply for a few minutes then climbed up to the roof.

“Information, my ass,” I said, plonking myself down between the gargoyles.

Two sets of eyeballs swivelled towards me.

“So, I made soap,” I said, conversationally, as if they hadn’t heard me screaming moments earlier. I brandished a soap at them. It looked even worse up here. They looked at it. I looked at it. “It’s not magical. Do you have an idea what I should do? I assumed there would be some spell or something, I don’t know. Perhaps I need to seek out magical ingredients?”

“No mana,” said Eggs, sniffing the soap.

“Yeah, all my spells have a mana cost,” I said. “But I don’t have a soap-making spell. And I think if there was one, I would have got it while I was making this?” I had half been expecting the notification. I had checked my stat sheet there was no information there. It just said soaps, recipes pending.

“No. Put mana … in soap,” said Eggs. “To make—Fffffff.” He made a grating, keening noise through his lips.

“Excuse him, I will interpret,” said Wistful. “Our compatriot is struggling with the simplicity of your language and his current human speech level.” He tilted his head fractionally to one side. There followed a series of barely audible noises that sounded to all intents and purposes like random low-pitched whistles and clicks from both stone creatures.

It was bizarre and rather fascinating to witness. Their language flowed like breath through rock, the slow shifting of stones, or the creak of a house settling. All of it done with extreme economy of movement. I listened hard trying to make sense of the sounds, but it was too alien.

“Our compatriot says you need to gather the mana and infuse the products of your witching with it. He says; do you understand that mana is energy, that it cannot be destroyed but only moved from place to place? Place it from within you, into the soap.”

“I did not know that.” It sounded simple enough, and I had exactly zero idea how to go about the process. Perhaps my confusion showed on my face, because Eggs made another series of whistles and clicks.

“He says do you have a mana manipulation skill?”

“I do not.”

“Do you have a mana perception skill?”

“No.”

“Start… there,” said Eggs.

“Mana perception?”

“You must be able to sense it before you can move it,” said Wistful.

That made sense. “Okay. Um how?”

“Meditate,” said Wistful.

“Watchff,” said Eggs.

“Watch what?”

“Everything.”

“Yourself. The world passing by. We are all one,” said Wistful, dreamily.

“Er. Okay,” I sat and crossed my tired legs. I had tried this a few times, usually with guided stuff off YouTube, although I’d never done it seriously. I scratched at my arm as I settled, the scabs were really itchy. My back ached.

“Still,” hissed Wistful.

I did my best not to move.

Another itch pulled at my attention until it became unbearable and I squirmed. The gargoyles’ stony gaze bored into my soul. “Sorry, sorry.”

They were doing their best to be supportive, but I could see they found my fidgety, warm-blooded body mystifying and not a little annoying. I sighed and did my best to settle again, breathing in and out slowly. My thoughts raced, demanding to be thought. What if I couldn’t do this? Why was the roof so uncomfortable? What if I never ate a potato again? What if the toad burst out of the cellar and attacked me in the night? What if, what if—

I tried again. And again.

My butt hurt.

I got up, ran around the ruin, had a wash, then came back and tried again.

In the end, I paid attention to each ache and pain, categorising them, before moving on to the next. That seemed to work better, and after a while, amazingly, I ran out of pains. My thoughts quietened, but my heart beat loud in my ears. The wind whistled. The unicorn screamed, far, far away.

The gargoyles made it look so effortless.

I pretended I was made out of stone too, patient and heavy, listening to each sound, watching the play of light through the leaves. After a while, my breathing slowed.

Then I fell asleep and awoke with a disgruntled snort as I slipped down the roof.

“Patience,” said Wistful. “Years it might take. It will come.”

I swallowed a retort and took my hot blooded, impatient body down to the ruin for something to eat. Then I tried some more. Nothing worth doing was easy or some bullshit. Hopefully this training montage would be over soon.

“What exactly am I looking for?” I said, after another hour or so.

“Mana!” said Eggs, helpfully. “Eggs!” This last was said with a little sadness.

I fed him an egg. “But what does the mana look like? What does it feel like?”

They both looked at me in surprise.

“Inffide. Energy,” said Eggs.

“It is you,” said Wistful.

With a sigh, I sat again. And again, the next day, and the day after that.

In a way it was a relief. My body was tired, it was time to let it rest, while my mind did something. I settled into a routine, cooking, [Foraging], hunting rabbits (I didn’t catch any, but not for lack of trying), gathering firewood, and meditating with my stony teachers every chance I got.

Several days later, I felt something. A twinge. A faint hint of something within me, something I had never noticed before, or something new? It stirred. The barest hint … something calling me in the darkness.

The notification, when it came, was subdued, lingering across my vision in misted blue:

[You have acquired the skill: Mana Perception lvl 1!

The elusive sixth sense.

Congratulations, Witch, you have taken the first step on the road to mana mastery!

You are no longer quite as blind as a new born babe, and have declared yourself a player in this game of energy and might. No longer an empty vessel waiting to be filled, you aim to take an active part in the shaping of your own destiny.

Good luck.]

Yes! Finally! I was getting somewhere!

Grabbing my soap, I held it up in front of me, carefully, carefully. Once more I sank into my meditation, easier this time, now I knew what I was looking for. Once I found it, I reached for the tingle of mana within, carefully, slowly, and shoved it into the soap with all my will. Or I tried to shove it into the soap. It was hard, like grasping at slippery cobwebs with hands I couldn’t see or control properly.

Absolutely nothing happened.

“Levelffs,” said Eggs, and I stuck my tongue out at him.

Meditation school continued, and days passed.

I caught my first rabbit after switching my base bonus to dexterity and was happy with the change of diet.

The second level came, after an obscene amount of practice. I worked on my soap, again nothing. After the third advancement, unexpectedly, I succeeded kinda. Holding my fat, and slightly less soft ball of soap in my hand, I felt that now familiar mana drain that I associated with casting a spell, only this time, I could tell the mana was pouring directly into the soap.

Which exploded with a wet splat, showering me, and the rooftiles with suds.

[You have acquired the skill: Mana Manipulation lvl 1!]

“Huh.”
