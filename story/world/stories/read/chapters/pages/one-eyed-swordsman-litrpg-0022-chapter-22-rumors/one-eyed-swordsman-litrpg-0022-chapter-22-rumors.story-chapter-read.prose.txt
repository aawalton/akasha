There wasn't much left for Oswald to do in Volstarn. His quest was finished, and thanks to Selene, his stay at the Hollow Oak Inn was covered. Lodging wasn’t a concern, and neither was food. For all intents and purposes, he could spend the next few days slacking off, waiting for Selene to return.

If this town weren’t such a mess, maybe I’d do just that. But Volstarn wasn’t in a good place.

Sickness constantly hung in the air. Many doors and windows remained shut, even in the afternoon when folks ought to be out tending to their business.

Since leaving Doctor Cecilia's house, Oswald had been asking around and keeping his ears open. He wanted to figure out what was really going on in Volstarn and why folks were constantly getting sick, and if there was more to that so-called “ghoul” he’d seen lurking in the forest. "Somethin’ ain’t right in this town, and ‘til Selene gets back, I might as well see what I can dig up."

For now, Oswald decided to make his way toward the market. The few people he passed on the way barely looked up.

The market square unfolded slowly as he approached, and with each step, a sinking feeling settled deeper in his chest. Half the stalls were abandoned. Wooden carts with their canvas covers pulled halfway down sagged under the weight of disuse. A few crates lay overturned, their contents long gone or left to spoil. What little business remained trudged on in quiet misery. Looks more like a ghost town than a market...

He approached a woman behind a lopsided produce stall. Stringy gray hair clung to her cheeks as she brushed it aside with a dirt-smudged hand.

"You buyin' or just lookin', lad?" she asked.

Oswald stepped closer, keeping his tone easy. "Just askin’, what’s got folks holed up like this? Ain’t normal, seein’ a market this empty."

"Ain't nothin’ normal 'bout Volstarn no more. Curse done took this place."

Oswald tilted his head, squinting slightly at the woman. Curse? That wasn’t exactly the answer he expected. "What kinda curse you think’s takin’ over Volstarn?"

"Don’t need to think it, lad. I know. That damned doctor brought it with her." The woman said, bitterness seeping through to her tone.

"Doctor Cecilia?"

"Yeah, her. She showed up, all high and mighty, talkin’ about treatments and cures. But the truth is, we barely had sick folk ‘round here before she came."

"Wait, far as I can tell, she’s been doin’ everything she can to help folks. And actually, she looks more tired than half the folks she treats."

The woman snorted, pushing a wilted cabbage aside with the back of her hand. "You ever think it’s because of her they’re all sick in the first place? Town was quiet before. Now look at it. Doors locked, kids coughin’ in their sleep, and good men droppin’ like flies."

"Then you think she brought the sickness?"

"Lad, ever since she arrived, this place ain't been the same. She talks about medicine, but maybe it’s curses she’s peddlin’. Either way, I don’t trust her."

"Alright... but if you do get sick, then what? You just gonna lay down and wait for it to take you?"

"Course not. I’ll go to the cave."

"The cave?"

"Yeah, heard rumors. Folk go there, and some of ‘em come back healthy as ever. No coughin’, no fever, no nothin’."

"And what cave you talkin’ about exactly? Must be someplace special."

"A place deep in the woods to the south. Nobody talks about it too loud. But it’s where people go when they’re done trustin’ the doctor. Place is said to heal any ailment or disease."

Oswald squinted at her, unconvinced. "You actually believe that?"

The woman met his stare without flinching. "I’ve seen it. Some folks I know went. Looked half-dead one day, walkin’ upright the next. Somethin powerful is in that cave."

"Sure, but you said only some people come back. What about the rest?"

The woman’s lips thinned. She looked away, as if even saying it might summon something unwanted. "They say... whatever’s in there can tell what kinda life you’ve lived. If you’ve done wrong, or if your heart ain’t clean, it won’t heal you. It’ll kill you. Right then and there."

Oswald blinked. That’s a load if I’ve ever heard one... He rubbed the back of his head. "Gotta be honest, sounds like a fairytale. A cave that cures sickness but only if you’re pure of heart? Kinda hard to believe."

"Then don’t believe me. Go talk to Merrick if you’re so eager for the truth. He’s the one takin’ people out there. Been runnin’ that little service for months now. Ask him how many come back."

"Merrick? He the one who owns the cave?" Oswald asked, brow furrowed.

The woman shook her head. "Name’s Merrick Vaingelt. He doesn't own it—just the one who leads people there and back, when they’re desperate enough to try."

"And where can I find this guy?"

"South end of town, most likely. He’s usually crawlin’ ‘round whatever cheap bar’ll have him. Look for the loudest voice and the emptiest mug."

"What's he look like?"

"The man's got a scar 'round his cheek, also likes wearin' tacky coats that are well past their time."

"Hmm, thanks. Maybe I’ll see him then."

"Good, and maybe you’ll stop wastin’ my time, too," the woman muttered, turning back to her stall.

Oswald didn’t say another word. Just turned and walked off, hands stuffed in his pockets. Volstarn’s fallin’ to pieces, and now there’s talk of some miracle cave hidin’ out in the woods? Either somethin’ was real wrong with this place… or the folks here had already lost their minds.

***

It took a bit of snooping, but it hadn’t been too hard to track Merrick down. Folks talked if you listened the right way. Eventually, all the hints pointed to a rundown bar near the south edge of town, located between a shuttered blacksmith’s shop and a half-collapsed stable.

Oswald stepped up to the door. The metal handle felt sticky under his hand. He grimaced and pushed it open.

A groan of old hinges cut through the muffled hum inside. A dozen or so patrons occupied the room, scattered and slouched in uneven clusters. Most hunched over their drinks like they were guarding secrets at the bottom of their mugs. Shoulders tight. Heads low.

Some heads turned as Oswald stepped inside. Their eyes lifted slowly, dull with drink and shadow, sweeping over him from boots to brow. Oh?

But none of them moved. No words, no threats. Just the quiet calculation of tired men deciding he wasn’t worth the energy. One by one, they turned back to their drinks.

Oswald stepped further into the haze, tapping against floorboards sticky with spilled ale and time. His gaze cut across the room, no need to guess who he was looking for.

Merrick Vaingelt made himself easy to find. He sat near the back, draped over a rickety table like he owned the place.

One boot was kicked up on the chair beside him, the other resting flat on the floor. A long, shallow scar sliced down his left cheek, disappearing into an unshaven stubble that covered his jaw. His coat might’ve been fine once, but now it sagged on his frame like a shed skin, frayed at the cuffs and stained at the collar.

He swirled a mug of ale lazily in one hand, the liquid catching faint candlelight as it sloshed in slow circles. His eyes met Oswald’s just as the last ripple settled.

Then he smirked. "You lost, boy?"

Oswald kept his pace steady and walked right up, dropping into the empty chair across from him. If this guy’s the guide, better not let him think I’m easy to shake.

"I hear you’re the one to talk to if I wanna find the cave."

"That so?" he said, setting the mug down with a soft clack. "An’ what’s a young pup like you want with a place like that?"

"Just need to find it," Oswald said.

"Ain’t much of a reason, that. Folk who go lookin’ for that cave don’t do it ‘cause they wanna, they do it ‘cause they gotta. And you? You don’t look like you’re dyin’."

"I don’t need to be dyin’ to go there."

Merrick tapped a finger against his mug, considering. "Now, see, I don’t take folk up there for fun. That road ain’t friendly, an’ the place itself? Even worse. So if I’m riskin’ my hide, I expect to get real comfortable for my troubles."

Oswald already knew where this was going. "How much?"

Merrick grinned. "How much ya got?"

"How's a silver sound?"

"Boy, you think I was born yesterday? Cough up twenty silver, then maybe we’ll talk."

Oswald didn’t so much as blink. Merrick’s smug little grin might’ve worked on some poor fool who didn’t know better, but it didn’t move him an inch. The bastard sat there looking like he thought he held all the cards, leaned back, boots up, acting like the room belonged to him. No way I’m payin’ this guy. Not a damn chance.

"I ain't here to play games, take me to the cave." Oswald said.

Merric swirled the dregs of ale in his mug, sloshing the liquid like it was part of some performance. "Boy, I ain’t doin’ nothin’ for free. So unless you got coin to line my pockets, you can fuck right off."

A long breath hissed through Oswald’s nose, barely audible beneath the quiet rumble of bar chatter that had started to dip around them. He didn’t move right away. Just stared, letting the moment stretch until it felt like a drawn bowstring.

"I ain’t askin’ again, you’re takin’ me there. Or else."

"Or else what?"

"This."

Oswald's fist shot up from beneath the table. Knuckles cracked against the underside of Merrick’s jaw with a sickening crunch, the force of the blow snapping the man’s head back violently.

Merrick’s chair screeched across the floor, scraping wood before tipping backward. It struck the ground with a heavy crash that rattled the nearby tables and knocked a half-empty bottle of ale off a ledge, shattering it against the floor. A few startled gasps rose from the other patrons, chairs creaking as people leaned away or instinctively shielded their drinks.

The table between them rocked from the motion, Merrick’s mug tumbling across its surface before hitting the edge and spilling its contents. Ale splattered across the floorboards and dripped off the table like runoff from a gutter, soaking into the layers of grime already staining the floor.

In a split second, Merrick hit the floor with a heavy grunt, one arm sprawled across the overturned chair, the other grasping at his jaw. His boots twitched as he laid on the floor, blinking up at the ceiling.
