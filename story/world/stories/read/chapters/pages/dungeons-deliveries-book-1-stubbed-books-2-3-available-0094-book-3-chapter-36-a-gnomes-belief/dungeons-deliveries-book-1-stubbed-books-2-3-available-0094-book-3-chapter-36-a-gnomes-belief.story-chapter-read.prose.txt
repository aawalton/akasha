The Blightwood was full of barren life disconnected from Gaia. Petal sensed the horrid mutations all around him as he laid devastation upon the affront. It disgusted him. The System and Gaia provided everything one needed. To malform creation and all its variety into this was the antithesis of both him and his people. Be and do what you will, he believed. Existence came in infinite forms, and Petal was not one to judge any. But this was a mockery of a mind that hated life.

He spotted a shambling monster attempting to surprise him from behind a gigantic pine tree that had been combined with slime mold, his [Ponder] skill notifying him that the enemy was a [Cancerous Crandel]. The forest was vast, and so much had been ruined. Petal reached for Gaia, hopped over a snaking vine coming to entrap him, and propelled himself with a mighty jump off a flat stone, headfirst directly at the tree.

Gaia smiled upon him from below, reaching through the dark sore of the Blightwood, and solidified the thread of her bond with Petal a little more. Without his aide or bravery to charge into the land, it was nigh impossible to fight back against the plague that was taking hold.

Petal spotted a bulbous eye peer around the tree. Then he extended his garden trowel and blasted through the rotted bark itself.

His trowel plunged into the monster’s warbly skull a second later. Twisting his wrist as if hooking a fish, Petal Gravewhistle held onto the handle tight as his body came out the other side of what had once been a tree.

Petal yanked the surprised monster by its head to the ground. It crashed, flailing its many rotted limbs to find him. One did, striking him in powerfully in his ribs, but Petal had been infusing Gaia over many battles. He was no longer only hollow porcelain.

He ducked another swipe with ease, tearing the trowel out of its skull and swinging it down with a blaring bellow. Gaia pressed some of her strength through her thin tendril connection.

The monster stopped thrashing as its head and body were separated in a single swing. Essence from the kill flowed into Petal’s core and trickled into Gaia.

With the rest of the gnomes fighting gloriously behind him and no time to waste, Petal stepped up to the towering pine and laid a hand upon it.

Salvageable? He asked through the thread hopefully.

She didn’t communicate in complete sentences or words, but even on the disconnected land, her presence sung through to Petal. Her songlike judgement came in a flitting wave.

Petal flared [Verdant Smite] as he swung the trowel like a wood axe. Green energy shaped like creeping vines suffused his arm and weapon.

His chop cut through the entire trunk with ease, [Verdant Smite] extending the force out in an arc that coated the area in the same luminous green. Planting his feet, Petal grunted as he pushed over the abomination of a tree.

Petal didn’t watch it fall, opting instead to turn back toward the dark depths of the Blightwood. Gaia laughed in her singsong way as the impact shook the ground, rejoicing in the progress of the gnome’s attack.

Shy by nature, it had taken a mountain of trust on her part to connect as she had. Anyone could find her if they looked properly, but a true bond was difficult and dangerous for her, and the gnomes, for all their love, held a faith that scared her. They believed her capable of so much, while she viewed herself as young and inexperienced. She had only come to sentience twenty odd years ago, after all.

So, she’d started the full process with the one that tried to understand her as much as he loved her, first granting him [Rite of Rootbound Chorus], and now hoping to empower herself from his efforts. The Ritual had gone perfectly. Its strength was the greatest performed yet. She just worried about the result and hid her fear from Petal. The Eternity Seed was a great promise, and she had more planned for him yet.

And like a scab, worry itched its way from the influence of a terrible user of the System, through the Blightwood, and prickled the thread connection of Petal to her.

The gnomes will enjoy bringing this forest back to you, Petal said to her as he kneeled and greeted the newfound growth around his feet.

So far, the monsters he’d fought had been the Beast of Blightwood’s weakest. He’d carved a zigzag path, verdant smiting the barren words and destroying the bodies of monsters. The five companies of gnomes behind took a slower approach, tactfully marching under the orders of Marzipan and surprising leader Dandelion to fell a wide path of the rotten crop.

Petal rolled his shoulders. His body was strong, infused with metal and mud, but it was beginning to bear permanent damage. Surely, the monsters deeper in were mightier. The question wasn’t if he’d fall under their attack, the question was how far he could bring Gaia and the Soil Mind along.

He took off, leaving the green patch behind, ready to unleash the full might of his [Verdant Smite].

Petal now knew he was not alone in his charge. Gaia was with him, and she feared that she’d sent her favorite gnome to die for nothing. She felt her worry mount to fear and could barely hold back telling him to retreat immediately. The bond between them had strengthened enough, however, and she wasn’t able to hide her worry fully.

As Petal spotted a cluster of monsters ahead, these ones not hiding and appearing to be waiting for his arrival, he sent her another word.

I am not dying for nothing, Petal laughed as he passed the thought. The Eternity Seed will not fail. And if it did, I’ve brought your beauty back where it belongs. That is something worth dying for. Now trust yourself as you have trusted me, and do not believe your thoughts in the Blightwood.

Gaia was too stunned to respond. She was vast, encompassing all things that grew without sentient thought, and here he was teaching her a lesson. She went to formulate a song, but Petal spoke again.

Allow me to remind you of the might of life, Petal announced to her, leaping fearlessly into the clump of monsters. She felt him pulling on her strength, and rather than restrict the flow in fear, she opened the spring willingly.

Gaia felt herself carried along Petal’s mood as he landed in the middle of a mess of mutated monsters and stood respectfully. There was more than a dozen, all towering, horrid, and formed both of Dungeon monster and forest animal. Even they were surprised by his casual entrance.

“Followers of the Beast of Blightwood, I greet you.” Petal said as he turned to regard each of their faces, or what passed for faces. “I am Petal Gravewhistle, Steward of Sun and Shade, Father of The Seven Stirred upon Thumb Rock, former Ritualist of the Allen Gardens Gnomes, and tonight, I name myself Arbiter of Verdant Land.”

Gaia snorted a melody at Petal for the ridiculous name he’d given himself.

Petal suppressed a grin and continued. “I see that my arrival has not brought your immediate ire. Your intelligence is noted. Perhaps it will be lauded. Might we speak on your and the Beast of Blightwood’s surrender?”

The monsters looked at each other, surprised that Petal was speaking rather than striking. Petal planted the tip of his trowel into the ground, activating a miniscule [Verdant Smite], and leaned casually onto it.

Then the monsters started arguing in a growling, grunting speech. The spies and more studious of the gnomes had tried to decipher the language to no success. It seemed to be a combination of snake, bear, and bat. Limbs made of bone and mould gestured angrily as the monsters barked at one another, gesturing to themselves, Petal, and also to the same direction deeper in the woods.

Guess that is where the Blightwood Dungeon is, Petal noted to Gaia. While they argued, he took the opportunity to press [Verdant Smite] roots beneath the circle of debate, trying to appear as if he was casually waiting for their decision.

Finally, one of the largest monsters that seemed to be a combination of deer and boulder roared, silencing the rest. It turned its head with eyes he recognized as intelligent. Petal kept his face calm, despite wanting to bellow a laugh and vomit at the same time.

“You know of the control Boss’s have on monsters?” It asked.

“That Bosses can make their monsters do as they say?” Petal responded. “Of course. But the enforcement extends in the Dungeon itself. Out here, you are can act of your own free will if you press back. Yet you still attack us without mercy, attempting to instill primal fear. You have filled gnomes, and try to breach our territory without end.”

“Rah!” The monster yelled. “He can destroy us with a thought! Unmake us! It's a control over our pitiful lives! If we do not do as he says, then we are as good as dead.”

Petal feigned a sad nod as his extension of [Verdant Smite] completed. He’d expected that response, and truthfully, he’d had quite enough of it. It was cowardly way to justify things.

“But then you've already died. You are nothing but tools.” He said, looking around the monsters while keeping a frown on with great difficulty.

The monsters laughed then, all together, the deer-boulder one speaking once more.

“You think we fear your attack? That you have a chance?” It laughed. “You have much to learn, gnome. Of death and hate. Tools we might be, but stronger than your tiny bodies and minds. You know nothing of what tools are available. What she is capable of. With her help, the Beast of Blightwood is unstoppable. She fills our Boss with with her power. And she is hungry for more.”

Petal clenched his jaw. The Beast of Blightwood and his Dungeon would be difficult enough, and he did not relish in the fact that their greatest enemy was emboldened further by Nina’s sister. What kind of power could influence a Boss like that?

“You understand hunger, then. Perfect.” Petal said. “We gnomes have no need to eat, though we enjoy food. Our—"

“Be quiet, gnome. Enough of your blithering.” The monsters assembled themselves in their fighting stances. “There is nothing worthwhile for you to say.”

The monsters charged together, coming to tear him apart. Petal didn’t panic, instead leaning further onto his planted trowel and activating his prepared [Verdant Smite].

Thick vines whipped up from the ground and lassoed themselves around the monsters. They were stopped as if clotheslined, the plant matter thickening as fresh leaves unfurled with Petal feeding more essence into the skill. He was thankful for Gaia’s involvement, limited as she was her connection to him.

He cast his hand down, and the vines pulled back into the earth, bringing the monsters viciously along. More crawling plants sprouted out, lashing them to the ground and digging into their flesh. Quickly, the monsters were bound in his hungry green cast.

Petal stood in the center of the circle, looking around at the thrashing monsters and admiring his power. The plants he’d sprouted were already beginning their bloom. Gaia felt the connection between herself and her favourite gnome strengthen again.

“You interrupted me. That is quite rude. Do you not know that Gaia is here too? Now, you will listen.” Petal said to monsters and Blightwood as he pulled his trowel from the garden.

He began to walk toward the direction the monsters had been gesturing.

“We don’t care for food, though we enjoy it. We do not even care for power for power’s sake. Do not misunderstand us. Together we gnomes weave what you separate. Our true power comes from our insatiable hunger. Not for power or ego. And it is why we will never be killed out. We demand to exist, and will do anything it takes. Our deepest hunger is for life.”

He squeezed a fist, telling the plants he’d bloomed to return to Gaia. The vines ripped through the pinned monsters, pulping their bodies and misting the air in blood.

Petal jogged on, leaving behind a bloody green garden in the lifeless forest. Gaia fear retreated as the Ritual threaded somewhere unexpected. If Petal was steadfast in her belief, then she would be too. Her own belief struck against the Ritual.

And the song of Gaia took on a faster beat as the System sent her a notification.

Now do you trust yourself? Petal asked, feeling the connection between them thicken.
