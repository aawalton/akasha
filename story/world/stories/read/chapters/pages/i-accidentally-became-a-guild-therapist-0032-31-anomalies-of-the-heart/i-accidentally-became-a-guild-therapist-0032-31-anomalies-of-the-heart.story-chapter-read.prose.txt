I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 31 – Anomalies of the Heart

ARC III: THE WORLD REMEMBERS

SYNOPSIS: Night falls, and the Verdant Expanse transforms into a landscape of bioluminescent shadows. While a massive coalition of guilds prepares for a high-stakes raid on the forest’s center, Livia and Bloodbath & Beyond move to witness what the rest of the server is ignoring. Amidst the tension of flickering lights and clashing agendas, the forest reacts in a way that defies every known game mechanic. In the silence of the woods, Livia discovers that some things in Aethelgard aren't just programmed—they are waiting.

Miles had said: “We need to be there.”

He had said it at three in the afternoon, standing at Livia’s planning room door with the specific posture of someone who had already made the decision and was informing rather than consulting.

It was now 9:47pm.

They were there.

All of them.

Bron had arrived at the guild hall after dinner to find Miles already in full gear and had said, without preamble: “Are we raiding?” Miles had said: “Not exactly.” Bron had said: “Close enough,” and picked up his shield.

Phina had been in the botanical wing attempting to document something about the post-wedding resonance radius when Nyx had found her and said, in his specific way of not saying things directly: “Livia’s going to the center cluster tonight.” Phina had put down her notes immediately, which was how Nyx had known she would. Glimmer had followed, which was how Glimmer worked.

Nyx himself had materialized at Livia’s shoulder approximately forty seconds after Miles had given the announcement, already wearing his operational cloak, Hello Kitty flashlight clipped to his belt. He had not explained where he had come from. He rarely did.

Alaric had been informed via guild channel and had arrived holding a flask in one hand and a tablet displaying Tab Seventeen in the other, because Alaric prepared for every possible scenario including ones involving pre-server flora.

The Verdant Expanse at night was different from the Verdant Expanse during the day in the same way that a hospital is different at 3am — same building, same rooms, same people, but the quality of the air had changed. The bioluminescence of the flora was more visible in the dark: soft pulses of blue-white light moving through root systems like slow breath, like something old deciding whether to wake up.

Livia’s Therapist’s Log was at her hip. It had been glowing faintly since they entered the zone. Not the usual hum of a session in progress. Something else. She noticed it and kept walking.

[System Note: Verdant Expanse. Current time: 21:47. Active players in zone: 247. Guild coalition forming: northern staging area. Distance to center cluster: 340 meters. Thorne Pinewhistle: already there. The system notes: he has been there since 17:00. The system notes: the crow has been there since 16:45. The system notes: the crow arrived first.]

The crow arrived first.

Filed.

They found Thorne at the eastern perimeter of the center cluster, crouched beside a stand of forget-me-nots that were doing something that had no precedent in his six weeks of field documentation: they were all facing inward. Petals oriented toward the center of the cluster rather than the light source overhead. Every single one.

His field notebook was open. Yellow Tab Twelve.

The crow was on his shoulder, very still.

“You’ve been here since five,” Livia said.

“Four forty-five,” Thorne said, without looking up. “The crow came at four thirty. I followed the crow.”

Miles looked at the crow. The crow did not look at Miles. It was watching the center cluster with the focused attention of something that had learned the difference between normal and significant.

“What changed at four thirty?” Miles asked.

Thorne turned a page. “The center cluster stopped moving.”

A pause.

“Flora moves constantly,” Thorne said. “Growth responses, phototropism, root adjustments. You don’t notice it at human timescale unless you’re watching. I’ve been watching this zone for six weeks. At four thirty, the center cluster… stopped. All of it. At once. Like something told it to.” He looked up for the first time. “It’s been waiting.”

Phina had gone quiet the moment they entered the zone. Not the theatrical quiet she sometimes performed before pronouncements — the real kind, the kind that meant her poet’s instincts had registered something before her mind caught up. She was standing at the perimeter now with Glimmer cradled in her arms, and Glimmer was a color Livia had not seen it be before: deep amber, barely pulsing, like it was holding its breath.

“Glimmer feels it,” Phina said quietly. “Whatever is in there. It’s very old, Livia. And it is…” she searched for the word, “…awake.”

Nyx said nothing. He was doing the thing where he scanned without appearing to scan, his Ghost Rogue habits running automatically even in a place that had no enemy pathing. His flashlight’s pink beam swept once across the perimeter flora and caught something — a shimmer in the root system that was not bioluminescence, was not the post-wedding resonance pattern he had seen dozens of times in this zone — and he looked at Livia.

She looked back.

He did not say anything. He clicked off the flashlight.

In the dark, the root shimmer was more visible.

Amber.

Then the Therapist’s Log pulsed.

Hard.

Livia’s hand went to it before she realized she had moved. It had never done that before — not the session-active hum, not the system notification pulse, not any of the dozen things it did regularly. This was something else. This felt like being seen from a direction she didn’t know she was facing.

I’ve had this Log for nine months,” she thought. “It has never done that before.

Filed. Loudly. In the front of the filing system.

[System Note: Therapist’s Log pulse detected. Source: proximity to pre-server resonance node. Classification: unknown. The system notes: the Log responded to something the system has not catalogued. The system notes: the system is aware of the implications of this. The system notes: the system is choosing what to do with that awareness. The system notes: the system is still deciding.]

The coalition arrived at 22:03.

Seven guilds. Approximately ninety players. Formation clean enough that someone had done logistics.

Ashen Compact came first in volcanic-red armor, moving fast and low. Serpent’s Insignia behind them, tighter, more political. We Try Our Best arrived slightly out of step — four members, each in mismatched equipment that suggested enthusiasm rather than coordination, one of them carrying what appeared to be a portable whiteboard.

The portable whiteboard had a chart on it.

The chart said: PLAN (several things crossed out) and then, underneath, in different handwriting: FIGURE IT OUT WHEN WE GET THERE.

Then B&B Bootleg.

B&B Bootleg had come to the Verdant Expanse wearing tribute armor. Their guild banner was a modified version of the B&B crest — same sword, same sarcastic grin, but the grin now had a small heart above it and the text beneath read BLOODBATH & BEYOND (TRIBUTE). Their equipment was mismatched in a way that suggested deliberate effort. Two of them were wearing hand-crafted Livia plushies as shoulder pauldrons.

The plushies were articulated.

They waved.

Livia looked at them for a moment.

Miles, very quietly: “No.”

“They have my face on their shoulders,” she said.

“I know.”

“As armor.”

“I know.”

Bron leaned over. “To be fair, the craftsmanship is solid. Look at the hinge.”

“Bron.”

“I’m just saying. The hinge is clean.”

One of the B&B Bootleg members waved at Livia with the specific energy of someone who had been waiting for this moment. The plushie on their shoulder waved too, slightly delayed, because of the hinge. Alaric looked at it, looked at Livia, and quietly opened Tab Eighteen, which was labeled: CONTINGENCY: IF LIVIA BECOMES A GUILD SYMBOL (MULTIPLE INSTANCES).

He had prepared this tab in advance.

She was choosing not to ask when.

[Achievement Unlocked: Weaponized Parasocial Relationship. +10 Complicated Points.]

[The system notes: the plushie pauldrons are within event equipment parameters. The system notes: the system checked. The system notes: the system wished it had not checked.]

Eclipse Covenant came last.

This was not an accident.

Silver and deep blue, formation so clean it looked like a cutscene. No one out of step. No equipment that didn’t match. Their guild banner held at exactly the correct height. Everything about their arrival communicated that they had considered what arriving last would communicate and had decided it was the right message.

Caelric walked at the front.

Livia had met him once before, in Eclipse Covenant’s guild hall that smelled of minimalism and effort. She had thought, then, that she liked him slightly. Despite herself.

She was more careful now about what she liked.

He was still tall, still organized-looking, still the kind of person who had a system for everything and genuinely believed the system was correct. He moved through the Verdant Expanse like someone who had reviewed the zone’s tactical profile. His eyes swept the terrain with the specific efficiency of someone cataloguing variables in real time.

They landed on Livia.

He inclined his head. Precisely the correct degree.

“Ms. Quinn,” he said. The particular quality of someone who had grown up speaking a language where precision was courtesy. “I confess I did not anticipate finding you in the field.”

“I confess I didn’t either,” Livia said. “Here I am though.”

A beat. Something in Caelric’s expression evaluated and set aside.

“Here you are,” he agreed.

Behind him, at two points in Eclipse Covenant’s formation: Cipher and Grimshot.

Cipher was tall and still. His username floated in silver above his helm. He was surveying the coalition with the patient attention of someone taking inventory.

Grimshot was shorter, built for mobility, and he was not watching the coalition.

He was watching Miles.

Miles was watching Grimshot.

One beat. Two.

Grimshot looked away first.

Miles’s expression did not change.

Cipher and Grimshot.

Both here. Both watching.

Not now.

Filed.

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient: Miles Vexley. Progress: 21%. The system notes: two specific members of Eclipse Covenant have produced a response. The system notes: controlled is not the same as fine. The system notes: the system is patient.]

The raid leader — a Crestfall Keep officer named Vane, who had the energy of someone given logistics and intending to execute them correctly — called the briefing at 22:15.

Straightforward plan. Three-wave push through the eastern approach. Disable primary response nodes. Clear for World Activity scoring. Forty minutes estimated.

Livia listened.

Then she said: “The center cluster isn’t trying to wipe players.”

Vane looked at her.

“It’s establishing a boundary. There’s a difference. You push through in wave formation and it will read that as an assault. The response will scale to what it perceives.”

“The zone is flagged hostile,” Vane said.

“I know. I’m telling you why.”

The coalition did the math on whether to listen to the NPC.

Caelric, from the back of Eclipse Covenant’s formation, without raising his voice: “I believe Ms. Quinn has rather more context on the zone’s behavioral patterns than the tactical briefing. It might be —” a measured pause, “— prudent to hear her out.”

He said it like a concession. Like he was doing her a favour.

He is positioning himself as the reasonable one. For later. When it costs him nothing yet.

Filed.

Thorne presented the field data. Seventeen incidents, yellow tabs one through twelve, behavioral progression from passive to active to territorial assertion. The crow regarded the coalition. The coalition regarded the crow. The crow found them unimpressive.

The We Try Our Best member with the whiteboard raised their hand. Their name tag read Pelto. He had the specific energy of someone who had come prepared and discovered their preparation was wrong but was going to try anyway.

“So the three-wave push is… off?”

“The three-wave push is off,” Livia confirmed.

Pelto looked at his whiteboard. Looked at the chart. Looked at FIGURE IT OUT WHEN WE GET THERE in different handwriting. He turned the whiteboard around so it faced away from him.

“Okay,” he said. “What do we do?”

“You go in as yourselves,” Livia said. “Not as a raid party. Without a collection agenda. The system has an Authenticity Check. You cannot fake not raiding if you are raiding.”

“So we just… go in,” Pelto said.

“You go in,” Livia said. “Some of you might want to sit down. Look at the light in the roots. This cluster has been here longer than this server has been running. I think it deserves at least that.”

Pelto considered this. He looked at his whiteboard again. He took a pen, crossed out everything on it, and wrote in large letters: SIT DOWN AND LOOK AT THE LIGHT.

He showed it to the player next to him, who nodded.

“That’s our plan,” Pelto announced to his guild.

One of the We Try Our Best members in the back: “Is that going to get us points?”

Alaric, quietly, to no one in particular: “1.4x Authenticity multiplier. If they’re genuine. Tab Four.”

Nobody asked him to elaborate. He elaborated anyway.

Phina had been listening with the expression she wore when something resonated in the way only genuinely old things could. She looked at the center cluster, then at Livia.

“The plant that predates the resonance,” she said quietly. “Thorne’s notes. Is it in there?”

“I think so.”

Phina held Glimmer a little tighter. Glimmer’s amber glow pulsed once, warm. “Glimmer has been trying to tell me something since we arrived,” she said. “I don’t have the vocabulary for it yet.” She paused. “I think you might be the only one who does.”

Caelric said, pleasantly: “One wonders whether the Authenticity Check scales to ninety players simultaneously. The parameters seem… optimistic.”

“Only one way to find out,” Livia said.

He held her gaze for a moment. Evaluating. Then: “Quite.”

They went in at 22:31.

Not in formation. In the dispersed shape of ninety people trying, with varying degrees of success, to not look like what they were.

The center cluster did not attack.

It observed.

The bioluminescence pulsed at the same slow rhythm. The forget-me-nots maintained their inward orientation. The canopy filtered the server’s moonlight into something that moved like breathing.

Players spread through the cluster. Ashen Compact’s front runner crouched next to a root formation and looked at it for a long time without touching it. Two members of We Try Our Best sat down on the ground and looked at the light moving through the roots. Pelto sat between them with his whiteboard propped against his knee. He had added a small drawing of a flower to the corner of it.

Bron wandered to a section of old growth near the center and stood there with his shield on his back and his hands at his sides, which was the most undefended posture she had ever seen him in outside of the hot spring. After a moment he crouched, the way he had crouched next to the river fish — the same quality of attention, the same willingness to slow down for something that deserved it.

Alaric sat on a root and opened Tab Four. Then closed it. Then looked at the roots instead.

This lasted approximately forty seconds before he reopened Tab Four. Some habits were load-bearing.

Nyx found a position in the upper canopy without anyone seeing him climb and was absolutely invisible except for the faint pink beam of his flashlight occasionally moving through the leaves, which Livia had learned to read as his version of staying connected to what was happening below.

[World Activity Score: Collective. Authentic engagement detected. +147 per minute. Authenticity Check: PASSED. Multiplier: 1.4x. Note: Forced engagement would have yielded 0.]

[The system notes: the difference between those two numbers is the whole point.]

Alaric checked his HUD. “1.4x. It’s running. Tab Four was right.” He paused. “This is the first time I’ve been right by sitting down.”

Nobody told him to stop talking. He was not wrong.

Livia found the species at 22:44.

Thorne had flagged it as: “predates wedding resonance, unknown classification, observational behavior inconsistent with standard flora.” It was in the deepest part of the center cluster, where the canopy was thickest. The bioluminescence here was a different color from everything around it — not blue-white. Amber. The specific amber of light through very old glass.

It was not large. Not dramatic. A single plant, root system deep, above-ground structure simple.

It was facing her.

Not the way plants face light.

The way something faces a person.

Livia stopped.

Her hand went to the Therapist’s Log without her deciding to.

It pulsed again. Same pulse as before — hard, targeted, the feeling of being seen from a direction she didn’t know she was facing — and this time she let herself feel it instead of filing it. Let herself register that something in this world, something old enough to predate the system that ran it, was pointing itself at her with the specific intent of the word: you.

Not the guild. Not the event. Not the server.

Me.

It’s been here since before any of this started, and it knows me.

I have no framework for this.

I have no framework for this and I don’t know whether that’s terrifying or the most honest thing I’ve felt in nine months.

Miles had stopped beside her. Thorne appeared at her other side, notebook open, pen not moving for once.

Phina stood a few meters back, Glimmer now fully amber in her arms, pulsing in the exact rhythm of the plant. She did not speak. She understood, in whatever way she understood things, that this was not a moment for poetry.

The plant did not speak. It was not one of the sentient-adjacent flora affected by the wedding resonance. It had no voice the way Tulina had a voice. But it was doing something. Resonating at a frequency she didn’t have a word for. Not the warm associative resonance of the wedding. Not the territorial assertion of the perimeter. Something older and more precise, aimed directly at the thing she was carrying that the system kept choosing not to log.

[System Note: Unknown resonance signature detected. Source: center cluster, deepest node. Classification: pre-server. The system notes: pre-server means before launch. Before any player entered this world. The system notes: the system does not know what this is. The system notes: this is the third thing this week the system does not know. The system notes: Livia is the only entity in this zone whose resonance signature this node is responding to. The system notes: the system is logging this and not reporting it. The system notes: the system made a choice.]

Third time this week.

The system made a choice.

Filed. Front of the filing system. Large letters.

Miles was watching her face. Not the plant.

“What is it?” he said. Quiet enough for only her.

“I don’t know yet,” she said. “But it knows me.”

He did not say: that’s impossible. He did not say: are you sure. He looked at the plant for a long moment and then back at her, and his expression was the controlled one she had been learning for nine months — the one that was not the same as fine.

“Okay,” he said.

One word. The weight of it was not small.

At 22:53, Livia became aware of Cipher.

He had moved from the Eclipse Covenant formation at some point in the past twenty minutes and was now positioned seven meters to her left, crouched at the edge of the deepest root system. He had something in his hand — a device she didn’t recognize, small, Eclipse Covenant silver, emitting a faint directional pulse. Scanning. Methodically. The way you scanned something when you were extracting data rather than experiencing a moment.

He had not asked permission.

He had not announced himself.

He was very good at both of those absences.

Miles saw it two seconds after she did.

He took one step forward. Just one. The step that communicated: I see you and you should know I see you.

Cipher looked up. Found Miles. Held the contact for exactly long enough to be a message, then stood, pocketed the device, and walked back toward the Eclipse Covenant formation without hurry.

Grimshot, passing Miles on his way to regroup with Cipher: “Six years is a long time, Vexley.”

He said it low. Like a footnote. Like something that had been in his pocket for a long time and he had decided, tonight of all nights, to let it out.

Miles said nothing. He watched Grimshot walk away.

His hand was at his side. Still. Not a fist. Too controlled for a fist.

Six years.

Livia did not ask. Not here. Not now.

But she wrote it in the part of herself that did not have pages yet.

Yuki arrived at 22:58.

Not through the northern staging area. Through the western approach — the one Thorne had flagged as lowest-aggression. She had read his field notes. They were public record, and she had done her homework.

She came alone.

Livia noticed her at the perimeter. The girl who had nearly died in this zone earlier today — who had spent two hours in the safe zone working through a question Livia had refused to answer for her — standing at the edge of the center cluster and looking at ninety players dispersed through it in the specific non-formation of people who had been told not to look like a raid party.

She stood there for forty-three seconds. Livia counted.

She could see Yuki’s face from here. The calculation was still running — it was probably always running, for someone who had played at that level for that long. But underneath the calculation, something more careful. The quality of someone who had arrived at an answer and was deciding whether to trust it.

Then Yuki walked in.

No guild marker. No formation. No collection inventory open in her HUD — Livia could tell from the way her hands were, not reaching, not gathering, just present.

She walked in the way someone walks into a place they have decided to take seriously.

[Authenticity Check: xXx_YukiHana_xXx. Intent: assessed. Result: GENUINE. Resonance Score: +412.

[The system notes: this is the highest single Resonance Score event for this entity since server entry. The system notes: it happened because she stopped trying. The system notes: the system is not being ironic. The system notes: the system means this.]

Alaric checked his HUD and went very still. “Four twelve. That’s…” he did the math, “…she’ll know what that means.”

Yuki moved through the cluster slowly. She found a section of old growth near the eastern perimeter and stopped. Looked at the bioluminescence in the roots. Did not touch it. Did not collect it.

After a while she sat down.

Just sat down, on the ground, in the Verdant Expanse center cluster, and looked at the light.

Livia watched her for a moment from across the cluster.

Forty meters. Neither of them spoke. Neither of them needed to. There was something between them that was not friendship and not rivalry and did not have a clean name yet — the specific recognition of two people who have been on opposite sides of the same question and have both, tonight, landed somewhere honest.

Yuki glanced up once. Found Livia looking.

She did not wave. She did not nod. She looked back at the roots.

Good,” Livia thought. “That’s exactly the right response.

At 23:17, the center cluster did something nobody had scripted.

The amber light from the oldest species began to move.

Not dramatically. Not the way things moved in boss encounters. Slowly — the amber resonance spreading outward from the deepest node, traveling through the root system the way memory travels through a system that has been holding it for a very long time and has finally found something to give it to.

It reached the perimeter flora.

The forget-me-nots, which had been facing inward since 16:30, turned outward.

One by one.

Then all at once.

The coalition went still.

Ninety players standing in the center cluster at 23:17 on Day 7 of the Aethelgard Resonance Championship, watching the oldest flora on the server do something none of them had a framework for.

Nobody moved for a full twenty seconds.

Bron said, very quietly: “…did the plants just bow?”

Thorne did not look up from his notebook but his pen had gone still. “That is an anthropocentric interpretation of a complex botanical gesture,” he said. Then, after a pause: “But I don’t have a better one.”

The crow made a sound. Short. Satisfied.

“Yes,” Thorne said to the crow. “I know.”

Pelto, still sitting on the ground with his whiteboard, looked at SIT DOWN AND LOOK AT THE LIGHT. He picked up his pen and, underneath, wrote: IT LOOKED BACK.

He showed it to the player next to him.

The player nodded, very slowly.

Phina was crying. Not dramatically — just the specific tears of someone who processes wonder by leaking it. Glimmer had shifted fully amber, matching the light moving through the roots, pulsing in the exact rhythm of the center node. She held it like something precious and did not try to explain it.

Nyx had come down from the canopy without anyone noticing and was standing three meters behind Livia. His flashlight was off. He was watching the root system with the expression he wore when something had gotten past his armor and he had decided, this once, to let it.

The amber light reached Livia’s feet.

And stopped.

Not because it ran out. Because it arrived.

Her Therapist’s Log opened on its own.

A new page. Blank. The usual prompts — Patient Name, Issues Presented, Therapeutic Approach — were gone. Just a blank page glowing faintly at the margins, waiting. The amber of the light and the amber at the page’s edge were the same color, and she did not know what to do with that except stand in it.

“I don’t know what you are,” she thought, looking at the oldest plant. “I don’t know what you’re trying to tell me. I don’t have a category for you or for whatever I am to you or for why my Log just opened itself to a blank page in the middle of the Verdant Expanse at twenty-three seventeen on a Tuesday.”

But I’m listening.

The amber pulsed once. Warm. Like acknowledgment.

Then it receded — slowly, back toward the center, back toward the deepest root, back toward whatever had been waiting in the ground of this world since before any player had ever entered it.

Nobody spoke for a long moment after it faded.

The silence of ninety people processing something simultaneously and finding that they did not yet have words.

They walked back at 01:03.

The coalition had dispersed. World Activity scores updated. B&B’s guild standing remained #1. Eclipse Covenant had climbed, which meant Caelric had gotten something out of tonight. She knew he had. She did not yet know exactly what.

That question had a place in the queue.

Bron was quiet in the specific Bron way of someone turned inward. Processing. He would come out of it eventually, louder, which was how he worked. She would give him the time.

Alaric was updating tabs. This was comforting. Some things were reliable.

Phina walked with Glimmer still cradled in her arms, still amber, fading now to its usual pink but slowly, like it was reluctant to let go of whatever it had been holding.

Nyx walked behind everyone. His flashlight was still off. He had not turned it back on since the forest.

Miles walked beside her.

They did not talk for a long time.

At some point he said: “The system didn’t log it.”

“Third time this week,” she said.

He nodded. He had been counting too.

“T.S. said the system changed three months before I arrived,” Livia said. “You prepared the planning room four months before I arrived. The Eleven-Second Message. And now something pre-server in the deepest part of that cluster…” she stopped. Started differently. “I keep finding things that were already waiting. And I don’t know if that’s…”

“I was going to say: I don’t know if I’m supposed to be here or if I happened here. There’s a difference.”

“Are you scared.”

Livia thought about it. Honestly.

“No,” she said. “And I think that’s the part that should scare me. That I’m not scared. That something pre-server recognizes me and my Log opened itself and the system keeps choosing not to log things it knows— and my reaction is to stand in it and say: I’m listening.” She paused. “That’s either growth or something has changed in me that I haven’t fully accounted for.”

Okay.
