Chapter 119

Imp King, Part II (The Last Stand)

The decapitated torso lifts off the pavement, boots hovering inches above shattered stone, arms hanging limp at its sides as if even gravity is confused. I stumble forward, throwing myself between it and Veronica’s crumpled form. But the Avatar’s body continues to rise through the air, like a puppet yanked upward by invisible strings.

For a heartbeat, the square is quiet—quiet in that awful, anticipatory way, like the city is holding its breath, waiting for something inevitable and terrible to happen. And none of us can look away, despite how much we’d like to.

The arms of the body jerkily move. It grabs itself, in what can only be described as the most awkward hug of all time. Then, fingers dig into skin, and the arms make a ‘ripping’ motion, and the body flays itself. Skin splits open like wet paper, flesh peels back in strips. Organs spill outward in a grotesque bloom, and instead of collapsing, the innards explode outward and remain suspended around the shell of the body—blood and viscera and shredded muscle forming a cloud of gore in the air.

Then the giant cloud of red mist twists, spiral and gathers in closer, rotating around an invisible axis. It forms what looks like a sort of cocoon. A pulsating egg of blood and bone and screaming biological wrongness. It pulses once, twice.

Each pulse shakes the air. Each pulse makes my stomach lurch as whatever System sixth sense within my core reacts to what I’m watching. My HUD pings, and the pulsing sensation in my mind is hard enough to feel like a nail being driven into my forehead.

SYSTEM MESSAGE:

You are within the presence of a True Avatar of Chernobog.

My throat goes dry.

Another message slams into my vision, right behind it.

SYSTEM MESSAGE:

A Foreign Realm’s presence detected.

Interplanar magical surge detected.

. . .

The Nether is near.

The sky responds like it heard the System and approved a mood change was necessary.

Night becomes something else, something darker, deeper. The normal darkness of Cleveland—full of streetlights and building glow and the faint reflection of light pollution off the lake—gets smothered. The air turns heavy, and the horizon dims as if someone is dragging a black curtain across the world. This true darkness, this absence of light, only lasts half a heartbeat.

Streaks of hellfire slash through the clouds like wounds opening in the atmosphere. My Behemoth Cap rumbles on my head. It’s a pleasure-filled low, satisfied purr, like a hungry animal smelling dinner. Crimson clouds churn overhead, lit by white lightning that forks and twists in unnatural angles, circling the square like a storm forming a noose.

And then the sky splits.

A gigantic red eye opens above us, tearing reality like an eyelid being pried apart. It’s enormous—too big to fit in the sky and still make sense, sitting where one might expect to see a full moon on a clear night. Veins of light pulse around it. The pupil contracts and expands like it’s tasting the world.

It looks down. At us.

Ping!

SYSTEM MESSAGE:

It Who Watches the End has entered your Realm.

The Eternal Eye Watches…

God dammit. What the fuck is going on? No, no, no, no!... My mind reels and I’m left standing there, helpless and feeling so small, so miniscule and useless. I’m nothing more than an insect who happens to be under a boot about to fall.

The gore cocoon pulses one last time. Before it explodes.

The egg bursts outward, raining blood and shredded flesh across Public Square. It slaps onto stone and cars and statues in wet sheets. The smell hits a split second later—iron and bile and rot, thick enough to choke on. The remaining imps in the sky erupt into a vicious symphony—screams, screeches, laughter—like the world’s ugliest opening act, celebrating the arrival of the headliner.

What comes out of the cocoon descends, landing with a shockwave that cracks the pavement.

It’s… wrong.

A gigantic demon, the size of a large two story house, with red skin slick with blood, a bloated stomach distended like it’s swallowed something alive. Muscled arms hang heavy at its sides, hands gnarled and tipped with black claws that look like they were designed to tear through armor and hope. Its legs are smaller in comparison, like it spent millennia skipping leg day. Stubby and thinner in comparison, ending in hoof-tipped feet that grind into the crumbling asphalt and cracked stone. Its face is animalistic, extending into a snout-like nose, with glowing yellow eyes shaped like a predator’s. The eyes stare without blinking, faintly glowing with System-empowered magic. It’s mouth is too large for its skull, filled with razor teeth that catch the lightning crackling from the crimson clouds like knives. Long dark horns curl out from its forehead, each thicker than tree trunks.

I force my shaking gaze to examine it.

The System answers without hesitation.

Monster Identified: True Avatar of Chernobog

Level: 175

Classification: Death Ritual Avatar

My lungs forget how to work. My mind goes blank except for one thought, repeated over and over like a prayer:

I swallow hard and the only word that comes out is the truest one I have.

“Fuck.”

The demon rises back into the air, taking the place high overhead, where its cocoon once hovered.  It raises both arms at its sides. Crimson runes snap into existence around the Avatar, two full rings of them, hovering at chest height and head height, perfect circles of glowing symbols that look like someone carved rage into geometry. They form fast, precise, and immediately begin spinning in opposite directions.

The lower ring rotates clockwise. The upper ring rotates counterclockwise. Both rings of glowing runes are tilted, crisscrossing as they spin around the demonic monster. Wind tugs at my back, being pulled towards the rotating runes.

My skin prickles, and my teeth ache like I just took a bite of aluminum.

The Behemoth Cap on my head rumbles again—pleased, hungry, excited—like it’s savoring the smell of inevitable destruction. Is this Avatar similar to what Shogmoth is, truly is? I suddenly don’t feel as happy about having the thing’s mouth on top of my head.

The Avatar tilts its horned head toward the sky.

And far above it another tear appears in the air. Similar to the tear that formed when that gigantic eye appeared. But this one is just a hairline rip in reality, like the seam of the universe has been pulled apart with a fingernail. Red light leaks out, an angry red light. It pours through the tear in thin beams at first, then thicker, like the hole is widening, like something on the other side is pushing, eager to spill into this world.

The air around the rip screams without sound, the energy of whatever is on the other side of that seam clawing at whatever primordial fear is cowering deep within my genetic code.

The wind that was tugging at me dies.

Then my HUD flashes and the ping I receive, usually a deep thrumming, pulse, now feels like an earthquake going on inside my mind.

A System message slams into my vision in bold, urgent text.

SYSTEM MESSAGE:

HIGH-LEVEL DIMENSIONAL BREACH DETECTED!

. . .

Ping!

NEW QUEST!

New Quest: THE NETHER APPROACHES

Description: The Avatar of Chernobog is attempting to pull the Nether to Earth.If this violation of System protocol is permitted to occur, Earth will be consumed by the Nether.

Note: If the Avatar of Chernobog is successful, the God Game will automatically enter itself final phase.

[System Incapability Detected!]

Note: If the Avatar of Chernobog is successful, all Realm inhabitants without System access will automatically perish. All inhabitants with System access will become residents of the Nether.

OBJECTIVE: Defeat the Avatar of Chernobog before the Dimensional Breach is completed.

TIMER: 5 Minutes, 00 Seconds.

A timer appears.

It starts ticking down immediately.

4:59… 4:58…

My mouth goes dry. My heartbeat slams against my ribs.

Five minutes. We only have five minutes to stop a Level 175 True Avatar from dragging hell into our world. Five minutes to prevent Earth from getting turned into some hellscape. Five minutes to save everyone without System access.

I look up at the widening tear of red light in the sky.

Then down at Veronica. She’s unconscious and injured, but breathing.

At Jelly Boy—reformed, vibrating angrily and re-equipping his legs, ready to die again if he has to.

At Reshma—hands glowing, face calm in that terrifying way, as though she’d been a combat medic her entire life.

At Matt… Across Public Square, at Jerome.

The timer ticks.

4:52… 4:51…

I swallow hard.

“Okay,” I whisper, voice thin in my own ears.

Then I grit my teeth.

“Okay,” I say again, louder—because if I don’t speak, the fear wins. Whatever happens, I cannot let the fear win.

Above us, a symphony of imps cackle.

POV: Jerome Williams, Vice Captain of Harvest Guild and Nothing But Business

Jerome did not pray.

He had never been the type to plead with anything. God, System, fate, or the dumb luck that kept most people alive. Prayer implied helplessness. Prayer implied the world was run by someone else’s rules.

Jerome wrote rules.

He sprinted, legs pounding the over the pavement and towards the others.

Public Square had become a cathedral of screaming: imps in the sky like a living storm, the red tear above them widening and pouring angry light into the night, the distant siren wail of a city that had no idea it was five minutes from its doom.

“On me!” he shouted, making sure the command cut through the shrieks and the thunder of the opening breach. “ON ME!”

He reached them in a few long strides.

The slime—Joseph’s slime, Jelly Boy—had reconstituted itself thanks to the healing magic of Reshma, and was reattached to its ridiculous humanoid legs, clicking in place as it turned, ready. Jerome took note with the same clinical attention he would give a weapon.

Reshma was crouched beside the other woman Jerome didn’t know, hands glowing softly as she checked her, keeping her alive even while literal hell was breaking loose around them. Jerome appreciated that. Someone who could do their job under this level of immense pressure was a rare, and valuable, thing. A healer who could do it while hellfire streaked overhead was rarer still. And healers were already extremely valuable.

“You need to move out of the line of fire,” he told her.

Jerome didn’t waste time with questions.

He reached into his Inventory and pulled out the Sending Stones.

They looked like small dark pebbles at first—then their enchantment shimmered, reshaping them into sleek, fitted earbuds, a custom job he’d commissioned for exactly this kind of situation.

He jammed one into his own ear and tossed the others.

“One each,” he snapped, flicking them toward Matt Bruck and Joseph. “Put these in. If you focus on it and speak, we’ll all be able to hear you. It will make it easier to coordinate. We’ll need a coordinated effort to take this thing down.”

Joseph caught his, blinking like he’d forgotten battlefield comms existed outside of video games. He was a good kid, really. But he had a lot of learning to do. Matt caught his with a smooth, indifferent motion that suggested he’d been doing this kind of thing long before Jerome ever met him. He fashioned the stone into his left ear, nodding. Reshma, similarly, caught hers and equipped it.

Jerome spoke fast, clipped, razor-clean.

“We have less than five minutes,” he said, eyes cutting up toward the widening tear, then back to the demon. “We need to take down this threat as quickly as possible. I’ll drop my [Rule] on the imps. I’ll focus on trying to stop its spell and make it an easier target for you two. You focus damage. Reshma, if you can heal from a distance, then do that. If you can’t then stay hidden and only reveal yourself as necessary.”

Joseph nodded once, jaw clenched.

Matt’s expression didn’t change, but his body shifted slightly.

“Go,” Jerome said.

Then, he let go of his containment of the imps. His earlier [Rule]—the one holding the imp swarm in the immediate area—fell away. The city would need to deal with the imps as soon as possible. That was true. But there were dueling priorities now, and the one in the sky was existential. The city had other methods of addressing standard monster outbreaks, even if it had never dealt with one so large in scale.

Besides, Jerome could already sense it: the new demon drew most of the imps like carrion drew flies. They orbited it, screamed for it, worshiped it. Keeping them penned in served little purpose at this point.

He pulled a mana potion from his Inventory and threw it back in one practiced motion, letting the thumb nail sized vial drop to the ground. Warmth flooded his veins. His internal pool surged, refilling like a reservoir after rain.

Excellent, he thought. Now for the work that mattered.

Jerome planted his feet amid broken stone and wet gore, stared up at the True Avatar of Chernobog, and began to write law into the air.

Three [Rules], in quick succession.

First, a [Rule] to bind himself.

“You will not fall in battle.”

The air quaked. He let the [Rule] take hold of his own soul. Even with willing third parties, there was a level of resistance to a [Rule] taking hold. When applying [Rules] to himself, however, they were applied without issue.

The Rule snapped into place with immediate ease, wrapping around his body like invisible armor. It wouldn’t make him invincible. It would drain mana if he was attacked, and quickly. But it would stop him from dying. At least instantly. And in a conflict like this, seconds mattered. Seconds were a matter of victory or failure.

The next two [Rules] were focused on the Avatar.

“You will not use magic!”

The instant the words left his mouth, his mana pool was cut in half. Dammit. But he expected that might happen.

The demon resisted the effect of his [Rule]. He felt it like trying to shove a boulder uphill with his mind. The [Rule] failed to take full hold. But it bit. The crimson runes spinning around the Avatar slowed slightly. Jerome could sense it: the demon would need to spend focus resisting the [Rule]. Focus spent resisting was focus not spent on other actions. Like attacking his allies, or any more interdimensional fuckery.

Then, his third and final [Rule].

“You will not move!”

This one took far easier than his last one.

Jerome felt the Rule latch onto the demon’s existence and lock, forcing stillness onto something that wanted only motion and violence. But the cost was immediate: his mana began to tick down, a steady drain like blood from a wound. The Avatar fought the constraint, and the resistance became a constant tug-of-war inside Jerome’s skull.

It was a price he was happily willing to pay. He did the math in his head and set the number his Current Mana Value would hit the moment his potion cooldown ended. Another mana potion was in his Inventory, waiting to be consumed. Now their enemy was held suspended in the air, a stationary target.

Above him, Joseph and Matt were already in motion.

Joseph’s barbell staff rose into the air, the two men balanced atop it like they were riding a missile. Joseph launched spells—force and fists and whatever else that man could throw without mana. The demon shrugged most of it off, the way a mountain shrugged off rain.

But Matt’s attacks landed—and though still subtle—Jerome could see that they were felt.

The Nimble Ghost leapt off the staff, blurred through space, struck with gauntleted hands and impossible angles, then snapped back onto the staff before gravity could claim him. Each hit wasn’t devastating, but it was annoying. It made the Avatar flinch. It made it snarl. It reminded it that even so-called gods could be pestered by gnats.

The demon tried to move, to lift one of its hooved feet. It couldn’t. It flared with anger, red skin pulsing, stomach distending as it hauled against the [Rule] holding it.

Jerome spoke into his Sending Stone, voice calm despite the screaming sky overhead.

“Keep it up. I’m going to keep it in place. In the meantime, I’ll keep the imps from swarming you.”

“How are you going to do that?” It was Joseph, voice delivered straight to Jerome’s Sending Stone.

“I have at least one long-range magical weapon in my Inventory,” he responded. He was mentally sifting through his Inventory at that very moment. There it is. He withdrew a long, thin wooden wand.

Item: [Wand of Firebolt]

Charges Remaining: 40 of 40.

He snapped his wrist, flicking the tip of the wand toward a small group of imps flying towards the barbell staff and the men riding it. Three projectiles launched from the tip of the wand, streaking triple tails of silver mist and light. They hit their targets, popping the imps like blood-filled balloons.

The Avatar howled, trying to turn its body, but failing as Jerome’s [Rule] wrapped itself tighter around the beast. Its yellow eyes swept the battlefield… Searching.

Then, its gaze snapped onto Jerome.

Jerome sighed. Of course.

Jerome was the anchor. The one holding it still and strangling its magic. At least it wasn’t targeting their healer. He was prepared for this. For being its target.

Eight meteors of hellfire formed in the sky—glowing red-hot stones wrapped in screaming flame—and rocketed down toward Public Square.

They struck the Sailors’ and Soldiers’ Memorial statue in the center of the square. Stone exploded and metal screamed. The impact shook the ground under Jerome’s feet. The meteors, each the size of inflated beach balls, didn’t slow down. They blasted through the dust and debris, rocketing towards him.

Jerome moved. Fast, but not fast enough. The meteors found their mark.

Fire slammed into him. Heat swallowed him whole, and he was blinded by smoke. The force of each meteor making impact crushing him into the ground. For a heartbeat, the world became flame and pain.

His mana dipped sharply as his [Rule] absorbed the damage and his System-empowered regeneration went into overdrive. When the fire cleared, Jerome was still there. Crouched behind rubble. His suit jacket was gone—burned away, ash drifting off his shoulders. His button-down shirt smoked, singed at the edges and one sleeve also scorched away up to his elbow, collar blackened. His tie was twisted around his neck so that it was running down his back instead.

But his body? Unscathed. His Health? Still in the green. His [Rule] held.

Jerome inhaled slowly, tasting soot, and let himself smile.

You’ll have to try harder than that to kill me.

He lifted his head, and tightened his grip on the [Rule] strangling the demon’s ability to move. He sharpened his focus on that specific [Rule], twisting it like a knife.

“Again,” he murmured into the Sending Stone, voice steady as law. “Hit it again!”

POV: Joseph, Creatine Conjurer and Nothing But Monkey Business

I circle the True Avatar like a mosquito, buzzing in and out at each opportunity. Despite Jerome keeping the monster locked into place, it still has free use of its arms, so we have to be careful.

The Full Metal Staff hums beneath my feet, cutting tight arcs through the air while the wind tries to rip my hat off and the sky above is full of crimson light and clouds of fire. The demon hangs there in the center of the square like an obscene blimp of muscle, hooves hovering a good twenty feet above the gore-slick pavement, its attention split between resisting Jerome’s [Rules] and whatever spell it’s using to pry open reality.

I’m hammering it with empowered [Force Blasts].

Each shot feels like firing a cannon from my ribcage. Blast after blast of pure concussive violence slamming into its red hide. And each one glances off it like rainfall.

Pfft. Pfft.Pfft.

The impacts kick up little bursts of steam and crimson sparks, but the Avatar doesn’t even flinch. It’s like I’m throwing tennis balls at a tank. I draw on the stored power in my staff, burning some of that banked Stamina and pouring it into the blasts until my arms ache and my teeth buzz from the continuous magic channeling.

These shots would obliterate anything I’ve faced so far.

But in the face of this Avatar, they’re useless.

“It’s not enough!” I bark.

Matt’s strikes seem to be doing some real damage, however minimal. He strikes in short bursts of surgical violence. We fly in close. He blinks in, strikes, blinks out, returning to my staff. Sometimes he strikes with his hands and feet, empowering his body with a black aura. Other times, he pulls weapons from his Inventory to strike out with.

I think he’s hurting it. He’s definitely annoying it.

“Alright,” I growl, jaw clenched. “Let’s lend you a hand.”

I double-cast [Wizard’s Fist].

Silvery mist blooms on either side of the flying staff. Righty forms first, huge and eager. Lefty quickly follows. Twin fists, happy as ever to punch anything that stands before them. Lefty gives the gigantic demon the middle finger.

“Get to work,” I mutter.

Lefty and Righty slice through the air, toward the Avatar and immediately start pummeling its torso—BAM BAM!—hammering its shoulder and ribs and bloated stomach in a relentless rhythm.

The demon flinches, and swipes at the spectral fists. To my surprise, it’s actually able to brush them away. He can fully interact with my spectral fists. Great, I mentally note.

I try something else.

I tap into the Behemoth Cap.

The hat’s stitched mouth flexes against my scalp like it’s waking up, hungry. I can feel the heat boiling out from the open mouth. The hat’s menu of spells pops into my interface, and each spell save for the largest is available for use.

I mentally select [Crimson Tongue].

A jet of fire rips from the cap, a thick stream of red-orange flame washing over the Avatar’s shoulder. It splashes across its hide like molten paint. And does… Nothing.

The Behemoth Cap grumbles in my mind, a low, displeased rumble that feels like a lion complaining. I hear something about ‘Resistances.’

Okay, so the thing that spawns hellfire projectiles resists fire damage. Go figure!

I click my tongue.

“Of course,” I mutter.

My brain spins. Time to try something else from my bag of tricks.

My summoning spell is one option. Though I need to pour enough Stamina into upcasting it to increase my chances of someone useful being summoned. If I cast the base version of the spell I’m as likely to summon one of the Pukwudgies from my first gate.

A sharp crackling sound snaps my focus down. And I glance down towards the source of the commotion.

Bullets of slime rip through the air, striking the backs of the Avatar’s knees. One leg shakes and wobbles, but doesn’t buckle.

Jelly Boy is beneath the demon. He’s running, leaping, and rapid-firing slime projectiles that explode with purple lightning as they hit. Each burst makes the demon’s skin twitch.

Way to go, Jelly! I think, fierce pride flaring through the panic.

Then my [Aura Sense] twitches.

Power emanates from the Avatar. Am I sensing… its spellcasting?

I don’t have enough time to really chew on that thought as chunks of buildings rip free from facades and rooftops as if by a giant invisible hand. They lift into the air, pausing for half a breath before hurtling towards both me and Matt, and Jelly Boy.

“SHIT!” I shout. “Jelly watch out!”

I corkscrew the staff hard, cape tugging my balance back into place, my new perfect stability saving me from tumbling off into a lethal fall. Debris screams past my shoulder, missing by feet, shattering against the pavement below in explosive bursts.

Another slab flies. I dip under it, barely.

Jelly Boy bounces sideways, a lightning slime bullet popping a chunk of rubble midair.

Then my HUD pings.

A new notification flashes bright enough to cut through the terror. I barely read it before minimizing it in a panic.

SKILL UPGRADE:

Your [Aura Sense] Skill has been upgraded to Master.

But the moment the upgrade lands, the world sharpens in a terrifyingly useful way.

The next time a corner of one of the nearby buildings is torn from its steel frame—when the demon’s telekinesis spell grips stone and metal—I see it. A faint red outline surrounding the debris. I inhale sharply.

“Hold on,” I tell Matt through the Sending Stone, just in case he can’t hear me over my shoulder.

He doesn’t respond, but he shifts his weight instantly, ready.

I dive.

I twist through the next barrage, using the red outlines like highway markers, threading the staff between floating chunks with inches to spare, and I aim us straight at the demon’s belly. Its bloated, softer-looking center mass has to be a weak spot.

“Now!” I bark.

Matt moves like a ghost.

He withdraws two blades from his Inventory in twin flashes of pixelated light—black and clean—and leaps through the air, plunging them into the beast’s hide as we pass, ripping downward in a long savage arc.

This time the Avatar reacts. The demon’s body jolts. A deep, angry sound rumbles from its throat. Dark blood—thick and almost tar-like—sprays into the air. That hurt. Finally! My heart slams in my chest.

“Again,” I growl, already banking for another pass as the Nether tear above us pulses brighter, opens slightly wider.

3:45 . . . 3:44

Jelly Boy sees the long wound running down the demon’s belly and goes feral. He launches into the air like a blue cannonball, legs flashing, fishnets and stilettos gleaming under the hell-lit sky. He spins mid-flight and the heels glow, turning into elongated blades. He slashes at the Avatar with the razor edge of his heels, carving two bright, wet lines practically tracing the slash mark made by Matt.

The demon snarls, a sound like a furnace coughing. I sweep the staff down and Matt relinquishes his grip on his blades, blinking back onto my staff without looking, landing with perfect balance like the staff is a sidewalk.

“Nice,” I mutter, and bank hard to keep us out of a flying slab of brick.

Then I feel it. Another ripple of magic from the Avatar.

My eyes scramble to find the red outline denoting the target of his next telekinetic attack. I’m shocked when my [Aura Sense] and [Perception] draw my attention to the faint, crimson glow… surrounding Jelly Boy’s animatronic legs.

My stomach drops. This can’t be good.

“Oh no.”

I watch as the surface of Jelly Boy’s body simmers, clearly the strain of resistance.

For a heartbeat, I think he’s going to win.

Then the resistance falters.

I sense the spell biting down.

The legs twitch. Then jerk.

Then move. Not in the dance routine style flow of movement characteristic of Jelly Boy, but as if someone else has grabbed the joystick. Jelly Boy vibrates furiously, blorping in outrage, trying to tear himself free from the legs, but the equipment holds him like a trap. The legs pivot and start sprinting away from the fight, dragging him along.

“Jelly!” I shout, but it’s too late. Before I know it, he’s blocks down Euclid Avenue, heels clacking on concrete.

I don’t know how long that spell lasts. I just know we’re down a fighter.

“Fuck,” I hiss.

I dive the staff toward the ground crew, landing hard near Reshma with a controlled skid. The air is thick with ash and gore mist. Jerome is still out there somewhere, burning mana to hold his [Rules] in place.

I don’t see Veronica. My chest tightens. I’m not sure if the Avatar is still specifically targeting her in any way.

“Where’s Veronica?” I demand, stepping off the staff.

Reshma’s hood is spattered with blood that isn’t hers. Her hands glow faintly, the light strained but steady.

“I’ve hidden her,” she says calmly. “You’ve taken damage. Let me heal you both.”

I glance down and, yeah, she’s right.

My arms are scraped and burned. My shoulder is bleeding where a shard of something caught me. Matt has a gash across his ribs and his breathing is a fraction too shallow. It’s all regenerating, but my Health bar is low enough that it’s not the instant “lol nope” healing I’m used to for such minor wounds.

Reshma steps in, palms open.

Magic washes over us—warm and clean—flooding my body like a nice bath after a long run. My wounds knit themselves back together, and I’m as good as new. My Health bar climbs and tops off with a satisfying fullness that makes my muscles feel full and alive.

Matt, without looking, flicks a potion to Reshma. She catches it one-handed like she’s done it a thousand times. She drinks. Her shoulders relax slightly.

“Thank you,” she breathes.

“No problem,” Matt says, then looks up as the demon’s shadow shifts. “We need it down.”

I nod, jaw clenched.

“But first,” I say, “we need more help.”

I grip my staff, feel the plates humming, and I cast [Lesser Summoning: Interplanar Ally]. I burn an entire plate from one side of the staff to upcast it with [No Pain, No Gain], feeding the spell with stored power until the iron vibrates in my hands like it wants to scream.

The air warps and light gathers.

But that’s when the Avatar notices. Its head snaps toward me, yellow eyes focusing like a sniper scope.

My spell starts to form, just as something hits my mind. A crushing, cold pressure that tries to smother my concentration like a wet cloth. The Avatar is targeting me with a spell of its own.

My vision swims.

I grind my teeth. I plant my feet and fight back, every muscle in my body tensing as if willpower is something I can bench press.

For a second, I hold.

Then, I feel the demon push harder. And it’s too much for me.

I lose.

My concentration shatters like glass. A System message slams into my vision.

Your Spell has been [Canceled]!

“No!” I snarl, and the half-formed summoning door collapses into sparks.

Above us, the Avatar flares again.

The sky responds with violence.

Meteors of hellfire form—dozens of the beach ball sized flaming projectiles—streaking down towards us.

Reshma’s eyes widen.

Matt swears, sharp and vicious.

I curse at myself for not distancing myself from our healer before attempting a spell that drew the attention of the monster.

I cast [Slimy Shield].

And I burn another plate to expand it, forcing the shield to balloon outward into a massive blue dome that covers all of us like a bubble of hardened gel.

The first meteors hit.

The shield shudders.

The second wave hits.

The shield cracks.

Then the hellfire tears through it like it’s tissue paper.

The dome ruptures and the impact obliterates the ground around us. Stone explodes, and heat slams into my face like a furnace door opening. I hit the ground hard, cape snapping over me, ears ringing.

When the smoke clears, the square looks like it got hit by a miniature apocalypse.

Reshma is on her knees.

Her white cloak is scorched. Her armor is dented. Blood runs from her mouth in a dark line, and her glow is dimmer now.

“Reshma!” I shout, scrambling.

Matt is already there, moving fast, kneeling beside her and shoving a potion into her hands.

“Drink,” he orders.

She does, swallowing with a grimace. Only to instantly vomit the healing potion back up.

Matt watches her for half a second, then his face tightens.

“Fuck,” he spits. “That was a Grand Mana Potion I gave her before!”

He looks up at the sky like he wants to stab it.

“She still needs to cool off…!” He punches the ground, cracking asphalt beneath the force of his knuckles in frustration. “This isn’t good.”

Jerome’s voice snaps into my ear through the Sending Stone.

“Joseph,” he says. “I’m setting a [Rule] that will protect Reshma. Get away from her. Don’t draw any more attention in that direction. Try and draw the Avatar’s attention elsewhere!”
