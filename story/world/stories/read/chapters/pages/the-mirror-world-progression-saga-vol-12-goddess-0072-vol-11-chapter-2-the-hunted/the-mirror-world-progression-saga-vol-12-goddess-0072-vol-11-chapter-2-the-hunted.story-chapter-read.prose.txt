Arthen Wybayer, Witchhunter, former Deputy Marshal of The Hunt, and now Master General of the Armies of the Dominion of Wisdom, walked over the battlements along the walls of the City of Heavenly Order, looking out over what was perhaps likely to be a battlefield if things continued as they were. It was known that quite a mind was behind those still-distant armies, and outthinking it would be a task for the ages.

Totally overpowered in the Borderlands, Arthen had accomplished an admirable level of attrition in a retreating, losing war, for armies bleeding out deserters left and right. He’d also established a great terrorism base that was ongoing in the Borderlands, thereby forcing resources to be committed there and flat out anchoring some domains out of the war, as they worried about securing their own meager lands.

The message became clear when non-participants had terrorism stop immediately, once their forces were pulled from the war. Unlike the Inquisitors, the Witchhunters operated entirely on pragmatism — you took what you could get. Knights never sent against you were as good as knights killed, and frequently at far less cost to do it.

Arthen paused as he looked up to see hundreds of hippogriff-riders high up, swooping around and doing drills. He shook his head with a scowl.

Waste of time. Our enemy rules the air now, without Palthennius. With them also having Palthennius, in the height of pathetic Matrazerian hubris, to somehow lose his loyalty with their insane stunt. In any case, no cost should be spared to take the sky away from them entirely, and it will be little loss to us. Our foot will be the greatest ever assembled.

Rumors from Matrazere had leaked far and wide, of course. Palthennius himself was around to give accounts and make claims, which didn’t help. The statements went out that only deserters and traitors had ever been made into undead, and so it would continue. As their story went, false as it was, Palthennius had unsuccessfully led a rebellion in Matrazere that exposed many as traitors, who were subsequently utilized in death, alongside the living who should’ve been their brothers.

Holes were easily poked in this, but it wasn’t as if Dominion citizens wanted to believe some rampaging demon army was in the right, after all. It worked well enough.

The plan for the battle would be a mixed lineup. Actual soldiers were key due to the nature of the undead and masters… it was difficult to produce numbers without centralized control, as was done in Matrazere. It should have worked… but they’d failed to plan for protecting the rune adequately. What would it have taken? A bit of extra fortification in the spirit world? A hundred specialists ready to defend and strike? How could they be so blind for so long?

Hubris, as always. They will not find hubris with my generalship, nor will I listen to the idiocy of the Inquisitors ever again. They will obey me, now. What’s bloody left of them, anyway.

So it had been commanded by his own new master, their very head — Inquisitor One. Everything would go through him, with him as the final say, ruffled feathers or no. No councils, no committees; the way it should be. And he was deeply proud of his own order, because his former superior, the Marshal of the Hunt, had accepted and bowed to his command instantly, even asked Arthen who he recommended as a new Deputy.

If the whole of the Dominion acted this way, the Witch Queen would’ve never won herself out of her tepid little swamp. But so it went: it was left to the true inheritors of the spirit of resisting the corruption of the supernatural, which existed even before the Sage himself in the weariness of the people… It was left to the Witchhunters to save humanity from the return of an old and obsolete predator.

Perhaps it’s worth it, as the Sage of Truth has hinted… this way, we can start over fresh, with a fire purifying the waste out of us. Even desertion and making undead out of the useless qualifies in that regard. Good fucking riddance.

He was snapped out of his contemplations by a voice in his head. An inquisitor. “His Ascendancy, the First Sage, cannot respond to your queries at this time, nor in the near future, but his assurances of your command remain. If you desire advisors, you may name your choices, or we could make suggestions.”

Arthen tried not to sneer. “Noted, Inquisitor. What of Ulraharrus, the Sage of Erudition? I’ve had his wise counsel in the past.”

“I’m afraid his whereabouts are unknown, and he cannot be reached.”

Arthen blinked, completely shocked. “Ulraharrus, a deserter? You can’t be serious.”

“We do not know his motivations, though it’s true he abandoned numerous duties in his absence. Searching has turned up nothing, as if he simply disappeared. Quite bluntly, if the Deacon of Knowledge doesn’t want to be known, he won’t be.”

Arthen mined his brain for those he actually knew whom he could trust… It was a short list. He didn’t know any of the current Seven Sages well enough. He understood that at least one would be needed for proper access, as he was equivalent to a bumpkin when it came to the capital’s secrets.

It came to him. “We will see,” Arthen said vaguely, “but one I wish to summon for now is Elder Savant Greizer.”

There was a pause, which was very telling from an Inquisitor, who rarely didn’t reply immediately in mental spaces. “You are aware he is merely an Elder Savant, and is not privy to the deepest knowledge the Seven and the Inquisitors hold?”

“Yes, Inquisitor.” Arthen didn’t bother moderating the tone of derision this time. “Thank you for simply doing as I ask in this regard, with haste.”

“Understood. The priority will be communicated with haste.” That was all, as silence followed thereafter.

Arthen had only had brief, cordial interactions with Greizer, but he was known as uncommonly inspired and well-traveled, which was a negative among his peers but entirely a positive to Arthen’s mind. On top of this, though, some of the reports Arthen had been combing through with his new access showed that Greizer had actually long, long ago been suspicious of a spirit utilizing mirrors. The mirror had technically been cleared of suspicion by Ulraharrus himself, but now it seemed clear there must’ve been a hidden aural connection that had simply faded by the time the sage had time to investigate, or she’d gained resistance somehow during that time.

It had been deeply frustrating to learn that no one had taken Greizer seriously, now that they knew the connection to the Samantha entity. If lesser investigators had tried harder, surely they’d have found something. A fact now completely useless because she was too strong to deal with remotely any longer, as was proven in the heinously epic failure of the Inquisitors' attack on her mind. In fact, she was now deeply dangerous to engage with at all. She’d been allowed to grow into a true monster.

That mirror connection was precisely the sort of thing the Inquisitors were built to uncover and follow up on, and they’d failed. Likely, numerous other signs had failed to be uncovered, showcasing their complacency and incompetence all the more.

Ultimately, Greizer was not just a decent advisor in his own right, with the solid base of a pragmatic and realistic outlook, but he could tell Arthen which of the Seven should be his primary authoritative and administrative advisor, which he’d need to properly command up into sagely echelons he did not have time to completely fathom. It was as close as he could get to a trustworthy opinion, as he saw it.

Sages should never be generals, regardless. The battlefield is the battlefield, out there, and the ivory tower is another thing entirely. You cannot understand the wholer truths of the grass without walking it and touching it. Amidst the land’s simplicity, elemental secrets resonate. Like a central place on a map, a general is a nexus of commonality between disparate functions… I am the generalist where the specialists combine to make the alchemy of victory.

As he waited, Arthen hopped up onto the battlements and sat down with one leg dangling, getting a full view of the city extensions below, and the terrain beyond, matching his eyes to the maps he knew. He’d have plenty of time to study it all and formulate plans and plots, as the Witch Queen had great territory to cover and continual challenges to secure hostile zones as she did. One way or another, her armies would crawl and suffer along the way, as the Capital continually strengthened.

His eyes were drawn southeast, where the ancient fortress of the Witchhunters perhaps still clung by its last thread of existence. More likely, it had already gone up in smoke. He knew a man still hunted him, and invariably, that capable hunter would find where he’d been. What capable one couldn’t track their quarry?

Arthen smiled. A hunter followed a trail, but his trail had gone to an inaccessible place, the greatest of predators now arrayed between them. Arthen would not be among the rest of the prey at all, instead continuing as the mastermind behind many puppets. Endless decoys, and his name was just another jacket he wore on the outside, his true name kept as the most secure of secrets.

We’ve known the truth for aeons of practice… a hunter is always the hunted.
