Screams were coming from everywhere. The air around them filled with thick, biting dust, and Sakura could barely see anything in front of her, only a gray haze with broken scraps of light and shadows cutting through it. Every breath scraped at her throat, made her cough, and tightened her chest painfully.

For one short, terrifying moment, it all reminded her too much of the subway: the heat, the smoke, the suffocating panic of every breath pulling the end closer. Sakura squeezed her eyes shut for a second, feeling raw, animal terror rise into her throat.

No.

Not again.

She clenched her teeth and forced herself up onto her elbows.

This time, history would not repeat itself. She would not suffocate. Lir still needed her. Gilbert still needed her.

She could still move. Coughing, Sakura pushed herself higher and tried to see something through the dust.

The room had changed. The wall was gone. Where the window and part of the masonry had been only a moment ago, there was now a jagged gap. To the left, Gilbert’s silhouette flashed into view. He was already on his feet. Not fully upright, more half-crouched and taut as a beast bracing for a strike.

Sakura’s gaze shifted, and she caught sight of Harlow. The chair had been knocked over, and the enforcer himself was partly buried under stones and splintered beams. One large piece of masonry pinned his leg, and several more chunks lay across his chest and shoulder. He was coughing so hard it sounded like he might tear his lungs out, and he was trying to curse, but for now only hoarse sounds came out.

Sakura did not hesitate. She crawled toward him at once, palms sliding over dust and shards. Her knees burned, and a ringing still filled her ears.

“It’s okay,” she breathed, not even sure whether she was saying it to him or to herself.

She grabbed the nearest chunk of rubble and dragged it aside with effort. Then another. The stones were heavy and sharp, shedding grit and scraping her hands. Harlow coughed again, turning his head to the side.

“I... told you...” he rasped through the coughing. “It’d be... bad... We’re done for.”

“No, none of us are done for,” Sakura snapped, throwing another piece of stone off him. “We’re all staying alive, Harlow.”

Gilbert was already beside her. Fast, without wasting words, he shoved part of a beam aside and freed Harlow. Below them, chaos still raged: screaming, cracking, crashing. Someone was shouting that everything was on fire. Someone else was calling for their mother.

“Get him up,” Gilbert said sharply.

Sakura nodded, still coughing. For one second, the suffocating spasm clenched inside her chest again, but she forced it down. She helped Harlow get to his feet and stay upright. The man twisted in pain, trying not to look at his leg, bent at an unnatural angle.

“My leg...” he forced out. “Damn it. My leg...”

Then Harlow suddenly went silent, staring at the breach in the wall. Sakura turned too. At first she saw only the dusty opening and, behind it, a tall, motionless shape blurred by the haze.

Gilbert changed instantly.

“We’re leaving,” he said shortly.

The silhouette in the gap was still hazy through the dust, but its very presence already pressed down on the room. Sakura did not even have time to make out a face or understand whether it was a man at all before something shot out of the gray haze straight at them.

An obsidian spear.

Sakura jerked her head up and did not even have time to scream.

Gilbert raised his hand. The air in front of him cracked sharply, and a lightning-like flash burst from his palm toward the spear. It struck the obsidian projectile in midair. The spear was knocked sideways, slammed into a surviving piece of wall, and burst into black shards.

Sakura stared at it, forgetting even to cough. There it was: Gilbert’s magic, finally free again.

“Gilbert...” she breathed.

But he did not even look at her. He stood slightly in front of both her and Harlow, hand raised, the remnants of light still trembling around it. His focus was locked on the breach.

“At last,” the mage grinned. He was far too lively and far too pleased, considering the situation. “My magic is mine again.”

The dust was finally beginning to settle, and a man stepped out of the gray veil. Large, with a build that made Harlow look like a boy beside him. He moved slowly, without haste, as if he already knew he would win this fight. Scars curled across his face and bare neck in strange patterns. In the half-light and dust, they looked especially unnerving.

Sakura swallowed hard, clenching her hands.

The man stopped in the gap and calmly looked over the ruined room as if it interested him no more than a broken mug. Dust settled over his shoulders and hair, but he did not even blink. His face was almost motionless, as if carved from stone.

He slowly shifted his gaze from Gilbert to Sakura.

“My name is Faerd,” he said in a low, deep voice. “And I am your doom.”

A cold shiver ran down Sakura’s spine. Faerd spoke without anger, triumph, or even impatience, and that calm certainty frightened her far more.

Gilbert bared his teeth in a predator’s readiness.

“Very confident.”

Faerd did not even look at him as an equal.

“Violators must be eliminated,” he said in that same flat tone. “The order has already been given. Resistance is irrelevant.”

Harlow sucked in a nervous breath. He was still standing, holding onto Sakura and barely putting weight on his injured leg. His bleary gaze flicked between Gilbert, Sakura, and Faerd as fast as if he had only just realized what kind of meat grinder he had stumbled into.

Sakura turned sharply toward him.

“Go,” she said. “Right now. On your own. He won’t go after you.”

Harlow stared at her with childlike bewilderment.

“Huh?”

“Go!” Sakura snapped. “While you still can!”

This time she did not need to persuade him. Harlow did not even try to preserve what little dignity he had left. He nodded jerkily, turned, and limped away, nearly falling with every step. For a second he looked back as if he wanted to say something else, then thought better of it. He disappeared past the doorway.

Sakura stayed beside Gilbert. For one short moment, she wanted to run after Harlow. Not even out of cowardice, but because instinct was already screaming at her to get out while she still could. But she did not move. Gilbert stood a little in front of her, partly shielding her. Light still trembled around his hand, and the sight of it made breathing a little easier.

“So,” he said almost lightly, never taking his eyes off Faerd, “now that the bracelets are gone, I get to see how much I missed this.”

Faerd remained just as still.

“You entered Ogrom. You destabilized order,” he went on.

“Did you rehearse that in front of a mirror?” Gilbert asked dryly.

“Maybe don’t provoke him even more,” Sakura muttered.

“Let him get angry,” Gilbert said.

And he did not wait for the enemy to strike first. He moved first himself, lifting his hand to loose another arc of lightning.

But Faerd was faster.

Sakura did not even understand how he moved or where he shifted to. She only saw his hand rise slightly, and in the next second the air before him darkened, filling with obsidian daggers.

“Hide!” Gilbert barked, and this time Sakura obeyed without thinking.

She threw herself down and to the side, behind the nearest large piece of broken wall. The stone slammed painfully into her shoulder, dust burst into her face again, and in the very next second the first daggers drove into the rubble above her head with a hard crack.

Gilbert also slipped out of the line of attack, diving behind a surviving jut of wall.

Obsidian punched through the room with terrifying ease.

“Gods,” Sakura whispered through her broken breathing.

Faerd wielded obsidian magic and served the government. A man who might easily have been mistaken for a monster himself.

Sakura did not even try to wonder where someone like him had come from. Deep down, she already understood. She only clenched her hands tighter, feverishly thinking about something else: would her skills even work on an enemy like this? And did she have any right to try?

[DEVOURER] was not an ordinary strike. It was not simply to wound, not simply to kill. It was consumption. Violent, dreadful, final. And yet the one standing before her now was still a man, not an obsidian creature.

At the thought of using it on a person, everything inside her tightened with a dull, almost animal revulsion. Not only toward Faerd, but toward herself as well.

A sharp crack rang out, and Sakura looked out for a split second.

Gilbert thrust a hand out from behind the wall and answered with a white-blue flash. The magic burst outward with the same sharp crack as before, like lightning. It struck where Faerd had been standing just a second ago.

But he had already shifted. He was too fast.

Gilbert cursed through his teeth and almost immediately struck again. The white-blue bolt tore forward, ripping through the air, but Faerd evaded again.

“Damn you,” Gilbert spat.

Faerd did not look impressed. Not a single muscle moved in his face.

“Too slow,” he said evenly.

Then he raised his hand.

At first Sakura thought the daggers would appear again. But this time the obsidian gathered differently. The black gleam pulled into one point, thickened, and swelled until a massive sphere hung in front of Faerd’s palm. Crimson veins pulsed inside it, as if a molten heart were beating there.

Sakura went cold.

The sphere was too large.

It would not simply kill them.

It would blow the Drunken Lady apart down to the last beam. And it would kill anyone who might still be below: wounded people, drunks, whoever had not gotten out in time.

“Gilbert...” Sakura breathed.

He had already understood without her. She saw the tension in his back and the renewed flare of light around his fingers. He was preparing another strike, but even she could see he would not be fast enough.

Faerd watched them with the same emotionless calm.

“Violators must be eliminated,” he said. “Collateral damage is acceptable.”

Something inside Sakura suddenly snapped. There was no more time to think. If she did not do this now, they would die.

And not only them.

Sakura slowly rose from behind the stone. Her heart pounded so hard it throbbed in her temples. Her hand trembled as she stretched it out toward Faerd. Toward a man.

She clenched her teeth.

“I’m sorry,” Sakura whispered under her breath, not even knowing whether she meant him or herself.

And she activated [DEVOURER].

A system message flashed before her eyes at once:

[Warning: irreversible effect.]
