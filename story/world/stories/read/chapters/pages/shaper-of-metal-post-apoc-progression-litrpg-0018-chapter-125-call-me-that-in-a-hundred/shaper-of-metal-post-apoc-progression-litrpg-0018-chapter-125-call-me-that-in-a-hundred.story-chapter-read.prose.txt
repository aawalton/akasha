Chapter 125: Call Me That in a Hundred

Délight laughed prettily at Jack’s quip. “That he does, Ferro. That he does.” After a pause, she said, “In any case, I’ll do whatever I can to help them. Hiccup and Chime, I mean. If they let me. I-... I don’t know if they’ll hate me. I’m dreading that possibility.”

Jack just nodded. He had no idea, really. He hoped not. The whole ‘PACCs teammates’ thing was a quagmire for relationships. That made him think of the other team they’d beat. “It might be inappropriate, but I gotta ask… how exactly did you get info out of Don Quillote?”

Délight shrugged with playful airs. “I gave him a massage. And I listened to him with a sympathetic ear. We made out a little, as I couldn’t resist the bonding moment, but that was afterward, ironically. Probably a good thing. If he wasn’t so relaxed already, we might’ve… well.”

“And he just… spilled the beans?”

“Yes. So many beans, too. He holds a lot of animosity toward Corroline and a not-insignificant amount toward the other queen bee of the group. I can’t blame him. Ten might just be trying to hold a group together as a non-natural leader, but Corroline is the absolute biggest bitch on the planet. Incredibly talented, but at what cost? A superiority complex dialed not to ten, but eleven — her own, very special number.”

Jack grunted disfavorably. “So he did it basically to piss them off?”

Délight was playful again, as she turned her head sideways and smiled. “Aren’t my charms enough?” At Jack’s snicker, she shrugged and continued, “I think that’s the primary motivation, yes. He’s young, and he’s acting out, disgruntled with his position, absorbing Corroline’s awful personality again and again, grinding his teeth and keeping his head down for the good of the team. I believe the first thing he said to me was, ‘I’m not supposed to talk to you.’ And I replied, ‘Do you always do what the girls want? Is it what you want?’ Something like that. That was practically all that was needed to stir the fierce, rebel heart inside, already ready to burst out. As he should! It’s terrible to keep that all locked up and festering.”

“I guess.” Jack shook his head, amused. “It’s too easy for you, sometimes, eh?”

“Indeed.” Her smile was cheshire as she eyed him. “Some trees have harder wood, but the flow is much sweeter when finally tapped.”

Jack rolled his eyes, though they both knew she got him good with that. His cheeks were feeling a little hot. “Quite an analogy you got there. Some aren’t tappable, though.”

“Or so they think, hmm?”

Jack snickered in what he intended to be a sign of exasperation, but somehow ended up smiling as he regarded her evenly. “I’m going to go back to my friends, now, Délight.”

“By all means, but before you do, a question: is… now a good time to resolve the girl glaring daggers at me, well-deserved though it is? I need to throw myself at her feet and beg for forgiveness, that sort of thing.”

“A new fetish?” Jack teased.

“Mmmn, might be?”

Chuckling, Jack shook his head. “I’d save it for the party. It’s not like we see the damn thing as a fun time, anyway, and I think Light is the type that needs a lot of convincing. She’s going to puzzle and de-puzzle your every word and action, going through every ulterior motive she can imagine, admitting any point grudgingly at best. You wouldn’t want to give her a convenient excuse to leave prematurely.”

Délight nodded slowly to this, absorbing his words carefully. “Thank you, Ferro. Do you know what it was with us, at the core?”

“I can imagine, but save it. If anyone shares it, let it be her. She’s way less open than you are, and you know it.”

Délight grinned. “Wise, as always.” Her grin faded. “Sorry if I’m pushing the envelope too much. I’ll stop.”

Jack shrugged. “Eh. You are who you are, and I like people how they are. It doesn’t bother me. It's been a little minute since I had this amount of attention and flattery from women. Actually, probably more than ever. I’m hardly fed up with it, I’ll put it that way. Just, ah, disarmed, maybe.”

“Disarmed,” Délight repeated, and then suddenly snapped her fingers, as if realizing something. “So that’s why I delight in teasing you!”

They both laughed. He felt just a bit reluctant that he was enabling her, but whether her accursed charms were still working on him somehow or not, he wanted her authentic friendship, fly to a spider that it might be. He didn’t fully know why, either, but he did. It wasn’t romantic — Jack had no inclination to be one of her ‘boyfriend jackets’ she wore with the seasons, and anyone who thought it could be persuaded or tamed out of her was an idiot asking for heartache.

Délight shifted her gaze. “I think I’ll try and be first in line for the cake-cleaning, to get the hell out of here.” She touched Jack’s arm briefly. “See you, Long and Gorgeous.” She winked up at him and slid away.

He watched her go, briefly.

Let’s all stay alive. Call me that in twenty, in fifty, like an old joke. Call me that in a hundred, as we dine in a rebuilt city in Europe, dreaming up the next wonder we’ll raise.

An odd thought out of nowhere, perhaps, but it felt like reason enough to make whatever amends could be made, with anyone so willing. He believed fully that they were all now in this together. Every step he took from here on out would be in collaboration with his fellows. Even the competition had been. If some were, quote, ‘stepping on’ others, it was entirely in pre-arranged agreement, to reach higher as a whole, as a society, and even as a species.

It could be seen positively as well as negatively/bitterly, as Lighthouse had previously framed it. The winners solemnly take on more responsibility, and the losers strive harder to achieve, coming from behind. Nothing stopped them from achieving even more, from overtaking — nothing except time. If they really wanted it, they’d get it.

Jack had to shake off the high-minded philosophizing, as he could still feel the judgmental glare of Lighthouse on him. Taking a deep breath, he walked over to face it.

As he came up, on cue, Lighthouse commented sarcastically, “Well, look who was just having a grand ole time with his bestest buddy, Délight.” She crossed her arms and squinted at him. “What the hell, man? Are you insane?”

Jack displayed his hands and made an apologetic face. “Look, I don’t want to offend you, Light, but we are bound to end up working together with her one way or another. The ANP world is a very small one. Aside from that, I authentically believe she’s making changes in her behavior, and wouldn’t be here if she were simply faking it. It’s not like she can fool Memoria.”

“Pfft!” Lighthouse shook her head, unmoved. “You’ve got no idea how bad the history is. You’re just buying the sweet song she’s singing to lure you into her web, like every other boy who catches sight of her.”

“And some girls,” Highfive murmured out the side of his mouth.

“Shut the frag up, Fiver,” Lighthouse said without missing a beat, never turning or mitigating her insistent glare at Jack. “It’s not like it’s just about me. I’m telling you for your own damn good, Jack. I mean, shit, you were selling it to me how little you wanted to do with her, not all that long ago!”

Jack blinked. “I mean, that feels like an eternity ago, Light. Shit happens. What’s included between then and now? A crazy ass field mission all three of us were a part of.”

“Hey, watch it, now,” Mini chimed in warning. “Don’t give details.”

As he should’ve expected, Highfive was lit up with curiosity. “Damn. Really? Must’ve been something.” He wore a wistful smile. “Wow. I’m… going to be doing field missions.” He thumbed at himself, beaming with pride, and repeated, “I’m gonna be doing field missions! Bro, I’m so psyched!”

Jack moved in and clapped him on the arm. “Damn right, dude! Ain’t that right, Light?! We should totally be celebrating in a fantastic, good mood right now!”

Lighthouse glowered at him sullenly, sighed, and rolled both her eyes and head the other way, to Bo, who met her eyes and shrugged in a silent exchange, in something Jack imagined to be mutual ‘exasperation with the boys.’

But he won, because Lighthouse dropped the subject. For now, anyway.

Bo’s eyes shifted to regard each of them. “So. Is it… us, now, you think?”

Everyone looked at each other, and each of them, aside from Lighthouse, either frowned or looked down.

Lighthouse huffed. “Wow. Don’t get too excited by the prospect.”

“Sorry, Roughcat,” Highfive said, apparently dropping an old nickname for his friend. “It’s just…”

“Inkblot,” Bo finished.

“Yeah.”

“Anyway,” Jack added, “we look forward to working together with you. Me for the first time, at least directly.”

“We,” Lighthouse repeated under her breath, then cleared her throat. “Right. Well. Metal and lightning? It doesn’t take a genius to see some synergies there.” Jack just smiled and nodded to this, and Lighthouse’s gaze shifted to Highfive. She punched his shoulder hard enough to make him either wince or pretend to wince. “They'd better let us work together! We’re practically married.”

Highfive rolled his eyes and rubbed his shoulder. “If you were straight…” He suddenly got a mischievous glint in his eyes. “Actually, I would’ve tossed you off the Plat by now instead of marrying you, if we were ever a thing. You’re already a pill as a friend.”

Lighthouse screwed up her face. “What?!” she squawked and tried another punch with more force, but Highfive was ready and dodged it, backing and sliding away defensively in an overboard manner, grinning. Lighthouse’s glare diminished as she tracked him eye-to-eye, pockmarked with subtler signs of playfulness breaking through. “Payback will be painful, Fiver. Believe it.”

Highfive moved his hands up and did a little ridiculous hip-sway dance. “Not with these moves, it won’t!” He even added biting his lip.

Jack laughed, and Lighthouse obviously struggled to keep a straight face. Bo seemed… happy, subtly, even as she shook her head.“Psh.” Lighthouse shifted her gaze around. “If I ever married a guy, it would’ve been Brushstroke. He still sometimes confuses my sexuality with his face.”

Bo made a throaty sound to that. “Mmm. Good taste. I flirted with him once. Had no clue he was taken, and of course, his girlfriend shows up. She wasn’t happy.”

Lighthouse gasped like she was watching a soap opera. “Nooo… How have I not heard about this?! Okay, spill every drop of this tea right now, damn it — I need it with so much work bullshit polluting my brain.”

“I mean, it sucked, is why I haven’t spilled, but sure, whatever… Right, so, I just happened to be passing by his table when…”

Jack quickly tuned out the girl talk and said low to Highfive, “Gonna say a quick word to Lindsay before this shit is done with.”

Highfive nodded and gave a thumbs up, so Jack circled widely around the girls to head over to Lindsay, who was talking with two gents. One was gaunt and skinny, the other baby-faced and rosy-cheeked, all smiles.

Lindsay cleared her throat and gestured at Jack as he approached, as she said, “Thread Bear, Snapper, this is Jack; Jack, Thread Bear and Snapper.”

Ah! The top team. Highfive and Bo talked about ‘em. A metamorph tank and a degen blaster. An invincible team all around, supposedly.

Jack and the smiling fellow both moved so simultaneously to shake hands that they nearly jabbed each other. Instead, they compensated, to momentarily do an awkward forearm grab that was soon just as awkwardly aborted back to a proper handshake.

I’m an idiot.

“Snapper,” the smiling man said, with a smile somewhat strained by embarrassment. His smile also seemed to refuse meeting his eyes somehow, in an effect that was slightly unsettling. “It’s an honor to meet the illustrious Ferronaut!” His voice was light, buttery smooth, and had an overly mature quality for his presumable age. “A fierce reputation earned in such a short time.”

“Luck was part of it, probably,” Jack replied, returning the smile as they shook. “But I’ve heard you guys have stood the test of time and all-comers.”

“Ach, well, we did okay.” The tilt of his smile was now ironic.

Jack chuckled but didn’t reply, as the other, Thread Bear, was moving in for a surprisingly firm shake despite his appearance. His eyes were very calm, and he gave off reserved airs. “Nice to meet you,” Jack added.

“Likewise,” the gaunt man said with a respectful nod, “and please, call me Thready.”

“You got it, Thready. I like it!”

“I like your handle as well, it almost feels… artistic. It’s a shame we didn’t get to compete. You know, with each other?”

“Indeed,” Snapper said, “it had all the makings of an epic showdown!”

Jack winced slightly and waved a hand. “Nah. You guys would’ve creamed us. I think that was clear to the brass, too, because here we are together anyway. There was no point.”

Thready seemed to consider his words, nodding hesitantly. “If that’s your opinion. I feel there is always a point; every battle, we learn new things. Nonetheless, match-ups are a consideration. Consider, though… You never know what you’ll face out in the field. I’m game for a post-PACCs exhibition match, if the opportunity ever arises.”

“Oh, come now.” Snapper held up a hand and glanced at Jack apologetically. “You’ll have to excuse my partner, he’s a bit overzealous about these things. Let’s not spoil the energy, let’s read the room, Thready, hmm?”

“It’s fine,” Jack said, nodding and including them both in it. “I’d be happy to, if indeed we ever get the chance. I might be a tad overzealous myself, honestly. Come to think of it, my teacher is right here.” He gestured to Lindsay, indicating her and getting her attention. “What’s the word, then? Was I epically zealous, inspired, brilliant, awe-inspiring, or was I some other equally great descriptor?”

Lindsay’s eyebrows climbed with each word as she made a comical, dubious expression, eyes shifting around. “Did you confuse yourself with someone else present, Jack? If there was one word for you, it would be difficult.”

As Snapper snickered at the exchange, Jack nodded in false smugness at Lindsay. “Difficult to find words for my genius? Excellent. Here I was thinking you might humble me.”

Lindsay rolled her eyes.

Snapper read the situation quickly. He looked over past Jack and snapped his fingers. “Is that Highfive free?! We have to congratulate him, Thready, he’s bounced back so wonderfully, come on, then!” He grabbed his teammate's arm and started pulling away.

“Oh…kay…” Thready muttered as he was helplessly tugged along.

When they were gone, Lindsay raised an eyebrow at him. “So, are you going to scold me for the trickery?”

Jack put on a stern expression, one hand on a hip, and wagged a finger at her. “Naughty girl. Bad.”

“Hmmph. Always. Okay, consider me scolded. Anything else?”

“Well, first of all, thanks for getting me here, Shifu. I appreciate all you’ve done for me, even though you’re a royal brat and a half.”

“Takes one to know one. You’re welcome, Agent Bratonaut. Hug?”

“Yes, please, Agent Lamesay.”

Grinning widely, Lindsay hugged him, and he returned it, squeezing with extra gusto at the end — enough to get that satisfying, ‘eugh!’ sound out of her.

She grimaced at him after they parted. “Damn you and your stupid muscles, you got way stronger on me — I can feel it. I’m such a lightweight…”

“Except virtually,” Jack corrected.

“Ha. Yeah. I’m a goddess, there.”

“So, what happens now? The PACCs are over, at least officially.”

“I’m not sure yet about you specifically, Champ. I think your, ah, ‘Big One’ mission still needs another level. There’s some shit going down, and they want recruits, so trust me when I say this ain’t just about you.”

Jack did a fake gasp. “This entire shindig, not just for me?! How will I ever recover from this disappointment?”

“I dunno, consolation from a girlfriend?”

Jack carefully moderated his visible response. “Any suggestions there?”

Lindsay remained coy. “Well, you gotta start by getting one.”

“Again, any suggestions?”

Lindsay gave him a headcocked sideways ‘look,’ eyes widening. A dangerous pause. This began to seriously confuse him; then, she said, “Ira, I think it is?”

Jack blinked and then coughed into a hand. “Right.” Lindsay was staring at him smugly. Knowingly. He took a deep breath as he gave her his own subtle ‘look’ of admonishment. “As you surely know, that’s just a tad bit complicated.”

Lindsay shrugged. “Are you going to put that on your social profile? Relationship status: It’s Complicated?”

“I might.”

“Look, I’m just getting you back for those flowers, you sonuvabitch.”

“Yeah, what an awful bastard I am, sending stupid, awful flowers.”

“Don’t you dare besmirch them! They’re really beautiful flowers…” She said it like that fact was somehow painful.

Jack just shook his head.

“Hey! I heard that!” she called with a pointed finger.

“Heard what?!”

She made some sort of mocking, miming stance like she was holding a cup, and made a ridiculous, mean face. In a bad, faux ‘man’ voice, she called, “Women!” then threw her head back and scoffed exaggeratedly.

Jack stared at her incredulously. “I did not react anything like that.”

“I got two ladies in my department telling me, ‘Girl, you better put a ring on it!’ I might be exaggerating, but do you get my point, now!?”

“I suppose…” He did not. “Sorry.”

Lindsay suddenly smiled in maybe false cheer. “No worries! Send alcohol next time!”

“Got it.” Happy to change the subject, he quickly asked, “Our clearance is higher, now, right? They can know about… Ira?”

“Not yet, at least not officially. The basic security level clearance of a full ANP is ‘V.’ Yours should bump from W-point-S to V-point-S after the ceremony. Certain things you know will still be higher.”

“Ah. Well, here’s hopin’, I guess.”

“You’ll be busy. You’ll be back in class and buried in homework. You’re still way behind in knowledge.”

“I see. I’ll survive, I think. Can I ask… What are the odds of Inkblot getting his jacket anytime soon?”

Lindsay gave a doubtful shrug. “He’s got baggage and joined a perfect group for two showings. You three are collectively enough to occupy four, without going down, for very intense periods, giving him free rein. Bo’s disruptions seal the deal. On top of it, you went out of your way to recruit him. Inkblot has to prove various things on a new team at this point. Firstly, getting on one. You damn sure helped him out. He’ll likely have multiple recruiters. But he’s still got to go through the process. Feel free to tell him this if you want, after the official ceremony.”

Jack didn’t bother complaining. He just nodded. “Thanks for trusting me with the info.”

Jack soon disengaged from the conversation with Lindsay and ended up shaking hands with a few others, including Homage, who cheerfully congratulated him. He’d been present because he had been Highfive’s assigned ‘handler’ or whatever.

The socialization period of the little hazing event died down as the ‘cleaner’ came, lugging a large, loud, backpack-operated machine looking something like a high-tech leaf blower. It made a hellacious racket internally. Instead of sucking or blowing, though, it just… annihilated all traces of the cake, even the smell. They were given heavy-duty blackout eyewear to avoid eye damage, but that was all. The air had a brief burnt smell, and there was a medicinal sting on the skin, but then quickly nothing as it finished. It was definitely a hair-raising experience. Homage said it was an old invention primarily for getting rid of scents in the field, and that in a pinch, it was a great ‘shower.’

When Bo first saw it in action, she looked at Jack and said, “I’m going to get one, just as a failsafe to defeat that cologne of yours.”

Jack scratched his too-smooth chin and frowned. “I dunno… The cologne might still win.”
