At the end of the second day, no news came from the far-ranging scouts. They’d been told to travel out for a day and then return the next evening, but the next evening came and went and no news was had.

“I’m sure Grisim is okay,” Kelia told Caleb as he paced in their tent waiting for news.

Bog sat on one side, heat radiating off of him as Kelia stood opposite of him, sapping all the heat from her side of the room.

Caleb could tell that she was lying to make him feel more at ease.

Before he could say as much a horn range out through the camp signaling the return of a patrol.

They made eye contact briefly before both wordlessly running out of their tent into the cold mountain night. Even with his boosted constitution Caleb was chilled without the proper clothing. Bog loped behind them, even he had been sad at Grisim’s absence though Caleb couldn’t tell if that was a result of their bond or some unseen relationship his brother and dragon had.

The ran straight to Canus’ tent, pushing through a line of legionaries circling it. They each wanted news of what was happening but lacked the rank to go any closer. Caleb was uncertain if he had sufficient rank to butt in on the meeting, but he found he didn’t care.

He’d not given the legion’s ranking system the respect he suspected he owed it since joining, and feared that would eventually bite him in the end, but that hadn’t happened yet and this wasn’t the time for him to be timid.

They burst through the tent to see Canus talking to a man, his legionnaire uniform torn to tatters under his armor. Mettius stood to the side listening intently.

“Magnus?” Caleb found himself asking the man.

All eyes turned to the new entrants and Canus looked up as if beseeching the elder’s for aid.

“Yes,” Canus said. “He is the only scout to return. He was just telling me what happened before you barged in.”

“Sorry,” Caleb said quickly.

“I suppose you can stay,” Canus said, gesturing for Magnus to sit.

He looked from Canus and then to Mettius, seemingly for approval, before continuing.

“We were a day out, and had walked our assigned perimeter near the rebel village of Frost Valley, but we decided not to go in. We’d seen no sign of corrupted the whole journey. I didn’t see what happened. Suddenly everything went dark and I heard an attack come. We were jumped, tied up and brought into a cave—though we couldn’t tell at the time. The darkness stayed with us. I managed to break free of my bonds and escape, but a guard noticed me before I could free anyone else. I killed him but the commotion drew attention and I couldn’t stay to cut the others free.”

“What can you tell us about those that captured you?” Canus demanded.

“They were initialized for certain. It took two men to hold me down, so they are either low level or low tier. They dragged us into the village through the front gate. The darkness effect was a bubble of darkness, not blindness so this would have been visible to anyone in town.”

“So the town is complacent,” Canus said.

“Some of the town,” Magnus cut in. “There were other’s captured in the cellar I was in, outside the darkness bubble. They looked like villagers—elderly, children, a few able bodied men. They were all tied up.”

“Loyalists?” Canus asked.

“If I had to guess.”

“Where did they get initialized from? Those villages shouldn’t have had enough to jump your company.”

“I don’t know. I never saw any. The one holding me prisoner remained in the darkness. I had to wrestle myself free of him.”

“What about my brother?” Caleb asked.

He’d been patiently listening but could hold back the question no longer.

“He was with me when I was captured,” Canus said. “But he wasn’t brought to the same room as I was.”

Canus ordered someone to fetch a map of Frost Valley, and Magnus outlined his trip as best as he could.

“Muster the men to strike camp, we will set out to take the village at first light,” he commanded one of his Decurions who’d entered—after knocking and being admitted.

“Morning?” Caleb asked. “We need to go now. Send us on horses and we could be there before you’d even struck camp.”

“No,” Canus said firmly. “We will leave at first light and be at Frost Valley by nightfall. At which point we will send a armed delegation requesting they turn over their prisoners. If they refuse, we siege the city until they comply and wait for reinforcements.”

“That will take too long!” Caleb demanded, and he felt Kelia’s hand on his shoulder pulling him back.

He hadn’t realized he’d stepped toward Canus.

Canus took a deep breath.

“I have given you too much leeway due to your value and inexperience, but you must control your tongue. Not even an Equites is above the chain of command. I understand you want to save your brother, but I was sent to scare a rebellion into submission, not wage a war against an unknown number of initialized. I will not march my men into an ambush.”

Caleb was about to protest again when Kelia squeezed his shoulder and Mettius shook his head once side to side.

“Fine,” he said. “I’ll go pack.”

“You have people for that actually,” Mettius said, “Let me get you a drink.”

Mettius gave Canus a meaningful look, and dragged Caleb out of the tent.

“I don’t want a drink—” Caleb said, being pulled along by one arm while Kelia held onto his other trailing behind.

Mettius ignored Caleb’s protests but did eventually let go of him when he reached their tent.

“Get your gear,” Mettius said. “We are going to sneak out while the camp is packing.”

“Wait? What?” Caleb said, still fuming over being taken to get a drink while his brother was held prisoner.

“What? You don’t want to go anymore?”

“No, why the change of heart?”

Mettius sighed.

“It’s called a lie. I know you are familiar with them. I have reason to believe waiting would be unwise, but not enough evidence to convince Canus of that.”

“What evidence?” Caleb demanded.

“Do you want to sit here and discuss it, or do you want to go and save your brother?” Mettius demanded.

Caleb looked at Kelia who nodded.

“Let’s go, but you’re explaining on the way.”
