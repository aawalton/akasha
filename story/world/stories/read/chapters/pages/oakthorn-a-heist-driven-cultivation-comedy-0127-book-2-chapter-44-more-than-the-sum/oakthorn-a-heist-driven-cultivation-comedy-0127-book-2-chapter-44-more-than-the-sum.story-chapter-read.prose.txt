Golden light burned through her eyelids as Chubbs strained against the magical bindings cutting into her wrists.

Each breath felt like swallowing fire. Her small body was suspended within the pulsing emerald sphere at the ritual’s heart. She couldn’t move. She couldn’t scream. Chubbs could only watch as everything she loved was being torn apart.

And it was all because of her.

Her vision swam with tears as Cade fought for her life across the crumbling cathedral. The boy who had protected her, carried her on his shoulders, and told her stories when nightmares came. The boy who had lied to her.

Why? The question burned like acid. Why didn’t he tell me?

Bernard—or rather, the thing that had once been Bernard—stalked closer to Cade, emerald energy seeping from his eyes and the crown’s six swiveling orbs. The ritual pulled at something deep within her, something ancient and familiar that she couldn’t quite grasp. It was like trying to remember a dream upon waking, only to have it slip away the moment she opened her mouth to explain it to Bunny.

“I see you understand nothing if you think me as low as one of Death’s progeny,” Bernard spat, his voice layered with something ancient and terrible. “And what you’ve done to my seal is meaningless. This ritual was designed to withstand the interference of gods. What chance does a mortal thief have?”

Chubbs watched Cade’s face, recognizing the look he got when he was planning something. It was the way he slightly narrowed his eyes and the almost imperceptible quirk of his lips. He was stalling—but for what?

“Fair points,” Cade replied, edging backward. “But you know what they say about old dogs and new tricks.”

She saw the moment his heel caught on debris—too perfect to be accidental. His hand brushed the floor and a flicker of crimson-gold energy briefly illuminated his fingertips. Chubbs felt the ritual shudder around her, its steady pull faltering for an instant before resuming.

He’s trying to change it, she realized. He’s trying to save me.

Her heart swelled and broke in the same breath. Even now, after she’d felt his betrayal like a knife between her ribs, he was risking everything for her. She needed to help him—needed to break free. But how?

Chubbs closed her eyes, searching inward as Cade had once taught her during one of their quiet moments on the road. “Magic comes from your core,” he’d explained, pointing to his chest. “It’s a part of you, like your heart or your lungs. You just need to learn how to listen to it.”

She stretched her awareness inward, past the pain, past the fear, searching for the well of power she somehow knew already existed within her. What she found wasn’t emptiness, but a wall—no, a lock. A complex, interlocking seal of golden light wrapped around her core like a cage.

The young girl poked and prodded at this metaphysical barrier. It didn’t budge. In fact, it appeared to grow more sturdy now that she was actively attempting to bypass it.

“You’re not supposed to be here,” she kindly informed the spherical seal in her soul.

This wasn’t part of Bernard’s ritual, that much she knew. The restraint felt... familiar. Almost like her own handwriting.

I did this to myself, she realized with a horrifying clarity. I locked my own power away. Stupid, me! Why would you do that to me?

The cathedral shuddered violently as Cade’s changes finally took effect. The ritual’s pull shifted, no longer draining her quite as fiercely—but still drawing something vital from her. She watched in horror as wisps of energy began streaming from everyone in the cathedral instead, including Cade.

“What have you done?!” Bernard roared, his corrupted face contorted with fury.

“Can’t you feel it?” Cade replied, the smug tone unable to mask his growing exhaustion.

Bernard staggered, momentarily disoriented as his own power fed the ritual. Chubbs could see green tendrils streaming from his body into the stone floor, the flow of energy now reversed. Despite her pain, a tiny smile graced her lips.

Clever Cade.

That part of her brain that never quite shut off, spiritual lock notwithstanding, explained exactly how her thief accomplished this. That same part of her told her that she’d written the book on how to do it.

But I don’t know how to read! the other, louder, part of her cried.

Her relief was short-lived. Despite Cade’s changes, she could still feel something being pulled from her—slower, but steady and unwavering. Golden essence continued to seep from her pores, feeding the growing fissure where massive roots pushed through from another realm.

“I see you understand,” Bernard said, circling Cade like a predator. “Your ‘solution’ merely changes who suffers. A fitting moral quandary for one who fancies himself a hero.”

“I’m no hero,” Cade replied, his voice hardening. “I’m just a thief who really doesn’t take kindly to you murdering my surrogate sister, is all.”

Sister. The word echoed through Chubbs like a bell, unlocking something warm and painful. Family.

Bernard’s next words sliced through her momentary comfort. “And what about my family? What I’m doing is setting the cosmic scales back into balance, you shortsighted dullard. This is about justice. Vengeance. Through this Throne, I will restore what was ripped from us.”

“Us?” Cade prompted.

With each word Bernard spoke, fragments of memory flickered at the edges of Chubbs’ consciousness—images of towering beings wreathed in light, of battles that shattered continents, of betrayal and blood and screaming that never seemed to end.

“Mock if you wish. But we are more now than either of us could’ve been apart,” Bernard continued, the mortal and monster speaking as one. Then the mortal’s voice took over. “He has given me such insights into the mind, Cade. He predates humanity and other mortalkinds by centuries. He’s not just a Progenitor. He’s the firstborn child of Life.”

Chubbs gasped, the sound lost within her magical prison. The words triggered something—a memory of cradling a newborn being of pure light, of teaching him to shape his magic with nothing but thought and will. Of watching him grow strong and beautiful and terrible.

My nephew, after a sort, she thought with a jolt of recognition that made no sense. But that can’t be right. I’m just a little girl. I don’t have nephews.

Yet the memory felt more real than the bindings cutting into her wrists.

“And let me guess,” Cade said, edging closer to her while keeping his movements casual. “Family reunion with pops didn’t go so well?”

“They murdered us,” Bernard hissed, genuine pain bleeding through the ancient rage. “Their own children. They made us, shaped us, then destroyed us when they discovered more obedient toys to play with.”

Each word drove splinters of memory deeper into Chubbs’ mind. She saw herself standing alongside other radiant beings, watching in horror as creatures of impossible beauty and power were unmade, their essence scattered to the winds. She remembered... choosing a side.

No, she thought desperately. That wasn’t me. It couldn’t have been.

But deep down, a part of her knew the truth.

As Cade and Bernard continued their deadly dance of words and magic, Chubbs redoubled her efforts against the golden lock within her core. She pushed against it with everything she had, but it held firm, unyielding as a diamond or Bunny with a pastry.

“Please,” she whispered to the power locked away inside her. “I need to help him. I need to save Cade.”

The lock pulsed once in response but remained intact. Not a rejection—more like a warning.

Bernard’s voice cut through her concentration. “Do you think if someone forgets a crime they committed, that they shouldn’t be held accountable?”

His hand hovered inches from her face, having partially breached the protective barrier Cade had created. Terror froze her in place as those cold fingers drew closer, tipped with claws of emerald energy.

“Answer me!” Bernard demanded of Cade.

“Whatever happened,” Cade replied carefully, “it’s between you and the gods. Not her. She’s just a kid.”

“A vessel,” Bernard corrected. “As am I.” His gaze softened momentarily as he studied her face. “Though I admit, this face she wears is a convincing one. The fear in those eyes seems quite genuine.”

Chubbs glared back at him, defiance replacing fear as she saw something flicker across Bernard’s face—recognition, confusion, horror. For a brief moment, the real Bernard seemed to surface through the corruption.

“She’s... just a child,” Bernard’s voice was suddenly his own again, confusion replacing rage. “What am I—”

The crown flared with emerald fury, silencing Bernard’s brief resurgence. When he spoke again, the ancient resonance had returned, deeper and more malevolent than before.

“Irrelevant,” the Progenitor spat. “The vessel’s innocence changes nothing. My aunt shall pay for her cruelty, even if I cannot savor her despair and regret when I do.”

Aunt. The word crashed through Chubbs’ mind with the potency of a thunderbolt. With it came a cascade of memories—of shaping worlds from nothingness, of breathing life into the void, of creating the first magical cores that would one day become the foundation of all mortal power.

Of creating beings like this one before her alongside her siblings. Of watching them tear each other apart in a civil war that ravaged the forests and cities she planted.

Of making the hardest choice imaginable.

His hand plunged through the protective barrier, emerald energy cutting through Cade’s modifications as he reached for her throat.

“NO!” Cade screamed.

What happened next seemed to unfold in slow motion. Cade’s body blurred with impossible speed, the air around him cracking like thunder as he threw himself between her and Bernard. Crimson-gold energy formed a desperate shield as emerald power crashed into him with mountain-shattering force.

Bernard was sent flying backward by the impact, crashing through a brazier of green flames. Cade remained standing between them, his legs wobbling and blood trickling from his nose.

“It’s okay, Chubbs. I won’t let...” He stumbled to the side, his vision clearly failing him. “I won’t... I won’t let him... hurt you.”

“Careful what you promise,” Bernard’s voice growled from the billowing smoke.

Terror clawed at Chubbs’ throat as Bernard emerged, emerald energy coalescing around his fingertips. Cade tried to raise his shield again, but he was too slow, too drained.

The emerald lance punched through Cade’s chest in a spray of blood and golden energy.

“CADE!” Chubbs screamed, her voice suddenly deeper, older than it should have been.

Something inside her cracked.

Not the lock on her power—that remained stubbornly intact—but something deeper. More fundamental. The knowledge that she was about to watch someone she loved die because of what she had once been. Because of secrets she had kept, even from herself.

Rage replaced terror, white-hot and all-consuming.

“How dare you?” she whispered to the lock around her core. “How dare you keep what’s mine from me when I need it most?”

The lock pulsed again, more urgently this time. It was a warning, not a refusal. But she no longer cared about warnings or consequences. All that mattered was Cade, lying in a growing pool of his own blood as Bernard loomed over him.

“I’ve existed for millennia,” Bernard said, gathering emerald energy for a killing blow. “I’ve witnessed countless acts of heroism, each as pointless as the last. Yet they never cease to fascinate me.”

Chubbs hurled herself against the golden lock with all her strength, all her will, all her rage. Yet it held firm, though hairline fractures appeared across its surface. Golden light started to spill through through the cracks after each time she smashed her tiny form into the seal.

“What did you hope to accomplish? Truly?” Bernard asked, studying Cade with detached curiosity.

“I just... wanted...” Cade whispered, blood bubbling at his lips, “to be enough.”

The words shattered Chubbs. Even more memories flooded through the cracks in the lock—not in fragments now, but in a torrent impossible to deny.

She remembered creating the first weapons that could kill immortals. She’d figured out a way to distill a Celestial’s soul into crystalline form, and then forge those into tools. Blades. Axes. Spears.

She remembered teaching mortals to harness magic, to shape reality as the gods did. Some of them joined their ranks and her family grew.

She remembered her sister’s face, twisted with betrayal as Creation chose mortalkind over her own divine kin.

But most horrifying of all, she remembered him—a figure wreathed in crimson energy that crackled like lightning. His face remained obscured in her mind, as if her own subconscious still refused to acknowledge his existence even as the memories returned.

Who… Who are you?

“Ah. The eternal human lament,” Bernard said outside of her struggle with mind and magic, and emerald energy formed a sphere large enough to obliterate Cade entirely. “Rest assured, thief—you were never meant to be.”

The sphere descended toward Cade’s chest.

Time stopped.

In that frozen moment, Chubbs understood what the lock had been protecting her from. Not just her power, but the knowledge of what she had done with it. The choices she had made. The beings she had betrayed and destroyed in the name of a greater good that now seemed hollow and distant.

The faces of countless Progenitors—her own creations—gazed at her from the depths of memory, their expressions accusing as they were unmade.

This is the price, she realized. This is what I locked away. Not just power, but responsibility. Guilt. An eternity of consequences.

She looked at Cade—brave, clever, infuriating Cade—bleeding out on cold stone for her sake.

If he knew me, really knew me, he wouldn’t be so quick to defend my life, that small part of her growled from the darkness. She was coming to hate that part of her.

But she couldn’t deny that truth any longer. Cade was doing this for a being who had once helped orchestrate the downfall of an entire race. He was dying next to a person who had once deemed her own creations too dangerous to exist.

She made her choice.

Chubbs grabbed the metaphorical lock with both hands and pulled, not with the petulant rage of a child, but with the terrible resolve of a goddess who had once shattered magic to her will.

“You will not have him,” she snarled, her voice echoing with power beyond her small frame. “Not today. Not ever.”

The lock shattered.

Power flooded through her body, scorching away her bindings like paper in flame. Golden light erupted from her skin, her eyes, her mouth as millennia of compressed divinity exploded outward. The ritual circle cracked beneath her as she rose into the air, no longer a toddler but a girl on the cusp of adolescence, her form struggling to contain the power suddenly unleashed within it. The shackles on her wrists went taut as she floated just above the altar.

Memories crashed through her consciousness in an overwhelming tide—creation and destruction, love and betrayal, impossible choices made with imperfect wisdom. She saw herself standing beside Life and Death as they shaped the first mortals. She saw herself fleeing from Destruction’s wrath when their visions for creation diverged. She saw herself fall at her sister’s hands, choosing dissolution over continued conflict.

But the conflict never went away. It consumed every inch of her memories. She saw it in Life’s eyes as he argued with her to give him more dominion. She witnessed it as Death vowed vengeance as he cradled her sister’s limp form in his lap.

Chubbs could see that despite the name of her dominion, she was completely and utterly saturated with blood and ruin.

And through it all, like a crimson thread woven through the tapestry of her existence, she saw him—the one she had loved, betrayed, destroyed. The one whose name she still could not bear to remember.

She couldn’t face that truth right now, possibly not ever, but she could help someone else who was starting to fill that old chasm in her heart.

With a flick of her fingers, Chubbs conjured a metallic shield directly between Bernard’s hand and Cade’s face. The Progenitor’s sphere curled the reinforced steel, but she wasn’t some chump at this old game. She’d instilled absorption runes into her contraption.

The green light flickered and died off.

Bernard turned from Cade, recognition and triumph mingling in his corrupted features as he beheld her transformation. The emerald energy around his hand dissipated as he bowed in mocking reverence.

“At last,” he breathed, the crown’s eyes swiveling wildly in their sockets. “Creation returns to us. And just in time for me to kill someone she loves. I didn’t know you were still capable of that. I doubt you knew either.”

His many eyes flared with vermillion power and he prepared another killing blow.

But Chubbs wasn’t looking at Bernard. Her gaze remained fixed on Cade, on the life draining from his eyes with each labored breath. On the boy who had carried her when she was tired, who had made her laugh when she was sad, who had fought for her even when she was angry with him.

The boy who had loved her not for what she once was, but for what she had become.

“NO!” she screamed, the word carrying enough power to shake the cathedral’s foundations.

Whether she was addressing the Progenitor before her or the memories threatening to consume her remained unclear even to herself. All that mattered was Cade. All that in this moment mattered was saving him.

Golden light exploded from her in a concussive wave, slamming into Bernard with the force of divine judgment. His body flew backward, crashing through stone pillars as if they were made of sand. Where the light touched him, golden cracks spread across his corrupted flesh like a shattering porcelain mask.

When he finally skidded to a stop amid the rubble, Bernard pushed himself to his knees, blood trickling from the corners of his mouth. But instead of fury, his face held only wonder as he stared at her transformed appearance.

Where once a toddler no older than four had laid, a young woman of approximately ten resided. But none of that mattered now, as he eyes drifted to what Bernard was conjuring via the ritual circle. Her mind hadn’t accepted what it was before, but now she recognized it for what it was.

Her golden eyes flitted over the runes of this multiplexed ward. The craftwork and masonry were… passable. But the design—the design of this summoning circle was her own, with a few crude alterations that had nothing to do with Cade’s childish scrawling.

No.

She knew what Life’s firstborn was after, and if he succeeded they would all pay the price.

“You seek an Oakthorn Throne for yourself, Mordred?” Chubbs demanded.

“I’m just applying what your kind taught us, auntie,” Bernard—or Mordred—sneered. “The best way to kill a god is to become one. And thank you, for your contribution to the cause.”

He smiled cruelly, and she knew right then that she’d fallen directly into his trap.
