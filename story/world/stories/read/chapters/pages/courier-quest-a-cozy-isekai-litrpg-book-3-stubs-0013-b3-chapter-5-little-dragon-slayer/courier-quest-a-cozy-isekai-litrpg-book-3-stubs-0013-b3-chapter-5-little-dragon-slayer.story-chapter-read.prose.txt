“Of course I tried to get him to stay in Vilderchtif,” Gabin shamelessly admitted. “You married the man; you know his value more than anyone.”

“I sure do,” Adabelle agreed, squeezing her husband’s hand proudly.

She had wanted to return to dancing when they had finished their meal, but they had been assailed by well wishers the moment they left their table. They had been waiting for the mingling phase of the reception to start. Now that it had, there was a not-so-subtle line of them waiting for the newlyweds to be done with their current conversation so they could step in.

“Let me tell you, he was very brave that day, stepping up to those earth elementals without hesitation,” the French lord continued, nodding as he stroked Clay, who was on his shoulder. “He had been the first to warn us about the elder earth elemental, and then he dove right in! No thoughts in his rush to protect us! Thankfully, all he had to do was talk Clay into being a friend and everything worked out. I’ve never seen anything like it.”

Adabelle smiled as she heard the story. She had received a full recounting from both Trevor and Wayne before, which included the parts where he absolutely hesitated, but it was fun hearing someone talk up her man.

“I mean, I wouldn’t go that far,” the courier said, scratching his cheek. It was embarrassing hearing tales of his heroics when they were embellished like this.

“Please, Trevor,” Gabin chuckled. “Did you not yell black core before jumping into the pit?”

“Yeah, that is what happened,” he admitted.

The dwarf smacked him on the back. “That was a great show of heroism, young man,” he praised before turning back to Adabelle. “But I won’t keep you. If you or your husband ever want to come and visit, we folk at Vilderchtif will happily accommodate you.”

“As the mayor of Tosa, I happily extend the invitation for you here, too,” the noblewoman said with a smile. “I hope you enjoy our town, Lord Gabin.”

“I have so far, and I’m sure I will until I return,” he said with a grin.

Once he had excused himself and Clay returned to Trevor’s shoulder, it was clear that the other guests were ready to swoop in. A single voice cut over the rest, however.

“Lady Adabelle, Mister Trevor, I’m afraid I can’t leave the river,” Glyrphizith stated. Even though he had heard her speak before, the courier was surprised by how delicate and proper a dragon the size of a cargo ship could sound. “Could you both be dears and come to me?”

“Apologies,” Adabelle said to those who were already approaching. “It seems we are being beckoned.”

There was some grumbling, but everyone seemed to decide they weren’t as high up on the guest list as an actual dragon. They allowed the newlyweds to pass.

“I do hope I didn’t pull you away from anything important,” the dragon said as they approached. “It seemed you were done with that dwarven man, and I thought to grab you before someone else could.”

“Your timing was perfect, Glyrphizith. We appreciate your presence here today,” Adabelle greeted warmly.

Their largest guest was all smiles, and Trevor had to once again wrap his mind around how large she was. Just one of her wings had been enough to shade all of the guests in the party. Every one of her teeth were as tall as he was. He had no doubt that, if they were on Earth, she could play with actual cars as if they were children’s toys.

And yet, she was also one of the most polite people here. Her presence was soothing, even, in a way he wasn’t sure he’d be able to articulate.

Also, she was a dragon. Trevor had been preparing himself to meet Glyrphizith for a long time, and she didn’t disappoint.

“That’s good, I didn’t want to interrupt,” the dragon said, resting her jaw against the ground so they wouldn’t have to look up too high to see her eyes. The sun reflected off of her green scales nicely, though not enough to blind them. “I only wanted to say that I enjoyed the ceremony, and I’m glad to finally have met you Trevor. You’ve been a topic of ours for some time now.”

“I have?” he asked, blinking in surprise.

“Oh, yes,” Glyrphizith answered. “Adabelle comes out to say hello every few weeks to make sure I don’t get lonely without old Jackson to talk to.”

“I genuinely enjoy our chats,” the noblewoman replied with a smile.

“As do I, dear, as do I,” the dragon said, her small nods digging her jaw into the island’s grass. A look of nostalgia crossed her face as she looked up to the sky. “It’s your passion that I enjoy most, I think, even if I didn’t see it that way at first.”

“Well, those days are in the past where they belong,” Adabelle stated with a firm nod.

Trevor, however, caught something in her tone. “What happened back then?”

The demonkin’s tail squeezed his wrist, but Glyrphizith spoke first. “Why, the first time we met our dear Adabelle tried to slay me.”

“Did she, now?” he asked. If it weren’t for the dragon’s conversational tone, he might have been concerned. His wife had froze, however.

“Oh, yes. I had just flown into town in order to get one of my favorite clocks fixed. Some of them need a delicate touch, you see, and there’s a wonderful gnomish lad who can work miracles on my antiques. Anyway, I landed and waited, as I normally do. My presence is hard to ignore, and it’s usually not long before I get visitors. Imagine my surprise when this young lady shows up, all bravado, and brandishes her sword at me.”

“I was a different person back then,” Adabelle said, trying to stand tall but Trevor could see that it was her turn to be embarrassed.

“You were, you were,” Glyrphizith soothed before shifting her attention back to the courier. “She even struck me. Landed a clean hit against my jaw. It didn’t do her any good, though. I am quite old, even then, and my scales are very resilient.”

“And very shiny. The sunlight makes it look like you’re covered in emeralds,” Trevor complimented. Clay nodded vehemently on his shoulder.

“Aren’t you a charmer,” Glyrphizith said in a fit of giggles that matched her as well as her voice. “Adabelle did say you were a smooth one. I spent a long time making sure that I looked my best for our little dragon slayer’s big day, so I’m glad it’s noticeable.”

“Absolutely, ma’am,” he replied with a smile, trying not to laugh upon learning his wife’s nickname. “I’d even go so far as to say that you’re the second most eye-catching woman here today, and it’s not even close.”

The dragon beamed and tilted her head towards Adabelle. “You keep this one close, you hear?” she whispered, though it could clearly be heard by both of them.

“Yes, I hear,” Adabelle mumbled. Her tawny skin was flushed as she stood there and remembered the past. “In my defense, my behavior was corrected very quickly and no harm was done.”

“No harm was done, but you were suspicious of me for some time afterwards,” Glyrphizith gently corrected. “It wasn’t until I invited you to my home, where you brought your sword sisters just in case it was a trap, and spent hours hearing me talk about my clocks that you finally settled down.”

“She is quite the firebrand at times, isn’t she?” Trevor said, beaming at his wife.

“One of her better qualities, for sure,” she agreed.

Adabelle cleared her throat. “I’m simply glad that we have such a wonderful friendship now, Glyrphizith. I do believe we are being called, however, and there are other guests to see. Thank you for calling us over.”

“I really must be going, anyway,” she replied before looking at Trevor. “It’s almost five, and I try not to be close to town around that time.”

“Because Glyrphizith always roars at five,” the courier stated, having heard that line several times before.

“Exactly. People set their clocks to me, you know,” the dragon returned, clearly proud of that fact. “I do tend to be loud, sadly. The realities of the job. Goodbye, though! Enjoy your honeymoon, darlings. Trevor, dear, don’t hesitate to come and visit once you’ve returned.”

“Don’t worry, I’ll come and see your collection with our little dragon slayer sometime soon,” he said.

“Trevor,” Adabelle whined.

Glyrphizith laughed as she stood up to her full height. Her legs were as wide as Trevor’s house, and he had no doubt that she could eclipse the whole of Serna Island if she wanted to. Instead of flying away, however, she moved into the forest, careful not to knock down any trees, and only flapped her wings when she was a safe distance away.

Even then, they still felt the wind from her liftoff. The breeze was nice in the absence of the shade she had provided, even if it was short-lived.

“I was told that it was customary to bring a gift,” Melvirn said from behind them, and the two turned before Trevor could tease his wife some more.

“Melvirn, we’re glad you made it,” Adabelle greeted warmly as she looked upon him and the two with him. “Dorimy, Greer, you as well.”

“How could we possibly miss such a momentous occasion?” the medusa asked, a smile on her face as she corralled her snake-hair and patted them gently. As usual, she wore thick goggles that prevented people from seeing her eyes, and was the very picture of a proper woman.

“I was also told that it would be terrible to have missed such a momentous occasion,” Melvirn added. His dress robes were slightly disheveled, but it was clear that they had tried to make him presentable. Greer elbowed him in the side. “Anyway, please accept this.”

“There’s a place to put gifts, you know,” Trevor said as he watched the wizard remove a pair of black cubes from his pocket.

“Yes, but I was told that anything that might be handy on your honeymoon should be separated, then I wasn’t sure where that was, and it’s really worth a lot, so I decided to give it to you myself,” Melvirn said before offering them each a cube. “It’s for both of you, but I think it will probably serve Trevor more in the long term. Sorry, Lady Adabelle.”

“It’s fine, Melvirn,” the demonkin said as she took the cube, prompting her husband to do the same. “May I ask what they do?”

“Of course,” he replied, some excitement coming to his voice. “You see, they are a pair of extradimensional storage spaces that work much like Trevor’s backpack trinket does. Anything he puts inside of it can be removed from either his own cube or yours, and vice versa. They’re tethered to the same place, you see. It holds a little less than an Anderson Bag, but not by much. I figured it may come in handy, especially since I remember Trevor saying something about receiving a new ability at level twenty?”

The courier couldn’t help but chuckle at the spatial wizard’s questioning glance. Whenever the conversation turned to extradimensional spaces, he lit up with enthusiasm and would go on for hours if you would let them.

“I did get a new ability, yes,” Trevor confirmed. “Quest Master, will this work as intended if I Annex it?”

He had two Anderson Bags currently attached to his inventory, so he would have to give one up. A small price to pay in his opinion.

As much as he wanted to add it now, he held off. The Quest Master mentioned letting Adabelle attune to hers first, and he would heed that warning.

“I’ll Annex it after you’re attuned to yours, Adabelle,” he told his wife before looking at Melvirn. “And I’ll certainly stop by after the honeymoon so you can get some readings. Not excessive ones, though. Sound good?”

“Yes, I suppose so,” the wizard replied. He seemed a little crestfallen that he didn’t get to see it now, but he nodded in understanding.

“I’ve placed our gifts in the pile of things you can take with you,” Greer said, the halfling putting his hands in his pockets. “Nothing as big as that, but there’s still some top quality things in there.”

Adabelle smiled. “Thank you, everyone. We appreciate all three of you.”

Dory took a step forward, one arm around her snakes, and wrapped the newlyweds in a hug. They paused for a moment as the alchemist wasn’t usually one for physical affection, but returned it after they got over their surprise.

“You’re both indispensable to Tosa, so be safe out there, got it?” she whispered. “It’s a big world, and not all of it is as kind as we are.”

“We will,” Trevor promised, hugging both the medusa and his wife.

“Don’t you worry, Dorimy, we’ll be back before you know it,” Adabelle added. “Probably better than ever with all these courier quests we’ll be on.”

“I know, but I have to say it anyway,” she replied as she backed away, letting go of her hair. The snakes hovered over her shoulder even though she no longer propped them up.

The trio excused themselves, and Trevor and Adabelle tried to reach the dance floor again. It seemed as though there was no end to the guests, though, as they came and greeted them. After a few more conversations, they wrangled Rashie into helping them form a path, and were finally able to get to where they were going.
