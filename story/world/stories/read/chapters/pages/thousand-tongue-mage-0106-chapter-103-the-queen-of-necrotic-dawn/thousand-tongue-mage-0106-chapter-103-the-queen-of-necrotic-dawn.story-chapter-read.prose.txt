Regalia looked down upon the dead man in the hall, and everyone forgot how to breathe.

Every pair of lungs behind Zora stalled at once. Lords, nobles, and soldiers, it didn’t matter who was who. Everyone’s hearts were beating too fast and too hard. Even the air around Enki seemed to thin, like his cryogenic mist was afraid to touch what stood before the throne.

Zora’s own throat clenched.

Of course he knew the stories. Of course they all knew the stories. A child in the Attini Empire could barely say their first prayers without hearing her name. In the old and new texts, they were called the Greater Insect Gods of the Swarm, but in the Six Swarmsteel Fronts, they were often referred to by a much shorter word: calamity.

The Six Greater Insect Gods had arrived in Year Thirty-Three, thirty-three years after the Swarm first descended. They were six shadows falling in from six horizons, each one staking a claim on a corner of the burning world. They were the true lords of the bugs, and wherever they planted their feet, the war refused to budge in humanity’s favor.

In the far north, past the borders of the Hellfire Caldera Front, there was Sunbringer, the Greater Firefly God, who lit the sky with so much fire the night forgot its purpose.

To the southeast, over the Mori Masif Front, there was Black Witch, the Greater Silkmoth God whose wings smothered entire lines of cannons in one sweep.

To the northeast, over the Rampaging Hinterland Front, there was Mammot, the Greater Beetle God whose titan-kind turned mountains into stepping-stones.

In the northwest, somewhere in the death-scarred Plagueplain Front prowled Pestilence, an unknown thing whose poisons had once burned through five armies in five days and was now rumoured to be little more than a fading cough.

In the southwest waters of the Deepwater Legion Front lurked Corpsetaker, the Greater Crab God whose legions of crustaceans boiled up from the deep whenever men tried to build fleets in peace.

And then there was the weakest of the six, though that was only relative to the other Greater Insect Gods. ‘Weakest’ was a very comforting lie men in the Attini Empire had chosen to tell themselves.

Regalia, the Greater Ant God of the south, commanded every last bug battering the walls of the Attini Empire. Lesser Insect Gods had been slain before—here and there, by brilliance or miracle—but the Six Greater Insect Gods had never been felled. Not Sunbringer in the north, not Black Witch in the east, not Mammot or Corpsetaker, not even Pestilence, though rumors whispered that the Plagueplain tyrant had grown so weak they barely stirred anymore.

But those were only rumors.

Standing this close to Regalia, Zora knew immediately—down to the marrow of his bones—that this one was no rumor. No story sharpened by frightened tongues. Her aura pressed against his as if the air itself bent to carry her weight, and though her silhouette mimicked that of a human woman, every instinct in him recoiled from her inhuman stillness. Her perfect posture. That… that slight, slight bit of wrongness that seemed to coil beneath her chitined skin.

He tried to raise his staff. His fingers trembled, as he tried to speak.

“Strike,” he whispered.

And for the first time in two years, nothing answered him. His spell fell out as only a word, harmless as breath. His Art didn’t even attempt to stir.

… Shit.

I can’t even imagine myself striking her with a punch or something?

In response, Regalia rolled her neck once, vertebrae cracking like chitin snapping into place.

“A tragic outcome,” she said, her voice slipping between tones: queenly one moment, insectile the next. “Let us get this over with.”

She lifted her right hand, and the resurrected dead around the hall began picking up their blades. A few more quiet gasps tore through Zora’s ranks as most of them took a step back out of fright, but Zora sharpened his hearing instead, focusing on the living corpses.

He heard them inside each corpse, within their muscles, within their cavities: ants. Thousands of them, tens of thousands of them, puppeteering old tendons and temporarily stitching together torn flesh from the inside out. Threads of mandible and nerves were twined together like grotesque embroideries, allowing the corpses to walk again.

Right.

That’s… what she is.

Growing up, he’d heard many whispers about how the Generals of the Attini Empire’s Twelve Forward Army possessed something called ‘Advanced Zombie Ant Classes’, and the bioarcanic essence required to make those systems were harvested from Regalia, the original zombie ant. Her Art allowed her to create zombie ants with her blood, which could then crawl into, heal, and temporarily reanimate corpses of all shapes and sizes. Even worse, he’d heard the resurrected could use all of their original abilities—including their Arts—which meant right now, they weren’t just facing down Regalia herself.

One by one, the undead Spore Knights and Noble-Bloods around the hall began activating their Arts, summoning fire ants, silver ants, carpenter ants, and all sorts of tiny ant waves behind them.

… This is bad.

On the throne, Regalia sounded faintly amused.

“Forward, my men,” she said. “Go and kill every last one of them, and then—”

Zora bit his tongue. Hard. Pain flared like a match struck along his teeth, and for an instant the fear in his chest had something else to share space with. He forced himself to remember heat. He forced himself to remember the way fire had once crawled along his ribs when he faced Nona, and he remembered the first time he had believed that if the Swarm wanted to devour the world, then he would become fire and eat it back.

He inhaled.

He exhaled.

The heat in his lungs didn’t leave.

“Witness divination!”

His wings snapped open behind him as flames crawled along the entire length of his staff, wrapping it in a sheath of incandescent heat. By the time he dashed forward with it, his entire body felt like an ember in the air. Wings hammering, feet barely touching stone—he crossed the entire Divine Hall in a single leap and reached Regalia, rearing his flaming staff behind him.

Behind her, space warped. Enki charged out of a wormhole as well, pulling back his diamond bayonet. Between the two of them, they drew a straight line through Regalia’s heart: one blazing staff from the front, one frigid bayonet from the rear.

Zora aside, an attack as sudden and soundless as Enki’s would’ve been enough to kill any Mutant-Class—or even an Insect God—but Regalia raised both hands. Her left caught Zora’s burning staff as it drove toward her chest. Her right clamped around Enki’s bayonet, stopping it so abruptly the impact cracked the marble beneath her feet.

Immediately, zombie ants poured out from the seams between her chitin plates, swarming over her wrists, knuckles, fingers, and then flowing out onto the weapons she held.

“Away!”

Zora yanked his staff back as a gust of wind jerked him away. Enki did the same, ripping his rifle back in a sharp jerk as he flung himself through his wormhole, but the damage was already done. As they both regrouped by the front of the hall, a small line of zombie ants had already crawled up his staff and onto his bare fingertips. Needle-sharp mandibles punched into the flesh of his fingers, and a cold-burn agony raced up his hand.

His fingertips felt wrong. Wet, then numb, then heavy. He couldn’t see them, but he could just tell that if he’d pulled away any later, it wouldn’t just be his fingertips that turned necrotic.

So that is what you are.

There were Standard Ant Classes, Advanced Ant Classes, and then Specialized Ant Classes. There were Standard Cicada Classes, Advanced Shrill Cicada Classes, then Specialized Magicicada Classes. Regalia was at the specialized stage. She wasn’t ‘just’ a zombie ant.

Apart from being able to control corpses with her zombie ants… any organic material her zombie ants touch becomes necrotic tissue?

In short, all she had to do was touch someone to win.

He snapped his head at Enki, heart suddenly too loud.

“Enki—”

“I am fine,” the boy said evenly. Zora turned his head and frowned. Zombie ants were still skittering around Enki’s arm, but most of them were already tumbling off and clattering to the floor, confused and directionless.

Unlike Zora, the zombie ants couldn’t bite through Enki’s half-metallic skin.

He’s immune to the necrotic bites.

That was something, at least. A very small candle in a very large night.

Meanwhile, the resurrected Spore Knights and Noble-Bloods were already moving again.

They dragged their feet as they charged, summoned ants boiling around their feet like foam at the front of a rising tide, and they came at Baya and Kita and the other soldiers with reckless abandon. Sawtooth blades rose to match them, but it was difficult for Baya and the other Regional Lords to slash the visage of other humans, let alone for Kita and the other younger soldiers.

Regalia watched them go, then rolled her shoulders as though loosening a stiff cloak.

“I did have some expectations for you two,” Regalia said, rolling her shoulders as the dead fought the living. “The Thousand Tongue and the Worm Mage. The little pair who gave Decima so much trouble. But it appears…” Her gaze skimmed the hall with faint boredom. “You are nothing special after all. You are simply human at the end of the day.”

She lifted her hand again, palm opened towards them, and Zora heard the sound before he felt it.

A tidal wave of zombie ants poured out of her palm.

They hit the marble in front of the throne as a living flood, churning and wriggling, a million-legged mass that washed forward with frightening speed.

Zora’s teeth clenched.

“Cinders to all!”

Heat thundered outward as a sheet of flame erupted across the width of the hall. The wave of zombie ants cracked and burst in the blaze, popping like seeds in a pan—but he wasn’t fast enough. Even with Enki flicking both wrists and spinning wormholes open to help him redirect some of the zombie ants and undead, there were gaps in their defense.

A thin strand of the zombie ant wave curved towards the rear ranks—towards Kita, Ifas, Machi, Sora, and towards the handful of younger soldiers struggling to fend off the undead—and Zora’s breath caught.

He moved to speak—

But someone else moved first.

Baya and the eight other Regional Lords vaulted ahead, forming a living wall. At the same breath, all of them cut their palms open to summon their own brand of ants, reinforcing the bulwark even further.

“Father!” Kita shouted.

The wave struck the wall. The first zombie ants shredded the summoned ants instantly. The second ants tore through the lords’ raised blades. The third ants punched and ate through everything, armour, flesh, and bone.

Zora was only a heartbeat too slow.

“Burn them all!”

A rolling inferno blasted down the corridor and swallowed the wayward wave in a scream of heat. Zombie ants vaporized by the thousands, turning to ash and smoke in the blink of an eye, but when the fire receded and all the undead were nothing but dust…

Baya and the other Regional Lords were already dropping to their knees. Their flesh sloughed. Blood spilled in slow, sick dribbles. Their breaths rasped and hitched, half-wet, half-hollow, because half their bodies were already rotten with necrosis.

Their hearts still beat, but Zora knew in an instant that they were already dead men walking.
