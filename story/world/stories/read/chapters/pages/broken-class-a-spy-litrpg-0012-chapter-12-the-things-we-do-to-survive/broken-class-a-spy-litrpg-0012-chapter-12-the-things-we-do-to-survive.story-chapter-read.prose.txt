Arthur had a plan.

He didn’t leave the cavern right after capturing the chimera. First, he slept. Deep, dreamless, and drooling onto the stone floor.

When morning came, he ventured out cautiously, using Mana Sight to scan the horizon. Several mana signatures popped on his radar. He skirted the big ones. Until he found… a big, slow one.

And started tailing it.

Grubtusk Devourer Rank: E (Common) Type: Herbivorous Beast

Drogan’s voice grunted inside his skull. “You can’t be serious.”

Arthur didn’t respond.

“You’re an F-rank shrimp with a shiny Class, not a taxidermist. Go kill something! Store one more monster and I swear I’ll teach you about violence firsthand.”

Arthur stayed silent.

Drogan hated being ignored.

“You deaf, boy? I said...”

“I promised my mother I’d do something important,” Arthur interrupted solemnly. “I can’t say what it is. But I have to honor her memory.”

Drogan paused. “…Fine.”

Arthur smiled internally. Bluff check: passed.

He continued tracking the Grubtusk for hours until the moment he’d been waiting for arrived.

It pooped.

Spectacularly.

Arthur crouched and approached the steaming pile with reverence.

“Monster dung,” he muttered. “Perfect.”

He scooped up a handful. The stench could fell a grown man. He rubbed it on his clothes and skin. But when he smeared it on his face, he gagged.

Inside his head, Drogan lost what remained of his sanity.

“WHAT ARE YOU DOING?! First the moss. Now this?! You’re smearing monster crap on yourself!”

“Mum’s dying wish,” Arthur said through clenched teeth, smearing a particularly offensive glob onto his shoulder.

“I have mentored champions, Arthur. Champions! Not dung-scented lunatics! My legacy!”

Arthur dumped the rest of the steaming treasure into his storage ring. Because if one batch worked, more was better. Right?

Now that he was thoroughly camouflaged in the scent of fresh despair, he shifted focus.

Food.

The moss had kept him alive. Yes, but at what cost? His taste buds filed for divorce days ago. Still, survival trumped flavor. He scouted for more patches.

His stomach growled in protest as he chewed the first handful.

“Never eating moss again,” he muttered.

But at least now, covered in filth and fortified with moss, Arthur had taken his first steps into the wilderness.

Not exactly the noble beginnings of a hero.

But definitely memorable.

And that counts too.
