“Ah, shit.” He’d been lucky not to have this happen before this point. The so-called “black wave” wasn’t that uncommon at all. “Let’s just get it over with.”

He walked through the passage, praying he wouldn’t get a black puzzle. And his prayers were answered. Unfortunately.

It wasn’t a black puzzle.

It was a black boss fight.

He appeared in the middle of the desert. Standing before him was a three-meter tall metal golem, a whole lot like the one he’d faced in Repentawa that one time. Except this one felt even more powerful. “Son of a…!”

Before he could blink, the creature wrought of metal lunged towards him, metallic blade-limbs growing out of it and swinging his way. Countless pieces of metal were launched in his direction fast and hard enough to pierce his defenses, and in mere moments, he found himself riddled with holes, desperately working to evade the utter deluge to slashing attacks and projectiles being thrown at him.

He ducked, weaved, and leaped back, but gradually, it was becoming evident that simply backing away wasn’t the play here. Distance only let it keep firing at range, and if he allowed it to do that for much longer, he was screwed.

The whole time, his Sanguine Avenger continued to shriek something ungodly. But Freddy knew that just a single strong attack in retaliation wasn’t going to be enough to take this thing down.

Thinking fast, he conjured a second Sanguine Avenger. This one wasn’t coiled in the Cycle Scythe, but it still built up retaliation as he continued to get hit. There simply wasn’t enough time to come up with any kind of involved plan. The terrain was working against him, the enemy was locking him down, and he was suffering too much damage.

He allowed the retaliation to grow, and then, once he believed there was enough, he swung the two swords, flat-sides facing each other, and sandwiched the golem’s head between them.

The weapons landed with a profoundly deafening clang, sending numbing vibrations through his arms. He released the swords and lunged towards the stunned golem, conjuring a Knuckle Blade on his right arm, moving the Cycle Scythe to it, then, double-starring Tsunami Strike, and activating the piercing attack at the last moment.

The tip of the Knuckle Blade went right into the metal, backed by the force of an explosion, and before the golem could recover, Freddy repeated the same combo with his other hand.

Golems technically didn’t have any sort of central nervous system, but they also did, kind of. They always gravitated towards a humanoid shape, and generally, all the… matter that held whatever primitive minds they had was contained inside their heads. Mostly.

The golem’s head was bent way out of shape, but it still stumbled around, taking swings at Freddy and continuing the fight. But it was now a far cry from the lethal monster it had been moments ago. It was slow and disoriented, and it had lost a great deal of precision.

Freddy picked up the Sanguine Avenger with his raw, mangled hands, and swung, battering the golem over and over. Despite being made of pure metal, it was somehow still a good deal softer than that obstacle monster from the labyrinth puzzle. And it only became less durable as the omnibleed made its wounds seep with false blood, sapping its body of its integrity.

The force of Sanguine Avenger made quick work of the thing. Soon enough, it died, a small burst of ether confirming the kill. Freddy fell to his knees, breathing heavily, teeth gritted in fury at himself.

Golems weren’t difficult opponents. They weren’t supposed to be. Sure, in raw power, they were some of the strongest beings at their rank, but they were borderline mindless. They were horribly vulnerable to kiting, restraint, traps, and stealth.

If Freddy had just a single attack that could restrain an opponent, he wouldn’t have had to struggle even a fraction of how much he had in the fight. Hell, if he had reacted fast enough to back away from the thing when he first appeared, he could have made it outside of its effective range and then whittled it down with javelins.

And why on New Earth didn’t he trigger Twisted Pendulum before entering the trial? It was only ten damned seconds of waiting, and it would have given him a massive head start on finding the ground under his feet.

This was an extremely embarrassing performance. He’d gotten complacent. Admittedly, he’d been having it too easy in the Tower so far, so he had allowed himself to get sloppy. He was still a bit afraid of some freak showing up and beating him to the 50th floor, so he was rushing ahead without properly preparing himself.

But this wasn’t a joke. The Tower wasn’t a place where he could afford anything less than his absolute best.

If he was struggling this hard against a black boss fight on the 47th floor, he should just leave the Tower, because he definitely wasn’t going to survive, let alone beat the 100th floor.

He got up to his feet with a groan, turning to face the passage. “No more playing around.”

3 doors waited for him on floor 48. He was still in first place. The doors in question were red, purple, and blue. He walked up to the purple door, but then paused before it. He reconjured all of his equipment, regenerating his Crimson Dragon Scales. He was pretty low on essence from the fight against the golem, so he forced himself to wait for it to recover.

He meditated, but the chamber was void of ether. Nevertheless, he forced himself to calm and regain equilibrium. He’d have caught some sleep, too, but he felt like he truly didn’t need it yet. Just a bit of rest would be enough.

Once he was composed, he wondered what he might face. He went through a list of the different kinds of possible opponents he could encounter, and took into account that he might appear in an unfavorable environment.

He performed a long mental exercise, committing to memory what he would do in each possible scenario, going off both his personal experience, and what he’d learned at the University.

Then, once he was full on essence, he stepped inside.

He appeared high in the sky, on a minuscule floating island, surrounded by dense clouds. There were a number of surrounding islands floating about, barely visible in the fog, with whatever boss he would face still yet to appear.

Freddy acted fast. He conjured a one-starred Blood Javelin, which he proceeded to ram into the stone ground, then grabbed it, to make sure he had an anchor he could hold on to in case something tried flinging him off the island, and then, he double-starred the ability to prepare two projectiles for throwing, raising one and letting the other float, anticipating a flying opponent.

For a long couple of moments, nothing appeared.

And then, a stone raised not far from where he stood, and a feathered bird-mole thing crawled out, rushing him on foot. He threw the javelin, and it pierced the thing straight through its head, followed by its twin projectile. And just like that, the boss fight was over, having gone much the same as the vast majority of the fights he’d gone through in the tower so far.

This was the reality of good preparation, and the main reason he’d gotten sloppy in the first place. Far more often than not, anything he prepared wouldn’t be necessary.

But it was those few times when he did need whatever he could prepare that counted, for if he wasn’t ready then, he would cease to be altogether.

He walked into the next floor. Two doors. Red and gold. Smiling, he walked through the golden doors. Inside, he was met with yet another blue orb. Yet again, Hydraulic Flex. 5% additional progress, raising it to Stage 3—10%.

For a brief moment, he found himself stunned. That progress was starting to seriously add up. The quality of these rewards was increasing at a surprising pace. And clearly, the Tower was prioritizing whatever he relied on the most; there was hardly a moment when he wasn’t using Hydraulic Flex.

This raised a serious question—”Could I possibly get enough of these orbs to reach 50%?” That would be… amazing. But he wouldn’t count on it. Regardless, he wouldn’t complain even if he fell short. He was already gaining far more than he expected. But those were just the benefits of being overwhelmingly faster than the other participants, he supposed.

With the orb consumed, he dashed for the exit, finding himself at floor 50.

Not counting the exit, there was only one door. He was still first, and once he entered the trial, all paths forward would be cut off, stopping the other participants from following after him, allowing him to finally take as much time as he needed—or wanted—with clearing the rest of the floors.

And the last door was green.

While the ooga booga macho murder man part of his mind insisted that he was best suited for the combat trials, he was forced to admit that he was almost perfectly built for the environmental ones.

In regards to combat mobility, he was far from the fastest fighter for his rank, but in regards to long-range treks through harsh terrain, he was probably at the very peak for his rank. His durability was immense, and with his Hydraulic Flex, tackling vertical obstacles was a joke. His stamina was borderline bottomless as long as he had food to stuff down his gullet, and he was accustomed to extremely little sleep.

Most likely, he would be favoring green doors going forward. It hurt his pride a bit to shun the fights, but that black boss trial had been a bit of a wake-up call. Taking needless risks at this point was plain dumb.

He turned, taking one final look at the passage behind him, almost expecting someone to pass through it. But no such thing happened. In hindsight, it was absurd for him to have ever been even remotely afraid of someone catching up to him.

Without further ado, he simply stepped through the doors before him, with a long, long journey waiting ahead.

Floor 50 was not quite as easy as expected.

There was one kind of environment that Freddy wasn’t exactly best suited for, actually.

Caves.

Tight, long, winding caves. That were wet. And full of water. And bugs.

His sheer bulk made it an ungodly pain to push through the tighter passes, and he was forced to repeatedly backtrack. He ended up getting stuck for nearly an entire week, and at several points, he quite literally couldn’t fit through the necessary paths without either chipping the stone and risking a cave-in, or mutilating himself to cut down on excess bulk.

Even then, he couldn’t quite say that it was a particularly dangerous trial, just annoying and time-consuming as hell. There had been a reason why he’d been avoiding green trials if at all possible—it was precisely in case some bullshit like this happened.

Nevertheless, with nobody on his tail, he had no need to rush.

Once done with floor 50, he appeared in the next chamber—this time, the door count was back up to 3, and it would remain there all the way until the end of the tower. Well, it should, but nobody really knew for sure, since nobody had ever reached floor 90, let alone 100.

His choices were red, red, and blue.

Rather than proceed, he set up a blanket, got nice and cozy, and prepared to rest.

Going forth, he would make sure he was at 100% capacity before entering every trial. The rest of the tower would be a marathon, not a sprint. He caught up on sleep, he ate, he healed, and he even did some light reading.

And then, he continued.

While making sure he cast Twisted Pendulum before entering, of course. He wouldn’t be forgetting that one ever again.

Nothing too exceptional happened until floor 61. That being said, the difficulty was certainly ramping up. And so was the amount of time he was spending on each floor. He was now actively aiming for green trials whenever they appeared, and those were starting to take days at the least—weeks at the longest, as was the case with an archipelago trial that had him running and swimming circles until he finally tracked down the exit.

But ironically, the longest trial he ended up coming across so far ended up being a purple one.

He appeared inside a dense jungle, facing a relatively small bird of prey—an eagle of sorts. And before he could even react, it vanished, turning invisible as the air distorted around it. “Huh?” He looked around, but the creature had hidden, and it wasn’t attacking. At first, he simply raised his guard, trying to figure out where it was, but that dragged on for hours.

Eventually, he gave up on waiting and started actively hunting it down.

It was simply nowhere to be found.

For days, he ran around the jungle like a total moron, and then, when he finally decided to take a break and rest a bit, the eagle finally appeared.

Something flashed right across his throat, leaving a deep rent and severing his jugular vein. “Son of a…!” He leaped to his feet, looking around as he held a hand to the wound, using his blood manipulation to stem the bleeding.

But again, the bird was nowhere to be found.

He healed from the damage, and this time, he encased himself into a tomb of blood to prevent the thing from attacking while he was asleep. It ultimately didn’t even make a move while he had a nice rest. Once he got a bit hungry, he ended up trying to hunt for prey, hoping to save as many of his food pills as humanly possible.

But as soon as he located an animal he could hunt down, the eagle swooped in and stole it away. “Oh… you little shit!” he shouted into the sky. “Now that’s just being petty…” He briefly pondered eating some plants or mushrooms instead, but there was a serious risk of poisoning, and he really couldn’t afford that in a trial like this.

He finally understood what the monster was playing at—it was aiming to exhaust him through fatigue and starvation, and then, it would strike. And what could he even possibly do to fight back?

Well… the little bastard was certainly about to find out.

For days, Freddy hunted down anything he could get his hands on before the eagle stole it away. It thought itself so smart, snatching the meat away, all while failing to realize Freddy was on the hunt for the blood. Once he finally accumulated enough, a good friend of his was ritualistically summoned into existence, at the smallest size possible, just about two meters tall, where it could keep as much of its blood powers as possible.

“Bloodshed, my good friend! Long time no see!”

The shawl-wrapped skeleton lowered its head. “Greetings, Master. How may I serve you today?”

Freddy grinned. “Let’s start with flooding the entire jungle with noxious blood gas.”

It wanted to starve him?

Well, two could play that game.

It wasn’t a quick process. Bloodshed could replenish its reserves by sowing further bloodshed and absorbing the blood, but there weren’t all that many animals in the jungle. Thankfully, bug hemolymph still counted as blood for the purposes of the blood affinity, thus, it could at least keep blasting fumes, albeit rather slowly.

The realm wasn’t too large, but it wasn’t exactly small, either. It was a C-class realm on the smaller side, meaning, it took weeks of work to spread the gas everywhere.

The transformation from jungle to withering wasteland was slow but inevitable. Disease and rot spread at an exponential rate, turning the once lush and vibrant ecosystem into a mesh of sickly yellows, greys, and reds.

After about a month, the eagle finally ran out of healthy prey to hunt and went on the attack.

But Freddy was a petty motherfucker.

Rather than give the eagle the satisfaction of an actual fight, he encased himself into a large ball of blood metal, where he comfortably meditated, pondering the depths of the vast sea of blood aura that his stunt was generating in the environment, all while the endless river of lifesteal kept him flawlessly healthy.

The eagle was no simple beast. It wasn’t for another month that it finally succumbed to some combination of starvation, illness, fatigue, and poisoning—Freddy wasn’t quite sure, but he did get the credit for the kill in the form of a moderate burst of ether. “Its dead, Bloodshed. Thanks for the help.”

“It was my pleasure, Master. I have thoroughly enjoyed myself accomplishing the task you placed before me.”

“I’m sure you did, buddy.” He laughed. “You’re the best! Kisses and hugs!” he said as he dismissed the skeleton, broke the blood metal ball, and walked out the passage.

At floor 61, things were finally shaken up a bit by the appearance of another golden door.

This time, it was another potion—a wispy cyan so pale it bordered on white. From what he could tell, consuming it made him a little faster. He nodded in satisfaction. Speed was always good, no matter what kind of trial he was faced with.

62 to 69 was more of the same, but even harder. It took him a little over an entire year to pass those floors. Frankly, he was almost starting to feel grateful for the Century of Solitude. If he hadn’t gone through that, he seriously doubted he’d have had the patience to persist through this. And hey, at least this time, he had plenty to focus on other than his own reflection.

He continued prioritizing green doors, just as he’d decided, regardless of the extra time they took.

Again, there was the bash n’ smash caveman part of his brain, telling him that he was being a wimp, or that he would get a worse reward for doing this, but he knew that that wasn’t the case, and he knew that for a fact.

A whole lot of very smart statisticians had done a whole lot of big brain math over the years, and they had concluded that the kinds of trials one took had literally zero bearing on the ultimate quality of the reward. It was only the number of floors one passed that actually made a difference.

Would that be different for floor 100? Perhaps, but really, he had absolutely no clue whether he’d even actually make it that far.

The few combat scenarios he was faced with hadn’t been too difficult, but they were still a far greater risk than facing a harsh environment. Even that eagle had been a pretty damn tough opponent, and something similar but slightly different might actually be capable of placing the dot at the end of his story.

In comparison, the environmental trials were merely getting more annoying, but rarely difficult enough to put him in any kind of serious threat. He was sure that others probably wouldn’t feel the same way, but alas, he was simply built different.

Floor 70 threw out another golden door. +7% to Hydraulic Flex. Floor 71 threw out three golden doors—again, +7% to Hydraulic Flex, bringing him up to Stage 3—24%, and granting him a pretty noticeable increase to the power of the ability.

72 to 75 ended up being a series of red doors, as the alternatives had all been either black, blue, or none. When he entered 72, he thought he was screwed for sure. It was a dozen wyverns, and they were all deviants. But ultimately, this ended up being actually quite easy, partly by pure luck.

One of the wyverns was a life and blood affinity deviant. It was one of the first to charge him down while the rest held back and threw all kinds of ranged attacks at him. But the creature had vastly overestimated its durability against Sanguine Avenger, which turned its head into mush. Killing it made Blood Sacrifice go mental, begging to be used.

The spirit ability sucked out a small lake of supercharged blood out of the dead wyvern, allowing him to summon a giant Bloodshed. The rest of the fight came down to Best Skeleton slapping the monsters out of the sky like oversized flies.

73 put him against a couple of small giants. It was one of the easiest fights he’d had in the tower, as he simply sniped them from range, much like he had the one attacking Repentawa.

74 was a nightmare. He appeared in a burned-down town of sorts, surrounded by a small army of undead, and he wasn’t using the word “army” lightly.

They were organized, equipped, and under command by a lich of sorts, who used complex formations to pin Freddy down and wear him out.

As undead, they had no blood to spill, and no life to steal, neutering both of Freddy’s greatest advantages against big numbers. His victory hadn’t come from any kind of special strategy. He simply fought back as they skirmished back and forth, wearing the opponents down, breaking their ranks, and ultimately, crushing the lich’s skull underfoot, his entire skin rotting away and his muscles sagging loose on his form.

This was definitely one of those scenarios that he really didn’t want to land again. That being said, he did make a few mistakes, and he’d definitely do better next time.

He thought 75 would be trouble, but it ended up being rather easy. He appeared in an infernal wasteland, surrounded by lava golems. And perhaps it would have been far more trouble due to the heat alone… if he didn’t have a piece of equipment specifically for increasing his resistance to fire.

In the end, he simply made some distance and blew them apart with supercharged javelins. He’d learned a thing or two about how to handle golems from past errors. This time, he had entered with Twisted Pendulum, giving him plenty of time to flee the encirclement and turn the fight around.

76 offered purple, black, black. He picked purple, and faced some kind of white ape with the lightning affinity. The thing fried his ass with repeated, high-powered attacks, staying at range.

Crimson Blood Scales ended up being surprisingly useful as they sapped most of the charge away and into the ground, but even then, he was almost wholly paralyzed for most of the fight. The lightning burned his body pretty hard, but again, the earrings saved the day again, drastically easing the effect heat was having on him.

But it didn’t seem like it would be enough.

Just when he was starting to think this would be it, the ape ran out of essence.

Lightning was pretty expensive, after all.

The ape impotently swung its arms, almost as if it weren’t aware of the concept of running out of power.

Meanwhile, Freddy raised the sword menacingly, a wicked, teeth-crushing grin of fury upon his lips. “Come here, little monkey… I’m only gonna hurt you a whole fucking lot.”

77 to 79 provided a bit of a lull as he struck green four times in a row. Neither had challenged him much, but damn was the time starting to add up. The scale of the environments he was facing continued to swell. A path across a mountain range that felt like it spanned half a continent. A titanic living maze that kept shifting. A vast desert, with several active volcanoes—and many fresh ones every so often, dotting the horizon.

It took him a bit over two years to complete just these three floors, and he was starting to think that the main downside of prioritizing green trials was literally dying of old age before one could finish the tower. Not that this was a problem for him, but still, it was getting a tad annoying.

80 finally forced him into a blue trial, and he could appreciate that he had managed to land a rather long streak without having to suffer through one. But that streak was over.

The trial was set inside what looked like a long-abandoned underground base of some kind of faction. It took him several weeks to piece together what the hell the point of the trial even was. There were so many winding corridors, traps, abandoned automatons that sprung into life when he entered the wrong rooms—the whole thing was full of danger.

He ultimately discovered some kind of heavily fortified vault in the depths of the basement.

There was an extremely elaborate series of little plugs and levers and buttons littering an entire wall, and most likely, the exact order in which they had to be triggered was the mystery he needed to solve.

He had found a piece of paper beside a skeletal corpse on a higher floor, with what looked like a cipher inscribed upon it. A patch of it was burned. Odds were, this was only one of many clues he needed to collect before he could open the doors the right way.

Honestly, he had absolutely no clue how to solve it, and from what he guessed, it would take forever to figure it out. “Well… I guess there is one thing I could try.” He simply approached the vault door, and then, threw a double-starred Tsunami Strike at it. His fist exploded, the whole room shook, but the vault doors looked unblemished. “No, wait, hold on—” He leaned closer. There was a… very faint disturbance on the surface of the metal.

He watched it for a long moment, expecting it to go away. And it did. The vault wasn’t just absurdly tough, it was also inscribed with repair runes. “Hmm…” He grinned.

There might just be another way to solve this puzzle.

Freddy raised a fist, conjuring a Knuckle Blade. Then, he attached the Cycle Scythe to it, making the almost foot-long blade turn dark, with minuscule golden lips scattered across its surface.

Then, he pushed the blade against the surface of the vault door, and pulled, slowly dragging it across the surface. The metal was ridiculously hard, so he could barely even leave a small scratch on its surface, but that scratch was enough.

Dark blood seeped through the long blemish in the metal, only a few drops, but alas. The repair runes sprung to work, sealing the bloody wound shut. And Freddy pulled again.

He moved across the surface of the door, going back and forth, back and forth, and despite the repair runes fixing the damage, the metal continued growing softer, making leaving a scratch in it easier and easier. In just a couple of hours, it had grown as soft as regular iron, maybe softer.

This time, when he threw a double-starred Tsunami Strike, the doors caved in, revealing the way out.

He couldn’t keep the grin off his face. “One of these days, Conquest. One of these days, you’ll give me a puzzle I can’t cheese.” He laughed. “But that ain’t today!”

He left the trial in a jolly good mood, and it only got better when he made it to the next chamber.

81 rewarded him with golden doors yet again.

This time, 9% progress to Sanguine Avenger, bringing it to Stage 3—14%. He was a teeny little bit disappointed that it wasn’t Hydraulic Flex, but he wasn’t going to complain too much. Briefly, the thought that Conquest was getting back at him for the joke he made crossed his mind, but he shook his head, thinking that couldn’t possibly be the case.

Still, he wouldn’t be making any more jokes like that.

He found himself before the next trial with a bit of trepidation. Red, black, blue. He took a deep breath.

If he passed 82, he’d be matching the all-time record for the two-star category.

Somewhere deep down, he realized that he might not be the first person to get that far. It was entirely possible that others had gone way past 82, and then died before they could reach the end, with nobody ever knowing of their accomplishment. And he could very well be added to those ranks if he got unlucky.

He took extra time to prepare himself this time around.

But ultimately, 82 didn’t end up being anything special. He faced these bulky bear monsters who mostly wielded stone. They launched a barrage of stone shards his way, and direct hits delivered one hell of a punch, enough to crack even Freddy’s bones. But ultimately, they were flesh-and-blood opponents, with blood to spill, life force to steal, and heads to crush.

He made it to 83. Black, purple, red.

It was a strange feeling being in that chamber. He sat down, pulling out his meat so he could get started on the healing process. His eyes involuntarily drifted to the wooden doors, sitting lone all the way in the right corner, far from the trial doors, the shabby, worn wood looking every bit the kind of thing a quitter’s exit would be made of.

Once, sometime in the past, a person had walked through that exact door, right on this floor, having given up the climb. He couldn’t help but wonder why. Had they reached their limit? Been forced out by three black doors? How did they feel doing so? Were they relieved? Ecstatic they had made it so far? Or disappointed; even heartbroken?

82 wasn’t close enough to floor 100 for the “I almost made it to the top!” effect to kick in, but perhaps that was exactly why 82 was the highest score.

Freddy wasn’t sure he’d be willing to give up if he made it to floor 90.

He rested up, getting his final preparations out of the way before tackling the next trial.

His options were black, purple, and red. Black was out of the question, but he wasn’t really sure which to pick between purple and red. It wasn’t like one was inherently easier than the other. Fighting several opponents or just one strong one was inherently different, and different people would have drastically different opinions on which was harder and which was easier. As for Freddy…

Admittedly, he wasn’t sure. He cared significantly more about the nature of creatures he was fighting rather than their numbers. It would be a different story if every trial gave him fleshy blood bags, but that wasn’t always the case.

If the monsters were capable of uh… donating their lifeforce and blood, he’d say he was better against several lesser opponents. But if they weren’t, he would say he far preferred a single, powerful opponent.

He slowly nodded. “Purple is the better choice.”

Once ready, he dove into the trial. He ended up facing a serpentine sea creature in its natural environment. A bit rude for the trial to throw him straight into water like that, but luckily, he was something of an expert with dealing with this hyperspecific scenario.

The monster swallowed him whole once he let it, and then learned the very hard way why not chewing Freddy properly was a horrible idea.

And just like that, he had broken the record. If he left through the exit, he’d have already done it. But he wasn’t going to leave.

From the very moment he had entered this place, he had never been planning to give up halfway there. He breathed in the smell of old stone in the chamber, facing purple, green and green.

From 84 to 90, Freddy took green, green, purple, green, purple, red, and green. It took him nearly five years to finish those trials. If he were any less determined, he’d definitely give up on prioritizing green, but there was no denying the stark difference in difficulty.

The purples had both pushed him to the limit, even if he’d gotten lucky with the red, but none of the green trials had ever really felt like anything more than an exercise in patience and endurance.

Even with the fact he was physically suited for environmental trials, it was really his mentality that made them best for him. Freddy was a hard worker; he didn’t think it arrogant to claim that much.

And he knew why he was like this.

It was tempting to believe that the Century of Solitude had forged his will into steel and made him capable of enduring anything, but really, he only made it through that dungeon due to what he’d lived through before. And it wasn’t the life and death fights. Or even the slavery.

The darkest part of his life had come long before any of that.

Back when his adoptive parents left, he’d been plunged into the deepest depths of despair. It was hard to overstate how difficult it felt to navigate that situation. Sure, all he’d really done was visit an acquaintance of his parents and asked for a job, and then worked for a few years, but it had felt like trying to scale the tallest mountain in the world.

He had been just a kid, and he’d been forced to juggle finances, look for a place to live, learn how to take care of himself, and work. Work, while even the children in the poorest neighborhood in the city that were his age were playing basketball with their friends, or going to school, or at the very least, suffering together with their family members.

Freddy had nobody. Sure, there had been people who had seen a kid in need and tried to reach out, but he didn’t let them. He couldn’t let them. There hadn’t been a shred of trust left in him, and there couldn’t until his step parents returned. He waited every day for them to knock on that rickety door. For years. And they never did.

Years of walking under the hot sun, or through freezing snow, or diving into dark caves was nothing. It was pleasant. It was a great time for everyone involved compared to the hell that he had lived through as a teenager.

Perhaps the environmental trials weren’t easy at all. Perhaps they would be hell to somebody else. But not for him.

91 blessed him with another golden door. He felt like they were coming at pretty regular intervals, but that was just a coincidence. There was no actual pattern to how they appeared.

This one granted him another blue orb. +12% to Hydraulic Flex. His heart was hammering in his chest as the ability reached Stage 3—36%. Just one more, and he might actually get it to the reformation stage. Getting an ability that high at the second star was extraordinary, but it was the kind of thing elites like Emily or even Georgie could accomplish.

Usually, though, such elites didn’t have their souls stuffed to the brim with absurdly overdeveloped abilities, though.

92 threw him a bit of a curveball. Purple, purple, purple.

He prepared just as he usually did, and then plunged into the trial.

Freddy appeared inside a dark chamber. A mere trickle of light seeped through a crack in the ceiling, but it was enough for him to spot his opponent. The thing looked like something out of some obscure village’s folklore. It was covered in thin fuzz of white fur. It was a bit like a deer standing on hind legs, but with a twisted, demonic face that would give children nightmares.

Making the most of his Twisted Pendulum, Freddy didn’t give it time to attack, lunging straight at it. It tracked his movements with unerring, uncanny precision, moving out of the way almost as if he were the one going in slow motion. Panic instantly swelled in his gut. Speedster!

He moved as fast as he could muster, lunging towards the creature, swinging wildly, all while trying to corner it somewhere it couldn’t dodge. Once he was happy with where he had it, he moved his Cycle Scythe to the Knuckle Blade on his right hand, then triggered the impact function.

He burst forward with an immense amount of speed. Air shifted, and the creature floated out of the way of the attack, and then, swung its clawed hand with the deafening bang of negative pressure dragging it forward, scraping across his chest and leaving a deep gash.

Without hesitation, Freddy conjured a ball of blood metal, then punched it with a double-starred Tsunami Strike. At this range, there was no way it could possibly avoid the explosion of shrapnel, and in a way, he was right. Many pieces had embedded themselves into the creature’s skin, but a shimmering layer of dense air had drastically slowed them down, even throwing some pieces aside altogether.

Still, the following shockwave of the blast sent the creature flying away, blood seeping out of its eyes. In that moment, its Air Cushion had been utterly shattered by the shockwave, and Freddy moved to utilize the opening by smashing another ball of blood metal.

And then, his Twisted Pendulum ran out.

Freddy’s speed immediately halved, giving the creature ample time to almost completely move out of the range of the second blast, with only a couple of stray shards skimming its side and leaving cuts across its skin.

The creature moved with a puff of air, a sudden burst of speed lunging it past Freddy’s side, where it delivered a deep cut across his ribs. Then it did so again, on the other side. Then it severed his right jugular. Then, it launched a claw across his face, wrecking Freddy’s right eye.

He kept trying to fight back, to find some way out of the situation, but the fact was, the creature was simply far too fast for him to fight back properly. He continued blindly swinging his sword, hoping to clip the creature by sheer luck, but it continued to evade his attacks. Eventually, he gave up on Sanguine Avenger altogether, throwing it aside in favor of using the Knuckle Blade instead.

The Cycle Scythe drastically empowered the sub-abilities that were part of his blood weapons, and the impact function on the knuckle blade accelerated him forward, pulling his entire body along with the attack. If coupled with redirection, it could deliver some serious speed, and at surprising angles.

He activated the function, randomly striking at where he guessed the creature would strike next. No luck. He did so again, three times, until he had to discard both his Knuckle Blades in favor of double-starring new ones, with fresh stacks of impact. With each miss, the creature delivered another devastating blow. One attack slashed across his leg. A second injured the tendons of his right knee, only barely failing to sever them altogether.

Turning right, he activated impact. The attack flew forward, and at the last moment, he redirected it to the right. The creature, which had seen the attack coming, tried moving out of the way, but as he redirected the attack, he clipped it across its left shoulder, delivering a blow that nearly cut its entire arm off.

The creature retaliated without hesitation, despite the injury, clawing his second eye out.

He was fully blinded. If he failed to wrap the fight up now, it was over.

Conjuring every shred of focus he could muster, he double-starred Terror Knuckles on the same hand that had nearly severed the creature’s arm, and immediately transferred the Cycle Scythe onto it. He pushed every shred of power he could manage, moving the arm forward, trying to just clip the creature even the tiniest bit.

The Terror Knuckles barely scraped against the creature’s wounded shoulder, delivering little damage—but an immense amount of pain, and hopefully briefly paralyzing it.

A shriek of agony filled the room, and before the creature could recover, Freddy shouted, “Leviathan’s Fury!” His entire body was shredded as his very bones were twisted into the facsimile of a draconic skull, biting down on the monster with the sound of bones snapping like twigs.

He received a burst of ether for the kill, and then, dropped to his knees.

Breathing heavily, he weakly moved his arm to extract the spark of undeath, and then, he got to healing in silence.

This was the first time he’d used Leviathan’s Fury in a long, long while. It was a truly devastating attack, both for him and whoever was unlucky enough to be on the other side of it. Every single bone in his body was broken, with only the touch of healing he received from killing the monster off holding him together.

No matter how tough he got, the cost of the spirit ability didn’t become any less drastic. But from what he could tell, the power of the attack did increase in turn.

He slowly healed up, then he remained seated on the ground, taking a long moment to collect himself.

That had been a very difficult fight. It had been close, far too close for comfort, and he hadn’t even made any real mistakes. He’d played that almost flawlessly, and used everything he had at his disposal.

His fists clenched. Taking a deep breath, he forced himself to move on to floor 93.

Once he entered, he fell to his knees in despair.

Black.

Black.

Black.

He punched the floor, anger fueling the strike with enough power to crack stone. “Are you fucking serious!?” he shouted, punching the floor again.

He didn’t want to admit it. Floor 93 was too close to the end for this to be happening.

But it was over, wasn’t it?

That last floor… that had been his limit.

Sure, that monster had countered him, but so what? The Tower wouldn’t accept excuses. The next fight could very well be another counter, and it was going to be a black trial. Just taking on the tower to begin with was a risk. That was true for everyone who entered it, and Freddy was no exception there. He’d made some mistakes pretty early in the run that could have very well cost him his life.

But there was a difference between taking a risk, and outright gambling with his life.

And he was standing right on that line.

“Hey… Conquest… Is this tower even meant to be possible to finish?” he asked into the empty chamber. Then, his eyes drifted down. “Or am I just too weak? No, too lopsided. No, that’s…” He went quiet again. “Everyone has their strengths and weaknesses. I’m not alone in having a style that…” He gritted his teeth. “Why black doors!? Why now!? Why when I’m so fucking close!?” Tears streaked down the side of his face. “Why now…?”

He felt himself hollowing out, his body growing slack as he stared at the ground. “Why now…?” he muttered again. “Everything I’ve done… I just wanted to have some power over things. But not just a little bit of power. I’ve seen what it means to have just a little bit of power. You’re still fucked. You still don’t get a real say in anything meaningful.” He looked up to the ceiling.

“I don’t think I’m the smartest guy in the world, or anything like that. I don’t know how to fix this fucked-up world.” He gritted his teeth. “But I want the power to at least try something… That’s all I want.” He spread his arms out, shouting, “Tell me, Conquest!” He growled. “Is that really too much to ask for!?” Breathing heavily, his fury ebbed. Then, he scoffed, laughing. “Of course it is…”

He swallowed heavily. “Of course I’m asking for too much…” Slowly, he leveled his gaze, facing the black door in the middle. “But I’m gonna ask for it anyway. No. I’m not going to ask.” He got up to his feet.

Walking up to the black door, he put his hand on it. “I’m going to fucking take it.” He turned around, eyes full of vigor. “So what if I have to gamble my life?” He laughed, voice breaking. “This worthless life is the cheapest thing I own.”

He took a rest. He meditated. He recharged on food, did some reading, and slept.

Once he was at his best, he got started.

Freddy was huge. Naturally, that did slow him down, and not just a bit, but not nearly as much as it would somebody else. Redirection was a truly divine combination with excess strength. But it wasn’t quite enough.

Right now, he held unbelievable physical power, and an overwhelming strength was a way to combat a weaknesses in its own right. But his build was simply too poorly organized. He didn’t have the right ways to leverage his strength. At the moment, he held the power to overwhelm certain kinds of enemies while being nearly hopeless against others.

But to beat the tower, he’d need to find a balanace—a spot where he was just barely able to handle right about anything.

He conjured Sanguine Avenger, raised it up, and then, he got to training.

The waiting chamber was uncomfortably similar to the Century of Solitude in some ways. But unlike the century of solitude, he wasn’t trapped here, and that made a world of difference.

And frankly, while he kind of hated to admit it, he couldn’t deny that he missed having absolutely no distractions. Just him, his sword in his hands, and all the time in the world. It was nostalgic, and just as it had been useful back then, it was a great way to progress now.

He’d gotten quite a bit more powerful since the Century of Solitude, and admittedly, he hadn’t fully grown into that power. Slowly, he found himself correcting errors he’d been making without noticing. They were small things, but as he ironed out the imperfections, he found himself getting a whole lot faster.

While training, he didn’t eat anything. His body could store quite a bit of excess energy, but constant training without pause or moderation was still bound to drain him, and before long, he felt starvation unlike anything he’d ever experienced before. His body yearned for food, any kind of food, but he ignored it, letting his muscles slowly shrink until they reached a more reasonable size.

All the while, he continued to train, only stopping to rest when his fatigue made things unproductive.

His speed continued to grow. It started increasing explosively at a certain point, but then, it slowed down again, while he felt he was losing a bit too much power in turn. That was the point he finally stopped starving himself, but he wasn’t quite done with training.

During his preparations, he continued to think of everything he could do to give himself an extra edge.

His ticket into the primordial domain of water hung above his head most enticingly, but… Frankly, there was simply too little point to entering it now. The absolute best thing he could get from there would be a second talent, but not only was that very very rare, there was also a rather considerable chance that he would get a talent that wouldn’t be all that useful until he ascended.

The main issue with unique prime vestiges was that… they really weren’t that special compared to regular prime vestiges. The literally only thing about them that made them exceptional was that they could give a second talent. But said talents had the exact same variability in power as those granted by ordinary prime vestiges.

They could be super strong, and also, borderline worthless.

Statistically… borderline worthless was the status of most prime vestiges. This was by one-star standards, by the way, since the talent would have never undergone an evolution.

And if he was just being honest… While he did qualify for the water domain, his comprehension of water concepts was miles behind his understanding of blood. The chances of him actually doing well in the water domain were low at best, and the odds of dying were too high to justify it.

Just as there was a difference between taking risks and gambling, there was a difference between gambling and playing the lottery.

Thus, with a heavy heart, he abandoned the idea of taking the water trial. But only for now. There were still 8 trials to go through; a lot could change in that time.

Eventually, once he finally felt like he had reached an acceptable level of speed, he braced himself, and walked into the black trial.

He appeared right in the middle of the ocean, falling straight into the water. For a brief moment, he thought it would be another fight against a sea monster, which was a setting he was pretty comfortable with, but he wasn’t going to be too thrilled just yet.

Particularly because no monsters were appearing.

A pang of dread shot up his spine. “No,” he dismissed the idea. “It’s probably an environmental trial.” But there was a problem. Environmental trials usually provided a pretty clear direction for where he needed to go, or at least a vague one. So this was likely either a combat trial against an opponent who hadn’t shown themselves yet, or… “Dear god, no.”

He wouldn’t jump to any conclusions just yet. This was a black trial on the 93 floor, anything was possible for all he knew. This was entirely uncharted territory. For starters, he got to swimming.

The ocean was brimming with sea life, most of which was ignoring him. But a few things did attack, such a shark-ish monster and a squid-thing. Neither was particularly tough, so if this was a combat trial, these weren’t the main enemies.

Just in case this was an environmental trial, he got started on lightly testing the fish for poison.

Mainly by eating a bit and waiting to see if he would shit blood or not.

He had brought a big supply of food pills, but he didn’t have an infinite number of them. And environmental trials could drag on for quite a while, making a consistent food supply critical. Now that he really thought about it, this was likely another thing that should massively increase the difficulty for the average participant.

His swim continued for a long, long while. Days, at first, then weeks. There were some strange things he was starting to notice. The waves had a very bizarre, and awfully consistent pattern to them. The weather was always perfectly clear. The stars above were arranged in a peculiar way. It all made him uneasy. He wanted this to be the hint for where he needed to go, but it could very well be something else.

Eventually, he spotted a change on the horizon. At first, he was elated.

And then, he plunged into despair.

The thing on the horizon was a wall. A wall the size of a mountain, with numerous strange inscriptions upon its surface, but they were not runic.

Holding on to fleeting hope, he scaled the wall, reaching the top. Behind it, there was the veneer of the false sky. He walked through it, almost like it were a faint fog, and behind it, he was greeted by the flawless mirror of nothingness.

He turned around, looking down the wall and across the unfathomably vast ocean before him. He looked left. The wall stretched on to beyond the horizon, the bend just barely visible.

This entire ocean was encircled by the wall.

The patterns in the stars, the waves, the runes—they were hints, likely, for how to locate the exit, which was somewhere within this mind-bogglingly massive body of water. And his task was to find it. “Hah.” He slumped, falling to his knees. “I’m so fucked.”

There was no easy way to cheese this puzzle.

Freddy dove through the world, finding abandoned ruins beneath the water, and some kinds of ancient, rotting vessels. Within them, there were orbs, there were keys, there were papers safely stored in glass containers, and zero damned clues on what any of this was supposed to do.

Usually, said locations were guarded by massive, ridiculously powerful beasts. From what he gathered, there were ways to lure them away by messing with some suspiciously placed ethertech not too far away, but it was impossible to figure out how to use it, so he just summoned a giant Bloodshed every time and bullied the beasts away.

At the very least, the puzzle was set in the ocean, allowing him to get some use out of perished water. But other than that, it had no redeeming features.

He refused to keep track of time, for the risk of being driven insane. The whole place reminded him far too much of his time in the Century of Solitude, and this time, he was trapped. The whole time, he held on to the faint hope that the exit would be behind some kind of vault he could force open, but his prayers went unanswered.

The exit was inside an underground structure, and it was inactive. If he was guessing correctly, some of the ruins he’d stumbled upon were the key to activating it, and the numerous knick-knacks he’d found were the means of doing so.

Perhaps if he could figure out what the actual hell the patterns in the stars, the wall, and the ocean were meant to be telling him, it wouldn’t have been so hard. But try as he may, the only real method of tracking down where he was supposed to go was through looking randomly and praying for a miracle.

He got stuck for years. How many, he really didn’t want to know, but given how much he felt like a damned fish person by the end, it was too many to count. This time, he was thankful for the Century of Solitude, because he would have absolutely lost his mind if it hadn’t been tempered there.

One by one, he tracked the relevant ruins down. Then, he found the puzzles within, and collected all the right pieces. He randomly experimented with arranging them, which triggered countless traps that would have killed him if he didn’t have a constant trickle of lifesteal from the army of perished fish violating the surrounding eco-system.

And ultimately, one by one, he managed to, by sheer virtue of trying every combination, activate all the ruins.

While he didn’t think he spent quite as much time in there, he still left the puzzle trial feeling the way he had when he’d done the Century of Solitude. Mainly, annoyed that he actually had a thing to keep doing, and that he couldn’t just go and relax for a couple of centuries.

94 had a touch more mercy on him with blue, purple, and red. He went for purple.

His enemy ended up being a salt golem. It was an extremely nasty opponent which nearly dehydrated him in the first few seconds, but he managed to reach it in time and slam a Tsunami Strike right in its center of mass, which mercifully ended the fight.

For 95, he got a red. It ended up being a massive army of some kind of demonic race. Well, maybe using the word “demonic” was a bit rude… or even racist. Just because they had horns didn’t mean they were evil. But they were very poorly equipped to deal with Freddy.

It was the kind of fight whose difficulty lay chiefly with the sheer number of enemies, which would exhaust almost anyone. Almost.

Despite being floor 95, this was barely a task, let alone a trial. They were competent and powerful, with varied strategies and means of attack, but against a single target who leaped into their ranks and got to slaughtering, they couldn’t exactly muster much of a defense. What were they gonna do? Send a barrage of explosions right in the middle of their own formation?

That was exactly what they ended up doing, actually. Maybe they were evil and Freddy wasn’t just being a racist. Anywho, it didn’t work.

By the time he thinned them down enough to break them into small parties, he had more than enough blood to summon Bloodshed and let it finish the job. All in all, if every trial was like this, he’d have cruised through the Tower even if it had 200 floors.

96 brought him back to form with a green trial. He was getting seriously sick of time-wasters, but alas, he was a bit relieved at a trial that didn’t require an overt brush with death.

Perhaps he should have held his relief until he actually saw what he was dealing with.

He appeared out in fucking space, standing on a tiny asteroid. The passage wasn’t too far, but it required navigating through a ton of fast moving space rocks.

He instantly panicked before forcing himself to calm down. The lack of air felt weird, but it wasn’t like his eyes popped out of his skull or anything. Still, he wouldn’t faint from lack of air for a while, so he wasn’t in a massive rush. From what he could tell, a single misstep and it was bye-bye, Freddy, so he better do this right.

Tracking the incoming space rocks—Comets? Asteroids? Meteors?—he noted a highly specific formation, committing it to memory before it even arrived. Then, when it was close, he leaped, bouncing off the rocks until he got a straight shot towards the exit.

Admittedly, if he didn’t have redirection, this would have certainly ended him. Redirection was a bit of a cheat for this specific trial, as it allowed him to deliberately aim at exactly where he wanted to go.

At any rate… well, at least it didn’t take years this time. He’d take his victories where he could get them.

And just like that, he was on floor 97. Four more floors remained until he was done with the trial. He couldn’t deny that some part of him was hoping for four straight golden doors, but it would be red again this time.

The fight was thoroughly brutal. It was more of a bossfight with three bosses than a fight against 3 lesser enemies. Again, some kind of alien race—this time, with saggy, super-wrinkled skin—made up his opponents. They were highly-coordinated, individually powerful, and well-equipped. If he hadn’t shed excess weight, he’d have lost, there was no doubt about that.

He was just barely fast enough to break the encirclement and prevent them from pinning him down. Once he was out, he gradually pulled back while wearing them down with javelins. They didn’t rage and chase after him, but rather waited, taking an economic, slow approach to wearing him out, first, launching their own counter-attacks.

Intelligent opponents were always going to be worse than rabid beasts. But at the very least, they could be bluffed.

Freddy hid most of his power from them, throwing javelins at a mere fraction of his full power, but still hard enough that it looked like a serious attack. And then, when they were confident that they had a good estimate of his capabilities, he threw a full-powered attack straight through the shield of the enemy in the front.

The projectile pierced through, spearing the alien’s chest and—hopefully—their heart, but Freddy wasn’t sure if these people had one.

But if they had no heart, they certainly had something important in the middle of their chest, since the shield-bearer collapsed like a puppet with its strings cut.

The others shouted in indignation and panic, and Freddy counter-attacked. Even with just two, they easily put him on the back foot. But ultimately, they simply didn’t have the endurance to outlast him. Before long, they were low on reserves, and after that, it wasn’t much longer until cracks started to show. Freddy released his sword mid swing, bowling one of them over and then lunging with an impact, crushing their head.

From then, it was a straight one-on-one, and it didn’t take much for Freddy to wrap it up.

He felt like he was getting… kind of lucky, sort of? Not quite lucky—especially given the sudden trip to outer space—but he wasn’t dead yet, so it felt like things were going better than they were supposed to. Speaking of—

98 appeared with a golden door, and Freddy lunged for it with all the glee of a child spotting candy. White potion, wispy-cyan. “More speed!” He swallowed it together with any complaints about not getting to max out Hydraulic Flex. No freakin’ way would he be talking shit about Conquest ever again. It just let him skip floor 98. Freddy was Conquest’s number 1 fan at the moment.

He stepped into the next chamber, finding himself at floor 99, two trials left.

“Oh fuck you, Conquest, you piece of stupid shit!”

Triple black.

Freddy hesitated. He forced himself to sit, arms crossed before him. There wasn’t much to think about, really. Well, there was. A lot, actually, but what was he going to do? Quit when he was so close to the end?

But the revulsion at the idea of another black puzzle was overwhelming. Or a black environmental trial. At least a monster would kill him in a couple of minutes. It was hard to overstate how much that ocean puzzle screwed him up. Hell, he still occasionally felt an urge to swim up and take a breath. He was half-way there to evolving his ass back into water.

Before he could even realize what he was doing, he got to his feet, ran forward, and then leaped through the door.

He appeared on the other side, overwhelmed by the terror of what he’d just done. “What the actual fuck is wrong with me!?” Grabbing his hair, he pulled. “But I felt like I was definitely going to give up if I waited any longer! Oh, god! I’m actually going insane! I wasn’t ready!”

Finally remembering where he was, he looked around. He was in a forest of sorts, with a thick canopy above his head. He breathed deeply. “Environmental… not? I don’t see any clear paths anywhere.” His lips quivered. “Don’t be a puzzle, don’t be a puzzle… please don’t be a puzzle… oh, god, please.” Stepping forward, he explored the nearby area.

Not too long into his walk, he stepped out into a clearing, and spotted a glassed dome a couple of kilometers away, strikingly similar to the one enveloping Starhold, but considerably smaller. “Huh?” He ducked down into the tall grass, watching. Something shimmered in the horizon, and then, a white burst of light flew right at him. “Shit!” He jumped back as the patch of grass he’d been hiding in exploded as a Light Ray struck it.

Back in the “safety” of the forest, his mind raced as he tried to comprehend what he was seeing. “I need to gather more information.”

Over the course of a few days, he spied on the domed city, finding paths closer without getting hit by snipers. Bit by bit, he pieced together the broader picture. The forest wasn’t quite infested with monsters, but they were plentiful and constantly heading toward the city. The city, meanwhile, sent out scouts and other kinds of defenders, with sniper towers closer by to take care of targets the moment they left the forest.

It was quite similar to Starhold. Because clearly, this was meant to be a frontier city of sorts. But what was the trial, then?

Eventually, he stumbled across a small party of what looked like hunters. It was an alien race again, but quite a bit more humanoid than the wrinkled people he’d fought before. They were just a bit off-pinkish humans with loopy ears.

But apparently, he looked quite a bit less pleasant to them than they did to him. They spotted him, and immediately, immense looks of hatred crossed their faces. “It is yet another of those foul things! Friends, we must kill it at once!”

They proceeded to attack. The ragtag group was no match for Freddy, and their actions finally revealed the nature of the trial. It was a combat trial, multiple foes.

This was a pioneer city, set up in much the same way as Starhold, but a lot less powerful, with many layers of defenders and an entire society within.

And Freddy… Well, he was one of the monsters.

A huge grin split his face apart when he finally realized the nature of the trial. “Oh… This is going to be so much fun.”

Perhaps he had been wrong to be so afraid. He had taken a risk, yes, but while things can go wrong, sometimes, they can go right instead. A few weeks of a crazed “talking man-beast” scouring the woods for blood later, something mildly troublesome appeared from within the forest.

A skeletal giant the size of a tall skyscraper appeared in the distance. Then, the creature picked up a boulder the size of a large house, and threw it towards the city. It crashed through the glassed dome with all the might of a falling star, obliterating a huge section of the dome and proceeding to devastate the city inside it. Not a few minutes later, another similarly-sized projectile followed.

An army was mustered to address the threat, all the while a disguised figure slinked into the city. It was just a trial, but even Freddy couldn’t quite say he was comfortable with what was basically the slaughter of the relatively innocent while their fighters were out solving a problem he caused. But damn, was he good at it.

The city was full of easy targets, which meant effectively infinite essence and effectively infinite healing. Buildings collapsed one after another as Tsunami Strike after Tsunami Strike shook foundational pillars, causing mass devastation and ending untold lives. Just as the army was about to take Bloodshed down, he released it, and then got to slaughtering the literal hordes of fleeing people.

Hours of bloodshed later, a second skeleton appeared—this time, within the city. And this one wasn’t the size of a building, but the size of a tall person.

Poison mist spread across the devastated city blocks, mixing with the thick haze of concrete dust and whatever the hell else all the destruction had kicked up. Once the army tracked back into the city, that was when the real fight began, but they were at a disadvantage in every way imaginable.

Freddy was dressed exactly as one of their own combatants, and within the thick dust and blood fog, he could kill indiscriminately while his opponents needed time to find and identify him. It was days of hard work, and there were some individual opponents who could definitely pack a punch in their own right, but the trial was nowhere near actually difficult.

The exit appeared well before he was done finishing the last person off. He supposed he was actually only meant to kill a certain percentage of the residents, or something like that, but at any rate, he was happy to be finally done with it.

But rather than lunge through the exit, he paused. Then, confused at what he was even doing, he put his hands together, and prayed. “Oh… Conquest. Uh… I love you—no, I mean… I really think you’re a cool and awesome concept! Yeah! You’re like the best concept ever!” He nervously turned to Bloodshed. He wasn’t sure if those hollow eyes were judging him or not, but the silence was uncomfortable nevertheless. “Anyway…

“I am just a single floor away from being done. I just want a green trial, ok? Hell, I’d even settle for a blue! Fuck it! Make it all blue! Just please don’t hit me with black trials again! Please, okay? We have a deal? Yes?” He stared up. Nobody answered. “We have a deal then!”

He marched forward, his face twisting in discontent. “Let’s just get this over with…”

He appeared within the last chamber, up on the 100th floor.

Before him stood three doors.

One, on the very left, black, its darkness whispering promises of great suffering.

Two, in the middle, black yet again, echoing the whispers of its kin.

And three, on the right…

A golden door.

Freddy was developing a bit of a habit of falling to his knees, but this time, it really felt warranted. He burst into tears. “Really…? It’s not a trick?” He laughed. “Is this even allowed? It feels dirty. Actually, no—scratch that. It feels perfectly fine and awesome. I did just pass a black trial on the 99th floor… I think I’m good on the fairness bit.”

Turning to look over the chamber, he couldn’t help but feel a little wistful. Technically, every floor hub was a different room, but they all looked the same, and he’d spent so much time in them that they were starting to feel like home. This was the place he’d always return to, where he’d rest, relax, be safe. It was always a little sad to move away, but alas, he had other places to be.

He made it through the golden doors, and there, he found an orb. Blue. Cautiously stepping towards it, he gently picked it up. “If it’s for Tsunami Strike, I swear to god I’m going to lose it.” He put it in his mouth and swallowed.

The shell for Hydraulic Flex swelled, and then, the runes unraveled, turning into a blurry, pluripotent mess. It had reached the first reformation stage: Stage 3—50%. He laughed. “Life can really be good sometimes.”

Without further ado, he walked through the final exit, unsure of where he would find himself.

He appeared in yet another chamber. But this one was different. The stone was pearly white, and gave off a regal aura. The air itself was… clean in a way he’d never quite experienced before, almost as if it would purge his very soul of anything filthy.

Right in the middle, stood conquest, in all its blinged out glory. “How does it feel, Freddy Stern?” it asked.

He grinned. “Feels pretty fucking good, I can’t lie.”

The concept laughed. “I know it does.”

“By the way, thank you for that last floor. That was extremely generous of you.”

“What do you mean?” the concept asked.

Freddy halted. “Well… I uh… I was under the impression you decided on the trials?”

“The trials are administered randomly, as are the rewards.”

Freddy stared at the construct for a long moment. “Seriously? You’ve gotta be joking.”

“I would do no such thing.”

He found his anger rising a bit. “But that feels… So someone could just get a hundred straight golden doors, entirely at random?”

“Unlikely, but possible.”

“Or a hundred black ones?”

It nodded.

He forced a curse down. “Do you at least get a better reward at the end if you take more black trials?”

“No.”

“What the hell!?” He failed to hold himself back. “That’s so unfair!”

“You are right.” Conquest looked up. “Conquest isn’t fair to anyone, young conqueror. Some have it easier, and some harder. But in the end, to conquer requires the same thing of anyone: to seize and overcome all which stands before you, no matter the form it takes.”

Freddy sighed. “Yeah, okay. Well, I’ve done my seizing and overcoming. What do I get?”

Prepare to receive your reward.

His heart hammered in his chest, feeling like he was about to pass out from anticipation. Conquest raised its hand, and then, a ball appeared upon it. Freddy’s eyes shot wide open. It was a mute gray color, with a neutral face upon its surface. But much too large to be a regular prime vestige. The unique slowly floated over to him, and he gingerly grabbed it, releasing a nervous breath.

“If you ascend within this chamber, you shall find the results most to your liking,” Conquest said. “And now, I must depart.” It vanished.

Freddy had barely heard anything it said. “Wait, huh?” He looked up. “Did it just insinuate that my ascension will go better if I ascend here?” Looking around, he couldn’t deny that the room did feel special in some way, but it wasn’t quite something he could decipher. “Okay… Okay…” He slowly breathed out. “Okay… Calm down.”

First, he needed to ask the unique prime vestige what it did. “What talent do you hold?”

It met his gaze, and then said, “What talent do I hold?”

“I don’t know…? That’s why I’m asking you.”

No response.

“Okay… What affinities do you hold, then?”

“What affinities do I hold?”

Freddy bit back yet another curse. The vestige was one of those that were supremely annoying to talk to. But he really, really needed to figure out what it did, since he wasn’t leaving the tower without taking it. “Okay…” Suddenly, a stray thought struck him like lightning. He looked down at the prime, his eyes widening. “Wait… When you… ask me what talent you hold… do you mean to suggest that…”—he was almost afraid to finish the sentence—”I get to decide?”

It was quiet for a moment, and then it said, “I will do my best.”

“That’s not a straight answer.” He pointed his finger at it. “Yes or no?”

“I will do my best.”

He released something between a laugh and a growl. “Okay, so if I tell you that I want a talent that will make me do a billion times more damage, what will happen?”

“I will do my best.”

“But what if I tell you I want you to hold the blood affinity?”

“Do you want me to hold the blood affinity?”

He halted. “Let’s just say, theoretically, if I wanted you to… would you, then, hold the blood affinity?”

“Yes.”

Freddy dropped the ball. “There’s no fucking way.” He looked down at the prime rolling across the ground. “So wait… When you say you will do your best, then… Do you mean that you will do your best to create a talent as close to what I ask as possible?”

“Yes.”

He nearly passed out. “I need to sit down for a bit.”

A custom second talent.

That was his reward for clearing the Tower of York.

Freddy had plenty of time to fantasize about what he’d get while he’d been stuck in the longer trials. But at no point had he ever considered this possibility. This was unheard of. This was a reward so extravagant that he struggled to think straight. And what talent should he even pick? “Okay… I’m in no rush. I have time. Think, Freddy… What’s the play here?”

First, he took a long time to recall every single talent he’d ever heard of. It took a long while, but eventually, he reached a point where he wasn’t sure he was actually remembering shit or making it up. While thinking, he occasionally threw a couple carefully-worded questions at the prime, while desperately trying to avoid accidentally setting the talent to one he didn’t really want.

He wasn’t sure if he could change it multiple times, but that wasn’t something he’d risk finding out.

The answer to any suggested talent was “I will do my best.” That was a bit of a bummer.

What he gathered was that the talent was limited in overall power, which made perfect sense, but it complicated the process a little. It made it so that he could only safely aim for “linear” talents, something that would only change in quantity, but not quality.

If he asked for something like more damage, he didn’t know what the number would land on, but at the very least, it would still increase his damage.

If he asked for something complex, like Sophia’s talent, he needed it to be capable of perfectly replicating all the important bits—otherwise, he’d risk landing a nonsense talent that did nothing useful in the slightest.

Thankfully, there was no shortage of overpowered linear talents.

He went through the list several times in his mind, and eventually, he landed on a couple of possibilities.

First—an essence discount talent.

There was no way to “increase” the amount of essence one had per star, but a discount was pretty much the same thing in practice. This was a pretty fantastic talent. Having extra essence was always good, and given the kind of ascension he was likely to go through, he’d hit some crazy numbers, maybe even eighty or more percent. An 80% discount on all abilities was an effective five times multiplier to the total amount of essence he had.

That would be an immense advantage, for too many reasons to count. An essence regeneration talent wasn’t a horrible idea either, but the main advantage of those was in the fact they purged reflux essence. He could already do that. And discounts mathematically beat regeneration in every other way. Even in regeneration itself.

And 80% discount to his abilities would make a three-star satellite worth 15% essence per second, since every percent would be five times more valuable. A talent regenerating 5% per second was already considered immensely powerful, so odds were, Freddy wouldn’t get better 10%. It was an easy choice.

Second, an elemental resistance talent.

It was a bit of a niche choice, but it would patch up one of his worst weaknesses. That one ape monster had been a bit of a wake-up call—lightning attacks could lock him down, and there was very little he could do to fight back.

Third, a compound talent of overall physique boost and added ability power.

Compound talents were simply talents that did several things. Usually, due to the added flexibility, they tended to lose a bit of overall power, but if the several features they offered had the right synergy, more could be gained than was lost.

Freddy’s abilities were mostly oriented either around empowering him or required him to be physically powerful to fully utilize. Boosting both aspects would be an immense boon in raw power.

Fourth—

He scowled.

“The fourth choice…” He chuckled. “Does it really matter what I pick?” he asked himself. “No, that’s not the right way to put it.” He pinched the bridge of his nose. “I could come up with any number of great, straightforward, well thought-out talents. But is that really the best choice for me?” He looked around.

He was in the final chamber of the Tower of York.

Sure, his build wasn’t perfect. He’d certainly had a good deal of luck coming this far. But nevertheless, out of the countless people to have tackled this challenge, he was the only one to beat it, and the second place was 18 floors behind him.

He might not make perfect decisions, but god damn it if he wasn’t doing something right.

Smiling, he relaxed a bit as some of the pressure left him. There was no perfect talent. He could agonize over the choices until the end of time, but ultimately, every choice had its strengths and weaknesses. And no matter what he picked, he was sure that his next ascension would evolve it to be more suitable for him.

So the question to ask was—”What kind of talent do I want?”

Frankly, it was easier to answer that than he expected

He wanted a weapon talent.

It was a kind of talent he’d always seen on the BC and just couldn’t help but envy. It was such a visible and overwhelming kind of power, and honestly, it was pretty cool.

While conjuring blood weapons was also cool, they didn’t have that oomph of a talent-backed weapon.

But what if they did?

A grin threatened to split his face apart as he thought of another kind of talent. And he was starting to think he might have been wrong about what he thought earlier.

Perhaps there was a perfect talent. One perfect for him, at the very least.

He picked up the prime. “I want you to have a talent that empowers every blood construct I create.” Not only would this make every single blood weapon he conjured far more powerful, but it would also empower Bloodshed.

Freddy couldn’t possibly think of a talent better than that.

“I will do my best,” the prime decreed.

As for how his constructs would be empowered? He didn’t know. But no matter what, it certainly wouldn’t make them weaker.

“And as for the affinity, I want blood.”

“I will do as you ask.”

This wasn’t just a way to get a second talent—it was also a way to get another unique affinity. Freddy could admit that perished water was a bit of a miss for him. He really needed to experiment with ways to get some use out of it, but from what he was seeing, it would be pretty hard to make it anything but a way to gain an extra edge if he found himself fighting in water. And that… honestly that wasn’t the worst thing in the world.

Few people out there could claim they had such an advantage.

But nevertheless, a unique affinity was meant to be more than just that. And as his more or less main affinity, blood was the best choice.

The prime suddenly morphed, changing its colors to red, and its eyes turned into a deep, threatening shade of black. “Vermin! Do you want me in your soul or not? Hurry, I do not want to dally any longer than necessary.”

Freddy laughed. “Hell yeah! Get in there!”

The prime vanished in a fog of ether that sank into his body, filling it with new power. A second talent, and yet another unique affinity appeared.

A golden glow was added to the soup of affinity colors in his soul.

Sitting down, Freddy excitedly channeled essence through the affinity. It… It didn’t really want to do much. This wasn’t unexpected, however. Unique affinities weren’t easy to figure out, after all. But eventually, as he fiddled enough, it stuck to his bone marrow, seeping into it. “It’s tempering the marrow?” He gasped “Is it the ichor affinity!?”

The ichor affinity made the bones produce, well, ichor, which was a kind of secondary resource, much like blood itself. Ichor could be used to empower abilities, like a second layer of essence. It was an immensely powerful affinity, but upon further inspection, Freddy deduced that this couldn’t be it.

Ichor essence was a pearly-looking white, not gold. This was something else.

He patiently channeled essence through his marrow, and after a while, he noted that a new resource was being produced. He could sense it moving about his blood with the essence of whatever affinity this was. Slowly, he allowed it to gather for a few hours, and then, he collected it, guiding it into his heart to store with the rest of his blood. There, it settled, separating from blood the way oil separated from water.

Then, he grabbed hold of it, and began moving it out of his body. No matter what he did, it just didn’t want to leave. “Okay… so… stuck internally? Maybe?” He scowled. He fiddled with it a bit more, and then, he felt something bizarre. Suddenly, it abandoned the vein it was travelling to and moved into Freddy’s muscle, but it didn’t harm him.

The little drop of gold felt like it was scanning whatever part of his body it passed though. He shifted around, but ultimately, he couldn’t figure out how to do anything else.

Then, out of curiosity, he checked his soul. “Uh…” There were countless potential abilities in most affinities. Just doing some essence exercises could stumble upon a whole bunch of seeds for entirely different abilities, and those seeds appeared within the soul, scattered about like barely-formed ability shells. They gave the ethercosm a playful backflow, and were harmless.

This affinity, whatever the hell it was, had filled his soul with an absolute shit ton of fledgling ability shells. Like, so many that his soul almost appeared to be glowing gold. “Okay… so my last unique affinity was locked to only two possible abilities, while this one is apparently the most complex affinity in existence?” He snorted. “Well that’s just swell.”

Examining some of the bigger seeds, he discovered something else that was strange. He focused on one, and realized that it applied essence differently than just raw manipulation. That was bizarre.

Leaving the ethercosm, he focused on the drop again, and tried replicating the effect of that ability.

The drop popped out of the palm of his hand, turning into a little nub. “Huh?” He touched it. It felt metallic to the touch, but also soft, almost like flesh. “What the hell is this…?”

He’d need a whole lot more of this golden blood to figure it out. That could wait for later.

For now, he had something else to play with.

He took a breath, and got to his feet, conjuring Sanguine Avenger. The weapon appeared, and immediately, Freddy could tell that something was different. It looked more sleek, its color having shifted a bit towards violet, with the design in the center having grown more complex, and the snake-head pommel turning almost life-like.

Dragging his finger across the blade, he tested the sharpness. And it had definitely become sharper, even with the thickness of the blade.

But that wasn’t the most interesting new addition. No, there was also something deeper. It had gained a new kind of function.

He raised the sword, then, activating the function, swung it, curious about what would happen. Suddenly, it felt like the sword was moving through molasses, and where it moved, it left a shimmering red gash in reality. Once the weapon ceased moving, the crescent-shaped gash suddenly shot forward, flying into the wall and slamming into it with all the force of a full-powered swing.

Freddy gaped at the display of power. “Okay, that’s badass as fuck.” He laughed maniacally. “A flying strike!? That’s just…” He mimed a chef’s kiss. “Perfection.”

He tested the Knuckle Blade next. It also looked a good bit sleeker than it used to, but generally, it had a pretty utilitarian design, and that didn’t change. Nothing had really changed about it that he could tell. “Hmm…” He pulled his fist back, preparing to test the impact function.

Once he activated it, something bizarre happened. His body suddenly lurched at immense speed, and he moved about ten meters forward in the blink of an eye. It wasn’t quite teleportation, but the sheer speed he’d moved at was immense. Eyes wide open in disbelief, he immediately tested the attack against the wall, but felt he knew what would happen.

The blade struck, but it didn’t hit like he was moving particularly fast. “Repositioning, then.” Repositioning was a kind of movement that didn’t carry any extra kinetic energy. He moved back, raising the Knuckle Blade toward the wall, arm straight out, not moving. He activated the impact function, and again, he moved in the blink of an eye, and appeared with the blade just barely touching the wall.

He aimed the blade up, and yet again, he appeared ten meters in the direction he was pointing, and then fell back down as gravity asserted itself.

His grin could barely be contained by the width of his face.

Repositioning might not add any extra kinetic energy, but it would still carry the energy of whatever attack he was in the middle of. Being able to suddenly blink like that and deliver an attack was… “This talent is already unbelievably powerful.”

Next up was Blood Javelin. Again, sleeker, nicer design—the head actually grew an arrow shape now, which, fair enough, the skewer design wasn’t really the best anyway. The instant he conjured it, the presence of another new function made itself known. This one begging him to pick a… direction? Target?

He turned to the wall, picking a spot. He threw the javelin right at it, and it flew just as expected, embedding itself. Scowling, he conjured another javelin. This time, he threw it a bit to the side of where he picked—and the weapon steered to hit the target anyway.

Freddy’s laugh was diabolical. If someone heard him, they’d think he was a—well, they’d… probably make a… moderately fair guess at what kind of mental state he is in, to be honest.

Next up were the forearm shields. Their redesign was the most drastic, with an actual feather pattern appearing across them, and their design resembling a folded dual pair of wings. He smashed the shield with the Sanguine Avenger, and those wings furled out, physically enveloping most of his body to protect him from harm. And they stayed like that for a few long seconds, too, shifting to flawlessly accommodate his movements.

That looked immensely awesome, but he wasn’t really sure how much better it was. Time would tell. Perhaps they would furl out to pre-emptively block an incoming attack? But he couldn’t quite test that on his own.

Terror Knuckles had received another redesign. The spikes had grown far denser, and thinner. And they… wiggled. He immediately felt a deeper connection to them, and with a thought, he made the spikes bundle up. He could spread them out to a whole bunch of thinner spikes, or condense them into a couple thicker ones.

It was a small upgrade, admittedly, but it would allow him to make them better fit different kinds of opponents.

And last but definitely not least—the Crimson Dragon Scales.

Visually, the effect was incredible. The tinge of violet really made them pop, and the scales had shifted around a bit, as well. But the new effect was… meh. They had a kind of shallow repulsive field around them. That would be great against gasses and maybe liquids, he supposed, but it wasn’t nearly strong enough to actually stop any kind of serious attack. Still, he definitely noted an increase to their overall durability, so he wasn’t going to complain too much.

He also really wanted to test Bloodshed, but that would have to wait until he ascended and left the Tower. Not much to kill here, sadly.

Freddy decided that smack dab in the middle of the chamber was the perfect place to ascend. It was thematically fitting, he supposed.

He sat down, and then, went through a quick mental list of everything he needed.

Pulling out the light and dark affinity shards, he consumed them. The fledgling affinities appeared within his star, but they wouldn’t stay there for long as he replaced them with their advanced sister affinities.

Next up, he consumed the Overpearl. The thing burned something wicked on the way down, and the effect on his soul was profound. He felt some nearly-imperceptible lingering soul damage vanish, as the pearl filled in the cracks, ensuring that nothing would leak. Other than that, a dense energy seeped into the deeper layers of his soul, suffusing his latent aura and funding his ascension.

And with that… He was done.

He was ready to ascend.

It was a somber moment, admittedly, almost like growing up a second time. And that wasn’t even the wrong way to put it. Many in the university stalled their ascension to the third star precisely because, for archhumans, it really was like growing up. A lot more was expected from three-stars than two-stars in high-level factions.

Freddy had never quite gotten that freedom to just play around without a care, but even then, parting with it hurt nevertheless. “Forced to grow up early again, huh…?” He smiled. “I guess some things never change.” And with that, he closed his eyes, embracing the roiling might of his second star, forcing it to birth a third.

But nothing happened. He scowled. “Why isn’t it—argh!?” He recoiled as wisps of ether began peeling off his body, then floating before him and manifesting into a pair of floating, orange eyes. “A choice stands before ye,” his first prime spoke. “Ye may accept one part healing per one part harm, or another, one that must be earned. State your choice.”

Freddy blinked. One part per—His eyes shot wide open. 100% Lifesteal!? How much—holy fuck! How much latent aura did I actually gather!? He froze. What the hell is the other option, then?

“What’s the second option?” he asked.

The prime rumbled. “That is for ye to discover by yeself.”

He scowled. “And what will happen if I choose it, exactly?”

The prime remained quiet.

Freddy laughed. “I just really…” He mimed strangulation. “Really love when you primes go  all cryptic. Just… I adore it!” He took a deep breath. There was no need to think. He had already conjured all his blood equipment, but he scooted over to where he’d left his sword, and then picked it up, coiling the Cycle Scythe around it. The prime did say it would have to be “earned,” and on the off-chance that meant a fight, he’d rather be ready for it. “Ye shall grant me thine uh… Just give me the second one.”

“As you wish.” The prime dissolved into wisps and sank into Freddy’s body again. And then, pain. He tried to scream as his body contorted, but he physically couldn’t muster the strength to do so. Wisps peeled off his form yet again, but this time, they were of the death affinity—little skulls, tumbling over to a spot not far from him, and then, slowly manifesting into a figure.

Freddy barely regained himself enough to get up to his feet as the figure resolved, leaving him frozen in terror.

It was an eidolon, of a kind, but a touch more lifelike than usual. And it looked like him—like a younger version of him, before he’d become an archhuman. A younger version, yes, but also a clearly dead version of him. With pale, sickly skin, sunken eyes, and green veins faintly showing under the skin. The thing slimed gently at him, its eyes narrowing into slits. “I am Freddy’s Death,” it said. “And you are Undeserving.”

Freddy’s heart thumped. He fell down, a stark paralysis washing over him. What… the… He felt his left arm go numb as a sharp pain spread from the center of his being, and a feeling of suffocation began to seep into him. Am I having a heart attack…? How is that even…? His thoughts were rapidly unraveling. Oh my god I’m dying.

Somewhere in the distant back of his mind, he heard the sounds of the Sanguine Avenger screaming bloody murder, the blade begging for revenge against the harm Freddy was suffering. Freddy moved with every shred of power he could muster, slowly raising the sword into the air. He activated the flying strike, and then pulled the weapon down with all his might.

A short, thin red gash appeared in the air, and then flew right towards the bizarre eidolon. The creature did nothing to move out of the way as the attack slammed right into its center with a great deal of force. It tumbled back, and then, dissolved into faint wisps that scattered into the ether.

Freddy stopped dying. His soul roiled with the force of an actual star preparing to explode within, and then, with a blinding light, a third one was born.

He rejected the ice affinity when it was offered, and replaced the light and dark with holy and unholy. Then, he lunged to his feet. His third star brimmed with power, his talents had evolved, and he felt his body changing, morphing as he grew a little sleeker, with something happening to his hair and eyes, but he couldn’t focus on any of it.

Freddy simply stared at his hands in terror as an immense sense of loss overwhelmed him. He felt something expand, a part of him grow, further and further and further and further into the distance, further and further—

Tears streaked down the side of his face as he took a whimpering breath, looking around as if searching for something gone forever more.

“What have I done?”
