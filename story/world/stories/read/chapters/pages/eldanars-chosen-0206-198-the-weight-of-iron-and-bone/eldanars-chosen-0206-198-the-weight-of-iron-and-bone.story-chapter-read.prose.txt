The retreat from the basin ridge was a miserable, desperate affair. The adrenaline that had fuelled their brief, violent skirmish with the Bone Goliath was rapidly bleeding away, leaving behind only the biting cold and the suffocating stench of the rotting forest. They moved as quickly as the treacherous, muddy terrain would allow, but their pace was agonisingly slow.

Josh and Perberos were carrying Bhel between them. Josh had a firm grip on the thick leather straps of the dwarf’s heavy belt, while the elven ranger supported his thick, iron-plated legs. Bhel was an incredibly dense, solid mass of muscle and metal, making him exceptionally difficult to haul through the clinging, freezing slurry that coated the forest floor.

For the first twenty minutes, the dwarf remained slumped in a troubled, pained unconsciousness. His breathing was a ragged, wet rattle, and his deeply tanned face was a sickly, ashen grey.

Then, as Josh accidentally jostled him while stepping over a decaying, moss-covered log, Bhel let out a sharp, bubbling groan. The dwarf’s eyes fluttered open, wildly unfocused for a brief second before locking onto the pale, pristine face of the elf currently holding his boots.

"Get your skinny hands off me, you pointy-eared twig," Bhel wheezed, his voice lacking its usual booming volume but retaining every ounce of its customary stubbornness. He weakly tried to kick his legs free. "Put me down."

"Be still, Bhel," Josh ordered gently, tightening his grip on the dwarf's belt to prevent him from thrashing. "You took a spiked mace directly to the shoulder. You are losing a lot of blood."

Bhel craned his neck awkwardly to look at Josh. He noticed his feet were dangling uselessly above the mud. A look of profound, mortified embarrassment washed over his grey face. For a proud dwarven fighter, being carried away from a battlefield like a helpless sack of grain was a humiliation almost worse than death.

"I said put me down, lad," Bhel demanded, his breathing growing increasingly shallow as he struggled against their grip. "I have two perfectly good legs. I can walk. I am not a child needing a piggyback."

"You will bleed to death before we reach the halfway point if you try to walk on that injury," Brett pointed out from the rear of the formation. The mage was holding his hands close to his chest, his dark red drake-scale gloves glowing faintly to provide a small measure of warmth against the biting chill. "Your pride is currently a secondary concern to your survival."

Bhel opened his mouth to utter a highly colourful dwarven insult at the mage, but the words died in his throat as a sudden, violent spasm of pain wracked his body. He coughed, a thick splatter of dark blood coating his braided beard, and his head slumped heavily backward. He was slipping in and out of consciousness, the necrotic infection ravaging his nervous system.

"He is fading again," Carcan warned urgently. The elven healer was practically jogging to keep pace with them, her amber-topped staff glowing brightly as she directed constant, minor pulses of golden magic towards the horrific wound on Bhel's chest.

Before Josh could respond, the dark, tangled undergrowth directly ahead of them violently parted.

Three skeletal legionnaires, clad in rusted chainmail and wielding jagged iron swords, stepped out onto the muddy path. They were a roving patrol, likely drawn by the noise of their clumsy retreat. The undead warriors turned their glowing green eye sockets toward the party and immediately charged.

"Drop him!" Josh shouted.

It was a clumsy, uncoordinated manoeuvre. Josh and Perberos were forced to practically drop Bhel into the freezing mud to free their hands. Josh ripped his longsword from its scabbard, the steel ringing sharply in the quiet forest, while Perberos drew his twin curved blades in a silent blur.

They dispatched the three skeletons in a matter of seconds. Josh crushed a brittle ribcage with his heavy steel shield, while Perberos elegantly decapitated the remaining two with a fluid, scissor-like strike. The undead collapsed into piles of loose bone and instantly dissolved into shimmering golden dust.

But the brief interruption highlighted a glaring, fatal flaw in their current strategy.

Josh sheathed his sword with an angry, metallic click and looked down at Bhel. The dwarf was groaning in the mud, the horrific wound on his shoulder oozing a thick, foul-smelling black sludge that mixed terribly with the dirt.

"This is not going to work," Josh stated flatly, his storm-grey eyes hardening with absolute tactical resolve. "If we have to drop him into the dirt every single time we encounter a stray group of monsters, the infection will kill him, or we will be overrun while our hands are full. We need our weapons free."

"I cannot carry him alone," Perberos admitted softly, wiping the golden dust from his elven blades. "His armour is too dense."

"You are not carrying him," Josh decided, stepping over the dwarf. He reached into his spatial inventory pouch and retrieved a long, incredibly thick coil of heavy hemp climbing rope. "You are our eyes. You need to be fifty yards ahead of us, clearing the path before we even see them. Brett, you cover our backs. Do not let anything follow us from the basin."

Josh knelt in the mud beside Bhel. He began uncoiling the heavy rope with quick, practised movements.

"Carcan," Josh said, looking up at the healer. "Help me lift him. I am putting him on my back."

Carcan’s silver eyes widened slightly in alarm. She looked at Josh’s heavy steel plate armour, and then at the massive, iron-clad dwarf. "Josh, the sheer weight of carrying him like that will be catastrophic. Your boots will sink straight into the mud. You will exhaust your stamina before we cover a single mile."

"I have the stats to manage it," Josh insisted stubbornly, looping the thick rope under Bhel’s thick arms. "My Constitution and Strength are high enough to bear the load, and my Juggernaut's Tread skill will keep me moving through the deep mud. It is the only way to keep our primary damage dealers free to fight."

He didn't wait for further argument. With Carcan’s reluctant help, Josh awkwardly hoisted the unconscious dwarf up against his broad back. Bhel’s chin rested heavily on Josh’s steel-plated shoulder, his thick, blood-soaked beard brushing against Josh’s neck.

Josh rapidly wound the heavy rope around Bhel’s torso, passing it under the dwarf's arms and bringing it tightly across his own chest piece, securing it with several complex, hopefully unbreakable knots. It was a crude, brutal harness, effectively turning Bhel into an incredibly massive, bleeding backpack.

When Josh finally stood up, the sheer, crushing reality of the weight hit him instantly.

His heavy steel boots drove deep into the soft mud, sinking several inches past his ankles. His knees groaned under the immense pressure, and the thick rope dug painfully into his side. He was carrying nearly three hundred pounds of dwarf and iron, on top of his own fifty pounds of heavy plate armour and weaponry.

Josh gritted his teeth, forcing his spine straight. He closed his eyes for a brief second, calling upon the latent, humming energy within his core. He didn't trigger an explosive combat skill, but he activated his passive strength enhancements, letting the magical energy flood his muscles to compensate for the absurd burden.

"Are you sure about this?" Brett asked quietly, stepping closer. The mage looked genuinely concerned, adjusting his imaginary spectacles. "You are essentially turning yourself into a pack mule."

"I am fine," Josh grunted, taking a slow, heavy, testing step forward. The mud squelched loudly around his boots. He found his balance, leaning slightly forward to offset the massive weight on his back. "Perberos, clear the way. Brett, watch the rear. Let's move."

The new formation was infinitely more efficient, though it required a horrific physical toll from their leader.

Perberos vanished into the gloom ahead of them, moving with absolute, liquid silence. The elven ranger became a ghost in the rotting woods. He did not wait for the party to engage obstacles. Whenever he encountered a skeletal patrol or a pack of wandering, corrupted native beasts, he simply eliminated them with ruthless, silent precision.

Josh would occasionally hear the sharp, deadly hum of a bowstring from the shadows ahead, followed by a faint thud, and a minute later he would trudge past a dissolving pile of golden dust. Perberos was carving a safe, clear tunnel through the hostile environment, ensuring Josh never had to stop his punishing, relentless march.

Behind them, Brett operated with cold, academic efficiency. Whenever the distinct sounds of shuffling footsteps or clicking jaws echoed from the path behind them, Brett did not even turn around. He simply snapped his fingers, sending a highly compressed sphere of roaring plasma backwards into the gloom. The brief, blinding flashes of blue and white light were always followed by the smell of scorched earth and incinerated bone.

But the true battle was taking place right in the middle of the formation.

Carcan walked perfectly in step with Josh. She did not stray more than a few inches from his side. Her face was incredibly pale, covered in a sheen of cold sweat, and her silver eyes were fixed entirely on the terrible wound resting just inches from Josh's ear.

The spiked mace of the Bone Goliath had driven deep into Bhel’s shoulder, cracking the collarbone and tearing a massive, jagged gouge down his chest. But the physical trauma was entirely secondary to the magical infection.

The wound was lined with a thick, pulsing black sludge. It was raw necrotic energy, a physical manifestation of the dark mana that saturated the forest around them.

Carcan kept her hands hovering just inches from the wound, her amber-topped staff tucked under her arm. A continuous, brilliant stream of golden holy magic flowed from her palms, washing over the torn dwarven flesh.

It was a horrifying, desperate tug-of-war.

Josh watched from the corner of his eye as Carcan’s golden magic aggressively purged the black sludge. The holy energy would force the necrotic rot back, sealing the smallest blood vessels and encouraging the torn muscle fibres to rapidly knit themselves together. For a few glorious seconds, the wound would look clean, and Bhel’s breathing would ease.

But the forest was actively fighting her.

The ambient necrotic energy in the air was so thick it tasted of rust and old graves. The dead, rotting pine trees and the stagnant, freezing mud radiated dark magic. The very moment Carcan paused her healing stream to wipe the sweat from her brow or catch her breath, the environment retaliated.

The black sludge would bubble back up from the depths of the wound, hissing violently as it ate through the newly healed tissue. The dark, chaotic veins would spider-web back across Bhel’s grey skin, and the wound would violently tear itself open again, spilling fresh, dark blood down Josh’s steel backplate.

"It is too strong," Carcan gasped, her voice trembling with exhaustion as she forced another wave of golden light into the dwarf's shoulder. She stumbled slightly over a hidden root, but quickly regained her footing, refusing to stop the magical flow. "The ambient saturation is feeding the rot continuously. I am pouring raw mana into him, but it is like trying to empty a flooding river with a teacup. Every time I close the deepest lacerations, the dark mana simply tears them apart."

"Just keep him stable, Carcan," Josh grunted, his voice tight with extreme physical exertion. Every step was a monumental effort. "You do not need to cure him. You just need to keep his heart beating until we break the treeline. Once we are out of this rotting atmosphere, your magic will hold."

"I am trying," she whispered, her hands trembling as the golden light flickered slightly.

Bhel groaned loudly in Josh’s ear, the sound wet and rattling. The dwarf’s head lolled to the side, his thick beard brushing against Carcan’s shoulder.

"Embarrassing," Bhel mumbled, his eyes completely closed, locked in a feverish delirium. "Carried like a prized pig. Do not tell Garrik. He will never let me drink in peace again."

"Nobody is going to tell Garrik anything," Josh replied smoothly, trying to offer a comforting tone despite his own burning lungs. "You just rest, Bhel. Let us do the walking."

"Spiked mace," Bhel continued to mumble, completely ignoring Josh. "Sloppy footwork. Should have dodged. Should have broken its knees. Disgraceful."

The delirious rambling was actually a comforting sound. It meant the dwarf’s stubborn spirit was still fighting the infection, refusing to quietly surrender to the dark magic.

The march dragged on for hours. The sun was entirely hidden by the dense, dead canopy overhead, making it impossible to judge the time. The forest was an endless, repetitive nightmare of rotting wood, black mud, and the constant, oppressive silence broken only by Brett’s occasional plasma bursts and Perberos’s lethal bowstrings.

Josh fell into a deep, almost meditative trance of pure endurance. He focused entirely on placing one heavy foot in front of the other. The thick hemp ropes were digging viciously into his collarbones, leaving deep bruises beneath his padded gambeson. His thighs burned with lactic acid, and his lower back screamed in protest from the unnatural, shifting weight of the unconscious dwarf.

He relied heavily on his Juggernaut's Tread skill. Even without activating the explosive blue lightning component, the passive benefits of the skill allowed him to trudge through knee-deep bogs of freezing mud without completely losing his momentum. He became a slow, mechanical engine of determination, putting his head down and refusing to stop.

Eventually, the air began to change.

The cloying, suffocating stench of decay started to thin. The thick, stagnant mud beneath Josh’s boots gradually transitioned back into firm, frost-covered dirt. The towering, completely dead pine trees slowly gave way to a more natural, albeit dormant, winter woodland.

"The necrotic density is dropping," Brett announced from the rear, his voice laced with profound relief. "We are crossing the outer perimeter of the basin's influence."

A few minutes later, they finally broke through the dense treeline.

Josh stepped out onto a wide, familiar dirt path, leaving the rotting forest behind them. The late afternoon sun was low in the sky, casting long, sharp shadows across the ground. The freezing wind whipped across the open space, biting at their faces, but it tasted miraculously clean. It tasted of natural winter, completely devoid of the foul, rusty tang of dark magic.

"We are clear," Perberos reported, stepping out from the shadows of a large oak tree just ahead of them. The elven scout looked tired, his quiver significantly lighter, but his silver eyes were bright. He pointed down the path. "The camp is less than half a mile away."

Josh let out a long, ragged exhale. He adjusted the heavy ropes digging into his chest, gritting his teeth against the fresh spike of pain. "Let's finish the march."

They pushed down the dirt road, their pace naturally quickening as the promise of safety drew nearer.

As they rounded a gentle bend in the path, the main forward operating camp finally came into view.

It was vastly different from the small, hastily constructed outpost they had left yesterday morning. The camp had exploded in size. The small cluster of canvas tents had been replaced by dozens of sturdy, heavy-duty military pavilions. Several large, roaring watchfires were burning brightly around the perimeter, casting a warm, welcoming orange glow against the gathering twilight.

Most surprisingly, the camp was bustling with people. The perimeter was patrolled by heavily armed city guards clad in the polished chainmail and tabards of Ashenfall. Several large groups of veteran adventurers were gathered around the central fires, sharpening weapons and studying parchment maps. The guild had mobilised a serious reinforcement effort to secure the frontier.

"They sent an army," Brett murmured, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose as he surveyed the bustling camp. "It seems Torin finally recognised the severity of the situation."

"Good," Josh grunted, his legs feeling like they were made of solid lead. "We are going to need every single one of them."
