A cool breeze gently tosses my curls over my face, brushing my cheek and making me stir. I feel wet earth beneath me, the blades of grass tickling the skin on my exposed arms and calves. Sunshine coaxes my closed eyes to flutter open. A bright blue sky and fluffy white clouds greet me.

Huh?

I sit up suddenly and my head swims. Slightly dizzy, I prop myself up with one hand while holding my head in the other, remembering my fall into the manhole in the city. This is definitely not the bottom of that manhole. Another cool breeze shifts the green grass around me and brings crisp, clean air with a floral scent to my lungs, causing me to glance around. I gasp as I take in my surroundings.

To my left, a glittering, deep blue lake is surrounded by steep mountains and hills that rise a few thousand feet on each side, adorned with lush greenery and snow dusted peaks. A waterfall cuts through one of them, creating the shape of a veil draped over the green mountainside before it cascades into the lake. Beyond, the jagged peaks of the mountains continue as far as I can see, their snow caps cutting a sharp contrast against the vibrant blue sky. Around me, a sea of indigo bluebell flowers with dots of yellow buttercups fill the glade I sit in, gently swaying in the breeze. Soft glimmering balls of light surround the bed of flowers I lay in, hovering around me like little fireflies. When I lean forward to try to touch one, I notice that they are fading away. My finger touches air, and they are gone, and I soon wonder if I just imagined them in the first place.

Am I dead? Is this...some form of the afterlife? I wasn't exactly a believer in Heaven, but I always believed there was something meant for our souls once we passed on.

I look down at myself and see that I'm still wearing my work clothes; a navy pencil skirt, navy flats and a frilly white blouse with eyelet embroidery, but I no longer have my purse and phone. Must have dropped it in the fall? Oddly, my body looks uninjured, and I don't see the bruising I expect to see from when my knees hit the curb earlier. Although, goosebumps are now gathering on my legs and arms from the chill of the wind. That's weird, I think. I didn't even feel the impact of the fall.

I look to my right, and see that there are more rising hills and mountains surrounding me. Beyond the glade I sit in, a valley extends in the distance, and I can see a natural, but rocky and uneven path though ancient looking, gnarly trees and the mountains beyond. I see no signs of civilization.

Suddenly, the thought of my potential demise starts to settle in, sending my heart into my stomach. I didn't survive that fall, did I? Oh my god...mom...my family.... I hug my knees into my chest tightly and drop my head, starting to shake, and not just from the cold. I try taking deep breaths to get my breathing under control and my heart rate down.

Isn't the afterlife supposed to be peaceful? Why do I feel this way? What's happening?

A sob breaks through my chest and I shut my eyes against it, hugging my knees even tighter. I stay that way for a while, before I gain control over my breathing and heart rate once again. It's a few minutes, or hours later, who knows, when I hear a baaaaaaa! that startles me enough to whip my head up. A scruffy, curly horned sheep with a matted white-grey coat, spotted black face and legs stands a few feet away from me, chewing some tall grass in its mouth and staring at me with its beady eyes. It grumbles another baaaaaa at me as its jaws slowly chew. I glance around and see that he has a few friends scattered nearby, also grazing from the tall grass. A few of them even have offspring, prancing around on awkward legs but still staying close to their mothers. I sigh, turn my gaze back on Scruffy and say, "you're right, I should get up and not wallow in my despair of my unexpected demise." Scruffy looks unmoved by my statement, and dips down his massive horned head to grab more grass. I snort and get up, brushing the back of my skirt in a futile attempt to remove the grass and peat stains I know are on my ass right now. I take a deep breath of the fresh mountain air, rub my arms against the chill and make my way to the natural path I spotted earlier.

The afterlife is definitely not what I expected. Maybe this is some sort of test?

I walk for an hour or two when the weather suddenly changes, a steady rain soaking me through as I hike. Great. It lasts about 20 minutes then stops, and the sun breaks through the clouds again. Shivering and miserable, I keep on, noting the path has become more of an incline.

Not much later I start to feel the grumbles of hunger in my stomach. I put my hand on my stomach and look down with confusion. Hunger? In the afterlife? This doesn't make any sense. Despite this, I trudge on, feet stumbling and squelching in the wet, slippery bog and rocks in my very non-hiking shoes, swearing occasionally in my frustration. At one point I don't catch myself and manage to scrape my knee on a rock. Hissing, I examine it and see that there is a small scratch of blood.

Blood. Am I really dead?

Confused as ever, I continue on my journey to who knows where. As I start to crest a hill that I trip and fall on again, this time muddying my knees and hands on the peat, I hear a rumbling behind me. I pause to listen closer and notice the sound is getting louder. It sounds like....hoof beats? I whip my head around, searching for the source, and see a group of about 40 men on horseback galloping my way from further inland. They wear the pelts of animals, armor made from leather and metal, braids in their hair and beards and carry various blades and axes strapped to their hips and backs. They have round, wooden shields covered with rawhide with metal knobs in the center, decorated with patterns and symbols that are strapped over their shoulders or in hand. Some of them wear rounded, metal helmets with a nose guard. They are a couple hundred yards away and closing in fast.

Shit!

I start sprinting, but I know it's just a matter of time before they will catch up with me and the bog sucks in every one of my frantic steps. My brain scrambles, trying to come up with some sort of plan when they will inevitably confront me. I have no weapons. I am outnumbered. Not to mention they are on horseback and I have no idea where I am or where I am going. Excited shouts in a language I do not recognize sound close behind me. Too close. I make the mistake of looking over my shoulder while my treacherous foot trips on another rock and sends me tumbling down the other side of the hill.

"Argh!" I manage to get out as I tumble, each roll causing a new source of pain and scrape in my already sore and bruised body.

Crack! Oh fuck, did I just break something?

Eventually, I come to a halt, in a heap on my side in a particularly wet part of the bottom of the hill. Everything hurts and I groan, but taking stock of my new injuries, just a few bad bruises, a rolled ankle and several cuts on my hands and knees. Phew! No broken bones after all.

The sound of hoof beats slow as the men on horseback surround me. A few of them laugh at my sorry state, one in particular with copper red hair matching mine throws his head back while his laughter booms above all the others. He is the size of a grizzly bear and holds his belly with one hand, pointing at me with the other as he bellows in laughter. Some of the men leer at me with hungry eyes, making comments to their neighbors in what sounds like Old Norse if I were to guess, while the rest appear to scowl in disgust at my apparent weakness. I glare back up at them. Fuck them.

As I look around, I realize that there are about 10 women among them too. They are also dressed in armor and armed with blades. Their eyes are fierce and unreadable as they look down at me, almost as if they are passing judgement.

Suddenly, the group quiets and parts a path on their horses for one man to ride up front. The way they move out of his way quietly and look to him indicates a sign of respect. Once I get a good look at him, I can see why. He is MASSIVE. Even on his spotted brown mare I can tell he must be about six and a half feet tall and sculpted of rippling muscle. Looking to be in his mid 30s, his sandy blonde hair frames a handsome face in light waves, part of it tied to the back of his head. A few beaded strands clink in the breeze. Unlike some of the others, he has a shaven face, showing off his strong, square jawline. His ice blue eyes are focused and narrowed on me with a sign of intelligence I don't see in the others, drawing me in and rooting me to the ground.

Something deep within me stirs.

His large, muscled frame wears padded leather armor beneath a shirt of mail and a tan tunic with a brown bear pelt draped over his shoulders. His arms sport leather bracers. A sheathed sword is strapped on a belt at his hip, the silver cross guard ornately decorated with two ravens interlocked in flight, heads facing away from one another, and what looks to be a tree with roots carved into the pommel. The scabbard is leather and covered with etched knot work. The men surrounding us stay behind as he rides in front of them. They appear to be looking to him for his next move. He must be their leader then. Unsurprising, as the man basically radiates power.

Remembering I'm still in a heap on the ground like an idiot, I stand up slowly, meeting his stare, trying not to wince from the pain in my body. This doesn't look good for me, but I am willing to fight if I have to. Wasn't I already dead? This is so fucking weird. Are they guardians of the afterlife?

Not even breaking eye contact, the leader effortlessly dismounts his mare and struts toward me with a swagger I'd expect from a man his size. He stops a foot away. In order to maintain eye contact, I have to tilt my head up at him because he's so freaking tall. I'm 5'7", but that still puts him at like 10 inches taller than me. This close, I can see that his eyes have flecks of grey and white in them, making them look like ice crystals floating on the sea. They really are beautiful. If I wasn't so terrified of him and my current situation, I would think he'd make the perfect main male character in one of my romance novels.

"Froðe, Hvað eigum við að gera með henni?" the bear man with copper red hair says to the leader, who appears to be named Froðe, (pronounced as Frode). He has a thick accent and looks pointedly at me. My eyes dart between bear man and Frode as I try to follow what's going on.

"Ég er að ákveða, Torsten," Froðe says, still looking at me intently, eyes glittering with curiosity. He looks at me like he's searching for a sign of something. Of what, I don't know. His voice is low, gravelly, and rough. It gives me the impression that this man is used to getting what he wants and is very dangerous. Froðe steps even closer, reaching his hand out to my face.

My alarm bells go off instantly and muscle memory takes over. I assume a Wing Chun defense stance and Pak Sau his hand away before it can reach me, preparing a maneuver to strike his jugular. Well, I try to. The guy is a fucking statue of muscle and for some reason I don't deflect his hand as much as I normally would in this maneuver. That's weird. I do manage to at least change the trajectory, which is enough to cause a glimmer of amusement in Froðe's eyes and the ghost of a smirk hits the corner of his mouth, hand still outstretched.

Torsten booms with laughter once again. "Bhaha! Froðe! Hún er ákafr..." I wince and lift my hand up with a hiss as a sharp pain erupts in my temple and a strange echo rings through my ears agh...what the fuck?!

"...woman. Watch yer back, she dinnae look so weak after all aye!"

Wait...I can understand them now? What is happening?

"What is she doing out here, in the middle of Lothlend, all by herself?" Another asks. Lothlend...never heard of it.

Froðe continues to study me, and tilts his head. His eyes are piercing and seem to somehow look right into me.

"What is your name, fiery one?" Froðe says. My heart is pounding right through my chest. Can he hear it?

"My name is..." I start, but it's not English coming from my mouth. My eyes widen in surprise. It sounds like...Old Norse?

What the fuck?! What's happening?!

My mind starts racing as I try to put the puzzle pieces together. I can suddenly speak and understand the language of these people I've encountered. I can feel pain and hunger. I can feel the breeze caressing my cheek and tossing my hair, and the wet earth beneath my soaked, muddy flats. I'm in a strange new time and place that is nothing like where I came from...almost like I'm a different world. Time slows as the realization dawns on me.

I am not dead. I am in fact, alive and breathing. I am now among the company of what appears to be vikings, in a place called Lothlend, as ridiculous as this all sounds.

"Holy shit," I whisper in disbelief, unable to suppress a chortle which causes Froðe to rise an eyebrow and look at me like I'm insane. I double over and my laugh turns hysterical. "I've actually been fucking isekai'd."
