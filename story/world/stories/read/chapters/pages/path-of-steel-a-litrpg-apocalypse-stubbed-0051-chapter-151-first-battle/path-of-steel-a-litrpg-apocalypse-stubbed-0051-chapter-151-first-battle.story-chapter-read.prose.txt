The white river served as a clear border between the two sides of this invasion. The gymnasium side held green grass, vibrant trees, and flowers lush with colors. The other side used to be like that, too, but it was now covered in a gray haze. The dirt was lifeless. The grass was dead, and the leaves had fallen off the trees to leave them jagged wood.

Through this dead terrain marched the horrors: a collection of zombies and corpses infested with grimy tentacles. Gary saw the body of a snake carried by a dozen spasming tentacle-feet. Its head dragged against the ground, the neck snapped. A spider followed, massive enough that a grown man could walk between its legs, and then came a spiked bear with half its body covered in squirming tentacles.

Climbers existed between the monsters, most coming from no particular clan. If they were repulsed by the monsters, they didn’t show it. Leading them was a familiar face: Alpha, one of the four brothers. His tall, bare-chested form led the entire charge.

This horrific menagerie rolled down the hill, descending to the bridge.

“For the gymnasium!” Boulderus Johnus yelled, leading Gary’s group down their own hill. Feet thundered on grass, quickly reaching the bottom and stepping onto the simple iron bridge. It was wide, more than a hundred feet from rail to rail and three hundred across, with white waters foaming below.

A width of a hundred feet might have been more than most bridges Gary knew of, but to climbers of their level, it was despairingly narrow. As the front of their group stepped onto the bridge, Gary slowed down, letting himself fall to the back. Most other climbers did the same, so he slowed down further, and again the other climbers followed. Only the Legion carried forward at the same speed, eager to meet the enemy on the very limited battlefield, as well as the gymnasium monsters, which ignored the slow climbers to rush head-first onto the bridge. A bunch of pro boxers, who had been shadow-boxing while running, charged into battle fully warmed-up.

Gary caught his beef boys about to speed into battle as well. “With me,” he told them.

“Boss! The ring is over there! Are we scared?” Osteo said.

“We aren’t scared, Osteo. It’s tactics.”

“I see!” Osteo said.

“The boss is so smart,” another beef boy said.

The two armies met in the middle of the bridge, and the ensuing aura shockwave split the river below. Gary’s robes fluttered. A few weaker climbers were thrown off their feet.

From the back, Gary saw the leading legionaries crash into the horrors as a shield wall. Multiple Core Formation clashes had to be contained within a hundred feet. Energies leaked far to the sides.

The legionaries were disciplined, but the enemy was also powerful. The first clash seemed equal, neither side giving way. Only instants later, however, the armies behind the first waves kept coming. Extreme amounts of aura pushed from either side, trapping the first waves of each army in the middle. They were compressed like an accordion. Energy pockets erupted everywhere as pressure built up.

The legionaries were affected, but they managed to hold on. They’d arranged themselves in parallel lines, leaving enough space in between to avoid stomping each other. The front lines handled the fighting. The back lines regulated the advance of the next waves so as not to overwhelm the front. Boulderus Johnus yelled orders from somewhere in the middle, while feather-helmeted legionaries coordinated smaller sections of the army. In the very middle of the bridge, Alpha broke through the shield wall like a bowling ball, and Boulderus Johnus rushed to meet him.

This wasn’t a formation they’d come up with on the fly. It was something they’d drilled relentlessly, all legionaries falling into place like cogs of a well-oiled machine. The back compressed orderly to stand the pressure of the less disciplined monsters and climbers behind them. The front was reinforced and given the space to absorb the pressure of the advancing enemy army. The sight was almost beautiful.

The Legion experienced heavy pressure from both sides, as well as casualties, but they held.

The horrors didn’t. They must have expected to blow through the gymnasium army, or maybe they had no plan. Only moments after their first wave was halted by the legion, the second wave crashed into them, and then the third, and then the fourth. Rows upon rows of tentacle monsters charged into their allies, creating a self-destructing, brawling mass.

Gary saw horrors fall over horrors, stomped by other monsters. He saw entire groups being squeezed between their own allies. Climbers launched all-out attacks against their own army just to stay alive. Many jumped off the bridge. The tentacle army collapsed almost immediately after first contact. Their first line, which had originally stopped the legion, was stomped from behind. They were killing each other—and facing them was a proper army.

“Advance!” Boulderus Johnus roared, breaking away from Alpha, who was trying to stage an orderly retreat. The legion formed shield walls with spears sticking out and marched. There was no rush in their movements. They were slow but relentless. Step by step, they crossed the bridge, slaughtering any tentacle horror or enemy climber caught in front of them.

For the first time, Gary realized they were supposed to kill other climbers simply because they’d picked opposite sides. This wasn’t even war. It was murder, for what seemed like a game. How did the clans agree to this?

Nausea came over him, but there was little time to think. The advancing legion conquered the entire length of the bridge, pushing the horrors onto dead grass. Finally not squeezed from all sides, the tentacle army realized they’d taken heavy casualties. They ran.

“Spears, witches, and boxers, pursue!” Boulderus Johnus ordered.

Gary snapped to attention. He stood on their side of the riverside, with the actual river being not far below the bridge. The enemies who’d jumped in it were recovering. Soon, they’d escape.

“Beefs, with me!” Gary roared, jumping into the river. Many other climbers followed, but of all the monsters, only hired mercenaries had remained behind. All others had participated in the bridge advance. The waters were white and thick, hiding visibility below the surface. Gary could only perceive as far as his aura reached, which was seven and a half feet.

He also discovered that the river deepened quickly. Only a few steps from the shore, he could no longer perceive the bottom, and he had to swim—or use his godly strength to slap the water and shoot himself forward like a torpedo. He did just that. Other spear-wielders followed suit, the beef boys swam freestyle, and witches flew overhead.

Some of the water got in Gary’s mouth. It tasted like vanilla.

The enemies had recovered by now, and they were trying to retreat. Most were climbers, but there were also many monsters who’d been pushed off the bridge by their fellows. Gary hesitated. Murdering real people just because they were on opposite sides of a silly war event didn’t sit right with him. He angled himself towards the monsters and directed his beef boys to do the same. He’d long lost sight of Asim.

They fell on the enemies. Battle broke out mid-swim. Weapons clashed, energies erupted, and aura flew everywhere in all colors of the rainbow. Gary’s ears were overwhelmed by shouting, screaming, explosions, and metallic clashes. A familiar monster emerged in front of him—a bushman, one of the third floor’s weakest monsters which specialized in ambushes. The tentacles must have made it stronger, because it was Level 24.

“Thunder Thrust!” Gary yelled, smashing straight through the monster to arrive in a mass of its peers. “Omnidirectional Nail Gun!” His spear reaped lives. In a corner of his vision, he saw his merit points increasing. He was already at sixty.

A Core Formation urchus set its sights on him. Gary noticed early, raising his spear to block the spiked bear’s paw. The impact sent him flying. A Core Formation legionary fell from above, landing on the bear with his gladius extended and starting a fight that upset the water around them.

“Are you okay?” someone asked Gary.

“I’m fine,” he replied, surprised himself. He’d tanked a Core Formation attack. Sure, he’d been sent flying, but he wasn’t even injured. He shot back into the fray. Suddenly, however, his fully extended aura picked up on something. He thundrestepped aside, barely dodging a sword that broke the surface where he used to swim. A cultivator surfaced after it, clad in purple robes and gazing coldly.

“You tried to kill me,” Gary said.

“Blame your luck for becoming my enemy,” the swordsman said, charging.

Gary frowned. This was a real person, fighting him just because they were on opposite sides. Yet, he’d really tried to kill Gary. If he hadn’t kept his aura extended, or if he hadn’t reacted quickly enough, that sword attack would have skewered him.

A dark flame lit up in his heart. They’re fucking with me again. They’re so needlessly cruel. Fuck this. Fuck them. You want to kill me?

“Black Thrust!” he yelled, meeting the swordsman head-on. Black lightning traveled across the water and air, radiating all around him. The sword was pushed aside. Gary’s spear pierced clean through the other man’s chest, and all he saw were a set of disbelieving eyes amid falling hair. Gary dislodged his spear, letting the dying man sink to the bottom of the river.

They really want to kill me. Idiots. Fine. Let’s all die for nothing.

His merit points ticked up again. The beef boys were making short work of a climber, and Gary didn’t stop them. He scanned the water for his next victim. A lone zombie was swimming to shore, which he shattered with a thrust. Thunder Step after Thunder Step, strike after strike, he was easily tearing through the enemy lines. Every attack slew a monster. Compared to opponents like Feng Shen or Sui Duang, these enemies were almost casual.

Merit Points: 230

He was deep into enemy lines now. Gary’s gaze flickered from side to side, briefly cataloguing the enemies around him. Not many came from the clans, but some did. They were swordsman, evil priest-looking guys, as well as…

“Oh no,” Gary muttered.

Suddenly, he realized why Chloe never messaged them before. Her clan had chosen the other side. She was an enemy.

Holy shit. At least she’s not here. There are so few people of her clan, what are the chances? Gary thought. A scream from the side arrested his attention, and he saw her. Chloe. She was flinging what looked like balls of ink at two spear-wielders who tried to box her in.

“Stop!” Gary roared, blasting an aura thrust in front of one of his clansmen. Both of them paused, recoiling in confusion, which gave Chloe the opening to retreat. She flashed Gary a grateful look, but not a surprised one—she must have spotted him first.

“What the fuck are you doing?” the spearman Gary had stopped yelled at him.

“Sorry, she’s a friend! I—” Gary started saying. Mid-sentence, something came from below again, and this time, it was faster than he could react. A tentacle wrapped around his legs and pulled. Gary was lost in the waters.

By the time he realized what was happening, he was already deep. He couldn’t perceive the surface. He couldn’t perceive the bottom, either. He tried opening his eyes but all he saw was white. He was surrounded by thick, creamy, vanilla-flavored protein water.

That, and the tentacle still wrapped around his leg, pulling him down. A torrent of bubbles escaped his lips. He moved his spear to thrust but found his movements hindered, weighed down by the river’s density. The current pushed him, too, but the tentacle reeled him in.

All thoughts of Chloe left his mind. He wished her the best.

A circular maw entered his aura field. He finally chopped the tentacle and escaped, but not before perceiving the full form of his enemy.

It was an octopus sticking to the river floor. Many more than eight tentacles rose from its flat body, in the center of which lay the multi-toothed, circular maw which had almost devoured Gary. Some tentacles disappeared upward, stretching much longer than it physically made sense. Even more tentacles held onto half-eaten corpses and struggling soon-to-be-corpses. The octopus only ate their heads, then saved the rest for later.

The swordsman he’d killed before was one of these corpses.

Gary didn’t know when or how this monster had snuck underwater, but based on the number of bodies, it had been snatching climbers since the start of the battle.

Lieutenant Tentaclopus, Level 34 (Field Boss)

Tentacle monsters are great in the water. Who could have guessed?

Everyone except the few idiots who chased them into the river, that’s who. That includes you. Please try to escape. I didn’t pour all this attention into you just to see you turn into the opposite of sushi. Ihsus?

Gary could hold his breath for a long time now, but bubbles still escaped his mouth as he saw the monster’s level. 34. The previous Core Formation monster, whose attack he’d barely blocked, had been level 27, and it hadn’t been a field boss.

He immediately slapped the water, thunderstepping away in quick succession, but the octopus wasn’t about to let its meal escape. Tentacles shot after him like torpedoes. They were blinding fast. Moreover, Gary was fully underwater, meaning that the electric outburst of Thunder Step affected him, too.

A tentacle wrapped around his waist. Another slapped at his spear, which he barely managed to hold onto. Nail Gun! he thought, shooting a rain of thrusts into the tentacle holding him. It ripped. Another grasped at his legs almost immediately, pulling them together at the knees. Even as Gary unleashed endless strikes, tearing the tentacles apart, new ones grabbed him. He was slowly pulled towards the mouth. The monster wasn’t even fighting him seriously, just grabbing him casually through the water among many other climbers. No matter how many tentacles he severed, more emerged from the monster’s flat body. Suckers stuck hard to his skin.

Gary had no illusions of allied Core Formation climbers diving this deep to fight a high-leveled field boss. They wouldn’t come even if they knew. Everyone was fighting for themselves first.

The only person who could save him was himself.
