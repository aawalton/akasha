There is no greater joy in life than staring at something complicated and knowing— instinctively—that you are going to bully it into understanding.

The runes carved into the machines flicker faintly as I approach, as if sensing they are moments away from being deciphered, categorized, deconstructed, and possibly mocked.

My ears tilt forward.

My tail curls in anticipation.

Poor things. They don’t stand a chance.

“Let’s begin.”

The first machine I inspect still has active runes—glowing faintly, humming with energy—and they look exactly the way any sane person would expect ancient mystical script to look.

Utterly ridiculous.

Random angular lines. Squiggly nonsense characters. A few symbols so aggressively overcomplicated that drawing even one looks like a five-minute endurance trial for someone with steady hands and a degree in calligraphy.

Beautiful. Nonsensical. Insulting.

Nothing immediately meaningful I can extract from just one set, though, so I move on.

The next machine’s runes are dead, no glow, no hum, just sad little carvings with the energy of a failed prototype.

Useful.

Because the moment I compare the inactive set to the active one, several pieces click into place in my mind with the smooth inevitability of a genius doing exactly what she was built for.

A simple circle, there in both sets. First in both.

An X, last in both.

Then shared runes: a vertical equals sign, a needlessly dramatic Y, and a lightning-bolt-looking symbol that radiates “I do something important” energy.

I keep going. Hopping from machine to machine. Pattern recognition kicking into overdrive.

Circle: always first.

X: always last.

Lightning bolt: consistently right before the X

Beginning. End. Trigger.

Then the Y, always splitting the inscription’s lines. Dividing logic paths. A branching rune.

And the vertical equals sign always precedes it, an ordering rune, a sequencing constraint.

In under a minute, the entire structure of the runic syntax starts forming in my head. A framework. Rules. Dependencies.

A language.

Flow. Enhance. Alter. Gate. Revolve. Merge.

The meanings fall into place one after another, but so do the distances. The gaps between runes aren’t decorative, they’re precise. Always ratios of 1:1, 1:2, or 1:3.

Not random.

A timer.

A delay.

Execution pacing.

Oh, that’s cute.

It’s… coding.

Not metaphorically. Not spiritually. Literally coding. A runic scripting language carved into metal, each symbol a function, each sequence a program, each spacing a timing parameter that dictates how energy is processed and released.

A runic compiler powered by mana flow.

My ears perk.

Which means…

I can write my own.

Starlight gathers in my palm, eager, obedient, molding instantly into new shapes. Tiny runes of solid light form in my hand—clean, sharp, perfectly structured.

Start. Flow. Equalizer. Branch. Enhance. Merge. Gate. Trigger. End.

I crouch and place them onto the ground in a perfect line, using my enhanced perception to adjust each spacing with sub-centimeter precision. Ratios pristine. Energy paths aligned.

Then—

With the gentlest breath of intention, I send a thread of heat-aspected energy into the structure.

The starlight inscription activates instantly, the energy thread running through my runes in a smooth, flawless cascade. It flows through Start, Flow, Equalizer, Branch, Enhance with no resistance at all, until it reaches the Gate rune and pauses, waiting.

Good. Expected. Designed that way.

I nudge the Trigger with the tiniest spark of intent.

The Gate opens with elegant precision, and a flame blossoms at the end of the sequence, cleaner, sharper, and undeniably stronger than any basic heat-aspect output I’ve produced before.

A notification appears at the edge of my vision.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

You have gained the general skill [Lesser Rune Crafting (Common)]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

I dismiss it without a second thought. The system will still be there in ten seconds. The miracle in front of me might not.

My eyes narrow at the fading inscription.

The flame dissipates after a few seconds, but my mind is already racing through calculations, implications, possibilities, because what I just witnessed should not be possible.

I used almost no energy.

Just a thread. Barely a sip of stamina.

Yet the resulting flame was ten, no, closer to fifteen or twenty percent stronger than a flame produced with that amount of power could ever be.

Which leaves exactly two options.

Either:

One, the runes stole energy from some ambient source I haven’t detected yet—

Or

Two, they created energy out of absolutely nothing.

My tail swishes slowly.

Because either answer means the same thing.

The runes here are not just a power system.

They are a violation.

And I am going to learn exactly how they did it.

So I continue testing.

Same sequence. Same spacing. Same ratios.

Light first.

The inscription flares, producing a flashbang bright enough to blind an entire rave. Stronger than intended—significantly—yet completely uncontrolled.

Noted.

Next, gravity.

The runes activate but stall halfway through the sequence. No effect.

Of course. Gravity needs a target variable. A solid metal floor is distinctly resistant to localized gravity manipulation.

Annoying, but not surprising. I’ll fix it later.

Wanting something more stable this time, I conjure starlight into my palm and begin shaping a knife, clean, balanced, and structurally sound. As the blade forms, I weave the runic sequence directly into its surface, the symbols arranging themselves with elegant precision.

I lift the completed weapon, ready to activate it—

—and freeze.

My brain finally catches up with what my hands just casually accomplished.

I stare at the dagger.

Good form.

Acceptable proportions.

Runes etched along the blade like they were placed there on purpose instead of aggressively negotiated into existence.

Still not perfect, but noticeably better.

I glance down at the floor.

Several complex runic constructs.

All clean. All stable. All made without me wanting to throw myself into a lake halfway through.

…

Was I seriously overthinking starlight manipulation this entire time?

This morning it took me seven attempts to make a comb.

A comb.

And now I’m out here casually engraving advanced runic sequences into a weapon like I didn’t spend thirty minutes losing a fight to basic hygiene earlier.

My tail flicks, sharp and annoyed.

Right.

Of course.

The moment I stop trying so hard, it just… works.

Naturally.

Apparently the secret to starlight constructs is: don’t micromanage it into failure.

Just stop thinking and let the genius cook.

Honestly?

Deeply offensive.

Also valid.

So I will continue doing exactly that.

I channel heat energy through the inscription.

Iridescent starfire blooms across the blade, unmistakably stronger than anything I can normally produce… for a noticeably lower stamina cost.

Efficiency improvements. Excellent.

I tap the Trigger rune to shut it down, sever the flow, switch to gravity, and re-activate the sequence.

This time every rune lights up in order, and the dagger instantly becomes significantly heavier in my hand.

I grin—brief and sharp—before cutting the effect and dismissing it into drifting motes of starlight.

Now we can begin the real work.

Stamina: manageable.

Runic understanding: acceptable.

Starlight manipulation: suddenly acting like it has always loved me.

Perfect.

Let’s make a proper weapon, shall we?

Starlight flares to life, threads of cosmic light weaving runic patterns across my mind like constellations arranging themselves into equations.

I add a secondary enhancement loop to the core inscription, doubling the number of Equalizer, Branch, Enhance, and Merge runes.

More flow paths. More amplification. More output.

Then I invert the entire sequence, flipping the structure like a mirrored circuit. The blade begins forming in my hand immediately, runic lattices shimmering along both sides as the starlight takes shape.

Long, sharp, optimized for cutting over brute strength.

A simple hilt follows—clean lines, no unnecessary flair—and with the final stroke of intent, the weapon completes itself.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

I nod, then turn my attention to the result.

My sword is long—roughly six feet—taller than I am. Its entire body shimmers with the shifting colors of newly forged starmetal, casting cosmic reflections along the factory walls.

If you look closely—past the celestial lightshow—two runic inscriptions sit just above the hilt, etched in luminous precision.

I am choosing to ignore the fact that the balance is completely off and the blade is still slightly wonky.

I will fix it later.

Inspect reveals:

I open my status and check my new skill:

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[Rune Smith (Uncommon)]

Unlocked by showing basic mastery over creating runic weapons.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

My eyes narrow.

System assistance.

The system casually suggesting I require assistance with anything at all is a direct assault on my pride.

A genius needs no help.

None.

But… if it wants to make itself useful while I ignore it entirely?

Fine.

I suppose I can allow that.

Next test.

I turn my attention to the runes etched into my boots and ring. My first-ever enchanted items, handcrafted by the tutorial with all the care of a disinterested IKEA employee.

I look closely.

I study.

I analyze with the gravitas of a scholar unraveling ancient secrets.

Patterns soar through my mind like flocks of white doves taking flight in a dramatic cinematic montage.

Connections spark in my neurons with the intensity of fans storming a K-Pop concert barricade.

Theories blossom, multiply, spiral into elegant fractal logic.

Everything threads together.

A perfect mental constellation.

I nod sagely, radiating the kind of timeless wisdom normally reserved for mountain hermits and suspiciously knowledgeable crows.

…No clue.

Absolutely nothing.

The runic patterns are so wildly different from the factory ones that they may as well be a different language. Or a different species. Or perhaps someone’s avant-garde attempt at performance art.

No overlap. No recurrence. No starting points.

And definitely no safe way to copy them that doesn’t involve a catastrophic explosion, a minor explosion, or the mild inconvenience of disintegrating my own feet.

I sigh.

“Wonderful. The Tutorial gave me mystery runes with no manual, and no warranty.”

My tail twitches.

The boots remain smugly inscrutable. The ring refuses to elaborate.

I stare harder, as if my genius alone can shame them into cooperation.

One ear flattens in betrayal.

“…Yep. Still nothing.”

With nothing to cross-reference and no reasonable way to experiment without risking detonation, I accept the tragic truth.

These runes will continue to be mysterious—

—until I choose violence.

Preferably later.

For now?

I’ll put them on the “solve with excessive intellect or mild arson” list.

It’s getting long.

So, I move on to something I can solve far faster.

Eliminating an entire ecosystem of mole-people.

I reach for my new sword with righteous scientific enthusiasm…

…and identify a problem exactly one and a half seconds later.

A new personal best for noticing the consequences of my own brilliance.

My weapon is, in fact, bigger than me.

Not “slightly oversized.”

Not “a touch unwieldy.”

We’re talking a full foot taller than me.

Which leads to two immediate realizations:

One: it absolutely will not fit in my spatial storage unless I commit a felony against geometry.

Two: there is no reasonable method to attach a six-foot blade to my body without turning myself into a walking hazard sign.

I stop.

I contemplate the nature of existence.

I wonder—briefly—if perhaps I got a little too excited about “long” and “dramatic” during the crafting process.

Then I sigh, accept my choices, and begin creating its sheath, while dropping my now useless starter sword in the process.

Starlight gathers and solidifies into a clean, simple structure—no frills, no runes, just a functional protective casing. I add one feature: a single loop near the upper third of the scabbard.

A handle.

Because if I can’t store it…

And I can’t strap it to myself…

Then I will simply carry the entire sword around by hand, like an aggressively magical briefcase.

I finish it, slide the blade in, and test the loop.

It fits neatly into my palm.

I nod, satisfied.

Completely impractical.

Entirely unreasonable.

Seven out of ten.

And the worst part?

Creating it drained almost all of my remaining stamina.

I tilt my head back and look toward the ceiling.

No sky. No stars. No bonus regeneration.

Just oppressive metal and poor life choices.

My tail flicks once—sharp and annoyed—before I lower myself onto the cold, unforgiving factory floor with all the elegance of a fox-themed ragdoll.

Fine.

I will wait.

There's nothing more I can learn from the factory runes, and I definitely can’t fight anything without risking death-by-poor-resource-management.

So I settle in.

Arms crossed.

Expression placid.

Soul buzzing with the ancient Foxkin art of sitting still while vibrating internally.

This is going to be a very long wait.

A very long, very stamina-less, very deeply uneventful wait.

The universe sighs with me.
