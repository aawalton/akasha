The meeting ran a little longer, and by the time Luke took the item off his temple, it felt like a gremlin had burrowed into his head and was in the process of stabbing his brain with an ice pick. Throbbing headache aside, things were starting to look up. Perhaps it was the fact that his expectations going into the meeting had been so low, but he thought they had all been pretty damn reasonable. They had even agreed to invade the Singers. Hoping their talk of skill and power wasn't all bluster, Luke even thought the pact stood a good chance of doing some real damage to their invaders as they closed the gates. Promises were also made about the two Lifeweavers. Not that they would be able to learn everything Luke could teach them, and he had serious doubts about that, but that they would be delivered to The Band without Delay.

Coming out into the common room of guild headquarters, he saw Milla talking with a small group at the plaque leading out. He blinked. It was them. How the hell did they arrive so fast?

"Ah, there he is," Milla said, waving for him to join them.

Walking up to the group consisting of Stine and Riak, along with two others, an older man matching Riak's complexion, and a younger woman standing next to Stine. "Hey," he said. "That was quick."

"I'm quick," the new girl said.

"Well..." The old man said, trailing off. Everyone waited for him to follow it up, but he didn't.

Instead, Milla took charge. "We have a room set up for you all. It's dungeonized by our Architect. Figured that might help."

The old man drew in a deep breath, as if to speak, but then didn't.

Stine gave him a quick glare, then turned to Milla and smiled, speaking in clipped but precise words. "Yes. Please. We want to start now and learn."

The four guests headed over to a new plaque, touched it, and disappeared. Once they were gone, Luke turned to his sister. "What's the point of turning the room into a dungeon... thing?"

"Dungeonize it, you mean?"

"I refuse to call it that. Too stupid," Luke grunted. "But yeah."

"Lana thought it might help. Won't hurt to know their levels either, right?"

Luke shrugged. "Guess not."

She gave him a long look. "So, what do you think?"

"About the meeting?"

"Well, yeah."

Luke considered the question for a moment, but his gut reaction from just when he left hadn't changed. "It went well, right?"

"You're not worried?"

Elijah exited a private room and walked past, nodding to them as he fingered the hilt of his new light sword. The Radiant Templar looked as if he'd recovered from the wound and was eager to get back out there. After exchanging a nod, Luke turned back to Milla. "Worried about what?"

"They all claim to be strong, but we shouldn't be taking the Singers lightly. What if the invasions all fail?"

"That would suck."

She raised an eyebrow. "That's your take on the matter? It would suck?"

"What do you expect of me?" Luke said, walking over to the plaque. "We have to accept there are some things we can't change. If things go south, they'll abort. There is no alternative to pushing those fuckers out. If the next step comes and we're still subjugated, we'll fall behind."

"Behind what?" Milla asked.

"Everyone," Luke said. "I don't know how to even begin fighting what I've seen out there, but we do know one thing: without more power, there's no standing up to the void. Now, if you'll excuse me."

Touching the plaque, Luke found himself transported to a much larger room than he had expected. Almost the size of a football field, the place had the empty feel of a PE hall, complete with fluorescent lights in the ceiling. The group of four stood around in the middle of the room, looking a little lost as to what to do. Since Lana had worked her magic on the place, Luke could gather at least a couple of small tidbits of information about them.

Integrated Stine. Lifeweaver. Level 31.

Integrated Riak. Lifeweaver. Level 24.

Integrated Dembe. Cartographer. Level 18.

Integrated Ella. Rusher. Level 36.

Interesting to see the one responsible for Stine's transport at the highest level. Thirty-six was nothing to sneeze at. The old man had been left in the dust, and Riak wasn't much better.

"Hey," Luke said as he approached. "So, where are you guys from?"

"Earth," Dembe said, straightening his shoulders and raising his hand as if to add to it. Again, he didn't. Such a weirdo.

Riak gave his older countryman a tired shake of his head and added in his stead, "Uganda."

"Norway," Stine said, pointing to herself and Ella. The two looked like they could be mother and daughter, with Ella sharing many of the middle-aged woman's features, along with her blond hair, but where Stine was short and squarish, the younger Norwegian was slim, almost to the point of looking emaciated.

"You two related?" Luke asked.

Ella was the one to answer this time. Her voice was deep and melodic, not at all what he would've expected from such a frail-looking gal. "No. She was my boss at UOH. My boss's boss, actually."

"At what?"

Stine stepped forward. "Oslo University Hospital. Now, are we going to learn today or not? I'm taking time away from patients for this."

"Right this way, doctor," Luke said, gesturing to a couple of thin mats on the floor. Better to sit there than on what looked like hardwood.

"Nurse," Stine said.

Ella winked at him as she backed off to give them room. "Nurse's assistant."

"Nurse. Sorry," Luke said as he sat down and crossed his legs. He gestured for them to join him. "Go on."

The old man and Ella walked over to a nearby bench, but the younger woman sat down on the floor instead, getting into the same position as Luke.

Once Stine and Riak were seated, Luke found himself unsure how to begin. "So, you're Lifeweavers."

"We are," Riak said, nodding as he showed a warm, toothy smile.

"Have you seen the weave?"

Their blank looks told him they had not.

"Well. Do you have..." He had to stop himself to consider what skill they needed to have for this, but realized that there wasn't any required except Threads of Mana and maybe Weaver's Grasp, the skill allowing them to move threads outside of their bodies and into the physical world. Clearing his throat, he changed direction. "Have you been near death at any point since becoming Integrated?"

Both of them shook their heads.

"What about mana? Ever been so low you start seeing double?"

"I have!" Riak shouted.

Stine gave a short nod. "I have as well."

"And," Luke began, looking from one to the other, "did you see anything strange then? Like threads in the sky? Everywhere, really?"

"I was too busy healing to look at the clouds," Stine grunted. The woman was getting grumpier by the second.

Riak looked crestfallen. "No. I am so sorry."

Not a great start, but he wasn't ready to give up just yet.

"Let's try again. I'll extend threads to you and siphon your mana until you're at ten percent or so. Close your eyes and try to stay calm and relaxed, okay?"

Riak looked doubtful and gestured to the space between them. "All the way from there?"

"Yes," Luke said, extending threads from his knees to theirs.

Stine blinked and nodded as she closed her eyes. "Far."

While his threads reached far longer than they had way back when he first acquired Weaver's Grasp, he was still far from satisfied. Reach was often a limiting factor in both healing and fighting situations.

"I have learned to request mana from others in my healing, but can you take so much?" Riak asked, a slight expression of discomfort on his face.

Luke knew the look. Having another Lifeweaver's threads inside your mana channels was not a pleasant experience. Unfortunately, in his situation, Luke couldn't afford to be gentle. Closing his eyes, he began draining them of mana. "I'll be fine."

While both of them had focused their attributes on mental attributes, judging by the width of their mana channels and the strength of their meta-hearts, Luke had seen larger mana pools plenty of times. Despite the massive need for the precious resource, Lifeweavers didn't start out with a whole lot of mana for some reason, not compared to mage-type Integrated or even other support classes. It was a little unfair and had made for a brutal start, but he was well past that stage now. Converting their mana to healing mana and storing it in his own meta-heart was enough to deal with about half of what he needed to take from them, so he let the rest bleed away into the air.

"This is uncomfortable," Stine said.

Riak shuddered. "I agree with Stine's sentiment."

"Bear with it a little longer," Luke said. "Just a little longer. Relax and enter meditation."

When they were down to ten percent, he opened his eyes and spoke in a soft, relaxed tone, trying to emulate the yoga instructor he'd been to at one point in an attempt to deal with the stress from school. "Now tell me, do you see it?"

Even at full mana, the weave was clear to Luke's senses, permeating everything, everyone, and the connections between them.

Riak opened his eyes. He'd turned pale with so little mana in his system, and he looked like he was about to vomit. "The room is spinning. Is that what I am supposed to see?"

"There are threads. Look beyond the physical realm. The weave is metaphysical. It is part of the system. It IS the system. Shift your perception and search for it."

"There is nothing," Stine said.

"You need to try a little harder," Luke replied, failing to keep the calm tone of yogi Matthew, who smelled of hemp and beard oil. He wondered if that guy had become some sort of relaxation Integrated.

"I am so sorry, Luke Quinn," Riak said after a full five minutes of silence. "Would you please let my mana regenerate? It feels like I am being strangled."

"There is nothing," Stine repeated. There was no hint of frustration in her voice. She was just stating a fact.

Luke withdrew his threads, masking his disappointment. The results of this little exercise were just what he had expected going into it, since very few Lifeweavers in the pink dungeon had been able to see the weave. Come to think of it, only the ratkin, Splinter, had been able to. Luke was beginning to suspect it wasn't a Lifeweaver thing at all, but rather one connected to specific people.

Behind Luke, Ella cleared her throat before saying. "I think I see it."
