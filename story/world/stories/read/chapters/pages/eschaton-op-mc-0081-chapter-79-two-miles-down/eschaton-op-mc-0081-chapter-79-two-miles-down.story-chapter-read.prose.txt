Danny peers into the blackness of the deep ocean, any and all light having long since been swallowed by it. The only source of light is Machina and the fire that endlessly burns inside his helmet, though Danny’s heat vision still allows him to see—if there was anything to see, that is.

With a boot on the hook and a hand on the cable, Machina sinks into the gloom with all of them. Normally the Guard could reach the bottom much faster than sinking if they wanted to, but it’s best to conserve energy. All three of them are holding onto the cable, floating just above Machina’s head.

A singular chirp pulses from somewhere—too loud to truly pinpoint. It’s eerily clear, unlike anything heard underwater. A second, fainter one is heard from above, likely having bounced off the hull of the George Cobb. Danny recognizes it, having been a professional diver for many years.

Sonar!

The sonar ping rings up from below, having bounced off the seabed and back. Despite having traveled such a distance, it’s still clearly audible.

He clambers down the cable and frantically gets Machina’s attention.

Don’t do that! Danny signs. Be quiet. Monsters listening.

Sorry, Machina signs back.

Several minutes later, a Claymore Fish, a Cat-1 fodder enemy, swims up and out of the gloom. It notices them, and telegraphs its signature dash attack with a tail wriggle.

A flash of Aura from Machina, overwhelming and immensely powerful, causes the fish to freeze in place for a brief second. Danny shivers for a second, and he isn’t even the target. A mountain is angry. He is but an ant.

Don’t do that! You’re just gonna draw in more! Danny frantically signs again.

It quickly turns away and dashes back into the gloom.

Monsters are afraid of you!? Danny signs at Machina.

Sometimes. Aura is a funny thing. Never tried to scare one on purpose before. Glad it worked.

Why didn’t you just kill it?

Quieter. Summoning my sword is loud.

Several more minutes go by and nothing appears from the darkness.

It’s oddly quiet, Danny signs to Tiffany. Too quiet. We should be fighting our way down.

Why would they be hiding? Some of the Moby Whales could eat the George Cobb for breakfast with room for seconds.

Tiffany points at Machina before signing a reply.

Maybe they’re not used to being lower on the food chain.

After sinking for thirty minutes, the crane finally bumps into the seabed. It sends a cloud of muck and marine snow into the water. The only light available is the faint glow from Machina, and though his light pushes the dark back, it’s ultimately unnecessary. Being 2-Star Empowered means their senses are already superhuman, and theirs are even better than most.

The Cerulean Guard can see the seabed, though only in shades of gray. There’s only a small bubble of color around Machina, though it’s truly impossible to tell the difference all that much. Down here, everything is shades of gray anyway.

They wouldn’t need to be able to see to find the nearest Fracture in any case. The Fractures scream and warble in whatever magical senses Empowered have. Machina silently points in the direction of the closest one, and they move off with him in the lead, the slowest among the party.

Each of his lumbering footsteps squelches into the seabed, and he sinks up to his ankles in the muck. His cloak helps, and more blue light emits from the grasping, plasma-like tendrils that help him not sink so deeply into the mud. Despite the artifact’s immense power, it’s far from perfect. It’s still slow going, but luckily they don’t have to go far. The Fracture looms before them, and it makes Danny’s head hurt with the way it spirals in on itself endlessly.

I go first, Machina signs. He steps in, and the rest of them follow quickly after. The feeling of falling gets worse with higher category Fractures, and Cat-3s are the highest—well, the highest any of them would ever willingly enter. Danny clenches his teeth and breathes deeply, trying to hold onto his breakfast.

Looking around, he automatically starts noting the environment, trying to predict what kinds of monsters they might see. The Fracture is studded with massive pillars of stone spewing evil black clouds of boiling water and chemicals. Hydrothermal vents the size of skyscrapers heat the entire Fracture from a real-world near-freezing 36 degrees to a blistering 90. The smell of sulfur and iron fills the water.

At the base of the hydrothermal vents are glowing cracks, from which bubbles pour out from. Danny can see a faint orange glow from deep inside them.

Tiffany gags, the thick and noxious mixture of chemicals in the water too much for her. The sound of her retching is muffled by the ocean and overpowered by the nearly all-consuming roar produced by the dozen skyscraper sized hydrothermal vents.

Like next to a jet engine! John signs, not even bothering to attempt to shout over the noise.

Eyes up! Tiffany signs to them both after recovering.

Danny nervously grips his harpoon. Simply holding onto it isn’t enough to actually activate the curse, but even so, it still feels like licking a battery. A constant, faint, tingling fizzle nips at his fingers, even through the heavy gloves.

Red worms, probably the size of buses, drift lazily poked half out of white shells in the sides of the hydrothermal vents. Something even larger scythes through the water, though too far away to really make out other than its immense size.

Eying Machina, Danny notices he stands a little straighter. Maybe the water pressure is less? Or maybe because the footing is better here. Instead of a mucky, silty seabed the bottom of this false sea is made of a dark, craggy rock. In either case, he deploys several of the scientific instruments. He pulls them out of his personal super dimensional backpack—Danny’s not really sure what it is.

Both Danny and John start helping him set them up while Tiffany keeps a look out. She’s easily the most observant of the three of them. Her Hydromancer powers let her sense the shifting of the water nearby, meaning she doesn’t even need to see something to know it’s there. Handy, that. Saved their lives more once.

The core driller scrapes and screeches against the rocky bottom, even sending small sparks flying. All of them except Machina visibly wince at the sound. Eventually the infused drill bit, made from steel recovered from one Fracture or another, bites into the stone. The screech turns into a grinding grumble, and rock dust goes flying.

A few curious deep sea creatures—not monsters, strangely enough, just regular animals, though infused—bolt as soon as the stone seabed starts shaking. John spears one with his trident, and it goes in a floatie after being wrapped up in a bag. Machina snaps out blindingly fast to grab a fish—too fast, in fact.

The pressure wave caused by him forces the water behind his hand to cavitate. A huge steam bubble, formed by flash boiling water through an extreme drop in pressure is brutally popped by the crushing weight of the ocean. The resulting pop is loud enough to slap Danny in the chest. It also pulps the unfortunate fishie, which explodes into a cloud of gore.

Danny stares at Machina and his casual demonstration of power. Machina shakes off his now stained gore-gauntlet.

Thought they’d be a bit tougher than that, he signs. Spear a few more for me, would you? These ones are too fast for me to catch without killing them.

John nods, and continues spear fishing. The Fracture is densely packed with animal life, far more than the real-world ocean. A brush of flowing water guides Danny’s vision off the side, and he snaps out with his harpoon on instinct. Whatever Tiffany is pointing him at must be worth the pain.

A huge octopus, easily the size of a speed boat slowly crawls towards them. Its skin is the color of the basalt seabed. As Danny grips the harpoon with two hands and the intent to cause harm, it activates. It coats itself in blood red energy, with most of it concentrated at the tip.

The unfortunately familiar body aches and lethargy returns. A sense of doom and dread washes over him, thought far less intense than normal. Just the presence of Machina banishes the thoughts of inevitable failure.

No courage without fear, it tells him.

John’s familiar healing magic soaks through his armor and wetsuit, pushing back against the aches caused by the hideously lethal harpoon. It feels like a warm sunny day, a nap in the park, and a cool pillow on a hot night all at once.

Perks of a specialized powerset. We might be worthless on land, but in the sea? Just the three of us can go toe-to-toe with a Cat-3 boss. If we’re willing to take the punishment.

“Cover your ears,” Machina says. Danny instead just braces himself for whatever the robot paladin is about to do. He can’t afford to loosen his grip on his weapon. Pain is a familiar friend anyway.

A huge sword, one that looks like a moving hole in space rather than a physical object forms in his hand. It’s more than twice Danny’s height, though he’s fairly short for an Empowered. Something constantly brought up by his teammates as a well worn joke.

When it forms, it sends a shuddering shockwave that hammers into Danny and even pushes him forwards even closer to the monster. Tiffany diverts the pressure wave with an instinctive flare of magic. John is thrown up into the water by the shockwave, but he quickly rights himself and returns to the group.

Machina, either unaware of them being thrown off balance—or more likely, trusting them to take care of themselves—marches on the octopus. He’s on the wrong side of the group to get there in time though, meaning it’s all up to Danny.

Time to make the big bucks. The familiar refrain is comforting, but he has little time for much else when the octopus makes its attack. A blindingly fast lunge is powered by a burst of noxious, sulfurous ink. He barely raises his harpoon in time. The octopus squeals when the cursed weapon rips into its flesh.

Hot magma-like blood pours out from grotesque wounds, boiling the sea water. The octopus writhes, trying to pull itself off the barbed harpoon. Long tentacles batter at him. He can feel the heavy impacts even through the tough armor he wears.

He leaps up, harpoon still embedded in it, and lunges forward. The octopus squeals again, louder this time as the harpoon drives in even deeper. He slams the octopus into the seabed, pinning it to the rocky ground.

I hate this part.

Danny activates the harpoon, and it takes even more from him. He feels sick, sweaty, and weak. Like the worst of a bad flu, and a migraine on top. Despite the agony, it’s well worth the sacrifice.

Murderous crimson energy tears through the octopus, and it shudders once, then twice. With a third barely perceptible twitch, it dies. Ink and blood seep out of sores and lesions torn open by the vile magic of the cursed harpoon.

Are you alright? Machina signs, blade still in hand. Danny leans heavily on his harpoon before the soothing magic from John whisks away the effects of the cursed artifact.

Yes, Danny replies.

I was too slow. I am sorry.

You didn’t bring us along to baby sit us.

I know. I trust you. Just know that I will be slower to respond that we’d all like. My speed is limited by cavitation.

That’s why you’re paying us the big bucks.

With Machina’s help, they cut up the octopus into more manageable pieces and stuff it into a floatie crate. John hucks it and a second one filled with samples and live fish through the Fracture’s entrance.

The floaties will activate a little bit after passing through the shimmer.

The shimmer? Machina asks

The entrance or exit formations. Once on the surface it’ll turn on a beacon for easy recovery.

Smart. Let’s continue on.

Machina points in a direction.

Let’s go that way. I wonder what’s over there?
