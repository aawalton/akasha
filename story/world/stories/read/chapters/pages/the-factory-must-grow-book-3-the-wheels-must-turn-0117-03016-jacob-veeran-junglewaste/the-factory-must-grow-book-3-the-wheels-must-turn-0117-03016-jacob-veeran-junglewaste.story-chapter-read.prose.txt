The [Frostblade] struck with the sound of a mighty gong, splitting both the skull and the Song of the dragon he fought. He grabbed one of its horns, then flicked his wrist. The blade wrenched its way out of the dragon's neck, half-decapitating the corpse, and most of the body slumped to the ground.

The remainder was the half of the beast's skull and neck which he was still holding.

Another dragon pounced, and Veeran simply allowed it to land upon the point of his blade, releasing his grip upon the weapon and guiding the monster to fall off to the side with a flourish of his cloak.

[Frostblade], he commanded. ⟨Rimeforge⟩. [Empowered Interdiction].

The tone of his personal Song shifted alongside his weapon. The flesh and scales liquefied, dripping to the ground below and coalescing into a lance, taller than he was. The interlude had taken long enough that yet another one of the beasts swooped down on him from above.

It was, to one capable of listening to the battlefield, trivial to avoid, and Veeran sidestepped it, then hammered it into the ground with his fist and crushed its skull with a stomp. Then, he brandished his lance and charged towards the primary threat of this wave.

The ground beneath him dented and deformed, the sandy soil insufficient to support Veeran's strength, but he managed to avoid falling. It did cost him a fair amount of momentum, meaning the resultant blow was far from lethal. Nonetheless, it interrupted the dragon's approach, and Veeran attempted to call upon the deeper magic of the lance in the way Ride had advised him.

His understanding of the skill, from both his own assessment and the assessment of those who knew magic far better than he, was that the focus he utilized ought to provide something beyond mere form. He had, one time, managed to utilize a placement bracket as a focus, and been capable of calling the resultant shield to his hand with scarce more difficulty than utilizing any skill.

Alas, he had yet to replicate the feat. The horn of a freshly-slain dragon ought to have been bursting with power, yet Veeran could not access it. Only time would tell whether it was merely a lack of practice or perhaps some more fundamental component to the magic which he lacked, and thus Veeran would endeavor to provide himself both.

Nonetheless, he presently had a fairly large dragon closing in on him, and a lance that was proving too unwieldy for his current situation. Therefore, he dropped the lance - it collapsed into swiftly-melting snow as it struck the sands beneath him - set his hand out to the side, brought it forwards, and released a particular burst of Force directly at the face of the dragon.

It did nothing, of course. He was unable to muster anything stronger than a light breeze without the assistance of a Skill. Yet that was not his aim, either.

Thunk.

A ballista bolt tore through its shoulder, annihilating muscle and skin. Yet that only seemed to embolden it as it roared in defiance, sweeping its head from side to side.

Veeran frowned. Very well, if that was not lethal...

He severed his cloak's clasp and dashed to the side, allowing the false fabric to flutter to the ground.

A trio of bolts flew through where he had been mere moments prior. One missed the dragon entirely. The second hit a glancing blow along its scales, carving a deep furrow into the creature's flank, and the third blasted a smaller dragon into a cloud of blood and hide, but was deflected as it did so.

The latest ballistae, after their latest upgrades courtesy of the Metalpress, were metal contraptions as long as Veeran was tall, and with bronze arms as long as Veeran's own. They used iron chains for both their string and to pull them back, and launched projectiles easily three feet in length, wooden stakes with pressure-fit iron arrowheads that entirely obliterated smaller monsters.

And much like their original iteration, tracked and fired at Force. Veeran was a few hundred feet from the walls of First Tower, within the Junglewaste corridor nearly all of their draconic assailants came from, and beyond the normal firing-range of the artillery.

However, he was still within their lethal range, and as such had learned a cantrip of sorts, releasing Force mana in sufficient quantity and form that the ballista would shoot where he indicated. He had an inkling cloak - perhaps it was a cape, as it only reached his waist - which obviously made shrouding his movements from the ballista trivial, and therefore dropping it in conjunction with his artillery support cantrip prompted multiple ballistae to fire.

He kept the cantrip running, and more bolts poured in. Though they were nominally aimed at him, they were easy to avoid, to the point where the only realistic way he could ever be hit by one being if he was entirely immobilized immediately after calling in a strike. Naturally, if a dragon succeeded in entirely immobilizing him... he had larger problems than a yard-long metal-tipped stick.

The fourth and fifth bolts were not much more effective, the former kicking up a spray of sand as it crashed into the ground and the latter striking where the dragon had been half a second prior.

However, the sixth one connected, and the deadly construct of iron and wood cleaved its skull in half, bits of bone and brain sent flying as they were displaced, and the beast dropped.

Veeran cut off the artillery support call, retrieved his cloak, and wrenched a scale from the newly-slain dragon for [Frostblade]. It formed into a greatsword with a blade longer than he was tall, and Veeran smiled.

The dragons, obviously, were less pleased.

Haleford swept his hands over Veeran's torso, [Unblemish]ing those minor abrasions and cuts which he had not noticed in the heat of battle, as well as getting some of the more stubborn dragon-blood stains off. The wave of attacks which had drawn him outwards had subsided, and the sands were quickly reclaiming all the bodies he had created.

Doing so was not ideal, as the wastes fed upon all within it, steadily strengthening the dragons which it produced, yet the bodies, so rife with Wild magic, were ill-suited for any purpose in The Factory. Inq had noted that even the inklings she had created from the bodies of their assailants proved even weaker than their normal counterparts than was typical, which Veeran attributed to a lack of support from their maker. Or, if their understanding of the local magic was accurate, a lack of support from their environment.

Inq agreed with him, yet a class more supportive of her summons was not particularly reachable at this time, and once it was, alchemy would be a superior focus.

The defense of their home, of their Factory, fell to Veeran.

"My thanks, Haleford," Veeran bid the boy once he was finished. "You do marvelous work."

"It is truly my pleasure," Haleford responded, without a trace of duplicity.

Veeran gave a bit of a chuckle as he left the First Hearth, with vague musings half-formed on how different the boy was than most nobles, both present and past. Veeran would not have described himself as a particularly devoted student of history, yet his time in The Binding Wars had introduced him to many individuals who had seen themselves as 'noble' in character or in blood. Few were quite so eager to serve their nominal role as the young Haleford, yet the title would be far better for it if they were.

The heat from their new Volcanic Crucible could be felt from quite a distance, and the Song it sang, rich in Metal and Fire, was simultaneously rich and full to a portion of Veeran's class, yet screeching and nigh-painful to another. The underlying beat of Force being tapped out by the conveyor belt leading into its side provided a nice degree of depth to its overall melody, all melding into a quite industrial pattern even if none of the individual components quite sounded mechanical.

That was not his destination, however. Instead, he passed by it to the walls themselves. Though still just an enchanted palisade atop an earthen embankment, here, where their enemies were redirected to try and assault, a dozen metal ballistae were mounted, each on their own swivels, and kept loaded by a trio of pseudowyverns sourcing ammunition from a box filled with the appropriate quarrels... and it was running a bit low. He would need to restock those.

The weapons themselves were imposing, and Veeran climbed up to the wall, balancing along the narrow ledge as he inspected the artillery, cleaning out bits of debris that had worked its way into the mechanisms. Though they were powered by clockwork hearts and therefore not as vulnerable to mere dust interfering with the metal-to-metal contact the original motion slides had required, all manner of detritus, even leaves, somehow worked their way into the mechanisms with startling speed.

Some additional rust had likewise crept its way into the various parts, he noted. Haleford would need to pay them another visit in the near future to cleanse that away. Though Smith's wards ablated the eternal march of Nature, ablation was not invincibility, and with no oil to separate the air from the metal, reddish discolorations had already begun to make themselves known along the weapon's stock. Its arms, made of bronze as they were, did not carry that same weakness, but they did tarnish nonetheless.

One of the pseudowyverns assigned to the station flapped up to the palisade next to Veeran and looked at him quizzically. He returned a measure of acknowledgement, and then paid it no more mind as he checked over each of the weapon's enchantments and functionality, utilizing his artillery strike cantrip to verify their Force-tracking enchantments were working, as well as that their trigger conditions continued to be a sufficiently large amount of the same. How much momentum was required to get a ballista to fire had been adjusted upwards not too long ago, yet even the older artillery pieces still worked admirably, if slightly trigger-happy and prone to shooting the Commander's inklings as they defended their base.

Veeran amended the accuracy of their weapons, fixed the malfunctions he could, and noted the ones which would require attention from either Haleford or Smith to repair, then packed away his tools and went to locate Smith, to request a Metalpress order of new ballista-bolt heads.

The Artificer was, naturally, working upon his automata. It hummed with such a delightful, rhythmic beat, a vestige of so much grand artifice Veeran had scarce heard since the Expedition had begun, and Smith was directing it to walk in a circle with an outstretched hand, commanding it to move with the cadence he set with his hands and magic. Its pointed legs barely sank into the hard-packed earth it walked over, and Smith had an extremely contented smile upon his face.

"Jacob," Smith greeted with a wave once he noticed Veeran's presence. "How are the ballistae looking?"

"Adequate. There are some things which require your attention, and we need to refill the ammunition before long," Smith winced slightly at that, before brightening.

"Right! Metalpress. That's going to be great."

"Indeed. Though it shall still require your assistance."

"Right, right. Um," Smith flicked his wrist, and his new staff emerged out of his arm, extending into his hand with a bright blue glow and the trill of chimes, then Smith took and planted one end on the ground, twisting it so that it stood upright. His drone picked itself up from where it had been resting and flew until it was above and offset from the staff. From there, it projected a hologram around the staff of a massive arrowhead. Smith, the crystal embedded in his palm fully alight, spun it around, made some adjustments, shrunk it down to a more normal size, and then extended the hologram such that it contained dozens of the design.

The entire process scarcely took a couple of minutes, and Smith nodded to himself then used two fingers to direct his familiar up into the air, presumably to the Metalpress such that it could start the pressing process.

"I love technology," Smith muttered to himself. "And... yeah, I'll get on the ballista fixes soon. I think I've got the walk-cycle for the bots working, which means I'm almost ready to start trying to diagnose the stupid pathfinding magic."

"What challenges does it present?" Veeran asked. Smith's artifice was endlessly fascinating, and the chances of Veeran being the one to maintain it at some point being as high as they were, it was but common sense to learn as much as possible at any opportunity.

"Well..." Smith hesitated. "Eh. I can just show you."

He turned back to the spider-bot, which had come to a halt while he had been working on the arrowheads, and spread his fingers to cast. Then he paused, grabbed his staff from the ground, and Veeran watched and listened as the metal sank into Smith's forearm. The magimorphic lines covering the Archmage's body connected more directly to the lines upon the staff, then those lines became shear points as the staff rendered itself first into pieces, then into melodic Technology magic as it embedded itself in Smith's flesh. At the end of it, his forearm contained a distinct outline of his staff rendered within his lines, including embedded-looking portions of metal corresponding to the staff's steel and bronze sections.

Smith shook his arm a couple of times and rubbed where the staff had ⟨Integrate⟩d with his arm.

"That appears to be moderately uncomfortable," Veeran noted.

"It mostly just feels weird," Smith replied. "The first time, when I took a nap with the Pillar in my hand and woke up with it in my arm, was an accident, but it really helps with initiating bot controls. I can even project holograms from my Light Crystal while I've got the staff stored, and having my fingers fully free is great, but it makes it harder to do normal staff things with it, because... it's not a staff when it's in my arm. But yeah, it feels like a combination of dunking arm in mercury and my arm falling asleep whenever I pull it in. Um, I was... right."

Smith turned back to his bot, extended his fingers once more, and started marching the automata around again. "So I've gotten the actual walking down, but I'm controlling this with ⟨Use Artifact⟩ right now. I'm telling the crystal to walk in a certain direction, and it's able to do so."

Slightly uncannily at that, with a stride that was simultaneously too smooth and too choppy.

"So I know it's not a problem with the actual walk mechanism or the PCEs or anything like that. But, if I go one step further, and test the pathfinding crystal... Okay, so watch the glowing light. That's its destination marker. It's supposed to move until it's touching the light with any part of it."

Smith briefly cast a spell, drawing light from the Light Crystal within his hand outwards, made it spiral around his hand, and then sent it out to directly in front of the construct. "Swapping over... now."

An instant after Smith said 'now,' the spider-bot froze. The light, mere inches in front of it, wiggled back and forth slightly. Then the bot jumped, all of its legs pushing it straight up and into the air. The legs stiffly stayed at full extension even after landing, and as a result it toppled over to the side with a crash, prompting a flinch from Smith, then a sigh. "So... yeah. That's where I'm at. I've got ideas, at least. And I'm still riding the high of getting the normal walk cycle working. Turns out the broadcast glyphs can interfere with each other... but only if they're not actually quite right. I had the middle-left leg, one of the glyphs there was slightly off, and as a result that was somehow... contaminating the local Network or something? I dunno. I had to replace it to get everything else working, even though there is no way the receiving glyphs can actually impose a change..." he sighed. "Oh, and that wasn't even the worst of it. So..."

Veeran nodded. Smith needed to speak, and he would give him the space required for that.

It was immensely interesting, regardless of how he understood less than half of it.
