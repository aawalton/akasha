Snapping my eyes away from Elarion, I stand up from my mostly-finished meal, looking back at the messenger.

“Tell the captain I’m coming,” I order. The messenger drops a quick bow and then turns and runs off again. “No rest for the wicked,” I sigh, then realise belatedly that I should have asked which captain it is. Instead of calling the messenger back, I look at Layton.

“Do you know who is on the wall right now?”

He considers the question for a moment.

“I believe it is Captain Ilas from Josiah’s contingent right now.”

That news doesn’t fill me with relief. Although I don’t know the man personally, I’ve heard enough of Layton’s reports to know that he’s competent, but hardly creative. A good second-in-command, but not a good leader in times of a crisis.

“If it is a Tier four, we’ll need more than just a single unit,” Elarion points out tensely, dissatisfaction creating faint creases around his mouth. I don’t doubt that he’ll fight if necessary; I also don’t doubt that he has no desire to. Join the club.

“Agreed. I’ll reach out to Maxen.”

So saying, I touch the Bond between me and the captain of my personal forces.

Maxen, are you and your unit in a state to fight?

Yes, sir, he reports after a moment, sounding a little groggy. I suddenly wonder if I woke him up. We are in the barracks but can be ready within a quarter mark.

I hiss in a breath. Hopefully we’ll have that much time.

Do it – and come to the wall, I order him. We have a potential Tier four incursion.

There’s a brief pause.

Another one, sir? Maxen asks, his tone faintly exasperated.

Another one, I confirm with a sigh. Spread the word in the barracks while you’re at it, please – we may need as many on the wall as can fight.

Yes, sir, Maxen acknowledges and I pull away from the connection to speak with my allies.

Elarion has already disappeared while I was speaking with Maxen and Layton has stepped away and is speaking into a mirror, something preventing me from hearing his conversation.

“Elarion went to warn the healers and mages,” Valence tells me, observing the way I look around to see where the other heir has gone. “He’ll meet us at the wall.”

“Good plan,” I judge just as Layton finishes his conversation and steps forward to rejoin us. I update the two on what I’ve instructed Maxen to do, and they nod.

“I’ve woken Josiah up and alerted him to the new crisis,” Layton tells me briskly. “He’ll join us as soon as he can.”

“Good. Do we have any more defensive scrolls or artifacts left?” I ask tensely.

Layton and Valence exchange glances.

“A handful, perhaps. Not powerful ones,” Valence replies. Layton nods in agreement.

“That few,” I sigh. They exchange a quick look again.

“The journey was hard on our disposables, as was the recent fight with the King and all of its controlled beasts,” Valence tells me apologetically.

“We also had to use most of our containment scrolls on the feral humans before Lady Keshaka was able to create the restraints as an alternative,” Layton adds.

My jaw clenches, but I force myself to relax it. Breaking a tooth won’t help in any way.

“If that’s the case, we’ll just have to manage,” I sigh. “We’ll deploy what we can of the remaining scrolls and artifacts if the beast appears hostile, and then…well. It’ll be down to us.”

Both incline their heads, expressions grim. They know as well as I what that means when involving Tier fours.

With nothing else to discuss, and time short, we make our way to the wall as quickly as possible.

We each have our own way of quick travelling. Valence has a Skill that seems at first glance to be a short-range teleport, but actually just enables him to move faster in short bursts than most people can keep track of. Layton, meanwhile, is able to speed the movements of his body somehow, turning him into a blur that is slightly slower than Valence’s top speed, but lasts longer. It seems to roughly equalise out.

I, of course, pull on my air wings, the rank up to Transformation meaning they appear in a second and send me soaring over the city with ease.

Bastet, wake up, I urge, feeling horrible at disturbing her after she went back to sleep – she’s still not entirely recovered from our fight two days ago and, of everyone, she certainly deserves some rest. Bastet!

Finally, I feel her rouse.

What is it? she asks me grumpily.

A possible Tier four is approaching the settlement and I need you with me on the wall. She might be a Tier below, but she’s the Beast Lord of the territory, and might be essential if we can turn this into a negotiation rather than a battle. While I’m confident that we could win against any Tier four by this point, two days after the battle of attrition against the parasitic controller is hardly the best time.

Another Immortal? Bastet practically yowls after a moment, sounding far more awake. I’m coming.

See you there. Maybe bring that beau of yours if he’s willing to fight.

He’s not my beau, Bastet corrects me forcefully. I will find out, she concedes a moment later.

The Tier three larnatis that took a fancy to Bastet decided to stick around. He wasn’t keen on the Bond, but I made it clear that it was not negotiable if he wanted to come inside the settlement. In the end, he decided to accept the Bond, though I’m not sure where he decided to sleep. Even if I’d been willing to let him into my bedroom for the night, Bastet wouldn’t have. She hasn’t accepted him as part of the pack yet, that’s for sure, though I sense it’s a bit more complicated than just that.

I could reach out to him myself, but I figure he’s more likely to agree if it comes from Bastet. Having two Tier threes on our side is definitely better than just one.

My conversation with Bastet has roused several of my other companions, and alerted those who were already awake. Trouble, Blaze, and Spot are none-too-pleased to be put back on young samuran baby-sitting along with the three Pathwalkers, but there’s no way I want a repeat of the battle a few days ago where a group of hatchlings found their way onto the battlefield. And when I remind them of that, Trouble changes his tune.

In the end, Valence, Layton, and I arrive at the wall at practically the same time – there’s only a few seconds difference between us. Still, I can’t help feeling a little smug that I’m first – though only because I boosted my speed with air-mana.

Captain Ilas quickly descends to meet us.

“My lords,” he bows. “Thank you for being willing to come.”

“Of course,” I tell him impatiently. “Now, your messenger indicated this could be a Tier four. Have you seen it?”

“No, my lord,” he answers.

“Then how can you know it’s a Tier four?” Valence asks, also impatient.

“The wards indicate that whatever is approaching is powerful – more so than a Tier three. I assumed it was unlikely to be higher than Tier four,” the captain reports.

I exchange glances with Valence and Layton.

“We need more information,” Layton murmurs.

“I’ll organise some scouts,” Valence states with a firm nod, then looks at the captain. “Which direction is it approaching from?”

“The river, my lord.”

Valence nods and takes several steps away, pulling out a communication mirror and starting a conversation with one of his subordinates.

“Did we miss something in the tunnels?” Layton asks me quietly, looking as troubled as I feel.

I consider it for a moment, then shake my head, though without complete certainty.

“It seems unlikely. We cleared them out and then set fire to them. If there was a Tier four there, it was incredibly well-hidden – and well-protected. But I can’t deny that this might be linked.”

A weight lands on my shoulder even as her back feet strike me halfway down my back. I reach up to stroke Sirocco’s breast.

“You were quick,” I praise. She sends me a sense of nonchalance, as if covering the distance between the command centre and here even faster than I did is just to be expected. “Are you willing to go out and scout?”

Yes, she agrees, opening her wings again in preparation to take off.

“Be careful,” I warn her. “We think it’s a Tier four. Again.”

I will stay above, she promises, then takes off and wings her way over the wall. Barely half a minute later, a group of scouts comes running towards us. Valence hurries over to speak with them briefly, then the door is opened for them and they disappear through it.

I wonder which group will find the beast first – we set the initial alarm wards quite far out so we would be alerted with enough time to prepare.

“Is there any other information we don’t currently have, Captain?” I ask Ilas. He considers it for a moment.

“We are lacking in strength as most of our forces are still recovering from the battle,” he informs me, though it’s nothing I didn’t already know. “We will fight as hard as we can, but we can’t win against a Tier four when so few in number.”

“Reinforcements are on their way,” I assure him. “They’ll be another five to ten flames.” I’m grateful that we placed the main barracks significantly closer to the wall than the command centre – once everyone is dressed and armed, it will only take them five minutes or so to get here. “Do you have scrolls to enhance the defence of the wall?”

“A few, but they will not hold up for long,” Ilas warns. I nod. “ Otherwise, there is nothing else I can think of that is worth the telling,” the captain decides after a moment.

I nod, then turn to Layton and Valence who, since his scouts left the settlement, has returned to join our group.

“I’m going up onto the wall. Would you like to join me?”

The other two lords agree immediately, and we climb up. For now, there’s nothing to be seen, not even when I pull a telescope out of my Inventory and survey the area towards the river.

Then I narrow my eyes. Are those trees moving by themselves? Or is that evidence of the Tier four’s approach? In this forest, it’s hard to say.

As usual in times of crisis, it’s a case of hurry up and wait. Until we have more information about this Tier four, we can’t make more plans than we already have. I find my mind wandering, niggling at the question of exactly who this Tier four is and why they’re here. And why now? Couldn’t we have had at least a week to rest after the last one?

There might be far more Tier fours here than on Moriax, but they don’t seem to just wander around willy-nilly. From what we’ve learned of the hierarchy here, Tier fours are fairly static, ruling over territories populated by lower ranks. Certainly, the Beast Lord Bastet defeated to gain her position warned that the territory was ruled by a powerful Tier four.

In fact, thinking about it, assuming we didn’t make a mistake and this isn’t the parasite controller returning for revenge, this might be the territory overlord coming at a bad time. Though didn’t the previous Beast Lord say that the overlord lives in the mountains? It wouldn’t make much sense for it to be coming from the river, then.

Is another Tier four trying to annex this part of the territory? Out of everything, I hope not – being at ground zero of two Tier fours battling over territory is the last thing we need right now.

I’m distracted from my thoughts as Noir lands delicately on the wall next to me. Ivor, bigger and less agile, stumbles a little in his landing and knocks Valence hard enough that he has to grab onto the wall itself to prevent himself from falling off.

“Sorry for that,” I quickly say to Valence, giving him a once-over to ensure that he wasn’t injured.

“No harm done,” the heir says, a little stiffly. He doesn’t quite glare at Ivor, but it’s not far off. The white alcaoris ducks his head a little, embarrassed.

Do you want us to scout, Markus? Noir asks, ignoring the interplay.

“If you can do so without being noticed,” I agree.

Of course, Noir replies, sounding almost insulted. Come on, brother.

With that, he dives agilely over the edge of the wall. Ivor is a touch more clumsy, but once he’s in the air, he regains his grace.

Be careful, I urge both of them and they send back slightly exasperated acknowledgement.

As I watch them fly, my attention is drawn to the moving trees once more. I’m pretty sure they’re evidence of the Tier four’s approach – the movement is getting closer and the trees that were moving before are now still. It’s coming quickly.

Glancing backwards, I see that the reinforcements from the barracks are finally here. Most of my beast Bonded are assembled below too, Bastet at the front of the group, determinedly turning her head away from the male larnatis who keeps trying to get her attention. I notice that Lathani and Ninja are running interference – mostly for their own amusement, I think.

Then I turn back to the approaching Tier four and the slight smile slides off my face. I hope this doesn’t become a battle, I think to myself. Can’t the universe just give us a break for once?

I steel myself. I don’t want to fight…but if this Tier four is coming for one, we’ll make sure it regrets that choice before we’re done.

And then Sirocco sends back an image of the creature approaching. It’s fragmented by the canopy cover, but it’s enough to make my breath catch.
