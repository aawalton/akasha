President Drayson is sitting at his desk in the Oval Office when Director Holden storms in.

“Ah, Director, please, come in,” he says dryly.

“Mr. President, I have some important updates for you. The Ascended appear to be organizing.”

Drayson’s spin stiffens. Even one rogue Ascended would be a disaster. If enough of them decide that there should be a new world order… the thought doesn’t bear consideration. For what must be the thousandth time, Drayson curses Senator Redding and his ego.

Hauling in both Machina and Morgan to grill them over collateral damage during a war of annihilation was self-destructive and stupid. Though Machina has still been working closely with the National Guard, Morgan has stepped away from the US government. She’s chosen to start her own independent laboratory rather than work with government funded ones.

A single narcissistic Senator carved away support from at least one of the most powerful individuals on the planet. With the Ascended organizing, even if it’s a fairly small, informal group, that support is more valuable than ever. A single Ascended from the Empire scraped a city off the map. Not a single building—or any of its ten thousand residents—remained standing.

A single individual equaling a nuclear warhead in destructive power is a sobering thought.

“Do we know what they discussed?”

“Only in vague terms. They discussed what to do about the Empire. They’ve decided on a proxy war rather than direct head-to-head conflict. China will be putting up most of the funding, apparently.”

“I assume General Guan Yu had something to do with that?” Drayson asks, hoping for a negative answer.

“I’m afraid so, sir. My sources stated that he seemed to have relatively high influence through the meeting. The entire Council seemed to be his idea. He sent out the invitations, at the very least.”

Drayson stands up with a sigh and turns to the window. Outside of the Oval Office’s window the entrenched defenses are clearly visible. Multiple interlocking trench lines, emplaced machine guns, anti-tank guns, and even a few dug-in tanks encircle one of the nation’s most important buildings.

It might be overkill, and given the number of High-Starred Empowered in DC, ultimately unnecessary. But the image of strength is more important than the defenses themselves. If the White House is truly threatened, one of the few 4-Stars the US government has on retainer will respond in minutes at most. But images are important, and so the 3rd Infantry Regiment, known as The Old Guard, mans the defenses around the White House.

Of course, an Ascended could walk through those defenses like it was a Sunday stroll. Machina certainly could, and Morgan’s been inside the White House so she could simply portal past those very same defenses. Though she could certainly scour them off the map with just a thought and a flick of the wrist. The only magically infused ammunition that might even give a magical shield food for thought was distributed across the nation and to cities that don’t have a pair of 4-Stars on retainer at all times.

“What a strange world we live in these days,” Drayson grumbles.

“Sir?”

Drayson gestures to a chair, and both of them sit down. Holden folds her hands in her lap while Drayson leans on the Resolute Desk.

“These are individuals who will likely negotiate the fate of nations, Director. Even one could easily steamroll even our advanced and frankly bloated military. We cannot force them to do anything they truly do not wish to. The last time we tried to tell Machina what to do he simply walked through a 4-Star. And he is probably the most amenable of them all.”

“I have contingencies, sir.”

Drayson snorts. “And the fact you won’t tell me anything more than vague implications means you don’t have much faith in them.”

“I wouldn’t want to bring you something that has a high chance of failure, sir. Not unless we were absolutely out of alternatives. There are still ways of keeping Machina off our backs until we are ready to deal with him.”

“If we have to deal with him,” Drayson corrects. “Just because you don’t trust him doesn’t mean others don’t.”

“You trust him, sir?” Director Holden asks, impassively as ever. She never gives away what she truly thinks. A valuable trait in what is effectively a spymaster.

“I do,” Drayson says firmly. “I have to. Most of the American public damn near worships the man. He’s basically untouchable. An anti-Empowered news site tried to smear him with his recent trip to China and had to shut down the backlash was so severe.”

“I feel that allowing Machina to gain such cultural power was a mistake, sir. It means he has a tool to undermine us.”

“Or to help us,” Drayson counters. “But we’re getting off topic. This Council is concerning.”

“An organization that can decide the fate of nations without our input is unacceptable.”

“I wouldn’t go so far as to say ‘unacceptable’. I truly do believe that its existence is a good thing. Not a single person on the planet truly wants a conflict between Ascended. I’m glad to see that the Ascended, or at least a large portion of them, also believe that. We just need our own influence on discussions. Both India and China have military servicemen on the Council. I’m sure they’ll make sure their home nations’ desires are brought to the table. Or at the very least, considered.”

“Machina certainly will not consider America’s interests, at least not how we wish. But being closely aligned with the National Guard might give others the impression he speaks for us.”

“And that’s the crux of the problem,” Drayson agrees. “He has the social power to shape public perception of issues, and the personal power to evade accountability of all kinds. To publicly stand against him is political suicide. Redding is up for reelection this midterm cycle and it doesn’t take a polling genius to see just how badly he’s going to lose. Machina has the standing and the power to seem like he has the authority to negotiate without any of the training or understanding of our needs to properly do so.”

“So how do we reign him in, sir?”

Drayson leans back in his chair, the aging but well maintained wood creaking with the movement.

“Well, we could try and spin a web of words and contracts and all kinds of agreements around him,” he says to himself more than anyone else. “But that would last until someone reminds him that he could splatter just about anyone across the nearest mountain with a backhand. Or more likely just walk away. He’s not a violent man by nature, and I thank God for that every day.”

“He doesn’t even need us,” Drayson continues. “Not really, anyway. There isn’t an organization or government on this planet that wouldn’t bend over backwards to pull him away from us. I’m sure China would give him just about anything he wanted, and they’ve got an inroad with their good General. The real question is whether he knows that.”

“Sir, I have a suggestion.” Drayson motions her to continue. “I mentioned before in our first meeting about him that we should simply ask him to help us. I feel that if we are open and honest with him, he will return that. Because you are right. We can tie him up in as many words as we like, but we live in a world where we simply cannot back that up with firepower. We cannot afford to have our bluff called, sir.”

“We already look weak enough as it is,” Drayson growls. “I don’t think I’ll ever hate a man as much as I do Redding.”

“Machina walking out of a Senate hearing with no consequences was not a good look for us,” Holden agrees.

“Even if we ask Machina to consider our needs when it comes to bringing concerns to The Council’s table—I don’t like the idea that we may have to kneel to a foreign organization—I don’t believe he has the negotiating chops to actually uphold them,” Drayson sighs. “I’m sure he’ll try, but ‘trying’ doesn’t mean much when it comes to geopolitics. Would Morgan work for us?”

“Unlikely,” Holden instantly responds. “At least, not for free. We would have to pay her. She’s far more mercenary than Machina is, though that bar is south of Hell itself. She certainly would have the ability to negotiate on our behalf if convinced to do so. But she was burned very badly by Redding and her focus is on magic, not politics.”

Drayson clasps his hands on his desk. After several minutes of deep thought, and a guard change outside the window, he finally comes to a decision.

“Let’s wait and see. I truly do hate the idea of America being reduced to a peanut gallery at the whims of a handful individuals, but I think we have little choice at this stage. If there’s another Category-4 Fracture or worse we will need Morgan or Machina to clear it safely.”

“We do have three 5-Stars on standby, sir.”

“And they’re strategic bombers,” Drayson retorts. “Other nations get nervous when they disappear without warning, valid excuse or no. There’s a reason we keep them plastered over the news.”

“No one would be stupid enough to start a war now, sir.”

“Emperor Berhane and his Empire beg to differ. Not to mention if everyone was perfectly reasonable all the time all of us would be out of a job. You especially.”

Holden nods her head in acceptance, and gets up to leave.

“Director? Keep a close eye on The Council. It’s the most important geopolitical event of the decade. Maybe ever.”

“Of course, sir. I keep my eye on everything.”
