Don looked at the dark pool at the bottom of the cavern for a long while in silent contemplation. As he was right now, he had no idea how he could defeat this monster. It was on a whole other level of threat. And what the hell was that thing anyway?

Whatever the hell that tentacled eldritch monster was, Don was pretty sure it wasn’t just some mutated variant of an octopus. It was something so profoundly alien that it had to have pre-existed before Earth’s integration to the Multiverse.

What is the story here? What are these dungeons exactly?

Don looked around the platform he was standing on. There had clearly been some kind of a civilization living here. Now everything looked abandoned and quiet. Don would have gotten the hell out of dodge with a neighbour like Morshoff too.

Don kept glancing down into the dark pool in the middle of the cavern. The water was still, apart from a few stray bubbles. No black tentacles slithering, no unsettling white mask. Don didn’t like the way the pool made him feel. He felt weak, like prey. He resolved to conquer that fear and defeat this boss. He would not let Morshoff live rent free in his mind. He would not let anything make him look over his shoulder and make him feel anxious.

But Don was no fool. He knew the reality of this situation. If he fought this monstrosity, he would die. So he would need to power up and come resolve these feelings later.

So in its pure simplicity, Don needed to hunt and kill something. Right now the boss was absolutely not an option. He would have to see what level 10 in the Evolutor race gave him to even consider fighting that abomination. But preferably he’d get a few power ups before this showdown.

If I don’t find anything here, I’ll go back to grind on the lizards.

Don was actually surprised the lizards were limited to that single cavern. He supposed it was just the way dungeons were built by the System or whoever. At least this particular dungeon.

Don decided to look around and move on. He walked carefully on the creaking planks of the abandoned strange shanty town. He checked one room nearby and saw nothing but broken furniture. But clearly someone had lived in the room. There was a cot on the floor with dried up moss as cushioning. In the corner was a cracked wooden bowl that had been of fine make. Whoever had lived here had been pretty decent craftsmen.

Don moved on and took a rope ladder to an upper platform. Even though Simian Physique had slimmed him down, the rope ladder had clearly been meant for someone smaller than him, and he struggled a little bit to climb up the swaying contraption.

When he got on the other platform, he saw it was a large one. It was the size of a basketball court and at the end, built against the cavern wall was a large hall with long tables inside. Some kind of gathering place.

It was here when Don’s instincts gave him a warning. In the flickering light of the braziers where shadows moved constantly, it was hard to discern movement. But there was something hiding behind the wall of the large hall, ten yards away from Don.

As the buildings were stout, Don jumped on top of the hall and moved to the ridge of the roof. He could hear a scuttle as something shimmied down from the roof. There was an exchange of frantic gibberish and then silence.

There’s people here. Are these the ones who built the platforms? Why are they hiding?

Don moved to have a look down from the ledge of the roof. What he saw were three little green humanoids huddled up.

[Grimoid Hunter, Level 9]

They had knives and spears, but one of them looked at him with wild, yellow eyes and immediately produced a blowpipe. A dart shot and Don dodged. It whizzed towards the roof and vanished.

Aggressive, huh?

“Are you guys people? Can we talk?” Don suggested.

He wasn’t honestly in the mood for talk, but he didn’t want to wantonly murder a bunch of scared primitives either.

“Shlarabak?”

Was the only answer. Then there was shuffling and silence.

When Don carefully peered down, the creatures were already gone.

Since the creatures were identifiable in the exact same way as the beasts in the forest, and Don was in a dungeon he reckoned these swamp goblins were aggressive and part of the… dungeon experience.

Don needed answers and he was tired of being careful and second guessing everything. He wanted a fight. So he’d go and find one.

He jumped down to the middle of the platform and spread his hands.

“Come on then!” he shouted. “You guys aggressive or not? Come get a piece of meat.”

There was a moment of silence, before a mounting chant began. A high pitched “Yi-li-li-li-li!” from different directions and soon the platform started swarming with dozens of these little green swamp goblins with yellow wild eyes and primitive weapons.

They charged at him with spear club and knife, but some of the smaller ones stayed behind and started loading their blowpipes.

Don grinned. This was about to be a huge brawl and he was ready to throw down.

I’ll take out the ranged ones first.

Don flicked out his claws and sidestepped a charging spearman swamp goblin. Another swung a hatchet at him. Don blocked it with his claws and kicked the creature down. Then he ran past a few in tight, straight movements, slicing left and right with his claws. The creatures howled in anger and pain and tried to pile on Don. But his physique was overwhelming. He ran the monsters down like a linebacker and was barely slowed down. Some spears and knives struck him, cutting up his legs and arms. But he did way more damage. He cut his way through towards the first blowpiper who was just about to shoot, pointing the tube at his face.

Another group rushed in from the side. Five of them. Two had nets. One had some kind of curved blade made of bone. The others had hatchets. Don didn’t hesitate.

He moved through them like a buzzsaw. One Grimoid tossed a net, and Don ducked under it and lunged forward. He caught the goblin by the throat, lifted him up like a sack of flour and hurled him into the next attacker. They both crashed into the side of a hut, wood splintering.

Don took the hit—felt the blade bite into his side—and responded with a full-power punch to the chest. He felt ribs crack. The Grimoid crumpled and didn’t get back up.

The mob was breaking now. They had numbers, but Don wasn’t just stronger. He was faster. Smarter. He was hunting them.
