“You want to spare me from the worst of these consequences?” Ezra asked. “Can’t you just… make it not have consequences? Like, you punched a detective in broad daylight with no real provocation.”

Georg laughed, though it sounded forced and hollow as he stood up and nodded goodbye at Riyal. “No, I can’t. I wish I could, kid, but there’s gonna be consequences. I’m going to take as many of them onto myself as I can, but… well, no matter how much I can help, it’s gonna suck.”

“What kind of trouble am I going to be in? I know I’m a slumrat, but you did even worse, so what’s worse about what I did?” Ezra demanded, his brief hope of relaxation raining away.

“The problem is that you took an example from an idiot who, as much as he dislikes acknowledging it, is kind of above the law in this little pond,” Georg shrugged. “I never fucking learn, so I’m going to do what I can to help. But I can get away with what I do, especially to the detectives, because I’m the Count's younger brother.”

Ezra froze as they walked quickly out of the office, Amy behind them. Georg continued walking without looking back at the paused younger man, and after only a few steps, Ezra jogged to catch up. The Lieutenant didn’t look at Ezra, instead standing in the center of the square, where dozens of soldiers looked unabashedly at the spectacle in front of them.

“Petr! Troy too! You’re with me.”

Only a handful of seconds passed before Georg’s second-in-command and the other senior soldier appeared.

“Get your sideswords, wherever they are,” Georg ordered. Both snapped out salutes, and Georg turned his attention back to Ezra. “Long story short, I can punch most of the detectives around here in the throat because, even though I willingly left behind any of my inheritance rights, I’m still the Count’s younger brother. As such, I’m still politically connected, and they’re still just detectives, so I’m just a headache, but nothing worth dealing with when it’s petty things.”

“But this detective in particular,” Ezra said, his mind racing, “the one who was waiting for me. She’s bad news?”

“Again, to make a long story short, yes. Her name is Rebecca, and she’s the only daughter of another one of the noble houses in the town, the Wainwrights to be specific, the second most powerful family around. Her father has always wanted to steal some of the control of Villy from the Standage family, and wanted to use a pairing between Rebecca and myself to take some of that control. I never wanted any part of that, and I renounced any of my holdings and so on to join the army to steer clear of it twelve years ago.”

“She seemed to hate it when I mentioned you,” Ezra mentioned, trying to understand his current situation. “Should I think that she wanted to be part of this whole take over the city thing?”

“Yeah.” The single word dripped from Georg’s mouth with a venom that Ezra never would’ve imagined from the kind-natured lieutenant. “There’s a whole lot of politics to it that I’m a little fuzzy on and you don’t care about, but yes.”

“I never hit her, though,” Ezra said, his voice, hopeful.

“Honestly,” Georg said, blowing out a long, slow breath, “that might be part of what saves you. They’re still gonna act like you did, but having no physical injury on her will be helpful to make sure they can’t paint you as some madman. Oh, shit.”

“What?” Ezra demanded, his heart rate thundering up as Georg perked up and looked around.

“Amy,” Georg said, ignoring Ezra‘s question. When she looked at him, waiting for his command, Georg pointed into the city and said, “go to the chapel. She shouldn’t be asleep yet, but even if she is, find the Acolyte Mabel. If she's grumpy or whatever, just remind her that it’s me that’s reaching out and she’ll come along, even if she’s grumpy. It has to be her, not another one of the Acolytes or even the Deacon.”

“Yes, sir.”

Amy ran off out of the headquarters at that, and Georg looked around, seeing Troy and Petr run forward. For the first time, Ezra saw them not with a spear in hand, but instead with their swords hanging from their left hips and wearing different uniform coats. These ones were much the same, just crisper and newer-looking.

“All right, we’re going to the Count’s mansion,” Georg said, turning on his heel and leading the way out. They walked together, and Georg took the lead, with Petr flanking him on his right and Troy doing the same on his left. Ezra started to step out of the formation, but Georg snapped, “Stay behind me, between them.”

Ezra did as he was commanded, feeling more and more overwhelmed by the second. The warning, the rushing, and now stalking through the streets with an angry Georg leading the way, left Ezra so completely on the back foot that he just walked along, silent.

Stand up tall, soldier, Jormun’s voice echoed from behind him. You protected yourself, didn’t do shit wrong. If you look guilty, it’ll be easy to pile on you. You’ve got a good commanding officer here, and he’s stepping up for you. Show your confidence in him. You’re a kid who became an Adept in two months, knowing nothing about the path to Sainthood. You’ll be fine.

With a steadying nod to himself, Ezra squared his shoulders and followed Georg’s path forward. Apparently, they were going to the Count’s residence, and he had no idea of where to go. They only continued on their path for maybe five minutes before the sound of marching, steel-shod boots echoed down a nearby street.

“Stay together,” Georg said, his voice low.

No sooner than he spoke did a troop of guards stomped out of a nearby alley. At least ten men under the command of two detectives stepped forward, and when they saw Georg leading Ezra and the other two soldiers, they halted, though their ranks weren’t nearly as disciplined as Georg, Petr, and Troy. Ezra attempted to do the same as one of the detectives came forward, and when he saw the man’s face, Georg seemed to let out a sigh of relief.

“I see you’ve brought the criminal for us,” he said with a nod. “You can hand him over, Georg.”

“That’s not going to happen Brom,” Georg shook his head, calmer than Ezra had ever seen him interacting with any member of the guard. “I’m bringing Ezra to the Count’s mansion, where I can make sure to both plead his case and keep him from being thrown into some hole to be forgotten about.”

“The Count’s constabulary would never misplace a criminal, Georg,” Brom answered with a shake of his head.

“Are you attempting to say that the Count himself sent you here?” Georg demanded, his voice incredulous. “I can talk Charles and get an answer straight away, and though I can’t do anything to your position, falsely invoking the name and authority of a noble is a high crime.”

“I never said that he specifically sent us. However, we are the Count’s representatives and have come on a legal pretense to collect someone accused of evasion of legally permitted fines and tariffs, assault of a squad of the constabulary, use of aural attacks in so doing, and attempted murder of Rebecca Wainwright. Regardless of if he’s found guilty or not, the severity of these crimes levelled against him requires that he be taken into custody.”

“Which he is,” Georg said, standing tall. “He is a member of the army and distinguished himself in the latest battle against the goblinoid army, standing as a member of the elite squad on the front lines and never breaking. Due to him saving my life from an orc’s assault, he merits at least my own continued presence as this supposed crime and trial is sorted out.”

Brom stared into Georg’s eyes, and after they looked at each other for a few short seconds, the detective slumped and sighed.

“Really?”

“Yeah.”

“Shit. Is this…” Brom trailed off, asking a question that Georg seemed to understand.

“Yeah,” Georg nodded, and Brom’s jaw clenched a few times before he seemed to give up.

“Alright. We’ll come along behind you. Don’t let the kid try to run, because that’ll just make it all worse for all of us.”

“You won’t need to worry about that,” Georg said, looking at Ezra. He nodded, trying to remember the confidence that Jormun tried to give him. Then, Georg turned back to Brom and said, “I’m sorry you’re somehow still tangled in this.”

“I should have known, knowing where the commands and reports were coming through,” Brom said with a shrug. “But let’s see if your boy can dig out of this trouble.”

Georg nodded and said nothing more as he led the way onward. True to Brom’s words, the guards fell in step behind the soldiers, talking quietly among themselves. Ezra couldn’t understand the words, and wanted to ask Georg something, but decided against it. For now, it seemed like he could only trust that Georg would continue to be his best shield. And he could trust the man, despite how little time he’d had around the lieutenant.

They continued on their path forward through the city, bootsteps echoing off the cobblestones and walls of the surrounding buildings. As night hadn’t fallen yet, they made quite a procession through the streets, the last light of day overhead fading as people returned to their homes. Seeing the soldiers flanked by the guards marching through the streets, nobody stood in their path, and once, a wagon driver desperately tried to figure out a way to get out of the road an hoped to avoid any interaction with the trouble that walked towards him. Instead of causing any dispute, the soldiers just walked around the obstacle and continued on their way.

Only a few minutes after they encountered Brom, Georg was marching up to a wrought iron gate, where a pair of guards stood.

“Lord Georg,” one said, bowing his head. “To what do we owe the pleasure? I don’t believe that you’ve sent word of your approach.”

“No, I haven’t. I’m afraid I didn’t have the luxury of time, as you may be able to tell by Brom’s presence behind me,” The way that Georg talked changed as soon as the guards greeted him. Ezra fought to keep his expression the same as always as he listened to Georg’s change in demeanor.

“Of course, the door is always open to you,“ the guard said, “but in order for all of your… companions to enter the manor, it will require the Count’s approval.”

Georg waved off the words, saying, “I would expect nothing less. I’ll wait here with the rest, if it’s all the same to you.”

“Thank you, Lord Georg.”

They didn’t exchange any more pleasantries as one of the guards jogged towards the building. Ezra watched, impressed at the quality of their armor, and wondering if the quality of their ability to fight matched the equipment. Instead of saying anything, though, he continued to maintain silence as best as he could. Only about two minutes passed before the guard returned, his posture seeming, to Ezra‘s perception, relieved.

“Count Villy bids you all welcome. Please, come in.”

At that, Georg nodded his thanks and started to lead the group in, Brom, and two of his fellow guardsmen following along while the rest seemed to have orders to go somewhere else. They snapped a quick salute to Brom and then jogged away. They all walked forward, and as they did so, a question occurred to Ezra, one that he didn’t think would cause him any trouble.

“Why is it that your last name is Standage while the name of the town is Villy? Aren’t the last names of the rulers like… I don’t know, connected to the name of the town?”

“In some places with a stronger hereditary backing of inheritance for leadership,” Georg said as they strode through the doorway, “that’s the case. In many of the Counties here in Fidelius, though, leadership is at least partially dependent upon the continued goodwill of the Dukes as well as the Royals. The Standage family has been the family of the Counts here for the past six generations, but it isn’t guaranteed to stay that way. Hence, different names.”

Ezra nodded as his mind was stolen away by the entryway of massive, gorgeous building he stood in. A portrait hung on his left, of a beautiful woman with long, red hair. She wore a stately dress and stood with an imperious scowl. On the other side, a coat of arms that Ezra figured must have been that of the County hung. The floors were carpeted, and he hesitated to step on them with his dirty boots.

“Welcome home, my brother,” a booming voice greeted them, and Ezra looked down the hallway to see a man whose stature did not fit his voice. The Count of Villy was a thin man, a wispy goatee on his chin and thick curling, dark hair styled on his head. He wore a heavy cloak trimmed with a white fur that Ezra didn’t recognize, and he walked forward with a broad smile towards Georg. He didn’t wait for Georg to say anything before he wrapped his younger brother in a hug, and Ezra could barely see the resemblance between them. Most of their characteristics, however, were contrast.

“I’m glad to see you,” Georg said. “However, I wish I could say that I bring you good or even easy to deal with news.”

“You so rarely do,” the Count agreed. “However, what else are brothers for? Now tell me, what can I do for you? You know what payment I will require from you. And since you’re here, you’ll be included in that payment, Brom.”

“Aaron,” Georg gave a wry smile while Brom let out a long sigh and nodded his concession, “if you can sort this out easily, then I’ll be here three times a week for the next two months.”

Aaron, which was apparently the name of the Count, grinned widely, though he did seem to look a little troubled at that news. “That complicated?”

Before Georg could answer, a guard opened the door behind the soldiers and guardsmen to announce, “Lord Wainwright and his daughter demand to be seen and have justice delivered.”

Aaron narrowed his eyes as he looked at Georg, who took a long, deep breath, and nodded. At that small concession, Aaron’s demeanor changed, becoming far less familiar and instead almost regal. Ezra’s fight to control his opinions and bearing continued as the Count nodded and said, “It would appear my brother has more than a slightly complicated matter for me. Show them to the sitting room, where I’ll receive them.”
