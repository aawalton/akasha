"Aarrghh!" He groaned, stars flashing across his vision as rough hands seized his severed thigh and forced it back into place. Healing energy surged through him, dragging his fading consciousness violently to the surface.

Aodhán roared, heels slamming violently into a healer's chest. "Get off me! Get—"

"Hold him down," someone ordered sharply, and hands clamped onto him from every direction, digging into blistering raw flesh as foreign lightning rushed through his tissues to stitch him together by force.

But they didn't understand.

Lightning Regeneration was already working. The storm within him was already rebuilding what had been torn apart—and their interference was only disrupting it, turning his body into a battlefield of competing energies.

"He's not healing!" one of the healers exclaimed in alarm. "Why isn't he healing?"

Aodhán tried to speak, but the words came out slurred and broken.

Fortunately, someone understood.

"Let him be." Darien's voice cut cleanly through the chaos. "Get off him—now."

The pain subsided in waves, and gradually, Aodhán’s consciousness clawed its way fully to the surface.

“By the storm,” Seamus’s voice echoed. “What kind of regeneration is this?”

“Lightning regeneration,” a healer breathed, her voice thick with awe. "He's regrowing tissues—remarkable.”

"You're alright." Darien said it like a statement rather than a question, cradling his head in his lap. "You nearly lost your life to a Mythic. Do you remember?"

The memory came back in fragments.

An ambush of blood Mythics. Illusions layered over illusions. A blood scythe swinging faster than he could track. Then pain—brutal, blinding—and then nothing.

"I remember," he grunted, shaking his head to clear it. This was his closest brush with death since the war began.

And there had been plenty of those.

"No." Aodhán shook his head. "I'm not letting any of you go out there. It's too dangerous."

Seamus's jaw ticked with irritation. "We are not weaklings, Aodhán. We can take care of ourselves. We are stronger than you."

"You have a higher tier than me," Aodhán corrected him with a piercing glare. "You are not stronger than me. You are not going."

Tension thickened between them—the kind born from grief neither had properly processed, manifesting now as overprotectiveness on one side and anger on the other.

"Aodhán—" Elder Ghaltris began, attempting to mediate, but before he could, the voice of a panicked elder interrupted him.

"Elder Ghaltris, we… we have a problem."

The four figures stared straight into the screen, each radiating an aura of power so potent it distorted the very air around them. Then as one, they smiled—a deliberate, controlled stretching of the lips that promised anything but friendliness.

Aodhán felt a tingle of fear race down his spine.

There was no doubt as to their identity.

They were transmigrants. Enemy transmigrants. And from the weight of their presence, it was obvious they were anything but weak.

Feeling a twinge of dread in his guts, Aodhán turned to Elder Ghaltris. "Send me back. Now."

Elder Ghaltris didn't hesitate.

The war had just changed.

Sophran Thunderstride was a prime example of happy, undamaged, and strong. A rare breed among transmigrants and cultivators alike.

He hadn't been born into slavery like Nymera or into war like Ashscar. He hadn't been hunted for his identity like Aodhán, nor had he been isekai'd literally into a lightning storm with a calamitous slaughterer as his patron.

He was the happy kid—the anchor of his friend group—the one who genuinely loved and cared about everyone. He'd never considered himself particularly powerful, especially against the other transmigrants, but Sophran wasn't weak either.

He was simply… different.

Cultivating the path of the Living Storm, Sophran had never been given to devastation. His storm was not ruin. It was not a cataclysm.

It was renewal. It was endurance. It was life.

Or perhaps it had been life.

Mirelle's death and the chaos of war had changed him. They had forced him to develop teeth. And somewhere between grief and necessity, his path had begun to change into something more dangerous. Something more… offensive.

He was still on the path of Living Storm. But he understood now that life did not survive by gentleness alone.

Life endured. Life retaliated. Life refused to be trampled upon.

But more importantly, life survived.

And he intended to do the same.

Arms outstretched, he turned his gaze to a host of blood cultivators rushing toward him and activated one of his newest skills, {Verdant Thunder}, channeling a surge of chaos and willpower into it.

The storm above him rippled violently, but there was no clap of thunder as the skill's name suggested.

Instead, the clouds darkened, the winds picked up, and the storm exploded in a torrent of lightning bolts that descended around him like verdant rainfall, each one thrumming densely with vitality. The bolts soaked into the earth just as the Mythics reached him, but Sophran wasn't afraid.

They raised weapons, killing intent radiating off them in waves. They uttered rules, manipulating reality to trap him.

Sophran smiled. Then clenched his fist.

And the earth beneath their feet simply shattered as hundreds of lightning vines erupted from the earth, bursting out like Pandora's box broken open. In an instant, they spread, rushing across the scarred earth like an army of snakes—entangling, constricting, and piercing.

The skill wasn't powerful enough to kill Mythics.

But that wasn't where its danger lay.

Jagged lightning vines struck energy-hardened skin and pierced armored constructs, razor-sharp tips slicing through flesh and tissue like a knife through butter. Screams erupted from the Mythics as they tried to slice the vines apart, but lightning couldn't be cut. At least, not physically.

Sophran grinned as his vines had their first taste of Mythic blood, and without hesitation, he activated Unbound Sky, his body destabilizing into mist to seamlessly bypass the cage of hardened reality the Mythics had wrought.

He teleported immediately after, unleashing a huge wave of willpower to push himself far, far away from the vines. He'd barely exited chaotic space when he sensed the explosion—a mighty earthquake as the earth convulsed, spilling more vines up to the surface. These vines were larger, sharper, and hungrier, ripping through skin and armor to drain the life force of their victims.

Blood constructs erupted out of the network in violent bursts as the Mythics tried to tear their way out of the vines. But with every injury made, every drop of blood spilled, the more life force the vines drained. And the more they drained, the more powerful they became, slicing the blood Mythics apart with a hunger and brutality that even the worst of beasts would fail to replicate.

Dying screams echoed through the air as both rules and skills failed against the vines, each one now brimming with so much life force they lit up the battlefield in verdance, their hunger palpable even from a distance.

In barely a minute, the vines had ripped dozens of Mythics apart, draining them all of their vitality and strength. But they weren't satisfied. Sophran hadn't created them to be satisfied.

And like a plague, they began to spread, bypassing any with the aspects of storm in their veins.

With each death, they grew stronger, faster, and more abundant, self-propagating like nature itself until the entire area was covered in humongous, writhing vines of pure verdant electricity powerful enough to drain a Mythic dry in seconds.

Sophran watched the devastation with a humorless smile, wondering just how much death and destruction the vines would wreak before a Calamity eventually took notice.

He'd created life. And life, given freedom and hunger, was far more brutal than any weapon.

Unfortunately, Calamities had long since learned that lesson too.

The vines had barely spread over a mile, after killing over five hundred enemy Mythics, when an orb of bloody destruction speared down from the sky to the root of the skill, eviscerating it all in an instant.

Sophran felt the backlash like a stab to the chest, and he stumbled, his vision growing hazy for a moment before a burst of willpower steadied him. He looked up immediately, searching for the Calamity who had cut off his skill, and the moment he found them, he felt his blood suddenly freeze in his veins as his gaze locked with runic irises as bright as blood suns.

The Calamity snarled at him, and Sophran realized he'd been found out.

His body morphed into mist immediately as he attempted to run—to escape the utter fury in the Calamity's gaze.

But Calamities weren't Mythics. They were the pinnacle of absolute destruction. And there was no running from them.

Sophran had barely taken two steps before his entire torso burst apart in a shower of blood and viscera, spraying his entrails across the battlefield. Fortunately, for someone cultivating the path of Living Storm, he wasn't so easily killed.

He would, however, be if he were attacked one more time. Eyes wide, he turned his gaze to the blood Calamity, expecting to meet his end.

So it came to him as immense relief when he found the Calamity distracted and now occupied by a storm Calamity.

But he wasn't out of the woods yet. All around him the battle still raged, and with his entrails scattered across the earth, it was only a matter of time before the wrong people found him or a stray attack blew him into oblivion.

Groaning, he gathered as much willpower as he could within his spirit and channeled it all into Ancestral Continuance, crying out in pain as tendrils of vitality rushed out of him immediately, latching onto any intact organs and regrowing any that were too destroyed.

In seconds, his bloodline put him back together, as good as new, but the close brush with death left a bitter taste in his mouth.

He turned to glare at a concentration of blood Mythics fighting against Wind Mythics. He may not be able to take on the Calamity who had almost killed him, but he could most certainly take on these Mythics.

Snarling, he stretched a hand toward them, chaos and quintessence surging through his spirit as he activated {Pressure Point}, unleashing a pulse of crushing pressure directly above them.

Thunder cracked the sky in response, force slamming down on the Mythics in an explosion so deafening it carved a crater several meters deep into the earth, completely eviscerating the bodies of over a dozen Mythics in an instant and leaving the others too injured to fight back.

Their deaths eased the bitter taste in his mouth, and Sophran turned to check on the others, grimacing as he saw a judgment of storm cut down swaths of Mythics in a single breath.

It was no doubt Nymera's handiwork.

Far to the east, he found another judgment of lightning—this one weaker than Nymera's and bearing the unmistakable crimson-gold accents of Ashscar's conjurations.

Satisfied that they were doing alright, he turned to search for Aodhán and the unmistakable aura of ruin and authority that usually accompanied every one of his attacks.

That moment of distraction cost him.

A glass spear skewered through his thigh in a blink, and another would have taken his head off had one of Elder Vasmos's automatons not taken the attack to the throat to protect him.

Fortunately for him, a formation of storm Mythics found him first and immediately gathered around him in defense.

"Are you alright, Special Disciple Sophran?" the leader asked, shouting above the chaos of war.

"No," Sophran growled, feeling that bitter taste once more as the injury closed. Scowling, he pushed himself to his feet, a furious anger driving him forward.

"You need more power, Special Disciple Sophran." The leader's gaze was resolved despite the blood running freely down his face. "We can give you that power. Use us."

Sophran glanced at the man, knowing exactly what he was talking about, yet hesitating. He glanced at the man's bleeding form and the injuries of his comrades, then nodded, accepting the man's permission to use his most powerful skill on them.

Before he could do so, however, Sophran felt a sudden change in the atmosphere—not power, not presence—but emotion as the enemies roared with what seemed to be renewed hope.

The shift was unmistakable. The battlefield had just changed.

Sophran turned his gaze to the sky immediately, searching for the source of this renewed hope, and the moment he found it, he felt his stomach sink with dread.

Floating high in the sky, seemingly unbothered by the Calamities fighting above them, were four figures, radiating an aura of power so tangible the very air shuddered from it.

They were each dressed in the colors of their affinities, and when they smiled—an expression so chilling it seemed to freeze the entire battlefield—Sophran instinctively knew what they were.

Enemy transmigrants.

One of them didn't need to search long.

Sophran felt a coil of dread in his guts as a pair of crystalline irises landed on him—detached, analytical, cold.

For a moment the world seemed to stop—and then it fractured, luminous crystal-blue eyes flashing before him in an instant. He stumbled, eyes widening as pain slammed into him, raw and blinding as his torso was nearly split completely in two.
