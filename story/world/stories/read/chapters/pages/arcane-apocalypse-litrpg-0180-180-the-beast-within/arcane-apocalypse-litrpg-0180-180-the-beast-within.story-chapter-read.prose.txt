Mia stared at her Attribute sheet, a big, goofy grin painted across her face. It was the most beautiful thing she’d ever seen-

Her gaze flickered down to the unconscious redheaded vampiress lying at her side, her unfairly pretty features holding a tiny frown as she slept. Camie’s head rested on Mia’s thigh, using it as a pillow, because of course Mia wouldn’t let a prime opportunity to give her girlfriend a lap-pillow go unclaimed. Her leg was going a bit numb, but it was worth it.

Okay. It was the second most beautiful thing she’d ever seen. Still, Mia saw big numbers and her stupid monkey brain released all the endorphins and dopamine it could. She never knew just how immensely satisfying it would be to see crystal-clear proof of her rising strength, proof that all her effort and near-death experiences hadn’t been in vain.

The sub-one multipliers caused by one-digit Base Attributes were annoying to look at, sure, but she’d fix that sooner or later. She still had some of those Training Potions that increased their training efficiency, but Gabe had been focusing on other types of training so far. Plus, he claimed she’d thank him for not letting her waste those things on gaining Base Attributes while she was still in the single digits. He’d said it was all but impossible to get the 15th and above Base Attributes without the help of those elixirs.

I wonder how ridiculous my Spirit Sense is going to be once my Sensitivity reaches 20 Base Attributes and gets a flat two-times multiplier.

It was the only Sub-Attribute she had that was capped at 20, not at 15 or 10. Flexibility was one of the few capped at 15, and a part of Mia couldn’t help but wish it had been Manifestation, which was sadly already capped out at 10.

Alas, Flexibility was apparently important. Gabe claimed it was a sort of ‘counterweight’ to Strength and Agility. It stopped those focusing on Strength from developing the physique of bodybuilders, and the absolutely atrocious lack of range of motion that came with it. For someone like her, who leaned more toward Agility, it would protect her body from constant sores, strained joints, sprained ankles, aching tendons and the like. Without Flexibility to counterbalance it, either Agility or Strength could all but cripple a physical fighter.

Memory was another strange one. Reason dictated that if she kept raising it, she would develop perfect recall and photographic memory sooner or later. What would the point of Memory be after that point?

Gabe had said that it would start functioning more like a weaker Multitasking Skill. If Cognity was synonymous with the CPU in the computer that was Mia’s mind, then Memory would cover everything else, including HDDs, SSDs, GPU and RAM. Cognity let her think faster, but Memory would let her think more.

She could probably swap out her Multitasking Secondary Skill by the time she reached that point, if she’d failed to merge it into some sort of a mental Primary Skill by the time it became redundant. She hoped it wouldn’t come to that, though; there was great promise in making a mental Primary Skill from Multitasking, Parallel Thought and whatever other mental Secondary Skills she could unlock. However, she’d probably wait for that until she had her second or third free Primary Skill Slot at Ranks 2 or 3, maybe even 4. She had a good number of ideas from the hours upon hours she’d spent brainstorming with Gabe, and a mental skill was far from the first choice she had for her first Primary Skill Slot, or even the second she’d get at the next Rank Up.

That empty Primary Skill Slot was burning a hole into her, aching to be filled and put to use, but Mia held herself back. She'd seen during her Rank Up that while swapping or altering Primary Skills was possible, it was costly. The Tokens it would take to do so could have gone towards buying Traits that might one day save her life, so recklessly rushing ahead and making a half-assed Primary Skill would be akin to shooting herself in the foot.

She had to think in the long-term, though that went especially for her Class Skill. Her Arcane Mage Class was graded as Rare, and it would only take her to Rank 3 and no further. Not if she couldn’t upgrade it to Elite, which would get her to Rank 4, or Legendary, which would allow her to ascend all the way to Rank 5. That was the main barrier, along with the lack of publicly open Rifts, that stopped most of the population in other Realms from reaching the higher Ranks. Grey Classes couldn’t even go beyond Rank 0. Common would cap out at Rank 1, and Uncommon would go no further than Rank 2.

Mia idly played with Camie’s crimson tresses, curling a silky lock of hair around her index finger while she thought. Then she let it go, sliding her fingers through that lustrous scarlet mane, letting her nails gently scrape across Camie’s scalp in a way that had the girl shuddering even in her sleep. That frown she wore before melted away like a snowflake in a desert, and the corners of her lips curled up slightly.

There was something about knowing just what Camie liked, knowing how to make her shudder and smile that pretty little smile of hers that filled Mia with a warmth she adored beyond reason. Having the big bad vampire sleeping in her lap, entirely at her mercy, shuddering and smiling softly in her sleep … it was better than any drug, drink or thrill she’d ever experienced.

Plans, worries and musings faded from Mia’s mind as she gazed down at her girlfriend’s face. Usually, her mind was bouncing between ideas and topics like a demented pinball, jumping from one thing to the next only to discard it for another new thing. Tens, sometimes scores of topics hung in the balance, waiting for her to revisit them. She’s always had trouble focusing on a single thing or working on a single project without getting bored or losing motivation. After a while, even things she usually loved started feeling like pulling teeth.

She functioned much better when she had two or more concurrent projects, able to switch and swap between them whenever one started to feel dry. It helped keep things feeling fresh and kept her from burning out. A psychologist would have dubbed it some manner of ‘-ism’ or whatnot, but she didn’t really care. She’d found her own workarounds long ago, and going to a shrink had never sat well with her. Not that she ever had the spare income to spend on something so … unnecessary either.

Her mind was calm now, peaceful, almost silent. Only the feeling of soft curls under her fingers, the warmth of another body, and the weight of Camie’s head on her thigh.

The vampiress shifted, then let out a soft groan before her eyelids started fluttering, then slowly slid open to reveal a pair of ruby-red eyes. A blink, slow and owlish, then another chased the dazedness out of those gorgeous crimson eyes.

“ … morning?” Camie mumbled, apparently still a bit sleepy.

“Slept well?” Mia asked softly, smiling with a hint of mischief as she resumed her idle petting.

“Yea-ahm-!” Camie said, only to still when Mia’s nails gently brushed her scalp. Those pretty red eyes narrowed, and something that might have been a pout crept onto Camie’s face as she huffed in mock-annoyance. She could be so cute sometimes. “We should-“

“The others are still asleep; we aren’t going anywhere,” Mia cut in, already knowing what the objection was going to be. Lina and Anna were all the way across in far corners of the tower’s top floor, far enough that they couldn’t hear a thing over the howling wind. There was no reason not to enjoy themselves for as long as they could before they had to head back into the Rift. “Let me have this, please?”

Camie huffed again, averting her gaze, but notably she failed to remove her head from Mia’s lap. Adorable. Mia had to resist the urge to coo at the sight or scratch the girl in a place that would make her even more embarrassed.

Despite her vampiric urges, Camie tended to be rather weak to physical or verbal affection, doubly so when it was in public. Mia wasn’t sure whether the girl was just embarrassed, or truly disliked being affectionate in public, so she held herself back from doing anything that might have gone ‘too far’.

The coming of mana, magic, Rifts, the System and all its madness would be described by many as an apocalypse. The End Times, or something of the like. Few were those who still lived nearly two months after the first day of the System who hadn’t lost someone precious to them, either to the monsters, accidents, desperate survivors or even starvation.

Anna Weber was no different. Like many others, on that day when the sky opened and bled a thousand colours, she was changed much like the world itself. Her humanity was not gone, but diminished and joined by a new, bestial reflection of her own self that was born in the depths of her soul.

That day had been one of the few where both her mother and father were home for dinner, and Anna had been there with them, picking at her food, when the world broke.

The rest of the night felt like a fevered dream, only echoes and fragments of it remaining in her memories. Anna had been fortunate in what the System changed her into. She ended up as a Shifter, a Beastkin second only to Magical Beasts in Bloodline purity. She had many forms, and her human self could usually keep her inner beast suppressed.

Her father hadn’t been nearly so lucky. Anna wasn’t sure how that happened; it was he from whom she’d gotten her Bloodline, so how did it make sense that she was a proper Shifter while he … while he was a Werebear?

Maybe it had to do with her mother; Anna had a Trait simply called Calmth that didn’t fit in with her Beastkin-themed Traits. Maybe it was that that gave enough power to her human self to come out victorious over her inner beast. Or maybe it was a whim of the System. Maybe it was sheer, dumb luck, some lingering genetic resurfacing in her that was stronger than in her father. Maybe she was more mutable than her father because she was younger, and the Bloodline managed to sink its claws into her much more deeply.

She didn’t know, and she would never know. The nightmares still haunted her, those accursed fragmented memories and echoes of that horrible day. She remembered collapsing as her body began to shift and writhe as if she was having a stroke, and she remembered the same happening to her father. She could still hear his agonised screams as his muscles tore, bones shifted, and fur grew where it never should have.

The screams of agony shifted into a bestial roar, a deep, reverberating bellow of a tortured beast. Then came the rage, those bloodshot, feral eyes that recognised nothing and held not even an echo of the man that raised her. He hadn’t been the best father, Anna could easily admit. He was often absent, happier at work or with his drinking buddies than with his wife and daughter, but he had always made sure his family never wanted for money. She remembered him spending an entire weekend in the scorching sun to build her a swing in the backyard. He hadn’t really known how to be a father; he never said he loved her, never hugged her or played with her, but he had tried.

The half-man, half-bear that stood in the middle of their family kitchen was a stranger, a man lost to rage and agony and to his inner beast. It was a sudden, absolute agony that tore Anna out of her own daze as the malformed beast kicked her hard enough to break every rib in her chest and send her crashing through a brick wall.

It was then that her human self faltered, the calm retreating as the Beast took over. Her body shifted for the first time, lethal wounds healing in seconds, and it wasn’t Anna that charged back through the broken wall, but a furious Dire Bear.

That was her last and final memory of the night. Letting go. Giving in to the rage, the pain and the fear she felt. She could have held on, she knew that now, she knew her surrender to the Beast within had been entirely voluntary.

When her senses returned to her, it was dawn, and she found herself standing above the ruined remains of her home, two bodies mangled beyond recognition lying at her feet.

Even now, months later, she still didn’t know whether it had been her or her father who killed her mother. She didn’t even remember how she managed to overcome the Werebear, but there were pieces of him scattered dozens of meters apart. His head was missing entirely, and a part of her knew where it had gone, even if the rest of her desperately denied it.

Was it any surprise then that when the Call went out, offering safety, family, protection and help, she answered it? Was it any surprise that she couldn’t resist when that single roar filled her with the same feeling she had back when she was a little girl again, sitting in her mother’s lap while she braided her hair? That comfortable, familial warmth had turned out to be a lie, but it had been irresistible to Anna at the time.

She had been so alone in those first few weeks, fleeing from monsters and hiding in abandoned buildings, wallowing in guilt and too afraid to approach any of the groups of survivors she saw from afar. They had all been so … fragile; it terrified her how her inner Beast always seemed to idly note and inform her how easy it would be to kill them all whenever she was about to work up the courage to approach someone.

But that Call. That Call made her feel safe, and some primal, bestial instinct told her that the one who made it was strong. It told her that he wouldn’t die if she lost control near him. That he would be able to put her down before she hurt anyone if she once more failed to hold back her inner beast.

She had made a promise to herself. She vowed to never surrender to the Beast Within no matter what happened. No matter how afraid she was, how much pain she was suffering from, or even if she was about to die. That monster inside of her could never be allowed to slip its leash ever again.

But to know that even if she failed, someone would be there to kill her before she could kill any more innocents was a balm to her soul.

Even now, she could feel it rage; she could feel it strain and struggle against its leash as Anna’s Dire Bear Form crashed into the crystalline bulk of the Rift Guardian. The monster was a strange, Ent-like creature, looking like one of the crystalline trees but with a face carved into its trunk and a pair of arms shaped from a bundle of roots. It had no legs, but it had uprooted itself when the fight began and was now moving around on its dozens of roots like some demented octopus.

Anna’s Dire Bear form was massive and heavy; it could also run faster than some cars could drive, and so when she crashed into the tree-monster, it was struck by the force akin to a landslide. It tilted backwards, its carved face twisting into a rictus of rage as roots desperately lashed out to dig into the ground. They found purchase, managing to halt her momentum, and then its fists fell, hammering away at her back and sides.

Each blow struck like a cannonball; ribs broke, her spine cracked, muscles tore, and organs liquified. Her Bestial Regeneration was healing her, not fast enough to return her to peak health between blows, but fast enough to keep her from dying for a few more minutes even if she just sat there and took the beating.

She didn’t; her Mana Drain Skill finally managed to latch onto the Guardian, and a steady stream of mana poured into Anna, beginning to fill up her dwindling reserves. A new wound bloomed across her stomach, feeling like a whip-strike, and she resisted the urge to glance back.

That had been a transferred wound, her second Class Skill: Aura of the Martyr having sapped a good portion of the force from a blow that was about to strike one of her allies.

The wound healed, and Anna forged on. The last nearly two months had left her quite familiar with pain, and while she’d never let the Beast loose, its rage flowed through her, soothing the aches and reinforcing her will with its own murderous intent. It was a dangerous balance, not quite like dancing on the edge of a blade, but not far either. Still, Anna trusted herself, and her mother’s Calmth Trait to keep her in the driver's seat of her body.

She deserved the pain. A part of her knew her mother would be appalled, but every sting, every wound, especially ones she took on so others wouldn’t have to, felt like atonement. Another part wondered whether her mother would have truly been so appalled if she knew it was her stupid, weak-willed daughter who — probably — ended up killing her?

Maybe she would think it a fitting punishment.

That’s a lie. A small voice said, but it was drowned by waves of guilt and bestial rage.

Some of the crystalline golems, the only other type of monsters aside from these walking crystal trees, were making a mess of things. Dozens of them were approaching in that slow, lumbering manner of theirs, coming from every direction with arms raised and Light Bolts primed and ready. Her new party members had their work cut out for them, keeping the golems from overwhelming them and keeping up the pressure on the golden treant trying to turn Anna into ground bear meat.

One of Mia’s fuzzy pink spells exploded against the Guardian’s face, leaving a tiny crater where its left eye used to be. It clearly wasn’t enough to kill, but the creature reeled back in apparent surprise. Anna used the window of opportunity to shift back into her human form and leap back to create distance before shifting again, taking on her Hybrid form … which the System called the Werebear form.

Her Hybrid Form was still bulky and large, but it had only half the Strength and defensive stats of her Dire Bear Form, but twice its Agility and Flexibility. It was fast, agile and much more precise in its brutality.

A new wound bloomed across her torso, making her feel like she’d just been hit by a car. Her ribs creaked and muscle tore, but it didn’t slow her as she leapt at the Guardian’s outstretched arm. If her guess was right, the bastard had just punched Mia hard enough to break the pink-haired girl’s Ward in one go, with a good bit of momentum left over from it. It would have pulped the girl's organs had it been allowed to hit.

Another life saved. She mused to herself; it was the only thing that helped lighten the wave of guilt she felt pressing down on her day and night. If only for a short while. She had no idea how Mia ended up in a position where she could have died; that girl was the slipperiest fighter of the entire party, with like a dozen contingencies and ways to protect herself.

It didn’t matter. The vampire girl was hauling the petite Mage away while the Storm Sorceress was picking up the slack, probably burning through her mana reserves with the veritable lightning storm she was unleashing. The line was holding, and Mia was out of harm’s way, and also given time to reapply her Wards and recall that Guardian of hers.

Her Hybrid Form’s maw opened wide as her body sailed through the air, reaching the apex of her jump and clamped down on the crystalline arm with enough force to crack it. Then she shifted back into her Dire Bear Form, her muscle mass almost doubling, the same as her Strength stat, and she felt the crystal beneath her teeth shatter like glass.

Anna landed with a heavy thud, her body already halfway through shifting back into her Hybrid form by the time her paws bit the ground. The Guardian’s other fist screamed towards her from above like a headsman’s axe, but she leapt away on all fours even before her body finished its transformation. She was right to do so, because the crystalline fist landed like a miniature meteor, cratering the ground and kicking up shards of broken crystal.

Some shrapnel hit Anna, but her regeneration would push them out soon enough. The shards were full of that disgusting chaotic Light mana, but she had figured out early on in this delve that she could smother them with her own Dark mana easily enough. With the Guardian so generously providing her with a steady stream of mana, she didn’t even need to bother with efficiency; she could just drown it in a deluge of darkness.

Gabriel, her new … tutor, or whatever he was supposed to be, had said that in a contest of magic, Light usually won out over Dark when it came to offence, its potent chaotic nature managing to bulldoze its way through most other spells. However, Dark could weave protective spells that would halt even the strongest Light magic spells by sapping their strength and using it to strengthen the Dark spell instead. Her own body was her own ‘domain’ though, so she had no need for a precise, finicky spell to purge the alien mana from it.

Anna ran, hunched over on all fours in her Hybrid form that probably made her look more like a charging gorilla than a bear or a human. It didn’t matter; this was the fastest, most agile and most precise she could be. In this form, she could dodge, evade and weave between the sluggish, enraged blows of the crystal treant. She nipped at its heels, figuratively that is; it had no heels, only roots and branches and leaves. Whenever it got fed up with running after the far-too-swift Werebear and turned to focus on someone less mobile, Anna would tear off a root, bite a chunk out of its trunk or snap off a branch. Each of those actions earned her another few minutes of the freshly engaged monster’s total focus.

There was something different about the next bolt of lightning the Sorceress threw; Anna didn’t know what, or how she could even tell, but her gut was proven right when instead of crashing into a golem, it curled. The bolt of yellowish-blue lightning arced through the air, weaving between the trees as it drew a wide circle around the battlefield. It chased its own tail like one of those witless dogs, circling around and around without pause, without showing the slightest intention to fade.

A golem walked into it, and the lightning bolt tore straight through its chest, killing it instantly. The lightning showed no signs of weakening. Neat. A way to set a proper fence around the battlefield and keep those annoying golems away. It wouldn’t stop them from shooting their Light Bolts, but a few would probably end up dead, and they would be kept at bay.

With Mia’s Black Knight and Spirit stalking the woods to kill all the golems they could before they got line of sight on the party, that should be sufficient to let most of the party focus on the Guardian and finally end this fight.

Mia took over from Helene as the primary ranged damage dealer after that, having healed up with a potion and reapplied her Wards on everyone she could reach over the last few minutes Anna had brought her. Fuzzy pink missiles began raining down on the guardian, tearing out fist-sized chunks one by one.

Carmilla and the swordsman Brent also joined her in the vanguard, harrying the by-then frantically writhing treant. It looked damn near apoplectic with rage on what little of its carved face remained, its roots, branches and single arm all lashing out at anything that came close with a rage that almost seemed petulant.

Brent’s sword slashed off a root that tried to wrap around him, his blade moving so fast Anna could barely see it unless she focused on it. The man weaved between the retaliatory strikes, swaying this way and that with economical motions to conserve energy, only to lash out with ferocity and precision whenever an opportunity presented itself. In half a minute, he’d trimmed away more of the Guardian’s limbs than Anna had managed over the entire fight.

Camie fought with that fluid, feline grace that was impossible to imitate, melding her Blood magic with her melee combat skills in a way that made it look effortless but wasn’t. She was cheating, of course; Anna had once overheard that the vampire had weird dreams where she got memories from her fallen ancestors, and apparently that included a not insignificant portion of their skills. Making such a complex and dangerous fighting style look so easy must have taken her ancestors centuries, or at the very least decades, to refine.

Lina, the Aeromancer, was also probably doing … something, but air magic didn’t seem to be one of the more overt types of magic, or maybe it was just hard-countered by the heavy, crystalline bodies of the golems. None of them would kneel over from a breeze, or even a hurricane.

Storm bolts joined the pink missiles, forcing the Guardian back, keeping it off balance and in a fighting retreat, although it was clear Helene was conserving her mana, considering the pink missiles outnumbered her spells four to one. It probably wasn’t even aware it was retreating, but with the way it had to keep moving back to avoid any one of them starting to hack away at its trunk, it had to.

Meter by meter, it was getting forced back. Anna had thought to circle around and force it to remain still, but Brent had barked an order not to. It took her a few moments of confused bewilderment to realise what he was playing at.

The back of its trunk met the still-circling lightning bolt, which had grown thick as a man’s wrist, and a sound akin to an explosion going off inside a castle of glass followed. Half the treant’s trunk was torn apart in a shower of shrapnel, and in return the lightning bolt petered out, dimming to a tiny flicker and vanishing after one last circuit around the battlefield, its power apparently expended.

The three of them pounced on the monster like wolves smelling blood, taking advantage of its limbs writhing without aim and its form going still. Camie’s crimson talons tore out chunks of its trunk, Brent’s blade hacked away at it like a woodsman’s axe, and Anna joined them, chewing through the crystalline bark while shifting back into her Dire Bear form. Even if the monster managed to regather some of its wits and fighting spirit, its struggles were weaker now; she was halfway certain she could keep it pinned beneath the weight of her bear form.

Anna wasn’t sure what did it; she was barely thinking, desperately clawing and tearing at the rapidly weakening but still struggling monster when it just … shattered. Its massive form just broke apart, crumbling into hundreds of golden, crystalline shards with none bigger than a man’s fist.

The party was silent for a moment, then a cheer went up and the flood of relief threatened to rob Anna of her strength. Alas, the golems were still about, a handful of them having already crossed the perimeter now that the circling lightning bolt was gone.

The cheer faded, but they threw themselves into the ensuing extermination mission with a lighter mood and renewed enthusiasm. It was more tedious than anything now that the entire party could devote themselves to the task; without a Guardian to contend with, Mia could easily keep the Wards on everyone fully charged, so even if the golems got off a Light Bolt or two, they never caused any actual harm, not even if the target failed to dodge in time.

Two hours after the Guardian’s death, they stood before the arched Gate leading to the final room of the Rift where they would claim their rewards. Their spatial bags were full, every last cubic inch of free space inside filled with the golden shards of Light crystals. These creatures had no true cores, but in turn their entire bodies were akin to monster cores. Less potent perhaps, but purer and containing solely Light mana.

The mood was relaxed, and Anna noted with some trepidation that no one was looking at her out of the corner of their eyes. Both Mia, Carmilla and even Brent had made sure to keep her within their line of sight throughout the delve. Anna recognised the wary glimmer in their eyes, the way their bodies spoke what their mouths didn’t. They didn’t trust her and fully expected her to betray them once Gabe wasn’t breathing down her neck. Or maybe they knew what she had done and were rightfully wary of her going berserk again-

No, no one knew that. It made no sense.

Still, she had appreciated their wariness. It made her feel safer, more certain that even if she proved to be weak and surrendered to the Beast Within, they would be ready. Their wariness made them safer, more ready, more prepared. She’d only dared to join this party because they were the most powerful Rift-delving group in the city and were predisposed to treating her with an appropriate measure of suspicion.

This measure of … trust — if it was that and not mere negligence — made her feel uneasy. However, when she considered doing something to renew their paranoia, or just speak up about her worries, the words refused to come out.

Being trusted again after so long, even though she didn’t deserve it, and being part of a group that wasn’t some megalomaniac’s gang of sycophants and slaves felt … pleasant.

That treasonous, pathetic feeling kept her from speaking, from acting out as reason demanded she should have. Pretending she wasn’t dangerous, that she could be trusted, was actively putting them in danger, and yet, she couldn’t bring herself to do anything about it. It felt like a betrayal of the meagre amount of trust they had placed in her.

She was still so weak. Too weak to do what had to be done. Too afraid. The self-loathing came with its usual force as she trudged behind the party with her head hung low.

Anna could protect them from monsters, but she couldn’t protect them from herself. Not unless she could keep the beast leashed, caged and under her absolute control.
