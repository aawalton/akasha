Theo blinked several times, trying to clear the blindness. He pushed himself up off the warm stone ground as the thick gore bubbled and hissed from the intense heat. The wound through his chest burned something fierce. As he tried to get a lungful of air back through his system, he glanced around.

Humphrey stood smoldering, wisps of smoke rising from his dark-plated armor. Lucius looked dazed, hands covering his face as animated stars circled his head. Norah patted out flames biting at her bandages, a scowl on her face.

“Dodged it,” the vampire whispered weakly to himself before his eyes found another figure standing nearby.

Bright white light snaked around Hunter in a spiral, moving down his arms and back around his torso, getting faster each loop.

“You caught some of it?” Theo asked.

“Somethin’ like that,” the shadowy man grunted through bared teeth. “Best pray I can remember how to use it, or we’re still fucked.”

The radiant bomb hadn’t left the Radoch army entirely untouched, but it was clear they were half-prepared for the attack. Some of the weaker ones had fallen, and now those just blinded were pushing their way forward to assault the bridge.

Theo flexed his numb hand, one eye on the living bomb beside them, and the other searching for a way to help in this situation. Escaping was possible, but meant letting this fleet of bugs flank Sally’s march.

Lucius shook out his head, a brief question mark appearing beside him as he clocked Hunter for the first time. Without hesitation, he sank away and became the man’s shadow. The pair wrestled with the overpowering divine energy until something snapped.

The silver revolver blew out a beam of pitch-black energy, sweeping across the bridge and grassy lands beyond. Fifteen-feet wide, it scoured every Radoch it hit into ashes, burning fighters and corpses alike. For each second the beam lashed back and forth, the glowing tendril gripping Hunter dimmed until it was finally gone.

Hunter stumbled, and the gun fell slack in his grip. Even his shadowy form seemed to weaken, becoming misty in places.

Theo stood in surprise at the damage wrought. The beam hadn’t just disintegrated any Radoch it came into contact with, but had dug thick scars through the dirt, and partially split the bridge itself. Fewer corpses made it difficult for the bugs to traverse the area once again.

“Think I got it,” the man sighed, coughing out a small amount of smoke.

Healing and bolstered attack buffs ran through them as Norah cast Rite of the Ancients followed by Mummy Knows Best. Humphrey readied to rally the group behind him while they finished recovering. The bridge defense was going to work.

Out from the tattered remnants of the attacking force, a large figure leaped through the air, landing at the start of the bridge. The Commander of this fleet. A Radoch with dark patterning across his chitinous plating and large metal legs in place of his natural ones.

Theo lifted his coffin-mace up to his shoulder. +2 Threat Level for him. Considering he had a Guardian, Champion, and whatever Hunter and Lucious paired together was on his side, that wasn’t too tough an ask.

But something didn’t feel right. Perhaps it was the injury still burning away at him. The air had been saturated with so much light and holy energy - plus they were near running water. Surely that was the reason he felt weaker than usual?

Humphrey put a hand on his shoulder. “Go and reset. I can buy you a few minutes.”

Rather than argue, the vampire just returned a nod. “Thanks, Pops.”

After ten seconds, the moon key teleported Theo back to the Outsider home base. As soon as he arrived in the meeting room, he pulled out a healing potion and raised it to his lips.

He paused, not drinking it directly. His eyes went to the side, to see Archie already there, facing away from him.

“I’ve been expecting you,” the wizard said.

The sound of thunderous charge echoed through the edge of the Forest area. With a low growl, Wolf burst from the treeline and staggered toward the crest of a hill. The ocean was clearly visible from here - more than normal. This was a cliff.

He glanced down at the rocky waters below before snuffling and turning back to face the woodland. There were six humans left by his side, as well as a goblin who was currently riding him.

“Looks like this is the end of the road,” Bella said, giving him a pat on the head. “Maybe we should just take as many with us as possible.”

The survivors were exhausted. A gradual retreat had baited the Radochs into losing positions time and time again, but there was no end to them. Eventually, attrition was working against the defenders of Sanctuary - and now there was nowhere left to retreat to.

“One last charge,” Wolf grunted. “As soon as the insects show themselves.”

They weren’t even good eating. Besides the crunch-appeal, they were too bitter, and their insides tasted bad. As his jaws were a major part of his offensive power, that made the fighting much less fun.

Bella pulled at the fur on his back. “I think they’re going to use the cannons again.”

As if on cue, a long screech came from deep within the woods. The Adventurers acted immediately, bringing shields overhead or casting defensive spells. This had happened twice before, but they’d been under tree cover before. Now, out in the open…

The bear reared up onto his hind legs, dropping the goblin to the ground behind him to act as her shield. Above the forest, a dark cloud thick with hundreds of arrows arced into the air. There wasn’t time to move and avoid it.

Wolf closed his eyes and raised his defensive power, waiting for impact.

The sound of arrows hitting the ground drilled through his ears, but he felt no pain. His nose twitched at something familiar.

Opening his eyes, a long panel of playing cards stretched over him and the remaining Adventurers, like a roof. Arrows pierced through many of them, but never made it all the way through. A wide smile crossed his face as he glanced down to see Max beside him.

“Not your curtain call yet, friend,” the magician said. “Although you’ve used your phoenix power already?”

“Yeah.” Wolf sank back down to four feet. “Bella kept me alive while I was still a cub.”

Max gave the goblin a deep bow. “My thanks for your service. Perhaps I shall entertain you with a never-seen-before trick as a reward?”

The goblin grinned. “As long as it kills bugs.”

“Of course. All the best tricks do.” The magician swept out a cloak beside him, as if he were courting a bull. With an appropriate amount of flair, he whisked it away, revealing Ren crouched on one knee with her rifle up.

Loaded with her new bullet, she fired off into the woods. Although most didn’t have her keen eyesight, she was able to pick out exactly what happened.

The projectile cored two roaches before detonating on the third, casting four Chain Lightning spell scrolls. Blue glow illuminated the woods as arcing bolts fried three dozen Radochs and overloaded their arrow-throwing cannon. This secondary explosion took out another couple of roaches as it set their fuel supply alight.

“I thought you were supposed to be waiting,” Wolf said.

Max nodded. “The schedule has changed for this act. Much like our fabled STAR, forces are converging at five points to put pressure on the Radochs. Now that you’ve stretched them thinly across the Forest, we are going to fight back and clear the path for Sally.”

“Theo and his group are on the west side,” Ren added as she swept the distance for her next target. “The reemergence of everyone else will happen once the Radochs are distracted enough.”

Wolf scratched at the side of his head. “Who is the fifth group?”

“Archie and-” She fired her rifle. “Chuck, supposedly.”

The wizard looked tired. Hands clasped behind his back, he regarded the vampire with a neutral look.

Theo drank his potion slowly, giving the man time to say something, but only silence filled the room. Eventually, he put the empty bottle away and frowned. “What’s… going on?”

“You came here because you felt something was off, did you not?” The wizard shook his head. “No need to answer. I already know. You are always sensitive to these sorts of things.”

“Did you use your Aspect?”

Archie raised an eyebrow. “That’s quite a leap. But yes and no.”

“Oh.” Theo nodded. “Are you going to explain? Or… you’re different, aren’t you?”

“The loss of power should be evident, even to those not as in tune as you are.” The wizard sighed. “Best I not over-explain as we have much to do elsewhere. My Aspect Ultimate is called Big Bang. Normally, we get to this point in the timeline, and as a last-ditch eventuality, I use it and reset everything.”

Theo opened and closed his mouth a few times. “This has been a loop?”

“The problem is that fate cannot be changed. It didn’t matter how many times I reset to the start. It was just playing the record from the start again. Same tune. Same outcome. The Radochs win, and I set us back.” Archie gave him a sad smile. “You’d offer your heart to me, and I’d cause the Death of the System.”

“Does that explain why I have time-and-space related issues?”

“Death is everywhere, after all. I’d offer my apologies, but the alternative was the Radochs take over the Sea.”

Theo scratched at the bandages still wrapped around his healing wound. “But since you’re telling me this… has something changed?”

“Up until the moment you arrived here, I would have just shrugged. I did not remember any of the previous loops. But now… the cycle has been broken.”

Archie moved over to the table, where a hologram of Sanctuary slowly revolved. He swept his hand in the air, dismissing it. Instead, a figure appeared in its place.

“I don’t know how,” the wizard continued, “but Hunter is new to this cycle. The planet he destroyed where we rescued my soul shard weakened the Radochs. In this current battle, previously your group died to that divine explosion. After the Radochs overran Sally, she sacrificed her final life to bring you back so that I may reset the universe.”

Theo pinched the bridge of his nose. “Assuming I can swallow all of that, why is it that you’ve lost power now?”

“Cause and effect? More than anybody else in the Sea, I am a cog that fits where the machine needs me. The weight of what is written is part of my soul, and if the future doesn’t need me to create a second—or hundredth—chance, then I cannot.” A dry smile crossed the old man’s face. “That doesn’t mean that winning is guaranteed now, of course.”

“So, Hunter was enough to throw a spanner in the works. Train has been derailed. We just have to steer it someplace safe.”

“It is no longer a fight for the universe, but for Sanctuary and all who live here. At least in the short term.” The wizard stood straighter and placed both hands on his cane. “Are you aware of how infestations can be eradicated, Theo?”

“I could name a thousand ways, but I feel like you want me to say ‘poison the food supply’?”

Archie smiled, some of his usual confidence returning. “The energy-source stipulation was not the only negative part of the STAR+ for the Radochs. It is a disease that spreads among their kind. Propagating through every world they inhabit or try to conquer. And in our System, they are the antagonists. The monster. The oppressor.”

The image hovering over the table flickered to show the Hive King.

“Across infinity, the Sea itself will arm reality to quell this alien threat, no matter where they try to hide.”

Death always casts the darkest shadows.
