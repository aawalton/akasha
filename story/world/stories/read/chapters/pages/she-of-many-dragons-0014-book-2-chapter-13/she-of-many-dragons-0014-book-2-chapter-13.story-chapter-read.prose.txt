Alice was wholly unsure if finding the fallen grey searcher was a good idea, but her dragons were so enthusiastic that she was committed.

She set Iggy and Spark to the task of watching the sky to see if any of the other grey searchers were heading their way. It didn't seem reasonable to her that they would leave one of their own, injured, behind. They were searching in a strict grid-like pattern, so the gap in their group would be noticed soon. Surely then, they would come to help.

But time and time again as they walked back through the burned area, Iggy reported that the other dragons were continuing their formations. They did not seem to notice or care that one of their own was missing.

Numi had pointed the way, and as they got close, the fallen dragon was easy to find. It seemed to have tried to catch itself on the remains of the trees, perhaps to break its fall.

It had been a ruinous plan.

Everything that remained of the fire was brittle and, in many cases, sharp. It had been like falling on tree posts driven deep into the ground that also had their tops weathered away into points.

He had fallen on spikes.

Several had pierced his wings, and Alice could only imagine the damage done to his body. He lay on the ground like a dead thing and was much larger up close than Alice had imagined. Every angle of the dragon was sharp and triangular, even its head. His mouth was gaped open to try to suck in air. His half-open eyes, also grey, were unfocused and vaguely confused.

Alice couldn't form any type of connection with this dragon through her class, but seeing it broken and forgotten like that made sympathy well up inside her.

Ignoring Iggy’s warning that he should check out the dragon first for danger, she broke into a run.

The grey dragon's eyes seemed to focus as she drew close. It rattled out words that were lost in its chest. Each breath was wet and painful.

“Hold still,” Alice said. “Is there any way we could call for help from your wing mates?”

“I don't need help,” that dragon rasped, every word visibly paining him. “I just need to find... to find...” He trailed off, losing strength.

Alice bit her lip. She didn't know what she could do for the dragon, and she hated it. For all her class powers, she didn't have the ability to heal, and neither did her aspects.

I don't even have an extra token to get me the aspect that I need, she thought. I should have realized I would need a healer eventually. Why didn't I think things through?

But as bad as she felt for the dragon, she wasn't so lost to sense that she was about to order Iggy to fly up and attract the attention of the other searchers. At least, not yet.

“Why are all of you here?” she asked. “Is this forest your land?” Had she burned their home? Maybe they were searching for the one who did it.

“If it is your land, why didn't you take better care of it?” Natura grumbled.

Her dragons had instinctively stood back and let Alice go first. Except for Iggy, who swam in the air above the length of the grey, suspicious and ready to dive at it within a moment's notice.

The grey tried to answer her, but it seemed something was caught in his throat. He shook his head, swallowed with a painful grimace, and said, “I failed... I failed him.”

His eyes started to slide shut.

“Failed who?” Alice asked, putting her hand to his muzzle.

But the cold feeling at the base of her spine told her that she very likely knew.

The dragon did not answer.

Prim fluttered to Alice's shoulder. “I believe that he was already dying when he fell from the sky.”

Alice shook her head, though not out of disagreement, but out of rising disgust. “So, someone just sent these dragons to search until they die of exhaustion? Who would order this? How could they?”

Prim was quiet for a moment. “It is someone who does not value his own aspects.”

Do you think it's the He of Many Dragons? Alice wanted to ask, but couldn't bring herself to say it aloud. Besides, it was a ludicrous question. Orris had spoken of a man who had lived centuries before.

Alice didn't know everything about high magics, having been a General Laborer all her life, but had heard gossip and stories. High-level classers could extend their health and lives somewhat, but certainly not centuries. And that only came by the way of sorcerer or mage magic.

My class isn't even magical. Only my dragons can be on my behalf, she thought.

On the heels of that, Bella fluttered up to her free shoulder and peered down to look at the dragon with a sense of glee.

“Alice, do you suppose I could have a portion of the meat? It would do very well for my Burnt Sacrifice skill. Even a finger would do.”

“Dragon meat? That is disgusting,” Prim said, outraged.

“It is not as if I am actually eating him,” Bella shot back. “The only disgusting thing would the letting all this lovely essence go to waste!”

“Bella, he is still alive,” Alice said quietly.

Bella shrunk back as if she had been harshly chastised. “My goal is only to serve you, Alice. The best way I can do that is by growing stronger,” she muttered.

Alice hushed her because the grey dragon's eyes were opening again. She got the impression that he had briefly passed out and had thankfully missed the last few moments.

“Are you thirsty?” Alice asked, hoping to at least find a way to ease his suffering. There was no doubt that he was dying. “Perhaps we could get you some water?”

Though all the streams in this burned area were either choked with ash or blood eels, or both. And it wasn't like she had a bucket.

But the dragon's attention was not on Alice at all. He focused completely on Bella. Something like joy flashed within his grey eyes.

“I have found it,” he breathed, and his entire body jerked as if he were trying to rise, but he was so weak that he could not do more than shift his head.

Alice rose to her feet in alarm. “Found it? What do you mean?” She put a protective hand over Bella's back.

Prim growled and tensed, wings outstretched. Everything in her demeanor said that she was preparing to throw herself between this dragon and Bella... even though she had just been sniping with her.

“You have no claim on Alice's aspect,” she said.

However, the grey searcher didn't seem to care what Prim said.

“I have not failed after all,” he murmured to himself, his eyes alight. In any other circumstance, Alice would be happy to see that he'd found a little joy in his last moments. Instead, she backed up a few steps. Under her hand, Bella shivered.

The grey dragon breathed out, and that breath was more like a death rattle—choked and wet—with red blood gathering at the corner of his mouth.

A glow shone out at the base of his throat and traveled upward to his mouth until it resolved itself into his shining diamond gemstone as large as Alice's fist.

“Alice! Stop him!” Numi suddenly cried out.

The glowing gem began to pulse so brightly that it looked like the flickers of the sun.

“I can't!” Alice had no ability to stop whatever the grey dragon was doing. He was not her aspect.

But she had other resources.

“Iggy!”

That was all she had to say. Her fierce red protector swooped down, a breath of fire already gushing out between his teeth. Behind him, Spark shot out arcs of lightning. Both hit the gem at the same time.

Perhaps the magical gem reflected some of the trauma that the grey searcher had gone through, or the dual assault of fire breath and lightning was just enough. With a loud crack, the gem split into two pieces and fell, dim, to the ground.

Alice cast a glance at the grey searcher. He was dead, his eyes unfocused. But his jaws were parted in a final smile.

“What was that?” Prim asked, sounding shaky. “A weapon?”

“No.” Numi scuttled up and hesitantly poked at one half of the gem. “I only saw it for a moment, but it was enough for my Appraisal skill to tell me that it was a transmission gem. I think that he was calling in to report—”

“To report on me,” Bella said lowly. “He said he was looking for me.” Her claws flexed on Alice’s shoulder.

Alice wanted to tell her otherwise, but… They had been walking through the forest for over a week, and Numi had discovered countless skeletons. Out of all those, a single remaining egg lay among them.

Someone had planted Bella among the dead.

Alice clenched her fist. It didn't matter. She had always known that Bella was going to be something special. She cast a worried glance up at the sky. “How much of that message was he able to get out?”

“I don't know,” Numi said, and promptly swallowed the two halves of the transmission stone into her storage space.

Prim made a sound of concern, and Numi turned to look at her. “What? Even if it's broken, it's valuable.”

“Alice, if this dragon was able to get a report out, we should leave immediately,” Iggy said with concern.

“Yes,” she agreed, then, not sparing another glance at the grey searcher, immediately turned to walk away. “Natura, find us the fastest path back to the forest.”

Then she broke into a run.

“I can do better than that,” Natura said, then to Alice's surprise, she galloped into her path and then stopped to crouch in front of her.

It took Alice a startled moment to realize that her dragon meant for her to get on. Natura was about the size of a horse, but she was still so young…

“We can't fly together,” Alice said.

“No,” Natura agreed, “but I can run.”

Bella shivered slightly on her shoulder, and that decided it for Alice. Someone was searching for her dragon, and she would never let them take her.

Somewhat awkwardly, she got on Natura's back, positioning herself right in front of her wings. It was an uncomfortable seat as Natura's scales were as rough as tree bark.

Then she had no more time to think. Natura stood and broke into a loping gallop, and it was everything Alice could do to hold on.
