The sun teetered on the edge of the horizon, no longer visible from within the tall walls of the kingdom’s capital. Though the sky had begun to dim, the streets remained lively. Lamplighters moved with practiced ease, lighting their posts one by one, racing to finish their work before the darkness took full hold.

It looked like any other afternoon. Despite the intermittent rain, the townspeople bustled about—smiling, chatting, living—blissfully unaware of the storm that loomed on the horizon.

The sight was surreal, though not entirely unexpected for Luke, who watched in silence from the shadows of a narrow alley. This was the calm before the storm—a storm that would decide the fate of the Marxx Kingdom and all its people.

“Are you nervous?” a voice asked as a firm hand clapped his shoulder.

“I’d be lying if I said I wasn’t,” Luke admitted, shooting his friend a quick glance. “What about you?”

Kayson let out a scoff. “Of course not. I’ve survived plenty of your plans before, Luke...” He paused, then added dryly, “But I’ve gotta say—this is probably the stupidest one yet.”

The false bravado followed by the brutally honest remark made Luke chuckle aloud, some of his lingering tension melting away.

“Well, if we survive this, I’m sure I’ll come up with even worse ones.”

Kayson cursed and shot him a sideways glare. “At least wait until I’ve married a beautiful woman before sending us on another suicide mission, alright?”

At the mention of a beautiful woman, Luke’s thoughts turned—unbidden—to Victoria. He didn’t know what state she was in now, only that she was still alive. He had checked the subordinates page often, perhaps too often, just to see her name.

The knot in his stomach twisted tighter.

As if sensing his unease, Kayson spoke again. “When do we make our move?”

Luke studied him for a moment, eyebrows lifting slightly. Kayson knew the plan well by now. He’d heard it a dozen times. There was no reason to ask.

And then, the realization hit.

A small, warm smile touched Luke’s lips.

He really is a good guy.

Kayson was trying to steady him—giving him something to focus on. It was a stupid question, asked with purpose.

Rather than call him out, Luke answered.

“When dusk settles. By then, the court should be fully assembled and waiting for the king to arrive to start the proceedings.”

“And what, we just waltz into the palace?” Kayson asked, grinning wryly.

“Unless you want to salsa on the way there?”

Kayson blinked, utterly confused. “Salsa?”

“Never mind.” Luke fought back a sigh. Moments like this reminded him just how far from home he truly was. He didn’t belong in this world.

Maybe the only person who might’ve appreciated that joke was the Bishop of Lhair—the same man who had tried to kill him... and was now dismantling the Marxx Kingdom, piece by piece.

Suddenly feeling even more burdened, Luke shook his head. His Dragon’s Heart pulsed in his chest—a steady, glowing beacon that reinforced both his body and mind.

The heaviness dulled. Clarity returned.

He and Kayson stood in companionable silence for nearly an hour as the last traces of sunlight faded. The crowds gradually thinned, and the lanterns along the street began to flicker dimly, casting long, dancing shadows.

Luke’s gaze shifted toward the palace gates. It was as if he could see through the stone walls.

“It’s time,” he said, rising to his feet.

“Well, it was nice while it lasted…” Kayson sighed dramatically, stretching his shoulders. “Living, that is.”

“You can be as cynical as you want later. For now, shut up and follow my lead.”

Without waiting for a reply, Luke moved—heading straight toward the palace gates.

The two soldiers guarding the entrance tensed at their approach, hands twitching toward their weapons. For a moment, Luke thought they might actually draw on him.

Before they could, he reached into his robe and pulled out a small token, flashing it with practiced ease.

“I, General Tao, have arrived to take part in this evening’s court proceedings. Move aside.”

His voice was calm, low, and commanding—steeped in the kind of authority that came from experience, not pretense.

The guards snapped to attention, startled.

“O-Of course, General. Shall I escort you to the court?”

“No need.” Luke dismissed the offer with a flick of his wrist, slipping the token back into his robe. It was the same acting General’s token he’d received before the campaign into the Qin Empire—authentic, but temporary. A closer inspection would’ve revealed that detail… but he wasn’t planning on giving anyone that chance.

With nothing more said, the guards opened the gates. Luke walked through with the confident air of someone who belonged.

Two more stood before the palace proper, but they too were easily bypassed. The token—and the bearing with which he wore it—was enough.

The next part, however, was what truly concerned him.

Inside the grand palace, servants roamed the halls with their usual grace, carrying trays and documents, whispering orders or offering bows to passing officials. A few inclined their heads respectfully as Luke and Kayson passed, but no one stopped them. No one questioned them.

Good. The fighting hasn’t started yet, he thought, releasing a bit of the tension in his chest.

“Don’t move unless I give the signal,” Luke murmured to Kayson beside him.

The man nodded silently. He hadn’t spoken since they'd entered, but his unease hung thick in the air, like a taut string ready to snap.

Luke focused ahead. One obstacle remained—the lobby.

He’d been dreading it.

As the hallway opened into the large receiving hall, a sigh escaped his lips. Standing ahead of them, waiting, was a familiar figure.

Short. Pale. Hands clasped behind his back. Sharp eyes that seemed to cut through layers.

Luke recognized him instantly—the same servant who had received them on the day they first arrived in Ralis.

Of all the people…

His pulse quickened. Things were about to get messy.

“State your business,” the servant said. His nasally, impetuous voice struck a nerve.

Luke hesitated for a few moments, half-expecting the man to recognize him despite the minimal disguise. But when no spark of recognition came, he allowed himself a quiet breath of relief and reached into his robe.

“General Tao,” he declared, producing a jade token. “I’m here to attend the imperial court.”

He returned the token to his robe in one smooth motion.

The servant frowned. “The court has already commenced. I cannot let you enter,” he said, his eyes narrowing with suspicion.

“Don’t lie to me, servant,” Luke replied, his voice edged with a frown. “The king has yet to arrive. The court is not yet in session.”

A flicker of shock crossed the man’s face, but he masked it quickly. Rather than admit his mistake, he doubled down.

“I’m not sure I’ve ever heard of a General Tao in our armies. Allow me to see your token.” He held out a hand expectantly.

This time, Luke sighed audibly, shaking his head.

“I see you still haven’t learned your lesson.”

He gestured wordlessly to Kayson, the motion carrying a note of resignation. He had hoped to avoid bloodshed, but of all the people they could’ve run into, it had to be this idiot.

Before the servant could react, Kayson moved.

His body surged forward like a wave crashing through stone. A metallic hum whispered through the air—sharp, clean, final. A heartbeat later, the man’s body collapsed in a heap, his severed head rolling across the polished floor, frozen in a mask of fear.

Luke’s eyes lingered for only a moment before stepping past the corpse. “This way.”

He quickened his pace, weaving through the halls toward the west wing—a part of the palace he’d visited a few times. Minutes later, they turned a corner…

And stopped cold.

A cluster of people stood in the middle of the hallway.

Kayson reacted first. He grabbed Luke and pulled him back behind the wall, pressing a hand firmly over his mouth to muffle any sound.

With some effort, Luke pried the hand away and cautiously peeked around the corner.

Then froze.

Fuck. Why the hell is he here?

Outside the chamber doors stood a massive man in pristine white robes, his spiked beard perfectly groomed and gleaming in the lantern light.

Ming Hu. The leader of the Black Tiger Gang.

“Isn’t that the guy who owns the Jade Silk Pavilion?” Kayson whispered. “What’s he doing in the palace?”

Luke raised a hand, silencing him. His mind raced.

He hadn’t accounted for this.

Ming Hu was unpredictable—powerful, sharp, and utterly unbound by loyalty. Just thinking about the raw strength the man possessed was enough to stir unease. And worse, Luke had no idea whose side Ming would choose in the chaos that was about to erupt.

But after a moment, Luke exhaled sharply through his nose.

He did know.

He’ll choose the winning side.

Suppressing the urge to curse aloud, Luke watched as Ming Hu and a few other officials exchanged pleasantries—shallow, meaningless words. Moments later, someone emerged from within the court chamber and gestured them inside.

One by one, they passed through the doors.

They shut behind them with a quiet thud.

Luke waited a few heartbeats before surging forward, unwilling to waste any more time.

Damn it. Things just got complicated.

A storm of thoughts churned through his mind as he jogged through the halls, Kayson close behind. The Loyalist faction was already fighting uphill—but with Ming Hu added to the equation, the balance tipped even further against them.

He forced the thoughts aside as he rounded several corners and reached his destination.

The alchemy lab.

Without hesitation, Luke pushed inside, his gaze sweeping across the well-maintained workbenches. Clay pots still sat where they’d been left, filled with raw materials, though the room felt noticeably emptier than before—at least in variety.

Medicinal herbs were conspicuously absent.

In their place were stacks of fresh wood, charcoal, and large containers of fine white powder. Luke needed only a glance to understand.

They’ve devoted everything to gunpowder.

A nod of satisfaction followed—though it was tinged with something heavier. Regret, perhaps. This had always been part of the plan, but the knowledge that he was the one who had introduced gunpowder into this world still left a faint ache in his chest.

“Luke…” Kayson spoke from behind him, his voice lowered. “What are we going to do about that big bastard? He wasn’t part of your plan.”

“Plans change,” Luke replied absently. “Help me look for finished gunpowder. Better yet—completed thunder bombs.”

The lab was clearly organized for mass production. Luke moved from station to station—the saltpeter drying racks, the mixing tables—until his eyes landed on a pile of empty clay spheres stacked neatly against the far wall.

His pulse quickened.

Moments later, he found a wooden crate tucked beneath a workbench. Inside were finished thunder bombs, each fitted with an oiled rope fuse and sealed with wax.

Yet instead of relief, a frown creased his brow.

“There’s only this many?” he muttered.

It had been some time since he’d last visited the lab—more than enough for production to ramp up. And yet…

Then realization struck.

Luke remembered.

He had deliberately given the Royal Alchemist incorrect ratios. He’d omitted proper storage methods. When progress stalled, he’d even shifted the blame onto the apprentices, using their supposed incompetence as a convenient excuse to withhold the true formula.

As long as these are the only ones they’ve made…

Turning to Kayson, he was about to instruct him to grab the crate—

When he noticed a figure frozen in the doorway.

Wide-eyed. Pale.

“W-what are you doing here?” the man stammered, fear thick in his voice.

Luke recognized him instantly.

One of the royal alchemist’s assistants. The very apprentice he’d scapegoated when the recipe failed.

“Wait—”

The word barely left Luke’s mouth.

Steel rang.

The assistant collapsed without a sound, his head rolling across the stone floor, eyes still wide in disbelief.

Kayson lowered his sword slowly.

“Oops,” he said sheepishly. “Sorry…”
