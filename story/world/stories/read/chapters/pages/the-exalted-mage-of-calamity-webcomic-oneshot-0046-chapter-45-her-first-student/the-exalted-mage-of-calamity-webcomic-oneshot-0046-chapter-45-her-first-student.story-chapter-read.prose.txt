The air’s chilly breeze moved gently over the stream outside, cool against Veronica’s skin as it passed. The cave had been stifling, thick with heat and tension. Even though she didn’t exert herself too hard, the whole situation left a sheen of sweat along her neck and shoulders.

She would definitely need a bath upon her return to Ronswick.

Gwen meanwhile was knelt by the water’s edge as the current flowed past the rocks. The tips of her sleeves were damp as she dipped her hands in. She scrubbed carefully between her fingers, then splashed water on her face, washing away the blood left from her scuffle with Lorence. She watched the pink ribbons, streaks of red brightened by the reflection of the water and sunlight, drift downstream before fading to nothing.

Deeper within the cave, Rocky slept heavily, a slow, rumbling snore echoing inside the tunnel. Veronica wasn’t entirely convinced that the creature could even fit through the entrance to leave. Gwen insisted that he could leave—but only if the crystal spires on his back were shed first. In a few more years, though, his body might grow too large for that to stay true.

“I should be good to go,” Gwen said at last, rising to her feet. She left the bank and joined Veronica by her side.

Veronica turned to face her. “Have you thought about my offer?”

Gwen pursed her lips, rubbing her still wet hands along her blouse. “You said… you could guarantee the mage tower could place him under their protection?”

Veronica shook her head lightly. “No. I didn’t say the mage tower would. I said the mage tower higher-ups could.”

Gwen blinked, tilting her head ever so slightly. “The higher-ups?”

“Those directly from the mage association.”

The disbelief on Gwen’s face was immediate, nearly reflexive. “Are you… are you like the daughter of an important person or something? Or a noble?”

A small chuckle slipped from Veronica’s mouth. “No. But I’ll be someone they can’t refuse listening to very soon. But for now, we can visit the mage tower in Ronswick, and I’m sure I can get the tower head there to oversee him for the time being. They owe me a bit of a favor.”

Gwen clenched her fingers. “Then… what about the guild? Even though what I said about Rocky defending himself was true, he—he still killed those adventurers.”

Whether that had truly been an act of self-defense, and not a command to murder, Veronica couldn’t be sure. Perhaps it was accurate, or perhaps it was her desperate attempt to keep protecting Rocky.

In the end, it didn’t matter much.

“I’ll report that I encountered some kind of mutated bear-like monster,” Veronica said evenly. “I’ll claim I fought, but collapsed the cave entrance in the process. They won’t be pleased about the lost equipment or missing identification for their dead adventurers, but there’s no reasonable way to retrieve anything from a melted cavern anyway. At least this way, they can’t verify things, unless they want to dig up an entire cliff side’s worth of rubble.”

Gwen considered that, relief flickering faintly across her face—until realization caught up to her. She opened her mouth to speak.

Veronica cut her off. “That means you’ll need to wake Rocky and move him somewhere else while we’re at the tower. At least, temporarily until they can arrange someone to look after him.”

Gwen hesitated, then looked up. “And if I refuse? If I don’t want to become your student?”

Veronica shrugged. “I’m not going to expose you or Rocky, if that’s what you’re thinking. As long as I get my guild reward, I’m happy. That’s all I came out here for after all. That Lorence guy having a binding ring was also something I couldn’t particularly look past.”

She turned from Gwen and looked over at the cave entrance. It was dark inside, and unless one stood directly in the tunnel, none could hear the loud snores that echoed within.

“If you refuse, we’ll part ways right here. But I’ll still have to collapse the cave for the guild to believe me, since I’ll be coming back empty-handed. That means you’ll need to wake Rocky and hide him somewhere else regardless. I already saved you from the Lorence guy, but that doesn’t mean I’ll protect you and cover up any crimes he, you, or Rocky committed for free. From here on, you’ll be on your own.”

Silence settled between them; there was thick tension, but not hostile. Gwen’s expression tightened as she weighed the offer, turning it over piece by piece in her mind.

When she finally spoke, it wasn’t to refuse.

“By… student,” she asked carefully, “what exactly would I have to do?”

“You’d travel with me,” Veronica replied. “Join me on my… adventures, essentially. I’d teach you magic—properly. Advanced methods of controlling mana. Spell formation. Everything I know. I won’t be staying in Ronswick long, so that means you’d need to leave with me.”

Gwen blinked. “Travel? Where are you going?”

“Annesheim.”

“The capital?” Surprise slipped into her voice.

Veronica nodded. “I intend to become a professor at Valeborn Academy.”

“A… professor?” Gwen’s eyes drifted over her again, doubt creeping in. “Not a student?”

“I’m only Tier-3 right now,” Veronica said evenly. “But it took me a single month to reach it.”

Gwen started to respond. “That’s still really impressive, but becoming a professor is—”

“I wasn’t finished,” Veronica cut in, a faint smirk tugging at her lips. “One month. From Tier-0.”

Gwen froze. The words clearly took a moment to settle.

“From… you weren’t even attuned?” she breathed. “It took me years just to reach Tier-2.”

Veronica folded her arms, satisfaction flickering across her face. “If you become my student, I guarantee you’ll reach at least Tier-7 in your lifetime. And I don’t mean reaching it the moment you’re on your deathbed, either. You’ll have plenty of time to enjoy yourself.”

“A Tier-7… that’s…”

Gwen heavily pondered it. The words seemed ludicrous, and yet, Veronica’s confidence was bleeding off her skin and infecting her like a disease.

Of course, having anyone guarantee such a thing with such fervor would get one excited. Veronica could probably elevate and help her become a Tier-9 mage, but that seemed a bit too unbelievable. So she tempered the girl’s expectations.

“Can… can I have some time to think about this?” Gwen asked.

Veronica exhaled softly through her nose. She’d expected that. “Of course. You’ll want to speak with your parents—anyone important to you in Ronswick. When we return, you can come with me to the mage tower. You’ll see for yourself whether they accept my request.”

Her eyes flicked briefly toward the cave entrance.

“You have a couple of days. I already accepted the guild’s request, so no one else should come investigating right away. But after that, the guild might repost the commission. I’m not sure on the exact logistics behind it, but they won’t allow me to monopolize this quest forever.”

“I… alright,” Gwen said, still uncertain. “I’ll go with you to the mage tower. To see if what you said is true.”

Veronica smiled faintly. “Good. Then for now, let’s head back together. I do need to make a quick stop on the way.”

Gwen tilted her head. “A stop?”

“Just a small detour to kill some goblins. Shouldn’t take long,” Veronica said.

“Is it really alright to leave all of this stuff with me?” Gwen asked.

In her hands was a small sack, holding half of the belongings Lorence had on him, fairly split between her and Veronica.

He had carried six artifacts in total. One had been the binding ring he’d used to control her. Another was the ring that allowed him to cast crude Tier-1 spells—the same one he’d used to blast the first crystal from Rocky’s back.

The man had become rich off the mana crystals he had been harvesting. Although now, whoever his buyer was—they’d likely get suspicious that he was gone. But a person like Lorence—there was no way someone like that would tell anyone his source of the mana crystals. His death was practically a closed case.

All that was left was the curse binding Gwen.

Veronica had destroyed the binding ring without hesitation. The moment it shattered, the dark markings along Gwen’s skin faded like ink washed away by water, leaving her clear and unblemished once more. That left five items remaining. Gwen kept two; Veronica took three.

Of course, Veronica had insisted Gwen keep the spell ring. She had no need for something so basic.

“Keep them,” Veronica said. “You’ve been through enough. Sell them if you need the money.”

Gwen didn’t protest when Veronica claimed the others. After all, Lorence would still be alive if not for her.

They walked side by side along the forest path back toward Ronswick. The sun hung low but not yet fading, painting the world in the warm gold of late afternoon; evening was approaching soon.

“Hey… can I ask you something, Veronica?”

“What is it?”

Gwen hesitated, fingers tightening slightly around the sack. “Why are you interested in taking me as a student? I’m only a second-tier mage and I’m already seventeen years old. There have to be plenty of people more talented than me.”

“There are,” Veronica said easily.

Gwen’s shoulders sank.

Veronica let out a quiet laugh. “I’m joking. They’re only ahead of you right now. You have something they don’t.”

Gwen blinked. “I do?”

“You can convert ambient mana into usable mana, can’t you?”

“Yeah,” Gwen said. “It’s just a basic technique some mages learn. It helps when we run out.”

Veronica nodded as they moved through the trees. The forests around Ronswick were open and breathable, nothing like the dense, tangled woods near Greystone. Here, light filtered cleanly through the canopy, and the path ahead was always visible.

“That’s exactly why I want you as a student,” Veronica continued. “For most mages, converting ambient mana is slow. At Tier-2, it would take me close to half an hour to refill myself if I were completely drained. But you… you replenished your reserves five times over in less time than that.”

Gwen fell quiet, absorbing the words. “That’s… that’s really that impressive? I’m… that good?”

“Better than you realize,” Veronica said. “That’s why I want you as a student.”

“I see…” she said quietly.

On the way, Gwen explained a little bit more about herself.

Apparently, she was relatively poor. She worked part-time as a waitress at a restaurant in Ronswick. Although she was a mage, learning magic was something that happened by chance. She had been one of those kids who stumbled upon feeling their mana. From there, she spent money on basic magic books, studying until she became a real tiered mage.

With that being the case, she had no mentor. No teacher to let her know how well she was doing. This, of course, meant she had no idea how quickly regular people converted ambient mana.

Veronica suspected it must have been partly due to staying inside a cave with crystals filled with ambient mana. She spent so much time in there, trapped under the influence of Lorence, that she naturally absorbed unrefined mana herself, in need of healing Rocky.

The fact she made it this far with no teacher, and still had the will to fight—that was something Veronica appreciated.

Both of them continued on. Instead of heading directly east, back to Ronswick, they traveled northeast, toward Veronica’s first commission. The one handed to her by the two suspicious merchants at the gate.

Of course, she knew it was likely a trap or some type of trick. However, Veronica was also the viscount’s guest. Leopold knew of her capabilities; if she died doing such a simple request, things would not turn out well for the merchants.

Nevertheless, those two were currently in Ronswick. As long as Veronica could expose the trap, she was sure there’d be some type of compensation awaiting her. Either the real loot that those two were hiding, or a reward—one of apologies and grace from the viscount for allowing such a situation to happen. She would just need to push for it—just like she did for the Kassal oil.

If it was truly an encampment of goblins, or even a camp of ogres, she could handle it. The surrounding trees, however, would probably have some complaints.

Before long, Veronica slowed, her eyes spying past the trees on broken shapes scattered across the ground.

“Is that it?” Gwen asked quietly.

They moved closer, and Veronica no longer needed to question it.

A wagon lay overturned in a small clearing, one wheel splintered and half torn from its axle. Crates had burst open on impact, sacks ripped and spilling grain into the dirt. Cloth bundles lay trampled, and shards of pottery glinted faintly in the fading light.

But the most striking sight was the goblins.

Several green bodies were strewn across the clearing in loose heaps, darkened with drying blood. Crude weapons lay beside limp fingers. Some bore clean stab wounds. Others had lost their heads entirely. Whoever had fought here had done so efficiently.

“Looks like someone already cleared your commission,” Gwen said.

Veronica frowned. “Taking someone else’s commission is against guild rules, isn’t it?”

Gwen gave a small hum. “It depends. If someone knowingly steals a job, then yeah, that’s a problem. But if it happens by accident—like an adventurer being attacked and defending themselves—then it’s handled case by case.”

They stepped farther into the clearing, but before Veronica could examine the bodies more closely, her ears twitched.

Faint, but distinct. The sound of a struggle carried from deeper in the trees.

Her eyes sharpened.

“They’re still fighting,” she said.

“What? How do you—hey!”

Gwen’s protest was cut short as Veronica broke into a run, already moving toward the noise and whoever was interfering with her mission.

“That’s my commission reward,” she muttered under her breath, irritation edging her voice. “You assholes.”
