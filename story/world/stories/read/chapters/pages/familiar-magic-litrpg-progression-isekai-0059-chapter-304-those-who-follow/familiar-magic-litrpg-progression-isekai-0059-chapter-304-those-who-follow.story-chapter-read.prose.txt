Ethan’s eyes followed Savilar, lost knight of the Church, as he sailed out into the open air. There was no time to react to the unexpected sacrifice, or to consider whether the act was any sort of redemption, or even to ponder the mysterious last words.

He was “right”?

Ethan had to push all those thoughts away as he raced to the cliff’s edge, grabbing onto Valinor’s left arm, while Prince Calevaro seized his right.

“Savilar!” the shield knight bellowed. “Unhand me! That’s my brother! I must save my brother!”

Ethan opened his mouth to tell his friend that there was nothing he could do. That the two warring Champions below them would kill any Twilight foolish enough to jump down. But as they all stared out at the red and white demon, locked in a grapple with the Emperor, the words became unnecessary.

“No!” Valinor roared again, but the words were a denial, not a plea.

They all saw the Emperor’s arm shoot forward, claws extended. They all saw it burst through the demon’s chest, and the once-proud Savilar spasm, then go limp as the pair fell into the cloud of dust surrounding the two Champions.

“No…” Valinor said again, in barely more than a whisper. The big man collapsed to his knees, and seemed almost unaware of those around him. “I could have saved him,” he said. “He was coming back to me.”

Calevaro kneeled next to the knight, one hand on the man’s armored back. Ethan moved to do the same, but Dean caught his arm.

“It isn’t enough,” he said quietly.

Ethan glanced from his older brother to the madness at the bottom of the cliff. The scorpiursi had emerged from underground, leaving a large impression in the earth that the Champions' rolling, flailing battle had only deepened. The fall was hundreds of feet now, and two giants still fought wildly. But…

“It isn’t enough,” Ethan agreed. “Not to kill a Master.”

The Emperor had disappeared, but this close, Ethan could still feel him. The corruption the creature had gathered made him shine like a beacon. He still lived, and would likely escape, given time.

“What do we do?” Dean asked. “I…I don’t think we can go down after him.”

“We can’t,” Ethan agreed.

He looked down at the battle, where pincers as large as houses, and tentacles as big around as trains crashed into one another. The sound of it all was like every animal in the world fighting at once.

“There’s another way,” Ethan said. Then he closed his eyes, and reached out to Apollo. His dimensional twin understood the request, and quickly complied.

When Ethan opened his eyes once more, he wasn’t a man on a cliff, he was a serpent the size of a skyscraper, looking down at the most horrific creature that ever walked this terrible world.

Ethan’s mind rebelled for a moment at the sudden rush of sensations, and the strangeness of inhabiting a body that simultaneously lacked arms and legs, yet possessed hundreds of flailing tentacles.

But [Mind of the Devourer] had prepared him well. He could split his attention in a dozen different directions without much trouble, and did so now. First he charted where the enemy’s pincers were, as well as its surprisingly less-dangerous mouth, and finally that terrible stinger, hovering above him.

Ethan understood immediately that the stinger was the real threat. Slightly smaller than the pincers, it held a bladed tip like an inverted radio tower, dripping ominously with a smoking liquid somewhere between poison and acid. While the serpent’s body had largely coiled around the creature to hold its legs in place, and reduce its mobility, nearly all of its tentacles were preventing that stinger from coming down.

Ethan turned his attention to the Emperor. Savilar had given them this chance, and it wouldn’t go to waste.

The Follower was challenging to pin down, both because of his relative size, and the fact that Ethan was actually sensing him from his own position, not that of the serpent’s. But eventually his Champion’s keen eyes spotted the Emperor, like a small black speck, dancing among the scorpiursi’s legs.

Ethan felt relief at confirming the Follower hadn’t managed to escape. Somewhat fittingly, the Emperor was having a difficult time dodging away from many, many arachnoid legs clawing at the ground. But he’d nearly managed it, and was now madly scrambling up the sheer cliff to safety, opposite to where Ethan and the others waited.

That wouldn’t do.

With a sudden heave, the serpent forced the two Champions into a slight roll, crashing into that side of the pit, and knocking the Emperor back down. That left the two giant bodies crushed up against the wall, and forced the Follower to actually climb the demons themselves to escape.

A moment later, Ethan spotted the black speck once again, and knew what he had to do.

Emperor Gallow would not die like this!

He’d been caught unaware by that filthy demon, and dragged into this deathtrap of a pit, but he refused to let it end like this. When he claimed The Throne, the first thing he would do was bring that demon back to life, just so Gallow could feel his hand tear through the filthy thing’s body once more.

But later. Sweet revenge would come later. Now, survival.

He’d managed to hurl himself away from the worst of the dueling Champions by pushing off against the dead demon’s body. He’d hoped to use his might and speed to quickly scale the cliff, and be back fighting the Chosen as quickly as possible.

Unfortunately, the Champions’ remarkable size made it easy to underestimate their speed. Gallow landed below dozens of hairy, multi-jointed legs that shifted back and forth with disturbing alacrity. Several desperate leaps resulted in him being thrown back to the loose ground.

Then, just when Gallow had managed to scramble up the cliff-side, forced to behave like an insect, the battle below shifted, and the two Champions crashed into the wall beneath him. He fell, the soil he’d been gripping betraying him, and Gallow was back where he’d started–nearly.

He managed to land on one of those grotesque legs, then began leaping from one to another, trying to climb the Champions themselves to get out. His own speed was far from negligible, and he embraced the feeling of being an insect among giants, shooting from one point to another, always moving.

The serpent kept coiling more tightly as Gallow moved, forcing him one way, then another. He considered taking direct control of the scorpiursi, but Bek was likely doing better than Gallow could, under the circumstances. And his concentration was needed for this desperate game of survival.

As he moved, his eyes kept going up to the serpent’s great head, poised over the battle and looming. Those immense teeth glinted even in Potentia’s dim sunlight, and Gallow couldn’t help but picture them clamping down on him, and putting an end to thousands of years of life in an instant.

The Emperor of The People couldn’t let that happen. He needed to keep moving, to stay ahead of those twisting coils of serpent, and waving pincers. Yet as he seemed to get closer to safety, racing toward the back of the fight, it became harder to see.

He was surrounded by a forest of tentacles, stretched and taught all around him. He couldn’t make sense of what he was seeing from this perspective, but he knew they belonged to the serpent, and was surprised they were reaching out for him.

Gallow just needed to move. He could see safety now, just beyond the strange lines of stretched, aquatic flesh. Between the bladed suction cups and through the cloud of dust, the cliff awaited him. He had an even shorter distance to climb now, thanks to the boost provided by Champions.

It was odd, being in that position. The creatures were so large, and so intertwined, that he could barely make sense of where he was. The hard carapace beneath his feet suggested he was on the scorpiursi’s back somewhere, and he thought he was running toward its side. But between the dust, and the flailing, and so much being obscured by the serpent, he couldn’t be sure.

All he knew for certain was that escape was just ahead, and Gallow put everything he had into the mad rush forward. As such, he barely managed to skid to a halt as a tentacle slammed down before him.

He made to leap over it, only for a second one to layer over the first, obscuring his path. Confused, Gallow dashed in another direction, only for more tentacles to drop from above. He didn’t think those limbs could crush him–even with a Champion’s strength, they were still rubbery–but they could block him with annoying effectiveness.

He dodged again, turned again, ran again. But the tentacles kept crashing down. That whole forest he’d been trapped in was falling, and leaving Gallow in a smaller and smaller area. He’d have to climb one, and escape. He just needed to–

Curiosity suddenly struck him, and Gallow looked up. Before, there’d been a canopy of stretched out and overlapping tentacles, but now many of them had released, and he could finally see what was above him.

Time seemed to slow as he saw what was poised in the air, and what those tentacles had been holding back. They’d been taught for a reason, as the scorpiursi had obviously been fighting back. Its greatest weapon was being denied to it, after all.

Above Gallow–directly above–was the demon’s immense stinger.

The truth became obvious as that bladed, burning edge dropped with impossible speed and unstoppable force. He’d been lured here. And he was trapped here, with tentacles on every side.

His ancient mind raced, looking for any way out. This wasn’t how it ended. This couldn’t be how it ended. So many years, so many lifetimes, so many sacrifices, so many worlds and lives lost. The People had to rise again. They needed their revenge, they needed to wipe the three scourges from existence, and Gallow would be their instrument! He would rise from this and there would be–

The stinger crashed down, piercing through the Emperor’s flesh, and even into the body of the scorpiursi beneath, interrupting one final, fitting thought.

–justice!
