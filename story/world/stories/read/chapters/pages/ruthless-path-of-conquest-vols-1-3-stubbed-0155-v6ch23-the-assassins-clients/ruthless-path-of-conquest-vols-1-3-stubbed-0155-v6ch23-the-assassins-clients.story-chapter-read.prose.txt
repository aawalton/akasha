James and Zhang Chāo made their way back in the general direction of their combined party. Both the Orlando group and the Taiwanese delegation were gathered in the same place, after having split up and reassembled.

It was James’s hope that someone would have found out something about the people who wanted to have him killed, especially after he had passed Mina the note that Zhang Chāo had given him. If his council members had failed to discover the intelligence that he needed, James would have to find someone who was neighbors with the would-be killers to give him the information he needed. Or he would just confront the enemies directly.

On the way back to the group, however, James and Zhang Chāo ran into yet more Rulers who needed just a few minutes of the Fisher Emperor’s time.

It was significantly harder to excuse themselves from these conversations than it had been just ten minutes before.

That was mainly because an increasing number of those who approached James now were the nonhuman Rulers, who seemed to have decided that James was the safe human to speak with after seeing his speech and observing how he interacted with fellow humans.

James was a bit nervous about causing an interspecies incident by being seen as snubbing the nonhumans, considering that they made up the vast majority of Rulers at the Summit.

The first couple to approach him were an Ogre and a Lizardfolk man, both of whom James quickly picked up had been human before the System.

“You’d never guess how hard it is to rule over people who distrust you on sight, man,” said Simon the Lizardfolk, flicking his tongue out as he spoke. He almost lisped, his strange tongue altering his vocal patterns.

“My sympathies,” James said. “I can’t imagine what it’s like to have people look at you and see an animal or a monster.”

Thank the gods that I didn’t choose those Race Evolution paths, James thought.

It would have especially bothered him to have Mina see him differently, but it was likely that many of his political achievements would have been impossible if he hadn’t had a reasonably appealing and approachable face.

He managed to excuse himself from this conversation easily enough, though. It was the next several Rulers, who James wasn’t certain had ever been human, who he took pains to be especially polite and attentive towards.

“It’s a pleasure to meet you, Mister—”

There was a little growl.

“Ah, I’m sorry, Miss Naked Mole Rat President. I had a flawed human assumption there. Almost all of our Rulers, as you can see by looking around, are male.”

“Wait, you’re males?!” the giant, beige-colored rodent said. She blinked her tiny, beady eyes rapidly down at James and Zhang Chāo as if she was trying to see them better. “If you are truly males, why can’t I smell your musk? Or see your external genitalia? I know my vision is not the best, but… listen, there is no shame in being female, even if your species has the strange convention of males being the leaders.”

“We’re wearing clothing,” Zhang Chāo said, visibly struggling to maintain a straight face. “It’s a funny human thing, like having a second skin on top of our skin. Sometimes a third skin. I would describe it as a manifestation of our facility with technology. Neither of us would be ashamed of being female… I don’t think.”

He gave James a look that said, Please, we need to end this conversation before I start laughing. I can’t be held responsible if this lasts much longer.

“Right, I have heard that humans are very fond of their gadgets and gizmos,” the President said. “That ‘clothing,’ as you call it, looks most uncomfortable, though. Are all of the humans around here wearing clothing? Is that why you are all so different in coloration? I cannot imagine how that would feel—”

“Well, I will see if one of the tailors from my homeland can make you something if you’re interested in trying it, Madam President,” Zhang Chāo said. “We really must be going now. James has to, um…”

“I need to figure out who wants to kill me,” James said bluntly.

“Oh, my, that is a serious matter!” she exclaimed.

“Yes, I’m trying not to be rude to any Rulers who approach me, but I’m afraid it is a matter of life and death,” James said. He took her paw in his, made eye contact as best he could with her strange little eyes, and gave her a close-mouthed smile. He wasn’t sure if her species would interpret showing teeth as a sign of aggression.

“It has been a pleasure, Fisher Emperor!” the mole rat declared. “Slay them dead!”

“Oh, I intend to.”

As they walked away, Zhang Chāo mouthed a “Thank you” to James, to which he responded with a nod.

There were several interactions like this, though most of them were slightly shorter and less absurd.

There were also those nonhuman Rulers who felt in over their heads and approached James with a frank request to come under his authority and bring their subjects into his territory.

Two different Races of toad or frog-like monsters, a caiman—he had spoken to Samuel about James—a Dire Hedgehog, the kappa, and some small blue people—they said they knew Mina and Yulia—all wanted to feel James out to determine whether his Fisher Empire would be a good place for their people to live.

Those conversations, he had to play carefully, attempting to be welcoming while also not drawing out the dialogue for too long. Attracting good citizens could be critical to the future of his young nation. This was really the sort of thing he had thought he was going to the World Leaders’ Summit for.

“You’re really not discriminating at all, are you?” Zhang Chāo commented after one such interaction had ended.

“I guess not?” James said. “What do you mean?”

“Well, those chupacabras that you just invited to stay with you are terrifying, from my understanding, to an entire cultural group within your homeland. Throughout the Americas, Hispanic peoples have reported such creatures killing their livestock. Since the System, these reports have substantially increased based on my intelligence… Had you never heard of these monsters?”

“Only vaguely,” James replied with a shrug.

You’re making me sound very irresponsible…

“I see,” said Zhang Chāo, furrowing his brow. “And… what will you do if your subjects report that the chupacabras are eating your livestock? I mean, the livestock that belong to your existing subjects… which are effectively yours to protect.”

“The same thing I would do if anyone else breaks my laws. Punish them. You are reminding me, I have to make sure to lay down the rules for any new arrivals upon my return. Right now, I have a system for enforcing the rules, but not so much for making sure people know what they are. So, if those chupacabra people arrived while I was absent from my territory and started eating people or stealing their livestock or something, they might wind up being swallowed up by the Earth without ever getting a warning first about what the laws are.”

Zhang Chāo looked as if he was trying to figure out if James was joking or not.

But the Fisher Emperor kept moving without offering any clarification.

Finally, they reached the party.

“Has anyone gotten me anything usable on the people I wanted to know about?” James asked.

“I have all the answers,” Rotter said instantly.

“We found some information together, yes,” Alan said, raising an eyebrow.

Rotter grew slightly red.

“This isn’t about credit,” James said without batting an eye. “One of you, start counseling me on this matter, please.”

“This appears to be a dangerous cult after you, Your Majesty,” Rotter said.

“Based out of the Middle East,” Alan added.

“They worship the God of the Old Testament, in some sort of strange fusion faith mixing traits of all three Abrahamic religions along with some new doctrines of their own.”

“And what are those?” James asked, frowning.

A fusion faith sounded disturbingly familiar.

“We really don’t know much,” Alan said. “Except that they want to create the Kingdom of Heaven on Earth. Which would seem to outright contradict most people’s interpretations of the Bible.”

“Unless you see everything that’s happening now as the Rapture, maybe?” Mitzi suggested.

“I’m not religious,” James said, waving off the attempts to try to understand the group’s religious views. “But I get it well enough. Religious authorities always, throughout history, try to extend their power into the political domain. Really, it’s not that different from any other interest group.”

I recall the Ancient Romans had the same sort of issue when they tried to run things in Israel. Religious authorities that interfered with their control. They ended up nailing a certain very charismatic speaker to a cross, trying to make an example of him, and then they had no end of problems.

James was thinking of Ancient Rome more and more often now that he was a Ruler.

“Do you have any idea why they’re targeting James specifically?” asked Mina.

“I think I have a guess,” James said quietly.

And if I’m right, they’ll end up targeting you, too, he thought.

A vision played out before his mind, as fresh and vivid as if it had been yesterday.

A mass of thousands of people entered his territory from several directions, all brandishing weapons and moving with a sense of urgency. Heading toward Mina and a little girl she had with her. A girl who James had assumed was his daughter. Now that Mina was pregnant again, that seemed like a very justified assumption.

By sheer weight of numbers and fanatical devotion to their cause, they smashed their way through James’s defenders and painted his city red with the blood of James’s people. The child Mina had with her had been hidden from their aggression, but they had cornered James’s wife.

Three male figures clutching long daggers. Their faces had been hidden from James by the vision, but he had seen that they wore a strange amalgamation of religious symbols on their necklaces. A cross, a Star of David, and a crescent moon all tangled together.

Is that the same symbol that I saw on those people today?

He would have to approach more closely to know for certain.

And the vision had ended in violence.

Mina had remained brave and stoic to the last, simply told the men what the consequences of their actions would be: “My husband will kill you for this. You and your families. Your children and your children’s children. Your friends and anyone who knows your name.”

Then the men stabbed her to death, cut out her heart, and chopped off her head. It was the same sort of violence one would expect to employ when slaying a vampire or some such creature.

Or if they were holy men, killing a Witch.

“Thou shalt not suffer a witch to live.”

The Bible verse popped into his head unbidden.

The vision had ended with the murderers raising Mina’s severed head high for their whole army to see. Everyone cheered. Then they razed his city to the ground, just to finish matters off.

His child had been missing from the vision by the end. The little girl he and his wife were to have in the future. James had looked for her, he remembered, when he was looking over the vision. Just as he had looked for himself, wondering where he was in all this.

But maybe he wasn’t there, because they had already had him assassinated.

What was the nature of the grudge these fanatics bore against him and his people? What would he have to do so that his family could be left alone?

Fucking barbarians…

In the present, James blinked and found that all eyes were on him. He realized, almost as soon as his presence of mind returned, that he was clenching his hands into fists, so tightly that his nails were digging deeply into his palms. He must have worn quite a disturbing expression on his face. The members of his Council had gone pale or looked otherwise perturbed.

“Forgive me,” James said, forcing himself to smile. “I think I need to go and confront these people directly. Sitting back here, speculating about their motives is valuable in its own right. But I’ll never know for sure unless I go and talk to them. Maybe this is something that can be worked out peacefully.”

“There’s not much time left in the Summit,” Zhang Chāo said. “If we’re going to do it, best to do it soon.”
