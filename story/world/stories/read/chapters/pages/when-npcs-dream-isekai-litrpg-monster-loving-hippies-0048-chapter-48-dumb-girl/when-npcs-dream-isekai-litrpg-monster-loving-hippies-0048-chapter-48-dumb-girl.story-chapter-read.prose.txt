Hunger claws at me.I haven't eaten a proper meal in days.My body feels hollow. I crave a real meal.

I'm walking close to a bakery.The air strikes me—warm bread, sweet crusts. The scent lingers, wicked and divine. I nearly weep.Drool betrays me.

I probably look like the dumb girl that I am.

I have no money.I already begged once.Maybe they'll give me leftovers.

I step inside.The baker looks at me with an annoyed face.

“Bread or sweetrolls. Pick what you want. Pay the coin,” he says in that dead monotone voice.

“Oh merciful baker!” I begin. “I stand before you, weak with hunger!”

“…” He says nothing. Just keeps looking at me.

“Grant me but a crust, a crumb,” I continue. “A scrap unworthy of coin!”

“…”

I think... these robot people don’t understand me well.

“Leftovers… please…” I mumble.

“Hungry? I sell bread. Not charity.” He answers coldly, sweeping the floor like I’m just dust to be brushed away.

I drop to the floor, scrambling like an animal. I clutch the hem of his trousers tightly.

“Oh please!” I beg. “Save me from the tragic fate of starving at your very door!”

His face turns angry.I heard when that happens with robot people, it can turn dangerous.

I’m dumb, but not that dumb.I release his trouser and stumble out the door.

I’m back in the street. I’m hungry.The street stretches on—dark, empty, endless.I stumble, catching myself, but only just.

How long can I endure this cruel night?

“Shooya? Is that you?” a female voice calls. “Oh my god! You look terrible.”

Then I see her. Jeeraha.

I’m taken aback for a moment by her sheer beauty.

Her antennae shimmer with health, long and restless, but it’s the dress that draws every eye. A daring slip of deep blue clings to her form, cut low to bare the polished shine of her pronotum like a jewel on display. The fabric traces the ridges of her abdomen, splitting at the sides to tease glimpses of the plated segments beneath. Thin, silken, and bold—it leaves little to the imagination, yet frames her carapace and folded wings with elegance.

Where I come from, this dress would be scandalous. Even the subtle curve of a female’s lower abdominal plates is considered private.But to me—it was only beauty.

“Hungry…” I manage to say.

“Shooya, you dumb girl,” Jeeraha sighs. “Wait here.”

I wait.A few moments later, she returns with a loaf of bread, rustic and golden.

I gorge it in a moment.

“Mmmff… gmmfh fohh…” I say. “mmphh gmmfh liiff!Hrrf obrrflohh wff grrtttood!Shhll rmmmbrr yrr kndnfff frrvrr!”

“What?”

I swallow. “You gave me food… you gave me life!My heart overflows with gratitude!I shall remember your kindness forever!”

“Why are you always talking so weird?” Jeeraha asks.

“It’s… a… Theatrical Speech Disorder,” I mumble.TSD for short. That’s what the doctor told my parents, anyway.

Jeeraha laughs. “I think that’s bullshit. Anyway, why haven’t you gotten a job yet? NPCs pay enough for food and shelter. Most shops aren’t that hard.”

“I tried,” I mumble. “Too dumb…”

It was true. I tried clothier shops, bakeries, even candlemaking at the chandlery. But I made such poor-quality items, no one wanted to pay me.

“That’s nonsense,” Jeeraha says. “Most jobs here, you just practice for a few days, make something of medium quality—that’s good enough for the NPCs. You just need to keep trying. Don’t give up.”

I nod silently.

Jeeraha sighs again. “It’s getting late,” she says. “Where are you sleeping?”

I look miserably around the street.

“Look, sleep in my and Thelia’s room tonight, okay?” she says. “Tomorrow, try again with the work.”

I’m close to crying. Jeeraha is a real angel.

“I… don’t want,” I manage, “to cause trouble.”

“Nonsense,” she says. “I’ll talk to Thelia. She’ll understand.”

I walk with Jeeraha back to her small rented room, shared with Thelia. I’m very nervous.

The reason? I’m not very popular in town. And surprisingly, it’s not even because of my dumb personality.

“What the heck is she doing here?!” shouts Thelia as soon as we arrive.

I shrink, trying to be small. I twist my antennae together nervously.

“Look,” Jeeraha starts, “She doesn’t have anywhere to go. Nobody is helping her. It’s not her fault being born to… such a family. And besides, we’re in this world now. We can leave all that behind us.”

“Like heck we could,” Thelia snaps. “Just because we’re here doesn’t mean anything. Millions are still suffering in that wretched empire! Her parents are probably commanding atrocities as we speak!”

“But she has nothing to do with that!” Jeeraha argues.

Silently, I step out the door and back into the street.I don’t want to make trouble for Jeeraha, the beautiful angel who gave me food.

I keep walking.From outside, I can still hear them arguing, their voices rising and falling, through the thin wooden walls.

Sleeping on the street isn’t that bad.I know a great place with soft hay and shelter from the wind.

Still, hating me because of my family is silly, I think to myself.There are better reasons to dislike me—like the fact that I’m useless, dumb, and take up space.

And it’s not like I personally crowned the Zorthican emperor. I’ve done basically nothing my whole life.In fact, my parents used to say I was an embarrassment to the entire empire.

So by that logic, empire-haters should like me.

Life’s unfair like that.

I get to the old storage cabin behind some farm shop—unlocked, as usual.I arrange a straw pile to resemble a bed.

I think about Jeeraha.Probably one of the few people here who truly doesn’t give a crap about the old world.And definitely the most beautiful in our little town.

I lay down on the straw pile, remembering that slick pronotum.I wouldn’t terribly mind tangling my antennae with hers.Maybe just a casual brush by accident.Or a small rub against those soft hidden hind wings…

Then the straw starts prickling again, interrupting my nice thoughts.

I roll around. The straw finds its way between my carapace plates, poking past my tegmina and into my soft hind wings.

Sleeping on straw piles sucks.It gets into every joint, every plate, every sensitive crevice.I wouldn’t terribly mind having a real sheet right now.

Or my old room bed.

A monstrous furniture article that was—towering canopies, carved mahogany posts, twelve pillows of various softness, a feather-stuffed mattress and lace curtains.

Also, a breakfast served on a silver tray by the maid. That wasn’t awful either.

And... my maid Hanaza.She was probably the only person who truly liked me, back home.The only one I really miss.

Thinking about her makes me cry a little.

I drift to sleep, hoping tomorrow might be a better day.

Not really believing it.

I wake up and start pulling straw from places in me I didn’t even know existed.I don’t have a mirror here. Probably for the best.

I’m hungry again.

Damn. Why am I always hungry?

Maybe I should give the chandlery another try. The shop owner wasn’t really mad at me.And I have a feeling that, with a few more attempts, I might be able to make candles that at least resemble… well, candles.

If there’s any job even I can do, it’s probably candlemaking.

I walk toward the shops at the edge of town, near the beginning of a small forest.

Then I see them—two guys leading a strange, bear-like creature.

It has a segmented carapace along its back but a soft, furry belly beneath, its wide, fearful eyes darting between them.

The poor thing is bound and injured, struggling weakly against the ropes.

I recognize the guys.

One is dark brown carapace—Linguth, I think.The other is a tall, ugly one. Forgot his name.They’re not nice people. And I don’t mean just to me—in general.

I know what this is.They’re going to sell it. The buyer will kill it and gain XP. Profitable business.

I really love animals.Always have, as far back as I remember.It’s hard for me to see one hurt, being led to slaughter.

This isn’t my business. I know that.And anyway, if I stick my nose in, I’ll probably just get bitten.

But even so, I find myself standing, dumbly, in front of the two men and the injured creature.

“What!?” Linguth says, surprised to see me.

“It’s just Shooya,” the ugly one says. “The blue blood. The empire dog.”

“Ah… m-might I trouble you with a thought?” I plead. “Can you not see its pain? Its terror? Such a noble creature… bound so harshly. Unbind it. Let it go free—have mercy, I beg you!”

“The fuck?” says the ugly guy. “What is she on about?”

“It’s our monster. Our business,” Linguth says, turning to me with a slow, cold tone. “You need to go now, dumb empire girl. Before you get hurt.”

I know he’s right. That would be the smart thing to do.But I’m a dumb girl.

“No…” I mumble. “P… Please… don’t hurt it…”

Then I get a sharp pain in my abdominal carapace.Not broken, but close.

The ugly guy kicked me.

I collapse to the ground.

Another kick.

My front wing bends at an unnatural angle. It hurts—a lot.

“I’ve wanted to do this since she got here,” he says, delivering another nasty kick.

Linguth catches my eyes, gives a small smile, and pulls out a long, twisted knife.

We’re at the edge of town. No one’s here to see. Not that anyone would care.

“Killing a player gives XP too, right?” Linguth asks, looking around.

“No… oh god,” I beg. “please no!”

“It may be better this way,” he says softly. “No one here will really miss you.”

It might be true.But still, I don’t want to die.

I keep thinking—maybe things could get better. Like Jeeraha said.Maybe I could find a job. Get food.Maybe even friends.Maybe even Jeeraha could be my friend.

I’m a hopeless optimist like that.

But what can I do, really?

Oh, did I forget to mention?In addition to being dumb and useless... I’m also very weak.

So, this is probably the end.

I close my eyes. I hate the sight of blood—my own included.

Then I hear a loud THUD.

I crack one eye open and see Linguth fly ten meters through the air and crash painfully into the ground.

What hit him makes my eyes go wide.A huge, horned, furry snake.

And there are three more of them!We didn’t even see them coming—they surround us.

Another THUD!The tall ugly guy goes down next, sprawling on the dirt with a stunned face.

I close my eyes again, guessing it’s my turn.

But… nothing happens.

I open them.

And I see something else.

A winged pink fluffball.Hovering in the air.It has tail, large yellow eyes and a sharp-toothed smile.

Creepy as hell.

This world has some weird creatures.But this one? This one is something else entirely.Like a toddler drew it and it came to life.

Then… it talks to me.

“My name is Uul,” it says in a soft, melodic tone. “It’s lovely to make your acquaintance. And what is your name?”

“Uh… I think… it’s Sh… Shooya.”My carapace plates shift with unease. I’m terrified.

“You think it’s Shooya?” Uul says pleasantly. “Well, your guess is better than mine. We’ll go with that.”

“O-okay…”

“I can’t control arthropod creatures like your species,” he says softly. “But nonetheless, you’re going to help me, Shooya.”

“I… am?”

“Yes,” Uul says, voice calm. “I was sent here by my master from a continent far beyond the endless desert. I must reach the communities of intelligent endoskeletal vertebrate builders species. They will craft airships for my masters, so they may cross the scorched desert and come to this land.”

“Oh…” I mumble, trembling. “Why… why do your masters… want to come?”

Uul looks around for a moment, then lowers his voice.“You must see it already. This land is ruled by chaos. People—even you—are suffering.My masters will bring peace, order, and prosperity to this continent.”

Peace. Order. Prosperity.

Oh, I know that one!I was an empire girl, after all.When someone says that…they mean something else entirely.

They mean conquest.
