Descending the stairs out of Curtis's building, Luke started the arduous process of finding a guildmaster, trailing behind Hannah. The others had opted to stay behind in the apartment.

Luke: "Hey. Remember when you said we should start a guild? Well, we're doing it. Get your ass to New York and be the guildmistress."

Milla: "Luke. I JUST moved into a new apartment. Thanks for not helping me move, by the way."

Luke: "Ah... I got busy trying to rescue my almost-girlfriend from a psycho, along with a bunch of other stuff. But, you know, I'm sorry."

Milla: "Apology accepted."

Luke: "So when are you getting here?"

Luke: "Bring the girl."

Milla: "Luke..."

Luke: "What?"

It was silent for a while, and Hannah looked up at him from a little farther down the stairs. "Any luck?"

"I don't know," Luke said. "Hold on."

Luke: "Milla?"

Milla: "Talking it over with Lana."

Milla: "Who you would've met if you'd helped us move, by the way."

Luke: "I said I was sorry."

"I'll make some calls. And by that I mean send a bunch of messages."

"Have them message Milla to ask about joining."

He stopped for a brief moment, then nodded. "Yeah, of course. Now let's see this shelter of yours."

The air felt crisp, and the sky was overcast, like it was about to rain. As before, the streets were empty.

"Right here?" Hannah asked.

"Why not? It's not like there are any cars."

Hannah leaned forward to check the street, then shook her head and pointed right. "Let's find a spot. Just because there aren't any right now, doesn't mean one won't show up."

"Sure," he said with a shrug.

As they walked down the sidewalk, a roaring sound reached down from above, and when Luke looked up, he almost froze. A streak of fire trailed someone flying up there, just above the top of the buildings. It took him a moment to gather himself and start walking again. The Solarian was dead. He'd fed Leslie's Meta-Heart to Smudge. There was no coming back from that. If this were another Solarian, it wasn't him.

"How about here?" Hannah asked, nodding toward an empty basketball court.

Luke gestured for her to go ahead. "Not much basketball playing going around. So, how does it work?"

"Not a clue," she answered. "Hold on."

A moment later, a perfect cube popped into existence right in front of her. The surface was rough, like concrete, without windows, and each side was about half again as long as Luke was tall. A slight indentation the size of a door stood out, but it wasn't a door, just a recessed part of the cube's wall.

"That'll be cramped," he noted.

She sent a smirk his way. "I'm sure it's bigger on the inside."

"How do you get inside?"

Hannah turned back to it, frowning. "Not sure."

Hand raised, she brought it forward to touch the recessed bit, and disappeared.

Hannah: "Whoa. I'm inside."

Luke stepped up to the cube and placed his hand on the same spot as hers, but nothing happened.

Luke: "Doesn't work for me."

Hannah: "Hold on."

Hannah: "Adding you to the whitelist."

Hannah: "Try now."

He did and blinked as it transported him inside, where he stumbled forward, right into Hannah, who stepped back with a yelp. It. Was. Cramped.

"Sorry!" Luke said. "Not bigger on the inside!"

You couldn’t even turn around in there. A raised stone area on the left had to be the bed, and on the opposite side, right in front of the door, was a small area with a toilet and shower combined. Hannah had fallen back to sit on top of the toilet, and she looked a little defeated.

"Won't be hosting parties in here," she said with a sigh.

"How do I get out?" Luke asked. "I'm getting claustrophobic."

She pointed. "Put your hand over there, I guess."

Turning, he found the same sort of recess on the inside. Luke put his hand on it and breathed a little easier when he reappeared outside. Hannah followed soon after, and the cube disappeared into thin air.

"How much was that one?" he asked.

"Almost five K," she muttered, shaking her head. "There are even cheaper ones."

"Don't want to know what those look like."

"No," she agreed, her frown turning into a slight smile that carried a tinge of sadness. "It'll be enough for me, at least. Enough for one."

"I guess," Luke said. "I'll see if I can get something a little bigger."

Hannah gave a slight shake of her head that he couldn't interpret before raising a valid point. "Don't waste too much. We'll need to save up for a guild, remember? Those aren't cheap. How much do you have?"

"Credits? Um." Luke checked his interface. "Almost fourteen thousand."

"We've got our work cut out for us."

"Sure do," Luke said. "Maybe we should get started."

She gave him a worried look. "Shouldn't you rest a little? You've been through a lot. And I'm not just talking about being blown up by a nuke."

Rather than outright dismiss her concern, Luke took a moment to consider. He had been through a lot and things had been happening at break neck speed ever since he touched the orb and became Integrated. One major event after another with little room for calm or to breathe. This might be the perfect opportunity to sit down for a little while and just relax. The thought stirred an uneasiness in him and drew the thoughts to Dot. Anxious thoughts that made his heart beat faster. No, it wasn't time to relax. Couldn't slow down just yet.

"I'm fine," Luke said, waving her worries away. "Just peachy. There's a dungeon opening up not too far from here soon. Let's check that one out."

"Just the four of us?"

They'd headed back toward the apartment building and spotted Curtis and Ray coming out of the door, looking around.

"Over here!" Luke shouted, and the two headed over.

"Curtis is leaving," Ray said when they met up.

"My brother messaged," Curtis said.

Luke narrowed his eyes. "That portal is ours."

That elicited a grin. "It's not."

"I'm sure we can all go together. Nine or six doesn't make much difference, does it?" Hannah asked.

Ray crossed his arms over his chest. "Three or nine does, though." After that, he grinned. "Guess we'll be meeting Curtis's brother sooner rather than later. Is he a lot like you?"

"No."

"How do we get there? Run?" Luke asked.

Curtis gestured across the street.

"Really?" Hannah asked.

Luke just sighed and started walking. A few minutes later, they raced down deserted streets on electric scooters, the wind blowing through their hair. The emptiness of the place still took some getting used to, but it let them get around at high speed without the risk of running into anyone. Curtis took the front and showed the way. Meanwhile, Luke perused the different shelter options.

Rough Shelter [Cost: 1000 Credits]: A roof over your head is sometimes all you can ask for.

Small Shelter [Cost: 2000 Credits]: Four walls, a ceiling, and a floor. Even a door.

Small Luxurious Shelter [Cost: 3000 Credits]: A small shelter with the added comforts of a place to rest and ways to clean and relieve yourself.

Medium Shelter [Cost: 4000 Credits]: Same as Small Luxurious Shelter with some added space to stretch your legs and a window.

The options just went on and on, with the more expensive options reaching the millions in price, far out of Luke's reach. As Hannah pointed out, this was not the time to be spending Credits on frivolous expenses, but he'd need at least a little more space than she did. She was a lot smaller than he was, after all. Luke settled on one that wouldn't take everything he had to purchase, but that would offer some possibilities the cheapest ones didn't.

Modular Luxurious Medium Shelter [Cost: 6000 Credits]: Same as Medium Shelter with even more space to move around. Separation of shower and toilet, two windows, and temperature control for added comfort. Allows for separate additions and expansions to increase the size of the Shelter.

After that purchase, he still had almost eight thousand Credits. A lot more was necessary to get the guild started, but they were in no real hurry. Hannah rode in front of him, the wind whipping her hair around, showing the shadows more than usual. She glanced over her shoulder and smiled back toward him. Luke grinned back and submerged himself in the moment, allowing the joy and freedom to take over as the road stretched out before them, and building after building came and went.

In several spots, they saw Integrated fighting against monsters of different sorts, but it looked like they had it all well in hand, so there was no need to stop. Curtis guided the group onto another road and stopped in front of a bank.

"We're here," he said.

Luke nodded. "Your brother on the way?"

Just then, the roar of an engine sounded in the distance.
