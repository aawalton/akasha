“I think it should be fine…”

Rusty replied to the mercenary leader, who, ever since he had arrived with Gleam on his shoulder, could not help staring at her. His shiny ant companion usually stayed back at the lair, so she rarely had the opportunity to interact with the other mercenaries. Right now, however, she sat proudly on his shoulder like a glinting piece of armor, her antennae twitching with curiosity.

Galiena reached out a heavily calloused hand, her usual rough demeanor softening into something almost comical as she carefully tapped the ant on the head. Gleam tilted her small head, which was harder than it looked, and tapped the large woman’s palm with her antennae.

“Woah... It’s feisty! Are you sure it won’t bite?”

“Gleam is very smart. She won’t hurt anyone unless they show animosity.”

Rusty was not sure why humanoids acted like this, but it seemed to be a universal truth, both in this world and in the other world he had visited. Whenever a humanoid saw something cute, they seemed to get the urge to pet it, at least the good humanoids did. Some of the mercenaries, however, looked annoyed by the display of affection their leader was giving Gleam and were not afraid to comment.

“It’s just an ant monster. What’s the big deal? That thing better not attack us while we’re fighting the orcs.”

Jakub spat on the ground, his knuckles white as he gripped his blade. His face was still swollen from Galiena’s warm-up the previous afternoon, leaving one eye bruised because the healing potion he had used was of lower quality.

“Don’t worry. Gleam is smart. She won’t attack anyone unless, of course, someone attacks her or me.”

Rusty replied while staring at the mercenary who was beginning to get on his nerves. He was not sure if this would end with just words, but he could not be certain. The third and fourth in command, along with a few other D-rankers, seemed to want more out of this arrangement.

“You’ve heard Second. If he says Gleam is safe, she’s safe!”

Galiena shouted while pointing toward Gleam, who seemed to be taking the attention in stride. Yet even with the blessing of their leader, not every mercenary or adventurer would appreciate her presence. Even though she was a tamed monster, some of the people here had lost family members and loved ones to monsters like her and did not trust something as unreliable as taming contracts.

“Now stop whining and double-check your gear. The contract says we move in ten minutes, and I won’t have any of you sluggish fools dragging us down.”

The men dispersed, muttering under their breath as they gathered their supplies. Aburdon’s dry chuckle resonated through the mental link, filled with mockery.

“Such predictable creatures. Let’s slaughter them later, my dear Rusty.”

Aburdon spoke quickly, but Alexander immediately protested.

“Let’s not do that...”

Rusty, on the other hand, simply turned around and headed toward the Adventurer Guild, where the group of adventurers was supposed to be waiting for them.

“If they don’t try to attack me, I won’t attack them.”

Rusty adjusted some of the straps on his armor. This frame was not the usual one he used, but an upgraded version of the adventurer armor. Edmund’s head was also inside it, and the helmet had been adjusted so that even a direct hit would not knock it loose. Several clasps on the inside held it firmly in place. Thanks to his Body Control skill, he could move them around as he pleased. At the moment, revealing that there was no actual body inside the armor would be disastrous.

“Are you with the mercenaries?”

“Yes, I am. You can call me Second.”

While the other mercenaries were still getting ready, he arrived at the meeting location. Although he was also known as an adventurer, many of the people gathered there were faces he had never seen before. One of them was the current leader of the adventurer parties stationed there. There were three parties in total, comprising eleven men.

‘Not really that many, but at least their strength is in the lower D-ranks. They should be enough against the orcs.’

The mercenaries had more people on their side, close to thirty in total, but only ten of them were D-rankers. Even with their larger numbers, some of their men had to remain in the city for protection. Several merchants had hired their group to safeguard their shipments while part of their forces marched into the woods.

The adventurers were there to make up for the shortage of available D-rankers. At first glance, however, they did not look particularly impressive.

“Second, is it?”

The leader of the adventurers, a scarred warrior with a broad shield strapped to his back, squinted at him.

“I’m Boros. I hope you mercenaries actually know how to hold a line. We aren’t looking to get swarmed by a bunch of greenskins because your men get skittish.”

“Don’t worry about us. Our line will hold.”

He replied in a monotone that did not seem to impress the adventurers. Their equipment was nothing special, and it seemed most of them had only recently managed to rank up. It was also obvious that, as a group accustomed to dealing with monsters, they considered themselves superior in this mission. Rusty, however, was not convinced that was the case.

“It better. Orcs aren’t like most brainless monsters. They fight with heavy weapons, use basic tactics, and won’t stop until you put a blade through their throats.”

“I am aware. I have fought orcs before.”

Rusty nodded while Boros frowned, apparently displeased that his warning had been dismissed so easily. Before they could continue, a small crowd of mercenaries appeared behind him.

“Looks like your main group finally decided to show up.”

Galiena strode over as if she owned the place. The mercenaries behind her wore matching equipment of considerably better quality than the mismatched gear worn by the adventurers. The sight immediately drew a few dark looks from Boros’s group.

“So, you’re their leader. Good. Just follow our lead, then.”

Galiena shook Boros’s hand in a test of strength, surprising the older man with the difference in their power. She was only a few levels short of reaching C-rank, while the other man was still somewhere around the middle of D-rank.

Rusty wasn’t sure how these humanoids managed to remain at such low levels, but it seemed to come down to either laziness or fear. Once they reached a comfortable level where they could face moderately strong monsters and sell the materials for a decent profit, they simply stopped pushing themselves.

They got comfortable, settled down, and avoided taking risks that might cost them their lives. To someone like Rusty, who needed to continuously evolve to survive, that line of thinking was completely foreign.

“The guard watch reported a gathering of orcs near the old abandoned Black Iron Mine. That’s our main objective, but we might encounter orcs along the way.”

Rusty was the one in charge of the plan, having been informed by Aburdon and Alexander about the true goal of the mission. It didn’t seem to be about reducing the orc population at all. Instead, their objective was an old mine that had fallen under the control of the monsters and had since been forgotten. Some even speculated that it connected to the Sunken Mine Dungeon in some way and could earn the ruling noble a considerable amount of money if they succeeded in defeating the orcs occupying the area.

“I don’t like it. They’re just sending us there without any idea of what’s waiting for us. For all we know, the orcs have a village inside those mines or somewhere around them.”

Alexander commented, as there wasn’t much information about what was actually waiting for them inside the old shafts. The guard watch report was sparse and outdated, containing information from a time when the settlement had been battling orcs at a much higher rate. The forest had grown and shifted around the mine since then, and there was no telling what they might encounter.

“If there’s an entire clan hidden inside that Black Iron Mine, those mercenaries and adventurer greenhorns are going to be turned into bloody paste.”

Aburdon said, his voice laced with dark amusement.

“Though I suppose that just leaves more corpses for us to harvest, doesn’t it?”

“I suppose you’re right... but I’m not sure how they’ll look at me if I’m the only one who comes back.”

Rusty replied while thinking about the loot he could gather. While it wasn’t anything particularly impressive, the equipment carried by the mercenaries and adventurers could be processed into higher quality metals and then sold. However, he was interested in something else at the moment, namely the Black Iron.

Black Iron was a type of raw ore that was superior to any steel he had used for his body or weapons. It was a resource used to produce superior high steel, as well as other alloys that could potentially reach C-Grade. If he could get his hands on some of the ore and process it, perhaps he could assemble a more suitable weapon.

“I propose we move out now. We should arrive at the mine close to nightfall.”

Normally, wandering through monster-infested forests at night was troublesome, but they planned to use the darkness to their advantage. Orcs weren’t as active during the night as they were during the day unless their chieftain ordered an assault, making it the perfect time to scout the mine and see what lurked around and inside it.

“You heard Second. Let’s go!”

Galiena laughed, smacking Rusty on the back with enough force to shatter an ordinary man’s shoulder blade. The steel plates of his frame didn’t even creak, but he was pushed forward slightly.

“My Second has everything under control! Now move out, all of you! We have green skins to butcher!”

The combined force of mercenaries and adventurers moved through the city gates, drawing a few curious glances. It was quite rare to see a force march directly into the orc-infested forest after an attack, but this was the best moment to do so. The orcs had lost many of their warriors and would likely have retreated into the innermost regions of the forest, far away from the mine they were trying to secure. If all went well, they would later return with a few miners and establish a defensive force. Perhaps they could even assemble a temporary fort if the area proved promising enough.

For the first two hours, the march was uneventful. Rusty walked at the front of the column, his visor silently scanning the brush while his mind kept track of every shift in the surrounding terrain. Jakub and a few of the other disgruntled mercenaries lagged slightly behind, whispering quiet insults whenever Galiena was not looking, but Rusty paid them no mind. They were a minor annoyance, and keeping his life detection skill active was far more important than keeping track of the strange humanoid insults they muttered.

“( •̀ - •́ )”

Suddenly, Gleam stiffened on his shoulder. Her small antennae began to twitch as she too used a detection skill to keep watch over the area ahead. They were now deep inside the forest, with trees surrounding them on all sides.

“Halt.”

Rusty called out as he raised his gauntlet. The entire column came to an abrupt stop. Swords were drawn from their scabbards, and shields were raised as tense whispers rippled through the ranks.

“What is it, Second?”

Galiena asked, gripping her heavy axes more tightly.

“I’m not sure. Let’s let Gleam investigate first.”

“Investigate? The ant can do that?”

Some of the adventurers overheard them and turned toward Gleam, who was still sitting on Rusty’s shoulder like a parrot. One of them seemed ready to make a comment, but before he could say anything, Gleam’s wings unfolded, and she flew into the sky.

“Oh, a flying monster ant?!”

Many of them were surprised by the display, but Rusty paid no attention to their gasps. He simply waited patiently while Gleam surveyed everything from a high enough altitude. Flying creatures were usually ignored and mistaken for ordinary birds, so even if the orcs spotted her, they would probably pay her no mind.

“She’s back.”

“(˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶)”

After around five minutes, Gleam landed back on his shoulder. The others leaned in expectantly.

“She says there’s a small orc camp ahead. Fifteen in total. We should be able to use the bushes to our advantage if we approach from the eastern side.”

Everyone was flabbergasted when Rusty picked up a stick and began drawing a rough plan of the area in the dirt before them. To them, it made no sense that a human could communicate with a monster. Even more unbelievable was the fact that the monster could remember the layout of an enemy camp in such detail.

“You were right, Second. Gleam is amazing.”

The only one who wasn’t shocked was Galiena, who simply burst out laughing and gave him another heavy smack on the back that rattled his shoulder plates.

“Hah! Now that is what I call a proper battle pet!”

“Gleam isn’t a pet. She is my partner!”

“Hah! Even better!”

Rusty corrected the mercenary leader, but before they could move, Jakub started complaining.

“You don’t seriously plan on listening to an ant monster, do you? We should send out one of our scouts to make sure what he’s telling us is true!”

The man clearly didn’t trust Rusty or Gleam, but Galiena simply shook her head and smacked him on the shoulder.

“Stop complaining and move, unless you want me to shove my axe up your arse!”

Jakub grumbled, rubbing his shoulder as he fell back into the group, though his glare remained fixed on Rusty. No one else dared to challenge the order.

With the plan established, the combined force moved silently, thanks to a few low-level silencing enchantments that kept the noise of their movement to a minimum.

“There they are, just like he said. That ant was right?”

Once they reached the large bushes Rusty had mentioned, everyone realized that the plan was solid. Even now, the orcs had no idea they were there. Their attention remained focused on the rotten deer carcass they were devouring.

“This is going to be easy. Archers first. Give them hell!”

Galiena grinned as she ordered everyone with a bow to start shooting. Rusty asked one of the E-rankers to hand him a simple bow, then joined a group of ten archers. Moments later, the initial attack began.

Arrows flew out of the bushes, embedding themselves in the unsuspecting orcs. Three of them collapsed instantly, several arrows buried deep in their chests and throats.

The remaining orcs roared in confusion as they dropped their half-eaten meat. Some hid behind trees while others grabbed shields as the arrows continued to rain down, but it was already too late.

Before the monsters could form a defensive line, Galiena burst through the foliage like a runaway siege engine. Her twin axes caught the nearest orc beneath the jaw, splitting its skull with a sickening crack.

“Charge!”

She shouted, and soon the small orc encampment turned into a bloodbath, signaling their group’s arrival in the orc-infested forest.
