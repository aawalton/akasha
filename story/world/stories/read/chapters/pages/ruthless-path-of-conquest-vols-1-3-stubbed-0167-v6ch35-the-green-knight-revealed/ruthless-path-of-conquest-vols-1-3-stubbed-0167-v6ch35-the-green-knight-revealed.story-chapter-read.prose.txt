He called me Dad, James thought. That’s sweet. Is that how our relationship develops in the future?

Then he reminded himself that he had just been transported across space and time to some unknown place. Perhaps he shouldn’t simply trust the word of the figure who had just been beating the crap out of him, even if he was the spitting image of someone James knew in his normal life.

“If you’re really our Abhi, then of course, you know your siblings’ names,” James said.

“Yeah. Indira and Deepam. I would also count James Junior and Margaret as siblings.”

“Okay, I have to admit, that was a pretty easy one,” James said.

Inside, his brain was whirling.

Margaret? So, Mina’s having a girl this time? I’m going to have a daughter! He concealed his delight, but it was difficult. Margaret’s a great name!

“You can ask me literally anything you want,” Abhi said. “As long as you make it reasonably quick. I bet you already know my fighting style is based on yours, after you refine it a bit more. You should ask me which fictional character my armor set is inspired by. Or maybe who I had my first kiss with…” The man wrinkled his nose. “Or maybe not? Are you… am I old enough that’s happened already from your point of view?”

You haven’t even started calling me Dad yet in my timeline, James thought.

He shook his head slightly. “I know the answer to the armor set thing. The Green Knight. That’s unique enough to you. You love those King Arthur stories. I believe it now. It really is you…”

Either that, or this whole situation is some kind of truly elaborate hallucination. I could believe my own mind would conjure something like this.

“You spoke in the present tense.” Abhi froze. “I love those King Arthur stories. So I’m still a little kid in your time. Anansi preserve us… you’re that far away from where I intended to find you.”

Is it really that bad? James wanted to say. But he cut to the chase instead.

“What are you doing here, anyway?” James asked. “You said something about me needing a wakeup call?”

“Well, it’s not current you. It’s an apparently pretty distant future you. You end up doing pretty well at the conquest game, you get complacent and start spending more time with your family. Which is great for us, everyone loved having you around more. You figured, since you weren’t aging, that you had plenty of time to keep on getting stronger. Well, spoiler alert: you don’t.”

“Good to know, I guess.”

“Yeah, but you’re not really the James who needed to hear this, though, and I probably fucked up this timeline by somehow encountering you instead of him…”

“Or you fixed it,” James said.

Abhi chuckled quietly, but there was a bitter note to the laughter. “I  can’t share your optimism, Dad. I mean, maybe I helped your timeline, if you take this conversation to heart and act like you’re on the backfoot for the next thirty years of your life.”

James frowned.

“See? See, that’s what I mean!” Abhi said. “It goes against your nature. Doing that is a really daunting prospect for you. But maybe things will turn out better for you than they did for the James in my timeline.

“How’s that James?”

“Oh, you know. Dead. That’s why Junior sent me back.”

Yeah, I guess I figured that.

“How does time travel actually work?” James asked.

“There’s a really old Japanese cartoon you used to show me and Junior once you had electricity and televisions working,” Abhi said. “Dragon Ball Z. Time travel works a little like that, except that timelines can outcompete each other and potentially delete preexisting timelines from existence. Which is what we’re hoping for. Since, you know, you’re dead, and we want a timeline that brings you back.”

You guys sound pretty desperate, if you’re willing to embrace destroying your own timeline…

“Are the rest of you okay?”

“If we were, I wouldn’t be traveling back in time, Dad,” Abhi said as gently as he could. “Putting it mildly, the future is kind of shit, and what it needs to get better is you. For you to still be around. For the present day version of you to get stronger, be ruthless, impose your brand of order, unite humanity by hook or by crook, along with as many monsters as you can. For you to fight the incursions, and win. Above all else, win.”

Uniting humanity is a tall order for me right now, when it’s not even clear whether I’m the strongest human, James thought. But considering Abhi’s obvious age, and the fact that he might be even older than he looks, maybe I have a fair amount of time in which to accomplish that miracle.

He was about to ask what the incursions were, when he suddenly remembered a vision he’d had alongside some of the others with Sister Strange. She had shown him Mina’s death during an attack on the Fisher Kingdom, James getting beaten up by this green-armored knight who turned out to be Abhi, Zhang Chāo decapitating James with a sword, and multiple visions of others in unrelated settings.

And she had also shown him a scene of James battling alongside an adult version of his son Junior.

We were fighting over an ocean, he remembered. There were all kinds of monsters and humans on my side, and we were struggling against… creatures whose bodies did not make geometric sense. Things that passed through a crack in the fabric of reality and invaded Earth. I was the only one in the scene who could even fight them effectively. Me and Junior.

The Fisher Emperor remembered how, in the vision, he had used swords made of light and a magic he did not yet know to defeat scores of these incomprehensible monsters. Even as countless allies fell all around them, father and son had continued fighting the good fight to keep Earth safe from the creatures. James had ultimately fallen shielding his son and passed his swords on to Junior in his dying moments, and the vision had faded on the younger man about to face off against the remaining creatures all alone.

He wondered if the strange monster of alien geometries that he had seen in the World Leaders Summit—and which was present in the melee as well—was also one of these creatures that the “incursions” let through, just on a smaller scale. A scout or something.

Well, no matter.

James allowed himself a small smile. Junior would turn out to be a good warrior. The boy would take up his father’s mantle, and apparently, he would even survive the confrontation that killed James.

That vision had left it ambiguous, but now James had the answer.

We’re going to live on. We’re going to survive. We’ll fight and win. Even if the fight grows more difficult with my death, my son—he looked at Abhi, and his smile widened slightly—my sons will continue. They’re little badasses in the making. I just have to form them strong enough to take on the burden when I pass on. The struggle will continue until humanity triumphs. I know it in my bones. Even if Abhi and the others might be feeling desperate now, they’ll win eventually even if they don’t alter the timeline the way they want to.

“You always did love the idea of a good fight, huh?” Abhi said, reading James’s expression correctly.

“Yeah. I just remembered… what the incursions are like. I practically saw the whole vision in my head again. I was really proud of how Junior handled himself. Although I’m surprised that Mina, Margaret, you, Indira, and Deepam weren’t there.”

Abhi’s expression turned slightly pained. “So, this is one of the timelines when you’ve had some glimpses of the future. You remember in advance… specifically the day you died. I was fighting on the other side of the world that day. So was the rest of the family. You and Junior were enough by yourselves to respond to that attack, or so the two of you claimed. I’ve always thought that you told him in advance what was going to happen. A secret just between the two of you. Because I probably also live in a timeline where you knew the future. Not that Junior would talk about that… and not that I would try to make him.”

“There are timelines where I didn’t have visions of the future?” James asked.

“Lots of different timelines,” Abhi replied. “Lots of permutations.” He shook his head and sighed. “I have to go soon, Dad.”

“The time traveler’s going to run out of time?” James asked, raising an eyebrow.

What, you can’t chat with your old man?

“It’s seriously complicated,” the man in green replied. “Rules that I don’t understand myself. Time travel is difficult, but traveling backward in time is significantly more difficult than going forward or even predicting the future. Traveling forward more quickly was possible even before the System. But the universe runs on magic now. That magical framework seems to be extremely hostile to anyone trying to change the past—well, the System specifically is. It punishes attempts to break its rules around time pretty strictly. Whether the offender is mortal or divine. We had to barter a number of valuables to a god to get the artifact that I’ve been using to go back. I’m a little bit of a fugitive now, and there are strict limitations on how it works.”

“A fugitive from the System?” James asked. That was hard to imagine.

“Yeah. It’s as much of an issue as it sounds.” Abhi forced a smile that was obviously insincere. “I’m starting to think we made a bad deal. Anyway, I’m pretty sure my time is about to expire and force me to leave. I wish I could stay and heal you, but—” he looked James up and down. The Fisher Emperor had been recovering through the duration of their conversation, his accelerated healing doing a great deal to restore health, fix injuries, and recuperate expended Mana. “Well, I guess you’ll be all right.”

“Yeah,” James said. “I’ll be just fine. And I’ll remember what you said. It was good to see you, in all your glory.” He smiled. “Say hi to the others from me when you see them.”

It’s weird to feel proud of an adult when I didn’t see him grow up. I know he’s still a little boy back home. But still, I am.

“Good to see you, too, Dad.” Abhi pulled James into an unexpected embrace and whispered into his ear, “Do me a favor and don’t die this time. It’s been hard on Junior, taking command. I’ll tell him that you say hi. But it’s probably just going to bring him down. The real glory days were when you were alive.”

James wanted to ask whether Mina was helping Junior out, but then again, maybe he didn’t want to do that. Maybe he didn’t want to confirm whether this Abhi’s timeline was one in which James’s Queen died.

At any rate, Abhi stepped back from him a moment later. A jeweled armband around the green-clad warrior’s arm glowed a gentle golden color, and suddenly Abhi disappeared. All that was left was the snow and ice, and the whistling wind.

I never asked where we were even having this fight, James thought.

Then, almost as quickly as Abhi disappeared, the Mana that had appeared around James before he vanished from the melee surrounded him again.

The Fisher Emperor didn’t try to escape it this time. If he correctly intuited the way this Skill worked, it was probably sending him back to where he’d come from.

This was strange and ominous, James thought. But the conversation with Abhi seems to prove that, at least in the timeline he came from, the future didn’t appear to be set in stone. He spoke of multiple timelines, and he was here in the first place trying to give me—a version of me—a wakeup call. He and Junior were banking on the future not being fixed. They would know better than me about that issue. So, regardless of what visions I might have had, the future is really what we make of it.
