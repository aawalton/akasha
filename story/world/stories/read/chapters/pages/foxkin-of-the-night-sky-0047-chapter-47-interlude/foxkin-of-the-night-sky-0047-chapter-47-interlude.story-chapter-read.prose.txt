The seal was almost undone.

Less than a minute remained.

Within the coffin at the heart of the palace, awareness stirred.

The Lich felt it as pressure first, a loosening of bindings that had constrained him for thirty long days. The ritual lattice woven into the stone floor thinned. The runic anchors dimmed.

Soon.

A second un-life was not something one expected.

When his phylactery shattered beneath the blade of a stray Hero—an infuriatingly fortunate one—he had assumed oblivion would follow. Dissolution. Finality.

Instead, there had been an offer.

A System interface unlike his own had manifested in the void between moments. Clinical. Impersonal. It spoke of opportunity. Of a “soul stock exchange.” Of transferable value.

Participation required terms.

He would be relocated to a different greater world. Asteria.

He would serve as the final adversary of something called a “Tutorial.”

He would relinquish his prior name, his prior affiliations, and his prior narrative.

In their place, he would become a designation.

The Lich.

It had been an acceptable compromise.

Identity was mutable. Power was not.

Though this System restricted him to what it termed Tier Two—a dramatic reduction from his former Eighth Ring authority—he was not concerned.

Power scaled. Experience accumulated.

According to his new System, his opponents would be recently inducted users. Thirty days of growth at most. Infants, by any meaningful metric.

Disorganized and inexperienced.

He would eradicate them. Claim their points. Convert their value into tangible assets through the System’s marketplace. Reconstruct influence.

Rebuild.

The final runes along the coffin lid dimmed further.

The palace above him was silent.

The forest beyond the walls—his forest—should be saturated with undeath. Mana-rich. Dense and obedient.

He extended his awareness outward in anticipation.

The seal continued to weaken.

Seconds now.

He allowed himself a measured, patient satisfaction.

This time, there would be no stray Hero.

This time, he would be prepared.

The final binding unraveled, and the Lich awakened.

The lid of the infernal coffin tore free and struck the marble floor with a resonant clang. Stone cracked where it landed.

Silence followed.

Then the Lich rose.

Ancient robes of funerary black settled around a skeletal frame wreathed in faint violet motes. Empty eye sockets flared with cold, steady light as he took in the throne room's high ceilings, pale stone, and gold accents.

Unfamiliar architecture.

Irrelevant.

A blue System window manifested before him, stark and clinical.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Quest Received.

Designation: Final Boss

Objective: Kill Them All

You are the final challenge of this Tutorial.

Newly integrated Users have entered your domain.

Eliminate them.

You are not meant to survive. They are.

Prove yourself worthy of your second chance.

Rewards scale with total Users slain and Points accumulated.

Rewards:

???

Progress:

9,999 / 10,000

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

All as expected.

Except—

Only one remaining user.

The Lich paused.

Then a low, hollow chuckle echoed through the chamber.

Wave tactics had been the only variable of concern. Attrition. Overwhelming numbers. Newly integrated users could, in theory, exploit scaling mechanics if properly coordinated.

But one?

With a single remaining target, the calculus simplified considerably.

An Apex Undead entity—though restricted to Tier Two—was not designed to fall to a solitary month-old user. Even diminished, he retained mastery of Necromancy and Soul Magic refined over centuries. Experience did not evaporate simply because a System imposed numerical ceilings.

Especially not experience earned across lifetimes.

He dismissed the quest window and opened his status menu.

The blue interface unfolded obediently.

The same skills, albeit considerably weaker.

Soul-contracts intact, although a significant portion were currently locked.

Stat distribution reduced but functional.

Far inferior to his former Eighth Ring authority, certainly, but sufficient.

More than sufficient.

He flexed skeletal fingers, testing responsiveness. Mana coursed cleanly through his frame.

Yes.

Everything was in order.

The Lich allowed himself a measured moment of satisfaction.

One user remained. He would extinguish them. Then he would collect his reward.

And this world would learn that soul mages, even when recycled, remained sovereign.

The lights behind his eyes flared brighter, violet fire swelling within hollow sockets.

His skeletal jaw opened.

“Come to me, my kin.”

The command rippled outward, threaded with necromantic authority. It should have been answered by the scrape of bone, the shamble of rotting flesh, the eager pull of lesser wills seeking direction.

There was no response.

No surge of mana, nor an obedient tide of undeath.

The Lich stilled.

He extended his perception farther, past the palace walls, beyond the ruined city, outward into what should have been a forest thick with death and soul-aspected resonance.

Nothing.

No undead network.

Only emptiness. Dry and silent.

Then—

A voice.

Calm, measured, almost polite in a way that sent a faint chill down The Lich's spine.

“If you were summoning the undead that once populated this city,” the voice said, clear as a bell within the cavernous hall, “I regret to inform you that you are approximately twenty-five days too late.”

The Lich turned.

Upon the throne above his coffin sat a lone figure.

Small in stature.

Composed.

Beautiful in a distinctly inhuman way, as if she had been shaped rather than born.

Her white hair—absurdly long—had been gathered into a high ponytail to keep it from trailing across the floor, the bound length spilling down the back of the throne in a pale cascade. Fox ears rose cleanly from her crown, upright and steady. Her attire shimmered with shifting, prismatic color, fabric that caught the dim light and fractured it into quiet, kaleidoscopic hues. It was neither ceremonial nor martial in any style he recognized.

Where the sleeveless design and short skirt left skin exposed, the illusion of perfection fractured.

Below her knees, the skin was unnaturally pale. Too smooth, and faintly glossy in the dim light, as though it had forgotten how skin was supposed to behave. Thin lines of scar tissue branched across her calves in jagged, bleached patterns, like lightning frozen mid-strike.

Her right arm bore a different imperfection. The skin there lacked the branching scars, yet it was no less wrong, slightly lighter than the surrounding flesh and smooth to the point of artificiality, as if it had been replaced with something nearly identical that simply failed to imitate warmth.

One leg rested over the other with unhurried elegance, the damaged skin displayed without the slightest effort to conceal it. Hands folded loosely in her lap, posture immaculate.

Leaning against the throne beside her stood a blade nearly as tall as she was. It was slender, light-edged, and iridescent in the ambient glow.

And her eyes—

Blacker than any void.

Not merely dark.

Completely absent.

They regarded him without hostility.

Without fear.

Simply… assessment.

“I do apologize for the inconvenience,” she continued, tone smooth and nobly restrained. “They proved… insufficiently resilient.”

Ash drifted faintly through the open arches of the throne room, catching in beams of dim light.

The air itself felt wrong.

Dry.

The Lich’s gaze fixed upon her.

One user. And she was seated upon his throne.

“Interesting,” she said, her voice smooth and almost contemplative. “You possess a race rather than a species. A class as well, although it won’t show me the exact name. And you spoke with intent.”

Her head tilted ever so slightly.

“Does that imply you are sentient, rather than a mere monster?”

If there was one trait the Lich respected, it was intellectual curiosity.

He had bound his soul to a phylactery for the sake of continued research. Knowledge was worth eternity.

So he answered.

“I am.”

The Foxkin raised a single finger to her cheek, thoughtful.

“I see. And how does that arrangement function? Were you once an integrated user who underwent racial transformation, as I did? Or are you… something else entirely?”

“Something else,” the Lich replied. “I hail from another greater world. I perished there and was offered a second existence, conditional upon my participation as the final adversary of this Tutorial.”

Her ears perked upright.

“Another greater world,” she repeated, the faintest note of interest threading through her otherwise measured tone. “How fascinating. What is it like?”

The Lich regarded her in silence for a moment.

“I have answered two inquiries,” he said at last. “Yet you persist in asking further. That seems… inequitable.”

She blinked once.

“Very well,” she replied calmly. “An exchange, then. We shall alternate. One question each.”

A pause.

“By my count,” she added politely, “that leaves you with two unanswered queries. Would you care to begin?”

“Then I shall,” the Lich said. “You mentioned racial transformation earlier. What did you mean by that?”

“When we were integrated, the System forcibly altered some of us,” she replied smoothly. “Our original world was entirely human-dominated. A mere month ago, I was as well.”

She inclined her head slightly.

“Is such a procedure not commonplace in your former greater world?”

The Lich gave a dry, humorless scoff.

“Absolutely not. Racial conversion is prohibitively inefficient and extravagantly costly. If it were otherwise, there would be Liches at every corner.”

He paused.

Why had he elaborated? It had not been her turn.

Her tail swayed lightly behind her, brushing stray ash from the armrest in an absent motion.

The Lich narrowed his gaze.

Then, regardless, he proceeded.

“You also stated that I possess both a Race and a Class. By what means did you acquire that information?”

“A general skill,” she answered without hesitation. “A common one known as ‘Inspect.’ It was granted as an integration bonus. I would presume you possess it as well.”

The Lich summoned his status interface.

There it was.

[Inspect (Uncommon)]

His version bore a higher rarity designation.

“How is it activated?” he asked.

The Foxkin’s smile deepened by the slightest, almost imperceptible degree.

“Is that your next question?”

A pulse of irritation moved through him.

That was petty. Deliberate.

Still, the skill in question appeared foundational. Knowledge was leverage.

“Yes,” he confirmed.

She inclined her head.

“Merely think its name. Vocalization is optional.”

Very well.

The Lich focused inward and invoked the skill.

Inspect.

A window materialized before him.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[Dragonslayer]

She has felled what stood above all others.

Pray that you are not next.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The chamber seemed to grow colder.

He did not move.

Dragons were apex entities. Powerful and extremely territorial. Utterly disastrous, even by Living Calamity standards.

They were not tutorial obstacles.

They were not introductory fodder.

His gaze lifted slowly from the System window to the Foxkin seated upon his throne.

Her smile widened. Just slightly.

Not enough to be overt. Enough to be intentional.

“That means it is my turn, does it not?” She paused only briefly. “What is your Aspect?”

The Lich regarded her in silence.

A surgical inquiry.

Not idle curiosity.

Assessment.

He raised one skeletal hand.

“Summon: Undead Battalion.”

A vast violet circle bloomed across the marble floor, sigils spiraling outward in layered rings. Contracted souls answered instantly.

Several hundred undead emerged in a single heartbeat.

Shielded spear-bearers forming the vanguard. Archers and mages arrayed behind. Healers positioned precisely within the formation.

A disciplined and orderly battalion.

The weakest tier of his arsenal, but sufficient for measurement.

The Foxkin rose from the throne without haste.

Without concern.

The Lich waited.

Pride ran through apex beings like marrow through bone. Striking while an opponent postured was crude. She had afforded him discourse. He would afford her readiness.

Her small hand reached behind her and drew the massive blade resting beside the throne. She held it in both hands, hilt at her waist, the point angled downward at her side.

“Very well,” she said evenly. “Let us begin.”

The undead surged forward in perfect formation.

And instantly—

Four prismatic vines erupted from her shadow.

They struck the shield wall with concussive cracks.

The formation held.

Barely.

Then the vines shifted. They wrapped, constricted, and eventually, they ignited.

Flames of impossible color burst along their lengths, devouring wood, steel, and bone alike.

Within moments—before the battalion had advanced more than a handful of strides—the formation collapsed.

Archers severed mid-draw. Mages silenced before incantations completed. Healers incinerated in violet and gold light.

Only the shield-bearers survived initial impact.

None survived the flames.

The Lich felt it.

Four hundred contracts. Severed.

Neither dismissed nor reclaimed.

Gone. Their souls did not return to him.

Instead, they were completely erased.

Only seconds had passed.

The throne room floor was already littered with ash.

“Oh,” she murmured lightly. “How intriguing. Those undead possessed souls. The ones outside did not.”

She lifted her gaze to meet his.

“Necromancy, then? Or perhaps a Soul Aspect? Or are those distinctions interchangeable in your case?”

The Lich did not answer.

She nodded anyway. As though confirmation had been granted.

The Lich could not determine what betrayed him. Perhaps posture, mana fluctuation, or perhaps some kind of infinitesimal delay in his movements.

Fascinating.

Still, it was irrelevant.

He raised his hand once more.

Purple radiance flared outward in a widening corona as deeper contracts stirred. Older, stronger, and far less expendable ones.

One thought crystallized with cold precision.

This girl is dangerous.
