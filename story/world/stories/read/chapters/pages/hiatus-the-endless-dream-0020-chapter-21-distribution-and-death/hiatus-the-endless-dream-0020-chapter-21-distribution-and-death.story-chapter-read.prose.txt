It took her a second to realize what had happened, and as she turned her head upward to look at me, I saw that realization come in waves.

That first, I was here.

That second, she was covered in blood.

Finally, that she was about to die.

I didn't savor it. I didn't particularly want her to go out in this way, but it just happened to be the best plan. I had hoped the scientist would be with her.

"You. You ungrateful wre–" She didn't get to finish her statement, as presumably the nearest creature barreled into her. The impact was so harsh against her side that her body bent, and her head lolled, trailing behind her body. She hit the floor, and her skull thudded against hardened dirt.

In a daze, I saw her try to focus on me. I could feel, through the hairs, more creatures scuttling forward. Several were already under the elderly woman's arm as the blood was being sucked from the veins.

From my vantage point, I watched unblinking, frozen from the creature's peripheral vision. I heard and felt the quick tapping of the monster's feet, and I imagined them as a tide of roaches.

The remaining hybrid had already killed one of the monsters and was currently tearing at another. I felt three of the plant creatures that were more hesitant. For some reason, they didn't seem as affected by the frenzy. Likely high continuity Visitors.

Maggie screamed, and I was no longer able to distract myself from the scene. I could barely make out her form now. Which was a cold comfort. Blood outlined the pile of bodies, blocking my vision of the elderly woman. They reminded me of kittens fighting over their mother's milk. Invisible limbs outlined themselves in blood, and for the life of me, I couldn't tell if they were from the jug or the old lady.

"STOP," she screamed. Her voice was infused with that same power from before. I could see a small sheen of metal in one of her hands. That must have been–

"Stop," the scream was begging, desperate. Spoken through a raspy half-sob. Some of the creatures seemed to pause, but not all of them and– seeing their brethren feasting – returned to the meal as well.

"Please stop. I'm sorry." I could barely make out the words. I tried to ignore them, but no matter how muffled they became, I could always decipher the meaning.

"I had no choice."

"We weren't going to."

"Stop them. Please."

Her voice grew weaker and weaker, interspersed with wracking sobs of pain. I finally found out if it was her blood or not. It was hers. I saw it as a chunk of flesh thrown between two viscera-soaked, finger-like tendrils. I think I saw a wiry yellow string of the nervous system being pulled out of the pile.

The words stopped. Instead, there was just the horrid mix of sobbing and screaming. I could tell she was trying to form words, and she did, but they were being split between the nineteen or so monsters. One syllable from the end came first. Two more overlapped from opposite ends of the pile. Another repeated three times. The first letter was quiet, and the last letter never came. They came from mouths that didn't know how to speak from a brain being disassembled, distributed, copied, torn, and broken.

"A" "I R" "O O O" "d "

There were other words after, but they all came out as gibberish, or maybe begging. Distinct voices and tones began to emerge from the pile---a random series of nonsensical scrambled letters.

My stomach roiled. Bile rose. I couldn't move, but my body didn't care. I threw up. It tasted sour, some leaked out of my mouth, but not all of it as I was forced to swallow or choke.

I thought about her grandchildren and wondered if she had thought about them, too.

Eventually, the noises stopped altogether, and the blood had dissipated enough that I could see the remains of Maggie in the moonlight. Not more than an undulating nervous system and a pile of bones underneath. The brain was gone, but her eyes remained. Still twitching.

Her eyes were brown.

The new monsters were becoming visible again; they moved, and the last of the yellow threads snapped.

Crumpling to the ground, my back against the old wood of the barn, was how I found out the paralysis was broken. The plant creatures had already started to change while consuming the horrid blood. I could feel the changes through the air, but not any specifics, but I knew if I turned the lights on right now, I'd be able to see every monster in here. The plant monsters had all changed, which meant the only Restriction I really had anymore was [Stalker]. I let static take my vision and confirmed the time. Forty-five minutes. It would have to be enough.

My heart twisted in my chest. My good arm shook from my elbow, and my grotesque monster foot shivered in place.

It didn't matter. All these feelings could go to hell. I'd do it again.

I didn't want to be here anymore.

I fought the urge to curl up. I weakly held Peeler.

There wasn't a lot in this world I had control over. That had been stripped away from me the moment I was stolen into the Dream. This was my life now.

I thought of Rowan.

I stood and turned to the old, half-rotted wall. I needed to close the barn doors, and hang the sheet I brought from one of the lights.

With Maggie's death and all the monsters in one location, I had just accomplished every goal I had set out. The only thing that could ruin it is if the scientist decided to run away, but I knew he wouldn't.

I had won.

I just needed the scientist to catch up now.
