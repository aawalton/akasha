Despite launching into the creation of this new magic with grand plans and boundless motivation, I hit a wall at the very first step: where to begin?

My goal was to create a magic with minimal backlash, one that exerted very limited strain on the mages' mental health. But to even shape a concept, I first had to define what Fate actually was.

Back when I was a human, I was convinced there was no fate or destiny. I had absolutely no doubt that our actions determined every second of our future. But as a god dragged into this world, where the Goddess clearly planned so many things for me… can I really say this wasn’t my destiny?

I glanced at the shining stars tethered to the Crimson Guardian manifestation in my realm.

Weren’t they also prepared for me? The entire race of Velmoryns trapped in the forest without contact with the outside world - wasn’t that the same as them waiting for me?

Even if this isn’t Fate in the absolute sense, because I could’ve avoided this outcome by taking a different route, there is no doubt that my present, and perhaps even my future, was heavily influenced by the Goddess. That’s what I should aim for - magic that isn’t guaranteed to work, but pushes the target toward a certain outcome by tweaking causality.

My goal wasn’t as significant as what the Goddess had done. I wasn’t trying to create magic that would allow my believers to alter a target's entire life. It would only act as a minor hex - a sudden itch, a stumble, a sneeze. Furthermore, it would be strictly limited in time; the hex could be planned for a maximum of one minute into the future.

By placing such strict limitations, I hoped to minimize the risks of using the magic. As Aurelion had already proven, when you gave up something, this world always balanced it with an appropriate reward.

The concept felt solid, but then it came down to casting the spell. I had already witnessed runes, diagrams, artifacts, and even markings engraved on the body.

For me, the most convenient method was engraving because it couldn’t be cancelled by disrupting the mage’s focus and could be easily veiled beneath clothes. But I wanted to try something different.

What if the mage chanted the spell instead?

Spoken words took time. They required breath and rhythm. They revealed the mage's position and, more importantly, if someone could understand the words, they were likely to predict the effect of the spell.

Wouldn't these disadvantages serve as a limitation that, according to the laws of this world, would boost the success rate or power of the spell?

I took a moment to properly consider the theory. That’s when my gaze drifted to the Red Grima. It had sat dormant and functionless ever since I fell into slumber. With its tether to me severed, it had lost all ability to communicate with my believers. It was strange. The basilisk had remained completely unaffected, yet my limited clone had become nothing more than an empty core.

Still, that was a mystery for another time. I would figure out what happened to the Grima later.

For now, I needed test subjects. I decided to create two new clones - one imbued with mana and the capacity to use my experimental magic, and the other a simple dummy to serve as the target.

Thanks to my new skill, splitting my essence and assigning specific functions came easily now. Soon, two small figures hovered in the void of my realm. The first took the shape of Othrien; I didn't know why, but his visage came to mind first. The second took the shape of Avenor.

That choice, admittedly, was entirely on purpose.

But while molding the vessels was effortless, converting my Divine Power into mana proved far more difficult than I had anticipated.

My understanding of the relationship between the two energies was limited to what the system had told me: divine power was a purer, higher form of mana. Basically, the magical energy was simply a derivative of divinity, so I assumed that if I diluted my essence, it would naturally degrade into mana.

The problem was, I had no idea how to dilute it.

So, as usual, the moment I felt stuck, I activated Guidance.

[Creation of Mana]

It is impossible to dilute your divinity, as your essence is already Pure.

To revert divinity to a lesser state would require gathering all latent impurities into a single fraction of your power - a feat impossible for any deity below a certain Rank. However, even if you possessed such mastery, you would still fail because there are no impurities within your essence to manipulate.

Suggestion: Attempt to create magical power directly, rather than seeking to degrade your divinity.

I felt stupid. And the tone of the system’s explanation only reinforced the feeling. In my attempt to be creative and clever, I had started reinventing the wheel when the solution was incredibly simple - create mana.

I didn’t dwell on the frustration.

[Warning: Creation consumed 0.1 Divinity Points!]

The amount of mana a mere fraction of my Divinity created was overwhelming. No mage, not even Karla, possessed reserves this deep. In a way, this little experiment helped me understand just how vast the gap between the two energies was.

With a thought I condensed the mana into a tiny orb and pushed it toward Little Othrien, who hung in the void like a lifeless lump of crimson mist.

The moment the magical energy entered its form, my tiny clone shifted. It had no sentient mind, but the simple mechanism I had engraved within it activated immediately.

“By arcane thread and woven sign, I bind your fate to this will of mine. Let balance break and footing fail; stumble and fall as these words prevail.”

Little Avenor remained entirely unmoved. It obviously wouldn’t fall as it had no legs to walk; it was merely a hovering mass of condensed mist.

However, its status window would reveal if the spell had affected it.

[Name: Verde’s Clone; Status: Normal]

It didn’t work. Was it the chant?

I tried to recall everything I knew about how spells functioned. Mana was the fuel. Diagrams or other mediums were what helped the mage shape the energy. But there was a third component - emotions.

Aria had once told Avenor that magic affected the caster’s emotional state because it fed on it. Emotion was the spark. Perhaps it was impossible to create a branch of magic that bypassed the caster’s mind because magic was inherently reliant on intent.

I’ll try it myself.

Perhaps my clone had failed because it was a hollow shell; it lacked sentience, and therefore, it lacked emotion. But despite being a god, I still possessed feelings. Not only that, but my own power constantly influenced my mental state.

If even gods weren’t immune to the emotional influence of their own divinity, then perhaps, in this world, it was simply impossible to avoid such a cost.

I chose not to overthink it. Pulling some of the mana away from little Othrien, I focused my intent on little Avenor. I tried visualizing - a flicker of desire, a wish for the target to fall, seep into the energy.

“By arcane thread and woven sign, I bind your fate to this will of mine. Let balance break and footing fail; stumble and fall as these words prevail.”

My entire realm shuddered the moment the final syllable left my lips. The mist surrounding me began to boil, swirling violently until a red notification flashed before my eyes.

[Warning!]

You are attempting to assume the Authority of Probability and Causality - one of the most potent domains that remains unclaimed.

The Pantheon has been alerted to your ambition. However, they have chosen not to intervene, as you have yet to achieve any meaningful progress.

Beware!

I froze. It never even crossed my mind that a spell so harmless in intent would cause such a commotion.

But what truly unsettled me wasn't the trembling of my realm - it was the system. For the first time, I felt as though it was on my side. The tone of the red notification wasn't indifferent, it read as if it were genuinely worried about me.

And that scared me even more.

The Pantheon’s decision not to intervene didn’t mean the danger had passed. The fact that they chose to spare me because I had yet to achieve meaningful progress sounded less like mercy and more like waiting for a better opportunity. They were watching until I produced something of value.

Then, they would come, extract the information from me, and I would likely share the same fate as the Netherworld Marquess - simply erased from existence.

I was still overwhelmed by the same grim thoughts when the red light was replaced by a golden sheen. Another notification appeared.

[Congratulations, Verde!]

You have created a completely new branch of magic: Thread of Fate.

A mage may cast a hex upon their target, cursing them with minor misfortune.

Success Probability: Dependent on the amount of mana supplied, as well as the mage’s Intelligence, Wisdom, and Insight. The probability can be increased by chanting the spell loudly and clearly describing its effect.

If the spell fails, there is a 10% chance the mage will suffer the same effect.

I quickly checked little Avenor.

[Name: Verde’s Clone; Status: Cursed]

At least something good came out of it.

Under normal circumstances, I would have been ecstatic. Creating an entirely new branch of magic from scratch was a monumental achievement. But that red notification had terrified me.

But in the end, I didn’t have the luxury of fear.

I couldn't afford to be paralyzed by caution, not before I had dealt with the Night God. To survive him, I had to use every tool at my disposal, even if it meant slaughtering a unique guardian like Kurraghal to hatch the ancient eggs.

I’ll wait until Karla defeats the creature. Then, I will reward her with this new magic.

The thought amused me, lifting some of the heaviness from my chest. I would be rewarding Karla with something I had intended to grant her anyway.

I shifted the Window, the view transitioning from the void of my realm to the dense, emerald canopy of the forest below. I needed to keep a close eye on the hunting party.

These were my elites. If they found themselves in a situation they couldn't handle, I had to be ready to intervene.
