Elder Malverin stood alongside the other elders within the watchroom, a wide smile on her face as she stared at the Crimson Vesper Engine—a cathedral of suspended arteries carved from blood crystals.

The weapon was no ordinary relic.

It was a sacrificial war-altar disguised as a weapon. It was hideous—an amalgamation of metal and flesh-resembling fibers capable of draining the life-force of thousands in an instant, and converting that vitality into a single, catastrophic pulse.

It wasn’t their first choice. Nor their second.

But after the Silence Sect had refused to cooperate—those pompous, self-centered bastards—they'd had to resort to more drastic measures.

Three days had passed since she proposed the hunt. And in those three days, they had worked tirelessly to make sure everything went perfectly. This was a one-shot plan. If it failed, they would never get the chance to use it again.

It had to work. It had to work perfectly.

She glanced at the clock, noting they had only five minutes left, before nodding to Elder Mournwind, who seemed ill at ease with the whole affair. Still, he was their de facto leader, the one with the most to lose should the Storm Sect win, and with an expression of hardened resolve, he turned his gaze to the two Mythics chosen for the attack.

"Are you sure you're willing to do this?"

The blood Mythic holding the engine nodded somberly. "Yes. I trust Elder Aspwick to save me."

Aspwick smiled easily. "Of course. We blood cultivators may be great combatants, but we are also the best healers there are. And among them all, I am the best." His smile turned serious as he looked to the second disciple, the illusion Mythic. "When the engine activates, it will rip you both apart. But I will be there to put you back together. You've seen me do it."

The illusion Mythic glanced at Malverin, then hesitantly nodded. "I trust Malverin to hold you to your word."

"I will, Silas." Malverin nodded. "You will live to experience the victory of this sacrifice."

Silas smiled—and with a nod to the blood Mythic, they hefted the Vesper Engine and vanished from all their senses. Not just sight. Every sense. The air itself forgot they had been standing in it.

Malverin shook her head slowly, her smile widening. "Flawless. Absolutely flawless."

Silas had always been a master of illusions, his touch even defter than hers. He didn't merely mask the five senses—he masked spiritual sense, core resonance, spatial disturbances, and even intent perception.

A genius among illusionists.

And so, when the Silence Sect had turned them down, he had been the first person to come to mind. The Storm Sect would never see him or the engine coming. They would be caught completely unaware.

And when Silas succeeded—because he would—this entire war would be over. Balance would be restored. And the world would be right again.

She glanced at the time just as it struck midnight. "One more minute."

"Then we better start moving." The blood Mythic's disembodied voice echoed through the room, and an instant later, the tracker they'd placed on them drifted away from the door.

Malverin turned her gaze to the screen, mapping their progress as they flew toward the Storm Sect while the other elders began calling back their troops. A second later, she did the same.

It was time to retreat.

No one hesitated.

The transmigrants pulled back first, then the Mythics, and finally the Calamities—their withdrawal so sudden and absolute it forced the entire war into confused stillness.

Malverin smiled and directed her attention to the screen tracking Silas and the blood Mythic. They'd reached the viewing deck already.

"Any second now," Elder Clearfall whispered from behind her. But he’d barely even finished speaking when the world split.

There was no gradual build. No swelling hum.

Just—

Light.

Brilliant, blistering light as a pillar of crimson tore through the midnight sky and ripped the Storm Sect's viewing deck apart.

The deck didn't explode so much as implode—runes flashing white as it collapsed inward with a detonation so loud and vast it flattened clouds for miles.

Malverin turned to smile at the others, triumph blooming in her chest.

Finally. After weeks of blood and failure and watching their transmigrants die—finally—they had struck back. The Storm Sect's leadership would be ash. Their transmigrants scattered or—

Thunder cracked the heavens. Reality screamed. And a sound Malverin had dreaded to hear echoed through her mind.

Through all their minds.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SPACE-TIME SOVEREINTY}

Dread pooled within her guts and her triumphant smile died instantly as her worst fears came to pass.

An icon.

A fucking icon.

Who had been foolish enough to use one, knowing the—

She didn’t get to finish her thought before her mind stuttered to a sudden halt, space and time freezing solid around her. It didn't last long—not with so many other Calamities on the battlefield—but it lasted a moment.

And for that moment, it was as if the entire world ceased. The storm above froze. Lightning hung in the air like shards of glass. Winds stopped mid-spiral. Cultivators locked mid-battle, mid-scream, mid-breath.

Then another boom of thunder.

And another.

And another—as dozens of Calamities unleashed their icons to counter the first. It was mutually assured destruction. Each activation spurred the next, and in the span of a single second, dozens of icon manifestations filled the air, their power bearing down on the battlefield with the weight of absolute destruction.

The power of a single icon was already calamitous. Enough to rip an entire battlefield apart.

But now with dozens of them bearing down on the battlefield, absolutely no one could survive.

Not Calamities.

Not Mythics.

No one.

And miles away, in the heart of the battlefield, Darien and Seamus looked up as the heavens caught fire.

Two seconds earlier.

Darien floated high in the sky, a crackling platform beneath his feet, confusion etched across his face.

One moment, he had been battling the serenity transmigrant—trading disruption for equilibrium. The next, Alaric had vanished, teleporting out of the battlefield like a shadow fleeing the sun.

But he wasn't the only one.

All across the battlefield, Mythics were disappearing—retreating as though their lives depended on reaching distance immediately. Darien didn't understand the strategy, but he understood the weight of it. The urgency behind it.

This was a Balance War. Enemies didn't retreat, because retreat meant extinction.

Whatever this was, it wasn't a retreat.

It was a—

The thought dissolved as a pillar of crimson brilliance split the midnight sky, tearing through a mass of roiling clouds dozens of miles away.

Even as the blistering column descended, understanding arrived ahead of it.

There was only one structure in that direction.

The viewing deck.

"No," he breathed, eyes widening as the pillar struck and lit the world in an eruption of gold and crimson.

His heart stuttered—shock and disbelief crashing through him in a single wave.

"No," he said again, louder, the word torn from somewhere deeper than his throat. His body shook with helpless rage, and his eyes began to water as the full weight of that attack settled on him.

His grief never had the chance to properly set in though, because thunder boomed an instant later—not the storm, but a voice echoing through his consciousness like the mandate of a god.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SPACE-TIME SOVEREIGNTY}

The world changed instantly, and this time, Darien felt his entire body grow cold.

Seamus appeared beside him in that instant, eyes wide with terror.

That was the last thing Darien saw before the world ground to a sudden halt—his body, his mind, the sky above, the earth below—all of it stilled. Only to snap back into motion as a second icon boomed through reality.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: CRIMSON APOCALYPSE}

The stranglehold on time and space slackened instantly as a crimson haze bled across the heavens, devouring starlight—an immense, indifferent hunger sweeping the battlefield clean of color.

But while the counter icon slackened the effect of space-time sovereignty, it wasn’t quite enough to shatter it completely. It, however, gave Darien and Seamus back control of their own bodies.

They didn't waste it.

They teleported—in a field still under the influence of space-time sovereignty.

That was their first mistake.

Space wasn’t fluid yet. And rather than unfolding cleanly, the fabric of space tore.

For a fraction of a second, Darien existed in two places. One version remained on the crackling platform while the other forced itself forward through distorted space.

Time buckled.

His vision split into overlapping frames.

Seamus appeared beside him—

And also didn't, his body flickering in and out of alignment with reality.

The world jittered. Space rejected the contradiction.

And an instant later, Darien slammed back into existence several miles from his intended destination. Only this time, he was alone. Locked in place at the center of the battlefield as time struggled to catch up with his body.

Thunder boomed directly above him before he could even understand what was going on, and Darien felt his entire body grow cold as a third icon manifested.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SKYFALL DOMINION}

Then a fourth.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: MOMENT WITHOUT END}

And a fifth.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SUNDERED CONTINUUM}

Like stacked verdicts, the manifestations bore down on the earth with a power so undeniable and inevitable that reality itself shook from the strain.

And Darien was beneath all of it.

There was no escaping it. No surviving it. The war had entered its final form and he was locked at its epicenter with nothing—not even his willpower—to protect him.

With a screech like the world itself being torn open, the icons descended.

Descended onto Darien.

He—a mere advanced cultivator, stripped bare against the ultimate expressions of dozens of Calamities, with nothing to protect himself save the core of his ideal—

—SANCTUM—

The icons descended.

Darien closed his eyes.

And refused to break.

Calamity Zerathis Starless stepped into the burning deck, his body cutting through frozen space like a blade through still water.

The deck was caught mid-implosion—debris suspended in crimson light, flames halted mid-expansion, Mythics frozen mid-horror, screens mid-glitch. Everything locked. The very air crystallized by Space-Time Sovereignty.

And in the center of it all: four transmigrants.

Zerathis smiled, a giddy excitement entirely unbecoming of a Calamity bubbling within him.

Activating his icon for a task a chain of skills could have replicated was, objectively, foolish. No doubt, the enemy calamities would unleash their countering icons soon.

But Zerathis didn’t care.

He had been following this war for three weeks now, tracking every update down to its smallest detail. What else was there for him to do at tier 186, within a vast stretch of space he had explored to his heart's content?

One might assume infinite space meant infinite adventure. But after three hundred years of existence, even the void grew routine—predictable, its mysteries long since exhausted by familiarity.

Zerathis had been sick of it.

And so, he had turned his gaze to the wider world—to a reality they had long neglected in favor of chaotic space, where technically they were alone, with no other sect to befriend or despise.

Zerathis was a political man. A man of schemes and tact. A man of opportunity and indulgent curiosity. And the moment he heard there was a chance to leave the sect for wider space, he had seized it like a man starved of novelty.

Which, to be honest, he was.

A chuckle escaped him at the thought of what he'd done and he let himself feel it fully. The war had grown too predictable. Too tame. He had needed to shake things up—to make it interesting again.

This was bound to do that.

Thunder cracked the heavens as an icon rose against his, but Zerathis’s smile only widened. Willpower flickered within him as he enforced his hold over the deck, leaving the battlefield to drown in the chaos outside.

"Well," he said pleasantly, pushing a fragment of suspended debris out of his path. "This is exciting, isn't it?"

The sound of straining space-time was his only answer, and Zerathis slowly turned, savoring the helpless, frozen look of the Mythics and transmigrants around him.

If he wished—had he been the enemy—he could end them now. A gesture. A thought. A simple shearing of the—

The sovereignty field shook violently as another icon manifested, shattering his hold on the deck completely.

The childlike excitement vanished. The smile with it. Every trace that might have suggested he was enjoying himself disappeared in the same instant, replaced by the composed, deliberate stillness of a Calamity who had lived long enough to know what speed actually meant.

He moved.

In less than a second, he gathered over a hundred people into a temporary space, displaced reality around them, and reappeared miles from the deck just as his hold on space-time shattered.

Time resumed its flow. Energy built dangerously, and the deck imploded, painting the world in a hue of crimson, gold, and orange.

But Zerathis didn't get the chance to admire it.

Around him, a dozen Storm Calamities materialized, their combined pressure enough to thread a genuine sliver of unease into his chest.

Zerathis smiled anyway.

"There's no need for hostility," he said, releasing the displaced mortals back into reality. "I saved them. Gratitude would be appropriate."

"Are you insane?"

One of the Storm Calamities stepped forward, fury rolling off him in violent waves.

"Did we request your intervention? Who granted you the authority to interfere in our war?"

Zerathis's frown was faint, but real. "I fail to see the issue. We are your newest allies—"

"You've jeopardized everything!" The Calamity's voice shook the earth. "You activated an Icon. Do you comprehend what that invites?"

"Do you?" Zerathis snapped—genuine irritation now, not performance—gesturing toward the disoriented transmigrants. "Four transmigrants were moments from death. I corrected that. You should be thanking me."

"We had it under control!" Another Calamity's aura spread outward, shielding the transmigrants from the cascading icon effects. But before the argument could continue, another icon manifested—this one so strong it jolted transmigrants back into awareness.

Their eyes flew open.

One of them, still half-drowned in the fog of unconsciousness, whispered.

"Darien… Seamus…"

"Special disciple Sophran," one of the Calamities began, but the boy wasn't listening. His gaze swept the space around him, taking rapid inventory and arriving at a devastating conclusion.

"They are not here." His voice was stronger now, cutting through the ambient thunder. "Darien and Seamus. Where are they?"

The question drove a spike through the tension, and almost as one, they all looked toward the battlefield.

The Calamities didn't hesitate. Even Zerathis moved—he was here to help, after all—but as he felt the weight of the descending icons multiply, even he understood the arithmetic of it.

He could not hold back several at once.

It was already too late.

And yet—

His lips curved faintly as the heavens began to fracture.

At last.

Something interesting. Even if this Darien and Seamus died, their deaths would not be dull.
