Returning to his room, Ferris settled in to meditate. His mind was full of possibilities. Growing up, he had always wanted to follow his clan’s path. But, as confusing as some of the parts were, the two primers had sparked something: a desire to see more. He had learned of more abilities today than he had in his entire life.

While imagining throwing fireballs or strangling monsters with vines was exciting, it did not help him cultivate. So, with reluctance, Ferris let the thoughts fade away. Eventually he reached the emptiness that revealed his soul.

Opening his eyes, Ferris was now in his soulspace. The two puncture wounds on his side from last night were gone, and the shirt he tore to bind it was like new again. Minor changes would revert to his mental image, given time.

Not much had changed since he’d left. The bodies of the three demon frogs lay next to the empty sack. The Heartfire burned merrily away in its hearth, and the Qi Pool lay serenely still. His lightstone had long faded away.

The statue had grown several new chains, digging into the ground. Ferris inspected them closer. He would have to do something about them soon. He didn’t want the statue to stay here forever, but apparently it wanted to bind itself tightly to a location.

The broken spear was sitting off to the side. If he had the skill, he might’ve been able to repair it, but for now it was easiest to just start from scratch. The first order of business was to create a new weapon.

Ferris looked at the demon bodies. His last spear had not been strong enough, but his uncle had told him about a refining technique to strengthen qi constructs. Everything he had made so far—spear, sack, and the lightstones—were basic constructs made of pure qi. If he used parts of these demons, they would be stronger.

First off, Ferris needed a container. Walking down to the Qi Pool, Ferris sat at the edge and submerged his hands. The chunks of crystallized qi from yesterday had all melted, growing the pool. He visualized a heavy cooking pot large enough to fit one of the frogs inside. Although bigger than yesterday, the Qi Pool was not large, so the amount visibly shrank as he pulled the large pot out with both hands.

Qi forming was strange. The amount of liquid qi used never matched the size of the object. He felt like he could use more or less qi if he wanted for the same sized pot, but there was a natural flow he followed. Experimentation would need to wait until he had more qi, right now every drop was precious.

Pot completed, he filled it halfway with qi, leaving the pool even smaller than it had started yesterday.

The technique his uncle had taught him was simple. Take the body of a demon, then boil it down with an equal amount of liquid qi. Ferris placed one of the demons into his pot. Now, he thought, I just need a way to heat it. The obvious source was his Heartfire, but he did not want to place the pot directly in the fire. Resigned, Ferris used even more qi to construct a frame to hang the pot.

When he brought it over, the frame magically attached itself to the hearth—surprising Ferris. Things in the soulspace seemed to understand his intent in unexpected ways. The hearth now had a hook that he could swing over the fire. Ferris hung his pot on the hook and waited for it to boil.

Typically, a real-life corpse wouldn’t boil down nicely. However, this was a mental construct—not a real body. Just like Ferris’ body was not flesh and bones, the demon’s body was not real. It soon melted and mixed with the liquid qi, and Ferris removed the pot from the heat. The liquid qi—usually a light blue—was now dark blue.

After it had cooled, Ferris placed his hand inside the pot. Using the same qi forming technique as earlier, he visualized a spear.

His new spear materialized as he pulled it out of the pot, until all the liquid was consumed. As before, the volume of liquid qi used did not equal the volume of the final product. Factors like complexity and durability increased the amount of qi required, and Ferris felt he could add more qi for a stronger result—although with diminishing returns.

Strangely, he felt a sort of restriction to his control of the spear’s final size this time. This didn’t stop him, but he hadn’t felt this while qi forming his last spear. The liquid qi could be used for any sized creation, within limits, but he got the impression that the melted demon corpse was more restrictive…

Examining his new weapon, Ferris hoped that it would be much more durable than his last one. He had spent a lot of qi, but it would be worth it if he did not have to constantly remake it.

Setting the spear aside, he half-filled the pot again and melted another demon frog. It was painful to spend even more qi, but Ferris did not see a choice. This time he constructed a heavy axe.

Hefting the axe, Ferris approached the oath statue. In the single day it had existed in his soulspace, it had placed several white chains into the ground. He felt that this was the nature of the oath; it would dig itself into him. Ferris accepted that the oath would be a part of him, but he was not happy with its location. Every day those chains existed would make them stronger.

With several mighty swings, Ferris severed the white chains. He would not be able to harm the statue itself this way, at least not without a lot more effort—and consequences. These chains would grow back, and he would have to cut them again each day until he moved the statue.

Oddly, the stark white chains faded away like mist after being separated from the statue. Strange, but weird things could happen in the soul.

The last demon frog corpse would wait until he had a use for it. At least the thing didn't appear to rot or stink.

Setting the axe down, Ferris grabbed his sack, constructed another lightstone, and then set off to collect qi. When he reached the Air Passage, he climbed down and started off. The water was all gone by now, but the ground was still damp.

It took two trips, but Ferris completely emptied his Air Passage of all qi. He saw several demons at the edge of his light, but none attacked him. It was a nice feeling to see his Qi Pool rise back up as he dumped the sack after each trip.

Growing the Qi Pool was an essential step, at least that was what Uncle Winton had told him. Every time he followed the man’s advice, he remembered the first few days after having become an Adept.

Everyone had been shocked, Ferris most of all. He hadn’t even been trying to unlock his soul. Not really. It just... happened.

It was like he’d lucked into the most coveted prize, but had no way to enjoy it. Ferris had even overheard a few neighbors lamenting over the tragedy of it all. Why not someone else, someone that could actually do something with it? Someone worthy.

Uncle Winton had sat him down, a serious look on the man’s face.

“Ignore those fools,” he had said, “they are just jealous. Focus on your family, we are happy for you. You earned this. Trust me when I say it.”

The two of them had spent long hours together, going over his options. When Uncle Winton offered to be his official mentor, Ferris cried.

Central to his uncle’s guidance to advance to the second tier had been: grow the Qi Pool, open meridians, and purge impurities. Specific details of how would require a suitable cultivation technique—which Winton had claimed required delving resources.

Still, Ferris had resolved to do what he could. Until he started delving, he simply knew he needed to grow the Qi Pool larger. As large as he possibly could.

Hopefully, by the time his uncle visited, he could tell his mentor how well he had listened.

Make him proud.

When Ferris was sure there wasn’t a single chunk of crystalized qi left in his Air Passage, he exited the soulspace and went to sleep, excited to see what else Clan Hollowfield had to offer.

The next morning Ferris arrived to find Elder Harper still reading, but her table had about twice as many books.

“Good morning, Elder Harper,” he said as he approached, wary of the stacks of books—yet curious for the knowledge they held.

“Ah, Ferris Bluereef. I read your reports from yesterday. Your aversion to the fire core was expected, but I urge you to not discount the plant core. We may not have many offensive abilities for it, but you only really need one solid attack skill to take you to tier two. It must be practiced to perfection, and then extended to cover any weaknesses, but it is possible. The rest of your needs could be met with non-aspected abilities.”

Ferris nodded. “Yes, my main concern is the time Regrowth requires.”

“The extra time could also be considered a benefit,” Elder Harper said. “Compared to your eventual path, it is but a moment. You will one day look back wondering how it passed so quickly.”

Ferris tried to keep a straight face, not showing his true feelings. He knew cultivators could live a long time, gaining more life force with each tier. There was no way to know how old Elder Harper was. There was a chance she had already reached tier three when Ferris’ grandfather was born. He didn’t want to insult her, as that would be a colossal mistake, but he did not consider a few years ‘but a moment.’

Either ignoring or not noticing his turmoil, Elder Harper continued.

“Additionally, the plant core gives you a safe backup path in agriculture while you search for and then develop more dangerous abilities.”

“I will keep it in mind.” Ferris replied, trying not to sound too unhappy with a plan that gave up on delving or had him sit around waiting for years. “I was really hoping for something with a water core.” If Ferris couldn’t follow his clan’s martial path of the spear, at least he would be able to use the water magic style.

Elder Harper frowned. “Indeed. Regrettably, I have not found any record of the type of ability you need with a water core.” She waved her hand at the table, and the piles of books. “I am confident such abilities exist, but Hollowfield does not have much knowledge of water cores in general. There are, however, other possibilities,” she handed him two more books, “yet they have their own drawbacks.”

He took the books over to his table, both disappointed and excited to learn more. The first book was not dedicated to a core aspect type like the previous books had been, but instead it described an item. Specifically, it was about a prosthetic hand.

Reading the introduction, the idea was to forge an elaborate artificial hand with a core embedded inside. Once bonded, the cultivator would find a Possess Limb ability—at least in all recorded cases—that made the hand move like real. This ability did not give full range of motion at first, but it improved over time.

Ferris’ eyes were wide as he read the ability’s description. Not only would it allow for full manipulation of the prosthetic, but eventually it would give tactile feedback. It described the sensations as odd, not the same as the real thing, but still incredible.

The book read more like a family history rather than the generic descriptions of the two primers from yesterday. Apparently, a cultivator named Felix Grey had been in a similar situation as Ferris, losing his hand before bonding a core. He had forged a new path for himself, and in the process discovered an amazing ability.

This ability, called Magic Hand, was a spellcasting ability. Spells were much more versatile than normal magical abilities of a bonded weapon… somehow. Although Ferris didn’t know the details, he knew that a cultivator with a spellcasting ability could create many different magical effects. Most early delvers only learned a couple single purpose abilities. Felix’s Magic Hand ability granted him a great deal of flexibility.

This resonated with Ferris, as a secret spellcasting ability was a core part of Clan Bluereef’s water magic. He didn’t know how it worked, but it was a central part of the clan’s martial tradition. Ferris hadn’t even been told about it until after he unlocked his soul and became an Adept. Uncle Winton’s casual ability to use water magic for drying Ferris’ clothes was an example of a spell. Sometimes the man seemed to have magic for any occasion.

Several of Felix’s descendants had attempted to develop the path into a martial tradition, going so far as to self-amputate their own hand.

Ferris had to stop reading and process that for a moment. To imagine, someone choosing to do to themselves what had happened to him…

The room started spinning. He closed his eyes and took several measured, deep breaths, until the nausea subsided.

Only his desire to learn everything he possibly could let him push aside his feelings and return to reading. However, the book felt a bit colder than before. Instead of feeling like an exciting story, it was a tragedy.

Unfortunately for Felix’s descendants, no one other than him was able to get the Magic Hand ability, and new cultivators eventually stopped trying. Ferris didn’t blame them.

There were other, easier, ways to acquire a spellcasting ability. The classic example was to bond a book—like Cyrus back at Lily House—which would let the cultivator create different spells on each page. The details likely varied, but it was a very common bond.

For Felix’s descendants to go as far as they did, there must have been a reason. Not all abilities were equal, and Magic Hand must have been quite special.

The advantages of a prosthetic were numerous. If the leg worked like Felix’s hand he could somewhat walk immediately, with no additional investment of time or resources. Even better, he could choose any core aspect type he wanted. Which meant he could have a water core and pursue at least half of his clan’s martial tradition.

On the other hand, the disadvantages were equally numerous. Felix Grey had lucked out with his magic ability, there was no telling what abilities a prosthetic leg would give. There might be powerful kicking attacks, but Ferris had never felt drawn to this kind of combat.

Lastly, the Possess Limb ability was a risk. It had only been shown to appear with prosthetic hands—and that was with a small sample size. Even then, it needed time and resources to become graceful. If he did receive the ability, it would probably be stiff at first. If I can’t run, how can I delve to improve the ability?

Finished with the book on prosthetics, Ferris wrote out his thoughts and moved on. The next book was even stranger. While the prosthetic book had been very specific, this one was more generic. It didn’t cover bonded items at all…

Instead, it was an introduction to familiar bonds.
