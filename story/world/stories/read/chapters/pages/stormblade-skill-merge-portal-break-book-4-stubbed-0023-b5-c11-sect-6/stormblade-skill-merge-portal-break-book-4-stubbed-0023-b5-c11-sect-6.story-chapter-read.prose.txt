Stormbreak fired, the flowers of lightning erupting around me, Retan, and three others—all of whom were joining the fight from the only way out, and all of whom were S-Rank. My Unique skill was strong for a B-Rank move, and electricity rippled through the oncoming S-Rankers, Retan, and me. I gritted my teeth and rode it out; one nice thing about S-Rank was that the skill still hurt, but it wasn’t debilitating.

It hit the incoming S-Rankers just as the light barriers blocking my escape vanished and they rushed in, stopping them in their tracks. I was already in Torrentborne even before Stormbreak ended, moving toward them as fast as Stormrunner would carry me.

Not fast enough, though. Retan slammed the gates shut again, stopping me in my tracks. I used Mist Trance to dodge his light blade—and the echoing blade of light that gouged a razor-thin gash in the wall behind me almost two feet deep. As soon as my body rematerialized, he opened the gates, and the S-Rankers flooded in.

I was outgunned.

One wielded a stone blade much like the one auctioned off earlier. As it swung at me, its blade grew in both size and weight; this wasn’t a fencer’s weapon, and I couldn’t treat it like one. I dodged—right into a thrust from a swordswoman whose saber split into nine different blades, punching into my storm armor. And the third shifted between me and the door, forming a labyrinth of swords mid-air around him.

Space. I needed space—but the treasure room had no space. Retan’s light-based traps activated and deactivated as I danced away from four powerful swordfighters’ blades, cutting off retreats and opening up angles for them to attack me.

Torrentborne helped. Mist Trance bought me time.

Not enough, but some.

“Noelstra, you’re making me make difficult choices,” Retan growled. Light swelled around him, and his avatar form erupted around him.

I summoned the Stormkin Avatar and Cheddar in response. “Likewise. I’m just trying to help my—“

“Partner. I know.” The Paragon moved. His avatar copied his motion, acting like a magical combat suit. Dad’s shield came up. It caught the light blade, but the sheer force of the blow still cut a massive gouge out of the circle of electricity. I didn’t have time to watch his counter-attack, though. Cheddar’s sunbeam vomited across the room, only for Retan to grab it mid-air and send it at me like a javelin.

It burned a hole in the floor as I dodged.

The massive stone sword cut the air to my left, and a half-dozen copied sabers slammed into my armor. Even with its maelstroms and lightning, it took the beating…poorly. So did my ribs. Something cracked, and I hit the ground hard.

Roll.

Another blow from the stone sword. An arcing blade of bright light. Nine sabers. All closing in from different directions. No Mana—or at least not enough to do anything yet.

I chose the stone sword. The impact sent me flying, but it was only one hit. Even better, the impact got me moving, and I rolled with it despite the pain.

Distance.

Space.

A four-on-one fight was already going to be tough. So was Retan—both because it was his home turf and because he outranked me. But both together? Not possible. Change of plans, then.

I shifted from searching for ways to stall to searching for ways to disengage. But every time I tried, a light wall was there. I couldn’t even get close to the treasure house’s walls without running into barriers and traps thick enough to force me to retreat.

“I know what you’re thinking. Step through the wall, step to freedom. Won’t work, though. It’s bound against teleporters and similar. And against ghostwalkers, too,” Retan said. He’d killed the Stormkin Avatar, and I hadn’t even noticed. I whirled to dodge another light blade as he kept talking. “The only way in or out is the front door. And as for your summon? He’s part of your aura, and I am a master of auras.”

I steeled myself. He was right; there wasn’t a good way out. His own Avatar pushed out, a glowing point of light that forced massive shadows behind the other three swordfighters and me.

Then the shadows started swimming, and a second later, Ellen stepped out of one, with Tanat right behind her.

“Sorry we’re late,” Ellen said. “It took a while to track her down.”

Tanat shot a glare back at Ellen, then turned it on Retan. “What are you doing with that Mana stone? Where are the Soaring Saber’s treasures?”

She paused. Her glare deepened, then hardened into something different. “Are these outsiders correct about what happens to the inner school’s students? Do you kill them and harvest their cores?”

The question hung in the air. So did a dozen different swords—five in people’s hands, nine for flying around the split-saber swordswoman. I’d landed a few blows, but only the Hand of the Thunder God and Stormbreak had mattered. But Tanat’s questions had cut the fighting off completely. The S-Rankers’ gazes moved from Tanat to me and then to Retan.

Retan stared at her. His eyes flickered to each of the S-Rankers in turn, and I saw the calculations in his eyes. I’d been making the same one: could I beat Retan, and then, could I beat Retan and his three S-Rankers? Now, he was asking himself if he could beat the S-Rankers, Tanat, Ellen, and me.

“Ellen, I need your help,” I muttered to her. “He’s using light barriers to seal the room. We need a gap in them so we can escape.”

“Where?” she asked.

“The door.”

“On it.”

Retan didn’t answer Tanat with words. He lunged, light blade arcing through the air to cut at her. I moved and deflected the hit up into the ceiling. Shadows flickered across the floor, but they seemed washed out. Fading to nothing. And, behind me, Ellen disappeared in a gigantic pillar of darkness.

I almost tried to stop her. She’d used the Tides of Darkness before—once. It had turned the tide of the Siege of Phoenix, but it had also left her core weak and fractured. But this wasn’t the same Ellen who’d used that aura, and it wasn’t the same core that had broken from it.

So, instead, I focused on defending her while she cast.

Stormrunner. Lightning Dance. My sword was everywhere an attack rained down on her. Light slammed into my blade, and I redirected it. A hammer-blow of stone crashed down on me. Tanat’s sword caught it, driving her feet into the stone floor hard enough to crack it. She spun. Three flying sabers cut across her face. Blood spewed across the room.

I ignored it. Ignored when she hit the ground. All I could do for her was keep Ellen safe while she pushed her aura into the light-filled entry hall. Shadow fought with light, and the shadow—at least for a moment—won.

That was all we needed.

I grabbed Tanat. Ellen broke into a sprint. And we erupted outward from the treasure hall and onto a dueling pitch covered in chaos.

It was madness.

The crowds of A-Rankers had come ready for duels, not for fighting—and certainly not for four S-Rankers and their sect leader, plus an A-Ranker with an incredibly powerful ability—to erupt from the treasure house, hammering at each other for all they were worth. Worse, there was no clear enemy. Was it Ellen and me? Tanat? The S-Rankers currently under attack by Retan?

There were no rules. No battle lines. The perfect storm for an escape—made even better by the wave of darkness emanating from Ellen as  Tides of Darkness rolled across the dueling pitch.

I let her go first, shoving Tanat at her. The woman bled from her head wound, but all I could do for her right now was hope it wasn’t bad enough to kill her. She was still stumbling, but still upright, so that seemed like a good bet. All around, panicked A-Rankers went for their swords, but the chaos worked in our favor—no one knew who the enemy was.

Retan’s avatar surged outward from the treasure room’s entrance, exploding upward in a pillar of light. I whirled to face it. “Ellen, Tanat, go!”

But he didn’t attack me. Instead, the pillar of light slammed its sword down, into the treasure house—where a stone blade caught it. The shockwave from the sheer impact sent A-Rankers flying. Not one of them was ready for a fight like this.

“He’s trying to kill you all!” Tanat screamed from the stairs. “Everyone, run! Master Retan has gone mad!”

It was the perfect thing to say. The panic around us turned to full-blown terror—and that terror only increased as I finished casting a full Hand of the Thunder God combo and fired it straight into his back.

It didn’t kill him. But it sent him staggering, and a pair of flying sabers cut into his shoulders. He whirled. A head rolled. Blood spurted from it as it flew through the air, and the flying swords dropped to the ground. At the same time, smoke curled around Retan’s body, and his eyes glowed red from the attack—just like they had before. He looked the picture of a madman.

“Kade, hurry it up,” Ellen shouted from the stairs. Darkness whirled around her, and pockets of black cloud filled the space between us. I plunged into the spells and erupted out the other side. An S-Ranker with a sword made from liquid metal rushed me; I ignored her except to defend myself until I could separate in the crushing, panicked throng of people.

From there, it took only time to escape. Up, up, and even higher up the canyon walls we climbed until, just as the twin suns broke over the horizon, we stepped through the portal back to Earth.

We’d done it. Tanat was safe. And even more importantly, Ellen’s core was intact. She was back and stronger than ever. Soon, she’d be S-Rank.

A half-hour later, the four of us sat in the Desert Wind Guild’s kitchen. Tanat’s face was covered with one jagged cut that had split her nose, destroyed one of her eyes, and ripped her red lips apart, but her delver’s healing was already working. She had a cloth pressed against the wound, but the bleeding had stopped.

Jessie couldn’t stop staring at her, and I couldn’t blame my sister. Tanat was striking. Thin, long-limbed, and the one eye that peeked out from the bandages was jet black. She looked nothing like anyone my sister had ever met. I just hoped she wouldn’t say anything—

“So, you’re the alien, huh? Hi, alien.”

…dumb.

I didn’t quite facepalm, but it was a close thing. Ellen shot Jessie a look, but before either of us could say anything, Tanat’s eye locked onto Jessie’s. “I assume by your appearance that you are Kade Noelstra’s sister.”

“Rude! I’m not anywhere near as ugly as him,” Jessie said, winking at me.

“Yes, you are his sister. I have heard you are a fellow meditation enjoyer. We should exchange processes. I may be able to learn from you.”

“I wouldn’t say I’m an enjoyer,” Jessie said, “but I’m pretty experienced. It’s a pain mitigation tool for me, not something I use to clear my mind or anything. But sure, we can sit down and practice sometime. Wait, does that mean you’ll be here for a while?”

“Yes. I have nowhere else to go,” Tanat said quietly. “I will stay as long as you can have me.”

Jessie turned to me, eyes bright. “We’re keeping her, right?”

“She’s not a puppy,” I said quickly. “But yes, that was my plan. I wanted to bring her to the Black Tower and have her help me with the Eye of the Storm. If she could do that, I could help Ellen get caught up.”

“And the Desert Wind would have three S-Rankers. The GC would have to take me seriously then!” Jessie said.

“They’re not taking you seriously? I wonder why?” Ellen snarked.

“It’s because you’re twelve,” I said quickly. Two could play at her games.

“I’m sixteen. Almost an adult. They should be treating me like it, but they’re brushing me off. Kade, you’ve gotta help me out here!”

“May I interrupt?” Tanat asked. “Kade, you said you would show me the Eye of the Storm, not put me in charge of it.”

I nodded. “Plans change. Unfortunately, I don’t have a lot of time. As bad as Retan is, he’s nothing compared to my former master, and I need your help. Will you do it?”

She didn’t nod. But she also didn’t say no. Instead, she looked me in the eye. “Show me the Eye of the Storm, and I will decide if I can help you or if you have to make it work on your own.”

Ellen coughed. “I’ll go with you. I’m curious about just what you’re doing, and just how you’re doing it. And I need to spend some time exploring my core and seeing what changes the reforging process actually made.”

“Well, that settles that. Jessie, we’ll be back in a couple of hours. Go ahead and excuse your absence from school tomorrow.”

“Actually, I was really hoping you’d take a look at this problem I’ve got. It’s something I really need your advice on, Kade.”

I was already heading for the door, though, with Tanat and Ellen right behind me. We had a lot of work to do, and I was just going to have to trust that Jessie would make good decisions here on Earth.

Jessie was annoyed.

She hadn’t wanted Kade’s help because she wanted to spend time with her brother. Sure, that’d be nice. He’d been so busy, first with the tournament, then the Siege and the tournament, and then the counterattack on the Carlsbad portal, that he hadn’t had the time for her. And truthfully, she didn’t mind most of the time. She was sixteen now, after all—practically a grown-up version of herself. And, more importantly, she had plenty of stuff to keep herself occupied. The ‘normal’ stuff a guildmaster had to deal with, for one thing. Council meetings. Portal runs. Cleaning companies.

Delvers sure made a mess, and the cleaning companies charged a fortune and a half to keep the Desert Wind Guild’s building clean.

But, in addition to that normal stuff, she also had the complex political dance with the Portal Tyrants and the Governing Council, and Kade just didn’t seem interested in it. She was trying to understand, she really was, but it was so frustrating. The whole reason she’d facilitated this mission was to get Ellen up and running again, and Kade had come through and then some with a new S-Rank ally. They needed to sit down and talk through how Tanat changed their strategy on Earth.

Jessie was pretty sure it’d cause a crisis.

That was fine, though. It wasn’t like everything else wasn’t going to cause a crisis. One more wasn’t a big deal in the larger picture.

No, Jessie didn’t even care that Kade didn’t want to do that. She’d corner him about it later. But she really needed help with an email that’d popped up on her official guild master email address. About a half-dozen emails, actually, but only one really put the pressure on her.
