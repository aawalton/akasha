The wardrobe I had emerged from was impressive, to say the least.  Dark wood that seemed to glow faintly, and enough gilt edging to appease the snobiest of the nobility, gave it a feeling of expensiveness that I couldn’t help but approve of.  I plinked a finger against it and nodded in satisfaction at the solid thumping sound it created.

“Nice wardrobe.  Philpott, I’d like to reiterate my question: why the hell didn’t you just speak to me in the dungeon?”

“Hi, Bob.”  He adjusted his untied kimono nervously.  “Well, it’s complicated.”

“You aren’t even a bloody mammal, so you don’t have that excuse for being ‘complicated’,” I growled.  “And do that belt up!  I am not spending yet another conversation staring at what you think passes for acceptable underwear.”

“You know The Fence?” asked a woman with a long scar running down her left cheek.  With her dark hair, green eyes and disturbingly symmetrical face, she would be a true beauty if it weren’t for that mark.  Her head swung round to Philpott, and one eyebrow quirked upward.

“We’ve had interactions before,” he said.  “Bob, I can’t speak for my fellow Captains; it had to be here.  This is Pocket and Burglar.”  Pocket was the woman, and Burglar was the other man in the room aside from Alad.

Burglar nodded to me.  A thin man with a long nose, he looked like he had dripped out of a pipe.  His light grey clothing glistened slightly, as though damp.

“Wasn’t Pocket wearing that hat a second ago?” I asked.

“Dammit!” Pocket snapped, and the floppy-brimmed purple hat reappeared on her own head.

“Bandit and Slitter are away on business, but they won’t be needed for this job,” Burglar growled in a deep voice.  “This one will be a smash and grab.”

“What one?  Philpott, you’ve dragged me halfway across the empire by the slowest form of transportation imaginable.  It was pleasant, don’t get me wrong, I needed a break, but I know that you know what’s going on out in the west.  We don’t have time for silly games with your thieving mates.”

“I like him,” said Pocket.  Her hat was once again on Burglar’s head, but I chose not to mention it this time.  I was fairly confident it had been Burglar who had the eye patch a few seconds ago.

“That’s sweet, lady, but I don’t like you.”

“Bob, this is all tied up with the Changer.  This is going to win you the empire, so you can go face that thing down,” Phipott said, carefully holding his kimono closed.

They were each sitting in large leather armchairs, and a small table lay between them and another chair.  Fighting down the urge to burn every thief in sight, I lowered myself into the vacant seat and tried to watch all of their hands at once.

“You’re a thief,” I accused.  Gods I hated thieves, so my words were thick with betrayal.

“I’m more on the disposal end of things, not acquisitions.  That is where my colleagues shine.”

“Don’t use that beautiful word in this company,” I snapped.  “You are a leader in the Thieves Guild; that makes you a thief.”

“Thieves are a vital element of any functioning society,” Burglar said with a shrug.  Not only had he acquired Pocket’s hat, but now one of her big loop earrings was hanging from his left ear.

“What is this heresy?”

“Hardly heresy.  The unauthorised reallocation of wealth and materials is often necessary, Bob.  People who acquire things unfairly are unlikely to give them up freely, are they?”  Pocket had added Burglar’s silver necklace to her attire in addition to his eyepatch.

“Stealing is wrong.  If any of you chucklefucks even think about nicking anything on my person, I’ll burn you alive.”

“Where do you think I get most of the goods for my shop from?” Philpott asked.

“I assumed there was some cash and carry retailer of magical crap, and you had an account.  Don’t tell me all your stuff is nicked?”

“Only the best things,” he said.  “The hard-to-get things.  The things that cannot be left in the hands of their current owners.”

“For example, House Delaney had managed to get their grubby mitts on the Kaliope Konfounder.  Ancient artefact, very powerful, etc., etc.  That could not be allowed to stand, so my predecessor ensured that they weren’t able to use it to make everyone in the city choose to forgo the concept of clothing.”  Burglar now had the silver-brocaded cape that Pocket had been wearing, and had reacquired his eyepatch somehow.

“It wouldn’t have bothered you, Fence, would it?” I sneered.  “Could you just cross your legs and then not move until after I’m gone, please?

“The point we’re making is sometimes a spot of burglary or larceny is a positive social force that ensures a satisfactory redistribution of resources that could not be officially sanctioned.  We’re basically civil servants,” Pocket said.

“I assume this Slitter you mentioned handles the messier side of the job?”

“He’s your biggest fan, by the way.  I mean, we all hold you in high regard, but after what you did to the forgotten library, Slitter has been up other people's eyeballs in work,” Burglar said.  He frowned and felt at his chest, but the chain for his pocket watch was no longer on his person.

Pocket checked the time and slipped the device into a breast pocket.  “And the removal of Big Kenny has made all our lives sooo much easier.  That really was a godsend. It opened up the docks to the Guild.  So I would like to personally reassure you that you will never be the target of a Guild operation.  Your hoard is safe from us, I swear on the system.”

A shower of golden sparks exploded from my body.  I wasn’t sure it was Wrath or Greed that was responsible.  While these people were the blackest sort of villains imaginable, professional thieves, if they would promise to stay away from me and mine, they could be useful for increasing my already vast hoard.

Perhaps not as efficient as figuring out a way to make the alchemist no longer need to sleep or eat.  Unfortunately, Kat had refused to allow me to turn him into a zombie, so in the meantime, this might be a good opportunity.  When I won the war, I’d be in charge of this vast pustule of a city, so I’d need control over these people.

“I’m not sure we’ve seen the last of Big Kenny.”  Might as well keep a threat hanging over them.  “I’ve killed him twice; maybe the third time is the charm.”

“Perhaps,” said Burglar, now wearing Pocket’s grey shirt over his own.  No, she’d stolen his shirt at the same time, somehow.  “Denarios, courtesy of his catspaw, our beloved Fence, has recapitalised The Bank.”

“I bloody recapitalised the bank,” I grumbled, and Greed cried a little inside my mind.

“Either way, Lucratis are now facing some real competition, but it won’t be enough unless we do something a little… extra to stir the pot,” Philpott said quickly.

“I’ve got a war on, Philly, or Fencey, or whatever you want to call yourself.  This needs to be sorted quickly.”

“Nothing in life is ever quick and easy,” Burglar added.

“Not what your wife says, buddy.  So what’s your big plan then?”

“A… reallocation of resources is needed.  Nay, required.  And we need someone with your talents to make it happen.”

I snorted, and a brief shower of sparks exploded around me.  “In case you hadn’t noticed, I’m not so good at the subtle, even when Bulb decides to lay off me for a few days so I can dick about in an over-glorified eating contest.”

“We don’t need you for subtlety.  We’ve got that covered.”  I glanced at Pocket and saw that all the items they’d been stealing from each other were now back with their original owners.  Nothing had been flying through the air; it had just materialised whenever one of them decided to steal something.

“Is this why you wear so little clothing?” I asked Philpott.

“Hmm?  Oh no.  I just like the fresh air on my short and curlies.  It’s a question of storage space, Bob.  Bags of holding are fantastic, and some of them can hold quite a lot of material, but we’re thinking… bigger than they can handle.”

“I was warned, by Tex way back when, that the Thieves Guild would try to entrap me when they figured out I had a pocket dimension.  I’m not going to get sucked into being your bloody mule.”

“Nothing like that!”  Burglar held up his hands.  “We’d even be willing to let you keep, say, half of the haul.”

“Which bunch of degenerate nobles has some superweapon you want to take?” I asked with a sigh.  “It’s got to be something like that.  Someone has a dildo that makes every vagina within a thousand miles melt?”

“Do you know where Orville’s Orgasmodo has been hidden away?” Pocked asked with rather more eagerness than I would have anticipated.

“Of course, there is actually something like that in this world.  No, Pocket, I don’t.  If it’s not that, it’s going to be a knife so sharp that it cuts reality.”

“It was a vegetable peeler, called Just The Skin, but that too is thankfully lost to mankind,” Philpott said.  “We need to carry out a little heist, but it will involve removing rather a lot of very heavy metal.  We just don’t have the bags to make it work.  But your pocket dimension makes it doable.”

“Heavy metal, you say?”

“Heavy,” Pocket said with a hint of a smile forming.

“Is it shiny?”

“Oh, it’s the shiniest,” Burglar added.

“And kind of yellow?”  My voice had gone up half an octave, and I was showering sparks like a Catherine wheel.

They all nodded solemnly.

“Why didn’t you open with that part?” I demanded.

“Fence has informed of your attitude towards theft in general and thieves in particular,” Pocket said with a shrug.

“And we needed you in the city in a way that wouldn’t alert the Mages Guild,”  Phipott said quickly.  “It had to be like this, Bob.”

“I could have used the public portals!”

“No, they’re all being watched.  Nyal has been catching the spies who try to sneak into the dungeon.  We’re up to twenty-four thus far.”

“What happened to them?”  This was not something that had been included in the increasingly long-winded reports I received from Kat and Mrs Sanderson.  Reports that due to their exponentially increasing length, I almost entirely ignored.  If something was important, they could mention it in person.

“Most of them went to Tim for… reeducation.”

“Jesus.  He’s branched out from unholy abominations and magic undies?”

“Simeon is worse.  He’s cooked up these potions that let him—”

“I don’t want to know!”  While it may have been done in my name, as long as I was ignorant of the details, it couldn’t be applied to my karmic balance.  Probably. “ I’m here now, and you want to steal a shit…load…of…gold…  Lucratis?”

“Got it in one,” said Burglar.  “We’re assembling a crack team.  There’ll be seven of us, including you.  Pocket and I will both be in on the operation, but we’ll need to bring in some real talents.  We’ve got it all worked out, we just need you to agree.”

“And I’m just the bag man?  I can fight, I guess, but if the powers-that-be figure out I’m here, I’ll be disappearing through a blue portal before the Immortals can gang up on me.”

“All we need is that lovely storage power of yours; we’ll do the rest.”

“And I will be keeping half.”   I did not phrase it as a question.  This would balance my books after helping Philpott prop up The Bank.

“Right down the middle.  After reasonable deduction for health insurance, retainers for the crew, wear and tear on equipment, that kind of thing,” Pocket said brightly.

“That doesn’t sound like half to me.  Maybe I should get Mrs Sanderson in to handle the negotiations?”

Philpott blanched.  “No, no, that won’t be necessary.  Upfront costs can come from the Guild's share.”

“And I need a contract signing, making sure you guys are team Bob all the way, and forever.”
