About a day and a half later, I come upon a small oasis. The scenery is quite beautiful, as the area teems with life despite the relatively desolate rest of the region. In other words, the perfect place to take a break, and do some really intense thinking.

“Yip!” Luma says as I let her out of the phylactery. Within minutes, she runs over next to the water. She looks like she is glancing out at something.

“What is it?” I ask, coming up behind the little fox. She raises one of her paws and points out towards the center of the oasis. I squint my eyes and just barely see a glint on the water. “Hmm, now what could that be?”

“Yip, Yip!” Luma says and jumps around by my feet.

“Fine, I’ll go get it.” I am not the biggest fan of swimming, and it seems neither is Luma. But now my curiosity has definitely gotten the better of me. I take off Stelan and dive into the water in just my tunic, pants and of course, cloak. The water is damn near freezing, but my cloak does its job and keeps me warm somehow.

Once underwater, I quickly spot what it is I am looking for. It looks like a large silver column has buried itself in the bottom of the oasis. Now, should I be careful about touching random shit that is likely some sort of artifact? Definitely.

Will I be careful? Fuck no.

The water moves around me quite effortlessly, and I quickly reach the object. I Identify it and get back a response.

[Astralite Fragment (Rare)]

I can sense Identify trying to give me a description, but it seems my skill isn't powerful enough to read the material. Though what is interesting is that this is my first time encountering some random metal out in the wilds. Sounds like yet more info I don't have. I reach out my hand and touch the metal, and it feels smooth, and powerful. This ain’t some random stuff, this is the stuff of myths, or so I think.

Using my dagger, I cut away the stone it is buried in. To my shock, the column starts to float up to the surface once it is freed. I float up alongside it and the surface, sucking in a deep breath of air. Not that I technically need to breathe, as my helmet provides me drowning immunity. But there is something satisfying about breathing, makes one feel alive, I suppose.

I slowly tug the rather large column to the side of the water, and heave it onto dry land. Luma is immediately at my side and sniffing it at. “Yip?”

“Yeah yeah, this stuff does seem quite valuable. Now that I think about it, I haven’t crafted anything in a while. Though is there really anything I desperately need? Gloves perhaps, but I don’t really have a good core material I don’t think.”

“Hey master, I have an idea.” Dovey declares.

“Well spit it out, then.”

“I have been sort of uh, not very helpful. Especially against the monsters in this area, their armor is just too tough. So what if you made something for me instead? Didn’t you say you could make us gear too?”

“That is… a shockingly good idea.” I say, “Dovey did you get some sort of massive intelligence boost or something?”

“Meanie! I am a genius bird, and always have been. You are just too metalheaded to see it, hmph.”

Perhaps I have been a bit mean to Dovey. He has helped me out quite a lot, after all. Not to mention that he is sometimes funny.  While it would feel weird to outright compliment him, I can give him something cool instead.  My mind starts to move faster and faster as I think about the perfect piece of equipment. Now, I can’t say I've ever designed stuff for a dove, but Dovey ain’t any random dove.

He is Dovey, after all.

First though, I need to cut up this astralite. Which is easier said than done. My dagger can cut it, but it is a painfully slow process. But the process of cutting the column apart and putting it in my inventory is quite enlightening in and of itself. With each piece I become more and more familiar with the material.

I learn that it is really fucking strange. The metal seems to have a strange property, some sort of magic absorption. But rather than nullifying the magic, it seems to take on its properties. For instance, when I cut it with my dagger, it resists whatever harvesting effect. But one time I accidentally bumped my arm into the column, and it damn near cut right through my wrist, even while completely blunt.

Which gives me a really good idea. Dovey was complaining about needing to be able to damage anything. So I lift up one of the cut pieces of astralite and start to focus. I activate my Imbuement skill and feel the world around me fade away.

I reach out a thread of mana into the strange material, and it is like I feel a response back. It is like it is telling me something, and I get the sense that it can do exactly what I am imagining.

Exiting the weird imbuement thing, I instantly sink deeply into meditation. There I start to blueprint. At first I started with what are flat plates of astralite. Even in my weird mental space, the material is strange. But I work with it nonetheless. Firstly, I shrink the plates until they are quite tiny, and I make them really thin. Thin enough that the relatively lightweight material becomes practically weightless.

Which is important, as I then curve the plates into what I am thinking of as armor. Not armor for the body though, but for the wings. My idea is that they will rest around Dovey’s wings, and due to the properties of astralite, they will amplify his magic somehow. Plus if I also give them some sort of sharp quality, they could be used to cut things.

To connect the two pieces together, I bring some of the Infernal Spider’s Silk into the picture. I wrap it around like a harness, which will likely have a side effect of providing some defense. But at the same time, the stuff is flexible enough to not impede any of Dovey’s agility.

As for the core, well I make what some might consider a controversial decision. I select an Infernal Hound Orb, one that I got from my time in the dungeon. Naturally, it is very heavily fire-aligned. But after forging Stelan, I have come to what I think is an important realization.

The affinity of the materials used in armor do not have to reflect the wielder, or so I think. It can be helpful to do so, naturally. But it isn’t some sort of super hard requirement. Especially when someone wants a specific effect that would otherwise not work. For instance, if I only make stuff out of light-affinitied materials, then I will never get something with the sheer destructive potential that fire magic could offer. I am already employing that idea with the astralite, which is certainly not of the air affinity.

I anchor the orb right in the front-center of the harness. Thankfully the magic of the system allows it to be sized down a bit. I was really worried about it being too heavy. But it is small enough to not be an issue now, I think.

With a few finishing touches, the blueprint settles firmly in my mind. I exit meditation and sigh. “Dovey, you are getting a big upgrade.”

“Ohmygosh, thank you, master!” The bird says and jumps into my chest, nuzzling me with his beak. I awkwardly try to hold him, but he really is smaller than I thought. He can basically fit in just one of my hands. Luckily I don’t have to be too precise on sizing for gear, whatever system fuckery automatically adjusts the size of gear, within some limits.

“First though, I need to craft it.”

“Of course! Is there anything I can do to help?” Dovey asks.

“Hmm, do you think you could find some firewood? I’ll want a campfire to keep monsters away while I work. Arguably it's really stupid to even do it out in the wilds, but I figure you can keep a pretty good watch too.”

“Yip yip!” Luma adds on, saying she will help too.

Dovey leaps out of my arms, “As you wish, master! I’ll be right back.”

While Dovey goes to find firewood, I set to the process of pulling out my domain.  The process is quite simple, I merely think of the skill and give a sort of yank. With a lovely popping sound, a crystal falls out of my chest and I catch it in my hands. Taking a few steps out from the water, I set the crystal down in the ground and pulse a bit of mana into it. The domain is quite small, so it barely requires anything.

With another satisfying pop, the domain expands outwards and it feels like I am enveloped within myself. A contented sigh escapes me, I really missed this feeling. I wonder if the leaders of cities always feel this sorta thing. I think I remember hearing that cities are basically just giant domains.

Though there is likely some other mechanism underneath. Otherwise when the leader dies, the entire city would be lost. There must be some secret to it. Not that I am particularly interested in finding out, I ain’t no citybuilder.

With the domain settled in, I sit down and wait for Dovey to get back. I saw some trees on my way here, and Dovey had a much better vantage point from the air. This place may be quite barren, but I am sure Dovey will find something.
