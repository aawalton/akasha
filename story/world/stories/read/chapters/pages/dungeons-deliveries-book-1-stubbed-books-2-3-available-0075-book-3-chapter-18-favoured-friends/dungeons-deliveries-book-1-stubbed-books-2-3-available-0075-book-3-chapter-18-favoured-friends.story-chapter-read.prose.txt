The receipt printer spat out another picture of a dungeon-spawn hybrid. Sighing, Aria tore it free, reached up high with her doll-sized frame, and placed it near the others on their wall of targets.

Staring up at the hundreds of similar images was overwhelming. Animals mashed together with rotted monsters, dripping and oozing even in picture, every one of them crazy-eyed and snarling. They weren’t even sure where these monsters were, and now they were supposed to find and fight them? How were they supposed to rid the world of this much evil? And now with the Tax Guild Stone loose and all of Toronto on the hunt? There was only so much time.

“Turn it off,” Aria said while analyzing the wall of Baddies. “Just…turn it off.”

Beepy made a deflated, dramatic noise behind her back, and before the printer could deliver another black and white image, it whirred down. Her brothers had thankfully turned off their Skill.

Ever since Beepy and Zippy had leveled their [Better Together] Skill to level 10, the range of horrors deposited into the Paladrone Clan hideout seemed to have expanded greatly. Now they received meanie-bobeanies from vaster distances, and there was seemingly no quarry for their company of three.

It had gotten to the point that her brothers didn’t want to use their Skill. While it made them operate at a drastically improved level, with their attacks and defense perfectly synchronized and their timings harmonized, using it also meant the printer would send them more. Didn’t the printer understand how exhausting it was to clear a nest of horrible, terrible people committing unspeakable acts, only to come back and find their Paladrone Clan hide out a mess of pictures on the floor?

Exhausted and overwhelmed was an understatement. It’d even taken plenty of energy to fake their excitement and mischief when Mary, their Creator, had called to warn them to not go out and get hurt while the Tax Guild Stone hunt is on.

Some devious meeting we’re having…Aria thought.

Zippy clicked open and closed a [Ancient Paring Knife], the tick he possessed and the sound grating on her nerves. Beepy sighed loudly again, the drone star fishing on the table, likely staring into space and preparing a speech.

Aria shook the frustration out of her head and clenched her jaw, thinking hard, and looked over the wall of monsters. There were others as well, pictured and organized to the left. Likely criminals with fetid souls that must be dealt. Their number just seemed so tiny in the face of the endless pictures of these monstrous beasts.

“Where are you all?” She wondered quietly, clenching her hand open and closed. “Where the hell are you all hiding out?”

She could keep making more weapons. Another barrel of [Void Grenade]s would do them well. Those were easier than using [Thrice This] on Zippy’s [Ancient Paring Knife]. Though she’d leveled the Skill plenty, making thousands of copies of the one-shot weapon that also provided enough umph to allow him to use his [Sacrificial Slice] and open portals took the wind out of her for hours. To create another crate of those would be…

“Ugh!” She grunted, spinning away from the wall.

“That’s it. We’re just sitting here moping around. We’re supposed to be the Paladrones. A Clan sworn to each other and sworn to fighting evil. We need to figure this out. These pictures aren’t stopping, and now with the Tax Guild Stone hunt going on, we can’t just wallow away in our self pity.”

Zippy, sitting in his child-sized rocking chair, clicked the [Ancient Paring Knife] closed and stopped his rocking. As the unspoken final decision maker of their Clan, where he lacked in speech, he more than made up for in intelligence and courage. Her silent brother looked down, as he did, mulling things over, as he also always did.

Beepy sat up from his dramatic laying. Sprawling on his back and staring into space in the middle of spaces was his preferred method, but Aria had just polished the table, trying to make semblance of their growing Clan hideout. Still, he looked back and forth between them, always the one ready to do whatever it took. That she did have admiration for.

“SISTER,” Beepy toned, nodding at her. “YOU ARE RIGHT, AND I THANK YOU FOR THE REMINDER. DUTY IS DIFFICULT. WE WOULD DO OURSELVES A DISSERVICE IN FORGETTING OUR ACCOMPLISHMENTS AND SUCCUMBING TO INACTIVITY,”

Now Beepy stood, clanking metal feet on metal table, gesturing towards the new additions of their Clan hall.

He turned his head to Zippy, who still stared down at the ground. “TAKE ZIPPY, HERE. BY HIS LONESOME HE ACQUIRED NOT ONE, NOT TWO, BUT THREE SIMILARLY SIZED CRATES, EXPANDING OUR CLAN HALLS QUIETLY SO THAT WE MIGHT BE SURPRISED, DELIGHTED, AND APPROPRIATELY GROW OUR POWER.”

At first, it had been a single tiny metal crate with a magical lock openable only by the Paladrones. A few weeks ago, in the dead of night and with Aria and Beepy unaware, Zippy had worked with the local mice crafters to add on three new crates, little drone sized doors and all inside to connect the crates, growing their Clan Hall from one room to four. He’d even ensured they were hidden from view on the outside, their under the bridge Hall tucked behind thorny bushes and appropriately camouflaged under garbage and rock.

“A ROOM FOR HE AND I TO SHARE,” Beepy pointed towards the brother’s bedroom. “HAND CRAFTED BUNKBEDS, WITH MY FINEST GARMENTS AND CAPES AT THE READY.”

He then pointed towards the room with the door heavier than the rest.

“AND A FULL WEAPONS CACHE, PROTECTED BY HEAVY STEEL, WHERE WE HOLD OUR FAVOURED TOOLS, BARRELS OF KNIVES AND GRENADES AND TROPHIES OF OUR VICTORIES TOO.”

Beepy clashed his mechanical hands behind his back and walked on the table to face the last room Zippy had added. It sported a metal door painted black with a skull on it, with a simple deadbolt keyhole.

“AND A ROOM FOR YOU, ARIA. A PLACE YOU CAN CALL YOUR OWN. JUST YOUR OWN. WHY, HE EVEN THREW THE EXTRA KEY IN THE LAKE AND HANDED YOU THE LAST ONE IN FRONT OF US.”

“Thanks again, Zippy,” Aria nodded toward the drone. “You really don’t have any idea how much it means to me.”

Still Zippy stared down at the floor, processing.

Beepy walked slowly around the table and admired the various markers of wins that decorated the central crate of the Paladrone Clan Hall. Lit by Core powered fairy lights dangling from the ceiling, it was cramped, with a metal table they played card games at and planned on, with a few small seats, Zippy’s rocking chair included. Hundreds of pictures covered the walls of unconquered horrors, the monster animal hybrids taking up the bulk of the photos. Yet Beepy didn’t look at the photos. He looked at the prizes they collected after wins.

He pointed at one such marker. A bottom jawbone the size of a tire and with the teeth sharp as needles. It sat on a plastic hook glued to the metal wall.

“THE VAMPIRE OF VAUGHN, EATER OF CHILDREN, WHERE ARIA EXPLODED HIS DEMONIC HEART WITH A WELL PLACED [VOID GRENADE].”

Spinning sharply, Beepy clanked over to the other side of the table and pointed at another. A glass eye similarly glued that still somehow moved.

“THE CREEP OF CALEDON, WHERE I NOBLY REMOVED HIS HEAD FROM HIS CRETENOUS CADAVER.”

With a crash, Beepy jumped to the edge of the table that faced the exit door and stared at another prize. A loose piece of filthy fabric sitting atop the door frame.

“THE ZOMBIE,” He said gravely. “AT FIRST, WE BELIEVED HER MISMARKED, MISUNDERSTOOD, AND WRONGFULLY MALIGNED,”

Beepy turned towards Zippy, flourishing out his red cape with a proud P sloppily stitched in and bowing slightly.

“YET IT WAS ZIPPY WHO WOULD NOT BE A MARK, STRIKING HER MUMMIFIED MAW, HER MALEVONENT MOLARS, HER MALIGNANT MIND, SAVING OUR LIVES WITH A WELL-PLACED STRIKE. THE FIFTY THOUSAND CREDITS IN BOUTY PROVIDED PASSAGE TOWARDS FINANCIAL SECURITY.”

He nodded towards his brother, finally turning towards the back of the Clan Hall and source of their ceaseless quest.

The printer sat connected to a broken tablet on a cluttered desk full of hand drawn maps, checklist, various bits, bobs, little useful Relics, Cores yet unsold, and their shared cellphone. Above it all was photos of their loved ones and a sign spelling out—

“FAMILY,” Beepy clasped both fists towards his chest. “WE HAVE PHILOSOPHIZED THE PURPOSE, THE ALIGNMENT, THE DIRECTION OF GOOD, ATTEMPTING TO UNDERSTAND WHY IT IS WE FEEL THIS BLATHERING, INCESSENT CALL OF DUTY TO HUNT EVIL AND BALANCE IN FAVOUR OF GOOD.”

Bowing his head, the most dramatic of drones turned on his heels in a way fitting for a grand stage towards Aria and Zippy. Each sentence was punctuated by a fist extension of full certainty.

“I BELIEVE IT STEMS FROM OUR MECHANICAL HEARTS. OUR MECHANICAL SOULS, PURELY FORMED OUT OF LOVE AND WANT OF LOVE BY MARY, OUR GLORIOUS CREATOR. A WANT OF FAMILY. A PROTECTION OF FAMILY. AND BEING CREATED WITH THE SYSTEM, WE WITNESS ALL CREATURES CONNECTED TO THAT VERY SYSTEM AS PART OF OUR—”

A little tone from Zippy cut Beepy’s impassioned speech short. Both Aria and Beepy turned to face the silent brother. Rather than looking down at the floor in thought, Zippy’s glowing eyes looked back.

Zippy beeped and blooped in quick succession at Beepy, turning up his weaponless hand as if explaining a simple question, and Aria felt the tug of a grin on her face.

Trying to understand their communication was always a learning experience. Typically, Zippy relied on Beepy for speech, though he’d been getting much better at expressing himself with little gestures, head nods, and noises that she’d absorbed instinctually over time. Strange indeed, though, that when the silent Paladrone spoke, everyone always listened.

Beepy, to his credit, did listen, and this time it appeared he was enraptured by Zippy’s computerized words only he could truly decipher. While the dramatic, yellow drone could…drone on from time to time, Aria knew Beepy’s heart was always in the right place. He just loved attention, leaning heavily towards the performative, though all grandeur disappeared when Zippy weighed in on serious situations.

A deep sigh of understanding hissed out steam from Beepy as Zippy finished, and the silent blue brother nodded towards Aria, telling his brother to speak on behalf of him.

“WE MIGHT BE LOOKING TOO BIG,” Beepy toned in a sightly less haughty whirr, the mark that it was Zippy’s words rather than his own. “WE ARE NOT RESPONSIBLE FOR RIGHTING EVERY WRONG. RATHER THAN LOOK AND GAWK AT EVERYTHING, IT WOULD BE BETTER IF WE SIMPLY ACTED IN GOOD IN AS MANY SMALL ACTIONS AS WE CAN.”

Aria smiled then. Zippy always had things the right way, delivered in such a way to point them on the right path and keep their anxiety at bay.

“SMALL ACTS, OVER A SERIES OF TIME, KEEPS THINGS MANAGABLE WHILE KEEPING US ON OUR PATH. WE ARE NOT GODS. WE ARE NOT MASTERS OF GOOD. WE ARE JUST TRYING TO DO OUR BEST WITH THE TOOLS THAT WE HAVE. THAT IS ENOUGH. THAT IS WHAT WE CAN DO, TOGETHER.”

Beepy finished Zippy’s message then, sitting down on the table and staring into his hands as if he’d been confronted with a true revelation.

“I agree, Zippy,” Aria said, still smiling and feeling much less frustrated. “But what are we supposed to do?” She thumbed towards the wall of horrors.

“Look at all these pictures. We’re not even sure where they are, and they keep coming and coming and coming, and something is brewing bad. It’s all fine and well to take some of the pressure off ourselves, but we still shouldn’t just sit here and do—”

Just then, their shared cellphone blared out the ring tone they’d all eagerly agreed on. It was after binge watching the full 42 episodes across three seasons.

“Transformers! More then meets the eye!”

All three of them leapt from their spots, eager to pick up the phone first, even if was Mary checking in again…

“Transformers! Robots in—”

“Hello?” Aria answered, flipping open the phone, nimblest and quickest while Beepy pouted behind her.

A tiny explosion of sound exploded out of the tiny speaker, making her yank away the phone from her ear.

She shook her head, positioning the phone a few inches from her head.

“Hellllooo? Who’s this?”

It sounded like a tornado mixed with a blender full of cutlery. Wind and explosions and screams sounded through the phone, while Beepy and Zippy positioned themselves around Aria to listen in.

“Hellllllloooo? Is this a crank call?!”

Finally, a voice could be heard over all the explosions and wind.

“Aria?!” A familiar voice asked frantically.

“Alex?”

Over the noise of explosions, they heard a similar voice yell Fuck, fuck, fuck, shoot it!

“No,” the voice said. “…It’s Brody.”

“Brody? What’s happening? What are all those noises! Why are you outside while the Tax Guil—”

AHHHHHHHH FUCKING KILL IT DANDELION!

A massive blast crackled the speakers, the Brody’s voice quickly coming back.

“Can you…help us?”

“What do you need!? What the hell is going on?!”

“No..time—”

CRASH

BOOM

WOOF! WOOF!

And the sound of a massive roar, followed by the frantic screeching of Alex followed by some high-pitched laughter that somehow sounded over everything else.

“Just…meet at Niagara Falls. Quickly.”

The line went dead, cutting off the yelling, wind, and bangs.

Aria closed the flip phone and turned looked at her brothers.

“WAS THAT A DOG?” Beepy asked as all three of the Paladrones scrambled to leave.
