>> The 13th day of the 3th month, year 8259 AE

“When’s Mom coming back?” Leo asked, for what felt like the hundredth time already.

“You heard her,” Viktor said, without bothering to look up. “She had told us, very clearly, ‘I’ll be back soon.’ So... just wait. Don’t be so impatient. Your mom will be here again before you know it.”

“When is ‘soon’?” Leo pressed. He turned toward the window. “She left when the sun was just coming up, and now it’s already sinking. It’s almost gone, but she still hasn’t returned yet.”

“I don’t know,” Viktor said, a bit annoyed by the endless queries.

“And what about Dad? When’s he coming back? You think they’ve found him? You think they’re bringing him back?”

“I said I don’t know! Stop asking your stupid questions.”

He frowned at his nephew, who stood near the stairs with his arms hanging stiffly at his sides, his usual restless energy completely absent. Viktor sucked in a breath, then let it out slow.

“Leo,” he said in a lower voice. “Your mom is going with those soldiers to find your dad. Soon, they’ll be back here, both of them. Together.”

The boy said nothing more, but the way his gaze dropped made it obvious he didn’t believe any of it.

Well, if Viktor were being honest with himself, neither did he.

It had been two weeks since Orion’s disappearance. One day, without any warning, without any explanation, he had simply stopped coming home. Vera, of course, had tried everything she could to look for him. She had gone from door to door, seeking out his friends, his acquaintances, anyone who might have seen him, anyone who might have heard something.

It turned out that several of Orion’s fellow protesters against the King’s decision had also gone missing, just as suddenly, while those who remained were shaken to the core. Some told Vera to stop coming to their doors; others warned her to keep her head down as well, if she knew what was good for her and the children.

And then, today, the soldiers arrived.

They stood in the doorway, and demanded Vera to go with them. She did not argue. She simply wiped her hands on a cloth, told them to wait a minute, and then turned to Viktor and Leo with that same calm expression before saying, “I’ll be back soon.”

And so, the two of them had been waiting for her for the entire day.

“What do they want from Mom?” Leo asked.

Viktor sighed. Of course the brat couldn’t stay silent for more than five minutes.

“I don’t know. Maybe they just want to ask her a few questions. If Orion was involved in something, then it makes sense they’d want to talk to her.”

“Then why didn’t they ask her right here?”

Viktor opened his mouth, but nothing came out. Because he didn’t have any answer to that.

Orion, where are you?

Why did you disappear?

Why did you leave us behind?

Aren’t you supposed to be the man who could fix anything?

So, please. Fix this.

Viktor heard the rumble of his own stomach.

Well, that was to be expected. Vera had left so suddenly this morning, and that meant today there was no warm meal waiting on the table, no comforting aroma to ease the kitchen’s emptiness.

For lunch, he had reheated the leftover soup she prepared for breakfast, and divided the remaining bread, which was barely enough to fill two hungry boys. And now, unsurprisingly, there was nothing left. He glanced at the stove as evening drew near, and it stared right back, mocking him for the lack of solutions.

I should start learning to cook someday, Viktor thought.

But that was for the future. For now, for this particular upcoming meal, there was nothing that could be done. So, he stood up.

“I’ll go to Marcus’s house. To see if he could share with us anything.”

“I’ll go with you,” Leo said immediately.

Viktor frowned. He had been hoping he could have at least a small window of quiet, a brief pause from the endless questions. Leo coming along meant none of that; it meant the barrage would follow.

“If we go together, he’ll probably tell us to stay and have dinner with them. And if we do that, Vera might come back only to see an empty house.”

That was, of course, just a flimsy excuse to leave the boy behind.

But Leo nodded.

“Alright.” Then he asked, “What if someone comes when you’re not here?”

“If that’s someone we don’t know, then hide yourself. You know the secret cellar Orion built, right? Just open the trapdoor under your parents’ bed and stay down there until I return.”

“But... what if they decide to steal from us once they realize there’s no one at home?”

“If they were thieves, then what could you even do about it? Run out to stop them? No. Just hide.”

“Alright.”

And so Viktor left the house.

He lingered for a moment after he crossed the threshold, casting a brief, hesitant glance back at his nephew. But then, he shrugged it off. Marcus lived just down the hill; he would be back soon enough.

The path downward was worn and uneven, carved into the slope by years of footsteps. Off to the right, the sun was lowering itself toward the distant edge of the sea, toward that thin, wavering line where light and water seemed to meet and dissolve into one another. The sky around it began to shift in color, fading from pale gold into deeper tones of amber and muted red.

Marcus’s house lay somewhere below, closer to the lower fields where the land flattened out. Viktor kept his eyes mostly forward as he walked, careful with each step even though he knew the path well. Still, his attention drifted again and again toward the horizon, where the sea rolled on in its slow, indifferent movement under the encroaching night.

Before long, he reached his destination, coming to a stop before the house that was a bit smaller than his own. He lifted a hand and knocked against the door.

It opened, and a man appeared, his frame leaning slightly to one side as he supported himself with a crutch under his arm.

Marcus.

A former adventurer who had once gone deep into the dungeon in search of fortune, until the day he returned with a lost leg and a life forever changed by whatever had happened down there.

“Oh, Viktor. What’s happened? Orion back?”

“No, he has not. And... some soldiers came to my house this morning. They took Vera away.”

“What? Why? Where did they take her? Why didn’t you come to me sooner?”

“She went with them voluntarily, and she told us she would be alright. Still... the sun is already setting, but she hasn’t come back yet.”

Marcus’s expression darkened. “Tomorrow, I’ll take you two to the Guild. We will make sure nothing bad happens to her.” He paused. “Anyway, if she left in the morning, what have you been eating today?”

“That’s exactly why I’m here. I was hoping you might spare something for dinner.”

“Of course.” Marcus jerked his head toward the inside. “You can come in and join us. As for Leo—”

“He’s waiting for me at home, so I need to go back.”

“Then I’ll get you some bread and cheese. Just wait a moment.” The man turned, shifting on the crutch—

—and he broke into a grin. “Ah. Faster than me, eh?”

A small boy came running toward the entrance, clutching exactly what his father had just mentioned, a lump of bread and a wedge of cheese held in both of his hands.

“Thanks, Ulysses,” Viktor said, ruffling the boy’s hair as he accepted the food. Then he turned to Marcus again. “He’s already three, right?”

“Aye, three. Soon he’ll be running wild with you and Leo.”

Viktor smiled. “I’m looking forward to it.”

He offered his thanks once more, then turned away, holding the bundle firmly against his side as he began the walk back up the slope.

The night had properly settled over the hill now. The path before him was barely visible, a narrow strip of pale dirt cutting through patches of grass and scattered stones, its edges softened by shadow. To his left, the sun had vanished under the edge of the world, and above him, the first stars had begun to appear, faint and scattered.

But then, he saw light.

It came from the top of the hill, from the direction of his house.

Has Leo turned on the lamp? he wondered. But he dismissed that thought before it could even fully form. After all, the shape looked... wrong.

He broke into a run, his feet striking against loose pebbles that rattled and rolled away with every step, scattering down the slope behind him. He stumbled more than once, but each time he managed to catch himself just before he fell, and so he pressed on, not slowing down even one bit.

And.

As he got closer, the truth clarified itself with merciless certainty, in a way that left no room for doubt.

The house was burning.

Flames poured through the roof in violent bursts, breaking the wooden structure into collapsing fragments. The walls glowed from within, cracks of fire splitting through them like veins of light. The doors had already begun to warp, sagging inward as heat devoured their shape.

He was about to let out a scream. But then, he saw them.

Other lights.

Torchlight.

Many figures were standing around the house, black silhouettes sharpened by the flickering blaze that glinted off their armor and shield and speartip.

Unconsciously, he brought a hand up over his mouth, pressing it tight against his lips to stop the sound that was trying to escape. The food he had been holding slipped from his other hand and disappeared into the grass. Paying it no heed, he dropped low, folding into the brambles beside the path. Thorns bit through his clothes, into his skin, but he didn’t feel anything.

Slowly, he edged forward.

Voices reached him through the roar of the fire.

Men talking.

Men laughing.

While the house burned.

“Serve them right,” one of them said. “Damn brats, hiding from us. Waste of time coming all the way up here.”

Another snorted. “Hey, isn’t all this your fault? If you hadn’t hit her that hard, we wouldn’t have had to return and drag the brats out too.”

“She bit me,” the first man barked. “Damn that bitch.”

What... what are they even talking about?

Viktor didn’t understand.

He couldn’t understand.

What in the world was going on?

The fire continued to consume everything in front of him, everything he had held dear for as long as he could remember.

And all he could do was watch.

Why?

Why did they do this?
