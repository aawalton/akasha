Jeff Carlton hadn’t told his parents about what he was about to attempt.

There hadn’t been time for a call—not if he’d wanted to visit the Crone, get fitted with the set of armor Bernard the Wall had left behind, and do all the other prep he’d have to do to fight at the level the Crone and the null core promised him. There’d been too much work to do, and not enough time for any of it.

But he’d left a note. Just in case. Because a lot could go wrong, and he didn’t want to leave them behind with nothing.

The last thing Jeff wanted was to leave his people behind, and the realization of just what the null core was had put him in a difficult position. His parents, after all, were his people. He couldn’t leave them behind. And he couldn’t abandon the team of E and D-Rankers he’d been working on building up, either. But, even more importantly, Kade was his people. The maniac had been his people since they’d sat outside of the principal’s office after their first fight.

So his choice had been simple. Not painless, but simple.

“Are you ready?” Kade asked. His hand was on Jeff’s shoulder.

Jeff brought his own up to Kade’s. He stared the dark-haired man in the eye. Then he nodded, and with his other hand, he crushed the null core.

Power. Power overwhelming.

Jeff’s core was a metal-bound, rivet-lined sphere just above his heart. Its burnished, not-quite-shining surface was unmarred by any spell or aura, the only breaks in its shape the bumps of the hundreds of small rivets holding it together. He hadn’t examined his core heavily since he’d hit C-Rank. There’d been no point. As a tank, with no magical ability to speak of outside of Split-Second Shield, and no ability to progress to B-Rank and acquire an aura, his core had ceased to interest him.

The first rivet popped loudly enough to ricochet all around the inside of his mental space, pinging off invisible walls and clattering to the ground after almost five chaotic seconds. Then another gave way. Another. After an instant that lasted seconds, it was all Jeff could do to dodge the bullet-like rivets as they broke, one after another, like popcorn if popcorn was as hard as steel and left dents in the mental space’s surface.

And as the rivets gave way, Jeff’s core began to expand. It pushed portal metal plates away, bending them and putting even more pressure on the rivets. Frigid, molten metal poured through the gaps, ice forming across the red-orange surface of the liquid steel. It bubbled out, cooled, then cracked as wave after wave broke free.

Jeff clenched his jaw against the feeling. It wasn’t pain. It wasn’t even uncomfortable. But even though he couldn’t define it, it was overwhelming.

That didn’t matter, though. All that mattered was that it was powerful.

Extremely so.

“I’m ready. Let’s go.”

The hard part was going to be getting the God of Thunder to open a portal to his world.

I took care of that myself.

Instead of charging through into my portal world as a team, I stepped into it alone. Then I waited.

Only a minute passed before a swirling gold portal appeared right in front of me. Before the God of Thunder could emerge from it, I threw myself into it, armor on and the Storm’s Blade in my hand.

The flashing red and blue sky pulsed with more energy than I’d ever seen before as my feet landed on the long, flat platform at the center of the God of Thunder’s portal world. All around me, towers sparked with lightning. Every one of those towers was a battery, and every one of them had been filled by the God of Thunder’s pupils. Pupils like me.

“Kade Noelstra?” The God of Thunder sounded surprised. “What are you doing here? Have you come to give up like Tallas? That’d make things easier for both of us, and it’d still be interesting to watch someone put up this much resistance and then—“

“No.”

The God of Thunder hovered mid-air, his body easily a hundred yards long. Lightning coursed between nodes of electricity, arcing through the air as the massive storm dragon curled back and forth. His head stopped. His eyes locked onto mine. “No?”

“No. I’m here for Tanat.”

“Wonderful. Simply wonderful.” If the dragon could have clapped his hands together, he would have. He looked genuinely thrilled. “I half-expected the bait not to work. She’s over there. Feel free to take her.”

I didn’t respond—at least, not with words.

I did respond with Stormbreak, though. I excluded Tanat—just in case she was still alive—and nothing else. Electricity pulsed. For a moment, the lightning blossomed just as it had every other time, with a positive zone around me and a negative around the God of Thunder. As my Mana drained away, though, something shifted.

I moved. The Storm’s Blade came up, electricity arcing up from the blossom around me to its blade. Stormbreak pulsed, and pain rippled through me, but it felt distant. Ignorable. The Storm’s Blade pushed forward through it, Laminar Blade pulsing not only with hyper-compressed water but with the storm’s fury, and I threw myself at the God of Thunder.

“Interesting,” The God of Thunder started to say.

He would have said more, but I hit him in the nose. The Storm’s Blade cut deep, though on a monster the size of the God of Thunder, the wound looked pathetically small. Stormbreak ripped at his cloud scales and tore at his storm-covered underbelly, futily trying to hurt the SS-Plus-Ranked monster. My opening attack hadn’t done anything.

But I hadn’t expected it to, and the God of Thunder hadn’t gone silent from my attack.

He’d gone silent as my friends and allies poured in through the still-open portal, Jeff at the front.

Then, without a word, he tried to disengage from me and move to counter the new, unexpected intruders. And I followed, the Storm’s Blade up, trying not to let him.

Jeff was all-in.

The jump up in power from using the null core was…incredible. He was temporarily an S-Ranker. The sheer Stamina and Mana pouring from his core as it came apart gave him the superhuman strength he’d seen Kade and Ellen using, and put him right up there with the Portal Tyrant and his idols, Bernard the Wall. This was everything he’d ever wanted, and everything he’d traded away.

It wouldn’t last, though. Even as he activated his taunt skill and popped Retaliation, the null core ate away at his own. He’d have minutes, at best.

He’d have to make them count. It was time to kick ass.

Kade, the lunatic, stood right in front of the God of Thunder. The gigantic dragon was easily the second largest single enemy Jeff had ever fought—right behind a humongous bone turtle in a D-Rank boneyard world—and even though Kade was strong, he wasn’t strong enough to distract the God of Thunder. Not alone. That was Jeff’s job. He raised the S-Rank shield he’d been lugging around, and it moved easily.

The God of Thunder’s assault slammed into him a moment later. Thunder clapped as two massive jaws closed on Jeff; he caught the lower one on the bottom of his tower shield, and the upper on the top, then thrust forward automatically. His short sword sliced into electric flesh, parting cloud as he hacked away and blocked the God of Thunder’s heaviest blows.

He didn’t get away with it free, though. Every blow dented the tower shield. Every impact crushed portal metal armor and bruised his body beneath the plates. Bones shattered, only for his fading core to pour power into repairing them. Every impact hurt worse than Jeff had ever been hurt before. Retaliation helped. It was powerful. But it wasn’t enough to keep the God of Thunder down.

Jeff glanced to the left. Terrel and Sarah dashed down the length of the platform, closing in on where Tanat’s body should be. Behind him, Ellen had skidded to a stop by the portal; half of her power was focused on it, and the other half was on a massive Shadow Cell that closed over their opponent, trying to lock him down as far from either Tanat or the portal gate as possible.

Then another set of bone-shattering blows rained down on Jeff as Kade dashed through the air, leaving a streak of lightning behind, and he went back to hammering away at the Paragon—and to trying to absorb blows that felt like entire mountains were being dropped on him. His people were counting on him.

And with every impact, with every swing of his sword, Jeff’s power dwindled.

The raid was going well.

Granted, we’d had maybe fifteen seconds of contact from the moment I used Stormbreak until now. And in those fifteen seconds, I’d used every drop of Mana in my tank, Jeff’s armor had gone from glorious S-Rank plate to something put through a scrap grinder and melter, and Sarah and Terrel had gotten less than halfway from the portal to where Tanat’s body, in theory, was.

But it was going well. No one had died yet, and the God of Thunder was, in fact, alone.

And he was annoyed.

In the first second after Stormbreak, I’d used Lightning Dance and Stormrunner to speed myself up as much as I could, then stayed in Torrentborne, sword in a high guard and body as small as I could make it. My job wasn’t to kill the God of Thunder. Yes, I’d have to do it soon. But right now, I was a thorn in his side.

So was Jeff. But where I was constantly probing, looking for an opening before unleashing everything I had and then shifting to his other flank to continue needling away at him, Jeff was a solid splinter, unmoving and taking the beating Eugene sent his way. He could survive the physical punishment the God of Thunder threw at him, and for now, he had the regeneration to recover as I distracted the Paragon.

But I wasn’t stupid.

The God of Thunder wasn’t all-in. Not yet. As annoying as the two of us were, he was also being entertained.

That’d change any second.

Shadows danced across the God of Thunder’s long, snake-like body. Shadow Cell ripped into him, the damage both catastrophic and totally trivial as his SS-Plus-Ranked Health repaired the wounds almost before they happened.

That was a feint. So far, everything we’d done had been a feint, with two exceptions.

First, Sarah Cullman. The Spark of Life had been mission-focused from the moment she entered the God of Thunder’s portal world. Her only goal was to reach Tanat, see if the swordswoman was alive, and if so, do everything in her power to keep her that way while retreating to my portal world.

And second, Jeff. Jeff was doing exactly what he was supposed to. Getting up in the God of Thunder’s face and holding his attention to the best of his temporarily boosted abilities.

As I hammered away at the God of Thunder’s lower right flank, he figured it out. His attention locked on Sarah, ripping through Jeff’s taunt skill and bowling both of us over. For a moment, there was nothing between the thunder dragon and our team’s healer. The plan was in mortal danger.

But we’d planned for this, too.

“Now, Terrel!” I yelled as I pushed myself off the ground. “Ellen, go!”

They went. Terrel broke off his course, massive warhammer coming off his back, and sprinted toward the closest pillar. Ellen dropped all her spells and skills, instead casting Abyssal Storm as she shifted into Voidwalker form. Her spell landed on a second pillar.

And both the fighter and mage poured their strongest attacks into the God of Thunder’s batteries—into centuries of invested Mana.

This was the true purpose of our assault. Recovering Tanat was critical, of course, but with Sarah on the team, if she was alive, we’d get her home safe. But destroying the God of Thunder’s energy reserves would take ascension off the table for him. It was a long shot, but if Terrel and Ellen were fast enough, it might even set him back enough to allow other powerful Paragons to finish him off.

Even if it didn’t, though, it’d be a blow against him—and one he couldn’t use Health to recover from.

The God of Thunder reacted instantly.

Lightning coursed out from his claws as, for the first time, he used a spell. It arced between the five of us, forming a star-shaped, pentagon-bound net, then pulsed once. Lightning ripped into Ellen, Sarah, and Terrel—and only Jeff’s activation of Split-Second Shield kept the two of us from suffering the same fate.

Ellen screamed behind me. Sarah did the same thing a hundred yards down the platform. She’d almost made it to Tanat, only to slam into the portal metal, twitching as electricity poured through her nerves.

But Terrel was still up.

He whipped his hammer back, then slammed it forward, his entire body bending almost comically as he swung the massive portal metal weight. For all that it was heavy, the actual impact surface was tiny; the moment it hit the towering, floating pillar, a crack ran up its side. Then he swung again. Another impact. This one spiderwebbed across the entire structure.

“No.”

The God of Thunder said only the one word.

Terrel ignored him. Another blow. This time, stone broke free from the spire’s far side, exploding out into the red and blue void like someone had detonated a mining charge.

“No!”

Now, he moved.

Thunder swelled around him. The air grew thick with electricity—both the real kind, and the kind that I felt when something big was about to happen. I launched myself back into my attack, trying desperately to stop him, but as the tension built and the space around us filled with static, the God of Thunder spoke. “You brought your friends into this, kid. You need to learn what happens when you rely on them. Clearly, my previous lesson to you wasn’t anywhere near enough.”

Flowers blossomed around my friends—and around Tanat’s body. For a split second, that was a good thing; she was alive! Then I realized two things.

First, that the lightning flowers around my friends could only mean one thing. The God of Thunder was using his own Stormbreak.

And second, that he’d excluded exactly one person from his SS-Plus-Ranked assault.

He intended to destroy all my friends and allies in one move, while forcing me to watch, and I had no skills in my toolkit that could stop it.
