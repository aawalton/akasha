Sakura stared at the pale text before her eyes and felt cold run down her spine.

SLUG M-17

Level: 28

She had already seen dangerous creatures. Obsidian monsters, Faerd, dungeons, dead bodies, horrible prisons. But now, there was something different in front of her.

It was a living wall. A murky white, almost translucent mass slowly crawled forward, filling the corridor from wall to wall.

Gilbert did not light his flame. There was no need. The slug lit the room on its own. Besides, the dying collector had warned them not to shine too brightly, and right now Sakura had no desire to test how literally that warning should be taken.

The creature did not rush at them right away. It simply crawled. Sakura involuntarily took a step back, and her sole made a soft squelch in the thin film of slime. The sound seemed monstrously loud to her.

Gilbert was already moving. Fast, but not frantic. He held one hand slightly in front of himself, shielding Sakura with his shoulder, and for a few moments looked at M-17 with a cold, analytical expression.

"Step back," he said quietly.

Sakura wanted to answer, but did not have time. She had the feeling that something very bad was about to happen.

White-blue magic flared at Gilbert’s fingers. A barely visible spark ran between the mage’s knuckles, then unfolded into an uneven bolt of lightning. The air suddenly smelled of ozone, the hairs on Sakura’s arms stood on end, and the old metal clamps on the floor reacted to the tension.

The lightning snapped forward.

It struck the front of the slug with a dry crack, tore through the creature’s murky halo of light, and its body flared even brighter and twitched.

For one brief moment, Sakura thought it had worked.

The electric thread did not break apart at once. It held between Gilbert’s palm and the slug’s body like a stretched nerve.

Then the slime suddenly moved along the lightning. It was very strange. The slime slid forward as if the lightning were an ordinary thread, wrapping around it, and splashed onto Gilbert’s hand.

He managed to jerk his hand back sharply, but it was too late.

Whitish slime covered his fingers, palm, and wrist. It did not burn through his skin instantly, but Sakura noticed how Gilbert clenched his teeth. His fingers twitched, then started moving worse.

For the first time since Sakura had known him, Gilbert looked genuinely unpleasantly surprised.

The slug seemed not to notice what had just happened. The lightning had done it no harm, and the creature kept slowly moving forward, blocking the exit.

"It conducted slime along the magical contact?" the mage asked in amazement, as if speaking to himself.

"Has that happened before?" Sakura asked, not knowing what else to say.

What she had just seen really did look damn strange. How could something wrap around lightning at all? It was like trying to grab sound with your hand.

"No. Never."

Gilbert had already raised his other hand. His face had gone hard. He was going to keep fighting, because that was exactly how his mind worked. There was a threat, and the threat had to be stopped. If the first method turned out badly, another one had to be found. But Sakura immediately understood that this would not end well. In front of them was an unknown, anomalous threat, completely unlike the obsidian beasts. What if the next attack rebounded into them ten times worse?

"No! Not like that, Gilbert. Let’s try something else. Not a direct fight."

She said it almost soundlessly, but clearly enough. Not only because the dying collector had told them not to make noise, not to cut, not to burn.

Gilbert threw her a brief look. There was irritation in it, calculation, and that same understanding that sometimes made Sakura want to check whether he could read minds. Then he lowered his hand and began retreating with Sakura, saying nothing.

Gilbert suddenly activated [Inventory], and a magic scroll appeared in his clean hand. He unrolled it, and cold white light sharply passed over his skin. The slime on his hand hissed, shriveled, and then disappeared as if it had never been there.

M-17 kept moving forward. Its heavy mass poured over the threshold of the room. Slime ran down its body in layers and slapped onto the floor with quiet wet sounds. Sakura and Gilbert continued to retreat.

And it immediately became clear that they would not be able to retreat gracefully.

Sakura took another step back and stumbled, grabbing the edge of a broken table. Somewhere under her foot, glass crunched. Gilbert turned to support her.

"Careful!"

M-17 jerked sharply.

The front of its body turned toward them faster than before. It moved forward, no longer quite so slowly.

Sakura froze. Gilbert froze too.

The slug noticeably slowed. Its front section swayed as if it were listening. Sakura barely breathed. Despite the mask still covering her face, the smell of rot and chemicals sat in her throat.

Nearby, a small shard of glass finally slipped off a table and rang against the floor.

M-17 turned toward the sound again.

Sakura felt a thought click into place in her head. The slug was blind, and the dark spots inside its body were not eyes. So the creature was not reacting to their faces or gestures.

It was reacting to sound.

Gilbert had figured it out even earlier and was already showing Sakura a gesture. Quiet. She nodded in response.

After that, everything changed. The laboratory was no longer just a place they had to run through in search of the exit the dying man had told them about. Somewhere “down, past the laboratory, following the red marks.” Right now, this room had become a trap. Because there was glass everywhere, metal, squelching slime, and hell knew what else. Every step could give them away. Every bumped tray, crunch, or rustle of fabric could make the creature move faster again.

They tried to retreat without making a sound, not speaking to each other. Sakura watched her feet so carefully that her eyes started to hurt.

M-17 crawled after them, but it was clear that it was already having trouble orienting itself.

Sometimes the slug froze and turned what might have been its head in search of new sounds. It was trying to understand where the people had disappeared to. When Sakura and Gilbert stopped moving completely, the slug also stopped or crawled insanely slowly. When stone scraped under someone’s sole, the creature went toward the sound again.

Sakura had never hated her boots so much in her life.

They had almost reached the overturned table by the far wall when Sakura, retreating sideways, suddenly backed into something cold and hard.

Glass gave a dull tremble behind her shoulders.

M-17 changed at once.

The front of its body snapped upward. The light inside it grew brighter, and the entire mass lunged toward them so fast that Sakura almost cried out. She had not even known this creature was capable of that kind of speed. Gilbert grabbed her sleeve and pulled her down. They both fell behind the overturned table, crouched, and went still, afraid even to breathe.

Fortunately, once they quieted down, the slug lost them. Sakura looked out from behind the table with insane caution and realized the creature was approaching the glass she had just bumped into. It turned out to be a giant vessel. Not like the cracked empty tank in the first hall. For one thing, this one was completely intact. Thick cloudy glass, coated in places with unknown filth and familiar slime, was held by copper arcs. Tarnished alchemical runes ran along them, as if fused into the vessel. Heavy clamps held it at the bottom. Thin glass tubes extended from its neck, many of them long cracked or blackened, but the container itself remained sealed.

Inside that suspicious glass tank was slime. Not white, like the slime spread across the laboratory and oozing from M-17’s body. This was different. Thick, pearl-gray, with a greenish amber tint. It also seemed softer and more fluid.

Gilbert threw a look at the tank, but quickly lost interest in it, because to the mage it was only another part of the laboratory, one that would not help them find the exit or escape the monster. Gilbert looked back at the passage, at M-17, at the possible path along the wall.

But Sakura kept staring at the vessel. And the longer she looked, the more she realized that the slime inside was not merely sitting there and bubbling.

It was moving, barely noticeably.

When M-17 shifted to the right, the pearly mass inside the glass also stretched to the right with a delay. When the slug froze, it froze too. When the slug lifted the front of its body, the mass inside the tank pressed a little higher against the glass, as if it wanted to get out, though the lid would not let it.

The slime in the tank seemed to be reaching for the slug.

Sakura blinked and, almost without thinking, focused on the vessel.

Pale text flashed before her eyes.

MATERNAL SLIME SAMPLE

Type: Bio-Alchemical Substance

And that was all. To be honest, it told Sakura very little.

M-17 was slowly crawling away to the side, having lost the people’s exact position. Its mass blocked part of the passage, but between the tables and the wall there was still a narrow path downward, and maybe there was the very exit from the laboratory the man had spoken about. Gilbert noticed it immediately. Very carefully, he tugged Sakura by the sleeve, showing her the direction.

Now. They had a chance to leave.

But Sakura did not move.

Gilbert looked at her sharply, already understanding that Sakura had thought of something. He was right. It was probably a bad decision. Possibly the worst one Sakura had managed to make that day, and the competition there was strong.

Sakura shook her head.

Gilbert frowned. Very slowly, almost soundlessly, he pointed toward the passage again.

She shook her head again.

Sakura looked at the tank, then at the slug. Its heavy movements were painful, scars marked its body, old clamps were buried in its flesh, and even its light seemed inflamed.

If they ran now, what would happen next? Would they really find the exit? Or would the slug block their path again and force them into a fight?

Saorex’s words suddenly surfaced in her mind. “The main thing is not to forget that cruelty is not the only path, even when it seems there is no other choice.”

Gilbert tugged her sleeve again, more insistently this time.

Sakura carefully freed her hand. She stood as quietly as she could. On one of the nearby tables, she saw a heavy alchemical pestle. She took the dark, dirty metal in her hand.

Gilbert understood. His eyes widened only slightly, but for him it was almost a scream.

Sakura took the pestle with both hands and made several steps toward the tank.

M-17 twitched again, hearing the faint rustle. The front of its body turned toward her.

Sakura did not wait for reason to explain how terrible this idea was.

She struck the glass.

The first hit came out dull. One pathetic white crack ran across the surface.

M-17 lunged toward her. In all the time they had watched it, Sakura and Gilbert definitely had not seen that giant mass move so fast. Sakura immediately understood that there was no time.

Gilbert broke into motion, moving toward her, already raising his hand. White-blue magic flared again between his fingers.

Sakura struck a second time.

The crack spread downward. The copper arc creaked. Inside the vessel, the pearly slime pressed itself against the damaged spot, as if it were also pushing the glass from within.

M-17 was already very close.

Sakura heard its body crawling over stone, heard slime slapping onto the floor, heard the grown-in rings scraping inside it. She suddenly thought that if she was wrong, then she was about to be dissolved for the most idiotic attempt at compassion in the history of this world.

Not a bad epitaph. Sakura Arai. Mistook mercy for suicide.

She struck a third time.

The glass burst. It collapsed outward in large cloudy chunks. Sakura recoiled, but did not scream. Pearl-gray slime crawled onto the floor. It was watery, but still did not pour. It crawled. The shapeless slime slowly pulled itself out of the vessel, leaving a wet trail behind.

The slime ignored everything around it, moving only toward M-17.

Gilbert was already beside Sakura, his hand raised with white-blue magic. M-17 was too close. The front of its body loomed directly over them, and the horrible toxic-sweet smell struck her nose and seemed to crawl under her skin.

Gilbert held his magic as if he were about to attack. Sakura grabbed his wrist and pushed his hand down.

The mage looked at her sharply.

M-17 stopped. Its mass still leaned forward, touching the edge of the broken vessel. But then the front of its body twitched and slowly turned toward the pearly slime crawling out of the tank.

The maternal sample touched the slug’s body. The whole enormous body of M-17 suddenly jerked, the light inside it flared murkily, then darkened. The front section rose higher, and the old metal rings in its sides screeched as if they were being twisted out from within. A convulsion passed through its body. White slime hissed as it ran down to the floor. Dark veins moved inside.

Gilbert tensed. Sakura also started to think this had been a failed decision, a mistake. Because now M-17 looked like it was truly suffering. What had she done to it?

But then another color began to spread through M-17’s body. Pearl-gray lines with a greenish amber tint stretched through the white mass, entered it, dissolved, and appeared farther on. Wherever they passed, the sick light suddenly became soft and clean.

One clamp came out of the slug’s side. Slowly, with a wet, unpleasant sound, as if the body had finally stopped holding inside itself something that should have been torn out long ago. The clamp fell onto the stone with a heavy metallic thud.

Then another fragment fell out of M-17’s body and rolled across the floor with a wet squelch. Then a copper ring grown into the creature’s side slid lower, slipped out of the slime, and struck the stone.

Sakura stood without moving. Gilbert froze beside her too, and neither of them took their eyes off the creature that seemed to be healing right in front of them.

The slug slowly lowered the front of its body and crawled to the side, leaving a wide glowing streak behind. When it crawled very close, Sakura accidentally took a step sideways and stepped on an old metal hook.

It rang under her sole.

She froze, feeling her heart drop again. But this time M-17 did not move toward her. It only twitched slightly, but remained in place, as if sounds no longer concerned it. Or as if it simply no longer saw Sakura and Gilbert as enemies.

Sakura slowly exhaled.

Her hand hurt from the blows with the pestle. Her fingers were trembling so badly she almost dropped it. Her whole body was cold from delayed fear. Gilbert stood beside her and looked at her as if he could not find anything to say about all this.

A system message suddenly flashed before Sakura’s eyes.

TALENT DETERMINED.

TALENT: BOND WEAVER.

Sakura froze.

The lines did not disappear. More appeared below.

BOND WEAVER

Effect: Improves recognition, formation, and stabilization of bonds with living, semi-living, and magically altered entities.

Limitation: Does not guarantee obedience.

Sakura stared at the window for a long time, barely understanding what exactly she felt. So this was what her talent in Veyr turned out to be? This was what she had been moving toward all this time?

Gilbert looked at her, still not knowing what she had just discovered. Then the mage said very quietly, "Sakura."

She turned her head toward him.

"What?"

For a second, he looked at M-17 crawling away, at the broken vessel, at the fallen clamps, and then back at her.

"Remind me never to underestimate your worst ideas again."

Sakura gave a weak smile, almost without any strength behind it.

"Only the worst ones?"

"Especially the worst ones."

Sakura laughed quietly and hoarsely, though it felt like she did not even have the strength left for laughter. Then she swallowed and nodded toward the passage.

They still had to get out.
