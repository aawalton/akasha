The next morning I awoke to a repeated buzz from the little phone device that I’d been given.

“Hello?”

I pressed the device to my ear as I’d been told to. Daryn’s voice came out of the receiver.

“Hey, buddy. Just calling to let you know I’m running an hour late. Try not to get too bored in the meantime, ‘kay?”

“Okay. Thanks for telling me.”

I didn’t know how to end the call, leading to me sitting there with it pressed to my ear waiting until I heard a long beep that made me realise the communication had ended.

The sun was still fairly low in the sky. I didn’t know exactly what time it was, but I figured that if Daryn didn’t turn up for a few hours, I would have plenty of time to explore the hotel’s facilities.

Before that, I revisited my system screen.

I’d wanted to combine and refine other skills. [Deer in Headlights] was pretty powerful from what I’d read of it, and a good indicator that combining multiple skills was useful. Another thing I’d come to realise—and enjoy—about the skill was the fact that I could tune out mana signatures I didn’t want to see.

The visual clutter around me from dozens of Drassians, and even hundreds at points, would’ve been far too much with each of them firing off wispy mana motes in every direction whenever they moved.

I still hadn’t had a chance to fully test out the skill, but I wanted to use this quiet moment to refine a couple more. I pulled up my skill list, eager to create something new.

[Subject name: Adam Tallow.]

[Bloodline: Rat]

[Skill List:]

[Deer in Headlights (rare): 1 (7)]

[Kinetic Shell (rare): 4]

[Moonlit Grace (semi-rare): 5]

[Pickaxe Mastery: 10]

[Trap Making: 10]

[Intimidation: 10]

[Persuasion: 10]

[Tinkering: 10]

[Sleight of Hand: 10]

[Haggling: 9]

[Stealth: 9]

[Grappling: 9]

[Mathematics: 8]

[Literacy: 8]

[Mining: 7]

[Polishing: 7]

[Cooking: 6]

[Marksmanship: 6]

[Whittling: 5]

[Fire Resistance: 3]

[Crafting: 3]

It was a long list. I considered it as I stared.

One thing that occurred to me was that I was able to break down skills for benefits of some kind. It was something I’d never really messed with as I’d always felt I needed the skills I had and that I might regret unlearning one, just in case it came in handy later.

But that [Polishing] skill, and to a certain extent, [Whittling] too, were sticking out at me.

Polishing was something I’d maxed easily shining shoes, as well as cleaning dirt off of thrown away items to sell. It was a marker of my previous place in life, a product of the toil and effort that I’d gone through in order to get to where I was now, as well as a constant reminder of the [Shoe Shiner] class I’d rejected all the way at the start of my journey.

Could I really get rid of it? Was I undoing a tie to my past self by doing so?

I couldn’t deny that the skill felt useless. And that seeing it in my list made me a little angry.

Even still, it was [Whittling] that I decided to sacrifice. For the first time, I dragged a skill to the ‘break down’ tab. I rationalised that if unlearning the skill gave few benefits, I could always get the skill back rather quickly.

[You have broken down Whittling, level 5. Your mana capacity has increased by 15.]

A mana increase? For breaking down a skill? If I remembered rightly, my mana score had been over 3000 when Maisie had analysed me, and my total had apparently increased by 50 percent on my Tier 1 evolution. If I assumed that my mana total was now around 4500, then 15 more wasn’t necessarily a big deal.

That said… was it?

Did breaking down a skill give fifteen mana, or was it something to do with the skill’s level? Had I gotten three points per level of the skill? Or did it work differently? Would learning and breaking down the same skill twice give me the same benefit?

It was something I needed to figure out by replicating. Even if I could grow my mana by three points per day, that was over a thousand points toward my mana capacity in the span of a year. That was a big deal.

The rewards didn’t end there:

[You have received one skill point to distribute to any common rarity skill that involves crafting.]

[Please select from the following options: Trap Making, Tinkering, Polishing, Crafting.]

Hold on, I could put the point in a level ten skill? What would that do?

I was curious enough to find out. I selected [Tinkering].

[Tinkering: 10 >> 10. Refined/combined skill will start one point higher.]

Holy shit. Had I just found a way to overcap my new skills?

That Major Advancement had been an anomaly. A quest reward. I’d seen the ability to get extra points in my refined skills as an awesome result of that plus two to all skills, but not something in any way replicable. The idea that I could do the same thing by breaking down skills was insane. I wondered if there was a limit to how many times I could repeat this?

That being said, I had one sobering thought: I’d only been able to place the point in a common skill. The skill also had to be at least somewhat relevant to what I was breaking down.

With that being said, it could be that only common skills could be upgraded in this fashion, or it could be that rare skills required other rare skills to be broken down in order for me to be able to put an extra point in them.

If that was the case, I might struggle to overcap skills of higher rarities. I’d yet to actually learn anything above Uncommon, all of my Semi-Rare and higher skills had come from refinement and combination.

Still, I was happy with my discovery, and while a part of me was eager to test more, thinking about breaking down [Pickaxe Mastery] if only for the fact that I now no longer felt I needed to worry about mining, I held off.

Thinking about combinations instead, I grouped together [Persusasion] with a few other skills.

One combo that was sticking out in my mind was [Intimidation] and [Persuasion], but I didn’t have a skill below level ten to go with them and I wasn’t sure if it was a waste to combine two level ten skills without having something extra to throw in. I also wasn’t sure that what I got out of it would really be applicable in a majority of social situations.

Honestly, the fact I’d levelled [Intimidation] so high was testament to my strange life.

Another combo I thought of was [Persuasion], [Haggling], and [Mathematics]. That sounded very merchant-like. I actually really liked the sound of that. I was sold as soon as I thought it up.

I would need to get [Haggling] or [Mathematics] to level ten in order to make that specific combo, however. [Haggling] was closer, and I had a bunch of free money I could burn right now, so I tried to make getting to ten with that a short-term goal.

I filled the bath, washed myself, and got dressed in some new clothes that had been left for me. Their composition was strange, the shapes different to what I was used to, but I pretty much matched most of the other Drassians I’d seen walking around, now.

I didn’t know if that was more or less weird.

I left my room, still thinking about skill combinations as I went. One other thing I did during the walk was take the stupid enchanted sword and stick it in the [Loan] tab. I’d been too overwhelmed to consider it at the time, but the useless weapon wasn’t totally useless. It was worth 20,000 gold, and while I was sure no one would borrow it, at an interest rate of .5% per week, I could expect to earn a hundred gold a week just by holding that broadsword in the [Loan] tab.

I supposed I had the Rat God to thank for that. Asshole.

I eventually found my way to the indoor swimming pool. The looks I got in there were really noticeable. Some people came up to me, asking me questions about where I was from and how I was liking Drassia, pulling out their own phones and creating flashes with them.

Others looked at me with the same disdain, disgust, or distaste I associated with being a rag-wearing orphan in a wealthier district. It wasn’t long after I’d gotten into the large pool that I’d realised there was a fairly wide berth around me in the water, with everyone pushed toward the outside like a shockwave had rippled through the water.

I hid underwater for a little while. Well, I wasn’t sure if I was hiding. It was just nice to swim without feeling all of those eyes on me.

I got out wet, and went to leave to my room until a staff member walked up to me and directed me to a changing room with towels.

Then a shop containing swimwear.

Oh. I supposed I might’ve gotten strange looks for more reasons than being human. I’d been fully dressed in normal Drassian clothes.

I dried myself—and my clothes—using a giant heating machine in the changing rooms, which I also found to be extremely comfortable, then caught myself in a mirror, my hair all poofy.

There were a lot of mirrors and other reflective things on Drassia. I hadn’t really looked at myself properly since entering the mines.

Now, shirtless, I could see that I’d put on a bit of weight. I was still likely thinner than the average person my age, but my frame was more shredded and defined than it ever had been previously, and my ribs weren’t sticking out of my chest nearly as much.

I was somewhat happy with what I saw. I still wanted to be better, to be bigger and stronger, but seeing actual progress was something positive.

I walked to the gym room after drying off, still thinking as I went. As I walked, I saw a Drassian woman walking around with her phone pushing halfway out of her back trouser pocket. I thought about how easy it’d be for that to fall out. Or for someone to rob her.

Another combo that occured to me then was [Sleight of Hand] with [Stealth], which I imagined would give me something pickpcoket or magician adjacent.

That being said, I wasn’t sure I needed either. [Hoard] already allowed me to disappear things with no fuss. Still, I supposed it might make my hands harder to see?

It was a slight consideration, but I didn’t know how much I expected to be picking people’s pockets. If anything, obscuring or hiding my hand motions still might be useful in a fight, but I wasn’t sure if a [Pickpocket] skill would carry over in that way, assuming I even got one.

Another thought for [Sleight of Hand] had been combining it with [Marksmanship]. I wondered if such a combo might make me more dexterous with ranged weapons.

That being said, I’d also been hoping there was a gun-related skill I just hadn’t learned yet due to lack of practice. Part of me wanted to hold out and see if one existed before I committed to creating a substitute from thin air.

I eventually reached the gym and a man at the front desk called me over. He asked if I’d ever used such a facility and I said ‘no’. He gave me a rundown of how all the machines inside worked, then told me to ask someone if I was unsure about anything and wished me a nice day.

He was probably the friendliest Drassian I’d interacted with besides Daryn. Didn’t act overly curious or rude. It might’ve just been him acting more proper because he was working here, I guessed.

Come to think, was that why Daryn was nice, too? Because it was his job to look after me while I found my bearings?

I hoped not.

I tried a few different machines, as well as doing some anaerobic exercise, and quickly determined that a facility like this didn’t do much for me that I couldn’t accomplish training on my own. That said, it was more comfortable and convenient, and the addition of weights to my training regimen was something entirely new to me.

In fact, after over an hour of lifting different weights and trying different machines, watching the few other people around the gym and trying to copy their lead, despite using much smaller weights than them, I earned a new skill.

[Weightlifting: Level 1 Acquired!]

Damn, guess that burn in my muscles was worth something.

I considered breaking it down. I could, couldn’t I? And then learn it again?

I decided to wait. I’d attempt to re-learn [Whittling] before I made commitments with any other skills. Weightlifting was likely something I could improve even without access to fancy gym equipment, and I needed to work on my strength. It was by far my worst attribute.

I was on a bench trying to push up a weight I found heavy when Daryn appeared above me.

“Hey dude! Feeling the burn yet?”

Before I could answer, he pinched the bar between two fingers. He lifted it back up and onto the rack.

“Wondered if I’d find you here. Seemed like your kinda place.”

“Is it yours?” I asked after shelving the embarrassment of how easily he’d lifted that bar.

“Nah. Don’t really bother with weightlifting. I’m more of a cardio guy.”

Of course he didn’t.

“Anyways, I’ve got a bit of a surprise for you, and it isn’t lunch.”

I finished wiping the sweat off my face and quirked an eyebrow.

“Guess who got out of surgery this morning?”

I smiled.

“I’ll give you a hint. It’s like… the only other person you know here.”

“Can we go see her?”

Daryn chuckled. “Yeah, sure. After you shower.”

Daryn proceeded to explain how a shower worked.

***

We went back to the hospital in one of the land vehicles, rather than a flying one. Daryn was the one driving, and I spent about half the travel time staring at him in awe as he moved a wheel in front of him that turned the entire vehicle left and right.

The pedals controlled the acceleration, he told me. Apparently, the ‘car’ could drive on its own but he liked to do it himself because he found it fun. He said this was a ‘sweet ride’ and that there was no point in driving a car like this if you were gonna ‘leave it on autopilot’.

He revved the engine for effect, driving faster as we approached an empty stretch of road.

“This… car is pretty expensive then?” I asked him.

“You better believe it. I had to save up for five years to afford this sweetheart. Even on my salary, a ride like this is something pretty extravagant.”

“What do you do?” I asked him, looking out at the road as we turned a corner. “I assume you don’t spend most of your time shepherding aliens around.” It felt strange to refer to myself that way, but it’s the word the Drassians seemed to have for me.

Daryn pushed air from his teeth. “Honestly, no. I’ve never done anything like this before. This job kinda got dropped in my lap last minute.”

“So what’s your usual job?”

“I’m part of the Drassian Defence Service.”

I replayed the words a couple of times in my mind. We rolled to a stop as a purple light which apparently meant ‘stop’ shone above.

“You’re… a soldier?”

“In a manner of speaking,” Daryn half-shrugged. “Drassia doesn’t really have many wars, so there’s never been a large standing army. But once we learned about alien life about a hundred years ago…”

He let the words trail. It started to click together in my head pretty quickly.

“So I am a security risk,” I concluded.

“You were at first,” Daryn said, his voice solemn. “That being said, we’re pretty convinced you’re a fairly normal kid. At this point, I’m just here to keep you safe and show you around. Be someone that can look out for you if you need something.”

“How’d you end up in the army?” I asked him, wanting a bridge off this conversation.

“The Drassian Defence Service,” Daryn repeated.

The purple light turned orange, and the cars began to drive.

“Yeah. That.”

“It’s a woeful tale,” Daryn said, waving his hand across the foreground. “Once, I was but a humble young Daryn who was deeply in love with a girl named Cylthissa.”

“Wait,” I snickered. “Cylthissa?”

“Well, of course Drassian names sound stupid to you,” Daryn pointed out. “You know how dumb the name Adam sounds to a Drassian?”

I felt a flash of annoyance, then got the point.

“Sorry.”

“Anyways, as I was saying. I loved Cylthissa, she was my girlfriend in college. I had dreams of starting a family, and she had dreams of becoming an aerospace engineer.

“I decided to join the academy with her, travelling hundreds of miles so we could stay together. I failed the entrance exam to her course three times, so I decided to do what my mother had always said and ‘use my brawn for something useful’, and thus, I enrolled in the academy’s defense programme.”

“Did you end up with… Cylritha in the end?”

“Cylthissa, and no, things didn’t work out.” Daryn kept his eyes on the road. “I was kidding myself with her. She was super smart and career-driven. All I wanted was to work a desk job and come home to a wife and kids. I realised I was dragging her down, and we ended things cordially.”

“Cordially?” I asked him.

“No one was an asshole to each other,” he explained. “Funny thing is, last we spoke, she’s got kids now. And I’m still on my own.”

I side-eyed him.

“Wait… doesn’t that mean that she lied to you about what she wanted?”

“Hah, it’s funny.”

“What is?”

“You sound like a grown-up most of the time, but sometimes, you say something that really reveals your age.”

I blinked at that. I felt my face flush.

“What are you talking about?”

“Cylthissa didn’t lie to me.” He turned another corner. The hospital was in sight now. “We just wanted different things at different points in our lives. If I’d stayed with her, knowing that I wanted more than she was willing to give, and she’d been lumbered with that expectation, I’d just have been putting her in a box. Both of us would’ve ended up disappointed. And if she’d changed her mind later, I didn’t want that to be because I’d pressured her.”

“But what if it wasn’t?” I asked. “What if she was just always going to change eventually?”

He ruminated on it for a moment. “I mean… yeah. That’s possible. Still doesn’t mean she lied, though. Would you carry on with something that wasn’t making you happy just in the hope that one day it might?”

“I dunno.”

I didn’t.

“So what about you? She changed. Did you?”

“I still want kids,” Daryn admitted. “I’m older now, though. It’s harder to meet someone who wants the same thing. A lot of people my age are already married or have kids. Not that I’d mind raising someone else’s. Still, the dating market’s rough.”

A lot that flew over my head. One thing stuck out to me, though.

“How old are you?”

“Oh, I’m forty-two.”

“Seriously?” I asked, my eyes bugging. “You look like you’re in your twenties.”

“We have a four hundred day year, if that makes a difference?”

“...that makes you even older in my mind.”

“Well, Drassians don’t really show signs of age until they’re eighty. We live til about a hundred and forty, so I guess I’m still ‘young’, technically. Don’t feel it, though.”

“That’s insane.”

It was and it wasn’t. To someone like me, who’d been raised around the low-tiered poor, living past eighty was an achievement, but I knew high Tiers lived longer. Some even for hundreds of years. The king was meant to be over two hundred years old.

“Anyway, we’re here now. You can go straight in. There’s someone in reception waiting to take you.”

“You’re not coming?”

“I’ll be in the car. I’ve had enough of hospitals for one week.”

I didn’t argue. I was about to ask how the person waiting would know it was me, but the answer was pretty apparent. I walked through the front door and was received by a Drassian nurse who seemed to think I had a virulent disease and was worried he might catch it. He walked at an extreme distance from me while keeping a brisk pace, and I struggled to keep up.

When we arrived at the end of a seventh long corridor, he said ‘through here’, the only words he’d spoken since I’d arrived besides a rushed ‘hello’, and then held the door wide open, careful not to touch me as I walked through.

“Come back the same way when you’re done,” he told me. “Follow the arrows to reception.”

I’d noticed the maps lining the walls on the way here. It was a good thing, too, because he didn’t seem interested in sticking around to show me the way out.

Once I’d taken a few more steps, I was greeted by another Drassian, green-skinned and skinny. She gave me a big smile.

“Hi there! You’re the human!”

I endured about twenty questions about everything from my home planet to my culture and a query about whether humans ‘really ate bark from trees’ before being showed to Maya’s room. Come to think of it, Alai really had held back in her questioning compared to some of the average Drassian citizenry. The funniest thing was the idea that I could’ve told them almost anything and they’d most likely believe it.

The thought exited my mind as I came across Maya.

She looked tired, sat up in bed with a couple of tubes running into her arm, more than I’d had. A few sticky pads were attached to her arms also, and her gown pooled around her at the ankles like it was too big for her.

“Hi there,” she said, her voice a little hoarse.

“Hi,” I responded, not really sure what else to say.

She motioned with her head toward a nearby chair.

“Come on. Don’t be a stranger.”

Hesitantly, I crossed the room and sat on one of the seats beside her bed. I looked at her, then looked away.

She was pale, and despite the fact she was smiling, her eyes looked sad. It was harder to look at her than I thought it’d be.

“How are you feeling?” I eventually asked her, hoping some conversation would help me get over my nerves. “They said your operation went well. That’s good, right?”

“Tired,” Maya answered, confirming my earlier assessment. “They’ve got me stuck in this hospital for monitoring for the next three days. Apparently, I’m not allowed to leave as an out patient, even though my vitals are all normal.” She switched her tone to a super serious male one. “It’s an unprecedented circumstance, no one has been in cryosleep for quite so long before, we have an obligation to ensure your health and wellbeing.”

The voice became progressively sillier as she kept it up. I cracked a smile listening.

“That sounds annoying. They only kept me in for a few hours and I felt like climbing the walls. Are you looking forward to getting back out into the world?”

The moment I asked her that, her smile faltered.

“Uh… yeah!” She pssh’d. “Like no, I wanna stay here all day.”

I looked around the blank room. “I dunno. Maybe you like the scenery.”

She chuckled some more. She reached for a glass of water on the desk, and I passed it to her.

“What about you?” she asked, changing the subject. “Mind if I ask where you came from?”

“A different world.”

“Well, duh. What’s it like?”

Her eyes were wide as she looked at me. I thought about how to put it.

“Well, there’s less technology,” I started. “Way less. People still use horses to get around.”

“What are horses?” Maya asked.

I realised there was no word for it in Drassian. She’d said it in my tongue.

“Big animals that pull carts, and that people ride on top of.”

“Oh! Like werchals. People used to get around on those.”

I simply nodded.

“Anyway, it’s not just the technology difference. People on my world can use magic.” I lowered my voice. “But don’t go telling everyone I said that.”

“Your secret’s safe with me.” She paused for a moment. Her eyes bugged. “Wait, did you say magic?”

“Damn right I did.”

“Show me.”

Oh, shit. I hadn’t really expected her to request that so brazenly.

I thought about what I could show her. I didn’t think that [Kinetic Barrier] was really something I could show off without risking hurting her, and while I could heat the mana on my body and touch her somewhere, that felt awkward…

Suddenly, I had an idea.

“Okay. Can I just grab something that I left behind your ear?”

She gave me an incredulous look.

“Dude, do you think I’m five? Everyone in the universe knows this trick.”

Funny that she’d say that. I knew it was something adults did to kids back home, but they didn’t usually do it with an inventory at their disposal.

“Try me,” I said with confidence. “I think you’ll be surprised what I can do.”

She looked as if she was going to burst into a grin as she leaned forward, her silvery hair draping down over the bed.

“Fine. But if it’s lame, I’m not going to pretend otherwise.”

“Deal.”

I leaned forward, brushing past her long hair as I reached behind her ear. I made a show of rummaging around. Once I was done, I pulled back, holding a single golden coin.

“Oh, there it is, I found it.”

She laughed at me.

“That was not magic!”

“Nah, you’re right,” I said, keeping eye contact with her. “To be honest, I could only find the one coin. It’s kind of annoying me, because I swear I left the rest somewhere…. Oh!”

I looked past her, over to the opposite side of the bed, where I’d summoned the rest of my bag of gold, all of it spilt out over the mattress.

Maya followed my gaze in shock. She stared at the bag of gold.

“H-how did you… what the fuck?!”

I chuckled as I walked across the room to pick it up. I made sure to turn my back before re-storing all of it in my [Hoard], then I turned back to face her.

“How’s that for a magic trick?”

“B-but… you were right there the whole time!”

“I was.”

“How the heck did you do that?”

“You know revealing a trick spoils it, right?”

Maya pouted at me, but she didn’t insist on me telling her for long. Before I knew it, she was asking me if I could do this or that magical feat, and for at least half of them, I was forced to say that while I couldn’t do it myself, I’d heard of someone who could.

“Everyone on your planet does magic,” she eventually concluded.

“Well, a lot of them do. There are a lot of non-mages, too, but magic’s pretty common in my world. It’s a part of our lives.”

“Your planet must be absolutely amazing.”

I hesitated on that. Was it amazing?

I mean, sure, I liked the scenery in my world. I think I liked the way people treated me a bit more than how Drassians did, at least back in the slums. I felt a sense of belonging on Tellos, and I liked the honesty of the system. Class selections might not be fair, and there might be other things about advancing in my world that were pretty shitty, but it was nice to feel as if you could always improve at something. Like there was always another level that you could reach.

Drassia didn’t have anything quite like that. No systems. No struggle to get ahead. No grand reward for success, either. The rich weren’t so different from the poor here, from what I’d seen.

It was a place that seemed to reward you simply for existing. A place where you could be anyone and do anything and get along just fine.

“My world is different,” I finally concluded, being careful with how I put it. “It’s a world where you can become a legend if you strive hard enough. It’s a world that keeps advancing and forces you to keep up with it. It’s a world where you’re defined by the adversity you overcome.”

“Sounds rough.”

“It is,” I admitted.

“But if it produced someone as awesome as you, then clearly you’re cut out for it.”

I blinked at that.

“What do you mean?”

Maya blushed.

“You saved my life, Adam. You know I was trapped in there for like… fifteen years, right?”

I hadn’t realised how long it was. That was somewhat harrowing to think about.

“I mean, my body didn’t age at all. I was completely frozen. I’m still physically and mentally nineteen. But everyone I knew has gotten older.”

Her voice cracked a little. She spoke more slowly.

“My dad’s gone,” she continued. “My mom died when I was little, so it’s just me now. All my school friends are in their thirties, and I didn’t have many to begin with…”

She sniffled. I looked away as she wiped her eyes.

“I think you’re a hero,” she told me, grabbing my hand. “I don’t know how you ended up in that rift, or what you’ve been through before now, but I remember what you did for me. I don’t remember much from my dreams, just fragments, but I remember you. I know you worked hard to get me out of there, that you risked your life doing it.”

She took a deep breath. “So… even if I’m scared, I’m just gonna try and be more like you. Because you’re fucking cool, dude.

“You can do anything.”

***

Maya got worse before she got better. She started crying more heavily, then apologised and told me that she didn’t want to make me upset.

I felt horrible seeing her like that, but she told me that she was going to try her best to get back out into the world and live her life, even if it was scary, and I believed her. I asked her if I could come back and see her again some time, and she made me promise I would before I left, if I did decide to leave.

If.

I didn’t argue with her phrasing. I hadn’t wanted to at the time and I didn’t want to think about it now. Nonetheless, I was forced to think about it some more when after returning to the hotel that evening, I got something called a ‘text’ from Alai.

Apparently, she also had a means of contacting my phone. Her text explained that a crew was making sure the integrity of the portal was secure and that all threats were removed from inside of the facility before I’d be able to be escorted back. She said the work would take somewhere around two weeks.

That left me with some time to burn. Thankfully, I still had plenty that I wanted to do in Drassia, and I was happy to use the time to get some training in.

As well as think about what…

Well, what I wanted to do.

Could I really leave my home behind?

I felt Maya’s words replaying in my mind as I tried to sleep again.

You can do anything.

Anything but make my mind up.
