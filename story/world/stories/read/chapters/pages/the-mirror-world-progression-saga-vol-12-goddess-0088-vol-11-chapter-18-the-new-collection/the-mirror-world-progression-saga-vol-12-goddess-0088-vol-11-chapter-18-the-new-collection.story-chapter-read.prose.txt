Samantha was still reviewing her changes from the level up. It looked like [Alter Spell: Amp] and [Alter Spell: Reinforce] had combined, which was, all-in-all, a useful upgrade, adding extra onto an excellent general boost. How useful would depend on the details…

Intensify (+4): Power and dependability. Add +2 caster levels to the spell for the purposes of overcoming resistance in contests, or being countered, negated, or dispelled. Add an additional +1 if the spell has some sort of apparent ‘material’ manifestation, regardless of the realm. Never adds to duration or range.

Samantha’s eyebrows rose. That expanded what it could be used on quite a bit, and opened up ‘converted’ physical effects in spirit realms, compared to Amp. Reinforce, she didn’t make much use of, as MP conservation was always a thing to make use of the latest and greatest stuff. [Alter Spell: Double] was her go-to, accelerating costs enough as it was.

For the rest of the abilities, she quickly identified those that were simply ‘+1 to effects’ or the otherwise standard, expected upgrades from the rest. She got them out of the way first.

[Dismissal] improved its basic contests from +1 Effective Levels to +2 for the AoE version and from +2 Effective Levels to +3 for the single-target version.

[Light Absorption] improved the resistance to effects with ‘energy components’ from +3 to +4 Effective Levels.

[Purify] improved all comparison tests from +3 Effective Levels to +4.

[Removal: Rip] improved the Lethality Grade from Effective Levels +2 to +3.

[The Dawn Will Rise] improved the active task bonus from +2 Effective Levels to +3, and the other special resistance bonuses, such as to fear and morale effects, from +4 Effective Levels to +5.

Unsurprisingly, [Mirror Sense] upgraded the 9x9 restriction to 8x8, and [Mirror Walk] improved distance by another 11 kilometers, to 77.

[Sheath of Light], which she rarely seemed to have time to use despite being pretty great in non-stealth situations, simply improved its wound negations from 3 to 4. That likely signaled the best divine spells getting 4 wound negations at Level 12.

If I know I’m gonna get into a knock-down, drag-out, physically oriented fight, I need to make time for this mama jama, especially because it isn’t subject to dispelling like most of my protection spells. Granted, my last big battle was all mental anyway.

[Conjure] had changed the ‘Sensory Expansion 2’ enhancement…

Total Sensory Suite: Your illusions fool all manner of senses you desire, even supernatural ones, provided their ‘base level’ (such as spell level) is below your deity level. Note that a Level 20 spell or effect will thus always see through your illusions. However, if you fail this comparison but pass the normal contest by 5 or more, the subject will be fooled for at least a moment.

Regardless of your choice, illusions cannot literally affect the real world, only fool subjects into thinking they do.

Samantha shrugged. It was excellent for when she did use illusions, but generally, she was just creating actual physical, if fairly weak, fake matter through the conjuration effect. Illusions did remain more widescale and flexible, and this was a significant buff to that flexibility.

This generates a tangible spirit entity that will serve you and to whom you may delegate deific tasks. Personality details determine the independence level. These entities can take material form if summoned by you or an authorized clerical agent.

A cleric requires a 7-minute ritual, or can summon faster with certain spells. A Servitor can be summoned up to three times per day. In the laboratory of the great tower of Itha Ranon, a combination smithy, apothecary, and wizard’s lab filled to the brim with books, ingredients, and tools of many varied sizes, there was a central runic circle far clearer than the rest. Here was the obscenely reinforced ‘operation table’ where a cluster of folk were gathered around a ritual in progress.

Seven of them were resurrected spirits housed within golem bodies: Sir Galahad, forged of a petrified troll’s leftover material, the first and perhaps most special, thanks to his unique healing abilities; Sir Lamorak of the Clay, the second, a man of raw brawn and directness; Sir Bedwyr of the Wood, the third, a one-armed man of guile and versatility; Sir Gawain of the Green Basalt, the fourth, a man that stumbles and wears, yet forever endures and perseveres; Sir Bors of the Granite, the fifth, loyal, steadfast, immovable against all pressure; Sir Kay of the Bronze, the sixth, a dignified seneschal of fluid pragmatism and administrative acumen…

And then me, Sir Lucan of the Steel, a vaguely serviceable old fool.

They awaited their awakener, the Blind Daughter, the Lifemaker, as she led a ritual of chants, gestures, and mystical manipulation composed of many Samanthian magi and witches, mostly feeding her the extensive energy required to craft the eighth golem. The material was upon the table, an elegant and beautiful construct made all of silver, glittering in the light.

So the true heroes — the swords that cut swiftly — are finally being summoned, as opposed to us old, rusty tools.

They were still struggling to gather the rest of the materials. Technically, they had everything for the tenth already, yet not quite the ninth, which was lapis lazuli, and rare on Calrenazzod. The rest beyond that, their odds of finding it all in the time left to them were… harrowing, daunting, sobering.

Getting the lapis lazuli will help. We need his wisdom badly. We need the seeker, the hunter, more than any other in this world. Only through him will we make the table complete, I think… What secrets from beyond can we mine to unlock the means of discovery to these elements here? The random inspirations of Bedwyr will not suffice for the bulk. Patterns will correlate as we ponder, though, as we dive deep and make a strategy out of the puzzleworks…

Soon lost in thought along these lines, Sir Lucan only turned his bulky metal head back to the current special occasion when it was done, and the Blind Daughter was being helped away from the table, as the golem made of silver stirred. Lucan felt his great brow draw down at that. When he’d awakened, ‘Ana’ had been smiling, and not near to fainting like this. Evidently, the true heroes required more out of her.

Hardly surprising when it comes down to it.

Glowing and glittering like an absurd ideal of the moon, the elegant, fine-featured golem sat up, looking at itself slowly, its every motion fluid as if mercurial liquid. Even the hair swayed, and his eyes were like twin stars twinkling, though flickering moodily, caught between existing and not. All-in-all, he was quite the work of art compared to the rest of them.

Again, hardly surprising; all too appropriate for this one.

Sir Lucan stepped forward, so the newly-minted being would see the one he needed to see first. But, of course, this one was still not looking, transfixed by itself.

Clearing his throat, Sir Lucan called in as moderate a tone as a massive steel golem could manage, “Declare and speak your name, knight and brother-in-arms.”

The silver golem simply glanced at him, then back at his hand as he studied it. He sighed prettily. “Quicksilver, apparently?”

Laughter rippled out from the likes of Lamorak, Gawain, and Kay, in disturbing contrasts of harmonics that made the ritualists gathered physically wince in pain.

Sir Lucan just sighed wearily.

Insufferable tart.
