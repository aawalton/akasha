As much as I wanted to just hide in the bathroom forever, I knew I couldn’t. I was dreading going shopping with Mom, but staying in the bathroom for longer than I needed to wouldn’t help at all. Still, I couldn’t help but hesitate as I turned towards the bathroom door, steeling myself before pulling it open. I half-expected to see my mom standing on the landing, waiting to chide me once more for moving too slowly, but she was nowhere to be seen.

I could hear her, though. Soft footsteps padded away from her bedroom door, and a muffled bang came from her room as she threw the wardrobe door open. She seemed to be making a lot of noise putting the laundry away, as if trying to pretend that was all she’d been doing, which only made me suspect she’d been listening at the door for me to emerge from the bathroom.

That wouldn’t have surprised me either. She did that sometimes, but I couldn’t bring myself to care. I just didn’t have the energy. She wasn’t shouting at me, and that was all that mattered to me as I walked back into my room and shut the door.

My eyes landed on my bed, and a wave of longing washed over me. I wanted nothing more than to sink back into it and pull the duvet over my head, to fall asleep again or slip into another world where I could do as I pleased. There had to be one. There must be a world where Mom hadn’t decided we were going shopping today, and I was still almost giddy with the excitement of seeing Duncan again. If I managed to find that world, I could disappear into it and still have my date with him.

Something seemed to pulse in the back of my mind. I could feel something pulling at me, reeling me in, and I did not fight back. I let myself be drawn closer, feeling warmth spread throughout my body as it became lighter. The exhaustion and numbness that had settled over me seemed to slip away, and a smile tugged at my lips. That was the world I was looking for.

The room started to spin around me, and my breathing hitched. A band tightened around my chest, preventing me from inhaling deeply, and I squeezed my eyes shut, letting myself slip out of reality before a flare of fear exploded in my heart.

My eyes snapped open as my pulse rushed in my ears. I stared around my room, panic surging throughout my body as I fought to suck in a breath. I was being foolish, and I knew I was. I couldn’t just leave my reality and go to a new one. That was too dangerous, and I knew nothing about that world. It could be just as terrifying as all of the other ones that I’d had to run from before. It felt good. The world seemed as though it was filled with happiness and joy, but I did not trust that.

Slowly, I peeled myself away from the door that I’d been pressed against, my heart still pounding. That was a close call. It was too close. I’d almost slipped into a new world without even realising what I was doing, and things were going too well for me in reality. I was about to go shopping with Mom, and that would be awful, but apart from that, things were going well. I had a good balance between my world and the Academy. She liked it when I dressed more feminine, rather than wearing leggings and a t-shirt, which she often said made me look like a boy, but I didn’t have the energy to do it. Mom always had the television on, and I could normally hear her wandering around in the kitchen or lounge, but the house was still as I crept down the stairs before coming to a stop.

Mom could emerge from one of the rooms at any second, and she’d catch me standing out there. It made it easier, and I usually did better when I had a chance to take a deep breath before speaking to her. My eyes snapped towards the bathroom door as the metallic clank of coins tumbling echoed quietly, and a smile appeared on my face. Immediately, I knew exactly where she was, and I was certain she would be in the bathroom for a while. Once she got sucked into the boring mobile game she was addicted to, she seemed to lose track of the passage of time.

It kind of made me laugh. Mom always told me that gaming was a waste of time. Only slackers and idiots spent their days tapping away on their computer or a games console, and Mom had always claimed she didn’t understand the appeal, but somehow, one game had managed to ensnare her. I had no idea how. It seemed pretty boring, but Mom seemed to enjoy it.

And it meant I had a few more minutes at least before having to deal with her again, and I was happy about that. I let out a deep breath before moving silently into the kitchen and looking around. I hadn’t anticipated having that time to myself, and I wasn’t quite sure what to do with it.

Putting my socks and shoes on and waiting by the front door was probably the smartest option. Then, as soon as Mom was done, I’d be ready to go out, but even just the thought of standing there with nothing to do for an unknown amount of time sent a wave of restlessness through me.

I wasn’t particularly hungry or thirsty, but I’d yet to have anything that morning, and I had no idea when I’d get the chance again. Mom hadn’t been too bad about it when we were in Scotland. She didn’t judge me too much when I ate, but sometimes, she seemed to forget that people needed to eat to survive. Food seemed to be an annoyance to her, and the fact that I needed it made her view me as weak.

Placing my socks and phone on the counter, I grabbed a glass from the cupboard and turned the tap gently. The pipes squealed softly as water rushed forth, and I winced as I twisted the handle a little more. There was a sweet spot with the cold tap. If I hit that, it was silent, but if I went too far or not far enough, it made a noise, and it was hard to find the right balance sometimes.

My eyes flicked between the doorway and my glass as it gradually filled up, checking to see whether Mom would appear. The sound must have been loud enough for her to have heard. It was normally audible from my room, but no noise came from the bathroom.

Most of the things needed to be cooked. They were ingredients that I could make a meal with, but I couldn’t see anything that I could just eat.

There were carrots, though. I probably had time to peel one quickly and eat it before Mom emerged from the bathroom. She’d probably judge me a little for doing that, and it was a bit of a sad breakfast, but perhaps that would be better than nothing.

But I didn’t reach towards the vegetable drawer. My hand stayed firmly on the door, the other hanging at my side as I stared at the carrots. I just didn’t have the motivation or energy to do that. It was too much effort, and there had to be something else I could eat.

My gaze flicked towards the cupboard, and I hesitated before shutting the fridge and moving towards it. Mom didn’t really like to have snacks in the house. She thought if they were there, I’d eat them all without even thinking about it, but there might be some cereal bars or something hidden away in there. Dad could have gotten them a while ago without Mom noticing.

I’d just crouched down, my hand resting on the cupboard handle, when a weird noise reached my ears. It sounded almost like the pipes grumbling, and I looked towards the kitchen door again, expecting to hear the toilet flushing, but I didn’t. The only sound that came from the bathroom was the sporadic jingle of coins.

Hesitating for a moment longer, I pulled the cupboard open and stared inside. There was cereal. It was kind of boring. Nothing with added sugar or any flavour, but that would fill me up. It was good for me, and I probably wouldn’t get hungry again until the afternoon if I ate a bowl of that.

But Mom would definitely hear the flakes falling into the bowl, and she’d get annoyed again if she needed to wait for me to finish it. Giving up and binning what I’d poured out would be worse, though. She’d accuse me of being ungrateful and wasting food, saying I was just throwing money straight into the bin and didn’t appreciate how hard both she and Dad worked to provide for me.

Tiredness washed over me at the thought of hearing that rant again. I’d experienced it many times before, and it always made me feel bad. It wasn’t worth it, and I wasn’t really hungry, but I still needed to have some food.

Glancing at the door once more, I carefully reached for the box of cereal and pulled it from the cupboard. My movements were slow, and my gaze darted towards the door almost constantly as I slowly opened the box and unfurled the plastic bag inside. I hesitated for just a moment before starting to tip the cereal into my hand.

I jumped as another noise sounded, and my head snapped up. It was louder that time, harder to ignore, and I barely hesitated before placing the box of cereal back into the cupboard and standing.

My footsteps were silent as I edged towards the kitchen door and peered through. The television was definitely off, I realised as my eyes found the blank screen. The sound couldn’t have come from there, but then… was it Mom?

Concern simmering within me, but I could still hear her playing the game in the bathroom, and I didn’t think the sound had come from her. I had no idea why she’d be making any noise in there, but she couldn’t be watching a video on her phone. She didn’t know it was possible to watch something and play the game at the same time.

But then what was it?

Fear started to pull at me, and I swallowed, looking around again before letting my eyes shut. I thought I’d managed to catch myself in time when I’d been looking for a world where Mom hadn’t ruined my plans with Duncan, but maybe I hadn’t.

Worlds crowded around me. I could feel each unique dizziness calling out to me, trying to tempt me in, and I clenched my hands into fists as I moved past them, hearing noises reach out to me from the world I was in. New ones had appeared. I was almost sure of it. Mitch’s world continued to shine out to me like a beacon, but there were some sensations, some worlds, that I’d never felt before. My world was not there. I did not feel the touch of my reality, and I took a deep breath before opening my eyes again. If it was not there, that meant I was still in it. I couldn’t just ignore the thing I could hear, whatever it was. I would have to face it at some point.

It probably wasn’t anything scary, I tried to tell myself as I turned slowly. The noises seemed to be coming from outside, so they could have been anything, but my reality did not have monsters. There were no mythical creatures that could rip me to ribbons with a simple swipe of their claws, and everything would be okay.

Still, my heart was racing as I moved back toward the sink and slowly lifted my eyes to stare out the window. My gaze scoured the garden, searching for anything that appeared out of place. I couldn’t see what it was from where I stood, and I had no idea how it had gotten into our garden. I knew that I probably should have been scared of it, but it looked like it was shaking in fear, and immediately, I knew I couldn’t just leave it out there.

The animal whimpered again, the pained sound reaching through the window and wrapping around my heart. It had seen me. I couldn’t see the creature’s eyes, but somehow, I knew that it had seen me, and it was begging me to go and help it.

I didn’t hesitate. Grabbing my phone in case I needed it, I padded out into the hallway and into my lounge. The quickest way to get to the animal was through the French windows, and I slipped my phone into my pocket, holding my breath as I twisted the key as slowly as I could. The lock made no noise, and I quickly slid the window open before stepping through and shutting it behind me again.

The animal seemed to have been waiting for the door to close because the moment it did, it let out another soft cry. The ache in my heart grew stronger, and I started to move towards it. I longed to run towards the creature, eager to do something about the pain in its cries, but I was worried that I would scare it if I did that. That might cause it to run or attack me.

It was a dog, I realised as I got closer, and my steps became slower. The pure black bundle of fluff must have squeezed through the chicken wire Dad put up behind the fence to stop the sheep from getting in between the wooden bars, and now, it was trapped. “Don’t worry,” I said in a soft voice, so I appeared as non-threatening as possible, as I stopped a little distance in front of the dog and held my hand out to it. “I’m not going to hurt you.”

Confusion washed over me, and I stepped forward cautiously, staring at the dog.

“Oh,” I breathed. “No wonder you were whimpering.”

The dog turned its head, watching my movements as I began reaching for the chicken wire again. My fingers laced through the sun-warmed metal, and I gripped it carefully, trying as hard as I could not to move the fence at all, but the dog made no noise.

Glancing at it again, I took a deep breath before pulling the fence upwards. The metal cut into my hands as I lifted it, but I was barely aware of the pain. All of my attention was fixed on the dog’s back as the wire finally came free.

“Okay, there we go,” I said to the dog. “Move forward, boy. Come on, just a bit!”

I had no idea whether it would know what I was saying, but thankfully, it seemed to understand. The dog crawled forward timidly, staying close to the floor as if scared of getting caught on the fence again, but I pulled even harder, making sure to keep it as far away as possible.

“There you go, boy,” I told him as I slowly lowered the fence again, certain that there was no chance it would catch him again. “All free.”

The dog’s tail wagged uncertainly, and it let out a soft whine.

“I know,” I said as I cautiously stroked its head again. “We need to do something about your back, don’t—”
