Looking at the description of this special scenario, it was clearly intended to push the three factions into conflict with one another. I was more than certain that, since we appeared to be the weakest side on paper, the other factions would eventually send divine warriors our way—whether for reconnaissance, infiltration, sabotage, or even a direct strike.

I mean, that was exactly what I would do. Even with a structured army under Boris, infiltration would not be particularly difficult. Among divine warriors, there were bound to be individuals with skills that allowed them to alter their appearance, conceal their presence, or even temporarily become invulnerable. An enemy slipping into our ranks was far from impossible. Luckily, I had already discussed this possibility with Boris and the others a day earlier.

For now, it would be better for us to scout the surroundings and inspect the supermarket that had most likely been assigned as our fortress.

“Men! Listen up! Let’s get inside the fortress and secure the perimeter!” Boris’s voice rang out across the street.

The divine warriors moved immediately. Under Boris’s command, the brigade leaders began issuing orders to their respective units. Groups split off with practiced efficiency—some forming defensive lines around the building while others advanced toward the entrances. Within moments, the area surrounding the supermarket had been secured with almost military precision.

Such coordination would not have been possible if I hadn’t suggested selecting a commander for Istellia’s forces. Otherwise, we would have been divided into numerous small factions, each vying for control and pushing its own agendas. Of course, despite Boris’s commanding presence and his decisive victory in the tournament, there were still some who were dissatisfied with serving under him. However, thanks to the authority granted to him by Raphael, any rebellious thoughts were nearly impossible to act upon. His commands carried an almost absolute weight. This was precisely why I had been careful not to let anyone untrustworthy take the position of commander.

Soon, Boris, along with several others, strode toward the main entrance, pushing aside the reinforced barricade just enough for us to slip through.

Inside, the supermarket was far larger than it had appeared from the outside. Long aisles stretched deep into the building, their shelves mostly empty except for scattered debris left behind from whatever catastrophe had once devastated this city. The interior lights flickered weakly overhead, casting pale illumination across the wide floor.

“First platoon, secure the roof,” Boris ordered calmly. “Second platoon, sweep the interior. Check every aisle, every storage room, every ventilation shaft. Make sure that there are no blind spots.”

Acknowledgments echoed as divine warriors moved to carry out their tasks. Meanwhile, several units began dragging additional debris toward the entrances, reinforcing the barricades even further. Within minutes, the supermarket was transforming from an abandoned ruin into a proper defensive stronghold.

As I watched the soldiers move with disciplined precision, someone suddenly shouted from deeper inside the building.

“Commander! Over here!”

Boris and I exchanged a glance before heading toward the voice. Several divine warriors had gathered near what used to be the center section of the supermarket, where shelves had once displayed promotional goods. Now the space had been cleared almost entirely, leaving a wide circular area in the middle of the store. At its center stood a statue, and it was a statue of Istellia.

The figure was carved from white stone that emitted a faint golden radiance. She stood gracefully with one hand holding a lantern-shaped object before her. Her long hair flowed down her back, and the delicate folds of her dress were captured perfectly in stone, though the features of her face were not clearly shown. Despite being nothing more than a statue, the craftsmanship was exquisite. Furthermore, I could sense a thick concentration of divine energy contained within it.

I wonder how much plausibility I would gain if I absorbed the power contained in the statue… I thought. Obviously, doing that would be incredibly stupid. The moment the statue was destroyed, we would immediately lose.

“So this is what we have to protect…” Michelle muttered from the side.

A translucent barrier surrounded the statue, forming a sphere roughly ten meters in diameter.

I stepped closer and studied it carefully. The statue was clearly the core of the fortress. As long as it remained intact, our faction would remain in the game. But the moment it was destroyed… we would lose.

“Set up defensive positions around this area,” Boris ordered immediately. “I want tight protection and constant surveillance. No one approaches the statue without authorization.”

The divine warriors responded instantly. Within moments, squads began organizing themselves around the statue’s chamber. Barricades were dragged into place, watch rotations were assigned, and defensive formations were established. Everyone here understood the same thing: the statue wasn’t some decoration, and it was the heart of our survival for the next seven days.

Seeing that everything was in order, I decided not to waste time staying at the base and told Boris and the others that I would be doing solo reconnaissance. I needed to locate the two enemy factions and perhaps find a way to sabotage them.

“Maxim, I think I should go with you,” said Michelle with worry. “My skills are suited for scouting, and I could support you whether from close range or from afar.”

“No need.” I shook my head. “There’s something I need to handle alone, and I’ll be able to escape easily if things go wrong. Don’t worry. Besides, your role here is far more essential than following me.”

I wasn’t lying. Michelle’s enhanced eyesight and detection skills would be extremely valuable in spotting approaching monsters or enemy divine warriors. If she accompanied me, the fortress would lose one of its most reliable sentries.

“Oh, and I have Tuilë’s upgraded comm-link,” I added. “So if anything happens, I’ll be able to contact you and request support.”

“I see.” She nodded slowly. “Be careful.”

“Gahaha, that’s Maxim for you!” Boris laughed heartily. “I’m sure he’ll be fine.”

“Agreed.”

“Hehe, the upgraded comm-link can cover a distance of one hundred kilometers. So it should be more than enough for the job.” Tuilë grinned, giving a confident thumbs-up.

Before the Battle of Divine Will began, Tuilë had handed out upgraded comm-links to all of us, including the trusted brigade leaders. The devices allowed communication across long distances, functioning much like walkie-talkies back on Earth.

In a battlefield that could span entire districts—or perhaps even larger territories—such coordination tools were invaluable. After all, those who controlled information controlled the battlefield.

Actually, the only thing I was truly concerned about was several strong groups whose intentions remained unclear, such as Kaela’s party and the Obsidian Order, among others. The former should be relatively manageable. As long as Kaela was given a strong opponent or assigned to operate independently on the front lines, she would likely cooperate without issue.

The latter, however, was another matter entirely. The Obsidian Order had always carried an unsettling air about it. Their motives were unclear, and they remained an enigmatic group. If not for the tournament, I wouldn’t have known anything about them at all. If anyone within our faction decided to pursue their own ambitions or stir internal chaos, they would be the most likely candidates. And in a scenario like this, a single unexpected move could jeopardize the entire army.

I left the supermarket shortly afterward and began moving through the surrounding streets, keeping my steps light and my senses alert. The ruined city stretched endlessly in every direction. Cracked asphalt crunched beneath my boots, and broken storefronts lined both sides of the street. The atmosphere felt eerily similar to the first scenario I had experienced when I was first summoned into Divine Will. Only this time, the scale was far larger.

I moved from alley to alley, climbing over collapsed concrete and weaving between rusted vehicles as I advanced farther away from the supermarket. The farther I went, the quieter the city became. Until I reached roughly one hundred meters from the supermarket, that was when I stopped.

“…Well, as expected of the Battle of Divine Will. The difficulty will never be easy,” I muttered.

The street ahead was crawling with monsters.

Hundreds—no, thousands of them filled the ruined intersection and the surrounding blocks. Most of them were the same creatures I had encountered during the first and second scenarios: flying octopuses and the humans they had taken over, transforming them into grotesque, zombie-like hosts. Only this time, they were far stronger.

[Octoferal – Lv.30]

Looks like an octopus, but don’t let appearance trick you, as it can turn you into its host. Tasty if cooked but will give you intense diarrhea.

[Aberrant Octoferal Zombie – Lv.55]

Originally a human host infected with an octopus-like parasite, this variant has undergone further mutation due to prolonged parasitic influence. Its body has grown denser and more resilient, granting it greatly enhanced physical strength and durability compared to regular Octoferal Zombies.

The zombies were taller and bulkier than before, their flesh swollen and distorted as though mutated by prolonged exposure to the parasites controlling them. The octopus-like creatures perched on their heads writhed restlessly, their slick tentacles twitching in unsettling motions. The sight alone was disgustingly grotesque.

Individually, they were extremely weak compared to me. But in such overwhelming numbers, they could still pose a serious threat even to the entirety of Istellia’s divine warrior army. If tens of thousands of them were to rush the fortress all at once while we were unprepared, then even if we managed to repel the assault, it would likely end in a pyrrhic victory. Just as they said, “There are strength in numbers.”

However, since I was only here on a reconnaissance mission, I had no intention of reducing their numbers for now.

I also noticed that stronger variants were mixed among them. Some zombies had elongated limbs that scraped along the ground like twisted apes, while others possessed hardened bone plates growing through their skin like natural armor.

[Brute Octoferal Zombie – Lv.90]

A heavily mutated Octoferal Zombie whose skeletal structure has partially surfaced through its flesh, forming hardened bone plates that act as natural armor. Its defensive capabilities and raw striking power far exceed those of ordinary variants, making it a dangerous frontline monster within the swarm.

Furthermore, their numbers were staggering. Even a conservative estimate placed them well into the hundreds.

Then I spotted something even more terrifying lurking deeper within the horde.

[Creeper Mutant – Lv.115]

A grotesque mutation born from prolonged parasitic corruption. This variant moves on six distorted limbs, crawling low across the ground with unsettling speed. Its swollen flesh is packed with unstable bio-organic compounds. When severely damaged or triggered by proximity to enemies, the creature detonates violently, releasing a devastating explosion capable of tearing through defenses and devastating tightly packed formations.

“An exploding mutant!”

If even one of those creatures managed to reach the fortress walls, the resulting blast would tear through our defenses like paper. It appeared that this was beyond just a monster incursion and more like a siege waiting to happen.

Considering that we needed to survive for seven days, it was highly likely that there would be multiple waves of attacks, which were most likely seven. This kind of structure was common in competitive defense scenarios, where the difficulty escalated with each passing wave. With that in mind, I immediately relayed everything I had seen to Boris and the others through the comm-link.

Once that was done, I continued moving farther away from the fortress to complete my initial objective: locating the two enemy divine warrior factions.
