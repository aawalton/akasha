Alicia had been walking for eight hours. It was now nearly nighttime.

No one had followed her.

She slowed, before sitting down beneath the canopy of a tree. There was no sweat on her skin. No breath. No pulse.

Nothing at all.

Of course.

She was already dead.

Walking corpses did not require either. Eight hours ago, she hadn’t known they could even walk.

Alicia raised her hand, turning it slightly as she examined it. The flesh responded immediately, smooth, intact, and completely unchanged.

Good.

The reconstruction was stable. She would not fall apart unexpectedly.

…Likely.

She lowered her hand.

The lack of body temperature was notable. The lack of emotion was not. It was all still there, grief, pain, and most of all, wrath.

They had not faded. They had simply settled.

None of it dulled. None of it distant. If anything, it was clearer now, stripped of distraction, sharpened into something precise.

She did not suppress it. There was no need.

Her gaze shifted, unfocused for a moment as the memory surfaced, not as a blur, but with perfect clarity.

The moment it all ended.

Her expression did not change.

Alicia exhaled—an unnecessary motion, but a familiar one—and straightened slightly.

The system had been trying to get her attention for some time now.
