Chapter 124: Facing Reality

It was difficult for Jack to determine how long the trip was. His paranoia was flaring up badly, on top of everything. Weren’t enemies infiltrating them in ways never seen before? What if this wasn’t even Lindsay?

If it wasn’t, they knew her well, from subtleties of voice to even how she carried herself, body language, movement, mannerisms… Jack knew all of those things very well.

This same supposed enemy would also need to be jamming their Mini-Mem. Was that even possible?

At this point, Mini chimed. “Jack, stop it. This is authorized. That is Lindsay. I can’t be jammed, not in our own sanctum. Yeesh! Don’t ask me for more details, either. I shouldn’t have even done this.”

Swallowing a dry throat again, Jack nodded reluctantly, his paranoia mostly abolished.

Finally, the craft could be felt to land.

“Still haven’t shit that brick, eh, Jack?” Lindsay asked. She pulled out a black blinding hood and handed it to him.

Jack couldn’t keep from blurting, “Seriously? Putting on this hood is an order? Why?”

Lindsay didn’t answer, just kept holding out the hood, a bit of sauciness to even this, somehow.

It’s definitely her. No one else could pull off that sass in full regalia.

Sighing, Jack took the stupid hood and put it on.

“You’re lucky I don’t have your hands bound, too, after that,” Lindsay said with an air of firm admonishment.

Jack replied flippantly, “The joke would’ve been on you, Lindsay: I’m into that shit.”

A suppressed snort. “You need to take this seriously, Jack.” A pause, and then softly, “Tell me… was it you?”

Jack had been smirking smugly to himself inside the hood. This wiped his amusement out like a snuffed candle, as a spikey flush of alarm hit him. “Was what me? Lindsay? What the hell is this about?”

Silence.

He was led out of the vessel into the feel of a heavy wind. There were a lot of other movements around, along with the subtle signs of activity. He was apparently handed off to someone else with bigger hands. It kinda worried him. The guy positioned him standing in a certain direction, and there was a lot of waiting. Movement, the shuffling of feet, a stumble, and a suppressed apologetic word. He didn’t recognize the voice.

More people are being positioned, though. At least I’m not being thrown into a cell.

Lindsay’s voice erupted from ahead. “Cadets.” There was a long pause. “There’s an issue we have to resolve tonight. It brings me no good pleasure to do this, but it is necessary and for the one — or those — who forced our hand… this will be well deserved. One, or a number of you, have gotten yourselves found out. I’ll hear confessions now.”

A long, eerie silence answered this.

Wow, that’s some artistic rhetoric. What the hell is this?!

“No one?” Lindsay said tauntingly. “Really? You won’t spare the rest of your fellows this ordeal by confessing what you’ve done? What if I told you the whole lot of you would be punished if you don’t? Absolutely no one has anything to say?”

“This is bogus ass shit!” came the voice of Bo. “And I know it when I see it.”

“I’m glad someone does,” a guy said, “cause I can’t see shit.”

“Would you first please tell us all what it is, Agent?” came the even request of Délight.

“Yeah,” Jack called, “at least tell us what sword you want someone to fall on. Or do you just want a random person to do it?”

“I just-” Someone else began.

“Enough!” Lindsay yelled, silencing everyone. “Atten-SHUN!” Another pause as Jack, and presumably everyone else, straightened up. “You want me to announce it, do you? Fine! How about I show you, hmm? Let me get one thing clear, though: you will not move a muscle from where you stand. That’s an order. Do you understand me?” The briefest pause. “Do you understand me?!”

That clearly wanted a response, so everyone belted out, “Yes, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil!” In some cases, it was halfhearted or sarcastic.

Once that was done, Jack found the hood ripped off his head from behind, into glaring artificial light. For him, with his enhanced eyesight, this was especially painful, though his vision adjusted quickly.

They were on the top of the Nimrod base, apparently in the center, which was nothing but some giant featureless metal plane of panels, with the building structures surrounding them. He was amazed and confused by this alone, but things got more and more bizarre the more he looked. Nothing moved. Not a panel, not a transport, not a drone. It was dead, still, and silent.

The biggest oddity, though, was the sky. It was visible. The whole damn dome over the base had opened up! He’d never seen it, not in the flesh. Maybe when giant airships docked, or some huge missile launched, but it was only ever seen in propaganda-type videos, for demonstration of power, stirring the pride, and so on.

What the hell happened? Who died?

Gasps resounded as everyone saw. Jack wanted to turn his head, but there were strict orders not to move. In his peripheral vision, he could see cadets to either side.

He could also see suited Nons in front of each cadet, all holding metal boxes with handles on the top.

When the one in front of him spoke, he realized it was Lindsay. “Alright, cadets! You wanted it? You got it.” In a coordinated fashion, each of the Nons opened their box with metal ‘shing!’ sounds.

It revealed a fat square slice of cake.

Immediately, Lindsay smashed it into Jack’s face. The metal plate soon slipped away and clanged on the floor. More such noises could be heard left and right.

Any instinct to defend himself or react had to be resisted due to still being under orders not to move. He closed his eyes and cringed as icing, goo, and moist cake were pressed and smeared into his face. He heard some coughing and suppressed groans from others trying to keep their composure.

Jack, as befuddled as ever, had some new feelings crop up at what this appeared to be, but there was no time to process them.

“It brings me no good pleasure…” Lindsay called out leadingly, as she scooped cake out of Jack’s eyes so he could see, “because it brings me great pleasure…” She walked a few quick steps, so she was centrally seen, as she pulled off her helmet and gestured grandly, revealing a devilish grin on her lips. “To officially inform you sorry sacks that while you can’t have your cake and eat it, too, you have become full! Agent! Nonpareil! You’ve earned your jackets — congratulations!” There was a brief, shocked pause. “Oh — at ease, by the way!”

Eruptions of celebrations began as hoops, hollers, and screams of incredible relief and glee resounded. Jack felt a contrast of building elation and lingering annoyance as he glared at Lindsay. He pointed at her. “You lying little turd, you made me think-... gah!”

Lindsay shrugged as she twisted her body sideways and tilted her eyes, continuing to wear a shit-eating grin. “Believable hazing is the best hazing!”

And then, there was Highfive, scooping him up and thrusting him high up, calling out, “You did it, you bastard! You fraggin’ did it, bro, haha! We made it, we fraggin’ made it!”

Unable to keep from laughing, Jack patted his shoulder. “So we did, my brother.”

Celebration mixed around between parties as the group converged into a cluster, pushing, clapping backs, or shaking hands amiably, as the case may be. There was also a little bit of flicking or picking off cake from faces, necks, and shoulders. Jack and Highfive walked over to Bo, who was sobbing uncontrollably on the floor with her head turned away, as a helmet-shed Lighthouse was huddled next to her, whispering consolation and trying to cheer her up.

Jack looked around. Amidst the cadets were equally happy helmetless ANPs conversing with them — it seemed like everyone had someone close to them pull the hazing on them. Lighthouse must’ve been Bo’s escort. Meanwhile, the dome above Nimrod was closing again, the brief, momentary honor complete.

Jack dropped to his knees and put a hand on Bo’s shoulder, opening his mouth.

Before he could get anything out, Bo turned to him and eeked out, “Sorry.” She dabbed at a cakey, mascara-leaking eye with a handkerchief and sucked in a breath. She glanced between Jack and Highfive. “Sorry, guys. The fraggin’ primadonna is making a damn scene, I know! Don’t let me spoil the fun.”

Jack gave her a soft smile. “We don’t throw sorries at you, don’t throw them at us. Hey, we get it. It’s important, it’s big. Emotional. Spectacularly so.”

Bo nodded, her face trying to contort again as she resisted it. She took another breath. “It’s a… weight that… I… Jack, help me up.”

Jack did so, and immediately Bo hugged him tightly as he returned it. She then moved over to hug Highfive, who promptly laughed and lifted her into a spin, as she griped and protested to the ‘mistreatment.’ But it was a weak protest, and soon she was laughing, as they shared some whispered commentary Jack didn’t catch.

Lighthouse punch-pushed his shoulder, grinning. “Great job, Team Leader. You turned them around quicker than my lightning, that’s for sure.”

Jack returned the grin. “Thanks. Ah, they turned themselves around. I just pointed the way. Your team did all the hard work. I was lucky to have them, really.”

“Mr. Humility over here, dropping pearls. Well, sometimes we need old guys like yourself to deliver the wisdom.”

Jack deadpanned her. “Thanks so much. Again.”

With an impish grin, Lighthouse turned to look at the other two. Bo was pointing her finger up in Highfive’s face, making some firm point or another, and Highfive had his lips scrunched comically to one side, nodding reluctantly with his hands on his hips. “Looks like she’s back to normal,” Lighthouse said with a touch of exasperation. “Anyway, I should be thanking you. You can’t even know how much it hurt not having them with me. I figured I’d be waiting months at best, and was worried they’d been so broken by what happened, they might not make it at all. Thank the heavens for amplified needs, I guess.”

“Oh, is that what it was, getting me in so fast?”

Lighthouse sniffed dismissively. “Looking for an excuse not to feel pride in what you did? Don’t. I might tease you, but the fact is, you already had life experience. That’s literal gold considering the baseline here, don’t you think?” She met his eyes challengingly, waiting for an answer.

Jack just shrugged and grunted.

Lighthouse shook her head and continued, “Anyway, I think it was mostly the top two teams, and a few other standouts, but they want to push recruits. Might be a new standard on the way. Today happened on a dime, though. Someone in the mix just said ‘Frag this, we’re doing jackets! Jacket ‘em!’ No planning, Central scrambling to put this shit together… yeah.”

“Mmn.” Jack didn’t know if his sudden suspicion held any weight, but he immediately thought of the powerful masked guy. It was rather biased to the fact that the mysterious figure had so recently powerslammed Jack’s brain with his presence, but the impulse was strong. “They always do this hazing thing?”

“Oh yeah. Just like this. Well, kinda. Seems like Lindsay is a bit more flamboyant. Maybe a little too convincing.” She chuckled.

Jack sighed. “Yeah, that’s Lindsay, alright.” Jack cast his eyes for her and saw her talking with someone else.

He also blinked as he realized an absence. He looked around furiously. “Is Inkblot hiding somewhere again?”

Lighthouse winced and shook her head. “Nah. He wasn’t here.” She met Jack’s eyes with knowing sympathy. “Heard rumors about him. I imagine he’s got more to prove than the rest of you, even if his stock went way up.”

Jack grimaced, suddenly feeling deeply conflicted about the whole thing. And he needed to tell the other two as soon as possible, or they’d feel even worse when they realized they’d forgotten about him in the hubbub, too. “Dammit…”

“Trust me, I know how it feels,” Lighthouse said with a sigh. “It is what it is, dude. We don’t make the decisions.”

As Highfive and Bo were walking back up, Jack asked, “So, we still go to that party, just with, what, secret knowledge we can’t reveal?”

“Bingo.” She regarded the other two with a frown that hinted at her one-time misery.

Highfive caught what was said, and his eyes widened. “Nooo… you had to do that?”

“No wonder you were so steely-faced for that,” Bo commented. “You barely ate, and it looked like you were chewing sand.”

“Tasted like it, too,” Lighthouse muttered balefully.

“Guess it’s like a mutual friend of ours says, eh, Light?” Jack queried. “What we always have to both shovel and eat.”

“What — shit? Yeah, pretty much. There’s a reason why this little shindig here is mock accusative. We’re assholes, you know? Stepping on and abusing our lesser brethren to get here. Beating them, breaking them, bullying them, whatever it takes, without mercy, to come out on top. Following orders, even against our own. Wholeheartedly embracing the call.” The last was delivered with bitter sarcasm.

Silence persisted to this for a spell. It seemed that around the same time, others were getting more sober about the situation, too.

Highfive suddenly let out a scoffing sound. “And our good brother, that sonuvabitch, he ate like a damn king, and was happier than I ever saw him. Unbelievable.”

Lighthouse was frowning and staring at Highfive. She nodded stiffly. “Exactly. That prick flipped like a switch once he knew he was free of needing us. I still wonder if he ever cared at all.”

If they really let someone like that through, it really must be all about capability. If we’re adding moral fiber, team spirit, or some shit like that, it’s just bullshit. That’s all in our heads. I might’ve had good motives related to such things, to help my teammates, but the key was bringing them to that capability, not the means.

The bitterness of the next silence was palpable. Jack’s eyes slid, and he saw Délight standing off alone, arms crossed and her head down. Utterly alone, absent of her teammates.

“Man,” Highfive exclaimed, “I dunno what I’d do if I had to just hold this shit in when a teammate didn’t get through. That shit is rough.”

Jack and Lighthouse stared at him incredulously.

Bo looked between them all, catching the significance, and then the gears started turning as her eyes flicked around. “Wait…” She was really scanning the others now. She realized the truth quickly, as her countenance sank. “Oh, shit.” She slapped Highfive in the gut with the back of her hand, making him flinch and almost double over. She met his eyes. “We’re pieces of trash, Fiver. We didn’t even notice who’s missing.”

It took a little longer for things to register for Highfive. He looked around in puzzlement, and then finally his eyes widened, and he looked harder. “No, no, he’s just-... he…” Highfive swallowed and looked back at the others insistently. “Well, there must be a mistake! He was a part of what we did, man! No way he didn’t get in!”

“There’s no mistake, Fiver,” Lindsay said loudly and insistently. As people turned, she met various sober faces evenly and continued, “Most of you are missing someone. It doesn’t matter what you think, Agentus. You don’t call the shots. You’ve got a long road ahead of you before you could ever hope for that, but if you eventually do become a shotcaller, by then you’d probably be seeing everything differently. Regardless, you’d have earned the right in the field where it counts. Where people die instead of getting bo-bos. So suck it up; eat this discomfort. Life’s not fair, and you beat the shit out of your fellow cadets to get here. You’ll be lucky to dodge worse regrets in the life ahead of you.”

By now, Highfive had his forehead in his hand, shaking it, obviously distressed and agonized by the knowledge that Inkblot hadn’t been promoted. Jack shifted over to pat his shoulder in support.

Lindsay continued, “In a little bit, you’re going to go play pretend at the imminent party. You’ll keep your mouth shut a little longer about what you’ve become. Your peers won’t know until you get the official nod and a date for the ceremony to be given your jacket. I can’t promise anything, but it’ll probably be soon this time around, which is a mercy. It doesn’t always go like that. If you want, see the party as something nice for those who didn’t make it. Be cheerful for them.” In a pause, she looked around insistently for a spell. “Someone will come around with a cleaning device in a bit and go one-by-one, eliminating any traces of cake. Other than that, just make sure you attend the party.”

After this, the conversation slowly stirred back up. Lighthouse seemed inclined to take up the task of consoling Highfive, and Bo was lost in her own thoughts.

Jack walked over to Délight, who saw him and smiled appreciatively. It was a little more charming for the dab of sprinkled icing on her nose. “Congratulations,” she offered, and, with some hesitation, held her arms out for a hug.

He took it, giving a polite hug and murmuring, “Congratulations.” After they broke, Délight seemed to deliberately not look over at the party of people Jack had just walked from. It felt like eyes were burning into his back, but he didn’t turn around.

Délight grinned faintly and said, “A real shame you’re not wearing that cologne.”

Jack coughed in laughter. “I’ll have to get you the name so you can buy it for y- for a boyfriend.” Délight nodded to this in amusement. Jack cleared his throat and said more seriously, “Sorry about your teammates.”

She nodded, her face sobering. “I had a feeling this would happen. I didn’t prepare them quickly enough, so it’s my fault. Well, at least in the case of Hiccup and Chime. Defiant is new, and he’s pretty set in his ways, honestly. He should be here, but he keeps pulling that surrender crap. I don’t think the brass like that even remotely, but he’s adamant he has to protect his secrets.” She sighed. “It is what it is, no?”

“Right.” Jack winced. Supposedly, Defiant said he would’ve surrendered anyway, but he’d still demanded the man surrender, and possibly exposed his secret. Jack was a little conflicted about caring. He didn’t like the ‘face’ of Defiant, but that last exchange had seemed to indicate it was just that: a face. “How is Defiant, really? Like, in private.”

Délight’s eyes shifted off, and she grinned in genuine, smitten affection. She leaned in and whispered, “Can you keep a secret?”

“Of course.” Oh, boy. She’s in love. Here we go.

“Sweet. Brilliant. Kind. Loving. Seductive. Easy-going. Horny. Practically a saint, other than the degeneracy, and thank the goddess for that particular chink in the armor. His persona is an act. Literally. He was going to be an actor! He’s in commercials as a kid. Don’t tell anyone. Seriously.”

Jack’s brows furrowed, and he shook his head, finally chuckling and shrugging it off. “Figures. He defies expectations.”
