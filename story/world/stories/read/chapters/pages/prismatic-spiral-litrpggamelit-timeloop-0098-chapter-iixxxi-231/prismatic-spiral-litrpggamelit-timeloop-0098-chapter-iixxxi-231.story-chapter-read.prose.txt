Chapter II.XXXI (2.31)

In this loop they arrived at the port of Porcelain Island in time to witness the chaos of the island. A few sculpts were collapsing in the streets, dragging themselves to their destinations. Building to building. But Mitsuko knew that this wouldn’t last. The cooling enchantments on the buildings would wear thin as time went on. Despite their calls for help to Amber Island, no mages or wizards would arrive to reinstate those enchantments.

No one even bothered to approach The Terror as it moored.

Carrying Holly’s stone body, Mitsuko and Sterling disembarked with little fanfare. Nobody acknowledged them as they walked away.

Mitsuko crossed the town over to the inn where she’d met Jay in the past loop.

She set down the gnome statue inside over in the room’s corner.

“You should be safe here,” she whispered to her stony friend. “I’ll come back and fetch you as soon as possible.”

She gave the statue a hug. If Holly was still sapient in that stone shell, then the gnome would doubtlessly know the lie for what it was. Mitsuko wouldn’t be returning in this loop, despite how much it pained her.

Then she examined the inn. Unlike the rest of the village, a bit of life remained in this building.

A family consisting of a bear Kemon, a pregnant human woman from Tross, and two young children were convened in their own private meeting on the far table.

While Mitsuko was tempted to greet Jay and Tori, she decided against it. Unlike their affable attitudes in the previous loop, the looks on their faces were grim as they conversed. This was likely the conversation that led to Jay remaining behind while Tori went with the remaining sculpts up into the mountain settlement.

Mitsuko exited the building without another word.

As she walked through the sculpt town, she glanced at the melting windows at the dying families huddled together, attempting to preserve their bodies’ frigidity.

An older sculpt hid in the overhanging shadow of a streetstall. Around him, the snow had already all melted and the dirt beneath had turned to mud. He raised his head as she approached. Still alive. But not for long.

Mitsuko kneeled and he flinched back as she reached out in his direction.

“Heat,” he said, his voice like gravel rubbing together. “Please. Don’t touch. I’ll melt.”

“Relax,” Mitsuko said with a comforting smile. She pressed her hand against his icy stub of a wrist. It was wet. But only for a moment. She cast Mend.

A few seconds later, the sculpt jerked away, his hand newly reformed and restored to his previous state of existence. A time from before the Prismatic Spiral began.

“A mistake,” Sterling chided. “If these people discover your spell’s utility, they will attempt to hold you hostage and you will never slip away without them hunting after you.”

Mitsuko ignored both Sterling and the sculpt man who was marveling at his newly reformed body. She simply stood up.

In the previous loop, Mitsuko had likely stepped over the remains of these people as she traveled into the mountains. They melted and became one with the mud. A horrible way to go.

“These people are hurting.”

“You can end their suffering far faster by freeing this island’s sage,” Sterling said.

Mitsuko ignored him. Instead, she stepped up to the nearest house and knocked.

Over an hour passed with her walking place to place, healing the damage to the sculpt bodies and restoring their visages.

When she arrived at the longhouse that housed King Lars, word of her magic had spread and she found the door open for her.

A wall of cold greeted her as she set foot in the wooden building. Glyphs, carved into the wooden walls, glowed as they cooled the building to a temperature below freezing.

“Jacqueline?” Mistsuko blinked in surprise. She hadn’t realized that the sculpt woman had more of a connection to the king. She’d been thinking Jacqueline as just a sculpt who enjoyed mountaineering and skiing. In hindsight, it made sense. After all, of all his subjects, the king had chosen her as Mitsuko’s guide. That must have meant some special degree of trust. Plus, she was likely meant to simultaneously act as a spy and report back to him after their return. A return that never occurred.

Jacqueline’s death played out in Mitsuko’s mind once again. The massive ice worm crushing her body and shattering the life within.

“Are we acquainted?” Jacqueline asked.

“No… not really. Just know you by reputation.”

Jacqueline cocked her head. “Reputation? What sort have I built?”

“Um… as a great skier?”

That was the right thing to say as the sculpt woman puffed up with pride. “I dabble in the sport. Do you come from the Betsu Range of Tross? That’s the only region where I’ve heard outsiders have any knowledge of our sport.”

“I’ve passed through,” Mitsuko answered evasively. She was silently cursing herself for getting involved in this conversation. In general, kings almost always had a magical means of truth-telling on hand and the last thing she wanted was getting caught in a lie. “Only tried skiing a couple times and I’m really no good. But that’s not important. I’m here to see King Lars.”

“He wishes to see you as well.” Jacqueline turned her back to Mitsuko and beckoned her forward.

Mitsuko followed the sculpt through the next door and into the main hall. A long table of ice was in the center of the room, with a throne at the head. A familiar sculpt sat in the seat, a crown on his head. Only a trickle of water marred his cheek like a tear, the beginnings of the heat taking hold of him.

“Welcome, traveler.” Lars watched her from his throne. “What brings a healer of your caliber out to our island? Did the wizards of Amber hear our pleas? Can we rely on your strength to keep our homes’ wards active until this threat has passed?”

“No,” Mitsuko said simply.

“You refuse?” The king leaned forward in his throne to look down at her. “Do you have demands? We are clearly at your mercy.”

“You’re under some misconceptions. I’m not a healer. I’m not a wizard from Amber Island. I cannot restore enchantment wards.” Mitsuko consciously made a point of keeping her language polite and bowed to the king.

King Lars frowned and his icy brow crinkled as he scrutinized her. “Who sent you, if not the wizards?”

“No one. I came of my own volition and it is a coincidence that I happened on you while you needed help. I’m just an explorer who planned to venture down into your island’s ice tunnels.”

“Fortuitous.” The king didn’t sound convinced, but he leaned back in his seat. “What is this magic you wield if not healing?”

Sterling stepped forward. “Perhaps it would be best if you didn’t—”

“Temporal,” Mitsuko replied. “I reversed time on the victims and restored them to their previous state of being.”

“Temporal,” the sculpt king repeated. “Fascinating. And can you teach any of my subjects this marvelous spell you’ve mastered? I will pay you with all within my means.”

Mitsuko shook her head, genuinely sad. “No. I can’t. Sorry, but even if someone here did have some affinity for my branch of magic, my own studies were unorthodox. I wouldn’t know where to begin teaching a student.”

“Not to mention the time commitment that would require of you,” Sterling added. “Temporal magic is not something that can be casually taught in an evening over some beers.”

King Lars sighed. His face seemed to have aged ten years from her response. “I understand.”

“I can still help your people before I depart,” Mitsuko offered as a compromise. “Simply send them to me and I’ll Mend any damage.”

“That will certainly help; however, is there no chance I could enlist your services for at least a day or two? If no help is coming, then I’m afraid we’ll need to go through with my original plan to retreat into the mountains. With your help, many lives might yet be spared.”

“We do not have—”

“One day,” Mitsuko agreed, cutting off Sterling’s complaint. “I can offer a day of my time. But, after that, I’ll need to take care of my own mission.”

“One day.” King Lars tapped his gloved fingers on his throne’s arm rest as he clearly took some time to mull over her offer. “Yes, I suppose I must take what’s offered to me. It will mean putting my emergency plans into motion sooner than I would have liked. However, I can make this work.”

“We do not have time for this,” Sterling insisted. “All of this suffering will be reset in a week. It’s absolutely futile and foolhardy to waste time acting a medic to these people. Completely irrational and it risks wasting your most valuable resource, time. We should depart immediately.”

Mitsuko ignored the cat and instead listened as Lars mapped out his plans for the next day. He intended to first rally all the sculpts into one unit, as to let Mitsuko put them back into peak condition, and then depart within the hour of assembly. They would move in a herd, with the children at the center. The gathering of sculpts would help keep the area cool as they marched.

”While I doubt we will be able to complete our march with everyone into the mountain settlement within your allotted time, I do believe that we should be able to ascend to a safe enough altitude before your departure.”

Mitsuko nodded, ignoring Sterling as he resorted to actually meowing in an attempt to get her attention.

When her conversation with King Lars ended, Mitsuko walked out of the longhouse and kneeled down to finally address the annoying cat at her heels.

“You said you thought me using Mend on other species would be a great way to clear that final level,” she said. “Now, you’re complaining that I’m listening to your advice?”

Sterling finally paused in his complaints. “You expect me to believe that you agreed to this out of a pragmatic reasoning? That you only wish to use this as a hurdle to advance your spellcraft?” he asked, clearly skeptical.

“Fuck no!” Mitsuko laughed. “But it will work regardless. So stop bitching.”
