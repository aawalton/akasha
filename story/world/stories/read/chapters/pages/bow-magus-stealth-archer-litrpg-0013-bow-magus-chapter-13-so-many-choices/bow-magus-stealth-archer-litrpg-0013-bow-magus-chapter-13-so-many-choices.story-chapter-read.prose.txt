“Woo, look at all those decisions to make,” Rex grinned as he eyed the notifications. “And, um. Effyana, if you’re listening. I’m totally still a loyal subject, y’know?”

Thinking about Borshan, the God of Murder, reminded him of the dark dwarves and orcbears from the tavern. That was not the kind of company he wanted to keep around.

Luckily, there was no answer, and no divine lightning struck him down, so Rex figured that was a good thing—not that he believed in divine lightning.

I mean, they did attack me. It was self-defense. Not murder… or was it…

While the hunters had been hunting him, technically, he had sprung an ambush on them, and he had attacked first.

Oh, right. And they’re humans, too.

Rex remembered reading that Borshan wasn’t just drawn to murder but had a tendency towards those who killed their own kind.

He shook the thoughts. He was getting distracted.

Oh, whatever. It is what it is. Okay, do I loot the corpses and run, or level up quickly?

Rex considered his options for a second. Ultimately, though, the forest was deadly enough as it was, and there was always a chance that more hunters could catch up to him. Sure, there was risk in sitting around for a bit, but the extra Feats and Spells might just mean the difference between life and death.

I’ll be quick.

[ Seeker Dart ] A spell that never misses. When cast, [ Seeker Dart ] launches a projectile that causes light magical damage to its target and cannot miss. A target in the user’s line of sight must be selected before casting the spell; otherwise, it will fizzle.

[ Transference ] Trade places with an ally. When cast, [ Transference ] empowers the user with a touch spell that, when used, will allow them to magically swap places with the next ally they touch.

[ Weightless ] Float like a feather, no matter your size. When cast, [ Weightless ] reduces the user’s weight drastically, allowing them to float harmlessly down massive heights as if they were a feather.

“Okay. Not a bad selection. I can work with this.”

Rex went down the list. He figured that [ Seeker Dart ] would be a clutch spell. Had Rex been able to cast a seeking spell at any hunter he could see, he probably would have been able to return fire against the archers while remaining in the tree.

On the other hand, it was just that. A clutch spell. He had already read about the spell before. It was common, and actually highly recommended. But it was rather weak. In fact, the books recommended it precisely because spellcasters had a reputation for dying at early levels before their powers really kicked in, and it was supposed to be easy to use.

It’d be useful. But it kind of feels like you’re admitting to being mid and can’t hit targets with your own skill. And that ain’t me.

Moving on, he eyed [ Transference ]. The spell sounded handy, and if it worked with [ Mana Thread ], it had a lot of potential. But he had no allies. He supposed that if he managed to meet up with Andy again, that would be cool, but…

I’m probably going to die before then if I don’t plan properly. I need to think in the now. How am I going to survive the hunters out here?

He continued down the list to [ Weightless ]. It was actually a–

“Hey! STOP!”

He was suddenly ripped from his musings as a small, blue ball of gloop rolled across the hollowed trunk and straight onto the first artifact he claimed.

Rex had left the machete lying at his side and reached out as the jiggly, water-like blob enveloped it, but stopped short of grabbing whatever it was.

“Are you a monster? And leave that alone! That’s mine! I almost got myself killed.”

While monsters were considered inherently dangerous, there were a few breeds of weaker monsters that posed little threat unless they managed to evolve. And while Rex didn’t know what he was looking at, it hadn’t attacked him and didn’t look particularly threatening.

“If you wouldn’t mind moving off of my loot, I’d really appreciate it.”

He was out of arrows and without a weapon. He could use [ Corrosive Touch ], although he didn’t particularly want to touch it; besides, he wasn’t sure if [ Rune Scribe ] would even work on its watery surface.

“What am I going—No, no, no! Stop it—”

Rex slumped and sighed. Within seconds, he had watched as the jiggly blue blob dissolved his first artifact within seconds.

“You… you… You little bastard. That was mine!”

The featureless blue blob did not reply, instead rolling over the hunter. Leathers, knives, boots, whatever. Everything it touched disintegrated into its slimy guts.

Rex hadn’t planned on looting the man’s clothing, as it didn’t look any better than his own. However, he let out a long sigh when the blob gobbled up his coin pouch.

“This little monster is stealing my loot!”

It wasn’t just the loot, either. The blob also consumed the corpse and the severed head. And when it was done, it turned to Rex and its blue, watery surface twisted into a face that looked far too much like the hunter it had just eaten.

“That is gross,” Rex winced and looked away.

He needed to put an end to the devilish monster. But he needed a weapon.

The other hunters had arrows and loot!

The blob hadn’t stopped, wiggling its way out through the hole in the trunk and continuing across the forest floor.

Rex jumped to his feet and ran. More important than even killing the monster was grabbing the rest of the loot before it had a chance to destroy everything.

“Spell selections can wait. No freaking way am I letting you destroy any more of my artifacts!"

Rushing through the brush, he quickly looted the corpses, grabbing anything of obvious value, and moving on to the next.

Soon, he had a couple of artifacts, many coin pouches, a few simple rings, and other minor valuables.

It was a decent haul.

“This is a decent knife,” Rex muttered and sheathed the knife on his belt.

He threw the coin pouches in his pack. Unfortunately, no defensive trinkets remained. After seeing how his arrow had been harmlessly knocked away, he would have loved to find one. That said, one of the artifacts was a bow.

Strongarm Bow [ Rare ]

This bow grants the Skill [ Powershot ], drawing the user’s stamina to activate. If the user already possesses [ Powershot ], the bow provides an upgrade to the Skill.

Attributes bonuses when wielding: +2 Strength.

“Powershot? Was that how the archer was shooting those arrows? That’s freaking sweet!”

He was already imagining an arrow fired with [ Powershot ] and [ Corrosive Touch ], and he had a feeling it was not going to be fun for whoever was on the receiving end.

As he made his way through the corpses, he also filled up his quiver and shoved any extra arrows into his pack.

Then, he checked out the other artifact—a ring with what looked like a small ruby at its center.

Dasher’s Band

Once per day, the user can activate [ Dash ] to move forward, instantly travelling up to 10 meters.

“Oookay. Not bad. I’m guessing the swordsman used this to close the distance.”

Rex hadn’t actually seen the man use it, but it made sense. But he figured he could make plenty of good use of it, too.

He looked around. He was pretty sure he had looted all the hunters, and then he spotted a patch of burned vegetation several meters away, and the blackened footprints of the swordsman that led to the corpse he was now crouched over. And the battle remnants immediately reminded him.

“The goblins!”

He sprinted forward, following the trail of battle. He had forgotten to loot the goblins, and as he passed through the brush, he spotted it. The blue blob had found the shaman and was about to roll straight over it.

“No, you don’t!”

He nocked an arrow, but it was too late. Even as he marked it with [ Corrosive Touch ], he heard the slurping of slimy disintegration.

“You’ve gone and done it now!”

He loosened the arrow, and it whizzed by with a clap. But when it hit the blob, flames exploded outward. It came too fast to dodge, and Rex was thrown backward by the wave of fire, and his brows were scorched.

“Oww. That sucked,” he groaned. He had landed on some particularly hard roots, and his back was not happy about it. “Lucky I wasn’t too close to it.”

How did it do that?

When he finally pulled himself up, he spotted bits of blue blob shattered all over the place. He was damn lucky he was some distance away when he shot it; otherwise, he too would have been scattered bits.

“Well, at least you won’t be stealing more of my loot.”

But just as he thought himself done with the thieving monster, he spotted one piece of blob start rolling again.

“You’re alive?” He raised a brow and had half a mind to finish it, but he was oddly curious about the hardy little monster.

It rolled over to another goblin, and as it fed, the body dissolved into its blob. A few seconds went by, and it started to regrow.

He nocked another arrow, ready to put it out of its misery, but halted. What if it blew up with flames again? And before he could decide whether or not that was a risk worth taking, the damn thing turned its back into a sad, human face pouting at him.

“Are you trying to guilt me? Where’d you learn that?”

In truth, Rex wasn’t too bothered about the other goblins, now that the shaman's corpse was gone. After all, the other regular goblins he had killed didn’t have any worthwhile loot, and he doubted these ones did either.

“I guess I’ll never know, thanks to you.”

I might as well leave the little guy. I’ve already looted everything I want, and I’d rather not get blown up again.

“You’re a real jerk; you know that?” Rex sighed and turned away. “They’re all yours. Have the corpses. I’ve got places to be.”

There was no point in sticking around anymore, and while he still had choices to make regarding his level up, he was getting a troublemaker vibe from the blob and decided that perhaps it was best to create a little distance between them before he got back to it.

The problem was that the moment he started walking, the damn thing turned and started to follow him.

“Why are you following me? If you want me food, there are bodies that way,” Rex said, gesturing in the general direction of the hunters. “Wait, do you have eyes now?

It was at that moment that he realized the newly spawned eyes on the monster’s face were staring at his new artifacts.

“Oh no! Not a chance. Look, I’ve decided that killing you isn’t worth my time. But there is no way you’re getting these. I fought too damn hard to get no loot!”

If the blob could understand anything he was saying, it certainly showed no sign of it.

“So, you’re just going to keep following me?”

Jiggle

“Argh!” Rex groaned. “Fine. Try and keep up with me then!”

He ran as fast as he could for a bit, and then slowed to a comfortable jog. There was no way the little blob rolling along the forest floor was going to keep up with him.

It would probably lose track of him soon and turn back for the corpses once it realized it wasn’t going to get any more artifacts to feast on.

I cannot believe that guy. First the machete, and now he wants my bow? Seriously, some monsters, huh?

Once he had created some distance, he slowed to a walk. It occurred to him that he had been obscuring his tracks, as more hunters, and potentially Jango, would come looking for him after everything that had happened.

He circled back around, reading in the direction of the river. And when he reached it, he followed the stream, walking in the shallows. His leather boots kept most of the water out, and his tracks would be washed away.

He kept walking until he found some good sturdy rocks to climb up, and leaped from them over a ledge of bush, and rolled into an adjoining gully. The act left debris, but they were shrouded from the river, and a search party would have to stop and look over into the gully to pick up on the tracks. And considering the length of the river, it would require a lot of luck, or a lot of time, to find his tracks.

That should be good enough.

From there, he continued until the late afternoon. In truth, he wanted to create more distance after his near-death experience, but he was exhausted and didn’t want to hike through the forest at night.

This should make for a good enough camp.

Rex climbed under some brush, finding himself a nice little place to hide, and opened his System.

“Time to pick a spell,” he yawned as he got comfortable.

He blinked and rubbed at his eyes, scrolling down the list of spells as he reminded himself of his previous thoughts, straining to keep his eyes open.

“What spells to pick…”

It had been a long day, and despite his attempt at focusing, Rex drifted off.
