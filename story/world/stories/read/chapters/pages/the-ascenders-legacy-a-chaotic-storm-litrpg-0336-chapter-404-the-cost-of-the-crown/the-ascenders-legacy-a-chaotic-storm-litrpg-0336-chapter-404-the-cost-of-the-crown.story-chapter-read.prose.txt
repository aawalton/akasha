Aodhán's response sent a ripple of shock through the entire rooftop. And for a moment, no one spoke.

No one moved.

As angry or conflicted as they all were, Calamity Amoz was still the leader of this expedition. Not even Mythics would have dared defy him. Yet Aodhán had done so. And with such resolute conviction too.

The silence stretched for a moment longer. Then Amoz cleared his throat, surprise evident on his face. "I'm not sure you heard me correctly."

"I did," Aodhán said, standing his ground. "I heard you loud and clear, but my answer remains no. This seed belongs to my mother. No one else."

Amoz blinked, stunned by Aodhán's courage. "Your mother isn't dying, Aodhán."

"My mother isn't foolish either," Aodhán shot back. "I asked her to go to safety more times than I can count. I asked her to stay back. She refused to listen."

"So we should let her die?" Amoz challenged, a dangerous glint entering his eyes. "Is this your principle? Is that your ideal?"

"Let the boy be, Amoz." Selia scowled, floating closer to Aodhán. "He has made his decision. You're thinking too emotionally."

"And you're thinking too logically!" Amoz turned to glare at her. "This is a human child, Selia. Not a cultivator. Why condemn her to death simply because she was reckless? We can save her."

"And what if another human falls to the verge of death right this instant?" Rezin scowled. "What shall you do, Amoz? Will you split the legacy in two?"

Amoz turned to glare at him. "Need I remind you that Yelena Alcatraz would not be in this situation at all had we not come here? We are the cause of this. Yet you expect me to stand by and watch while she dies when I can do something about it?"

"Mundanes die all the time." Cephas spoke, his voice quiet. "We cannot afford to let guilt cloud our judgment."

"Perhaps," Amoz allowed. "But perhaps you all are not feeling enough guilt for what we have done to this city. I grew up in a city like this. My parents died in a city like this. So forgive me if sleeper lives mean more to me than simply a regrettable death."

"They mean more to all of us." Rezin sneered. "You're not the only one who feels guilty."

"Maybe not." Amoz's voice dropped low, his eyes hardening. "But I'm apparently the only one willing to do something about it." He pointed to Yelena's pale form. "This child is dying. Saving her life could be the one good thing we do today."

Selia grimaced, feeling a twinge of guilt curl in her chest. Still, she pressed. "The seed was promised to Aodhán's mother. We have no right to force him into giving it to us. If he's to do so, then he has to be willing."

Amoz stared at her for a moment, then glanced back at Aodhán. "I gain power to give it. That's your ideal, isn't it?" He gestured toward Yelena's pale form. "You'll never find a more willing recipient."

"I doubt she's willing with how much she hates us," Rezin countered before Aodhán could, his tone hard. When Amoz looked at him, he continued. "I understand your words, Amoz. I even understand the intent behind them. Just a few hours ago, we stood above this city wondering how they could go about their lives so unaware. Half of those people we saw this morning are dead."

Everyone winced. Even Selia.

"We are to blame for all this. We have not done well. But giving a mundane girl who hates us a legacy seed—and not just any seed, but a double inheritor's seed—is not wise. Better to give it to Aodhán's adopted mother. Someone whose son has done tremendous things for the sect."

"We don't have time," Verity cut in sharply, gesturing toward Yelena. "We have a few minutes at most before she finally gives up the ghost. Whatever we need to do, we need to do it now."

She looked at Aodhán, her voice softening. "You're trying to spare your mother a death that may be decades away, Special Disciple Aodhán. But as noble as that is, you'll be taking a daughter from her father today if you do not help."

"She should have listened!" Aodhán shot back, his expression torn. "She should have known better than to rush recklessly into a battle between cultivators."

"But she did," Amoz said, his voice growing quiet. "She's wrong for it. But we will be wrong to condemn her to death for it. Will you be able to live with yourself if she dies, Aodhán? I know I won't. Every time you look at your awakened mother, you will see Yelena's face. You do not want that on your conscience, trust me. I know what I'm talking about."

"But..." Aodhán grimaced, feeling the truth of Amoz's words sear his heart.

Already he felt guilty. How much worse would it be if she died when he could have saved her? How terrible would it be to look at Synové for all eternity and always remember this foolish girl who had stepped in twice to save him from near-certain death?

But then...

How terrible would it be if Synové died because he had given her chance at immortality to this girl he knew almost nothing about? How guilty would he feel every time he looked at Yelena and saw Synové's aging face?

Or worse, her dead body?

"No." He shook his head violently, his scowl deepening. "There has to be another way, Elder Amoz. There has to be. I did not face down a Mythic and a Calamity, and nearly die more times than I can count, only to surrender the seed to a stranger who hates cultivators with every fiber of her being."

"Aodhán—" Amoz began again, his voice strained, but Verity cut him short.

"We do not have the time, Elder Amoz. What do we do?"

Amoz stared at her, then at Aodhán, and finally at Yelena's pale form. And his gaze hardened.

"Fine," Amoz said, his voice a whisper. "If we won't save her, then we'll all watch her die."

"I don't think that's appro—"

"We will all watch!" Amoz snapped, his eyes hard. "It is the least we can do."

Aodhán glared at him, knowing exactly what Amoz intended with his decision. He wanted to refuse, to say no lest his resolve weaken, but his conscience refused to let him.

So he hardened his heart, drew in a deep breath, and shifted his gaze to Yelena's pale, nearly ashen face, trying to hold on to his resolve.

He could do this.

Yelena Alcatraz wouldn't be the first death on his conscience after all.

Opposite him, Sophran grimaced, the conflict plain in his eyes. He met Aodhán's gaze for a long moment before slowly looking away.

Aodhán felt the first crack form in his resolve.

He swallowed, telling himself that anyone else would do the same in his shoes. However, as the seconds passed and Yelena's condition grew even worse, Aodhán's conviction began to fray. He shifted uncomfortably, clutching the box tightly in his hands.

Amoz did not stare at him. No one stared at him. Yet it was as though he was surrounded by a cloud of witnesses. Thousands of eyes all watching this moment—marking it down for posterity.

A minute passed. A minute and a half.

Yelena's pallor grew worse. Aodhán's heart grew heavier—and heavier still—until it thundered in his ears, cracking beneath the weight of his own conscience.

He shuddered as the second minute passed. Some muttered a curse. Someone cried.

Aodhán didn't look at any of them, guilt eating away at him until he could scarcely breathe. And yet he still hesitated.

This was the first legacy seed he had seen in over a year, and even that was more than what many saw in a lifetime. If he handed it over to Yelena now, would he ever find another? If he did, would he ever find it on time? Or would he spend the rest of his life wondering if, just this once, he should have been selfish?

Another half minute passed. Yelena turned blue.

Her chest stopped rising.

And in that moment, Aodhán's world narrowed to a single question: How would Synové feel if she found out about this? If she discovered the lengths he'd gone to obtain this seed?

The answer came easily.

She would never accept it from him. More than that, she would be terribly disappointed in him.

The thought sent a sharp stab of frustration through him, and Aodhán scowled as his conscience finally gave way. His shoulders sagged, and with a surge of anger and frustration, he glared at Amoz.

"You will find me another. Promise it now."

"I will try," Amoz replied immediately. "I will try my hardest. That is what I can promise."

Aodhán clutched the box tighter, holding it for another second before finally extending it toward Verity.

She snatched it before he could even speak, slamming it into Yelena's chest even as Sophran sent vitality shooting into her chest once more.

Several people shuddered in relief, even Crixis, while Selia and Rezin just watched impassively.

"Thank you, Aodhán," Amoz said a moment later, his runic irises glistening. "I knew you would come around. That you would do what was right."

Aodhán stared at him for a moment, before simply turning away, feeling an immense sense of defeat settle over him.

"I'm sorry this was done to you, Aodhán." A voice spoke, and Aodhán looked up at Selia. Her expression was sympathetic. Angry. She forced a smile for him. "Rest easy, though. I'm sure the High Elders will do something about it when they hear."

"You mean to report me for saving a life?" Amoz scowled at her.

Selia didn't glance at him. She simply held Aodhán's gaze for a moment as though to tell him she was on his side before slowly looking away.

Aodhán turned away too, his gaze falling on Tianyu and Crixis who had stayed behind him the entire time.

Tianyu spoke first, offering him a wet smile. "You did the right thing, Aodhán."

Aodhán gave a small smile in response, and without a backward glance, he moved to stand at the edge of the roof, staring aimlessly at the clouds as he tried to parse his emotions. Crixis followed silently behind him, his presence comforting even without words.

After a long moment though, he asked. "Are you alright, elder brother?"

Aodhán glanced at him, then slowly shook his head. "I feel defeated. Tired. Frustrated. Angry…" he sighed. "I'm feeling a lot of things, none of which are alright."

Crixis nodded slowly. "That's okay. This was a big deal for you."

Aodhán pressed his lips together, staring at the now-clear sky for a moment before sighing once more.

"Am I evil for hesitating?"

"No," Crixis replied without hesitation. "Anyone would have. This was a very complicated situation." He looked at Aodhán, his gaze sincere. "I would have hesitated. Even though she may have indirectly saved my life."

Aodhán wanted to smile at that, but no smile came. Instead he closed his eyes, and lifted his face to the sky, thinking.

For a long moment, neither of them spoke. Then after a few minutes, Aodhán broke the silence.

"The High Elders warned me that Fate eventually chooses who receives a legacy seed. I'd hoped..." He sighed. "I'd hoped it would honour my desire, just this once, seeing how interested it is in my life, but I suppose not."

"I'm sorry," Crixis said quietly.

Aodhán shook his head. "It's not your fault, Crixis. I just… I nearly got myself killed four times for that seed. I stood up to a Calamity for it. I nearly lost you for it only for it to be given to someone who hates us more than anything else."

Crixis grimaced. "It doesn't seem like fate has a good sense of humor, does it?"

"More like a twisted one," Aodhán snorted, then shook his head again, suddenly feeling tired. It was barely an hour past noon, and somehow it felt as though several days had passed since sunrise.

His gaze drifted back to find Yelena encased within a cocoon of solid lightning, almost identical to the ice coffin that had enclosed Daruk during his own awakening, and he grimaced, wondering if she was speaking to Edrick Ghaltris now as he had spoken to Az'marthon.

Would that meeting change anything? Would she emerge understanding cultivators a little better, or would she simply hate them more?

Sighing, Aodhán let his gaze stray to the enemies they had caught, each one bound in null chains.

Now that he wasn't fighting for his life, or racing against time, he could finally see the resemblance between the three of them. It was subtle. The line of their jaws. The shape of their noses. The fury in their eyes.

They were family. One with deep-seated hatred for the Storm Sect, if he understood their plan clearly.

The two women, he could understand. They were illusion cultivators after all, and his battle against Malverin had shown just how personal her vendetta had been. The Calamity, however, was a different case.

Aodhán couldn't sense his core, even as diminished as he was now, but there was no doubt as to his affinity.

Soul.

That alone marked him as impressive, since there were only a couple of them on the entire continent. But it also gave Aodhán an idea as to why he was here. Why Edrick Ghaltris's legacy had been so important to him.

"Calamity Selia says he wanted to consume Edrick Ghaltris's soul fragment," Crixis whispered, apparently thinking the same thing.

Aodhán nodded, having deduced the fact. He stared at them for a long moment, unable to muster any interest or sympathy for them. And after a while, he simply lowered himself to the cracked concrete and closed his eyes.

Daruk's awakening had taken two whole days. No doubt, this would be the same.

Already, he could hear the Calamities discussing how they could securely transport the cocoon without disturbing Yelena's awakening. But even if they wanted to, the question wasn't how. It was where.

And more importantly...

What would they do when Vaymoz eventually found out they had made his daughter a cultivator without his permission? Without hers?

Aodhán grimaced. They hadn't thought this through at all, but there was nothing they could do now. They would simply have to cross the bridge when they got to it.

He let his breath slow as he settled into meditation, reflecting on everything that had happened that day, trying to distract himself from the crushing sense of defeat weighing on his chest. The voices around him gradually faded into the background.

His breathing steadied. His thoughts quieted.

However, just as insight began to stir within his spirit, a loud voice cut sharply through the silence.

"Yelena! Yelena! Where are you?"

Everyone tensed immediately, their gazes straying to the desperate voice in the distance. There was no doubt as to who it was.

Aodhán stood to his feet immediately, looking from the shimmering cocoon of lightning where Yelena lay to the ruined city beyond and let out a weary breath.

It seemed the bridge they had expected to cross later had already arrived.

Vaymoz Alcatraz had found them.

Vaymoz Alcatraz walked through the ruins of his once-beautiful city, his eyes watering as he pushed through the haze of dust and debris choking the air.

At first, fear and desperation had kept him from truly seeing the devastation around him. But after nearly an hour of running, searching, and shouting himself hoarse, exhaustion had dulled the adrenaline coursing through his veins, forcing him to confront what remained of his home.

It was catastrophic.

An entire district had been reduced to rubble. Shuttles and transport engines burned where they had crashed, sending pillars of black smoke into the sky. And every so often, he would come across a broken body.

A soldier. A bureaucrat. A mother. A father.

A child.

There were dozens of them. And those were only the ones he could see. The ones he stopped to dig free, praying each time that the lifeless face beneath wasn't his daughter's.

Shutting the vacant eyes of a dark-haired woman impaled by a jagged slab of concrete, Vaymoz slowly rose to his feet, feeling the weight of responsibility and guilt settle heavily on his shoulders.

This was his fault.

He should have turned the cultivators away the moment they arrived. He should have limited their numbers. He should have…

A sharp breath caught in his throat as grief threatened to overwhelm him.

He would make this right. He would rebuild, and he would ensure a tragedy like this never happened again.

But first, he needed to find his daughter.

Turning his gaze forward, he shouted again. "Yelena! Yelena!"

His voice rolled across the devastation around him before echoing back unanswered.

Vaymoz refused to be discouraged.

He pressed onward, calling her name again and again in the hope that somewhere, somehow, she would answer.

Minutes passed in this manner, until finally, he crested a pile of rubble that was formerly the census building and called out again.

"Yelena! Where are you?"

This time he received an answer. Not as a voice, but as the unmistakable pressure of magic. It pressed down oppressively—tangible in a way only cultivators could replicate.

His eyes widened, hope and terror surging within him in tandem. If a cultivator was holding his daughter captive…

He broke into a run before he could even finish the thought, fury lighting up his eyes.

"Yelena!" he cried, racing across the rubble toward the immense pressure that caused his muscles to tighten and his belly to churn with terror.

But fear had never stopped him before. And it didn't stop him now.

"Yelena!" he cried again, nearly cutting himself as he scrambled across the wreckage of his city, until finally, he hauled himself up and over a collapsed façade, breaking onto the flat, cratered stretch of a rooftop where not one, not two, but over a dozen cultivators stood, each one staring at him.

Vaymoz felt his soul tremble at the sight of them. And for a brief, instinctive moment, he froze, every muscle in his body locking in terror.

But it was only for a moment.

Anger broke through the chilling terror the next instant, and with a snarl, he shouted. "You monsters! Where the hell is my daughter?"
