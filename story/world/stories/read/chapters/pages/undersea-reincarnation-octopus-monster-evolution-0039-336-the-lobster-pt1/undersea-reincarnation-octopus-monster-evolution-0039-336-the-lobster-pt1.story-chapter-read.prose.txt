Seeing the majestic, crackling lobsters rush at him with the speed of a cannonball, Henry was reminded of the first time he’d taken one of the Riptide sharks head on. Of that first time where he’d really thrown caution to the wind and taken the risk of baring his beak at a carnivore many times his size, proclaiming himself to be a predator of his own. And while his nerves and hearts were singing in his ear, he could frankly say that he wasn’t nearly as terrified as he’d been back then.

But maybe he should have? After all, this being was of the same caliber as that massive turtle he’d encountered not so long ago. But then, he recalled all the fighting that had taken place in the Wave. And he recalled facing Velistraine.

Henry evaded the charge with a quickly timed Blink and turned. The lobster’s momentum carried it a few paces past, but it slowed easily enough and glared back at him.

I’m on a whole different playing field nowadays, Henry thought. Especially with the extra boost I’ve gotten from the recent evolution.

Henry circled, his form close to the sand below while the lobster let itself drop back down to the seabed from which it could launch another charge. Like the seals Henry had fought months ago, a barely visible, multicolored sheen stuck to his skin, overlapping the translucent jade scales created through Ingested Memory. The former made Henry’s movements seamlessly smooth, while the latter provided him with some extra defenses. Which was why when Henry blurred forward, activating Lightning Dash, he reached the lobster in the blink of an eye.

Even to his own eyes, with all the processing speed backing his perception, he’d been fast. He didn’t think there would be many creatures around who could match his speed. But unfortunately, the A-rank lobster was up to the task. Both of its pincers were already raised in a casual guard, ready to receive the two stabbing arms coming at its face. It even had time to angle its pincers so they didn’t just parry the stabs, but completely nullified them. As if the lobster was daring Henry to pierce through its chitinous armor.

The stabbing teeth were only inches away, and Henry was sure the lobster’s show of force would have worked.

If he hadn’t been planning for it.

Henry channeled his mana into the leviathan’s teeth, and he had the pleasure to see sense them puncture the two giant claws, sinking through the tough blue exoskeleton. The twitch of alarm was unmistakable, but Henry couldn’t enjoy it for long as almost immediately, a jolt of wild current burned up and through his arms.

Wrenching himself back with another Lightning Dash, Henry came to a stop some distance away, and immediately, his green Octomind got started on healing his electric’s burn while he assessed the damage on the lobster’s claws. The leviathan teeth had pierced the claws deep, but not all the way through. There was still some powerful resilience there. And now that the lobster knew his shell wasn’t that impervious, he might not let Henry get to him easily.

As for the lobster, it didn’t even glance down at the damage. Instead, its glowing eyes brightened, their light harsh, while a pulse of outrage slipped out of the being.

Then it blurred.

Henry barely had the time to switch places with a nearby clone, right as a pulse of violent blue magic exploded out of the crustacean’s frame and vaporized the clone before it could detonate.

Got it. Don’t stick around after I land a hit, Henry concluded, his eyes wide as he sensed the intensity of the attack. Every from a couple hundred yards away, he could feel the raw power unleashed. Henry would really rather not be on the receiving end of that one. Still, he couldn’t help the satisfaction radiating through his chest. He had landed a good hit. Drew first blood against an A-rank in a straight fight. That alone was worth celebrating, even if the lobster was clearly pissed off about it now.

The light was blinding, sending electricity arcing through the water and even as it faded, the bolts of lightning continued to shudder and flash around the form of the A-rank, which made Henry think. Could it immediately do that again?

Probably not, he thought. But there was only one way to find out.

Taking the initiative, Henry teleported above it, then plunged down, his own form crackling as he sped toward the lobster like a glowing bullet. The lobster’s antenna twitched and it looked up, its claw raised to intercept as thinner and faster deep blue bolts rippled on its chitinous shell, and Henry, beak gritted, pulled on his Octominds and a good dollop of Thaumaturgy before shoved the pincers down with Telekinesis. He could only moved them a little bit, but it was enough. From behind the pincers, the furiously gnashing maw was exposed for a brief moment, right as Henry’s Predation-empowered arm stabbed toward the lobster’s face.

Then two of his Octominds blared at him to immediately back away.

Before he could fully process why, Henry aborted his stab and threw himself to the side with a couple of short-ranged Blinks before he looked back and right below the lobster’s head were two smaller clawed arms. Well, relatively smaller. They were still larger than a fully grown man each, and unlike the two massive pincers, these two smaller ones were glowing with an intense blue as lightning danced between them as if they were tesla coils, to the same rhythm of the rippling electricity that coursed down the lobster’s body.

Oh, Henry realized, seeing the trap for what it was. Yeah, that might have hurt.

Arms still trembling from some residual electricity, Henry eyed his opponent for, considering his problem of range, then he reached a decision. He needed longer limbs, and he needed them to hit harder. So, Henry pooled three of his frontal arm, lengthening them, then tipped the improvised weapon with three teeth, aligning the sharp end to create one single point.

He couldn’t afford to get too close, so he needed a little bit of range.

And maybe it’s time to revisit an old skill, Henry thought. But it was a little annoying how weak it was going to be. After all, until now he hadn’t needed that many ranged attacks. He was mostly focused on getting on a creature and just blast and cut into it until it died. But with the lobster, that wasn’t a good call, and Echoing Arm was decent for range, but it wasn’t as great with something that sported such high physical defense.

I mean I haven’t tested it yet, but if that shell needs a mana-charged leviathan teeth to pierce, then Echoing Strike will struggle anyway, he realized. I’ll test it still, but I don’t have high hopes.

Eying the annoyed lobster below as it ignored him to examine the holes in its arm, Henry grumbled.

Alright. Screw it, Henry thought, looking into his Maw. Time to get some spicier ranged attack. With a flex of his will, a piece of familiar green-scaled meat popped out of his Maw and into his arm before he used one of his suckers to bite into it, and just to be quick about it, he pushed a little of the hungry Predation concepts into the sucker. To really taste the meat and make sure Magic Mimic got all that it needed.

Magic Mimic: Toxic Ray (D) was copied temporarily

At least it showed up stronger than it had been before, he thought as he ordered one of his Octominds to begin integrating the Skill within Predation. That way, it would settle comfortably in his soul and if he ended up using it, it would see some progress. For now, he needed to kill some time and see if an opening showed itself up. Spreading out his non-fused arms, Henry whipped them one after the other toward the lobster, and giant arcs of blue arcane flew down like thrown half-chakrams, kicking up large clumps of sand whenever any of them missed. Unfortunately, his theory turned to be right. Henry saw the couple of first one hit their target, but the ghostly strikes just didn’t pack that much weight to bridge the gap in levels, and they washed harmlessly against the shell, so basically, Henry was just flailing around for nothing. Aside from kicking up sand and giving himself a chance to reposition, that was.

Ugh, knew it, he thought as he changed tactics. Pulling on his Lifeweaving and Dancing Shadow essence, Henry went invisible, and made sure the skill nexus was tightly controlled so no mana waste could be detected. Floating above the cloud of kicked up sand, while a new clone—one that just like him, flew out of to the side. Henry allowed an Octomind to control this one, just to give it more credence, and waited for the lobster to appear.

Henry didn’t take his eyes out of the cloud and waited, tense. He wasn’t underestimating the damn thing, and while his essence-empowered camouflages had historically being effective, the lobster had also shown itself particularly perceptive. So Henry wasn’t about to be ambushed while he himself was trying to catch the monster unaware.

The sand parted to show the lobster still in its spot, antennas waving about as it peered left and right.

“Go,” Henry said, summoning an Octomind right to his decoy before infused it with Predation. The red hue spread out through the ghostly projection as it swelled with power, then it exploded forward. At the same time, Henry sent twenty of his clones down, primed and ready to blow up while he positioned right above the monster with the three-arm spear pointed down. A quick look inwardly told him that one of his Octomind was still working on moving the flimsy, temporary Toxic Ray deeper into his soul. With the skill being temporary, it was as if it had been appended to the edge of his spirit by Magic Mimic, and it would be washed away or destroyed in time if it wasn’t maintained. But that wasn’t enough. Henry needed it to set its roots deeply and safely.

“Keep going,” he urged the Octomind as he watched the fight below. There were two other Octominds away, one of which was overseeing the looting of the lobster’s stash, but Henry couldn’t focus on it for too long. The detonating clones were already annoying lobster, especially when combined with the decoy sending Echoing Strikes down and the red Octomind that screeched and wailed on the monster like a rabid thing. Henry waited, watching carefully, and when the lobster finally turned its attention to the red Octomind, he made his move.

Henry sped down. He didn’t activate any Skill. Not yet. He recalled very well how the lobster had detected his presence when he’d used an ability in the past. Henry pulled his arm back as he urged his red Octomind to go wild, drawing as much of the lobster’s attention as it could, and when Henry was only a few yards away, he activated everything.

Krakenbane’s Wrath and Crimson Surge bathed the world in red as his body coiled with barely contained strength. Lightning Dash made him cut through the remaining distance in the time it took the lobster’s antenna to twitch. Evershifting Strike accelerated his strike and promised a tremendous amount of pain wherever the point would strike.

Henry stabbed down, mind straining with the focus of holding all the Skills together, and right as his spear was about to impact, a blue ball of wild lightning bloomed out the lobster’s back.

Then there was only light and pain.
