Throughout the night, Rex had run as far as his legs would take him. And even though he was Classed, with more endurance and strength than ever before, exhaustion did finally take him.

His body ached, and he knew that he would need energy for the following day. And at least he had made it through the burned section of forest.

They’re going to be looking for me…

He looked around, and failing to see anything worthy of hiding in on the ground, he climbed a tree and did his best to get some rest for the night.

His sleep wasn’t particularly deep. He was sitting on a branch, resting against the trunk. And so when he felt an aura of killing intent early the following morning, he was quick to wake.

Rex had thought about auras the previous day. The most emotional and angry of the hunters radiated thick, easily sensed auras. Auras that could be picked up from some distance away.

I need to remain calm, Rex thought as he took his bag and several deep, calming breaths.

He wanted his aura to be invisible. This was considerably easier at level two, as one’s power naturally increased the strength of their aura. But from what he could tell, emotion was a multiplier.

That’s better.

Climbing down the tree, he got moving again. But he needed more of a plan. These were experienced hunters who knew the forest. And he doubted that just running away would keep them at arm's length forever.

I have to think this through. On the plus side, they probably don’t even consider me a real threat. If I had to bet, that’s why they’re not putting any real effort into masking their auras. To them, I’m just another newbie, level 1 boy who ran away from goblins and hasn’t been able to kill a damn thing out here.

Rex nodded to himself. This was good. If the hunters underestimated him, maybe he had a chance of surviving the shitty situation he was in.

He was thankful he didn’t have much gear. It allowed him to move fast. But nonetheless, he was increasingly hungry and thirsty. He had been out for an entire day and hadn’t eaten a proper meal since breakfast. And the bush turkeys were calling to him.

I don’t have time to sit around and cook breakfast. Not until I’m sure that I’m safe.

Rex kept going, ignoring his stomach. But skipping breakfast wasn’t going to save him. He could sense their auras, and they were getting closer.

Ookay, maybe they had every right to underestimate me.

Rex was already pushing himself. Moving as fast as he could without exhausting himself. They were just straight up faster than he was.

They’re probably Freedmen with higher Attributes than me. This sucks!

It was increasingly clear that he simply wasn’t going to outrun the hunters on equal footing. He needed something. A plan to turn the odds in his favor.

Wait, I got an idea!

It was risky and probably stupid. But he was running out of time, and he didn’t know what else to do. Every second he wasted, the gap between them narrowed, and the options available to him decreased.

There was no world in which he was going to beat them in a fight, though. However, what if he threw a little chaos into the mix?

Yeah, chaos. Lots and lots of chaos!

Rex circled around the goblin camp. He knew they would follow him, but first, he needed to make the hunters attack at just the right place.

First, he pulled the cowl cloak over his head and touched it as he cast [ Harden ], and was pleasantly surprised that he could still feel his mana tether he had with his other harden spell.

“Nice. This should work.”

He pulled the hardened coat off him, and hardened to iron, it held his figure perfectly. With a little fiddling, he propped it up onto a branch right at the edge of the thick shrub before the clearing where the goblin camp was.

“I should probably sort myself out as well.”

Rex pulled on his tunic so it wasn’t tight against his skin and cast [ Harden ] again. It felt a little awkward to wear, but it might just save his left. Besides, if he needed his mobility back, he could easily dispel it.

“Okay, armor, check. Trap, check. And it looks pretty damn good, too.”

He eyed the cloak up and down. It had no limbs, but from a distance, and amongst the thick shrub, that wasn’t easy to see.

Hmm… It’s far from perfect, and it shouldn’t be too hard for trained hunters to spot the inconsistencies. Then again, if they think I’m just a weak kid, hopefully they won’t think too much about attacking.

Before he stepped away, he decided to hang the bush turkeys over his mannequin. They added a little flair, and with the value they held, it made sense to keep them close.

“And one more thing.”

Rex closed his eyes, and he remembered his mother lying there. Her final moments. How sickly she had been.

A tear welled in his eye, and he brushed it away before taking some deep breaths.

That should do it.

He then jumped to a nearby branch, pulled himself up, and climbed to the next. With surprising agility, he moved from one branch to the next until he found a suitable tree some distance away with good vantage over his little trap.

His plan was far from perfect. If he drew their attention too soon, he would die. But if all worked out, hopefully, he would draw them into a confrontation with the goblins, and from where he was sitting, he would be able to take the hunters out as they battled one another.

Would a dozen goblins alongside a shaman be enough to turn the tides in his favor? Rex had no idea. But he’d rather take his chances than just accept whatever those bastards had planned for him. At least this way, he would die on his terms.

The hunters caught up even faster than he had expected. And when they spotted his cloak, two of them rushed straight for it. But then they stopped.

Fuck! Come on, just a little further.

He had a terrible feeling that they had sensed his aura, and as they got closer, he realized that it was the lingering aftermath of his emotional outburst.

They were still walking forward, though. One had equipped a bow, while the other had a sword drawn, and they were scanning their surroundings as they carefully walked toward the cloak. Meanwhile, and far more concerningly, the remaining three were starting to pan out, one pointing to the other two as they walked in opposite directions, bows held in hand.

Oh fuck. I can’t let them surround me.

Rex wasn’t completely screwed, though. They hadn’t found him yet, and he had a feeling they hadn’t sensed the goblins yet, either.

The archers on the outside were getting further apart by the second. He had to do something quickly, and he didn’t have a lot of options.

Effyana, protect me. Please.

He nocked an arrow, targeted the archer closest to his cloak, and loosened. He had cast [ Corrosive Touch ] on the arrow and was already nocking a second before it even landed.

Gold flashed up around the archer, and a trinket cracked on his belt, as the shield it generated flashed into existence for a second, completely nullifying his arrow.

He loosened the second, but it was too late. They turned to him in an instant, their training kicking in.

“There he is!” The hunter with the sword shouted, pointing directly at him.

The other hunter had dove forward almost immediately, but they had been a second slow, and his acidic arrow burned straight through their leg.

“Fuuuck!” He heard the agonizing scream echo out.

The archer had rolled behind a log, giving him cover from Rex, but he was still wounded all the same.

Rex was drawing another arrow when splinters exploded beside his head. An arrow had missed by inches, hitting the trunk. And he was fairly certain they couldn’t cast spells. It was just pure power. And a fist-sized chunk had been blown straight out of the tree.

Reflexively, he rolled back behind the trunk. But he knew that wasn’t going to save him for long.

Then, he heard the goblin battle cries ring out and smiled. The fight was far from over, or won for that matter. But at least he had a chance now.

He could hear them yelling. They had clearly been taken by surprise, but he needed to take a look. To see how they had reacted, and where they were moving to.

Rex peered around the trunk and—

“Argh!”

He was too slow. An arrow blasted straight past his face, blowing a chunk of flesh off the end of his ear. He had felt the wind against his face and knew it had touched him, but he was pumping with far too much adrenaline to register pain.

Worse, though, he had barely got a glance at them. It looked like the sword-wielding hunter had moved forward to where the goblins were, while the one barking orders and the closest archer to the rear were moving in his direction.

But he couldn’t be sure.

The goblin cries grew louder, and he heard them climbing through the brush. A hunter shouted, and the clashes of combat followed.

I can’t just hide here. If they kill the goblins, I’m done.

He didn’t dare poke his head out again, though. Instead, he decided to risk climbing down, using the trunk for cover, and dropping into the thick shrub below.

Keeping low, he glanced around frantically, desperate to get his eyes on the hunters.

“There!”

Rex turned. It was the two that had been moving around to flank him, and they had spotted him first. He was there. Stuck open for the kill as one of them drew back on their bow. He might have been Classed, but there was no way he could move that fast.

His mind clicked as the archer nocked his arrow. They were standing over the leaves, and with a quick mental command, they lost all integrity.

“Raaah!” The archers cried out as they fell into the hole, and the powerful arrow shot out into the sky.

That was too close.

Groans emanate from the hole, and he turned to where the other three hunters should be. He actually had a chance of surviving if he could just help the goblins take them out.

Moving around the tree, he got a glimpse of them just in time to see a ball of flames shoot out from the goblin clearing. The hunter with the sword dove out of the way, and he heard a gut-curdling scream as what he assumed was the hunter he wounded burned to death.

To the left of them, the other archer was already firing at the goblins, and he watched him shoot down three of the little green monsters in seconds as they reached out toward them.

That guy was a problem, but he wasn’t looking at Rex when he returned fire, and he did not have a protective trinket like the other hunter.

Rex’s arrow slammed straight into his face and melted it away in seconds.

For a second, he actually thought he had done it. He had turned the tides, and victory was in his grasp. Until he heard panting behind him, and felt the furious aura bearing down on him.

He was already running when he glanced back over his shoulder to see the wounded, bloodied hunter with a machete in his hand. He had climbed out of his trap. There was only one, though.

The goblins were obviously going to kill him if they got a chance. But he was a lot less scared of them, and even wounded, the higher-level hunter was already closing the distance between them.

Rex took his chances and ran straight toward the goblin clearing, bursting out into their camp, and leaping over dirty beddings as several goblins turned to him with their crude weapons.

One of the goblins stepped into his path, but Rex nocked an arrow and sent it flying into the monster’s eye without stopping.

“Get back here, you little bastard!” The hunter roared, bursting out after him a second later.

The hunter was faster, but the goblins had already moved to intercept Rex, and while they had failed, they were now standing in the man’s path.

“Outta my way!” He shouted as he swung back and forth, cutting a line straight through the goblins and covering himself in their blood.

At the corner of his vision, flames exploded, the shaman still obviously in the fight, not that the hunter seemed to care.

He had to take advantage of the moment. He knew he couldn’t outrun the man.

Swinging around, he nocked an arrow as the man cut down another goblin and loosened.

The hunter was quick-reflexed, but had no shield trinket. If he had had one, it had already been used. Mid-fight, he moved to the side, but not enough to completely save himself, and Rex’s arrow grazed his arm.

“HA! Useless! I’ll skin you a–”

The hunter had looked manic with rage as he cut down the last goblin in his way, but then he realized that acid was melting through his arm.

“The fuck?! You are gonna pay for that!”

He ran straight for Rex and ducked at the last second to avoid his follow-up arrow.

And then, he was there. Right before Rex. He had fought a good fight, but there was a difference between a newbie and a Freedman.

The machete came hard and fast, slamming into his chest. But instead of cutting, it hit hard cloth and sent him hurtling backward. Still, it hurt. His chest pounded, and he could already feel the bruises growing.

“Saved yourself? Good. I get to hear you scream longer,” the hunter hissed as he trudged toward him.

Bright heat lit up around them, burning his hair. It was the shaman.

“Fuuuck!” The hunter screamed as he patted the fire away.

Now was his chance. Rex gritted his teeth, bit down on the pain, and forced his legs to walk, fleeing into the forest.

He couldn’t win this. Even if he took the chance to take out the hunter, he would leave himself open to the shaman. He just needed to get away.

Fuck, fuck, fuck! Move, move, move! Faster, you dumb legs.

He felt like he was wading through water as he climbed through brush, desperate to create distance between himself and certain death.

And just as hope started to rekindle within him, he heard a man scream at the top of his lungs.

“YOU WON’T ESCAPE ME!”
