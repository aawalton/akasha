The rain gradually dwindled. What had once battered the earth with weight now fell gently, as if the storm had tired of its own rage.

Oswald stood in the settling quiet, Zareth still warm in his grip. Steam curled from the blade where water touched its edge, but even that was fading now, hissing less with each passing second.

He looked toward Selene. She stood in the mist, still and watchful. Her Animus still burned bright in her gaze, piercing violet eyes etched with the black five-pointed star at their center.

Then Selene blinked once, and everything changed. When her eyes opened again, they were no longer lit with runes, but the deep, steady violet he knew. Her horns receded back into flesh like smoke pulled into the wind. The scales across her skin shimmered one last time before dissolving into her skin, vanishing without a trace.

At the same time, the fog began to lift. Not all at once, but gradually, curling backward. The rain thinned, then stopped altogether.

The last few droplets fell from the edge of Oswald’s hood, landing in the mud with soft, final plips. Water trailed down his cheek, past the cut beneath his eye, down his jaw, and off his chin.

He felt everything, the cold, the soreness in his legs, the sting of that gash in his side, but none of it registered as much as the silence that had taken hold of the world. It truly was over.

Selene let out a breath, almost too soft to hear, and lowered her sword. Her expression had returned to what he recognized, cool and distant. But no longer inhuman, or whatever she was before.

Selene stepped toward him. The rain had left her silver-white hair clinging to her shoulders, strands plastered against her cheek and neck.

She stopped just in front of him. “Do you feel any regret?”

Oswald dropped his gaze. His hand, still curled around Zareth’s grip, twitched slightly. “Eh, not really.”

The words felt light in his mouth, but there was weight behind them. No point regretting what’s already done. Not after everything we’ve seen.

Selene’s expression didn’t shift, but something in her posture eased. “Good, hesitation births weakness. And weakness reaps death.”

Oswald blinked. He lifted his head slowly, eyes widening. “My system just said the exact same thing.”

“It is a warning whispered to every Animus Host at least once in their lifetime. So I am not surprised to hear it.”

"There's just… so many questions in my head right now. Some that I thought I could ask you before, but never got the chance to."

“That’s understandable, you’ve endured quite a bit in a short span of time. And through it all, you’ve learned.”

Selene gestured toward Zareth. “You’ve even managed to acquire a Pact Weapon already. That’s no small feat.”

Oswald glanced at the sword. “I got the system message right after, talked about stuff I didn’t understand. Said it was a relic of the Old Was, made to serve the will of an Emperor. I don’t know what that means. There’s a lot I still don’t get.”

Selene nodded once. Then, gently, she placed a hand on his shoulder. Her palm was cold, but steady. “I'll teach you when we return to the inn."

He didn’t answer, just nodded. And when she turned to walk, he followed.

The streets were empty, slick with rainwater. Puddles reflected the dying light above, broken by the quiet ripple of their steps. It was just the two of them for now.

Oswald kept his pace alongside her for a while, listening to the soft splash of their boots. After a minute, he cleared his throat. “Selene, can I ask you somethin’?”

Selene turned her head slightly. “You’re free to ask whatever you wish.”

He hesitated, stuffing his hands deeper into his cloak pockets. “Back there… when you changed, when those horns came out and your skin had those… scales. What was that?”

"A complex technique. One I’ve developed through deepened resonance with my Animus Spirit. It’s something you’ll be able to access in time, with Zvarah's help.”

Oswald looked down at the water trickling past the toes of his boots. “Dunno if I want that.”

“It will become a necessity eventually. You may choose to avoid it for now, but understanding the possibility is important.” Selene said.

“I guess, but is there any downside to it?”

That was when Selene fell quiet. She didn’t stop walking, didn’t glance his way, but the silence stretched just a little too long before she finally spoke again. "There is indeed a price that must be paid, what that is exactly will depend on you."

His frown deepened. Sounds about right comin' from her.

Oswald stared ahead, and for a long moment he just watched her walk beside him. Then, before he could stop himself, the question slipped out. “Do you, you know, think of yourself as a hero?”

Selene shook her head. “No. And I sincerely hope you don’t think of yourself as one either.”

“Why not? Not that I think of myself as a hero, but I'm just curious."

She glanced forward again as they walked. “Because the word is meaningless. A hero, in one land, may be a butcher in another. A general who leads his people to victory may be revered by his kingdom and reviled by the one he conquers. To some, he is salvation. To others, he is terror.”

“A healer who denies care to an enemy soldier is a traitor to compassion. A rebel who topples a tyrant may simply clear the path for another. Everyone wears a different truth. So the title of ‘hero’ means nothing to me.”

Oswald frowned slightly, letting that sit for a moment. “I get that, but if you ain’t a hero, what are you then?”

"I am, above all else, a weapon, forged and tempered to strike down the true enemies of mankind. That is my singular purpose, and it is a duty I shall uphold until the moment I draw my final breath."

"Is that it, then? Killing vampires? That’s your whole goal?”

“Yes, it's why I became an adventurer in the first place. I wanted to move freely so that I could better serve my purpose."

"I get that, but does that mean you didn't become an adventurer for the money? Not even a little bit?"

"Not at all. When I was younger, I entertained the idea of becoming a traveling merchant. But merchants must stop often, build contacts, keep inventory… ties I could not afford.”

Selene's cloak shifted as the wind stirred gently through the alley. “Adventuring offered a different kind of freedom. A license to wander. Monster hunting provided the coin I needed, and more importantly, the souls my Animus required to stay sharp. It was the pragmatic choice.”

She gave a faint shrug. “But enough about me. What of you, Oswald? What do you see yourself as?”

He didn’t answer right away. The question hit harder than he expected.

His gaze dropped to the cobbled road, wet and glittering beneath the moonlight. Water pooled in the cracks, reflecting the stars above. “To be honest, right now, I'm a bit confused."

He kicked a loose pebble out of his path. "Before all this Animus stuff, before the vampires and and the powers, I was just some kid trying to make it to the next day. Now? Seems like I'm a part of something way bigger."

“That is a normal part of growth. You are still young. And in your long life as an Animus Host, you will continue to grow in strength and wisdom. Confusion is not failure, it is a sign that your world has grown larger.”

Oswald scoffed, half-laughing. “Long life, huh? I don’t know if a guy like me is really built to last.”

“At the bare minimum, you should expect a century. Perhaps two.” Selene said.

He stopped walking, turning to look at her with wide eyes. “You serious?”

“I am completely serious."

“You’re saying I can still be around in a hundred years?”

"That is, in fact, the lower end of an Animus Host’s natural lifespan. Provided, of course, that you manage not to get yourself killed."

He blinked. “That’s a big ‘if.’”

Selene allowed herself the faintest trace of a smile. “Indeed, but if you still doubt it… I am three hundred and forty-seven years old.”

Oswald stared at her. His mouth opened slightly, but no words came out. Three hundred…? She looks closer to someone in their early twenties at most.

He blinked again, trying to measure her with fresh eyes, but nothing added up. Selene just didn't look that old at all.

"I... I really can't believe that."

“You’re free to believe me or not, it changes nothing.”

Oswald didn’t answer right away. He didn’t know how to. The number still sat on his tongue like a stone. That’s a really long time. What do you even remember after all that?

His thoughts chased themselves in circles until his feet stopped of their own accord. Up ahead, the Hollow Oak inn stood quietly.

Selene reached the door first. “We can continue this conversation inside."

Oswald nodded. “Yeah. Alright.”

The door creaked open under Selene’s hand. The low murmur of fire from the hearth was the only sound inside, steady and calming.

Vivi still slept atop the desk near the front, curled up in a small black circle, one paw twitching now and then as if she were dreaming. Behind the counter, Gilbert straightened from where he’d been scribbling in a ledger. “Welcome back, y’all alright out there?”

Oswald gave a tired half-smile. “We’re fine, if that’s what you’re asking.”

Selene didn’t return the pleasantry. She stepped ahead briskly, her rain-soaked cloak trailing behind her. “Gilbert, we need a ride. Fast.”

Gilbert’s grin faltered. His eyes sharpened as he closed the ledger. “You’ll have a wagon in a few hours,” he said, stepping out from behind the counter as he passed her.

Then he stopped beside her, glancing sideways without turning his head. “Any trouble?”

“Unfortunately, the town will need new physicians. I’ll do what I can to see replacements are sent from Loria,” Selene said.

Gilbert exhaled through his nose, half snort, half grunt. “Ain’t exactly cryin’ over it.” His hand rose briefly, offering her a casual half-nod, and then he turned and walked away without another word, disappearing into the back room.

Oswald watched the door swing shut behind him. Does he know?

Selene glanced back at him, then flicked her chin toward a nearby table. “Sit. We’ll be leaving Volstarn soon. But until then, we may as well talk.”

Oswald dropped into the nearest chair. It gave a quiet creak beneath his weight. “How much does he know?”

Selene eased into the seat across from him. “Enough, I’ve worked with Gilbert a few times over the years. He used to be a street brawler, before that, a thief. And before that, a drunk.”

Oswald raised a brow. “He doesn't look like someone who used to throw hands for coins.”

“He still could, if pushed,” Selene said with a soft chuckle.

“You’re serious?”

“Let’s just say our friendship was sealed over a misunderstanding, a missing satchel, and a very angry barmaid who mistook me for a bounty hunter. It was... an eventful evening.”

“Right, sounds about your style.”

Selene offered a faint smile in return, just enough to crease the edge of her lips. But the warmth didn’t linger. Her features settled back into their usual composure, sharp and unreadable.

She sat straighter, folding one hand over the other atop the table. “Regardless, now that we have a moment, as promised, I’ll tell you about the Emperor. And the Old War.”

Oswald straightened a little in his chair. He then let out a quick nod.

“First and foremost, you must understand this: vampires were—and still are—an existential threat to humanity. Not merely an infestation or a political power. A true, enduring threat to the survival of our kind.”

Selene paused only briefly before continuing. “Long before I was born, in the earliest days of the Old War, the vampires stood at the peak of their power. Their strongholds stretched across continents, they fed in the open, and ruled without fear."

Oswald swallowed, the weight of her words settling in his gut. Doctor Cecilia and her nurses were tough enough, which makes me wonder how strong those old vampires were.

“It was only thanks to the Emperor’s efforts that humanity survived. It was he who united the fractured kingdoms and led humanity against the pureblood clans. Without him, humanity would have lost."

“What kind of emperor are we talkin’ about here, anyway? What’s his name?"

“His true name has been lost to time. But he is remembered by many titles. Most famously as The Emperor Beyond Time."

Oswald raised an eyebrow. “That’s real fancy for one guy.”

Selene nodded. “The Emperor earned his title due to his ability to see the future. It was thanks to his visions that he foresaw humanity’s extinction... and created the Animus to prevent it. Everything about the Animus was all his design. And with it, he ensured humanity would always have a weapon capable of fighting back."

“Does anyone know where he's buried?"

“He was never buried.”

“Why not? Did he die in battle? Got torn up or something?”

“No, according to the records I’ve read, the Emperor simply vanished. Some believe he crossed into the Veil and never returned. Others say he left when his task was done, knowing what would come next.”

Oswald gave a low whistle. “Guy’s really one of a kind, huh. Can’t believe I never heard of him before.”

Selene turned her gaze toward the hearth again. “Humanity has had centuries to forget, such is—"

Her words caught mid-breath. A violent cough tore from her throat, sharp and sudden, folding her forward before she could stop it.

One hand shot out to brace against the edge of the table. The other pressed to her chest as she coughed again, harder this time, like her lungs were lined with ash.

Oswald was on his feet in an instant. His chair scraped against the wooden floor as he rushed to her side. “Selene, are you alright?” he said, reaching out with one hand, steadying her shoulder as she hunched forward. That's weird, she was fine a second ago.

Selene held up a hand. “I’m fine, just… tired. That’s all.”
