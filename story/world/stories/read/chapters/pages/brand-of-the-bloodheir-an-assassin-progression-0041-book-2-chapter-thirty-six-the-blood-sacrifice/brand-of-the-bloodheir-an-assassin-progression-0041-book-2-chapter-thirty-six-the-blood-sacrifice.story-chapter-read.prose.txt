Risens wiped the blades clean on the pelts of the twisted creatures that attacked him. The first chamber had seen an assault by massive, disfigured beetles. Oversized, deformed, vicious rats had been his second test. Whatever curse had twisted their bodies into a corrupted version of the natural creatures he was all too familiar with had fueled their vicious and violent intent.

They attacked with an insatiable urge. A chill rolled through his body as he recognized the lure, the lure of the violent sensation. It screamed at him each and every time he held the Raven Talons in his grasp. The perpetual longing of the mocking blades had imprinted concern of his own descent into their bloodlust and madness.

Mother Raven had called these foul creatures carrion—whether in the shape of bugs or rodents, they were the same. Evil, vile, violent.

Were the carrion that lay dead before him proof of his apprehension?

With the doors closed, the pervasive sense of worry and imminent danger faded into an afterthought. The warning that swelled from the blades in his hands had ebbed as the last of the creatures died. Unlike when the blades tasted the flesh of his human enemies, the symbols denoting their remaining time ticked down a digit instead of increasing.

The kills here had a bearing on their longevity, though it was adverse to his expectations. The Tears of the Under had instilled in them the strength to destroy the beasts that resided within this realm, though. As it seemed, their blood did nothing to add to the strength or to enhance the power of their bite. As he surveyed the weapon, he noted the pale glow that traced along the crest of the blade as well as the center of the pommel. What glowed a pale blue only moments earlier had now faded to a dim, dull yellow.

Collecting the feather before it settled to the blood-soaked ground, Risens took stock of his surroundings. With the death of the trio of carrion, he had expected that the doorway, his exit from the trial, would have been granted. However, nowavering portal split the blood-splattered stone walls.

It seemed a crucial task, but the key to his exit had yet to be fulfilled.

There was little in the room for him to investigate, to help him understand the reality of the next required action. The focal point, however, was clear—the altar held the key. Risens paced slowly around the carved stone, running his hands carefully over the thin grates slashed into its surface. He understood the purpose of the grooves. They allowed the blood to drip through the stone, funneling along the channel into the vial positioned purposefully along the side. If it was blood that was desired, he had left an adequate supply pooling around the dead carrion on the floor. Here was his cylinder for collection.

Careful to avoid the teeth, Risens retrieved one of the corpses, slapping it down on the top of the pedestal. Its dead body still twitched, its awkward final motions forcing small spurts of blood from the gaping wounds.

Black liquid slipped through the altar’s slats, funneling downward toward the vial. Drip by drip, he watched with morbid fascination as it slowly filled the cylinder. The process that followed was gory and sluggish. The blood of the carrion reeked. The foul odor was tainted, almost like flesh rotted in the sun combined with untold refuse. He was well accustomed to the familiar iron tang of spilled blood; this churned his stomach.

The vial, wrapped in feathers, was surprisingly capacious for its compact size. It was immediately clear that it would take the wreckage of more than one of these beasts to suffice. Unfortunately, it took all three before he was satisfied that the vessel hanging below the funnel was sufficiently filled. Careful not to get any more of the blood on him, he removed the small vial, stopping it with the topper connected by a thin chain, and screwed the top tight, preventing the blood from spilling.

The moment the vessel was sealed, a quiet wisp sounded behind him again. The portal—his exit from the chamber—appeared on the back wall.

That the blood of the creatures that inhabited this realm—vile as it was—would be precious to him was clear.

“I can’t believe you drink this stuff,” Risens said quietly to his Talons as he stalked toward the portal.
