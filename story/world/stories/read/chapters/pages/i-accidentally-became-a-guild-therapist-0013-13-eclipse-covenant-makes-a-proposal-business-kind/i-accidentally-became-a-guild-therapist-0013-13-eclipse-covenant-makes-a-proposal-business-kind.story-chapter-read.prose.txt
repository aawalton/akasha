I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 13 – Eclipse Covenant Makes a Proposal (Business Kind)

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: A formal proposal arrives from Eclipse Covenant, the server’s #2 guild. It is twenty pages long. It has four appendices. Bloodbath & Beyond’s reaction to it reveals more about the guild’s internal dynamics than expected—particularly Miles’s.

The proposal arrived three days after the wedding planning.

It came via official guild messenger—an NPC in crisp livery, carrying a sealed scroll with a wax crest depicting two crescent moons forming an eclipse—and was delivered to the guild board with the kind of formality usually reserved for server-wide declarations or legal disputes. The messenger placed it precisely in the center of the board, said “For the Mental Supporter, from Eclipse Covenant, Guild #2 of the Aethelgard Server, regarding a formal professional engagement inquiry”, and then glitched slightly and had to restart his exit animation twice before successfully leaving.

Livia read the scroll.

Then she read it again.

Then she looked at Miles.

“They sent a twenty-page proposal,” she said.

“I know,” Miles said.

“With appendices.”

“Four of them,” Miles said. “I counted.”

“They included a projected return-on-investment analysis for therapeutic intervention.”

Miles looked at the ceiling. “They did.”

[You have received a formal business proposal. Achievement Unlocked: Professionalized. +10 Legitimacy Points (Complicated).]

The proposal, in summary, requested that Livia Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 4, serve as a part-time therapeutic consultant for Eclipse Covenant for a period of thirty days, with the option to extend. Compensation offered: 2,000 gold per session, access to Eclipse Covenant’s premium supply cache, and what the document described as "priority scheduling rights for any future server-wide events in which emotional mediation may be required."

The appendices included:

Appendix A: Current emotional health metrics of Eclipse Covenant membership (seventeen charts, color-coded by distress category)

Appendix B: Projected impact of untreated emotional dysfunction on raid performance (with graphs)

Appendix C: Comparative analysis of known therapy NPCs on server (Livia ranked #1 with note: "only entry")

Appendix D: The Healer Problem (described in detail, tone: desperate)

Livia read Appendix D twice.

The Healer Problem, she thought. That sounds like either a very good or very bad name for whatever is happening.

The guild’s reaction to the proposal was, as usual, varied.

Phina read it with academic interest and noted that the emotional health metrics in Appendix A showed several patterns she recognized from her own pre-Glimmer period. Nyx read the first page, put it down, and went to a corner with his flashlight. Alaric immediately began calculating what 2,000 gold per session, thirty sessions, would mean for the guild treasury and whether he could bet on the probability of extension.

Bron picked up the proposal, looked at it, and said: “Two thousand gold is a lot.”

“It is,” Livia agreed.

“That’s, like, a hundred and forty body pillows.”

Livia put down her tea.

Miles said nothing. He had read the proposal once, set it on the table, and not touched it again. His expression was the controlled kind—the kind that had learned not to be anything.

Filed, Livia thought. That reaction is filed.

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient: Miles. Surface: Neutral. Beneath: Something specific about Eclipse Covenant. Not general distrust. Specific. The system notes this is the third time Eclipse Covenant has produced this response. The system suggests you ask him about it. The system acknowledges you will file it instead. The system respects your process.]

I will ask him about it eventually, Livia wrote in her margin. Not today. He is not ready today. I am not going to push a door that is not open yet.

Livia called a guild meeting.

This was unusual. Guild meetings were typically called by Miles, with the agenda being raid strategy or resource allocation, and they occurred in the main hall with everyone present. Livia’s meeting was called for the smaller planning room, with only the core five, and the agenda was: The Eclipse Covenant Proposal: What Do We Actually Think About This.

She had prepared notes.

She had formatted the notes.

She was aware this was possibly excessive for what was going to be a conversation, but she had found that having a structure helped when the people involved had a collective tendency to derail into either philosophical tangents or betting scenarios.

“The proposal is legitimate,” she said. “They’re not trying to kidnap me or coerce me. It’s a standard consulting arrangement.”

“Eclipse Covenant,” Alaric said, with emphasis, as though the name itself was an argument.

“Yes,” Livia said. “Guild #2. What’s the specific problem with them?”

Brief silence.

Bron looked at Miles. Phina looked at Miles. Nyx was looking at his flashlight but he was also, clearly, listening.

Miles said: “No specific problem.”

There is a specific problem, every person in the room communicated simultaneously via body language.

“Miles,” Livia said.

“It’s a business decision,” he said. “Guild resources, guild reputation. If our therapist is consulting for a rival, that has implications.”

“Does it?” Livia said. “Therapists consult across organizations. That’s standard.”

“This isn’t standard,” Miles said. “You’re not a standard therapist.”

He said it flatly, factually, the same way he might state a stat or a quest difficulty. Livia looked at him.

He means it as a tactical assessment, she thought. He also means it the other way. He does not realize he means it the other way.

Filed.

“I’ll go once,” she said. “Assess the situation. If it’s legitimate, I’ll take the consultation. If anything feels wrong, I come back and we decline.”

Silence.

“I’ll go with you,” Miles said.

“You don’t need to—”

“I’ll go with you,” Miles said again. Not aggressive. Just final.

There is a specific problem with Eclipse Covenant, Livia wrote, later, in her log. He will tell me when he’s ready. Until then, apparently, he is going to personally accompany me to make sure nothing happens.

I did not point out that this is also a form of trust.

Eclipse Covenant’s guild hall was designed to communicate that they were very serious people who did serious things seriously. High ceilings, clean lines, the kind of minimalist aesthetic that required tremendous effort to maintain and was intended to project effortlessness. Their color scheme was silver and deep blue. Everything matched. Nothing had been accidentally set on fire recently.

Livia walked in beside Miles and immediately felt underdressed.

Their guild leader, a player named Caelric, met them at the entrance. He was tall, organized-looking, the kind of person who had a system for everything and believed the system was correct. He had clearly prepared for this meeting. He had notes.

“Livia Quinn,” he said. “Thank you for coming. I’ve read your session outcomes from the public forum data. Impressive methodology.”

“I mostly just listen,” Livia said.

“Yes,” Caelric said. “That’s the impressive part.”

I like him slightly, Livia thought. Despite myself.

Miles said nothing. He was doing the thing where he looked relaxed and was not.

The Healer Problem was explained by a very tired-looking officer named Sora, who had been designated the point person for this particular crisis and had clearly been managing it for several weeks.

“Our head healer,” Sora said, “is Level 99. Specialization: Mass Restoration. She has been, for the past month, continuously optimizing her healing output because she believed the guild needed to be at peak health for our ranking push.”

“That sounds good,” Livia said.

“It was,” Sora said. “Until it wasn’t.”

She pulled up a holographic display. It showed a graph of Eclipse Covenant’s collective emotional and physiological state over the past month. The line started normal, climbed steadily upward through various optimization thresholds, and then, around week three, entered a region labeled "ABOVE NORMAL" that kept climbing until it disappeared off the top of the chart entirely.

“She over-healed them,” Sora said. “All forty-two members. Everyone is functioning at one hundred and forty percent of baseline health. Physically perfect. Optimal stats. No injuries, no fatigue, no physical discomfort of any kind.”

“That’s—” Livia started.

“The problem,” Sora said, “is that human emotional processing requires a certain amount of physical sensation. Stress. Tension. The minor physical feedback that tells your body something matters. When you remove all of that, the brain—” she checked her notes, —“apparently starts to feel like nothing is real. Our members are reporting widespread dissociation, inability to feel urgency, complete loss of competitive motivation, and—” she paused, —“an unusual tendency to stare at the middle distance and describe reality as” she checked her notes again, “quote, a pleasant dream from which I cannot wake.”

Silence.

“How many people are currently staring at the middle distance?” Livia asked.

“About twelve,” Sora said. “At any given time.”

[Therapist’s Insight: Active]

[Situation: Over-healing induced dissociative state. Guild-wide. The body’s stress signals were optimized away. Without physical feedback, emotional grounding has collapsed. This is not a combat problem. This is not a stat problem. This is, specifically, Livia’s kind of problem.]

[Note: The healer did not do this maliciously. She did it because she cared and because nobody told her when to stop. This is also, in a different way, Livia’s kind of problem.]

Oh, Livia thought. Oh, that’s sad.

“Where’s the healer now?”

“Currently,” Sora said carefully, “she is in her room. She found out what happened two weeks ago and has been experiencing significant guilt. She is also,” a pause, “very thoroughly healed, so the guilt is somewhat dissociated.”

“So she’s feeling guilty but it doesn’t feel real,” Livia said.

“That’s one way to describe it,” Sora said.

I need to talk to her first, Livia thought. Before anyone else. The guild is fixable but she’s the origin point and she’s going to carry this.

The healer’s name was Mira. She was sitting in her room when Livia knocked, staring at the window with the specific quality of someone who was present but not quite there.

Livia sat across from her.

She did not say anything for a moment.

Mira eventually said: “They told you what I did.”

“Yes,” Livia said.

“I was trying to help,” Mira said.

“I know,” Livia said.

“I made it worse.”

“You did,” Livia said. “And you already know that, so I’m not going to tell you twice.”

Mira looked at her for the first time.

“I wanted them to be okay,” Mira said. “The ranking push was brutal. Everyone was exhausted and hurting and I kept thinking: if I could just take that away. If I could give them one day without the pain.”

“And then one day became a week,” Livia said, gently.

“And then a week became…” Mira stopped.

“The thing about pain,” Livia said, “is that it’s not always the enemy. Sometimes it’s the signal. The way your body says: this matters. This is real. You’re here.”

Mira was very still.

“You couldn’t give them one day without pain,” Livia said. “You gave them one month without consequence. Those aren’t the same thing.”

A long silence.

“How do I fix it?” Mira said.

“First: you talk to them. Not as their healer. As yourself. Tell them what happened and why.”

“They’ll be angry.”

“Some of them,” Livia said. “Some of them will understand. And some of the ones who are angry now will understand later. But they deserve to know.”

Mira nodded, slowly.

“Second,” Livia said, “you stop healing at one hundred and forty percent. Return to baseline. Let the normal range of physical experience come back. It’ll take a few days. It’ll be uncomfortable. That’s okay. Uncomfortable means real.”

[You identified the origin point and addressed it before the symptom. +900 EXP.]

[Patient: Mira. Progress: She is going to carry this guilt for a while. That is appropriate. The goal is not to remove it but to help her hold it without being crushed by it. Direction: correct.]

Miles was waiting in the corridor when Livia came out.

He had been leaning against the wall, arms crossed, doing nothing in particular, for the approximately forty minutes the session had taken. Several Eclipse Covenant members had walked past him during this time. He had nodded at two of them. The others had moved to the far side of the corridor.

“How’s she doing?” he asked.

“She’ll be okay,” Livia said. “She needs to have some hard conversations and then she needs some time.”

He nodded.

They walked back toward the main hall in silence. Then Livia said:

“You know people here.”

Not a question. Miles’s posture changed slightly—almost imperceptible, the micro-tension she had learned to read.

“The server’s not that big,” he said.

“Two of them moved away from you in the corridor. One of them looked at you the way people look at someone they used to know.”

Silence.

“You were here before,” Livia said. “Not visiting. You were part of this guild. Or adjacent to it.”

Miles looked straight ahead.

He did not deny it.

He said: “Old history. Doesn’t affect the consultation.”

“I’m not saying it affects the consultation,” Livia said. “I’m saying I noticed.”

A beat.

“You notice a lot,” Miles said.

“Occupational hazard,” Livia said.

He almost smiled. Not quite. But almost.

Old history, she wrote, later. Not the guild that betrayed him—this is a different one. But connected somehow. The server’s not that big. Filed: Miles has more history on this server than he has mentioned.

Livia took the consultation.

Thirty days, eight sessions, the focus being gradual re-grounding of Eclipse Covenant’s members as Mira walked back their healing to normal range. It was methodical work—less dramatic than dungeon bosses, less poetic than slime weddings—but steady. People who had been existing in a pleasant dissociative haze started coming back to themselves one by one, and their return was not always comfortable, but it was real.

Mira had her hard conversations. Two people were angry. Three were understanding. The rest, slowly, came around.

By day fifteen, nobody was staring at the middle distance.

[Consultation: Eclipse Covenant. Status: ongoing. Sessions completed: 4/8. Guild emotional baseline: returning to normal range. Healer: processing. Progress: significant.]

[EXP gained: +1,400 total across sessions. Running total this arc: 5,250 EXP.]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 6: 5,250 / 6,000 EXP. Progress: 87.5%. The system notes you are close. The system is not going to tell you what happens at Lv. 7.]

[Achievement Unlocked: Cross-Guild Practitioner. The server has updated Livia’s classification from “Guild-Specific NPC” to “Server-Wide Asset.” The system notes that Bloodbath & Beyond has been informed of this update and Miles Vexley’s response was, quote, “that’s not how this works.” The system does not adjudicate guild ownership of NPCs.]

Livia read that last notification.

Server-wide asset.

Miles said that’s not how this works.

I am choosing not to have a feeling about that right now.

She wrote it in her notepad: Miles: said "that’s not how this works" about my server-wide classification. Unclear if this is territorial, protective, or something else entirely. Will not ask yet. Will absolutely ask eventually.

She was closing the notepad when the system sent one more notification. No fanfare. No achievement tone. Just text, appearing quietly at the edge of her HUD like something that had been waiting for the right moment.

[Server Administration Notice: The classification “Server-Wide Asset” has been flagged for review by an external party.]

[Party designation: Unknown.]

[Review purpose: Unknown.]

[Estimated review completion: Unknown.]

[Recommended action: None at this time.]

None at this time.

Livia read it twice.

Then she looked across the table at Miles, who was still looking at nothing in particular.

She did not tell him.

Not yet.

[END OF CHAPTER 13]
