The corridors of the Cinder-Fox were a stark contrast to the ancient, pressurized sanctity of the Fold. Here, the air smelled of expensive mahogany polish, ozone from the engines, and a faint, citrusy scent.

After parting ways with Vane and Pelka, Aiven and Virelle walked toward their private quarters. Vane had offered a brief, respectful nod—a silent acknowledgment of the hell they had just walked through—before disappearing toward the bridge to file an initial report.

The silence between Aiven and Virelle lasted until they reached the more secluded residential wing.

"Master," Virelle said suddenly. She wasn't looking at him; she was staring straight ahead, her hair shimmering like moonlight against the ship’s brass fixtures. She drifted slightly closer to him, the hem of her layered skirt brushing against his trousers. "I feel as though it has been an eternity since you paid me any proper attention."

Aiven stopped, looking at her with a start. He realized then that despite being together in the Sunken Fane, they had spent most of that time separated by stone or combat. Even in the Fold, he had been isolated in that library while she was left to bicker with a vampire.

To him, it had been a few hours of terror and revelations. But to Virelle—who had existed solely for his protection since the moment of her summoning—those hours of separation might have felt longer than what it really was.

"I'm sorry, Virelle," Aiven said softly, his hunched shoulders dropping with the weight of his exhaustion. "So many things happened back-to-back."

Virelle turned to him, her violet-magenta eyes searching his face. The theatrical arrogance she usually wore like armor was momentarily translucent. "And what did you talk about in that room? Did that delusional man plant any weird thoughts into your mind?"

Aiven managed a small, tired smile. "Actually, it was the opposite. He seemed very interested in keeping us together, although he did say some...disturbing things."

Virelle’s expression darkened instantly, her prismatic orb spinning with an aggressive, jagged whistle. "I knew it. I should have blown that place into cinders the moment we arrived. Anyone associated with those blood-drinking leeches is up to no good."

She hovered closer, her cool, lavender-scented presence enveloping him. She reached out, her fingers hovering near the bandages on his arm. "You are stressed, Master. Your mana is fluctuating like a guttering candle. Shall I head back? I can turn that gloomy palace into a graveyard of dust and regret."

"No," Aiven said, placing a hand gently on her wrist to stay her. "There’s no need. I’m fine, really. Besides, the portal is gone. I don't think even you could find your way back there."

Virelle huffed, a delicate but sharp sound. "You underestimate my spite, Master."

She settled back into her floating stride as they reached their door, but her hand remained near his.

The door to their quarters hissed shut. For a moment, the silence was heavy, broken only by the sound of Aiven’s ragged breathing. He set the Loom-Breaker on the small wooden desk, the crimson crystal casting long, rhythmic shadows against the walls.

Virelle didn't sit. She floated in the center of the room. Her eyes were fixed on Aiven, sharp and searching. "The suspense is quite unrefined, Master," she said, her voice dropping its usual theatrical edge for something more grounded. "What did the delusional man say to you?"

Aiven sank into the edge of his bed. Slowly, he recounted it all—the world beneath the clouds, the dying continents, the parasitic nature of the dungeon cores, and how the "God of the Skies" was merely a man performing a mass abandonment. He told her of Lucien’s plan to stop the cycle of greed.

He omitted one thing, however. He couldn't yet find the words to tell her that she might have originated from that dark, silent place.

Virelle listened, her expression shifting from skepticism to a cold, mocking amusement. "A savior of the world?" she purred, her prismatic orb spinning with a dismissive whistle. "How adorable. He thinks that by playing the architect of an apocalypse, he can earn the right to call himself a hero? And the audacity... telling you to assist him? He should be on his knees offering his life to serve you, not the other way around."

"I didn't fully buy his explanation," Aiven said, rubbing his face with his right hand. "But I can’t rule out the possibility that it’s true."

Virelle drifted closer, stopping until she was hovering directly in front of him. She reached out, her cool fingers lifting his chin so he had to meet her gaze. "Forget the fate of the world for a moment, Master. What do you want?"

Aiven went still. The question was a spear through his chest. He thought of his years as a clerk—the safety of the desk, the boredom, the predictable misery. Then he thought of the last few hours: the blood on Virelle’s face, her cracked shields, the way she had looked when he lost his arm in the thicket.

"His proposition was tempting," Aiven admitted, his voice a low, rough whisper. "He offered to unlock my power. To teach me how to use the Loom-Breaker so I’m not just a liability with a borrowed arm. He offered me a life where I could actually protect you."

He looked up at her, his gray eyes shining with a sudden, desperate intensity. "Virelle... if joining him means you never have to fight a mythical monster again... if it means the vampires and the government stop hunting us... I thought about it. I thought that if it meant seeing you live properly—not as a weapon, not as a shield for my weak self—I might actually want to agree."

Aiven’s grip tightened on the bedsheets. "I’m tired of seeing you in pain, Virelle. I’m tired of being the reason you have to risk your existence. If Lucien can give us a lavish home and a world where you don't have to bleed for me... isn't that worth the cost?"

Virelle’s expression softened, her magenta eyes widening slightly. The arrogant Scion was gone, replaced by the fierce emotional core she usually kept hidden behind her smugness. She didn't pull away. Instead, she leaned in, her forehead almost touching his.

"You would sell your soul to a delusional god just to see me in a silk dress?" she whispered, a faint, bittersweet smile touching her lips. "Master... you truly are a fool. A deeply, dangerously loyal fool."

Aiven stared at the Loom-Breaker, its crimson pulse acting as a silent metronome for his racing thoughts. "It’s not just about our safety, Virelle," he said, his voice cracking. "I’m afraid that if I accept his offer, I’d be falling into a hole. A deep, dark hole that I wouldn't ever be able to climb back up."

He looked at his hands—one human, one obsidian. "I never asked to be a hero. Not like this. I don't have some grand aspiration to topple governments or fight a war in the shadows that the rest of the world isn't even aware of. I wanted glory, sure... but the straightforward kind. The kind where you defeat a monster, save a village, and people can actually see the good you've done. Not this... secret crusade against dungeons that are supposedly sucking the life from a world that most people think is perfectly fine."

Aiven let out a long, shuddering breath. "How long does Aerilis even have? Lucien says it's only a matter of time, but that could mean years, decades, maybe centuries. Do I throw away my soul and my freedom for a timeline I don't even understand? I just want to be free. I want to go on adventures. I want to stop being a burden to you."

He slumped his shoulders, his head hanging low. "I think I need to talk to Cyria. It’s pathetic, isn't it? After everything, I’m still looking for someone to tell me what to do. But she knows Lucien. She’s the only one who saw his move and maybe thought of double-crossing him. I need to know what she’s really playing for."

The room was silent for a heartbeat before Aiven felt the soft, cool touch of fabric. Virelle didn't offer a witty retort or a smug smile. Instead, she leaned in and wrapped her arms around him, pulling him into a firm, grounding hug.

"Not knowing what to do is not pathetic, Master," she whispered into his ear, her hair brushing against his cheek like silk. "It is human. And quite frankly, I find it delightful that I am so central to your motivations. To think my safety weighs so much in your heart..."

She pulled back just enough to look him in the eye, her gaze burning with a fierce, absolute devotion. "No matter what you decide, I will follow. If you need me to destroy every dungeon in the sky, I will burn them to ash. If you need me to keep shielding you from the world, I will be your Aegis until the stars go dark. And if you need me to turn that delusional man into a sobbing, broken baby for ever suggesting he could own you... I will do it with a smile. I would burn the world for you, Master."

Aiven looked at her, his eyes stinging. "But...what if I make the wrong choice?"

"Then we shall be ruined together," she said simply, a small, genuine smile touching her lips. "And I shall make sure the ruins are quite spectacular to look at."

Aiven didn't say anything else. He simply leaned forward and hugged her back. In the quiet of the room, with the Loom-Breaker pulsing on the desk behind them, he could feel her warmth and the steady, rhythmic heartbeat beneath her ribs.

The future was a chaotic storm of falling islands and divine tyrants. His decision would likely alter the course of history and determine the fate of millions. But for now, Aiven Roan didn't want to be a savior, a partner, or a clerk.

He just wanted to savor the warmth of the person who had truly chosen him.
