Sometime in the few hours between when Ellen and Kade had arrived and the time she stepped out of the inner school and onto the dueling arena, it had been cleared and transformed.

Ellen stared at the lights. They floated mid-air, unsupported balls of fire the size of her fist, with thin cloth domes hanging beneath them to throw a colorful yet warm-feeling glow across the arena. Below the magically suspended torches, wooden platforms covered all but two of the sand dueling circles. An armed, armored man or woman stood next to each of them, hands on their swords’ hilts.

They were the only people with armor she could see, but everyone carried a sword. Each blade was sheathed, and a paper-thin band of light glinted between each hilt and scabbard, but swords were everywhere.

Really, that was the biggest difference between the auction galas she’d attended as Bob’s assistant hostess or ambassador—or just as a way to trick his soon-to-be business acquisitions into complacency. It wasn’t that everyone at those events wouldn’t have been more than happy to kill their rivals. But here, they could. That added a totally different element to the evening’s festivities. It wasn’t just about coming away with the core. It was about getting it while surviving the night.

She’d dressed in a light robe, borrowed from Tanat, for this. A spicy-smelling perfume hung in the air around her, and around Tanat, who cleared her throat. “I will return. For the evening, the only duels that will be permitted are over disputed items, and only under the honored teachers’ observation.” She pointed at the two uncovered sand pits.

“So, as long as I don’t try to get an item I can’t even bid on, I’ll be fine?” Ellen asked.

“Yes. Stay in this area. I have to select my S-Rank reward.”

Tanat disappeared, leaving Ellen alone in a sea of A and S-Rankers. She took a deep breath. It really was just another gala with an auction; she was pretty sure she even saw a few S-Rankers drinking something from clay flutes. To pass the time, she looked for groups.

There were always groups. Allies, or friends. She identified at least four major clusters—most of them built around a small group of S-Rankers and a larger smattering of As—plus a dozen or so smaller groups. It made for a good time-sink until, eventually, Tanat appeared at the treasure house door, a sword in hand and Retan at her side.

“Attention.” The assembled students fell silent as Retan spoke. “Sister Tanat has selected from my treasure house the sword known as Stratus Fall. Any student wishing to challenge her for it may do so after two sunrises and two sunfalls, as is our tradition. In honor of her success in clearing her Lonely Gate and her ascension to S-Rank, I will now offer some of my favorite treasures for auction to you, my students.”

Tanat slipped away, appearing beside Ellen as the first weapon—a sword that shimmered like bubbles blown by a child—went up for bid. “I am glad he still had Stratus Fall. The cloudblades are a common choice for thunder spellblades like me, and most of his set has been cannibalized over the last few years. He never had a complete set, either. Your Dual Skill Progression partner carried Nimbus Edge for a while, but not today, correct?”

“No, he picked up something new. I don’t know much about it,” Ellen said. The bidding picked up, and Tanat let her watch for a few minutes, until the bubble-blade went to an A-Ranker in exchange for two A-Rank cores and a B-Rank Paragon one.

Then the black-eyed woman pointed at one of the small groups Ellen had identified and started gently guiding her toward them. It was one of the smaller ones, but to her surprise, it also consisted of nothing but S-Ranked, inner school students. The only exception stood on the outside of the circle, looking in. “Our first objective will be to make an arrangement with Yetal. He’s the last of my clique to reach S-Rank, and his Lonely Gate has proven every bit as difficult as mine. He’s also an expert coresurgeon.”

Ellen nodded slowly and let Tanat lead her to the group. Introductions, polite bows, and small talk. Fluted clay glasses and strong alcohol. She’d been here before, and if she had a moment, she’d be ready to start navigating the space herself.

Retan was hiding something.

As I strode up the tunnel hallway and into the garden, that wasn’t a question. It wasn’t even a question of what he was hiding. The only thing it could possibly be was his attempt to play the same game as the God of Thunder. It was a question of where he was hiding it.

My money was on one of two places.

The first was relatively simple, but still complex enough to be a problem, especially with my limited resources. I had a couple of each type of charge—Rainfall, Wind, and Lightning—but not enough to spend them like candy. Worse, I had no way of recovering them short of picking a fight, and while I was confident I could beat anyone but Retan, I was less confident that I could take on one of the Soaring Sabers’ teachers without alerting more. I needed this information. But I wouldn’t compromise Ellen’s mission to get it.

So, the first obstacle was the sheer size of the great hall looming over me. Its frame and walls were all built from the same dry wood. I was willing to bet I could use a spell to slam myself through it, or Lightning Chain to rappel up the sheer surface to the top. I was also willing to bet that the moment I did, someone would see the flashy, bright chain.

So, one Wind Charge for Stormrunner made sense here.

I used the skill, stepped into the air, and pushed off, flying near as many trees as I could on my way up. Half of me expected an alarm to go off right away. Surely, the inner school was constantly being intruded on by outer school students. But no. No alarms. No warning shouts. Nothing.

The path upward became clear quickly. Barred and shuttered windows looked out over the garden and the dueling arena beyond it, keeping me from seeing if anyone was watching, but if I hugged the building closely, I could avoid their lines of sight. A single line of approach led all the way to the building’s wide, tile roof. I took a deep breath and launched myself upward.

Three seconds later, I touched down on the roof, landing on the balls of my feet and ducking down to keep my profile low. Two moves with the Storm’s Blade cut through the roof, and I dropped inside, coming face to face with…

A locked, portal metal door.

“Fine. That’s how it’s going to be?” I asked no one, looking around at the rest of the room. I’d ended up exactly where I wanted to be. It was clearly an office—and judging from the decor, it was the office of someone incredibly powerful. At least two S-Rank cores were powering the various lights and devices, and that was only what I could see. It had to be Retan’s study. That meant that whatever was beyond the door was important.

But something was off. The Storm’s Blade still sat in my hand, lightning crackling quietly along its edge. I unsummoned it, keeping my armor on, and started looking around. My feet kicked up tiny clouds of dust as I did.

Dust.

There was dust on the books. Dust on the shelves. It covered the desk and caked the lights and the cores powering them. That didn’t make any sense. Why would there be dust? Didn’t Retan use this room?

No. No, he didn’t. He probably hadn’t been here in months. In fact…

I took another look at the notes on the desk. They were yellowed and cracking, their ink fading. The books’ leather covers were coming apart, only held in place by their tight-packed proximity on the shelves.

This place felt like it had been abandoned a long, long time ago. Longer than months. Maybe even longer than years. That alone was evidence of what I suspected, but it wasn’t the kind of evidence I needed. It wouldn’t convince Tanat to leave with us.

Where was that evidence? I wished I’d talked to Tanat more about the auction and about the leader of her sect. That would give me more of an idea of where to go.

I stared at the portal metal door. One Wind Charge, two each of Lightning and Rainfall. Laminar Blade consumed my first Rainfall Charge, and I sliced through the door, then unsummoned the Storm’s Blade and caught the metal before it could hit the ground. It went onto the desk; something about the dust on the floor felt…uncomfortable. I didn’t want to disturb it if I could help it.

Beyond the door—which had been shockingly easy to cut through, considering it was, in theory, the portal to an SS-Ranker’s sanctum—lay a long room with wooden stands lining its sides. Each held an item. An artifact. I recognized the worlds they came from. A silk-spun egg sac from the Arboreal spiders. A glass-and-metal tank filled with a sickly green fluid from the vivisectioners’ world. The obsidian pillars of Lithic portals, and a hobgoblin’s skull. Trophies taken from different portals. Retan had been a trophy collector—I was curious what he’d taken from Rime worlds, or from something like Yalerox’s castle.

But even so, these weren’t the evidence I needed, either.

A second door loomed over the far side of the hall. Just like the first, this one was portal metal—and just like the room I’d left behind, the trophy room’s floor was caked with dust.

Why was there so much dust? It felt…wrong. There shouldn’t be this much.

I took one step into the room.

Dust billowed around my feet.

“…so, this woman requires your assistance,” Tanat finished. “She’s willing to trade a pathway to S-Rank for it. It does not require a Lonely Gate or the use of a Paragon core, and is safe for future progression and learning.”

What Kade had said weighed on Tanat almost as much as the two swords tucked into her belt. She’d grown so used to her blade over the years and years of diligent, methodical struggle against her Lonely Gate that even Stratus Fall’s relatively light weight was enough to make her steps awkward, and she thanked Retan for the two-dusk grace period before she’d have to fight to keep it. But Kade’s words stuck in her head. There were a great many outer school students, and very few inner school ones. And the true cost of running the sect was…unknown.

She shook her head to clear it as Yetal nodded slowly, examining Ellen’s chest dispassionately, like a butcher staring at a cut of meat. After a moment, he nodded. “Yes, this is a fairly standard break. You had much worse, if I remember correctly, Tanat. We can repair this easily. The knowledge in exchange for that work is fair.”

“After you fix my core,” Ellen interrupted, “then Kade can give you the alternative method.”

Yetal didn’t even meet her eyes. Instead, his black ones flicked to Tanat. “I assume you have a core picked out?”

“We do,” Tanat said quickly. “I have other deals to make to ensure we get it.”

“Then I will be at my studio.”

“You’re not bidding on anything?” Ellen asked.

Yetal laughed bitterly. “No. I cannot afford it. Currently, my two investment partners are in their Lonely Gates. If and when they succeed, then I will have enough to ply the auctions for a time, but not now.”

“Unfortunately, they are even slower than Yetal,” Tanat said.

“Says the woman who set a record for slowness in hers.”

“I’m thorough, Yetal, not incompetent.”

The long-limbed, black-eyed man laughed as he walked away. “Speak of yourself how you wish, but leave your lies at home, Tanat. I’ll see both of you at my studio soon.” Then, to Tanat’s relief, he disappeared, leaving just Ellen behind.

“How much did he invest in those two?” Ellen asked quietly.

Tanat shrugged. “Several A-Rank cores, and a handful of B-Rank ones. Enough that they could buy the weapons and armor they needed. He struggles with the sword. S-Rank is likely beyond him without a different method. But that makes him valuable as a trading partner, both for those still trying their Lonely Gates and for us.”

Ellen went quiet, and Tanat allowed herself to hope she’d stay that way. Instead, she started to hum quietly.

“You want to stay something. What?” Tanat asked when the humming got to be too much.

“Fine. Kade isn’t only here for me,” Ellen said.

Tanat cocked her head. Up near the treasure house, Retan had a two-handed sword made from stone, and a bidding war had broken out over it.

“What I mean is, Kade’s here for me. We both want my core fixed so I can get back into the action and help him out. But if you weren’t a possible ally—if you hadn’t fought beside him before—he’d find a different way forward. One that’s easier, or safer, or faster. He’s here for you. And he’s also very good at staying alive in dangerous situations. If he’s got a concern about your sect’s leader, you should listen to him.”

“It isn’t that simple,” Tanat whispered. “Retan took us here. He gave us life, purpose, a reason to continue. The Soaring Saber Sect has been everything to me, and—“

“And you don’t want to lose it,” Ellen said. “I understand. But at some point, you’ll see what Kade does. I don’t even see it yet, but I trust that it’s there.”

The stone sword went to one of the A-Rankers, and Retan pulled the first of the S-Rank Paragon cores out. This one pulsed with a fiery energy, and bids poured in. Tanat watched for a while. “I think you may be right,” she finally said, “but I need to understand better before I make any choices.”

Ellen nodded. She started to say something, then stopped as Retan froze in place for a split second. He moved, faster than Tanat could even follow, and whispered something to Teacher Pakar. Then he returned to the auction, almost like nothing had happened.

But Teacher Pakar walked through the crowd, abandoning the duel she was overseeing and heading for the great hall.
