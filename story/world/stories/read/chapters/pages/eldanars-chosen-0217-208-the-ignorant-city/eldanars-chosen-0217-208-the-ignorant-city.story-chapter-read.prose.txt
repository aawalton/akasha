The final descent towards the massive wooden walls of Ashenfall felt like walking out of a freezing nightmare and directly into a tragic, heartbreaking illusion. As the retreating convoy slowly lumbered past the outer perimeter of the refugee camp, the sheer scale of the displaced population became overwhelmingly apparent. It was a sprawling, chaotic sea of canvas tents, wooden lean-tos, and dying campfires that stretched for miles in every direction. The air here did not smell of rotting meat and dark magic. It smelled of damp wool, burning pinewood, and boiling porridge.

The refugees were a deeply mixed collection of races from the outlying farming villages and northern settlements. There were human farmers clutching thick woollen blankets, displaced beastkin families huddled tightly around small braziers, and a few rugged dwarven miners who had been driven from the shallow foothills.

As the heavily battered, mud-caked adventurers and the groaning supply wagons passed by the edge of the tents, a ripple of movement stirred through the camp. The civilian refugees did not look terrified. In fact, seeing the heavily armed city guards and the towering, steel-clad adventurers returning from the dark plains brought a profound, visible look of relief to their exhausted faces. To them, the arrival of the convoy meant the perimeter was secure. It meant the monsters had been held at bay.

A small group of human and beastkin children, their faces smudged with soot and dirt, ran right up to the edge of the muddy road. They waved excitedly at the returning fighters, their high-pitched voices cheering for the heroes in shining armour.

Josh looked down at a little girl waving a small, crude wooden sword at him. His storm-grey eyes tightened painfully. His heavy steel plate armour was covered in a thick, freezing layer of black mud and congealed gore, and the deep, magically healed scar on his cheek was still throbbing. He forced himself to offer the child a small, weary nod, but the gesture felt entirely hollow.

"They have absolutely no idea," Brett whispered, his voice trembling slightly as he walked beside Josh. The elemental mage had pulled his heavy fur cloak tightly around his face, unable to meet the hopeful, smiling eyes of the refugees. "They think they are safe here beneath the shadow of the walls. When that horde breaks the treeline, these canvas tents will offer less protection than a wet paper bag. The city guard needs to get them moved inside the gates immediately."

"There is no room inside the gates for a fraction of this population," Perberos noted quietly from Brett's left. The elven ranger kept his silver eyes fixed forward, his expression completely unreadable as he observed the sprawling camp. "Who would have thought the people who were relocated into the goblin dungeon would end up being the safest civilians today."

It was a grim, yet accurate assessment. The dungeon they had cleared was heavily fortified, easily defensible, and a long way away from the creeping necrotic fog above ground.

"Keep the pace," Josh ordered softly, refusing to let the despair completely anchor him. "We cannot save them standing out here in the cold. We need to report to the Guildhall and start organising the defence."

The convoy finally reached the massive, heavy iron portcullis of Ashenfall. The city guards stationed on the high ramparts quickly hauled the heavy chains, the grating metallic screech echoing loudly in the early morning air as the gates were raised to let the retreating column inside.

Passing through the thick timber archway and entering the city proper was a completely surreal experience.

The sun was finally beginning to crest the horizon, casting a pale, cold morning light over the cobblestone streets. Ashenfall was waking up, entirely oblivious to the apocalyptic threat marching towards its northern borders. The stark, jarring contrast was almost sickening to the exhausted rescue squad.

Merchants were calmly sweeping the frost from the stone steps of their storefronts. The warm, inviting smell of freshly baked bread and roasting meats drifted heavily from the open windows of the local bakeries, completely clashing with the lingering stench of the black sludge still clinging to the adventurers' boots. Normal citizens were walking through the streets, carrying woven baskets and complaining loudly about the sudden winter chill.

There was absolutely no sense of urgency. There was no panic. They did not know the doom that was approaching them.

The weaker fighters and the civilian labourers hauling the supply wagons immediately broke off from the main column, heading towards the military storehouses near the barracks. The medical carts, carrying Bhel, Elara, and the other critically injured adventurers, were quickly diverted towards the grand temple of the Light in the upper district.

The remaining high-tier fighters continued their heavy, clanking march through the winding city streets, heading directly for the massive stone structure of the local Guildhall.

As they walked, Josh found himself walking a few paces behind Caistina Tharistrar and Ronald. The elven mage trainer and the beastkin scout were speaking in hushed, incredibly tense tones.

"We do not have the manpower to hold a sustained siege against a magically coordinated horde of that size," Ronald hissed quietly, his jade-green eyes darting nervously around the peaceful city streets. "The city guard are trained to handle bandit raids and minor monster incursions, not a fully unified undead army led by a Bone Wraith like that."

"I am entirely aware of our tactical deficiencies, Ronald," Caistina replied sharply. She gripped her pristine white staff so tightly her knuckles were completely white. "A briefing of our situation has been sent to the capital. We simply have to hold the line until the Royal Guard arrives."

"That is a minimum of seven days," Ronald argued, his tail lashing angrily beneath his cloak. "We will be buried in bone by day three."

Caistina let out a long, highly uncharacteristic sigh, the deep lines of exhaustion around her amethyst eyes becoming more pronounced.

"I wish Hopeless was still here," Caistina admitted softly, her melodic voice carrying a heavy weight of regret. "If he and Terry had not taken to trying to save the world, we might actually stand a chance. His root dome magic would be incredibly useful for fortifying the main gates right now."

"And there’s absolutely no sign of the two higher-level fighters returning," Ronald added grimly. "And we have absolutely no way to contact them. We cannot rely on ghosts to save us, Caistina. We have to work with the weapons we have."

Caistina did not reply, simply quickening her pace towards the imposing wooden doors of the Guildhall.

The adventurers began to file slowly into the massive building. The grand hall, usually filled with boisterous laughter, the clinking of ale mugs, and the loud bartering of loot, was currently a scene of chaotic, exhausted silence. Fighters were collapsing onto the heavy wooden benches, dropping their dented shields and ruined weapons onto the floorboards, completely drained of all physical and magical energy.

Josh, Brett, Carcan, and Perberos moved to follow the crowd inside, eager to drop their gear and find a quiet corner to rest their aching bones before the inevitable siege preparations began.

As they approached the main entrance, a muted green blur suddenly blocked their path.

Ronald stood firmly in the doorway. The feline beastkin did not look relaxed. He looked incredibly serious, his slit pupils dilated entirely despite the morning light. He held up a sharp, clawed hand, halting the four of them before they could cross the threshold.

"Do not go inside just yet," Ronald ordered quietly, his tone dropping the usual cocky, sarcastic edge he maintained. He gestured sharply with his chin. "Come with me. To the side of the building. Now."

Josh and Brett exchanged a confused, weary glance, but they followed the beastkin. The twins fell into step silently behind them.

Ronald ushered them quickly down a narrow, shadowed alleyway running down the eastern flank of the massive stone Guildhall, away from the prying eyes and sharp ears of the other returning adventurers. He checked the street twice to ensure they were completely alone before turning to face the group.

The beastkin crossed his arms over his leather chest piece, his tail wrapping tightly around his leg. He looked at the four of them with a piercing, analytical intensity, studying their faces and their magical auras.

"I need to ask you a question, and I need absolute, unvarnished honesty," Ronald stated, his voice barely above a whisper. "Have you all gone through a body evolution?"

Josh blinked, completely thrown by the question. He ran a heavy, steel-clad gauntlet through his messy, dirt-caked ginger hair. "A what? I don’t know what that means."

Brett mirrored his confusion, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose. "I have read extensively on magical theory and class progressions in the Guild library, Ronald, but I have never come across the term 'body evolution'."

Ronald did not look at the humans. He shifted his jade-green gaze directly to the elven twins standing quietly in the shadows.

Perberos and Carcan remained entirely silent for a long, heavy moment. They exchanged a brief, entirely unspoken conversation through a shared, twin glance. Finally, Perberos gave a very slow, deliberate nod.

"We have," Perberos confirmed quietly.

To demonstrate, the elven ranger slowly raised his right hand. Right before Josh and Brett's completely shocked eyes, the solid physical form of Perberos's fingers began to lose their definition. His pale skin blurred, turning into a wispy, translucent shadow that seemed to actively absorb the ambient light in the alleyway, before slowly solidifying back into solid flesh.

"My physical form has begun to permanently integrate the aspect of shadow," Perberos explained smoothly, dropping his hand back to the hilt of his curved blade. "It allows me to move without disturbing the air, and makes my stealth skills infinitely more efficient."

"And I have taken on an aspect of the Mother Tree's healing light," Carcan added, her melodic voice perfectly calm. She did not demonstrate physically, but her pale green robes seemed to naturally repel the shadows of the alleyway. "It is why I am able to channel such vast quantities of holy magic without suffering severe burnout. My body naturally purifies other conflicting manas."

Josh looked between the twins and the beastkin, his exhaustion momentarily forgotten, replaced by deep, burning curiosity.

"Okay, so you both have superpowers," Josh noted, pointing a gauntlet at them. "But what does that have to do with Brett and me? We are just regular humans trying to survive."

Carcan turned her luminous silver eyes towards Josh, a faint, highly amused smile touching her aristocratic lips.

"You are many things, Josh, but you are not a regular human," Carcan corrected gently. "A body evolution occurs when a fighter's physical form takes on an aspect of a specific magical element or physical trait, actively purifying their internal mana and making their core skills dramatically more efficient."

She gestured towards Brett, whose hands were still glowing with a faint, residual aura of white-hot heat from the desperate retreat.

"Brett," Carcan explained, "your skin is entirely cold to the touch, yet you are capable of summoning and controlling living flame that can melt solid iron. A normal human mage would have incinerated their own hands casting the volume of fire you produced in that sinkhole. You have clearly taken on a high-tier thermal aspect."

Carcan then turned her gaze back to Josh, looking pointedly at his massive, broad-shouldered frame and the heavy steel plate armour he wore like it weighed absolutely nothing.

"And you, Josh," Carcan continued, her voice dropping to a serious whisper. "You activated an electrical aura while standing knee-deep in freezing water. You took a direct, executing strike from a twelve-foot Bone Goliath, a blow that would have instantly liquefied the spine of a normal heavy fighter, and you simply stood back up. Your muscle density and your skeletal structure have clearly evolved far beyond standard human parameters."

Josh looked down at his heavy steel gauntlets, clenching his fists slowly. He thought back to the intense, burning pain he had felt in his chest weeks ago when his skills had forcibly combined, creating Stormforged Adamantite. He had assumed it was simply a unique system upgrade, a perk of his specific class path. He had never considered that the system was actively rewriting his very DNA.

"I think," Carcan concluded softly, "that the intense skill evolution you both experienced before you even hit level twenty-five was actually your body evolution."

Ronald let out a harsh, incredulous breath, dragging a clawed hand over his face. The beastkin looked genuinely shocked.

"That is exactly what I suspected when I watched you fight in the pit," Ronald muttered, shaking his head in absolute disbelief. "A body evolution is a massive biological milestone. It does not typically happen until an adventurer reaches at least level forty. I am level thirty-eight, and I haven’t gone through it yet."

Ronald looked at the twins. "Has the dwarf evolved as well?"

Perberos offered a slight, dismissive shrug of his shoulders. "Bhel is a creature of stone and stubbornness. It is incredibly difficult to tell if he has integrated an earth aspect, or if he is simply just being a dwarf. We do not know."

"Either way," Ronald said, his voice dropping into a tone of absolute, terrifying reverence. He looked at the four exhausted adventurers standing in the dirty alleyway. "Having a single evolved member in a party before level forty is considered an insane anomaly. Having four is completely unheard of. You are breaking the fundamental rules of the system."

Ronald pushed himself off the stone wall, his tail lashing with nervous, restless energy.

"Keep this entirely quiet," Ronald ordered strictly, looking Josh directly in the eye. "Do not advertise this to the Guildhall. When the capital finds out what you are, things are going to change very quickly."

Without another word, the beastkin turned and pushed past them, heading back towards the main entrance of the Guildhall, muttering darkly under his breath about the impossible mathematics of their growth and the massive things coming for them.

Josh and Brett were left standing in the freezing shadows of the alleyway, completely reeling from the revelation.

They had survived the corrupted forest, the sinkhole, and the impossible retreat. They had hit level twenty-seven, securing a crucial victory. But as Josh looked at his own hands, realising the system was physically changing him into something entirely different, the impending siege of Ashenfall suddenly felt like it was only the beginning of a much larger, far more terrifying war.
