As I opened my eyes, the world had turned white. One moment we were standing in the Rift Square, and the next, we were here. The sensation wasn’t like teleportation between scenarios, which usually felt weightless. This was heavier—much heavier—as though something vast had reached down and deliberately repositioned me.

When my vision stabilized, I found myself standing on a massive circular platform suspended within an endless white expanse. The surface beneath our feet was smooth and faintly etched with divine patterns. Nearly three thousand divine warriors surrounded me. Boris stood at the very front instinctively, radiating the authority granted to him by Istellia.

However, we weren’t the only ones here. Across from us, separated by a considerable distance yet clearly visible, stood two other circular platforms identical to ours. Each was packed with divine warriors of its own, their numbers even greater than ours. By my rough estimation, the one on the left had about four thousand divine warriors, while the other had more than five thousand.

In total, there were three platforms arranged in a triangular formation. At the exact center between them hovered a sphere of condensed light, rotating slowly in silence.

I examined the divine warriors on the other two platforms. Their auras were distinctly different… The faction on the left felt somewhat similar to ours. But the one on the right… Why does it feel like something’s off with those divine warriors?

I couldn’t really explain what was wrong with them, but they looked more absentminded and strangely in perfect synchronization.

In any case, there were only three platforms present, which meant three factions. This also meant that three newborn gods had been selected for this stage of the Battle of Divine Will—Istellia… and two others.

Suddenly, the sphere of condensed light at the center pulsed once. Space above it split apart, and a pillar of radiant brilliance descended from the void. White-gold feathers scattered through the air as if carried by an unseen current. From within the column of light stepped a tall figure clad in resplendent armor, six vast wings unfurling behind him in immaculate symmetry.

It was an archangel—similar in nature to Raphael.

He hovered effortlessly above the central sphere, wings spread in exaggerated elegance. His gaze swept across the three platforms slowly, as though assessing livestock before auction. Then he clapped once. The sound echoed unnaturally loud across the white expanse.

“Oho! All three factions have arrived safely. Wonderful, wonderful.” A broad smile spread across his face. “My name is Michael. Archangel of Judgment… I will be serving as the referee for this stage of the Battle of Divine Will.”

The sphere beneath him brightened instantly, ripples of light spreading outward like a stage responding to its host.

“Welcome, divine warriors,” he continued smoothly. “Be honored. You stand not merely before your enemies, but before the gaze of countless gods and higher beings.” His golden eyes flickered with faint amusement. “This stage of the Battle of Divine Will will determine who among you is worthy of their attention… and who will be quietly cast aside.”

He tilted his head slightly. “Think of this as your grand opening performance and do try to entertain them.”

Indeed.

Before gods and other higher beings, we were nothing more than ants meant for their amusement and entertainment. Even divine heroes were just bigger ants. If I intended to reclaim my throne one day, our side had to win this Battle of Divine Will. After all, just as there were consequences for losing, there were considerable benefits for standing victorious.

“Now!” Michael clapped his hands again. “For the first stage, each of you will be assigned a fortified stronghold.”

A projection appeared above the sphere. Three massive stone fortresses materialized in miniature, arranged just as our platforms had been. At the heart of each stood a towering statue radiating distinct divine energy.

“Within your respective forts stands the statue of your patron god,” Michael continued. “Your objective is simple: protect it. Monstrous incursions will begin shortly after deployment. You have to repel them and safeguard the statue at all costs.”

He paused for a moment and continued, “But that is not all.”

His smile sharpened as the projection shifted, now showing one fortress launching an assault against another.

“You are permitted to attack enemy strongholds. You may sabotage their defenses. You may infiltrate their ranks.” His golden eyes gleamed faintly. “And you may kill opposing divine warriors without penalty.”

A murmur rippled across all three platforms.

“Kill opposing divine warriors…?” Michelle murmured, her eyes widening slightly as the weight of those words sank in.

The notion of killing another living being—one who was neither a beast nor a monster, and certainly not a construct born from the scenarios—would undoubtedly unsettle those who still clung to their old beliefs. Even if Michelle was accustomed to hunting monsters, including humanoid ones, taking the life of another divine warrior was a different matter entirely. Hesitation in such a moment could prove fatal. If she failed to adapt, that very hesitation would become her downfall.

Fortunately, she didn’t appear overly disturbed. Perhaps it was because we had already clashed with Graham’s underlings before—fighting them with the full intent to kill. That line had already been crossed, at least in spirit.

“The army whose statue survives the longest will be declared the winner. However, there is a time limit, which is seven days.”

He raised one finger.

“If only one statue remains standing before the week concludes, that faction wins immediately.”

A second finger rose.

“If more than one statue remains at the end of seven days… it will be considered a tie. In that case, all surviving factions will each suffer a deduction of two thousand soul coins per divine warrior. However, contribution-based rewards will still be distributed.”

This kind of system, one that deliberately exploited human psychology, was common in the first stage of the Battle of Divine Will. Under such conditions, conflict between factions was inevitable. It would always come down to you and them, and no one would want to be the one who lost. There was little to no chance that all three sides would agree to a tie. In an environment like this, trust was nearly impossible to establish, let alone maintain.

“First place and second place will each receive individual rewards based on personal contribution, which include—but are not limited to—damage dealt, enemies slain, defensive success, strategic impact, and overall performance,” the archangel continued.

“But for the losing faction…” He narrowed his eyes slightly and folded his wings. “Each divine warrior under that banner will be required to pay five thousand soul coins. Failure to pay will result in forced debt penalties imposed through your respective patron god.”

In such cases, if a divine warrior didn’t possess enough soul coins, their patron god would have to settle the debt with Divine Will. This deduction was different from the nightly tribute demanded in Fantasia. The punishment gods imposed afterward could often be far harsher than simply failing to pay the tribute. Of course, there was always a small chance that a god might cover the cost and not give punishment if that particular divine warrior proved valuable enough to keep.

Still, five thousand soul coins might not sound like much at first. However, when multiplied across thousands of divine warriors, the total could easily swell into the tens of millions. That was a sum large enough to leave even a god’s reserves feeling the strain.

Only Gimmel-rank divine warriors and above could pay such debts without much difficulty. Anyone weaker would struggle considerably, which became one of the reasons why I’d suggested not to include Aleph-rank divine warriors for this. Worse still, the financial burden would drag the losing faction even further behind in the overall competition.

“So defend well and attack wisely, and use everything at your disposal to win,” he added before spreading his arms theatrically. “Be sure to entertain the gods!”

At that moment, the white space trembled. The central sphere flared with blinding brilliance, and the three platforms began to dissolve into particles of light. A powerful pull seized our bodies, dragging us away as though the entire space had suddenly flipped upside down.

For a brief instant, everything turned weightless, then the world slammed back into focus. I found myself standing in the middle of a ruined street. Crumbling buildings surrounded us beneath a dull gray sky. Rusted cars littered the road, overturned and half-buried in debris. Streetlights leaned crookedly over cracked pavement, and distant structures had collapsed entirely into piles of broken concrete.

The scenery was eerily familiar. In fact, it looked almost identical to the ruined city from the first scenario.

“What a familiar scene…” Boris muttered beside me.

“Isn’t this from the first scenario…?” Michelle uttered.

Ahead of us stood a large supermarket building. Its glass front had been reinforced with thick steel and concrete barricades, while heavy metal shutters sealed most of the entrances. A faint barrier shimmered around the structure, clearly marking it as the designated fort.

From a defensive standpoint, it was surprisingly ideal. The building had limited entry points, wide sightlines across the surrounding streets, and ample interior space for supplies and personnel.

Before anyone could begin organizing, a familiar blue notification window appeared before my eyes.
