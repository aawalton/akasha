After the battle they scouted the area. They found signs of more corrupted creatures.

“Did anyone else get a ‘error’ message on their kill notifications,” Caleb asked.

Mettius’ neck snapped in Caleb’s direction.

“What did your kill notification say exactly?”

Caleb was certain he’d just revealed a secret, he just didn’t know what it was.

He read out the deer’s kill notification.

“You have elevated system access,” Mettius said.

Caleb nodded.

When he’d found Kelia had it as well, he just assumed it came with a rare class—though now that he considered it, Grisim didn’t have it.

“What does that mean?” Kelia asked.

“It means you probably should have told Canus and he would have valued you even higher toward the recruitment quota.”

“That doesn’t answer the question,” Caleb said.

Mettius sighed and muttered “barbarians” under his breath.

“It means you have access to the system’s knowledge. Your skill descriptions will be from the system and you will be able to use identify type skills more effectively.”

“We don’t have any of those,” Kelia put in.

Mettius’ eyes widened.

“You have it too?”

Kelia nodded.

“Well I suggest you learn one soon.”

“How common is it?” Caleb asked.

“It’s based on how much—” Mettius began then stopped himself.

“Essence?” Caleb asked.

“Yes,” Mettius admitted, looking around warily in the forest. “But I wouldn’t discuss that word openly. People who have a certain amount of it when they initialize to a rare class or higher will get elevated access.”

“What’s essence?” Kelia asked. “And why can’t we talk about it?”

“I can’t talk about it,” Mettius said.

They dropped the subject at that. Mettius’ words had a finality to them the pair didn’t want to press and Magnus was silent and stoic as ever.

They spent the walk back wary of their surroundings

Back at camp they reported their findings. Canus doubled the watch and planned to send out initialized scouts—including Grisim—in the morning to get a better understanding of the situation.

“We are here to take care of a budding rebellion, but we can’t leave these strange corrupted monsters in our wakes.”

Canus dismissed them to get some rest, but Caleb and Kelia stopped at the flap.

“Where do we stay?” Caleb asked.

“I’ll show you,” Mettius offered and lead them through the camp.

They passed the rows of tents each housing four men until they reached a section with a cordoned off guard.

The tents on the other side of the guard were twice the size of the rest of the tents.

“This ones yours,” Mettius said, gesturing them inside. “Sorry it’s so spartan, but you can requisition a larger one since you are a couple. Nothing to be had about the quality of furnishing unless we raid a villa. But that’s not likely to happen up here.”

Inside was the nicest room Caleb had ever been in. Thick artistically woven carpets covered the floor and equally thick draperies hung on the walls dampening the sound of the camp outside. Near the door sat a wooden table and chair set, each covered with carvings. Rare fruits lay in a bowl on the table, and every corner of the tent was filled with odds and ends of similar quality.

Mettius left them in silence at they stared around in awe of the nicest room they’d ever been in.

***

The next morning they woke to a horn blast with the rest of the camp that wasn’t on duty. They stood in line for food with the rest of the legionnaires until a messenger was sent to bring them to a separate location where officers sat at tables. Food was still served in a line, and it didn’t seem any higher quality, but the tables sat four people each instead of the long bench tables of the other mess area.

“We don’t eat with the rabble,” Mettius said when Kelia and Caleb sat with him.

“Why? Would it sully your princely dignity?” Kelia asked. “Would they possibly dirty your tunic?”

They’d already noticed everyone seemed to watch their words around Mettius, but since he’d not reacted poorly to Caleb’s initial less than formal introduction to the man, they’d decided to not treat him any different than they would someone of the same rank.

“No,” Mettius said, irritated at the ribbing. “Because during a battle, we often sit on the sidelines, watching them die as we wait for an enemy hero, or knight, or whatever the barbarians we face choose to call their high level initialized. Its much easier when you don’t know them.”

“Oh…” Kelia said, then softly added. “I’m sorry.”

“It’s fine. I would have expected the same if one of my siblings had said as much.”

Caleb didn’t think it was fine though, and sensed comparing Mettius to his siblings in may not be a wise choice in the future.

Caleb asked after Grisim and learned he’d been sent out at first light with the other initialized scouts. They had many. Many of the towns under the empire’s control knew the secrets to unlocking classes the empire used as scouts in their army. Gavin, Doren and Revin had all enlisted in the legion despite there being no conscription, and they’d all begun their training as scouts. The empire even knew of means to upgrade some common classes into the uncommon Imperial Scout class.

As a rare scout adjacent class Imperial Monster Hunter, Grisim would eventually be put in charge of a scout contingent for the legion—should he survive his meeting with the Legate. Caleb thought it dumb that class rarity alone could determine something like rank, but that was just how the world worked. He supposed the ancient empire had found a good system with the enchanted blades that could be used to grant specific classes to those who needed them. He only wished he could learn to make the same.

After eating, Mettius took them to a training yard.

“We need to have a better understanding of each other’s capabilities,” he said. “I’ll start. As I said, my father—may he reign forever and all that—in his infinite wisdom, chose to assign me a diplomatic focused class and send me out to the front lines where we have no diplomatic relations.”

“I have two questions,” Caleb said.

Mettius stopped and gave Caleb a put upon expression but waited for him to speak.

“How did he give you a class and why that one?”

Mettius sighed and mutter “barbarians” under his breath.

“The Emperor, the class, not my father—craters, may he reign forever,” Mettius let out a breath and started over. “The Emperor class and most noble classes can confer classes onto others. Equites for example is actually a rare class my—the Emperor class can give out to some number of people. I don’t know how many. It’s a secret. I was sent here, because I am not one of the contenders for the throne. I was given a class as was my birthright, but I was not deemed worthy of vying for the throne.”

“So… you’re just here to die?” Kelia asked.

“No,” Mettius said firmly. “If I was of a mind, I could have simply loafed around and been a burden on the legion, never partaking in battle or leaving camp. But, I’m not of a mind to do so. Which brings us back to the topic at hand.”

He held up his gauntleted hands at that.

“Regardless of my class, I am a wizard. My class gives me conditional bonuses to willpower and acuity. These gloves absorb mana from the environment and convert it to time, space, and force mana. I can draw it into my pool, giving me a massive mana reserve. I have taught myself the spells Force Bolt, Shield, and Haste. Together this allows me to focus on mental stats while still being effective in battle. After enough time suffused in the mana it generates I will gain affinities for time, space, and force mana and begin to craft my own spells.”

Mettius demonstrated his spells, showing off the destructive potential of his Force Bolt, the speed of his Haste, and the strength of his Shield.

For Haste, he told Caleb to try to hit him with a training staff, and then moved out of the way with speed Caleb could only match with his acuity.

For shield he allowed Caleb to swing his hammer at full strength against the barrier. Caleb refrained from using a skill of his own, but the translucent bubble barrier that appeared in front of Mettius withstood the combined might of Caleb’s strength and willpower. He sensed a little give in it and saw in his Magical Perception that the barrier was close to breaking from the blow.

“Now try again, but this time I’m going to keep talking to you,” Mettius said.

Caleb though it odd, but obliged. Mettius talked about some strange quirk of imperial etiquette while Caleb swung, and this time the shield had no signs of breaking.

“Try again with a skill,” Mettius said, then continued talking about fork lengths.

Caleb used Arcane Power Strike, using only 5 mana, but that 5 mana would greatly amplify his power. This time the barrier held better than his first strike, but still he sensed the slightest strain to it.

“If I am talking to someone, I get a boost in perception, willpower, and acuity in effects that involve them. It only works on creatures capable of understanding me though.”

Caleb went next and described his abilities honestly— though he made careful to not claim to have an Iron Affinity and let that be told implicitly by careful wording. He suspected Mettius had the ability to detect evasions and lies to some degree. Then he explained what he knew of Bog’s abilities, including his recent addition of mana steel talons on his right claw.

Kelia went next.

“I have a beast bond with Avis,” She began. “And from her I get a wind affinity. I have my Cold Affinity from my bloodline as well as a skill that makes my cold magic more efficient with as my mana pool becomes purer cold mana. Then I have Frost Bolt with… is not very good as I think I saw last night.”

“How long have you had the class?” Mettius asked.

“Two days,” Caleb and Kelia answered together.

Mettius laughed.

“Of course you’re no good, you’re new. How many months did it take your husband to get where he is?”

Caleb and Kelia looked at each other uncomfortably.

“Like… a week,” Caleb said reluctantly.

Mettius did a double take and he smile vanished.

“How? Have you just been in constant battle?”

“No,” Caleb said quickly to explain. “Well, at first. I was trapped in a cavern. I had to forge my own equipment, and in doing so I created a lot of new techniques to aid my smithing using magic. Then, when I went into battle those techniques crystallized into skill.”

Mettius nodded along.

“That makes sense… The crafting gives you the opportunity to challenge yourself in a way the system recognizes and rewards.”

Caleb didn’t mention that he was fairly certain the system wasn’t assisting his crafting. He was beginning to think he was above average in his magical skills and didn’t want to draw more attention to himself less people investigate and discover he can craft magic items.

“Kelia, you need to experiment with your magic like your husband has. I want you to practice purifying our mana pool and while you do so, practice freezing the air. You have a skill that does it for you, but you need to learn to do it without the skill. Caleb did it backwards from what most initialized do. Once you have learned that technique”—he used Caleb’s term—”you can work on pouring more power into your ice bolts. As you purify your mana pool, you’ll be able cast large bolts for the same cost you pay for small ones.”

“Will I ever be able to use wind magic?” Kelia asked.

Her and Caleb had talked about their magic a lot the night before and both wished to one day learn to fly.

“Eventually,” Mettius said. “There’s an advanced technique to separate your mana pools. If you master that one day you could have access to both. For now you must get used to keeping it pure. It will eventually become second nature—you might even get a skill.”

“Caleb, I want to see if you can…” he stopped talking as he noticed Kelia’s hand. “What does your ring do?”

Kelia looked at it surprised.

“Nothing. Caleb made it for me as a wedding gift”

“It’s made out of mage steel,” he said, turning to Caleb. “How did you forge mage steel?”

“A lot of heat,” Caleb said. “Why, what can it do?”

“Well it’s worth almost as much as the material in my gauntlets,” Mettius said. “It can fit more enchantments than most metals, and if it’s empty of its natural mana it can be used as a mana battery. If you can get someone to purge its mana, you could push all your wind mana into that, drawing on it when you need to cast wind magic and keeping your pool pure the rest of the time.”

“Is purifying metal like that hard?” Caleb asked probingly.

Mettius shook his head.

“Drusus the legion smith can do it.”

“I already purified it when making it,” Caleb said, once Mettius confirmed it not some rare skill.

From the talk he’d heard so far Drusus was not a Blacksmith but just a blacksmith and if he could do it, then there was no reason Caleb couldn’t.

“You really undersold yourself to Canus,” Mettius said. “What else aren’t you telling us?”

Caleb’s eyes grew wide, but he said nothing.

“Ok, keep your secrets. It’s time for training.”
