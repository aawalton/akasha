I’m cold and hungry. The guard brought me dry bread, a bit of water, and some disgusting porridge. I ate it all, and I’m still hungry. Dungeon cell life fucking sucks.

But I did have, in abundance, free time. And even in here, I had things to do.

First, I cried a little. That’s understandable. But after that, I started searching the cell—for anything: a hidden key, a loose brick, a scratched message from a previous prisoner that will trigger a quest. That kind of thing was common in games like this.

I searched for, like, two hours.

I found… rat poop.

Then I tried yelling: “Someone! Hello?!” to check if any other prisoners were nearby. A few minutes later, the guard showed up, banged the bars with his spear, and told me to shut the hell up.

Okay. On to the next thing—testing the leveling system.

I tried doing pushups. Got to seven. My arms were trembling.

So my strength starting stat is like my real life: zero.

Still, I kept at it for maybe an hour, doing random awkward calisthenics, hoping I’d see a popup: “Level Up!”

Maybe get engulfed in golden light. That’d be nice.

Didn’t happen.

The next thing to do—thinking. Actually, really thinking—for the first time since I got here.

There were a lot of weird things that happened yesterday, and now that I’m not busy panicking or humiliating myself, it’s time to go through them.

Let’s start with this world.

When I saw the medieval town and fantasy tavern, I took it as proof I’d been isekai’d. But there may be another explanation.

There was this TV show—Westworld—about a super-realistic amusement park where rich people could live out cowboy fantasies and interact with robots that looked and felt human.

What if I’m in something like that? Maybe the “NPCs” are actors. Or well-made robots. Maybe I was drugged and dumped in this place.

And maybe… people are watching me. Millions of people. Watching me suffer and flail around, laughing their asses off while I scream “Menu!” like a moron.

I hate that theory. It’s too embarrassing.

But it could be disproven. I just need to see something undeniably magical. Something truly otherworldly, that no near-future technology could fake.

I… actually haven’t seen anything that unusual. The dwarves and elves could be costumes or advanced robots.

Damn—I should’ve checked the stars last night! Seen if they matched Earth’s constellations. That could have been a clue, and there is no window in the cell.

They say in science that the more boring explanation is usually the true one.

Between “isekai’d by magical fate” and “thrown into a super high-tech fantasy amusement park,” the second one is actually a bit more boring. So… more likely?

I need to keep my eyes open. Stay alert. Look for inconsistencies.But I am not going to pull some elf’s ears to see if they’re real. I’m not that stupid.

Now, about the NPCs.

The Baron. He was different. His speech was fluid, like he wasn’t just reading off canned dialogue.

Could he be a more advanced AI or not an NPC?

There might be a clue here. I need to find a way to speak with him again… without getting myself executed in the process.

And Hildi... Her mood changes, but her speech doesn’t. What does that even mean?

Wait, is there a system for NPC marriage here? Skyrim had that.

Could I—technically—marry Hildi?

Arrrgh, why would I want to marry the woman who tried to stab me yesterday!? What the hell is wrong with my brain?

And this line of thinking isn’t helping. I need to focus—on something useful.

This place stinks. I need to get out of this fucking cell.

Seven days—seven days is way too long! I… I’m going to get sick. Or starve. Or… freeze to death!

I have to escape this fucking place!

I’ll hide under the straw bed, then shout “HELP!”The guard comes, sees the empty cell, panics. Opens the bars to search for a secret tunnel.Then I jump out, punch him in the head, knock him out cold, take his clothes and key, lock him inside, dress up as the guard, and walk out free.

Perfect plan. Nothing could go wrong.

Yeah… sure.

I need to stop the thinking. Everything is getting depressing. I need something to do.

I could count… bricks.

Or straws! Yes, something to pass the time!

Wait, shit—there is no window. How can I know how much time has passed? How many days?

I could go crazy in here.

Actually—I am feeling a little crazy already.

I hyperventilate a bit, try to breathe slower, cry a little.

Then I calm down.

Eventually, I put my face against the bars and look out into the hallway. At the far end, I see a small beam of light. Probably from a narrow window or slit in the wall.

That’s something. I can look at the light and track the passage of days.

That helps. A little.

Okay. New goal: stay sane.

I’ve got straw. I’ll make dolls.

Straw dolls. And a straw house. And a table. And cute little straw cups and do a fucking tea party!

I am totally not going to get CRAZY!!

After a few hours of crying, half-napping, and trying to twist straw into humanoid shapes, I hear footsteps coming down the hallway.

Different footsteps. Lighter.

I look through the bars.

It’s someone short, wearing simple clothes. A… girl?

Yes. I recognize her. One of the maids from the Baron’s hall.

What the hell is she doing here?

Okay. Focus. No crying. No begging. No whimpering. This might be important. Don’t screw it up.

I sit up, look at her calmly, and wait for her to speak.

“Who are you?” she asks.

What? That’s not what I expected.

“I’m an adventurer,” I answer carefully. “I came to this kingdom…”

“How much time did you get in the cells?” she interrupts.

Hold up.

No NPC has ever cut me off before. Something is very off about her.

I try my own test. “Tell me about yourself,” I ask. That question always gets NPCs talking. Even the guards will answer.

“You didn’t answer my question,” she says again. “How much time did you get?”

“Uh… a week,” I say. Then it hits me. “Oh my god—you’re not an NPC! You’re a player, like me!”

I jump to my feet and grab the bars. She takes a step back, clearly startled.

“No, I’m not,” she says quickly. “I’m an NPC.”

“Come on!” I insist. “No NPC would say they are an NPC. I know you’re a player! Please—you gotta help me get out of this place!”

“I… I know what you did,” she says. “You’re a bad person. You deserve to be here.”

“What? No!” I go pale. “Please, it was a misunderstanding!” I’m shouting now. “You don’t know what it’s like in here!”

She flinches. Then turns and runs off.

“Wait!” I shove my face through the bars, “Please come back!”

Shit. Shit! I ruined it. Goddamn it! Me and my big, pathetic, stupid mouth.

I could’ve learned so much just by talking to her for a few more minutes.

She might’ve known something. Anything.

And even if she didn’t… just talking to someone would’ve helped. Would’ve made this whole thing feel less like solitary hell.

Back to the straw doll-making, I guess.…About an hour later, she came back.
