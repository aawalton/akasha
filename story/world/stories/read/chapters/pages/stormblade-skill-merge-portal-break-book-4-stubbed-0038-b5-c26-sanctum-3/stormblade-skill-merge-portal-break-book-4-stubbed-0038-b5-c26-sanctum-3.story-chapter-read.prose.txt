Black slime and seawater dripped from a half-dozen gargantuan tentacles. An even darker, black orb hung high overhead. Ellen’s Void Garden buckled and folded as the Placid King stretched out.

I broke into a sprint. Stormrunner activated, and wings of lightning picked me up. I catapulted into the air just as the first tentacle slammed into the lightning rod I’d been defending. It pierced the Placid King’s skin. The portal metal tip sliced through flesh, and a fountain of blood erupted from the tentacle’s top as it ripped an exit wound. But the Placid King didn’t even seem to notice. As the many-suckered limb rose, it ripped the lightning rod from the ground and whipped it toward me.

A ton of sharp, gore-coated portal metal ripped past me, missing my head by feet.

In all my portal delves, I’d never experienced anything quite like this. The Placid King’s aura was almost suppressive. No emotions got through it, only a calm sense of certainty that I was outmatched. It wasn’t fear—fear would have given me something to ground on and build into fury. It was just certainty.

Stormrunner kept running as I rolled in the air, angling toward the Placid King. A tentacle crashed through the air toward me. The sonic boom hit me a full second after the top-most sucker clipped my foot, dragging me down almost fifty feet before I could cut myself free. Too close. The Storm’s Blade cut into monstrous flesh, only for it to heal shut before it could even cauterize.

The Placid King hadn’t even fully entered my portal world yet, but already, his half dozen main tentacles filled the space between his body’s bulk and the Lightning Tower. Another four tentacles, slightly smaller but longer, trailed behind him.

“Noelstra. The time has come to give in to inevitability. I will ascend. You and your former master will not stop me.” The voice echoed against nothing, and the SS-Plus-Rank Paragon’s aura redoubled.

I turned and fled.

The Placid King’s strategy was obvious. He wanted to kill Ellen and me, thus denying the God of Thunder a resource and gaining a step in their race—as well as preventing both of us from becoming competition in the longer term. All he had to do was fill the world with his body and squeeze us out. If we left, we’d lose the Lightning Tower. If we stayed too long, he’d cut us off from the escape portal and kill us. And if we left, he’d follow us to Earth.

And then there was the aura. I was a beat too slow. I couldn’t bring myself to feel fury or rage, or even mild annoyance, over the Placid King’s assault. Without those emotions, I couldn’t truly lock in.

“I’m going to stop you here,” I said. But just like when I’d crossed blades with Tathrix in Queen Mother Yalerox’s portal, my words were empty and tasted like ash.

The only difference was that, with every second I survived, the Eye of the Storm grew closer to opening—and this time, it would open for me, not for my enemy.

I turned my attention to the tentacles, refreshing Stormrunner and then dropping into Thunderlord stance.

Stamina: 291/620, Mana: 194/850

My Mana was already filling up nicely, and my Stamina had recovered slightly, too. I had…nowhere near enough resources for this, but I didn’t care. The closest tentacle rose, and I landed on it, sword first. The blade sank into the Placid King’s arm all the way to the hilt; the monster was incredibly strong, but his durability wasn’t in his armor.

It was in his healing. The Storm’s Blade started to rise out of the SS-Plus monster’s flesh as his Health went to work, fixing the wound while the weapon was still lodged in it.

Another tentacle crashed down on me. The weight was incredible; I dodged it by inches, and the impact flattened both limbs for a full two seconds before they rebounded. The air filled with a horrific popping sound as suction cups ripped like Jessie’s favorite shoes’ straps. And over the sound, the Placid King’s laughter thundered off the portal world’s edges.

“You’re trying to hurt me? Pathetic.”

It was pathetic. I had no weapon in my toolkit that could hurt a monster of this scale. Nothing. Nada.

But overhead, the Eye of the Storm was—

Pain.

Overwhelming, crushing pain.

Something buckled. I poured Stamina into it before I realized it wasn’t just something. A tentacle had clipped my back—not even a direct hit—and my armor had taken the worst of it before the impact shredded it like sandpaper on wood. Then it had ripped my back open to the bone.

It hurt! But the Stamina helped, and for all that my entire back was on fire, it was…shockingly minor. Already, my Torrentborne was hard at work, covering the wound with a scab that healed even as it formed.

Still, I couldn’t take another hit like that—especially not a direct one.

I stayed in Torrentborne. This time, I had a handful of Lightning Charges, and as the next tendril swiped toward me, I activated Lightning Dance and vanished. I appeared on the ground—or rather, on a thin platform of brick that hadn’t yet been toppled, with another lightning rod sticking out from it. All around me was water. It rippled as the Placid King’s bulk started moving for the Lightning Tower.

“No,” I said. I felt something. An emotion. It was fleeting, but as the Placid King continued to laugh, his aura flickered, and mine pushed out just long enough.

I used Lightning Dance again. This time, I landed on the thick part of one of the gargantuan arms and sprinted straight at the Placid King’s main bulk. The skin below my feet was almost too smooth to find purchase on. I fell, caught myself with one arm, and kept running. Behind me, another tentacle closed in, faster than I could possibly move. Another Lightning Dance threw me forward. I landed on the Paragon’s body.

He had to be a hundred yards tall. The Lightning Tower, in the new configuration Tanat had set it to, was taller—but not by much. He’d be able to reach its pinnacle with his tendrils easily, to say nothing of the room where Ellen and Tanat were posted up.

And speaking of Ellen…

She hadn’t stopped, either.

Mana poured into me as her Abyssal Well continued its work. A shadow puppet rose up, its bulk a pale imitation of the Placid King’s, and crashed down into him. I threw myself clear and used my fourth Lightning Dance. This time, I landed in the water below.

The Placid King actually staggered. His inexorable march toward the Lightning Tower stopped for a full second as the Shadow Puppeteered version of himself hit. The air filled with more of the same squelching pops, each sending a shiver down my spine. Was Ellen…trying to rip the Placid King apart? Could that work?

No.

The shadow clone ripped like cloth as the Placid King’s fourth tentacle bore down on it. “A mockery. You mock me?” Another emotion surged forth for a split second, and I clamped down on it for as long as I could.

Contempt.

“How dare you mock me!?”

How dare the Placid King attack my family? How dare he threaten Jeff and Yasmin? How dare he put Jessie in danger!?

“When I finish here, I will flatten Earth. There will be nothing left. Nothing!”

When I was finished with him, I’d make sure that not even his core survived. There’d be nothing left of the Placid King.

“I will break your world personally, and then I will ascend. Not even your precious God of Thunder will be able to stop me.”

I wouldn’t stop until I’d broken the Placid King and ended his threats for good.

The Storm’s Blade hummed in my hand, and my Mana had repaired my maelstrom armor. I growled in rage, baring my teeth, and threw myself back into battle. And as I did, the Placid King’s first tendril reached the Lightning Tower.

The tendril shook the entire tower from foundation to tip as it wrapped around its base. The Lightning Tower was technically thicker than it—it was thicker at its base than even the Placid King’s bulbous body—but it was also a hollow cage of portal metal. I sprinted down the monster’s arm, sword lashing out at yellow eyes whose glares quickly shifted to emotionless stares. The battle trance I’d slipped into faded, and I clung to its last vestiges.

If it went, I’d lose an edge, and I needed every edge I could get.

The Placid King began to move. His body slid through the wreckage of the Void Garden. Hedge rows and dark streams buckled as he moved. And his myriad eyes opened.

From each of them, a jet of hypercompressed water surged out. They sliced into everything—the portal world’s ground, the remnants of hedges, and the few remaining lightning rods. I dodged and weaved. One ripped into the maelstrom gauntlet covering my off-hand and left a bloody line across my palm. A second slammed into my chest. This one didn’t cut. It bored, then erupted out of my back in a fountain of blood and water. I coughed. More blood erupted from my mouth.

I was in trouble.

Torrentborne. Mist Trance. I didn’t have a better move, and I needed a second to find one. My body went incorporeal; a dozen more jets of water cut into it, but I ignored them all.

A second tentacle hit the Lightning Tower.

With it, the Placid King slid forward even faster. I solidified again just as his beams cut off, landing at the base of a trailing tendril. It whipped upward, and I jammed the Storm’s Blade into it to steady myself.

The Lightning Tower rippled with energy. A black ball of nothingness had caught a single arm, and Ellen poured her Abyssal Storm spell into it. A second was caught—for the moment—in a Shadow Cell. She was in her Voidwalker form; the flow of Mana from her core to mine had completely shut off, and she was actually demanding it from me.

I looked up at the sky. The Eye of the Storm crackled with electricity; it looked like a thousand strobe lights facing down toward my portal world. The wind howled all around it, and rain whipped through the air. Hurricane walls were already forming toward the edges of the portal world and working their way toward us.

The time was almost upon us.

I’d been holding back. Not much, but a little, for just this moment. Even though I’d had Mana, I hadn’t cast a single spell. The reason was simple; I needed to be as close to empty as possible, and I needed to land the best blow I could before the Eye of the Storm finished opening. I couldn’t stand up to either the God of Thunder or the Placid King in a stand-up fight. They’d simply weather my best blows and win a battle of attrition.

So it’d all come down to leveraging the world itself into one massive hit.

But we’d planned this out. Everything that had happened was…well, nothing had gone according to plan. But we could still execute the mid and endgames correctly. Even Ellen’s Voidwalker and her spell spam was part of it.

Cyclone Forms. Rolling Thunder. I threw an Ariette’s Thunderhead into the sky and doubled it. The clouds tore away, becoming part of the swirling, raging storm overhead. So did the copy, and the second and third doubled Thunderheads I cast.

The Eye of the Storm rippled. It flashed. But it didn’t open.

And the Placid King reached the Lightning Tower.

His tentacles wrapped around it. Two, then three, then four. They spiraled upward, each long enough to reach halfway up. Three quarters. Ellen cast, her Voidwalker form giving each Black Hole and Shadow Puppeteer extra oomph, and her Arcane Resonator copying the damage from her Shadow Cell.

But it wasn’t enough. The Placid King continued his assault, and the first of his arms reached the same level as Ellen and Tanat.

I took aim.

Brendan’s Storm Sigil.

Hand of the Thunder God.

The beam of electrical energy slammed into the tentacle. It bored through it cleanly, right at the base, where it connected to the bulbous, round body. I panned it back and forth like a swing, slicing into the unarmored, squishy limb as the combined power of the storms overhead tore into the Placid King.

He screamed.

The tentacle came free. It slid down the Lightning Tower, crashing to the ground. Watery blood and chunks of yellow flesh poured from the wound, sizzling on the ground. It had cauterized, but even disconnected from the monster, it still tried—and failed—to heal. The stench of burning flesh and salt water filled the air.

The Placid King was silent. His eyes stared at the thrashing, twitching limb as its thrashing slowed, then stopped. For a brief few seconds, the only sound in my portal world was the Eye of the Storm, howling overhead.

Into the silence and the howling, the Placid King spoke. His voice was quiet, the picture of perfectly controlled calm. He hardly seemed to notice his missing limb. “A valiant effort, Kade Noelstra. You’ve injured me, and even with my Health, this kind of wound will take time to recover from.

“But look around you.

“This pathetic world is already broken. All your hard work has done nothing but scratch me. And in return for your most powerful, most desperate attack, I have cut you off from your only retreat. There’s only one outcome now. You three will die. I will feast on what cores I can and destroy the rest. And then, my ascension will leave your homeworlds in ruins. I am, after all, an inevitability.”

“I disagree,” I said, coughing. The wound in my chest was healing, but it was healing far too slowly for my liking—and in the meantime, my Stamina kept dropping. The storm overhead had hardly noticed my Hand of the Thunder God. It was still building. Just a few more seconds.

“You disagree? The logic of this situation escapes you, then?”  The Placid King’s two remaining tentacles broke out from inside the Lightning Tower, thrashing below me. Their eyes opened, staring at me. “No amount of struggling—nothing a handful of S-Rankers can do—will buy you more than a few seconds of breath. The ocean’s still waves come for you, as does its Paragon.”

“Nah.”

“’Nah?”’

“That’s right. You’re in check, your majesty,” I said.

It was at this point that the Eye of the Storm finally opened.
