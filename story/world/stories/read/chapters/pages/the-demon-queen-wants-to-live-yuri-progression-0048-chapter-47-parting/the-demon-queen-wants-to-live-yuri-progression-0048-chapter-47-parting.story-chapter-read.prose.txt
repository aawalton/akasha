I pulled Ash through the corridors of the Academy without explanation. She asked twice where we were going. I said "training" and kept walking. She asked a third time. I did not answer, and she stopped asking.

People glanced at us as we passed. Word of the Arbiter had spread, and today, more people than not gave me a wide berth as we passed. They pressed their backs against the wall, stopped their conversations at our passage, only to then resume them once we left. Ash said nothing, though I was starting to understand her now. She wanted to say something.

I did not know how to get to where I intended. One hallway blended into another, which blended into another still. When I finally considered forcing the answers out of a student, we turned a corner, and there it was. One of the training yards I had seen before.

The training yard was a long stretch of grass bordered by stone pillars, open to the sky. Perhaps three dozen students occupied the space. Most sat cross-legged on the ground with their eyes closed, their marks glowing faintly in the morning light. Most of these had Lines, though I saw the occasional Sigil. A few sparred near the far end. There were scents, dozens of different Essences blending into each other.

I glanced around, then steered us to the farthest corner, away from everyone else. A few students glanced at my horns as we passed. Whatever focus they'd had cracked in my mere presence. They stared, and they stared openly. One of them fell over from where she was sitting cross-legged in the grass. Ash walked close beside me, her hand resting near the sword at her hip.

"I don't understand what we're doing here," Ash said. "We are going to train, aren't we? Like we did in the forest? But…why here?"

"We are," I said. "This is a suitable enough place for it." I sat on the grass, and slowly set down the satchel, before I gestured for Ash to sit across from me. She lowered herself slowly, the sword settling across her lap. I looked at it. Then I looked at her.

"My power." I held up my right hand, "cannot heal the boy," I said. "The wrongness and his channels are fused. Ruin decays what it touches, but it cannot peel apart two things that have grown together so completely that they have become the same thing. Not without destroying both." I held her gaze. Then I pointed at her left arm. "But your power can. I am certain of it."

Ash stared at me. I watched the understanding settle over her face, followed swiftly by something else. Something that made her look smaller than she had been moments ago. "I can't do something like that," she said. She said the words quickly. More quickly than I was used to from her. "I know I did something…with her," Ash said, looking around. There was no one near us. I had made it so. "But this is different." Her voice was lower. "I didn't even know what I was doing then. It was all instinct. This time you want me to use my mark…inside him. This is way harder. Even I can tell that much. If I do something like this...I'll hurt him, or worse."

The speed and certainty of the refusal struck me as odd. One would think a Hero would reach for anything that might help the frail. "You will practice," I said. "As you are now, you will do him only harm. We will change that." Ash only looked more concerned now than she had before. I still did not understand.

I picked a small pink flower from the grass at the edge of the yard. I set it on the ground between us. The petals caught the morning light and held it. It was a simple thing, pretty in its own way. I might not have noticed that once. "I want you to Sever its color from the rest of it," I said. "You will leave the flower whole, while removing only the pink."

Ash looked at the flower. She looked at me. "That doesn't make sense! You're telling me I could do something like that? What does 'removing the pink' even mean?!"

"If I am right about your power, yes. I believe I am. As for what that will result in, I do not know," I said, arms folded.

Ash looked at me for a moment longer. Then, she drew her sword and moved it towards the flower. I saw her mark light up. It lit up with red, the color of the disguise she wore. Ash moved the blade atop the flower, from petal to stem. The flower split into two perfect halves, each one still brilliantly pink, and drifted apart in the grass.

I reached over and picked out another flower. "Again."

Ash drew the blade through the air and made two halves. I picked another, and again there were two halves. I gave her a dozen samples and no result was different from the other. The cuts were all the same too. Perfect and smooth. Eventually, I took a small stone from the dirt and set it in front of her. "We will try this next. Sever the hardness from it without touching the whole."

Ash frowned and used parting again. It split into two equal halves, both of which remained just as hard as when they had been part of one greater whole.

Next, I reached for a leaf. I held it out in front of her. "We will try this. See how it stands? Sever its firmness. Do not split it in half as you have everything else."

The Hero did as I asked. The leaf split, exactly how everything else so far had split. There was a pattern in how Ash was using her power, or perhaps in how her power was using her. I had only just begun to notice it. "Very well then. Let us try something smaller even than this." I gestured at the grass all around us.

Ash squinted. I pointed at one of the many tiny insects in the grass. Ash followed my pointing. It was a red-shelled thing that moved very slowly across a tiny stone.

"A beetle?" she asked.

So that was what this thing was called. I would remember that. "Yes, this 'beetle'. Your task is to sever the red from its shell," I said. "While leaving the creature whole."

Ash took a deep breath and raised her hands again. She drew a line in the air with her sword, and the beetle was severed so cleanly that for a few steps it did not separate at all. Then one half slid from the other as the beast died. Its two half-shells remained as red as they had been before. I found another insect, a brown worm-like thing that curled when I picked it up. Ash did not know the name of this creature. The beast split and died. Its color remained the same.

Another failure. In exactly the same fashion as all of the other failures. If Ash were any other, I would have assumed that the task was hard, and that any progress, however slow, was to be expected. But this was Ash. I looked up at her.

The Hero's hands were trembling. I might not have noticed if I was not watching her so closely right now. The Hero hid it by tightening her hands. She tightened them until they turned pale. Demons had done this before me often enough out of fear. I did not think it was the same thing here.

There was a light buzzing in the air. A flying insect flew between us and slowly landed on a blade of grass. It was no bigger than the beetle had been. Ash looked at it. She looked at me. On my nod, she raised the blade. The wings separated from the body in perfect silence. The creature writhed in the grass. And there was that trembling again in Ash's hands.

I reached down and gently lifted the creature. It was still writhing, and so it was still alive. It would not be alive for long. I reached into it with a whisper of Bloom. The thing's life was flickering. There was a gap where Ash had used her power, and all I had to do was simply fill it with the Cradle, while holding its wings to where they had detached. Perhaps I could have done this without reaching for the wings at all, but this would be easier. The wings attached again. The creature twitched, then fluttered, then lifted from my palm and disappeared into the air.

I looked at Ash. "You should have told me this was difficult for you."

The insect took a few moments to begin flying. It slowly walked forward, its wings twitching, as if it could not believe it was not dead. Then it flew and went. Ash watched it go the whole time, her grip white around her sword. "It wasn't bothering me that much," she said. The morning light was in her hair, almost making it glow. "I do not mind killing, when there is a purpose to it. I'm good at killing. That's what I was made for, after all," she said it simply as if it were an undeniable fact of the world. And she was not wrong. That was what the class of Hero was for.

She was silent. A wind blew from somewhere to my right, and carried with it the many scents of all the flowers around us. I had been right all along then. I held out my hand. "Ash. Give me your sword."

The Hero went still. She went so still she hardly even breathed. I had seen this look before often enough. She had heard something she did not wish to hear. "Lys-"

"You heard me clearly, Hero," I said, arm still outstretched. Ash stared at my hand. She looked at it as if at any moment I might let Ruin loose. She looked from it to the sword on her lap. The fingers of her right hand had whitened around the grip. "I can't use parting without it, Lys. I've tried," she said quickly. The Hero was almost pleading. I had never heard her sound like this before.

"You used it on yourself when you were carving your Essence Core," I said. "There was no blade then. You needed no sword. You needed nothing but your own will." I kept my hand extended. "The sword is making the cutting be about the weapon. This power is yours alone. You must learn to use it, alone. Give me the sword, Ash."

The Hero did not move. The only part of her that moved at all was her face. There was a war playing out there, one plain enough that even I could see. And I could understand some of why. She had buried her armor in Hamel, but I had not seen her consider burying her sword beside it. I waited, arm still out. I had rarely ever been so patient, rarely ever waited. If I wanted a thing, I would simply take. Even the demand was rare from my lips. Right now, I would wait. For Ash, I would wait however long she required.

Eventually, she relented. Her grip loosened one finger at a time. Slowly, she lifted the sword from her lap and held it out toward me. I took it, and set it on the grass between us. Then I picked it up again, and this time I set it behind me. Far enough that reaching for it would require reaching and choosing.

"You will try again, and you will learn. The sword makes the cutting about the weapon," I said. "Without it, the power is yours alone. If you think about it only in terms of the blade, then you will only learn how to cut as a blade does. Your power is more than that. It is you who decides how and what you cut." I looked at her steadily. "Am I wrong?"

Ash stared down at her own hands, empty now. What she was seeing, I could not guess. The image of the blade that was no longer there, perhaps. She squeezed her hands shut. I tried to make my voice soft for her. "You are not a Hero anymore, Ash. The class is gone. If you wish to keep the sword, then keep it, but do not carry it because you think you must."

Ash looked back up at me. "Then what am I now, if I'm not a Hero?" she asked.

It was a foolish question. I answered it immediately. "You are a retainer. Not just any retainer. You are my retainer." The Hero just stared at me. Her eyes had gone slightly wide. Heat crawled up my face, though there was no reason for it. What I had said was true. I opened my mouth and tried again. I wanted to say much, but did not know how to say any of it. What I wanted to say was too large for the words I had. "You can be more than a Hero, Ash," I said. "I would not mind seeing it."

"More than a Hero," Ash murmured. She kept looking at me, and I held her gaze. I did not look away, though I wanted to. After some seconds, Ash turned back to the grass and raised her left hand again. She had raised it above a flower, the same kind she had already split so many of.

She swiped her hand and nothing happened. She swiped it again, and nothing happened this time either. On her third attempt, there was a ripple in the air, one I barely saw at all. The power was there, clearly. It seemed this strangeness of hers went deeper than I had thought. Perhaps I should have taken her sword away earlier.

Ash took a deep breath and tried again. The flower exploded. That was the only way to describe it. Petals shredded and flew in every direction. I had never seen her use Sever this way before. Ash stared at the empty stem. Her jaw tightened.

"We will need to start from the very beginning, Ash. From control."

"This is going to take so much longer," Ash muttered. "Even if I do manage to use this as you say."

"It will." I nodded. "That does not matter." I picked another flower and set it between us. Ash looked at it, then looked at me.

And so we tried. Again and again. A blade of grass disintegrated. A pebble shredded down the center. Each attempt was either nothing or too much. Ash did not have even the faintest of control. I knew what her sword was doing to her, but it was still strange to see the difference without it.

Time passed and the sun climbed high in the sky. The Ember Core hummed inside me and eventually that hum turned into a sharp grinding pain. I pressed my hand to my chest when Ash's eyes were closed in concentration. I breathed through it and said nothing. I picked another flower and set it before her.

The bell for the mess hall rang, not once, but twice. The other students in the yard began to thin. I did not notice, nor did I care. I was fairly certain Ash was the same. Occasionally I would stretch my arms or stand and walk. Sometimes I would check on the egg, though it needed no checking at all. At some point, the yard cleared completely. Only then did I set the egg on the grass. So that it may rest on what it would one day walk upon. We continued. Then the first success happened, and it happened so suddenly I almost missed it.

Ash was hovering over a pink flower. Around it were a dozen more pink flowers. Or what had been flowers before Ash's power had ended them. Ash took a deep breath and drew a line in the air with her index finger. Nothing happened. I almost turned away, but then-

The flower's pink faded. It was sucked into its edges, as if something was pulling it through a hole too small to see. The color that remained was far fainter than it had been. The flower stayed like this for ten seconds, before the color came back all at once, until the thing was exactly as it had been.

I stared at the flower for a few seconds. There was a smile on my face. I looked up at Ash, who was staring at her left hand. "That is good, Ash. Again."

She looked up at me and nodded, and then she tried again, over the same flower. It was the first time her power had worked, and left something behind to try again with. This time, the faded color held longer. It held for fifteen seconds. Then it returned. Another success, right after the first one. This was a good sign.

Ash was breathing hard. There was sweat on her temples. I did not think it came from her depleting her reserves. She had been fine until just now. Perhaps this was simply the toll of the concentration required to use her power precisely.

"Lys." Ash's voice was strained. She sat back on her heels. "We should stop. We've been here for hours."

"Are you done already, then?"

Ash looked at me. Her face was flushed and her hands were shaking. "You must be growing frustrated. Don't tell me you aren't."

"Not at all." I picked another flower and set it between us. "I will be here for as long as you are here. I should not need to say this." Ash stared at me. I could see the question forming. I answered it before she could ask. "And no, I am not in pain. Do not concern yourself."

This was a lie. Ash knew it was a lie, since she could feel the humming. She did not press it. Instead, she asked, "And what if I can't do this?"

"You can."

"How do you know that?"

"Because you are the Hero who killed me."

She was quiet for a moment. "You let me win, remember?" Ash said. "And it wasn't like I beat you. It was all of the Gods who were using me as a vessel."

So that was how Ash regarded herself then? As if I would have lost to someone who was merely a vessel. "Perhaps some part of that is true," I said. "Perhaps you were no more than the right vessel, and I let you win, as you said." I paused. "But it was you, and no other. I do not think it could have been any other, no matter how many centuries might have passed. I still do not understand how you curried the favor of so many of those fickle bastards. Only that you alone did it."

Color rose in her face. She looked away from me, at the grass, at her own hands. Then she knelt again and raised her bare hand. I sat across from her. I picked another flower.

The sun had fully set when it happened. The yard was empty. Lanterns along the stone pillars cast long pools of orange light across the grass.

Ash knelt over a single blade of grass. She raised her hand. Her index finger extended. She drew a line in the air above it.

The green drained from the blade of grass. The blade remained as it was, but the color simply left, and what remained was a thing that we could see through. It stood in the lantern light. A blade of grass that was itself in every way but one. How strange. I would have expected to see white or dark instead of this, though I could not have said why.

Ash stared at the blade. Her arm was still raised, her fingers were still extended. The colorless grass sat in the lantern light, whole and altered. The silence stretched. Somewhere past the stone pillars I could hear the faint sounds of the Academy.

I looked at the blade of grass. I looked at her. The smile came before I had allowed it. "Your performance is adequate," I said. Ash's head snapped up. Her expression was one even I could see plainly. It was pure disbelief. "I mean that in the way Sara would understand it," I said. I let the smile stay.

Ash paused a heartbeat, then she smiled, and it was bright. I was the one forced to look away.

Ash rose slowly. She looked around the yard. Then she looked at the sky. The stars were there. We had really been at it for some time. We had hardly moved from our spots, even to eat. Even as the bells for the mess hall had rung. It was strange, to find someone with a drive that matched my own. I did not mind it.

Ash looked back down at me. Something in her eyes glowed. I did not think it was a trick of the light. "Let's go see if I can do the real thing, then," she said.

The mess bell rang again, for the fourth time today. I pressed the egg back into the satchel and rose myself. My legs were faintly sore. I caught Ash's arm before she could take another step. "First, we eat. Then, perhaps we rest."

"Wait! Lys, I'm not hungry. We should go find them. I think I can do this if we do it now, while I still have a feel for it."

"You will have a feel for it tomorrow, and the day after, and every day after that," I said. "Unless you can subsist on air, you are as hungry as I am. We eat first."

Ash opened her mouth to argue, as Heroes always do when faced with good sense. I tightened my grip on her arm. She closed it. She let me pull her toward the mess hall. She laughed, and it was warm.

The sword still lay in the grass behind us, where I had set it that morning. Neither of us turned back. It was just a sword, after all.
