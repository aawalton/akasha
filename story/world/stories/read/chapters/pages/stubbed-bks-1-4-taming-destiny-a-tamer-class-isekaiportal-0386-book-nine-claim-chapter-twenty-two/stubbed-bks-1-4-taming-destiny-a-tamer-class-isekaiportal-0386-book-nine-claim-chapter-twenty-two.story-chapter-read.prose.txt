“Shields are down to the last bar,” warns Captain Tolly. He’s lashed himself with rope to the steering wheel to allow him to stay in control of the vessel’s direction even when the unpredictable gusts forced us to furl the sails entirely. “An’ water’s getting choppy.”

He didn’t need to tell me that. Though the roc may not be intending on creating massive waves, that’s the effect of its ongoing attacks. Gusts of wind that blow people overboard and tornados that touch down with surprising delicacy both serve to whip the sea into a frenzy. Several people have already been lost because they were too weak from motion-sickness to hold on when a gust hit.

And it’s just getting worse. Captain Tolly is trying to steer us through the biggest waves rather than taking them flat to the side, but that may not last. If we don’t somehow manage to calm the seas a little, we run the risk of the waves becoming unpredictable enough that Tolly won’t spot a big one coming before it hits us side-on. And that is likely to damage us, cause us to take on too much water, or even knock us over entirely.

In that situation, I’m not sure that all the Water Mages working in concert would be able to keep us afloat.

But I have an idea. It’s a risky one, but is it more dangerous than what we’re already experiencing?

I pull up one of the mirrors.

“Elarion, can you do anything about the waves?” I half-shout, the howl of the gale above us threatening to drown out my words.

“I can’t freeze them all,” he replies immediately, the lines on his face tight. As I glance over his way, I see him grow another bush-like wall of ice to deflect a gust as the last one shatters under the force of the wind. At least the one filling the hole in his ship is holding – the roc isn’t focusing on it directly.

“You don’t need to freeze them all,” I reply to Elarion as my mind races. “Just break up the surface cohesion of the waves – reduce their power and stop them from building up the way they are currently.”

Elarion’s eyes narrow thoughtfully, considering my idea.

“Ice can damage the ships even more than waves,” he points out, as if I don’t know that – it’s the very tactic we used to grind down the shields on Torrent’s fleet.

“Try to keep it away from the ships if you can – it’s the semi-open water that’s most vulnerable anyway. But we can’t use our physical shields above deck as it is – we might as well concentrate them onto the ship themselves and double their durability.”

Elarion nods after a moment more of thought.

“Warn the others. I’m starting now.”

He disappears from my view in the mirror, but I see a flat ice floe already beginning to form on the open water between his ship and mine.

I grab the other mirrors and start communicating with the other ships, using the time to get an update about how things are going for them in the battle.

The news is unanimous: badly. Everyone is harried and no one has yet found something that works against our assailant.

The Water Mages are struggling to control the waves at all, the Air Mages are overwhelmed by the roc’s control. Classers with no ranged attacks or magic are limited to whatever they can do on the ship. The best off are the non-elemental mages like Tiria who are managing to pierce the roc’s domain, but we have few of those.

Meanwhile, the lords are doing their best. Layton is trying to reduce the effect of the gusts on his ship by changing the temperature of the air – to some effect. Josiah is battling the roc for control of the wind, but the best he can do is something similar to Layton – lessening the effects on his crew.

Valence is using his family’s imbuement on a ballista to send up bolts that actually do some damage to the roc – one of the few to have actually obvious effects that linger. But since he’s unable to reposition himself due to his damaged steering system, he’s the most vulnerable to the vagaries of the water and wind.

On the other ships, Sor’az and Garous are much more limited in their capabilities – both are targeting the roc with the weapons they have available to them, but the magic of the damayar is apparently far less directly combat-capable than that of Moriaxar mages.

“Mana shield bar is blinking,” warns Captain Tolly, his raised voice grim. I curse.

Maxen, have everyone take cover.

Non-combatants, Classers or not, are already sheltering in the ship, but there are still lots of people out there who are vulnerable to the tornadoes that will soon be striking our deck. “And all of you take cover too,” I order, looking around at Sarran, Loran, and Tolly.

I shoot a look at Loran when it looks like he’s going to argue. He closes his mouth, dips his head and then hurries down the stairs. Sarran looks at me for a long moment, then nods too.

“I’ll do what I can from the doorway below,” he states, then turns and follows Loran.

That leaves only the captain.

“Tolly, the tornados are coming. You need to move too.”

He shakes his head firmly.

“I go, an’ this ship’s rudder will be useless. The wind’ll crash us into our allies and we’ll both go down. I ain’t goin’ anywhere.”

I curse under my breath but…he’s right. I’ve already seen how much losing their steering system has affected Valence.

“Then let’s see what we can do to tip the odds more in our favour,” I decide with determination.

Tolly sends me a startled look.

“My lord, you can’t mean to stay here with me?” he exclaims.

I nod shortly.

“Until we’ve found a sustainable solution,” I confirm a moment before a thought occurs.

Actually, Noir? I could use your help, I call.

Immediately, the black dragon appears behind me, emerging from my tattoo.

What is it? he asks eagerly, then I see as dismay goes through him at the destruction already visible on the deck. Destruction that rapidly worsens as the mana shield drops entirely and suddenly the gusts are no longer semi-directed after-effects of a wind limb but the limbs themselves.

Boxes and crates are blown around, and the masts themselves start to groan as the wind tentacles wrap around them and test their strength. Noir hunches down as the air currents grab at him.

No time to explain, I shoot at the alcaoris. Use your Stealth – hide Tolly with your wings.

Noir obeys enthusiastically, too much so, really – his wings wrap around Tolly entirely even as his tail and four paws anchor him to the quarterdeck itself.

“I need to see, blasted beast!” his muffled voice complains from inside the tent. Embarrassment comes down the link from Noir and he opens his wings a little so there’s a gap through which Tolly can peer.

I glance over the two critically. Hopefully Noir’s stealth will help against a Tier four beast, but I don’t see any other option right now.

The masts groan even louder – the roc is playing with them like a child with a new toy, testing to see just how far it can push them until they break. I need to do something.

Not trusting my wings in the tumultuous currents, I run towards the main mast, jump into the rigging, then climb like a monkey.

Halfway up the mast, I close my eyes and concentrate. Air-magic fills the space around me, more than I’ve ever seen before, and I almost despair at doing anything about it.

And then I realise that, higher up in the air, I'm more exposed to the roc’s domain, and I force away the sense of inevitable failure that grips me.

I concentrate on the main mast. That’s the most important. If we lose it, we’re scuppered until the carpenters can repair it, and if it’s destroyed too badly, even they might not be able to do anything.

The roc has both far more magic and far more control over it than I do – all I can do is try to use finesse, to be the sharp knife that slices into the behemoth.

I know it’s dangerous, but I don’t have much choice: I close my eyes and focus on sharpening my intentions into a blade and then coating that blade with my most tightly controlled air-magic.

My Willpower provides the strength; the air-magic creates the edge, and I begin slicing away at the tendrils wrapped around the mast. This isn’t like Kyrian’s water-serpent, which was a whole, single being. Instead, this feels like many fingers on a hand, if the hand was as long as an airplane and the fingers completely boneless and capable of winding like string.

For a moment, it seems to work. The mast stops creaking quite so loudly and the roc’s pull lessens.

And then the roc tightens its grip and double the tendrils appear from nowhere. I can’t cut through them fast enough and the mast is beginning to sway unnervingly.

Then I have an idea. The roc isn’t an elemental, but it’s gone so deeply into its air-magic that it’s only really a step away from one. Aingeal can be hurt by his opposing element. Perhaps this roc can be too. And what is wind’s opposite, but earth?

It’s only a theory, but I sense that it will work. Creating rock out of thin air is highly challenging and mana-intensive, but replicating rock that’s already been formed is easier.

Quickly, I pull out a mana potion, one that will give me a boost to my pool and then also to my regeneration, I knock it back and use magic to ensure I have sucked down every drop. Then, holding the empty glass vial to the mast, I send earth magic into it. Glass is only melted and cooled sand, after all, and well within the scope of my Master Earth-Shaping.

Like Elarion’s ice lumps that are having a noticeable effect on cutting the crests of the waves down to size, even if they also increase the choppiness, the glass grows from my hand in both directions and creates a small island of calm.

Satisfaction goes through me as I notice that the wind tendrils have far more difficulty finding purchase on the smooth surface of glass than they did on the rough wood of the mast. Hope goes through me – if this works….

A screech blasts from above, the sonorous boom of the sound made worse by the flattening gale that comes with it. My strength fails me and I’m torn away from the rigging, ropes tearing at me as I’m forced past them. If I didn’t have my armour, I’d definitely have rope burn. As it is, I only have the time to activate both my tattoo shield and my armour impact-absorber before I strike the deck even faster and harder than I expected.

I burst straight through the top deck and land heavily on the second, the planks groaning threateningly despite their magical reinforcement.

Shouts of surprise ring out around me – I’ve fallen into where the fighters from the earlier battle are now sheltering. Even at a glance, the numbers are noticeably reduced from what they were at the start. But I have no time to chat – just as I pick myself up and leap back out of the hole I created, I hear an ominous cracking sound.

The first thing that meets my eyes as I emerge is the main mast mostly obscured by a tornado. Evidently, the roc took my earlier attempts as an affront and concentrated its efforts on destroying what I was trying to protect.

I can do nothing but watch helplessly as the mast cracks and then falls.

And then I see which way it’s falling.

Noir! Watch out!

The little dragon doesn't move. Instead, though the fear I can feel over the bond indicates he’s well aware of the danger, he’s struggling against something, biting and clawing at it.

And then it hits me – Captain Tolly is tied to the steering wheel!

The main mast catches briefly on the mizzenmast, but that won’t last long.

I run towards them, pouring all the speed into my legs that I can.

But I’m just not fast enough.

The mast crashes down, a billow of sail that’s broken loose from its bindings blocking my vision.

My heart in my mouth, I cling to the Bond thread that leads to Noir, using it as proof that he’s still alive.

A moment later. I see him soaring on the air currents as he rounds the bow of the ship.

He’s alive. But he’s not safe. Even now, I see a tornado reach towards him, the roc eager to munch another tasty morsel.

Giving no thought to my own safety, my wings sprout from my back and I throw myself off the ship.
