I hid my reaction as best I could to Dain’s poorly concealed disappointment. Too bad. He was going to have to live with only the reactions of my companions after fishing that hard.

“The Vereden?” I heard Walter mutter in confusion.

“What, like the planet?” Renauld finally spoke up, furrowing his brow. “Did the Netherim name it after the damn planet?”

I eyed the suddenly renewed Korvokhan as questions from my people began to pour out of every mouth inclined to it. Particularly those who had ventured back and forth through the cleansing field. I didn’t blame them for becoming just a tad more comfortable after the seemingly warm welcome we had gotten here in the Aorta. These people had been fighting tooth and nail for…God, it had to have been nearly a day now, didn’t it? There had been the fight through the streets of Karag Varla, then, almost immediately, a move into Karag Korvok, the grueling battle to reach the lake, right on through the towers, and into the depths of the Heartspire itself.

It was entirely understandable that their guard might have fallen, just a bit. The Inner Heart had been only a little bit horrific, especially in comparison to the charnel house that the rest of the Hold was.

Still, it was gratifying that I wasn’t the only one who hadn’t entirely fallen for Dain’s seemingly genuine enthusiasm to meet us. Or rather, me.

Bleddyn was lurking on the outside of the huddle, like a ravenous wolf denied his kill. Lurking, planning, and eyeing the vulnerable throat of Dain. Meanwhile, Meia had faded into the crowd with her hood drawn up, and only through all the workings we’d done together could I feel her Mystical senses carefully probing everything within range.

Good.

They were holding back, though I could tell they weren’t terribly pleased by it. Neither was, in truth. But I was almost always inclined to find the path of least resistance to our goals. I was certainly willing to descend into a bloodthirsty rage and slaughter our way into the depths of this place, both in search of all our captives and to free Nehushtan…

But there was suddenly information I needed, a true need that thundered through my veins with every passing second. My Core lattice hadn’t stopped nearly gibbering in excitement at even the idea of a possibly functional Netherim spacecraft since we’d laid eyes on it.

I would learn more about the ‘Vereden’ if it killed me.

Because that ship was going to be mine if it was the last thing I fucking did.

And so, I voiced a suspicion that had grown over time, in the back of my mind, since I had learned about the Netherim. One of many, many possibilities that had kept me up at night.

“They didn’t name the ship after the planet, did they?” I asked shrewdly, my voice pitched to cut right through the chatter. More than one set of eyes turned to me with confusion, but…

I noticed that Dain wasn’t one of them. Instead, the Korvokhan lit up with very obvious excitement. Near glee, really. Still, he retained enough peace of mind to restrain himself, doing a poor job of giving me a mysterious smile.

“All will be answered in good time, Nathan,” Dain gestured down the hallway. “Please, let us continue. Once we reach our destination, there’s someone who can answer your questions far better than I can. In fact…”

I didn’t like the suddenly malicious tint that crept into his grin.

It was far too evocative of the Korvok I knew he must be at heart.

“He’ll need to.”

…………………………..

For as ominous as that was, I was still grateful to get all of the blood off of myself. I was actually enormously tempted to study the cleansing field that had done so. I’d seen similar things during my Abjuration education, as I fully expected that had been a form of modified bounded Ward. Only, I had never actually seen one so effective as to dry clean your clothes as well.

If I could manage it, I was going to steal that before I left.

With my fucking spaceship.

It was with a decidedly far more pleasant-smelling complement of warriors and soldiers at my back that we finally arrived at our destination. Dain had led us further down the central trunk of the intersection where we had been cleaned so thoroughly. Unlike the main connection to the viewing room where I’d met this oddly cheerful, skinned madman, the rest of the hallway had been nearly an uninterrupted straight shot down the length.

No curious Immaculata heads poking out of doorways here.

I was somehow, yet, at the same time, not surprised that it terminated in a way that was equally as familiar to me. Just like in the Netherim bunker, the end of this hall was dominated by a massive steel vault door, easily twenty feet in height and double that in width. Only that door had been large and thick for a very specific purpose.

Was I about to find another bound star of mingled Ki and Mana on the other side of it, in the form of an Aetheric Fusion Collider?

God, I hoped not. If another one of these things went critical, I’m not sure if anyone would be able to get clear in time. Both of the mountains holding Karag Korvok and Varla put together weren’t even as large as Mt. Gorenzen had been.

And the last one had atomized the entire mountain and its inland sea basin.

Dain smiled at me mysteriously before he approached an innocuous marble plinth off to the side, bearing a single, angled face on it. He laid a broad, maroon hand upon its surface, and I felt a sudden sense of vertigo. Not because his hand was suddenly outlined in a faint golden light.

Because he started speaking in haiku.

“Leave the stars to wait.

Guard the seed until it blooms.

Then open the gate.”

What the Hell. Was there something in Vereden’s water that encouraged this kind of thing? I swear, poetry cropped up more often in otherwise entirely practical situations than I would have thought.

An odd chirping noise sounded from nowhere in particular that I could tell on the plinth. A grinding noise, accompanied by the sudden clanking tension of unseen chains, echoed out of the walls on either side of the steel gate before us. As they began to grind open, Dain turned back to us, or me in particular. “We have waited so, so long for this moment, Nathaniel Hart. Welcome, travelers…to the Throne of War.”

A ray of bright, burning, brilliant golden light suddenly shone through the crack between the parting vault doors. It outshone the already bright Mana lights of the ivory hall we’d been led down to such an extent that I instinctively raised my hand to shade my eyes. As I squinted in the sudden illumination, I was startled to feel an actual warmth on my skin. An incredibly familiar one, that anyone who had ever once walked the surface of a world would recognize at once.

It was akin to the warmth of the sun. A heat I had not felt in months upon months, not since the coming of the Skyfall. Cries of disbelief echoed out around me, from countless throats. For a single, solitary moment, I dared to hope. To dream that somehow, in some impossible way, the pall that had fallen over our entire planet had somehow been banished in a manner beyond our control. That someone had saved us while we were slaughtering mad Dwarves, deep beneath the mountain.

I pushed forward as the doors opened, aware that I wasn’t the only one, all of us praying to see an almost forgotten sight.

But no.

What I saw through those doors was not the star of Tarus.

It was a throne. A golden one, in a very literal sense, shining brilliantly enough to mimic the rays of the sun, emanating a heat powerful enough to distort the air around it. I had never seen Mystical gold infused with so much pure Aether, and yet still retaining a solid shape. There was some kind of shield around it, preventing even more of the warmth from reaching us, completely encompassing it in a dome only visible in a barely visible shimmer.

The throne itself was massive, easily larger than anyone, of any race, could comfortably sit in. The backrest alone must have been double the height of even my full transformed state, nearly as tall as the steel vault doors I was stepping through, even now. That wasn’t even counting the seat, nearly half again as wide and deep as the entire edifice was tall. The entire thing was large enough that I had trouble imagining what kind of giant it must have been created for. I had seen rooms that were smaller than this thing.

It sat in the center of a massive, half-spherical room that immediately reminded me of the same core where the Aetheric Fusion Reactor had been housed. And yet, there were differences. The walls were not bare, instead bearing massive silken tapestries that depicted what seemed to be scenes of battle. War, bloody in blade and brutality. In each of them, in one form or another, a massive, a giant, stocky figure clad in golden armor with a wild mane of oddly familiar, crimson red hair both on his face and his chin, strode across bloody fields. Always, his battles seemed to end in one form of victory or another.

Either with his foes kneeling in supplication before him, or with his axe buried in their skull. Or, in one particularly callous case…

I grimaced and looked away from that tapestry.

So.

This must be the story of Yorgun. That’s who the central figure of the tapestries must tell of.

I shouldn’t be surprised. From everything I had learned about the Dwarven God of War, what he and his conspirators had undergone to ascend as they did…no monstrosity was beyond those half-mad creatures.

As I stepped further into the massive room, furtive near the throne briefly caught my attention. It was only brief, and I only caught the slightest of glimpses, but it was enough to draw my eyes to the rest of the room. Behind the throne where I had caught movement, and across from the entrance, there was a large, wall-spanning void. It almost seemed like an exit of some kind, wreathed in impenetrable darkness, the start of it seeming to be a ramp, stretching off into the gloom.

And off to the side, on the right side of the room, there…seemed to be another vault door, not unlike the one we had just stepped through. Another entrance, perhaps? I had no idea, because I saw the movement once more. To my surprise, it was coming from somewhere on the throne itself.

What I had mistaken for a tall, decorative rod on the right armrest seemed to be something else. I…think it was a staff of some kind, and I was both startled and not to see that seemed to be a solid length of Primordium, glimmering with its own light. And it seemed like the movement might be coming from either it…

Or something on it.

I was only able to see what it was when I drew closer, the heat leaking through the shield increasing with every step. It never quite became uncomfortable, but it was close, not unlike the heat of a roaring campfire.

My breath caught in my throat at what I saw.

There was a singular snake on the staff, twined around the haft of the Primordium staff. But unlike the coruscating ivory of the stave, the serpent itself looked to be forged of simple brass. And I do mean forged, because this was not an organic snake. The sight of the creature was almost evocative of the Sculpted, as if someone had created a facsimile of a snake out of bronze and gifted it life. The thing wasn’t even that large, merely a foot or so in length like a true, real serpent. It writhed sluggishly up upon the surface of the staff, in a manner almost like it was suffering an old, familiar pain. Something it still felt, flinching away from, and yet…oddly defiant in the face of.

I was startled when the movement of the snake suddenly stopped, and the small triangular head lifted from the stave and turned to face me directly.

The eyes opened, and even through the shield, I was startled to realize…

I knew that amber gaze.

Bronze-scaled lips parted, and a familiar voice echoed from the serpent. “It took you long enough, failure. Did you perhaps take a wrong turn somewhere?”

My lips parted in realization.

Nehushtan.
