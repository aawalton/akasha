Chapter II.XXXVIII (2.38)

The slowed time allowed her to drift down to the bed of the gorge a fourth slower than she normally would fall. It was kind of odd how the time slowing worked. There were some things like her muscle movement that responded instantly, just like usual. But other things, like walking, required her to put extra effort into stomping down to propel herself at a normal speed because she couldn’t count on gravity’s pull the same way as outside the spell’s effects. It would certainly take some practice to get used to.

The troll forgot about the mound of mud he had been crafting as he turned to snarl in her direction.

“Toll!” he bellowed. “Pay the toll!”

“I’ve got your toll right here.” Mitsuko made a fist and launched herself at the Troll with Deteriorated speed.

Mitsuko was far from a brawler. She prided herself in her swordplay and her ring meant that disarming her was near impossible. But that didn’t mean she was clueless about how to throw a solid punch. After all, she hadn’t always had the artifact ring on her. Unfortunately, she lacked the height to get a satisfying impact with the troll’s jaw and she settled for his upper stomach. Even that was above her head.

The loose skin rippled as she connected with the mad beast. As she felt his coarse skin under her knuckles, she cast Retrospection on him, forcing his memories backwards.

The troll stopped howling mid roar and he stared at the raised fist he had been posed to strike her with. His confusion only lasted a second. Then he spotted Mitsuko and his rage returned like he’d never lost it.

“Pay the—”

Mitsuko punched him again. And, again, the Retrospection spell made him lose focus.

This went on for an hour. The troll’s belly was covered in brown welts from Mitsuko’s repeated strikes and she had to Mend her own fists to keep her knuckles from bruising. She probably could have just snuck around his back and left her palm on him and cast repeatedly that way. There was a few second cooldown on each cast of Retrospection so this was functionally the same thing but it let her take out some pent up aggression.

Every time she struck him, his mind went back just a little further.

“How long are you planning to continue this?” Verna called down to her.

“As. Long. As. It. Takes.” Each word was punctuated by another punch followed by a cast of Retrospection.

“Any idea how far back you’ve sent his mind so far?” Verna asked. “I imagine you’ll need centuries at the very least.”

Mitsuko punched again. Then side-stepped out of the troll’s line of sight. “I have no idea. Probably should have tested out the spell’s capabilities previous to throwing myself down here.”

“Well, keep at it. If nothing else, it is rather entertaining. I’m enjoying the scenery over here. There’s so much to look at! You have no idea what a lucky little shit you are to exist in a body that doesn’t need constant cooling enchantments to explore the world.”

With a grunt of acknowledgement, Mitsuko resumed her barrage on the troll’s flabby belly.

She tested out several other spells during her assault on the troll. Both Deteriorate and Dilate helped her land blows on her opponent and sometimes she even let the troll fight back for a half a minute to test out different techniques.

Occasionally, Mitsuko took intermittent breaks to quickly get a few words of chat in with Verna. It helped break up the monotonous chore. Punching someone repeatedly lost its charm after a few hours.

“Ugh, t-toll,” the troll groaned. He clutched at his stomach.

In preparation for a strike from him, Mitsuko readied herself to throw another punch to send his memory back once again. At this point, she was well familiar with his standard movements and fighting techniques. She knew how he’d react to her, as she’d seen it hundreds of times over.

However, the strike never came. At least, not in the way she assumed.

The troll teetered on his feet. Then his body collapsed.

Mitsuko cast Dilate on herself then rolled out of the way, barely dodging the full weight of the troll’s bulk.

Only once he collapsed into the mud did Mitsuko realize her mistake. She’d been Mending her own body while in combat but never even considered lending that courtesy to her foe.

“Wow. Not many people can claim to have slain a troll with their bare hands,” Verna commented from above. “I’d clap but well, you know, stubby little legs right now.”

Mitsuko attempted to Mend the troll. His bruises faded and the displaced flabs of skin righted themselves. She pushed his body to return to a healthy state. Or, as healthy as he had been before her arrival, at the very least. But for all her magic, she couldn’t bring back the dead.

“Fuuuck.” Mitsuko fell back on her ass in the mud and stared up at the barrier overhead. Her repeated punches must have ruptured a lung and caused internal bleeding to fill it up. A young troll would heal up any puncture or damage within seconds. This was not, however, a young troll. “I’m such an idiot.”

“At least you got to take out some of your frustrations,” Verna offered. “A consolation prize.”

“That’s not helpful. I need a new target to take out the frustrations caused by killing my first target. And I still don’t even know how far back I managed to push his mind.”

As per usual, the dome overhead remained stagnant. No change. Ever oppressive sunbeams gave the dome a prismatic gleam.

Mitsuko sighed and pulled herself up from the mud. She felt so tired. Physically, she was as good as ever. Mentally though, she felt taxed. She recalled what Holly and Sterling had said about the importance of rest for the mind. “I suppose I’ll go check into an inn next. Maybe take a nap.”

She climbed her way out of the gulch, Mended her clothing to remove the filth, snatched up Verna from where the sage hid from the sun under a large leaf, and made her way back into the city.

“Oh, stop for a second!”

Mitsuko froze at Verna’s demand. Shifting into a battle ready stance, she scanned the purple foliage, seeking out an unnoticed foe.

“I knew it! The snails here are purple!”

“What?” Mitsuko’s stance relaxed. She glanced down at a nearby leaf and spotted what had snagged Verna’s attention. A snail the size of her thumb was nibbling on a large leaf. It had left behind a trail of purple slime, streaking the leaf’s surface.

“My uncle once told me that the Prismatic Archipelago's creatures were native to the islands. He insisted that all invasive species, snails included, were fundamentally different from the make up of island native creatures. I argued that in time they would adapt and take on characteristics of their environment. It would seem that I have emerged from this argument vindicated, as this species did not exist during my age.”

“Who is your uncle?” Mitsuko asked, a bit lost.

“A wizard from Amber Island. He was my father’s sister’s husband. Occasionally he made the journey out to Porcelain to visit with me. He’d always bring me gifts as well. Actually, he was a big reason why I’m literate to begin with. Without his influence, I’d likely have died a lonely savage.”

“So you won an argument from thousands of years ago?” Mitsuko sighed. “Is this uncle still alive? Did he discover immortality?”

“No, he died when I was first growing Porcelain Island into a kingdom. It involved a conflict between wizards about relations with Azure Island. He was eaten while attempting to tame a serpent.”

“I need to make some normal friends,” Mitsuko muttered as she left the snail behind.
