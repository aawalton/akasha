Pulled out of my investigation of the earth by Lathani's cry for help, I flash a glance at Happy, Hunter, and Fenrir.

“You guys stay put. I’ll go and help Lathani,” I order them tensely. Hunter nods absently, already focusing too much on setting up some sort of catapult to pay much attention to the rest of us. Her expression tight, Happy lifts her chin in acknowledgement, while Fenrir hisses and deflates from where he was preparing to run with me. I'm glad to see that Happy has already pulled out several weapons and is tipping the vial I gave her into one of them. That's the best thing they can do for the fight right now.

Taking off, I run around the perimeter of the battle, not daring to risk travelling through the area controlled by the plant – not yet. By pulsing Inspect, I get a good idea of what’s happening on the other side of the monster and it’s not great.

Bastet and Ninja are fighting to get through to Lathani, but the dangerously whipping thorny vines are preventing them from getting anywhere fast.

I don’t doubt that they would get through eventually – Ninja's air-enhanced claws are cutting through the thinner vines, and Bastet's combination of Air-Shaping and Lightning Claw is making steady progress through even the thicker vines.

However, their progress isn’t fast enough: Lathani is being steadily pulled straight towards one of the biggest tubular flowers. If that isn’t a mouth of some sort, I’ll eat my hat.

Lathani herself is doing everything she can to get free – ripping at the vines with her teeth and claws, uncaring of the way their thorns are sinking into her flesh. As I round the corner of the plant, I can visibly see her efforts as her form flickers between completely visible and shadow. However, whether it's the size of the shadow or the plant's domain, she’s not succeeding in freeing herself. And even with the progress the other two are making, they won’t reach her before she’s consumed.

It’s immensely annoying that the plant seems to be largely fire-resistant. Bastet’s Fire Strike and Fire Aura are having little effect; Aingeal is also struggling.

As so often, it comes down to me to save Lathani. And while I have one option that I’m almost certain will work, it might take me out of the battle entirely for a while. Frustratingly, though, the skills I've honed since we boarded the ship will serve little use against a mobile plant in a forest. Or will they?

Thinking quickly, I drag a stone up from below the soil I'm standing on, heating it as it rises. By the time it bursts from the ground it's already glowing red. I use Lava-Shaping to enhance it further, then Air-Shaping to lift it and throw it like a basketball.

Thankfully, Air-Shaping makes up for my naturally poor aiming skills and the ball of fire flies unerringly for the tubular flower that is about to engulf Lathani whole.

It narrowly misses my companion by a hair and sinks straight into the centre of the flower.

The creature writhes. If it were capable of making sound, I strongly suspect its screams would be ear-piercing. As it is, the thorny vines whip wildly while uncontrolled root spears stab up from the ground in unpredictable patterns.

Get clear! I call in warning even as I throw myself backwards and engage my tattoo shield to avoid being torn to pieces. The flower I hit withers and crumples into itself, utterly destroyed  – but that doesn't seem to deter the plant monster. It simply diverts Lathani towards another nearby flower almost as large.

I curse. Every attack we've levied so far has had little overall effect. Even Lava-Shaping, though enough to cause damage, is clearly not sufficient to take it out immediately. It looks like I have no other option but to use Bond Teleport, regardless of how mana-heavy it is and how much it will limit my impact on the rest of the fight.

I'm teleporting you, I warn Lathani, and then do just that, pouring in the mana and concentration required. As expected, it’s a heavy cost, but worth it as Lathani flickers out of existence within the tendrils' grip and reappears at my side. At least it only took half my mana pool. I guess that's what practice gets me.

The flower monster is clearly far less pleased. Its vines briefly search the area as if expecting Lathani to have merely slipped its grip, but when it realises its prey has vanished entirely, it flies into another rage, vines whipping wildly, its tubular flowers expanding and contracting with rhythmic pulses.

Ninja and Bastet are forced to jump back again, losing all the ground they'd gained.

We need to get to its centre, I say with frustration over the bond network. Anything we do to the outer vines or roots has limited effect.

Aingeal sends me a pulse of determination. I can see he's continuing to bore his way through, accompanied by his clone. Fortunately, the flower can do little more than batter a fire elemental, causing more damage to its own vines than to him as they occasionally swing through his flames. Even so, too many passing through his form at once would severely weaken him, and I urge him to be careful while pressing on.

My weapons are ready, Happy tells me with grim satisfaction. Shall I start throwing them, or will one of you come and get them?

I consider quickly.

Bastet, can you take them, fly above the flower, and drop them into its centre?

I could do it myself, and perhaps more accurately given I have hands rather than paws, but I feel I'd be better used continuing to attack through the earth. Bastet can at least fly, which Lathani and Ninja cannot.

I will, Bastet replies. I watch her large form leap into the air and arc over the flower, testing the range of its vines as she goes.

I don't feel so good, Ninja murmurs over the Bond network, starting to sway where she’s renewed her attack on the plant.

Are those pink nundas? Lathani questions, taking a single step toward the plant and stumbling over her own paws.

I suddenly realise that the world is starting to swim before my own eyes as well.

I curse weakly as I realise what's happened.

Everyone who can, back away or put a bubble of air around your head! It's released an attack.

Following my own advice, I create a bubble of air around my head and send a pulse of Flesh-Shaping through my body. Finding the source of the poison in my lungs – sneaky tiny particles of pollen that have adhered themselves to the membranes inside and that are starting to sink into my system – I expel it, coughing out faintly yellow-tinged air that I force out of the air bubble.

Bastet, do you need help? I call even as I pull Lathani into my spatial tattoo. The poison isn’t deadly, just disorientating – she’ll be fine there until I have a chance to clear it out of her. Simultaneously, I grow my air-wings, jetting through the air to rescue Ninja.

I barely reach her before a vine wraps around her – instead it grasps at thin air.

I’m fine for now, Bastet tells me as I soar up into the air, avoiding the thorny vines that whip at me. My companion is flying towards me with two of Happy’s metal weapons grasped in her front talons. It is not strong enough to overcome my system. The difference between Tier twos and a Tier three, I have to assume.

Good, I tell her in relief, deciding to take advantage of the height while I’m up here. I begin pouring mana into a ball of bright orange lava.

Even as Bastet continues her own aerial assault with the poison-loaded spikes Happy has prepared, the bombs from Hunter flying through the air with regularity to strike at various parts of the flower, I release my lava ball over the section where Aingeal has been boring his hole. The fire elemental sends me a pulse of gratitude as my ball of molten rock sinks straight through the centre of the flower monster, making his job easier.

The creature writhes again in fury, reaching fruitlessly first for us in the air, and then for those on the ground. We’re out of its range. But this is still slow-going, and gives the monster far too much opportunity to change its tactics – or to break through the stone dome and hurt those within, if that hasn't happened already. The key to this fight will be destroying it from below as well as from above.

To that end, I descend once more to the earth to join Fenrir and the two Pathwalkers.

Sinking my mind back into the earth, I feel a moment of satisfaction. The effect we're having on the monster is clear. Its roots are no longer free to strike at us; instead, they're drawing deeply on the soil's Energy, the only way the plant is able to continue fighting despite the damage Aingeal and I have already inflicted. I can see where the two balls of lava sank in, and how desperately it's trying to repair itself.

Aingeal is doing an excellent job sustaining the damage, but he's not quite strong enough to overcome a Tier 3's natural regeneration when it has no specific weakness to fire. I begin building up earth-aligned mana in the ground below, packing it in as thickly as I can.

I'm vaguely aware of the progress my companions are making above ground, and I can see the effects of the rune bombs and herbicide in the sections of flower monster that abruptly wither or are destroyed outright. Equally, I can see exactly where the flower’s priorities lie based on whether it chooses to repair an area or not.

Then I see a cluster of roots snaking deliberately toward where my companions are on the ground.

Watch out! I warn over the Bond network, just as they burst from the earth. I feel a brief spike of panic from Fenrir, Hunter, and Happy. The roots begin withdrawing, their movement followed by a disturbance to the earth that looks like something is being dragged through it.

A moment later, before I have time to reach out and use the earth to cut the roots off, the entire area ahead of them explodes, roots and earth blown to smithereens. I'm more than a little impressed by the scale of it. Did Happy combine multiple runes into one massive bomb, or simply throw everything she had at once? I file the question away for later.

For now, my trap is almost complete. I pack in the last of the mana and release it, sending a forest of earth spears exploding upward through the centre the flower monster has been protecting and regenerating with so much assiduity. However, it cannot resist against the might of the earth, and its most protected area is torn to shreds.

Amazingly, the creature doesn't die immediately. Although it's been cut off from most of its root network and its area of deepest concentration is in fragments, its vines continue to writhe and threaten.

However, with its centre gone it has lost most of its fire resistance. As I pull my consciousness back from the earth, I can see that Bastet's passive Fire Aura is having far greater effect than before, and Aingeal is creating absolute havoc at the centre – every movement of his and his clone's body leaving blackened, withered vegetation in its wake.

Use your Fire Strike, I urge Bastet, and she wordlessly adapts her strategy. Her Fire Strike is indeed effective, as is the Lightning Claw she uses to blast the areas just beyond her reach.

The plant monster is dead. It just hasn't realised it yet.

Satisfied that they have it in hand, I pull Ninja and then Lathani out of storage and begin treating them.

By the time I'm finished, the battle is over, all sections of the plant monster still on the ground.

I eye the scene of destruction with misgiving. I hope that was the only one of those, because that was not easy and I shudder to think what might happen if one of our normal patrols happened to come across one.
