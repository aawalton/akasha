As they sat around the campfire, cooking the deer Rex had dug up, it occurred to him that he had killed multiple men now, and he felt nothing. It made him wonder if something was wrong with him, or perhaps it was just a side effect of growing up on Dorog.

It was a harsh world. Even if he hadn't witnessed the Gate opening and how much death followed, he had seen plenty of other things. Bluelung, people getting sick well before they should, not to mention, collapsing mine shafts were all too common.

Compared to all of that, he couldn’t find any empathy for killing a few thugs who were likely slavers. If anything, they deserved it. Far more than anyone on Dorog did, even Lavrian.

Maybe I’m broken, maybe I’m not. Either way, my lack of empathy is probably good for this line of work.

As he considered his thoughts, he looked up at Ernie, who was sheepishly picking at the poorly barbecued meat. He liked the boy, but just couldn't see how he fit this adventuring life. He was too meek.

“Ernie, I don’t mean for this to sound rude, but…” Rex said, searching for his words. “How did you manage to kill the guardian?”

“Oh,” Ernie looked up, fixing his glasses. “It’s fine. I get it. I get scared. A lot. It’s true, what you’re probably thinking. I didn’t want any of this.” He gestured about and sniffled.

“So, then, why?”

“Well, it’s kind of been my fate ever since I was born. My father is actually a minor lord. He rules a keep on a poor world. I’m also his firstborn, and since lords have to be Classed, I had tutors. They taught me a bunch of stuff while trying to figure out what I was good at, so that I’d be prepared for the guardian.”

“So, you’re secretly really skilled at something?” Rex poked.

“Kind of. It’s embarrassing, really. Back home, they call me the lute genius,” Ernie grimaced, like he hated saying it. “I’m decent. Not that special, though. Anyway, I guess I was good enough to impress the Shrine. The guardian that spawned really wasn’t too strong.”

“Wait, so the more impressive your skills are, the weaker the guardian? I thought it only made a difference with multiple skills?”

“That’s correct,” Ernie nodded. “It’s pretty common for noble families to spend huge sums for a chorus of tutors to train their kids. Especially the richer ones. If anything, mine were kind of basic.”

“Interesting,” Rex nodded alone, and Andy joined him, similarly in the dark about the finer details of the Shrine and leveling. “So, how weak was this guardian?”

“Pretty weak,” Ernie grinned. “It was some kind of humanoid lizard, not much bigger than a child. It didn’t seem to have any special skills, either. And so, even though the Skills I got are not really direct combat skills, I managed to get through it. I had my rapier that they took from me. Between that, my Attributes, and the sword lessons I was given growing up, it was pretty easy.”

“So, you’re like, rich?” Andy asked. “Was your sword an artifact?”

“No,” Ernie shook his head. “Well, Dad’s kind of rich, I suppose. But we wanted me to… man up. That’s what he said. Can’t have a coward inheriting his title.”

“So he sent you out alone?”

Ernie nodded. “Could have easily put me in a party with his own men and sent me into a low-level hunting ground to get easy levels. But that wouldn’t toughen me up, would it? So, he threw me through a portal and told me not to return until I’m a Journeyman.”

A Journeyman was the next major threshold after Freedman, which was gained at level 15, and provided another Attribute boost.

“Damn. Sorry about that. But if it makes you feel any better, your experience against the guardian sounds much more enjoyable than mine was.”

“Really? What did you face?”

Rex described his encounter with the meat centaur in detail.

“And you survived?”

“Wow, no wonder you’re a badass,” Andy said.

“Yeah, well. It wasn’t ideal, and I almost died. But I suppose it was good training. Anyway, what’s all this about lute genius?”

“Oh, that. It’s silly. I’m really just decent with a lute. I’d show you, but they took it from me when they took my sword and threw me in the cell,” he sighed.

“Wait, Ernie. You said your Skills don’t help in combat? Are you saying the Shrine blessed you with a musical Class?” Andy asked with a bewildered look.

“Yep. A Minstrel Archetype, to be precise. It’s a bummer, though. Both my Skills require an instrument. I’m kind of useless without one, really.”

“So, it’s decided then. We need to get our Minstrel an instrument,” Rex declared. “And get my artifacts back, of course.”

“Oh, no, no! It’s really okay,” Ernie said. “I can live without it. Actually, I think I’m doing quite fine.”

“No way! We can’t have our Minstrel denied an instrument!”

“I mean, I dunno about that,” Ernie said, pulling on his collar. “I mean, haha. If it’s safe. We certainly don’t have to go doing anything dangerous for one.”

“Nonsense! We’re a party now, aren’t we?”

“Yeah, that’s right!” Andy cheered. “A party!”

“Oh, oh no.”

“To getting Ernie a lute!” Rex said, raising his canteen, which Andy gleefully cheered with his own, and both of their gazes turned to Ernie.

“Huh? Me?”

They both nodded.

“Oh, I suppose so,” Ernie grabbed his canteen and met theirs.

“Hoorah!”

“What happened to not making noise?” Ernie hissed.

“Oh, sorry. Rex got me excited.”

“Eh, I think we’re fine,” Rex said. “With any luck, they’ll be busy with the fire for a bit. Besides, I haven’t sensed anything for ages. We should celebrate by joining an actual party, though. Mine, since I already have one. And I’m the leader.”

“You have a party?” Andy asked.

“Yeah, with Slimer over there,” Rex pointed to the tiny slime dissolving bones and offcuts.

“Hmm,” Ernie furrowed. “I have heard of higher-level monsters joining parties before. But usually intelligent ones that have undergone multiple evolutions. But a lowly slime?”

“He’s actually level—wait! Level 13? When did you…” Rex gritted his teeth and tried to hide his jealousy. “I suppose you did kill all of Jango’s men.”

“Huh?” The two boys looked at him, confused.

“Apparently, Slimer here is level 13.”

“Level 13?” Ernie blinked. “That’s a high level for a slime. They’re rare, but normally very weak. The books I read said they are normally found around levels 1 to 3. Because of their rarity and their value in alchemy, most lords strictly prohibit their hunting within their lands.”

“How in the hells did Slimer reach level 13 then?”

“No idea. But if slimes spawn in this forest, there should be people around. And not just people, lords. Either that, or we’re a long way away from anything and anyone, or there’s something else going on.”

Something else was clearly going on. They just weren’t sure whether or not Slimer was related to it.

The two boys joined the party, confirming that they were both level 1, to nobody’s surprise.

“Wait, you’re level 6 already, and you were whining about Slimer?” Andy said.

“Oh, yeah. I forgot to mention. I’ve been busy.”

“I’ll say.”

“Hey Ernie,” Rex said. “You should know this. Do monsters have thresholds like we do?”

“Yes, except that instead of an Attribute threshold, they evolve.”

“Are you saying that if Slimer gets two more levels, he’ll have a Journeyman-tier evolution?”

“He should do, yes,” Ernie adjusted his glasses.

Rex looked at his slime friend a little differently. According to the books, Journeymen were what most considered proper adventurers. It was the point at which people started taking on genuinely daring and deadly contracts.

Slimer seemed to notice, turning slightly and wiggling at him.

“At least he’s on our side,” said Andy.

Damn that cute little bastard. Based on level, he is closer to being a real adventurer than I am.

“In light of this new, ahem, information,” Ernie said. “What are we going to do?

“Whaddya mean?” Andy said.

“He’s talking about the farm. Since Slimer is here, there’s probably a slime spawn nearby. And the distinct lack of civilization, or lord, likely means we’re a long way from anything.”

“Oh, right,” Andy nodded.

“We don’t have a choice. We need to deal with the farm. That means taking out Mulvaney and his men. But there’s one problem,” Rex said. “Slimer is weakened. Look at him. He’s tiny. And I don’t want to kill him, but his fire attack, whatever it is, is too powerful to go into battle without it. At least when we’re facing off against multiple guys higher level than ourselves.”

“You’re right about that,” Andy said.

“So, he needs to get bigger. I can’t go blowing him up like that. Which means, we need to find him more things to eat.”

“Can’t argue with that,” Andy agreed. “That thing blasted Jango and his men away. I could hardly believe it at the time.”

“Y-yeah,” Ernie nodded. “I think you’re right.”

“So, I’ve been thinking, we need to do some monster hunting.”

“What about Credo?”

“Yeah, I’ve been thinking about that, too. Have either of you seen any channelers around the farm? I didn't think so. I have a feeling that they can't create Gates, even if they wanted to. Not until Credo opens another one.”

“We don’t know when that’s going to happen, though.”

“You’re right, we don’t. But those things are expensive. We’ve only been here a few days. Honestly, I’d be surprised if they open a Gate even once a week. It’s likely less often than that. Otherwise, how would they make any coin?”

“That makes a lot of sense,” Ernie said.

“You think so?”

“Alright, so, we have time. Let’s use it smartly. Take out a few monsters. Help Slimer recover. Maybe even get you two a few levels. Then, we go kill those fuckers.”

“Now, that’s a plan,” Andy said, raising a fist, which Rex met with his own, and then they turned their gazes on Ernie.

“Oh, okay,” Ernie said, joining the three-way fist-bump.

It was time to go looking for monsters. But first, they needed rest. And they set off very early the next morning. After a couple of hours of walking, they spotted a clearing.

Rex raised his hand and stepped forward to get a better look. By now, it was obvious that he was far superior at hiding his aura. And he explained as such.

All Gate worlds were originally inhabited by various monsters and dotted by spawns. It wasn’t until Classed moved in, secured the Gates, and started building that civilization rolled in. And so, most clearings and other signs of activity outside of civilization suggested monster inhabitation.

As he got a bit closer, he spotted rough leathers stretched out and used as makeshift shelters. He could already sense multiple weak auras and, only moments later, spotted a few gnolls.

He kept watch for a while, trying to get an accurate count of them, and then headed back to Ernie and Andy.

“So, we’re in luck. Monster camp, and they’re gnolls. Downside, I counted seven.”

“Seven? Think we can take them?” Andy said.

“Gnolls? And you’ve already come across goblins and a slime. This forest is obviously a low-level one,” Ernie said. “If a slime spawn wasn’t strange enough, this certainly is. Low-level hunting grounds are usually prized. I can’t stop wondering what world this could be.”

“Good point,” Rex said, remembering reading something similar.

Lords had a penchant for claiming low-level hunting grounds. They were perfect for training new recruits. They could even charge fees for hunting inside them. However, he remembered the warning about the shadowbeast. The mystery might be as simple as the roaming beasts making the forest too dangerous. If it were, there was no surprise that a local lord wasn’t sending greenhorns into it.

“Anyway,” Rex said. “Let’s focus on the task. Ernie, what can you do without a lute?”

“Me? N-not really anything. I have a little skill with my rapier, but I don’t have it. And my Skills won’t work without an instrument.”

“So?” Andy said.

Rex had already summoned his Mystic arrow, which he was suddenly a lot more grateful for, and he took it out and marked it with [ Corrosive Touch].

“I’m thinking. What Skills do you have, Andy?”

“Skills? Oh… the thing is, the chief told me not to tell anybody the specifics. But I suppose I can trust you. I’ve got the [ Shout ] Skill. At a basic level, it draws lesser monsters with low intelligence toward me. And [ Durable ], which greatly reduces my chance of injuries and disease, while slightly increasing my healing rate.”

“Okay, basic tank and taunt Skills,” Rex murmured, rubbing his chin. “Not bad. But you’re still only level 1. We can’t exactly risk having you draw seven gnolls.”

“True, but gnolls are not very defensive,” Ernie said. “They normally attack wildly with their claws and fangs, and wear little to no armor, instead relying on their thick fur and ferociousness."

“Huh, looks like your lordly education paid off,” Rex said. Of course, he had already seen the gnolls and verified their equipment. But the information about their fighting style was useful. “That gives me an idea. Andy, if they charge you wildly, you should be able to find openings. You won’t need to look for deathblows, since that stun sabre of yours should work on them.”

“Yeah, you’re right,” Andy nodded. “That’s actually a good point.”

“Yeah. You’re still only level 1, though, so here’s what we’re going to do. I’m going to expose myself and hopefully take one of them down with me. Once I’ve drawn them to me, you rush their flank. Try to hit as many as you can. Don’t worry about me. If they catch up to me, I’ll switch places with Slimer. I don’t plan to activate his fireball thing, but I doubt they’ll attack the slime while we’re actively attacking them.

“Are you sure that’s wise?”

“Sure? No. Not even close. But if you’re worried about Slimer, or anybody else, you can always use [ Shout ]. If we’re safe, it doesn’t really matter, though. If they leave their backs exposed to you, you should try to get kills. ”

“And if they don’t?”

“Hopefully, you’ll have stunned a few. If so, it should be easy for me to join the melee and finish off the stunned ones.”

Rex had considered making another trap for the fight, but to avoid being seen, it would have needed to be made in the forest, which would require drawing them out. And there were two problems with that. His friends didn’t seem anywhere near as adept at masking their auras as he was, and could easily give them away. And secondly, despite what he had said about the Gates earlier, he still wanted to get things done quickly. He doubted Credo was arriving anytime soon, but he didn’t know.

“Okay, so you got all that?”

Andy nodded.

“What about me?”

“You want to help?”

Ernie shied away, but then forced his gaze up.

“There’s going to be a lot of chaos. If you can find a weapon or some other kind of opportunity, go for it. Look for easy kills, if you can. Anything to help us?”

Ernie nodded, an unfamiliar defiance in his eyes.
