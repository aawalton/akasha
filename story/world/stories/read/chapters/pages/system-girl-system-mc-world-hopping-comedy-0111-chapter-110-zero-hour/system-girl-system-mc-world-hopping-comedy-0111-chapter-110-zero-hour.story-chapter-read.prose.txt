Just as Mira departed for her parents' hut late in the night, a light, steady drizzle began to fall outside. The staff had already settled in for the evening, hunkering down under the heavy wool blankets Ren had provided. Several of the younger elves had been lulled into a deep, dreamless sleep by the rhythmic pitter-patter of the rain drumming against the timber roofs over their heads.

The night air smelled rich, earthy, and damp, carrying the scent of wet soil and salt from the ocean.

Varon manned the front gate this particular night. The elven rogue was utterly relaxed. He leaned against the heavy oak pillar of the gatehouse, nursing a steaming cup of strong, black tea, fervently hoping the caffeine would get him through his shift. The gentle, ambient sounds of the rain threatened to lull him into a standing sleep.

He couldn't help himself. He closed his eyes, surrendering for a brief moment to the alluring melody of the falling water.

Suddenly, slicing through the comforting, rhythmic notes of the rain, he heard something deeper. A rhythmic, dull thud. It was immediately followed by a continuous, heavy rolling sound crushing the gravel on the coastal road.

Varon didn't like it one bit. It ruined the random, beautiful notes created by the rain. The sound grew louder and louder. Finally, before Varon could fully shake off his drowsiness and grab his sword, a large, ornate carriage screeched to a halt directly in front of the main iron gates of the Fenris estate.

Varon struggled to cope with the reality of the situation. It was well past everyone's bedtime. In about an hour, it would be midnight. Who would come at this time? Was it some drunk coachman mistaking the House of Fenris for their own estate?

A man exited the unassuming carriage. He covered his head with a thick leather satchel to shield himself from the rain, stumbled over to the smaller pedestrian gate next to the guard post, and began violently banging his fist on the metal bars, shouting into the wet night.

"Open the gate! The esteemed Lord Cornwall wishes to enter!"

Varon looked down at his tea. He frowned. Did she mix rum in my tea again? That maid's pranks are getting completely out of hand. But wait, he hadn't even taken a sip yet.

The man banging on the iron door continued shouting, his voice shrill. "Please! Open the gates! The rain is getting worse!"

Varon initially thought it must be one of the high-paying customers intending to seek shelter for the night at the Seaside Inn. Finding the inn doors locked and barred, they must have wandered up the private road to the main estate looking for a bed. He needed to confirm this with the Lord of the house first.

Varon stepped out of the shadows, projecting his voice through the heavy iron bars. "I need to seek permission from Lord Ren before I can open this door. Please wait. I shall return shortly."

The attendant on the other side of the gate shouted back, shivering visibly in the downpour. "Do make haste!"

Varon made a dead sprint for Sylas's quarters in the main house. This situation was well over his paygrade.

...

Lily had finally fallen asleep. She lay flat on her belly in the center of her crib, her face buried deep in her small, plush pillow. Her chubby arms and legs were spread wide in all directions, taking up as much space as physically possible.

Ren sat in the heavy oak chair next to the crib. He let out a long, exhausted sigh. He was entirely out of practice with this. Now that Mira had taken over the bedtime routines recently, he had forgotten how much energy it took to wrestle a toddler into submission.

He yawned, covering his mouth with the back of his hand. "Let's pull an all-nighter," he whispered to the sleeping baby. "Send you off with a bang."

[Ana: Ren, don't be childish. You have so much work tomorrow. Another entire batch of elves will arrive tomorrow morning from the Greenwood liquidations.]

This is not my first rodeo, Ren retorted mentally, resting his chin on his hand. You wouldn't believe how many pitch decks I finished running on coffee alone for thirty-six hours straight back on Earth prime.

[Ana: Fine, fine. Ruin your sleep schedule.]

Even though I chided him, I was incredibly happy for the extra time. I could spend a few more hours just sitting here with him.

Our quiet mental conversation was interrupted by a gentle, hesitant knock on the heavy bedroom door. Ren looked through the wooden railings of the crib. The sharp sound threatened to wake Lily from her hard-won sleep. She rolled over, now lying flat on her back, her brow furrowing slightly.

Ren rushed toward the door, tiptoeing the entire way across the carpet. He gently opened the latch and slipped into the hallway, pulling the door shut behind him. He found Sylas waiting in the dim corridor, holding a flickering oil lamp.

"Master," Sylas said, keeping his voice low. "We have a situation at the main gate. A Lord Cornwall seeks shelter in the rain, according to the gate guard."

Ren closed his eyes, pinching the bridge of his nose. He was just about to tell Sylas to turn the man away and end the matter when Caladorel's ancient, raspy voice echoed down the hallway from the shadows.

"He is not the kind of man to seek shelter," Caladorel stated, stepping into the dim light of the lamp. "He definitely has some ulterior motives. After all, the man is the impromptu leader of the Old Circle. I bet he is here to talk with you. It is just like him to arrive under the cover of night. One only meets creatures like him at the dead of night."

Ren had entirely had enough of these people from the Old Circle. Was this some kind of sick joke? He gets rid of one rat, and another immediately takes its place to chew on the floorboards.

Ren looked at Sylas, his jaw setting hard. "Have the man brought to my study."

Ren turned, beginning to walk back to his room to change out of his comfortable sleeping tunic, when Caladorel gently interrupted.

"It is best you have me and the girl with you," Caladorel advised, "It is better to give them a show of force so they leave you alone."

Ren thought for a moment. He didn't have the patience to deal with this bureaucratic bullshit tonight. The clock ticking down in the corner of my vision was the only thing that mattered to Ren right now. He agreed with the old Mages logic and headed toward Parisa's guest room.

Parisa was violently rubbing the sleep from her eyes when she opened her door. But the moment Ren explained the situation regarding Lord Cornwall, the sleep disappeared entirely. A predatory, aggressive look replaced it. She wasn't going back to bed knowing her dear little brother was being targeted again.

...

Soon, Ren sat behind the massive oak desk in his study. Parisa and Caladorel were seated in the designated guest entertainment area, which consisted of two plush leather sofas with a low mahogany table placed between them.

The heavy study door swung open. Lord Cornwall stood in the doorway. He possessed an air of absolute superiority and unshakeable confidence. The man oozed control. He was so incredibly assured of his own power that he walked in with his eyes closed and his head tilted slightly upwards, taking a deep, arrogant breath of the room's air. He slowly lowered his head and opened his eyes, ready to deliver his demands.

The theatrical entrance was evidently designed to maximize intimidation. He fully expected to see a flustered, inexperienced new Lord, ready to butter him up and grovel for mercy like most minor nobles do when faced with the head of the Old Circle.

What he actually saw forced his political brain to work overtime just to cope with the reality of the room.

At the end of the room, sitting casually at the desk, sat Lord Fenris. But sitting between the door and the desk were two figures that absolutely could not be ignored. Moreover, the two figures weren't currently at each other's throats.

The look in Lord Cornwall's eyes made it clear he had expected Parisa to be here during the day, due to her official posting as a Royal Inspector. But he certainly hadn't expected her to be here at midnight, casually sipping tea on a sofa with her mortal enemy and magical rival, the Arsenal Magus.

Lord Cornwall frantically adjusted his posture, offering a stiff, awkward greeting to the two revered mages.

"Lady Parisa, and... and Sir Caladorel. Greetings. I was not expecting to see you here at this time. What brings you to this estate so late?"

Parisa didn't beat around the bush. She leaned forward, resting her elbows on her knees. "I, for one, have pledged my loyalty to House Fenris and became one of Ren's vassals after being deeply moved by his vision. It is the same for my companion here. So it is perfectly natural for us to be here in our Lord's home. The more important question here is, what brings you to our humble estate at this hour, Cornwall?"

"Um… I see.. You see…" Cornwall stammered. He reached for his tight, starched collar, slipping a thick finger underneath it in a desperate attempt to loosen the fabric. It appeared the man was suddenly struggling to breathe in the suddenly oppressive atmosphere of the study. "Could I... could I get a glass of water?"

Ren gestured silently to the seat in front of his desk. He looked at Sylas. The butler pulled the chair out for the Lord, helping the shaking man sit down, and poured him a fresh glass of water from a crystal pitcher.

"Would you also like a cup of tea?" Ren asked, his voice deadpan.

The man chugged down the entire glass of water in three frantic gulps and stammered, "Um.. no. It is quite alright."

It appeared the man had been completely humbled with just one single line from Parisa.

It was no stretch to say that several massive noble houses had been aggressively vying to bring either one of these mages under their banners for a very long time. Even the King actively encouraged it. Because both of them possessed elven lineage, neither Caladorel nor Parisa could legally hold or head a noble house of their own in the traditional sense. They needed to serve under another noble family to be of true political use.

Parisa, being a half-elf with massive military merit, could hold a proper military rank, but she couldn't continue her own noble line due to her half-elven blood. The powerful human nobles were always scrambling to curry favor with them. Any house that secured their loyalty would elevate their political standing to unimaginable heights. Lord Cornwall was no different. He had tried to recruit Caladorel himself and failed.

Watching the man assess the crushing tension in the room and begin internally grumbling was highly entertaining. I could clearly make out the micro-expressions on his face; he was viciously cursing Greenwood for making an enemy of House Fenris in the first place. The man finally resolved to get this nightmare over with as soon as physically possible.

He pulled a folded piece of heavy parchment from his coat and placed it on the table with a trembling hand.

"I was originally here tonight to invite Lord Fenris to become a member of the Old Circle," Cornwall admitted, his voice lacking all its previous arrogance. "But after becoming aware of the current situation, I realize this is a wholly inadequate offer. Considering your current standing, I thought you would rise through the ranks faster given your new control of the ruined Greenwood trade routes. But considering the recent things that have come to light in this room... I shall petition with the head table and push for you to join at the head table from the very beginning. You will have a deciding vote."

Ren looked down at the piece of paper. He looked profoundly annoyed by the interruption. He didn't care about the Old Circle. He cared about the clock ticking in his head.

"I shall think it over tomorrow morning," Ren replied flatly, sliding the parchment back across the desk. "It is quite late. We shall discuss this on a later date. You may go."

Lord Cornwall realized his mistake instantly. He heard the sullen, dismissive meaning behind the words. He had indeed messed up by coming here so late, trying to intimidate a man who commanded archmagi, and had effectively insulted Ren by assuming a simple seat at a table was a worthy bribe.

The man shot up from the chair and apologized frantically. "Apologies, Lord Fenris. I shall not disturb you any further. I shall come back on a later date with proper due notice."

The man turned on his heel and practically ran for the hills, fleeing the study as fast as his legs could carry him.

Leaving Ren, Caladorel, and Parisa alone in the quiet room.

Ren looked at Parisa, rubbing the bridge of his nose. "And exactly when did I accept you as one of my vassals? Caladorel, I understand. But you have yet to show me your financial ledgers, Parisa."

Parisa whistled innocently, looking up at the ceiling as she casually exited the room. "Well, it appears we can't do anything about that now, can we? The word is out."

She was entirely right. She had already announced it to the head of the Old Circle. She had effectively sidestepped Ren's scrutiny and forced her way into his household. The woman was quite resourceful and cunning when she needed to be. But her heart was in the right place.

Caladorel took his leave a moment later. "I am far too old to be up so late dealing with politics. I shall also take my leave for the night. Good night, Lord Fenris."

...

Ren returned to his bedroom and focused on the quiet of the nursery.

Lily had, yet again, rolled over onto her belly, returning to her deep, sprawled-out sleep.

Ren sat back heavily into the padded chair next to the crib.

[Ana: What will you do? Will you join the Old Circle?]

I don't know. I'll think about it tomorrow, Ren replied mentally, leaning his head back and closing his eyes. For now, let's talk about something else. I don't want to waste what little time I have left with you talking about pointless political riffraff.

And so, we talked. We reminisced about our time together in this chaotic world. We joked about the burning duck, the identification orb, the panic of the first crab boil. The hours passed before we knew it.

With barely a few minutes left on the clock, the outside sky started to lighten up with a gentle, pale blue glow.

It was time for our farewell.

Well Ana, Ren thought, his mental voice tight and wavering. I guess this is it. Farewell, and see you soon. Come back to us as soon as you can.

I could tell he was holding back hard. Big, heavy tears were threatening to stumble over his lower lashes. His jaw was clenched tight. He was trying to be brave.

[Take care of everyone and yourself. I'll be back before you know it. Goodbye for now. Peace out.]

The temporary ticket completion screen appeared, a massive wall of sterile text blocking out my view of Ren's face. The surroundings began disappearing around me, glitching into static.

My connection to Ren severed completely.

I had a feeling I would be back here one day, for I would make it happen no matter what it took. But still. It hurt.

I was yet again alone in the void. My ethereal tears dripped down my invisible face, dissipating into nothingness before they could ever land on the ground.
