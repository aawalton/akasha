“Hey, I’m going to pick some food in the new zone. Are you guys coming?” James asked.

“Ah… Me and Bob will come with you. Don’t forget to bring the ropes as well. We need as many logs as possible. I’ll ask the citizens to build barracks and shelters next,” Andrew said as he would bring Bob, one of James’s volunteers, with him.

Bob had a unique skill—Interdimensional Storage. It was only level one, so it could contain just one cubic meter of goods. James hadn’t thought he would be useful before; he always carried things himself during dungeon dives. But Andrew saw the potential of his skill now.

“Hm? I thought your horse refused to be a pack animal?”

“Well, humans are fine for her, by the way. It was never about the weight—just pride. I can even bring some luggage with me. The important thing is not to treat her like a pack animal.” Andrew shrugged. It didn’t make sense, but most rules made by the system were like that. James ignored him and continued with the exploration.

An hour later, James arrived at the new area—the orchard. Just like its name, the orchard held various fruits: apples, mangoes, oranges, to name a few. In addition, there was also a vegetable farm further in.

Bob and Andrew dismounted from the pegasus and combed through the trees and vegetables, gathering as much as they could into their storage and buckets. James bit into an apple—the sweet taste of the red fruit flooded his mouth. He ate a few more apples and pears before putting some into the bucket.

“We still have twelve hours. I’ll get some logs while I’m at it, then take a break,” James said to Andrew as they headed back toward the settlement.

“See you, James. We can handle it from here.” Andrew nodded.

In the next eight hours, James made three trips to the logging area and brought back over two hundred logs. With the logs and tools, the group erected a rough barrack so everyone had a roof over their heads. Construction of a new watchtower was also underway. This watchtower was meant for those with archery skills—allowing them to defend the settlement even if it was attacked from two sides.

“Wow, we sure did this fast,” James said. But then a question popped into his head. “Andrew, do we really need a roof over our heads?”

Andrew blinked at James’s statement. From the military survival lessons he had learned from his peers, shelter against the elements was one of the basics they had to secure. James had always been on the move during his dungeon dives before, so he never knew nor care.

“Yes. Our purpose in this dungeon is defense, not exploration. These civilians might get sick, and the food supply could get cut off. We not only need more buildings, but we also need to expand—build storehouses, drying racks, bathhouses, and latrines,” Andrew said.

“...Alright. Do as you wish,” James nodded. He still had his doubts, but in the end, he agreed. He couldn’t help but wonder what punishment might await them if casualties racked up.

When the timer ticked down to zero, the sky darkened. A purple moon rose on the horizon, its light shining across the terrain, enveloping everything in an eerie glow.

[Very Weak Life Drain Activated. Survivors are advised to take shelter. As long as survivors are in direct contact with the moonlight, the drain will be activated—sapping their strength and eventually killing them.]

The effect was immediate. James felt his body shudder; his knees weakened, and his stomach churned with nausea. A timer appeared in his system interface, indicating that he had about four hours before losing consciousness.

“Shit! Everyone, get to the barracks!” Andrew commanded.

The barracks they had built was small, barely seven meters wide—just enough for seventy people to huddle together, and definitely not enough space to fight.

“Shit. We should’ve extended the roof of this barrack to the palisade wall,” Andrew cursed. “James! Go near the door and defend it with everything you have. How long can you last under the moonlight?”

“About four hours.”

“The civilians can only endure it for ten minutes before losing consciousness. After twenty, it becomes fatal. Do you think you can finish the horde in four hours by yourself?”

“Don’t ask for the impossible. Even if I stay conscious, my powers are weakened, you know.”

“We’ll provide support.”

“Boss, they’re coming from both portals! Shit—they’re all heavy hitters! Dozens of minotaurs and those eagles are back! They aren’t affected by the moonlight at all!”

A survivor with the Clairvoyance unique skill reported. James drew his bow; golden motes of light gathered around the arrow before it shot toward one of the birds. Just like in the novice dungeon he had conquered with Sanders, as long as the moonlight didn’t shine directly on him, there would be no effect on his body. The birds dropped one by one.

Through the gaps of the log house they had built as a barrack, several survivors with bow skills followed James’s example. But the real challenge came when the ground forces closed in. With no one guarding the gate, it was smashed to pieces with a single weapon skill.

James fought hard. As the monsters closed in, he dashed out, betting his life to fight under the purple moon rather than in the cramped barrack. Any weapon skill used in such a confined space would’ve been fatal.

The humanoid bulls came at him in droves—a stampede conjured by the dungeon with the sole intention of eradicating the survivors. James felt the moonlight burn against his skin, sapping his strength. Time was ticking down, his power fading under the moon’s glare—yet he had never felt more alive.

He slashed and slashed away with both swords in hand. Unlike Kouki, who swung his blade with grace, James swung with brute power—like a butcher with a cleaver. Methods aside, the result was the same: his enemies were torn apart. Spears of ice and fire rained down upon his foes, while waves of purple magic disoriented them. James proved himself the king of the battlefield, cutting down monsters left and right despite the overwhelming odds.

But not all was well. James was still just one man, and the swarm came from all directions, engulfing the wooden barrack with their sheer numbers. Andrew decided to take the plunge, much like James—but unlike him, they didn’t go all the way in. Andrew and his team rotated in and out of combat, striking the minotaurs with sword skills before retreating to the safety of the barracks.

“Shit!” Andrew cursed as three minotaurs unleashed their axe skills on the barracks. The wooden wall shattered as if made of glass. The charging beasts were met by spears and sword skills from Xiaoming and Bernard’s groups.

The battle turned bloody. Chaos erupted on both sides as Andrew and James fought desperately under the draining moonlight and the relentless assault of beasts.

When the last monster finally fell an hour later, a system notification slid across their vision.

[You have cleared the fifth wave. Casualties: 15. You have failed the quest. You will now be ejected into the real world.]

“Ejected?” James muttered, tilting his head. A flash of white light engulfed him, and in an instant, he was transferred to the waiting room. Andrew and the rest of the team were tossed in beside him like trash bags. Some of them groaned while others collapsed because of the moon’s effect. They turned toward one another, question marks practically floating above their heads.

“What happened?” Sanders asked. Xiaoming and Bernard voiced the same confusion.

“We failed the main quest. I assume that means we’ll be returned to the real world,” Andrew said, glancing toward a loading screen hovering in one corner of the white room.

When the progress bar reached one hundred percent, another flash of light enveloped the group, sending them back to the desert where it all began.

James looked down at his body—still armed and ready for battle—but all around him, people were wandering aimlessly across the sand, as if nothing had happened.

“We are back in Australia,” Bernard said as he looked at the scenery. Andrew turned back toward the translucent purple barrier where the dungeon was and touched it. It was still there—the dungeon had not been freed.

“System, did we fail a dungeon?” James asked.

[Affirmative. Survivor James and his team have failed Medium Dungeon AU-52. Retry is possible. Please go toward the dungeon and reenter if you wish to rechallenge it.]

“Well… for once, I’m glad we failed.” Andrew smiled wryly.

“What? I lost three of my men! Are you saying they died in vain?” Xiaoming pushed toward Andrew, his rage uncontrollable. He roared as he strode toward Andrew, ready to give him a taste of his clenched fist. Bernard and the others had to grab his arm to stop him.

“Xiaoming, do you think you could’ve survived in that condition? The barracks are destroyed. The moon would’ve killed you in an hour. From what I saw, the moon’s glow didn’t fade even after we beat the wave. That means it will keep shining in the subsequent ones,” Andrew surmised. Unlike James, who relied more on instinct, Andrew used his head. In such a short time, he had already analyzed the dungeon’s characteristics.

Andrew’s logic shoved the hard truth down Xiaoming’s throat. He spat several choice words and profanities in his native language but ultimately accepted Andrew’s reasoning. Andrew was right.

“So, are we going to challenge this dungeon again, or do we find a new one?” James asked.

“Of course we’ll challenge this dungeon again. Where else can we find a dungeon so safe? If things get dicey, as long as we can survive one wave, we can go back home. We just need to kill some local civilians.” Andrew smiled.

James was taken aback. Andrew didn’t see the dungeon’s people as humans—merely as characters in a game. He might like them, but he wouldn’t sacrifice his men for them. That’s why he could speak about genocide so easily.

“Let’s stay at a local inn to rest. We’ll tackle this dungeon again first thing in the morning,” James said. He wanted to dive into the dungeon again, but since it was a medium dungeon, he wanted to go in at his best condition.

“Great. I also want to know how the red dungeons have progressed. I wonder whether things are going well over there. How long were we gone anyway?” Andrew said as he walked toward the nearest outpost, greeting a local tour guide and asking for their phones.

James sighed. This dungeon was hard in a different way than he imagined.

Unlike other dungeons, failure was an option here.
