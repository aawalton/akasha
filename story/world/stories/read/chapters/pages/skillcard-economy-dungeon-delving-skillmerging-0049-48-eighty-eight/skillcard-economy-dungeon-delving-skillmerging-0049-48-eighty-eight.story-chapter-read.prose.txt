Palik first entered Walker society two years ago. The son of a moderately rich conglomerate owner, he was able to collect a couple of Silver-grade Skillcards. Suspected to have three Silver cards, his build centers around flexibility.

What that meant, I had no clue.

“What are you doing here?” Toby asked.

“The receptionist was cute. Came for a quick fuck.”

He pointed behind him with his thumb.

I used Shadowstride and expanded my senses as far as I could. I sensed a woman. She was fixing her clothes.

“Heard a loud sound, came to look, and look what I found.”

This had grown even more complicated. If Toby and I were to fight Palik now, it was bound to make noise.

This far inside, there was zero chance for someone to hear us, except the receptionist. We couldn’t let her go and alert the whole building.

I looked back at Toby.

Our eyes met.

He was thinking the same thing.

Toby pulled out his gun.

I vanished and emerged a few feet away from the woman. I shot her with the sleeping bullet and went back.

For the four seconds I was gone, the chaos had already started.

Toby shot and shot, yet none of his bullets hit.

Palik moved in a way that no human body should. His thorax moved to the side while his leg and head stayed in the same place.

He jumped to the wall, hung there, then leapt forward at Toby.

I emerged in his path and tried to tackle him.

He evaded me.

I did not understand how.

“Hahaha, good luck catching him.”

Palik reached for his shoe and took out a shiv.

“Fuck this.”

Out of bullets, Toby tossed his gun aside and raised his arms to shield himself.

This time, I saw Palik.

He morphed into a paper strip for a second and flew past Toby, reached his back, and transformed back.

He locked his legs around Toby’s waist, then shoved his weapon into Toby’s back.

He took it out.

Toby screamed.

Then he did it again.

And again.

And again.

After the third time, Toby couldn’t scream—not because he was dead, but because his throat had gone hoarse.

Toby grabbed Palik’s legs and fell back to the ground, crushing Palik beneath him.

He turned into paper and slipped away.

“What the fuck are you?” Palik exclaimed.

“You ruined my fucking coat. How the hell am I supposed to blend into the party now?”

I hopped beside Toby and helped him get to his feet.

His coat was ruined from the back. The fabric and skin had been cut apart, blood flowing out of the wounds.

“We need a plan, or we won’t be able to pin him,” I whispered.

“Well, we know his skills now, at least.”

I appreciated the confidence Toby had in me, as if I had figured out all three of Palik’s skills.

His first skill was great flexibility.

The second was increased perceptiveness; otherwise, he wouldn’t have been able to dodge the bullets.

And the third was turning into paper.

Still, I couldn’t be sure.

His flexibility and perceptiveness could just be secondary effects of some other skill, or he could also have a fourth skill in his arsenal.

I couldn’t be sure about any of it.

It was better to assume the worst.

“You are really good at switching between your Skillcards,” I told Palik. “Switching between your flexibility skill and increased perceptiveness to dodge bullets. Looks like you actually deserved your rank in Walker Magazine instead of your father buying it for you.”

That angered Palik.

“That fucking magazine! I should have been at least Rank 10, and they ranked me 88. EIGHTY-EIGHT! I will kill that editor.”

Tch.

I was looking to get confirmation about whether his flexibility and perceptiveness were two separate skills or not, but the idiot decided to get offended by my compliment.

“I got a plan,” Toby said.

“Ah, fuck. My back hurts.”

He said it aloud while taking off his coat. The back of his white shirt had gone red.

“I would need a new coat. Whoa, yours looks nice.”

Palik clicked his tongue, then looked around, assessing his surroundings.

His eyes fell on the hole, and he smiled.

“Is this what you were doing? I wonder what it is for. Theft? Terrorism? And how is Cherry related to all of this?”

Shit.

Even if we were to defeat him right now, we would have to kidnap him or kill him just to keep Cerise’s identity a secret. I would very much prefer the first, but I didn’t know about Toby. I would prefer if no one had to die.

Toby gave me his coat. Confused, I held it.

“Wait for my signal.”

Suddenly, enlightenment hit. I understood the plan and got into position.
