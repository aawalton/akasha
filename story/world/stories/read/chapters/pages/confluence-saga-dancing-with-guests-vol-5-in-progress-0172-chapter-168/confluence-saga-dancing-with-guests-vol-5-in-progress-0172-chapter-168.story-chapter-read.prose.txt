Meeting the Fae changed more than we realized.

We discovered the truth of her heritage, we met members of her family. We made friends. And enemies.

We found out that the WidePond we only barely understood the size of was only a fraction of the whole. That we were caught between two cultures, two societies, two worlds.

We realized all this fairly quickly. It was obvious, at least once we had time to think things through. Especially when we spoke with our family, all of it.

What we didn't realize at the time… Well I'm sure you know, you've seen what Holding Syl has become.

~ * ~

Clyde found himself sitting outside the Prince's office. Anyone who saw him would think he was waiting patiently. He wasn't fidgeting, he wasn't shifting, he wasn't giving any indication that he was unhappy with the delay. He wasn't even frowning.

So much had changed while he was in the Depths. Last time he'd been here he'd barged in, no one had stopped him. He'd spoken to the High Duke, he'd complained, he'd been sent on his way. He wouldn't have waited back then, not even impatiently. He wouldn't have sat in a chair and kept a pleasant grin on his face. Even if it had meant he'd have remained an actual scion, that it would have kept him living in the lifestyle he deserved.

The look on his face didn't change despite his thoughts. He didn't show a single sign of how badly he wanted to get up, stomp across the wood floor, and push the doors open. He didn't even shift to take advantage of the cushioning of the chair. All he did was sit and wait.

He'd crossed the Wall. He'd unlocked an Affinity and made it home. He'd gone through his Path Selections and smiled at everyone as he found his Archetype changed from that of the noble Velli Manipulator line, the path all Velli children aspired to, to that of the Velli Thug. He'd only had a single choice for his Specialization, that of the Thug itself. Where was the improved ability to charm, to sway both Velli servants and others? Where was the subtle benefits to the Velli Glamour that let others perceive his best qualities and for everything else see what they most approved of?

He'd gotten none of that. Not one of the myriad benefits he knew he should have had. Oh, he could club something into submission with ease, he could snarl and remind everyone he was a Velli the moment they saw him, but that meant nothing. His Path had become one of combat and intimidation, not of charm and subtlety.

He blinked, the first visible movement he'd made since he was told to sit and wait to be admitted. His smile never wavered, his posture never relaxed. His Unc- The Prince's assistant, a member of the family of course, hadn't raised his voice, hadn't laced the words with any Energy. He hadn't needed to.

Clyde knew his Affinity was broken. It was who he was, he understood that of course, but it wasn't who he was meant to be. He was a Velli Scion, they'd even announced it publicly. But he listened when spoken to, he waited for others. He was agreeable when pressed, especially if he'd been told to be.

It was her fault. He'd tried to look her up when he got home and found nothing. Less than nothing. He couldn't even find records of that Holding. He knew she existed, he knew they weren't letting him see. They didn't want him to see, they didn't want him to know the truth.

The door opened and he rose, not bowing but standing attentively.

"Go back to your quarters boy," Mahk said as he walked past, not even pausing to greet him. "You've gotten all the answers you need."

Clyde turned, his feet leading him away.

"Sir," he managed, his teeth gritting with the effort. "Why name me a scion when I have become what I am?"

"Stop."

He froze, waiting.

"The others are all what people will expect of our family. Most will see the danger, will keep their distance or send those who do not matter. You however are the rare specimen that appears safe, that they might find a use for."

"I am not something to be used."

"They are right. You will be useful. You have the basics, you can apply them to a degree. They will come to trust you, you will make sure they have every reason to. And when we finally find a use for your position call on you, you will betray their trust answer our call. Now go, we have no more time for you."

Clyde went, his mind warring with the words. He knew what the Prince had done, he'd felt the gap between what he thought he had heard and what was meant. He knew it had happened, he just didn't know what had been done.

He made his way to his quarters. He knew he was to attend a gathering that evening. He would have to look his best.

New Year staff meetings were supposed to be calm affairs. They weren't supposed to be filled with tension and unspoken accusations. That was supposed to be finished with once the rankings were determined. Of course the students usually believed the rankings were entirely based on their performances in the competitions, that the teachers didn't find reasons to adjust the rankings to fit their plans.

Sarna sighed as Jean finally joined everyone at the table. Normally he'd have been waiting for them when they arrived, ensured everything remained civil. She had the feeling he'd taken his time to see just how far tempers would flare in his absence.

"Where is Marten," Elienne asked. "He was not at the presentation, he is not here now."

"He will not be," the Dean said. "His replacement will be here in a week."

"Replacement? He quit?"

"It was agreed that he would not renew his commitment."

"This has to do with him, doesn't it," Elienne snarled as she glared at Lord Percivus.

"I have never spoken to the man."

"Of course not," she said. "He wouldn't have let you come close enough. He knew what you are."

"Elienne," the Dean said. "I know what Marten told you. I have enough access to infer what he based his words on."

"He showed me the reports. He showed me what the traitor did."

Sarna frowned and glanced at her husband. His matching frown made it clear he was just as confused. Traitor? What was Elienne talking about.

"Ah," Percivus said. "Those reports."

"Yes. Those reports. How could you."

"Elienne, that's enough," Dean said.

"No," Percivus said. "I will address an aspect of what I'm being accused of. The rest? Your grandparents weren't born when any of this happened. Details have been forgotten."

"But you remember perfectly I'm certain," Elienne said.

"Not at all. But I remember enough."

"By the simplest of views I sided with the Fae against Humanity, against my people. I took to the field in the Wars and earned a reputation. I deserved it."

"Traitor!"

"I followed my liege. I served my Sovereign, I still do. His decisions? His actions? Pray you never need make that choice. He sided with those who welcomed him and treated him fairly. He saw injustice and acted. He ensured lasting peace between those involved."

Sarna saw Elienne about to speak and let her hand drop onto the table to draw attention.

"Do you mean harm to any of the students? Do you intend to pit yourself against Humanity in any way?"

"I do not and I do not. I know what peace costs, I know what the agreements state. I would not be here if there was any remaining ill will or intent."

"You drove Marten away."

"Elienne…" the Dean sighed. "Lord Percivus had nothing to do with the departure. None of the staff were told of it until this meeting."

"He could have said goodbye."

Sarna caught Leo's grin and hid her own smile. What other instructors did in their own time was up to them. If it led to additional drama at times? That was for the Dean to handle, not for a simple Dueling instructor.

"Leo," Jean continued. "Are all the teams in the first batch aware of their departure tomorrow?"

"Aye. I told Syl before they left the dining hall, Morgan just outside and the nobles at their dorm. Mithrax already knew. I hope he's as ready as he believes."

"He made it through the rankings. He even spoke with Syl briefly," Sarna said.

"Well there's that at least," Leo said.

"Does anyone have anything else?" Jean asked.

Sarna looked around at the rest of the staff. Half of them were eyeing Elienne, the other half seemed to be trying not to get caught looking at Percivus. She'd dealt with the man enough over the last several months to take him at his word, she wasn't going to hold something that happened in the distant past against him. Just how old would he have to be? How powerful to not look it?

If dealing with this is what being Dean meant, she was glad she'd never taken on the role.

As the Holders grew so too did the Holding. Many spouted this wisdom, especially when it was new. By the third generation the growth was rarely visible, rarely significant enough to disrupt the lives of anyone present.

But in the first generation the changes could be drastic. Cores became more stable, more attuned to the Affinities that helped define them. Seeds did the same, while growing vines and sprouts that explained the name.

Energies had lingered near Seed Syl. Energies with intent that outlived their creation. Energies flavoured by one the Seed knew was close to the Holders. The Energies did not survive, did not grow. But the Seed was aware that they had been there.

What would it be like to hold those Energies, to entangle them in vines. To feed from them and feed them in return. What shape would flow forth, what would it face.
