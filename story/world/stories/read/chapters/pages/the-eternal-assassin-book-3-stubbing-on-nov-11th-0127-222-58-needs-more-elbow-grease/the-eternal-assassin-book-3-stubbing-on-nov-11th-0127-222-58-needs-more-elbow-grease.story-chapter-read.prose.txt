As it turned out, sending his mind back and forth across the realms to check in on his different Eternal Marks wasn’t exactly the most exciting thing to practice. While the effort got a tiny bit easier with every attempt, it was still a rather hefty toll on both his mind and soul. It also used up a solid chunk of his own energy every time he did it, no doubt to protect his awareness from the void realm he was forced to pass through each time. It was incredibly boring to practice, and he really didn’t know if there was going to be any real-world benefit to all his effort. That said, there was one solid reason to keep at it.

The one-hour mark had come and gone. And Richard had never reached out to bring him back.

In fact, according to Asher’s best guess, he’d been stranded in the wild realm for close to four hours now. At this point, he had three different fuzzy deer lounging around him, one even with its head in his lap as he absentmindedly scratched between its ears while waiting for his energy to recover. The deer didn’t seem fazed in the slightest that their weird, almost constant changing and evolution had been disrupted by his new skill. It was nice that the deer were so friendly and cozy, because Asher was functionally treating them as one would a comfort animal.

The fact that he might be permanently trapped on an entirely different realm of existence from everyone else was causing him to freak out just a little bit.

“Richard’s just being a bit of a dick,” he told Scritchems, continuing to scratch behind the deer’s ears as it bleated happily. “He was obviously annoyed with me giving him the run around all week. Any minute now he’ll decide he’s made me wait long enough and he’ll create the portal needed to bring me back. You’ll see.”

But as much as he hoped that was the case, Asher didn’t even believe his own words. Richard was definitely a prick, but he wasn’t stupid. He had to know better than anyone just how dangerous the wild realm truly was. Throwing Asher into it without warning was risky enough, but Richard was one of the few people who knew Asher was practically impossible to kill. He must have considered it a worthy risk to get his revenge on Asher.

Stupid and needlessly risky, but still understandable.

But to send him here? And then leave him here for hours on end? He didn’t think even Richard would do something so reckless.

“Something must have happened,” Asher muttered, wondering what said something could be. “Most likely, some sort of emergency popped up that required Richard to use his tritessence to help with. If he doesn’t have any energy, he can’t get me back from the wild realm. Alternatively, something might have happened to Richard. If he was knocked unconscious, or even killed, that would certainly explain why my ride is late. Granted, this is Richard we’re talking about. There’s a good chance he laid down to take a quick nap and has just been sleeping for the past four hours.”

Scritchems bleated yet again, nodding his agreement as he shifted his head and pushed harder against Asher’s lap, indicating he needed more scratching.

“Even so, I’m not just going to sit around and make a new life for myself in the wild realm,” Asher said, staring out at the horizon. A few minutes ago, he’d had a beautiful series of crystal arches to admire, the distant sunlight shining through them and creating a dazzling scene. Now, those same arches had been replaced by a single, gigantic tree stretching upward a few miles into the sky, with what looked like owls the size of dragons flying around its base and attacking one another without mercy.

There was certainly a lot of beauty in the wild realm, but it definitely wasn’t worth the risks.

“I think I’m just about ready,” he informed Scritchems, patting the deer one last time on the head as his energy fully recovered for what he hoped was the last time.

After freaking out slightly at the realization that Richard wasn’t coming to get him, and all but breaking the artifact he’d been given as he clicked it again and again and again, Asher decided it was up to him to take matters into his own hands. After that, he began practicing.

Having already come to the understanding that certain skills worked differently on different realms, he began scrying Samantha again in earnest, desperately trying to figure out a way to actually influence the world around her and get some form of message to her. But as much as he tried to become some sort of poltergeist, he quickly realized that wasn’t going to work. What those attempts did do, however, was confirm his earlier suspicions that he could train his ability to connect his mind from one realm to another just like a muscle.

After that, he started training.

After almost four hours of repeatedly establishing the connection from the wild realm to the material realm and hurling his mind through it, broken up only by his need to recover his energy, Asher had gained two important rewards for his efforts. The first one was obvious. When he’d first arrived in this realm, his Eternal Mark had only been level 24. Now…

He’d managed to level his skill at speeds he’d previously thought to be impossible. Leveling an already evolved skill three times in only four hours was practically unheard of. It just went to show how difficult what he was doing truly was, and while he’d never thank Richard for throwing him over here and then completely abandoning him, he did have to admit that his hopefully brief stint in the wild realm had been incredibly beneficial to his progression. Still, leveling the skill three times had been just one of the two rewards for all his effort.

The second was having gotten far more skilled at forming that connection between realms.

When he’d started, throwing his awareness from his body all the way over to the material realm had taken a ton out of him. Almost like forcing his way through a tube of molasses. Now, it was simple. While he wouldn’t necessarily go so far as to call forming the connection easy, it no longer left him panting, and it was something he could do with barely a thought. This was rather important, as throwing his awareness between realms was one thing. But it wasn’t Asher’s end goal.

No, if Richard wasn’t coming to get him, it was up to him to make the journey on his own.

“I think I have just enough energy, or tritessence, to not only get me there, but to protect me from the void while I’m in transit,” he informed Scritchems. “My awareness has made the trip enough times now that I have a pretty good gut feeling as to how much it’s going to take me. I wish I had some hard numbers to work with, but this is all napkin math and instinct. Some people might like that, but seeing as I’m risking throwing my body into the void realm for potentially all eternity, personally, I’d like a little more reassurance than that.”

Scritchems was silent, his eyes closed as he continued to receive the best and most likely only scratches of his life.

“Oh, to be a freaky, egg-laying space deer living in a chaotic realm without rules,” Asher mused, realizing he was going to miss the fuzzy guy. Honestly, the deer had helped him have someone to simply talk to while he was working out how he was going to get home. Part of him was a bit worried as to what was going to happen to Scritchems once Asher left and the deer was no longer protected from the chaotic whims of the wild realm any longer, but he wasn’t about to kidnap his new friend and bring him home with him. The idea was certainly tempting, and he was certain Samantha and Brian would love the fuzzy little guy. But he doubted Scritchems would want that.

Still, that didn’t mean he couldn’t watch over him.

“Throwing my awareness between realms has proven to be incredible practice,” he explained, pausing in his scratching just long enough to place a sliver of his soul on the deer, making him Asher’s eighth Eternal Mark. After leveling the skill more, the total number of marks he could have out at a time was now up to nine. “Not to mention if this works out like I’m praying it will, this won’t actually be goodbye. But now that you’re marked, you need to make sure you survive, alright? I’ll be able to feel if anything bad happens to you, understand?”

Scritchems cracked open his eyes and looked up at him, bleating his agreement. Either that or he was demanding Asher get back to scratching behind his ears, it was hard to tell.

Realizing he was just stalling out of fear of what might happen to him when he finally put his plan into action, Asher sighed. Patting the three deer around him one last time, he carefully got to his feet.

Alright. This is either going to kill me… or it’s going to kill Richard. I mean, what I’m about to do isn’t going to kill Richard, but if I make it back there in one piece, I’m going to kill him myself.

Deciding that was as good a goal to focus on as any, Asher cracked his neck, imitating Moxy as he hopped up and down a few times while shaking out his limbs. It wasn’t like limbering up was going to help him any, but it still made him feel a little better. Checking for the fourth time that his energy was topped off and he was ready to go, he went for it before he could change his mind.

Focusing on the Eternal Mark placed on Samantha, a connection he’d spent the last four hours training with, Asher made his move. But rather than using the second-tier ability of the skill to scry her, instead, he attempted to trigger Recall. Just like when he’d initially tried to Recall back to the material realm, the skill failed to activate, giving him the sense that the skill simply wasn’t strong enough to transport him between realms. That was fine, however.

The skill was on the precipice of evolving, after all. All Asher needed to do was give it a little push.

Just like when the greater demon had grabbed his arm and attempted to lock him down with its tremendous weight, Asher refused to give up on the skill. Straining with every fiber of his being, he pushed against that too-thin connection between him and Samantha, attempting to force his skill to bridge the distance and bring him back home. Before, when he’d battled against the demon’s attempts to keep him locked down, he’d ended up having his arm torn off as his skill went one way and the demon another. This time, however, it was all or nothing. Either he’d manage to force his skill to make the journey, or he’d be trapped in the wild realm for who knew how long.

Or he’d misjudged how much energy the venture cost and he’d end up in the void, which was probably the worst-case scenario.

“Come on!” Asher hissed, feeling something pop in his eye as he continued pushing everything he had at his skill. Recall fought against him every step of the way, insisting that it wasn’t meant for this kind of journey. But Asher wasn’t about to let that stop him.

His body had already been ripped entirely across different realms once by the lich when he’d first been snatched up from Earth, and then a second time just a few hours ago by Richard.

If they could do it, so could he.

“Come on!” he shouted, mentally focusing on his shards in his core and trying to shove them into the ether, for all the good it would do. “You need shards?! Take them! Take as many as you need!”

He didn’t know if it was his goading or him just being rewarded for his repeated attempts at using the skill, but eventually, something gave. His breath caught as another hundred thousand shards were ripped out of his core without any sort of notification, and the world shifted around him more violently than ever before.

Asher had just enough time to be grateful that he’d distanced himself from Scritchems and the other deer before a swirling sphere of energy was formed around him and he was ripped out of the wild realm as though by an invisible giant. Hurtling across realms faster than ever before, he wasn’t sure if he was laughing or screaming, but one thing was for sure.

For the first time since he’d been taken from Earth and brought over to this new world, he truly felt like a realm wanderer.
