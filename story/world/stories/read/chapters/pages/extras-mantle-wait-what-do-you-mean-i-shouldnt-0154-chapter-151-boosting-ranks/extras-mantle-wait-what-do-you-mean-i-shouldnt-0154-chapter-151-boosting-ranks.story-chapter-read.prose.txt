They walked Bram back to the same table he dreaded—he knew it was the very same one—where he was first strapped to the operating table and a sorcery was etched into his very mind. There was a chip out of the front left corner of the table where he'd gripped it too hard the first time, the metal gone bright where his fingers had worn the coating off, and his eyes went straight to it the way a tongue goes to a missing tooth.

The incense was already burning. That sweet, herbal reek that was supposed to loosen a man up. It hadn't loosened him up the first time. It had just made the whole thing smell like a temple where something bad was going to happen.

"In you get," Vera said.

The blue-haired girl kept her gaze fixed firmly on her clipboard. Bram actually appreciated that. He was currently commanding the 1st Mage Company, and he was also currently terrified out of his mind. If she had looked him in the eye, he might have turned around and walked right out the door, rank be damned.

Bram swallowed the coppery taste in his mouth, stepped into the booth, and sat on the operating table before his legs could refuse the order. The metal leached the warmth straight through his uniform shirt.

He stared at the heavy iron door, waiting for the boss to walk through.

There were things nobody in the Bastion said out loud, but everyone collectively danced around. Jin Winters did not look like a commander. He had lean shoulders, light hair, and the exhausted, pale face of a student failing a midterm exam. He looked seventeen. Maybe eighteen on a rough day. Half the volunteers swore blind the boy-face was a magical disguise, and Bram understood the impulse. It was simply easier for soldiers to take orders from an adult than a teenager.

But Bram had watched him closely over the last few days. He’d heard the same stories circulating the camps—the masked figure who dove into the dark under the Bastion alone and crawled back out covered in an Underlord’s blood. The kid Commander Mathew had handed the entire war to without a single argument. Half of the stories were absolute rubbish. Rumors grew in the dark like mold.

The trouble was the half that wasn't.

The heavy door clicked open.

Jin Winters stepped into the booth, casually shoving his sleeves up to his elbows. He wore that easy, utterly harmless expression that always made Bram’s stomach tie itself into a knot, and behind him was the crazy tall red-eyed one—Joe, he called himself, and Bram didn’t believe for one second that was his real name—the one whose smile never once reached his eyes.

"Bram," Jin sounded genuinely pleased to see him, which was somehow worse. "Or should I say, Captain Bram?"

"Just Bram is fine, sir," he grunted, his knuckles already whitening on the chipped edge of the table.

Jin stopped at his side, tapping his fingers against the cold metal. "Don't look so tense. The process is going to be exactly the same as last time." He offered a smile, pausing for exactly one beat too long. "It won't hurt. Much."

Bram opened his mouth to say something but never got the chance as he saw Joe move past the boss. The tall Underlord leaned over the operating table, and Bram felt his hand clamped down onto his shoulder.

"Time to sleep, friend," Joe murmured. "I will do my absolute best to make sure he doesn't scramble your brain."

Bram stared up at the ceiling. The sickly sweet stench of the incense suddenly felt suffocating. He waited for the punchline. He waited for the kid or the red-eyed madman to laugh, to drop the tension, to tell him it was just a twisted hazing ritual for the 1st Company commander.

Neither of them did. Jin just stood by the table, watching him with that perfectly polite, empty smile.

A crushing pressure flooded out from Joe’s grip, bypassing flesh and striking directly at Bram's consciousness. The ambient hum of the room vanished. Bram couldn't even form the breath to scream before the world collapsed inward, dragging him down into the dark.

Bram woke up gasping.

Every muscle between his jaw and his calves seized in a synchronized cramp, locking his body rigid. He groaned, his joints grinding together as he forced his heavy eyelids open.

Medical ward. He knew the ceiling. He'd been carried through it enough these last two weeks.

Moving with excruciating slowness, Bram rolled his head to the side. The adjacent cots were occupied—Jack, Basta, Veric—the entire lot who had stood in the training room while the boss promised them a miracle. They were all resting.

So it had worked. On all of them. It had—

Bram felt it then. The power. Tucked down in the middle of him like a coal that hadn't gone out, and when he prodded at it, curious, the way you'd test a sore tooth, it unfolded and rose up through him in a wave that felt like being twenty again, like the first cup after a double shift, like standing up straight for the first time in years—and a laugh tore out of him, loud and startled and delighted, because gods, he had never in his life felt anything like—

A hand landed on his shoulder.

The power slammed flat. Just gone, pressed down under something vast and cold and utterly unbothered, and Bram's laugh died in his throat as he turned and found his boss standing over the cot giving him a look that could strip paint.

"'Ry," he said. "'M sorry." It came out mangled, his tongue not fully back under his own command.

Jin exhaled a long, ragged breath.

"Easy there, tiger." He took his hand off Bram's shoulder, and the power came creeping back, meek now. "Veric and the others had decades of combat to build a foundation on, and I just poured an ocean into a cup. So don't go flexing it around like a birthday present. Rest. Let your body work out where to put it all."

Bram nodded. It was about all he could manage.

The boss turned away, his boots dragging slightly against the floor tiles as he moved toward the next occupied cot.

Bram closed his eyes, and the heavy, dreamless dark pulled him under.
