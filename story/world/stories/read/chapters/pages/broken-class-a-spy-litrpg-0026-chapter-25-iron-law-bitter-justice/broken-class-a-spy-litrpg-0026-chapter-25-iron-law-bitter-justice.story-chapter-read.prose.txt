Arthur's pulse still thundered in his ears. A second slower, and Yudursh's sword would have taken his head off.

"Too close," he muttered.

Only a desperate surge of mana to his brain had saved him, enhancing perception just enough to react before the blade struck. Even then, it had come down to the wire.

Arthur turned around and scanned the aftermath. Bodies lay strewn across the ground, weapons tangled in cooling limbs. Amid the carnage, glints of metal caught his eye.

He moved with the practiced precision of a scavenger, collecting anything useful: intact armor pieces, weapons, potions, backpacks, waterskins, other basic utilities, monster cores and most importantly, identification tokens. Each body became a ledger, each token a potential advantage. Some bore noble crests. Those would be worth more than gold.

His hands were bloodied and reeking by the time he returned to the cavern in the mountain.

After a moment's hesitation, he whispered, "Drogan, how am I supposed to trust anything that comes out of the princess's mouth?"

Drogan’s voice oozed into his mind with condescension. "You finally ask something worthwhile. A soul oath is a binding contract enforced by the system itself. No tricks, no loopholes. Once it's made, neither party can lie, mislead, or act against the agreed terms without facing system-enforced consequences. If either side breaks it, well, let’s just say the system is not merciful."

Arthur bowed his head slightly, his voice low. "Understood. Can... can it be used to secure loyalty?"

"Obviously," Drogan sneered. "Your kind can’t grasp discipline without consequence. The system provides the pain you need to behave."

Arthur said nothing. But his eyes sharpened. That was exactly what he needed.

Arthur looked down at the ring on his finger. He hesitated for only a second before activating it. A shimmer of light, then the princess appeared. She was caught mid-turn, still reacting to the nearby commotion when she was stored.

Still dazed from the sudden reappearance, she gagged, coughed violently, and vomited at her feet.

Arthur sighed. “Charming.”

Drogan cackled in his mind. "She took one whiff of your monster-core breath and lost her royal lunch. Bravo. A scent fit for tribunal records."

Arthur’s eyes narrowed. He hadn’t considered that. Before the princess could do more than wipe her mouth, he reached out, touched her shoulder, and willed her back into the ring.

He walked back to the creek, stripped off his bloodied clothes, and used a bowl to gather the thin stream of icy water. He poured it over himself again and again, scrubbing until the filth and stink of battle finally washed away. Afterward, he brushed his teeth with a splintered twig. "God, I miss toothpaste," he muttered, grimacing as the wood scraped along his gums.

One of mankind’s finest inventions, really. He couldn’t imagine how people lived before it, everyone must’ve walked around breathing death at each other.

Once done, he dressed in clean, well-fitted clothes looted from the travel pack of one of the participants killed in the ambush. Almost like stepping into someone else's life.

He returned to the cavern again and activated the ring. When the princess appeared, she was still hunched over, wiping her mouth. He threw her a waterskin.

"Drink. Don’t die."

She drank hesitantly.

She looked up at him slowly, her gaze sweeping across the cavern walls.

"Who are you?" she rasped. "Where are we?" Her eyes narrowed. "What did you do to me?"

Before Arthur could answer, she lunged. Her hand shot to a hidden blade tucked in her sleeve, and in a single fluid motion, she aimed it at his throat.

Arthur’s mana surged, reinforcing his body by reflex. With minimal effort, he caught her wrist mid-strike, twisted the knife from her hand, and shoved her to the ground.

She grunted in pain beneath him.

"Calm down," he said firmly, pinning her with his knee. "Listen to me."

Still pinning her, Arthur leaned in, his tone low and firm. "You just tried to kill me. So let’s skip the niceties. You’re going to make a deal. One that benefits both of us. Refuse, and I won’t be as patient next time."

She spat at him, eyes full of fire.

He calmly wiped his cheek, meeting her glare without flinching. "You can keep posturing if it makes you feel noble. But I’ve run out of time and patience. This is your only chance to choose the easy way."

Keeping one hand on the princess to hold her down, Arthur reached awkwardly into his ring with the other and withdrew Yudursh.

Yudursh hit the ground mid-swing, his blade still arcing as though the fight had never stopped. He stumbled, crashing forward and landing in a heap. Dried blood flaked off his wounds, and his breath came in ragged gasps. The princess's eyes widened.

"He’s dying. You can heal him. But only after you swear a system oath to me. Truth and loyalty. You defy me, he dies. You serve me, he lives."

She stared at Yudursh, eyes wide with panic, and reached out as far as she could, hands already glowing as she tried to summon healing mana.

Arthur immediately touched Yudursh and willed him back into the ring before her spell could reach him.

She froze, stunned.

She stared at the empty space where Yudursh had vanished, heart pounding, powerless. Every instinct screamed to fight, to resist, to cling to pride. But that was a luxury she no longer had. The stranger pinning her had power, knowledge, and no mercy. What he asked for wasn’t just cooperation. It was submission. It was servitude.

And yet... Yudursh didn’t deserve to die for her stubbornness.

Her lips trembled, her throat dry. "Fine," she said at last, her voice trembling but sharp. The word dragged from her like it cost her blood.

Arthur allowed himself the faintest smile. "Good girl."

The irony wasn't lost on him. The princess of a slaver kingdom had now surrendered her own freedom to him. Poetic justice, really.
