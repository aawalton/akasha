Thousands of people gawked up at me from the bottom of the clay pyramid, along with dozens of the Gree-Cek warriors.

It seemed strange to me that Boretek was the only one that had four arms. But then again evolutions were known to change physiology. My faction hadn’t seen anything quite like added appendages yet, but something told me it was only a matter of time.

Considering what Alex might look like with two extra arms, I took in my surroundings.

Red adobe sprawled out in every direction, painting a vivid contrast to the rolling hills of light green sage. Many of the buildings were adorned in creeping vines and colorful flowers of deep purple somehow still vibrant despite the chilly weather.

Open villas with water features and wide streets with delicate archways. It was hard to believe this was the city Boretek was building.

It was beautiful.

It would have been more pleasant if he didn’t know what caused the rivets in the dirt paved streets of the city.

An entire faction, tens of thousands of people were led like lambs for the slaughter directly into the lion's den.

It bothered me that I couldn’t fight a creeping thought that told me that they’d gotten what they deserved. How could I not feel that way? Taylor and his faction were only here because they abandoned my dad, and hundreds of thousands of other humans to the leader of the Gree-Cek.

Instead of uniting, they sold themselves out.

But now that I was here...and could actually see the rivets caused by literal streams of blood, my heart was softened. It also reminded me of just how fortunate my faction actually was.

It didn’t mean that I agreed with Taylor, but his people didn’t deserve their fate. But, as discouraged as I was, I couldn't change the past, and there were still thousands of innocent people who were still alive.

With barely more than a thought I felt my new wings fold back away into my spirit robe. The addition was different than I expected. Instead of the wings acting as part of my body they performed more like equipment and my thoughts were their guide. It took some getting used to.

So rather than gliding from the top of the pyramid, I opted to use the steps.

It was hard to ignore the many eyes following me each step and by about halfway down the long steps I was feeling so awkward I just jumped the last fifty or so feet into the midst of the seated prisoners.

I’d already made up my mind on the fate of the Gree-Cek and I set my aim at the first one pushing his way through the crowd. There were only about fifteen in total and it was obvious from their movements they wanted to protect their assets. That didn’t surprise me. These were Boretek’s chosen, his favorites. His subordinates wouldn’t hurt them if they could help it.

The first one died without a sound. A single lunge split the air gliding above the humans' heads.

There was a feeble attempt to deflect piercing lunge, but it was pitifully unsuccessful, and a simple ethereal blade entered and exited his skull. He was still standing when I got the kill notification.

You have killed Cree-Gek Enforcer Level 42

During my fight against Boretek I learned a valuable truth. Most of the Gree-Cek had impressively high levels. They were even higher levels than the Chaos spawn waves from the scourge trial.

But even though their levels and stats were great, fighting with them exposed their true natures.

All those impressive stats were hollow.

Sure, compared to a faction like my dad’s, the difference in volume was more than enough. But against me? Or anyone in my faction? They were paper tigers.

None of these Gree-Cek had really earned their levels. And now, when those numbers were challenged, they crumbled.

Skills and spells were rebuffed by Phantom Blades, never even making it to my defensive barriers.

Tranquility burned brightly, granting me her shield and armor disrupting power up.

The fight was over in twenty seconds.

The whole thing felt mundane.

Seventeen piercing lunges, seventeen dead bodies. None of them even closed the distance. Killing like this always felt like such a waste. If things were different, could the Gree-Cek have become another faction like the Dark Elves, or the Elder Gnomes?

I dismissed the thought.

It didn’t do any good wondering what might’ve been.

Besides, it wouldn’t do much good anyway. They were all dead now.

Congratulations! You have Conquered the faction of the Gree-Cek. You have inherited their territories and properties.

Reward Calculating for Elimination of a major faction in the region...reward calculated.

Reward: Multi-regional map, Teleporter, Temperance (Rare Grade, upgradeable).

A brief scan of the rewards already had my interest piqued. There would be time to review them later.

In the meantime...

I exited the screen to find myself still surrounded by the human slaves of the Gree-Cek. And even though the slavers who were guarding them were dead as could be, no one so much as breathed.

Not sure how to proceed, I waved both my hands out to the crowd.

“Hello! My name is Layton, I’m from the faction Layton Mischief, and I’m here to set you free!”

My heroic proclamation was met with shifting glances and uneasy shuffling.

I crinkled my nose.

It wasn’t like I expected to be hoisted up by the masses and celebrated with shouts of praise or anything like that, but this?

A man with brown skin, dark eyes, and a straw hat raised his hand. I shifted my attention toward him and saw him subtly flinch away.

“Excuse me sir.” He said with a light Hispanic accent. “I don’t mean any disrespect, but you know, that was just a couple of dudes. They got an army you know. They’re gone now, but they’ll be back. You gonna kill them too?”

I laughed, realizing the problem. The Hispanic man shared concerned glances with some of the other people around him. They were probably terrified Boretek would show up at any moment and find his men dead.

Feeling a twinge of guilt, I pulled an item from my spatial storage and stuck it into the hard packed earth of the pavilion. One of Boretek’s spears vibrated gently from the force of me, slamming it down.

“My faction already handled Boretek and his army. If you don’t believe me, feel free to check your status screens. This territory is now a part of faction Layton Mischief.”

The man and many of the others seemed to take the suggestion seriously. Wide eyes dropped from the spear onto invisible screens.

“Dios mio...”

the man removed his hat and ran his hand through dark hair matted down from sweat and dirt and pushed his way through to the front of the crowd stopping only a step away from the spear.

“Rot in hell, four-armed bastard.” He said, spitting onto polished wood.

The action seemed to breathe life into the shocked mass of people, and in one motion the cheers began.

After placing the teleporter in the Gree-Cek capital and connecting it to LM Township, I left it up to the freed prisoners to decide what they’d like to do.

To my surprise, the teleporter that I earned from liberating their faction was much different than all the others we’d placed earlier. For starters, it took up considerably more space. I had to shoo away the people in the pavilion to clear enough room to place it. Once I’d cleared enough room It sprang into life.

Rather than a mere portal like what I was used to, it was an entire covered structure made of red adobe like the other buildings in the city. Four walls at least twice as tall as me enclosed the twenty-foot-wide portal with tasteful wide gates made of a dark wood I wasn’t familiar with.

The other convenient part was that I didn’t need to place its partner in LM Township. The teleporter here automatically used the interfaction teleporter as its destination by default.

With the connection placed, I didn’t feel a great need to stick around. If the people there decided to use the portal, great, if not, we’d be sending people to inhabit the area soon enough.

Jared was about to be very busy. In less than a few hours our faction exploded in both size and population, and it was coming off the back of several months of being locked away from our own region while at the tail end of a scourge trial.

We needed to get that man a team. He was doing impressively on his own for now, but with so many added people there was just no way he’d be able to keep up. Knowing Jared, he probably already had a list created of capable candidates.

For right now, he was taking stock of my dad's added faction. After my stop at the capital of the Gree-Cek we now owned all of their territory along with the eliminated Taylorite faction that Boretek massacred before heading to New Boise.

The thought of an entire city being butchered made me sick. The wind tussled my hair and I decided not to dwell on it. A decision that was becoming increasingly common lately.

I breathed in the crisp air and focused on the positives, feeling the rise and fall of my spirit robes newest upgrade.

I could fly.

That was a pretty nice positive.

From my vantage point high above the earth, I could see for miles in every direction. I urged the wings to bring me higher, feeling the rush of cool air passing through the delicate fabric to electrify my skin.

From so high, the city of New Boise crept into view.

Somehow, it seemed so small. I could see their Interfaction Teleporter at the city center, the grid like streets my dad had insisted on, wooden walls, and many freshly constructed plain wood homes. If I didn’t know any better, I might’ve mistaken it for a bustling new suburb from the olden days.

It really did seem like the city appeared from nowhere. It wasn’t like the mountain home of Layton Mischief Township where the entire city was surrounded by forests. New Boise floated in a sea of sage green and rolling hills as far as the eye could see.

Who knew what waited beyond those horizons. More monsters like Boretek? Humans that needed help? Or even other non-human factions? Something worse?

My world just became so much bigger.

We just barely broke free of the scourge trial, but somehow it felt like things were just getting started.
