The speed didn't lessen in the slightest before Luke slammed into Magan. What he hadn't expected was how it would feel like his body was trying to shatter a damn mountain. With threads extended in front of him, Luke reached into Magan even before he realized something was wrong, and he managed to drain a sip of mana and perform the one other action he always made sure to start a fight with before wind whipped against his face again. This time, he was flying straight up into the air.

Magan stood there in front of the portal, looking up at him with what looked like a smirk. No spellweaves formed, but a ritual circle full of pentagrams, stars, and other symbols sprang up around him, right under where Luke now reached the end of the attack’s momentum and began a too-fast descent. A spellweave formed in the circle, and Luke didn't need to get more than a quick look to identify what was about to happen. Not a fireball, but a pillar. It would burn him from below.

Since Luke wasn't about to let that happen, he weavestepped through Magan's echo thread, appearing right behind the Master Ritualist. Being able to teleport to an opponent was too much of an advantage to pass up, so Luke had decided to start healing everyone he faced, just for moments such as this one.

Luke lashed out with threads of mana, focusing to add the damage potential from what had once been Weaver's Whip, as well as the strengthening aspects now inherent in the threads. He aimed right at Magan's neck, but the threads fell apart even before they reached their mark.

"I thought you would be stronger, Lifeweaver," Magan said, turning around to show a relieved expression.

"How do you know my class?" Luke asked, even as he felt it. What Magan had used to push Luke off wasn't physical power or a spell. It was will. Magan was using his will to push back against Luke's, and he was wielding it with frightening expertise.

Rather than answer Luke's question, Magan lashed out again. This time, it was a frontal assault that pushed Luke backwards and through the portal, back to Earth, where he bounced once on the street before coming to a skidding halt back up on his feet.

Hannah: "We're just about done here. What about you?"

Luke: "I'm going to need a little more time."

Hannah: "Need help with anything?"

Luke: "Maybe. I'll let you know."

Facing the will of Magan was a lot like crashing into an actual mountain, one that moved and pushed like an inevitable force. Luke wasn't too proud to ask for help if he needed it, but he wasn't at that point yet. This might even turn out to be some good practice.

Magan strode through the portal, stopped, and looked around at the annihilated city. Rather than comment, ritual circles appeared on the ground next to him, three in total, but that wasn't all. More circles appeared in the air, all of them facing Luke. That done, the Master Ritualist began forming spellweaves. There would be no monologuing or chit-chat with this opponent.

Rather than wait to receive the myriad of different spells and curses, Luke weavestepped and appeared behind Magan again. This time, he focused for all he was worth and pushed threads forward with an unbending will. The threads would work this time. Even with Luke's conviction, they did not, in fact, make it through and into Magan's mana channels.

"Better," Magan said. "But you are far from proficient enough to contest my will."

The threads fell apart, and Luke soared through the air again, tossed upward like a ball. While the force driving the will-based attacks was powerful, they didn't do much damage. Was Magan toying with him?

The Master Ritualist raised his hands as if to unleash spells, then blinked and glanced at the ground, to where the ritual circles had been a moment earlier.

"That's right, asshole," Luke muttered even as he began falling. "You should pay better attention to your circles."

He'd hoped the attack would go through, of course, but a secondary objective had been to neutralize all those circles. Fighting Magan was enough on his own, and there was no need to make things more complicated by adding undead, and Luke was pretty certain that was what the ritualist had been attempting.

Luke weavestepped again, but this time his opponent was expecting it. A pushing force drove him back a step, but no more. Pushing back with his own will, straining to remain in place, Luke brought out his staff and slammed it home on Magan's head. A thin stream of blood ran from the ritualist's scalp, but he seemed otherwise unaffected, and the threads Luke extended from the weapon didn't find their mark either.

"My inferiors were a means to an end," Magan said just after tanking the blow. "The world you just visited is dead. Those born there, they who live and die, do not see it, but I do. Tell me, Luke, why did you come to the place I have spent all my life trying to escape?"

Rather than answering, Luke pushed back against another wave of force. Magan narrowed his eyes, and Luke found himself wrapped up in a great, invisible bear hug. Squeezing, Magan repeated his question. "Why did you come?"

Pushing with his arms didn't work, and there was nothing for his threads to cut apart. Extending his threads would mean reaching his opponent, but Luke knew how little that would change things. Rather than seethe and struggle, he felt a calm come over him. He wanted to learn, so he had better get to it.

"Your world does not exist," Luke began with a groan. If he breathed out, the force holding him tightened, like a great big snake squeezing the life out of him. "It did once, but not anymore. You don't exist."

Magan held up a hand, fingers splayed, and looked at it. "I assure you, I do exist. Did you create that waygate between our worlds? It is interesting, to say the least. The moment I felt its presence near my camp, I was compelled to go there without delay. Would you teach me the ritual in exchange for your life?"

"It wasn't a ritual," Luke grunted. It was difficult to speak while holding off the immense pressure threatening to grind him into paste.

Trying for a few seconds to breathe, Luke used The Healer's Moment. Without the active pushing of his will, Magan's invisible cage fell away, but the relief was short-lived. Even time being frozen wasn't enough to stop Magan's will, not for long. The moment shook and shuddered, then shattered before it was time. Magan himself stumbled, a look of surprise on his face, but he soon regained his footing.

Rather than launching himself at the other man, Luke remained still, keeping his concentration. "You needed to go through the portal because you are a creature from the void. A monster."

Magan raised an eyebrow and looked like he was about to reply, but he stopped himself before getting a word out and frowned. Wetting his lips, the Master Ritualist glanced down at his feet and put a hand to his own chest. When he looked back up at Luke, confusion warred with fear and anger on his face.

"My rituals. As I've improved my art, there have been moments of my consciousness touching upon something, something out of my world. A darkness, a terrible darkness."

"The void," Luke said, keeping his voice calm.

Magan shook his head. "I would call you a liar." Tears formed in his eyes.

"But you know I'm not lying," Luke said, leaning forward a little. This was not the reaction he had expected. He wasn't sure what he did expect, but not this. Not awareness. "You have a monster core, not a meta-heart. Your world is not real. You are not real."

"The whispers," Magan said, his own voice soft, as if on the verge of finding some great truth, or perhaps realizing Luke's. "I've heard singing in that darkness. Laughter in the void."

Magan closed his eyes. That's when Luke struck. He'd never get a better chance. Learning more about how the dungeons and the void came together to create vehicles of experience and power distribution was more than a little interesting, but this opponent was too strong, too dangerous.

Threads whipped through the air with all the concentration Luke could muster, all the will and focus he found himself able to gather. Even with all that, it wasn't enough.

At the last possible moment, Magan opened his eyes. The threads still wrapped around his neck and arms, but they could neither cut through skin nor enter his mana channels. Rage and sorrow shone from Magan's eyes, and he bared his upper teeth in a snarl.

"A monster, you say? Not real, you claim?" The threads burned into nothing, and the Master Ritualist stepped right up to Luke, his breath wheezing. "Yet I stand here in your world. I reason. I have a past. A life. With what right do you call me monstrous? Perhaps I was brought here to claim your world as my own? The void is just another mystery for me to unveil and bend to my will!"

The words were convincing, and Magan's presence was frightening in a way that even the demon lord hadn't managed, but there was something lacking in them. For the first time, Luke's opponent wasn't focused.

Seizing the opportunity, Luke pushed, not with threads or his staff, but with his will. Magan wielded his will like a weapon and a shield, so Luke would do the same. The instant of advantage gave him the edge, but it wasn't enough. Luke's will, honed to a weapon, sought to destroy Magan's monster core and put an end to the fight, but the Master Ritualist was too quick to react, and an invisible force appeared to block the attempt.

The two of them stared at each other. Luke's still raw and unhoned will was like a raging torrent against the steadfast, unrelenting mountain that was Magan's. Still, Luke wouldn't give up, because that was not an option. Losing? No way.

An even more terrible thing he'd noticed when tossed around on the Earth side was how the void, or the beings therein, seemed to take greater notice of Earth when Magan was in it. Was that it? Monsters needed to get out of dungeons through the portals to attract the void through their presence? It was a reasonable conclusion, but plenty of monsters had come to Earth already. What about those times? Luke realized the answer to that question the moment he spared it a thought. Others had been there to keep Earth safe then. That was no longer the case. When the realization dawned on him, Luke sensed it. The weave trembled. The void was trying to get in at that very moment.

Hannah: "Luke? I think something is wrong."

Luke: "Yes."

Luke: "It's the void. See if you can find the others."

Magan had noticed it too, and wore a thoughtful expression. Luke's scattered attention was enough that the Ritual Master could have toppled him, but that hadn't happened.

"It's calling out to me," Magan said, once again touching his chest, right over the monster core. "I can feel it wanting me back. There's something..." He paused for a moment, growing very still before nodding to himself, like he'd come to some decision.

Magan locked eyes with Luke and gave a brief nod as his will dissipated. The immense pressure disappeared in that moment, and it was as if the weight Luke had been pushing against had vanished. Taken off guard, Luke didn't lessen the force of his will, and Magan, the Master Ritualist, died in an instant, his chest exploding in a shower of gore.

The portal closed. Panting, Luke fell onto his hands and knees. He noticed a glint of light bouncing off an object within Magan's remains. It was his monster core. Luke reached out with a thread of mana and retrieved it. Magan must have done something to protect that part of himself. Was there something in there of the Master Ritualist, some part of what he'd once been? According to Guide, as far as Luke understood it, there might once have been a real Magan in that now-dead system. It was something to consider, he thought as he looked into the swirling light of the spherical, unblemished monster core. Magan had felt the void and known what he was. Rather than embrace that, he'd opted to tap out. An honorable choice, especially for a supposed monster. This core, Luke wouldn't feed to Smudge.

Even as drained as he was, there was no time to recover. The void was closing in on several points in the weave, and there was no one coming to save Earth. They would have to save themselves.

Good thing Luke had just gone through several rounds that seemed almost tailored to hone his will-craft.
