Ciro stood still, the uneven wooden flooring creaking under him. A strange flicker of light danced on the edges of his vision. It was unlike any of the other screens and messages he had received. The message wasn’t translucent blue, instead it was covered in red, like a child painted over it in blood. An unrestricted message. What does that even mean? questioned Ciro.

The message clung to the center, refusing to be ignored. He stepped outside to be with the other three men. He tried to see their reactions, peering over the message. Neither Luka nor the twins seemed to have a reaction.

A sinking feeling of worry washed over Ciro. A message only he received was bad news. He didn’t need someone to tell him that. He knocked his fist against his thigh, unsure of what to do. Can they read my mind? Are they listening?

He looked around the barren landscape. The air had a rotten, dirty quality, as if time not only destroyed civilization but also the environment. Was it anxiety, or just pure paranoia? Ciro didn’t know, but his instincts rarely ever failed him. Not when he focused. He was being watched.

Arbiters ran the games. Ciro was at the mercy of the man calling himself Palou. A man whose appearance he didn’t even know. A realization that he could be watching them at all times washed over him. Ciro felt exposed, naked, everything laid out bare. He thought back to the jungle when he received that coin.

His fingers trailed his pocket, the familiar circular shape still bulged from within. Clan Kuta, he remembered the name. They knew he stole from the Xyphons and they sent the message to let him know. Within the deep expanse of space, his every action was monitored.

Ciro was small. Insignificant. Even if they watched him, why would it matter?

The message that lay in front of him defied that assumption.

A message error. Ciro didn’t know what it meant. He wasn’t supposed to get this message; he knew that much. Worst of all, he couldn’t ignore it.

“What’s going on with him, Birk?” Ivo’s voice broke Ciro’s thoughts.

Birk put a hand over his mouth. “I think he’s been stunned by doing all those push-ups.”

Luka stepped towards him. With his tall stature, he had to bend his back slightly to meet Ciro’s eyes. “You okay?” he asked.

Ciro forced a smile. “Yeah, I’m okay. I just keep looking at the timer for this event, is all.” He tried looking at Luka’s face, but the red screen completely covered him. There was no way for him to read a facial expression, but he could sense the worry from the three of them. Ciro appreciated their empathy, a fresh feeling compared to being with Alya and Carina.

Sender: @#$& - Sender unknown. (Message not part of Arbiter’s monitored network.)DO YOU SEEK POWER?

Ciro swallowed hard. He looked around them once again. “Let’s just get moving,” he said weakly. Luka and the three men walked ahead, allowing Ciro a few feet of space. Focusing on their heels, he walked behind them, slow enough to maintain his distance.

Sender: @#$& - Sender unknown. (Message not part of Arbiter’s monitored network.)THE ARBITER COULDN’T FIND YOU. HE WILL SOON.

What does that mean he couldn’t find me

Sender: @#$& - Sender unknown. (Message not part of Arbiter’s monitored network.)FOLLOW MY INSTRUCTIONS, AND YOU WILL BECOME STRONGER.

What the hell was this? A trick made by the Arbiter? Ciro knew he wasn’t hallucinating, he already had a voice in his head to contend with. Yet with each step he took, he found himself constantly glancing at the message. Let’s hear him out, Ciro thought.

the arbiter has found you. you’re good at making enemies.

Why is the Arbiter looking for me? As far as Ciro understood, the Arbiters role was to carry out and manage this whole fiasco. Why did Palou care about him? All types of beings were dying left and right, yet he was searching for him. That begs the question, how did he supposedly lose me in the first place, Ciro pondered.

Don’t worry I have this all setup.

Ciro wasn’t worried, or at least that’s what he thought anyway. Anxiety maybe, but the frustration of knowing he was just some animal that they could watch for entertainment. It sickened him.

Keep walking.

Ciro’s eyes shifted back to the heels of Luka and the twins. He questioned what this mysterious person was plotting. Why am I trusting it, he racked his brain, trying to rationalize his actions. When it came down to it, a big red screen latched onto his face. It was offering him help, but if this were some sort of trap…then so be it, Ciro thought in finality. If there was a chance he’d get something out of this, it was worth the risk.

Turn left.

Stopping in his tracks, Ciro looked to his left. The screen didn’t budge an inch. It seemed to react to his frustration because the screen disappeared finally. The red blockade disappeared, revealing a large abandoned building. A fence surrounded the building, knitted with eroding metal. With a single touch, it would most likely crumble.

The building was perhaps the most stable thing Ciro had seen on this planet. Made out of red brick on the bottom layer, with more metal protruding out from the top. It made Ciro think of a factory.

There were other buildings that lined across. They seemed to watch Ciro, daring him to enter inside.

“Anything caught your eye, Ciro?” Luka asked, moving beside him, looking at the same building. “I’ll be damned, a building that hasn’t been completely toppled.”

Go inside

Pointing the finger towards the gate, Ciro looked towards the group. “Let’s check it out.” He tried to make his tone as calm as possible, despite the tension in his heart. The screen disappeared after a few seconds. The message was clear.

The twins shivered. “No way we’re going in there,” they hissed in unison.

Rolling his eyes, Ciro moved forward towards the gate. The ground was surprisingly even, the slabs on the path were still smooth, an occasional crack decorated them. A place that was less tainted by destruction still showed the signs of what happened to the rest of the city.

“I don’t like this,” one of the twins whispered.

“There might be someone inside, besides this building looks like it’ll have something hidden.” Luka said, shooting daggers at Ivo and Birk. “I was able to find a revolver inside a building, it was a shame it only had 4 rounds.”

Ciro reached the gate, slowly pushing it open. The fence fell apart, keeling over onto the floor. They stepped past it towards the entrance. A green door with paint that Ciro could’ve probably peeled off if he wanted to. Just as he was about to open it, an arm came between him.

“Let me do it,” said Luka. He tried opening with the handle, but the door didn’t budge. Clenching his jaw, he rammed his shoulder into it. They crashed open.

The twins let out a whistle. “The boss is so strong.”

They entered the room. Ciro stepped cautiously, his boots echoed against the metallic floor. Just like all the other buildings they had been in, dust clung to every corner. It became apparent that it truly was a factory, as Ciro brushed his fingers against a conveyor belt. The factory had a distinct smell of rust that wafted from the different appliances scattered around.

A faint beam of green light penetrated a crack in the ceiling, casting shadows that danced eerily.

“Just another abandoned building,” whispered Ciro.

Luka looked around, occasionally having a duck, so his head didn’t hit one of the appliances. “What did you expect? It’s another place too small for my body.”

The twins rushed ahead, jumping on the conveyor belts and laughing. I thought they were scared, Ciro grumbled. He ignored the twins and moved deeper inside. He was waiting for another message to appear, but nothing happened.

Why did it send me here?

A strange sensation pricked the back of his neck. He turned to the farthest corner of the factory. Tucked beneath a pile of broken metal and debris, something stood out.

A briefcase.

It wasn’t covered in dust like everything else. Its metallic surface glinted under the green light. Ciro looked towards the three men. They were all preoccupied rustling through wooden boxes and inspecting the equipment.

You’ve found it. Now head towards it. You won’t be dissatisfied.

Ciro peered once again at Luka and the twins. He stepped towards it. He crouched in front of the briefcase, reaching out cautiously. No markings. No symbol. Just a simple, silver briefcase that clearly didn’t belong here. His fingers hovered over the latch, hesitation creeped in. He let out a dry laugh. What if it was a bomb?

“Oh look, Ciro’s found something,” one of the twins called out.

The three men grouped around him, inspecting the briefcase. Luka tapped the casing. He looked towards them.

“Who knows what it could be?”

The twins seemed to gain a realization, stepping away from the case. “It’s a trap,” their eyes narrowed in suspicion.

A pang of guilt struck Ciro. If it was a trap, it would be all his fault. He sighed, the curiosity overwhelming his rationale. He flipped the latch open.

CLICK

The sound caused the twins to jump, leaving Luka to roll his eyes as he watched Ciro over his shoulder.

Both of them looked inside with confusion.

“What is it?” Luka asked.

“I don’t…know,” replied Ciro, who noticed a note attached on the top of the briefcase:

Use it well. Don’t expect anymore messages soon. I’ll see you on the Exofront,

M.B

Palou had finally found Ciro Adams. In a way, his insatiable desire to see Ciro die had smothered Palou’s obsession with his star. He peered at his map, showing all his contestants. Even with Ciro showing up through the camera, he still didn’t appear on the map.

How was it possible? He didn’t know. Trying to find out would be a pointless endeavor, considering he had already found him. The only problem was that Adams wasn’t the only person who didn’t appear on the map. The three new friends he made also weren’t showing up. Someone was trying to hide him. Palou couldn’t fathom why.

To make matters worse there was clearly a glitch with that man's system. The buffoon had yet to realize he had an unopened mystery box. When Palou tried deleting it from his inventory, it only came up with more errors.

“Perhaps this mystery is a part of the story,” whispered Palou. But a story was only good when he was writing it. I’ll cut this short soon enough, he thought.

The predator lottery was an idea that Palou had once he saw the stigmata known as “Feral”. It turned the unfortunate soul into a depraved monster that only sought blood. It was a Stigmata that was banned from the directory, citing that it took all rationality from Exofronteers and was simply cruel. This meant it wasn’t allowed for Arbiters to gift.

Already breaking so many rules, he didn’t feel an ounce of regret handing the stigmata to the unlucky few who were selected. Their transformation was irreversible. It was their own misfortune that led them to that fate. A good story always had a hint of unpredictability. Those who were selected to be predators were destined to die.

He watched Adams and his group head into another building. At first Palou thought little of it until Ciro moved towards the corner of the room. That specific corner. Palou furrowed his brow.

Warning

Droid Too Close To Contestant, Unable To Get Closer

Adams had moved to the exact spot where his droids couldn’t reach him. The droids had a limited radius; there were the slightest, almost negligible of blind spots.

Why did I just find this out now? Palou cursed inwardly. How did the engineering team overlook such a flaw?

Programmers set each one to stay a certain distance from contestants; otherwise, they would breach personal space. High level Exofronteers can sense the cameras, so it made them feel on edge with them so close by. Palou couldn’t make the droid go any closer.

Adams moved just to the right spot where he couldn’t see him. He held his monitor, shaking it in frustration. Someone was playing with him. Palou’s burning desire to see what Adam was hiding cooled.

He let out a maniacal laugh. This was his world. He decided every person’s fate on that planet, only him alone. No one could dare take that away from him. Not even Marinus Ballack himself.

He moved over to his monitor with his admin controls. “Let’s see, hmm, lovely, a predator is near enough, no need to Zeta beam him.”

He clicked onto the mini-map of the nearest predator, with a single button he had pinpointed a waypoint. If the creature had any rationale left, he’ll head straight there.

Straight to its prey. Straight to Ciro Adams.
