Chapter V.XXVI (5.26) - Family Dinner

“Hand me another jar of special paste you made,” Ione said. “We’ve nearly run out over near Professor Knoff’s cave.”

She sat on a pillow in a meditative pose with her eyes closed. She was at the center of eight different summoning circles sketched on the ground in chalk. The most recently added had a green creature crawl out of it and waddle in Kizu’s direction.

Kizu handed over a jar and brush to a dopey kappa who waddled off with it balanced on the bald dish at the crown of his scalp.

Rather than wander the hallways, posting signs that explain the usage of the different water fountains in the academy, Ione was acting as a hive mind unit and sending out summons in all different directions. Her coordination was exemplary. However, even more surprising than that, was the fact that she actually volunteered this solution. She explained that it was a system she’d studied during an insectoid monster research binge and that she’d always wanted an excuse to try it for herself.

Kizu’s job was simply to organize and hand out the supplies to the summoned monsters.

“Do you know where Sene is?” Ione asked suddenly.

Setting aside a poster that explained a specific knocking pattern, Kizu examined his girlfriend. She made no movement and kept her eyes closed in concentration.

“No. Why would I?”

“She’s in town.”

“Fine.” Kizu waited, knowing this must be leading somewhere.

“On a date.”

Kizu held back his surprise. “How do you know? Do you have a bird watching her from above? You know your sister is allowed to have a romantic life.”

Ione cracked an eye open wide enough to roll it, then closed it again. “I’m just making small talk. Plus, my summons are too busy to spy on her.” She paused. “But if you wanted to….”

“I’m not going to spy on her on your behalf,” Kizu said flatly.

“I didn’t say that,” Ione grumbled. “Still, I want to know who he is. She deserves payback after gossiping to our mother about you.”

“Didn’t she only say good things about me?”

“Who’s to say I wouldn’t do likewise?”

Now it was Kizu’s turn to roll his eyes. He went to the next room where Faible marked red X’s on a map of the academy that was covered in black dots. Each unmarked dot signified where a sign needed to go. He was sorting and labeling them appropriately.

Kizu reported the most recent wave of Ione’s monsters and Faible adjusted the map’s markings.

The chore continued for another two hours. By the time they distributed all the posters across campus, dinner was getting served down in the dining hall. Kizu was tempted to join Ione and Faible there, but with Mae just returning to the island earlier that week, he decided he should prioritize having dinner down at Taroe’s place instead. So he bid the two of them farewell.

As he exited the academy’s campus, he noticed the bluff overlooking the town. It was the same spot where he’d seen Harvey practicing his flute in the past. His pace slowed a bit as he tried to recall the last time he’d seen the Tainted boy. It must have been all the way back at his birthday party. And, even then, it had only been briefly in passing. Harvey hadn’t even been participating in the weekly fights recently.

“I should follow up with him sometime soon,” Kizu muttered to himself as he continued down the path. “Make certain everything is fine. It could be that with the Tainted students back on campus he’s having troubles again.”

As he walked down the trail into town he noted that the Dragon corpse in the center of town looked to have been completely dismantled and removed. The damaged buildings were rebuilt, looking better than ever.

While Kizu still had a long way to walk, Mort was already down in Taroe’s home, snacking on some strawberries he’d stolen when the grumpy ex-Elite hadn’t been watching. A glance through Mort’s senses revealed more to Kizu than just a juicy strawberry flavor.

Wakino sat at the kitchen table across from Taroe. For once, the spatial mage had her entire face uncovered and present. That included both eyes she currently was using to stare down her ex-husband. The two adults sat there, silently locked in a staring contest that neither looked to have any intention of breaking.

None of the kids were currently in the room. Mort was only up on a shelf because he’d stolen the strawberries.

There was a roasted bird off to the side, garnished with sprigs of green sprouts and accompanied by wisps of heat. The scent of the roasted bird through Mort’s senses made Kizu’s mouth water. Though that could also partially be a result of the sweet mouthful of strawberry in the monkey’s mouth.

Without consciously considering it, Kizu realized he’d picked up his pace.

In record time, his legs carried him through the town streets.

A sight brought him up short. He genuinely hadn’t intended to encounter her, but Sene stood outside a restaurant talking to a young man dressed smartly in an old fashioned suit. A metal chain crossed the suit’s left side over into the pocket and there were three times as many buttons as what was on a normal outfit. Kizu was certain Basil would be able to place it on the exact fashion trend timeline, but Kizu just knew it looked older. Like something he’d expect Vincent to wear.

Sene’s posture was different from normal as she stared up at the young man, still stiff but more nervous than her standard prim or irritated flavor of stiff.

As Kizu watched, the man leaned down and pecked her with a kiss with his deep scarlet lips. Sene took a step back in surprise, but then retraced that step back in his direction.

“Slow down, Kon!” a child yelled.

Kizu, realizing once again that Sene’s love life wasn’t any of his business, shifted to focus on something that was his business.

A pair of girls were chasing a white fox through the streets. Kizu winced as the fox darted through the legs of a woman carrying a large basket of fruit. Thankfully, she regained her balance. But then she was subject to new threats as Mae and Anata nearly smacked the basket out of her hands as they dashed by.

Kizu chased after the children, but, before he left the street behind, he glanced over his shoulder. The young man gazed back. He stood under the eaves, in the building’s shadow. Even still, Kizu thought he saw a glint in his eyes, as if caught by the setting sun. For some reason, it sent a chill down his back.

Most people, thankfully, stepped aside as the children darted through the town streets but Kizu still muttered apologies to the bemused townsfolk as he ran after them. Once they exited an alleyway into an empty street, Kizu jumped forward. Not directly in front of them, he wasn’t about to risk accidentally colliding with them, but in a clear enough position that they obviously saw him as he appeared.

Kon skittered to a halt and the girls tripped over the suddenly immobile Kitsune. They tumbled into a heap on the cobblestone street.

Sighing, Kizu strolled up to them and crouched down.

Instead of reprimanding them, Kizu simply lifted Anata back to her feet. Then did the same for Mae. While he didn’t say anything, he was surprised by Anata’s weight. She was nearly the same as Mae now. A far cry from the state where he’d met her licking moss off the walls of a prison cell.

“We’re trying to get home!” Mae said hurriedly. “I’m sorry we’re late getting back. We lost track of time.”

“I’d rather you’re all late than ramming through half the people in town,” Kizu replied. “Come on. Let’s get over to Taroe’s place. If he complains about you being tardy just blame it on me. Tell him I got distracted and was walking slow.”

When they arrived at the house, Taroe didn’t reprimand their punctuality. In fact, he didn’t even look at them as they took their seats around the dinner table. Kizu doubted the man had even blinked since he last checked in through Mort. His gaze remained fixed on his ex-wife.

“Good of you all to join us,” Wakino said, also not breaking eye contact. She raised a hand to the side. The plates and cutlery shot out and placed themself in front of each person at the table. Piles of white mashed potatoes followed the plates, plopping down in circular chunks. Then the roast bird rose up above the center of the table. She jerked her hand down, two fingers extended, and the meat split into pieces, falling onto the tableware.

“Did you just… split space?” Kizu asked. He lifted his slice of roasted bird with two fingers. It remained warm to the touch. The cut was perfect through, severing straight through in a seamless slice.

“Yes,” Wakino replied. “It is my next lesson for you. I noted during the Dragon attack that you had a unique style of using dimensionalism to launch objects at your foes and snap space together. I believe a simpler offensive spell will be easier to utilize effectively in most fights. You already have a basic comprehension of barrier creation, this is simply a branching step from that.”

Kizu lifted his fork and chomped down on a mouthful of meat while he considered her words. The bird was a bit dry but still a cut above what he was likely to find at the academy. That wasn’t to say the cafeteria food was bad by any means. This was simply better. Even dry.

“You’re sharpening the barriers,” Kizu concluded. “After that, you force them at the target to cut into it. Not that different from an elemental mage who creates daggers of ice.”

Wakino nodded. “Good.”

“Instead of spatial school, why don’t we discuss why you’ve decided to unexpectedly drop by today?” Taroe said. He had made no move to eat the food in front of him. His face could have been cut from stone for all the emotion on it. Kizu thought he saw more life in most golems he’d met.

Wakino picked up a knife and carved into her meat in two, succinct movements. “Fine. I will cut the pleasantries and save our lessons for another day. Excuse my blunt words. As one of the few people trusted by the Hon government outside of the current lockdown, I had extremely sensitive information reach my ears yesterday. I do not blame any of you for keeping these secrets from me; however, I now find myself in a position where I feel I must step in.”

Kizu nearly choked on his potatoes. Nobody made a move to help him as he spluttered and drowned the potatoes with a glass of water.

“What, exactly, were you told?” he croaked once he’d mostly recovered. “And who else knows?”

“I spoke to none other than Wan, assistant to Emperor Sasaki. As far as I am aware, only those in this room are privy to this information. Anata is not my ex-husbands bastard daughter but instead is the Harbinger of the Blood Lord. And both Mae and the fox at her feet eating the scraps she drops to him, are the children of the Harbinger of the Kitsune.”

A moment of silence. After being called out, Mae had frozen in place, a strip of meat dangling from her fingers on the way to Kon’s snout.

“And… what will you do with that information?” Kizu asked.

Wakino continued to only have eyes for Taroe as she answered him. “I will do what I must. Now that I am aware that Taroe has no blood connection to the girl, Anata, one of my priorities is to lessen his grip on her. He is an incompetent parent and should never have been given this responsibility.”

Taroe growled.

“You want to help take care of Anata?” Kizu asked, ignoring Taroe’s response. “That’s fine. But you need to talk to me about it, not Taroe. I’m her true blood relation.”

Finally, Wakino tore her eyes from her ex-husband to examine Kizu. “Truly? And here I believed that it was simply your connection to Emperor Sasaki and Princess Aoi that gave you the position as her minder. Interesting.” Then she returned to matching Taroe’s glare. “We will discuss more on this later. The concern I currently find the most pressing is the information I’ve been given about the Kitsune girl, Mae. My understanding is that she was recently adopted by one of the witch covens of Tross. Is that correct?”

“Yes,” Kizu said. “She—”

“I wish for her to answer me,” Wakino cut in. “Speak, Mae.”

Mae shifted in her seat and looked over her shoulder at the front door. Her eyes lingered on it for several seconds. But Wakino was not someone who could be easily escaped. In fact, Kizu was willing to bet the spatial mage might very well be the most difficult person on the island to flee the presence of.

Either Mae understood the fruitlessness in attempting to bolt from the room, or she just realized that evading the questions wouldn’t get her anywhere in the long run regardless. She slouched and pulled her witch hat’s brim down to cover her face.

“Yes,” she mumbled. “I’m a witch now.”

“What, exactly, did you tell these witches about your origins? Do they understand that your mother is a Harbinger of the Great Labyrinth Sekai?”

“No. They assumed I had some weird druid blood in my lineage that transforms me into a fox sometimes. I didn’t say anything about my family.”

Wakino took a bite of her meal. She didn’t chew or swallow, the food simply disappeared from the end of the fork as it entered her mouth.

“That was my greatest concern. Your evasiveness proved very prudent and you have impressed me. I doubt that lies by omission will stand long when placed against your master so we will need to discuss how to approach her with your true background. However, that will keep for another day. First, tell me everything.”
