Asher popped into existence in the back of a dark room, trying not to cough at the sheer amount of dust in the air as he looked around. They’d gone ahead and instructed the pegasus courier to drop off the package with the village elder, and from there, it looked as though it had been tossed into some random study that was cluttered with papers and in desperate need of a good dusting. The package itself had been a simple letter addressed to a completely made-up person. Rather than let some poor soul spend a few hours trying to figure out who it was supposed to be given to or what to do with it, Asher stole it back, shifting his Eternal Mark to the floor for the moment in the event they needed to leave and come back.

Slipping into the astral, Asher walked through the wall and into the village, looking around curiously at the people going about their days. Overall, the village was pretty similar to many of the others he’d seen, other than the fact that this one seemed a bit more rural and primitive than most. Rather than standard clothes fashioned from wool or linens, the men and women all wore simple pelts, and the children smaller outfits that appeared to be made primarily from long feathers. After watching a few kids laugh and toss a leathery ball back and forth as they ran through the village, Asher moved out of sight behind the building the letter had been in, returning from the astral before yanking everyone out of his rift.

“We’re right in the center of the village, so don’t be alarmed if we freak a few people out,” he reminded them, getting nods from everyone. While they’d debated whether keeping a lower profile or not was a good idea, at the end of the day, they’d decided shock and awe was a bit more important for their purposes. They wanted to get whatever information they could out of the villagers, and if the villagers were amazed at how a group of five strangers had managed to appear in the center of their village out of nowhere, that was presumably better for them.

Just as they’d predicted, the moment they stepped out from the shadow of the building, they drew plenty of attention. Asher tried not to laugh as a man actively sharpening a stone axe head missed the blade completely and smacked the person in the head next to him with the sharpening stone, or how a woman in the middle of butchering some sort of large chicken dropped her knife completely.

“Fifty shards say they come at us with weapons,” Moxy muttered, already cracking her knuckles in anticipation.

“We’re here for intel, not a battle,” Asher replied, putting on his best smile as he nodded toward an older woman with a feather-filled headdress who was quickly approaching them. Her aura was like a soothing pit of mud, bubbling and swirling around her, hinting at both depth and danger if one didn’t tread carefully.

“Who are you and how did you get past the outward scouts?” she demanded, her eyes narrowed as she looked hard between the five of them. Her gaze lingered on Kroult for a moment, but Asher figured that was mainly due to his outfit. While Kroult did have a simple illusion spell that made himself look like a normal person, if a bit more pale and frail than most, he didn’t like to rely on it. Instead, he preferred going with the tried and true method of wearing a really big cloak and keeping the hood pulled down over his face.

“Woah, that’s a new one. I’ve heard a few other languages before, but I don’t have the faintest idea what language she’s speaking,” Moxy admitted. “This place is pretty far indeed.”

“Me neither,” Samantha frowned.

“I’ve heard it before and can get by if needed, but not well,” Rulfar said, turning toward Asher. “This one is all you.”

With a nod, Asher turned back toward the woman who now looked even more confused after hearing them all converse in some foreign language.

“Sorry for the intrusion. My friends and I are here for a very important mission, and we were hoping to get your help with it.”

“A mission?” she repeated, fidgeting with one of the feathers in her headdress as she looked at him. “What kind of mission? We’ve rejected recruiters before, and we will do so again. None of us are going to war.”

“No, we don’t need anything like that,” Asher promised. “Just some information. We’re here to try to take care of The Lost Lady once and for all.”

“The Lost Lady?” the chief said, recoiling slightly at the news. “You are serious? It has been many moons since the last team arrived in our village with that goal in mind. I was but a girl, still in my feathers when I watched them enter the caves. They claimed to be specialists when it came to undead and spirits, with decades of experience between all of them and all sorts of fancy equipment. They never came back out.”

“We’re hoping to have a bit more luck then they did,” Asher admitted. “But first, we wanted to pick your brains a bit. Maybe there’s something you can tell us about The Lost Lady that would help us take her down? I read her last attack on the village was nearly three years ago. Do you remember what happened?”

“I am not so old that I can’t remember what happened three years back,” the chief frowned at him. “Come. Follow me.”

Motioning for his friends to follow him, Asher let the chief lead them over to the side of the village where they found a woman hard at work in a small field beside her home. There were two young boys that looked to be about seven or eight busy plucking seeds out of some unknown fruit, and they watched from afar as the chief approached the lone woman.

“Ulma. Do you have a moment to spare to speak with a few strangers? They wish to hear the tale of your husband.”

Shoving her shovel into the ground, Ulma frowned at the six of them, narrowing her eyes as she took them in. Her gaze lingered on Rulfar for a moment, but for an entirely different reason than the chief’s on Kroult. Rather than suspicion, Ulma looked a bit more hungrily as she took in the muscular, seven-foot-tall bloodhound with the giant battle axe strapped across his back. Asher tried to ignore the way Moxy snickered and elbowed Rulfar in the gut as Rulfar himself did his best to smile politely.

“I can make time,” she said, leaning on the top of the shovel. “What do they want to know? And why?”

“We’re here to try and put The Lost Lady down,” Asher repeated, getting the appropriate wide-eyed look he’d expected. “Anything you can share about her attack would be helpful. Weaknesses, how she attacked, if she said anything. Whatever you can think of.”

“As much as I’d love to see that spirit get what’s coming to her, there’s not much to tell,” Ulma admitted, turning and gesturing back to the house. “Though I remember it like it was yesterday. The boys were getting dinner prepped while Grant and I were finishing up in the field. The sun had long since gone down, but we wanted to get one last row planted. Then, out of nowhere, she appeared.”

Ulma paused, shivering at the memory.

“At first, I thought it was some crazy foreigner. She stood there in the moonlight, naked as the day she was born with long, white hair purer than any bone sweeping around her. She looked no older than myself, so I didn’t realize who she was. Not until she strode forward and grabbed Grant by the throat. It wasn’t until I tried to drive my shovel through her back that I realized what was happening… that The Lost Lady had come for my Grant.”

“I’m sorry… that sounds terrible,” Asher said, hesitating as he went to ask her to keep going. As important as all this was, he wasn’t exactly a fan of encouraging people to relive traumatic events. Thankfully, Ulma nodded and continued talking without him having to say anything.

“My shovel struck true, piercing deep into her chest. Yet The Lost Lady didn’t even seem to notice. Grant stabbed at her with his trowel, forcing the sharpened tip into her chest again and again. But just like my own attack, his did no better. Her flesh… it moved like water. No blood emerged from our strikes, and no wounds were visible when our tools were pulled out. She didn’t make so much as a sound as she turned and started to drag Grant away. I screamed and tried to hack at her legs, hoping to hobble her and slow her down. That’s the last thing I remember.”

“We found Ulma the next morning, lying flat on her back in her field with her head cracked open,” the chief explained. “It was a miracle she managed to survive such a wound. Somehow, enough of her hair got caught up in the wound that it slowed down the bleeding.”

“I’m glad at the very least you were alright. For both your sake and your children,” Asher said, his mind racing with possibilities as he thought about the story. The fact that The Lost Lady hadn’t retaliated until Ulma had tried to hinder her mobility felt like an important piece of information. It was a shame that she couldn’t remember exactly how The Lost Lady had attacked her, but Asher would take what he could get.

“You and me both,” Ulma snorted, hefting her shovel as her knuckles whitened around it. “I’m planning to make sure leaving me alive is the biggest mistake of that spirit’s unnatural life. I’ve kept this very shovel that I failed to kill her with on me at all times whenever I work the fields, and I’m going to drive this thing into her damn chest as many times as it takes to kill her the next time she comes to the village.”

Nodding along with her spirit, Asher found himself staring at the shovel clenched between her hands. It was completely mundane, Identify made that perfectly clear. Nothing more than a smoothed over piece of wood with a carved rock attached to it. Though something about it continued to draw his attention. It was almost similar in a way to those strange pillars they’d spotted in the wild realm that tried to warn them of the undead monster hiding within the crimson mists. Back then, Asher had thought that cloudy feeling of information he’d been struggling to get from them was something to do with the translation aspect of his Knowledge element, but now…

“Ulma, can I see that shovel for a second?” Asher asked, his brow furrowed as he tried to figure out what it was that was bothering him. It wasn’t Identify, and it wasn’t Secret Seeker, he could tell that much. But something drew him to the shovel. It was like a gentle buzz in the back of his mind that he couldn’t quite quiet down.

“So long as you don’t go anywhere with it,” she said, tossing it over to him. The moment Asher’s hands closed around the wood, his eyes shot open as the world around him twisted and rippled. Without warning, Asher found himself standing in the same field he’d been in a few seconds ago, only the sun had been replaced with a glowing moon, and far more importantly…

Barely a dozen feet to his right was a naked woman who radiated power and confidence. With pale white hair draped down to her ankles, she walked straight at him, her eyes focused directly on his own. Asher’s breath caught and he froze as she stalked ever toward him. He didn’t know where he was, what had happened, or why he was suddenly alone, but he did know one thing.

The Lost Lady was coming right for him.
