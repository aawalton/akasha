A hundred thousand humans held their collective breaths within the tall walls of Daha as ten thousand demons assembled beyond the Grand Moat, every one of them salivating over the prospect of conquering the first of humanity's great walled cities.

If Daha fell here, there would be no tomorrow. No future in which children grew up in peace and harmony. As free beings, unfettered by the nightmares of demonkind.

Jalasa had fallen, her own Raaka had fallen, and Princess Tiyana Matali’s plan to gather up a honeypot had failed to materialize. Politics and logistics alike had stalled the gathering of all of humanity’s forces, and without their full might, Daha stood little chance even with their five-to-one advantage in numbers.

A loss here would confirm to every demon that the entire human realm lay for the taking. It set a precedent for the invading army, and moreover, a loss on this day would kill Tia’s grand plan before it ever had the chance to be born.

Yet today, Hiranya had an advantage no army could boast in the past. Not even the one that fell at the battle of Khantaghati Pass.

Today, they had a Saint on their side.

For at long last, after untold deaths and uncountable losses, Maiya had had a sudden turn of heart.

For the first time since the beginning of the war, she would take to the field.

It should have made Tia happy. It should have set her fears at ease, and it did.

But she had to question whether it was truly a change of heart their Saint had… or whether she’d simply snapped.

For it seemed that she had not only neglected her armor or any weapon with which to defend herself… she hadn’t even thought to wear shoes.

Tia watched on from the ramparts of Daha’s great wall with hope and fear… and prayed their Saint could, if nothing else, convey a message to the enemy.

She could not have known at the time how correct she would be.

Wearing nothing but a thin, flowing red dress that matched her hair and walking barefoot upon the grass, Maiya thought she could not project a better image of a deity gracing the land.

With small steps, she strode calmly forward for the most effect, and what an effect it had.

The humans behind her cheered with roars so loud she wondered if the soldiers would retain enough hearing for the battle ahead.

Of course, the flames of prana billowing off her body in every color of the rainbow might have had something to do with that.

Indeed, their Saint had taken to the field, not as relief, crippled by her oath of nonviolence, but as a frontline combatant, free to deploy the full might of her wrath.

The might of a human who had braved the deepest depths of the Ash. Who made Mahādi her home and called the Akh Nara her husband.

There wasn’t a single demon on that field of battle—not even the Samsara—who didn’t respect and fear her.

Maiya’s heart was strangely calm as she walked, her prana-reinforced body harmlessly snapping the thorns and branches that threatened her bare soles.

Perhaps this was what taking a stance finally felt like. Perhaps this was what it meant to fight back.

She had to admit it was liberating. Freeing in a way she’d not felt in years, back before she became a Rajni. Before responsibility shackled her, threatening to pull her into the ground.

And while her hands might’ve been empty, anyone in the demonic army who could sense prana knew better. In addition to the burgeoning energy coming from her body, no less than ten S rank orbs, two hundred A rank orbs, and three thousand B ranks sat in her Imperium-granted storage space. All precharged. All ready to deploy their energy into a focused beam that would turn her enemies into nothing but a memory.

She allowed just a fraction of their prana to leak out, multiplying her in a maelstrom of roiling energy a hundredfold.

Her prana became a visible wall, each step taken pushing the attacking forces back. Each pace bringing them closer and closer to routing.

All this, however, was but the opening act.

As impressive as she was alone, Maiya simply couldn’t compare to a Vimana that stood overhead.

A Vimana that projected ten thousand tiny pinpricks of light at every demon Warrior’s forehead. To show them exactly where their fatal wound would come.

A Vimana that belched a stream of jet-black airships, all bearing the symbol of the Akh Nara.

Airships that came in low behind her, depositing thousands upon thousands of Asura Warriors who jumped off, striking the ground before assuming formation behind Maiya.

She had never seen Jagath look so shocked and confused. More than that, he looked scared.

And when he turned his eyes to his ultimate weapon, he found the Samsara avert their eyes.

In the face of such overwhelming force… in the face of Maiya’s determination not to let this massacre continue, the demon army hesitated.

And so, it was with no shortage of surprise, no lack of shock for both armies that Maiya’s advance was abruptly cut short.

It was not, however, any of the soldiers who broke it. No hotheaded warband captain who broke ranks to surge forth.

No, that honor went to the gray demon who rose silently from the shadow of a small shrub in her path, jet-black horns protruding from the top of his head.

She remembered when she’d first seen those horns.

She’d been tied up, dangling above a vat of boiling water, about to be executed in the most heinous fashion in front of a cheering crowd of Kin’jals.

Vir had appeared with those horns then, as he had now.

To save her. To exact retribution upon their enemies.

He’d dumped Andros into the vat then, to the shock of the world.

If only their enemy this time could be dealt with so easily…

“Vir,” she muttered, her melancholic smile a perfect counterpart to the grave seriousness etched upon his face.

For the first time in what seemed like forever, she felt butterflies in her stomach, and it was with a mix of both fear and hope that she greeted him.

The Asuras behind her stilled, while the demons behind him erupted in cries of joy, and if humanity had been ecstatic to her, the reaction paled next to the reception demons gave him.

The Akh Nara had taken to the field, and with his presence came the blessing of their war they had so long sought. His assurance that a divinity walked beside them, just as she’d hoped to convey to her own people.

Vir approached slowly, in his eyes a terrible mix of happiness and anguish, and with each step that diminished between them, Maiya’s elation grew, the fear she’d felt earlier fading like a distant memory.

How I’ve missed you, Vir… How I’ve wished to tell you everything I’ve kept inside. Of all I hid from Siya, to not distract you.

She said none of those things, for they weren’t important now. Instead, she said what did matter. The only thing that would reach him in this moment.

“I love you, Vir,” she whispered. “Now, and forever.”

He was close now. So very close.

Even then, Maiya saw the dagger coming. For how could she not?

And yet, where she would have flinched… would have activated any number of Talents or deployed one of several layers of shields had the attack come from anyone else… she did not.

Instead, she looked into Vir’s eyes. His deep, black eyes as the seric blade plunged deep into her stomach. It encountered no resistance at all.

All that strength. All that training. Such irony that a mortal wound could be so easily inflicted.

She felt no pain. The agony ought to have been overwhelming, she knew. That she ought to have panicked, and yet as her legs grew weak and she stumbled only to be caught in the arms of her beloved, Maiya was at peace.

For the first time since she’d become the Blessed Chosen of the Children of Ash and the Fateweavers’ foremost agent in the realm. Maiya was at peace.

Because she knew. That with this one action, more lives would be saved than all the rescue operations she could ever mount.

That with her death, their enemy’s unknowable cosmic designs would be thwarted.

Certain in this outcome.

And as they both knew well, certainty these days was all but priceless.

For one did not defy Fate. To fight against it—to truly deny its power—one had to erase it entirely.

As the cold bloomed through her stomach and death seeped into her every vain, Maiya reached a hand up to Vir’s face.

He was crying. Her poor, poor lover, who’d been with her from the beginning. More stalwart than any mountain or anchor stone. Who’d unwittingly given her the greatest blessing a soul could hope for.

Her only regret was causing this most precious existence such sorrow. To add to the mountain of burdens he carried.

With the last of her strength, she wiped away his tear, only to find that her own vision had gone and blurred.

How odd, she thought. Wasn’t this what she wanted? To finally be granted the escape she’d so dearly wished for? To deprive the enemy of their greatest weapon for the good of the realm?

So cold… Maiya hadn’t known dying could chill her so. From her body to her very soul. She scarcely even felt Vir’s hand on hers. She barely heard his last words.

“Your side… when the world turns…”

She couldn’t make out the rest, but she didn’t have to.

She knew those words, etched into her heart the day she’d learned her parents had died. It was raining, that day when they’d returned to Brij in disguise. When they’d seen her father’s temple empty and abandoned.

When they’d found that note.

Darkness encroached on the edges of her vision, steadily worming its way into her.

Her breath caught. Not in terror of her own impending death, but at the realization that after so long and after enduring so much, it was all coming to an end.

That her life as Maiya would soon be over. A flame snuffed out on that vast tapestry Vir had once glimpsed.

Images of Brij flowed unbidden into her mind. Of her and Vir under the tree on Rabbit Hill, gazing up at the stars late into the night, far past curfew. Wondering what grand and glorious future awaited them, and all too overeager to reach it.

They’d pictured the grand vistas they’d witness, the varied cultures they’d experience. The farthest flung countries and the most epic of adventures.

All while knowing, deep down, they’d likely never leave the village for as long as they lived.

That was alright, they’d thought. They had each other, and with each other, nothing else was necessary.

If only we’d known how right we were, Vir… and how wrong we were…

Now they were, if not adults, about to be. Separated for years in a desperate search for one another, each of Maiya’s plans to reunite with her beloved foiled time and time again.

Then they’d gone and found each other in the most likely of places. Her, as an agent of Princess Ira in a Kin’jal stronghold. Him, as a friend of the Pagan Order liberating captured demons and a golden-headed brat of a princess between them.

It was almost endearing, now. The cloaks and the daggers. The double meanings and the deceptions. All their meaningless worries…

But not the joys. Those, she treasured more than anything. More now, than ever before.

The scene disappeared, to be replaced with the grassy hill at dawn in Balindam. After she’d flown to the Pagan Order’s capital and they’d sorted out that great misunderstanding.

The moment he confessed his love to me… the moment we’d kissed for the first time.

Maiya remembered the flutters as if it were yesterday. It was the happiest time of her life.

She’d been sure she’d have to be the one to confess. To convince her stubborn childhood friend that a partnership was beneficial for the both of them. She’d even prepared a list…

And then he’d gone and done the deed in the most awkward way possible. Avoiding her gaze and blushing madly.

She’d have had it no other way.

Those were beautiful days. Happy days.

Oh, Vir… If only we could go back to those times now.

The love of her life. Her partner to the end.

Her murderer.

The darkness took her vision fully now.

Not even her Prime Mejai’s might could stop its unrelenting advance.

She let it.

Maiya of Brij let out her final breath before her time stopped.

All faded to blissful nothingness… and Maiya embraced it gladly.

Tia’s mind was blank. No thoughts went through her head, and when someone asked her for order, she ignored them.

She simply stared at the pool of blood that stained Maiya’s beautiful dress.

At Vir, knife in hand.

At an impossibility so absurd, her mind rejected it.

This was a deception, right?

A sick joke the two of them planned together.

To get back at their people, perhaps?

To have a few laughs. To—

“Tia!” Vason shouted in her ear, but he may as well have been a realm away.

She swayed on her heels, clutching him for support.

She heard the voice in her head that said she’d witnessed the downfall of humanity’s greatest mejai.

The shock paralyzed her. The disbelief made her weak. But it was when the anger flooded into her that she found her footing.

Disgusting.

It was disgusting, and she just didn’t know what made her want to vomit more.

That the foremost of humans—who outstripped her own power tenfold or more—could fall so easily—so pathetically—or that her husband, the demon’s god and a man she’d once called a friend, could kill his wife so ruthlessly, and in full view of humans and demons both.

But above all, Tia was angry because she’d been betrayed.

Betrayed by the woman she’d idolized for so long—that she’d fought hopelessly to match up to—to lie that she’d ever had a change of heart.

Once a demon lover, always a demon lover, a small voice whispered in her head.

Tia ignored it. She was no longer the child who hated demonkind. She knew better now. There were good demons and there were bad demons, just as there were humans.

And yet, when faced with an army, none of that mattered very much, did it?

Maiya could have saved lives today. She could have delivered them the victory humanity sorely needed.

At the very least, Tia had hoped their glorious Saint would take out a tenth of their number.

Now, she was dead.

And that monumental task, it seemed, rested upon her far more modest shoulders.

Rage seethed in the Matali Princess’ heart. Rage unlike anything she’d ever felt.

Tia refused to accept it. Everything she was rejected this reality, and every shred of guilt and goodwill she’d ever had towards that demon evaporated in an instant.

She wasn’t the only one.

Maiya’s death was a blow for humanity, yes, but one would have thought it the other way around between the demons’ hesitant steps back and the clanging of dropped weapons.

For the first time Tia could recall, the Warriors of demonkind regarded their god with something other than awe and blind worship.

For the first time, there was hesitance in those faces. Hesitance… and fear.

A good Warrior would have run out into the field to exploit this moment, leading her soldiers into that field of battle while the enemy was rattled.

A good leader would have uttered some words of motivation to rile up her army.

Tia was neither of those things.

Instead, she did the first thing that came to mind.

She drew her voice amplification orb and bellowed at the top of her lungs.

“Vir, you wretched scum! You despicable Ash-damned bastard! If you are even half a man, fight me! Fight me right here, right now, in this field of battle!”

She said it to vent her feelings. To bare her unfiltered heart against the world. She had not expected a reply.

And yet, the reply she got was one she would not, in a million years, have ever guessed.

From far away, the tear-stained gray demon looked up at her, Maiya’s body still in his hands, and with an expression of grief, spoke directly into her mind and the minds of every soul present on that battlefield.

“For fifty days and fifty nights…” he said, choking out each word as if it were his last, “there will be no battles. No fighting. No more death! You will mourn her with me. I… I don’t…”

Leaving his sentence forever unfinished, the Akh Nara—Vimana Destroyer and Uniter of Realms—supreme leader of demonkind, sank into the shadows, taking their Saint’s corpse with him.
