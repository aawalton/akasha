Finally, his eyes turned to the shore where he had been walking just days ago relishing in joy, and for a moment, he had no words. He reached into his coat and took out the divine artifact that had a mere flicker of red within it at this point. Just like when he had fought the Arthra forest spirit, he was back to square one.

He closed his eyes as he gripped the tip of his staff tightly, and that’s when he heard it.

“Human slave, are you interested in making a deal?”

The voice of the Demon God that had just graced the world minutes ago was speaking straight to him. Dargon frowned, but before he could reply, it continued.

“You have potential, unlike your organization, so why not…”

The words were sweet like honey, and by the time the Demon God finished, he couldn’t help but smile. He turned and within the space, he saw Ralven standing as he gazed at the battlefield. His body looked withered and had cracks all over. Evidently, the fellow council member hadn’t fared much better than him from the battle.

“So, we have failed,” he lamented.

But Dargon ignored him as he raised his staff. “I accept your deal.”

Ralven shook his head with a chuckle. “What deal? There are no deals to be made anymore. We have⁠—”

A concentrated blast of Space Magic hit the councilman, breaking his Life Seed. The Botany Archmage slammed against the wall with a splatter of branches and straw. Now on the ground, he looked at Dargon with wide eyes, his entire body creaking.

“Dargon, you⁠—”

“Supernova.”

Another blast hit the mage, evaporating him before he could say anything else. The Demon God was right.

Dargon observed the Demon of Genesis trying to kill everything, and he couldn’t help but smirk.

“It’s time for me to make my own future.”

* * *

But not all things were bad on Vir.

All across the world, people celebrated that the monster in the sky had failed to enter the realm. Some thanked the gods, others merely felt relief, and people like Asil or the Primordial Spirit Val’leri placed their gratitude onto a single person.

The Cursed Berserker, Amber.

The apocalypse and end of the world had mostly been prevented. Even though some were displeased, like Remmel Wolfe, who had no choice but to give up on his revenge plans, the people of the world at large were happy. Even those of the Saints of Truth were no different.

After all, this whole thing had only been prevented because of a single valiant hero who had triumphed against all odds. Bringing peace to the realm once more.

And said hero was just now waking up.

She groaned for a moment before snapping her eyes open, summoning Cursewelder, ready to fight for her life. But what she saw was not a scene of eyes or open mouths that floated in the air like she expected, nor a scene from Earth, but rather, a dimly-lit, moist cave.

A small red flame floated at her side in the air, made out of Demonic Essence.

“Where… am I?”

The Demon of Chaos’ voice came in reply, “I used the Traitor’s journal while you were unconscious. I barely saved your rings.”

Amber looked down at herself, and the first thing she noticed was that her armor was gone. Aside from some rags, she had nothing. Still, she found her rings still secured onto the makeshift necklace she had made a while back.

She summoned her old armor onto herself — the Armor of Frenzy that had been modified by Val’leri — and stood up.

“How long has it been since I died?” she asked.

“Four days, you were unconscious for that long,” he said simply.

At that, Amber jolted straight, walking forward with a sudden sense of urgency.

“I need to get back to the others and ensure they escape⁠d, or if they’re still struggling now, I need to help them. Otherwise⁠—”

“No, Amber, you need to listen to what I’m about to say,” the Demon of Chaos called out to her, making her stop. “This place is no ordinary place. It’s filled with traces of⁠—”

And a soft female voice interrupted him, shining a blue light. “Relax. Your friends are fine.”

Amber blinked, and she turned only to freeze. Standing there was a woman with felt-lined white robes and an over-sized witch hat. She wore round glasses and had freckles, and unlike the vision, she saw she had a hint of fur peaking from the sides of her chestnut hair.

It was the woman that she had seen during her Special Advancements, the one that could use Essence. But the main reason she froze wasn’t that, but because the woman’s hand that gripped her staff was made of a light blue layer of concentrated Essence. Her intuition told her one thing, and one thing only…

“Still, to think the recipient of the deal made thirty-thousand years ago would be you, Amber,” she said with some surprise. “I only thought I’d meet you if you continued to excel in magic. Fate sure is interesting.”

…this woman was none other than a Primordial Spirit.

And it was none other than the Primordial Spirit of Lathyzia and Arveil.
