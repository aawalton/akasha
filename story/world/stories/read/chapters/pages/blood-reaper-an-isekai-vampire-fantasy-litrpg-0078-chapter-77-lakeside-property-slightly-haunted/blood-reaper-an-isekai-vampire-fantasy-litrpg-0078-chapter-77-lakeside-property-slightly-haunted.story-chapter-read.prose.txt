As Ace studied the vast obsidian lake, nothing happened.

The water stretched before them toward the distant waterfall. The obsidian surface was unnaturally still despite the falls thundering at the other end of the lakebed. No ripples disturbed the glass-like plane. The water absorbed light rather than reflected it, creating a void so perfect it seemed to erase the boundary between water and air. The dark expanse extended for hundreds of yards until it reached the base of a sheer cliff face, where the waterfall crashed down in sheets of black steam.

Ace stood on the bank, his breathing finally settling after the battle with Raven. The shadow essence he’d absorbed during the fight pulsed beneath his skin in irregular waves like a heartbeat. It felt both foreign and familiar—a cold, electric sensation that seeped through his bones, settling most heavily in his chest and fingertips. The remnants of Raven’s power clung to him like a second skin, whispering secrets his conscious mind couldn’t begin to process.

Behind him, his team followed suit. Tara crossed her arms and watched the water, her normally vibrant eyes dimmed after all the energy she’d expended during the fight. Marcus sat on the ground, massaging his temples as he fought off a persistent tremor in his left hand. Only Olivia alone seemed energized rather than depleted, and she hummed softly to herself as she playfully spun her staff around her head. The battle’s violence seemed to have awakened something in her that normal people would find disturbing.

But at this point, none of Ace’s team was normal.

Their wounds had mostly closed thanks to Tara’s abilities, but fatigue hung heavy on them all. Even Acey struggled to replenish the resources he’d burned through in the battle. Moonfall rested in the sheath around his waist and, for whatever reason, felt heavier than before.

The air above the water’s surface shimmered with an oily iridescence, and prismatic colors that had no place in nature slithered like snakes through the water. Small motes of silvery light occasionally broke through the surface, rising like reversed raindrops before dissolving into the mist that hung over everything.

Something about the stillness felt deliberate. Expectant. Almost like the lake itself was waiting for something.

Or someone.

“This can’t be right,” Marcus whispered, his voice tight. “Our map says the Crucible entrance should be here, but all I see is a big pool of death water.”

“How do you know it’ll hurt us?” Olivia challenged. “It’s just water.”

“Are you serious?” Marcus gestured toward the lake with both palms. “It’s black, for Christ’s sake. And why aren’t there ripples? Waves? Anything at all from the waterfall?”

“I wonder if it’s a creature,” Tara said absently as her gaze swept across the lake. “It seems… unnatural.”

“A creature?” Marcus asked, his voice rising an octave. “Like, something that would eat us?”

“Possibly,” Ace admitted. “Everything else tries to kill us, so why not a lake?”

“Funny,” Marcus said dryly. “Real funny, boss.”

Ace shrugged.

That hadn’t been a joke.

“Trust the map,” Tara murmured, studying the black expanse before them.

As if responding to her words, a deep rumble emanated from deep underground, like the growl of some ancient beast awakening from centuries of slumber. The vibration traveled up through the stone beneath their feet, rattling Ace’s bones and setting his teeth on edge. The obsidian water shivered, first with subtle ripples that distorted his reflections into a grotesque caricature, then with increasingly violent convulsions that sent waves crashing against the shoreline.

Ace took a cautious step backward.

Something big was coming.

In a deafening rush, the surface split down the middle. The liquid rose on either side in defiance of everything Ace knew about physics, building upward rather than crashing down. It formed towering walls that stretched thirty feet into the air, their surfaces rippling yet somehow maintaining their impossible vertical shape. The walls glowed with an inner light—blues and purples that pulsed like heartbeats—that illuminated the cracked stone pathway that cut through the lakebed.

At the lakeshore, a stone staircase led down to the lakebed path, its steps unnaturally dry despite having been submerged moments before. Ancient runes carved into each stair flickered with dull crimson light, creating an undulating pattern that beckoned them downward.

“Hell of an entrance,” Marcus said under his breath. “Think they’ve got a gift shop at the bottom?”

Ace chuckled. “Maybe they sell hats.”

“I could go for one,” Marcus quipped. “Get ‘the Fang Gang’ embossed on it in big letters. I’m thinking—hmm—yep, Comic Sans. Perfect font for a terrifying romp through a death lake.”

“Will you two focus?” Tara chided.

The two of them went silent, but Ace still grinned.

Tara moved closer to Ace, her normally steady hands trembling slightly. “If those walls of water collapse...”

“Look on the bright side,” Ace said. “At least we can’t drown.”

“Is no one else seeing the death water?!” Marcus said, pointing once again at the rushing black waves before them. “That’s not normal!”

“Drama queen,” Olivia said flatly.

The walls of water loomed impossibly tall on either side, their steep edges defying gravity as the waves roared backward and away from the path. The liquid barriers towered a good fifty feet above the cracked stone that crossed through the lakebed. Occasional surges sent water splashing into the air.

It hit Ace, then, why the water looked so wrong. This wasn’t water at all—it was something else, something thicker, with the consistency of mercury but the color of spilled ink. The rippling surface captured and swallowed every reflection and every beam of light that touched it.

“Are we really doing this?” Olivia asked, her voice uncharacteristically uncertain.

“I won’t make you do anything,” Ace reminded her. “None of you have to do this with me. It’s entirely up to you.”

He pivoted on his heel to find all three of them staring at him with wide eyes. Olivia stood at the rear of the group, stiff as a board, while Tara and Marcus wrung their hands in silent terror.

All of them had come so far.

They had fought through monsters and betrayal, survived the System’s cruel games, killed when necessary, and learned how to survive a world that was actively trying to kill them. These three were no longer the frightened newcomers who’d appeared in this world weeks ago.

“Look at you,” he said as his eyes swept over the group. “Three weeks ago, you were scared, confused strangers thrown into a slaughterhouse. Now? Now you’re fierce. Deadly. Formidable. I mean, fuck, we took out those vampire bandits like they were nothing. You think we could’ve done that on our first night here?”

Tara frowned, but the team was otherwise silent.

Still wary.

Still afraid.

With a bracing sigh, he took a few steps closer and crossed his arms across his chest. He straightened his back, determined to make them see what he saw when he looked at them.

“Think about the arena,” he said. “Think about how the System laughed while we fought off the crystal wolves. Think about how the crowd screamed for our blood when she let the fleshstitchers loose. Those dragon shifters didn’t make it—but you did. They lived in this place their whole lives and prepared for what we went through. They faced trials and probably unspeakable horrors to get that far. Well, they’re dead. You’re not.”

This time, Tara stood a little taller, and a hint of hope flickered in her eyes.

“Look how far we’ve come,” he continued. “We’ve killed things that should have eaten us. We’ve survived traps meant to break us. We’ve faced betrayal and loss and came out of it stronger than we were before.”

Ace’s hand rested on Moonfall’s hilt, and the weapon hummed in response to his touch.

“This place—this Crucible—it’s designed to make us doubt ourselves,” he pointed out. “Everything we’ve heard about it paints it as a trial meant to shatter us. These sisters, whoever they are, probably want to make us question each other. They’re going to try to shake our faith in each other, but we’ve already passed tests they couldn’t imagine. We killed a Behemoth with nothing but determination and trust. We survived an alpha slug’s hive mind by standing together. If we did all that, we can face whatever’s in there.”

“You really think so?” Marcus asked quietly.

“I know so,” Ace replied without faltering. “I know you’re afraid. I am, too. But fear didn’t stop us before, and it won’t stop us now.”

His words hung in the air, seeming to push back against the oppressive atmosphere. The System, these Sisters—they think fear controls us. They don’t understand that fear is what forged us into what we are. They don’t realize that every monster we’ve faced has carved us into what we are.”

“And what exactly are we?” Olivia asked.

He paused, and for a moment, they simply watched each other. Her eyes softened, somewhat—enough to show a bit of weakness. A bit of hesitation.

A bit of that still-lingering fear.

“We’re survivors.” He set his hand on her shoulder and offered her a reassuring smile. “And we’re a team.”

His words hung in the air. For a time, there was only silence punctuated by the rush of water before them.

Slowly, something shifted in the team’s collective presence—a tangible change that rippled through them like a current. One by one, their fear dissolved. Spines straightened, as if invisible weights had been lifted off their shoulders. Jaws clenched with renewed purpose. Fingers that had trembled with tension and terror now curled into determined fists. The nervous energy that had surrounded them condensed into something sharper, something more focused, like scattered light through a magnifying lens.

But their eyes changed most of all.

The haunted, wary gazes that had darted constantly toward the water walls now hardened into something predatory. Pupils dilated. Irises gleamed with crimson hunger in the strange half-light of the twilight sun.

These weren’t the frightened newcomers who had cowered together in their first blood-soaked night. Through bloodshed and broken bones, they had become apex predators who feasted on nightmares.

“Nice speech, boss.” Marcus’s voice cut through the tension, and the telepath winked playfully. “It doesn’t make those water walls less terrifying, but I’m all warm-fuzzy now.”

Ace shook his head, but he couldn’t hide the grin tugging at the corner of his mouth. “Glad I could help.”

Olivia’s sultry gaze shifted toward the distant doors. “Let’s go make them regret inviting us in.”

With a collective breath, they stepped onto the first stair.

The sturdy stone sent a chill through Ace’s boots, colder and colder as he descended with his team in tow. Each of their steps emitted a soft, bell-like chime as they continued, the notes forming a discordant melody that echoed unnaturally between the walls of water. Twenty stairs down, the air grew thicker, heavier with moisture that carried the metallic tang of blood.

Fifty steps.

A hundred.

Two hundred.

The stairwell seemed to stretch on forever, impossibly long.

At the bottom of the staircase, a narrow path of cracked stone stretched before them, cutting straight through the parted lake. The black waters loomed, the walls narrowing to create a claustrophobic corridor barely eight feet wide. Ace placed his boot on the path, and the stone beneath him groaned like a living thing.

Ace’s boot made contact with the wet stone, producing an echo that reverberated far longer than natural acoustics should allow. The sound multiplied, transforming into something that resembled distant, pained whispers.

Tara moved closer to him, her normally steady healer’s hands trembling slightly. “Those aren’t normal echoes.”

“Nothing about this place is normal,” Ace replied. “Stay close. Don’t touch anything unless I say it’s clear.”

Half-expecting an ambush of some kind, he drew Moonfall. Light glinted across the polished blade as they walked in tense silence through the surging corridor. He strained to hear anything beyond the rushing thunder of the lake’s suspended waters, but there was nothing.

He paused, half wondering if the walls would indeed crash down around them, but the makeshift road along the lakebed held.

“Stay in the center of the path,” he warned, eyeing the jagged fissures that ran along the edges of the walkway.

“Don’t have to tell me twice,” Marcus said with a nervous glance toward the black water on either side of them.

They headed forward in tight formation, each cautious step taking them closer to the massive doors at the far end.

Halfway along the path, a ripple passed through both walls simultaneously. The water pressed inward, narrowing their passage to barely five feet. They were forced closer to the surging water, so close that the occasional droplet splashed onto their shoulders. Each drop burned briefly, like acid, before dissipating into vapor.

Oh, fuck.

A rush of alarm shot through Ace as he stared up at the towering barrier protecting them from the lake beyond. The walls were going to crash in on them—and there was no way in hell they could make it back to the staircase in time.

“Well,” he said under his breath. “I sure hope you all can swim.”
