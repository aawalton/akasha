Before the hostility became palpable Maus spoke again.

"Hi, hi. Hello," he repeated, not particularly concerned.

Nobody spoke. Even the low murmur that had erupted around the space had diminished to almost nothing.

Then several figures stepped out of the group and approached, though they still stopped a fair distance away.

Maus could feel the imprints of the people around him. Four of them were Tier 1.

That was more than he’d been expecting, but all in all not the worst outcome.

He might have had trouble escaping if they all decided to attack him at the same time, but Maus didn’t think they would. Even if they did, he was at least confident in his ability to escape.

Maus’s mana manipulation was crude at best because Boss wouldn’t let him get the Skill until he became more proficient with his space mana. But he didn’t need much fineness to construct a crude stone pillar beneath his body. As it rose, so did he, lifting himself about 2 metres off the ground.

He wasn't eye-to-eye with anyone, but at least they no longer had to crane their necks down to see him.

When nobody spoke, Maus took it upon himself to break the silence.

"I'm looking for the FelisCanis boss, and the Matriarch."

Nothing happened for a while. Then two individuals stepped forward. One was a vulpian, and the other struck Maus as canine in feature, though Maus wasn’t sure what race he belonged to.

"Who are you?" the canine person asked.

"I'm Maus."

"You're a mouse?"

"I am a mouse."

The canine person blinked, opened their mouth, then closed it. Before they could decide what to say next, the vulpian cut in.

"State your purpose. You are not authorised within these grounds without a Hunter's pass."

"Why not?"

"What?"

"Why can't I be here? Who authorised it? Why do I need a pass? This isn't a city. Does it belong to you?"

The questions were innocent in their delivery. In all honesty Maus had been wondering about them for a while. However, instead of answering, both the canine and vulpian representatives seemed to take them as provocation.

"This Portal World is property of the Felis Canis faction," the canine said.

"Contested property," the vulpian corrected.

"Oh." Maus tilted his head. "So, you're Administrators too?"

Both representatives went silent. Yeah, that didn’t seem right. Then what would all the fuss over Gelken have been about?

“Then you’re the bosses of this place?” He tried. When he received no answer Maus realised he’d been duped.

These were just the speakers. Mouthpieces, the Boss would call them.

Scanning the crowd with his spiritual senses rather than relying on normal perception, Maus found the signatures of the two other Tier 1s. One was a vulpian and the other looked feline in nature.

The second vulpian signature was significantly stronger than the mouthpiece standing in front of him. The feline signature felt both weaker, and yet more dangerous to Maus’s senses.

"The Administrator belongs to our Faction," the canine representative said.

"The FelisCanis Faction?" Maus clarified.

"That is correct."

"That is incorrect. The Administrator is one of ours," the vulpian snapped, their lip raised in a snarl directed towards the FelisCanis representative.

Maus simply nodded along. Who they believed the Administrator belonged to wasn't really any of his business. He'd been sent here to facilitate a negotiation. Which is exactly what he said.

"I'm here to negotiate," he said. The vulpian and the feline person looked away from their silent battle and returned their attention to Maus.

"You’re what now?” The feline one growled.

"If you have no badge, it would be best you leave before you are no longer able," said the Matriarch.

"Is that a threat?” Maus asked, the question genuine. He’d heard about ‘implied threats’ from the Boss and thought this might be one of them. “That's not very nice," Maus gave his whiskers a quick brush. "Also, I thought you'd wanna know where the Administrator was. Maybe talk to them?"

The canine representative scoffed. "The Administrator is under FelisCanis protection."

"You’ve imprisoned them," the vulpian representative snapped.

"For their own safety," the feline didn’t even try to hide the sneer.

"I'm sure," the vulpian said, though their face remained placid.

"Um..." Maus's nose twitched towards the vulpian signature, then towards the feline person he presumed was the actual Felis Canis leader. "They're actually in the Portal World."

"What?" The question came from four directions. Both mouthpieces, and both leaders.

"The Administrator. They're in the Portal World. They said you might want to speak. No, they said, you might wanna ‘Negotiate terms’." Maus nodded to himself. He was really good at remembering.

This was going swimmingly. Information delivered.

Apparently, the factions didn't feel remotely the same way.

The feline leader stepped out into the open, no longer a disembodied voice looming through the crowd. A moment later, so did the Matriarch. The shift wasn't immediately visible, leaving both representatives looking around in confusion until they noticed their superiors separating themselves from the crowd.

"You're their bosses?" Maus asked. It was a chipper statement. Maus was super proud he’d remembered everything. His improved, Tier 1 INT helped too.

"You will explain exactly what you mean by that," ground out the feline person.

Maus tilted his head. "By the Administrator being in the Portal World?"

Now he was confused. He wasn't sure he could have been much clearer.

The Matriarch crossed her arms, her gaze somehow colder than her spiritual impression, which his previous deployment of Echo told Maus was quite a feat.

"How do you have this information?" The Matriarch asked.

"Oh. I helped them get in there."

"You what?" the feline roared.

“Baaldot,” the Matriarch simply said their name and lifted a hand, and the feline fell quiet.

Baaldot displeasure rose as they realised they’d obeyed without thinking.

“Could you explain?” the Matriarch asked, ignoring her seething counterpart.

“Sure. The Administrator was in the jail, but I got them out. Did you know they didn’t even do anything? All they did was be an admin person, and that seems more like a punishment than a crime. So, I helped them get out, and now they're in there." Maus turned and pointed a paw towards the portal.

Already unhappy, rage began to build in the FelisCanis faction. The Matriarch appeared to be feeling something closer to resignation, though by the look on her face it could also have been constipation.

Maus wasn't very good at reading faces. His special senses didn't tell him much about what people were feeling. They told him more about who people were; the shape of their experiences and the way they affected their surroundings.

During his brief time studying people’s impressions, Maus had noticed something. The brightest souls weren't necessarily the most powerful, but they were often the ones that had the greatest impact on the world around them. In contrast, the densest souls might not have had much external effect at all, but those people were strong.

The Matriarch was both.

Maus would have to tell Scarlet about his discovery.

Later.

The Matriarch was speaking again.

"So, you wish us to believe you broke the Administrator out of their imprisonment, then somehow snuck them past the entire encampment and into the Portal World without any of us being aware either of you had made it into the camp?”

Maus thought about it for a moment, then bobbed his head.

"Yeah. I mean, here I am," he said.

The Matriarch's people shifted uneasily.

"My Lady," one of the Matriarch’s subordinates said. The words held both recognition and self-admonishment.

The mouse had gotten past every line without anyone noticing he was there. Still, just because entry to the Portal World had been sealed, and the mouse was present, that didn't mean the Administrator was inside.

A Portal World could be administrated remotely.

It did, however, suggest that that regardless of where the Administrator was, she felt confident enough in defending herself to risk such a bold move.

Baaldot’s jaw ticked. "So, what are your terms?" they conceded, barely.

"I don’t have the terms, you have to go in and meet her," Maus said. "But only you two." He gestured towards Baaldot and the Matriarch. “And only one at a time.”

The Matriarch and Baaldot looked at each other, then turned back to face Maus.

"So, how do we go about this then?" the Matriarch asked.

Maus brightened. His plan was going great!

"I'll just pop in and let them know. When the portal opens, one of you can come in. Okay?" Maus said. The vague noises and gestures of reluctant agreement were good enough for him.

It was clear Maus had been given access to the Portal World because the moment he scampered onto the portal platform, he vanished.

It didn’t take long for the portal to flare bright again, admitting general entry.

Outside the Portal World, the Matriarch and the Felis Canis leader looked at one another. Neither made a move to step onto the platform themselves. Instead, they each turned and made gestures to their people.

As though they were on standby, guards from each faction surged towards the portal, for once not working in opposition to each other.

Seven had made it through before the platform suddenly flared red once more, cutting off entry.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then bodies came back through.

Not all of them intact.

Not one of them alive.
