Sally peered out of the window of the townhouse’s second floor, all the while spinning a trigger in her hand.

“I’m just saying.” She glanced back at the cat. “If I could summon ghosts, skeletons, and flesh abominations, then I could be more efficient with a corpse. Get… three or four different uses out of each person I kill.”

Archie maintained a dull expression as he sat on the nearby bed.

“I suppose just zombies isn’t too bad.” She grinned and looked back out at the town.

Her undead army filled every street in sight. Packed in almost like sardines, breaking into buildings to find more room to stand.

“Odd question…” Archie eventually replied. “And maybe not the right circumstances, but you enjoy doing this, right?”

Sally ran her tongue across sharp teeth, not even looking back at the cat. “Morally gray anti-hero mass-murdering her way into saving lost planets?”

“Not exactly how I’d word it, but yes. The traveling. Putting yourself out there to rescue parts of the System.”

“Of course.” She shrugged. “We both know I get cabin fever idling in Sanctuary for too long. I just wish there was more… structure to it? Everything is tentatively stepping on new ground right now.”

Archie tilted his head. “You mean you don’t like being split from Theo.”

“Can you imagine us being regular partners in this sort of thing?” She turned to grin at him. “You do have plans for that, right? Once we repel the Radoch attack.”

“It… depends on certain factors. The Monster is here, however.”

The zombie turned and leaned out of the window slightly. It wasn’t the best viewing angle, but there was no mistaking the large shape making its way into the edges of the town. Difficult to pin down exactly what it was, but she could sense the first wave of zombies getting cut down almost instantly.

“Oh no, they’re making short work of my pals.” She flipped the protective cover of the trigger up and placed her thumb on the button. “Looting is often a waste of time—or my brain cells—but using their own weapons against them is pretty neat.”

Archie didn’t respond, but waved his tail back and forth. His emerald eyes weren’t even looking at the zombie by the window, but off to the side, as if he could see something through the walls.

The Monster sent by the Radochs lumbered into view, onto the main street that Sally could see. It looked like an elephant, but much taller and wider. Instead of a trunk, it had a long arm. She watched as it scooped this limb through the street, tearing through the wall of a tavern as it brought several zombies up into the air.

Instead of a mouth, the monster had a gaping pit that resembled a wood chipper. Rows of dagger-like teeth spun around in spirals within, leading toward a gullet, part-car wash and part-torture device. The grabbed undead fell into this weird maw, turning into shredded pulp as soon as they collided with the moving blades.

“Huh.” Sally wrinkled up her nose. “I wonder if the living see me like that.”

Even the shamblers that had avoided the grab weren’t able to do anything to the trunk-like legs of the Monster. Any who tried to slide their way past to surround the beast were cut down by a small group of elite Radochs sent to accompany the Monster.

“If that were the case, you wouldn’t be the first pick when we need someone affable to assist a world.”

She glanced back at the cat. “Eh, that’s just utilizing what you have. What if the heroes from Theo’s venture are much more charismatic… and not brain-eating undead?”

“That would just mean we have more tools in the box. With your experience and competence, you are far from becoming antiquated.” Archie stood and stretched his back out.

“So complimentary.” The zombie rolled her eyes. “We both know the elephant in the room is that the true threat from these bugs isn’t something my power can help with.”

Sanctuary was very unique within the Sea of worlds, in that its inhabitants could respawn. On the surface, this made the Radoch fleet soon approaching seem futile. A war of attrition would easily go to the side of the planet, even without the Outsiders there.

Sure, there were odd cases in which the cockroaches might get an edge. Interesting and novel ways to make living on the planet impossible or a constant hell.

But the actual threat was external.

Why land on the planet of experienced fighters that were nigh immortal, if you could just blow the world up from orbit? Chuck had no answer for that. Things worked differently in the System compared to the real world. Technically, there was no real distance between planets, only… resistance.

While she had mostly tuned out the repeated attempts to educate her on the fine details of the Sea, she knew that any Radoch ship could only exist in two places during their assault. Outside of the world, but in a low-latency state—like the Omen currently—or actually within the boundaries of the local System. This was how the current Radoch vessels were able to attack this world.

The latter wouldn’t work on Sanctuary. With Chuck as the god-like Architect, he would just be able to swat them out of the sky. They controlled the STAR there, to some degree. So, if the Radochs sat around the ‘shore’, there was an awkward neutral ground that made it difficult for the two parties to interact with each other.

Unless they had some heavy-duty weaponry that could attack the world itself…

Sally blinked away her thoughts as the shadow of the creature loomed closer, over her horde close to her current position.

“It’s powerful,” she murmured, mostly to herself. “Called an Ever-gorger, which I can relate to. Gluttony is a sin, though. Hypocritical for me to be the Judge Judy and executioner, but it’s not like the space police will come after me.”

She paused. Against the sound of the town and dozens of zombies being torn apart, several small gears ticked around in her head.

“Oh, we should become the space police.” Sally grinned to herself. “That way we’re above the law, right?”

“I doubt the Sea will ever be unified enough to have an official law enforcement agency.” Archie hopped up to the windowsill beside her and glanced down at the carnage. “Perhaps our vigilante group could have a logo in the future, should we survive the looming war.”

“Now you’re talking.”

Sally pressed down on the button, activating all the thermite charges she had tied to various zombies with her army.

Theo sank down in the shade of a group of trees and opened up his messages.

//Theo: Pretty explosive, don’t you agree?//Chuck: It ties into our theories, plus adds more…//Chuck: For every answer is three new questions.//Chuck: I’m adding a little bandwidth to send you something.//Theo: Not enough for my full power?//Chuck: Unfortunately not.//Chuck: We’ll need resources to bring the population over when ready.//Theo: :(//Theo: As it shall be.

He closed his STAR down with a glum expression. Having his abilities truncated to something this current System could understand was rather stifling. He made a mental note to really go all out next time he was back to his full self.

Still, what the two magicians told him was interesting.

Max was actually two versions of himself molded into one - with some demon mixed in for good measure. While the Outsiders had known of the multiple Earth theory, he could never have imagined that one of them actually had real demons and a literal Hell. Whether all Earths were this way or not wasn’t clear, but it painted a picture.

They had also seen modern-looking weaponry, but not from Earth. It sounded like a version of the fantasy world Othea had advanced more than the one Ren was from.

Theo furrowed his brow as he looked up at the gently shifting canopy. Not only that, but the trio here were also Guardians. It made sense that he could sense some power in them, as it felt like a shard of his Aspect. Maybe if the world wasn’t half-corrupted, they’d have ascended like he and Sally did. There could even be a chance still…

No point in speculating until Chuck sent him the scanning device. He pulled another blood bag from his Inventory and tore the seal. With any luck, he’d be back on Sanctuary in no time.

While he drank, he pulled the piece of folded paper Max had given him out of his suit pocket. The magician had scribbled the name of the book it had originally come from on one side.

Demonic Rites and Foul Magicks

He gingerly opened it up. There was a diagram of a portal device. Several notes describing pieces of the stone archway. Apparently, this was how the magician ended up here, and Ren had mentioned a similar pink portal on her world.

It meant little to Theo, but he was sure Chuck would be interested. Anything that explained or could be used to halt the transfer of people from real worlds to the System was already appreciated - and Max seemed to hold the picture in high regard.

The vampire folded it back up and put it away.

Now all he had to do was wait for an answer.

Sally pulled herself from the rubble of the building, thin threads circling around her body to patch up all her injuries. Dust stuck to patches of fresh blood. She scratched her matted hair as her spine clicked back into place.

“What… were they… thinking,” she managed. “That was a higher yield than expected.”

“Explosives tend to be rather concussive in nature.” Archie shook powdered stone from his fur as he hopped up beside her. “That was enough to-”

“Take out a whole ship.” She nodded and glanced at the sky. It was difficult to see beyond the large plume of smoke, but the two Radoch vessels remained in the air.

Whatever type of charges the bugs had been using was much bigger than what she had been expected. Two-thirds of the town now lay in ruins. The Ever-gorger was nothing but a shriveled hunk of burned meat in a wide crater, blood and internal matter decorating the destroyed buildings for hundreds of feet.

The same could be said for her army.

Most of her zombies had either evaporated in the main blast, or the shockwave had burst their weak innards. Just on sense alone she could tell she had… barely twenty still in functional shape. Not to mention her own near-demise.

With a grunt, she pulled one of the Monsters teeth from her shoulder. The wound closed up almost immediately as thread worked through it. Although that would have been an anticlimactic way to go out, it was certainly impressive.

“We should hurry along,” the cat pestered. “The artifact isn’t far away now, and you’ve made our position clear to those undecided.”

The zombie held her hand out, and fresh undead arms tore up through the rubble.

“Lead the way, Arch.”

He hesitated for a second before leaping away in his decided direction.
