Mission Acquired Walker #5939

A new [Gate] has been discovered near you and your teammates’ Soul Realm. Please explore and make contact with the non-hostiles within. You are to establish a trade route between Flore-Del-Grotta, Marr, and Unknown Non-Hostilities to deliver an offering of trade and inter-Otherrealm Alliance. Further details are attached.

The gloss notification blinked across my vision, dragging me out of the lingering warmth of the bed. Fallias’s bare skin pressed against mine, her scaled arms and legs tangled with the parts of me that were furred, skinned, and scaled—a patchwork of Hydra heritage that was somehow absurdly comfortable until reality hit.

I sighed, shifting carefully to avoid waking the house. “Fallias… off,” I murmured.

“Fiave…more…minutes…” she grumbled, voice muffled against my shoulder.

I exhaled and pinched the bridge of my nose. “No Fal-Fal. You’ve gotta get up and dressed. While I’m certain our activities were…not unnoticed, they certainly will not be by the maids. Speaking of…” I sat up a little, squinting at the ceiling. “Do I actually have a maid named Mary?”

I reflexively checked the domain crystal floating near the foot of the bed. A quick scan of schedules, personnel, and faces confirmed what I already suspected: nope. No Mary. Not even a Marianne or Maris.

Note to self: learn the names of the maids. All of them.

Fallias groaned, curling slightly as if the idea of leaving the warmth of the sheets was an offense against physics itself. “You’re cruel,” she muttered, eyes still half-closed.

“I’m practical,” I replied, voice calm but firm. “We have a Gate opening nearby and is close to our Soul Realms. I’d rather not have you caught in a robe of scales and fur while the maids decide to deliver ‘morning etiquette lessons’ in front of the household. And trust me, they will—no exceptions for…whatever we were doing.”

Her nose wrinkled in mock indignation. “I thought we agreed the maids are mythical beings who do not interfere with our…morning rituals.”

“I think you dreamed that part,” I said, sliding my legs off the bed and stretching. “The rest of reality disagrees.”

She rolled her eyes, finally disentangling herself. “Fine. But you’re making breakfast then. I demand eggs.”

“I…When did you ever expect me to cook? I’m terrible at it! Ask Ten provided she isn’t creating a dragon egg omlette…that reminds me, is she being fed dragon meat?”

Fallias only shrugged, eyes still half-closed as she flopped back against the pillow. “Probably. You know how she gets. If it breathes fire, she assumes it’s edible.”

Before I could retort, the familiar flicker of the team link shimmered across my sight. The group chat interface opened in the corner of my vision — blue translucent glyphs spinning lazily as the others’ mana signatures pinged online.

5939: Hey. What’s breakfast today?

The response came fast — too fast.

Ten: Boy oh boy do you have STONES asking that in the group after last night.

I frowned. “After last—oh no.”

Fractal: [Blush emoji] You were rather loud.

Fallias groaned, dragging the blanket over her face. “Tell them to mind their own damn business.”

“I would,” I muttered, “but then they’d just start speculating.”

V: Hey. We tango with death. Might as well do a bit of the tango of life.

Cordelia: Children. All of you. Breakfast is dried mutton jerky, bread, berry jam, and wild fruit that Ten and Sven found. Eat what you can and report to the staging point before noon.

Fractal: Wait. Sven told me he was surveying… Sven, dearie? Explain to me why you were with the gorilla?!

Sven: I was surveying. I was also picking you up a present. It’s, after all, technically our engagement anniversary.

Fractal: Awww. Honey. That’s so sweet. Except it’s nowhere near our anniversary—now explain.

At that point, I closed the channel with a sigh. The chatter kept going — text still blinking at the edge of my vision — but I’d learned the hard way that Fractal and Sven’s “domestic” exchanges could last for hours.

“Group’s awake,” I said, pulling open the wardrobe and scanning for something remotely presentable. The white and black of my Walker’s robe caught the morning light, its faint mana lines pulsing softly, alive in that quiet way relic gear always was. I slipped it on, the weight of the fabric grounding me, familiar and foreign all at once.

It felt strange, wearing it again.

So much of my recent life had been spent pretending — performing the part of a Bastion noble, all posture and perfect diction, every smile carefully measured for effect. But this robe wasn’t about lineage or influence. It was simple, utilitarian, and yet somehow heavier with meaning than any noble’s uniform I’d ever worn.

For once, I wasn’t representing the Duarte name or the Hydra legacy. I was representing myself — a Walker of Demeterra.

The thought should’ve been liberating. It was. And yet…

I paused, my reflection caught in the mirrored surface of the mana crystal.

My last mission…

The images came unbidden: the Abbess’s shrill voice echoing through stone corridors, the smell of incense and burning flesh, the screams, the stampede of feet in the dark.

And that awful stillness after.

I swallowed hard. The hum of the mana lines in the robe shifted in sympathy with my pulse. “Not again,” I muttered under my breath. “Never again.”

A faint chime sounded from Lumivis’s card, lying on the table — a small, polite tone that almost sounded like concern.If you stumble upon this narrative on Amazon, be aware that it has been stolen from Royal Road. Please report it. “Your heart rate spiked, sire,” his voice murmured, smooth and crystalline. “Would you like me to initiate grounding protocol?”

“No,” I said quietly. “I’m fine.”

He didn’t argue — but I could feel the faint echo of disapproval anyway.

I turned back toward the bed. Fallias had already fallen back asleep, sprawled diagonally across the mattress like a dragonling claiming its hoard. She had my blanket pulled halfway up over her shoulder and both arms wrapped around my pillow.

“Fal-Fal,” I called softly. “A monty is for breakfast this morning.”

No response.

“Monty,” I repeated, louder this time. “As in the thing you keep swearing you’ll never eat again because it tastes like chalk and guilt?”

Her only reply was a muffled groan and the tightening of her grip on my pillow.

I sighed. “Right. Sleeping beauty wins again.”

For a moment, I just stood there, watching her. The light filtered through the half-drawn curtains, painting her scales in pale golds and soft ambers. Her breathing was slow and even — the kind of deep, dreamless sleep I rarely allowed myself anymore.

She looked… peaceful.

It struck me how surreal mornings like this were. Between missions, reports, and endless expectations, there was rarely space to just exist. To wake up with someone you trusted enough to let your guard down around.

Maybe that’s why I didn’t wake her.

I reached down instead, brushing a strand of hair away from her face, careful not to disturb her. “Sleep, Fal-Fal,” I murmured quietly. “You earned it.”

***

Breakfast was plain—mutton jerky, crusty bread, and jam that was more seed than fruit—but it felt like a feast after waking up tangled in Fractal’s warmth. I tried not to think too hard about the way she’d looked at me before falling asleep again. Or the way my own heartbeat still felt embarrassingly out of rhythm.

Focus, Alexander. Work.

I spread the holographic map across the small camp table, the projection lines flickering as the device stabilized. Everyone gathered around—Cordelia standing at full height with her arms folded neatly behind her back, Ten still chewing, V pretending he wasn’t paying attention while absolutely paying attention, and Fractal perched on a crate nibbling berries.

“Okay,” I began, circling my finger over one glowing region. “So a new [Gate] has been found somewhere around here.”

The dot pulsed faintly, deep in the mountains north of our current outpost.

“It’s stable,” I continued, “and the scouting party confirmed it’s not inside a hostile biome. So our mission has three parts.”

I took a bite of jerky, chewed, swallowed, and tried to sound like someone who slept more than four hours and did not fall asleep holding a dragonoid girl who stole all my blankets.

“One,” I said, tapping the map. “Establish a supply line between the nearest city, the Gate, and any non-hostile inhabitants beyond it. The usual logistics, nothing fancy.”

Cordelia nodded once, crisp and silent. Her silver eyes flicked to each of us in turn, assessing readiness as only she could. She said nothing yet—she rarely interrupted mission briefings unless someone was already being stupid.

“Two,” I continued, “we protect the supply line from Others. That means full Walker authorization.” I lifted my wristband. The interface glowed, displaying the sigil of Demeterra—the tri-leaf crest. “We are cleared for lethal force.”

Ten whistled low under her breath. Fractal shivered beside me, her feathers rippling like an oil-slick rainbow.

“And three…” I let my hand fall, exhaling slowly. This part always felt heavier. “If the entire realm on the other side is hostile, our job is to subjugate and claim. Secure the Gate. Declare dominion on behalf of Demeterra.”

Everyone nodded. Even V—though he did it with that theatrical flourish of his, chin tilted like he expected applause.

This was normal. Routine, even.

So why did my chest feel so tight?

I swallowed and projected the scouting images—forest, mist, a river cutting through unfamiliar terrain.

Cordelia stepped forward at last. “Hostility assessment?”

“Low to medium likelihood,” I said. “Mana density is stable. Ecosystem appears balanced. No obvious environmental corruption.”

“If we do encounter Others,” she pressed, “expected class?”

“Class One or Two at most,” I answered. “Maybe Class Three if we’re unlucky.”

Cordelia accepted this with a short nod, expression calm and utterly professional. “Understood. We’ll run standard formation.”

“Right.” I tried to match her steadiness. “Sven and Ten scout ahead. Cordelia and I take central defense. Fractal and V monitor the rear and airborne threats.”

V placed a hand dramatically on his chest. “Ah, yes. Let me guard the rear. A position worthy of my talents.”

Cordelia didn’t even blink. “V, if you attempt to ‘guard’ anyone’s rear in the way you’re implying, I will personally break your kneecaps.”

V’s grin only widened. “General, you wound me.”

“I will,” she replied simply.

Fractal giggled, wings fluttering.

The briefing continued for another ten minutes—routes, timing, load weights, emergency return signals. Cordelia took notes with military precision, occasionally making small corrections where I overestimated or underestimated certain supply intervals. She never spoke down to me, never challenged my position as Walker… but she also never allowed error.

Strict. Efficient. Reliable.

Exactly what we needed.

Exactly what I needed.

But every now and then, her gaze flicked toward me—subtle, assessing. Like she was making sure I was steady. Like she remembered the last mission too.

I tried not to think about it. About her dragging me out of the Abbess’s crypt, half-conscious, blood everywhere that wasn’t mine but felt like it should’ve been. About the screams.

About the failure.

Focus, Alexander.

The display dimmed as I shut it off. “We leave in thirty minutes.”

Everyone dispersed to gather gear.

I stayed behind, pretending to double-check our rations so I could breathe for a second. Just one second.

Fractal padded over, her small claw resting gently on my arm. “Alex… you’re doing well.”

“I’m trying,” I murmured.

She tilted her head. Her mask-like haze-face shifted—curious, soft. “You smell worried.”

“That obvious?”

“To me.”

Before I could answer, she leaned forward and brushed her forehead against my shoulder. A gesture somewhere between affection and reassurance. The same gesture she’d given me last night before—

No. Don’t think about last night. Don’t think about her lips so close to—

“Alex,” Fractal whispered, voice quiet enough that even V couldn’t eavesdrop, “you’re not alone today.”

I swallowed the knot in my throat. “Yeah. I know.”

“Good.” She squeezed my arm, then skipped back to her pack with feather-light steps, tail swaying.

I watched her go for a moment too long. Then forced myself to move.

Cordelia approached next, stopping a respectful distance away. “Your presentation was solid,” she said. That was high praise coming from her. “Your judgment remains reliable.”

“Thanks,” I said, rubbing the back of my neck. “Just… getting used to being back on mission.”

Cordelia studied me with that unreadable calm she’d perfected. “Your last assignment was traumatic. But you are not compromised.”

“Feels like I might be.”

“Then rely on us,” she said. “That is what your Adjutants are for. You are not required to carry everything alone.”

I nodded, throat tight. “I’ll try.”

“Good. Try harder.” She placed a hand on my shoulder—not gentle, but anchoring. “We believe in you, Alexander.”

Then she left to join the others.

I stood there for a long moment, the quiet settling around me like a second cloak.

Eventually, V wandered by, flicking my forehead with one perfectly manicured finger. “Darling, if you brood any harder you’ll sprout wings and ascend into a new species of depressed bird.”

“Not helping,” I muttered.

“I wasn’t trying to help,” V said brightly. “I was trying to be entertaining. Now hurry—Cordelia’s counting down the minutes, and Fractal is vibrating like an excited hummingbird. She’ll combust before we leave.”

I exhaled sharply. “Right. Let’s go.”

The sun was barely climbing the sky when we stepped out from camp, gear on backs, weapons ready, a Gate waiting for us somewhere in the mountains.

My heart steadied.

Not because I wasn’t afraid— but because I wasn’t walking into this one alone.
