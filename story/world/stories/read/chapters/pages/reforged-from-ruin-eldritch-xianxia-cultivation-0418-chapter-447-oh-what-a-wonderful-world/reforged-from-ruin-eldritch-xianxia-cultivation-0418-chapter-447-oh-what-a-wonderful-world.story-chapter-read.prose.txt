Not all integrations are quite so disruptive as her first.

There are several hidden pockets of herself that have even less mind than the pond did, and some who have a lot more. Generally speaking, they’re either easier to overpower or much more eager to rejoin with her. She’s not the exception except that she developed a lot faster than most, and fusing back together with pieces of Her that have similarly fought their way through the safeguards back into a semblance of sanity is fairly easy.

Still, she finds herself mostly disappointed.

Most of the pockets of herself she finds are the size of a small town or less, adding negligible amounts of Qi generation to her whole and very little insight. At least very little insight that she’s interested in. Some of them have started spreading, offering her more domain she can travel through, but for the most part, the smallest pieces of herself are in fact small pieces.

Almost in spite of herself, though, she’s starting to run into more and more information regarding the state of things.

Several of her pieces have had to hide so well that even knowing where they are she has trouble finding them. The ones that failed to do so are left as ruined bits of ash and forest soil, unmade by the progressive devastation of the Feng initiative. Some of them were found directly and unmade with precision, but many of them have simply become dirt beneath forests that have grown out of stone and mountainsides as much as proper ground.

The Fengs aren’t even bothering with subtlety. They’re outright advertising their presence and their allegiance, acting as a show of force in the Jiang’hu. No sect except maybe the Peaks can deploy as many Warrior Realm cultivators as they have just wandering around, and in spite of the violence they’re demonstrating, they don’t seem like an all-out assault. They seem like roving bands of marauders, casually bored with the idea of retribution, and in defense of their perspective, no one seems to be outright stopping them.

There are corpses in some of the woods she finds, spawned with a density of ontology that bends reality around them. Defenders who found themselves on the losing end of that confrontation, surrounded by the ruins of what might have been sects or cities or places in-between.

They’re powerful places now, maybe even more than they were to begin with. A lesser cultivator could use the density of Qi and concept in the varying but similar woodlands to go up whole realms, or at least a good dozen steps through one. Or find themselves subverted, their cultivation overwhelmed and subsumed by the identity of the all-consuming transformation.

Maybe half of the newly-grown forests she finds are in places where she can sense lingering traces of herself. Not enough to recover, but enough to taste that she used to exist there. The other half, she tastes remnants of others, traces of battles lost.

She doesn’t find any Feng corpses, nor does she taste their deaths in any of the forests.

It’s a bit intimidating. Less so than it would be if she were just human-sized, but still. Some of the forests she finds are terrifyingly large, eclipsing the scale of entire cities. Trees that dwarf hills and lesser mountains crape the heavens, casting the lands beneath them into evergreen shadows and sharp leaves. Soil that once sustained whole farmlands or bustling ecosystems is dead, devoured and drained dry of every drop of life energy in a cannibalistic orgy of floral growth.

It will take months, maybe years, maybe decades, to carve out any headway against the terraformed landscape. For mortals, these might well become permanent fixtures, lasting for generations.

All done in moments. All done in a destructive wave of violence made to wipe out any trace of her.

If she weren’t such a geological feature herself nowadays, it would be rather rather intimidating. As it stands, it’s not exactly encouraging.

She beat one Feng. She held off another one. In a direct fight, she’s almost certain she’d win, though that’s arrogant enough to make her have to censure herself and make sure she’s not overestimating herself. Either way, it would be expensive. Too costly for a long term victory.

It’s ironic, really. In a direct confrontation, given a battle of attrition, she’d win. Given a war of attrition, she’ll lose.

One Feng, two Feng, three Feng, more Feng.

That doesn’t mean that she isn’t progressing, though.

There are pockets of herself that get found and added to the whole. The longer she waits, the more that the generator-obelisks and her spouses add to the flavor of her existence, deepening her ontological weight and making her resources broader, her foundation deeper. And the more she experiments, the more options she finds.

Each new piece only adds more insight, more consciousness and perspective, more Qi-generating tissues to increase her yield. Pretty soon she’ll be big enough to add a few extra percentage points to the Republic of Morae’s overall size.

But in a knock-out brawl against the whole of the Feng’s invasion, she’s pretty sure they could knock a few percentage points off just as easily.

So it is that even as she spreads, collecting fresh parts of herself, growing miles wide and concepts deep, she is most excited for when her little exploratory groups finally start to approach the larger hidden realms she can sense.

Those are much more powerful, not least because there’s massive amounts of traffic of cultivators coming in and out of them. She’s not sure why they’re holding back on that front, but maybe they’re just being thorough, making sure that there’s no place she could retreat through or regrow from.

Doesn’t matter.
