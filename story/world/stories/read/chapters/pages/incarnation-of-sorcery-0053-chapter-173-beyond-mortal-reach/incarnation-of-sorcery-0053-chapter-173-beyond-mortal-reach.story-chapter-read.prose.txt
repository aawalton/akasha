Ryland rifted back to a partially destroyed Rhel. There was, unsurprisingly, no one there.

He had so much to do, was overwhelmed with so many thoughts, feelings, and intentions, that his mind had more or less turned into a storm. It was just the state of his body and his manasoul that kept him sticking to one spot. Ryland, after a long time, needed to recover.

Taking on the sun itself was difficult even for him. Its deadly energy had eaten into his Incarnation form. Thankfully, he had been right in assuming Mavar wouldn’t have been able to survive a blast of that scale either, not when the energy wasn’t Inscribed with his Soul Glyph. As such, that problem had been dealt with permanently.

The notification from the Grand Frame had confirmed it. Deific Prints. A modified form of Glyph Prints that would allow him to recreate high-Tier versions of practically any Glyph he came across. Perhaps that might be useful.

But his thoughts kept returning to one thing. To one absence.

Acrypha.

He had dealt with Mavar as quickly as possible and had then hoped to find her on his return. Or whatever she had become. Instead, there was nothing. It was possible that as soon as she had taken the links of the Living Mana away from Mavar, perhaps maybe as soon as she had rescued the Pure Manifestations of her parents, Acrypha had returned to safety.

But why? How? What exactly were the Flourinus Order planning with her? Why had they helped her retrieve those two Pure Manifest—

Ryland’s eyes slowly widened. Two. That was an extremely specific number.

He had killed two of the Order mages a few days before this whole debacle with Mavar had started. Two people whom the Order would likely have been interested in reviving. Two people Ryland had smashed into the artificial moon where Mavar had been gathering his Living Mana all this while.

It took a while, perhaps several hours, before Ryland’s strength fully returned. And all the while, his worries and anger kept stewing and growing. All the while, he kept wondering just what condition Acrypha was in.

For if something had happened to her, after everything that had gone on, he wasn’t sure he’d allow the Order to exist. Not any longer.

Ryland took a deep breath and sighed. “It’s time to check.” He took a glance around at the remnants of Rhel. While it appeared to be on the verge of collapsing in on itself, Ryland was quite certain he’d be able to return it to a habitable condition before too long. He just had other very important priorities first. “[Translocate].”

The freezing rains of Realm 78 welcomed him with their unliveable chill. Ryland’s entire form would have turned into a brick of ice had his Incarnation form been blazing. His eyes pierced through the gloom and attempted to scour his detection across the entire planet.

“[Omniscience],” Ryland incanted. “[Seeker’s Enhancement].”

His magic rose up within him. It welled like a volcano about to burst before an eruption of his mana swept away like an ocean, covering as far as he could send it out.

Thankfully, it was subtle. Most creatures weren’t affected, and even most magically sapient beings were unaware. The few who picked it up either ignored it or understood that whoever was behind wasn’t one to confront.

Ryland was once again straining himself, but it had to be done. He had to find Acrypha.

There was no sign of her. To some, his search might have felt too cursory. Not in-depth enough. After all, what if there were spells hiding her away from being detected? What if she was locked behind powerful Inscriptions and barriers?

Having considered all that, Ryland had brought out one of his treasures from his personal Realm pocket. The little dimension he had taken Viren to get a book on the Turnalians.

The gem-encrusted compass glowed and spun if it was able to detect what its bearer sought. It didn’t matter if there was a barrier. It didn’t matter how well someone or something was hidden. If there was a sign of its target anywhere within its search radius, then it would inform its owner.

Sadly, it didn’t seem capable of that in this circumstance. Or rather, it found nothing. No signs of Acrypha at all. Even after Ryland turned the dial at the bottom to its maximum setting to allow the compass to sense anything in a radius of over fifty thousand leagues.

He [Translocated] around all over the frosty planet but found no signs. Ryland was forced to give up and look elsewhere.

Realm after Realm passed by, yet he didn’t have better luck on any of them. These were all worlds Ryland had visited before, each and every one a location where the Flourinus Order had visited or was known to be active.

Acrypha wasn’t in any of them.

She wasn’t on Realm 40, where the sky rained blood and the ground bore the stench of butchered meat. Nor was she on the broken Realm conglomerate of Celesteaia, where a planet-sized tree held islands of moons aloft on its branches.

Ryland peered into the centre of black holes, at the core of stars to see if there was a hidden compartment or something along those lines, in underground rivers of lava flowing like blood through the veins of many Realms, and so on. Several times, he tripped alarms and set off wards, alerting people to his presence and giving the misperception that he was here for them.

Cities rang bells at his presence, fortresses blared out sirens when he was looking under the lake nearby, metal contraptions took flight when he broke through the planet-wide forcefield around Realm 17 just to see if Acrypha was located there or not.

The Kurodarr mine on a lonely asteroid didn’t have any signs of her either, nor was she to be found anywhere on the back of the Archdragon carrying a whole civilization on its colossal back across the cosmos.

Ryland flew past the feast of a would-be god eating a broken moon, past a flight of winged folk in crystal cosmos-ships escaping the galactic blast of a dying star, past a sea of brilliant energy where no monsters had swallowed Acrypha or anyone like her, and past a world of liquid gas where none of the wrecks of ancient civilization had her trapped or secreted away.

Needless to say, Ryland was growing a little too worried and desperate.

He even found himself revisiting Alendra again. She was fighting off a black, leonine creature with a spiky tail and a metallic mane. The world Ryland had dropped her in was still undergoing that mad transformation, though it was quite obvious that Alendra had gained a great deal of power, going by how she handily defeated the beast.

Once she was done, she even turned to check upon a presence that probably felt suspiciously similar to Ryland, but he was gone by that point. There was no sign of Acrypha anywhere on Alendra’s world either.

Ryland returned to Rhel and just stood there for a while, trying to let his emotions run their course.

Defeated.

He had been defeated by the Flourinus Order. All because he had decided to focus on taking care of potential Calamities while enjoying life at Arcoryx Academy.

Stomping a decisive mental foot down on the growing feeling of regret, Ryland cast [Translocate] once more.

Arcoryx Academy was reeling on multiple fronts. For one, they were the centre of a minor political upheaval.

Their little exchange semester was what had brought the hundreds of thousands of Rhellian refugees flooding Vyrd. Androvia didn’t have the capabilities of helping that many people all at once, not in the most humane way possible. They were struggling.

“It doesn’t help that Jure and Kuldor have taken far, far fewer Rhellians than we have,” Headmaster Alaric was saying. He and Ryland were meeting after he had returned to Arcoryx to relay all the news. “I would think they’d possess enough integrity to assist, but it seems my faith in them was entirely misplaced.”

Ryland couldn’t blame him at all for getting angry. Androvia was in quite the mess.

“It is indeed a deplorable situation,” Ryland said.

Alaric seemed to sense the slight impatience in his words. “But I will cease my complaints for I understand that things spiralled beyond any of our control. Horrified as I am regarding what Acrypha and Viren went through, I’m not sure how I can help.”

That wasn’t something Ryland could blame Alaric for either. After all, Ryland himself had failed.

“I’m afraid I don’t know either,” Ryland said. “I really did only come here to inform you about it all.”

They fell into troubled silence. Ryland understood that Alaric would much rather return to his duties as headmaster, especially at a time like this. It wasn't that he was callous about Acrypha's disappearance. But in the absence of knowing what to do, practical people like Alaric tended to focus on what they could do.

“I will inform you if I find anything,” Ryland said.

“You intend to keep looking, yes?” Alaric asked. “Is there any way I can assist?”

“It’s alright,” Viren said. “I can go.”

He had joined them not that long ago. When he had first heard the news, he had taken it rather poorly. It almost seemed like his entire worldview had been shaken. He had recovered, seemingly, but there was still that steely but depressed determination in his eyes.

“Are you certain, Viren?” Ryland asked. “I’m not quite sure where my search will take me.”

Viren looked like he was in a bit of a mental quandary. Ah, right. His faith had been shaken. Even after all that had transpired, he had believed in Ryland. He had kept his faith. But now, when Ryland had failed to return with Acrypha in tow, it felt like a grave betrayal almost.

Like his belief had been misplaced. Maybe Ryland was fallible…

Viren shook his head. “I’m going, sir. If you’ll have me. I’ll do whatever you need me to. We’ll find Acrypha. I’m sure of it.”

Ryland nodded. “Then let us not wait any longer. I have a few more areas in mind. Let’s get going, shall we?”

They did just that. After bidding farewell, Ryland and Viren headed out. He offered to rift him wherever he needed to go, but Viren said he didn’t want to grow too dependent on Ryland and promised he’d take care of his things within half an hour.

Ryland decided to wait outside the academy, watching the city of Arcoryx move on as though nothing had happened. People went about their daily lives, priests and priestesses among them…

He blinked. Priests and priestesses. Ah, curse the Calamities.

Heart thudding all of a sudden, Ryland [Translocated] himself away. Every iota of his being felt more fired up than it had in ages. He reappeared far, far above the atmosphere of Vyrd, where a cast of [Oneself World] ensured he had a pocket of stable atmosphere to keep his bodily functions ongoing.

Then he began casting divine energy.

“[Gods’ Tangle],” Ryland incanted. “[Mortal Anchor].”

The chains keeping his Incarnation form tied down now broke entirely. His manasoul manifested, blazing with enough light to probably make a lot of people down on Androvia very, very concerned about the sudden second sun lighting up their sky.

Just like last time, the cracks in the universe opened rifts straight into the Overrealm, the Plane of the Great Gods. Reality broke into black debris of nothingness, which “fell” into the Overrealm even when the rifts were technically above Ryland. Not that he needed multiple rifts. Each was big enough for him to go inside.

So, he passed on into the Overrealm.

As an Incarnation, Ryland had little trouble existing in a plane of existence reserved only for those few, select beings that had ascended beyond the shackles of mortality.

Ryland took a deep breath. There was nothing like normal air here. All he inhaled now was the fabric of reality that made up this extraplanar dimension—divine energy. The substance similar to mana that the Great Gods channelled to construct this whole pseudo-Realm.

Normally, when Ryland used [Omniscience], he channelled mana, which left him unable to peek into the Overrealm. Now, however, he was using divine energy to cast the spell.

“[Immortals’ Truesight],” Ryland incanted.

The Overrealm turned into series of layered sections arrayed one over the other, with their contents now visible to him. Ryland had no trouble going over everything. The problem, however, was that those within each of those sectors were also aware of those spying on them.

Essentially, the Great Gods were growing aware that Ryland was taking a long, hard peek at them all.

Which wasn’t a good thing, thanks to the history they had.

Nevertheless, Ryland wasn’t about to cow down. Not now. For all the Realms and for all across the cosmos that he had looked, he was yet to search for her in the divine realm. In the one place where the Flourinus Order could be sure to be protected against Ryland.

The pressure arrived moments later. It was the Great Gods, just as Ryland had feared. Just as he had thought would occur. Unlike him, they hardly even needed a spell to locate just what was causing the disturbance. So it was that Ryland was soon surrounded by an aura of deadly intent, determined to delete that which was disrupting the general flow of divine energy.

But he forced himself to remain steady. Just a little longer. Just a bit more…

The first presence arrived like a thunderbolt the size of a mountain. It was less a Great God and more a continuous stream of power that was decimating every minute of his existence. Even in his Incarnation form, he was suffering a tremendous deal, forced to retain his hold on reality while it felt as though he was being blasted by several suns at the same time.

“It’s you,” the presence said. The words of the Great God crashed in like a waterfall of blinding, blistering energy. “After all this time. After everything you’ve done. You return.”

Ryland didn’t speak. Getting into it with a Great God was not at all how he envisioned things going, and he was determined to not have to take any action against such a being. Not now. Not when his goal was Acrypha.

Because this time, unlike when he had been facing Mavar, he was determined to find her.

“Answer me, Realmbreaker!” the presence said. Ryland didn’t have typical ears in his Incarnation form, but simply listening to the Great God was causing his figure to deteriorate further. “Speak the truth. Will you or won’t you leave the Overrealm before you are forced to depart?”

“I…” Ryland had forgotten a lot. Just speaking in the divinity’s presence was causing him a great deal of pain, using up a tremendous amount of energy. “I intend to reclaim what I seek.”

“Even here? Even in the Overrealm? Even where you of all people do not belong?”

“I have lost someone dear to me, and there is nothing in the universe that will keep me from finding her.”

The Great God scoffed. He wasn’t sure whether it was the brazen claim that he couldn’t be stopped or the fact that he, Marionys Ryland, actually had someone he cared about enough to risk the ire of the Great Gods themselves.

But that scoff gave him the opportunity he needed.

“[Omnipresence],” Ryland gasped out as his mana channelled through his besieged Incarnation form.

He disappeared. This was essentially [Translocate], just modified to use divine energy to take him to any point of the Overrealm he needed to reach. And this time, with the Great God distracted, he threw himself straight to the target he had acquired.

The location where he had finally spotted Acrypha.

“I’ve found you,” he ground out.

There, above an abyss of crimson energy sinking into fathomless darkness, floated a misshapen, twisted edifice that he was regrettably familiar with.

He had been right. The Flourinus Order had taken themselves straight into the Overrealm. They had found a new home, one where they believed they could remain safe from Ryland. For in the embrace of the Great Gods, they could rest assured that Ryland couldn’t simply drop in and wipe them all out of existence.

And that was all besides whatever other nefarious plans they had within the Overrealm.

The presence pulled him back. Even this far out, the Great God’s reach extended and reeled Ryland back in like a fish caught on a hook. The ease with which Ryland was simply almost re-[Translocated] back to where he had started from was almost frighteningly concerning.

Then he was back. Floating before that massive waterfall of power, a supernova bombardment that had him shredding away rather easily.

“Escape is impossible,” the Great God said. “You should never have returned.”

“I… didn’t intend to… run.” Ryland’s Incarnation form flared one last time to grant himself a modicum of dignity. “In fact, I intend to come back again.”

That was perhaps the last thing one ought to have said to a divinity. Ryland felt the energy around him turning sharp and heavy like jagged crystals yawning open a dreadful, crushing maw.

“You have invaded the Overrealm,” the Great God continued. “Forced entry to where you do not belong. Your insanity has alerted every divinity that calls this Plane their home. And yet, you have the gall to think that you can return of your own free will and do whatever you think is best? They have not forgotten your slights, Realmbreaker. They won’t stand for another treachery.”

“Your mistake is thinking that I care,” Ryland said. He knew where he stood in relation to the Great Gods, and even he had to admit that their existence superseded his own. Yet that didn’t mean Ryland would be daunted or dissuaded from his goal. “Beings who shelter the worst evil in all of the cosmos will face their reckoning, sooner rather than later.”

The Great God’s waterfall of energy roared into such a furious state, it felt as though Ryland was being sucked into and ejected from a black hole at the same time. His form was compressed, stretched, and shattered into unseeable iota.

And yet, the backdoor he had created kicked in before he could be truly harmed. [Mortal Anchor] finally played its part.

The tether he had created with one end in the normal cosmos now dragged him away. It wasn’t powered by his own mana. Against a creature such as a Great God, it would have been impossible even for Ryland to overcome it via normal, overpowering means. If anything, he’d be the one who was overpowered in the end.

Thus, he had to resort to other means. Such as using the entirety of the cosmos as an anchor and then rapidly shortening the tether which had no Conceptual Glyph of Shattering to break with even under the stress of unimaginable forces.

“No!” the Great God screamed. “You will not es—”

Ryland did escape. The Overrealm receded as his tether dragged him back through the rift created by [Gods’ Tangle]. A rift that no Great God could use to interfere with the natural cosmos.

Still. Said god could create a great hue and cry, however. The presence slammed against the rift like a ravenous shark trying to get to a prey hiding in the reefs. Sparks of energy longer than solar flares burst from the rift, under duress from the Great God’s ceaseless determination to haul Ryland back in. Violent motes of divine energy burst away from the rift to explode elsewhere.

But the point was that Ryland was fine. For now. He shivered despite being wrapped in a layer of atmosphere he had created with [Oneself World]. There was a lot he had to consider in his head after the fallout. So much he had do. So much he had to face.

It would be perhaps the greatest endeavour Ryland had conducted yet. But he was already forming plans on how to succeed. Even against the overwhelming nature of the Great Gods.

“Sir, what is it?” Viren asked when Ryland rifted over to the boy. “You look like you found treasure.” His eyes widened. “Wait, you haven’t…?”

Ryland grinned widely. “Yes indeed, Viren. I think I’ve found Acrypha. The only problem is that we’ll need to fight the Great Gods to get her back.”
