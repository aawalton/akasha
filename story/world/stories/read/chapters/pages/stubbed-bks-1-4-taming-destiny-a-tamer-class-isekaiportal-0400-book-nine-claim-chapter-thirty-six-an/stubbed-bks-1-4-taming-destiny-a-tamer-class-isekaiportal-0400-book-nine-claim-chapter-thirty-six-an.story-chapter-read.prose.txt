In a reverse of what happened earlier, the mountain rapidly begins to shrink once again, faster than when it rose into the air.

Alarmed shouts echo around the deck as our ship begins to pitch and roll alarmingly – the Tier five’s domain-controlled current may have abruptly disappeared, but the descent of a mountain-sized beast is bound to have an effect on the water around it and we’re still right next to it.

Spotting Vellus at the railing, I stagger over to him, using every hand-hold I can reach to steady my movement. In my exhausted state, even my Class-enhanced Dexterity isn’t enough to keep me stable.

When I reach the Water Mage, alarm spikes in me at the sight of his pale and sweaty face.

“What’s wrong?” I ask urgently, my mind flipping through various possibilities – all bad.

Vellus just shakes his head, his mouth working soundlessly. Then, before my eyes, he makes a considerable effort to pull himself back together. Swallowing, he dabs at his forehead with a cloth, removing the sweat manually rather than using his magic.

“The beast is not returning, thank the gods, and though the currents are significantly disturbed, as long as we avoid the whirlpool in the centre until it settles, we will survive this intact.”

I frown at the obvious good news.

“Then why were you looking so terrified?”

Vellus looks like he wishes to protest the description, but thinks better of it, perhaps admitting to himself that it’s apt.

“It… As the beast’s tentacle descended, I was able to glimpse a hint of the beast’s whole.” He swallows, sweat appearing once more on his forehead as fear glints in his eyes.

My frown deepens, my attention caught on one word.

“Tentacle?” I demand.

Vellus nods solemnly.

“The beast itself lies deep below, and I cannot see its end.”

It’s my turn to swallow and feel prickles of sweat develop on my own forehead. To think that the beast is below us, capable of reaching up to consume us at any moment….

If it was planning on eating us, it’s had ample opportunity, I remind myself. It was only interested in the roc. As long as we don’t fight any more Tier fours, I doubt it will pay attention to us.

I might not practice Water-Shaping on the sea anytime soon, though.

Still, Vellus’ words are already proving true – the pitching and rolling of the ship is beginning to calm. Over where the beast – the tentacle – used to be, the whirlpool is slowly stabilising, its centre not quite as deep as it was.

With the emergency over, the fliers who have been desperately keeping our ship on an even keel now come into land. Ivor and Noir perch on the deck, their wings drooping in exhaustion. Sending them gratitude but feeling unable to move to join them, I beckon them over and they trudge towards me, disappearing into my tattoo wordlessly. While it’s not an ideal environment in which to recuperate, it makes up for poor rates of recovery with safety.

Bastet, too, soars closer, then lands heavily on the deck, almost collapsing. For her, I manage to convince myself to stop clinging onto the railing, though my body protests strongly about it. I attempt to send a flicker of healing magic through myself, then wince as even reaching for my magic makes it feel like burning shards of glass are forcing their way through my veins. No magic for now, point taken, I think wryly to myself, attempting to push away the worry.

I’m not the only one who approaches the newly-Evolved raptorcat. Ninja and Lathani reach her before I do, and Kalanthia arrives at the same time. Fenrir and Sirocco, who had both been sheltering in my tattoo for most of the battle, also emerge, excitement and curiosity emanating from them in different measures. Even Aingeal darts out of the hot air balloon envelope and zooms closer to float above us all. I make a mental note to go and collect the balloon before it loses so much height that it gets caught in the water. And to check it for structural integrity before using it again.

Finally tuning into the wider Bond network, I’m practically drowned in the relief of my Companions on the other ships that we’ve survived, and their excitement about Bastet’s Evolution.

I was so worried when the chain broke, River confesses to me when she feels my attention on them.

What caused it? I ask, my mind drawn back to that awful moment and my dark suspicion. Was Elarion anywhere near it?

I don’t want to suspect my ally of attempted murder, but given how much he has resisted my command so far, I can’t dismiss it as a possibility.

He was not, River answers immediately, her grim mental tone indicating that she has already considered it.

No one was, Iandee confirms, having clearly been paying attention to the conversation. Artemis and Orion were on deck the whole time, guarding the anchor and the chain beyond them.

Then it was an accident? Some part of me refuses to accept that as a possibility. Have I become so cynical after my exposure to Moriaxar politics that I see shadow-plots in every moment of misfortune?

We’ll need to investigate it nonetheless, I say finally. Don’t let anyone near the chain even now – if it was sabotage, they might try to cover up any evidence that remains.

Understood, Tamer, Iandee acknowledges.

I briefly check in with Hunter and Catch on the other ship – everything is fine with them – and then return my attention to where it’s desperate to be.

Kneeling on the deck next to Bastet, her increased size is the first thing that strikes me, both because she’s bigger, but also because she’s not so much bigger. She’s certainly not rivaling Kalanthia for size, nor even the Tier three raptorcat matriarch we encountered in the other world.

Instead, she’s still smaller than Lathani, though now only slightly so. But there have been other physical changes. Her wings are at least double the size of before, perhaps even more, and the head that she shifts to lay on my lap feels lighter, for all that it’s larger than it used to be. Her eyes are brighter, a light from within almost making the bronze glow, though it’s guttering as they droop from fatigue. Her claws and teeth are sharper and something about them indicates that they’re stronger than they were before. And, of course, there’s a flicker of flame that licks out from every feather, though it doesn't burn me when I reach out to stroke her.

Closing my eyes, I dip into my Corespace first. Fortunately, that doesn’t hurt, though I wince as I see the damage I’ve done to myself. No wonder every attempt to use magic is agonising – several of my channels are inflamed and damaged, black cracks and strain marks littering my system. Even my Core has lines of black crawling on it – not cracks, but lines of weakness that could easily turn into them if I push it. That’s another reason why cleansing potions are only used in emergencies – the after effects of them take time to heal. Fortunately for me, I should be able to speed up my healing – if I have the time to dedicate to it.

But I’ll do that later. For now, I just want to make sure that Bastet’s unexpected Evolution hasn’t caused issues with her internal matrix. And I won’t deny that I am incredibly curious about what her internal matrix will look like. I’ve never seen Kalanthia’s, and I have strong suspicion that Bastet’s will be different from Flower and Tarra’s internal matrices after they made it to Tier three.

My instincts turn out to be correct: Bastet’s internal matrix is different from any other Tier three I’ve seen. She’s developed five Cores, for one. Each is smaller than the one she originally had. That, at least, is similar to the Enlightened Pathwalkers.

However, what is most interesting is that the Cores don’t all give off the same kind of Energy, nor are they the same colours. One has a faint red tone and gives off a strong impression of fire magic, two are white, with just as strong an impression of air-magic. A fourth Core, tinged with red and with no elemental alignment sits in the centre of her chest and a final one appears to be at the base of her tail, and has a strong feeling of potential – like it is not yet dedicated to an element, but will be later.

It’s clear that the Evolution was a success, despite the unusual way in which it developed. It’s just as clear that Bastet was dancing on the knife’s edge throughout it. Her system is clearly strained – nothing that time spent Meditating won’t help, but she probably ought not to push her abilities to their limits until she’s done so. I don’t see any indication that long-term damage has been done, or anything that might stop her Evolving to the next Tier, but that’s a long way off and, honestly, I don’t even know what to look for with that.

Pulling out of her Core space, I gaze into her exhausted and worried eyes. Around us, the rest of our Companions are standing tensely, waiting for my verdict. Even Loran and Alyna have joined us, with Sarran watching from slightly further off.

“Congratulations,” I tell Bastet, smiling down at her with all the love I hold for her in my heart. “Despite everything, your Evolution was a success.”

As our Companions shout, roar, or otherwise exclaim in joy, Bastet emanates smugness, as if to say that she knew all along that it would be a success – because what else could it be? But the way her eyes slid shut in relief tells me that, even with her instincts probably telling her she’d been successful, she hadn’t been certain that she hadn’t done something serious to herself.

While everyone in the Bond network – and some of those who are not – celebrates, I pull up the information I have in my Bond screen about Bastet.

BastetRaptorcatCompanionTier 35000 HP7200 MP1570 SP0% progress to Evolution~345y lifespanFire Strike, Fire Aura, Flight, Air Current Manipulation, Lightning Claw, Lightning Whip, Frenzy

I whistle.

“Impressive,” I tell Bastet quietly. Unlike last Evolution where she concentrated on health and stamina, this time she’s poured most of her advancement into her mana pool. Perhaps that’s one reason she hasn’t increased that much in size. Still, to go from having a couple of hundred mana points to over seven thousand is more than a little impressive.

It also seems that she’s gone down the route of multiple abilities, like Raven, rather than a few powerful ones like Kalanthia did. Perhaps that’s why she’s ended up with so many Cores – Fire Strike and Fire Aura are probably aided by the Core specialising in fire-magic while Flight and Air Current Manipulation are probably supported by the two air-magic Cores. The two Lightning abilities are a bit of a surprise, though – she’s never shown indication of being inclined to lightning, and the roc wasn’t capable of it either. Perhaps it’s an interaction of Fire and Air… I set the thought aside for later – maybe it will help me learn how to use lightning myself.

I just want to be able to protect my pack, Bastet tells me just as quietly, sincerity in every word. I stroke my hand over her head gently.

We wouldn’t have survived without you, I tell her honestly. She rumbles in contentment and presses her head more firmly against me, almost knocking me over as she underestimates her new strength.

I react by leaning more heavily into her, appreciating a moment to relax after all the stresses of the last few hours – and that on top of the fight with the roc.

I wish I could stay here forever, but shouting brings me back to our wider reality.

“Ships approaching!”
