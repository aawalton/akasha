Stones. Firsts. Free for all.

Everything he’d heard so far from the mage made his skin feel a bit tighter than it was. His mind couldn't even pinpoint what exactly irked him about the whole thing. So many things seemed off. This new reality he’d stepped into made him feel like a small fish that had been thrown into deep waters.

Can I survive and grow a lot stronger to protect myself? Yes, but my reality is more chilling than I’d imagined.

All he could do was sigh and look forward. The rhythmic movement of his mata enough to anchor his mind to the present.

Xara seemed to be expecting more questions, judging by the way that she looked at him. The mage's eyes had gone soft; her demeanor was more supportive than he expected. Maybe it was the talk of death that muddled his perception of things.

He kept his eyes on the mage for a moment, noting how radiant she seemed in the early hours of the morning. He noted how the flecks of gold in her hazel-colored eyes reflected the rays of daylight. “How long till we get to Plantrith?”

“Three or four days, give or take.” She said, “If we’re lucky, we’ll get into a dungeon before then.”

Levi nodded at her time estimate. Three or four days wasn’t bad. Plus, if he was being honest, the lack of traffic had started to make him feel better as opposed to when he’d started the journey.

The lack of traffic meant he had to look over his shoulder less. “Yeah, I do need a dungeon run.”

More than anything else, he needed a dungeon run. The fact that he’d survived this long as a level five synced was nothing short of a miracle, and now he’d have to do his best to rectify that situation. He needed to level up quickly and do so as soon as possible.

Speaking of levels, he had to admit that his earlier reluctance to visit his system screen had started to wane, and curiosity had started to bubble at the back of his head. He could feel the desperate need to know what had happened to him, crawling from the recesses of his mind.

I might as well scratch the itch. Besides, not looking at it doesn’t change the fact that whatever has been done… has been done.

Levi clenched the reins of his steed tighter, not because he was angry but because he was anxious. In his line of work, he had grown accustomed to the phrase that ignorance was bliss. He’d had to kill a few unfortunate people who’d caught him stealing from them, just because they’d seen him in the act. Those people would still be alive if they hadn't gazed at his visage. And here he was about to shatter his own ignorance and gaze upon the face of Cerpital.

Levi stared at the information in disbelief. What he’d just read sounded like the kind of thing that people would kill for, which was the case with Ghost.

With everything he’d just read, he knew that if he played his cards right, he'd be able to level up faster than he’d ever imagined.

This stone has to be worth more than everything in Gantus. So why exactly had they moved it through the town?

More importantly, why had the party who'd escorted the stone been so weak? If something this powerful had been on the move, then surely it demanded the care of an A-ranked synced?

The entire thing stank of foul play. Something or someone had a hand in how the movement of the stone had been handled.

Then again, there was Ghost to consider. The rogue had known where and when to hit. More alarming was the fact that the rogue had clearly outclassed the party, which made for pretty bad optics. Whoever had been in charge of the whole thing had to have been compromised. Something bigger was at play here, and he was close to being swallowed up by it.

Subclass

[ This screen is unavailable to you. Reach Level ten to unlock it.]

Levi let out a silent curse at the system's refusal to allow him to see his subclass options. Furrowing his brows in frustration, he dismissed the screen and focused on what he’d read.

While the subclass issue was undoubtedly important, it wasn't the only thing that had caught his attention. The matter of special tasks directly from Cerpital also intrigued him, because he heard something of the sort from Conor.

During one of their friendly chats, the priest had told him how life as a synced priest worked. According to his childhood friend, the system usually sent special tasks to priests around small-town areas like Gantus to come and perform the syncing rites in exchange for level-ups.

The way that Conor had explained it made it seem like sync day was just a day for priests to earn free points. For the most part, priests, although few in their numbers, did travel in different dungeon-running parties.

For obvious reasons, synced priest healers were in high demand for dungeon runs. They earned the most coin participating in those runs, but at the end of the day, such runs came with the risk of death—unlike the safety that came with just performing syncing rites.

I suppose that means I now have an extra source of income or opportunities for level-ups in special tasks.

He clicked his teeth before asking a question to the only person who could help him at the moment. “Hey, do you mind explaining what subclasses are?”

“What?” she asked, startled by the sudden question.

“Subclasses. What does that mean?”

She blushed at his repeated question and then pushed a lock of errant hair behind her ear. “Subclasses simply refer to your specialization. A class is the broader term that encompasses all.”

“So a subclass is like an apartment and the class is the building itself?”

“That is one way to put it.”

The explanation from the mage was easy to grasp. The class and subclass thing was akin to a smaller circle within a bigger circle. “What's your subclass?”

She glanced at him from the corner of her eye and then faced forward. “Normally, I wouldn't answer that since it's personal, but since it's you, I might as well. I’m an enchanter.”

It now makes sense why I find her enchanting.

Levi suppressed a smile at his thoughts. It was a foolish thought, and yet he found himself struggling not to say it out loud.

There was no doubt that the mage was beautiful, but that was besides the point. Her enchanting subclass referred to something else entirely. He'd seen her pull out an enchanting knife back at the Broken Torch. Back then, he hadn't thought too much about it as he’d been preparing to escape.

“The fireball, was that an enchantment or skill?” Levi asked.

“A skill.”

“And how does that work exactly?”

“It's quite simple," She said. “Instead of the normal five class skills offered to you at every multiple of five levels you achieve, you get a mix of both. Specialized and general class skills. Or you could call it subclass and class skills.”

“The system will offer you five skills to pick from as usual. The only difference now is that you’d be offered two class skills, as well as three subclass skills, to fill the quota of five skills in the list.”

Gears in his head clicked, and he had to admit that it was a pretty good way of organizing things, even if he wasn’t particularly thrilled with the notion of having his original subclass blocked from him. He had to admit that the ability to choose from a mixture of his own class skills and another class subclass was… appealing.

“So two from the main class and three from the subclass?”

“Correct,” she said.

“What do you think about that system? Wouldn’t it have made more sense for the list to be filled with just subclass skills?”

“Not necessarily. I think it offers variety in the mix.” She pointed out. “Take me for example. I’m an enchanter, but I can still conjure up fireballs on my own. In essence, the two-three system offers unpredictability.”

“That is something we can agree on,” he said.

Unpredictability was something that he could appreciate. However, he had to point out the fact that the same unpredictability was a two-edged sword. Just like her, many other smart synced would diversify their skills. Making for way too many iffy confrontations. There was no way to accurately tell what a person's subclass was on the fly, or maybe it was just this version of his [analyze(I)] skill that made him feel that way.

“I know that you're only level five, but have you decided on what your subclass is going to be?”

“I haven’t.”

Of course, that was only partly true. He’d heard things back in Gantus of what some classes offered, and he’d gone to the run-down library a few times to see what they had on syncing. The information in the slum library was limited, but it didn’t stop him from reading up on whatever he could.

He’d spent the little time he had off from thieving and taking care of his siblings at the library. The books had offered him an escape from his reality, which had led him to spend so much time there that his few friends thought he planned to become a scholar or a mage—but they weren’t even close.

He wanted to be a rogue, and from the sparse information in the books he’d read, he could tell that there were at least two specializations in the class: thief and assassin. The obvious choice was the thief subclass, but he didn’t commit because there was a lot more money to be made by becoming an assassin.

He had bounced the two options within his mind just to end up at this point and figure out that he could have neither.

Levi sighed and shrugged his shoulders. He turned to face the mage, another question at the tip of his tongue, but stopped when he saw something glinting in her hazel eyes. “Get down!”
