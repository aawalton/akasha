Chapter 10: The Path of Pain

"Wider stance. Lower your center. No, lower. Your center of gravity is not in your chest, it's here." She stabbed me in the abdomen with two rigid fingers, the impact precise and painful. "Feel it here. Your power comes from here. The blade is just an extension."

The training room had become my world. Days blurred together in an endless cycle of instruction, practice, and correction. This particular session had begun four hours ago, and my muscles were already trembling with fatigue. Yet Vash showed no signs of tiring, no hint that she might grant me reprieve anytime soon.

I was standing shoulder width apart, knees slightly bent, my back was straight, my shoulders were relaxed, with my right foot forward, that was my dominant foot. The stance felt unnatural at first, but after hundreds of repetitions, my body was beginning to accept it as its default position. The weight distribution, the subtle tension in specific muscle groups, the positioning that optimized both stability and mobility, all of it felt less foreign with each passing day.

Vash was attacking me with her blade. It was the color of twilight, touched by the purple of dawn's first rays, shimmering with the core of an infinite void. The weapon hummed as it moved through the air, a sound almost like distant singing. Unlike my own blade, which manifested as pure white light with just a hint of crimson at its core, Vash's weapon seemed to drink in the surrounding light, creating subtle shadows where there should be none.

She would do it slower, as I had no doubt that had she wanted to, she could have vivisected me. Her control was impeccable, the blade stopping just millimeters from my skin when I failed to block properly. I would need to block, because if I didn't, the Resonance Blade would burn my skin like I had just thrust my hand into an open flame. The first time I had failed to parry correctly, the blade had seared a line across my forearm, leaving a welt that took days to heal despite the healing potions.

"Again," Vash commanded, resetting to her starting position. "Your movements are still too rigid. The Resonance Blade is not a club. It is not even a sword as you understand them. It is an extension of your will, your intent. Move as if the blade were part of your arm, not a tool gripped by your hand."

I nodded, resuming my stance. My own blade hummed softly, its weight almost nonexistent yet somehow substantial. The paradox of it still confused me, how could something made of light have mass, resistance? Yet it did, responding to my movements with a kind of living intelligence.

"Ready," I said, bracing myself for Vash's attack.

She came at me again, her movements like flowing water. Three strikes in rapid succession—high, middle, low. I managed to block the first two, the blades meeting with that distinctive sound of crystalline chimes, but the third caught me across the thigh. Pain flared, hot and immediate.

I gritted my teeth, refusing to cry out. Pain was beginning to seem like an old friend to me. I had begun to embrace it, to welcome it within my mind, but more than that... I began to use it. Where once I had tried to push through the pain, to ignore it, I now let it fuel me, feeding the ember of rage that Vash insisted was the source of my power.

"Better," she said, though her tone suggested 'better' was still far from 'good.' "Again."

The ship continued its journey through space, its destination still unknown to me. Through the viewport in the far wall of the training room, stars streaked by in lines of blue-white light. I was permitted to see Elisa once per day, brief visits that served as both reward and reminder of what I was fighting for. She seemed to be adapting well, learning about the Flux from her caretaker Lyra, who treated her with more kindness than any other Nyxen I had encountered.

After our last session, where I had nearly killed Lt. Zorin, the crew had begun to treat me differently. Not with respect, exactly, but with caution. Even Zorin himself kept his distance, the memory of invisible fingers crushing his throat still fresh. Only Vash remained unchanged, pushing me harder each day, unmoved by my progress or my suffering.

Pain, I would learn, is an excellent teacher. Touch a hot stove? I bet you won't do that again. Move in the wrong way and have your master's sword smoulder your flesh? You think twice about that mistake. The lessons written in pain are carved deeper than any others, etched into both body and mind.

"Your form is still sloppy," Vash criticized, circling me like a predator. "You telegraph your moves before you make them. Your eyes give you away, then your shoulders. By the time your blade moves, I've already countered in my mind."

She demonstrated, moving so quickly I could barely track her. One moment she was three paces away, the next her blade was at my throat, having bypassed my guard completely.

"Dead," she said flatly. "Again."

And so it went, hour after hour. Attack, defend, fail, pain, repeat. The cycle was endless, merciless. Yet I found myself improving despite the brutality of the method. My reactions became faster, my movements more fluid. The blade felt more like a part of me with each passing day.

Pain was always with us, a constant companion you could not be rid of. People try to shield their loved ones from pain, because it causes them pain to feel it. I would soon discard such weakness. In the world of the Nyxen, pain was currency, a measure of commitment, a forge for transformation.

You needed pain as surely as you needed to breathe.

During one particularly grueling session, Vash had explained her philosophy: "Pain is clarity. Pain is focus. When you are in pain, all pretense falls away. All the lies you tell yourself, all the comforts you hide behind, they mean nothing when pain is your only reality. In that moment of pure sensation, you can see truth."

I had scoffed then, believing it to be more of her sadistic justification. But as the days passed, I began to see the wisdom in her words. There was a strange clarity in moments of intense pain, a simplification of priorities, a stripping away of hesitation.

Pain was a fundamental part of the Primal Aspect of the Flux. That was a secret many a Nyxen Lord or Lady ever puzzled out. Vash knew it, I was certain. Pain fueled rage, and rage was a power. Not blind, chaotic rage, but focused, controlled fury, a furnace that could be harnessed, directed, used as the engine for greater strength.

I had experienced it when Elisa was threatened. That perfect moment of clarity when all that mattered was protecting her, when all my scattered emotions coalesced into a single, overwhelming purpose. The power that had flowed through me then was unlike anything I had known before—pure, potent, primal.

Before our training session had begun, the Flux flashed a notification before me. It was simple. Yet it would define many aspects of my path.

Bloodline scan complete. Nexus Bloodline detected.

That was it. No other explanation, no other notifications of abilities. Just that simple message. I dismissed it as Vash stared at me. I did not want another slap. The information tantalized me, though. Nexus Bloodline. The term meant nothing to me, yet the way Vash had reacted when I first manifested my powers suggested it meant a great deal to her.

I had tried to ask about it once, only to receive a curt response: "When you are ready to understand, you will be told. Not before." That was the Nyxen way, information as reward, knowledge parceled out like food to a starving man, just enough to keep him alive but never satisfied.

After a couple hours of this stance, and movements around the room, Vash would have me go through the cuts. The basic forms of whatever sword style she was teaching me. She referred to it as Kaji-Onro, though I had no idea if that was its actual name or just what she called it.

"The Kaji-Onro style has some basic cuts every initiate starts with. They are known as the Ten-Chi-Jin. Heaven, earth, and man. First, we start with heaven."

Vash demonstrated, slow enough so I could watch. She brought the blade overhead, both hands on the hilt, then brought it straight down in a vertical slash at the centerline of the body. The blade ended waist height, point forward and utterly straight. The movement was deceptively simple, a clean line from high to low, yet I could see the precision in it, the controlled power.

"Now, chi, earth." Vash moved with liquid smoothness, her body was art in motion. There was an inhuman grace to her movements, a perfection that reminded me she was something other than what she appeared to be. The Resonance Blade was pointed downward, and it came upward and outward with the slash at a forty-five degree angle. It ended at shoulder height. The cut was different from the first, where Ten had been direct and straightforward, Chi was dynamic, rising.

"Jin, man, is next." Vash moved again, the blade cutting across an imaginary solar plexus height. Her arms were slightly extended at the end, blade pointed to the opposite side. This cut was horizontal, a clean sweep across the middle space. Together, the three cuts formed a kind of star pattern in the air, covering high, low, and middle ranges of attack and defense.

"You will perform a thousand of each of these slashes. Every day for the next week you will do this."

I sighed, already imagining the tiredness in my arms and muscles. A thousand repetitions of each cut meant three thousand movements total, every day for a week. My arms already ached from the defensive drills we had been practicing. The prospect of twenty-one thousand cuts in the coming week seemed impossible.

"Is this really necessary?" I asked, immediately regretting the question as Vash's eyes narrowed dangerously.

"Do you think mastery comes without repetition? Do you think your body will learn what your mind cannot teach it? These cuts are the foundation of everything that follows. Every technique, every application, builds upon these three movements. They must become as natural as breathing, as automatic as blinking."

She gestured for me to begin, her patience clearly wearing thin. I raised my blade to the starting position for Ten, feeling the now-familiar warmth of the Flux flowing through me into the weapon. The white blade hummed, eager for movement.

One. The cut descended, not as smoothly as Vash's demonstration, but passable. Two. Another vertical slash, slightly wobbling near the end. Three. Better control, but still far from perfect.

By fifty, my arms were burning. By one hundred, they felt like lead. By two hundred, I wasn't sure I could continue. But I did. Cut after cut, pushing through the pain, using it to fuel my determination. When I reached five hundred, something strange happened. My mind seemed to disconnect from the action, my body taking over, finding a rhythm of its own. The movements became less conscious, more instinctual.

Vash watched silently, her expression unreadable. When I finally completed the thousandth Ten cut, sweat pouring down my face, she nodded once. Not approval, exactly, but acknowledgment.

"Chi," she said simply, indicating I should continue with the next form.

And so it went, hour after hour. Cut after cut. Pain transforming into numbness, then into a strange kind of euphoria. By the time I completed the final Jin cut, I could barely stand. My arms hung limply at my sides, the Resonance Blade deactivated and the hilt nearly slipping from my trembling fingers.

"Adequate," Vash pronounced, which from her was high praise indeed. "Tomorrow we add footwork to the cuts. Movement changes everything."

I nodded, too exhausted to speak. The promise of even more complex training tomorrow should have dismayed me, but instead I felt a strange anticipation. Despite the pain, despite the brutal methods, I was learning. I was growing stronger. Every day brought me closer to... what? Revenge? Power? Freedom? I wasn't sure anymore what my ultimate goal was, only that I needed to continue on this path I had started.

"When will we reach our destination?" I managed to ask, my voice raspy from exertion and thirst.

Vash scowled, the question clearly displeasing her. Her perfect features arranged themselves into an expression of cold disapproval, reminding me once again of the vast gulf between teacher and student, master and property.

"When I say we have. Begin again, initiate."

And so I did, raising my trembling arms once more to the starting position. The blade ignited, white light with its crimson core, an extension of my will made manifest. As I began the cycle of cuts once more, I reflected on how far I had come since that first night of the Awakening. From a broken man clutching his daughter in the ruins of his former life, to an initiate of the Nyxen, wielding powers I had never dreamed existed.

The path ahead remained shrouded in mystery, as did our destination. But one thing was becoming increasingly clear, the man I had been was dying, cut by cut, pain by pain. What would emerge from this crucible of suffering, I could not yet say.

All I knew was that pain would be my guide along the way.
