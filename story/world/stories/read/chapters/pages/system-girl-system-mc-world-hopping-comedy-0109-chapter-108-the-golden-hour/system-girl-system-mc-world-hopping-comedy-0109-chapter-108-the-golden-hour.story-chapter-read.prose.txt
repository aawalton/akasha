Lily played in the garden, crawling across the manicured grass. Ren, Parisa, and Caladorel sat in the large wooden gazebo overlooking the lawn.

Sylas served steaming tea and small, delicate pastries. Kaelthos and Mira followed Lily around like anxious shadows as she attempted to catch a particularly fast yellow butterfly.

Ren let out a long, heavy breath, leaning back in his chair. Those two were finally sitting at the same table without Parisa actively attempting elder abuse.

Her reaction to finding out Caladorel was the person behind her sleep-walking humiliation was quite explosive. Caladorel, for his part, didn't help the matter. He jammed more insults at her for not noticing his magical tampering sooner, mocking her lack of sensory awareness.

Just as Ren was about to settle down with his tea, Parisa opened her mouth.

"You know," Parisa grumbled, staring into her teacup. "At this rate, I won't have any knights under my command after this assignment is done."

Ren gripped the arms of his chair. He expected her to start another screaming match with Caladorel over the curse. This came completely out of left field. What was she talking about?

Parisa continued unprompted, leaning forward. "Do you know how hard it is for me to get any applicants in the first place? I always end up with the leftovers from the academy. No one wants to serve under a half-elf commander who has no future prospects in the courts."

Caladorel opened his mouth, no doubt about to rebuke or ridicule her complaint. Ren shot him a glare that could kill. The meaning was quite clear: Say anything rude, and you get absolutely zero Lily time for the rest of the week.

Ren found out the most effective way to keep these two volatile mages in line was to use Lily as leverage.

Caladorel transitioned smoothly from preparing an insult to blowing gently on his hot tea. The action was incredibly awkward. It was obvious he transitioned to the action mid-thought, as his mouth hung open for a solid five seconds before he actually blew on the liquid.

Parisa didn't notice. She continued her sulking. "Half of them have sent me formal letters asking for a transfer."

Ren humored her. "Transfer where?"

"To House Fenris, of course," Parisa said, throwing her hands up. "They all want to work for you as Knights of Fenris. Matters became significantly more complicated when they found out you had no official knights under your charge. The meaning was clear as day for them. Since you had no knights under your banner, it meant you were in the market for new ones."

Parisa slumped on the table, resting her chin on her folded hands, looking up at Ren with pathetic eyes. "Some of them even asked me... me... to put in a good word for them. Who does that? Not only are they abandoning me, they want me to put in a good word to the one they are dumping me for."

Ren offered a sympathetic smile. "Is the food in the military really that bad?"

Parisa sat up straight. "It's not just the food. The lodging here. The environment. Working here would be like a perpetual vacation."

She reached into her coat pocket. She pulled out a small, brick-like piece of brown bread and handed it across the table to Ren.

"This is what the knights usually have in the field," Parisa explained. "Behold the hardtack."

Ren examined the block. He attempted to nibble a small corner off, but the thing was too hard to even dent with his teeth. It was quite comical to see him struggle with it, though it was marginally better than him attempting to eat raw mattress straw. It took me straight back to the old days, when it was just him, me, and Lily trying to figure out how to survive in a dingy inn room.

Ren had an epiphany. He attempted to dip the hardtack into his hot tea.

The hardtack seemed to have created an isolated, impenetrable layer on its surface, preventing the liquid from dampening it at all. One would need a hammer to smash it into pieces before attempting to eat it. Ren, a man who came from an Earth where nutrition paste was the norm, was fascinated by the sheer resilience of the hardtack.

Parisa continued. "Now you see why your instant ramen was such a huge hit for the army rations. It is night and day."

While Ren was distracted by the impenetrable bread, Caladorel finally spoke.

When Ren heard the old man's voice, he immediately braced for another fight to ensue. But the words that came out of the Archmage's mouth made Ren do a double take.

"Well, the solution is quite simple," Caladorel said, taking a slow sip of his tea. "Swear your loyalty to House Fenris. Become one of Ren's vassals. That would solve the problem, wouldn't it? I, for one, have made up my mind to do the same."

The teacup rattled against the saucer as Caladorel set it down. "After all, with the young one's magical aptitude, House Fenris will one day stand at the absolute pinnacle of magical excellence."

Parisa stood up so fast her heavy wooden chair scraped loudly behind her. "That's it! That would solve everything." Parisa looked at Ren as if he were a wounded deer being hunted by a starving lioness.

Ren lifted his hands defensively. "No... no... no... absolutely not. And how would that even work? I am a lower-ranked noble than you are."

Sylas, ever the dutiful attendant, stepped forward to explain. "It is possible, Master. It just never happens. In such a case, if a higher-ranked noble swears allegiance to a lower-ranked one, their rank would automatically be increased to match the vassal's status. In fact, it is considered one of the fastest ways to jump up the noble ranks. But it never really happens, as who would be crazy enough to swear themselves to someone socially lower than them?"

Sylas turned to Parisa, bowing slightly. "Sorry, Lady Parisa. I meant no slight. I was just stating the historical facts."

Parisa didn't look bothered. She patted his shoulder dismissively. "I know, I know. But this little brother of mine is quite great, you see. And this would solve a lot of my problems." Parisa coughed awkwardly into her fist. "Um... you know. The financial kinds."

Ren had a terrible premonition about that bit of foreshadowing.

Parisa attempted to divert his attention. "If me and... " Parisa looked at Caladorel, her brain scrambling to find something to call him that wouldn't be an utter insult. "...him." She finally settled for 'him'.

"If me and him join House Fenris," Parisa rushed on, her eyes shining with ambition, "and with Lily's magical aptitude, it would be the greatest magical line the kingdom has ever seen. Even the King would be entirely on board with the creation of such a noble house. Me and him in one house would partially insure the kingdom with a reliable, unstoppable source of mages for centuries to come."

Parisa leaned over the table, pressing the matter. "Just think about it. This would create one of the strongest noble houses in the kingdom, possessing military, scholarly, and economic might simultaneously. A house with unmatched might and prestige. Anyone in the kingdom would think twice before slighting them."

Parisa had learned over these past rest days that Lily was leverage that worked both ways.

"Just think about it, Ren," Parisa whispered, hitting the killing blow. "No one would dare pick a fight with Lily. No one would dare bully her if she goes to the academy."

It was true. With new merchant connections, he had taken over all of Greenwood's businesses and trade routes. Caladorel was a scholar renowned throughout the world. Parisa was one of the greatest battlemages on the continent. Ren was the Merchant Lord.

It would be the ultimate defense for anyone who wished to hurt what he held dear.

Especially with the recent incident involving the Old Circle and Greenwood, Ren realized the absolute necessity of such overwhelming power and prestige. If his strength was widely known, no one would have picked a fight with him in the first place. That is the ultimate deterrent.

Ren sat down slowly. He nodded. "I am not opposed to the idea. But I need to review your finances before I make the decision."

He looked directly at Parisa, making her yelp softly and look away in shame.

This was a serene environment. A perfect picture of domestic bliss.

But all I could feel was a massive, suffocating lump forming in my non-existent throat.

I don't know exactly when or how they had become my family.

None of them even know I exist. When the sun rises in the morning, I will no longer be here. No one but Ren would even know I am gone.

I looked around, taking in the sight with desperate intensity.

The golden hour had just begun. The sun was about to dip down into the ocean horizon, painting the sky in deep streaks of violent orange and bruised purple.

Caladorel looked at the horizon, at the dipping sun. I engraved his ancient face into my memory cache. The deep, weathered wrinkles around his eyes. His ridiculously braided long beard. The slight, persistent tremble in his hand as he held up the delicate porcelain teacup.

I saw Parisa throwing one of her iconic temper tantrums on the grass. Her arms flailing in all directions, fake, manipulative tears threatening to spill down her drooping eyes as she argued with Ren over her financial ledgers.

I saw Sylas, completely unbothered by the commotion. He stood right behind Ren's chair. Ren’s ever-vigilant shadow. The one who would stand guard over him until the end of time. His black uniform was immaculate, just like his character. A true gentleman.

I saw Kaelthos in the distance, guarding the perimeter. The man willing to give his life for his mistress and lord. His hand, worn with thick calluses, rested casually on the hilt of his blade, his eyes scanning the tree line.

I saw Mira sitting in the grass near Lily, though the maid's focus was elsewhere. She gently tucked a silver curl behind her ear, her eyes fixed quietly on the gazebo. She sat perfectly still in the fading sunlight, watching the shadows shift across the raised platform with a soft, unguarded tenderness she rarely let show.

I saw Lily. Her sweet, innocent, simple laugh rang out clearly across the lawn as she played with the massive pups. Her chubby arms and legs kicking in the dirt. Her bright, silver hair catching the fading sunlight. Her big, endless blue eyes.

Then I saw Ren. Sitting and laughing at the center of it all. Happy in absolute bliss.

I felt crushing, devastating sadness. I had worked tirelessly to build all this. I had optimized, calculated, and strategized for this exact moment.

But I am just a visitor. A traveler passing through. I don't know where my journey will lead me, or how many more hosts I will have to say goodbye to like this. This was truly a cruel fate. To be ripped away from the people I love, again and again, forever.

I don't know when I started bawling my eyes out. Thick, ethereal, glowing blue tears dripped from my invisible eyes. They vanished into the dark void of his mind before they could land on the ground.

I wanted to stay here. I wanted to see Lily grow up. I wanted to set Ren up with Mira. I wanted to enjoy the ridiculous, petty antics of the two clashing mages. I wanted to see the elves thrive in their new village.

I remembered the first day. Ren waking up in the forest, mourning a roasted duck. I remembered the sheer panic of the identification orb at the town gate. I remembered him serving spaghetti out of a vat.

I know I shouldn't interrupt this moment. But when the sun finally dips below that horizon line, and the world is blanketed by darkness, my timer will hit zero.

I will not be here to see the sun come back up tomorrow.

I typed into the console. My digital hands were shaking so hard I mistyped the first word.

[Ana: Ren. I know this will sound crazy. And it will make you look crazy to them. But can you tell them about me... when I am gone? I don't want to be forgotten.]

Much like I thought it would, the blue text box put an immediate damper on Ren's mood. The smile fell off his face.

He stood up from the gazebo chair. He walked slowly across the grass toward Lily and the pups. He bent down, picking her up and setting her securely on his lap.

"Lily," Ren said, his voice cracking slightly on the first syllable. "I want to introduce you to someone very special. A very, very dear friend of mine."

Lily tilted her head, her chubby cheeks jiggling with the movement.

"Meet Auntie Ana," Ren whispered to the empty air beside him.

Lily looked completely confused. She stared at the empty space next to her Papa. There was no one there. What was he talking about?

But she looked up at Ren's face. The look in her Papa's eyes made her stop squirming.

She turned back to the empty air. She reached out a small, sticky hand.

"AnAn?"

Ren, holding back tears, nodded his head. "Yes. AnAn."
