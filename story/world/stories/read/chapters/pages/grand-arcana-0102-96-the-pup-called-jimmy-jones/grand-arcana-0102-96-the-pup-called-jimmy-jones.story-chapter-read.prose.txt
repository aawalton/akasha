I stood in the main hallway, blankly looking at the opposite wall. This was where I had first appeared when I had been snatched by the Grand Arcana, and this was where our new members would arrive. There were a lot of unspoken rules when it came to the merging of groups. In general, the participants who joined were considered junior to those that were already there. The only exceptions were pact holders.

Most of the time that worked without issue, but every now and again there were instances in which the newbies outnumbered the ones already present. Without exception, that always led to tension and a few needless deaths during the following trials. How would things turn out this time?

If Harlequin was to be believed, we were expecting seven new participants, most of them, if not all, pact holders. Even if we were considered the rule-master’s favorites, that title meant squat if all seven were set on killing us.

“Anything you can tell me?” I asked Eleonor.

She glared at me as if saying, “Didn’t you have all the answers?” then looked at the wall I had been staring at.

“They won’t be from the same place,” she replied. “A few will be pact holders. Most will just be skilled, like you.”

I doubt it, I thought.

“A few will be like me.”

Instinctively, I almost said oracles, but I caught myself in time.

“Goths?” I gave a measured answer.

“And a few will be like her…” Eleonor demonstratively looked at the bedroom door.

She and Sparta still didn’t get along, although I could sense this was more than their usual squabble. Two different organizations were in play.

There was constant talk of factions back during my previous run. Back then, the term indicated a group of participants clustered around a strong leader. No deities were involved and, in most cases, everyone knew what the sides were. Right now, I was in the dark.

“I’m sure,” I said, pretending to know more than I did. “The siren had a message for you, by the way.”

That certainly got her attention. The goth turned my way faster than a turtleclaw sensing an invader.

“What?” she asked.

“She said: more yes than maybe. Does that mean anything to you?”

The following silence told me that it did, even if I was never going to hear about it. I considered using my special to obtain some additional information when seven new faces suddenly appeared on the far side of the room.

Using Harlequin’s Sight, I checked their status windows before I checked out their faces. Of the seven, only two were pact holders, both linked to some minor deities, which I had never heard of before. Everyone’s state was the lowest possible. The cards would have been impressive if I had met them during my previous run. Now, they looked only slightly above average. It was the weapons that impressed me more than anything. All of them were atypical, no doubt bought from a merchant.

None said a word, looking at each other and at us with Eleonor.

The perfect start of a group, I thought sarcastically.

I had no idea what they had been through, but they were definitely not inclined to trust me. The goth, though, was a different matter entirely. There were three dressed in black, just like her. One was wearing mascara—likely an indication of status within their organization. Seeing Eleonor, he smirked.

“What’s black eye’s story?” I whispered.

“You don’t need to know,” Eleonor replied, looking right at him.

“How much like you is he?”

“He’s very, very different.”

According to his status box, the man’s name was Jean Evered. He had several specials, among which was the ability to dreamwalk. The number of manipulation cards suggested that he liked to frequent people’s dreams. The deity he had formed a pact with was called Midnight Shadow, though that gave me no indication what powers he might share.

All in all, he looked like the type of poser that had posters of The Cure on his walls without listening to a single one of their songs. Everything screamed fakeness. More than likely he had gotten where he was through connections and special sponsorship.

I glanced over the other two goths. My intention was to move onto the rest of the new arrivals when I suddenly froze.

Pup? I stared.

The boy was maybe fifteen, wearing black jeans and a black hoodie that didn’t suit him at all. He looked a lot younger than when I had met him, but there could be no doubt it was the same person. Just to make sure, I skimmed through his status box again.

Jimmy Jones

ALIGNMENT: UNSPECIFIED

LEVEL: 11

Strength: LOW

Reflexes: LOW

Memory: LOW

Resistance: LOW

Perception: LOW

Magic: NONE

Divinity: NONE

CARDS: 17

Major Arcana: 7

Minor Arcana: 10

SPECIALS: Eagle Eye, Dexterous, Quick Step, Fear Immunity

INVENTORY: 10 Slots

Jimmy Jones… The name was so generic that everyone had made fun of him. Maybe that’s why “Pup” had stuck. He had definitely done okay for himself, although the number of cards was far less than the general average of the group. Interestingly enough, he had focused on ranged combat this time around. Had it always been this way? When I had first met him, he was a lot more into hit-and-run close combat. His general role was to thin out waves of weak monsters or rush in for the kill of stronger ones once we had destroyed enough armor to reveal a weak spot. That’s why Avon and he worked so well together.

Why was he with the goths? There hadn’t been any such indication at the time. All I ever considered him was a starry-eyed, overeager kid that had stuck to Avon like glue. Had that been an act all along? The only thing I was absolutely certain of was that he had been killed. I had seen it firsthand, even helped Avon bury him.

If there was one thing that had shaken the man up, that was Pup’s death. Could that mean that Avon himself was part of the goths?

“Eleonor.” The lead goth went up to her. “Always nice to see you’re fine.” He gave me a quick look-over as if he were examining fish at the market.

“Jean,” Eleonor said in a reserved fashion. Even without the Observer card, I could tell that she could hardly stand him. “Didn’t think I’d be seeing you this soon.”

“You almost didn’t. I was about to choose another group when I learned you’re here.”

Arrogant piece of shit, I thought. It took less than a minute for me to wonder how anyone managed to stand him at all.

There were two interesting things in his statement, though: he had a way of knowing about the groups and, more importantly, was aware of which one Eleonor had chosen. Additionally, it created the impression that despite what Harlequin claimed, his wasn’t the only “greatest’ group there was.

“Welcome, new arrivals!” Harlequin appeared at the top of the room.

Instantly, all conversations ceased.

“It’s a pleasure to see so many skilled new arrivals to the group.” His tone was completely different from what I was used to. It was so foreign that I couldn’t tell whether he was actually praising us or it was all sarcasm. “Of course, greater skills earn greater trials.”

Three cards appeared on the wall. All of them were face down, making it impossible to tell what they were.

“The first trial taught you how to walk,” he continued. “The second—how to think. Now, you’ll learn how to strategize.”

That had definitely not happened during my last run, though by now I had gotten used to changes. From here on, for practical purposes, it could be said that I was starting something completely new. The only difference was that I knew way more about monsters and places than the other participants. Most of all, I knew a lot more about people themselves.

Red and yellow smoke descended from Harlequin. Reaching the floor, it formed a single portal doorway.

“Look at your status boxes,” the monster said. “You will notice a few changes. From here on, claiming cards won’t be enough. You’ll have to boost your strength and other things.”

I tried not to smirk. Leave it to him to say something in the most misleading way. Not a mention that cards could be boosted as well. Still, there was one silver lining. If he were saying this now, there was a good chance that there would be a means to acquire more booster spheres.

“From this point on, you could use the gambling tables to wager and obtain booster spheres.” Several mock spheres floated out of Harlequin’s clothes. To my surprise, they had the colors of actual booster spheres. “Also, you will be able to obtain them at the arena as before. However, that practice will be limited to one per day.”

Limits already? That was rather interesting.

“Do not despair, though.” For a split second, the smile on the porcelain mask almost appeared to grow. “There will be other ways for you to obtain such prizes.”

Small altars rose from the ground, surrounding the portal doorway. Each of them was three feet in length, made of white marble. The only thing that distinguished them from one another was a strange symbol carved on them.

“The deities, in their benevolence, have decided to assist you in the course of your trials. From here on, all rewards shall be granted by them personally at the altars that you find during your trials. But what about the time in-between? I hear you ask.” Harlequin expanded, turning into a balloon with hands and feet. “Statues and altars will emerge here as well, based on your performance. Since you are just starting, six deities have offered their assistance on a temporary basis. Go to the altars, look at their requests, maybe even form a pact. Upon each success, you will be bestowed with a gift.”

That made it official—the mid and major level deities were getting involved. I still doubted that it would be the top deities. From their point of view, we had merely evolved from worms to rats. Still, it was a nice step up. Back in my previous run, there was no such thing as altars in the preparation area, but the principle was the same. There would be many of them during the long-trials and it was always a good idea to pay them a visit if possible.

“Normally, no details would be given to you before the start of the trial, but in order to mark the creation of the new group, an exception will be made.” The monster floated about. “Your goal is a monster hunt. There will be numerous exquisite monsters where you will be going. The first to find and kill one worthy of the deities’ attention will be sent back here. Once everyone is done, one way or another, the winner will be free to choose their reward, as it has been up to now.”

“What about the order, Harlequin?” Jean interrupted. “How do you determine who goes first?”

I pictured the jester splattering the cocky goth into the wall. Instead, the porcelain mask turned his direction and did nothing.

“The order is the same as before,” Harlequin said. “The winners go first, and the losers go last. Naturally, the one true winner in this group is none other than my favorite, Greg.”

All eyes fell on me. Only this time, I was used to it. No longer was I the scared, weak soul cowering in the corner. The attention I was getting was a recognition of my strength.

Calmly, I went to the first altar.

SPONSORSHIP OFFER

A deity is offering you an Arcana card in exchange for a pact.

Just a card? I thought.

It was probably a bottom-feeder. Every decent deity would specify the top card. I didn’t expect to be given Grand Arcanas so soon, but at least I hoped to be offered a Major.

“I kindly decline.” I moved on to the next altar.

Four of the requests were roughly the same: a minor boost of some sort in exchange for a pact. I wouldn’t put it past the major deities to deliberately trick participants into getting a bad deal. Statistically, there was always the chance that someone might bite, and that would be hugely to the deities’ benefit.

SPONSOR REQUEST

A deity is offering you five booster spheres in exchange for a living skyfox. Additional rewards possible depending on breed.

I paused, rereading the request once more. The offer seemed rather good. If someone didn’t know better, they might be tempted to accept it out of pure ignorance. Five boosters were a lot and the name suggested that an animal of some sort, potentially flying, would be involved. What most couldn’t know was that skyfoxes were dream beasts.

“If I accept a request, will the other participants see it as well?” I asked.

“Another magnificent question.” Harlequin clapped from above. “The answer is, depends. Some deities might assign a task to one participant alone. Some might choose not to.”

The standard, noncommittal answer. The rule-masters always enjoyed watching participants squirm and doubt every decision. Yet, he hadn’t explicitly told me that I was wrong.

“I’ll find you a fox,” I said with a bow.

The message instantly vanished.

Taking that as a good sign, I went to the portal. It was time to see where I’d end up.
