Chapter 14

Client

Mr. Golem was not pleased with our rematch. In fact, he went all to pieces over how much I’d grown in the short time since our last meeting. Shot right to the heart, that did. And no, I’m not making a Bon Jovi reference. Even I have standards.

He was surprisingly grateful for the opportunity, though. I never would have imagined that dusty, old guardian would have an actual instruction manual for building your own golem on him! That Hero luck bonus must be putting in overtime with the critical dungeon level.

It’s probably an introductory-level book, to be fair, like the kind of book on crystal radios meant for pre-teens that opens with a shopping list for their parents to take to Radio Shack.

… Ah, that’s a hit of nostalgia. I loved those books.

More importantly, every hole ever dug started with the topsoil, and every study of a new engineering field has to start somewhere. The most valuable thing this book will teach me is what else I need to learn, and between the Essence System and access to a place like Lurei, I’m confident that I’ll be able to find what I need in that regard.

Or I could be totally wrong, and it could be a complete, comprehensive treatise on arcane circuit assembly for the mobilization of magical automata. I won’t know for certain until I get some good curl-up time with it, a warm blanket, and a cup of Essence Milk Tea this evening. Given the power level of the Forest Dungeon in its previous form, though, it’s not something I’m particularly expecting, even with it in a critical state where it’s practically shoving its most high-energy rewards at me.

But, hey, this world keeps surprising me, so who knows?

I slip the book into the opening of my leather bag, but the heavy thump of it hitting the bottom never happens, as it vanishes into my storage space the moment it crosses the drawstring threshold.

For now, I have a meeting with an incredibly important client. It’s no exaggeration to say that the future of the region may be riding on my ability to close this deal. So I square my shoulders, make sure I have the papers neatly posed, start reciting my pitch in my head, put on my winning smile, and step through the magical sub-planar gateway that is the doorway to the client’s office.

The dungeon core is pulsing in a slow, steady cycle as it sits atop a plain, unadorned plinth about as high as a dinner table that I’m not entirely convinced isn’t just a slab of granite that the core pulled up out of the ground. A wide, slim offering table of the same stone spreads out before it, the ancient expectation seemingly as old as the core itself.

It’s surprisingly easy to stare into the creamy, off-white flow of shifting lights, and my breathing naturally synchronizes with its idle rotations. I’ve seen dozens of these things. The more powerful ones are brighter, more active, with more ornate shrines, but they’re all beautiful.

Man, it’d make for a great desk ornament. I could make something with that pattern really easily and sell it in this world or my own. Calming stone. Meditation focus. Magic crystal. Feng shui zen ball. Whatever you wanted to call it, it’d be money.

… Not that I actually need that. Maybe I’ll just make one for myself, and as gifts for friends.

But there’s another problem: I still don’t know why it listened to me before. I don’t dare corrupt it again, even if I had the means to do so, but I can’t be sure it will listen to me if I just walk up and start talking either. However, if I err on the side of caution with that route and it doesn’t work, by the time we find out, it will be too late to fix.

I pull out my gun and switch my fireball rounds for a magazine of holy ones. I have blanks specifically for the purpose of avoiding causing damage now, but they came long after the last time I was here. If I want to minimize the variables, I have to use the same thing I did back then.

Exposing the core to purification magic while it is in a critical state and already on a countdown, on the other hand, could be unfathomably bad, but I’ve already defeated the boss, claimed a rare item, and technically culled the dungeon. Hopefully, that bought me enough wiggle room to pull a stunt like this.

“Excuse me,” I say as I train the sights on the orb, then let loose a Purification Beam of radiant light into it at nearly point-blank range.

In the wake of the attack, the lights in the orb are certainly brighter and faster, but the world isn’t collapsing around me, so I put the gun away and bring the papers forward instead. “Sorry if that hurt you. I really don’t know how you work, so I’m just trying to recreate what worked before. Hopefully, it just gave you a shot of caffeine and not anything worse.”

I move around in front of it and put my happy grin back on. “The last thing I want to do is hurt you. In fact, I want to help you! I hope that’s not presumptuous. Angel’s a great step forward in behavioral stabilization, and I love what you’ve done with the main hall, but you’re still lacking a draw, a hook! The whole reason you’re about to overflow is because you haven’t actually given anyone coming here anything to actually do!”

I let the core’s copy of my notes hit the offering table dramatically. “But I’ve got some ideas! And I think they have the potential to make both you and everyone else very, very happy.”

I go over everything, and I do mean everything. I open by covering the core problem at hand, why it’s a problem, how it got to be a problem, the ways in which the current arrangements are exacerbating the problem. I stress how much everyone adores Angel, how much help she’ll be, and how my coworkers are teaching her Imperial Common so that she can interact with everyone. I even bring up how flattered I am that the core liked my apples enough to copy them.

I lay out the issues with the dungeon's current expansion area and how taking it would cause panic, but express my understanding of how much trouble it might be to rezone at the eleventh hour. I present my alternative placement for the new building expansion, not as something that must be done, but as a solution that would meet everyone's needs, increase public perception, and protect the company's relationship with its consumer base. I include praise for the beautiful, tree-lined driveway and threshold.

Finally, I move into the design proposal itself. I explain the importance of rapid, distinct turnaround to maximize throughput, but stress that if the core starts growing tired, it's alright to close the exhibit early, as there may be growing pains until it catches on enough to really provide the juice. Of course, a small on-site inn would give customers a place to spend the night without leaving and taking their essence with them, but that doesn't need to be done immediately if it's not in the budget. It would allow more people to benefit from the arcana crops if they stayed for dinner and breakfast, though. I even add a cookbook I picked up in Lurei for its reference. I can get another copy at literally any time, so it's a great way to raise both my client's interest and its appreciation. Clients love small gifts, especially if you already have a relationship with them.

I explain that this design will be revolutionary in the services provided, and so working together with the local branch of the Independent Exploration Service Guild will be crucial for both managing the crowds and security for handling any trouble without damaging the dungeon's reputation. I present Angel as the perfect representative, of course. Dina will adore her and will defend her like a mother bear if necessary. She'll also teach the girl her numbers, bookkeeping, and about the wider world, all of which will mean greater value in Angel and more data for the dungeon.

I talk about the entrance, not the actual dungeon entrance, but the one to the new zone. Like a dungeon within a dungeon, is how I frame it with excitement in my voice. And then I reel my tone back to conspiratorial as I explain how it needs to be performatively dramatic about it. Keep matching iconography, but make the doorway big and heavy, and sealed shut when a team is inside. Have a grand bell when it opens, the same bell whether it's due to completion or failure. That makes finding out which it is a show in itself. And when a new team goes in, it closes with a booming slam of inevitability. But don’t be afraid to establish a cooldown period between runs long enough to recuperate and generate a new course! People will respect and appreciate the routine.

I make suggestions for alterations to the main “lobby” of the cavern park, some of which maybe the core just didn't consider, and others that would help entertain the customers while they wait or provide for administration. A raised stand of natural stone to accommodate performers, which should be outsourced. Even if the core learned to provide it on its own, allowing service providers to come in and only giving them a place to ply their trade means data and essence not just from the adventurers, but from everyone coming to support them. Don't staff the inn either. I even explain the concept of aged spirits and suggest the core put in an open-air bar for smoothies and alcoholic beverages. And, of course, there needs to be a medical pavilion for treating injuries from runs. All of this includes precise recommendations of locations, dimensions, and provisions.

And, the meat of the new expansion, I sell the randomized dungeon design as a unique training opportunity where adventurers new to dungeons can come to get safe, hands-on experience with real points, real loot, and real monsters! One would think this would be a provided niche, but no! People will come from all over the empire just because this is the only place they can get this product! I go into detail about the necessity of stopping the run short and kicking the current participants out before they’re killed, with their accumulated goodies removed from them. Injuries are fine, but once they start getting low on health, knock-out blows only! Dead customers can’t become repeat customers, but defeated customers will come back out of pride.

As for the dungeon itself, I make sure every feature is precisely explained. The room generation, every point on every table, every variation. After all, unlike a regular job, it’s the client who has to do the work, so it’s the client who has to know how to do the work, and I have no way of knowing if the dungeon core can read what I write. Especially since I wrote my document in English. I didn’t figure the language would matter, since Essence always has to translate dungeon script even now that it can do so. If the core can’t understand Imperial Common or English, trying to translate my natural flow of thoughts out of my native language would have just been an extra speed limit on my work.

It’s hours, and a couple more shots of Purple Sludge, of work, but even after explaining every component, I don’t stop until I’ve shown the actual system in action. I’m using paper, but the system I set up is medium-agnostic. By purely relying on the tables, I generate an entire map in a few minutes, with distinct environments and populated by monsters. Even the precise statistics of the loot that such a run would generate are listed. I even walk through a hypothetical run, describing how the team might start going forward, then spreading into the rest of the map when they reach a dead end. I have them fall to the golem so that all of the features are seen in action. When they collapse, I describe the golem stepping back and falling still before a rescue team of harpies swoops in to bring the team to safety.

Once my entire demonstration is complete, I thank the core for considering my proposal, expressing my sincere hope that it proves helpful and brings everyone to a new future. And then, with the proposal, the map, the cookbook, and the samples, I place a tub of Essence Caramel-Drizzled Custard on the altar for the core and Angel to enjoy. Finally, to complete the reproduction of that first encounter, I include another large holy arcane crystal and pat the orb.

“Let’s behave ourselves together, Forest Cavern Dungeon Orb!”

There’s a glow as the core absorbs all of the offerings. All of my hard work disappears from the table, and I consider it a good exchange.

But then the orb doesn’t stop glowing, and the entire chamber begins to tremble as if struck by an earthquake.

… Otsui …
