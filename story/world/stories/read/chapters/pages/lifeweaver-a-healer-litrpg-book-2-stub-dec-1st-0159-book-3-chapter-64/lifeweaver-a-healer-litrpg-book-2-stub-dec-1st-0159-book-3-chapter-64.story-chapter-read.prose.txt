Luke stumbled as The Healer's Moment ended. In the corner of his eye, he saw the half-demon's body start to glow, even as sludge poured from between its legs and from its neck. Unimaginable pain bloomed in Luke's chest for the briefest of moments before he slammed a Needle of Life through the pain center of his brain, turning off all sensation in his body. It was a poor solution, since it also messed with his body's spatial awareness, making all movement more than a little awkward.

Weavestepping to his left, Luke avoided being drenched in orange, burning sludge. Smaller demons leapt up and nipped at Arazek's legs and arms, as if they were sensing weakness. The glow subsided, and Luke breathed a sigh of relief. He'd worried the half-demon was about to transform into something even more difficult to deal with, like the one they'd fought in the Lilineese keep.

Looking inward, Luke wondered what the hell he was supposed to do about the new type of infernal mana surging through his channels. The pain was one thing, but it was almost like an infection as it pulled in his own mana, staining it. Healing it did nothing, and keeping it out of his Meta-Heart took incredible concentration at a time he didn't have much to spare, since Arazek had destroyed the other demons without too much trouble and was coming for him.

Weak. Luke felt weak. It was an uncomfortable truth that had gnawed at him for days now. At Level 39, he should be among the strongest Integrated on Earth, but the more powerful monsters, and the Singers, made him feel like he was back in the Tutorial Dungeon, fighting for his life. This time, death meant death. Luke wouldn't just get booted out. The power he had spent so long to accumulate, the skills he'd honed, and the way Luke had steeled his mind and body weren't enough. Not anymore. No matter what he tried, he kept coming up short.

Glancing up at the broken window where his staff disappeared, Luke shook his head. No, a weapon wasn't the answer.

"My sword," Arazek said as he tore a smaller demon in two with his bare hands. "I would have it returned to me."

Still on the ground, Luke got his hands under him. He pushed up onto his knees. Jaw clenched from exhaustion and a deep frustration at his own weakness, Luke stood, casting about for a solution. Ray struck again, but the half-demon was ready for him this time. Rather than attack Ray's decoy, he jabbed at the empty air. Somehow, Arazek knew Ray's location just as well as Luke did. One punch was all it took. Ray crashed into the bus stop he'd been standing on before this demon appeared, his head slamming into a metal pole with a crunch. That was a killing blow, unless Luke got to him.

There was no way he'd get there without full control of his body, so Luke acted. Healing the pain center in his brain allowed him full use of his limbs, but it meant the pain returned with a vengeance. Knees buckling, Luke Weavestepped once, then again mid-air, tossing himself at Ray. Using the diluted infernal mana was like torching himself, bathing his body in flame, but it did what it was supposed to.

Weaver's Renewal brought Ray back from the brink of death, but the unconscious Assassin wouldn't be much help during the rest of the fight.

"A wasteful action, one that will do naught but prolong your suffering," Arazek said, taking a step toward the two of them.

"Shut up," Luke said, getting to his feet. Using the infernal mana made him feel a little better, since the purge allowed his own mana to surge through his mana channels and take its rightful place. That cleared his head a little, and Luke saw the monster before him for what it was, or to be more precise, for how damaged it was. Health bars weren't much to go on, but at a little under half its health remaining, the damage to the half-demon had added up.

With so many smaller demons around, circling Luke and his opponent, things were getting complicated. If they had been working together, any chance at coming away from this battle with his life would have been nearing zero, but since they were mindless beasts attacking anything they saw, those little bastards helped more than they hurt. Still, it meant Luke couldn't leave Ray's side, not without risking them attacking him.

To correct that problem, Luke slapped Ray. "Wake up and get into stealth!"

Ray didn't open his eyes, but he did as he was told and disappeared into thin air. That was one less problem to deal with, but things weren't looking good.

Help from the guild was coming, but that thought didn't give him hope. Instead, it pissed Luke off. Working together was one thing, but having to be rescued? No way. It wasn't a pride thing. Well, maybe a little, but it had more to do with the fact that he kept pushing up against an immovable wall of incompetence. As a Lifeweaver, he had so many tools at his disposal, so many ways to win, but it wasn't enough. Not with what he'd been using them for. A perfect tool is useless in the hands of an amateur, and Luke felt like an amateur.

"Enough," he said, getting up to standing after killing two small demons who had approached and tried to drag Ray off. "I've had enough."

"Oh?" Arazek asked, running a hand through his ridiculous hair. "Are you surrendering? Give me my sword, and I will consider it. Either way, you will not leave this realm with my belongings in your inventory. I will torture you until you hand it back and beg for my forgiveness."

"Just shut up," Luke said, hands clenched into fists. "You're boring."

He was a Lifeweaver, a weaver of life, but also of death. Enough was enough. It was time he stepped in and committed to what he was. A healer, yes, but life was about so much more than health. The weave was so much more than the sum of its parts, more than individuals, objects, and even emotions and connections. The weave was everything, and Luke was its weaver. It was time he started acting like it.

A power surged from within. Familiar and strengthening. Will. Willpower was an attribute, but its description did not do it justice. As a Lifeweaver, it was his primary tool, the will to shape the weave.

Perhaps he should have stayed cool and collected, but a glowing rage had begun to build within him the moment he stood to face the smug half-demon and its yipping demonic monsters. "Let's get this over with," Luke growled, stepping forward.

A smaller demon leapt at him, but didn't even reach its target. Luke reached forward with a thread of mana and yanked down, forcing the thread to hold by sheer force of will. It stayed intact, and the demon found itself slammed onto the ground. Luke took a page out of the half-demon's book and slammed the heel of his shoe into its head, crushing its skull.

Arazek let out a guttural bellow that might've been a laugh before rushing in to meet Luke. Ready for the straight punch, Luke wrapped a thread of mana around it. Rather than try to pull the giant off balance, he stepped forward and followed along, using the thread as a rope to maneuver around his opponent. It was much more than that, of course. Even as Luke ducked under a second punch and reached out with a second thread, wrapping it around Arazek's ankle, he used the first thread to stab at Arazek's main mana channel, cutting through it.

The second thread was far from the half-demon's head, but once Luke touched his victim and entered its mana channels, reach was no longer a problem. That second thread exited right near the monster's temple, reached forward with Needle of Life, and punctured its left eye, followed by the right. Orange sludge spewed forth, burning the many demons as they fought amongst themselves to reach Arazek and Luke, as the half-demon let out a shriek of pain.

A blind swipe of its arm took Luke in the side, but he bore the pain - it was nothing after what he'd just experienced - and used the opportunity to get another thread of mana wrapped around Arazek. Most beings he fought against had a physical side, their body and whatnot, and a metaphysical one with either mana channels and a monster core, or something similar. Attacking one or the other was a weak use of his Lifeweaver powers, since he was one of the few people he knew of who could affect both. If Luke wanted something or someone dead, he needed to stop being so squeamish. To that end, Luke sliced into the half-demon's throat as well as its mana channels. Its physical insides might be nothing more than churning sludge, but if you took something's insides and forced them outside, that would do the trick in most instances.

Orange-glowing, too hot to touch, sludge poured from cuts all along Arazek's body, but Luke wasn't done. Not yet. Neither was his opponent.
