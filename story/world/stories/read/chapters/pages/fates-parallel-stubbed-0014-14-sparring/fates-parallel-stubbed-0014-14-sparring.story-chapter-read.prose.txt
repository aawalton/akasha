Takeda waved to greet Guan Yi as she finished stretching.

“Hey there! Are you tutor number two?”

Guan returned Takeda’s greeting with a polite bow.

“Greetings. If by that you mean I am here to serve as a sparring partner to Lee Jia, then yes. Strictly speaking, that is as far as my obligation goes, but I suppose I could offer some combat instruction in the interest of wiping the smug grin off of Yan’s insufferable face.”

Takeda blinked up at Guan’s expressionless face, then grinned.

“I think we’re gonna get along just fine, big guy.”

Soon they were joined by Lee Jia and An Eui, and Takeda began to explain her training plan.

“First we’ll do some quick spars. Each of you two against each of us. Lee Jia can start with Guan, and I’ll spar with An Eui. You guys don’t really even know how to throw a proper punch yet, so it’s gonna feel kinda like bullying, but it’s just to demonstrate the difference between a martial artist and a spiritual artist.”

“We prefer the term cultivator.”

Takeda shrugged at Guan Yi’s interjection.

“We’re kind of all cultivators, but sure. Anyway, after we’ve bulli—er, demonstrated for you, Guan and I will spar so that you can watch how each of us tries to counter the other. After that, we’ll play it by ear depending on how things go, ok?”

Jia and Eui exchanged a worried glance, but nodded their assent. The first bout was Lee Jia with Guan Yi. Eui and Takeda could have had theirs at the same time, but Takeda suggested that it would be more educational for them to watch each sparring session.

Lee Jia took her position opposite Guan Yi and they exchanged a bow. Guan Yi had a wooden polearm of some sort that he had taken from the racks of training weapons that flanked the platform in the center of the field.

“How come he gets a weapon? That seems a bit unfair.”

Guan Yi shook his head in response to Lee Jia’s protest.

“If you want to face me as a cultivator, then I am afraid the weapon is a necessity, Miss Lee. I assure you that I will not bring excessive harm upon you.”

Lee Jia grimaced. ‘Excessive’ he said. Wasn’t the whole purpose of this exercise to try to avoid a beating? Oh well, too late to back out now. Lee Jia took a fighting stance as Takeda signalled them to start.

Lee Jia approached slowly, eyeing the heavy looking polearm warily. Wooden though it was, the broad head looked like it would hurt quite a bit if it struck her. Guan Yi spoke up as she was wondering how she would get inside its reach.

“Against immortals, it’s best not to make assumptions about their reach. For example—”

Guan Yi punctuated his statement by swinging his weapon down in Lee Jia’s direction. If not for her mana sense, Lee Jia would have completely missed the wave of qi flying at her. She hurled herself to the side to avoid the attack, quickly rolling back onto her feet.

“Good instinct. Most low level cultivators would not have been able to sense that attack.”

Lee Jia ignored Guan’s words and charged forward. If he could attack from a distance, then she had to close that gap at any cost. Even if it meant taking some blows, she needed to get close enough that the long and awkward polearm wouldn’t be as effective. Guan took a stance and prepared to meet Lee Jia’s charge.

Once she was within reach, Guan Yi swung a wide, sweeping blow that she would not be able to sidestep. Lee Jia had anticipated the blow and ducked under it, just barely avoiding having her ears chopped off as she flattened them against her head. She had to remind herself that it wasn’t an actual blade. She had made it inside his reach and neutralized his weapon. Now she could—

The butt of the polearm struck her in the stomach, causing her to double over. The follow up blow sent her sprawling, and before she could get back onto her feet, she was struck by a wave of qi that knocked the wind out of her entirely. Lee Jia rolled onto all fours and gasped for air. She was still trying to struggle back onto her feet when she felt Guan Yi gently place the end of his polearm against her neck.

“That’s match. You did well for a beginner.”

Lee Jia groaned as Guan Yi helped her to her feet. It didn’t feel like she had done well—in fact, it felt a lot like she’d just gotten beaten up without so much as getting a chance to attack. Takeda clapped as she approached.

“Good job. Getting inside his reach was a good idea, but you have to remember that there’s more to a weapon than just the business end, and there’s more to a combatant than just their weapon.”

Lee Jia nodded as she rubbed at her sore ribs, still a bit too winded to speak. A few moments later, Eui and Takeda had taken their places.

“Unlike the big guy I’m not going to use any weapons or advanced ki techniques. I’ll just stick to basic movements, ok?”

Eui eyed Takeda warily and nodded.

“Sure.”

The pair exchanged bows, and then Guan Yi signalled them to begin. Unlike Guan Yi, who had been mostly stationary during his spar, Takeda exploded into motion as soon as the fight began. Eui barely had time to react before Takeda had closed the distance, throwing a clumsy punch at Takeda’s head. In one fluid motion, Takeda sidestepped the attack, grabbed Eui’s outstretched arm, and twisted into some kind of throw that slammed Eui into the ground on her back. Takeda stood grinning over Eui as she groaned in pain. Extending a hand in much the same way she had the first two times she had met Eui.

“And that’s how a martial artist does it.”

Eui scowled at Takeda as she sat forward. Begrudgingly, she reached out towards Takeda’s hand—

“Aaaagh! Shit!”

—and stabbed her in the wrist with a hidden knife. As Takeda was clutching her wrist in pain, Eui wasted no time grabbing a leg and pulling it out from under Takeda, knocking her to the ground. Eui pounced on Takeda’s prone form and pressed one hand against her neck while raising the other in a fist.

Before Eui had a chance to strike, Takeda swung upwards with her uninjured hand and struck her open-palmed in the temple. Eui immediately collapsed like a puppet with its strings cut. Takeda shoved Eui’s unconscious body off of her and rose to her feet, clutching her wrist to staunch the flow of blood.

“Shit! Ow! Holy fuck she fights dirty.”

Lee Jia ran over to Takeda as she complained, casting a worried glance towards Eui who was thankfully still breathing.

“Are you ok!?”

“Hell no! Ugh, the wrist is a bad place to get stabbed. I’ll be alright, just a second. Can you take over holding this? I need my hand. Just put your palm over the wound and squeeze tightly.”

Lee Jia did as she was told and Takeda produced a roll of cloth which she used to bandage the wound. She calmed down a bit as she did and gave Lee Jia a sidelong glance.

“So do you want to tell me what the hell that was about?”

Lee Jia averted her eyes and coughed awkwardly.

“Eui, uh—she has a very particular fighting style.”

“Yeah, no kidding. For a second there I’m pretty sure I felt her intent to kill me. I’m glad she eased off after that, or I might have had to be a bit more forceful. She does know that this was just supposed to be a spar, right?”

Guan Yi approached, regarding Eui with what might have been an appraising look. It was hard to tell with him.

“I believe the fault was yours, Miss Takeda. You gave yourself a handicap, but did not specify any conditions for your opponent. You underestimated her, and then let your guard down when you thought you had won. Had Miss Lee turned a lethal weapon against me during our spar, I would not have been fazed. It is our duty as seniors to hold ourselves back against the full force of those we tutor.”

“Tsk, easy for you to say! You didn’t get stabbed. Besides, we can’t all be career soldiers who are used to facing lethal attacks every day.”

Guan Yi looked down at Takeda’s scowl and shook his head.

“I am not sure what kind of assumptions you have about me, but I am only sixteen years old.”

Lee Jia and Takeda both blinked in surprise and stared blankly at Guan Yi’s hulking figure.

“...I have been told I look older. I will forgive the assumption this time.”

Their conversation was interrupted as Eui groaned and began to stir to consciousness. She sat up and blinked blearily at the group.

“I guess I lost, huh?”

“Of course you lost! It wasn’t supposed to be a fight, it was just a demonstration. What were you thinking pulling out a knife like that—ow!”

Takeda winced as her clenched fists aggravated her wound. Eui looked down guiltily.

“Habit, I guess. If someone stronger is attacking you, you have to take any edge you can and kill them before they can recover or retaliate.”

Guan was nodding along, but stopped when Takeda shot him a baleful glare.

“Get that bloodlust under control! There is no shortage of practitioners who would kill you for something like that. If you hadn’t put away the knife before going for my throat I might have done it myself.”

Guan took a step forward and cleared his throat.

“If I might make a suggestion. Murderous intent can be a powerful tool, even in spars and non-lethal duels. Cultivators are particularly sensitive to the emotions of themselves and others. This is why sect doctrine trains us to leave worldly emotions behind us. Miss An, your murderous intent caused Miss Takeda to panic, this is the only reason your attacks landed.”

“Tsk, I did not panic. I was just surprised and didn’t know how to react.”

Guan did not acknowledge Takeda’s interjection as he continued.

“If you can learn to control your emotions rather than allowing them to control you, they will become a powerful tool on the battlefield. Allow me to demonstrate.”

Guan Yi’s expression didn’t change as he stared down at Eui, but the atmosphere suddenly changed. Guan’s aura—which he shouldn’t have been able to control yet—seemed to change, and Lee Jia broke into a cold sweat even though it wasn’t being directed at her. Some indescribable quality of Guan Yi just seemed infinitely more dangerous than it had a moment ago. Even Takeda unconsciously took a step back from him. Then, just as suddenly as it had started, the feeling abated.

“See?”

All three girls stared up at Guan Yi in awe and nodded.

“Now then, Miss Takeda, are you well enough to continue as planned with our demonstration or do you need to recover?”

Takeda looked at the bloody bandages around her wrist and flexed her hand open and closed a few times, wincing slightly.

“I think I’ll be ok—I’ve had worse. It’ll have to be full contact though, I can’t fight like this without ki.”

Guan nodded.

“That should not present a problem.”

As Guan and Takeda prepared for their spar, Jia sat next to Eui and glanced surreptitiously at her friend.

“So...what was that about?”

Eui sighed, still looking a little downcast.

“I don’t know, it was a lot of things at once. I guess I kinda panicked a bit—Jia, your hands.”

Jia looked down to where Eui was pointing and saw that her hands were still covered in Takeda’s blood. She felt slightly queasy as she tried to wipe them on the grass.

“Oh! Uh, not mine. Takeda asked me to help her treat the wound. She uh—she was bleeding quite a lot.”

“R-right. Sorry about that. I really don’t have any excuse—that would have been a life threatening wound on a normal person. Actually, how is she still standing let alone fighting? Martial artists are pretty impressive.”

Takeda called over, interrupting their conversation.

“Hey! We’re all set over here. One of you needs to give us the signal to start.”

Lee Jia gave the signal and watched with rapt attention as Takeda and Guan began their bout. Unlike his spar with Lee Jia, Guan was quick to begin applying pressure with a sustained barrage of swipes—firing waves of invisible qi that Takeda had to dodge.

With her mana sense, Lee Jia noticed that some of Guan’s swipes were just regular strikes, feints without the qi empowering them. Takeda, without a mana sense of her own, was forced to dodge each swipe whether it sent a wave of qi her way or not. But her movements were so swift and graceful that it quickly became evident that Guan would not be able to keep her at a distance or land a blow from range.

When Takeda inevitably closed the distance, however, it was Guan who had kept control of the positioning and timing. When Takeda found herself off balance under an incoming strike from Guan Yi’s wooden polearm the second she came within striking distance, she kicked impossibly off the air, instantly changing direction and avoiding the strike. The change in momentum slowed her charge, however, and Guan Yi was able to back off and keep her within his ideal range as he struck at her again and again.

The duel seemed to have reached an equilibrium. Guan’s footwork was slow and careful, but precise and calculated. Conversely, Takeda moved with supernatural speed and grace, but was kept at bay by Guan’s constant pressure and was unable to close the distance to get within her own striking range.

The balance shifted when Takeda caught the shaft of the polearm after a missed thrust. In an amazing display of raw strength, Guan Yi swept the polearm upward, flinging Takeda into the air. As Takeda fell back towards him he swung his weapon in a powerful arc to catch her helpless.

Takeda was grinning as the broad head of the wooden polearm swept towards her. Just before it landed, she kicked off of thin air once to avoid the strike, then again to redirect her descent back towards her opponent. Twisting in the air, she landed knees first on Guan Yi, who managed to stay standing thanks to his huge physique. Takeda wasn’t deterred by his display of strength, and began to rain blows down on his head as she held on with her legs. Guan Yi threw down his weapon and held his hands up.

“I yield!”

Takeda dropped back down to the ground and frowned. She looked disappointed.

“What do you mean you yield? You definitely still have some fight in you.”

“Perhaps, but you have a distinct advantage in a close quarters fight and I am running very low on qi. Besides—”

Guan Yi pointed at Takeda’s bandaged wrist, where the blood had soaked through the bandages and was now dripping down her elbow. Takeda grimaced.

“Ah, crap. Yeah ok, you’re right. We should probably call it here, then.”

Lee Jia stared starry-eyed at the pair as they returned. She had seen Takeda’s duel with Yan Zhihao, but it was still exciting to watch the impossible feats of practitioners. The two were chatting amiably as they approached.

“You’ve got really impressive control of that territory within your reach. You never landed a hit, but I couldn’t relax at all and it felt like a single strike would have ended it!”

“Your movement was equally impressive. I should have known better than to think that I had sealed your mobility by rendering you airborne. Though in future you should think twice before grabbing the weapon of a metal user. Had that been a real guandao...”

Takeda shrugged with a smirk.

“But it wasn’t. So, girls, did you learn anything useful from that?”

Jia and Eui exchanged glances. Jia spoke first.

“I learned just how ridiculously outmatched I am. How am I supposed to get on that level in a week!?”

Eui nodded gravely.

“We definitely need some kind of edge.”

Guan Yi shook his head.

“Do not sell yourselves short. Miss Lee’s ability to dodge my long range strikes was better than Miss Takeda’s, and in terms of raw speed you are already close to her level.”

Takeda had a look of consternation on her face as she sighed.

“I hate to admit it but he’s right, in terms of sheer physicality I think you’ve got a big advantage as yo—as half spirits. You’re just naturally stronger and faster, and you’ve got better perception too. We might not be able to get you to my level in a week, but maybe close enough that whatever mage tricks you end up bringing along are enough to tip the scales?”

Lee Jia thought about it for a moment. She’d kind of forgotten about her natural advantages. In the first place, back home she was only slightly above average in terms of bloodline strength so it wasn’t much of an advantage. She’d met a lot more regular humans since coming here, but they were all practitioners! They weren’t regular at all. Now that she was gaining the same abilities, the difference was finally beginning to show itself. She was distracted from her thoughts by Guan Yi.

“Practice is important, but be sure not to neglect your meditation as well. I will only be able to join you for practice on days when I am not training under Ienaga, but it is important that you make time to cultivate. How often have you been doing so?”

Jia and Eui looked at each other and blinked before looking back towards Guan Yi.

“Uh, it’s been a pretty busy week. Other than when we awakened, and today during training, we haven’t really—”

Guan remained impassive, but Lee Jia couldn’t help but feel as if his eyes had gone flat. Takeda just scowled and crossed her arms.

“Tsk, this is why talented people are the worst.”
