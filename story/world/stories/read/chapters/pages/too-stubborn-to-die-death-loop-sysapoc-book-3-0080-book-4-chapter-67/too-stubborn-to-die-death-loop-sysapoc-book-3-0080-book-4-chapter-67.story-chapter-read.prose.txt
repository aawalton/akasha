As Goliath worked up the crowd and winked at Aaron, he sank further into his chair. Had most of the elites selected from their entire quadrant to move onto the next floor been selected because some intergalactic creeps from a distant world wanted to see how his romantic life played out?

Why me?

Aaron never did like being the center of attention. And this was way, way too much attention.

“That brings us to the end of our second chances! It seems that Team Obsidian has wiped the floor with the other sectors of their quadrant. A true powerhouse sector in the making! As the curtains closed, Team Obsidian were reunited to make their final selection. It was easy enough to make. Since the same people were voting, they essentially just voted for who they thought were the strongest, picking Mandor, Voidrin, and Ikran to join the next floor.

The curtains dropped, and the audience got one last chance to see the ten who were moving on. Cheers and whistles filled the room, and a second later, they were transported back into the dungeon.

In a near instant, everything had changed, and they were standing on a floating platform in a cloudy sky.

And there were three other platforms nearby.

The other champions, Aaron thought as he looked around.

He could already feel the powerful auras coming from the other teams. And for a moment, he was a little pissed that weaker people were selected to move on because of some romance fantasy. He didn’t care for Chelias, but the man was undoubtedly a top elite. Likely one of the top five strongest in their entire quadrant, and the viewers hadn’t selected him.

Well, we still have a strong team. Especially if Mala can get over her anger and work with us. Maybe we’ve got a chance at this.

Aaron looked at the other teams, trying to size them up. These were the absolute peak of peak. The greatest talents of their integration, and it didn’t take him long to spot the standouts, their auras rising above the rest.

First, he noted Casryn the Red, the drakaran scion from the Randorian Sector. The red, dragon-like humanoid stood proudly with an aura comparable to Mo’han’s. The drakaran were a species of formidable and feared dragons, who were known throughout the multiverse and held in the same regard as asuras.

Aaron remembered the dragon race being mentioned in the trials, and he immediately felt a shiver when he looked at her. She was no one to be taken lightly.

He turned his gaze to another that stood out. Heavy, cold metal armor stood as if empty, no sign of life besides glowing blue eyes. It was The White Warden of the Hexbane Sector.

Aaron didn’t know anything about The White Warden, but as he looked over the imposing figure, a missive appeared in his System screen, as if the System wanted them to know who they were up against.

Apparently, the ghostly knight had long ago forgotten their name. They hailed from a world that had long ago wiped itself out via nuclear apocalypse, and when the integration came, the System had found life extinguished on the once vibrant world. And in the System’s infinite wisdom, it brought them back. An entire world, brought back to life as a species of living dead in an instant, and thrown into the Tutorial like everybody else.

“What the,” Aaron muttered as he read the explanation. His perception of the System was altered once again.

Turning his gaze to another figure that stood out, he spotted Mark Manifold, the man of mirrors, and disciple of Lenriel. Even as the man stood there, he stood as many. Dozens of reflections flicked about, almost like he was glitchy and never in one place.

Aaron squinted and rubbed at his eyes, turning away. It sucked to even look at the man.

On another platform, the number one elite appeared to be a beast. It was Sharrek the Shade Bender. An unsuspected humanoid turtle dressed in a gi. If not for the incredible and suffocating aura they let out, Aaron would have thought them a rather mundane opponent. Like Mala, they were a beast that had evolved and become a master of their world.

And finally, he dared a glance at Vardrak the Allseeing. Vardrak was a giant floating eye with dozens of spiked tentacles whipping around it, and only looking at its eye for a couple of seconds made Aaron feel like his skin was starting to harden, and he quickly looked away from the imposing eye. The floating eyes had quickly dismantled competition on their world, and while this was the first Aaron was hearing or seeing of them, he had a feeling that the terrifying race was on par with the other peak species.

Was it trying to petrify me or something?

They hadn’t even gotten a message yet, and as far as he was aware, the dungeon floor hadn’t even started, so he didn’t know how that was even possible. But he felt it all the same.

These guys are serious.

His hair stood on end, and there were a good deal of talents beyond just the peaks that he inspected. Everyone who had made it this far was a serious challenger.

What does the System have for us now?

Aaron looked around, waiting for the dungeon notification with growing anticipation.
