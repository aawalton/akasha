Risha studied the demon woman for a long time. There was a strength to her that stood out under Risha’s gaze, something more than a simple will.

“What do you mean?” Risha asked.

“Y-you know who Freera was, right?” Siyah shifted, suddenly unsure of herself.

Risha nodded, then stepped closer to the woman, causing her to step backwards in surprise. “Your spirit doesn’t look like a Grand Mother’s.”

“I’m not, Mother. At least not yet, I’m not exactly sure what it means. But I gained the class after… at a young age.”

“Class?” Risha hummed, she hadn’t actually bothered to check whether the demons evolved or not. She’d assumed based on their different sizes that they had different evolutions, but it looked like she was incorrect. “What does the class do?”

The woman glanced at Klu, who stood at the edge of the tent with his arms crossed. “It lets me speak with Freera, learn from her.”

Risha shared a look with Klu. Did Freera’s spirit survive her death somehow? It seemed like a strange oversight from Grand Mother Sarah, but maybe Risha had a better understanding of the spirit world than the other Grand Mothers. The thought seemed unlikely, and didn’t sit well with her fear of the angel.

“She’s told me to seek you out. That you could protect me from the others.” Siyah looked between the goblin and the orc, and Risha saw the smallest piece of fear show itself.

Risha placed her hand on the demon’s forehead, startling her. After a second the woman calmed, and Risha placed her forehead against hers. “You have my protection, Siyah. Do not worry.”

A tenseness left the woman, and she sagged into Risha. Risha held her as she once again shook with tears.

A quick glance at Klu, and he got the message to leave the tent. Whatever he wished to tell Risha, it could wait.

Risha led the woman to her own bedrolls, and held her there gently until she fell asleep. Then Risha slipped away.

The air was cool outside her tent, a misty rain falling onto the swampy waters. Klu waited there, his gaze distant.

“You draw wayward children to you, like spiderlings to the webs of their mother.” Klu lowered his gaze, “It is strange to think of all the choices that brought us here to this moment.”

Risha studied the orc, his red skin still showing the scars of his many battles, despite the evolution she’d gifted him. A choice from the orc, a reminder. Many of her people choose to keep such reminders when she brings them their new strength.

“Sometimes Risha thinks the same strange thought. So many moments where Risha could have failed.”

“And yet, you’ve never failed your people.”

“Like you feel you have.”

The orc stiffened at her comment, then looked at her. She didn’t meet his gaze, instead looking towards the mountains.

“Risha is not blind, Klu. You still carry many scars.”

“I do.” The orc placed his hand on the sword at his waist and followed her gaze to the mountains.

“You did not fail them.”

Klu’s fist tightened on the handle of his sword.

“You saved them.” Risha continued, “I would not have had mercy in my heart. I would’ve killed every last one of them, seen their blood turn the river red. And I would’ve failed because of my anger and my pain. Your people, my wayward children, they make my dreams possible. You brought that change to Grand Mother Risha. You made her a mother to all, not just the goblins.”

Klu gave a short laugh, “You have a way with words for someone so young.”

“I don’t feel young anymore.”

“War and pain does that. I had forgotten the innocence my own children are allowed to have. It is good that you keep your childishness, Risha.”

Risha’s eyes narrowed at the orc, then she stuck her tongue out at him. He glanced over and she quickly looked back to the mountains.

He laughed loud, then quickly tried to quiet himself. They both quieted, worried that they’d woken the sleeping demoness, thankfully she didn’t wake.

“I cannot stay long, Risha. Something comes from these mountains, and I am the only one of your children who can face it.”

Risha looked at the mystic. “I was wondering why you left the city.”

Klu nodded, “A warning came, from a spirit I don’t know. You can’t fight all the battles yourself, and it seems this one is meant for me to face in your stead.” Klu paused, mulling over his next sentence for a long moment before speaking, “If I fall, Rilok will inherit my powers as the next mystic.”

“You cannot fall.” Risha said. “There are too many dangers ahead, and without you, I won’t be able to face them. Don’t expect Risha to fight the angels alone.”

Klu showed his sharp teeth in a half smile, “The Grand Mother orders, and I obey.”

Risha turned to the orc, then pulled him into a hug. “You are foolish to give into death so easily, Klu. It does not matter the strength of this threat. You will return.”

The orc stiffened, then sagged into her. “Yes, mother.” The words were said in jest, but a hint of truth shone through and that was enough for Risha.

She pulled away, and turned towards the camp. “We should duel. Follow me.”

The orc watched the back of the Mother Of Monsters, the strength in the small figure still amazing the old mystic. He fell into step behind her, raising his chin with a newfound willpower.

Klu left the next morning, and they continued their march.

By evening, they reached the last bastion of their scaled enemy.

Walls protected them, rising high between two cliff faces, and surrounded by lakes of deep water.

On the cliffs and the walls rested a hundred drakes, roaring their challenge. Only two Draconics answered the challenge, the rest hiding themselves for the real threat Risha knew waited somewhere. On the walls themselves, scaled warriors stood, bows ready for Risha’s approaching forces. In the waters, serpents swam through the shallows.

Risha had her own defences in the water, a rare evolution for a few spiders granted them strength in the waters, and the serpents who fought for her had met her late in the night to receive their own evolutions.

Her people stopped far away from the walls, outside the range of the bows. Then the spiders began to dig and set their own wall of webs. Blood filled the water as the forces met in the depths, but it was only a test before they retreated to their lakes.

Risha stood at the head of the army atop Kliks, watching the opposing force. Her spiderlings crawled through her hair and over her cloak, bringing her comfort in the face of the coming battle.

Opposite her, several figures glowed with the power of the Spark who controlled them. She counted ten in total, but knew that more waited behind the walls. They also watched her, their gazes prickling her skin.

She turned her gaze away from them to look at the mountains rising in the background. The dragons were here somewhere, she knew it.

She tapped Kliks’s side, and the spiderling led her back to the command tent. Glo stood in the middle of a maelstrom of lieutenants, running back and forth as they prepared their defences and the siege.

A large sapling with the features of an oak tree bowed his head to Risha as she entered.

Next to the sapling, Klaz’zks was in her human form, speaking quietly with Sun-Chaser. Elder Oltak and Elder Kulos readied themselves in their own ways. Draconic Rider Caleb watched them all with his arms crossed.

All of them turned to her as she dismounted Kliks, a hush falling over the most powerful in her retinue.

“Where do you need me Glo?” Risha asked.

“We will have need of you soon, Grand Mother.” Glo paused, “They have powers they have not shown, and I will want you ready to meet them.”

Risha crossed her arms, unhappy to be waiting, but understanding the wisdom in her general’s words. Thankfully, she didn’t have to wait long as an Under Goblin ran into the tent, bowing to the general. “General Glo! The tunnels have hit an entrance to the labyrinth.”

Glo frowned, then met Risha’s eyes.

Risha smiled at him, showing all her teeth.

He let out a sigh, “Klaz’zks, accompany the Grand Mother into the depths. Sun-Chaser, Oak, Elder Oltak, Caleb, start the siege. We want to grab their attention while the mother searches for the Spark.”

The Sapling stood tall, his head reaching the top of the tent, then the four of them left.

Risha followed the Under Goblin, to the center of camp, where the spiders had begun their tunnels into the swamps.

The magic of several Under Goblin Miners kept the swamps from flooding the tunnels that the spiders dug, but Risha still had to ignore the fear of the crushing waters that filled her as they started into the depths.

Draconic Roars echoed through the earth as they walked, then the howls and warcrys of her people.

Warriors joined them as they traveled into the depths. Here, the power of the Under Goblins and the few demons who accompanied them, showed itself.

Risha recognized more than a few familiar faces as they approached the doors of stone that marked an entrance to the labyrinth. Siyah as well, traveled with her hood up.

Under Goblin Lolo stood at the doors, leading a unit of warriors that stood with weapons facing the door.

He bowed to Risha, “Can you open them, Grand Mother?”

Risha nodded, then walked up to the doors, her hand reaching out as she remembered the way in which she’d opened the entrance under the Orc City.

Fiery script lit up across the door, Risha’s spirit leaving her to ignite the flames. With a creak, the doors unlocked, then burst open.

Magic flew towards her as Scaled warriors attempted to kill the intruders.

Nyx.

The spirit answered Risha’s call, and shadows rose from the ground, consuming the approaching magic.

Spiderling streamed from her body as the warriors around her let out a warcry.

White fire of the chained spark, wielded by the Under Gobliins, met the earth and water of the spark they faced. Spiders met large lizards and Risha found herself facing against three Spark-Touched.

She smiled.

Igni, Nyx.

Fire and shadow combined in a torrent of power that rushed two of the Spark-Touched, while Klaz’zks dropped on the third.

Risha drew her swords, Nyx’s shadows expanding around her to rule the battlefield.

She stepped, appearing next to the first Spark-Touched. He slashed at her with his sword, and she caught the weapon with Scarlet’s chain. Crimson pierced deep into the scaled body.

The scaled warrior smiled a bloody smile as the earth rose around her to crush her. Risha smiled back, and she fell into the shadows at her feet, drawing Crimson free as she fell.

The scaled warrior crushed himself with his own rock, and she appeared next to the second Spark-Touched.

Spears of ice attempted to pierce her from every direction, but Risha called upon Igni.

A sphere of fire expanded around her, turning the ice to steam instantly as she threw Crimson into the Spark-Touched.

His shield deflected the sword into the wall, and she pulled herself to it. Her feet hit the surprised warrior’s shield, throwing him off-balance as she pulled Crimson from the wall, and stabbed forward with her other sword.

He blocked the strike, and she stepped through the shadows to appear behind him. Crimson flew into his back. Scarlet then proceeded to cut his head off.

She pulled both of the swords to her hands as she turned to the battle around her. Undergoblin Lolo’s fiery sword cut through the last of the defenders as Klaz’zks wrapped the body of her own Spark-Touched in web, and proceeded to drink him dry.

Risha walked forward, her people finishing their bloodshed to follow her.
