Ferris looked down at the legacy chest his uncle was trusting him with, thinking about clan loyalty.

“Uncle,” Ferris said slowly, he had been working his way up to this question, and now seemed the time to ask, “the oath is putting down chains into my soul. Will that… can it affect my mind? Change me?”

The man was silent for a moment before responding.

“I’ve never made an oath like yours,” he admitted, “what I know comes from others. I know it binds to you, and that connection causes a backlash if you break your word. I’ve never heard of it influencing someone’s mind…”

Uncle Winton sighed, then placed a hand on Ferris’ chest.

“At the end of the day, cultivation is a journey that will strip bare your heart. You must discover who you are, and be true to yourself to climb high. Even if the oath makes you predisposed to Hollowfield, it is but a stepping stone on your path. This is like being worried about dirt on an oyster’s shell, when you plan on cracking it open to reach the pearl inside. In the end, you simply need to be stronger than the chains it places on you.”

Ferris didn’t love the metaphor of cracking open his soul. He had been hoping to hear something like ‘that’s impossible, don’t worry about it.’ Instead, the answer was ‘things will get so bad, this won’t even matter.’ Not really reassuring.

“Thank you, uncle,” he said. “I will face it head on.” If the solution was to get stronger, then that is what he would do.

“I believe in you, my boy,” Uncle Winton said, pulling him back into a final hug, then releasing him. “Now, there are two more things to show you. Pull out that box.” He pointed into the chest.

Lifting out a plain wooden box, Ferris found it surprisingly heavy. The lid was latched, which refused to open when he tried.

“I’m afraid it’s locked, and can only be opened after you reach the middle stage of tier one.”

He frowned. “How do I do that?” He remembered this was also a requirement to access more abilities at the library.

“With an advancement method,” Uncle Winton said.

Ferris was a bit surprised, as in the past the answer had always been ‘you’re not ready.’

“That is the last part of the legacy,” Uncle Winton pulled a book out of the chest, then handed it to him. It was titled Raging Stream Jagged Point Advancement Method.

“Now that you have chosen a bond, we can discuss if our clan’s method is right for you.”

Eagerly, Ferris leaned forward. He had heard that advancement methods were vital to spiritual development, but his uncle had always refused to discuss them.

“Clan Bluereef,” Winton continued, “practices the Raging Stream Jagged Point method,” he gestured at the book gripped tightly in Ferris’ hands. “The water spear we bond contains a large river of magical water. Tell me, what is the difference between mundane and magical water?”

“Control,” Ferris answered. This was an easy one, as he’d grown up hearing about it. Magical fire may burn hotter, and as he was learning magical acid was better at causing chemical reactions. Magical water could flow through the air. More powerful versions came with improved speed and strength.

“Yes,” Uncle Winton said, then lifted his palm. Water once more began streaming out. Instead of circling wildly, this time the stream moved directly beneath Jade. It lifted the slime up, floating them above Ferris’ head. “When working in your soul, you must understand that normal logic no longer applies. There are still rules, but you can do more here than would be normally possible. I could lift Jade like in the real world, but in my soul, I can lift mountains.” Winton moved his hand, and the water set Jade on the ground.

The slime swirled a bit, then bounced over to Uncle Winton.

“Oh, want to go again?” The man asked, then obliged the slime. As he lifted Jade around the room, he turned back to Ferris. “Remember, this is specific to the cores we bond. Water aspects may present differently in others. Now, open to the first step of the book, and read the instructions.”

Ferris opened Raging Stream Jagged Point Advancement Method. There was an introduction, which he skimmed through, then he jumped ahead.

The book was elaborately illustrated, showing images of a cultivator. Each picture had detailed descriptions explaining a series of steps. The cultivator first collected special water in a pot. Specifically, water from the water aspect’s ‘spiritual representation.’

For Jade, their acid aspect was an ocean of acid. The pictures displayed a turbulent river for the water aspect.

Taking the pot of water collected from the river, the cultivator then mixed in liquid qi from their Qi Pool.

Lastly, the images showed the cultivator place a hand above the mixture, focusing on imposing their will on it. With the correct mental image, the water would lift into the air under the cultivator’s control.

“Adepts in our clan start by learning how to spiritually control the magical water from our spear in their soul,” Uncle Winton said, still playing with Jade. “The ‘Raging Stream’ portion of our cultivation method involves manipulating rivers of water to carve away at the land in our soul, terraforming it. For example, the next technique involves widening the Air Passage by eroding away the walls with a stream of water.”

Ferris flipped the page. There was a picture of the cultivator holding their pot, controlling a stream of water coming up and spraying into a wall.

“I’ve heard about abilities that give similar control for acid,” Ferris said, tentatively.

“Yes,” his uncle sighed, “but that would rely on an ability, while our techniques use the basic nature of magical water. This makes it easy for us to manipulate and move in mass quantities, especially in the soul. I’m talking about rivers and oceans. Everything would be harder with only a manipulation ability.”

“So,” Ferris said, “I can’t use it?”

“I don’t know,” Uncle Winton, “there may be a way. Perhaps pieces of it will still work for you. I wouldn’t give you the manual if I thought it was hopeless. But, trying to make our method work would be the hard way. Hollowfield will have advancement methods without the same restrictions. Don’t read too far ahead in this book yet, wait until you have a few delves under your belt. That’s an order, as your mentor. Then consider it, compared to what Hollowfield has to offer.”

“What should I look for, when evaluating different methods?” Ferris asked. Hollowfield usually provided some basic information about techniques before purchasing them.

“Well,” Uncle Winton considered, “a complete method provides at least three things: a pattern to flow energy, a way to unlock meridians along the flow, and aid in constructing the spiritual pattern. You can read about the ‘Jagged Point’ pattern from our clan’s method, but you would have no way to carve it in your soul or magical water to fill it with. The best advancement method for you would both support your fighting style and aid in developing other techniques. For example, the ‘Jagged Point’ pattern makes it easier for me to unleash powerful strikes, followed by a quick retreat. This works well with the clan’s hit-and-run tactics. Other aspect types wouldn’t be able to immediately change directions, making the pattern less useful.”

Ferris wasn’t sure how that would help him, but it was more information than he had before. Advancement methods sounded amazing, and the part about aiding in construction would make everything so much easier—and quicker.

“I am currently saving merit to focus on healing my leg. Would that go faster if I purchased an advancement method first?”

“Almost all of them require resources from delving to truly be used.” Uncle Winton shook his head again. “The general advice is to get strong enough to delve the easiest rifts, then start feeding resources to your cultivation method to break into the early stage. Those two jade slips you mentioned from Hollowfield are likely enough to start delving. I would suggest waiting to purchase a method until you can delve, as they likely cost a lot more.”

“The early stage…” Ferris repeated, looking back at the wooden box. “So, the advancement method will let me get to the middle stage, which lets me open this box?”

“You’ve got the right of it!” The man smiled. “And we saved the best for last. Inside, you will find instructions on one of the greater secrets of our clan. Our spellcasting ability.”

Ferris eyes got a little wider, looking back down at the box. “I can use it with acid?”

“Yes, although I will warn you now, it will have problems. Consider them obstacles to overcome.”

“What kind of problems?” Ferris asked, marveling at the plain box.

“Well, most often spellcasting abilities are slow and inefficient, but give great flexibility. Usually, this is a poor trade, which is why all the good abilities cheat.”

“One of my housemates has bonded to a book,” Ferris said, thinking of Cyrus.

“That’s one answer to the problem. A book bond lets you slowly write out a spell beforehand, then later cast the spell almost instantly. It doesn’t matter how long it takes, if you can do all the work before even entering the rift.”

“Then, how does our ability work?”

“I won’t say too much, but it involves moving a flow of energy through patterns in the soul,” Uncle Winton explained, “this is slow, but we use our magical water to move the energy faster.”

The grin on Ferris’ face cracked. Here it was again. It shouldn’t surprise him that the foundational advantage of the water aspect was at the heart of the ability. That was the entire goal of a martial tradition: layering synergistic techniques and abilities together.

Yet somehow, he’d thought he was past it. After the spear style. After the advancement method. Now this as well.

“Ah, don’t look so glum,” Uncle Winton said, floating Jade over into his arms, “like I said, it is just an obstacle to overcome. The ability should work fine, it’ll just be slower until you figure out a solution.” The man looked off to the side. “Maybe a lot slower, but don’t worry about that now.”

Ferris hugged Jade, then packed everything back up into the legacy chest. After securing the lock, he held the key in a clenched fist. He nodded to his mentor. He knew things wouldn’t be easy. At least now he better understood the challenges ahead.

Returning to the chairs, the two of them talked about his life over the last two weeks. Ferris told his uncle about Lily House, and the other people who lived there. He mentioned he was involved in a research project Elder Harper started, although Ferris had to be sparing with the details due to his oath. He complained a bit about the job refining spirit gems and his constant lack of qi, but his uncle just smiled and said he was glad Ferris was working hard.

When Ferris mentioned purchasing a journal to keep notes on Jade’s behavior, his uncle said something surprising.

“I applaud you for keeping the notes in cypher. I was unable to break it—not that I am particularly skilled at such things. I would advise you not to trust it too much, especially around the people who taught you the code. Only keep true journals in your soul!”

“Sorry, uncle… but what cypher?” Ferris was a bit dumbfounded, unsure of what Uncle Winton was talking about.

“The one in your journal. I got a glance at some of the pages when you opened it.”

“Ah,” Ferris remembered the lists of numbers he had written down, testing Jade’s memory, “that is not a code, it is just random numbers I was using to test a theory.”

Uncle Winton laughed. “Well, you can bet some of the higher-tiered researchers at that fancy clan took a look. A mundane book like that would be trivial for someone specialized in espionage to read, even closed and in your bag.” Uncle Winton raised an eyebrow, then said, “I wonder what they thought of it.”

Ferris blushed a bit. He hadn’t even considered that someone could read his journal… he was so caught up in the excitement of recording the data. He would just have to start keeping a spiritual journal, like his uncle. Intent forming should let him make paper and ink, at the low price of qi… that he didn’t have.

Still, Ferris didn’t like the idea of attracting the wrong kind of attention. A code someone couldn’t break could turn into a mystery they start investigating. Ferris didn’t want anyone to learn about Jade’s memory problems, or to be the focus of unwarranted suspicion.

“Uncle, we are being so careful,” he gestured to the spiritual room around them, “but Hollowfield has mundane books with valuable knowledge, and Elder Harper discusses sensitive topics freely.”

“Clan Hollowfield is one of the stronger organizations within Scalefort Bastion,” Winton answered, “on their home turf, they assuredly have protection well beyond me. I bet you those mundane books are kept in specific locations, never allowed to leave the magical defenses. Likely, the only ones spying on you within their walls… is them.”

He remembered the rules stating that the books stay at the library. Ferris didn’t see or detect any special protections, but they must have something. He had also suspected that areas like the training halls were protected against damage. Maybe they also kept the practice secret from spies?

Unfortunately, their time came to an end all too quickly. Ferris took the legacy chest from the spiritual device to his soulspace, setting it down next to his Heartfire. He didn’t have anywhere really safe to keep the key, so he placed it next to the Assimilate Object seed on the core’s pedestal.

When he woke up in his chair at the tea house, his entire body was stiff. Most of the morning had passed, and they had to say goodbye once more.

As they were leaving, Uncle Winton handed Ferris a small bundle.

“Here are some special incense sticks, breathe in the smoke while practicing your breathing exercises. You’ll like the effect.”

Ferris was thankful, but once again wished his uncle would be a bit more specific. He couldn’t really complain, after so many of his long-term questions had just been answered.

After they parted ways, Ferris’ eyes threatened to water up again. He had been looking forward to this visit so much. Why was he somehow worse after having it?

With a final wave, he took Jade and returned to the Hollowfield compound.

It was almost time to meet Serena for the research project. They had just enough time to drop off the incense sticks and eat a quick lunch at Lily House, before heading off to the training hall.

This time, he was ready.
