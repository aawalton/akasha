“And there you have it!” Vio’s voice echoed across the arena, drowning out the raucous cheering that was vibrating the stones beneath Edge’s boots. A shit-eating grin split his face as feral satisfaction flooded every atom of his being.

“With that electrifying performance, the Stage-Two Duos’ Clash comes to a close. What a thrilling end to one of the most exciting exhibitions I’ve ever seen. Today’s triumph was an astonishing upset by two of our newest gladiators, Edge Vasher and Melenia Rosales!

“Ladies and gentlemen, we may well have witnessed the first chapter of a new legend in the making. I have no doubt that we will watch our champions take center stage in many matches to come, including the Carnage Cup a little later in the season. Congratulations on your win and Edge’s dramatic evolution to stage three! Stomp your feet and clap your hands to show your support while I make my way down to the fighting floor.”

With that, Vio left the announcer’s booth and descended a narrow staircase that had emerged from the arena wall.

They stood there, staring up as the crowd let out a thunderous roar that filled every corner of the free city—alive with adrenaline and the visceral ecstasy of victory. Then, in front of tens of thousands of eyes, Mel pulled Edge in for a passionate kiss that made the blood that was pumping through his arteries gather below his belt.

The display only made the audience cheer louder as flower petals and betting stubs fell in a flurry. Krix appeared from a gateway, along with eight gladiators who had qualified for a lesser prize. They had already been patched up by the healers’ magic, and while frustration darkened several of their expressions, most seemed pleased by their performance.

Vio and Krix worked their way up from fifth place, offering a brief recap of each pair’s performance and giving them a moment in the spotlight. All eight were awarded a token they could exchange for a Rare skill gem in the arena’s collection, along with a pile of Credits that scaled with their placement. Edge and Mel had a separate prize ceremony that would take place in a few minutes, which would be broadcast to the crowd via illusory screens.

By now, his exhaustion had faded away, making this amazing moment feel even better than it already did. Everyone took a bow together before exiting the arena floor. The runners-up left through one gate, while the announcers led the duo toward the annex where they would receive their reward.

When the gate shut behind them, Krix turned to Edge and said, “That was one hell of a show. The two of you are dynamite. The crowd loves your battlefield romance, and that mid-match evolution is guaranteed to be one of the highlights of this season. Best of all, since you’re new, only a handful of people bet big money on you, which means we just made a killing.”

Vio laughed and added, “I can’t wait to see Edge in the Carnage Cup—both of you if Melenia can reach stage three in time. I hope you manage to evolve before the cutoff. They love it when you fight head-to-head even more than when you battle side by side.”

“Thanks,” Mel replied. “I know we’re attending some kind of prize ceremony, but I’m not clear on exactly what that entails.”

“It’s a relic from before the disaster.” Krix gestured. “A little game designed to maximize our profits from the feed. The locals love it too, so we decided to stick with the format, even after the anomaly cut off the Prison World broadcasts. The rules are simple, and all the gems are good, so you should be happy no matter which box you pick.”

The man didn’t offer any additional details. Edge shrugged and followed the administrators through a series of passageways leading to an annex guarded by some of the strongest warriors he’d seen in World’s End. They weren’t part of the city watch and must be part of the elite force employed by the venue—heavy hitters paid to protect the audience, defend the vault, and keep the gladiators from getting out of hand.

Vio and Krix came to a stop at the entrance to a spacious chamber. Inside was a ring of twenty boxes arranged on pedestals, each shielded by a miniature privacy device that prevented anyone from peering inside by magical means.

“We’re about to resume our broadcast to the crowd above,” Vio said. “Remember to smile and try to play up the big moment. Don’t worry, the details are confidential. No one will know which skills you acquire until you use them in the ring. It’s a nice way to build hype for your next match. You’re welcome to sell them or give them away, although we hope you will use them yourselves and keep climbing the ladder.”

Before Edge could ask for clarification, an assistant let them know the transmission was live. Vio resumed her announcer’s persona and gestured for the pair to follow her into the center of the room.

“Welcome back, esteemed patrons of the arena. It’s time for our champions to receive their prizes! In addition to a generous 70,000 Credits, our winners have earned an Epic skill gem from our collection. We could reward them with a skill of our choice or let them make a selection from a list, but where would be the fun in that?

“Instead, we have placed twenty Epic gems in boxes arranged in a circle. They are set to display the category of the skill they contain when you draw near, which means our champions can’t read them until they reach each container. Once they pass a box, they can’t go back, and they can only consider each option for thirty seconds. With those details in mind, who would like to go first?”

Edge looked at Mel, who shrugged and said, “I suppose I’ll take the first pick.”

He stood beside the administrators as Melenia walked to the line that would take her to each pillar in turn as she made her way around the circle. His pulse pounded as she approached the first box and read the label attached to the base, which was blank to his eyes.

The guardswoman took her time. She walked from one pillar to the next until she stopped in front of the twelfth box, mulling it over for the full thirty seconds before reaching inside, removing the gem, and returning to his position.

“The lethal lady has chosen an earth-aspected Epic gem!” Vio’s voice rang out. “I can’t wait to find out what skill it contains, and I know you feel the same way. Now it’s time for our glaive-wielding champion to make his own decision.”

Edge had been hoping to get some guidance from Mel, but a privacy bubble wavered into existence around her position, obscuring her form as he stepped onto the line and approached the first pillar. While the rules were designed to make the process more appealing to an audience, he had to admit that the game was thrilling from his perspective too.

He came to a stop before the first box as the magic obscuring the contents faded away to reveal the word Ice. He might have picked it if he could use skill gems, since it would synergize nicely with his Frostfury Medallion and the winter bonus from Fear the Seasons that would replace wind any day. But since he intended to give the prize to one of his friends, he made a show of considering it until the last second, then shook his head and kept walking down the line.

He passed by Fire, Air, Water, and Acid, followed by Lightning, Force, and Gravity. He considered Meta and Passive for quite a while, ignoring some less interesting options along the way. By the time he reached the box Mel opened, his heart was drumming almost as quickly as it had during the battle—his greed to select the best prize warring with his fear that none of the other offerings would be as good as the ones he’d already forsaken.

He was surprised that she’d chosen Stone, but then he realized she must have picked it with Jumo in mind, since he and Sakura didn’t have an Epic skill yet. Trapper didn’t either, but she had the ultimate ability granted by her Epic core, which made all her trap skills more powerful. Beautiful, strong, and selfless. He grinned while glancing in Melenia’s direction. I can’t believe how lucky I am.

Edge didn’t want to get stuck with the last gem in the circle, so when he came across a box labeled Aura, he reached inside and removed the gem. The Epic power it contained was certain to be strong, and Sakura didn’t have an aura yet. Even if it wasn’t an ideal skill for his fellow scion, someone on the team was bound to make good use of it.

He tuned out Vio’s banter as she concluded the prize ceremony, focused on the reward he’d selected. There was a slip of paper wrapped around the gem that provided a full description of the skill it contained.

Mirrored Retribution <aura> (Epic)

Any creature that attacks one of the aura’s recipients will be struck by an identical attack. Mirrored Retribution can mimic most physical and energetic strikes but cannot replicate the effects of mental assaults, Epic skills, Unique skills, ultimate abilities, or complex magitech devices. The aura’s wielder must pay a mana cost that is proportional to the strength of the duplicated attack, or it will not manifest.

Edge’s jaw dropped when he realized what he’d won. Mirrored Retribution was an incredible aura, although it sounded like it would be expensive to maintain, especially when tackling powerful foes. It wouldn’t be ideal in every fight, particularly against opponents that could resist their own attacks, but it was still an amazing skill. It would be perfect for Sakura, whose informational magic and mana sensitivity would help her pick the best moments to mirror her opponents’ strikes.

He stored the gem in his vault while walking to the exit. “Congratulations again,” Krix said as Vio nodded her agreement. “We expect great things from both of you and look forward to your future matches.”

They bid the announcers farewell, left the secure area, and went to join their friends. Along the way, the duo switched papers, letting Edge see what his partner had won.

Fury of Stone <earth> (Epic)

Dense spikes of razored rock will emerge from the ground beneath the skill’s target, creating a continual attack that will follow them until the duration expires. At rank 1, the duration of Fury of Stone is 30 seconds. The skill may only be used on soil, sand, or rocky surfaces. Ground cover and debris will not prevent its manifestation.

He let out a low whistle of appreciation as he read the last line. Since Fury of Stone was an Epic spell, Edge was certain the damage would be top-tier, and a continual attack that followed its target would be incredibly difficult to dodge. Even if they managed to get out of the way, dealing with a constant barrage of attacks from below would provide one hell of a distraction.

Jumo wouldn’t have room to equip it until he evolved, but after obtaining all that experience in the Haunted Vale, Edge thought Jumo, Mel, and Sasha could hit stage three in less than a month, dramatically increasing the crew’s combined combat prowess.

After storing both slips of paper in his rune, Edge took Melenia’s hand. Five minutes later, the duo emerged through a side exit where they could avoid the crowd, since they would have been stuck in the arena for hours otherwise. Krix had been kind enough to send an attendant to fetch Trapper’s crew, along with the rest of their friends.

They laughed and bantered as they made their way to the Lost Traveler, ready for a well-deserved celebration.
