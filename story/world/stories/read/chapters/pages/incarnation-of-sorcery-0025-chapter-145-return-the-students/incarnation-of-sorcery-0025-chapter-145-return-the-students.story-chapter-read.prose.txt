It is oft said that Rhel’s history had its greatest inflection point at an argument. When the ruler and the founder argue against each other’s philosophies, when they pit their beliefs and ideals against what the other favoured, it was then that Rhel changed drastically.

In the secret histories, we call it the Great Schism.

Of course, the history of many nations in many Realms is littered with all sorts of disagreements. That is what often leads to war, to rebellion, to death and destruction of terrific scale.

Yet, this was far more personal. This was far more severe than any death could have ever been.

For once the greatest of beings, He Who Carves Reality, departs from one’s home, then the changes are undoubtedly drastic. Historic.

“Of course they’re awarding you, sir,” Acrypha said. “Who else are they going to award at this ceremony of theirs?”

“That’s right!” Viren said with enthusiastic agreement. He quickly mollified his tone, affecting a more reserved and formal one. “Much as it pains me to agree with Acrypha, you deserve all the awards, sir. They’d be stupid not to make you the guest of honour and the highest achiever and all that.”

Ryland wasn’t sure he quite agreed with that sentiment. He certainly hadn’t done anything expecting to be rewarded for it.

At least when it didn’t involve the Prime Minister of Androvia trying to make him do something.

He and the other two were strolling the institute grounds after their classes for the day were over. Ryland still didn’t need to attend any course, which the two children were more jealous of than the fact that he could cast magic beyond anything they had ever seen, oddly enough.

Not that he didn’t understand it. But in this case, with the freedoms afforded to them by the institute, they were both actually having fun with their own courses.

“She’s actually beating all the second-years from all the academies, sir,” Viren said, somewhere between proud and envious and just plain flabbergasted by Acrypha. “She’s mad.”

“I don’t think I’m insane at all,” Acrypha said with a sniff. “I just like to work hard.”

“I work hard too! You just have that weird memory thing that never turns off so you can remember literally anything.”

“No, Viren, I don’t have an eidetic memory.”

“Liar.”

“Am not!”

Ryland chuckled as they continued to bicker. The institute’s grounds were interesting. As they had seen earlier with the enormous walls and the fortress-like buildings, the whole place had the atmosphere of a repurposed fort. It was almost as though this had been one of those old bastions used during the Realmbreaker Wars, now given a new life as a school for youth.

Keeping with that military theme, many of the Rhellian Institute’s enforced policies almost seemed to treat its students as conscripted soldiers.

There were patrols to be maintained, “captains” selected from among the students’ ranks, artillery stations and guard posts to be manned, ditches to be dug, and all the other chores that people drafted into a war were expected to carry out.

“They’re not actually preparing for war, are they, sir?” Viren asked quietly.

He didn’t really need to lower his voice. They were giving the armoured soldier-students a wide berth and no one was really paying them any attention. Although, Ryland did catch the occasional student giving him pointed looks, when they thought he wouldn’t notice them staring in his direction.

“They do have a certain rebellious faction to deal with,” Ryland said, explaining a bit of what he had discovered about the Hand of Freedom. “I don’t know all the intricacies behind it, but right this moment, it does make sense that they’re remaining ready for confrontation.”

“And yet, they invited us,” Acrypha said. “Knowing the rebels might attack.”

Ryland sighed. He couldn’t quite fathom it either. That was why he had only said a bit, for even though he could surmise a great deal, a lot was still unknown.

There were a lot of whys he couldn’t explain.

As guests, none of them had to help with the preparations or anything like that.

It felt nice, if Ryland was being honest. The last time, he had been busy assisting the preparations at Arcoryx Academy When the Prime Minister had arrived to the city. Things had been so hectic, especially with the interferences from the Enforcers and then Sokolo Dyron whom he had needed to deal with. Not to mention the assault by the insurgents from Kurin.

Now, there were no such concerns for him to worry about. If anything, he was the guest of honour, as Viren had insinuated, so he was supposed to stay back and let the institute handle matters.

They did it pretty well. Over the next few days, the institute took care of preparations with typical military efficiency.

A wide field was cleared and then modified to handle the big event with a stage, a seating area, and so on. People came and went, handling different matters of organization. There was one mage in charge of the food, another for ensuring the invitations and such were taken care of, yet another handling scheduling, and so on.

Ryland had still asked if he could at least assist with putting up the decorations, but they had politely declined his assistance. The council of High Academs running the institute instead of a formal headmaster was very expressive about doing it on their own.

Hmm. Had they heard about what he had done at the Crypts? It was almost like they were wary of having their thunder stolen or something.

Ryland supposed he could understand that somewhat. Especially after recent events.

The whole thing felt a bit like reparations. It was as though the entire institute was trying to make up for the incident at the Crypts. This was emphasized by what happened during one of Ryland’s classes.

At the very end of his lecture, when he had finished early for the day and was about to dismiss the students, the Rhellian contingent all stood up, their armoured robes clinking together.

“Sub-Academ Ryland,” said the student who had led the attack on Ryland. He bowed his head. Following his lead once more, the rest of his companions all did the same. “We’re deeply, deeply sorry for how we acted a few weeks ago. There is no excuse for how deplorably we acted, and I would never assume that we have any right to be forgiven. Nevertheless, we apologize.”

Ryland observed them for a few moments. There were quite a few things he figured he could ask just then, but he decided to be merciful and just let it go. Youth was the time for stupidity, after all. “Your apology is accepted.”

They looked up quickly, surprised at his easy tone and how quickly they had received the absolution they had been doubting. Remembering their manners, they went on to thank him profusely for his kindness while also expressing a great deal of awe at his prowess.

Honestly, it got a little awkward for Ryland, but he managed to extricate himself from the Rhellian students and get going.

After that meeting with Mavar, it was just difficult for Ryland to determine what was real and what was fake. When it came to the Rhellians, at least. Almost nothing that happened on this planet was supposed to escape the Demiurge’s purview. Did he know about this apology, then?

Did he know about the award ceremony that the institute was holding?

Of course, not everything was peachy, nor was everything happening in a way that Mavar could affect or control directly.

About a week or so before the ceremony for the awards, there was a bit of a kerfuffle. A delegation had arrived from Jure. They were here to retrieve their students from the institute and head back to Shorn University, before something else happened that could put their students in danger.

They weren’t alone either. Somehow, a large group of Rhellian citizens had gathered at around the same time, their intentions aligned with the foreign delegation. Like the dragonkin, they also wanted to take their students back home.

If the institute couldn’t protect them, then they’d be better off with their parents, apparently.

Ryland was quite surprised by developments. It made sense for one of the foreign delegations to decide they’d had enough of Rhel, even if it was a singular incident that technically hadn’t even harmed any of the foreign students.

Wholly another for Rhellians themselves to join up with the Jure party and demand their children back from the institute.

Because all it did was confuse Ryland. Surely all of these people had the Glyph of Unity Inscribed on their very manasouls. Their thoughts, their feelings, their intentions, those should all be well known by Mavar himself. So why was he allowing that to happen? Did he actually want a pretext to take down his own institute?

Unless the Glyph of Unity had weakened. Maybe that the Glyph’s effect had deteriorated over time or there was something else at play, something like the strength of their emotions regarding their children, allowing the parents to overcome the Glyph of Unity’s influence.

Ryland couldn’t fathom the end goal of all this. There was something going on, but the truth wasn’t easy to arrive at, especially from an exterior perspective.

“There has been no word from Arcoryx about this,” High Academ Tyra was confiding in Ryland a little while later. “They haven’t sent in any intentions about leaving the institute or anything of the sort.”

“Well,” Ryland said. “I think Jure is being a bit hasty. Not that I blame them for their caution, but I’m not particularly surprised that Arcoryx hasn’t acted.”

“Perhaps.” The High Academ was frowning, her eyes far away. “But I don’t know. Something smells fishy. Not sure what, though. Just keep an eye out please, Ryland.”

“Always, High Academ.”

Speaking of smells, Ryland met Sint later on too, and she said basically the exact same thing.

“I can’t escape it, Ryland,” she said. “The scent of deception is in the air.”

That was admittedly a bit more concerning than Tyra’s instincts. Sint had once sniffed out Ryland’s own rather troubling history. Her olfactory senses were superb. If she smelled something was off, then there was definitely more to this whole business than met the eye.

“But you can’t pinpoint it exactly?” Ryland asked.

She shook her head, her whiskers falling in disappointment. “I’m afraid not. It’s too… not exactly faint, but more like a thread that I can’t really perceive. It’s strange. I never thought I’d be saying this, but it feels as though I’m not supposed to be smelling it because it’s on a very different plane of existence.”

And that was even more concerning. Ryland considered going to the authorities with it, but what was he to say? That they had rather keen hunches that something was amiss? Something so vague wouldn’t fly.

So for now, Ryland decided to merely observe. And send a message to Yulkaviel.

The Second High Adjutant came to meet Ryland personally two days before the award ceremony. Ryland was surprised. It felt like Yulkaviel had been avoiding him after the incident at the Crypts. A few weeks had passed and the old elvhenan hadn’t met Ryland even once. Yet he had come personally now?

Maybe that too was a sign that something was going on. Considering he had accosted Ryland just before the Crypts…

“Greetings, Ryland,” he said. “It’s good to see you again. Apologies. I’ve taken so long to come and see you personally again. I’ve been swamped with my partner’s duties recently.”

“Please,” Ryland said. “A month is merely a noonday nap when compared to how long I’ve lived. But it is good we meet again.”

“Yes. I suspect you have something of import you wished to relay, yes?”

He nodded, then went on to explain the suspicions he had confirmed from the others. “But again, I haven’t looked into anything personally, so I can offer nothing concrete. Just a friendly warning to remain cautious.”

“Of course. Believe me, after the debacle with the Crypts, we are all attempting to remain as cautious as we can.”

That was fair enough. No doubt, the Rhellian institute wouldn’t want any sort of repeat of what had happened before.

“And as for the last incident…” Ryland said.

Yulkaviel paled a little. Ah, yes. That would basically explain why he hadn’t yet met Ryland again. “Yes, the last incident.” The old elvhenan sighed. “What happened there was rather deplorable. As others have said it, so shall I—I sincerely apologize for everything that transpired.”

“I accept your apology.”

Yulkaviel blinked. “You… do?”

“I do. I understand it isn’t really your fault. You’re not truly yourself, after all.”

Yulkaviel stared at Ryland for a long while. It was true, but that didn’t mean it was necessarily the right thing to say. The statement could be seen as stirring the pot. After all, Ryland was basically casting aspersions on the entire order of operations on Rhel. On how everyone lived their lives from the moment they were born.

It made sense, Ryland realized, why so many Rhellians disliked him on sight. Right now, he was practically spitting on their way of life. Of course they’d be upset and hate him for it.

No matter how much he was actually right about it all.

Yulkaviel finally just shook his head and started walking away. He was far too much of a consummate professional to show any real emotion or let it sway him into taking any actions he might later regret. That said, he couldn’t help himself from trying to take a parting shot.

“If that’s so,” he said. “Then perhaps you can show me how to be more myself later, Ryland.”

He didn’t wait for a reply.

It turned out Ryland’s suspicions were reasonable. The next day, instead of the final stretch before the ceremony for the awards, instead of the small kerfuffle being resolved in time, it grew a bit more complicated.

All because one of those standing against the Rhellian Institute, who turned out to be one of the clergy, came up to Ryland himself to ask for his assistance.

“You know, don’t you?” she said, looking at him with pleading eyes, her hands held before her in supplication like Ryland was some kind of wish-granting deity. There was something vaguely familiar about her, though he couldn’t place it just then. “You know what we need.”

“I don’t understand what you mean?” Ryland asked.

He was feeling a little awkward about the meeting, trying not to look to see if there was anyone else in the hallway who could help extricate him from his current predicament.

But those notions fled at the priestess’s next words.

“You know everything,” she said. “Everything about the past. Everything about our… our souls. And that’s what we need to… to survive.”
