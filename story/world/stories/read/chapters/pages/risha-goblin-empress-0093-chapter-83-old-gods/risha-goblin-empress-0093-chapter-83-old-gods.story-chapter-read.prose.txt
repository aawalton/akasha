Risha stirred as something called to her.

Someone was summoning her in the Council.

Glo noticed the movement and turned towards her, but she simply shook her head as she answered the call, and fell back into him.

***

Risha opened her eyes to the black void for only a moment, before the call drew her through the spirit realm and into a large hall.

Torches lit the hall, illuminating the large round table that took up the space. Seventeen chairs rounded the table, each of different style and size. One, was larger than Risha by three times, while another was openbacked, some had vines crawling up them, while others were thrones.

Shyanne appeared moments later, her eyes meeting Risha’s with a small smile. Shyanne moved to one of the thronelike chairs, all too similar to the one she first met Risha in, and motioned for Risha to join her in the chair next to her.

Risha walked over, studying the simple wooden chair, unadorned, unlike all the others at the table.

Another woman appeared across the table. A woman that Risha did not recognize. Short and curvy, a large sword rested on her back, and her eyes held a strange golden glint to them. It took Risha a moment to recognize her as a dwarf.

“You can change it.”

Shyanne’s words brought Risha back. Risha looked at her chair, then mentally commanded it to grow and change. Its back turned into two spears crossed by intricately carved boards. A vine grew around each spear, blooming into a purple flower.

Risha sat down, then looked across the table as more Grand Mothers appeared.

Maya, the Dark Elf lounged with her legs over the armrest of her chair, while Cala walked in with her features covered by cloth.

Risha took a deep breath as Sarah appeared, her wings spread out wide behind her. Risha’s eyes narrowed at the woman, the memory of Klu’s missing arm filling her mind as some part of her desperately wanted to call Scarlet and Crimson to her.

Sarah matched her gaze with hatred equal to her own.

Shyanne gently touched Risha’s arm, bringing her attention to the Human Grand Mother. Risha took a deep breath, then let it go slowly, calming her racing heart.

“What’s happening?” Risha finally whispered to the human Grand Mother.

“Iris has called us.” Shyanne answered, calmly looking over the others. “A full gathering has not happened in a long time.”

Another woman appeared that Risha did not recognize, a cloak covering her features as she smoothly walked up to a simple wooden chair, unchanged from its original appearance.

Shyanne watched the woman with narrowed eyes.

A large woman stepped into the room, standing at the height of one of Risha’s warbeasts, the woman was covered in furs, and her gaze carried an icy chill with it as she glanced at Risha with disinterest. She sat in the largest chair.

Space crackled, and a long limb made its way into the space, followed by several more as the gargantuan eye of Hwahwei watched them. A sigh escaped the Ocean Mother before her limbs came back to her and a woman stepped through the space. Looking at her gave Risha a strange feeling, as if her eyes were deceiving her. Still, the humanoid Ocean Mother stepped up to her own seat and sat in it with an annoyed look on her face. Occasionally, looking at her, Risha would see a long suckered limb that was simultaneously there and not there at the same time.

The Elf Lalaila stepped into the space next, green nature growing where she stepped as she sat in her chair that looked more like a tree than a chair.

Grand Mother Maya and the Elf Mother had a long staring contest that was only broken by the crackle of thunder and a bright light as another woman appeared sitting in her seat, her features hidden by a crackling yellow light.

Out of the corner of her eye, Risha saw a small woman with insect-like wings float to the small chair next to Risha, hidden by the distraction of the thunderous woman.

The thunderous woman glanced at one of the empty chairs, and snorted, “She hasn’t shown?”

“Leave the young’un alone.” The dwarf responded.

“All of us were called.” The thunderous woman leaned forward. “She has no right to refuse.”

“She has my permission.” Iris’s voice quieted the table, and everyone turned to her. The Dragon Grand Mother was different from how Risha usually saw her. Her eyes held a fierceness to them, their usual ageless kindness hidden by something primal.

With the appearance of Iris, four chairs stayed empty. One, covered in vines, another made of stone, the third a normal chair, and the fourth covered in the dormant fire of a demon.

“Iris, what was so important, that you had to call us here?” Lalaila called, her voice quiet, but carrying the presence that every woman in the room commanded.

Iris waved her hand, and a corpse dropped onto the table, long decaying wings extending from its back. Its face was pale with death, but the black orbs it had for eyes were still visible.

Sarah leaned forward, her forehead creased. “He’s not one of mine.”

“And he’s not one of the crows.” Iris said, glancing to the empty chair. “He came from across the ocean.”

The women in the room broke into a cacophony, startling Risha.

Iris raised her hand, stopping them from speaking. “Hwahwei.”

The Ocean Mother shimmered as she stood, her striking purple eyes looking over the gathered with seriousness. She did not speak with her mouth as the words flowed over them. “I sensed him many days ago. Any monster that crossed his path perished, and I have not heard from the chosen I sent to meet him. I called Iris, since she knows more of the other lands than any of us.”

“His swords drew my blood when we met in the skies.” Those words sent a ripple through the room, and even Risha understood.

The Mother of Dragons must be one of the most powerful creatures here, for someone to draw her blood…

“He wielded the power of a god.” Iris said with a finality that brought heavy silence.

“A god?” The large woman covered in fur leaned forward.

Iris nodded. At the confusion of many of the women in the room, she continued, “They left the world in the first age. They were manipulators of the system, controllers of reality. You cannot understand the way they changed the world around them, unless you witnessed it yourself. The Sparks pushed them from this world in much the same way the monsters pushed the Sparks.” Iris glanced at Risha for a brief moment, and many others followed the gaze to the newest of their number.

“Like the Sparks, the gods never fully left.” Hwahwei spoke, drawing the attention back to her. She was a woman that could not be ignored, even by those here. “I have seen whispers in the darkest depths. And my children have died discovering that much.”

“And you fear that these creatures are coming for us.” The giant of a woman crossed her arms as she studied the dead creature on the table.

“He was a scout. I sensed more on the horizon, watching.”

“Gods. You expect to fight gods.” The dwarf woman spoke this time, raising her eyebrows at Iris.

“They won’t come now, present.” A quiet voice spoke from next to Risha, making the goblin turn to face the small woman with dragonfly wings. The others continued talking, but Iris as well noticed the small comment.

“What do you know, little one?” Iris asked the Mother.

“They're afraid, wary. They only know the fires of the sparks, the old ones. And the light of your radiant scales, your iridescent jewels. They will come slowly, like a fog on the beach, searching, seeking.”

“Then we have time?” Iris asked

“Time? Now? Then? Could be. There is no knowing. Yes, time will be had.”

The thunderous woman let out a long sigh, “Her words are as annoying as ever. Maybe some sparks to the brain would help.” She held up her hand, and lightning jumped between her fingers.

The tiny Mother yelped, then flew behind Risha’s shoulder. She stuck her tongue out at the thunderous woman, then ducked behind Risha again.

Iris cleared her throat, a tired look in her eyes. “There is nothing to be done now, except to be ready. I will see all of you soon.” With that said, she stepped out of the space, leaving the corpse on the table.

The other Grand Mothers left one by one.

Risha turned around to see the Faerie gone, and the clap of thunder signaled that the other Grand Mother was gone. Sarah sent an unreadable look towards Risha before she was gone too.

Shyanne placed a gentle hand on Risha’s shoulder, startling her. “I must talk to Sarah. A truce will be called, but that won’t stop her. Be wary of daggers in the dark.” Her hand left her shoulder as she stepped into a realm where a large tree stood tall, and white feathers fell.

Risha looked around the table, finding it empty once more, except for a single woman.

Hwahwei stared at Risha, the large tentacles around her becoming visible once more before disappearing.

“It is time to come to the Ocean, Risha.”

With that said, the woman disappeared, and Risha was truly alone.

“Well that was interesting.”

Risha jumped in surprise, then turned to see a familiar Shaman. “How did you get here?”

“You invited me? I thought that was what you were doing when you brought my flower here. Or did you not know?” He pointed at the purple flowers that perched at the top of her chair. The ones she’d placed there. “I would not have come if you did not allow it, and you can forbid me from the space. This is your realm, I did not mean to come uninvited.”

Risha shook her head, “it’s fine. You just startled Risha.”

The shaman nodded, then walked over to the corpse on the table. “The old gods are returning then.”

“That’s what they say.” Risha stood up from her chair and joined the shaman.

“They never left the Spirit realm.” He said, his voice hard. “It is why knowing the path you walk is so important. Void gods are terrifying, even to me.” He leaned down to study the black orbs the creature had for eyes. “There’s a whole world beyond our oceans, you know? It’s not just gods, but monsters, slaves, pirates, lands strange and magical. I suppose it's about time you discover the world out there.”

“You’ve been past the oceans?”

“As has Mother Iris, when she was a child. The Sparks had ships that could sail those seas without fear.” The goblin reached down and touched one of the blackened wings, “But whatever god gave power to this creature is not one I would like to meet. Do you see how it twisted him? You can see how it starved and stole from his spirit, turning him into little more than an animal. Disgusting, really.”

Now that it had been pointed out to her, Risha could see it, the strange wrongness of the body, even with its spirit gone.

“A fallen god.” Nyx spoke to her quietly, as if she were trying to avoid the attention of the Shaman. “It saddens me to see.”

“You need not fear me, spirit.” The Shaman looked through Risha to the spirit who rested inside her. “I am your mistress’s brother, in name and in spirit.” He smiled, as if at a private joke. “What is a fallen god?”

Nyx left her, taking the form of a small black cat. The stars and moon that normally decorated her fur were hidden, and Risha suspected that they were hidden from the shaman with purpose. “A god that has fallen to their inner desires.”

“I see. I did not know that was possible.”

“It is rare, but such gods corrupt their followers and the world around them.” Nyx hopped into Risha’s arms, and licked her fur.

The Shaman looked at her for a moment then started chuckling. He turned away as it turned into full laughter.

“What?” Risha asked, wondering what realization had set him off.

He glanced back at her with a wide smile “No, you’re just too cute.”

Risha dropped Nyx as she thwacked him.
