Lira returned to the library moments before lunch and immediately started setting up the dining space with Tessa as the voices of noble children filled the library. Prince Lyth wasn't alone today.

Eight young nobles accompanied him, boys and girls ranging from perhaps ten to fifteen years old. Half were scattered around the library, while the other half remained clustered around the prince, arguing about whether five knights could win against five mages. A few adults were also present: three bodyguards, including Sir Henry; two governesses, who were supposed to chaperone the girls but were too busy gossiping; and a couple of maids dragging trays of food from the kitchen.

One of the housemaids rang a small bell, and the governesses remembered their charges and stood up to herd the children toward the table.

As lunch was served, the library became even louder as the argument evolved into whether a knight could defeat a dragon.

Prince Lyth called Lira over to weigh in on the matter, and she was forced to explain that there were no dragons, giants, or any other magical beasts; they were only myths.

"The only monsters you can run into are other humans, I'm afraid," she said.

"What about the gods?" asked a boy. "They can create monsters."

"The gods were only known to bless and curse people physically," Lira corrected. "They do not interfere with free will."

"What about trolls?" asked another boy. "Trolls are monsters."

"What we call trolls are a species of large, hairless apes," she explained. "They are similar to bears in strength and temperament, but their thick skin and blubber make them too difficult to kill with arrows. That's why knights are often dispatched to kill them."

"They aren't magical?" asked a girl.

"No. Magic is only for nobles like yourselves," Lira said.

Once the lunch break was over, Lira spent the next half hour helping Tessa clean up and collect the empty dishes as the children gradually drifted back toward the bookshelves.

Once everyone seemed occupied… she decided it was time to set up her bait.

She walked toward the foreign-language shelves and selected one of the Magari novels she had already read and liked. Unlike most books in the library, its pages were filled with a logographic script where each character represented an entire word, completely unlike the Healtherian script, which used a phonetic alphabet.

She returned to the front desk and, without making any attempt to hide what she was doing, opened the book and began reading.

She only made it through three pages before she heard a voice calling her name.

"Miss Lira?"

She looked up and found Prince Lyth standing beside the desk, staring curiously at the unfamiliar writing.

"What language is that?"

Right on schedule. Lira smiled inwardly.

"It's called Magari."

The prince leaned closer.

"It doesn't look anything like our writing."

"It isn't." She turned the book so he could see it better.

"The alphabet is completely different. Each character is a whole word rather than a sound."

Lyth traced one of the characters with his finger without touching the page.

"Can you actually read this?"

"I can, and so can your brother Aric."

"He does?"

"Yes. Aric can read Magari."

The young prince looked genuinely surprised.

"I wonder if he learned it at the academy."

"He probably did," nodded Lira.

"I am going there next fall," said the prince happily. "I wonder if I can learn Magari too."

"I believe you can because it's offered as an elective class."

"Have you been to the academy too, Miss Lira?" asked the prince.

"No. I am not a noble."

"But you are so smart."

"That's because I read a lot," she smiled. "You'll probably become as smart as me when you grow up."

"You think so?"

"I know so. You like reading too, after all."

Lira laughed.

The prince's eyes returned to the book.

"What is it about?" he asked.

"It tells the story of a young prince who discovers that his kingdom was built upon the ruins of an older magical civilization," she explained. "He begins searching ancient ruins for forgotten magical secrets while trying to stop several evil people from claiming them first."

"That sounds amazing."

"It is."

"I wish I could read it."

She pretended to consider the idea.

"Would you like a head start in learning Magari?"

His eyes widened.

"Can I?"

"Of course."

"You would teach me?"

"I can prepare a few study sheets."

His entire expression brightened.

"They won't replace proper lessons," Lira cautioned, "but if you study a little before bed each night, you'll already know the basics by the time your tutors begin teaching you."

"I'd be ahead of everyone else."

"You would."

A huge grin spread across the prince's face.

"Please."

"I'd be happy to."

The next few hours passed surprisingly peacefully.

Lira spread several blank sheets across the desk and began constructing a miniature textbook.

She started with the fundamentals.

Father... Mother... Brother... Sister... Teacher... Student.

Below each Magari word, she carefully wrote its pronunciation using Healtherian letters before adding the proper translation beside it.

From there, she moved on to common animals.

Dog... Horse... Bird... Cat... Bear... Fox.

Prince Lyth remained beside her almost the entire time, occasionally asking how a particular word was pronounced before enthusiastically repeating it aloud.

His accent was terrible, but Lira politely chose not to mention that.

After nouns came greetings and expressions of politeness.

Good morning... Good evening... Thank you... Please... Excuse me.

Every phrase included a pronunciation guide, followed by a short explanation of its meaning and when it should be used.

She found herself enjoying the work far more than expected.

Maybe in another life she would have been a teacher. Sadly, now she was using her student to facilitate the killing of his brother. She would have felt more guilty if she hadn't genuinely believed that Lyth's life would improve once she was done with Kavard.

By the time she finished the first collection of notes, the governesses and bodyguards were already escorting their charges out of the library.

Prince Lyth reluctantly stood as Sir Henry waited nearby.

"I have to go."

"You do."

He looked longingly at the unfinished papers.

"Can I take them?"

Lira shook her head.

"Not quite yet."

She lifted another blank page.

"I'd like to finish them first."

"Oh."

His shoulders drooped slightly.

Then she smiled.

"Why don't you go to dinner?"

"I'll bring them to your room afterward."

His disappointment vanished immediately.

"Really?"

"I promise."

"Thank you, Miss Lira!"

The prince hurried away.

Lira watched him disappear before allowing herself a small smile.

All according to plan.

Half an hour later, carrying the completed study sheets beneath one arm, Lira left the library and made her way toward the inner bailey.

This would be her first time entering the Main Hall and the royal residential wing.

The difference became apparent immediately, as the palace became far more opulent. The walls were lined with polished marble instead of ordinary stone. Runic lamps' light clung to the ceiling, bathing the corridors in warm golden light without a single visible candle. Decorative suits of armor stood between tapestries depicting victories won generations ago, each one subtly enchanted to keep rust and dust away.

She wanted to use Mage Sight, but she held back. She had left everything magical back in her alcove, even her mana-obfuscation charm, just in case.

Unlike the bored guards in the outer bailey, the guards here were terrifying. They wore immaculate ceremonial armor polished brightly enough to reflect the corridor around them, and they carried long polearms. Their movement looked practiced, and every gaze remained sharp and alert.

These were the Royal Guard, the elite knights personally handpicked by Lord Estophon to protect the king and his family.

As Lira approached the corridor leading deeper into the royal apartments, one guard lowered his halberd across her path.

"State your purpose."

His voice remained calm.

"I am the library caretaker." She held up the bundle of papers. "I've brought study notes for Prince Lyth."

The guard looked at them before answering.

"These are His Majesty's chambers." He pointed toward another corridor. "The royal children's apartments are upstairs."

"My mistake." Lira bowed her head, “Thank you.”

She followed the indicated staircase until three more guards blocked the landing.

"State your purpose."

Before she could answer, a nearby door opened.

Sir Henry stepped into the corridor.

"She's here for Prince Lyth."

The guards immediately lowered their weapons, letting her pass.

Lira doubted this level of security had been present during the Maren incident. It was probably part of Kavard's punishment, which probably meant that killing him was far too dangerous now.

Maybe she should focus on Kelroth and give up on Kavard...

But then the sight of the buried bones flashed into her mind, restoring her resolve.

She stepped forward into the corridor, taking advantage of every step to memorize the layout.

The corridor stretched farther than expected, lined with four doors, two on each side.

Sir Henry had emerged from the first room on the left: Prince Lyth's room.

Across from it was another room with a polished brass nameplate: Prince Aric.

Her eyes continued moving naturally down the hallway.

Near the far end stood another door distinguished by a deep crimson carpet instead of blue.

She didn't see any nameplate, but from the two guards standing watch over it, Lira didn't need anyone to tell her whose room that was: Prince Kavard.

She carefully committed every detail to memory without lingering long enough to appear suspicious.

Sir Henry opened Lyth's door.

Inside waited a comfortable sitting room furnished with low couches, shelves overflowing with toys and books, and a fireplace large enough to warm the entire suite. Two additional doors stood farther inside, one almost certainly leading to the prince's bedroom while the other likely connected to a private bath.

Lira's attention drifted briefly toward the windows, noting their position so she could later match them from the outside.

While Prince Lyth enthusiastically thanked her for the study sheets and immediately began sounding out Magari words, Lira smiled, nodded, and occasionally corrected his pronunciation.

The perfect servant, if you ignored all the murder plotting that came with her.
