Trevor and Adabelle returned to their room in the evening after a day of wandering the Red Dream, playing games, and relaxing. There had been some pleasant chit chat with other passengers, though nothing as in depth as their conversation with Stammy, and they had plans to spend the rest of the evening cloistered in their suite.

“Sir, ma’am!” a voice called out as the courier was unlocking their door, and they turned to see Horatial approaching. The purple-skinned demon smiled as he shifted around some documents in his hands. His tail seemed glued to the wall, trailing along the hand rail.

“Good evening,” Adabelle greeted as Trevor succeeded in unlocking the door.

“It is a good evening, yes,” he replied. “I have some news for you, if you have a moment.”

“We were about to retire for the night, so your timing is literally perfect,” the courier said with a smile.

“I’m glad to hear it, sir,” Horatial stated as he found the right paper. He handed it to Adabelle. “You two have been booked for a private spa session, including a couple’s massage, in the late morning, courtesy of Klarkartar, if you so choose to accept it.”

“That sounds pretty nice,” Trevor said as he looked at his wife, who nodded in agreement. Once he was sure she was okay with this, he tilted his head and thought about the layout of the ship. “Also, there’s a whole spa here?”

“It is small, but luxurious,” the crewman informed them with a smile. “Aivy and Garlett will be there to take care of you. Your appointment will last for one hour, and you can enjoy the spa afterwards for as long as you’d like, though I wouldn’t suggest staying too long.”

“Because we’re booked for parasailing?” Adabelle asked enthusiastically.

“Yes, in the early afternoon.”

“Good, I was hoping there would be a slot open,” she said, taking her husband’s hand as she looked his way. “I’ve been excited to try it ever since Trevor explained it to me. Especially since we’re so high up, and he had only ever seen it from boats before.”

“It’s all because of the captain,” Horatial praised. “Speaking of, he would like to invite you to dinner on the bridge tomorrow evening.”

Adabelle looked at Trevor, and he nodded. “That also sounds nice,” he said. “I wouldn’t mind talking with him more.”

“Excellent, I’ll let him know. Those three items were the only ones I had on my list, but is there anything you need from me? Perhaps I can bring you down some dinner?”

“Food would be nice, but perhaps give us an hour first,” Adabelle stated. “I’m not too hungry now, though I should be right around then. Feel free to bring whatever you recommend so long as it’s not too strange, please and thank you.”

“The same for me,” Trevor added. “But maybe something like that steak we had yesterday. It was perfect.”

“As you say, ma’am, sir, it shall be done,” Horatial said before giving them a slight bow. “I’ll be off, then. Please enjoy the rest of your evening.”

“You, too.”

They entered their room after the demon left, and Clay oozed out of Trevor’s pocket. Their body snapped back together once they hit the ground, bounding towards the circular chair again. They jumped up to the seat, then the back before curling up in a perfect circle.

“I think Clay’s really taken a shine to that chair,” Trevor chuckled as he walked to the bedroom with his wife.

“They sure have,” she agreed. “While it’s not all that practical, I wouldn’t mind getting one for the house once you and Clay move in.”

The courier paused as the demonkin began to disrobe so she could change into comfier clothes, but he didn’t stop to stare. “Huh.”

“That’s not usually what someone likes to hear when they mention moving in together, Trevor,” Adabelle teased.

“Yeah, sorry. I just realized that, when I move in with you, the Hero House is going to be empty again,” he said as he activated Quick Change to get into his pajamas. “I’ve been the only one there since Rashie moved in with Wayne at his ranch, so it’s just going to be Mr. Tiggles.”

“You’re right. No one has been summoned to take our places,” the noblewoman said, pausing as she stared at the dresser. She had been ready to pull out a shirt, but now she just stood there.

Sensing her mood, Trevor approached and wrapped his arms around her from behind. Her tail whipped his leg gently before wrapping around it. “Are you still worried about the summoning rituals?”

“I wasn’t until now,” she admitted. “With everything that’s been going on, I had forgotten all about my numerous failures.”

“Your learning experiences,” he corrected as he turned her around to face him. Adabelle’s tail was reluctant to let go of him, but he stroked it near the base, causing her to shiver, and it loosened enough for him to finish moving her. She squeaked as he lifted her up and set her to sit on the dresser. “This trip is exactly what you need to get out of your own head and reset back to zero. You’ll see, once we get back to Tosa, you’re going to become a summoning machine. The next five, no, eight rituals are going to go exactly according to plan.”

“From five to eight, you say?” she asked with a small laugh.

“That’s right, lady. Nine is going to give you trouble, but only because Mr. Tiggles decided to nap in it,” Trevor said sagely as he hugged her and scratched her back. “Ten will be good, but eleven will take place during such a storm that rain will go sideways into the Summoner’s Tower.”

“Even with the magical protections from the weather?”

“It’s going to be the storm of a century, maybe even two,” he confirmed with all the confidence in the world. “Twelve is going to go decent, but not phenomenal. Everything’s going to be humid and your hair will be frizzy, so it’s not going to work as well.”

Adabelle clicked her tongue and shook her head. “You'd think I'd know better.”

“It’s a learning experience,” Trevor repeated. “Luckily, thirteen through forty-seven are going to be spectacular. No issues, nothing but good eggs, and then we can retire onto the island I own after taking over the world.”

“We went over this, honey,” the demonkin chastised as she slid her hand up his shirt to tap on his chest directly. “You can’t take over the world because then you’ll have less time for me.”

“That’s right. Dang, I forgot,” he said before quickly correcting himself. “I mean, Vacation Trevor forgot. Yes.”

Trevor smiled as he listened to his wife laugh. “You’re ridiculous sometimes, you know that?”

“Hey, whatever puts a smile on your face is free game, lady. If being ridiculous gets me there, then I will happily play the jester.”

“I know, that’s one of the reasons I married you,” she replied before kissing him. Her tail unwrapped from his leg as she shimmied away and stood back up so she could finish changing. “I think I’d like to give the mirror a try. While I’m not sure what time it is back in Tosa, I feel that it’s been long enough for Rashie to be out of school.”

“Getting a little homesick?” he asked as he sat on the bed.

“Not yet, but I think it’ll be good for me to touch base, as Wayne puts it,” Adabelle answered. She started moving the shirts around to look for something, frowning when she didn’t find it. When Trevor tapped her on the side, the demonkin turned around to see him holding up a hair tie. She pouted and took it. “You didn’t put them where they’re supposed to be.”

“My bad,” he said as he watched her put her hair in a ponytail. “I’ll try to do better next time.”

Despite Trevor’s words, he didn’t put the rest of her hair accessories in the drawer, instead opting to smile at her. Adabelle stuck her tongue out at her husband before motioning for him to follow her into the living room, and he used this opportunity to playfully bat at her ponytail. Without looking, her tail intercepted every attempt until they reached the couch.

She turned around to look at him, and he was the very picture of innocence. They sat down as he retrieved the Two-Way Blackglass from his inventory and handed it to her.

“Thank you, Trevor,” she said before closing her eyes to concentrate.

The courier didn’t have the Magic stat required to activate the mirror, but Adabelle was a different story. Even though she needed at least one point per hundred miles of distance between them and the item’s twin, she easily connected the two. The black glass became shadowy as the magic took effect.

They stared at it for a few moments before a close up of Rashie’s face appeared in the mirror. “You called!” the shark girl squealed.

“Yes, we did,” Adabelle giggled. “You answered rather quickly. I hope you weren’t waiting for us.”

“Not waiting, but, I’ve had it nearby just in case you wanted to call and—” Rashie’s eyes dilated as she inhaled, smelling something. In a flash, she turned her head and opened her mouth. A meatball went flying straight into it before she snapped her jaw shut with a triumphant look. It was swallowed in a moment. “Nice try, Rupert. You’re gonna have to do better than that!”

“Rashie,” Adabelle said.

“Yeah, Addy?” the shark asked, looking back.

“School’s out, right? You’re not answering this during class?”

She shook her head vehemently. “It is! I wouldn’t activate the mirror in class. Deputy dad’s already given me the ground rules and I’m not allowed to use it during school hours.”

As if to prove she was right, Rashie flipped the mirror around. She was still at the school, but it was clear that all of the kids were leaving for the day. While Tosa was growing, it still wasn’t all that big and they were used to walking home. Some did loiter, though, and the picture panned over Rupert, who was messing with something in his bag.

“See?” Rashie asked when the view returned to her. “Out of school, so I’m allowed to use it. I’m being good, I promise!”

“We didn’t doubt you, shark,” Trevor said as he rested his head on Adabelle’s shoulder so he was more in frame. “How are things back home?”

“Great, awesome, better than ever,” she replied. “I’ve been getting top marks in class because I’m so smart. The teacher said that, if I kept this up, I might even get to move up a grade.”

“Oh? So it’s easy, then?” Adabelle asked.

“I don’t know about easy, but I definitely understand it pretty well. Especially math. I’m good at math.”

“Ugh, math,” Trevor snorted. “Never was good at math.”

“It doesn’t matter, anyway,” Rashie said with a shake of her head. She took a moment to turn to Rupert and waved at him before walking off of school grounds. “Where are you? What are you doing? Are you enjoying your honeymoon?”

“You might find this hard to believe, but…” Adabelle smiled as she turned the mirror around.

“Hi Clay!” the shark girl shouted, and the demonkin briefly paused.

The familiar raised their head and waved back before Adabelle continued moving the mirror. It stopped once it was pointing out of the window.

“Is that the sea?” Rashie asked, excited. “You got a little room by the water? Wait, why is it moving so quickly? Are you on a ship?”

“We are on an airship, actually,” the noblewoman answered.

She nearly dropped the mirror as the shark girl shrieked with joy. “An airship!? You’re flying right now? I’m stuck at home and you three are flying?”

“Not only are we flying, but we’re not even on the same continent as you anymore,” Trevor said as he laughed at her response. “We’re heading to Aletander, the country of demons.”

“That’s so cool!” Rashie whined. “I want to go on an airship ride. Do you think if I get married, I can go, too?”

“Maybe, but I think you have a long time before you have to worry about that, shark,” Adabelle replied with a smile.

“Man, I feel bad for any boy you’d bring home,” Trevor added.

“What? Why?” Rashie asked. She was about to become indignant when the demonkin turned the mirror back around.

“Because they’re going to get a stern talking to from Wayne,” he answered before kissing his wife on the cheek. “I got the good ole daddy-daughter gun talk back when I just had a crush on Adabelle, so I can’t imagine what he’d do for anyone you’d bring home.”

“Now, to be fair, he also tried to do the same to me about you,” the noblewoman laughed. “The difference is that I laughed at him until he decided to drop it.”

“Yeah, two very different scenarios there,” Trevor agreed.

“Papa’s not going to be like that,” Rashie disagreed with a pout. “He knows I can take care of myself. I’m super strong, you know. I could beat up Trevor with a single punch.”

“Whoa! Why am I getting heat over here?”

“Because I’d have trouble beating Addymanatine, duh.”

Trevor laughed. “While true, that doesn’t mean you gotta point it out so readily.”

“No beating up my husband, Rashie,” Adabelle said as she put her arm around him. “Otherwise, I’d have to step in.”

“I’m not gonna, I’m just saying I could if I wanted to,” Rashie claimed. “At least Trevor could handle it, unlike some mundane people who are trying to steal your job.”

“Oh?” she asked, arching an eyebrow. “Has Bocoro finally made his move?”

“No,” the shark girl answered, grumpy. “He’s not doing anything! Just paperwork and making sure the town is in working order. He hasn’t tried anything, and it’s been days since the last time I told you he was up to something!”

“It sounds like the thing he’s up to is making sure that Tosa is well-maintained,” Adabelle replied.

“That’s still just a cover. But also, don’t come back until your honeymoon is over. I can handle him.”

“I know you can, sweetie, just don’t give him too hard a time until he begins his coup, okay?”

Rashie nodded. “That’s better than what papa had me promise, so I’ll take it!”

“What did Wayne say?” Trevor asked curiously.

“He blessed my heart and told me not to bother Bocoro with anything that’s not small time,” the shark girl answered. “And he said it in that tone, too. The one where he says some Earth saying and it’s clearly different from what he actually said. No matter what he meant, though, I’m still watching him, so you can count on me.”

“Keep up the good work, Rashie,” Adabelle said with a smile. “I still think Bocoro will continue to act as a perfect temporary mayor, but I feel better with you there to protect everyone.”

“I’ll do my best, Mayor Addy,” the girl replied, puffing out her chest. “Nothing gets passed Rashie Williams.”

“That’s my girl. Alright, I just wanted to check in, so you get home safe.”

“Of course I will, but so should you! Being on an airship is fun, but dangerous,” Rashie said seriously.

“Thank you for the warning,” Trevor chuckled.

“Oh, um and…” The shark girl hesitated. “Instead of turning the mirror off, could you, maybe instead point it at the window so I can watch the waves? At least until the magic runs dry and turns it off.”

“I believe that will be possible, yes,” Adabelle answered as she turned the mirror around. “How’s that?”

“Perfect!” Rashie replied. “Thank you, Addy!”

“You’re welcome, shark.”

After that, they sat in comfortable silence as they watched the outside of the ship. The only thing that would disturb them was their dinner, which would be on its way eventually. It was a good first day of their airship ride.
