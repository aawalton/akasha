Lifesworn

These are not simply retainers, not simply bodyguards or group members. The ruling clans of the fae are the only ones still known to leverage the Lifesworn bonds.

On rare occasions these are commitments established later in life, however in the vast majority of cases the bond is formed while still infants, waiting only long enough for initial personality to be displayed.

The Lifesworn is raised alongside the royal, treated as a near sibling yet constantly reminded that their role is to protect and shadow. They are there to serve as companion and bodyguard, as well as as a confidant who is unable to betray their charge. The bond is one way, an enforced contract that ties in to the family lineage unless and until the royal is recognized in their own right.

~ * ~

Anya found her thoughts drifting as Leo went over the basics of Diving in the Depths. He'd made it clear from the start that the initial information would be generic and likely already known, but that he would cover it regardless to ensure everyone had shared understanding. She could feel Seb paying attention, or at least keeping track of what was being said.

Her thoughts kept going back to the duel yesterday. Cara had said they were winning, yet it hadn't felt like it. They were off-balance, barely managing to keep from being hurt, constantly adjusting to the situation without any victory in sight. Seb had been racing to try and help her, she had been certain she was going to be injured badly enough, or come close enough to real injury to be declared defeated and then what would Seb have done two versus one?

She wanted to ask Cara, or even Gwen, why the fight had seemed different to them. She understood Bladesong changing the odds, she had felt the improvement before she'd seen them falter. The fact that they both knew what it was so quickly that Gwen had made it quite clear that it was the reason they surrendered raised even more questions. Fay had found next to nothing, Gwen had said it was less rare among the fae, but less rare didn't mean common enough to recognize instantly, not unless they knew someone?

Anya.

She felt Seb guide her focus back to the present, to Leo's words.

"Finally we come to Depth Artifacts and materials," Leo was saying. "I'm certain most of you have encountered them, particularly those of you who use weapons or armor. Equipment not created by other Awakened but found, or created from materials obtained in the Depths."

We never… Seb sent.

Maybe we didn't know to look?

"Typically these rewards match the Energy density of the Depths they are found in, so yes, you can find them even in the earliest of levels. They are rare, but learning what to look for is critical as you Delve deeper, especially if you are looking to upgrade equipment or fund purchases."

Anya started to tune the instructor out once more. The information was interesting, Holding Lys had mentioned the existence, but had no information on how to look for these prizes. She knew Seb was paying attention for both of them, could feel his amusement at her disinterest.

I'll listen next time, let you ignore, she sent.

Sometime, when the topic is boring.

There hadn't been time yesterday to ask the questions she'd wanted. The Hammerswells and Blehmans had both insisted on a second round, the results ending up even more lopsided than the first time despite neither group having changed their tactics one bit. Sarna had set them all to using private halls after that, told them to consider what they'd noticed about the duels and come up with ideas as to how they could improve.

Seb had answered the question with an obvious answer, use Bladesong from the start, then they had spent a few hours trying to treat the question seriously. They hadn't come up with any good answers, only more questions to test next time they got the chance.

They'd spent the rest of the afternoon experimenting with other weapons, flailing at each other in their best approximations of what they'd seen of the rest of their dorm mates. Neither of them liked the other weapon choices, their styles already too ingrained to shift entirely, but the sight and feel of a polearm jabbing at her face before lashing out horizontally was something Anya was glad to have first experienced in private. Seb might not have been able to do much with the weapon, but letting him attack and merely defending had forced her to confront the momentum of the weapon when wielded by someone who wasn't predictable in the way Depthsborn were.

"Dismissed," Leo said. "I expect each group to provide an example of a threat in the Depths that only posed a challenge due to improper preparation or overconfidence next class."

Seb?

He was emphasizing planning and preparation when training in the Depths, and discouraging wanton exploration of new Depths and passages without extensive contingencies.

She could feel Seb's barely repressed laughter. Other than their first trip to the Depths they had never been on pre-explored paths. They had never taken the time to consider the unknown hazards or slowed down because they might run into a surprise. They'd pushed and pushed, they'd challenged everything they came across, and they had gotten here hadn't they? Were they expected to take things more slowly and carefully from now on? Were they really supposed to not take risks?

Anya stared at her dinner, shifting the food around aimlessly as her thoughts mingled with Seb's. They'd spent their afternoon wandering the grounds of the school, exploring what they could without entering any of the buildings. Sarna had explained their time would be taken up by trips to the Depths and by other teachers in the next few days, apparently everyone was expected to find ways to occupy their time until then.

Seer knowledge tomorrow, she sent for what felt like the fifth time today.

Hopefully he actually has useful information.

Think he'll try to get back at you?

For saying we know Valene? Would he be that petty? he sent back, clearly expecting the answer would turn out to be yes.

She smiled, shaking her head before taking another bite of food. Seb was right, Marten had seemed the sort to be that petty. Or at least the sort to form wrong opinions about people.

"May I sit?" Cara asked.

Anya glanced up, not startled by Cara's proximity so much as by the fact she'd actually paused to speak. She hadn't consciously noticed her until that moment, neither of them had, but it was hard to sneak up on Seb, something Sandra had found out more than once.

Like Sandra going after bacon.

Huh?

You always know, which means I always know, even if we don't pay attention.

"Of course Cara. Join us," she said as she felt Seb's agreement with her comparison.

"Gwen asked me to speak to you. Bern didn't want her to, didn't want me to, but…"

"But you don't have to answer to him and Gwen will handle his attitude?" Seb guessed.

She saw Cara smile faintly before the expression disappeared.

"Humans who can use Bladesong are, as I'm sure you're aware, all but unheard of. Those who can leverage it at such a low level even more so."

"Our Seer mentioned she could find next to nothing. She didn't mention anything about levels though," Anya said.

"Many fae will have opinions about this, few of them positive," Cara clarified.

"Bern?" Seb asked.

"Yes. Especially since you…" she trailed off, her hair dimming as she considered her words. "I…"

"He doesn't like me," Anya said sharply, trying to keep her temper in check. "He has issues with who my mother is. He doesn't like Seb because I'm still fae enough for it to matter, and he especially doesn't like that I matter enough to matter."

She could feel Cara staring at her, she could feel Seb's supportive presence. He didn't try to calm her down, didn't try to intrude in the moment, only let her know he was there. She felt herself calming regardless, knowing he was there.

"I grew up being treated as a problem because of how I look. None of them passed the Wall, even my mother, the Sovereign, did after we did. He's not the first, probably won't be the last."

"I… see." Cara said softly.

"Sorry," Anya said. "I thought I was over them. That I-"

She felt Seb's focus before he cut her off, catching her momentary hesitation perfectly.

"It's a reminder you didn't expect to face."

"Gwen, we, did hope we might practice dueling again. Assuming our classes allow it."

"Why did you think we were winning," Anya asked. "You cut me, I was vulnerable and off balance."

"Gwen couldn't face either of you head on. If she committed she would have fallen. I couldn't defend us both, I couldn't land a solid hit."

"If either of you had landed that blow on her I had no chance," Seb said.

"We wouldn't have landed something solid enough, not in that match. Mistress Sarna doesn't know our actual capabilities."

She felt something click in their shared thoughts. She'd had to be careful with her strikes a few times, hold back rather than committing to avoid real injury. She didn't know how well they'd handle the blows, if they'd be able to avoid real injury. She knew Seb could, she knew how in sync they were and when they had to actually pull back. She knew they had the same problem, but hadn't considered it when wondering about the fight.

"You didn't want to cause real injury, and couldn't show you would have without risking actually doing so."

"We weren't trained to duel with that restriction," Cara said with a growing smile. "To have to pull our blows beyond clearly lethal strikes."

"And no one other than the other fae would realize the limitation," Seb said.

"Bern was furious, he said we threw the fight."

"I understand," Anya said. "I worried I might actually hurt one of you, assuming I actually got past your shield."

Cara nodded then started to stand, her head turning towards Gwen.

"May I?"

"Of course. You don't need to ask permission," Anya answered.

Cara all but rushed off, nearly stumbling as she paused to glance back.

"Thank you."
