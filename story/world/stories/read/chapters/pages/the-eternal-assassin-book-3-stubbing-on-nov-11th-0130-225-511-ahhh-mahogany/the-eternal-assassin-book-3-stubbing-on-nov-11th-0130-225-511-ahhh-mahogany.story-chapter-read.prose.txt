The four of them stood in a loose square formation a few miles out from the capital city. This was their designated training ground that they’d claimed for themselves, and they’d spent day after day churning up the grass and spilling blood and sweat all over the place. Only now, rather than throwing themselves at one another in another sparring session, they were still.

Staring at the three magic staffs Asher had pulled from his Personal Rift and placed between them.

Banishment… Cage of Suffering… and Rapid Summoning, Asher thought, his Identify making it quite clear which staff was which. After snagging the three staffs from the lich’s secret room in its tower, he hadn’t really known what to do with them. He and Samantha had hypothesized at the time that the staff of Banishment might be a good last-resort against the greater demons in the event they proved too powerful, but they hadn’t managed to come up with any ideas for Cage of Suffering or Rapid Summoning. Cage of Suffering wasn’t a skill listed in any book that he could find, and summoning more demons to fight was the exact opposite of what they were trying to accomplish.

After a few moments of silent study, Rulfar eventually stepped forward, staring down at the staffs with a hesitant look on his face. Seeing any sort of hesitation from the large bloodhound was unusual, but Asher couldn’t exactly blame him.

None of the three spells linked to the staffs sounded like they’d be much fun to accidentally set off.

“I haven’t fully explained this to you three, but I have two primary methods of tracking people,” Rulfar said, crouching down and frowning at the staffs as his eyes swept over them. “First is through my Blood element. If I get my hands on even a few drops of someone’s blood, I can find them again no matter how far they run.”

“That’s not going to do us a lot here, is it?” Moxy snorted. “I doubt someone who’s been dead for a few millennia is going to have a lot of blood on them.”

“No, I doubt that as well,” Rulfar agreed. “Thankfully, that’s only one of my tracking methods. The other isn’t quite so precise, but it’s far more useful, as I don’t often have blood to track in the first place. I have the Predator’s Nose skill from my Bear element. As long as I have something that my target interacted with, I can track them.”

“Really? I think I fought and killed a senior assassin with a similar skill back in Whikoga,” Asher admitted, thinking back to Leonard and his terrifying battle form. The man’s Wolf element had been quite the challenge to take down at the time. “Do bears have a good sense of smell then?”

“One of the best. Far greater than any dog’s,” Rulfar frowned, as if the comparison was an insult directed at him specifically. “That said, this skill does have some caveats. For one, the distance over which I can track someone is based on how long they’ve spent interacting with the object I’m getting their scent from. A new belt that they’ve only worn a handful of times would probably only let me track them a few thousand feet. A favored book that they read every night as a child would cover a city. I once tracked down a woman who’d been kidnapped by bandits on the road, dropping her treasured locket in the process. According to her husband, she’d worn that locket every day for nearly forty years. With it in my possession, I managed to track her down sixty miles from where she’d been taken and rescued her, killing all the bandits in the process. The point is, my skill fluctuates, so I can’t make any guarantees. That said…”

“If you were able to track someone sixty miles from an amulet they wore for forty years,” Samantha said, staring at the three staffs hungrily. “…How far could you track a wizard with three of their personal staffs they presumably have been alternating between for a few thousand years?”

“That’s the million-shard question,” Rulfar nodded. “Though there are plenty of factors at play here. For all we know, these personal staffs may have only been created within the last few decades. Or, more likely, the lich has wards and defenses in play to prevent someone from doing just this. We really have no way of knowing before we try.”

“In that case, why don’t we stop standing here staring at a bunch of oversized sticks and you get on with it?” Moxy asked, punching her fists together. “We’ve been training long enough. I want to punch a demon in the face!”

“I’m with Moxy. We’ve spent enough time delaying, let’s see what we can do,” Asher said, giving Rulfar an encouraging smile. “Just do what you can. If this doesn’t work, we’ll find another way.”

Taking a deep breath, Rulfar stepped forward, reaching out to the three staffs. He hesitated for a moment, before selecting the staff of Banishment. They’d all discussed this already, but if they had to guess which of the three staffs the lich primarily kept on its person, it would probably be that one. When it had first summoned Asher to this world by accident, the lich had mentioned that Asher didn’t look like an eldritch abomination, which was a strong hint as to what it had actually been trying to summon. Presumably, it would want a quick rejection button at the ready in case it accidentally summoned something too powerful for even it to take down. That meant it probably held on to the staff of Banishment rather frequently.

Asher watched as Rulfar clutched the staff in both hands, closing his eyes as he brought it up to his face. Slowly, he ran his nose along the wooden staff, his eyelids flickering as he took in a deep, deep breath that seemed to go on for far longer than should be humanly possible. Once the bloodhound's chest had fully inflated, he let out the breath with a heavy sigh, his eyes snapping open as a grin formed on his face.

“Good news. This staff is old. And from the sheer amount of its scent I’m getting off of it, the lich has been using this for at least the last two thousand years. I’ve never experienced anything quite like this. Ten hells, I think I would have just leveled my skill if it wasn’t already sitting at level 40.”

“Ten hells?” Samantha asked, giving Rulfar a curious look. “Aren’t there only nine?”

“My people believe that there is a tenth one reserved for a special sort of monster,” Rulfar said curtly, running his nose along the staff again and taking yet another deep breath. “That is a lesson for another day. What is important now is that I have the lich’s scent. And enough of it that I can tell the direction it’s in, despite the fact that it feels like it’s over a thousand miles away.”

“That’s great!” Asher said, his heart already starting to race as Rulfar confirmed they once again had a lead. After everything The Council of Death and this lich specifically had put him through, finally, they were getting a real chance to strike back.

“There is one thing I feel I should mention,” Rulfar said, causing Asher’s excitement to flag slightly. “The scent almost feels… dulled, somehow. With how long the lich has been using this staff and how powerful the smell, I should be able to pinpoint its exact location even from this distance. Instead, I can tell all I’m getting is more of a general sense of its location. If I had to make a guess, I think that confirms the lich has some sort of personal defenses set up to deter this exact thing. We just got lucky that my Bear element has one of the most powerful scent skills on record, and that we have such an old object to track it from.”

“Honestly, I’d be more concerned if you weren’t encountering any sort of defensive measures,” Asher admitted, his excitement returning in full force. “In theory, you’re leading us back to the lich’s main base of operations. Or at least some sort of backup, assuming the tower it abandoned used to be its main base. That tower couldn’t be seen from the sky, so we know the lich is capable of using magic to hide. If it could hide itself from vision, it’s not a stretch to assume it can do the same for smell.”

“Maybe from some pathetic wolf, but not Bear-boy here!” Moxy said, clapping Rulfar on the back as she grinned. “Way to do what we hired you to do in the first place!”

“You didn’t hire me. I chose to join you three on your mission out of my own free will,” Rulfar pointed out, sighing at the new nickname. “And I don’t think ‘Bear-boy’ is all that much of an improvement over ‘Carrot.’”

“You’re the one who said you wanted a new nickname,” Moxy shrugged, raising an eyebrow. “Personally, I thought Carrot was more fitting anyway. I mean, we did find you buried in the dirt.”

“I asked you to stop using that nickname, not to come up with another one,” Rulfar snapped, shaking his head. “Rulfar the Hound is already a nickname!”

“Eh, more of a title, really. Just doesn’t have the same ring to it as ‘Carrot,’ you know? Not nearly so personal.”

Asher couldn’t help but quietly laugh at the exchange, and he shared a quick smile with Samantha as Rulfar began arguing with Moxy in earnest. It was almost impressive how easily Moxy was able to needle the usually stoic and calm bloodhound. It was like she had a gift for figuring out the few things he cared about enough to be dragged down to her level, such as the nickname she’d given to him.

“Alright, as entertaining as this is, don’t forget we’ve got a job to do,” Asher reminded them, stepping between them before things could get bloody. “Moxy, you do realize the longer you spend antagonizing Rulfar, the longer it’s going to be until you get to fight a demon one-on-one, right?”

“Damn it, my two great loves, conflicting at last,” Moxy sighed, her own demonic grin coming out full force at the reminder of what was to come. The sight of it almost made Asher shudder, and he briefly felt bad for whatever poor demons decided to try their claws at taking the warrior down. “Alright, I can pick this up later. I’ll probably have a whole slew of new nicknames to try out soon enough.”

“If you weren’t sweet on the captain of the royal guard, I’d think this was some sort of messed up attempt at getting my attention in a romantic fashion,” Rulfar frowned, earning a snort from Moxy.

“Sweet? I’m not sweet on anyone. It’s precisely because Christian is capable of taking my full-force punches to the face without dying that he’s caught my eye. Granted, we’ll see if anything actually comes of it. I still haven’t managed to convince him to truly go on the offensive in our spars, which isn’t attractive in the slightest. This might just be a passing phase.”

Asher shared yet another look with Samantha, this one of an entirely different sort. The two of them had heard from Donvath himself that more often than not, when using his Time element to gaze into the future, Moxy and Christian ended up getting married to one another. The High Prince wasn’t a big fan of the idea at all, but Asher was just glad to hear that Moxy would end up with someone who truly got her one day.

Assuming they all survived of course.

“Okay, we can talk about nicknames and Moxy’s love life later,” he said, clapping his hands and rubbing them together in anticipation.

“Right now, we’ve got a lich to hunt.”
