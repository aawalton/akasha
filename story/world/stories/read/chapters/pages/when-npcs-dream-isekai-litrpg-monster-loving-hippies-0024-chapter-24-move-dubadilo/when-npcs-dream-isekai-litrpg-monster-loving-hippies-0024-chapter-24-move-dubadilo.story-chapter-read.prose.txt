“Come on, Dubadilo, you’re eating forever!” I tried to shove the armadillo care bear forward as he calmly grazed in the clearing. We had been walking for barely an hour, and the beast had already decided it was lunch break. I pushed, leaned, cursed, but the thing didn’t budge an inch.

“SCREEEE!” Kim Jong-un screeched from Samar’s arms, commanding his new minion to march. Dubadilo glanced at him, blinked slowly, and went back to chewing.

“Thought you said he was trained and ready,” I hissed at Kim.

“You should be more patient,” Juliette scolded me, “let the poor animal eat.”

And so we waited. Another. Fricking. Hour.

By the time Dubadilo finally lumbered onward, the sun was already close to noon. My dreams of reaching Kuriolis before dark tomorrow were melting away.

I checked the map we’d bought. “If we keep a steady pace, we should reach this waterfall by evening,” I told Juliette, pointing at a mark. “We can camp there, and probably reach Kuriolis tomorrow.”

Juliette leaned over, frowning at the parchment. “Look here—the terrain changes. The forest turns into rolling woodland hills.”

“I guess so?”

“So the fauna will probably be different too,” she explained. “We’ll likely encounter new alien species we don’t recognize. Even Kim Jong-un might not know them. All our success in the forest so far came from knowing the local dangers and how to avoid them.”

She was right. “Okay, what do you suggest?”

“We detour,” she said firmly. “This path keeps us in the familiar forest longer. Eventually, yes, we’ll still have to cross the woodlands, but for a shorter stretch. And when we do, we go twice as slow, twice as careful. the trip will take half a day or maybe a full day longer, and we’ll need to camp a second night. But it will be much safer.”

“You’re totally right,” I admitted. “You know what? You’re good at this. I think you should be leader.”

Juliette gave me a look. “What's with the leader thing? It’s just us here.”

“Because sometimes we’ll disagree,” I said. “And in a real emergency, someone needs final say.”

“I agree!” Samar chimed in immediately. “Juliette will be an awesome leader. All hail our mighty leader!”

“All hail!” I echoed, raising my hands dramatically.

Juliette blushed. “I… I’m not sure I’m the right choice. But fine. I’ll do my best.”

I felt good about it. It wasn’t just for this trip. If we ever grew into a larger group—a tribe of players, maybe—Juliette was the one who could hold it together.

The next hours were smoother. Dubadilo stopped to munch a few times, but he also chased away a pair of furry snakes, so I had to grudgingly admit he was useful. Later, though, when Kim and I tried to train him with a spear, he flat-out refused to even hold it. I started to suspect we’d adopted the dumbest armadillo bear in the whole forest.

“Hey, Samar,” I called. “That nest of giant leaves we passed earlier?”

I thought I’d quiz her a bit about forest stuff.

“Gorilla birds,” she answered without missing a beat. “Get too close to their eggs and the whole pack attacks.”

“Good. And the sand pits?”

“Zebra antlions,” she said, rolling her eyes. “Even Hyun-woo knows that one.”

It was a little embarrassing, but I had very little left to teach Samar about the forest. She’d learned tons in such a short time. To be fair, she cheated—being basically glued to a furry scorpion forest guide named after a fat dictator.

And also to be fair, the little guy, despite being like three years old, knew almost as much about the forest as his father, Bob.

Still, they were a fourteen-year-old girl and a small Suricata, and I didn’t like them sprinting ahead, out of our view. She’d get herself killed, and then Hyun-woo would murder me.

“Samar! Too far! We can’t even see you!”

“Sorry!” she called back.

“Can’t you just stay with us?” I begged.

“It’s hard!” she whined. “You’re too slow, and Kim gets bored!”

Oh my god. Was I the responsible adult here? First time for everything.

“Hey,” Juliette called, bringing us to a stop.

She pointed at a dense thicket.“Too risky. Something could sting us without warning. We’ll circle around over there.”

She was really leaning into this leader role, making small calls confidently. I liked it.

We pushed on for hours. Despite Dubadilo’s grazing breaks, we made solid progress—almost to our planned camp before dark. I felt relief. Halfway to Kuriolis, and better than I’d expected.

“Oh, Dubadilo, not again!” I muttered as he found another patch. “These purple leaves will give you a stomachache for sure!”

“Quiet,” Juliette hissed. She beckoned us together. I listened—and heard it too.

Not animal sounds. Voices. Shouting. High-pitched, squeaky voices. Getting closer.

We crouched by a bush near the trail.

“Are those… curse words?” I whispered.

The voices grew clearer:“Shit-ball-licker! Fuckbrain! Wankstain! Pisspopsicle!”

Then: “Hayfork fuckwit!” “Arse-butter!”

And stranger still: “Stool-sniffer!” “Mattress maggot!” “Mallet-nose!”

And finally: “Mattress-guzzlewort!” “Shit-wobblearse!” “Saw-grundlefart!” “Broom-snogglepiss!”

“I like Broom-snogglepiss,” Samar whispered. “I’ll definitely use that on you when you annoy me.”

“I think it’s the system translator struggling,” I muttered. “Probably makes more sense in the original language.”

“But is that even human?” Juliette whispered.

“Wait and see.”

We crouched, the barrage of insults growing louder until—finally—they appeared.

Five humans. They matched the goblins’ description perfectly: one dark-skinned, one brown-skinned, and the rest pale — all about our age. Among them, one was a female, Asian I think, while the other four were males.

And behind them, tied with a rope leash, was the source of the swearing.

Big Bird.

And I don’t mean a bird that is big. I mean literally Big Bird from Sesame Street. Well, not exactly—more orange than yellow, with fur instead of feathers. And it never stopped cursing. But the resemblance was still striking.

“Table-splonker!” “Chamberpot-spawn!” “Ladle-licker!”

I blinked. “Should we reveal ourselves?” I whispered to Juliette. This was a leader call.

She sighed. “I don’t like it. But yes. We should.”

We stepped out. The group froze, then one of them shouted “Its Humans!”

Then they all rushed toward us, grinning like old friends.The brown-skinned guy reached me first, shaking my hand with a huge smile.

“Hello, friend! I’m so glad to meet other humans. Please, let me introduce us. My name is Arjun—I’m from Kolkata, India.”

He kept shaking my hand, smiling all the while.

“We’re part of a larger group of twenty-five humans. We’ve built a base in Kuriolis, and actually, we’re heading back there now.”

He gestured to the others. “This is Muhammad from Pakistan, Sergei from Russia, Emeka from Nigeria, and Chen Jie from China.”

25 players. A large base. Wow that was huge! They must have been organized — probably discovered so much already.I realized it was my turn to introduce us.

“Uh… hi,” I said. “I’m Geshem, from the U.S. This is Juliette, from France, and Samar, from Egypt.”

“Juliette, you stool-snarfler!” Big Bird screeched.

Juliette flinched as the cursing bird addressed her directly.

“Geshem, you bench-bollocks! Samar, you’re a blibberwank-licker!”

Samar puffed out her chest, pointed dramatically at the bird, and shouted back: “BROOM-SNOGGLEPISS!”

The bird froze, stunned, as if deeply contemplating this insult.

“Ah, please don’t mind this cursing monster,” Arjun said. “She’s tied well, no threat .” Then he spotted Kim in Samar’s arms. “But… that thing—a Scorpion Cat, is it safe?”

“Of course!” Samar hugged him tighter. “This is Kim. He’s sweet.”

Then the Russian guy, Sergei, spotted Dubadilo. “Armadillo bull!” he shouted, already drawing a knife.

“No, no!” I raised my hands. “He’s with us too. Both the Armadillo bull and the Scorpion Cat—tamed. They will not harm you.”

“Ohh. You’re a monster tamer!” Sergei said, impressed.

“Something like that,” I said, dodging the topic. “So what’s with the talking bird? Why is it tied up?”

“It’s a dangerous monster,” Arjun said matter-of-factly. “Three of them attacked us. We Killed one, another fled, this one we kept.”

“LIAR! Stool-snarfler!” the bird shrieked. “YOU attacked us, murderous human forknobs!”

Arjun flinched, then shrugged. “We usually kill all monsters for XP. Easier that way. But our boss will want to see that one.”

I exchanged a look with Juliette. Then, carefully, I said, “We saw a goblin patrols in this area, did you.. ah.. manage to kill some?”

“Oh, we actually met some yesterday!” Arjun said. “Good XP, usually easy kills—but that one group was too strong. Not worth the effort.”

“HEY!” Samar snapped. “You can’t just walk around killing things for XP!”

Arjun blinked, confused. “What’s she talking about?”

“She’s new. Still adjusting.“ I sighed. “All of this… this is not easy for her.”

“Of course, of course I understand,” Arjun said warmly. “It was hard for all of us at first, too. Look—our camp is close, and it’s getting late. Please, come join us! It’s amazing to finally meet more humans. We have so much to talk about!”

I looked at Juliette. She gave a small, reluctant nod.

“Sure,” I said brightly. “We’d love to.”

But when my eyes met Juliette’s again, the silent understanding was clear: These guys weren’t exactly on the same side as us.

And more importantly, I had a strong feeling that the foul-mouthed bird was a player.

An alien.
