“Dee mentioned [Dark Benediction] as our natural next step,” Nate said with a nod to Romulus, who looked vindicated. “But he wasn’t really sure what we could expect.”

“Likely because he has not properly studied the [Liber Nox],” Mortarius noted, glancing at Romulus, who immediately looked sheepish. “Open the tome, Romulus.”

Kate shifted when the Autarch obeyed, muttering about being busy trying not to die while he pulled the platinum-chained black Libram from his hip and opened the book on his lap. Almost as if sensing his need, the pages started to flip rapidly until they settled on a section Kate could read as “Benedictions of the Dark Faith,” printed at the top of the first page while the others shifted closer to the seated Autarch.

“Oh. There’s literally a list…” Romulus grumbled. “Okay, yeah, I really need to study this.”

“Your delinquency aside,” Cora said impatiently, “give us the deets, Dee.”

“Fine, fine…” he muttered, crimson eyes tracing the page before he started to read. “It looks like Benediction bestowals are listed as Nightstalkers like Kay, Sepulchral Hierophants, Abyssal Magisters, Dark Templars, and Black Celebrants.” His eyes lifted to Mortarius, who nodded, and then returned to the pages while the others—including Kate—watched him intently. He looked beautiful when he was concentrating, which was entirely unfair of her brain to notice.

“The Nightstalker Enclave is charged with protecting the Dark Autarch, assassinating his or her enemies, working as intelligence operatives, hunting and destroying renegade Necromancers, slaying apostates marked by the Dark Inquisition—that’s neat—and the forceful opposition against an Autarch who betrays the Dark Lady’s precepts, edicts, or sacrosanct commands.”

“Yep!” Kate said cheerfully. “I’ve got to stab him if he gets too big for his own good and ignores Deathmommy.”

“That is not your literal job description,” Romulus said with immediate alarm while Mortarius momentarily closed his eyes.

“Close enough,” Kate said slyly. “Heresy, ego, same difference.”

Their friends laughed while Romulus gave her a long-suffering look and then returned to reading when Mortarius opened his eyes with a pointed glance.

“Next up are Sepulchral Hierophants; grave-keepers, anti-Necromancers, soul-guardians, and defensive enchanters. They wield [Sanguimancy] to heal and attack, [Shadowmancy] for assault and control, and are charged with the sanctification of graveyards, the repulsion of unsanctioned necromancy, and the enforcement of the cycle. They act as the highest Priests and Priestesses of the Dark Lady’s Clergy, and are the foremost authorities on the Cycle, death, and bereavement, and are permitted the ability to summon and command the willingly-returned dead.”

Mayarella’s eyes brightened at the designation, and Mortarius nodded slightly when she turned to him. “The role of a Hierophant has always been celebrated and revered,” the specter said solemnly. “They are the Dark Lady’s most respected Clergy.”

“So they’re like a healer, enchanter, and summoner class?” Mayarella asked hopefully. “But with offensive ability as well?”

“That is an accurate summation of their function, Mayarella. You should not, however, expect it to be easily attained,” Mortarius answered with an edge of firmness that Kate saw Maya pick up on. “Hierophants are an Archetype, a very rare and prodigious one, granted only by the Lady’s direct blessing. They ascend from a different endowment, Mayarella; one which can be granted at a lower level such as yours. We shall come back to that shortly.”

Maya deflated a little at the clarification, but all eyes turned to Romulus, and Kate squeezed the back of his neck in support, drawing a smile, before he continued. “Up next are Abyssal Magisters”—Corva’s gaze sharpened immediately, and her fiddling fingers fell still—“which hold a distinct position of offensive war-making capability and martial enchantment. Abyssal Magisters wield the powers of [Shadowmancy], [Tenebromancy], and [Entropomancy] to bring the Dark Lady’s judgment upon Her Faith’s enemies, and obliterate the shields that would keep in abeyance the wrath of Her Faithful.”

“What is [Tenebromancy]?” Kate asked immediately after Romulus finished.

“The art of wielding Abyssal Summons,” Mortarius answered patiently. “It permits a spellcaster to summon and bind Abyssals to serve their will, either multiple smaller manifestations or—at higher levels of expertise—a single powerful Abyssal servant.”

“Dibs!” Corva said without hesitation. “I will do anything for that. I will kiss Deathmommy’s toes; I’m not joking. [Shadowmancy], [Tenebromancy], and [Entropomancy]!? Sign me the fuck up!”

Kate laughed, Romulus sighed, Nate chuckled, and Maya looked positively amused.

“Abyssal Magisters are not so easily created, Corva,” Mortarius said between them while his features flickered with faint pain at her enthusiasm. “It requires far more than unbridled enthusiasm to master their arts. You would be best served beginning as a Dark Mana Channeler and focusing on raising your abilities prior to seeking endowment.”

Corva immediately looked disgruntled, but bit her lip instead of arguing, while her elven features—perfectly suited to her Korean beauty—remained attentive despite the pout forming at Mortarius’s words.

“Like a Hierophant, it requires the Lady’s direct approval to ascend as an Abyssal Magister. Level is not always the whole of it—existing strategic and warfighting acumen can bridge the gap levels do not, but in your case, Corva, I would not expect such an endowment.”

“Right,” Corva muttered. “Prove I’m not an idiot, then ask for the badass warmage class.”

“Archetype,” Romulus corrected.

“Archetype! Fine!”

Nate and Maya laughed, and Kate grinned at Corva while the woman sighed.

“Next up is—oh. Oh man,” Romulus said with sudden glee. “Nate, this has you written all over it, brother.”

Nathanius’ expression brightened at his best friend’s words, and he stepped closer as if to peer at the book while Romulus spoke.

“Dark Templars: sacred guardians, temple knights, defenders of the Faith, and the elite heavy infantry of the Dark. Dark Templars traditionally serve with shield and blade but are known to wield any weapon that best suits their purpose. Their talents lie in [Shadowmancy] and defensive Spellforms like the [Dark Aegis], allowing them to intercept and disperse hostile spellcasting with their tower shields, defend against Affinity-imbued attacks, and hold a line even against overwhelming odds.”

“Holy shit,” Nate said with feeling, while Romulus nodded vigorously.

“It would be nice to have a proper shield to duck behind while sniping people,” Kate agreed brightly. “Dee likes to charge ahead like a madman, so having your bulwark back to fill the absence would be excellent, Nate.”

“I’m not that bad!” Romulus objected, only for all four of them and even Mortarius to give him a skeptical look, at which point he sighed. “Point taken. Nate with a shield will make Kate’s sniping easier. Proceed.”

A collective laugh followed the words, and Kate rewarded his self-awareness with a gentle pat to the shoulder.

“Dude,” Nate continued afterward, “Dark Templar is fucking perfect. I could totally protect Kate and the others while you went crazy. Though, wait, is there a level restriction on that one, too?”

All eyes turned to Mortarius, and the specter met the expectant looks with faint amusement. “No,” the former Autarch answered as Kate felt her burgeoning smile widen. “Dark Templars are not restricted by level like Sepulchral Hierophant or Abyssal Magister. They are a sacred endowment, but are ideal for growth as opposed to growing into them. I cannot lie and say you will be as powerful or capable as someone of Specialist Tier or higher, which would be the normal point at which such a [Dark Benediction] would be offered, but you would not be limited in seeking that path.”

Nathanius’ answering smile was radiant when he received the clarification, and he and Romulus bumped fists immediately with a mutual murmur of “dude”, followed by Maya and Cora releasing matching sounds of discontent.

“Of course the meatheads don’t have an entry requirement,” Corva fumed. “Unbelievable!”

“If you gloat about this, Nathaniel,” Mayarella warned, “I swear I’ll pinch you.”

“Girlfriend aggro,” Kate said approvingly to Maya. “A truly fearsome weapon to wield, Maya.”

“And one I know better than to test,” Nate said dutifully, though he couldn’t disguise the pleasure on his features, nor could Maya resist returning a warm smile when he sent her one first. She may have been outraged by the lack of equal restriction, but she loved him, and Kate saw her melt the moment their eyes met.

“Yeah, yeah, you two are disgustingly good for one another, we get it,” Cora said without real disgruntlement, hiding her own smile at their lovey-dovey reaction. “Keep going, Dee. What’s next?”

Kate returned her gaze to the man she loved while he scanned the pages and let out a thoughtful sound.

“Only one,” Romulus replied. “Black Celebrant: consecrated ritualists and field clergy who conduct ordinary rites, maintain funerary wards, tend the wounded with [Sanguimancy], support the Faith’s armies with both it and [Shadowmancy], and serve as the daily backbone of the Faith. They aid the function of the Dark Lady’s followers in-between the greater miracles of higher callings, and provide the institutional foundation from which Hierophants may eventually emerge.”

His eyes moved to Maya at the same time as Kate’s and everyone else’s did, and they saw the Dominican woman grin. “So,” Maya said intently, “that’s what you meant, Mortarius?”

“Indeed,” the specter said. “Black Celebrant is where your path may begin, Mayarella. I will not promise that ascending past it shall be either swift or painless, but it is where your journey must start if you seek a Hierophant’s appointment.”

“A good starting place,” Maya said decisively. “I’m fine with that. Thank you, Mortarius.”

“Of course,” the specter said. “However, before any of you petition for a [Dark Benediction], there is one more thing we must discuss, and you must all heed it well.”

Romulus closed the [Liber Nox] when the specter spoke, and Kate saw her childhood friend’s attention sharpen on Mortarius, which made everyone else—including her—turn to the specter in kind.

“We must speak of protocol,” the former Autarch said with deliberate emphasis. “Both toward Romulus, and more importantly, toward the Dark Lady.”

“Dee already told us we need to show reverence,” Corva said carefully.

“And you must,” Mortarius agreed, “but reverence is not limited to Her alone; it must also be offered to Her Autarch.”

Silence greeted the ghost’s words, and all eyes turned to Romulus, who lifted his hands defensively before turning to Mortarius. “Maybe be more specific, Morty?”

The former Revenant-King sighed but answered without audible complaint.

“Your familiarity, kinship, and long-standing friendship are not improper—what is improper is how you conduct it in public sight. The Dark Faith will not remain solely you five and myself for long, not if Romulus acts as an Autarch must. Thereby, you must understand what is expected of you in public, lest you err on the side of undermining Her Autarch, and in the act, undermining She of the End.”

“I take it that would be bad?” Maya asked carefully while Cora, Nate, Kate, and Romulus all watched Mortarius with equal levels of caution.

“It could result in Her obliterating your Eidos,” Mortarius said calmly. “Permanently.”

Kate’s fingers froze against the back of Romulus’s neck, and she looked down at him immediately, instinctively waiting for him to protest, object, or crack wise about his friends betraying him by getting themselves obliterated. He did none of it. Instead, his eyes remained locked on Mortarius, and the absence of humor in his expression was more telling than the warning itself.

“He isn’t joking,” Romulus said quietly, turning to their friends. “Not even slightly.”

Nate met the look while straightening behind his shield with a grimace; Cora’s amber gaze darted briefly toward the entrance as if measuring an imagined audience, and Maya frowned quietly with a look of self-reflection for their previous discourse. Kate noticed Cora’s attention and followed it, finding herself imagining a filled chamber instead of an abandoned one.

They had been treating the entire thing with the same relentless, casual indulgence they had brought to Avalon Online or RunEscape, but she already knew better. Lilith had been explicit when Kate had undergone her own [Dark Benediction], and the sacred charge with which she had been entrusted had carried its own warnings—namely, the terrible price of failing the Divinarch.

“He really isn’t,” she said finally, finding her voice while everyone else remained silent. “I’ve met Lilith, too. She’s hot. She’s indulgent. She loves agency and free will because She likes Her followers to learn their own limits and test themselves against temptation—but She’s not tolerant of disrespect.”

Kate lowered her gaze back to Romulus, who met her look with quiet understanding as she continued. “She literally charged me to kill Dee, or rather, Romulus, if he betrayed Her precepts. She wasn’t joking about it, either, guys. She made that really fucking clear.”

Silence followed her words as their friends looked from Kate to Romulus and back to Mortarius. No one spoke for a long moment, until Corva finally broke the silence.

“So, what do we do differently?” Cora asked carefully. “How do we know what is and isn’t allowed?”

“Begin by listening,” Mortarius said, drawing all five of their gazes to him. “I will not leave you with a dire warning and no way to correct your missteps. We shall start with protocol, then distinguish between private and public conduct. From there, we shall move forward.”

All five of them nodded, and Kate felt Romulus relax under her hand as her fingers resumed massaging his neck.

“May I ask a question, first?” Maya ventured cautiously.

“You are welcome to,” Mortarius said approvingly. He seemed to be pleased with the curiosity, just not the flippancy of their earlier remarks, which Kate felt like she and Romulus should have been better about remembering. They really had gotten carried away, despite warning their friends against precisely that.

“We came to Eternus to work with, um, Romulus,” Maya said, visibly halting herself from saying ‘Dee’. “I don’t think any of us are against learning, but we didn’t sign up to be servants, even just for show. Can we still be his companions without needing to become, um, servile?”

Kate felt Romulus tense at the question and turn to Mortarius when the others did, and she could feel the hope and stress he held at the question. He wanted the answer to be yes. It didn’t mean he could force it.

“Servility and respect are not the same thing, and nor should they be confused,” their ghostly mentor said with faint reassurance. “Romulus has tested my nerves, but he has not proven to be inclined toward a desire for debasement, and nor would She of the End demand such from an Autarch’s closest attendants. You will be required to respect his title as Revenant-King, address him with formal adherence to his station, and reinforce his authority without making yourselves less than who you are. You may observe all of those needs without diminishing yourselves.”

Kate nodded at the explanation and looked at their friends, who all seemed to understand as well.

“So it’s literally just a royal court,” Corva said thoughtfully. “We could be his ministers or officials or whatever, but the King is still the King.”

“Guild Hierarchy,” Nate offered, nodding along at the thought process. “It’s no different than saying ‘Guild Master’ or ‘Sir’ instead of using the name, except in this case it would be ‘Your Majesty’ or something,” he postulated while glancing at Mortarius, “right?”

“As a comparison of hierarchy, that suffices, but you should not conflate the authoritative element—a Guild Master and an Autarch are as different as a merchant and an Emperor,” Mortarius said with qualifying approval. “In private, you may call him a dimwitted simpleton if you wish—I certainly enjoy it, but what is private cannot be what is public.”

Romulus gave Mortarius a look of profound betrayal, but Kate saw their friends brightening immediately while the Autarch slouched on his throne in resignation.

“We can live with that,” Maya said immediately.

“Absolutely,” Kate agreed with a laugh.

“I’m in,” Corva added gleefully.

“Alright, Mortarius,” Nate said finally while smiling at Romulus. “Educate us, please.”

The specter smiled at the request, the first full smile since they entered the throne room.

“Gladly.”
