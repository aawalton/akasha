Yes. Mia thought, watching the System window slowly fade from view. Those rewards were nice, the health potions could certainly save lives later down the line, but she couldn’t help the feeling that it was all a bit-

“Well, that’s underwhelming as hell for how tough that tree-guy was,” Lina grumbled, giving voice to Mia’s thoughts.

“I suppose now we know just how much that 500% quality and quantity boost we get for rewards when we actually destroy Rifts means,” Mia mused aloud. “At least we can farm this thing. Even without the crystal shards, if this Rift consistently drops health potions, it’s going to be a goldmine … did any of you get Natural Treasures?”

“I have,” Helene said, and Anna was the only one to nod along while the others shook their heads.

“So those can drop,” Mia muttered.

“Why, what did you get?” Camie asked curiously.

“A hairclip that fends off mundane ailments,” Mia said. “Plus a pair of Runic Lexicons for Illusion magic, Mana Gems and a Greater Healing Potion.”

“Mostly the same, except I got Runic Lexicons on Blood magic instead of Illusion, and a pair of enchanted boots instead of the hairclip,” Camie said in her usual soft tone that was barely above a whisper.

“Enchanted boots?” Mia perked up.

“Supposedly my feet won’t hurt no matter how much I walk in them … which is a rather pointless effect for a vampire,” Camie said. Yeah, she could heal her sore feet whenever she wanted or turn off the nerves in her legs on command. She clearly wasn’t the target audience for that type of enchantment.  “I suppose we’ll see if anyone needs it.”

“That would be a useful effect to have on the boots assigned to soldiers and regular militia,” Brent mused. “If no one else feels a pressing need to have it, and it wouldn’t be too much trouble, I’d like to have the enchanters study it to see whether they can replicate the effect.”

“Sure.” Camie shrugged, and that was that. They continued chatting as they made their way through the Gate, which let Mia figure out what the others got from the Rift.

Brent received scrolls detailing some neat Ki Techniques, and a bottle of magical deodorant that eliminated the smell of sweat. Helene got her Natural Treasures, a pair of raspberry-looking things with bright white colour and the same hairclip Mia had; then there was Lina, who received books on Air Elemental manipulation and Air magic. Anna didn’t offer up her own answer, so Mia left the girl alone.

Mia still wasn’t sure how to feel about her, but Anna had been acting as the perfect tank throughout the delve, so she deserved some respect. But Mia knew an introvert who didn't want anyone to talk to her when she saw one, so she left the girl be. Mia would be the last person who tried to terrorise an antisocial introvert by trying to ‘get them out of their shell’ or whatnot. She’d been on the receiving end of that from well-meaning and extremely annoying extroverts enough times to know those efforts would be entirely unwelcome. Maybe if Anna showed signs of actually wanting to socialise, Mia would suck it up and make an effort to reach out.

Just imagining how awkward that first conversation would be made her want to grimace and scrap the idea wholesale. Alas, if Anna was to be a more permanent member of the party, it was probably inevitable. Maybe.

“Well, no one seems to be dying, so I guess the delve went well enough?” Gabe asked once they were through the Gate, trying to look like he hadn’t spent the last few hours pacing and fidgeting. He mostly succeeded, but Mia could see the signs on his face.

“Yep,” Mia said, deciding not to mention how she got punched by the Guardian. That had been so damned sloppy of her.

She’d sent her White Guardian out to guard against the golems, and she’d failed to pull back to recharge her Ward after it soaked up a Light Bolt. The result was that it shattered when the treant’s fist crashed into her; she had only had time to consider Spirit Blinking, but Sparkle had been dancing around four golems at the time and teleporting there with a depleted Ward would have been suicide. Then the fist hit before she could activate the pre-cast Arcane Shield spell. All it took to activate pre-cast spells was a single flick of her mind and positioning her hand, but with a fist the size of a trash bin flying towards her as fast as a loosened arrow, she didn’t have the time. Not with the moments she’d wasted checking where her Spirit Blink would have taken her.

“Mia nearly got pasted,” Lina said cheerfully, and Mia froze, slowly turning a betrayed, vengeful glare at the treasonous blonde.

“I was fine, Anna’s Aura Skill saved me, it was just some bruising,” Mia said with feigned calmness, trying to forestall a panicked round of fussing from her brother. To tell the truth, though, it would be a blatant lie to say she hadn’t been scared shitless when she had felt the Ward of Protection shatter. It was probably her rising Will stat she had to thank for not passing out from the shock.

“It did?” Gabe asked, turning towards the small Shifter girl who’d perked up at the roundabout praise. Under Gabe’s attention, whatever confidence she’d built up withered like a snowflake in Hell. “Well, thank you. I prefer my sister un-pasted, even if she’s more annoying this way.”

“Hey!” Mia said, plastering a mock-offended look on her face. She didn’t really feel like it, but this little bit of theatre would probably help her stupid, fussy brother relax a bit. Those five years he’d spent away clearly had some rather severe effects on his mental health that would have long-term consequences. Just coming back home would hardly be enough to cure all of them, even if it probably soothed his anxiety and evened out the sharp edges.

Over the next four days, they went back to the Crystal Forest Rift each day and cleared it once, stacking up a pile of crystalline shards that could fill several rooms by now with their sheer volume. Light Elemental Mana Gems turned out to be more refined versions of the crystals the golems and treants dropped, and health potions were always welcome, so it was hardly a waste of their time.

The random Common items were a bit hit or miss, always being some sort of wearable item that had minor utility enchantments. Mia had a brooch that would keep her cool in the summer heat, a pair of magically warmed compression socks and a straw hat that had the effects of polarised sunglasses. That last one would go unused, since her Soulbound Witch Hat was perfectly capable of casting a shade over her eyes if she needed it with its wide brim. Unfortunately, only the first run gave her Runic Lexicons, and all subsequent ones only rewarded her with edible Natural Treasures.

Those would have to wait. She was at 118/120 in her Gained Attributes after eating that Golden Apple and still had 3 unassigned Free Attribute points to play with. Her next Level Up was close; she could feel it. She had thought today’s Guardian fight would do it, but it just barely fell short.

Mia was lounging on a couch, half hanging off it after having spent the last two hours squirming around, trying to find a comfortable position to read in. How that ended up with the book on the floor, her head hanging off the edge of the couch and her feet up on the backrest, she’d never know. It was comfortable though, for now.

Illusions were a fascinating subject, and a pretty useful part of Arcanism that Mia was somewhat regretting not having learned sooner. She now had a Runic Lexicon for both Novice and Junior Illusions, but she was only just now reading through them to see whether any spell was worth learning and discarding a spell from her Spell Book.

Minor Visual Illusion, Minor Auditory Illusion, Flashbang and Disguise Self were the available Novice spells. The first could create an illusory replica of an object, though how convincing it turned out to be depended on the caster’s visualisation, memory and mana control. The next could mimic any sounds the caster could remember, ranging from a whisper to a scream. Flashbang was rather simple; it released a blast of blinding light and deafening sound in a cone from the caster’s hand. Disguise Self was likewise rather self-evident in its function, though it was apparently surprisingly effective since the caster could spend hours altering it after casting to refine the image until it was convincing enough.

The Junior Lexicon was a goldmine in contrast to the neat, but mostly niche spells. Invisibility did as the name implied, though it couldn’t fool magical senses or hearing, so it would be entirely useless against someone like Mia or Camie, for example. Blur, a spell to distort the caster’s image to always appear in different spots in a small radius. Then Silence, which was probably the Illusion version of that barrier spell Gabe had used to muffle any noise they made while walking through the woods.

Of course, the Junior grade Lexicon also held upgrades for the previous Novice spells. Minor Visual Illusion could, for example, replicate the image of an object with 15 cubic metres of volume with the upgrade, instead of the single 1 cubic metre it could replicate by default. It was still just an unmoving visual illusion that people could walk right through, but its use cases expanded drastically with the upgrade, and that was just one example of a possible upgrade among nearly a dozen.

Mia shifted again, her head and shoulders now resting on the carpet while the rest of her body remained up on the couch. Flexibility was such a nice stat; it made her feel so free in her own body, and she found herself wondering whether this was what it felt like to be a cat. Her spine and joints could bend in ways that sometimes made her feel like she had no bones at all, as if her entire body were just liquid that flowed into shape. It obviously wasn't the case, but it certainly felt like it.

Holding the book up above her face, Mia stared at the diagrams describing a viable version of the Blur spell. It was fascinating, so full of new runes, novel concepts, paradigms, symbolism and syntax. Illusionism had an altogether different flavour to it than her other spells.

There was significant overlap between Conjuration, Abjuration and her more simple Bolt-type spells, but there was almost none with Illusion. All of them, at least in part, conjured solid constructs made of arcane energy, either to form them into shields, to throw at enemies in the shape of bolts or to fashion them into vessels for summoned creatures. Even Warding, more often than not, shared those features, so there were familiar portions in nearly any new spell Mia had learned so far.

Figuring out how a school of arcanism that had no familiar aspects in it functioned was just so-

The book slipped from Mia’s tired fingers, and her eyes only had a moment to widen before the hefty lexicon fell on her face. Which was, of course, when her mind registered the fact that someone was approaching the door. Letting out a soft, pitiful groan, Mia scrambled to her feet while doing her best to keep the book safe despite its violent crimes against her face.

Her nose ached. Did the stupid book have to hit her right in the nose!?

Flopping back onto the couch, Mia took up a more dignified reading position with the book in her lap and her spine approaching something that might have been straight if you squinted. Her ears twitched, and she finally identified the soft but measured footfalls that could only belong to Carmilla. There was a characteristic way the vampiress walked, always with soft steps but never without a predatory grace that oozed confidence despite the girl herself rarely feeling the emotion.

The door opened, and Mia glanced up from her book with a small smile, doing her best to ignore the fact that Camie had absolutely heard her make a fool of herself just now.

The door opened, and Camie walked in- or shuffled in, more like- one arm behind her back as if to shield something with her body. Mia raised an eyebrow, amusement dancing in her eyes as she watched her girlfriend’s eyes dart around, refusing to meet her gaze. God, the silly vampire looked more awkward than after their first kiss.

Mia gently closed the book in her lap and set it aside; whatever this turned out to be, it clearly deserved her full and undivided attention. Camie being all awkward and precious was a sight to savour and treasure. She waited, patiently and with a smile she tried to rein in but couldn’t. The best she could do was keep it from morphing into a full-on grin.

“H-hi,” Camie said after a few seconds, her eyes flickering to meet Mia’s gaze, then she suddenly found the carpet under her feet immensely fascinating. “I- uhm-“

The words seemed to catch in her throat, and Mia saw the moment when annoyance overtook the embarrassment. A handful of steps with those long legs of hers carried Camie across the room until she stood before Mia and, without speaking, almost aggressively, she pulled the hand she was hiding behind her back in front, presenting what it held.

Mia stared at it, blinking slowly and owlishly for a second or two … and then the most radiant smile bloomed across her face. It was a bouquet of flowers, fresh ones and very likely gathered by the adorably awkward redhead in front of her. They were pretty, but it was the effort that had clearly gone into the bouquet that truly warmed her heart.

Camie finally dared to glance up at Mia’s face, nervously measuring her reaction and only relaxing ever so slightly when she took in the brilliant smile on her face.

“Thank you,” Mia said, reaching for the flowers and Camie all but dropped the bouquet in her haste to hand it over. She sniffed it, letting the fresh, fragrant scent of wildflowers fill her nostrils.

“ … you like them?” Camie asked warily. “I wasn’t sure- I mean, it’s just wildflowers, but the stores don’t sell flowers anym-“

“Of course I like ‘em,” Mia said, bouncing to her feet and standing on the tips of her toes to place a chaste kiss on Camie’s cheek, though if the edges of their lips touched, that was just a coincidence. “You just wait here; I’m going to find a vase and some water, then kiss you until you can’t think straight. Be right back!”

Camie just stood there, stupefied as Mia skipped away; a crimson flush crept up her cheeks, and she finally relaxed. Her shoulders slumped in relief, and her nervous, halting expression gave way to a dopey grin. When Mia glanced back over her shoulder, she saw Camie preen like a triumphant general after a victorious battle. Adorable.

A vase was found in short order, and Mia quickly emptied one of her water canteens into it. Sure, she had that water rock that could gather the liquid infinitely, but who knew when you needed litres of water right now and not after the dozens of minutes it sometimes took for the rock to condense any significant amount.

Placing the vase down at the small table in their shared bedroom, Mia skipped back into the apartment’s living room and found Camie sitting on the couch, that dopey smile having faded away in favour of another nervously expectant look. Before her own nerves could catch up with her, Mia plopped herself down in Camie’s lap, her back facing the redhead for now, and the girl’s arms went around her waist almost without thinking. Squirming around a little had her sitting sideways, enough to throw her arms around Camie’s neck and look into her eyes while working up the courage to deliver on her promises.

Which is, of course, when the System decided to poke her right in the brain with an exceptionally persistent notification. Mia groaned, then glanced at the small blinking icon that appeared in the corner of her vision, which of course replaced the tantalising view of Camie’s face up close with a boring System window.

To gaze into the Orb is to glimpse the Arcane in its purest form.

Its depths shift endlessly, revealing fleeting patterns, forgotten symbols, and truths that seem to lie just beyond the edge of comprehension. Those who ponder an arcane mystery while peering into the Orb often find inspiration where none existed before—a sudden insight, a missing connection, or the faint outline of a solution.

The Orb may also serve as a potent focus for Divination magic.

The Orb's effects are strongest in those deeply Attuned to the Arcane Element, though Spirit, Mind, and Sensitivity appear to influence its potency as well.

Numerous arcanists have reported that the Orb greatly accelerated their spellcrafting, whether refining existing spell circles or attempting to create entirely new ones. More accomplished practitioners have described seeing unfamiliar runes emerge within its depths—symbols they were able to reproduce and incorporate into their work. In several recorded cases, these runes proved to be the final pieces needed to perfect an unfinished spell circle, or even to bring an entirely new one into existence.

Whether the Orb reveals knowledge that already exists, or merely inspires the mind to discover it, remains a matter of considerable debate.

“Huh, that’s a nice Orb,” Mia said, finding herself fully distracted by the shiny new thing in front of her. With a thought, she accepted her Rewards, and while most of them materialised on the unoccupied half of the couch, the books stacked in neat piles and the more fragile natural treasures and potions in storage boxes, the Orb popped into existence in her grasp.

Leaning back, she held it in both hands, cradling the crystal sphere that was roughly twice the width of an apple. She stared at it, watching as the opaque milky whiteness of the orb gave way to ever-shifting colours and hues towards the core. No matter which way she turned it, there was always an indistinct ring of milky whiteness at the edges as thick as her thumb that gave way to the magical kaleidoscope only in gradients. Only the inner core that was no larger than Mia’s closed fist was entirely bereft of the milky whiteness’ shroud.

Mia couldn’t help the giggle that escaped her when it occurred to her that the System was giving her all the tools a proper wizard or witch needed, at least according to fantasy novels and such. She had a neat wide-brimmed hat, a wand, a Spell Tome that was practically a grimoire and now an Orb she could ponder to discover arcane mysteries or use to spy on the poor commoners.

“All I need is a proper wizard tower, maybe a staff too,” Mia muttered, earning an amused but curious look from Camie. “I mean, I have a growing collection of wizard stuff: a hat, the wand, a spell tome, and now an orb.”

“Why a staff if you have a wand?” Camie asked.

“I mean, staves are apparently better for when you need to hit hard; they can take larger miscast backlashes than wands, and they are also large enough to have space for actual potency amplification enchantments,” Mia said, recalling what she read about staves in one of her older System books. “Like, a staff can actually make spells you cast with them stronger, and they can also act as anchors for rituals or spells if they have the proper enchantments. I like wands, but they are the pistol equivalent of magical foci when I could have a rocket launcher. Rings can also be made into magical foci, but those are kinda garbage, like miniature spy guns hidden in your sleeve if we want to stick to the firearm metaphor.”

“Neat,” Camie said, and Mia recognised the tone of someone who knew all that but decided to indulge her anyway. The silly vampire liked to listen to her ramble about just about anything. “Getting you a wizard tower is going to be a challenge; would a witch’s hut in some bog be a good replacement?”

“Nah, bog witches are lame, I’m not eating children either, a tower though,” Mia muttered. She knew for a fact that ‘mage towers’ were a thing and that many a mage’s guild in the Mystic Realm was headquartered in one. There was something about the circular foundation and the symbolism in reaching for the sky that made towers hold enchantments woven into them super well. After all, the foundation was the perfect shape for a massive ritual circle, be it a circle, a hexagon or an octagon, depending on the type of protections they wanted to weave into the tower. Apparently, the richer guilds enchanted every single brick one by one and tied them to the larger circle in the tower’s base.

“No flying broom then?” Camie asked, and Mia pulled her thoughts back to the present. This wasn’t the time to daydream about her eventual tower — it was inevitable; she would have a tower with her name written on it, even if it took centuries to get it.

“I’m more of a flying carpet kind of girl,” Mia said with some amusement. “Though I’d much rather like to learn how to fly under my own power. I’m pretty sure there is a spell for that I could learn that isn’t as convoluted as Gabe’s flying barrier solution.”

Maybe those Kinetomancy lexicons she had would hold the secret? Even if they wouldn’t provide her with a solution at Junior grade, maybe there would be a simple Fly spell in the next grade she could get at Rank 2? That was worth thinking about, and also some research. Kinetomancy wasn’t a popular aspect of arcanism from what she’d learned, but it had some niche uses that made it a mainstay of any Arcane Mage who had enough spell slots to keep some utility spells in their Spell Tome.

With a free hand, Mia pushed everything she had, including the orb, the books and the edible natural treasures into her spatial bag, then put the bag on the coffee table. That freed up the entire couch, just in case the space was … needed.

Mia’s gem-like eyes flickered in the light like faceted gems that shifted colours between sky blue and deep sapphire depending on how one looked at them. Her gaze held Camie’s eyes with an unspoken promise, and unspoken tension mounted between them. Blue eyes flickered away first, lingering on a pair of full, pouty lips before returning to those ruby eyes above with a newfound playful glimmer in them.

The two girls stayed still for a heartbeat, then the tension broke as they moved as one, lips meeting in a passionate embrace as Mia did her best to deliver on her earlier promise.
