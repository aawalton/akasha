Vetica

Vetica’s dagger hummed under the gentle blue glow of her blue hex blade. It was too dangerous to use her unique hex here. If she lost control, it could put Charlie and her friends at risk, not to mention jeopardizing the chance to save Mary once and for all. No, Merlin had played his part and won his trial. She wouldn’t throw a wrench into their efforts now, not after working so hard just to locate Mary.

Sir Thespin stared at her for a few more moments before sighing. “Are you sure about this? There is still time to set our blades aside. We can talk and—”

“Silence!” Vetica shot forward with speed that surprised Thespin. She aimed a swipe of her blade at his throat, but the Hex Knight had well-honed instincts. He stepped back, just out of her range, and eyed her dagger with a nod.

“Your movements are effective. Not surprising for a Rawlin,” he commented.

Vetica sneered and shot forward once more. This time Thespin drew his sword.

Clang.

Clang.

Clang.

She went purely on the offensive, delivering blow after blow in an attempt to break through his defenses. Thespin blocked her attacks almost effortlessly. He moved his rapier uncannily, as if the wind itself pushed it into place each time to block her strikes. Each time their metallic weapons met, they clanged together before pulling apart. As Vetica’s frustration grew, Thespin’s smile deepened.

She feigned an opening, which Thespin frustratingly didn’t take. Then feinted and pivoted to strike him elsewhere. Thespin’s eyes moved in time to see her sneak attack, but his blade did not. Her dagger found purchase, slipping inside. But it wasn’t Thespin’s flesh she’d cut through.

It was that frustrating barrier of wind again. She grunted in annoyance and pulled her blade back, stepping away.

Thespin looked down and nodded his approval. He placed a hand where her dagger had struck and waved it back and forth. It seemed as if he were repairing something. When he was finished, he looked back up and smiled at her. “Don’t stop on my account. Let’s continue, my love.”

Vetica scowled. “Don’t you dare mock me. Treating our duel as some amusement!”

Thespin looked genuinely surprised. “I…I would never. Don’t you feel the beauty of this moment? The give and take of our feet as we spar? Is it not like a beautiful dance between destined lovers? Does each forceful impact between our blades not remind you of a passionate kiss in the moonlight?”

Her eye twitched. She’d seen his technique when he’d first ‘saved’ them from fleeing the komodon scalers and invited them into the fort as they’d planned. Thespin was a powerful hex knight, and she was using a base hex to fight him. No, was that really her problem? This was the hex she was most familiar with; she’d crossed blades with many powerful foes using its power in the past. It wasn’t this hex that was bothering her.

It was the other one.

Her mind kept drifting to the forest where she’d lost herself during combat. When she’d unlocked the unique hex that her father sought, Astral Hex Assassination.

That power had utterly consumed her mind, and while it had defeated the remnants of the bear hybrids, it had also threatened Charlie, Merlin, and the scalers fighting alongside them. She’d unlocked a powerful new hex only to immediately allow it to take control and threaten her friends. She hadn’t fought since then. Even now, after days and days had passed, the events still lingered in her mind.

The guilt, the shame, the anxiety.

What if it happened again? What if she lost control and hurt the people she cared about?

“Vetica…something is bothering you. That much is clear to me.” Thespin took a step forward. “I can ease your burden. I understand you’ve chosen to fight, and I’ll respect that as a warrior. Forgive me if it seems I haven’t so far. But if you need a moment to speak your mind, I am here.”

Vetica looked up. How insulting it was to—she paused when she saw his blade pointed towards her.

Thespin looked down at it. “I know it is not words you wish to exchange. Both of us are warriors, that much is clear to me now. So, I’ll offer you a different form of therapy. The form that we find only while crossing blades with a powerful opponent. I sense hesitation in your heart! While it’s all I want to save you and take you away from here, I understand there is a lot I don’t know. So tell me! Cut me with your blade and imbue the message directly into my blood! Leave your marks on my body so I can understand your anguish!”

Vetica sighed. This man really was strange. Perhaps strange was too kind of word even. But in a way, he was right. Her anxiety was born of doubt. She questioned whether she would lose control again. If she used that hex to win this fight, would she risk losing everything? Merlin was in the previous room, and Charlie had gone on ahead. If she released that deadly cloud by accident, it could threaten everyone in the tower, Mary included.

That wasn’t an option. But at the same time, there would come a day when she needed to use it. They would only face more and more powerful foes in the future. If she didn’t get used to it by then, she risked holding Charlie back.

She focused on the power, hesitantly calling it forward. She would master it. Vetica would master that power here and now. “Is this room secure?” she asked.

Thespin eyed her for a moment before smiling. “It is. This tower is a fortress in itself. There are certain security measures in place to ensure fights from the trials don’t spill over into other portions of the tower, or even outside. Our only focus in this room need be one another.”

Vetica nodded. “In that case, sorry if this kills you.” And she meant it. Thespin was a fool, but it wasn’t her intent to kill him. Still, if the tower really was capable of containing collateral damage, then it was too good an opportunity to pass up.

She needed to release her hex.

She held up a hand and chanted. “Astral Hex Assassination.”

A cloak of void and bright stars covered her dagger at once.

This was her power. She was done living in fear of it.

Her body felt lighter than ever before, as if gravity itself had eased its hold on her. She kicked off the ground and closed the distance between her and Thespin in less than the blink of an eye. Thespin’s smile hardly had time to fade from his face as she disappeared from in front of him. It was still turning into a frown as she brought her boot crashing down on his face. She shattered the barrier of wind around him and sent him crashing into one of the tower’s walls. The frames of paintings exploded at the impact. Some were torn in half; others were reduced to mere splinters. Thespin shook his head, collecting himself from the blow.

“Very good.” He straightened his hat and ran two fingers along his mustache to ensure it was unharmed. “Impressive.” Thespin cleared his throat and drew his rapier. “I won’t underestimate your…communication skills again,” he promised. He suddenly jumped, pressed the heels of both shoes against the wall, and shot toward her. Thespin drove the tip of his rapier directly towards Vetica’s core. She swatted it away in the same way she might a child’s hand from a jar of sweets before dinner. Then drove a punch into his face. He dodged partially, reducing the force of the blow and narrowly avoiding serious injury. The man landed just behind her, and spun back to attack.

Clang. Clang. Clang.

Rapier versus dagger. Her speed was such that she could more than make up for the added range his weapon gave him. With each moment, their battle became more intense. Thespin manipulated the surrounding wind to push his body to new limits. Vetica’s astral hex matched and exceeded each improvement Thespin painfully forced upon himself.

In a battle of the elements, a simple wind just didn’t match up to the power of the stars.

Thespin gave no openings; he was a master swordsman and a veteran fighter. But Vetica didn’t need an opening. Under the effect of her hex, she simply created them.

“Void Assassination.”

She disappeared. It felt as if she stepped out of reality and into a dark plane. A void of black enveloped her, and she watched through a glassy veil that glimmered as brightly as a star. Thespin whirled around in search of her. “Vetica?” he called out, his voice equal parts alarm for himself and worry for her. “Are you there?”

So this was how she’d moved quicker than even she could comprehend in the fight against the sin bears in the forest. She took careful aim and exited the portal.

It felt as if she were being propelled through space itself as she broke through the veil. In truth, she likely could’ve taken Thespin’s head off. But despite the gnawing urge pouring from her hex to kill, that wasn’t her goal. Instead, she aimed for his blade.

His rapier shattered into a hundred pieces as she struck it. She whirled around afterwards and redirected her dagger to his throat. “Surrender,” she said, leaning close and staring at the side of his face.

Thespin smiled. “You’re so close I can feel your breath.” He turned and stared her in the eye. “Can’t you feel the chemistry between us?”

She scoffed. “You’re delusional.”

“I’m a romantic.” His eyes flickered downward.

She followed his gaze and saw a palm aimed toward her core. Her eyes opened.

“Sorry about this,” Thespin said, just as a blast of wind sent her flying backwards.

She caught herself on the wall, pressing her feet against it. Instinctively she stabbed her dagger forward, and it tore into a veil just like the one she’d used to cross back into this reality. It stuck and found purchase. It was as close to floating as Vetica to get. Her hand resting on a dagger stabbing into the air itself. Her feet perched on the flat wall behind her. This hex was capable of so much more than she’d realized.

“Kill him—”

She focused and silenced the voice inside.

Nope. You’re in my body. Don’t speak unless spoken to.

Charlie had faith in her. He had her back, and he’d shown her it was possible to conquer even the most unbelievable of powers. If a baby could overcome the powers of a dungeon, Vetica could overcome this curse of a family inheritance. Even if she’d lost her family name, she was still an assassin. She’d still inherited a coveted, unique hex that even her father believed could upend nations. Vetica was done being a tool or a weapon for her family. She was an assassin, but not one for hire. No, she’d found new people to wield this blade of hers for. She’d found a family of her own that had her back and looked out for her. The only people she’d kill for was them.

Thespin was a problem to be dealt with, that much was true. But the real target of hers from here on out was clear.

Cendra and his allies, and anyone who got in the way of her goals. Even Merlin had won his fight. So she didn’t care how strong Thespin was. She would be the victor of round two.

She shot forward. Thespin held out a hand and the floating shards of his blade shot towards her. She slashed outward with her dagger, cutting open a slit in reality in front of her. The shards flew harmlessly inside.

Thespin adjusted quickly. He condensed wind in his arm and created a rapier of pure elemental magic. She struck at him in passing. He defended against her attack. Vetica flipped in the air and landed on the far side of the wall. Without wasting a second, she kicked off and shot towards him again.

Parry.

Parry.

Parry.

She didn’t stop. She moved quicker, shooting past, attempting to cut him, landing on the opposite wall, and repeating it all. Over and over, her speed and power picked up by the second. She felt the urge to release the black cloud that poisoned all who came into contact with it. She bent the hex to her will, silencing it from demanding Thespin’s death.

Over and over she shot past him like a speeding arrow until finally she was too much for him. She cut him across the chest, just deep enough to end the fight.

Thespin cried out and fell to the ground. Blood dripped from his wound. A lot of it. He’d live if he tended to the injury, which was her intention. But if he exerted himself more, his life would be in actual danger.

“That was a good one,” he admitted. He tried to push himself off the ground but grimaced in pain.

“Admit defeat, Thespin. I don’t have time for this. You’ll die if you try to keep fighting.”

Thespin raised his head to look up at her. “I’d die if I believed I’d failed you here and now. I’d die if I failed to see our conversation through.” He pressed a hand against his knee. “I’ve got to go a little longer. I can see in your eyes that you have more to say. Perhaps more even to learn about yourself.” He coughed, and blood spurted from his wound. “Let us speak one more time,” he forced the words out through gritted teeth. Thespin held a hand out in the air.

“Emerald Hex Elemental.” Wind surrounded him like a coat of armor and pressed against his wound. He flinched at the sensation, but at the very least it seemed to stem the flow of blood. “Perhaps I was too eager to get you to unleash that power of yours. But it is a majestic thing to see. Now coat your tongue in the ink of power and let me hear the words of your heart.”

Vetica sighed. “Thespin...”

He kicked off the ground and shot upward into the air. He hovered there, standing on two discs of wind toward the roof of the room. “I’ve learned a lot about you from this fight of ours. But now it’s time to bear it all. Show me who you are, Vetica. Let me see you clearly. Are you a pawn on a board of evil men, or are you in control of the path you walk? Show me the latter, and I’ll admit defeat.”

Vetica couldn’t help but laugh. “All this, to make sure an absolute stranger isn’t being taken advantage of?” she asked.

Thespin nodded. “A stranger whom I just so happen to find myself extremely enamored with. Though, admittedly, I would come to the aid of any stranger. I’m a knight after all.”

“Yeah, well, the knights we’ve encountered in the past have been far from friendly as far as I’m concerned.”

Thespin smiled. “Show me. The boy is waiting. If your cause is just, then show it to me. Speak with your blade and I will believe you.”

“Very well.”

She released the mist. It flooded the lower half of the room and surrounded her.

Thespin’s eyes widened at the sight.

As it overtook her face, she lost her sight, but she knew where Thespin was floating. The mist shot towards him, and the moment it hit him, it would seal his fate. But Vetica was in control now. She was in a state she’d almost forgotten herself capable of. On the battlefield, she was at home. She’d grown up in a sea of blood and death. She was a Rawlin, an assassin through and through. This hex wasn’t a curse, nor even some miraculous power. It was a tool. Just another tool in her arsenal: a lock pick, rope, a dagger. This hex was just another tool. And she would demand nothing less than absolute obedience.

Vetica kicked off the ground, utilizing the smoke to obscure Thespin’s sight of her like a smoke bomb. She shot up under the effects of her hex’s enhanced speed and strength, towards her target. She kicked off the left side wall and flew over to the right. Again and again, she scaled the wall one kick at a time on the way to its ceiling where Thespin was waiting. He held his rapier of compressed wind at the ready and prepared to face her once she emerged from the smoke.

She kicked off one last time and shot towards him. The smoke in the room stopped just shy of touching Thespin and shot back toward her body as her hex reabsorbed it under her command.

“Void Veil,” she whispered, shooting out from the last remnants of the smoke. She burst forward and plunged her dagger into Thespin’s chest. It sank in all the way to the crossguard

She’d flown too quickly for him to react, bypassing his defenses and penetrating his wind armor.

She stood on his wind disc, leaned close once more, and stared into his eyes. “Have you heard me now, Thespin?” she asked.

The hex knight looked down at the dagger penetrating his skin. Or rather, penetrating a small tear in reality just in front of his skin. She’d stabbed the veil, plunging her dagger into another plane rather than Thespin’s body. It only looked as if she’d stabbed him.

His mouth fell open as he processed the gravity of what she’d done. He smiled. “No servant of evil would spare a knight such as I.”

She shook her head. “Nope. Especially not one as annoying as you,” she said.

Thespin chuckled. “The third round won’t be easy, you know? Gregory is a man of conviction.”

Vetica nodded. “The thing about Charlie is he always wins when he needs to. We’re going to win.”

“I wish you luck,” Thespin said. His eyes fell closed, and the wind beneath them faded. Both of them fell toward the ground. She caught Thespin’s limp body and braced herself for the impact. But her enhanced body met the long fall gracefully. She landed and placed the unconscious knight on the ground. She set to work, cutting fabric away from his sleeve to create a bandage for the open wound on his chest. He’d live. The absolute fool of a knight would live. She turned her attention toward the door leading higher into the tower and smiled.

“Go get her, Charlie. She’s waiting for you.” Vetica smiled as the match was officially called.

“The Challenger Wins.”
