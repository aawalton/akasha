Oswald could barely believe he made it out of the cave. He ran like he never ran before, never stopping until he had somehow left the mouth of the cave.

After that, he allowed himself only a brief stop to catch his breath and put his eyepatch back on. Because there was still a long way to go before he could get some proper sleep at the inn.

The sharp crunch of gravel underfoot mixed with the thud of Oswald’s boots as he limped back towards town. The fabric of his trousers clung to one leg where the gash refused to clot, the torn threads sticky with grime and dried red.

His right hand hung limp at his side, bruised and swollen, while the other stayed clutched to his ribs, fingers trembling slightly. His cloak was tattered, crusted with dust and something darker. A flap of it dragged behind him, catching on loose stones that lined the roadside. Just keep movin'. Don’t stop. Just... walk.

The gates of Volstarn creaked in the wind, their hinges groaning like the town itself was too tired to welcome anyone. No guards stood watch.

He didn’t really care who was watching him though. Gotta get to the Hollow Oak.

Slowly but surely, Oswald made his way to the inn. He reached out with his good hand, missed the handle, then caught it on the second try. The door creaked open.

Warmth met him. Light, then muffled voices.

And then the floor. Oswald hit it hard, the impact sending a dull throb through his side. He didn’t get up. Didn’t even try. Made it…

***

Something wet dragged across Oswald’s cheek. He flinched.

Not from pain, though there was plenty of that to go around, but from the strange ticklish feeling nudging at his face. His brow twitched, eyes squinting open against the light filtering through thin curtains.

A rough tongue grazed his chin. That who I think it is?

His vision swam for a second, blurry and unfocused. Then it cleared just enough to reveal a pair of golden eyes staring back at him, unblinking and far too close. “Vivi...?”

The black cat purred louder in response, tail flicking happily as she continued licking at his cheek. She climbed onto his chest with no regard for his bruises, curling into a small loaf right over his sternum.

A dull ache pressed behind Oswald’s eyes, throbbing in time with the steady pulse in his neck. Sweat clung to his brow, dried to a sticky sheen.

The blanket draped over him felt heavy and damp. Beneath it, strips of linen bound his chest and shoulders, dotted with red where the dressing had soaked through.

His fingers twitched at his side, brushing over the bandages wrapping his ribs. Did Gilbert do all this?

Oswald shifted, and pain shot through his back. A sharp hiss broke from between his teeth as he clenched his jaw, muscles locking on instinct. The weight on his chest shifted with him.

Vivi, who had been curled up just beneath his collarbone, let out a quiet chirp of protest and gracefully climbed down. She stepped lightly across his bandaged side and settled near his legs before curling around her body.

He pushed himself upright with a grunt, trembling slightly under the strain. Every movement tugged at his injuries, but he managed to slump back against the pillow. I’m still in one piece, at least… kinda.

Regardless, his attention drifted to the familiar black shape now nestled by his ankles. “Ain’t you supposed to be downstairs with Gilbert? Y’know he don’t like animals in the guest rooms.”

“That is correct,” came a voice.

His head turned slowly. Ah, figured it was her.

The porcelain cup in Selene’s hands caught the light first. Her fingers rested with poised stillness around the rim, unmoving even as faint curls of steam spiraled into the air. The steam caught thin shafts of sunlight leaking through the shutters, casting soft glows along her knuckles.

She sat in a plain wooden chair positioned just beside the window. One leg crossed neatly over the other.

Oswald blinked. Once. Then again. The dryness in his throat scratched raw every time he swallowed, but he rasped out, “Right... I mean, thanks. But... why?”

Selene brought the teacup to her lips and took a sip before answering. “Because Vivi insisted on entering your room. So, I told Gilbert I would take responsibility if any of the furniture were destroyed.”

Oswald glanced down. Vivi was still sleeping on top of his legs. At the sound of Selene’s voice, her tail gave a slow, pleased flick, but she didn’t move. Just blinked, slow and lazy, like she’d decided this was her bed now. She looks so… peaceful.

He let his head sink back into the pillow, exhaling through his nose. “I owe her one, and I owe you, too.”

Porcelain clicked as Selene lifted her teacup and took a quiet sip. The soft clink when she set it back down broke the silence that followed. “It looks like you’ve been through quite a lot."

“That’s one way to put it.”

“I suppose next time I should leave you with a map and a haunted ruin or two. Keep you entertained.”

Oswald gave a short snort. “Don’t forget snacks. I’m not fightin’ monsters on an empty stomach.”

A faint smile tugged at the corners of Selene’s mouth, softening her features for just a moment. But then her gaze dropped slightly, and the smile faded. “Oswald, do you resent me for leaving you?”

The boy stared up at the ceiling for a second, then shrugged against the pillow. “You had stuff to do. I got into my mess ‘cause I wanted to. Nothin’ to blame you for.”

“I had my own task to attend to, but I must admit, I regret leaving you on your own. Given your nature as an Animus Host, attracting unwanted attention was all but inevitable. And from the looks of it, you've endured quite a lot.”

“C’mon, don’t start blamin’ yourself. You didn’t make anything happen, I went lookin’. Nothin’ that happened was on you.”

Selene's eyes dropped briefly to her lap before she reached into the folds of her cloak. “At the very least, you should know what I was doing,” she said, drawing out a small pouch and carefully opening it.

Inside was a single mushroom: fragile, exquisite, and utterly alien. Its cap was as big as a coin, with a rich indigo hue. Delicate silver filaments wove through the surface like veins under translucent skin. The stem, meanwhile, shimmered with a cold luster, as though it had been touched by frost.

She held it between her fingers with great gentleness. The way her grip barely pressed against its surface told him everything he needed to know about its fragility, and how much its worth.

"Each one costs a fortune, even among the right circles. They’re nearly impossible for ordinary people to find. In fact, it took me several days to secure even this."

Oswald leaned forward slightly as he studied the strange thing. "So what’s it for? You gonna make some fancy soup or somethin’?"

“No, I intend to make a potion that can bring someone to the very edge of life and death. A state where the soul becomes unburdened, and hence, where true enlightenment becomes possible.”

“Huh, you’re... actually tellin’ me? I thought you were just gonna throw some cryptic answer at me again and call it a day.”

“Normally I would, yes. But it’s time you started learning more. Especially now that you’ve encountered our true enemies.”

Oswald frowned and looked down at the blanket covering him. “Vampires.”

Selene’s expression shifted in an instant. “Tell me, Oswald, how many have you encountered so far? And where?”

“Just one I think, was hidin’ out in this cavern behind a waterfall. Had to use my Animus just to notice it was there at all."

“Is it still there?”

“The cave's gone. Vampire killed himself and took the whole damn cave with him."

“Self-destruction magic isn't uncommon, but certainly unusual. Most vampires are too selfish to end their own lives. This one must’ve been part of something larger."

“Well, the one I met in the cave was definitely weird. Talked like he was some kinda noble, but he could control those pale things, thralls, or ghouls, whatever the locals call ‘em. Had a bunch of ‘em wanderin’ around like they were guard dogs.”

“Then there were the knights, big things in busted black armor. All of ‘em bled black blood too. Every last one.”

Selene’s expression hardened, and she gave a small, knowing nod. “They were all vampires.”

Oswald blinked. “Even the pale ones?"

"Yes, though... not in the way you’re thinking.”

She reached once more for her teacup but didn’t lift it. Her fingers hovered along the rim as she continued, her voice taking on a careful cadence.

“There is a hierarchy into which each vampire is born, etched into their very blood. Though they spring from humankind, their place is determined by how flawlessly their bodies submit to the curse of vampirism.”

“So... what you're sayin' is, the stronger ones are just better at bein’ vampires? Like, their bodies just... take to it easier?”

“Yes. Because vampirism, at least in part, functions like a disease. For instance, when two people are exposed to the same affliction, there is no guarantee their symptoms will be identical. One may endure little more than a fever, while the other is consumed by it.”

“Alright... so were those pale freaks at the bottom of the vampire ladder?”

“Precisely. Thralls are often regarded as failures. They are born when the curse of vampirism is resisted too forcefully by the host body. As a result, while they retain certain beneficial traits such as heightened strength, speed, and endurance, their affliction comes at a greater cost as their minds deteriorate. What remains is little more than instinct and aggression. They are, for all intents and purposes, beasts wearing the skin of men.”

So that’s why they acted like wild animals. Always moving like starvin' mutts.

“What about those weird knights I fought? I mean, they could talk, sure... just not sure how much was brains and how much was instinct, y’know?”

“Based on your description, they are likely thinbloods. They are a step above the thralls, but not by much. They retain some sense of self, but it’s dulled. They’re usually hesitant to act without instruction. Hence, most thinbloods are used as expendable foot soldiers."

Oswald scratched his chin. “So that guy I ran into deep in the cave, the one bossin’ around all those thralls and knights, he’s gotta be even stronger, yeah?”

“He was likely a pureblood vampire. Unlike their lesser ilk, purebloods generally retain their intelligence. This is because they are more thoroughly attuned to the curse of vampirism."

“But all vampires, regardless of rank, share one flaw. They must consume blood to survive. Animal blood can sustain them for a time, as can monster blood. But pureblood vampires especially must consume human blood in order to live."

Oswald felt a chill crawl along his spine. So even the smart ones are still monsters underneath it all.

“Had I known how deeply entrenched the vampires were in Volstarn, I wouldn't have let you face things alone.”

"It's alright, really. I made my own choices, and it's not like I can always count on you bein' there for me all the time."

Selene paused, her fingers tightening ever so slightly around the arms of the chair. “I suppose what matters most is that you survived, and that is not insignificant. You are stronger now than you were before. More aware."

Oswald let out a tired breath and scratched at the edge of his brow. “Yeah, well... I didn’t survive on my own.”

“What exactly are you implying?”

“It was Zvarah, my Animus Spirit, she stepped in before I was completely surrounded.”

Selene sat forward slightly, her eyes narrowing with interest. “Your Animus Spirit intervened?”

“Yeah. After I fought one of those knights, I was really injured. Got caught off guard and there were more thralls closin’ in. Thought that was it, but then Zvarah showed up and said she’d help me, for a price.”

“Souls, yes?"

“Yeah, let me use up the ones I’d taken in. In exchange, she gave me a new skill that helped me fight off the thralls. Surprised she didn't ask for anything extra to be honest."

“Very interesting."

Oswald looked at her, frowning. "Thing is, I don't understand why she would even do that? I always thought Zvarah didn’t care if I made it out or not. I’m just some kid swingin’ a sword to her, right?”

Selene gave a slow nod, folding her hands in her lap. “Normally, you’d be right. If you died under ordinary circumstances, say, torn apart by a beast or impaled by a bandit then Zvarah would be free. The contract would break, and she would return to the Veil.”

“Makes sense.”

“But if you die to a vampire… then both your soul and Zvarah’s, would be devoured immediately by Baal.”

Oswald blinked. “What’s a Baal?”

“He is the god and sovereign of all vampires. Ordinarily, when a vampire dies, their soul returns to Baal. There is, however, one exception, if a vampire is slain by an Animus Host, their soul is instead ensnared by the Animus, unable to return to their master."

Oswald went quiet for a moment. He frowned, looking toward the window before speaking again. “When I'd slain the thralls, the knights... and even that vampire in the cave, when he blew himself up... I saw their bodies turn into red particles. Like... dust, or ash, but glowing. And then it all just... seeped into my right eye.”

He hesitated, then tapped the eyepatch with a finger. “Any reason why the Animus does that?”

Selene didn’t look surprised. “That is precisely what the Animus was created to do. It exists to fight vampires. Every vampire soul absorbed by it becomes one less for Baal to use in his machinations.”

So it’s not just fightin’ ‘em. It’s takin’ from ‘em. Starvin’ their god.

“This is why the Animus bestows certain abilities, such as vision in complete darkness. Vampires, by their very nature, are creatures of the shadows. Their eyes are adapted to thrive within it. The Animus, in turn, compensates by granting its host the means to perceive and pursue them.” Selene explained.

Oswald huffed a breath through his nose. “Whoever made this thing must’ve really hated vampires. I mean, makin’ a cursed eye with a demon locked inside it, just to take down vampires? That’s... that’s a lot.”

“Vampires are one of humanity’s oldest and deadliest foes. Countless lives have been lost to their kind, entire kingdoms brought to ruin. The creation of the Animus was not an act of hatred, Oswald. It was one of desperation.”

“Were things really that bad before?"

“Worse than you imagine. Vampires originated from Baal himself. It was he who first granted the curse of vampirism to his most devoted followers. They became the first of their kind, monsters born not of nature, but of divine corruption.”

“So... he made ‘em,” Oswald said, trying to piece it all together.

Selene nodded. “Indeed. And because of this origin, vampires cannot reproduce naturally as humans or animals do. To survive and spread, they must turn human beings into more of their kind.”

“Wait, then... that vampire I ran into, the one with all those thralls and knights... was he tryin’ to make more? Just... buildin’ a whole pack of ‘em?”

“That is certainly possible, but turning a human into a vampire is no simple matter. Most will not survive the process intact and become thralls. Only a rare few can fully embrace the curse and become purebloods. Fewer still rise beyond that.”

“There’s vampires stronger than purebloods?”

“There are, to be precise, three tiers that rise above them: nobles, exalted... and at the very pinnacle, the progenitors.”

“Progenitors? Sounds real fancy."

“Originally, that title referred to the first thirteen vampires ever created by Baal. But none of them walk the earth any longer. Whether they perished or fell into eternal slumber, none remain.”

“Then the ones around now... they ain’t the same?” Oswald asked.

“No. The current thirteen progenitors are entirely different individuals, yet each was still chosen directly by Baal. They stand as the embodiment of vampiric supremacy. Nearly every vampire born within the last century owes their existence, whether directly or through distant lineage, to the blood of these few.”

“Does that mean those progenitors are just... goin’ around makin’ more vampires all the time?”

“Some might, but more often, they convert only a handful. Those chosen few go on to create more, and so it spreads, with each generation diluted further from the progenitor’s power. But the connection remains, like blood through a family line. That is how the curse endures.”

Oswald rubbed at his chin, eyes narrowing in thought. If every vampire comes from one of these big shots... then maybe there's a way to tell which one made which. “Is it possible to know which vampire came from which progenitor? Like, is there a way to trace it back?”

Selene didn’t answer straight away. She lifted her teacup once more to take a sip before setting it down again. “If a vampire is closely descended from a progenitor, then there may be clues in their abilities or even the way they behave. But if they’re too far removed, the signs grow faint. In many cases, they’re entirely lost.”

“So basically, no way to tell unless they’re fresh off the tree.”

“Precisely. And such matters are best addressed when the need arises. Until then, it’s unnecessary speculation.”

Oswald leaned his head back with a soft sigh. “Fair enough.”

He shifted under the blanket, careful not to dislodge Vivi. “Do you think there are more vampires in Volstarn? I mean, I found one down in some hidden cavern, so there’s gotta be more out there, right?”

“There may well be, which is why I plan to begin investigating at once." Selene said.

“I can help.”

“You’ve already done more than enough, Oswald. You need rest and let your body recover. I’ll return shortly.”

He raised an eyebrow, smirking faintly. “So what, I’m just supposed to sit here and do nothin’ for a couple hours?”

A flicker of amusement passed over her face. “Not quite. While I investigate, you could try speaking with your Animus Spirit. I imagine you’ve collected a fair number of souls from that cavern, haven’t you?”

“Right, I guess I can do that. But how do I speak with Zvarah again without… y'know."

Selene stood, smoothing down the front of her cloak with a hand. “Close your eyes and focus. Because you have a pact with your Animus Spirit now, it should be much easier for you two to communicate."
