How DARE she.

Did she bother to think about what this would do? What people will say when they hear about this?

It's all Her fault too. And his. All four of them are to blame. I don't know how, but they're all responsible for this. Moving in together, spending time together, being friendly with one another.

She's not family, she's not. She can't be. So how did she manage it? How did they manage that drastic a shift. No alliance or agreement to work together would account for this. And how would that even work, she didn't even know who she was a year ago, she has no standing for her to associate with.

Fay nodded to the Doorkeeper before stepping through into Scattered Creek. It had stopped scaring her after the first few visits, now she felt a renewed need to be polite. She knew there wasn't much it could physically do to her or her family, at least not directly, but there was no reason to tempt fate or have it pay any more attention to her than necessary.

She sped up as she caught sight of Anya and Seb waiting for her. They'd have a few hours to themselves, a few hours as just family to talk about all the good news. She couldn't tell them everything about the breaks she was taking with Bri, but she could tell them she was taking breaks, tell them where they'd been staying for a few days at a time.

Except Gwen and Cara were standing a few feet behind them, clearly there as part of the group. Why were they there. They weren't part of the Holding. They had nothing to do with Syl business. This was a meeting for the Holding, a meeting that would let her see her family between family visits.

"Fay!" Anya yelled before giving her a hug.

"Anya?"

It had only been a month, hugs as greetings made sense when it had been several, when they had gone too long without seeing each other. Did that mean a month was too long? Was she wrong about hugs?

"We've missed you," Seb said. "And Anya's been a little emotional these last few days."

"So have you," Anya said. "We all have. It's been an interesting month. They have actual permanent encampments in the Depths. It feels too fancy."

"It makes sense, Anya," Fay said. "Most of those staging points get used by multiple groups, this Academy alone will probably keep whatever level they have you Diving at busy day in day out until you graduate. And the other levels will be used by other Academies, or by teams willing to pay the premium to ensure they have a consistent base camp. It's standard practice for bigger Dive Administrations."

"I know," Anya said. "We were taught about that, twice. Marten as a way to explain why successful families and Holdings became more successful, why they could field better Divers as a rule. Leo to point out how foolish it was to do otherwise unless you were actively exploring."

"They're right," she said before taking a breath and pushing the topic to the back of her thoughts. "I need to tell you something…"

"So do we," Seb said.

"But Holding first or I'll never… you do?"

"Holding first," Seb said before giving her a brief hug. "If you're worried about being distracted the other stuff must be important."

"It is," she said, feeling her cheeks start to warm. "No. Holding first."

They made their way to a nearby table. She'd come to realize they were set out for these visits specifically, family visits lasted long enough to find somewhere better to talk, meetings were just meetings.

She forced herself to ignore how Gwen and Cara sat at the end of the table without a word. They weren't part of the conversation and weren't pretending to be, but they were still clearly staying close enough to be included at a moment's notice.

Alex had been right, it only took a handful of minutes to update them on Holding matters. Well on the trivial matters at least. The single most important Holding matter was the incorporation of Seer Constructs into the Holding itself, something she hadn't directly told anyone about except for Yshe. Bri knew the general idea, but not what it would entail, what it would do.

"There's one last Holding matter, but it has to stay secret. Valene gave me some information and advice, he showed up to talk to me."

She glanced at Seb, hoping he'd get the hint. She didn't want to speak about this out loud, especially not when she wasn't sure who or what might be listening.

"If it's about the Fae I'm sure Gwen and Cara can hear," Seb said.

"Oh, there was that too," she said, her mind latching on to his words despite her desire to focus. "He gave me access to a comparison between Fae and Humanity Techniques. How there are basic differences in approaches that are actually quite fascinating once you get in to the-"

Fay?

She heard Anya's words come over the band in the same moment as she felt Seb reach out. They'd coordinated so perfectly that her name felt like it was the connection.

Sorry, Seb distracted me.

Seb!

How was I to know.

What's so secret? Anya asked.

Fay explained how she'd accidentally created a technique that lingered, how 'Seer Constructs' were Energy based Constructs that started as techniques rather than physical objects, how the Doorkeepers were some of the most complex examples. She could feel Seb's thoughts leaping at the words, not asking anything, not even making guesses or predictions. He was letting her explain while the band filled with growing excitement.

I want to try anchoring some to a generator in our Holding, she sent. To make sure things work the way they seem. Simple things like lights reacting to our presence or the ability to relay a message from one part of the Holding to another through the Construct just by speaking.

That isn't all though, is it, Seb sent.

No, she admitted. If they tie in through the Seed they can grow, they can adapt, they can be taught to fit our Holding, to fit with us. To be extensions of the Seed and extensions of our future.

Doorkeepers? Anya sent.

No. Well, yes. But the information I have will make sure they aren't… strange. I hope.

I like the idea, Seb sent. But only the testing for now? Even if you tie to the Seed as a test, nothing comprehensive.

Seb? Anya sent.

We've watched a few of those movies about Artificial Intelligences.

Oh! You're right, that is what this sounds like, sort of.

Tests only for now, Fay sent and smiled before dropping the band.

She could have told them everything that way, could have sent all the knowledge and happiness through. But using the band that way still felt strange. With Bri it felt nice, safe, intimate. With her family it felt safe, but they'd spent so many years talking out loud that words felt more natural.

"I have news!" she said, forcing herself not to move despite finally having no reason to delay.

"We know," Anya said. "I guess this means Holding business is covered?"

She noticed Gwen and Cara shift slightly closer along the bench, still apart but only as a technicality. This was a family moment, it should be just family.

"Can it be news for family? As family?" she asked.

She saw Anya take a deep breath and pause. Why was she pausing?

"It is, think of them as cousins."

Cousins? But they weren't cousins. Not hers. Gwen was almost certainly Anya's distant cousin but Cara was her sworn, not a family member. And cousins implied something closer than distant.

"What do you mean?"

"Do you know anything about how the fae Royals order themselves within their generations?"

Royal ordering? Generations? Wait, did she mean their rankings? Why would the rankings matter, they weren't anywhere near a fae Pond nor near any members of the court except for Gwen and Bern.

"Their rankings? Something about how it is implicit knowledge that leaves them aware of relative importance?"

"Yes," Gwen said. "It's more complex than that, but that's the heart of the matter."

"Anya isn't part of that, she'd have to be recognized, which you've all told me she isn't. And even then."

"I follow her," Gwen said.

Follow. The words felt like a physical blow. It tied Gwen to Anya, to Holding Syl by association almost irrecoverably.

"Anya was not aware when I made my choice. She did not know I made the choice until her attempt to describe our relationship turned challenging. She did not know the rankings existed until Cara and I explained."

"You didn't tell her?" Fay said, frowning.

"It was wrong of me but I do not regret it. I would not have regretted it even had she named me nothing more than retainer and treated me as a menial. Better that than what likely would have happened to Cara had we remained associated with Bern."

"Anya wouldn't have," Fay said reflexively.

"I didn't," Anya said. "I did ask what would happen if I were to and had to stop her."

This wasn't how things were supposed to go, she was supposed to be telling them her good news, not trying to understand how family suddenly mean Royal political games.

"Instead you..?"

"Extended family," Anya said.

"Both of them," Seb added. "Cara isn't going to just follow along as an addition."

She shook her head ruefully at the words. Of course it was both. It had to be both, because they were a pair, but treating Cara as an addition wasn't something either of them would have ever done.

"I apologize," Gwen said, bowing her head slightly. "We interrupted your news and seem to have upset you. We will give you space if you will allow us."

Why was Gwen being so formal with her, giving her the choice directly? It was polite, but she was following Anya, not her. If she was worried about permission why not ask the one who had authority? She'd understand them asking Seb as Anya's partner, but she was just the Seer and sis- Really?

"Extended family and I'm direct family? Sister outranks cousin even if the cousin is technically a Royal?"

"How else?" Gwen asked as Cara nodded.

Fay couldn't help but grin as she did her best impression of Briana behind the counter at Kaf-Fay.

"Jump."

Watching the pair of them stand and Gwen deliberately hop almost made her laugh. Seeing Cara stop her from sitting down before looking at her attentively left her wondering what she'd missed.

"Cara?"

"You told us to jump, she only hopped. And you didn't say how high you wanted to see us jump."

"That wasn't what…"

She trailed off as they all turned to look at Seb as he burst out laughing. Not a light chuckle, not laughing the way he did when Anya had clearly joked about something over the band. This was Seb nearly toppling off the bench.

"Seb?" she asked then turned to Gwen and Cara. "Sit, I don't need you to jump. I just imagined how Briana would react if you'd told her that at her cafe."

Seb took a deep breath, then another.

"It's a saying in the Earth Shallows from movies and stories. There's probably truth to it somewhere but I've always seen it used for exaggeration. 'When I say jump, you ask how high.' Cara asked how high and…"

She saw him flinch slightly and glance at Anya before settling comfortably next to her.

"No more distractions," he said. "Sorry."

Fay smiled and opened her mouth to speak then shook her head at her planned words. They deserved a bit of confusion for all these distractions.

"Alex told me to tell you that Seb won the bet."
