Saorex was completely calm, while Sakura was doing everything she could to hide her terror. She watched the little spiderlings scurrying back and forth, and everything in her stomach kept tightening. In truth, those spiderlings were not little at all. Each one of them was as big as a human hand.

“The trial is s-simple only at first glance,” he said evenly. “Moving through this web, you mus-st avoid the traps-s and find the item that, in your opinion, needs-s to be found. And hand it to me.”

Sakura did not even know what to say right away.

“What traps?” she asked, turning sharply toward him. “And what item? Can you be more specific?”

Saorex slowly shifted his gaze to her. Something like irritation flickered in his completely black eyes.

“If I named the item,” he said, “it would not be a tes-st, but a hint. And hints teach nothing except lazines-s.”

Sakura tilted her head slightly to the side. She had not thought a trial from an ancient being would be easy, but she had at least hoped for actual details.

“S-sometimes, to find s-something, you need to analyze, compare, and notice the connections-s between things-s. Not everything important lies in plain s-sight. In the s-same way, not everything necessary looks-s valuable. And not every road leads-s where it s-seems to.”

Sakura frowned harder, staring at the vast maze of webbing.

“And if I choose the wrong item?”

“Then that will mean you chose the wrong item.”

“I see. Hard to argue with logic like that.”

If her tone bothered Saorex, it did not show.

“You have no more than an hour,” he continued in the same even voice. “I will return here, and by then I expect to find you in this s-same place. With the right item. Here.”

Sakura raised her eyebrows.

“Just an hour?”

“That is more than enough, if you do not waste time on us-seless fus-sing.”

The words sounded lazy, but Sakura still felt an unpleasant sting. Apparently, Saorex had already been expecting exactly that from her: pointless fussing.

Sakura opened her mouth, about to ask something else, but Saorex had already stepped back. His long spider limbs shifted over the stone with ease and without a sound.

“Analyze,” he said before leaving. “Compare.”

After that, he simply turned around and disappeared into the darkness so quickly and so quietly that it looked as if he had dissolved into it. Sakura was left alone. For several moments, she simply stood there, trying to understand what exactly she was supposed to compare.

“Great,” she exhaled at last, almost soundlessly. “Just great.”

The giant web shivered now and then as the spiderlings moved across it. They wove new tangles into it, vanished, and reappeared among the glossy silk strands.

Sakura slowly crouched down at the very edge and peered more carefully. Far below, more layers of webbing glimmered in the dark. Sakura understood with sickening clarity that falling probably would not kill her. It would only leave her trapped somewhere below, wrapped up, helpless, and deeply available for Saorex’s disappointment.

The choice was not much of a choice at all: either she kept standing there like a post and staring at the web, or she finally took a step. Time was passing. Saorex did not look like the kind of being who would take long hesitation in one spot as a sign of intelligence.

Sakura drew in a deep breath, held it in her chest, and very carefully stretched one leg forward, trying to place her boot on the nearest dense patch of web. It looked strong, like a tightly stretched rope net.

In reality, it turned out to be something else entirely. The sole stuck at once.

Sakura jerked in surprise and snapped her gaze downward. The pale strand, damply gleaming in the light, clung to her boot and would not let go. In the same second, the whole web around her gave a nasty shudder, and the spiderlings, who had been scurrying around their own business only a moment ago, seemed to receive one shared signal. They ran straight for Sakura.

“No, no, no!”

Sakura yanked her leg back, but the boot only got stuck harder. The web tightened. Even more spiderlings rushed toward that spot, so coordinated they looked like well-trained soldiers. One got very close, and the others came quickly behind it. Their round little bodies were covered in short dark fuzz, which only made Sakura feel even sicker. Up close, each of them really was the size of a hand, and that alone was enough to make her want to scream.

She probably would have screamed if one of them had not leaped at her first.

Sakura lashed out with one arm. The spiderling flew aside, but that solved nothing. More were already on the way. She lunged wildly, without any logic, trying to rip her foot free from the sticky mess. And she managed it.

Sakura flew backward onto the stone with one bare foot, barely keeping her balance. Several spiderlings swarmed over the trapped boot so fast that it looked like some rare military trophy. One was already dragging it off to the side, while another had planted itself proudly on top.

“You have got to be kidding me,” Sakura breathed, staring at it in pure horror. “Seriously? Seriously?!”

The spiderlings, of course, paid her no attention. They were completely absorbed in the boot.

Sakura took another step back, away from the web, pressing her bare foot against the cold stone. Her heart was beating much too fast. For a moment, it really did feel like some kind of nightmare, one of those dreams where you try to run, and everything around you only sticks harder and drags you back.

And apparently, she had always been afraid of spiders. Not to the point of screaming at the sight of some tiny harmless dot in the corner of a room, but somewhere very deep inside, that fear had definitely been there. And now it had been given full permission to unfold as wide as it wanted.

Sakura slowly lowered her gaze to her second boot.

“Looks like I’m going to be left without shoes,” she muttered with grim resignation.

A few seconds later, she took off the second boot too and tossed it aside.

As soon as Sakura moved farther away from the edge, the spiderlings gradually scattered back, hiding the poor stolen boot somewhere in the web. Apparently, those little thieves had no intention of returning anything.

For a while, Sakura simply stood there, trying to calm down. Then it occurred to her to gather the stones lying around. She picked the sharpest ones in case she had to fight them off. Angry, frantic ideas were already spinning through her head: hit them, knock them off, throw whatever she could, clear a path by force.

She clenched one stone in her hand and froze.

No. Something about that did not fit. Saorex did not look like the sort of being who would set up a trial that boiled down to “crush a hundred spiders and claim your prize.” And the spiderlings themselves, if she thought about it, were not especially strong. Sakura instinctively focused on one of the ones still scrambling along a bright strand.

Forest SpiderLevel: 1

Sakura slowly let out a breath through her teeth. They relied not on strength, but on numbers and sticky webbing.

“I’m not going to hurt you,” she muttered, rolling the sharp stone between her fingers. “There has to be another answer.”

She set the stones aside and walked back to the edge of the web. She needed to look at it closely, analyze it without fear and disgust.

The strands that had been woven more recently gleamed brighter. The light lay on them almost like moisture, and they ran more evenly. Those were the strands the spiderlings moved across most often. The others looked duller and older. They had a grayish film to them, as though dust had already settled there. They were still strong enough to hold, but they looked different. And there were almost no spiderlings on them.

Sakura held her breath.

The spiderlings really did move only along the fresh, sticky web. That was where they circled, where they wove something new, where they reacted to the slightest vibration and rushed toward it at once. The older web seemed to fall outside their world. Not completely, no. They did not ignore it entirely. But it did not interest them. It was not their territory in the same sense.

“The old roads,” Sakura murmured under her breath, not even noticing the thought as it rose in her mind.

She was not sure. But it at least sounded like something.

Very carefully, Sakura stretched her bare foot forward and touched the nearest gray stretch of web with her toes. It sagged a little under her weight, but there was no stickiness to it. It just felt like tightly stretched fabric.

The spiderlings on the pale strand nearby froze for a moment.

Sakura stopped breathing.

But they did not run at her.

Then she shifted a little more weight forward and stepped fully onto the old web. It rocked, and Sakura’s heart did too.

It was a strange feeling. As if she had stepped onto a trampoline stretched over an abyss by people with a very bad sense of humor. The strands sprang beneath her feet, dipping a few inches under her weight. Barefoot, she could feel every shift of the web much better, but that did not make it pleasant. The silk pressed into her skin, cold and tight, and every wrong step still threatened to throw her off balance.

Sakura moved very carefully, trying not to think about the abyss stretching below. Even if there was another layer of web somewhere deep beneath her, it did not make the drop look any less terrifying.

With every movement, everything inside her grew colder. But the spiderlings did not attack. They only watched. She could feel it almost physically, as if dozens of tiny eyes were following her every jerk forward.

“So that’s how it is?” Sakura muttered. “You let me move along the old web.”

The next step went worse. The old strand dipped more sharply under her weight than she had expected, and Sakura’s foot slipped down to the ankle into empty space between the weaves. She swayed so hard that her stomach jumped into her throat, and the black abyss below her seemed to open even wider.

Sakura grabbed at the nearest strands with both hands and hung there for a second, breathing hard.

“Wonderful,” she hissed through her teeth. “My life is not in danger at all. Completely safe. Just like we agreed.”

A second later, she realized she had grabbed the wrong web with one hand. A fresh strand pulled sticky-tight against her palm, and the spiderlings sprang into motion.

“Damn it.”

They rushed at her from several directions so quickly that everything inside Sakura dropped. She yanked her hand away too sharply, then swatted once more at the nearest spiderling, nearly knocking it back into the web, but in the next instant she forced herself to come to her senses. She could not keep jerking around.

Carefully, almost inch by inch, she removed her palm from the fresh sticky strand and set it back on the old gray one. The web around her still trembled for a few seconds, and the spiderlings darted at the boundary as if deciding whether they should go farther. In the end, they simply stayed where they were.

One of them even rose slightly on its hind legs and froze, as though studying her. Sakura stared back at it with the same unkind attention.

“That was an accident,” she murmured quietly. “I won’t touch that web again. I’ll try not to.”

The spiderling, naturally, did not answer. But it did not come any closer either. Sakura slowly let out a breath. Now it all looked almost like permission. They were letting her go farther, but only without breaking the rules.

She gave a quiet snort and kept going.

Step by step, Sakura moved deeper in. The web under her feet kept springing and swaying ever so slightly. Sometimes she had to make a wide detour around patches that were too fresh, pressing close to the stone wall.

The farther she went, the more objects she found caught in the strands. Old coins, chains, odd stones. On another level of the web, something that looked like a clasp flashed in the light. A little farther off, a small knife without a handle was hanging. There were so many objects that Sakura had long since lost count. Any one of them could start to look important if she stared at it long enough.

Sakura forced herself not to grab the first thing she saw.

Find the item that, in your opinion, needs to be found.

The phrase still sounded like a mockery.

She walked and compared. Sorted things out in her head. Tried to understand what was just trash, and what might actually matter. And the deeper she went, the more everything around her began to look the same.

White and gray blurred together. There were too many strands above her head now, and too many at her sides. Sakura could no longer see the path she had used to get here. The web maze had completely tangled her up.

But Sakura kept going.

Now she had to move even more carefully. The old web still held, but there was more and more of it all around her. It hung overhead, stretched out from the sides, and lay beneath her feet in several layers at once, sometimes weaving together so densely that it became hard to step through. More and more often Sakura had to stop and literally stare before she could take the next step. Sometimes an old strand led her into a dead end made of fresh web, and then she had to back up half a step and look for another way around.

The farther she went, the denser the web became. There seemed to be less air too. Sakura stopped on another old strand and looked around.

There was so much web all around her that it no longer felt like just a labyrinth. It felt like the inside of something. A core. A place where everything came together and where an outsider was never meant to be.

The center?

The thought came unexpectedly, but it felt plausible.

And there were no spiderlings here.

At first Sakura did not even realize it. Then suddenly she understood that for the past several minutes she had not heard the familiar rustle of little legs nearby. On the fresh strands, something still darted around in the distance now and then, but those tiny swarms that had rushed at the slightest vibration were no longer anywhere in sight.

How long had she been walking here? Ten minutes? Thirty? Almost an hour? Time had long since stopped feeling real in this web. Saorex had said he would return in an hour. But what did that even mean? Would he come get her if she failed? Or would he simply return, see that she was stuck somewhere in the depths, and make his ancient conclusions?

Sakura did not know. And still she kept looking around.

There were more objects here, and many of them no longer looked like ordinary human things at all. Strange objects were caught among the threads, and Sakura could not even guess what some of them were meant to be. Stones carved with symbols. Dark little figures that could have been idols, or just ancient statuettes. Metal disks covered in patterns like unfamiliar writing. Some of the things looked so old that Sakura could not begin to imagine who they might once have belonged to.

Then something glinted.

Sakura froze and carefully turned her head.

There, among the threads, lay two gold coins. Hanging very close beside them was a little key.

So plain that it could easily have been mistaken for just another scrap of metal if it had not been lying so close to the coins, almost disappearing beside them. But as soon as Sakura looked at it a little longer, she noticed the engraving of a note on it. The image of the music box from the cave came to her on its own. The one with the note on the lid.

Sakura slowly frowned, and her hand reached forward on its own before she had fully thought it through. She wanted to know the melody inside that music box.

She could not honestly say that she felt some sudden, absolute certainty. There was no triumphant revelation, no inner voice telling her, there it is, that is the right thing. No. But Sakura chose.

The little key was light and cold in her hand. Sakura closed her fingers around it, and for some reason she felt a brief, strange stab of relief.

Now she had to get back out.

Sakura turned back almost at once. Looking for anything else would have been pure madness. Even if this was not the right item, she would not find anything better if she stayed here until she passed out.

She stopped, swept her gaze across the web before her, and forced herself not to rush. She only had to keep studying the web, keep analyzing it.

Sakura looked at the woven strands around her and suddenly caught another pattern. The denser and thicker the web became, the deeper she had gone. The looser the strands lay, the more air and light there was between them, the closer she probably was to the edge. To the exit.

Sakura started moving again. Now she was not so much remembering the route as reading the space around her. Where the strands hung too densely, she turned. Where the web became a little looser and less layered, she kept going.

Several times it felt like she was going the wrong way, but then a new gray passage would open up ahead, old enough to hold her weight and not disturb the spiderlings.

Gradually, it actually worked.

The web around her began to thin.

A few minutes later, the stony edge finally appeared ahead of her. The same one.

She covered the last few feet on pure stubbornness and climbed back onto solid ground.

Sakura was breathing hard, the soles of her bare feet burned from the strain, and her fingers were still gripping the key so tightly that it had bitten into her skin. She lifted her head, and only then saw that Saorex was already there. His silver brows were drawn together as he looked at her with reproach.

Had she failed the trial?
