The small black pot simmered on the stove, short rib stew bubbling away inside it, enough for two solid meals if he was being responsible about portions, which he wasn't planning to be. The rich, meaty aroma had taken over the whole apartment hours ago, curling through every room.

Ning stirred the pot with a wooden spoon, watching chunks of radish and carrot rise and sink through the broth, and couldn't stop the grin creeping across his face.

Twenty-four hours of braising. He'd started this thing yesterday afternoon, and now, finally, it was done.

"Today's a good day," he muttered, entirely pleased with himself.

For Ning, in this busy world, food was a much-needed comfort. A good meal could melt away all the stress, leaving him warm and content inside. It was his favorite way to unwind after a long day.

That was why he had taken the time to make this dish. Normally, he would just copy some quick recipe from social media, throw it together in ten minutes, and call it dinner. But some days, you just had to take the long way.

Salivating without the slightest bit of dignity, he slipped on a pair of oven mitts, lifted the pot from the stove, and carefully set it on the table.

"Dinner~ is~ served~," Ning sang to himself.

Out of habit, he reached for his phone before he'd even sat down. He always liked having something to scroll through while eating.

"Right. It's still charging."

Ning got up and crossed over to the counter where he'd left it. The screen lit up as soon as he picked it up, a notification sliding into view.

"Hm?"

Ning glanced at it instinctively, and then something inside his skull exploded, pain striking so suddenly that his thoughts simply stopped. His vision blurred and flooded with crimson as a wave of dizziness overwhelmed him, his legs buckling before he could even process what was happening. He barely felt himself hit the floor.

Yet somehow, through the confusion, Ning understood what was happening. He wasn't going to open his eyes again.

Maybe it was instinct. Maybe people simply knew when the end had come. Whatever the reason, the certainty settled in before he could even begin to panic.

No... not now.

His body went limp, and consciousness began slipping away.

People always talked about profound realizations in their final moments, but Ning's last thought wasn't about the meaning of life or everything he had left undone.

It was a single, heartfelt lament.

Damn it! I just wanted to eat my stew.

...

"Hey, you. You're finally awake."

The unfamiliar voice pulled Ning out of the haze.

His eyelids felt unbearably heavy as he forced them open, only to find himself staring at a rough stone ceiling illuminated by several glowing crystals embedded in the walls. Before he could make sense of his surroundings, movement near the cave entrance drew his attention.

An old man stood there dressed in flowing green robes, looking as though he had stepped straight out of an ancient Chinese period drama. His long white beard reached his chest, and his wide sleeves fluttered gently despite the complete absence of wind.

"To think someone would actually lose consciousness during the Qi Drawing stage." The old man stroked his beard, seemingly more amused than concerned. "For a moment, this old man thought your soul had already departed. Fortunately, your breathing gradually stabilized. The elder presiding over the ceremony mentioned that such things occasionally happen when one's consciousness struggles to adapt to sensing qi for the first time."

Ning stared at him blankly.

The words reaching his ears sounded like nothing he knew. If he had to compare them, they sounded vaguely Chinese, though he couldn't say for certain.

Yet somehow, he understood every word. The meaning came to him as naturally as his native language, without him having to stop and think about it at all.

"...Ugh." Ning's head throbbed relentlessly, and every attempt to make sense of the situation only made the pain worse.

"Some dizziness is normal," the old man continued. "Since you have regained consciousness, there is no longer any need for me to remain. Stay within this cave and cultivate until you enter the First Stage of Qi Condensation. You were already instructed on what to do before entering, so settle your heart and focus on your cultivation."

As he finished speaking, the old man raised one hand. Light spread across the cave entrance, and the heavy stone doors began to rumble shut.

Those words finally broke through the fog clouding Ning's thoughts.

Qi Drawing? First Stage of Qi Condensation?

"Wait!"

Ning hurried forward as questions flooded his mind, but the old man either didn't hear him or simply had no intention of answering. The stone doors slid together with a deafening boom, cutting off the last sliver of daylight and leaving Ning alone beneath the pale glow of the crystals.

For several seconds, he simply stood there staring at the sealed entrance.

"...What the hell just happened?"

The pounding in his skull hadn't faded, but at least he could think somewhat clearly now.

Ning first examined the stone doors blocking the entrance. They were as solid as any rock he'd encountered, and he couldn't find any mechanism that would allow them to open and close so quickly.

"Open the cave!" Ning shouted, banging his fists against the stone. He even tried pushing against the doors, but his meager strength wasn't nearly enough to move them.

"Am I trapped?"

At this point, Ning was beginning to realize that he'd found himself in one of those trapped-room situations from the movies, except this time, there didn't seem to be any way out.

His breathing quickened as the realization sank in. Everything around him was so strange and unfamiliar, from the glowing crystals to the bizarre terminology the old man had been using.

Still, Ning knew that panicking would only make things worse.

He forced himself to take a few deep breaths before turning his attention to the cave.

It wasn't particularly large. A straw mat rested in one corner, while a simple wooden cupboard stood beside a stone stove. Other than those few necessities, the place was almost completely bare.

Then his gaze landed on a polished bronze mirror hanging from the wall.

Ning walked over almost absentmindedly, only to stop the moment he saw the reflection staring back at him.

The young man in the mirror wasn't him.

Long black hair reached almost to his waist, loosely tied behind his back, while the face beneath it looked several years younger than Ning remembered. His features were softer, his skin unblemished by the stress of modern life, and instead of his usual clothes, coarse ancient robes now covered his body.

He raised a hand and watched the reflection follow.

Ning touched his face next, slowly running his fingers across features that somehow didn't feel as foreign as they should have.

Looking into a mirror and seeing someone else's face should have been deeply unsettling, yet the longer Ning stared, the more natural the reflection seemed. There was no instinctive rejection of the body, no sensation that he was wearing someone else's skin. If anything, some part of him seemed to recognize it as his own.

The realization disturbed him far more than anything else he'd encountered so far. After all, body dysmorphia was a real thing, and his situation definitely wasn't that far removed from it.

"If this is a dream, it's one fucked-up dream," Ning mumbled. He stepped away from the mirror before his thoughts could spiral any further and resumed searching the cave. Unfortunately, most of it was bare, and he couldn't even find any food.

Still, his efforts weren't entirely fruitless. While checking his robes, he discovered a small piece of folded paper tucked inside a sewn inner pocket.

"I should've searched myself first." Ning clicked his tongue and unfolded the paper.

It was more of a diagram than anything else, depicting a man sitting in the lotus position, accompanied by brief instructions on regulating one's breathing until the mind became calm. Once that state was achieved, the practitioner was supposed to attempt to perceive the spiritual qi in their surroundings and continue cultivating until reaching the First Stage of Qi Condensation.

"The old man must be talking about this." Ning also noticed that he could somehow read the unfamiliar writing, which he decided to count as a blessing for now.

With something to go on, he decided to follow the instructions.

Ning returned to the straw mat and sat down cross-legged. He closed his eyes and steadied his breathing, attempting to clear his mind as the diagram instructed.

Blank. Think of nothing.

Surprisingly, entering that state proved easier than he'd expected, and before long, Ning began sensing something unusual in the air around him.

Spiritual qi? It's actually real?

He had barely come to that realization when something flashed across his consciousness, and a sharp pain erupted inside his head as countless foreign memories suddenly came rushing to the surface.

N-Not again.

Ning barely had time to register the thought before his vision went dark and he collapsed onto the straw mat.

...

Ning's eyes slowly opened once more.

This time, the headache had subsided, leaving behind only a dull ache. The torrent of memories no longer crashed through his mind all at once. Instead, they settled piece by piece, allowing him to sort through them without feeling as though his skull was about to split open.

He sat there quietly for several minutes.

He had just experienced a clichéd plotline straight out of the "I transmigrated/reincarnated into another world" genre.

It was only through those memories that he was beginning to grasp this absurd situation.

The memories belonged to a young boy named Ji Ning.

The son of a martial arts master from a small town, Ji Ning had lived an ordinary life until a month ago, when a cultivation sect arrived to test the local youths. Out of hundreds of candidates, he had been one of the few fortunate enough to possess a spiritual root, and not just any spiritual root, but one of mid-grade quality.

Naturally, he had been accepted into the sect.

Unfortunately, his luck appeared to have run out immediately afterward.

According to the memories, he had lost consciousness during his first attempt at Qi Drawing. By the time Ning opened his eyes again, the current situation had already unfolded.

Did 'I' really choke at the tutorial? Ning couldn't help but think as he continued going through the memories.

So, what could Ning do about the situation he found himself in?

"Nothing. There's not a single thing I can do."

Ning collapsed backward onto the straw mat, staring blankly at the ceiling.

Seriously, what was he supposed to do? He'd somehow ended up in another world, and now he was expected to survive in a place where people could probably flatten mountains with a wave of their hands.

Just what kind of ridiculous situation have I gotten myself into?

Ning let out a long sigh. He had no idea what the future held for him, and frankly, he wasn't even sure how to begin dealing with all this.

In fact, it was already strange enough that he'd managed to remain relatively calm throughout this entire shitshow.

Still, he knew that the only choice he had for now was to accept his circumstances, and only then could he transcend them.

"I suppose, from now on, I am Ji Ning."
