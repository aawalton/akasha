Okay so first off, I'm unsure about how quickly Adam goes from entering the rift (chapter 7) to first going to the Underground with Toar and Marcois (chapter 9). Making the Rift Delving Association feel like a living, breathing entity that meddle in their workers' affairs (while mainly leaving them to self-govern), showing some examples of audits, hand-ins, Adam purchasing stuff or selling stuff, examples of intergroup trade or exploitation, as well as some special interest being taken in Adam by the administration, and other things, like two groups fighting over a mining spot, or seeing Adam's group have to run from a more powerful group early, might really help to set the grounding of a place that feels more grounded and detailed. I fill in a lot of this in later chapters, some readers might feel, but it takes time, and some of that content feels like it's maybe compensating for a lack of early foundation. Would you like to see more stuff like this early on?2.I think Toar's characterisation is a little bit wonky early. I'd like to straighten it out some. The crafting situation:Okay, so I'll just be frank about this one. I find crafting stuff pretty hard to keep track of, and the current configuration of [Hoard] makes NOT crafting feel kinda strange a lot of the time from an MC perspective while the amount of time the story would have to devote to crafting while balancing plot/etc. makes it difficult to both A) satisfy those who want to read about all of the things Adam could potentially make with his [Hoard] and B) keep the story moving smoothly.I'd assume that a majority of my readers aren't HERE for crafting, and it's annoying to give Adam items that I mentally label as 'future crafting components' and then never find a convenient place to put them to use. That said, I don't want to cut crafting out of the story, I just want to shift how [Hoard] works to better accomodate crafting as something that can be done during downtime without it feeling strange for Adam not to be spamming item combinations and break downs.Here's what I'm currently thinking—a shift in [Hoard]'s level 3 upgrade to make it so that Adam can learn crafting recipes by MANUALLY creating something once, meaning he can now auto-create the same item with [Hoard] (to the same skill level) immediately, assuming he has the correct components. This makes [Hoard]-assisted crafting more based around automation of things Adam learns himself (which can still partially be influenced by his ability to appraise items) rather than an... item gacha where he can just throw stuff in and maybe gets something useful out, but how useful the thing is depends heavily upon his existing crafting skills.Find this and other great novels on the author's preferred platform. Support original creators!The way it currently works is counter-intuitive. Adam's more likely to break stuff or waste it by combining or messing with it if he doesn't have pre-requisite skills, but because he has the ability to DO it, a lot of readers wonder why he doesn't or when he's going to get the chance to.I'd call this less of a nerf and more of a recontextualisation for the sake of clarity. I expect Adam still to be able to craft all of the same things in time, but the required learning is now more understandably frontloaded.4b.Crafting again (and some other potential Hoard changes):When it comes to [Hoard]'s capabilities, a lot of readers are keen for Adam to combine up or break down items when his carrying capacity is getting high. While the ability to gain interest on stored items is revealed in chapter 36, up until that point, it might feel strange for Adam to leave stuff behind and not 'dust' it, but realistically, dusting feels somewhat counterintuitive for the fact that you lose value, and that isn't satisfying.I've got a proposed change that I think will satisfy this issue and also lead to some increased dopamine in the story: I want Adam to unlock the ability with level 3 [Hoard] to break down items for a mana boost, and occasionally, some other kind of small buff, temporary or permanent. More valuable items will run a higher chance of conferring buffs and will generally give more mana.As for inflating Adam's power too heavily, I plan to make it so that the more Adam's mana capacity increases, the more valuable of an item Adam must consume in order to gain mana. This stops him from just consuming a warehouse of chairs or something and getting a power boost from it, or just vacuuming up whatever room he finds himself in permanently.I'd love some thoughts on this and the general crafting considerations, but I'm very likely going through with this one.
