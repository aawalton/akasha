In the dank, quiet cave, Henry meditated.

Just as he had expected, his Aspects were significantly uncooperative about fusing. It wasn’t about their rank, he could tell as much, now that he’d had a couple of hours of practice under his belt. Henry was pretty sure of that. No, this was about both of their essences having a strong, established identity.

Simply put, both Aspects were being headstrong. Thaumaturgy has always been about Henry’s arcane prowess, his mana, telekinesis, telepathy. About his cognitive prowesses through his Octominds and plenty of other abilities, while Dancing Shadows had been mostly about his movements. About obfuscation, positioning, and mobility through his network of clones. On paper, the fusion should be seamless. They complemented each other well.

But unlike Henry’s exercise with the malleable essences of the Aspects themselves, he was now trying to convince the sources of the essences to bend to his vision, and neither Thaumaturgy nor Dancing Shadows wanted to. Each one of them wanted to take over the other Aspect. Thaumaturgy wanted to include the cornerstone of Dancing Shadows as a small bundle within its umbrella, while Dancing Shadows wanted to absorb Thaumaturgy and make its casting a part of its clones. And while the latter did fit Henry’s vision a little, he could tell that the method Dancing Shadows wanted to do things would be wasteful. It was as if it wanted eat up Thaumaturgy for a little boost and discard everything that wouldn’t fit.

Henry wasn’t having that. That wasn’t what he wanted. He wanted them to fully meld and become something bigger than either of them. But that was the challenge. It was like trying to shepherd two different cats at the same time. Challenging. Annoying. Because both of them were distracted and only listened when they felt like it.

So Henry used bait. Sort of.

Coaxing a thread of essence out of each of them, Henry combined the two in the vision he wanted. A fusion of both. All of his strengths and abilities, equally spread out through his clones, giving them more of a presence and weight until they were all part of him, just like his Octominds. Not simple clones who followed orders anymore, but autonomous extensions of himself. Extensions of his spirit. Parts of a whole where the distance between the nodes wouldn’t matter. A network of capable, powerful… Henrys. With all of his knowledge and memories. A step up from his clones and Octominds. Each as real as the next so that neither them nor he could truly die, so long as one of them was still around.

Henry held on to the resulting essence which, to be fair to Maurice, did look like a bead full of intensely magical purple roe, and tucked deeply into his core, he displayed it to the Aspects, which he pictured as two large crystalline boulders grinding against each other, too different to mix.

Until he introduced a magical catalyst between them, and that got both of the Aspect to listen and reach out.

That was how Henry had gotten the first two specks of the Aspects to fuse and between the two Aspects, a small, tiny purple fleck pulsed with power, a confirmation that his vision wasn’t completely unrealistic. He now only had to do it a few hundred times again, and he’d be done. But Henry didn’t even give that train of thought a second glance.

There was no need to tempt the universe to throw complication at him.

***

Henry was vaguely aware of time passing outside while he worked on his new Aspect. He could sense Maurice doing things by the small flares of mana here and there. He could tell the noise and temperature around him had changed a few times since he’d started. But so long as the cave wasn’t falling on top of their heads, Henry gave the process his full attention, as the less mess-ups there were in the fusion, the faster his recovery would be. So kept going, working through the two Aspects one section at a time. But unfortunately, a new issue was slowly but surely approaching as more and more of the two Aspects were assimilated.

Just like the Aspects themselves, the cornerstone skills—the beating hearts of the Aspects—were both spitting and hissing at each other, like two feral beasts suddenly being forced into proximity.

So Henry had to play peacemaker again. While still working on the Aspects fusion. Thankfully, his cognitive boosts were not affected much by the process he was conducting, so Henry spared a bit of his attention to keep the cornerstones from making too much of a fuss, and he focused on the Aspect fusion. Once the latter bit was done, he could give his whole attention to the former.

Henry finished the Aspect fusion… sometime later. Probably around a couple days or three. And as much as he wanted to take a break and celebrate, he had to continue working. Because his cornerstones needed his attention. Just like a couple of plants suddenly moved into a foreign, different soil from what they had been used to, the cornerstones weren’t happy campers. So Henry had to fuse them as soon as possible, and while that process was still challenging, Henry was glad that the skills weren’t as opinionated as the Aspects they had come from.

It still wasn’t easy, though.

Hovering somewhere in his soul, four skill nexuses shone and spluttered, one failing after the other before they lit up again. Henry had to figure out a way to activate both skills through the same nexus and get a result he liked, at which point the resulting configuration could resonate back to the source of the skills in his soul.

Henry lost count of how many failed attempts it took before he got his first—well. Not success. Barely working prototype would be a better term. But from the fluttering and sputtering of the skill nexus, he knew it was no good. So he continued, though by now, Henry was actually feeling thirst and hunger, which was alarming.

That usually didn’t just take a couple of days.

How much time had it been since he’d started? Was Maurice alright? Henry could still feel him out there, doing stuff. But while he felt guilty, Henry didn’t dare stop. He wasn’t sure what would happen if he put his fusions on pause, but he didn’t want to risk it, especially considering there didn’t seem to be any emergencies.

So he kept going and slowly but surely, the stable nexuses overtook the failures, and with every one of them, Henry’s perfect skill floated closer to his grasp. He couldn’t test the results just, of course. He just wanted a stable combination first and he knew he was close. By now, Henry trusted his own guts. Occasionally, he’d use a little essence nudge the fusion in the right direction, tweaking things here and there, until, finally, he sensed it and immediately, he focused on the nexus in question.

One of the attempts of fusing The Council of the Outer Minds and Trickster’s Shadow had clicked together well, and a shock-wave of electrifying energy rippled out of the nexus, making Henry’s eyes shoot open, while deep in his soul, he could feel both skill roots nudge toward each other.

Some said that in a compromise, no party was truly happy. Which meant that this... days-long process of wheedling and negotiation was no compromise. Because all parties involved—Henry included—were happy.

It was night, Henry vaguely realized. But that wasn’t because the cave was dark. No, it was the dark waters and dark horizon that told him that no sun was out. But the cave itself was brightly lit with an iris-blue, ghostly kraken floating in front of him. Just like himself, it had pale spots across its surface, but the spots seemed to move and lazily swim about, like the reflection of stars on running water. Then the creature’s flesh rippled, and as if it had been a liquid and was suddenly subjected to an impact, the creature’s skin solidified, dimming while the magic turned to blood and flesh, until Henry found himself staring at a small copy of himself.

That wasn’t even the most disturbing bit.

Henry could see himself through the creature’s eyes. He could see himself from both directions. He could sense himself think of the same issue from two direction, and there was no confusion whatsoever.

Then both slowly turned to find Maurice asleep, while a bunch of pale crabs swarmed around him, clacking their pincers protectively at the floating kraken. Also, the cove definitely looked different. Maurice seemed to have carved and straightened the walls. He even dug out a pool.

Henry vowed to return to the cave’s examination later, but for now, he closed his eyes. He had work to finish, and so he nudged the skill roots together, guiding their fusion while he engulfed them in the energies of the cores he’d accumulated and in the essence of the new Aspect that would be their home. This was the last stretch. Like watering a plant after going through the painstaking work of digging, unearthing, transporting, and refilling the hole with dirt. This was the last step, so Henry didn’t falter and eventually, he felt the process wind down, and he knew that he had succeeded. He was done.

It was only then that he allowed himself to check the notification, right as the sun of a new day lit up the cove.

Unfettered Echoes of the Trickster (B) - Level 7

Cornerstone: Avatars of the All-Seeing Legion

It seems like the fusion lost me a few levels, Henry thought as he jubilantly read through the cornerstone’s description, which, he somehow knew already before the System put them to word. After all, he had guided both fusions from start to finish.

In the end, Henry felt that a few levels were definitely worth the boost he’d gotten. He’d take that deal any day.
