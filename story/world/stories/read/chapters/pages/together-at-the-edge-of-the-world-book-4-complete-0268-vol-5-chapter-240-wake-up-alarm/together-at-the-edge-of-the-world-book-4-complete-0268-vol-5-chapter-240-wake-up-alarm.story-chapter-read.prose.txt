Under a sky just beginning to dream of the sun, Leandro, Marshalla, and Anna gathered to give their farewells to the ones who would be departing for the Hummers' territory.

They had kept the departure gathering small, not wishing to disturb too many people's lives; the real farewell party had been last night, shared over dinner and a reasonable amount of alcohol.

David and Niala listened to the manalogists' last few ideas about what they might encounter. Anna and Sammie spent their time in an embrace, exchanging sweet words, the bunnykin softly chuckling now and then. Sybil was playing tug of war with Buddy, both sides pulling at a length of heavy hemp rope. Buddy was letting her win.

On a silent signal, the groups split, and the ones leaving mounted their steeds; Niala, David, and Jordo on the aetheric, house-sized bird, and the two scion guards on their falcodrakes.

In a burst of flapping wings, they were gone. A strong tailwind soon began carrying them forward.

The trip was eventful, in that they encountered many creatures of the Hungerwoods and Ruinlands along the way. At no point were they in any real danger, however.

The sheer size of Niala's summon, and its relative power, ensured that most encounters were short-lived, with whatever creature they met quickly turning around to look for prey that was not five times their size.

Anything that had more aggression than brains and insisted on attacking was met by walls of turbulent winds that flung them away. The few who persisted were harassed by some very irate falcodrakes who showcased to both interloper and riders that their talons didn't just look dangerous.

They landed in clearings or small plateaus, using the small cabin attached to David's carriair to spend the night, with Jordo patrolling and keeping the wildlife at bay.

After a couple of days, they crossed the invisible barrier which separated the hunger woods from the dead woods. The skies cleared of large, bloodthirsty beasts. The nights brought a deep chill that no amount of blankets could hold at bay.

Or maybe it was how quiet these woods were, and how their minds screamed to stay on alert for a threat that failed to materialize.

The forest wasn't actually dead; small animals lived there, and the vegetation was pervasive. The wrongness was in how everything was muted, from the colours to the sounds, as if everything was afraid to attract something's attention. Even the group found themselves whispering at night without knowing why.

Except for Jordo, whose booming, cheerful voice never abated.

The oppressive atmosphere became an intimate part of their travel after a few days. The crawling sensation of being observed had everyone on edge and looking over their shoulder. Sleep was light and disturbed, with everyone waking in a fright at least once during their travel.

More and more of the hummers' sepulchral edifice and constructions peeked at them through the canopy, the trees almost cautious to grow too close. Many were the same mausoleum-like construction as when they encountered the first octuple. Several other types could be seen, but all of them appeared unoccupied, or perhaps dormant.

The pyramid came into view on the fourth day. By this point, their travel was a procession through the slumbering cemetery of an ancient, terrible civilization. They spoke little, mirroring the quieting forest.

Still, they met no one. No flying whale, or strange white people, or anything to oppose or even question their encroaching. The feeling of being observed, however, only grew in strength. By their last rest before reaching the pyramid proper, the group had to resort to some of Niala's sleeping potions to catch even just a few hours of sleep.

They reached the outskirts of the pyramid proper right after noon the next day. Expecting defensive weapons, they flew low, ready to dodge, even if they were all protected by a humming shield.

Nothing tried to stop them. Perhaps, as the hummers had said, Niala really had been identified as a One, and the pyramid was accepting her presence.

They landed on a vast stone plaza facing the triangular edifice. Its surface bore symbols, somewhat religious-looking, engraved deep and sharp, untouched by time or the elements. From the air, the carvings formed linked circles. From the ground, they gave the impression of walking on a giant stone tablet.

Almost a hundred meters ahead of them stood wide stairs leading up to a tall yet narrow entrance.

The group looked around, tensed and ready for action. The longer they waited, the more their attention dragged toward the entryway, the blackness within heavy with dark whispers.

Sammie was the first to break the spell. “I was kind of expecting more action.”

Sybil snapped her mouth shut as she looked at him. Niala nodded as an afterthought, still staring ahead, ears straight, with only the tip of her tail swaying.

David stepped closer to his wife. “What is it?”

She spoke low. “I can almost hear voices, like if the wind was carrying murmurs my way.”

“Sounds ominous. Do you want to go in?”

Glancing at him, she returned her focus to the entrance. “I don't know. This is totally different from the temple we fought the One at. They both feel alive, but this one is...” She trailed off, grunting, turning her head to look at him. “I don't know how to describe it. It's like my mana is a wolf and it caught a scent and it wants to go sniff it out.”

He slowly nodded. “We knew it was going to be dangerous, but, Kitten, we don't have to do this. If you have a bad feeling, we can go back.”

She held his gaze for a while before shaking her head. “No. Whatever is in there is evil, or uncaring enough to be just as. We go in.”

Looking back toward the pyramid, she tucked her ears down. “Just... carefully?”

He gave her a quiet smile. “Alright,” he said before looking at Sybil and Sammie. “You two, stay close to Niala. If the hummers are right, that will identify you as a One's servants. If anything happens, I expect you to prioritize Niala's safety before anything else. Got it?”

The scion guards saluted, speaking as one. “Of course, Sir!” They confirmed before recalling their mounts and taking position on Niala's flanks.

David met his wife's gaze. “Ready?”

“No, but let's go.”

The full size of the pyramid dawned upon them as they walked, and it only seemed to become bigger instead of getting closer. The entrance, which they had thought to be two or three stories high, ended up tall enough to swallow a bell tower whole. Standing at the base of the stairs, the edifice might as well have been a mountain.

Yet, the only thing that assaulted them was their nerves. Every one of their steps brought nothing but the soft rustling of the wind. Each step climbed brought them closer to the thrumming hum of the cavernous entrance.

Nothing moved except them.

As they stood before the way in, Sammie's voice nearly made everyone flinch. “It's totally a trap.”

David looked back at the man. “I have no doubt.”

“So why are we walking into it, again?”

Niala opened her mouth to speak over her shoulder, but Sybil beat her to it. “To be heroes of justice, Sammie! To save all the dead people!”

“...Right, sorry, I forgot.”

David shared a look with Niala, both nodding at each other. He faced forward and walked through the threshold, hand gripping Heartsong.

One by one, they stepped in. The main corridor was high enough that the light from outside didn't fully reach the ceiling, and the few dim light orbs lining the walls offered enough illumination not to trip on their own feet, but little else.

No door slammed behind them.

No monster jumped down from the ceiling to greet them.

There was only the silence of a place that had forgotten to wake up.

They walked several minutes down the straight, featureless hallway, devoid of side passages or doors. The light orbs awoke about a dozen meters ahead of them, leaving the far end of the corridor bathed in darkness. The sound of their steps was muted, as if absorbed by the off-white stone the pyramid was made of; the same material the temple had been built of.

This kept going until, ahead, the material changed, from the carved white to a natural granite grey. They approached the seam, finding it irregular and jagged, as if...

Niala walked up to the demarcation, running her hand down the strange juxtaposition. She turned her head toward David, ears alert, eyes wide. “David, I think this place is literally a mountain! They built a pyramid around a mountain!”

He frowned and joined her side, inspecting the grey stone, before looking at their golem companion. “Jordo, what do you think?”

The construct's eye pinned then flashed. “I concur, sir! The grey material indeed appears to be igneous rock, a very common material for mountains!”

The group looked at each other before resuming their march forward. The hallway shrank as they kept going, the ceiling lowering before levelling out at about six metres.

A quarter bell later, the smooth carved stone stopped, the hallway turning into a natural cavern. After a few more minutes of walking, a dim yellow glow resolved itself in the distance. It soon became their only source of light, the walls devoid of any more orbs.

David and Niala stopped in their track as the details of the path ahead became clearer. It was a familiar sight, something they had seen before: a path leading up to a perfectly round stone door, decorated with engravings, with a lone yellow ember in the middle.

It was a replica of the door they had seen beneath the Montaroyal valley. In the still silence as their group stopped, they could hear the murmur of a body of water.

They shared a look, then resumed their procession. Soon, the same underground lake, split by a path, greeted them. The cavern might as well have been copied over.

They reached the landing before the circular door, itself containing the same runes and shallow bowl. They stopped before the limit of the engravings.

David looked at Niala. “What do you think? We open this one?”

Her ears wiggled. “We don't really have a choice. I didn't see any other path. What about you, Jordo?”

The golem's eye dimmed. “No, Madam. My sensors did not perceive any other path. I must point out, however, that the white stone was mostly opaque to me, as is the door and wall ahead of us.”

David glanced at their guardian. “So there might have been hidden rooms closer to the entrance.”

Jordo bowed his head. “Correct, Sir. It is also quite possible that the stone would just meld out of the way to form a door, as we have seen that material do in the past.”

The man nodded and looked at the guards. “You and Jordo, watch our back while we try and get this door open.”

Sammie and Sybil nodded, turning around and watching the way in, while David faced the other way and walked up to the shallow bowl, Niala following after him.

As soon as she set foot on the runed circle, the yellow light on the door flared, and the circular stone slab split down the middle, both sides retreating into the wall. Ahead, a well-lit path, made of mirror-polished granite, awaited.

David, having dropped into a combat stance, eventually straightened up and turned his head toward his wife. “Do y-”

A metallic, toneless voice drowned his next words, loud enough to be unpleasant. “Afflicted individual identified. Lack of essence identified. Remedial: instruct and inform.”

A second later, the voice spoke again, but with a hint of inflection this time. “Proceed, chosen. Your divine task is at hand. Approach alone.”

David and Niala shared a glance. She looked toward the source of the voice. “Hi, I'm Niala. We'd like to talk, but there's no way I'm stepping into the ominous and possibly evil cavern. Not alone, at least.”

Silence was her only answer. With a frown, she opened her mouth to speak again when the voice came back, flat and mechanical once more. “Individual is unwilling to cooperate. Individual must cooperate. Alternative is coercion. Individual self-determined as Niala. Individual called Niala, cooperating is optimal.”

David stepped forward, putting himself between the cavern ahead and his wife, his stance low. “No one is coercing anyone.”

Behind him, Niala brought out a few vials and handed them off to the wind as they began floating around her. “David is right. We want to talk, and we won't attack, but we'll defend ourselves. Please, let's be civil about this.”

Another short pause.

“Individual Niala persists. Directive is clear; the afflicted must serve their purpose. Ultimatum: Will you serve your purpose?”

“I... what is supposed to be my purpose?” She asked, ears pointing forward.

A subtle flash of yellow light from deep within the hallway shot toward them like a billowing, luminous cloud. Warm air enveloped them as if a giant had sent a breath their way.

The voice was imperious, this time.

“The only purpose the transients of this world are meant for: to ensure the continuation of his existence. To right the injustice dealt upon his divine being. To give him back his stolen name.”

Niala blinked. “You mean, to resurrect a god?”

“No, to rouse him from his shackling slumber.”
