Grimroost had erupted into chaos. Bells tolled frantically as guards barked orders, their voices barely audible over the terrified cries of townsfolk. The dragon loomed on the horizon, its massive body silhouetted against the fiery sunset, its wings blotting out chunks of the crimson sky. Each of its footsteps sent tremors through the ground, cracking cobblestones and dislodging tiles from roofs. People screamed and scattered, some clutching their belongings, others herding children away from the gates.

Sigurd leaped from his horse, his sharp gaze scanning the pandemonium. He turned to Ada and gestured firmly toward the Guildhall. “Stay here and help the Guildmaster coordinate magical defenses. Keep the civilians calm.”

Ada’s pink eyes narrowed as she clutched her staff tighter. “Are you insane? If you get yourself killed, who’s going to invest in my business? I’m coming with you!”

“Ada, you’re a witch, not a warrior,” Sigurd retorted, his tone firm. “Stay where it’s—”

“Safe?” Ada cut him off with a loud snort. “Oh, sure, I’ll just sit here sipping tea while my benefactor becomes dragon chow. That’s a brilliant idea! Besides, if you die, my future investor is toast. Literally.” She jabbed a finger at his chest. “I’ll follow you whether you like it or not.”

Sigurd pinched the bridge of his nose, muttering something under his breath. “Fine,” he said at last, his voice heavy with resignation. “Just don’t get yourself killed.”

“That’s the plan,” Ada replied with a smirk, already falling into step behind him. “But seriously, you’re *incredibly* reckless for someone funding my future success. Try not to die, alright?”

“Noted,” Sigurd replied dryly as they strode toward the town gates, the looming shadow of the dragon stretching wider with every step.

The dragon roared as it approached the town gates, its jagged scales glinting like shards of obsidian, each movement accompanied by a tremor that threatened to upend the very foundation of Grimroost. It was a creature of nightmares, its glowing molten eyes radiating malice, steam hissing ominously from its nostrils as if the earth itself was its ally. With a single swipe of its massive tail, it toppled a watchtower, sending guards scattering like leaves in a storm.

Sigurd stepped forward, the enchanted sword Zhelyazko crafted glowing faintly in his hand. "Focus on evacuating the civilians," he barked at the guards, his voice cutting through the chaos like a blade. "I’ll handle the dragon."

From behind him, Ada snorted loudly, clearly unimpressed. "You’ll handle the dragon? Oh, sure, let me just sit back and relax while you hack at its ankles like a lumberjack attacking a boulder."

The dragon unleashed a deafening roar, its massive jaws spewing a torrent of molten lava that sizzled across the ground. Sigurd leaped to the side, narrowly avoiding the searing flow. The earth beneath him trembled violently, and with a sickening crack, jagged stone spikes erupted upward, narrowly missing him.

“It’s an earth dragon,” Ada observed, her tone a mix of irritation and reluctant admiration. "Great. We’re fighting the ground itself. It’s using terrain manipulation—quakes, spikes, dust clouds. You’re not going to beat it by brute force alone, big guy. Think smarter, not harder."

Sigurd glanced over his shoulder, dodging another spike as he retorted, “Any useful advice, or are you just here to critique my form?”

The dragon reared back, releasing another deafening roar, its massive claws raking the ground as fissures spread outward. Ada ducked behind a pile of debris, muttering to herself. “Yeah, sure, just keep taunting the walking mountain. That’ll go well."
