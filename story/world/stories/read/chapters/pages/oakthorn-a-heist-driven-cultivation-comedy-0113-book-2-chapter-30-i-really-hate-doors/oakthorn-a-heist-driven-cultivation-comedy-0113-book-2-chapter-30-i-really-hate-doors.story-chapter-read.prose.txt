The chamber still reeked of sacrifice and loss.

Water pooled at Cade’s feet, each ripple a mocking reminder of what had just been stolen from them. His ears rang with the echo of that final door closing—the sound of Jer sealing his fate and the crypt claiming its prize. The sound of failure.

His failure.

“He can’t be gone,” Elena whispered, her voice raw and hollow as she stared at the now-sealed doorway. Droplets fell from her sodden clothes, joining the receding flood in a rhythm that matched the thundering in Cade’s chest. “He can’t be.”

Cade’s fist connected with the obsidian door, the impact sending pain lancing up his arm. The sensation was almost welcome—physical pain to distract from the crushing weight in his chest. “Open, damn you! OPEN!”

The door remained impassive, seamless as if it had never existed at all. As if Jer had never existed.

“We have to get through,” Elena said, her grief transforming into frantic energy as she ran her fingers along the wall, searching for seams, for hidden mechanisms, for anything that might grant them passage back to her twin. “There has to be a way. Magic or—or something. You’re silver-ranked. DO SOMETHING!”

“Don’t you think I’m trying?” Cade snarled, destruction magic flaring around his fists as he slammed them against unyielding stone. The energy dissipated uselessly, not even scoring the ancient surface. “It’s not working! Nothing’s working!”

He drove his magic deeper, calling upon reserves he rarely tapped. Fire and wind coalesced around his hands, forming a drill-like vortex of pure destruction energy. He drove it against the stone with all the force his silver-ranked body could muster.

The magic shattered against the door, rebounding with enough force to throw Cade backward. He landed hard, skidding across wet stone, pain exploding across his back and shoulders.

“Useless,” Elena spat, the word dripping with venom as she glared down at him. “All that power, and you can’t even open a fucking door. What good are you?”

The accusation cut deeper than any blade. Because she was right. What good was all this power if he couldn’t save those who mattered most?

First Orro, vanished without a trace, likely drowned in the chaos of their escape or swept into some forgotten corner of this nightmare labyrinth. Now Jer, sacrificed to the crypt’s insatiable hunger for life. Two of his crew. Two of his family.

Gone, while he lived. While he failed.

“We need to keep moving,” he forced himself to say, the words tasting like ash on his tongue as he pushed himself to his feet. “Waiting here won’t bring them back.”

“So we just leave them?” Elena’s laugh held no humor, just brittle, breaking grief. “Walk away like they meant nothing?”

“They meant EVERYTHING!” The shout tore from Cade’s throat, raw and bleeding with emotion he couldn’t contain. His magic pulsed with each heartbeat, tiny flames flickering around his clenched fists. “But we can’t help them by dying here too. The only way forward is... forward.”

For a moment, he thought she might refuse—thought the grief that clouded her eyes would overwhelm reason, driving her to stay in this forsaken chamber until the crypt claimed her too. Then, with visible effort, she straightened her shoulders and nodded once, a quick jerking motion that looked physically painful.

“Fine,” she said, the word clipped and cold. “Let’s go. But don’t pretend this is about helping them. This is about the Crown. About your precious mission. It always has been.”

Each syllable landed like a knife between his ribs. Not because of the anger behind them, but because of the seed of truth they contained. How many more would he sacrifice before this was over? How many more lives would his quest consume?

They made their way back in silence, the only sounds their footsteps echoing against ancient stone and the occasional drip of water from their sodden clothes. The crypt seemed to watch them, patient and predatory, as if savoring their grief before demanding more.

When they reached the others, Cade’s heart clenched at the tableau of devastation before him. Rayka sat with her back against the wall, tears streaming silently down her face as she stared unseeing at her hands. Nora stood rigid, her greatsword planted before her like an anchor, knuckles white around the hilt. Gav held Chubbs close, the telepath’s eyes amber with barely contained rage as he murmured soft assurances to the toddler goddess.

No one asked about Jer or Orro. The emptiness where they should have been spoke volumes.

“We need to move,” Cade announced, forcing authority into his voice despite the hollow ache in his chest. “Bernard’s still ahead of us. The Crown’s still waiting. We finish this, then we get out.”

“And Jer?” Rayka’s voice cracked on the name. “Orro?”

Cade met her gaze, unable to offer the comfort she sought. “We can’t help them now.”

“Can’t or won’t?” Elena muttered, just loud enough for all to hear.

Cade ignored the barb, though it lodged beneath his skin like a splinter. “Every minute we spend here is another minute for Bernard to reach the Crown. Another minute for this place to spring more traps, demand more sacrifices.”

“He’s right,” Nora said, her voice steady despite the grief etched into the lines of her face. “We honor their sacrifice by finishing what we started. By surviving.”

Rayka nodded, wiping tears from her face with shaking hands. “Orro would say the same. Focus on the mission. Complete the task.” A broken laugh escaped her. “He’d hate that I’m crying instead of moving.”

“Jer would make some stupid joke about dramatic exits,” Elena added, her voice brittle. “Probably something about making a splash.”

The weak attempt at humor fell flat, but it served its purpose—a reminder of what they’d lost, of why they needed to continue. For them. Because of them.

“Let’s go,” Cade said, extending a hand to help Rayka to her feet. “One foot in front of the other. That’s all we need to do right now.”

They moved as a unit, each step leaden with grief but propelled by determination—or perhaps simply momentum, the inability to do anything but continue forward when going back was impossible. Cade took point, his silver-ranked senses scanning for dangers as they navigated the twisting corridors that seemed to pulse with malevolent awareness.

The crypt felt different now, as if Jer’s sacrifice had triggered something fundamental within its ancient architecture. The bioluminescent fungi that illuminated their path flickered with new intensity, casting writhing shadows that seemed to move with purpose across water-slick stone.

“Do you feel that?” Gav asked, his voice hushed as they rounded a corner into a wider passage. “Something’s changed. The energy... it’s shifting.”

Cade nodded, relief momentarily displacing grief at the confirmation he wasn’t imagining things. “It’s waking up. Whatever this place truly is, it’s responding to something.”

Elena fingers ghosted over her twin’s matching amulet—a simple wooden charm he’d carved for them both years ago.

Before Cade could respond, a pulse of energy rippled through the stone beneath their feet—subtle at first, then growing in intensity with each passing second. Ancient runes carved into the walls began to glow with ethereal blue light, illuminating in sequence like a wave spreading outward from some unseen epicenter.

“What is that?” Rayka breathed, instinctively drawing closer to Nora as the pulse quickened.

“Nothing good,” Cade replied grimly, his destruction magic rising in response to the threat. “Stay close. Be ready for anything.”

The runes formed patterns across the ceiling and floor now, interlocking symbols that pulsed in rhythm with the crypt’s heartbeat. The corridor ahead widened suddenly, opening into a vast chamber they hadn’t seen before—or perhaps it had been hidden beneath layers of ancient magic now stripped away by whatever power surged through the structure.

At the chamber’s center stood a massive circular door, its surface carved with symbols that matched the awakening runes. Energy rolled from it in palpable waves, distorting the air like heat rising from summer-baked stone.

“Evie...” Nora’s warning came too late.

The siren stepped forward as if in a trance, her eyes fixed on the door with an intensity that sent chills racing along Cade’s spine. Before any of them could stop her, she raised her head and began to sing—not her usual bubbly melodies, but something ancient and haunting that seemed to resonate with the very stones around them.

“Beneath the crown of souls untold,

A King of Ash shall rise.

His heart consumed by vengeance cold,

His kingdom built on lies.”

Her voice carried power unlike anything Cade had heard before, each syllable resonating with the glowing runes. Her eyes had gone distant, filled with an otherworldly light that transformed her usual vibrant sea-green irises into twin galaxies of swirling stars.

“The blood of gods in mortal veins,

Shall feed the ancient tree.

As death and life their dance maintain,

Creation’s child shall see.”

Nora moved to stand protectively before her, greatsword raised high, a barrier of flesh and steel against whatever waited beyond that doorway. But Evie continued, her voice rising in both volume and intensity as the prophecy poured from her lips. Cade started when he glimpsed thin streams of blood descend out of Evie’s nostrils.

“When chaos sleeps within the flame,

And order breaks its chains,

The child of ruin speaks the name,

That only one remains.”

Evie started to float into the air right as the circular door began to move. The massive structure rumbled through the crypt with the sound of grinding stone against stone as it slowly rotated. As it opened, it revealed the chamber beyond one sliver at a time. Cade motioned for the others to prepare, his own magic gathering around his fists in coils of destructive potential.

“The throne of oak shall bloom again,

In soil fed by death.

The forgotten god reclaims his reign,

With borrowed crown and breath.”

With a final, shuddering groan, the door completed its rotation, fully exposing the next room. Evie, who’s collar was now stained with her own blade, began to descend out of the sky in a heap, Nora catching her before she hit the ground.

Cade’s breath caught in his throat at the sight that greeted them.

The room was vast, its ceiling lost in shadows far above. Ancient columns carved with scenes of battle and sacrifice supported massive stone arches that seemed to bend inward under the weight of centuries. At the center stood a dais, elevated above the main floor and surrounded by channels that pulsed with liquid energy—not water, but something more primal, more alive.

Along the base of the elevated platform, four of seven slots where filled with glowing orbs ranging in vibrancy and color.

And atop that dais...

Bernard.

The gold-ranked earth mage stood triumphant, his massive frame silhouetted against a backdrop of swirling energies that put Cade’s destruction magic to shame. In his hands rested an object that radiated power—an intricate crown fashioned from metal so dark it seemed to drink the light from the very air around it.

The Soulkeeper’s Crown.

The gang leader’s eyes found Cade’s across the distance separating them, triumph blazing in their depths with the intensity of a bonfire. His lips curved around his mustache in a predatory smile that contained no warmth, only savage satisfaction. By his side, the five Stone Britches members made a defensive line between Cade and Bernard, weapons already drawn. They looked utterly and completely… Fine.

Cade hated them for that.

“I win,” Bernard declared proudly, the words echoing with terrible finality through the chamber.

And before Cade could move, before he could summon his magic or cry out in warning, Bernard placed the crown upon his head.
