Chapter 195 / B3-40: Epic Rewards

“You’re frowning again,” Kenda stated as they reached Founder’s Plaza. “What is it this time?”

“Sorry. Just thinking of all the stuff going on today, and I still haven’t figured out how not to level up during the ceremony with Mercy Suku and Margen. I need to stay level seventeen for eight more days, or I’ll miss a use of [Anyone].”

“Just use a [Potion of Level Lock], Joe. They are common enough that we could have grabbed one on the way back, but I know there are plenty at the guild. Wait, wait, wait,” she chirped, switching her focus to her spatial object. “I do still have one. Here,” she said, offering Joe a simple potion bottle filled with a red fluid.

[Level Lock Draught] (Item: Consumable - Uncommon): For 24 hours, you will not gain experience. {Time}

“Why? Why would you have this?” he stammered, a bit confused, wondering why people would want to sabotage their experience gains.

“Oh, there are all sorts of cases where you want to put off leveling. Sometimes, you are better off not crossing certain thresholds when trying to master a crafting process or martial maneuver. Plus, there are some achievements you can only get if you are under a certain level. There are also orders that you can only join as a novice,” the savant declared.

“I bought that when I was trying to meld two class skills before I leveled,” she continued. “I needed the combined skill to make the next skill I wanted available on leveling. I managed to get the meld after the first day, but I had bought a couple just to be safe. They are a good thing to have. You definitely should grab some for [Anyone].”

“This is perfect. Thanks, Kenda,” Joe expressed, while he stowed the bottle into his [Alchemy Belt].

As he reached for the door to the guild house, a smug window popped into the corner of his eye.

How hard was that, Joe?

“Yeah. Yeah. That was easier than I thought it would be,” he thought back as he held the door for Kenda.

“Well, don’t you clean up nice,” Myllo complimented on seeing Joe in his new outfit. The guild captain was in the atrium as they entered the building. “We got a couple of hours between now and our meeting with my cousin Dorian, try not to get too scuffed up in the interim. And maybe try and run a brush through that tangle on yer head.”

“I was going to tackle his hair after you two met,” Kenda admitted.

“Guys! I can brush my own hair.”

“That has not been our experience. At least I ain’t never seen it in anything other than its current shaggy state,” Myllo teased. “But I trust our lass will get it in hand before we go. So, are you ready to talk rewards?”

“I am,” Joe blurted excitedly. He knew exactly what he wanted for his epic reward. He had both [Bring Him Home] and [Plug the Leak], but he only turned in the first one since it did not have an experience point reward. [Plug the Leak] would bring him way too close to eighteenth level with still days of cooldown left on [Anyone].

BRING HIM HOME (Epic)

You have found the lost Founder of Fort Coral, Margen the Mighty. Bring him back to Fort Coral alive or contained within the legendary sword, DoomHerald.

Reward: +2,000 Reputation with Fort Coral, 7,000 gold, and one Epic-level reward of your choice from the city’s Adventurer’s Guild.

PLUG THE LEAK (Rare)

You have discovered that the source of the necromantic malignancy is the breach in the wards around the valley of Glen Cumha, also known as Margen’s Mourn. Will you stop the corruption from spreading across the Hornlands?

+50 Reputation with Fort Coral

Quest completion reward: 900 exp.

The coins appeared in a tiny pouch, which Joe opened to find four glittering discs that seemed to send all of Joe’s thoughts scattering to the wind. Each dragongeld coin was worth a thousand gold pieces. Three of them were golden in color, but they possessed a gold so luminous that it made actual gold look like cheap brass. There were two red ones that put rubies to shame, and a green one that gave Wen’s eye a run for their money. The last piece of geld was a black one that made Joe feel like he was staring into a void.

“Oh wow! I was not ready for those,” he sighed. He had always wanted a dragongeld coin ever since he had seen Edror get some on the day he joined the Fort Coral Guild, but they were not overly practical. He decided to keep one and sent the pitch-black one into his new dimbag with a thought. He handed the rest to Kenda. “I owe the guild forty-six-eighty. Can I get thirteen-twenty back in elemental coins and gold, please?”

“Sure,” she replied. “Given your killer earth affinity, how about Terrium chuns?”

“Sounds great. Thanks. “ Turning to Myllo, Joe took a breath. He was a bit nervous about stating his wish out loud. “Okay. My idea for the epic reward is probably going to be a bit unusual. Hopefully it’s possible.”

“Epic rewards are the largest this guild can hand out, so there ain’t much that falls outside their limits. What are ya thinking, son?”

“Living space. Currently, I have an apartment, but if I am going to be working with Finn and the rest of the akhluts, I need to be on the shore. I also want a place where Yuk will feel at home. Did you know the poor guy lives in abandoned basements and storm drains?”

“Oh, Murrcee,” Kenda sighed. ‘We didn’t know.”

“That boy hides more of himself than a squirrel and its nuts,” the korrigan huffed. “But now that you say so, I can’t say I’m surprised. Well, we can see what is available in Netting and Seaside, but those are some pretty busy districts. You won't find it relaxing, and your neighbors will not appreciate you bringing them brutes into either harbor.”

“Actually, that is not exactly my idea. Could I commission something to be built as my reward? I was thinking of a houseboat. Something I could park just offshore most of the time, but something that could be moved further out into the bay when I want to work with the akhluts.”

Joe watched the guild artificer’s eyes literally begin to sparkle. Twinkles of fey-lights also flickered through his massive mane of hair and down through his beard. His furred lip churled into a wide smile. “Now that’s a fun challenge!’ the stocky man declared. Out of nowhere, he produced a large roll of parchment and was about to spread it out on the front desk when he reconsidered and headed for the library instead. “I think we can keep this inside an epic, depending on how many bells and whistles we add.”

Myllo unrolled the poster-sized page on one of the tables and summoned pens and rulers from his spatial storage. He drew a waterline and began sketching. The first two sets of lines he must not have liked because with a wave of his hand, the marks vanished from the page and the gadgeteer started over.

“So what are you thinking, Joe?” he started as he drew the outline of a new vessel’s hull.

“Ok, the most important fact is it has to be big enough and sturdy enough to handle akhluts. I was thinking of a forward cabin, big enough for several rooms, and a large back deck, large enough that Finn and friends can come aboard.”

“Akhluts, plural. Eesh. They weigh what … a ton a piece? I think two at a time could be doable. Anything more than that, and you're gonna have to have a monster of a ship. Too big to bring anywhere close to shore.”

“Two is fine. I could settle for just one if we have to.”

“Alright. We’ll balance loads as we go and see what it can bear. What else?”

“Self-repairing and some serious under-armor. I’d hate to go through all this just to have the first monster we encounter sink it.”

“Makes sense. With those caveats, she is not going to be fast, but we can make her sturdy. Any idea what ya might want to name her? A name may give us a feel for her design.”

“I don't know.” Joe hadn’t gotten that far in his thinking. He knew he wanted a place where he, Yuk, and Finn could hang out together. Someplace, Bayu could shout her heart out, too. The houseboat concept had occurred to him yesterday and had been slowly gaining traction in his mind. Yet until now it had all been theoretical. It wasn't until now, looking at Myllo’s emerging blueprints, that this idea was becoming a reality. “Hmm. Slow and steady …” he uttered, seeing a new hull shape take form on the page. “I like that one, Myllo. How about ‘Turtle’ or ‘Seaturtle’, or something like that,” he suggested. “You know, slow and sturdy.”

“I like it. Oooh. That gives me an idea! Kenda, do me a favor, lass. Go see if Puqmup is around and ask him if we still have that dragon turtle shell in the vault. If we never sold it, then that’s your hull right there, Joe! You ain’t gonna get much tougher than the shell of one of them. Even leviathans can’t crack dragon turtle shells.”

Kendell threw her boss a salute before squeezing Joe’s shoulder and darting off. She looked just as excited by this idea as Joe and Myllo were.

“Oh, that sounds awesome,” Joe beamed.

He had never had a chance to watch Myllo work and found the man was an amazing artist. The artificer barely needed a ruler to draw perfectly straight lines. He only used one when he was whipping out a whole bunch of them right next to each other. Myllo etched out an upside-down turtle shell in just a few minutes, cross-hatching included.

When a tree emerged at the back of the boat, Joe cleared his throat. “What is that for?” he asked.

“I have an idea. Did you know that the undine, one of the seafolk races, have floating little forests that bob their way around in the Dragon Sea?” After Joe shook his head ‘no’, Myllo continued. “Undine Groves are made up of brineroot mangrove trees whose roots are all woven together. Their complex root structures are perfect for spreading centralized enchantments throughout the whole grove. We can use the same idea to bind this vessel together. The tree will be the hub of your warding and the roots will be used to tie the shell, deck, and cabin together.”

“Ok, that is really cool.”

“There are some other advantages here as well. Your proficiency with primal magic should make it easy for you to learn a druid spell or two to maintain the health of the tree,” the guild leader stated, holding up one finger. He extended a second one as he added, “The brineroot mangroves draw all the nutrients they need from seawater. As a bonus, they leave behind freshwater, which you could gather up if you want to save yourself a Sweetwater enchantment.”

Another finger was raised. ‘Thirdly, we can use the tree’s weight to balance a larger cabin up front. I think we should add a bit of dimensional sod around the tree as well,” he suggested, quickly flicking some lines to represent grass at the back of the boat. “It will only be an inch thick in weight, but we could give you at least a foot of spatial depth in case you wanted to plant something around the tree.”

“The big drawback, though, is if the tree dies, so does the ship. Yet I still think it makes sense for you. You would be healing your ship if it gets damaged rather than mending it. I think that plays into your strengths.”

‘Seriously, Myllo,” Joe breathed, staring at the drawing. ‘That is so much better than anything I imagined. Are you sure it’s not too much? I can’t afford anything much beyond the epic reward.” Joe had a little over two grand in gold pieces to his name, and it looked like Myllo wasn’t pulling out any stops.

“It’s pushing what would typically be considered an epic item. We can definitely get it built. On the downside, we are going to have to go light on the enchantments for now and beef them up over time. You being a warder helps. You’ll have to keep refreshing the base-level wards until you level up your warding or can afford high-level ones. But the warding structure will all be there. You’ll just have to upgrade it as you can.”

“I can live with that. It will give me something to keep practicing my wardcraft on.”

“Alright, then. I’ll get the shipwrights working on her. Once they find out that this is for the team who rescued Margen, I’m positive they’ll burn the lanterns to get her done in no time at all. Hopefully, by the time it comes back to me for the magical integration, you have settled on her name.”
