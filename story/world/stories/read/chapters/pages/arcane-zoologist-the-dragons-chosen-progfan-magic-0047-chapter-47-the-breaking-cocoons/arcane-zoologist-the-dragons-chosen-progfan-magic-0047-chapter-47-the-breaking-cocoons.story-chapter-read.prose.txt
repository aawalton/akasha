"I'm fine, Dad. I really am," Callie tried to calm Dad, who was fussing over Callie, wiping the bits of blood away from her nose.

"Like hell you are! Your nose is bleeding!" Dad roared.

"Rowan…" Mom gave Dad a significant look, indicating that he wasn't speaking as nicely as he should.

Dad cleared his throat. "Sorry. But look! The magic made her nose bleed. It's harming her."

"Dad, it's not harming me. I just pushed myself a little too hard," Callie said.

"She had been going at it for a while, trying to grow one carrot after the next," Callen added. "I never thought it would exhaust her so much."

"I admittedly don't know much about nature magic. It might require more energy to get things done, and Callie is still small. It wouldn't be surprising for her to have pushed past her limit without even noticing," Mom reasoned.

"Maeve, it's too dangerous! We can't let her use it anymore," Dad said firmly.

"NO! I can't leave glimmerhares to starve. And we still need those mushrooms for Callen's coat," Callie said. The dizzy spell she had for a moment had already left her, and while she felt tired, it wasn't as bad as Dad was making it out to be.

"We can't risk your health for the sake of a coat!" Dad objected.

"I am fine!" Callie insisted.

"Enough arguing," Mom glared at Dad. "We're clearly not getting anywhere like this. We're going to consult Granny Ethel on this matter. If she believes the magic is taking a toll on Callie's health, we'll stop. If not, then we'll let her continue to grow carrots with magic."

"Maeve, you can't be serious!"

"Yes, I am, Rowan. Look at this," Mom said at the row of carrots on the table: rotten one, overgrown one, dry one, and the perfect one. "She's actually learning to control her magic. This is what she needs. What we need. If growing these carrots helps her understand and control it better, then she needs to keep doing it."

Dad didn't look too pleased. "Only if Ethel is absolutely sure that it isn't impacting Callie in a negative way."

"Of course," Mom agreed.

So Callie was taken to Granny Ethel for a thorough examination. They explained the situation to her, and Callie didn't hold back in explaining about the glimmerhare colony she found and the arcane beast's voice she had heard in the swamp. Granny Ethel listened patiently while checking Callie all over.

"She seems to be in perfect health, only exhausted. I suspect it's because she used a bit too much magic in quick succession. She should be completely fine once she rests," Granny Ethel said with a smile.

Both Mom and Dad sighed in relief, although Dad looked somewhat nervous. "Wouldn't it be better for her to stop using magic?"

"Goodness, no! Rowan, you should know that this child needs to learn to control rather than suppress it. Let her grow those carrots, but take it slow. No more today, and tomorrow I suggest you grow no more than two. If you feel fine, then you can try growing more the day after."

"But, Granny Ethel, what about the glimmerhares?" Callie worried.

"I'm sure they'll appreciate it even if you only grow a few carrots per day," the elderly woman said solemnly.

"But merchant Silas is leaving tomorrow, is he not?" Callie looked at her brother.

"The day after tomorrow," Callen corrected, looking uncomfortable. "I'm going to be fine, Callie. Don't worry!"

"No! I'm going to talk to glimmerhare's again. Give them carrots that I grew. Maybe they can bring at least a few more mushrooms."

Mom pondered the matter for a moment, then turned to Dad. "Let's give those glimmerhares a bushel in exchange for the mushrooms."

Dad's eyes went wide. "Maeve, what are you saying?"

"It's not like we're giving away everything we have. We can spare a bushel right now, if Callie can slowly regrow what we're missing. But mushrooms and Callen's coat are not something we can postpone. Let's trade with the glimmerhares and have Callie grow the carrots we need later."

"That's risky. What if she's not able to…" Dad argued.

"I can definitely do it!" Callie insisted.

"Rowan, if you have any other ideas for getting Callen's coat, then please share them. Maybe you will sit inside the whole winter and give him your coat?" Mom argued.

Dad looked torn. He glanced at Callen, then at Callie, and let out a loud sigh. "I… I just don't want Callie to struggle so hard. She's not even ten yet."

"Dad, don't worry about me. If it's for the sake of my brother and for the glimmerhares, I am happy to do it. Besides, Mom and Granny Ethel are right to say that I need magic practice."

Dad glanced around the room again, then finally nodded. "Very well. But if Callie looks too worn out by the magic, we're going to stop."

"I'll pick up more hunts if it comes to that," Mom assured.

"I… I'll join her!" Callen added, albeit reluctantly.

Mom and Dad looked at Callen in surprise.

"I thought you weren't interested in hunting," Mom said. "You've always resisted the idea so much."

"It's because of me that we're in so much trouble. I should do whatever I can to solve it, even if I don't like it," Callen said with his fist tightly clenched.

Callie felt that Callen shouldn't go out to hunt when he clearly didn't want to. But if the family was starving during winter, they wouldn't have much of a choice.

"For the time being, pick up whatever skills you can from that merchant. Let's leave hunting as the last resort."

So the family had made up their mind. The next day, first thing in the morning, Callie and Callen headed back to the glimmerhare colony with a bushel of carrots in tow. They made sure to leave early in the morning, so the neighbors wouldn't see them. It would be difficult to explain why Callen was carrying a whole bushel of carrots into the forest.

"Snowglow, Snowglow!" Callie called once she was inside the dragon's cave.

It felt eerie in the early morning dark, so when Snowglow squeezed through the opening with her antlers glowing, Callie felt immense relief.

"I bought a whole bushel of carrots for your colony! Would this be enough?" Callie asked.

Snowglow stared at the carrots in amazement.

So many…

"So it's enough?"

Yes! Yes! I tell everyone!

Snowglow disappeared back into the hole, leaving Callie alone in the dark for a moment. But it wasn't for long, because soon, the dragon's cave got filled with curious glimmerhares stiffing at the carrots.

Tasty root.

So much.

Yum, yum!

Along with their voices, the glimmerhares also produced a soft cacophony of gentle squeaks. It was the most adorable thing Callie had ever heard.

The leader glimmerhare with the largest antlers hopped closer to Callie.

Thank you. You save colony. We bring mushrooms.

Soon, the cave was teeming with activity as glimmerhares began to drag the carrots inside the tunnels one by one, and also bring out the swamp lantern mushrooms. Callie was amazed at how diligent they were, working as tirelessly as ants.

It took them a while to bring all the carrots into their tunnels, and even longer still until they brought out the last lantern mushroom. There was a small pile of them in front of Callie now, definitely more than enough for Callen's coat.

"Thank you so much, everyone! With this, my brother won't freeze during winter," Callie said to the glimmerhares.

Thank you for tasty root. Little ones eat. Little ones not die!

Snowglow hopped up to Callie happily, looking rather proud.

"There's no way I could just let you all starve! In harsh times such as these, we all have to work together. Help each other," Callie said.

Help each other.

Work together.

The glimmerhares echoed Callie's words as they retreated into their tunnels. Callie gave Snowglow a last friendly pat and called for her brother. He had been waiting patiently by the entrance, so as not to spook the skittish glimmerhares.

"Whoa… That's a lot of mushrooms," Callen noted with a smile. "With those, we might be able to afford more than just a coat. I'm going to bring these to Silas right away."

With that said, the two of them hurried home. Mom and Dad were elated to see so many lantern mushrooms. Even Dad, who looked very unsure about this trade, looked like he was almost smiling.

Callen hurried away to talk with Silas, while Callie decided that it was time to update her journal entry for glimmerhares.

Name: Glimmerhare

Appearance:

Snow white rabbit with deer antlers. During winter, their coat grows thicker, and antlers are covered with lighter fuzz.

Behaviour:

Skittish and shy, glimmerhares prefer to spend their time hidden underground. They live in colonies that work together to secure food.

Abilities:

Glowing antlers

Tunnel digging

Warning:

Very skittish. Approach slowly and cautiously.

Notes:

Released from the trap, healed. Found a starving colony underground. They love carrots

Once she was finished, she showed the entry to Mom and Dad. Dad seemed skeptical while Mom looked at it with genuine curiosity.

"I wonder if they have any other, more magical abilities?" Mom asked.

"Snowglow once said that they can make plants grow, but she was too weak at the time to do it. Maybe I should have asked her?" Callie wondered.

Mom pressed her hand to her chin in thought. "It likely isn't easy for them. Surely, if they could grow plants easily, they wouldn't be threatened by starvation. There must be a limit to their ability."

"Oh! I have to find out!" Callie said, eager to head back to the colony.

"Not today! They worked hard to gather the mushrooms for us. Let them rest. I'm sure when the time is right, you'll see their ability with your own eyes," Mom said, gently patting her hair.

Callie nodded. It was better to let the glimmerhares rest. In the meantime, Callie began sketching her page for frostmoths. They would certainly be worth studying. After all, nobody even thought that they were arcane beasts. So what was it that made them different from other bugs? She had to find out.

Callen returned home about an hour later with a new coat on, grinning from ear to ear. "I got it! Better yet, since we managed to get so many lantern mushrooms, he offered to take me along to town and teach me how to sell them."

Dad frowned. "Going to town? It's risky. The snow isn't too deep yet, but if a snowstorm comes, you might not be able to return home, or even worse…"

Callie instantly clung to her brother. "No! You can't leave!"

"It's alright. We won't be gone for long. For a week at best. Silas said that he needs to stock up before any real blizzards come. He'll be spending the winter in Greyfen and in spring, he'll head far south."

Mom and Dad shared a look. They were doing that silent communication thing again. Callie had to ask one day how they were doing that. Was it magic that only married people could do?

"Very well. We can't exactly forbid you from going, since you are not a child anymore. But you must remain careful," Dad said.

Callen nodded firmly while strutting out his chest. To think that her brother was already an adult. It made Callie sad. She wondered if perhaps Callen would want to leave with the merchant Silas during the spring. It seemed like a real possibility. He was travelling with the merchant more and more often.

But she knew that she didn't have the right to object. Even if she were terribly lonely without her big brother.

When Callen left with Silas the next day, Callie was determined to keep herself busy. She, too, had to grow up and be able to do things without her big brother. So she focused on getting her magic under control.

Each day, she would grow two or three carrots under Mom's or Dad's watchful eye. At first, not every carrot was perfect. Some were a bit too dry. Some were overgrown. But each day, she got a little better. She managed to keep her magic under control until the number of mistakes she made steadily decreased.

"Can I please study the frostmoths now? From a distance! I promise I'll be careful," Callie pleaded to her parents.

They were apprehensive at first, but eventually agreed. Since Callie was making strides in getting her magic under control, they felt it was safe for her to approach frostmoths.

"But from afar! While growing another petal wouldn't be the end of the world since you're learning control, it would be better if you got even better first," Mom said.

Callie was elated. Distance or not, she was able to study the little arcane bugs. At first, they really seemed like any other caterpillar. Crawling on trees and bushes, walls, and pretty much anywhere a caterpillar would.

But what they ate was most peculiar. They would seek out places where frost had formed on trees, bushes, or other bare plants and then eat the said frost. They would eat the snow or ice formed on windows or other surfaces. Only on plants. It made Callie wonder if there was something special about that frost.

Ten days after Callen had left, Callie began to worry about her brother. He should have been back by now. Mom and Dad said that it wasn't unusual for them to be back late. One could never account for road conditions during winter. There just might be more snow near Drymon. But Callie was still worried.

"I hope Callen returns soon," Callie whispered as she went to observe the frost caterpillar again. There was a bit of free time before she had to go to Granny Ethel's.

But something weird was going on. Instead of climbing up on the plants, they were all climbing down, settling down in nooks of roots or houses. Weirder still, some of the frost caterpillars had begun to emerge from their cocoons.

Not as frostmoths, no. They were breaking out of half-formed cocoons and crawling down to the ground. Callie had never seen them behave this way before.

When Callie went to the healer's hut later, she told Granny Ethel about this observation.

"They're just bugs. Who cares what they do? Maybe they just went crazy. Stupid bugs," Tristan chortled.

Callie glared at him.

Granny Ethel, on the other hand, nearly dropped the wicker basket she had been holding.

"Granny Ethel, are you alright?" Callie ran up to her, as did Tristan. Granny Ethel was far from young, so they worried that something might have happened to her. The cold wasn't exactly pleasant, even with the fire going.

"Kids, you have to go around the village and warn everyone. A blizzard is coming. That is the only reason why frost caterpillars would break out of their own cocoons before being fully grown."

Callie felt her hand turn as cold as ice. A blizzard? But her brother still hadn't returned home!
