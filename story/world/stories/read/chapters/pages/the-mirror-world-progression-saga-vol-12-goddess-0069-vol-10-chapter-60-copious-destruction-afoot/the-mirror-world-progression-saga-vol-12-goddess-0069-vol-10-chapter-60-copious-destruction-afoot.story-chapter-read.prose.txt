Samantha zoomed out over the apocalyptic scene, the torn asunder, scorched ground seen in all directions, the environment smoking hot with continually falling, smoldering rocks and cinders. It was much like hell had rained down on Matrazere.

It pretty much had.

Every soldier was crouched down and huddling fearfully behind shields, whether physical or magical. An army of zombies was one thing to the common soldier; the fury and wrath of one’s world ripping apart was quite another. No fear was more primal than that summoned by what seemed an act of deity. Perhaps they wondered if their goddess was demonstrating her wrath? Fear and humility were all the more logical to display in such a case.

Fortunately, the all-out defense mode had protected everyone. They’d just had to huddle up, shrinking the total size of the army.

The reaction of zombie-kind was mixed, though many remained down. If not torn apart or damaged by the destruction they’d gotten no protection against, most were simply not inclined to rise. What few did were standing or meandering around aimlessly. No red eyes glowed any longer.

Samantha quickly arrived at the place of the battle with the undead champion, but the battle was well and truly over. The sword and the head of a whip were lying on the ground, the champion nowhere to be seen, presumably having simply ceased to exist without the mechanisms of magical control coming from the wizards of the tower.

He was simply a personification of this army. Now, it has no soul to speak of. It doesn’t have a purpose — not even survival — and so it doesn’t exist.

For a brief moment, it finally hit her: they’d won. Matrazere and all of its denizens were gone. Disintegrated. And she’d crippled much of the Dominion. Likely, all that could truly challenge her now was the Capital, as well as who waited there.

I’ve already accomplished the impossible. Why not again? Who can stop us?

Despite the inclinations of celebration, there were still things to do.

First, she rushed over to the remains of Nyomel, finding a shriveled-up giant snake’s carcass still resisting total disintegration. But unlike the others around who couldn’t see, Samantha detected a spark of life in its belly.

She ripped through the carcass and found the mutilated, blackened body of a strange, malformed creature inside. Not pulling her out, Samantha instead utilized [Spellfyr: Cleanse], instantly wiping away all detrimental status effects, including Entropy-born ‘permanent’ damage, and then healing the creature completely.

It shot awake with a terrible gasp, and shocked eyes met Samantha’s. Quickly, the twisted shape morphed back into the familiar form of Nyomel. “Thank you,” she breathed. “D-Deikmorn?”

Those impossibly hopeful eyes pierced Samantha’s heart, even through her mirrors. It was only worse through the Third Eye. She felt the pain acutely. Nyomel knew, yet the words came out of the shapeshifter’s mouth anyway, because here she was, staring at a goddess who’d miracle-worked her from the brink of annihilation. Why not her lover, too?

The voice within Samantha spoke again. ‘Sacrifices are unavoidable; costs for everything. So the natural order demands. We take wounds for our children, but they also take them for us. Gladly, in both cases, tragic though this reality is.’

Samantha shook her head, both in answer and to shake herself free of the voice. “He’s dead. I can’t save the dead.” As Nyomel squeezed her eyes shut, spilling fresh tears, Samantha continued, “However… it was just his body, and he was a Follower. His soul remains in my grasp. You can go to him in Heaven.”

Her eyes shot back open to stare. “Me?”

“As if you haven’t done enough for me?” As if her mistress hasn’t done enough. Now I feel like an ass for even being mad at this one. Me and my petty insecurities. Samantha scoffed and held out her hand. “Come on. I think you’ll want to witness what is going to happen here. You missed the fireworks, but someone will catch you up.”

Blinking in confusion, Nyomel took her hand and nodded. “Why are your eyes closed and bleeding?”

“Long story,” Samantha muttered. She helped the shapeshifter out of the carcass. Because Nyomel’s — ahem — eye-catching form was nude, Samantha utilized [Conjure] to cover her in a robe. As shell-shocked as she remained, the Naugite didn’t comment on it, nor even seem to notice.

As Nyomel looked around in complete bewilderment, Samantha left her to the Resemblants to deal with, because she had others to help.

Zadkiel had somehow held on, his servants having dragged his gravely injured body away from the reach of the wraiths. Samantha utilized [Spellfyr: Cleanse] on him, too, to preserve his Avatar before it succumbed to the continual Entropic damage.

“Thanks,” he said, looking down at himself in amazement as he rose from the ground, now completely hale and healthy after being paralyzed and overcome with black rot. “That was… unpleasant. And Red-” He cut himself off, grimaced, and let out a breath with a growl. “Damn it. I failed her…”

Samantha floated up to touch his shoulder and offer a supportive smile. “She’ll be okay. I have to go help others, Z-man.”

He nodded, and she zipped away. She saved a few more lives right at the brink due to Entropy damage, including a minotaur hero who dropped himself prostrate in the dirt as he thanked and worshiped her for her miracle work.

Yeah, these minotaurs overdo it a bit, but that’s their culture, I guess.

Running low on SP, Samantha held off on anyone who could survive the deterioration, as her top healers could probably handle it in the long-term. If not, she’d regenerate SP over time.

Meanwhile, the environment was finally clearing up. Smoke was dissipating, fires were going out (thankfully, vegetation was almost nil in the area), and the sun was coming out of hiding for the first time.

As the area around the crater became clearer, all eyes gravitated there. The buildings of before were long gone, incinerated completely. But over the top of the crater, stone was flowing through the air as if molten, coming from all angles and pooling together, like a great nexus of many rivers of matter returning to their source.

All the heroes around ended up gathering close as they watched.

“Something tells me that’s all from the meteors,” Dax commented.

“Do we need to worry?” Gelmak asked in confusion.

“I’d say we can take it,” Keinaz bellowed, hefting his greatsword. “If it wants to start something after stealing our glory, that is!”

There were numerous scoffs and chuckles. Zadkiel remained subdued but said, “After what happened, nothing is going to want to face me right now, but I’d be bloody happy to redeem myself.”

“You did admirably,” Odysseus said, leaning on his sword in front of him, staring dead ahead at the empty space where the tower had been. “It’s no shame to falter against that monstrosity. There was no defeating it except to destroy the source. I stand in disbelief still, that this actually occurred…”

Keinaz studied him. “I heard him call you Palthennius. A name well-despised among fae-kind.”

Many heads turned at that.

Odysseus sighed. “It is not my name any longer. Nonetheless, for my sins, I imagine many of you would want my head. I’m not sure I should deny it. Seeing this… I’ve accomplished what I set out to do. There’s nothing left for me in this world. My actions made this grave, formed of all who trusted me…”

An awkward silence persisted.

Canmore, his armor scorched, walked over, and Odysseus turned to face him. Canmore took off his helmet, revealing the bedraggled, busted-up face of the former mercenary. He stared across at the still-helmeted man soberly. “I’d say you’ve proven and redeemed yourself, whatever your origins. I’ve fought on both sides, too, you know. So has Daxerris, our general, and many others. We’re not so different. I’d be happy to fight alongside you all the way to the end. How about a new quest, Heaven Knight? Showing the true Heaven to a place that claims that source falsely.” He held out his hand. “That’s our destination, after all, isn’t it? After the grave.”

Odysseus stared at the hand, and then pulled off his own helm, meeting Canmore’s eyes. His face was close to a mirror of the other in the signs of toil, weariness, and injury. He took the hand, and they shook, and new fire seemed to enter the former Heaven Knight’s eyes. “A new quest it is, Sir Canmore. A final one, to the Capital, where it all began… and ends.”

In the spirit, Samantha saw significance in it that transcended the moment. In many past lives, these two, her ‘children’ in a way, had fought and been rivals — in every previous life, one way or another, she was certain they had. Such was their destiny, and they would never clasp hands like that before it was decided who was superior, either to become foe or ally. In this world, if that had been tested, Canmore would likely be dead, as the other was much more accomplished.

But they had not truly crossed blades in this way… not on Calrenazzod. Azure had fought him instead. She’d interrupted destiny.

It filled Samantha with a strange inner hope. The cycle can be broken! Here is the proof.

She wiped her face, wondering why she was crying. But she wasn’t — it was fresh blood still pouring out.

We take wounds for our children, was it?

They all watched as the stone formed into the silhouette of an enormous serpent the size of a building. In the spirit, Samantha saw the serpent with many heads, but rapidly merging before her eyes.

The physical form began to shrink and compact itself with rapid grinding sounds, smaller and smaller, becoming a less distinct form, even as it began floating and moving toward them, over the inactive remnants of the zombie army. As it did, it slowly lowered and took on more humanoid characteristics.

Finally, the familiar form of the gorgeous, super-muscular gorgon Samantha had once met took shape and landed with a thunderous impact, even being only a meter above the ground. Her foot ‘happened’ to come down on a zombie skull, smashing it into a spray of gore like a grape.

Nude, the gorgon strolled up casually to them, waving and smiling. “Hail and well met, all! It’s an honor to be here.”
