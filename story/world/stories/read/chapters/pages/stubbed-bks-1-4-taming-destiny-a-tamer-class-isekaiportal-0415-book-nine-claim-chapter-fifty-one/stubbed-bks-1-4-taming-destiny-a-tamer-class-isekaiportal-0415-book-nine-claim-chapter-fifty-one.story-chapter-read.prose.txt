I stand once more in front of the wooden wall that I was admiring earlier. I’m inspecting it closely again, but this time I’m not marvelling at its defences or the speed at which they had grown. Instead, I’m looking at the bloodstains spread across it with a sinking feeling in my stomach.

Though, despite the subject matter, I have to admit that there’s an artistry to the display, something more than just the random splatter of death. These bloodstains that shine wetly in the early morning light are purposeful.

I turn to the captain who had been in charge of the watch at the time of the attack.

“And you say you didn’t hear anything at all until the screams?”

“No, my lord,” the captain confirms. “It was silent. Still. And then suddenly they cut through the pre-dawn darkness – ear-piercing cries, unmistakable. When we hurried to the gates, we found the four I’d stationed there – dead. And…you see the results here, my lord.”

I nod, not needing any of my Skills to know that he’s telling the truth. He’s shaken, though he’s no stranger to death or injury. The sheer speed of these murders, and the deliberate nature of the bloodstains, are clearly affecting him, as they are the rest of us.

I exchange a look with the lords behind me who came as well. Only Josiah stayed on the ships, just in case this is a distraction and we’re attacked from the sea while investigating.

“Thoughts?” I ask.

Valence crouches beside one of the bodies. They’ve been laid out in front of the gate in a neat row – that’s how the captain found them.

“Could one of the other candidates have found us?” Layton wonders, concern in his voice. “It seems unlikely, but…could this really be a beast’s work? It seems too subtle.” He glances down at one of the men and shakes his head, a sorrowful expression crossing his face. “Poor Talin. I overheard him saying only a few days ago that he was planning on proposing to his sweetheart – a seamstress he met on the ship. A life cut too short.”

I sigh heavily and nod in agreement, my eyes shifting between the grey-faced carcasses. I can’t put any names to their faces, but they’re all at least vaguely familiar. How many of the others have a similar story to Talin?

Valence rises to his feet.

“It would be subtle for a beast on Moriax, perhaps. But I’m fairly sure I know what did this.” He looks at me grimly. “The Tier three we were talking about – the one my scouts are almost certain is the Beast Lord of the area. These claw marks match what we’ve seen on trees around here – warning signs to other beasts threatening to encroach on its territory, we assumed.”

My mouth settles into a grim line.

“You think this is a warning?”

Valence nods.

“If it killed four this quickly, it could have killed more.”

“It only attacked non-Classers,” Layton interjects. “If it had attacked Classers, it would have been a different story.”

“But that just indicates intelligence, doesn’t it?” Elarion points out evenly. “We know beasts can distinguish Classers from non-Classers. The fact that it targeted non-Classers in this warning suggests strategy.”

“And the way it arranged the bodies in a line and decorated the gates with their blood suggests more than just basic animal cunning,” I add.

Elarion nods in agreement.

I sigh again. Intelligence is always the most dangerous factor, in my experience. A powerful but mindless beast is far easier to defeat than an intelligent enemy capable of planning.

It seems this Tier Three is a more urgent issue than I’d hoped.

I was planning on trying to negotiate with it, but this ‘warning’ of killing four of our own makes me disinclined to do so. But I can’t let my dismay at losing guards sway me towards a decision that might mean losing more. The fact is that we know little about this creature. We don’t even know what it looks like, yet it clearly knows enough about us to create this display. If we go in, weapons raised and blood boiling, we might lose far more than four.

And, while this attack has proven it can be brutal, it also proves that it has restraint and intelligence. Negotiations, while less immediately satisfying, might prove to be better for us in the long run. However much that grates as I stand here next to the bodies of the men it has killed.

I breathe in and out slowly, trying to force my frustration away. The space my breath gives me is enough to consider how it might be from the Tier three’s point of view. If, before we got to know each other, I’d entered Kalanthia’s territory with a group of dangerous fighters as I have here, I have no doubt that her response would have been less measured. She slaughtered the raptorcat pack, after all, and I’m still not sure whether that was in defence of Lathani or me. And yet, she has proven to be an excellent ally. I’m not necessarily hopeful that this might become the same, but I shouldn’t act in a way that precludes the possibility.

Anyway, if the beast rejects my attempt at negotiation, then our position won’t be much worse than it already is. In fact, if we can learn more about its capabilities and nature during the discussion, we might even come out ahead.

However, that still leaves the question of how exactly to contact it in order to begin those negotiations.

“I need to arrange a meeting,” I murmur. “The question is how to get the message across.”

“A meeting? With the beast?” Elarion sounds incredulous. He gestures at the bodies. “When it did this?”

“I understand how you feel,” I sigh, then explain my thoughts. When I’m done, Elarion looks thoughtful, Valence’s face is troubled, and Layton’s expression is mostly blank, though with a crease between his brows.

Silence extends between us, broken finally by Elarion nodding slightly.

“The beast has left a message here for us. Perhaps to contact it again, we should leave a similar message of our own?” he suggests thoughtfully. “It killed four of our own and laid them out; perhaps we should kill four beasts and lay them out similarly?”

“And put them where?” Layton questions, his tone demonstrating how unhappy he is with the idea. “The beast left four of our guards’ bodies lying in a row in front of our gates; we should lay them somewhere equally impactful. Yet we know not where its den is.”

Elarion turns to look at Valence questioningly. The heir shakes his head in response to the wordless inquiry.

“We’ve followed its tracks all over this territory, but we haven’t found anything that looks like a den. It’s been careful to keep that hidden.” He glances back at the wall. “And judging by the way it approached this situation, that caution isn’t accidental.”

“I’d agree completely,” I say, nodding at Valence. “Given the subject matter, I think we need to get an expert on the case.”

“An expert?” Layton asks warily.

I give a small smile and put up a finger to request silence while I reach out mentally for Bastet. She’s currently out with a scouting party. Just as Valence has been sending out parties to explore the local area, my companions have been conducting their own hunting expeditions to learn more about the wider territory.

But the moment she senses that I need her, I feel Bastet turn in the air and begin flying back to me. With her come Noir and Ivor, her companions on this particular excursion.

What is it? Bastet asks urgently.

I explain the situation, sending her images of the bloodstained gateway before me. I also bring Kalanthia into the conversation. I don’t know exactly where she is or why – she’s been secretive about her objective and I have forced myself not to pry. The Bond at least gives me a vague sense of direction that’s indicating she’s moved far to the North. Fortunately, she’s not travelled so far out of range that our mental connection has become reduced to emotions alone, though it is somewhat muffled.

It is definitely a warning, Bastet agrees. The local Tier Three is establishing its territory and declaring its claim. However, it is not yet an expression of enmity.

No, Kalanthia agrees. I suspect it is trying to see what your response will be. At present it knows that you are numerous, and that some among you are powerful, but it wishes to see how you will act. At the same time, given how quickly it has acted against you, I suspect it is under pressure from others of its level within the surrounding territories – and perhaps within its own. It cannot be seen to allow interlopers to enter unchallenged.

Do you think we can negotiate with it? I ask.

Kalanthia’s reply carries a note of uncertainty.

You must prove yourself to be significantly stronger. If you do, it will submit. Few reach the second Evolution without developing a strong instinct for self-preservation. However, if you fail to demonstrate your strength, it will attempt to force you to submit – or to leave.

Can we coexist in some way without forcing the other to submit? Like you and Raven, I ask, probably falsely optimistically – I remember how difficult it was to get Kalanthia and Raven to coexist. However, it did happen. And something that’s happened once can happen again.

Kalanthia’s response carries clear doubt.

That will depend very much on the beast itself. However, since we are the ones entering its territory, I suspect it will not accept a relationship of equals. It only worked that way between Ravenous and me because we both knew I was stronger than he was, yet I acknowledged that it was ultimately your territory, and decided not to press my advantage. This is a very different situation.

I sigh and tap my fingers thoughtfully against my thigh. Another consideration has occurred to me.

Who would be best leading the negotiations? Me – or Bastet.

Me? Bastet asks, startled. Why not Kalanthia?

Sure, Kalanthia would probably be a good choice – if she was actually here, or was planning on sticking around and wanted the territory. I don’t get the sense that that is the case, I reply pointedly. A faint sense of amusement drifts from Kalanthia’s side of the Bond, but no denial. Face it Bastet, you’re the matriarch of this pack. I might bring the leadership, but you bring the warmth and fierce protection. Who better to face a murderous Tier Three on our behalf?

Bastet still feels startled, but seems to be considering my words.

Bastet might be a better option than you, Markus Wolfe, Kalanthia muses. As we have discussed before, your presence feels odd in ways that do not reflect your true power. The Enlightened beast may underestimate you, and if your objective is to avoid bloodshed, that would be dangerous. I have had enough experience with humans to know not to make assumptions, but you cannot assume that this beast will have it too.

However, she continues, if Bastet leads the negotiations, then you must both be prepared for it to come down to a fight between Enlightened beasts, with no other interference.

You mean… it might come down to a challenge for the territory? I ask.

Precisely, Kalanthia agrees.

Bastet’s response carries clear uncertainty.

Markus, I’m not sure I could take on another Enlightened yet. It hasn’t been that long since I Evolved, and I’m still not certain I could defeat Kalanthia in open combat.

I send reassurance back along the Bond, forcing away the fear that curdles in me at the thought of losing her.

Hopefully it won’t come to that, I tell her, though I suspect there is a high chance it will, given what I know of Enlightened beasts. But we will do everything we can to prepare in case it does.

You’re not alone, Bastet. Even if you are the one who must face the Enlightened beast, you’ll do so with the rest of us standing behind you and supporting you.

She’s silent for a long moment but then I feel the acceptance press over the Bond between us.

I will do my best, she promises.

I know you will, I affirm, sending her the equivalent of a hug over the Bond. Now, let’s discuss how to make contact with the other Tier three – and what preparations to make ahead of doing so.
