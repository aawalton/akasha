Arthur ran until his legs burned, weaving between towering trunks with no clear path. Only when his gut told him he was far enough from pursuit did he slow, ears straining for signs of movement behind him. Nothing. Not a footfall. Not a breath. He was alone.

Arthur exhaled slowly and took a moment to rest. His right arm still throbbed from the bone-shattering counterforce of his earlier strike. The emergency healing he had used during the battle had only sealed the worst of the damage and dulled the pain. The fractures remained. With the battlefield behind him and the forest hushed, it was time to deal with his own wreckage.

He reached into his ring and pulled out the remaining mana cores he'd looted from slain monsters. Sitting cross-legged, he placed them in a line beside him and swallowed them one by one.

Each core released a surge of raw mana in his gut. Arthur reached inward, drawing the threads out with ease because of his infinite wisdom, which granted him unprecedented precision in controlling mana. What would have taken even a skilled healer days of painstaking effort, Arthur executed in moments. He guided the energy through his channels with surgical finesse, threading it where needed without a flicker of strain.

He directed most of the energy into his arm. Bone knit. Ligaments reformed. Muscle reattached. Nerves aligned. With every breath, his right arm inched closer to full functionality.

His right arm still pulsed with latent pressure. It wasn’t pain exactly, but damage barely held at bay, like a dam threatening to crack. He knew it would worsen if left alone. He had to fix it before anything else.

By the time his hand was completely healed, he had only a couple of monster cores left in his ring. He made a mental note to hunt more monsters to replenish his supply.

He stood, flexed his fingers, and began the return journey, retracing his steps through the tall, shadow-laced trees until the edge of the battlefield came into view once more.

He moved cautiously, making sure he wasn’t walking into another trap. But when the battlefield finally came into view, it told its own story. Chaos. Destruction. Bodies everywhere.

It looked like the ambushers had been caught between Yudursh and the Stoneclaw Howlers, ground to a pulp in the crossfire. Then somehow, Yudursh had done it. Monster and man lay still. Arthur wasn’t sure whether to be impressed or disturbed.

Arthur activated Mana Sight. A flickering blue glow pulsed faintly from behind a shattered tree. Yudursh. Slumped against the trunk, his body a tangle of wounds, one leg torn open and soaked in blood, but not dead. Not yet. And that was the problem. Of everyone in that skirmish, Yudursh had been the greatest threat. Not just in strength, but in sheer tenacity. Arthur had feared the warrior might track him through the forest, might survive long enough to mount a rescue. But now, with the ambushers slaughtered and Yudursh barely clinging to life, Arthur could finally lower his guard. He could regroup, recover, and plan the next move.

Arthur narrowed his eyes and pulled up the system notifications blinking at the edge of his vision. He hadn’t had a proper moment to check them during the fight.

Notifications:

Stats Increased:

Skill Levels Increased:

Arthur let out a reluctant breath and unmuted Drogan, hating that he had no better option.

"Master Drogan… if I may, why did those E-grades seem so much weaker than Rajak?" he asked.

Drogan snorted. "Oh, now you’re listening? After days of shouting into the void, this is what finally gets your attention?"

Arthur waited.

"You’ve got a Legendary Class, idiot," Drogan snapped. "Your growth rate’s not just high, it’s absurd. Physically, you’re already at the level of an average E-grade warrior. You’ve got twice the Constitution of a tank, and your Intelligence? Easily double that of a typical E-grade mage. And you’re not even halfway through F-grade."

Arthur blinked. That was… significant.

Drogan wasn’t done. "You’ve been soloing monsters that teams of five are supposed to hunt together. And instead of grinding, you’ve been playing shadow games."

Arthur didn’t rise to the bait.

"You’ve got the princess now," Drogan continued, voice hard. "She’s your answers. So what are you still doing here?"

Arthur’s gaze flicked back to the battered form of Yudursh.

"Leverage," he murmured. "She cared about her team. I saw it. That means he’s valuable to her."

Even if she refused to talk, Arthur could use that bond. He wasn’t against torture if it worked, but with a healer? Pain alone might not be enough. Better to play the long game. Use her attachment. Milk it for more than just answers.

He crouched behind a ridge, watching Yudursh struggle with every breath. The man was tough. Still breathing despite half his ribs likely shattered and one leg trailing blood. It was admirable, in a suicidal sort of way.

Arthur considered ending it. Just one quick throw. No more loose ends. But he didn’t move. Didn’t even reach for a weapon.

"This isn’t pity," he muttered to himself. "It’s strategy."

He knew that if he played his cards right, Yudursh could be more valuable alive. Not just as leverage over Ysara, but maybe more. A rival turned asset? Stranger things had happened.

It was the perfect time to get himself another hostage. Arthur stepped away from the ridge and began moving toward Yudursh, silent, measured steps, eyes locked on the wounded warrior slumped against the tree.

A Few Minutes Earlier…

The battlefield stank of blood, scorched moss, and something worse - the bitter rot of a plan gone wrong.

Bodies littered the clearing. A mage facedown in the mud. Two warriors shattered near the treeline. Claw marks. Splintered armor. Gouged earth, broken steel, and a silence thick with death.

I remember crawling.

My leg felt like it had been chewed by a wild hound. I dragged it behind me, gritting my teeth. Each movement was a lightning strike of pain.

I found a tree. Dropped against it with a grunt. Armor scraped bark. I fumbled at my belt pouch for salve, my fingers clumsy with blood loss.

Eventually, I patched the worst of it. Enough to stop dying. Not enough to fight.

Then I replayed what had happened.

The archers dropped first. I thought it was Kaelin, some final act of defiance. But then I saw him. Not Kaelin.

A human with glowing blue eyes, releasing monsters like they were summoned pets.

Even while I was fighting five warriors, I felt him approach. The blur, the impact. He shattered a rogue and flung the body into me.

By the time I got up? He was gone. And so was the princess.

I cursed under my breath. I had to find her. No matter the cost.

I pushed myself upright.

Then darkness. Consciousness slipped.

When I came to, something was wrong.

My innate skill, it screamed at me. A threat. Behind me. Silent.

I didn’t move. Didn’t breathe too loudly. My ears caught nothing.

Stealth predator. Had to be. If not for the skill, I’d already be dead.

I kept still. Let it get close. Pretended I was unconscious.

Eight yards. Six. Four. Two...

I twisted. Pivoted from the hip. My right sword lashed out, full force.

Steel met resistance. But not the kind I expected.

My blade stopped mid-strike, caught in a vice.

My eyes widened.

Then the world went white.
