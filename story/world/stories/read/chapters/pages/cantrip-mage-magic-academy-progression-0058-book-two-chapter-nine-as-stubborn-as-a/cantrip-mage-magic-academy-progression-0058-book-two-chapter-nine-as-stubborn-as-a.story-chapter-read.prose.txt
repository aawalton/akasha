The next day, Eola started Patrol Duty’s final official class sweat-covered and shaky-legged, as usual.

Unusually, however, Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe was not in attendance. He’d left the gymnasium almost as soon as Spellsword Tactics ended, pausing only long enough to post a large sheet of parchment on the wall nearest the door. Eola limped over and skimmed it.

Each group of students must sign up for your patrols. Your names must appear eleven times on the left side if patrolling the library, or six times on the right side if patrolling the Spine Mountains. You may sign up your teammates if they aren’t able to do so themselves.

Once you’ve signed up, you are dismissed.

As she glanced at the lines and dates that made up the rest of the sign-up sheet, someone touched the small of her back. She jumped and glanced over her shoulder. Then she relaxed. “Hello, Colin.”

“Hello. Today’s the day, huh?” Colin’s cloak, as usual, hung from his shoulders at the shabbiest possible angle.

She nodded, then pulled on his cloak until it sat a little more correctly. It still wasn’t right. If anyone had enforced Varin’s Academy’s dress code in the last year, he’d still almost certainly be in violation, but the code seemed the barest hint of a suggestion rather than actual policy. “I’d rather go sooner than later, if it’s all the same to you.”

“And Roth?”

“Well, he’s not here, is he? But he’s got the same pressures I do.” All around them, the combat track second-years pressed close, staring at the sign-up form on the wall. Eola sighed and dug into her pocket until she found a pen and ink, then quickly wrote three names on the next weekend’s line, under the Spine Mountain column. Eola Lemiene, Colin Tremory, and Roth Gerr. Then, that done, she got out of the way before Catrine Andrese could kill her with a glare.

Catrine, for her part, said nothing. She signed up for the library, along with, to Eola’s complete lack of surprise, Garreth Skagget. Then she turned on one heel, her smallsword tapping against her boot, and left. Eola breathed a sigh of relief; so far, Catrine hadn’t said more than a handful of words to her this term, and they’d all been…not civil, exactly, but professional. Focused on the task at hand, not on the young noblewoman’s usual pettiness. It’d been a relief. Still, she couldn’t help but feel like the other shoe would fall soon.

As Catrine left, Colin stared at the form. “You know, Eola, we haven’t talked to Roth about how, exactly, we’re supposed to survive a patrol. Not the monsters. We can handle them between the three of us. But, well…”

“Well, what?”

“It gets pretty cold this time of the year, and the Spine Mountains will only get colder.”

“Ah.” Eola hadn’t really considered that. “I’ll check my spellbook and see what I can come up with. Now, hurry up. We’ve got a nearly free forty-five minutes, and I want to spend it studying.”

Instructor Tarik waited until the end of their apprenticeship time—and just before dinner time, to Eola’s chagrin—to pull both her and Patrice aside. “Miss Lemiene, Miss Clerk, with me.”

He didn’t say anything else, and Patrice show Eola a quick eye-roll before falling in behind him as he left the classroom and hurried through the halls, peeking around the corners as he led them to his office. He held the door for his two apprentices, then shut and locked it behind him.

The room was, as usual, a mess. Stacks of paper and books, piles of balled-up parchment, and the smell, unlike Eola’s visits last year, of burning juniper berries. This time, though, Instructor Tarik’s black rabbit was nowhere to be seen. The professor checked the door, drew a rune, and cast a Locking spell on the door in addition to the physical lock. Then, and only then, did he relax, slumping into his chair and closing his eyes as he ‘stared’ at the ceiling.

Eola waited as he sat there. Then, after nearly a minute, she cleared her throat. “Sir? Why are we here?”

Instructor Tarik blinked, and his eyes slowly refocused on Patrice. “Right. Sorry. There’s been a shift in the political situation across Alemia—and specifically, at Varin’s Academy. You shouldn’t need to worry about it, but suffice it to say that the Cult of Y’aer’s leadership experienced a shift late last month, and the ripples are starting to reach us here. I’ve been working hard to remain in compliance with the new dominant faction’s policies.

“But that’s not why I asked the two of you here. I’ve been reviewing your performance during the Library Exam, Miss Clerk.”

“I did exactly what I had to do,” Patrice started, voice angry.

Instructor Tarik raised his hands, palms down. “I agree. You did well with the tools you had. However, I think it’s a mistake to write off your attunement. I know the four of you have started looking at a few ideas.”

“Fat lot of good that’s done me.”

Eola pursed her lips, then nodded slowly. “We’ve tried a few things. Mostly Colin, actually. He was trying to help Patrice last term. I mostly just acted as a training dummy for her stupidly long hammer.”

Patrice snorted. She still looked angry, but Eola caught a hint of mirth in her eyes.

“Miss Lemiene, I’m hesitant to offer my advice in this situation, because I believe anything I suggest has probably been tried already. However, have you attempted an alternative method of binding a familiar? I know you came up with one last year.”

“Won’t work,” Patrice said at the same time Eola said, “It might work.” The two girls stared at each other, Patrice’s glare deepening. Eola matched it, then continued. “Spell-chaining is a lot harder to cast than Ordered Magic, but we might be able to find a workaround to your problem.”

“I said it won’t work. It still requires Child’s Magic, and you know what happens when I cast cantrips.”

Tarik nodded slowly. “We’ve all seen the…variable…effects of your attempts at magic. That being said, you have a better chance of solving this problem here, at Varin’s Academy, than anywhere else in Alemia. You have friends who understand magic in ways no one else does, professors who are interested in supporting you, and a safe place to explore that magic in the cells. I’d like the two of you to focus your efforts—when other classes’ requirements don’t take priority—on the Call and Calm ritual, the version of it Eola casts, and how to modify it for Patrice’s…unique…needs.”

Patrice said nothing. She glowered at Tarik instead.

Eola, though, nodded seriously. “I’ll sit down with you tonight, after dinner. We’ll get this figured out. I promise.”

“Don’t make promises you can’t keep,” Patrice muttered.

“If there’s nothing else, sir…?”

“No.” Instructor Tarik cast a second spell, and the door unlocked behind Eola. “Miss Clerk, Miss Lemiene, I’ll see you both in class.”

As Patrice stalked back toward their dorm, Eola scratched her head and stared at the back of her friend’s head. Patrice had been asking for help a few weeks back. She’d wanted help with her remedial schedule. Then, that had gone silent. And now that Tarik was getting involved, she seemed furious about it.

Why?

And also, why would Instructor Tarik take the precaution of locking the door behind him? He’d never once done that last term.

Just what was happening with the Cult of Y’aer, and how was that impacting him? And why hadn’t she or her friends heard anything about it except for Patrice and Colin’s rumors of heresy?

Eola had no answers to any of those questions.

An hour and a half later, after a dinner of roast duck—or some sort of poultry—smothered in a gray-brown gravy, half of an oven-baked potato still in its skin, and boiled cabbage and broccoli, Eola knocked on Patrice’s door.

“Go away.”

Eola knocked again.

No response.

She turned the handle, and it opened. Patrice glared at her from the bed. “I said to go away.”

“You’ve been crying? I’m sorry. But I’m also not going away.” Eola stepped in and dumped her notebook and an armful of textbooks on the wide, well-lit desk against Patrice’s far wall. She wasn’t jealous of the girl’s room. Not at all—Patrice had no window and no loft. But by Y’aer, it’d be nice to have this much space. She could move in Patrice’s room.

Patrice, for her part, didn’t move, except to flop onto the bed, face first.

“Right. So, we’re going to start with the standard Call and Calm ritual,” Eola said, ignoring the redhead’s moping. She sat at the desk and flipped through the first textbook, Ideograms for Beginning Ordered Mages. Call and Calm was on page three hundred sixty-seven, and when she got there, she started talking again. “Your fundamental problem is different than mine. I don’t have an attunement modifier, and I can’t cast any First Order spells because of that.”

“I know. We’ve talked about this.”

Well, at least Patrice was engaged, even if she couldn’t see what Eola was actually pointing at. She kept talking, walking Patrice through the process of casting Call and Calm the correct way, just like Instructor Tarik had taught in his Ideograms classes last term. It was a waste of time, and Eola knew it. Neither of them could possibly cast Call and Calm that way. But she’d been working with Colin a lot over the last few weeks, and even more over the previous term, and his organized, methodical approach might help here.

As she worked through the Ordered Magic approach, two things became abundantly clear.

First, that Colin’s complaints from last term—when he’d worked closely with Patrice to get ready for the Library Exam—and an incredible amount of merit. He’d said that working with Patrice was like working with an actual toddler. After nearly an hour, Patrice hadn’t moved from the bed, nor had her tantrum lessened. Eola only knew they were making progress because she’d started translating the other girl’s grunts and groans into something along the lines of yes, maybe, or no.

And second, that her own method wouldn’t be viable for Patrice.

“It’s unfortunate that you don’t have cantrips under control. If you did, this problem would be easy. However, I think it’s in both of our best interests to table this until next week.” Eola snapped Ideograms for Beginning Ordered Mages shut, trying not to let her annoyance show. “And next week, you’ll be a little less grumpy about this. Agreed?”

“It’s not my fault,” Patrice mumbled into the pillow.

“That’s not what I asked. Listen, Patrice, I’m trying my best to help you. I talked to Instructor Tarik, and I tried to get your remedials under control for you. Now, I’m trying to solve your magic problems for you, when I have plenty of my own. You and I both know what’s really going on here.”

“What?”

Eola rolled her eyes as she stood up and tucked her borrowed library book under her arm. She lowered her voice to make sure it wouldn’t carry into the common room—not that she owed Patrice that courtesy after the last hour of being mostly ignored. “You’re afraid. Afraid that we might figure something out, or that we won’t. But either way, at least you’ll know. So how about this? You think really hard about what you want, and next week, you show up ready to try some stuff. We’ll go to the cells this time.”

Then, before Patrice could throw another mopey, tantrum-like response her way, Eola opened the door and stepped out. She had other things to do before the end of the school week, and one of those was to sleep.

The next morning, Eola awoke to a horrific hacking sound coming from the cat perched on her blanket.

For a brief, terrifying moment, she was back in the hospital after her fall from the library’s third floor, Atta pinning her to the bed, unable to move. Then she summoned all her wakefulness, wriggled out from the heavy blankets, and wrestled the hacking, flailing familiar off her bed and onto the floor—and just in the nick of time. A few seconds later, the blue cat vomited up a ball of sticky paper.

“Message for you, silly girl,” she said, then hopped back onto the bed and curled up on the now-vacated and warm pillow. “You’re welcome.”

“Thanks…?”

There’d be no getting back to sleep after that, so Eola sighed, changed from her bedclothes into her robes—shivering in the cold that had leaked through her unshuttered window as she did—and carefully unfolded the parchment using the bare minimum number of necessary fingers.

Eola,

Meet us at the South Varin’s Town Stables.

Roth

That was it. No time. No directions. Nothing. Eola groaned and reached for her boots. As she pulled them on, she narrowed her eyes at Atta. “How long ago did he leave this here?”

“I’m a cat. I have no concept of time.”

Eola glared at Atta until the cat shrugged in the most human way possible. “Fine. Thirty minutes ago.”

“Thirty minutes, and you already ate it and threw it up!? Atta!”

“Relax. It’s fine. You’re not late, are you? You should be thanking me again, you stupid girl. You’re welcome. Now, get a move on so I can go back to sleep.”

Eola managed not to scream. Instead, she pulled on her cloak and gloves, flipped the hood over her hair, and set out. Her stomach rumbled in protest, but she ignored it. Thirty minutes. There wouldn’t even be time for breakfast.

But twenty minutes later, shivering in the light snow that had started sometime in the night, Eola arrived at the South Varin’s Town Stables.

Horses were…rare, to say the least. Eola’s mother had known how to ride. It was practically a requirement to be one of Lord Card’s guards. But she hadn’t owned a horse herself. Instead, she’d shared a single mare with three other spellswords, working together to take care of the swaybacked, foul-tempered, majestic beast. Eola had gotten to touch it once—but only under the most careful of supervision by no fewer than three of the spellswords, who’d watched her like a hawk.

The South Varin’s Town Stables were almost empty of horses. There were a trio of old-looking mares in gray, dappled black spots over brown, and a beautiful honey color, but a big man with a cudgel stood between the beasts and the general public, looking menacing. She took a long, wistful look at the three mares, then shook her head and pushed deeper into the stables, looking for Roth and Colin.

She found them next to…

Well, it wasn’t a horse. But it was close. The gray animal had the right ears and tail, more or less, and it was about as wide as one, but where the horses Eola had seen towered head and shoulder over her, this…thing…was shot, squat, and fat. Its mane wasn’t right, either. While a horse’s mane flowed softly, this one’s looked more like a bristle-brush.

She raised an eyebrow skeptically. “What is that?”

Colin turned and waved, beaming suddenly. Roth didn’t move from his place by the creature’s neck, where he was currently rubbing a brush against its short…hair? Fur? Eola had no idea. “This is a mule,” he said.

“And we’re visiting a mule here at the stables why, exactly?”

Roth turned, raising his eyebrow. “How often have you gone hunting in the woods for more than a single day?”

Eola blushed. She’d been on plenty of trog hunts with her uncle, but those had all been short expeditions. And she’d overnighted on the trip from Greenarbor to Varin’s Academy—three nights, in fact—but only with a caravan of wagons. She hadn’t slept on the ground, but in a bed-roll up on the floor of a covered carriage. She shrugged. “Never have been. Why?”

“I’m stuck with two city fools,” Roth said. “Listen, Eola. This is a mule, and the best thing we could possibly have with us if we’re heading into the Spine Mountains is one of these. They’re strong, eat anything, and can walk forever with a few hundred pounds on their saddles. And, more importantly, they’re extremely aggressive toward most monsters. This one’s name is Bellana. She’ll be coming with us. ”

“Ah.” Eola reached out to touch the mule’s nose, then pulled her hand back as two rows of gigantic teeth clacked shot on the air. “Rude, Bellana!”

Colin laughed nervously from his post at the beast’s flank, and Roth shook his head. “You two have a lot to learn before this patrol.”
