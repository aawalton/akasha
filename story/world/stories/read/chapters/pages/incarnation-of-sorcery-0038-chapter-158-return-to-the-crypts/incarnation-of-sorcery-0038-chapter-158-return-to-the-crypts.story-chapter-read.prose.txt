Sometimes, Viren wondered if he had been transported into a new reality when he had met Marionys Ryland. He had many, many excellent reasons for believing it. Because he could accept that magic was supposed to be fantastical and unbelievable and truly unfathomable at the peaks that mages could achieve.

But then, Ryland just went about shattering that limit of believability, that suspension of disbelief grounding Viren, over and over and over again.

Sometimes, he felt like he couldn’t get enough of it. Other times…

Other times, he couldn’t help but doubt just what in the world he was doing here.

After all, was he really supposed to believe an entire Realm of people had been duped and manipulated by one person? And that too by having a Soul Glyph Inscribed on their very manasouls? For nigh on centuries now?

Thinking about it started to break Viren’s mind because there were just too many questions surrounding the incident. Why was this only coming up now? How had no one else raised an alarm properly before this? What sort of events had transpired all that time ago to even allow something like this to become an established practice in the first place?

Because that was the most unbelievable part of this whole mess. The people of Rhel—a vast number of them, at least—wanted this process in place.

“You need to stop thinking about it, Viren,” Acrypha said. “You’re only going to drive yourself mad.”

Viren stared at her. “How are you not obsessing about it?”

“Why should I obsess over something that doesn’t affect me?”

“Doesn’t affect you?” Viren stood straighter, trying not to glare at her. “Well, it affects a ton of other people, just so you know. A whole Realm’s worth of them.”

She crossed her arms. It had been a few days since the revelation and instead of calming down, things were actually still rather chaotic since more and more Rhellians were waking up in their new, un-manipulated state, leading to a wide range of unforeseen complications.

Viren, Acrypha, and most of the rest of Arcoryx students were staying away from the mess. They were in a bit of a limbo at the moment.

Several of their contingent wanted to head home and not have anything to do with it all. Acrypha was one of those. Viren was honestly disappointed with the whole lot of them. Faced with a crisis, they were tucking their tails between their legs and leaving the unfortunate to fend for themselves instead of stepping up and assisting where they could.

Thankfully, they couldn’t do that just yet because no official word from their homes had arrived. The Androvian administration hadn’t said anything besides to wait, and neither had the delegations from Jure or from Eviliyen School.

So, Viren still had time to convince the others that helping was the right thing to do. Just as Ryland was doing.

“What exactly do you expect to do about it, Viren?” Acrypha asked.

“I don’t know,” he said. “I’ll figure it out. But even if it’s just running around passing water, it’s still better than nothing!”

A Glyph glowed above Acrypha’s palm. “We have literal magic, Viren. We don’t need anyone to pass Calamity-cursed water.”

“You know what I mean!”

“Think about it this way, then.” Clearly, having failed to convince Viren from one perspective, she was stubbornly about to try a different tack. “How can you trust everything that’s going on after everything you’ve heard? What if they somehow manage to slip this Soul Glyph into your manasoul, Viren? What then? What if you become a plant inside Arcoryx?”

That was so preposterous, Viren couldn’t help but just stare at her for a while. Then he counterattacked. “That’s exactly what I mean. Think about it this way, Acrypha. What if you were affected by this manipulative Soul Glyph? Wouldn’t you want someone to help you get out of it?”

“No! I’d want them to get away from me as fast as they can, to a whole different Realm if possible.”

Viren raised both his hands skyward. “Why do I even bother arguing with you. You’re as stubborn as a cow.”

She scowled. “Do you know the concept of mirrors?”

Viren felt like tearing some of his hair off. The problem with arguing with Acrypha was that she wasn’t really wrong. She had a point. Curse the Calamities, but there were so many people who would say Viren was in the wrong here.

But he couldn’t betray his morals like that. It wasn’t a matter of power or responsibility or anything of the sort.

People helped people. That was it. That was the fundamental belief guiding Viren to be who he was, to act the way he did, to aim to be the hero who inspired others to do the same.

They probably would have continued bickering, but the real reason for their meeting finally showed up. Tezar was ambling towards them with an even more blank look than before, a mask of doubt clouding his entire expression.

“Finally.” Viren went over and grabbed the older boy’s arm to start pulling him along faster. “We can get going.”

“I still think this is a terrible idea,” Acrypha said. “We should tell somebody where we’re headed before we go.”

“That’s why you’re here, Acrypha. I’m going to take Tezar to the Crypts while you run off like the dutiful student that you are and make sure everyone knows exactly where we’ve gone and why. Meanwhile, Tezar and I will take care of his little problem. Isn’t that right, Tezar?”

The older boy still looked unsure, even though this whole thing was technically his idea. Well, going to the Crypts wasn’t exactly his plan. But he was the one who had been insistent that there was something missing within him now, something that felt like it could become whole again in that deep, dark, dank place under a mountain range.

“I don’t want to force anyone to come with me,” he said.

“Yes,” Acrypha said. “But the Crypts? You remember what happened the last time we went there, right? You Rhellians literally got possessed by the ghosts there.”

Viren shivered at the memories of the place. He really didn’t want to go back there, but Tezar was convinced, so he couldn’t back down. Not when a friend needed him.

Tezar’s words grew stronger the more he spoke. “I think I’m right. There’s something there, something that was trying to… join up with my soul? I don’t know. I felt full, in a weird way, like I had eaten too much.” He sighed. “I feel so lost. I don’t know where to go. I don’t even know if this is the right thing to do.”

Viren frowned. Even Acrypha remained silent. The hints of despair in Tezar’s voice were there for all to see.

“Fine then,” she said. “Let’s go. And I’ll make sure nobody gets into any trouble.”

Viren grinned at Tezar. “I really should bring you along more often. You can teach me how to make Acrypha agree with me.”

She scowled at him so hard, he started thinking she’d renege on her decision to go with them. Then she led the way herself. Viren grinned, then dragged Tezar along.

They didn’t leave without any insurance or backup. It would be highly irresponsible otherwise.

That was why Acrypha had Inscribed a new spell she had learned while at Rhel.

“[Siren Messenger],” she incanted.

The spell circle was Inscribed onto an unassuming path of earth within the institute’s boundary. It was simple, really. Just a remotely activatable spell in case something went wrong at the Crypts, Acrypha could send a message that they needed help.

As they headed out—or, well, sneaked out—Viren wondered if he could have done with some protection from Ryland. But the man had been missing, apparently off on some mission or other.

Viren was intrigued, but he had his own quest to carry out.

Rhel wasn’t exactly in the most operational state at the moment, so they decided it was best to head out on foot. The problem with that was the overland journey would take a few hours without any transport. They could rely on their Inscriptions to hasten their travel, but there was only so much they could push themselves.

Tezar thought they wouldn’t need very long at the Crypts themselves, so they should still be able to return before the day was up with none the wiser.

They didn’t talk a whole lot, choosing instead to focus on the journey.

Until the fireworks started.

“What the…?” Viren stared up at the lights in the distant sky. “It’s not even evening. Why would anyone do fireworks now of all times?”

They all stared up at the lightshow. Soon enough, it became extremely evident that it wasn’t fireworks they were witnessing.

Far in the distance, devastating magic popped and exploded. The scale was starting to boggle Viren’s head. It was so far in the distance, the sheer size of the mountainous figure rising high into the sky could only have been even more titanic up close. And Viren himself was leagues away from the location.

Then it was exploding. Dying. Falling like a mountain chopped in half, about to crash down onto the earth.

“I really think we need to get to the Crypts and get inside,” Acrypha said. “Fast.”

She looked guilty that she hadn’t suggested returning to the institute instead, but they couldn’t. Not when they had come this far already.

“I’m sorry,” Tezar said as they increased their pace, their surroundings rushing past them as they hurried on. “I shouldn’t have dragged you into this whole mess!”

Viren shot him a brief glare. “Stop stealing my credit. I’m the one who dragged us into all this.”

Their conversation had to take a pause because another devastating bit of magic came into play. A crimson explosion turned the whole world the colour of glowing blood for a few moments. Viren’s skin itched violently, like it wanted to pull off his flesh. They all cast barrier magic as fast as they could.

“Don’t worry,” Acrypha said through the madness. “I’ve got a backup plan if needed.”

Viren could barely hear her over the massive detonations rocking the world. The more important matter was that he really didn’t want Acrypha to resort to the magic she was referring to. He had seen the toll it had taken on her the first time, and though she had appeared to get over it in that battle against the Flourinus Order, he didn’t want her to go through all that again.

So, their best bet would be to get to the Crypts as fast as their legs could carry them. Viren pushed himself as hard as he could.

“I think I see the entrance!” he shouted.

By that point, the terrifying crimson reality had broken, so they didn’t need to worry about that. Plus, the destructive magic they had seen threading through the sky remained airborne. None of them wanted to face a shower of stars of all things.

“I don’t see anyone there,” Acrypha said.

Tezar huffed a little. They had been pushing hard for a while, so this was taking too long. “Probably taking refuge just in case the magic comes this way.”

“But it’s so far off…”

Her words trailed away for good reason. Just because it was so far off didn’t mean they were free from whatever might happen next. That crimson devastation had been proof enough of that.

They received proof of it just as they neared the enormous doors leading into the Crypts. The world twisted and reality shifted. In the far distance, Viren could spot what looked like a messy painting coming to life. Magic took on the form of smudged watercolours at such a gargantuan scale, he was almost tempted to stop and gawk at the inconceivable display.

But that wasn’t what alarmed them all. Not really. What followed that impossible magic was something far more familiar.

The world turned into a gridded collection of tiny points in space.

“This is his magic,” Viren said, like it wasn’t already obvious after everything they had seen. “So we don’t need to worry, right?”

Acrypha didn’t look at all convinced. “We didn’t exactly tell anyone we’d be out and about at a time like this…”

That was terribly true. Ryland’s focus was entirely on whoever or whatever he was fighting against. Right that moment, no one could blame him if one of his rifts accidentally swallowed up three children who really shouldn’t have been out of the purview of their elders.

It made sense, then, why Acrypha cast that spell of hers.

“[Afterlife—”

“Wait, Acrypha!” Viren said, turning to her in alarm.

“—Guardians]!” she finished.

Her manasoul surged. Power sizzled out of her in waves of white and green and blue, crafting two figures next to her in the blink of an eye. One was large and muscular, the other small and lithe.

“What are those?” Tezar asked, stumbling away from the sheer intensity of the Manifestations. That was what Ryland had called them, right?

“They’ll keep us safe.” Acrypha’s words were a little strained, like she was dragging a terrible weight behind her. “So just hold on.”

Viren thought about protesting, but Ryland’s magic was coming alive. In the far distance, he saw another enormous figure burning to life, though this one was familiar. Ryland had used the same kind of magic against that newborn Calamity. Thankfully, its effects didn’t reach them despite the nodes everywhere, but it was astonishing to see it tear that “painting” apart.

Calling it gargantuan-scale magic just failed to capture the sheer enormity of what Viren was witnessing.

But Acrypha’s Guardians were already carrying them through the Crypt’s doorway, hurrying further into the dimly lit tunnels. They were moving astonishingly fast. Viren couldn’t even be sure if he saw a few people here and there within the tunnels, probably a few of the priests and priestesses who had originally helped the students during their first time at the Crypts.

“I can still hear it,” Tezar muttered when they finally came to a stop. “What kind of magic is that?”

Viren couldn’t tell if he ought to be laughing or crying. Everything was shaking every now and then. Magic burst and exploded far out there, somehow audible despite the depth within the land that they had reached.

“That’s Ryland for you,” Viren said. “And we’ve only been seeing glimpses from afar. Believe me, it’s a lot more… intense when you’re right in front.”

He shuddered, remembering the Calamity yet again.

“Tezar,” Acrypha said. “How exactly are you feeling?”

There was a certain pointedness to her question that Viren didn’t fully understand. She wasn’t looking at their friend, though. Instead, she was frowning around at the walls like she was expecting a ghost to pop out any second now. That just made Viren shiver harder.

“I’m already starting to feel like that,” Tezar said, voice low enough to almost be a whisper. “Like something is filling me up. As if I’m unknowingly taking in a very deep breath.”

Viren followed Acrypha’s turning gaze. Now she was looking at her Guardians. At the two Manifestations sparking and sizzling with frightful energy.

“What’s going on with them Acrypha?” Viren asked. “Can you, uh, dispel them?”

“I can, but that’s not my point,” she said. She didn’t dispel them, much to Viren’s chagrin. “The point is that I know what’s causing the issue, and I know what’s affecting Tezar too. I know what’s making him feel full.”

Both Tezar and Viren looked at her impatiently, moments away from yelling at her to get on with it.

She rolled her eyes at their impatience, then turned back to her Guardians. “They’re all being affected by the same thing. These ghosts inside the Crypts? They’re just Manifestations of Living Mana.”

Viren tried to parse that for a while. The only connection he could make right then was the oft-repeated fact that Rhellians had already dealt with all the Living Mana on their world. But right here, right this moment, Acrypha was insinuating that it wasn’t the truth. That there was something more going on.

For the time being, however, Viren didn’t need to think about what it properly meant, especially in relation to Tezar and the “hollowness” he was feeling.

They were all interrupted by the arrival of a priestess. She looked positively horrified that they were all here for some reason. But her expression was quickly morphing. Instead of telling them off or asking what in the world they were doing there, she focused solely on Acrypha.

“Young lady,” the priestess said, approaching with something akin to wonder on her face. “Could you kindly come with me?”

Acrypha frowned, her hand on her sword. “Come with you? What for?”

“You seem to possess an integral connection with the Living Mana.” She peered at Acrypha very intently. “You have… Manifestations of it, don’t you?”

Acrypha’s demeanour instantly changed. “You know about it?”

“Of course. You’ve already noted the Living Mana all around us, yes? How would we mind it if we didn’t know about it.”

Viren was starting to look between the two of them with some concern. “Acrypha…”

She raised a hand to forestall any of his words and started walking towards the priestess resolutely. “It’ll be fine, Viren. If I don’t come back from wherever I go with the priestess, just tell sir.”

That was… a foolproof plan, truth be told. Ryland would turn Rhel itself upside down just to find Acrypha if she went missing. Viren had zero doubts that his mentor would absolutely find Acrypha, no matter where she ended up. But none of that assurance meant Viren was willing to entertain the possibility of something happening to her.

“Acrypha,” Viren said with a lot more explicit warning.

“It’ll be fine,” she said. “I need to find out what Living Mana is doing in my… my Soul Glyph.”

“Soul Glyph.” The priestess looked at her even more sharply, her eyes almost hawklike. “How intriguing…”

Viren didn’t like the sounds of that, but he also knew that he could neither stop her nor accompany her. Someone needed to tell Ryland and he wasn’t about to trust Tezar with it.
