Chapter 380

Eik’s own troops—members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys—were arriving in droves along with the Alliance troops.

Everybody knew exactly how dire the situation was. This was a fight that needed to be won. It was a war that involved them all, and there was no use hiding away from it.

“Boss!” a familiar voice called as Eik and Andihar armed their way through the crowd. Eik would have loved to just barrel through at speed, but there were many people here who would not survive a clash like that. It was the same reason why they had landed a little ways away, to make sure their arrival didn’t kill some poor F or E-ranker. “Boss! Over here!”

“Kalavax,” He raised a hand above the many heads. He could see the top of the man’s head more than two dozen bodies away. The two shields strapped to the gigantic man’s back jutted up above most and made him very easy to spot, even in a throng like this.

“All of our guys are on the way!” the huge shield bearer shouted. “We will see you on the other side!”

“Good man!” Eik answered as he redoubled his efforts, a wedge of aura repelling bodies like nothing else.

“Boss!”

Eik turned to look back, his legs never stopping. He sent his loyal subordinate a questioning eye.

“Don’t die!”

Eik huffed a chuckle. “Remind me to never let you carry the banner of EGG(G). You’re full of death flags!”

Andihar let out his own laugh behind Eik as they stepped through a fracture.

The first thing that struck them was the stench. For a moment, Eik was reminded of the Gohkamorian capital of Rorgherd. It was a smell that pierced the nostrils like needles. Bitter smoke.

But they both knew that this was not smoke from an artisan’s district or a factory. It was the stench of burning buildings and burning bodies. There was roasted flesh in that stench.

A shimmering aura barrier widening to a thirty meter radius from Eik’s body rejected and swallowed up any smoke generated inside its parameters.

The second thing to hit them was the silence.

Well, silence was not the correct word, perhaps. Screams of the mangled and dying filled the air just as thickly as the smoke did. It stung terribly in the ears. But those screams should have been drowned out by ringing explosions and the deafening sounds of high-ranked combat. And yet the screams were all that could be heard.

“It’s over,” Eik concluded before they could see anything of the scene at large. “We’re too late. The Lord is already gone.”

As Eik’s aura cleared the air and expanded outward continuously, it only became more apparent that he was right.

Coming through the fracture from Gimleh, they should have been spit out inside some manner of spacious portal hub, but above their heads was nothing but the open sky. Even with an aura zone more than three hundred meters around, not a single building could be seen still standing.

“Hey, you!” a voice shouted suddenly from behind them. They turned as the speaker continued. “Hey! Come over here and give me a hand! What are you just standing there for?”

It was a man. His clothes were torn and dirtied, as if he had been inside a building as it collapsed to rubble.

He was covered in blood from fingers to elbows, frantically trying to resuscitate a downed woman with a massive hole in her stomach. The man didn’t, but the woman had feelers on her forehead, likely denoting her a citizen of Yanglith.

Eik could feel her vitality dwindle away by the second. “Can you tell me what happened here?” he asked as he approached calmly. From the portal they had arrived through poured and endless stream of scattering troops.

“Are you kidding?” the man shouted angrily. “Help me get her to a healer! I can’t carry her and staunch the blood on my own!”

A streak of Panacean Quintessence slithered into the dying woman’s abdomen, knitting together the fatal wound in a couple of seconds. Even the blood on her clothes and up the man’s arms was cleaned off.

“All right, I got her to a healer. Now, tell me what happened.” This guy who seemed to have been here during the attack was just as good a place as any to start digging into the devastating blitz invasion.

“What is— How…?” Only then did he seem to actually get a proper look at Eik’s face. There did appear to be some recognition there, but only when he did a double take at the green toxin finishing up around them did it click. “Ei— M-Mr. Eik Magnasen. What are you… doing here?” he breathed.

“Well, I was hoping to wipe the dirt off the soles of my boots with the Lord’s face, but I can’t seem to find him anywhere. Where did he go?”

“I don’t… I don’t know.” It almost sounded like a question.

“Who would?”

“Some of the higher ranked Awakened from the military, maybe. I’ve only lived here for a few years, so I’m not entirely familiar.”

“Andihar?” Eik asked.

The elf shook his head. “Unfortunately, I’m not well informed on Yanglith.”

“It’s…” the man began but appeared to struggle to navigate the city he’d been living in now that it had been reduced to a pile of rubble. “I think the closest base is a handful of minutes in that direction, or, well, for you, not that long, I suppose. But I don’t know if anybody’s alive there.” On that last remark, his voice cracked as a sudden sob cut through. Silent tears began to flow. “My wife is somewhere over there.”

Eik exchanged a quick glance with Andihar as they began to move. “You’re a good man for trying to help that woman there. Take her back through the fracture, and I will make sure that anybody here with even a spark of life left in them will survive. You have my word. If your wife is out there, I will get her back to you.”

Through another heaving sob, the man spoke as he picked up the woman. “Thank you.”

There was no way to help each person individually right now, but Eik could do something even better. Help them all at the same time.

Soaring above the smoking ruin, following the distraught man’s general direction, Eik shot a seed of green into the sky. It spread out into a horizon-spanning Apocalypse Canvas ala Panacean Toxin and sent a literal torrential rain of healing liquid pouring over the city.

“I’m seeing nothing that looks like a military base,” Andihar noted after a few minute’s search.

“Yeah. It’s all gone.”

“But if the Lord of the Moon was here himself, then why does it look like this?”

Eik frowned. “What are you talking about?”

“I mean, if this had been you obliterating one of those miasma-covered worlds, what would it have looked like afterward?”

Eik’s eyes widened in realization. “Nothing but craters.”

“Exactly. This looks more like the work of an army of C and B-rankers with a day to have fun. Not to mention the fact that there are survivors from the lowest power ranks. That man back there couldn’t have been any higher than E-rank.”

“What does he want?”

Andihar shrugged. “You know him better than me. Maybe he wanted to spread fear, in which case it worked. This day will never be forgotten.”

Eik’s jaw tightened. “I know that the spawn get their tendencies from their parent. They all enjoy the torment. This fucker might just have done it for his own pleasure. He doesn’t even see us as an opponent to worry about.”

***

The auralic signature left behind by the Lord of the Moon and his spawn was thick and clear, leaving no doubt about the truth of the attack. It had been large-scale and indefensible.

Unfortunately, just as quickly as the Worldbreaker had appeared with his army, they had all suddenly turned around and mounted an incredibly coordinated retreat into the southern mountain ranges where they had disappeared completely. At least, that was how it had gone according to those among the survivors who had not been unconscious or buried when it happened.

The period from abrupt beginning to abrupt end had been so short and chaotic, that only an extreme minority had really managed to understand what was going on until after it was all over. In a few moments, the entire continent had been enveloped in a blanket of complete darkness, making it impossible to tell exactly what was happening. If nothing else, that convinced Eik that the Lord had indeed been present in the flesh.

And Eik had followed the trail of the retreating army, but to no avail. They had seemingly vanished somewhere deep within the mountains, and even when he brought out trackers and fracture specialists, it had mostly led to more confusion and uncertainty. The only thing any of them could say for sure was that something had happened there.

Well, duh…

Leaving the green Apocalypse Canvas where it was, Eik deployed a little over five hundred thousand Living Manifestations, and then returned to Gimleh.

With this escalation, there was no time to waste.

“Mn’Toakh.” Eik had been waiting for her with Andihar. General Pölse had been summoned as well, but Eik would only stay as long as it took to relay his orders.

“Sir,” she replied tersely. She had taken to calling him that recently. The apparent disrespect of simply calling him Eik or Mr. Magnasen had become too much, although he still caught her slipping back to that on occasion. He had given up on changing her mind.

“Station fracture specialist on the worlds most likely to be the next targets and have them ready to portal in Chop the Blade and I within ten seconds of any sign of invasion. We can’t allow them to take us with our pants down like this another time. Use my guys from EGG(G) freely, but I want the Alliance to chip in as well. I’m not letting those cloud-nosed bastards skimp on this shit.”

She nodded. “I was thinking the same thing. Yanglith is a bloodbath.”

“I saw,” he said grimly. “What’s the scale?”

“The entire capital city. A few generations after incorporation into the Unified Mass, civilizations tend to gather in larger mega cities. It’s just more convenient for trade and cooperation with other civilizations. Naturally, there are still fringe cities, but large cities provide safety from monsters and environment. And preserving flora and fauna is good for ecosystems,” she said, not one to miss a chance to get a little lesson in. “That means a large portion of the Yanglith civilization was annihilated today.”

Eik saw her fist tighten and her knuckles turn white. It would have been a lie to pretend he wasn’t feeling a similar way.

“If his goal truly is to cause terror, then it’s probably that he’ll go for another minor civilization again, but make sure to make the same preparations on Gimleh.”

“Naturally.”

“And on Earth.”

“Earth is…”

“My home,” he told her, gaze steady. “That’s enough of a reason to expect him to come there.”

“Of course, sir.”

“Anything else?”

She shook her head. “I will have reports brought to you as we uncover more information.”

“Just the crucial reports. I have some important stuff to take care of and it will be keeping me busy.”

She raised her eyebrows. “May I ask what?”

He breathed a deep sigh. “With more urgency than ever, I will be returning to my own personal hell. My crafting hell.”
