“I would like to submit my mission, please,” Ling Yinran said, placing a jade box on the counter.

She opened it, revealing several fine strands of hair resting inside.

The disciple stationed at the Mission Hall counter checked her jade mission tablet, quickly scanning the description before examining the contents of the box.

“The whiskers of a Mirror-Walking Moon Cat, collected during its mating period at the most yin hour,” he read aloud. “Everything seems to match the mission requirements.”

He carefully examined the whiskers before subjecting them to a spiritual appraisal, as required by the Mission Hall's regulations.

After all, simply presenting an item wasn't enough to prove that a mission had been completed. Every submission had to be verified, especially when the requested materials came with such specific conditions.

One might think that attempting to deceive the very sect one relied upon would be preposterous, but in this world, if there was one thing that never seemed to be in short supply, it was people courting death.

“Everything checks out,” the disciple confirmed.

“Yes. It was quite a pain to obtain,” Ling Yinran admitted.

It had taken her two months just to locate a wild Mirror-Walking Moon Cat, and even after finding one, she had to negotiate with the creature to obtain its whisker. The timing made matters worse, as the cat's whiskers were said to accumulate a unique yin property during its mating period, making them particularly valuable for weapon refinement. Unfortunately, that also meant they had to be collected under very specific conditions.

“I can imagine,” the disciple replied sympathetically. “Missions with requirements like these always take an unreasonable amount of time.”

He glanced at the mission description again and sighed. “Speaking of which, these collection missions are getting more and more ridiculous lately.”

Since it was the middle of the night and the Mission Hall was practically deserted, he didn't mind indulging in a little conversation to relieve his boredom.

“Just yesterday, an elder submitted a request for three drops of morning dew that had formed on a sword blade but hadn't been shaken off during practice. And last week, that same elder requested the liquid secreted by an Ear-Piercing Gu.”

He shook his head helplessly. “Oh, and don't even get me started on the mission asking for hair from the left side of a Thunder Ape's buttocks, collected immediately after its breakthrough.”

“…”

Ling Yinran blinked. For some reason, that last request seemed a little different from the others.

“Sometimes I wonder whether these seniors are still cultivating or have simply developed a hobby of coming up with increasingly strange ways to torment us juniors,” the disciple muttered.

He stamped her mission token and handed it back. “Anyway, your mission is complete. You're exempt from red-paper missions for the next three years.”

Ling Yinran let out a small breath of relief. Red-paper missions were always troublesome, especially since they were mandatory assignments issued by the sect. At least she wouldn't have to worry about another one for the foreseeable future.

Just as she was about to leave, however, a notice on the nearby mission board caught her attention. More specifically, it was the word URGENT, written prominently across the top.

Curious, she stepped closer to read it.

[Mission: Investigation of an Unidentified Figure

Multiple disciples have reported sightings of an unidentified figure near their courtyards. The entity appears intermittently and leaves no detectable aura.

Witness descriptions are inconsistent. Some report seeing a man, while others insist the figure was a woman. Several describe it as a ghost-like apparition, whereas others claim the figure entered their residences and removed various objects.

In certain cases, the figure was only observed briefly outside a courtyard before disappearing without a trace.]

Ling Yinran frowned. From the description, it sounded like some kind of mischievous ghost, but that shouldn't have been possible.

The Pure Qi Sect was protected by both ghost-confining and ghost-repelling formations. With such defenses in place, it should have been nearly impossible for an ordinary spirit or ghost to infiltrate the inner sect unnoticed.

“What happened here?” she asked, turning toward the counter.

“Oh, that mission?” The disciple followed her gaze and nodded. “It was only posted recently. The incidents started about a week ago. At first, everyone assumed it was just cultivation fatigue. You know how some disciples start seeing things after spending too long in seclusion.”

Ling Yinran nodded. Such cases weren't particularly uncommon.

“But then more reports started coming in, and the descriptions were similar enough that the matter couldn't simply be dismissed,” the disciple continued.

She glanced back at the notice. “But what about the sect's formations? They should have detected any intruder, especially a ghost.”

“Exactly. That's what makes this whole thing so strange.” The disciple crossed his arms. “The elders have already inspected the formations, but they couldn't find any damage or abnormalities. Everything is functioning as it should. Because of that, most people suspect it's some kind of prank, perhaps by a disciple with an unusual technique, or even an elder with too much free time.”

He shrugged. “Of course, there's also the possibility that it's simply a thief.”

Ling Yinran considered that explanation. It was certainly more believable than a ghost somehow slipping through the sect's defenses.

“Then why is the mission marked urgent?”

“Ah, that's the interesting part.” The disciple lowered his voice slightly. “Among those who reported seeing a phantom, a few claimed that its appearance resembled Founder Li Changsou.”

“Founder Li Changsou?” Ling Yinran stared at him in surprise.

How had a series of strange sightings suddenly become connected to the sect's founder?

“That's what they said,” the disciple replied with a helpless shrug. “Whether there's any truth to it is another matter, but once the founder's name was mentioned, the higher-ups couldn't simply ignore the reports.”

He gestured toward the notice. “That's why the mission was placed in the urgent section. Otherwise, even if it turned out to be a ghost or some troublesome thief, there wouldn't be much reason to alarm the elders.”

After all, this was the Pure Qi Sect, a true behemoth of the Azure River Domain. With its strength and foundations, a single wandering spirit or petty thief was hardly something worth worrying about.

“I see.” Ling Yinran nodded thoughtfully.

Although the matter was strange, it had little to do with her, and she had no intention of getting involved in another troublesome mission so soon after completing her previous one.

With that, she pushed the matter to the back of her mind and left the Mission Hall, making her way toward Spiritual Flower Peak.

It was around two in the morning, and the roads were mostly deserted, a stark contrast to the usual hustle and bustle during the day. This was especially true of Spiritual Flower Peak, which was already more secluded than the other peaks.

“I wonder if Aunt has been eating properly,” Ling Yinran muttered to herself.

After unlocking the entrance formation with her token, she stepped inside and headed toward the alchemy room. It was empty, though the lingering medicinal aroma suggested that someone had been refining pills not too long ago.

She then made her way toward the kitchen, intending to put away the spiritual food she had brought. Knowing her aunt, Ling Xin had probably forgotten to eat again. The woman could spend days obsessing over alchemy without remembering such trivial matters, although she somehow never forgot about sweets.

However, the moment Ling Yinran opened the kitchen door, she froze.

Someone was inside.

A figure covered from head to toe in thick robes was rummaging through the cupboards, seemingly searching for something.

Ling Yinran immediately knew it wasn't her aunt. For one thing, her aunt couldn't cook even if her life depended on it.

“…An intruder?” she muttered, narrowing her eyes.

Then she remembered the strange mission notice she had seen at the Mission Hall.

Don't tell me…

“Stop right there! Intruder!” she shouted.

The figure turned toward her, its voice muffled beneath several layers of clothing.

“Huh?”

Without waiting for an explanation, Ling Yinran flicked her wrist and sent a handful of talismans flying forward.

Fire, lightning, and spirit-repelling talismans activated almost simultaneously, unleashing a barrage of attacks toward the suspicious figure.

Ordinary cultivators could generally activate only one talisman at a time, but Ling Yinran was exceptionally skilled in their usage. Controlling several at once was well within her abilities, allowing her to overwhelm an opponent before they could react.

“?!”

The figure was clearly caught off guard, but its reaction was surprisingly quick.

BOOM!

A deafening explosion shook the kitchen, sending thick smoke billowing through the room.

“Got it!” Ling Yinran exclaimed, feeling rather pleased with her successful ambush.

However, as the smoke began to clear, her satisfaction quickly disappeared.

The figure was still standing.

“…What?”

That barrage should have been enough to seriously injure even a late-stage Qi Condensation cultivator, yet the mysterious intruder appeared completely unharmed.

Ling Yinran gritted her teeth and immediately reached for another talisman, this one radiating the unmistakable fluctuations of a second-tier artifact.

“This should finish it.”

Upon seeing the talisman, the figure hurriedly raised both hands.

“Wait, wait, wait!”

He quickly pulled back his hood, revealing an entirely ordinary human face.

“I'm not a bad guy!"

“Eh?” Ling Yinran blinked, momentarily taken aback.

“Who are you?” she demanded, keeping the talisman ready in case this was another trick.

“I'm Ji Ning, a disciple of Peak Master Ling Xin,” he explained hurriedly.

“…Huh?” Ling Yinran frowned. “I've never heard that Aunt took in a disciple.”

Nevertheless, she lowered her hand slightly, giving Ning a chance to explain himself.

Ning let out a small breath of relief. Since this woman had managed to enter the peak's restricted residence, she must have possessed his master's token or some other form of authorization. And considering the hour, there was one person she was most likely to be-

“Niece! You're here.”

Ling Xin's voice suddenly interrupted his thoughts as she appeared at the kitchen entrance, apparently drawn by the commotion.

“Aunt!” Ling Yinran immediately turned toward her.

Ling Xin glanced around the kitchen, taking in the scattered objects and lingering smoke before looking between the two.

“I sensed Foundation Establishment-level fluctuations just now. What exactly happened here?”

Her disciple and her niece were fighting in her kitchen?

“Yes, I would also like to know,” Ning chimed in. “Why was I suddenly attacked?”

If he hadn't reacted quickly and activated his protective equipment, he might have been in quite a bit of trouble.

“T-this…” Ling Yinran rubbed the back of her head awkwardly. “I thought there was an intruder. I heard about that strange figure appearing around the inner sect, some kind of ghost or thief, so when I saw someone suspicious in the kitchen, I thought it had come here.”

Ling Xin blinked.

“You do realize that entering this residence requires my authorization, right? There's also a second-tier mountain-guarding formation protecting the place.”

“I know,” Ling Yinran defended herself weakly, “but that mission notice made it sound like the intruder could appear anywhere!”

She then pointed accusingly at Ning.

“And it didn't help that he was dressed so suspiciously!”

For a moment, Ning was speechless.

Even if he had been an intruder, shouldn't she have at least tried something like Swiper, no swiping! before attacking?

Nope. She just started blasting.

Ning didn't quite know how to feel about that. On one hand, her decisiveness was admirable, especially considering how dangerous cultivators could be.

On the other hand, he had been on the receiving end.

Still, he had to admit that from her perspective, encountering a fully covered figure rummaging through the kitchen cupboards at two in the morning was rather suspicious.

As he considered the situation, Ning recalled what Ling Yinran had mentioned about the mysterious figure appearing around the inner sect.

Xiao Fan harmed me, he cursed inwardly.

As someone who firmly believed that information was power, Ning had naturally heard about the recent rumors. A strange figure appearing near disciples' courtyards, leaving no detectable aura, and occasionally resembling the sect's founder?

And all of this started shortly after Xiao Fan entered the inner sect?

There was no way that was a coincidence.

This was practically screaming xianxia plot development.

Ning could practically smell the plot from a mile away. He had simply never expected to become collateral damage in something he wasn't even participating in.

Pushing those thoughts aside, he turned back toward Ling Yinran.

“As for my clothes, I'm wearing them for protection,” Ning explained seriously. “You know Master's tendency to cause explosions, right? It would be suicidal not to take precautions.”

Indeed, Ning was currently dressed from head to toe in protective robes, complete with reinforced goggles and several additional layers of equipment he had painstakingly acquired after repeated incidents.

The armored robes themselves had been borrowed from his master.

“…That actually makes sense,” Ling Yinran admitted after a moment.

She fell silent, seemingly unable to find a reasonable argument against that explanation.

Ling Xin, however, burst into laughter as she finally pieced together what had happened.

“Hahaha! You really are my niece!” she exclaimed, looking thoroughly amused. “The first time Ning came here, he got blown away by my experiment. And now, the first time he meets you, you attack him with talismans!”

“Th-that!” Ling Yinran's face immediately turned red, and she looked as though she wanted nothing more than to dig a hole and hide in it.

“I remember someone giving me a lecture about being a responsible adult,” Ling Xin continued teasingly. “Attacking your junior brother the moment you meet him doesn't sound very responsible to me.”

“S-shut up!” Ling Yinran snapped, thoroughly flustered. “You're the one who keeps blowing things up and calling it research! I was just… being cautious!”

“Hahaha! Fair, fair!” Ling Xin laughed even harder, apparently finding the accusation just as amusing as everything else.

After all, she couldn't exactly deny it.

“It's alright, Senior Sister,” Ning interjected, deciding it was probably time to ease the situation. “It's not like I was hurt.”

Judging by the way Ling Yinran was glaring at his master, she looked about ready to finish what the explosion had started, except with Ling Xin as the target this time.

Ning could only shake his head inwardly.

Alas, it had been quite an explosive first impression indeed.

Pun intended.

Heavenly Taste Pavilion was one of the most renowned restaurants in the inner sect, famous for both its exquisite cuisine and the spiritual properties of its dishes. Depending on the ingredients and preparation methods, the food served here could nourish qi, strengthen the body, and even aid cultivation.

The moment Ning stepped inside, he was greeted by the rich aroma of food infused with spiritual energy.

“Over here!” Fatty Li waved enthusiastically from a table near the window.

Beside him sat a large, broad-shouldered man with a calm and approachable expression.

“So, this is my senior brother, Xuan Fugu,” Fatty Li introduced as Ning took a seat. “He's the one I told you about, the one interested in your recipes.”

Xuan Fugu greeted him with a friendly smile. “I've heard quite a bit about you from Junior Brother Li. He mentioned that you've created some rather unique dishes, and as a spiritual chef, I've always enjoyed collecting interesting recipes. That's why I wanted to meet you personally.”

“Greetings, Senior Brother. Fatty Li has told me quite a bit about you as well,” Ning replied politely.

In truth, Fatty Li had been rather enthusiastic about convincing Ning to share his recipes. Although the dishes Ning made weren't traditional spiritual cuisine, their novelty alone was enough to attract attention, especially since they didn't require expensive spiritual ingredients.

Ning had no objections to selling them. For one thing, it was another opportunity to earn spirit stones, and he certainly wasn't going to complain about that. More importantly, he genuinely enjoyed cooking and wanted to see whether the dishes from his previous world could be elevated further using the extraordinary ingredients and techniques available here.

After all, among the hundred professions, spiritual chefs were particularly skilled at handling rare ingredients and bringing out their beneficial properties. High-level beast meat and certain spiritual plants, for example, often contained violent or incompatible spiritual energies that needed to be properly processed before they could be safely consumed.

Naturally, Ning was curious about what someone trained in such methods could accomplish with his recipes.

With that in mind, the three began chatting about cooking techniques, ingredients, and various dishes. The conversation was pleasant, and before long, the food they had ordered began arriving.

The first dish was presented on an exquisite platter covered by a translucent lid.

“Eight-Treasure Spirit Lotus Harmony,” the attending disciple announced.

Beneath the lid lay delicate slices of lotus root shimmering with a faint golden hue. Each layer had been infused with different spiritual ingredients, including jade fungus, crimson dates, frost petals, and several others, all arranged with meticulous precision. As the lid was lifted, a gentle wave of spiritual qi drifted outward, carrying a fragrance so refreshing that Ning felt his mind clear slightly.

Xuan Fugu's eyes immediately sharpened.

“The layering… flawless,” he murmured, leaning forward to examine the dish. “Each slice carries a different balance of the five phases, yet none of them interfere with one another.”

Fatty Li closed his eyes and inhaled deeply. “I can already sense it. Metal, wood, water, fire, and earth, all perfectly interwoven!”

The two exchanged an appreciative glance before picking up a slice each and placing it in their mouths.

For a brief moment, neither spoke.

Then Fatty Li's entire body trembled.

“It melts!” he exclaimed. “The moment it touches the tongue, the lotus dissolves completely, but the essence doesn't scatter! Instead, the flavors unfold one after another. First comes a gentle sweetness, soft and comforting, followed by a refreshing coolness, almost like clear spring water flowing through the meridians!”

He clutched his chest dramatically as his expression grew even more excited.

“And then comes the warmth! A subtle heat rises from the dantian, encouraging the spiritual qi to circulate in a perfect cycle!”

Xuan Fugu, meanwhile, chewed slowly, his expression becoming increasingly serious.

“The texture is extraordinary. The lotus fibers have been processed to dissolve at different speeds, creating a layered sensation despite their softness. Even the heat was controlled precisely enough for each ingredient to release its essence at a different moment.”

He picked up another slice and examined it closely before continuing.

“The jade fungus provides a slight elasticity, while the crimson dates add depth to the sweetness. And that faint bitterness at the end…”

“Yes! Exactly!” Fatty Li interrupted enthusiastically. “It sharpens the palate and makes you want another bite! Just when one flavor begins to fade, another takes its place!”

He promptly demonstrated his point by stuffing another slice into his mouth.

“And the aftertaste! It's so light and refreshing that the spiritual qi continues circulating smoothly without any sense of heaviness!”

Xuan Fugu nodded in agreement. “Indeed. This dish nourishes the body while calming the mind. Even one's thoughts seem clearer after eating it.”

Throughout their enthusiastic discussion, Ning had been quietly watching the two of them.

He finally picked up a slice, placed it in his mouth, and chewed thoughtfully before swallowing.

“…It's pretty good.”

“That's it?” Fatty Li asked, staring at him.

“Yeah.” Ning nodded. “It's delicious.”

And he meant it. The lotus root had a wonderful texture, the flavors complemented one another beautifully, and the spiritual energy left him feeling pleasantly refreshed. Compared to ordinary mortal cuisine, dishes like these were certainly on another level.

He just didn't know what else they expected him to say.

Fortunately, the next dish arrived before Fatty Li could lecture him further.

“Flame-Tyrant Beast Soul Roast!”

A large slab of roasted beast meat was placed before them, its surface charred to perfection while the interior remained tender and faintly red. Wisps of fire qi still crackled across the meat, carrying a rich, smoky fragrance that immediately whetted Ning's appetite.

And just like that, the commentary began again.

From the origin of the beast to the precise temperature required to roast its meat, Fatty Li and Xuan Fugu discussed practically every detail imaginable. They analyzed the texture, the distribution of fire qi, the seasoning, and even how the meat's spiritual essence changed depending on which part was chewed first.

Ning listened while enjoying his meal, occasionally nodding whenever they looked toward him.

At least their clothes weren't bursting apart from sheer culinary ecstasy, unlike in certain food anime he remembered from his previous life.

Sometimes, Ning felt that his painting style was quite different from everyone else's in this world.

Evidently, the two spiritual chefs felt the same way.

Fatty Li sighed after receiving yet another simple “It's good” from him.

“Brother Ning, as someone who enjoys cooking, you should understand that describing food is also an important skill. How else are you supposed to appreciate the effort that goes into preparing a dish?”

Xuan Fugu nodded solemnly. “Indeed. Taste must be experienced, but understanding why something tastes good is equally important. A skilled chef should be able to distinguish the smallest differences in flavor, texture, and spiritual energy.”

Ning looked between the two of them.

“…”

He had only come here to eat and discuss a few recipes. How had this turned into a lecture on food appreciation?

Fortunately, Fatty Li soon changed the subject.

“Still, it's a pity we're so unlucky today. I thought we'd get to watch a fight while eating.”

“Fight?” Ning repeated, looking up from his plate.

“That's right! This restaurant has a rather unique attraction. Cultivators can settle their grudges in a designated arena, and some even fight purely to entertain the customers in exchange for spirit stones.”

Fatty Li sighed regretfully. “Last time I came here with Xiao Fan, we got to watch a masked female cultivator fight. It was quite a spectacle.”

“I see,” Ning replied, feeling somewhat numb.

A restaurant that doubled as a fighting arena?

Somehow, that made perfect sense in this world.

“The fights between female cultivators are especially popular,” Xuan Fugu added casually. “I once saw two of them get so carried away that they started pulling each other's hair. Quite entertaining, actually.”

“…”

For someone who had spent the past several minutes discussing the profound intricacies of spiritual cuisine, Senior Brother Xuan certainly had some interesting hobbies.

Fatty Li leaned closer and nudged Ning with his elbow. “You can even place bets on the matches. If you're lucky, you can earn quite a few spirit stones.”

“Okay?” Ning raised an eyebrow.

Why was he telling him that?

Did Fatty Li think he was some kind of chronic gambler?

Well, considering how he'd behaved during the Outer Sect Competition, perhaps that impression wasn't entirely undeserved.

Ning decided to ignore him and return his attention to the food.

After they had eaten for a while longer, Xuan Fugu finally brought the conversation back to the reason for their meeting.

“By the way, Junior Brother Ning, what would you like in exchange for your recipes? Would three hundred spirit stones be acceptable?”

Ning considered the offer for a moment.

Three hundred spirit stones was certainly a respectable sum, especially for recipes that relied mostly on ordinary ingredients. However, he had something else in mind.

“Senior Brother, instead of spirit stones, would it be possible for you to teach me the basics of spiritual cooking?” he asked. “Nothing too advanced. I'm mainly interested in learning how to process spiritual ingredients and properly incorporate spiritual qi into food.”

He had no intention of dedicating himself entirely to the spiritual chef profession, but acquiring a foundational understanding would certainly be useful. After all, he planned to learn as much as he reasonably could about the hundred professions, and spiritual cooking was one of the few that directly aligned with his personal interests.

More importantly, if he could prepare spiritual meals himself, he wouldn't have to spend a fortune at restaurants whenever he wanted something delicious.

And considering how expensive some of the dishes here were, that alone made the knowledge worth acquiring.

Xuan Fugu looked at him thoughtfully.

“With your creativity and understanding of recipes, I believe you could go quite far if you seriously pursued spiritual cooking,” he said. “Learning the fundamentals shouldn't be too difficult for you.”

He considered the request briefly before nodding.

“Very well. I have a jade slip containing the basic methods of spiritual cooking. I'll give it to you later, but make sure you don't spread its contents without my permission.”

“Of course. Thank you, Senior Brother,” Ning replied sincerely.

It seemed his collection of inheritances had just grown by another one.

After receiving a substantial amount of spirit stones, Ning had invested most of his newfound wealth into improving himself, and naturally, that included his cultivation environment.

Unlike the typical xianxia protagonist, who either stumbled upon some heaven-defying treasure after tripping over a rock or stubbornly cultivated with nothing but sheer “determination,” Ning preferred a more… practical approach.

Currently, he was using a high-tier Spirit Gathering Formation alongside Flawless Purple Cloud Pills, both of which had significantly improved the efficiency of his pressure cultivation method.

He had also purchased Mind-Stabilizing Incense, a higher-grade spiritual incense that helped sharpen his concentration and maintain a stable state of mind during cultivation. On top of that, strips of Meridian Warming Silk were wrapped around his arms and torso, promoting smoother qi circulation while reducing the strain on his meridians.

All in all, his cultivation setup had become quite luxurious.

At the very least, just as people back on Earth used to brag about their expensive PC setups, Ning could now proudly show off his cultivation setup as well.

Of course, maintaining all of this was far from cheap. Between the pills, incense, formation upkeep, and other cultivation resources, spirit stones were disappearing from his pockets at an alarming rate.

But after spending so many years in this world, Ning had come to understand one fundamental truth: cultivation was everything.

The higher his cultivation, the more opportunities would become available to him, and the easier it would be to earn spirit stones. In that sense, spending money to accelerate his progress was simply another form of investment.

Money makes money, after all.

“To think a hundred spirit stones used to seem like a fortune back then…” Ning muttered, recalling the days when he had to carefully calculate every expense.

He couldn't help but sigh at how much his standards had changed.

Still, there was little point in dwelling on it. He had already spent the spirit stones, and now it was time to make sure they were worth it.

With that thought, Ning closed his eyes and began circulating his qi, gradually immersing himself in cultivation.

His next goal was clear.

The Seventh Stage of Qi Condensation.
