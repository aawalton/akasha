Rosen was practicing in the martial hall at 7:00 PM when it happened. His fists were wrapped with electricity at one moment, and then they just… snuffed out. He looked at his hands and tried to activate his spirit again—but it wouldn’t call. “What the hell’s going on?”

A dozen other students had the same experience and began to panic. Meanwhile, the duo responsible were in their room, aware of what they were doing but completely apathetic to the other students’ plight.

“See?” Hayden said, rubbing Gluttony’s cute little head. “Your ability to intake cora far exceeds the norm. That allows you to deprive other spirits of cora, and thus they can't work. That’s how we’re going to win. And when we win, I’ll find women who want to hug you. How does that sound?”

Gluttony squeaked and jumped onto Hayden’s shoulder, rubbing its body against his ear. Hayden smiled. He loved animals and spirits—both were far better than humans.

“Okay, let’s do this again. But this time, I’m going to teach you how to funnel it into my body. The more we do this, the stronger we get, okay?”

Gluttony nodded resolutely and then waited for Hayden to start breathing.

The air suddenly warped as Hayden circulated cora without restriction. Gluttony followed suit, closing its beady black eyes and activating its recently unlocked spirit core. Their minds linked, and the winged mouse started absorbing cora like a lightning rod attracting electricity.

“Okay,” Hayden said. “Now release that energy in a clean stream.”

Gluttony obeyed, releasing a beautiful stream of cora. It was so dense that it could destroy a normal person's cora channels, but Hayden accepted it greedily. He then purified it within his core and distributed it to his ripped muscles. The cora accelerated his body’s healing and made him significantly stronger. This infusion was usually a gradual process, but with his superior skill and little super-attractor, Hayden would speed-run his way to godhood.

People noticed the results immediately. In fact, the reaction to his changes was so aggressive that it drove Kayline's sister crazy.

Lina stomped through the halls, yelling, “If I hear his name one more god damn time I’m going to flip!”

Everywhere they went, the conversation was always about one person: Hayden Reskco. It didn’t matter where the conversation started; it eventually led back to Hayden. It was driving Lina up the wall, and Kayline was following her around, giggling up a storm.

Lina suddenly stopped when she heard a conversation of interest.

“Hey, did your spirit stop working last night? About seven?”

“Yeah, it did. I heard it happened to Rosen, too.”

“Seriously?”

“Yeah. I guess they’re doing an investigation to make sure people don’t have… I don’t know what it’s called. Something that steals cora.”

“Steals it?”

“Yeah, something like that.”

“Weird… Hey, what do you think happened to Hayden? Did his rat just… disappear or what?”

Lina screamed and charged off as Kayline burst into uncontrollable laughter. The two flew out of the main building, with Kayline snorting and saying, “I’m sorry! I’m sorry! It’s just so…” She laughed harder, and Lina ran faster. Soon, they were on the path leading to the meadow, and Kayline could barely keep her eyes open. That’s why she bumped into Lina when her sister abruptly stopped.

“Hey!” Lina snapped. “Watch it!”

“Sorry… What’s going on?” Kayline scanned the area and saw a dozen students standing at the edge of the forest.

“I don't know. Let's ask.” Lina walked up to the nearest man. “Hey. What's going on?”

He turned to her and back. “Oh… Um… You know that guy who—”

“Hayden,” Lina stated as a fact.

“Yeah, that guy. Well, apparently he went from loner to dramatic kung fu master in like… a day… and now it’s just…” He sputtered with laughter. “I don’t know. He just looks so serious.”

Kayline pushed her way through the crowd eagerly, only to stop and blush when she reached the top.

Hayden was out in the forest, shirtless, showing off his scrawny body as he practiced punches. No chest—no abs. He was as twiggy as his clothing implied. But contrary to the way people were making it sound, Hayden wasn’t jumping around and karate chopping the air. He was just in the middle of the forest, practicing single punches—arms trembling. It was clear by the way his body was reacting that he had never done it before—but he did it with absolute confidence.

The watcher walked up with a wide grin. “See? He’s so serious about it. He's over there like…” The man threw out a single punch and screamed, “Waaaah!” and shook his head rapidly, letting his tongue thrash about. The spectators burst into laughter.

Kayline rolled her eyes and turned, only to see Lina laughing with the others. Kayline’s shoulders slumped. “Seriously?”

“What? It’s funny.”

Kayline huffed and walked off.

“Hey!” Lina followed after her. “I get that he’s your boyfriend and all, but seriously?” Lina punched and let out a “Waaaah!”

Kayline stopped and folded her arms. “Lina, you know damned well what he's doing. You've done it your whole life.”

Lina frowned and folded her arms, too. “Yeah, my whole life. I didn't wait until two weeks before a life-altering tournament, take off my shirt, and start doing it in public. He's making a clown out of himself.”

“Oh? He's making a clown out of himself?” Kayline grinned. “How ‘bout we have ourselves a bet?”

Lina regarded her sister seriously. “What’s the bet?”

“Don’t come back here for seven days. If people are still mocking him when you return, I’ll give you my Horgan expansion elixir.”

Lina’s eyes widened. “Wait—seriously? Like without saying anything?”

“Not a peep. Not to dad—not to anyone. But you can’t come back for a week.”

“And if people don't mock him?”

“You gotta ask him out.”

Lina’s eyes snapped open. “What? No way!”

Kayline giggled. “I’m just joking. He’s mine, so that’s off the table. But come back anyway, I think you’ll be surprised.”

Lina clenched her jaw. “I can’t tell if you’re serious… Whatever.” She turned and stomped off.

Kayline was serious. No, Hayden wasn't “hers,” but she was serious about the bet. She had a feeling he was the real deal—and if he was, she hoped his story would help Lina heal.

Lina was always the person who stuck up for people when they were kids, but her diseased core had taken a serious toll on her confidence. It made Lina feel like an invalid, despite the fact that neither sister would be a rifter. The rifting license wasn't even necessary—it was just a formality—but Lina focused on it because the contrast between the twins was so striking. Kayline could harness overwhelming water spirits; Lina couldn't use one at all. That was psychologically damaging, and now she was taking it out on people.

Lina was going down a bad road—and Kayline couldn't do anything about it.

But then Hayden came along.

Hayden wasn't using spirits. He refused to open his gate and turned down a C-rank battle spirit in favor of a famously useless spirit pet. Instead, he was focusing on martial arts—Lina’s specialty. He worked out and practiced—like her. Now, he was rapidly becoming popular simply because he stuck his head out.

Sure, students were mocking him—but it wasn't negative. It was laced with interest, and it would soon solidify. And if Hayden won? It would signal to Lina that she could become popular and triumph without a spirit. That was the hope, anyway.

Kayline just wanted her sister back—the nice one. She was placing faith in Hayden to help her.

Hayden wasn't so noble. Far from helping anyone, he was about to help himself to all the cora in the tiny campus.

It was five till 7:00 PM, the time he had chosen for infusion. Gluttony sat on his shoulder excitedly.

“Okay, Gluttony,” Hayden said as he sat down. “It's time to eat.”

Three days later, Rosen was in the martial hall, taking out his frustration on a cora-reinforced dummy. He screamed and hit it in the face, alternating fists between words.

“Even. When. He. Clowns himself. He’s. Popular!”

Rosen push-kicked the dummy, but it self-corrected. That pissed him off, so he kicked it harder. It came back up, so he kicked it with brutal force, but it still bounced back yet again.

“You son of a bitch!”

Rosen wrapped his fists with electricity as he prepared to leave savage Lichtenberg figures on the recalcitrant freak, but before he struck—the electricity suddenly disappeared.

He instantly looked at the clock—and screamed.
