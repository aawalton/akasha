Night fell over Ronswick. The city crystals, all powered by mana, lit the streets, casting soft hues across the stone ground and walls.

Few people walked along the sidewalks. Many chose to take night carriages to reach their destinations. Those who did walk were mostly out for a quiet stroll, enjoying their drinks or the company of their partners.

Tonight, Veronica made her way toward a fine restaurant on the wealthier eastern side of the city. It was one of those establishments that required a reservation. She didn’t have the wealth or status to book a place like this, at least not currently, but Nasha, as the city’s Crimson Chanteuse, had secured them a table without issue.

As she rounded the corner and her eyes fell on it, she knew it had to be the place.

A custom signboard sat above the entrance, bold lettering reading, “Allsman’s.” The building itself was grand, with intricate craftsmanship worked into its exterior. Small, raised pillars stood at each corner, each carved with stone birds frozen mid-flight. Large, neatly trimmed bushes lined the outer walls, carefully maintained.

As Veronica approached, she felt a slight… tenseness in her body.

Was it strange that she felt… nervous? Gwen’s ramblings earlier had lingered in her mind. Veronica had expected a girl her age to have an active imagination, but Gwen’s certainty that something would happen… it refused to fade.

Her words about things being different outside the capital stirred further thoughts. In Annesheim, Veronica had never heard of a female couple before. How would that even work?

As she approached the building, she slowed, glancing around. “Did I arrive too early…?” she murmured.

“Hey, sweet thing.”

Her eyes widened as she turned, spotting Nasha standing right beside her. “W-when did you get here?”

“Just now. Hope you weren’t waiting long,” Nasha said with a smile.

Veronica’s gaze drifted down to her dress. It looked similar to the one she had worn at the Crimson Court. Or… perhaps it was the same one.

A sleek red dress that didn’t bother hiding her long legs. As Veronica looked up, she noticed her short dark-red hair had been styled into soft waves. Long red sleeves covered her arms, and her lipstick matched the color perfectly. Whatever her beauty routine was, it left little else to be desired.

“No, I just got here,” Veronica said.

Nasha smiled and slipped her arm through Veronica’s. “Shall we go in?”

“Ah… yes. Let’s go.”

As they stepped through the entrance, they were met by a waiting lobby. A sharply dressed man in a black suit stood behind a short podium. His eyes swept over them, as if quietly judging their appearance.

“Reservations?” he asked.

“Nasha and Veronica. Ten o’clock,” Nasha said.

He glanced down at a small notepad, scanning the entries. “Hmm. Ah, yes. Couples seating. Table B3.” He crossed off the listing before reaching into a lower compartment, pulling out two small cards.

“Take these and present them to your server once seated.”

Nasha took them without hesitation. “Thanks, Tharen.”

The man gave a small nod and gestured for them to proceed.

Still linked arm in arm, Veronica walked forward, though she couldn’t help but ask, “Did he say couples seating?”

Nasha glanced at her briefly before looking ahead again. “Ah, yes. Most of the tables were reserved. It was the only two-person spot available.”

Veronica hummed softly. That made sense. They were in a high-class restaurant. It was likely filled with nobles and wealthy merchants, especially on a late Friday night.

As Veronica stepped into the dining hall, she took in the scene before her, surprised by how grand it felt for a city of this size.

Warm golden light filled the room, cast from crystal fixtures set into the ceiling. The glow reflected softly off polished floors and dark wooden paneling, giving the space a quiet, refined atmosphere. Rows of neatly arranged tables stretched across the room, each one dressed in clean linens and topped with glass orbs filled with motes of bubbling light.

There were many guests present, but the room was far from crowded. Conversations stayed low, blending into a steady murmur that never rose above the clink of utensils and glass.

As they walked further in, Veronica’s eyes moved from table to table.

Most were arranged for small groups, though she spotted a few that were open for two. Some were placed along the outer edges near the walls, offering a bit more privacy. Others sat out in the open, no different from any other seating.

Perhaps it gets a bit more packed a little later.

Once they reached their table, they each took their seats.

Not long after, a waitress appeared, almost without warning.

Nasha handed over their cards, and the woman took them without hesitation before placing two menus on the table.

“Drinks?” she asked.

“I’ll take the house specialty wine,” Nasha said.

The waitress nodded and turned to Veronica.

“I’ll have the same.”

The woman smiled. “I’ll be right back. Please take your time with the menu.” She stepped away, leaving them alone.

Veronica lightly gripped the edge of her menu. As she looked down at the prices, she saw that most dishes sat in the upper hundreds, with some of the more elaborate entrees reaching into the thousands. It wasn’t surprising, but it was still high considering how much she currently had.

More importantly…

“Hey, Nasha?” Veronica asked.

Nasha tilted her head, smiling. “Yes?”

“You said you’d be fine with us going to dinner together instead of me paying you the ten thousand vix.”

She hummed. “I did.”

Veronica frowned slightly. “Then why… are we here? In a place like this? Restaurants like these usually have an entry fee. It couldn’t have been cheap. Why suggest this instead of just taking the money?”

Nasha shrugged. “The money isn’t that important to me,” she said simply. “It’s more about the experience, I suppose. I don’t mind spending a few vix for that.”

Experience, huh? So that’s what this is all about. Just like I thought.

“Well… what do you want to know?” Veronica asked. “You overheard my conversation with Galland and Gwen. So you already know I’ve come back from the future, right?”

Nasha hummed, tilting her head. “I did hear that. I also heard about Gwen.” Her expression softened slightly. “I didn’t mean to listen in on that part, but… it sounds like she’s been through quite a lot.”

She leaned forward, her gaze settling directly on Veronica.

“But you have too, haven’t you?” she asked quietly. “Been through a lot, I mean. A survivor of the end of the world?”

“And you believe me?”

“Maybe,” Nasha said, almost absentmindedly. “It doesn’t hurt to believe. I should at least hear you out, shouldn’t I? Seems interesting. Those two didn’t seem to have any trouble accepting it.”

“Is that what you want, then?” Veronica asked. “To know about the future?”

Nasha smiled, her voice slow and smooth. “I do. I want to learn about everything. What happens. And about you.”

That last part caught Veronica off guard.

“Me?” she asked hesitantly.

A soft chuckle slipped from Nasha. “Of course. You said you were one of the last survivors, didn’t you? One of the strongest mages in the world. I’d like to hear your story.”

Before Veronica could respond, the waitress returned with their drinks, setting them down.

“May I take your orders?”

Veronica pressed her lips together. The questions could wait.

Veronica began recounting the future: what she had lived through herself. From her life within Valeborn Academy, to the late nights spent sneaking around and the bonds she formed with Victor. The times she drove Viya to frustration, and the arguments she had with Martin over whether magic or magitech was superior.

Of course, that also included the demon invasion. When she lost contact with her closest friends. When she learned that Annesheim had fallen. When she watched her mentor die on the battlefield.

All of it had begun with the attack on Twines Village.

The fires. The screams. The people she couldn’t save. The little boy named Milo that she couldn’t ever forget.

It went on longer than she expected. Her food had cooled, nearly to room temperature. And yet, despite her rambling, Nasha listened without interruption.

“And you couldn’t find Victor after that?” she asked.

Veronica shook her head. “I tried. I went looking, but Parek told me it was too dangerous. There were too many places that needed help.”

Nasha continued to listen, asking questions along the way.

Some… sharper than she expected for a simple dinner.

“Do you regret it…?” Nasha asked quietly. “About Milo? About his sister?”

“I…” Veronica began. “…of course I do. It was my fault he died. If I had been faster. Stronger. Maybe I could have saved more people.”

At some point during her recounting, Nasha had reached across the table and taken her hand, holding it firmly. Veronica hadn’t even noticed when it happened.

Of course, there were lighter moments as well. Moments where Nasha laughed, clearly amused.

“And you really sold the Bow of Artemis for gambling chips?” Nasha said with a chuckle.

“It—it was on impulse, alright?” Veronica replied quickly. “We got it back later.”

Hours passed. Night gave way to midnight.

A few others remained in the restaurant—mostly couples, some too drunk to pay attention to the time displayed on the wall.

Veronica noticed that after they had arrived, very few new guests had entered. There were clearly more seats available than just the couple’s section Nasha had reserved. Still, she chose not to comment on it.

“And your plan is to make everyone stronger?” Nasha asked softly. “So we survive the invasion?”

“That’s the goal. That’s… always been the goal since I came back,” Veronica said. “I’m trying to speed things up. To make magic more accessible. Put the right people in place earlier, like Galland. If the invasion is inevitable, then I have to make sure we are strong enough to fight back.”

A quiet pause settled between them. It wasn’t the first, and it wouldn’t be the last.

“You know…” Nasha began, lightly tapping her fork against the plate. “For such a lofty goal… I’m surprised you’re still willing to go this far, considering everything you’ve been through.”

Veronica raised a brow. “What do you mean?”

Nasha looked up at her. “You were cursed to die,” she said plainly. “And to save you from that, they locked you away in another kind of prison. Forced you to study and learn.”

“That was the only way I could live,” Veronica said. “If I didn’t grow stronger, the curse could have taken me at any moment.”

“Mm. But did it feel that way to you?”

Veronica lowered her gaze, confused. “What do you mean?”

Nasha lifted her fork, gesturing loosely. “They kept you at Valeborn Academy to learn magic. Fine. But they also forbade you from leaving? To the point you had to sneak out?”

“They… probably didn’t have a choice. They couldn’t risk losing me if I wandered freely. I had a fragile constitution.”

Nasha hummed. “How many other geniuses were at Valeborn?”

“Geniuses?” Veronica repeated. Her gaze drifted upward as she thought. “Well… it’s Valeborn. There were many. But if you mean only prodigies… then maybe over a dozen.”

“And were they allowed to walk freely?”

Veronica looked back at her. “What?”

“Those other students. If they were just as gifted, then were they allowed to move around freely?”

“I—well, yes, they were, but—”

“You said you didn’t have any duties at Valeborn, correct?”

She nodded. “Not until I reached the 6th-Tier. That’s when mages learned flight magic.”

“And after that, your first assignments were monster eliminations?”

She nodded again.

“And once you reached the 7th and 8th tiers, your tasks became more violent. You became a war mage for the Kingdom. You fought when other countries attacked. When trouble brewed between borders. You fought and killed for the Kingdom.”

“…yes. I did.”

Nasha pressed her lips together, lowering the tip of her fork against the plate.

“It sounds to me like they weren’t just trying to ‘save’ you. They used you,” she said.

Before Veronica could respond, Nasha continued.

“You weren’t given the freedom to enjoy anything, despite living in the safest, most heavily guarded city in the Kingdom. You were confined to a dorm, rarely allowed to socialize with people your own age. They ran experiments on you—you said you agreed to them, but that doesn’t mean much when they only started after earning your trust. And once you became strong enough, you were turned into a soldier.”

Veronica frowned. “It sounds worse when you say it like that, but it’s still true that I survived. I became a 10th-tier mage with their support.”

“One the Kingdom could use whenever they wanted.”

“Your words are starting to sound like treason, Nasha.”

“You don’t exactly like the Kingdom either, do you?” she asked.

Veronica paused, meeting her eyes. “What makes you say that?”

“I’m good at reading people,” Nasha said, folding her hands together and resting her chin on them. “I told you and Gwen before that I enjoy moving around during my performances. Watching how people react, it’s what I do. It’s what I’m good at.”

She paused. “When you spoke about those kids who died, I saw the hurt in your eyes. When you talked about Victor, I saw happiness. But the others? That assistant—Viya? Your teacher—Maeve? It wasn’t the same. It looked more like… respect. Or dependence. And whenever you mentioned the duties you did for the kingdom… I saw hollowness. A void of nothing in your eyes. No respect. No compassion.”

“What… are you trying to say?”

Nasha exhaled softly. “I’m saying…” She slowed, choosing her words. “Your life was terrible.”

Veronica raised a brow, surprised by her bluntness.

“You had no real friends aside from one man who spent his time gambling—and even then, he was probably old enough to be your uncle or grandfather. The people you trusted most were those assigned to you by the kingdom. Viya, Maeve, even that Parek guy. You mentioned Martin, but like everything else, you barely spoke to him unless politics required it. You had no lover. And you weren’t even allowed contact with your own family—not until you were too strong to control. Your entire life was controlled to the point that you know less about the world than even a noble does.”

She leaned back slightly.

“It’s like you’re one of those magitech golems I hear about. Designed to just execute orders and nothing else,” Nasha said. “You’re trying to save the world because others expect you to, isn’t that right?”

“Well, of course people expect me to save them,” Veronica replied. “It’s what I should do.”

“Yeah?” she repeated, amused. “And because they told you so, that means you have to do it?”

Veronica frowned. “Are you saying I should just quit and let people die?”

Nasha shook her head. “No.” She held her gaze. “But I think you’re confusing your own sense of kindness with something others decided for you.”

“What are you talking about?”

“It’s your job to save the world, right?” Nasha said, leaning back. “It’s your fault people died. It wasn’t their fault they kept you from socializing. Of course you have to fight for the kingdom that ‘saved’ you.”

She leaned forward, her expression sharpening even more. “Of course you’re the one who has to go back and fix everything. No, you don’t get to rest. Everything falls on you now. Twelve years until the invasion? That’s twelve years of nonstop work, every day spent pushing yourself forward, isn’t it?”

Veronica said nothing.

Nasha paused as well, letting the silence settle between them.

“I’m sorry if I’m being harsh,” she said after a moment. “But hearing your story… it makes me angry. It feels like too much for one person. You were kept isolated your entire life, like some subject in a lab. Only after you proved useful did they give you more freedom—and even then, they kept watching you. The leash was always there, wasn’t it?”

Nasha turned slightly, signaling to a waitress waiting near the kitchen doors.

She looked back at Veronica. “You said earlier that you don’t like killing innocent people, even if they’re from another nation. Because they’re just following orders, like any other soldier. You stay in the sky, casting from a distance like an archer… because you’re afraid to look them in the eyes as they die.”

Nasha's gaze stayed steady, staring directly into her eyes, as if reading them like words on a page. “Assassins and swordmasters are trained for that. It’s easy to kill someone who deserves it. It’s much harder when they don’t. Even a young boy can kill if he sees someone attacking his mother—but try handing that same child a knife and telling him to kill his neighbor. He won’t be able to do it.”

Nasha frowned, ever so slightly. “And you… holding a child who’s crying because you couldn’t save them in time… that’s not something you just brush off. That stays with you. It always will.”

The footsteps of the waitress approached, but Nasha added one last thing before they arrived. “Even if they meant well… Viya, Maeve, whoever—it doesn’t change what they did. Whether they realized it or not, they still treated you like something to be used. They made you kill when you didn’t want to. Forced you into wars you never wanted to be a part of. You were a tool. Something the kingdom raised.”

Her voice softened, just slightly.

“Not something they truly cared for.”
