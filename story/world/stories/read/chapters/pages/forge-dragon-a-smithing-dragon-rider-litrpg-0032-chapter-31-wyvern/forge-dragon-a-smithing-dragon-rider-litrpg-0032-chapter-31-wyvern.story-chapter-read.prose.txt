“It’s beautiful,” Kelia said as Caleb handed the ring to her.

He’d wanted to plan something special to give her the ring, but after he’d spent so long making it right in front of her, she was losing her patience.

“No stats?” she asked, not disappointed, but more as an observation as she put it on.

“It’s supposed to have a mana aspect to it. Don’t you see it?”

Kelia shook her head.

“Hmm.”

Caleb thought over the implications.

Is it that she doesn’t have the system, or that she doesn’t have mana?

Caleb looked at Kelia through his mana perception and saw that she had a vague bubble of mana around her typical to everyone else. The only person in town whose mana could be described as anything other than a “vague bubble” was Master Dimel and his was a “faint bubble” at best.

“I love it,” she said, place it on her finger and giving Caleb a kill.

Grrrr

Came Bog’s protest at the contact and from out the window Avis let out a screech.

“We are going to need to fix those two,” Caleb observed.

As was his custom after a long working session, Caleb washed up and went to bed—regardless of the time of day.

***

He woke the next day around noon to his brother’s insistent pounding.

“Wake up! There’s a wyvern!”

Caleb jumped out of bed.

Wyverns were like dragons. Magical beasts which served as terrors to the village. Unlike dragons, no one had ever been initialized after slaying one and as such, no one would mind if the two brothers killed it. They were beasts with two rear legs and wings as front appendages like birds. Also like birds, they had feathers all over their bodies. Unlike birds they bore dragon like scales beneath these feathers and some could breath elemental attacks if they had an aspect for it.

Caleb had felt like he was on the verge of some sort of breakthrough whether it was a level up in his skills, class level or even a new skill, but he couldn’t exactly explain how. Crafting that ring had only made the feeling grow, like he had a large amount of energy and no outlet to spend it on.

He suspected that he needed to kill a beast and absorb its essence to break through. He suspected that progress towards skills and levels could be made outside direct combat, but it took battle to unlock the breakthrough. If he was right, it explained why his brother had gained two levels from killing a dragon while Caleb hadn’t even gained the one. He’d been fresh off a level up from the basilisk and his brother had been stagnant for months despite hunting.

Caleb opened the door to see Grisim decked out in his thick hunting leathers holding the spear, helm, and breastplate for Caleb.

“Master Dimel said you could take these back for now,” Grisim explained, helping his brother into the breastplate.

“Did you see?!” Kelia asked, running into the forge herself from the back.

“What do you think we’re doing?” Grisim asked, teasing. “Going to tea?”

“I’m coming with you,” Kelia demanded, earning no protests from the other two.

She was the best tracker out of the three of them if you took enhanced stats out of the mix. She’d grown up in the forest while the brothers had just grown up near it. Avis would be helpful in keeping sight of the wyvern should it flee and if Caleb was lucky, the dragon bird hybrid would eat the hawk.

All geared up, they ran out towards the mountains as Grisim explained the sighting. Bog had travelled out into the wilderness straight from the mill while the others had travelled through town on a more direct route. Caleb had tried to convince the dragon to stay but there was no reasoning with him when he was worried about Caleb. He’s sent assurances that he’d stay hidden and would find them.

“It ate some goats, it’s not too big. A bit smaller than that dragon we killed,” Grisim spoke with confidence Caleb didn’t think he’d have felt a week back. “No aspect anyone can tell. It was brown.”

As they ran, Caleb reconfigured his ingots to give him a +4 to constitution to aid him in the travelling. It was easy for him to get used to his enhanced strength from the ingots, but being able to run so long without tiring would never grow old for him.

On they ran, past the scene of the dead goat at which point Kelia sent Avis up into the air. Caleb equally marveled at his wife, that she was keeping up with the pair of them without being initialized. Yes, she also had an ingot of constitution from Caleb, but even without it he knew she could run for hours at this pace without slowing.

A short while later the hawk returned and circled above, waiting for them to follow.

Avis led them up the mountain, for an hour before swooping down to land on Kelia’s shoulder. The clever bird then directed them across the mountain and then grew still tilting her head in a way signaling caution.

Kelia crept ahead, leaving the two loud boys behind as he followed her bird’s direction.

“That bird is too smart for its own good,” Caleb said to his brother.

“Is that why it hates you? Because it sees something about you, we can’t,” Grisim teased out of habit, but he was focused on Kelia’s path and at alert with his spear drawing.

Kelia returned and gestured to them to follow quietly.

“We are on a ridge above its nest. It has eggs.”

That was unexpected.

They’d never seen a wyvern that nested in this region, typically the dragons scared them away. But, since Caleb and Grisim had just killed the oldest dragon on their peak, it made sense something else would move in.

“If we can catch it in it—” Kelia began when a shriek broke the quiet.

“Bog!” Caleb shouted, sensing the sudden fear from his dragon.

They all ran toward the nest in time to see a confused Bog working up a molten spit to fire at the wyvern that was swooping down onto him.

He barely fired off the molten steel before the wyvern struck him in a dive. The steel missed, crashing into a pine tree that went up into smokey sooty flames.

The talons of the wyvern did no damage to the dragon, but the force of the blow sent him rolling uncontrolled down the mountain slow.

Kelia began firing arrows at the wyvern, drawing its attention while Grisim and Caleb charged screaming a battle cry.

The wyvern called out again loudly and an answering cry came from above.

“Two!” they all shouted in unison.

Caleb knew they had to move decisively. The wyvern possibly only saw them as mere humans, and they had a chance to take it by surprise.

Grisim was in the lead, charging the first wyvern. Caleb pulled his spear out over his shoulder then hefted it to throw. Covering his attack, Kelia shot arrow after arrow at the wyvern’s eyes. The beast closed its eyes and turned its head, the shaft deflecting harmlessly, but it gave the brothers the opening they needed. Caleb switched out his constitution bonuses for strength, bringing it up to 16. A wave of exhaustion went through his body as he lost 4 points of constitution, but he pushed through it.

He fed his Arcane Power Strike half of his stamina, and a third of his mana, and threw the spear. It flew from his hand like it had been fired by a giant crossbow and flew straight for the heart of the wyvern. Unfortunately, in its dodging of the arrows its body shifted, and the spear struck it in the base of its right wing, passing through the comparatively thinner flesh and passing out the back, sailing through and falling down the mountain side in Bog’s wake.

The wyvern reared up in pain, expositing its underbelly to Grisim who’d finally closed the distance, striking it in the chest.

Ding!

Caleb heard but ignored.

He spun around looking up to the sky to see a larger white wyvern swooping down at them.

Kelia was firing arrow after arrow into it as it came down for her. Grisim pulled his spear out of the dead wyvern and was running back up the mountain to meet them.

Caleb had only his hammer and a sack of nails. He threw the nails, pushing them with the full force of his Imperium Metallorum and then switching to his Ventorum pushed the air below the wyvern up. The dragon bird hybrid had begun a to breath frost when the metal fragments hit it. It turned away from the pain just as the wind shifted and lifted it out of its path. Together the two attacks saved Kelia from a direct strike from the frost breath, but she was skill grazed by it.

She let out a scream of shock at the cold but moved right back into firing arrows at the retreating wyvern.

“Gris! I’ll pull it into your spear!” Caleb shouted to his brother who had regrouped with them. “Lia, keep it blind!”

There was no further discussion as the creature came back around. This time it swooped down with its breath leading the way, blinding the group in the cloud of deathly cold snow.

Caleb had to change the plan and blow up to save them all from freezing to death. The ice flew into it’s creator, blinding it partially and altering its course. The wyvern, sensing the air was not on his side, landed snapping out at Caleb with his tail.

Caleb saw the white barbed appendage flying toward him and knew he lacked the agility to dodge. Instead, he threw his hammer into the ground in front of him as he pushed with his Imperium. The hammer crashed into the stone, crumbling the comparatively soft material into sand and then stopped. Caleb felt the stone crumble as a brief soft push on his body as he began to propel himself away from the hammer, but as soon as the stone was crushed the hammer stopped and the soft push became a neck jerking shove.

He flew back ungracefully and landed on his butt, though he cared little for the embarrassment of it all as the wicked barb flew through the space he’d just occupied.

He recovered to his feet, only to see that the wyvern hadn’t given up with the first attack. It’s tail came right back at Caleb’s chest and he was too far from his hammer to push off of it. Instead, he braced hoping that his breastplate would withstand the blow but not trusting in it blindly. Drawing on his experience with the quenching he reinforced the breastplate, holding it all firm and together with his willpower.

The barbed tail struck the chest plate in the weakest part that was Caleb’s repaired. With the impact, Caleb’s mana dropped dangerously low as all his magical might when into holding the metal together. He felt it give way and a slight prick as the tip of the tail pierced shallowly into his chest, but that was nothing to the pain of the impact as he went sailing, crashing into the ground.

Grisim and Kelia had kept up their attacks as the tail had come for Caleb, Grisim had landed to thrusts ineffectually at its scaled chest, and earned a wing strike sending him flying up the mountain. Kelia kept trees between herself and the head as the dragon seemed dedicated to land a blow with its frost breath on the girl.

Avis, to the bird’s credit, saved Kelia’s life at least once with a well-timed dive at the eyes, and Caleb regretted his joke from earlier. Kelia had run out of trees to hide behind as the wyvern approached on her when a loud familiar hacking sound broke over the noise of battle.

A glowing red glob appeared on the neck of the wyvern, exploding into a hiss of steam before falling to the ground. The wyvern reared up to find Bog gripping its rear talon in his iron mouth. The dragon was glowing red as he let all of his heat out of him and horrible red welts were appearing on the wyvern’s talon and working their way up its leg.

Kelia took advantage of the distraction, and ran at the wyvern, drawing the metal-aspected blade Caleb had crafted. The wyvern had presence of mind enough to try to fend off the attacker who’d yet to attach itself to him, and he blasted her full on with his frost breath.

Kelia ran through the cold, seemingly unhindered and stabbed the wyrmling through the roof of its mouth.

The wyvern collapsed, and the mountain grew quiet, save for the sound of notifications in the two boys’ minds.
