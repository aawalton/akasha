Vaul had never been one to feel much in the way of fear. Sure, there had been a certain tenseness when he climbed the mile-high thorny vines that made up the giant forest that covered his homeland. There were also the times when he had hunted some of the larger beasts within the Thorn Aegis, or fought with the other tribes that dwelled there over resources like the fishing pool that connected to a great underground river. Such events had left him feeling like a drawn bowstring, taut and ready to explode into action. None of them had caused the hunter to feel anxious or scared, after all, the worst that could happen was that he died. Even when Soren Creed had cornered him, and death had seemed mere heartbeats away, Vaul had felt only resignation and exhaustion. It was completely different from the pulse-pounding terror that infected him now.

Vaul was looking out at a gathering of people and buildings so large that he didn’t have the words to describe it. When he had climbed this mountain to find the mad god and the supposed grand destiny that awaited him, Vaul had stuck to the north-facing side of the mountain which, while not entirely without people, had seemingly been populated by small villages and shepherds tending flocks of goats and other woolly beasts he didn’t recognize in valleys and plateaus alone.

After his experiences with the townsfolk further north, along with some very late nights contemplating his new reality, Vaul had taken pains to avoid the people he had seen on the northern side of the mountains. It had been easy enough with how sparsely populated it had been. The…town? He was looking at now was anything but sparse. Even just the sheer amount of noise alone was enough to make him want to recoil, 2it wasn't that any single person or group was particularly loud, but that the combined mass of humanity created a background chatter he found almost deafening.

The part that struck him as perhaps the most insane was that Vaul wasn't yet in the pulsating mass of humanity, but still above it, looking down from a moving platform called an elevator that was descending down the side of the impossible tower that the mad god called his home.

On either side of Vaul were the blonde man and woman who had been commanded to look after him by Soren. The pair of them shared many features with the mad god, including strangely orange eyes and hair so blonde it was almost white. They carried themselves how Soren did too, moving through the world as though reality would bow down to their whims simply because they existed. It had initially left Vaul unimpressed by the sheer arrogance of their every word and movement, and while that assessment hadn't changed, having learned that they were the mad god's siblings, he understood it.

Both of the pair were dressed in white robes that extended to just above their slippered feet. The front of the robes were embossed with lines of vertical script made of golden thread that Vaul couldn't read, with golden wing icons on their backs made of the same thread that glimmered in the mid-morning sunlight as the elevator descended.

Vaul had been offered similar robes, but had insisted upon the far more practical tunic and fur-lined cloak and pants like those he had seen the shepherds wearing. After two decades of life in which the tribesman barely even wore a shirt, he felt extremely overdressed. Still, he had to admit the fur and wool that made up the clothes he had been supplied were both extremely comfortable and an excellent answer to the utterly freezing air that permeated everywhere in the collection of mountains that made up the society known as the Final Peaks.

The choice of clothing had resulted in the pair of ‘Creeds’, as they called themselves sneering at and mocking Vaul, but they seemed to do that about everything he did. It was enough that the hunter didn’t understand why they had been ordered by their brother to look after him before the god had taken a group of his closest followers and headed south.

Back in his home, among the Frostvein tribe, people were often given chores or jobs they didn’t adore. So long as they contributed to the good of the community, they were not, however, expected to commit themselves to tasks they utterly despised. And these two definitely despised him. The man whose name was Brevin constantly made little mocking jokes that he seemed to think Vaul didn’t understand, while the woman Reilyn had told him to his face several times that Soren had invited him here to give him a Deck no one could control, and would instantly kill him.

The attitudes of his minders didn’t bother Vaul; those were merely impotent frustrations of people who didn’t understand him coming out in voice. It wasn’t like looking down from the elevator; that was truly terrifying. The Final Peaks was a collection of five mountains, each of which had been carved out and worked upon until the peak of each mountain had been turned into a vast tower of stone pillars and statues standing dozens, sometimes hundreds of feet tall.

The towers might be feats of miraculous engineering and enormous effort, but it was the valley that connected the five mountains that made Vaul's heart race.

While the valley dipped down low compared to the mountains whose shadows it sat in, it was still thousands of feet in the air. Every inch of the valley was covered in signs of human habitation. Here a hall of stone and bronze that could have easily fit Vaul's entire village, there a highway with hundreds or even thousands of people on it traversing the valley. There a market ringed by multi-leveled blocky buildings, here a temple that was surrounded by gardens and statues, and everywhere people, people, and more people.

“Welcome to Parthis, you bumpkin.” Said Brevin with an obvious sneer. “The city at the center of the world. Vaul didn’t know what a bumpkin was, however he had heard the man call him that several times and knew it was an insult of some sort.

For a few seconds, Vaul considered holding his tongue; it was what he had been doing for the past week whenever the pair let their frustrations get the better of them. Now however, he simply couldn’t do it. Not with the elevator mere seconds from depositing him at the heavily guarded base of the tower that Soren, and now he called home.

If Vaul was going to have to descend into this swirling, roaring mass of humanity, he would do it with his head held high, and as much acid on his tongue as he could muster. Vaul knew that the pair of ‘Creeds’ had noticed how much the sheer population in the Final Peaks unnerved him, and that was why they had insisted it was time for him to see the place where most of the people here dwelled.

“You know.” Said the hunter with a thoughtful expression on his face as he turned to look at Brevin. “I’ve noticed that there are about thirty or so of you with blonde hair and orange eyes. Soren has you all in positions of honor and import. Generals, warriors, advisors, and wisefolk in charge of important things like water and food for thousands of people, at least one of you is a high priest of some sort.”

Before he could finish, Reilyn interrupted, with obvious anger flaring in her fine features.

“You are about to deride us? Make mockery of the fact that we two are acting as mere minders. An ignorant fool like you has no idea the importance of what we do for the Lord of Creeds!” Brevin, on Vaul’s other side, nodded.

“That's right, savage, you could never understand our importance.” The man added in a pointless display of bluster, basically just repeating what his sister had already said.

“Well, that’s the thing,” Vaul replied as the circular platform that was the elevator groaned to a stop. “ Every one of you Creeds has an important job, which must mean minding me is important.” Vaul turned and kept both of his antagonists in view as he stepped backwards off of the elevator onto the squared platform that sat at the top of a wide series of thirty stone steps, each large enough that a warrior dressed in ornate bronze armor could stand at attention at the far side of them.

“You are an arrogant fool.” Snapped Reily. “Like I’ve said for days now, once The Lord of Creeds returns, he will force you to bond with The King of The First Epic, and it will destroy you like it has everyone else.”

Vaul smiled and raised a single finger, which he shook towards the robed woman as though to demonstrate that she had made a good point.

“There’s the other thing.” He replied as he moved down the first step backwards. “Your god told me I should call him Soren, not any of his fancy titles like you have to, and he forced me to accept this Deck you keep saying will instantly kill me almost a week ago.”
