Phasima, the Empress of the Forest, was having a particularly lousy day. For one thing, it was incredibly boring being stuck in a forest with nothing but a few winter-themed creatures to obey her whims. None of them were even sapient. What was worse, she had a whole host of potential playmates at her disposal, but her instructions were to keep them frozen.

She couldn’t even thaw one of the heroes out and force them to have tea. A pity.

The Empress rose from her rime-covered throne and paced up and down the chill tiles that paved her castle. She felt inexplicably anxious. Perhaps it was the desolation, or maybe the isolation, but she found herself missing her sister, who was also playing a role in the Elite Systemic Games. The two of them had been inseparable for centuries, ever since their home universe was destroyed, leaving them the only surviving members of their race.

Phasima hoped that her sister was having a better time than she was. Then she changed her mind and hoped that her sister was having a worse time than she was. That would be better.

Stupid Prysma - always making out better than me, she grumbled to herself. Her sister had always been the lucky one: born with a stronger connection to the elements, cherished by the System, feted by the Vessarian elders. Phasima always seemed to be riding her sister’s coattails, even though she had been extremely talented in her own right. What others saw as nepotism was merely the result of years of grinding effort.

The Empress of the Forest sighed as she turned around, having completed her usual circuit around the ice palace that served as her temporary home. The instructions from the System stated that she only needed to wait until the Knight arrived and then attempt to kill them, along with any allies they happened to bring. Beyond that, she had no duties and few subjects. She sighed: the waiting was simply killing her.

Perhaps she should release the winter wolves from their cages and see if she could successfully ride one. But in order to make things really fun, she’d need someone to compete against. But the only humanoid minions she had were ice trolls, and she wasn’t allowed to permanently damage her underlings for fun. Not that she really wanted to, for she wasn’t really a vicious person at heart. Merely inured to the countless horrors of the multiverse, and supremely bored.

Phasima pushed the intrusive thoughts away and continued her pacing. Much to her surprise, she suddenly heard the flapping of frenzied wings. Moments later, the possessor of the wings burst into view. It was one of her birds, bearing a message from the forest’s perimeter. He held out a finger and allowed the little avian to perch. Its tiny chest rose and fell as it panted, trying desperately to catch its breath while delivering its report.

“Tweet, tweet, tweet!” it sang.

“I’m sorry, I’m afraid you’ll have to slow down a bit,” the Empress of the Forest said with a chuckle, amused at her scout’s urgency. “My birdspeech is a bit rusty.”

“Tweet, tweet tweet!” the little bird repeated.

“Are you sure about this?” the Empress asked, still not believing what she had just heard.

The bird simply nodded.

“It’s far too early for the Knight to have entered the forest. It’s been, what, only three hours since this simulation started? This doesn’t make any sense. At the very least it should have taken them a day to get everything organized, even if they skipped most of the optional objectives. And then there’s the travel time to consider…did the Knight hire mounts? I know that the city has some fast horses, but they aren’t THAT fast.”

“Tweet,” the scout explained.

“The Knight possesses wings, and it can fly faster than the speed of sound?”

The tiny bird nodded again.

“Then how did the message reach you so quickly? None of your kin are that fast,” the Empress noted.

“Tweet!”

“That makes no sense! How could a tweet relay have made it here before the Knight if it’s flying FASTER than the speed of sound?”

“Tweet!”

The Empress of the Forest sighed again.

“I’m not even going to dignify that with a response.”

Its message delivered, the small bird scout flapped away, no doubt having successfully fulfilled its role in this ridiculous charade. The Empress of the Forest watched it depart, wondering what would happen if she encased it in ice. Given its innate resistance to cold, she could probably keep the bird free from any serious harm until the scenario ended. She smiled as she considered how she would go about it.

Despite the scenario limiting her raw Attributes somewhat, since the Knight was only level 49, Phasima had lost none of her skill in manipulating the elements. Her opponents could never match her mastery of cold …well…that is if she excluded that ludicrous boss from the Systemic Games Battle Simulation, which she had failed to defeat despite several attempts. But she had learned much since then, and grown more powerful than she could have imagined possible back in those days.

The memory soured as her thoughts turned toward her team. She and her sister had teamed up with a group of physical fighters that coordinated by sharing their senses during battle. With Phasima and Prysma providing ranged damage, buffs and debuffs, and control, the team made it through their Battle Royale with ease. Then they won their next two matches before being completely annihilated by a two-person team that focused on extreme physicality, blitzing the sisters before their melee could react. It had been a humiliating defeat, but an instructive one.

After that, Prysma leaned even more heavily into the element with which she showed the highest aptitude: fire. She eventually evolved her fire affinity into something far greater. Phasima, on the other hand, chose to diversify. While she had cultivated fire herself for decades, the main reason she had done so was because she had been expected to follow in her sister’s footsteps. But once the System informed her that she no longer had a home to go back to, the young Vessarian slowly began to realize that she was no longer bound to the expectations of her Elders. She could develop her powers as she wished.

To that end, she had worked tirelessly to upgrade her basic Fire Core into something more flexible, drawing inspiration from the multiverse as she travelled it in service of the System. Eventually, her Fire Core evolved into a Manifold Core, able to handle many different types of aspected mana. To celebrate her achievement, she challenged her sister to a spar, thinking that she would handily defeat Prysma now that she could wield fire, ice, lightning, wind, and even more esoteric elements.

She was dead wrong. Prysma had defeated Phasima with fire alone, her mastery over what seemed to be the simplest element allowing her to use it in ways that Phasima could only dream about. The contest wasn’t even close: if anything, Prysma had shown her sister that the gap between them was wider than ever.

A sonic boom shook the Empress of the Forest’s castle, bringing her back to the present and announcing that the Knight had arrived, just as the bird scout had promised. The Empress’ smile widened as she allowed her minion to escape unharmed. She had a visitor to receive.

There would be no third humiliation.

Charlemagne hiccupped as he approached the majestic palace that seemed to be made entirely of ice, his stomach still processing the enchanted metal that he’d consumed an hour earlier. The deep green of the forest’s thickly packed trees, broken only by a solitary road penetrating into the heart of the wooded area, had been supplanted by a shroud of white, which also covered the one road. But it wasn’t just the bleak surroundings that caused the pale blue edifice to stand out. An interesting mana signature was radiating from inside the castle. Someone or something strong had taken up residence there.

“Perhaps you could drop us in the courtyard there,” Phatagin shouted over the rush of air as Charlemagne began to dive straight toward the source of the powerful mana signature.

The rooster Knight ignored his Squire’s suggestion as he plummeted downward. Automated defenses built into the tall, fragile-looking towers sprang to life, sending shards of razor-sharp ice ripping through the air in dense streams. Charlemagne adjusted course to dodge, but whatever devices or enchantments that were controlling the icy projectiles were up to the challenge. In just a few moments, they had already accounted for the rooster’s new trajectory.

Josephine screamed and shut her eyes as the first wave of shards slowed considerably before striking true, right into a fiery mana shield that Charlemagne managed to conjure at the last moment. Then, without warning, he suddenly accelerated, lurching through the converging streams of projectiles and forcing the turrets to once again adjust.

But by then it was too late. With an incredible crash, Charlemagne slammed straight through one of the castle walls. A veritable avalanche of ice followed in his wake as the bird turned into a literal wrecking ball, his mana shield absorbing the heavy impacts even as it softened the icy walls. The rooster finally dropped his passengers a moment later, after the group had burst into the cavernous throne room.

Josephine, having overcome her previous fear, was exhilarated by the violent entrance.

“Oh yeah!” she exclaimed.

Phatagin, still in the Attendant’s arms, paid more attention to their landing, utilizing his Momentum Core to bring the pair down safely to the ground.

Charlemagne, on the other hand, paid his ex-passengers no heed, having only eyes for the Empress of the Forest.

“Bawk!” he called out to her as she rose from her throne and into the air with an effortless grace. She floated forward until she stood mere steps away from the Knight who had so boldly challenged her.

“Thank you for calling, Ser Knight. This assignment has been incredibly tedious…I don’t even have a sparring partner to train against. Perhaps you would care to relieve me of my boredom? Just the two of us sharing a battle to the death?”

“Don’t listen to her, Charlemagne,” Josephine called. “She’s trying to trick you! Let Phatagin help!”

“If I am being forced to assist, so should you,” the pangolin retorted. “But I am certain that our Knight can best this ‘Empress of the Forest’ without aid.”

A frown flashed across the Empress’ face as she launched an attack past Charlemagne, aiming straight for Josephine. But, despite the attack being literally lightning-fast, it was intercepted by another one of the rooster’s flaming barriers. The icy stone floor sizzled beneath the shield as the lightning bolt dispersed its stored energy into the air. The smell of ozone stung Phatagin and Josephine’s nostrils as they processed what had just transpired.

“I thought all your powers would be ice-themed!” Josephine choked out, more confused than angry.

Phatagin scurried to take cover behind the warm blanket of mana separating him from the Empress’ wrath.

“I concur, strange noble women lying in wait inside a palace made of literal ice should have powers related to the cold,” he added. The pangolin was more angry than confused, largely because he was already chilled from the flight over.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne added.

“I’ll have you know that I possess an affinity with more than just ice! I am Phasima, the Empress of the Forest, and I possess a Manifold Core, which allows me to channel any of the basic elements, and I have mastered them all!”

“Bawk?” the rooster inquired.

“Well no, it’s not exactly like a Mana Core,” the Empress explained, feeling rather confused that the strange group was interested in such pre-battle conversation. These entities represented the cream of the multiverse, the best out of quintillions of entities. Despite feeling foolish, she continued, “Mana Cores are very rare, you see, because the requirements to create one are incredibly steep for the payoff. Well, at least where I am from. Perhaps it is different elsewhere.”

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked as he waved a wing back toward his Squire.

“Well, no, it’s not like a Momentum Core either, which is even rarer in my experience. Wait, is this creature your Squire? Your Squire has a Momentum Core?”

“Bawk!” the rooster announced proudly.
