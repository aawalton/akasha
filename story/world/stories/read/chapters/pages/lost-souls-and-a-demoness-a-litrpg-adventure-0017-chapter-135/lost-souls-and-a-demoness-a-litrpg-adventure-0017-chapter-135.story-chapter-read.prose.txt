"Now that's a view."

Jade joined Luis at the end of the hidden bridge, which extended onto a broad ledge at the edge of a deep ravine. He was standing near the precipice, looking down into the depths of the chasm. The persistent mist was thinner here, giving them a view of the ravine's bottom some hundred feet below. Squinting, Jade could just make out the far side through the fog, and she realized that it was less of a canyon and more of a giant sinkhole. Water from the swamp spilled down the cliffs into the basin in several places, forming magnificent waterfalls that filled the air with sparkling mist.

"This shouldn't be possible." Jade said, examining the sheer walls of the nearby cliff faces. "It should be completely flooded."

Jade didn't know much about environmental science, but she knew enough to understand that a giant sinkhole in the middle of a swamp with this much standing water was more than a little implausible.

"The Labyrinth tends to combine different environmental elements in the same quadrant, even if the result is something you wouldn't find anywhere else." Tara said, joining them. "With enough magic, anything is possible. And this world is filled to the brim with it."

"Well, we aren't getting across this." Naomi said from the rear of the group, staying a safe distance away from the edge. "We'll have to find a way around it."

"I don't think we will." Fenrin said, pointing towards the center of the depression. "Look, there."

Jade followed suit, peering through the rising mist. Much of the bottom of the hole was filled with water, but there was an island in its center. It was too far, and the air too hazy, to make out any details, but Jade could see the faint outline of a tall archway rising from its surface.

"That looks familiar."

"Think it's the entrance to the corespace?" Luis asked.

"We can check."

Jade cast her pathfinder spell, conjuring the compass-like arrow above the back of her hand. It spun around a few times before coming to a stop and tilting down, pointing directly at the bottom of the sinkhole.

"There you have it."

Fenrin gathered everyone together and Jade repeated the spell, pointing out the suspected corespace aperture to everyone.

"How are we getting down?" Aylin asked, eyeing the sheer, slick cliff face with trepidation. "That doesn't look like an easy climb."

"Well, I can fly, but that doesn't help anyone else." Jade said, crossing her arms. They'd briefly experimented with her ability to carry other people while airborne. After the third injury, that had quickly stopped.

"My suit has a slow-fall enchantment." Tara added, conjuring the magically reinforced composite panels over her dark bodysuit. "I can get down."

Fenrin and Eita both had various wind powers that would allow them to descend in relative safety, but that turned out to be the last of the relevant powers. As none of them had the ability to help others do the same, they quickly reached a dead-end.

"I think we need to remember that we'll need to get out of the pit, too." Naomi reminded everyone. "Unless you'd like to be a permanent resident."

"I've got it!" Luis said, snapping his fingers. "Remember that ladder, Jade?"

"Ladder?"

"Yeah, the one you pulled out of your mystery bag, before it turned into that weird undead summoning item."

"What undead summoning item?" Aylin asked, giving Jade a piercing look.

"It's not what you think!" She raised her hands defensively. "It happened during the stronghold expedition in Lysara. I'll explain later."

Jade's bag of mystery had undergone something of a metamorphosis when she'd been forced to shove the necromantic gem inside, right before the bone titan boss emerged to fight them. But before that, she'd been able to pluck random, potentially useful items from it. She recalled the first such item they'd received when testing the bag and grinned.

"Good call, Luis." she said, reaching into her dimensional pouch and pulling forth a large bundle of rope and wooden slats. "I nearly forgot this was in there. It'd be really nice if these things listed the items you put in them…"

"My dimensional bag works like that." Tara said, giving Aylin a smug look. "But I suppose it'd be unrealistic for most artificers to be at a tek'kalla level."

"How about we focus." Aylin said, ignoring the comment. She inspected the rope ladder Jade and Luis were disentangling, stretching it out across the ledge. "That's a start, but I don't think it's long enough."

"I have some rope." Parker offered, pulling out a bundle from his own magical bag.

After pooling resources, they had enough supplies to cobble together a ladder long enough to reach the bottom of the sinkhole. Luis's preparations back on Earth came in handy, with him able to supply a collection of spikes and pitons to secure it with. Jade took to the sky after it was successfully lowered, anchoring the ladder to the cliff wall in several more places to prevent it from flopping around.

"Are we sure this is safe?" Naomi asked, giving the finished result a rather dubious look.

"We're climbing down there to fight monsters, remember? None of this is safe." Luis said.

"I reinforced it with a strengthening ritual, it'll hold up fine." Aylin reassured the group.

"In that case, be my guest." Naomi said, gesturing for her to proceed.

"…Maybe I could do another one, just to be safe."

In the end, Eita was the first to descend, since his wind powers would cushion his fall if their improvised ladder failed. Jade kept watch from the bottom, ready to leap into action if anything went awry. She was almost surprised when the last of them reached the bottom of the sinkhole without incident.

"Am I getting too pessimistic?" She asked Luis as they navigated the large pools left by the cascading waterfalls, alternatively wading through them and clambering over piles of rocks as they made for the island with the archway. "I was sure something was going to go wrong."

"You've always been that way, amiga." Luis chuckled, slapping her on the back. "That's why you've got me around. It's good. We balance each other out."

"What's up with Tara?" Naomi asked quietly. "She was bickering with Aylin all the way through her rituals."Jade glanced over her shoulder at the tek'kalla. She was walking next to Urgraan, the rhyssit member of Parker's team. Rather than wade like the rest of them, he was happily swimming through the thigh-deep water, propelling himself forward with slow, powerful strokes. Jade had a hard time picking out the tek'kalla's emotions from the rest of their group and shrugged.

"I'll ask her about it later."

Jade was first to reach the island, courtesy of her wings. The wisps of mist curling through the surrounding air were no longer fog, but steam. The water, stagnant and cold up above, was clear and warm in the sinkhole. The change was another reminder of the strange magic suffusing the world around them, but it was a welcome one.

The corespace arch itself looked just like its counterpart near the desert quadrant. It stood twice Jade's height at its peak and was formed from a dark, glossy stone that glistened with condensed moisture. The only difference was that this one was empty, with no sheet of dimensional energy filling the space beneath it.

"How does this work…" Jade murmured, running her hand along the smooth stone while she waited for the others to catch up.

As if in answer to her question, a series of blue runes lit up along the arch's curve. The structure hummed with faint vibration and Jade's felt a tremor through her magical senses. A prompt appeared before her.

System Notice

Would you like to activate 'Earth Core #7 Portal'? (Y/N)

That seemed easy enough. Jade mentally accepted the prompt. A sheet of purple light shimmered into existence beneath the arch, and the vibrations increased dramatically. She stepped away, watching the dimensional energy flow like a liquid until it completely filled the archway, bathing the surroundings in an amethyst light. Then, the portal flickered. The sheet of energy shrank down until it barely filled half of the area before abruptly snapping back into place. Jade was forced to scramble away as a fountain of sizzling hot sparks blasted from both sides of the archway, bouncing off the protective shield her armor conjured to protect her. In her haste to escape she almost bowled over Aylin, who had been rushing to join her.

"Sorry." Jade grunted, steadying them both. "What's going on? All I did was accept the prompt to open it."

The others, seeing the commotion, picked up the pace, hurrying to join them.

"I don't know." Aylin replied, not taking her eyes off the archway. The portal was still fluctuating in size, belching more sparks out of one side, and then another. "But I don't think this is normal."

"You aren't sure?"

"I've never seen a corespace portal open before. Did the other one do this?"

"No. It was pulsing a little, but nothing like this. It was already open when we got there, though, so I guess it might have when the demons opened it."

"Let me see what I can find out."

The half-elf rummaged through a pouch at her waist before pulling out a notebook and a large, clear crystal. She raised the gem to one eye, peering through it at the unstable aperture, and sat to start scribbling notes.

"What's wrong with the portal?"

The rest of the group arrived with Luis in the lead, all of them casting uncertain looks at the newly energized archway. Tara flipped down her visor, and Jade saw tiny characters scrolling along its bottom edge as she took a closer look.

"Wow. That is one unstable portal. I can confidently assert that trying to go through it right now would not be a good idea."

"Yeah, I guessed as much." Jade said, although she took another few steps back for good measure. "Is there something we can do about it?"

"Not without a lot of time and effort." Aylin said, stowing her implements and joining Jade with the rest of the group. "But we shouldn't have to. Whatever is happening here, it's slowly stabilizing."

Indeed, the eruptions from the flickering energy inside the archway were growing smaller, with longer intervals between them. They continued to watch as, over the next few minutes, the portal slowly returned to a steady, even shimmer.

"That portal is emitting a lot of energy." Tara commented, still viewing it through her visor.

"What does that mean?" Parker asked, hands on the hilts of his twin swords.

"Either this portal leads somewhere very far away, or there's a strong source of magic on the other side of it. Damn, I should've analyzed the other one when we were there. I don't have anything to compare it-"

The tek'kalla was interrupted by a figure emerging from the sheet of dimensional energy. They were covered from head to toe in full plate armor, with soft radiant light emanating from every gap. The person… creature? Stopped and stared at them, turning its helmeted head slowly from side to side. They all stared back, unsure of what to do.

"Okay." Aylin said quietly. "That definitely isn't normal."

Wary, Jade pulled up the newcomer's information.

Celestial Knight

Level: 31

Creature Type: Spirit

Origin: Labyrinth-born

"Labyrinth creatures can't leave corespace apertures." Tara said, having apparently done the same. "One of the academy instructors made a point of it."

"Apparently, this guy disagrees." Jade muttered.

Level 31 was more than a little concerning. That was by far the highest out of any of the creatures they'd found on the third floor. Indeed, Jade's understanding was that it was higher than should be possible for this region of the Labyrinth. The group continued to stare at the lone entity with varying degrees of wariness. For its part, the celestial knight stood motionless, regarding them impassively.

"Uh, hello?" Luis offered, taking a step forward and raising a hand. "We come in peace?"

The way he said it made it sound more like a question than a statement. Jade was about to remind him that they were here to destroy the Core, something she doubted this creature would consider a gesture of peace, when three more identical knights emerged from the portal behind the first. As one, they clenched their gauntleted hands into fists, conjuring swords of radiant energy that flickered like the rippling portal behind them. Jade suppressed a sigh.

Couldn't the Labyrinth just be normal for once?
