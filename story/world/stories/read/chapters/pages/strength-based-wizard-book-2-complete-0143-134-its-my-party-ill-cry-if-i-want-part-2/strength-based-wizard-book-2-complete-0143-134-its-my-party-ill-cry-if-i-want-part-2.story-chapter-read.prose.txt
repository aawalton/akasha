Chapter 134

It's My Party, I'll Cry if I Want (Part 2)

I read the Quest description once, then read it again.

Well… Seems simple enough, I think. With a simple mental command, I close the System notification. By the looks on my Party’s faces, some of them are still mulling over the exact same System notification. For several seconds, nobody speaks. Then Ivy makes a small noise inside her helmet.

“Huh.”

I turn toward her. “What?”

“That’s peculiar.”

“What’s peculiar?”

“Um… the Quest.”

“Really?”

“Yes, you must explain Ms. Ivy,” says Raul. “I see this as a simple task.”

Ivy uses a gauntleted finger to tap her helmet where I suspect her chin to be. There’s the briefest flicker of blue-white light from within her visor. I realize she’s re-reading the Quest notification.

“There are definitely hidden objectives,” she says.

“Agreed,” Clyde says from my left. He’s near a wall, and I almost don’t notice him as his armor practically leeches into the shadows between the glow of the cavern’s crystals.

I look between them, considering. I recall having completed hidden Quest objectives in the past. “What makes you think that?” I ask. With a mental command I re-summon the Quest description. The faint blue window springs back into my vision, and I re-read the details one more time.

Ivy continues. “Look at how the Quest is worded. Something has happened in the Crystal Palace. Something is wrong. But it doesn’t tell us what happened, what caused it, or what we’re expected to do about it.”

“Except reach the Throne Room,” I say.

“Exactly. That’s too vague.”

Raul scratches his mustache. “Perhaps the throne is very difficult to reach.”

“That goes without saying,” says Clyde. “The bigger question is what’ll be waiting for us when we get there.”

I consider the intel on this Realm that we were provided by Jerome before entering the Gate. He mentioned that, in the Palace proper most of the mobs would be crystalline humanoid monsters—golems—acting as palace guards. The boss monster was usually a crystalline behemoth. This Realm, unlike so many of the others, seemed to quickly return to a ‘status quo’ upon the Gate to Earth closing. It’s not like the Realm Yomhalde hails from, which continues to exist wholly separate from our interactions with it.

Perhaps that’s because of the lack of organic lifeforms in this Realm? I muse, until something Ivy says draws me back into the Party’s discussion.

“Clyde, what do you think the rewards are for completing the hidden objectives?” she asks.

He steps away from the wall, his dark armor blending back into full visibility. The movement is so smooth it looks like the shadows are reluctantly giving him back. “I’m thinking the Tickets we’d get would get upgraded to Gold.”

That gets my attention.

Jelly Boy’s also interested in the potential upgrade. He buzzes excitedly, gooey body flexing up and down.

Clyde folds his arms. “We know Silver and Gold Tickets are both valid for advancing to the next part of the Game. If this Realm has hidden objectives, completing them could upgrade the reward from Silver to Gold. I’m assuming Gold Gates have a higher opportunity for getting a Divine Tear.”

I look at Ivy. She nods.

Charlotte picks up Jelly Boy and strokes the side of his body. The slime’s surface rippling before emitting a purr-like buzz. “That would make sense,” she says. “The primary objective may just be the minimum completion condition. Reaching the Throne Room could satisfy the Quest, but it might not resolve whatever happened here.”

“So you think we need to solve the mystery,” I say.

“I think we need to identify the root of the issue and address it. Whatever caused the issue at the Palace,” says Clyde. “Probably why we spawned in the caverns.”

Raul grins. “My Intelligence is currently at full capacity. I will happily lend it to solving this mystery before us!”

“Good. Keep it that way until we find the butler.”

“The butler?”

“In Mysteries I’ve read, it’s always the butler.”

“Sometimes… It’s the gardener,” Sang-hyeok murmurs.

I point at him. “See? We're already narrowing down suspects!”

Charlotte laughs softly.

I rub the back of my head and give everyone a stern look. “Er—joking aside—I think we to form a quick plan about how we’ll be navigating out of this cavern.” I glance down the cavern, where crystals glitter like frozen stars and shadows lie between the formations in thick, uneven strips. It’s the perfect place to get lost and stuck walking in endless loops. The limited intel on the caverns in this Realm included the fact that they’re usually crawling with traps and monsters alike.

“We should keep an eye out for anything unusual, including in the caverns,” Clyde says.

“Okay,” I say. “That makes sense. We’ll keep an eye out for anything unusual.”

Clyde looks around the glowing cavern. “As for where to start on getting out of here, I can use a couple of my scouting Skills to get a lead on which direction we should head in.”

“You should look for air currents,” says Charlotte.

“Yeah?” asks Clyde.

Charlotte nods, stroking Jelly Boy once more. The slime sinks into her hands. “Drafts of moving air often point toward an open entrance to outside a cavern.”

“Good to know,” he says. “I think I can actually do that. I’ll need a moment to head further in.” He makes to head towards the shadowed end of whatever room or tunnel we’re in.

“Hold on,” I say. I flex my muscles, strike a pose, and cast [Light]. I mentally focus it into a small sphere of light, which I have hover in front of Clyde. “I’ll come with you.”

He nods. “Just let me lead, as I have an Ability equipped that can detect traps.”

I move to follow him when I’m interrupted by another pulsing sensation and deep, resonate chime that rolls through my skull.

Ping!

Everyone in the Party stiffens, telling me that they just received the same notification. The System generated window slams into my vision.

SYSTEM ANNOUNCEMENT (All Active Participants)

The first Divine Tear has been claimed.

Unclaimed Divine Tears remaining: 9 of 10.

“Fuck,” someone hisses. I can’t even tell who it is, as I’m frozen in the moment by the words floating inches away from my nose.

One Divine Tear already claimed. There are only ten possible Divine Tears up for grabs, and claiming one is the only way for a Party to advance to past this stage of the Game. And somebody already has one.  We’ve barely started our first Gate since this phase of the Game formally started. Hell, we’ve barely finished reading the Quest description. And some asshole somewhere has already sprinted through the process, gotten a qualifying Ticket, secured a Tear, and is presumably celebrating holding the current speed run records for this divine contest we all find ourselves in.

I squeeze my eyes shut, and push away the waves of doubt and fear crashing into the back of my head. We’re already behind. Just like that, our chances of surviving this phase dropped ten percent. When I open my eyes, I close the System notification.

“That’s fast,” Charlotte says quietly.

“No shit,” Clyde replies.

Raul’s forever-grin is gone too.  Ivy looks like someone just informed her that they accidentally ran over her cat backing out of her driveway. Sang-hyeok’s expression is hard to read.

The most frustrating part is we were given no information on who claimed the Divine Tear. Was it a Party from Earth? From one of the other Realms? Pegasus Guild? Sloth?

Who the hell knows.

Suddenly, the cavern feels colder. I think about Dad. The way he smiled at me the last time I visited the hospital, even though he looked exhausted just from sitting upright. I draw a slow breath.

Then I look across my Party. “We need to move fast,” I say. “Clyde, let’s go scout ahead and find the entrance to this fucking Palace.”

Clyde moves a few paces ahead of me, his dark armor swallowing the blue-white glow of the crystals embedded in the tunnel walls. Ahead of him, my [Light] spell provides additional visibility to the cavern’s natural glow. Every one of his steps is quiet. Every movement deliberate. Meanwhile, I’m following behind him in a wizard hat, a cape, and three-inch denim jorts. Stealth has never been my brand. Admittedly, it’s pretty hard to move as silently in these boots.

The tunnel narrows as we move away from the main cavern. Crystals protrude from the ceiling in crooked clusters, their pale light catching on the uneven walls. The air is cold and dry. Our footsteps echo strangely, bouncing between the crystal formations before disappearing into the darkness ahead.

Clyde raises a hand. I stop.

“Something?” I whisper.

“Checking.”

His eyes flicker with System-blue light. He stands perfectly still for several seconds, head tilted slightly to one side.

“Using a Skill?” I ask, voice still hushed.

“Yeah. Scouting Skill. It’s called [Detection]. It builds on [Perception]. Sort of takes the information I don’t realize I’m collecting and categorizes it for me… I can also have it focus on specific things by honing in on a specific part of my [Perception]. Smell, sight, sound…”

“Nice.”

He continues forward. I follow. We reach a fork where the tunnel splits into two passages. The left passage slopes downward, its floor covered in loose crystal fragments. The right bends around a thick column of dark stone, disappearing into a wash of pale blue crystal light. Both look equally inviting.  Which is to say, neither looks inviting at all.

Clyde studies them.

“Let’s check the left first.”

We move several yards down the passage. The crystals become smaller here, packed tightly into the walls like glittering scales. My boots crunch against fragments scattered across the floor. Clyde crouches, pressing two fingers against the stone. His eyes flicker again as he re-triggers his Skill.

“No airflow,” he says. He looks farther down the tunnel. “There's something else.”

I tighten my grip on the Full Metal Staff. “What?”

He points toward the ground about fifteen feet ahead.

At first, I don’t see anything. Then [Perception] sharpens the image. There's a faint seam running across the floor. Almost invisible. A thin line between two stone slabs, partially concealed beneath loose crystal dust.

“Trap?” I ask.

“Yep.”

“How'd you spot it? I definitely wouldn’t have noticed it.”

“Picked up the [Trap Spotting] Ability shortly after joining Chase Guild.”

I look at him. “How many new tricks have you actually gained since we last worked together?”

“Not enough.”

“How does this Ability work?”

“Highlights traps in my HUD.”

“Like… a glowing red outline?”

He smirks. “Actually, yeah.”

I stare down at the seam. To me, it looks like a crack in the floor. “That's bullshit,” I say. I make a note to myself that I need to add new Skills and Abilities to my repertoire. I’m too one-note, and it’d help to have a bit more utility, even if my Party provides plenty to cover my weaknesses in that department.

We backtrack to the fork.

The right passage is wider, though not by much. Tall crystal formations line one wall, throwing distorted reflections across the opposite side. Every time I move, five different versions of me appear in the faceted surfaces. One has a particularly unfortunate angle on the jorts. I reach down and adjust the back of my cursed magical trousers.

Clyde walks ahead, occasionally pausing to scan the environment. We make it another hundred feet before the tunnel splits again. Three passages this time. Clyde studies the openings. His eyes glow faintly.

I wait. An excruciatingly long thirty seconds passes.

“Anything?” I ask.

“Yeah.”

He points toward the middle tunnel.

“Airflow. Like Charlotte said.”

I pause. Now that he mentions it, I can feel it too. “You’re right.”

“It's stronger in there,” he says. “Wind’s moving toward us through that passage.”

“Meaning?”

“Meaning it probably connects to a larger open space. Maybe the Palace proper.”

I glance down the tunnel, smiling. “That’s exactly what I wanted to hear, buddy.”

Clyde starts forward.

“Uh… Should we head back and get the rest of the Party?”

“Just want to scope out a little more of this passage and ensure nothing really nasty is waiting for us.”

I hesitate, but only for a moment before shrugging and following. I can’t let him head in completely alone. The breeze grows stronger as we move deeper into the passage. It’s not enough to stir my cape, but I can feel it against the leg hairs on my exposed hamstrings.

The tunnel bends around a jagged outcropping, and Clyde suddenly stops. His arm shoots out across my chest.

“Hold.”

I freeze.

“What?”

He points down.

Another trap. This one is more obvious once I know where to look. A patch of smooth crystal embedded in the floor, surrounded by rough stone. It forms a near-perfect circle, about two feet across, with a faint discoloration running through its center.

“Pressure trigger,” Clyde says.

He crouches beside the crystal.

“Looks like it activates when something applies weight.” He scans the rest of our surroundings. “Can’t tell what it does, though. Probably not worth tripping it to avoid accidentally doing so when we circle back this way.”

“Can you disable it?”

Clyde clicks his tongue. “Nope… Not one of my many new tricks, unfortunately.”

He steps carefully along the right wall, avoiding the crystal patch. I watch where he places his feet. Then I follow.

We continue another twenty feet.

Clyde’s head turns slightly. “Another one.”

“Seriously?”

“Yeah. Left side.”

I glance down and see nothing. Just uneven stone and scattered crystal fragments.

“Where?”

“About ten feet ahead. Small plate, near that purple cluster.”

I squint. Still nothing. Then, my [Perception] suddenly sharpens the edges of the rock, revealing every tiny crack and mineral vein. I see the purple cluster still, but also see a patch of loose gravel. I see what looks like a harmless piece of pale stone protruding from the floor.

“The whitish stone?” I ask.

“Not that. Just past it.”

“Okkkayyyy…”

I move forward, keeping close to the right wall, carefully to avoid the area he pointed out. Meanwhile, Clyde watches the passage ahead.

“The wind's definitely stronger. I think—”

Click.

It is a tiny sound, barely audible. Like someone snapping a fingernail against glass.

Clyde stops talking.

I stop moving.

We both look down.

My boot rests on a narrow strip of crystal that has sunk perhaps half an inch into the floor. A second pressure plate, near the specific area he had pointed out, but not quite. Did his Ability have a built in rate of failure, or had I simply messed up?

I slowly look up at Clyde.

He stares at my foot. Then at me.

“Joe.”

“Yeah.”

“Did you just—?”

“Oh yeah.”

A deep metallic clunk echoes somewhere ahead of us. We turn toward the sound.

Another clunk follows.

Then something grinds. Stone against stone.

A heavy mechanism begins moving deep inside the tunnel. The noise rolls toward us in a low, grinding vibration, rattling the crystals embedded in the walls.

“Oh,” I say. “That’s probably not good. Right?”

Clyde closes his eyes. He sighs. Opens them and gives me a look. It’s the expression of a man who has accepted a job and discovered that the primary occupational hazard is his employer.

“Probably not.”

The grinding noise stops.

Silence fills the tunnel. For half a heartbeat, nothing happens. Then a distant shriek tears through the tunnel. It’s high-pitched. It sounds like a hundred pieces of glass being dragged across a chalkboard by something with a laundry list of grievances it’s looking to express through very particular modes of violence.

Another shriek answers.

Then another.

The sound multiplies.

Dozens of clicking noises erupt from somewhere deep in the passage, accompanied by the unmistakable scrape of claws against stone.

My [Perception] catches the vibrations through the floor. Whatever’s making these sounds, there are multiple moving bodies. And they’re coming fast. Really fast.

I look at Clyde. He looks back at me. In that instance, we know exactly what we need to do. Run like hell, and get back to the rest of the Party as quickly as we can. I tighten my grip on the Full Metal Staff.

Clyde’s body springs into motion. In a blur, he’s rushing past me. I dispel my [Light] orb and turn to follow him. We haul ass, the wall of sounds growing louder, and louder behind us.
