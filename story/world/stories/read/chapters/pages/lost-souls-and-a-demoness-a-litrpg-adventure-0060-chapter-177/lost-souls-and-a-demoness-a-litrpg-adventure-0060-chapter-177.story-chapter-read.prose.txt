"Hold still, please."

Jade tried not to wince as Siora tipped a few small drops of a green elixir on her aching wing. The concoction made a sizzling hiss on contact with her skin that made Jade's skin crawl. It sounded like the fluid was burning through her body like acid, and the feeling wasn't that far off.

"Ow… Ow!" This time, she did wince as the elf used a clean rag to rub the solution in. She felt the bones, or cartilage, or whatever it was that lay within her wings shift, making a series of disconcerting clicking noises.

"Still!" Siora commanded, firmly pressing the wing against the wall to keep it from moving. "The potion isn't done yet."

"I'm trying." Jade hissed through gritted teeth. Her stomach twisted with sudden nausea, the result of feeling her skeleton twist and move in ways it wasn't supposed to. There was a final jolt of heat, one last lurch, and then everything fell still. Jade leaned back heavily in the airship's seat, resting her head against the bulkhead and breathing hard.

"How do you feel?" Naomi asked, dropping into the seat beside her.

"Like I got run over by a dump truck." Jade muttered, rubbing her temples. Siora released her grip, and Jade took that as a sign that she was allowed to move again. She tested her newly healed wing, flapping it experimentally. "Or that's how it was."

"And now?" Siora asked.

"Better." She gave the elf a tired smile. "A little better."

"Good. That potion should stay in your system for the next day or so, so please don't drink anymore until you're sure the effect is gone. You really don't want to mix a regeneration elixir with anything else."

"No more potions for a while, got it." Jade nodded. Her wing was still sore, and she'd probably have to keep flying to a minimum for a while, but at least it was in working form again. "Thanks, Siora."

"It's why I'm here." The elf said, a hint of rose coloring her cheeks. "I mean, I'm still not much of a fighter."

"Every good team needs a healer." Luis said, entering the rear compartment from the cockpit. "Everybody knows that."

"Absolutely." Tara agreed, nodding her head sagely. "They teach as much in the academy."

"Hey, I heal too." Naomi said, crossing her arms.

"Every team needs a bard, too." Luis continued, clapping Naomi on the shoulder. "Someone has to cheer the team on and write heroic ballads about our great deeds."

"That, they didn't teach us." Tara snorted.

Jade hid a smile while Naomi rolled her eyes. Here they were, the first people to enter the lost demon world in who knew how many centuries, and her friends were still joking around like normal. That didn't surprise her, there came a threshold upon which you simply became so used to danger that it no longer evoked the same level of response as it once had, but she appreciated them for it, nonetheless.

"So, where to next?" Seris asked, turning to look back through the open door. "How do we find this demon emperor, or whatever he is?"

"That's what this is for."

Jade reached into her pouch and withdrew the Destruction splinter. The dark crystal made her palm tingle, like it was electrically charged. She focused on it, enveloping it in her magical senses, gently probing it. According to the goddess, divine essence like this was attracted to power of a similar nature. They'd been told that this entire world had once been infused with the powers of creation, and Jade could sense the echoes of that through the relic held in her palm. But it was faded, now. Drained away. Now, there was only one source of concentrated divine essence left in this world. And it was… there. Jade felt it as a soft, spiritual tug on the relic, pulling it towards…

"That way." Jade made her way into the cockpit and pointed towards the horizon, about forty-five degrees right of their current heading. A darkness hung in the sky there, standing out against the shades of crimson filling their view.

"Great." Seris muttered. "Just fly towards the big, ominous shadow, then?"

"Where else?" Jade deadpanned. "I guess we didn't really need the relic for this part."

She slipped the splinter back into her pouch. It would be nice to keep it out and ready to use, but she didn't want to risk anyone sensing the divine energy and becoming alerted to their presence. The demon Lord Vastrozun was known to prefer remaining in isolation. This would be challenging enough without drawing a swarm of other demons to them. And the longer it took for him to realize they were here, the better.

Seris changed course and engaged the thrusters. The journey would take at least an hour or two, their top speed was limited while cloaked, and they used the time to inspect and prepare their equipment. Tara made some adjustments to her suit, Fenrin sorted the arrows in his quiver by type, and Naomi tuned and retuned her guitar. There was an air of nervous energy in the airship, and Jade could feel her friends' anticipation ratcheting upward with each passing minute. She closed off that sense for the moment. Feeling everyone else's cumulative anxiety wasn't helping her achieve the calm and collected headspace she wanted to go into this with. That, combined with her unruly succubus instincts, made it hard to stop herself from fidgeting. Releasing those urges during her recent battle may have been a mistake. Each time she let them loose, it was harder to bottle them back up.

Remember. She told herself, taking a deep, calming breath. If the plan works, there won't even be a fight.

Of course, the plan relied on too many assumptions for Jade to be completely confident. The goddess could be wrong about what Lord Vastrozun's strength, and the essence they carried might not be enough to defeat him. Or they'd arrive, only to find that they weren't even in the right place, divine detection notwithstanding. Or the goddess could have been lying to them, and this entire mission was an elaborate setup for some purpose they couldn't begin to fathom.

Jade didn't truly believe that last one, if Elaria had done one thing during their meeting, it was be sincere. She was better equipped than most to be the judge of that. Still, that didn't preclude the possibility that her plan would fail. By her own admission, this entire situation was the result of the gods' plans gone awry.

Unfortunately, they didn't have a better option. Better to take the chance and go for the win, rather than not enter the competition at all. There was too much at stake to do anything else. Fortunately, the ship they'd "borrowed" was proving to be as capable as advertised. That was good, because whether they succeeded or failed, there was little doubt that they'd need to make a quick escape.

Jade did her best to relax as they silently glided over the desolate landscape, slowly drawing closer to their goal. Far below, a half-dozen other flickering portals passed by in the distance, most surrounded by great hosts of indistinct figures, all slowly filing through the glowing gates. How many had gotten through, already? It was impossible to tell. But what Jade did know was that, however this played out, the cosmos would be forever changed.

Elyx sat astride a large desert lizard, following Zertrolk's mount through the shallow canyon. An unnatural darkness clouded the sky above them, and the lesser demon could see dark shadows streaming up from the palace plateau as they approached Lord Vastrozun's private residence. Power filled the air, so thick she could almost taste it. Elyx swallowed, trying not to look at the imposing structure looming large before them. Her anxiety had nothing to do with the divine energy saturating the world around her. No, Lord Vastrozun's prowess with the tools of their oppressors was something to be awed by, not feared. Rather, it was the prospect of who they were here to meet that filled her with dread. Here, in the sacred halls of their emperor, where so few had ever set foot, awaited one of his most trusted councilors. The Lady Ira, architect of their plan to escape into the Labyrinth, had summoned them personally. And, considering the current state of the invasion, Elyx didn't imagine that she would be pleased with her performance.

"Have you ever been in Lady Ira's presence before, lead ritualist?"

It took Elyx a moment to process the words, for they were the first Zertrolk had spoken to her since their return to the home world several hours ago.

"No, commander."

"Mind your words in her presence. She doesn't accept anything other than the truth and will give you only one chance to speak it."

"Does it matter what I say?" Elyx asked, her resignation again dampening any inclination to show respect to her superior. "I won't be walking out of this regardless."

"Don't presume to know what Lady Ira or Lord Vastrozun will or won't do, lead ritualist." Zertrolk reprimanded. He finally turned his head, giving her a hard glare. "You would be wise to remember your station. I expect that, if you comport yourself well, they will yet have use of your services."

Elyx didn't believe that for a second, nor that he did. It was far more likely that Zertrolk simply didn't want her insolence to reflect poorly on him. The commander certainly still had his uses, he knew more about the disposition of demon forces in the Labyrinth than anyone else. Elyx, on the other hand? She was as disposable as one could get. Sure, she had a talent for magical improvisation and perhaps had a better grasp on ritual magic than most, but that was hardly worth sparing her after so much had gone wrong under her watch. The commander certainly wasn't coaching her for her sake, he wasn't even addressing her by name. Hell, he'd probably forgotten it already.

"Yes, commander." She replied, putting as much respect as she could muster into the words. Elyx had little intention of heeding his advice, she just wanted this conversation to be over. If these were to be her last few hours, she'd rather not spend them in conversation with the man who'd put her in this position to begin with.

The pair rode on in silence, each step carrying them deeper into the great shadow cast by the curtain of darkness rising from the central palace high above them. The metaphor wasn't lost on Elyx, but she hadn't given up completely. There had to be a way out of this, if only she could find it.
