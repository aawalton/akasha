Clark didn't wake up to birdsong.

Even though their sleeping hut dampened sounds from outside, it was not to the point he was deaf to the rest of the world. For the past months, maybe a year, who even cared, Clark had always heard the chorus of birds and animals at the start of each 'day.' Earlier on, they had been accompanied by the sound of falling water, but since they'd left Shelter, that was less common.

But today, he was unable to hear that glorious cacophony that was The Jungle. The unfathomably deep and imposing and beautiful land had been drowned out by something even more awesome.

The clank-clank-clank of metal and machinery.

Yesterday, Oliver had put up a big hammer right next to the Blast furnace outflow, and done something to make it beat the iron it produced into more of an ingot-y shape. The Commander had a bunch of inklings in charge of moving the iron over, and though they didn't tend to... survive... very long, as the metal popped and exploded at all kinds of things, she could replace them easily!

They did not really feel pain. It was fine.

But, now the forge ran constantly! It was quite loud, as the magic that powered it caused all kinds of clanks and bangs and screeches, but it was amazing that they had built it.

Tully was already gone from his chest, as she usually woke up a bit before him, and so Clark pulled himself out of his bunk, swept [Unblemish] across his body and clothing with little more than a thought, and gave out a.... wonderfully satisfying yawn and a stretch, before leaving the hut and greeting his fellows.

Alyssa was half-dead, yawning mightily even as she picked at her food. The Commander was somewhat more awake, sipping upon one of her teas.

"Good - good morning!" Clark had a brief yawn interrupt him, but he was undeterred. Alyssa merely grumbled, while Henrietta gave a more articulate greetings.

Alyssa slumped forwards, looking like she was falling asleep, then jerked up at the next clanging sound originating from near the Blast furnace across Tower Stream.

Clark fixed himself breakfast and got a bit of food ready for Jacob and Oliver. Jacob was the next out, and he and the Commander engaged in a bit of discussion that Clark had tried to listen to but quickly lost interest in, in favor of marveling yet again at everything around them as he did his work. The Jungle was always such a sight to behold, of course, but so too was the rushing waters of Tower Stream, the heat haze surrounding the wood kiln, the steady stream of dragons being torn apart by inklings while their pseudowyvern cousins bringing in freshly hunted and foraged foods for him to process, and the scattered machines carrying on with each of their jobs. Seeing the haze of Light around those was always neat, too.

Tully nuzzled up to Clark's hand as she emerged from a pool of shadows. He grinned, giving the cutest petalfur a bit of a nuzzle, and then a freshly-removed treerabbit heart. She gave a contented purr-yip, snuggling against his ankle. Clark slipped a bit while butchering the treerabbit, puncturing the stomach and making a bit of a mess over the butchering block. But as always, [Unblemish] fixed it right up and saved the meat from being unappetizing, and cleaned the board of whatever germs might have gotten onto it. He also used ⟨Refine⟩ now, rather than later, to make the meat that much better! It was an important step, as even if he might not know how it did so exactly, the end was far more magically nourishing.

A few more hits with a cleaver, and the treerabbit was broken down. A smaller knife, whatever it was called, then came out to debone the meat. While it had once taken Clark ages to do properly, some practice and a few extra points in Dexterity had made everything super fast! Nana Pan would be so proud if she could see him now. The bones got tossed into one pot to make broth, the scraps of meat into another to be cooked for their dinner tonight, and once Tully rejected most of the other bits, they were dumped into the trash pot for disposal in the river later.

Clark was so engrossed in his task, he almost missed when Oliver got up! By the time he realized, he and Henrietta were already talking.

"Seriously though?" Oliver was incredulous. "Enhancing senses helps with filtering, how does she not..."

"It's dangerous to dampen senses in untamed lands, Smith," Henrietta seemed to be at least somewhat deflecting, but Oliver never noticed those. "And it is rather annoying even when not sleeping. Hopefully it shouldn't prove too challenging?"

Alyssa seemed to rouse herself just enough to speak up, "I just want to sleep, Wizzy. But that stupid thing is..." she trailed off with a mumble.

Henrietta sighed. "Can you do it?"

Oliver shrugged. "Probably. I can't do a traditional sound-dampener. None of us have a Sound class, and Sound is... sticky. Lots of Associations. So without a simple way to isolate the element like I could do for Hatred, my Skills can't really grab onto it that well. But... a mid-strength wind barrier around the hammer is probably going to be enough?"

"Give it a try before you start to move on." Henrietta nodded. She then stopped talking, in a way Clark thought looked like she was trying to figure out what to say, and Clark decided to see if he could help.

"What did you want Oliver to do next? I was unable to hear earlier, but I am most curious what wonders shall be the next to appear!"

"I mean, I'd kinda figured it was just more process automation for the clockwork hearts?" Oliver said with a shrug. "The Metalpress still has a ton of direct intervention needed to work properly."

Henrietta shook her head, a bit hesitantly at first. "No. Or at least, not necessarily, I want..." she paused again. "Smith. What things take you the most time, as of right now?"

"Enchanting?" he asked. "Also some blacksmithing stuff."

"No, that's not what I mean. You've been working on the clockwork hearts for some time now, optimizing that. Knowing what you do in regards to what is needed for making our portal, what will help you along that goal?"

Oliver froze. "Right... Making a portal. That is definitely something I've been thinking about."

Alyssa barked out a bit of tired laughter.

The Commander sighed. "What is intimidating you right now?"

"It's just so... big. Where... what..."

"Okay. So do you want to turn your attention more fully to large-scale constructions?"

Jacob spoke up, "It could prove useful if the assembly of our grand construct-stations, such as with the wood kiln, did not require our personal intervention with its creation."

"I can't do that with my inklings," Henrietta said. "Smith. I know we discussed that you might create automata once we had a proper source of hearts. Will those be capable of construction?"

"Probably? I'd..." Oliver had been slowly curling in on himself, overwhelmed with thoughts, but he relaxed and became more confident as his mind raced, thinking about all the awesome things that he could do. Clark couldn't help but smile. Just like watching Alyssa's gymnastics or Jacob fighting was awesome, Oliver making things or just figuring out how to make things was always super great. "I should get some more practice with creating them. Maybe creating a proper familiar with my core. Better tools would be good. I... think. Yeah. I think we have good enough tools to make a staff. And the sooner we get a Pillar of Industry started, the better. Oh, and maybe I can make a flight platform, that would be nice, um. Oh, I've been thinking about making automata."

"Do you need some time to work on yourself? You have been pushing fairly hard, and you have several skills which might benefit from some personal crafting."

"Yeah... okay. Okay! Yeah!" Oliver was thinking about things and seemed excited, which Clark was happy about.

Henrietta turned away from just talking to Oliver and addressed the rest of them. "Actually, as celebration for getting such a good supply of hearts incoming, what concerns do you all have in regards to taking a bit of time to rest and recover? Focus on filling out your abilities, see where you need some technological breakthroughs."

Alyssa murmured agreement.

Clark raised his hand. Henrietta smiled at the gesture, which he knew she would do. She'd told him he didn't need to raise his hand before, but because it always seemed to cheer her up, he did it anyway. "Yes, Haleford?"

"What about maintenance?"

"Well yes," their Commander acknowledged. "We will still need to maintain things. But I believe that things ought to be mostly set up to run on their own well enough at this point?"

"The smelter enchantment continues to require manual maintenance." Jacob mentioned. "It is not time-consuming, merely requiring a shovel of charcoal into each of its braziers twice per day, but it cannot be allowed to fail."

Henrietta frowned. "I thought that had been corrected back when we were setting up the spellcord drawer, wasn't it?"

Jacob paused. "I do not recall anything akin to that occurring."

"Well," Henrietta tapped the table in front of her, "We'll have to fix that, I suppose."

"The Charcoal Furnaces still needs me, too." Clark added as he remembered. "The sniffers do a great job of stopping the burn, but they still need me to get it started."

"We have plenty of charcoal already made, right?" she asked.

Clark nodded. They had lots of piles of everything lying around. Piles of iron ore, chunks of iron, iron ingots, iron rods and iron bars, bronze ingots, sand, lumber, and charcoal itself. Copper was the only material which didn't accumulate, and of course actual artifacts usually wound up being used pretty quickly.

"Don't worry about it for the moment, then. We can look into making something that doesn't take quite so much work, especially now that we have both the wood kiln and Blast furnace to learn from."

Clark saluted in agreement.

"There's always going to be room for improvement," Henrietta said in her subtly-inspiring way. Even though the words themselves might not have been fantastical, Clark felt himself stirred to action. "But I want us to keep track of these things properly. Whenever you see something that needs our direct intervention, fix it if you can, then tell me what you needed to do. Focus on yourselves for right now, then on The Factory secondarily. If you can think of a good way to improve something, if you notice something that might be good for us to make but we aren't, let me know. We have a long road ahead of us, but I'm confident we can get everything working."

Her words were accentuated almost perfectly with the clank of machinery behind her.

"We've done well this far, and we shouldn't stop now," she finished. "And while we realistically have... more than enough iron for right now, that's not going to be anywhere near enough for everything we need."

Clark smiled. He'd been able to see some of the sites that Portals had been built. Some were the size of cities, others the size of a regular door. But he knew that with all of them, it had taken a ton of stuff to get to that point. Except for one of them. There had been exactly one Forerunner Expedition which had landed on a world with magic that worked really well for portal-making, and so that one had only required the millions of years of development which that particular magic had taken to become itself, at which point everything else was really easy!

They had not been quite so lucky. But also, how much more luck did they really need?

“Haleford,” Clark perked up at his name. “How far do you think you can push [Unblemish]?”

“It is predominantly touch-based, Commander. But I can attempt to extend that if that is your request!”

Alyssa let out another very giggly and tired laugh.

“Not literally, Haleford.” Henrietta clarified, “Have you noticed any of your practice drawing closer to a new subskill, or think there's something you could practice that would help you reach further?”

“Oh!” She should have said as much. “I have been working on trying to do my inside-fixes for when there's something like a sprain or a broken bone, but making that apply to objects I fix! But would that be range in its own way?" Clark paused for a moment, then thought about something else he could say. "Also, I have noticed that I can sometimes change how things look?"

"How they look?" Henrietta asked, "How much of a change are you talking about?"

"Color?" Clark hadn't expected that to be of interest to the Commander. She hadn't expressed very much of an emphasis on the appearance of things, which he had mostly just started to accept. Even if there was nobody around to behold the glory of the First Empire of Humanity save for its own champions, it was still quite important to live in beautiful places! Art was one of the oldest creations of mankind, and they had been completely ignoring it.

Yeah, what they made still looked cool in that way that industrial things could, but it wasn't elegant, it didn't blend, and the colors could often clash. Not that it mattered much right now, but as they got busier, the colors and shapes of their magic might start to cause issues! Even their clothes were only half-functional, materially covering their bodies yet not spiritually enriching them.

"Also texture! I was able to change how some of the leather I have practiced upon felt to my touch."

Henrietta was impressed, which made Clark quite happy. "Have you tried combining those at all?"

Clark shook his head. They each took a bunch of pushing, sometimes with his other subskills and sometimes without. Plus, they had both been super hard! He needed to make sure his Skill was lined up just right, and doing its magic in the right way. Alyssa was way better than him at just getting his skills to do things, and she made it look so easy that Clark often found himself inspired to work on his own magic after seeing what she could do.

"See if you can formalize those into subskills, then?"

"Okay!"

"Ride," Henrietta nudged the dozing Ranger. "Do you have any ideas for how Haleford might be able to expand his Skill?"

"Mmm? Mmm. Mmmm...." Alyssa mumbled. "Mmmm. Mmmmaybe." She paused for a moment, then took a deep breath. Clark had barely any warning before magic coiled around her and she rocketed into the air. He looked up to follow her flight trajectory, then watched as she tumbled back down, landing in her chair with a slight rush of wind. "Okay! I can be awake! I can do this. It's just one lost night of sleep, wake up girl.

"Where were we? Right! Skill practice. Deeper changes and cosmetic changes. Ooh, those could be pretty cool if combined. Maybe full-on shape transmutation? That would be super cool. Shape transmutation, shape transmutation... maybe! Oh! Okay. Get Ollie or Jacob to show you how to use the forges. Maybe work with some hot metal, use a hammer as a focus, learn what it feels... looks, I suppose, what it looks like when your skill is interacting with things that are dramatically changing in shape and being refined as you do so. Start off just trying to get your skill to engage at all, then figure out if you can get it to change the end result. Once you get that, make the metal cooler and cooler, until it's fully cold, then try ditching the hammer."

Oliver looked up, suspicious. Then his gaze softened. "Ah. Skill practice. Okay. Not spellcasting. Carry on."

"Yes, Oliver, I know all about your little rant about spell simplification." Alyssa rolled her eyes, then blinked very heavily. "I am not tired." She lied, yawning. "Wide awake! One night no sleep, I can survive!"

Henrietta turned back to Clark, "Does that make sense, does it give you ideas?"

"It sounds most wonderful to attempt!" Hitting hot pieces of metal was a very cool thing to do, so even if it didn't work out for helping with his skills, he could still have fun with that!

Afterwards, the Commander moved on to the rest of their team. Alyssa got told to rest and to see just how interesting she could make her skills. Clark was looking forward to that.

Jacob and her talked for a bit longer, and came to a bit of a general agreement that he should probably try to get an ability which accelerated the conjuration of his [Frostblade]. The new abilities he'd figured out from his other subskill had been mostly underused, because the time needed to actually use it was impractical for the kind of quick-switch that it helped out. Clark found the talk really interesting, even if he kept needing to stop listening while he prepared food.

"Does anyone have anything they need from me?" Henrietta eventually asked. "I think... I think we've been here long enough that we can all generally figure out what we can and can't do. So I'm going to try setting myself as more of a resource than a traditional Commander. Practically, that means you should come to me if you get stuck, need something you can't provide, anything like that, and I'll make it my mission to get you unstuck, get you what you need."

Their Commander was the best! Clark shot her two thumbs-up and an enormous grin.

"I need a new length of rope," Alyssa mentioned.

"Are we out of spares already?" Henrietta seemed surprised.

"We have spares?" Alyssa and Oliver asked in sync, then glared at each other.

"Okay. So, it sounds like I could do better when it comes to saying where things are kept. And that we have things being kept," Henrietta pulled out their divination tablet and did something with it. "Noted. But yes, they're in a chest over by the instant-firing kiln. I'll show you where in a few minutes."

“But alright, anything else?” the Commander asked.

"If I'm going to start working on automata, I want your opinions on some stuff." Oliver mentioned. "I think I've got a decent framework sketched out, but..."

Clank

Clank

Clank

Clark sighed and allowed the sounds of The Factory to wash over him as his super impressive friends did their stuff.

How did his life ever get to be this cool?
