Are you serious that we had to enter the one cabin that had someone with an Emblem!? Harry added internally while pointing a knife at Manes. Vela gave him one of his candles, whose flame still burned strongly, making him put the knife back in his bag.

In circular movements, they stood back to back like two adventurers in a dungeon. Erratic gazes, Harry scoured the darkness looking for any weakness. The problem, just like any other Emblem users, was that if you don’t know what exactly the user controls, you won’t know how to react. Some, like the Locksmith’s doors and Vela’s candles, were obvious, but in this case? It was as if the night itself gave him power!

Corollary, if his Emblem allowed him to mess with that concept, he wouldn’t be confined to the cabin but out there instead. That, in turn, would give him more power and freedom, but for some reason, he stayed here. It wasn’t the moon or the night that gave him power, but the complete and total absence of light, which would explain his strips blocking the moonlight coming from the windows.

In precise and quick attacks, the bandages and strips tried to grab their faces from behind the flames, but were quickly repelled by the defenders, or attackers, depending on perception.

“Just go to sleep, man!” Harry exclaimed with a forced smile. “We just want to take a look around the cabin and leave. You won’t even feel our presence.”

“Who are you?” The figure asked. “A candle freak and a hobo? That's fun.”

A hobo?

In response, the candles intensified as they continued in a circular motion, pushing the darkness back.

The light coming from the window had limited his control over the place a little, and with the flames in the center, there weren’t many places left for him to hide. However, Harry wasn’t fast enough to hit him directly. Inside the cabin, it was as if the shadow was faster than the light.

“Nice little party trick, huh?” The Locksmith taunted him with a smile. “This Emblem of yours is really limited, don’t you think? What did you draw to get this? A sleeping mummy?”

“It’s a thousand times more complex than your illusory doors and the retard’s candles!”

Arrogant and insecure. Harry noticed his personality and drew up a plan; to say anything, he needed to stay in a corporeal form, which meant he couldn’t slip through the shadows, at least for that brief instant. He also avoided Vela's direction, meaning his voice was always facing Harry. Even with the enhanced flame, the tiny candle that Harry had still wasn't enough; he could use Vela's full power.

Calling Vela’s attention, Harry whispered to attack with full force and to count from 10. Before reaching three, he spoke at Manes again.

“You’re alone here,” He said, still with a mocking smile. “While everyone else is getting drunk and dancing, you’re here alone like a mole. How sad is your life that you prefer darkness over company?”

Not that I wouldn't do the same. The Locksmith added inwardly. But it should be enough.

“Darkness is the only company I have!” Manes replied with a more human, almost teenage voice. “You’d never understand, would you? With your tie and glasses, you just make yourself look like a pretentious failed academic! If you tied your hair, you’d even look like a beggar!”

“Now!” he added together with the number three and, at the same instant, opened a door in front of Vela and another exactly above where the voice was coming from.

The candle man, in turn, responded with a potent and short jet of dense fire, strong enough to burn any organic matter in seconds. He held it short so it wouldn’t hit the floor and concentrated enough so it wouldn’t spread to the logs on the sides.

With the voice now silent, Harry was about to lower his guard when he felt a presence to his right.

“Ah, yes…” The shadow replied with sarcasm. “It was a beautiful idea, honestly… Too bad I’m not as fragile as you. I’m not an idiot either.”

Maybe he only stayed in that form for stability, but that didn’t necessarily mean he had to stay there. Harry thought as he felt a strong pull on his foot and noticed several black strips, almost like the devil’s bandages, trying to break his balance.

They were probably sneaking closer to the source little by little so as not to draw attention. With that, the strips gathered to make even stronger tugs. While Harry used all his concentration not to fall, Vela remained as rigid as a golem.

Being pulled and hit by strips that came from the darkness, they knew this was a battle of exhaustion, and they were in the enemy’s domain.

Afraid of falling and causing a fire inside the cabin, Harry quickly thought of another solution.

"Can you give me more of those candles?" Harry asked in a low voice, to which Vela simply nodded. "Oh, and how much of them can you ignite at once?" Even by looking only at the corner of his face, the Locksmith could tell he was confused. "When I say, throw all the candles in front of you and ignite them, as well as mine."

Although it was a dangerous idea, Harry trusted Vela's control in his candles enough so the place wouldn't burn down. Even if the candles were small, if they were all dispersed enough, even the weakest of flames can lighten a mountain.

“Get down!” he shouted to Vela, who immediately followed his command.

The Locksmith and Vela threw a bunch of candles into the dark, only for them to ignite a second later. What happened, however, is that they were being thrown from side to side all around the cabin with Harry's doors, illuminating a big portion of the place.

As he could determine the size and toughness of each door, he created dozens of small ones directly onto the path of each candle. Of course, some still fell to the ground, their small flames quickly fading away and revealing a dark wick. But even as he tried to minimize the damage, he could feel his soul stretching to enforce the Emblem's power upon the world. It was too much doors.

“What—” Manes’s voice was swallowed by a bright light.

Half a second later, they rose with their eyes open, scanning the cabin for any weakness as the light from the candles faded; they had to be quick. The furniture and symmetrical architecture only showed several beds on each side and a second floor that led to a door, probably a bathroom.

Thump! Thump! The Locksmith's heart began pounding as if a beast lived inside him. Panting, the man ignored the pain in his chest as he searched for the shadow man. Shit, I need to be more careful... And Harry was once again shown the existence of the Emblem Exhaustion.

Could Manes’s Emblem have mixed with the entire cabin? Harry’s worries were then burned away by the sound of someone grunting softly under one of the beds.

“There!” Vela pointed with his candelabrum. “Under that bed!”

Promptly, they rushed to the spot and pushed the bed aside, the sight surprising them both; a young boy, maybe around 15 years old, curled up in fetal position with messy, unkempt black hair.

Dirty, his clothes were half torn, making him look like a slave. Vela frowned with an expression of both disdain and pity at the same time. Before the boy could make any movement, however, he pulled his candelabra and placed it at the boy’s throat as if he were a trained assassin.

Doing so made the boy visibly uncomfortable, grunting in fear at the weapon’s now barely lit candles. The wax, constantly regenerating, dripped on the boy's shoulder. After a few moments, Vela closed his eyes and withdrew the candelabra, the boy breathing a bit easier.

“He’s just a kid…” He said to Harry while shaking his head. “He wasn’t even supposed to be here…”

Taking a deep breath, Harry knelt down and got close to him. “Is it you? Manes? The boogeyman?”

“I…” Black strips lightly emerged from his arms but retracted. “Seeing you up close, you look more like a drunk than a professor.”

He said that with a laugh, then spat in Harry’s face, who didn’t even move. Closing his eyes and wiping the spit away with his sleeves. He then grabbed a convenient rope that lied around near one of the beds and began tying him up to the bedpost.

“This should be enough to keep him…” Harry said, defeated. “Damn, we almost killed a child…”

“Age doesn’t matter!” Manes shouted from the bed, smiling.

“Oh, so you don’t mind if we call you a child?”

“I’m not a child!” His smile quickly turned into a scowl.

With that, he tried to pull his strips through the ropes, but the pressure was too strong. Apparently, the source came from his wrists.

“Well, now that we’re already inside.” He said, optimistic. “Vela, can you see if you find any passage or anything leading to a cell? I've... I need a second to breathe. Don't worry, I can watch out for him.”

The young thief didn’t expect to find the prisoners in Manes’s nest, but it was worth a shot.

Nodding and staring at the boy for a brief moment, Vela went downstairs with his candelabrum to check on the underground area.

“So how did you end up in the tower? Actually, the more important question is how are you going to survive… you almost lost to a child!” He said, mockingly. “Well, you’ll have to do that for what, let me count, 4, 5, 6…? Six cabins, I think, haha…”

"I thought you weren't a child."

"Well, it's easier to accept what people already think of you, isn't it? At least I don't need to keep correcting them."

Like a drunk, his eyes looked empty now that he was captured.

"What do you know about this place?" The Locksmith asked while sitting in a nearby bed, who stank of beer. "Why is there hands in front of the cabins? Why is there a fucking church with a mask in front of it?"

"Look, who cares?" He replied. "It's been like that since... forever. Each cabin praises the flame, and the big church oversees it all; that's the floor's structure. It surely has gotten into the other knuckleheads that this is a 'sacred' spot. Like, come on, really?"

"Why do they think that?"

"They just want something to believe, I guess." Manes continued. "They would even sell their mother if it meant they could touch the Astronomer."

The Astronomer?

"Have you seen him? He comes here often?" The Locksmith said, visibly curious.

Manes looked at him with a quizzical look. "Um, no, man. He doesn't. That's just what they believe. The Shepherd says he already climbed the tower, though; met with the old man himself."

He's probably the one in the church. Harry went through his thoughts. So it really is a cult up here... Could there be a chance that the two prisoners were sacrificed? Or even worst; they joined them. After all the talk about strange hooded figures around the forest of New Cavebrook, a bunch of cult stories sprouted. Yeah, probably most of them were being sensationalized, but Harry wasn't oblivious to their existence. If anything, the publicity only hid them even more.

"...Do you know about two prisoners who—"

"I don't know shit, okay, man!?" Manes snapped. "I was fine just sleeping by myself when you two dipshits showed up. We have a contract, okay? They think I am some sort of angel of the flame; a son of the Tower. They don't bother me here. At least, they shouldn't..."

"Well, at least you're safe here."

"You call this safe?" Manes seemed genuinely indignant. "I can't even grow up, man; I'm a forever child. My sole entertainment is throwing rocks at the flame and sleeping. What purpose do I have in life other than to dream?"

The Locksmith wanted to reply, but what could he say? Having himself suffered this apathy towards life and touching poverty firsthand, Harry kept quiet.

How many people were in the same situation as this boy? Living and reliving the same 24 hours surrounded by these hardships? What if someone came on this place near death but not quite dead? A potentially infinite existence of pain? Sure, one could heal or patch the wounds, but it would be gone 24 hours later. Now adding a couple of years to that? It would be hell.

“That’s right, don’t say anything.” Manes said, pointing outside. “At least your buddy is coming and you can get the hell out of here.”

Waiting for Vela to climb the stairs, Harry went to the kid with the intention of tightening the ropes, but changed it midway. When he sat on one of the beds near him, with a smile, he started playing with his doors.

"W-what's that shit, anyway?" Manes asked, curious, "Is that like your super trick? Your quirky way of telling the world you exist—"

"The Door Emblem." Harry replied, much to Manes surprise. "Allows me to conjure doors and create passages between them. Pretty cool, right? What about you?"

"I..." The boy felt compelled to answer, but rapidly changed his demeanor. "It doesn't matter."

"You can control shadows..." Harry said, almost to himself. "Since they come from your body, I assume it's in the Quadrangular Form."

"Yeah, anyone could've seen that coming."

"How does it feel, then?" The Locksmith asked, slightly twisting his head to the left.

...Am I doing therapy? Manes added inwardly but shook his head and sighed deeply.

"It feels nice and warm." He replied. "That's it. It's good. It's also durable as shit, so besides making me feel fucking amazing, it's also a protection."

"Defense and offense. That's nice."

"Stop talking like you want to be friends—"

"Only if you stop pretending you don't want one." Harry retorted loudly as he stared at the ropes.

Manes, puzzled, then felt his hands growing looser and the tightness of the rope fading away. With an agape mouth, he narrowed his eyes as he looked at Harry, who simply smiled.

At the same moment, Vela’s flames could be seen coming up the stairs. He shook his head at Harry; the place was empty.

"All right." he said, frowning, "Let’s go to the next one then. I just hope there isn’t another super-strong brat..."

"Are we leaving him here, Mr. Locksmith?" Vela asked, his face screaming concern.

"I think so. Even if he were free, he wouldn’t have the guts to leave this place anyway, right?" He looked at the boy, who hid his emotions like a sphinx; his face an enigma.

"Right..." Vela agreed, and so the two of them left the cabin.

As they prepared for another round of jumps, Harry grew thoughtful.

I hope I did the right thing.
