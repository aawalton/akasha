Theo stood back up to his feet and wiped the blood from his mouth. “You know, I would have thought your clone would be a temporary illusion or something.”

Max adjusted his collar as he glanced around their new surroundings. “Indeed. A biological duplicate is certainly convincing, although exceptionally over the top.” He shot the elf a sheepish grin. “Then again, over the top is my middle name.”

“At least you grew out of using it at every opportunity to traumatize our companions.” Ren maintained a dull glare as she swept the area with her sniper rifle.

Rather than a sprawling cavern or dark castle, the entrance to the dungeon was something rather underwhelming. This second bubble of reality, somehow maintained out in the void, was barely bigger than the control room and was essentially a stone gateway surrounded by cobbled ground and overgrown greenery. The entrance to the door was a shimmering portal of swirling green energy.

Chuck had self-destructed to help them pinpoint this location, and they’d been able to teleport as one - using a little of Max’s blood to empower the vampire. Still in range to retreat to Wolf back on the mainland if required… assuming the scrolls worked while inside dungeons.

“We’re not exactly a party of five, as is tradition,” Max said. He approached the sign next to the entrance. “Not that I have any doubts when it comes to our power level.”

Theo offered no response, but smiled. His fangs didn’t pick up the light, as there was barely any in this odd slice of land. The pair of magicians had certainly been through a lot; that couldn’t be denied. He had been through this sort of thing a few times and knew how it worked out. If there was anything in this world that could test the three of them, it would definitely be in this dungeon.

“No dedicated tank or healer,” he noted. “But you probably have enough consumables to outfit an army.”

“A magician is never caught lacking.” Max grinned. “Out of all the overpowered bullshit the System gave me, the ability to manipulate my Inventory with the speed of thought was probably the most powerful.”

From what Theo knew, the showman could also turn invisible, attack from range, summon demons, and create a demonic Domain. Doing all that while being able to switch held and stored items in an instant was rather impressive. The fact that Max tied each of those abilities into the magician theme was slightly less palatable, but he wasn’t one to talk.

While not as versatile, Ren was a ranged damage powerhouse. She had picked up a few of Max’s abilities as some manner of shared class deal with the System, but most of her attacks relied on utilizing her ranged weapon. Elemental attacks, crowd control, and group passive buffs to accuracy and critical rate.

The Outsiders didn’t really have someone who was exceptional at ranged attacking, aside from Jackie. Even the magic users suited mid-range and supporting roles better. He could also see the jack-of-all-trades Max being equally destructive in the midst of combat, just as he and Sally were.

All he had to do was pull them out of the other side of this dungeon alive, and he was sure they’d join up.

“Well, we’re not getting any younger.” Ren sighed and rolled her shoulders. “Being in this pocket feels claustrophobic. Let’s start killing already.”

Hunter scratched the side of his shadow head with the barrel of his large revolver. “Looks like it’s almost time to start killing.”

“Finally.” Sally sat up from the ground. “Maintaining all this magic passively makes me feel like static is slowly filling my skull. There’s way too much free real estate in there.”

The man grunted and lowered his gun. With a gentle flick, he flipped the cylinder open to check the ammunition for the fifth time. As noted by the zombie the first time, it was not filled with traditional bullets. Rather, shadowed mist pooled down from his wrist and glove hand, tendrils of dark energy flowing into each of the five open chambers. Neither of them knew what it really meant.

Recon from the Omen put the remaining Radoch forces at the base of the mountain, deep within the forest. Contact would be soon.

Just as Sally righted herself into a slouched standing position, a crackle of energy to her side illuminated her with blue light. With a quick flash, a wireframe figure flickered into being.

“Ahh, motherfu…” The Architect held his head in both hands and hissed.

“Chuck!” Sally hopped over to him, her eyes wide. “How are you here?”

He shook himself and grimaced her way. “Emerging technology. Magical bullshit. The usual. I wouldn’t be here without the Omen to anchor from, but limit testing blah blah.” He straightened himself up and looked over her shoulder to Hunter. “This must be our newest ally.”

“Pleasure to meet you.” The shadowy man doffed his brimmed hat. “You must be the guy in charge of Sanctuary.”

“The one holding it together, at least. Unfortunately, I don’t have the resources to stand around and get better acquainted.”

Sally crossed her arms. “Aren’t you supposed to be sweating over finding a way to actually bring us back home so we aren’t stuck here?”

“Naturally.” He gave her a pained smile. “That’s in part why I’m here. I needed to see him in person now that I have that ability.” With this, the wireframe man turned to the side to face the mountain.

The other two followed his gaze to see that Archie was walking back over from the cave entrance.

Chuck tilted his head and flickered, a curious expression crossing his face. “You know, I think I preferred you as a cat.”

“Life certainly felt easier as one. We have much to discuss.” The wizard returned a strained smile. He appeared even more tired now, since finishing his meditation. “Have you come to deliver us the bad news in person?”

“No. Well, not the worst-case news, at least. Unless you’ve come up with anything, we have an emergency protocol in place, but nothing is guaranteed.”

Archie rubbed at his beard and closed his eyes. “Last Second Retreat? Problematic, but I think we can make that happen.”

“I’ll leave that to you, then.” Chuck winced as he flickered again. “Best I return, otherwise I’ll stretch myself too thin.”

Sally tapped her foot on the ground. “What about Hunter? Are you bringing him back too?”

The Architect paused. “Ah. He’ll find us, I’m sure of it.”

With a pop, the blue light evaporated, and the wireframe outline of the man faded away. A silent static remained in his place for a few seconds before also vanishing.

Sally shook her head and sighed. “Your little micro-nap wasn’t any help then?”

Archie gave her an apologetic shrug. “An ideal instead of meditation usually requires a day or two to come to a satisfying conclusion. I figured my magic would be better suited to helping you fight off the remaining Radochs.”

They’d need to. From what she could remember about the Last Second Retreat, the less data remaining in the world, the better. Anything here that wasn’t her or Archie would complicate and slow their return to Sanctuary. That didn’t just mean sentient entities either. Every house, tree, or handful of dirt added to the danger. It was meant as a last resort for a dying world.

She glanced over at Hunter. Perhaps he’d need to utilize more of his power to help them after all.

“I am no Necromancer, Sally,” he continued. “But when we do return—and we will—would you me to teach you about magic? Some of the underlying basics might help you progress both your classes.”

She took the arcane tome from her back and allowed it to hang in the air beside her. “Studying to earn new abilities? Sounds rather dry, but sure, I’d like that.”

Hunter grunted and pulled back the hammer on his revolver. “They’re making their way up now,” the shadowy man stated. “Can see the trees shaking and… well, the bugs are making quite a ruckus.”

Sally spun her dagger in her hand idly as she calmed the itching exhaustion that was prickling at her body. “Alright. Let’s show these roaches what death looks like.”

Theo lowered his hand and pouted. “Being without my full Death powers is somewhat disappointing.”

The courtyard full of small monsters remained uncontested; the horde of creatures still moving around idly as the small group of adventurers crouched by the entrance. On the opposite side of this stone expanse was a temple. An extremely large one, by all accounts. More of a jungle-themed castle. Rather than a moat, there just seemed to be scores of these short creatures that were a mix of goblin and river trout.

Max raised an eyebrow at the vampire. “You expected them to just fall over without a fight?”

“It’s more of a visceral explosion, but yeah.”

Ren slowed her breathing as she looked down the scope of her rifle. The gun rested on a rut in the stone wall the three had paused behind, and she slowly swept it across the busy courtyard. “Rather low level,” she noted impassively. “Not what I’d call dangerous, but our presence won’t be a secret once we get stuck in.”

“Stealth is overrated.” The magician shot her a flashy smile. “Want to support us from the wings while I take main stage?”

“Go break a leg.”

Max stood up as he finished pulling white gloves onto his hands. He adjusted his posture and expelled a theatrical sigh. Once content, he gestured for the vampire to join him.

Theo had fewer preparations to make, but adjusted his collar and glasses all the same. With a grin, he rested the sledgehammer across his shoulder.

“That doesn’t seem like a common weapon for vampires to use,” Max noted, his eyes still on the courtyard.

“My Aspect makes me competent in every weapon, even improvised ones. My preference is punch blades, but I mastered two-handed hammers because it seemed amusing.” Theo’s fangs caught the light. “Plus, I have other abilities that make them as light and dexterous as daggers.”

The magician held out his hand, and three playing cards of purple light bloomed into life. With a subtle gesture, they started to rotate in place. “Interesting, and surprisingly extra. Perhaps you won’t be too appalled by the way we do things.”

“The stage is all yours, showman.”

Max stepped out onto the short stairs leading to the courtyard proper. Standing tall, he greeted the arena with confidence, while the purple cards now circled his entire body in slow circles. With a short gesture from Ren, faux stage lights snapped into being, illuminating the magician. His purple suit sparkled in the bright light, making him stand out further from the drab terrain.

“Welcome one and all,” he boomed out, his voice carrying across the entire area, “to the greatest, and last, show of your lives.”
