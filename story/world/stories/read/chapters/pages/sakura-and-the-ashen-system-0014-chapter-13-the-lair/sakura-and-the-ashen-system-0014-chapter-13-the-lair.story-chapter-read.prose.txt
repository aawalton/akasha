Gilbert did not stop laughing right away. His laugh was quiet, a little rough.

“So that was a joke?” he asked, still smiling faintly.

Sakura looked at him from under her brows.

“Do we have another option?”

Gilbert stopped smiling so openly, but the amused glint in his eyes remained.

“So it wasn’t a joke.”

“If there’s no other option, then no,” Sakura said. “I’m completely serious.”

He was silent for a few seconds, studying her face as if trying to decide whether she was just saying reckless things from exhaustion and too much madness packed into one day. Then he nodded anyway.

“That explains a lot,” he murmured. “Like why you went into the dungeon for me instead of running as far away as possible.”

“I needed you. There wasn’t a better option,” Sakura said dryly.

“Nice to hear.”

He rested the back of his head against the tree trunk and shut his eyes for a second in fatigue, but when he spoke again, his voice was more focused.

“It’s extremely dangerous for us to show ourselves in cities now. In any city. After what happened in the arena, word will spread fast. Maybe not tonight, but soon enough. Patrols will start sweeping the roads, the outposts, the villages, and everything in between. And yes, you understand this correctly, Sakura: now they’ll throw everything they have into catching you. Compared to you, I’m a secondary target.”

Sakura nodded silently. She already knew that, but hearing someone say it out loud still made her throat go dry. She swallowed nervously and looked away toward the river.

“Because of your magic,” Gilbert said. “Because of Pampli. Because of what you did in front of everyone. To the people in power, you’re no longer just a suspicious girl who can be questioned, intimidated, and quietly hanged. You’re proof that Crowlis’s legacy did not die.”

He shifted one shoulder and nodded toward the beast.

“And speaking of proof. Pampli is going to be a problem. A very large problem. He draws far too much attention.”

Sakura did not even have to think about her answer.

“We are absolutely not leaving Pampli behind,” she said at once. “That’s out of the question.”

Gilbert looked at her, then at the peacefully dozing beast, then back at her.

“I thought so.”

“And?”

“And I’m not arguing. I’m just saying it will be difficult.”

The wind moved through the trees, a little stronger than before, brushing the wet strands of hair at Sakura’s temple. She tensed without meaning to. For one second it seemed to her that there were voices in that rustling again. She lifted her head, trying to listen more carefully.

“What?” Gilbert asked at once.

Sakura turned toward him.

“Do you hear something?” he asked, more attentively now.

She hesitated.

“The wind,” she said at last.

Gilbert watched her for a long moment. His expression changed, not much, but enough for Sakura to notice. The dry amusement left his eyes.

“Not the wind,” he said quietly. “Something in it.”

Sakura did not answer.

“Crowlis heard it too,” Gilbert said.

Sakura frowned.

“Really?”

“Voices in the elements. That was what people said about him. A lot of them thought he was insane long before he became something people used to frighten others. Crowlis claimed he did not hear just wind, or just water, or just fire. He heard voices inside them. And if I remember correctly, it started with the wind for him too.”

Sakura straightened up, forgetting her exhaustion.

“Tell me more.”

But Gilbert shook his head immediately.

“Not here. Not in this place.”

“Then where?”

He looked away for a moment, as if deciding whether he should continue at all, then finally said:

“I know a place. We can hide there for a little while, sleep, think about what to do next, and talk without risking the nearest patrol or some kind local who wants to earn the throne’s favor overhearing us. But the place is... specific.”

“How specific?”

“Very. Specific enough to match the owner.”

Sakura arched a brow.

“The owner may scare you,” Gilbert added.

Sakura let her gaze move over the dark riverbank and the sleeping obsidian beast.

“After everything that’s happened in the last day, I doubt anything can still scare me.”

Gilbert gave a quiet huff.

“I don’t believe you. But I won’t argue,” he said, and, lowering his eyes slightly, added, “You’d better hold on to that confidence for at least a few more hours.”

They sat by the river in silence a little longer, letting their bodies recover at least a little. Sakura did not even notice when she had stopped trembling after the chase. The exhaustion remained, but it no longer felt like it was tearing through her muscles from the inside.

After a while Pampli opened his eyes. He lifted his head, looked at Sakura as if he did not quite understand where he had woken up, and yawned sleepily. Sakura went to him at once, ran her hand along his neck, then over his cheek. Pampli gave a quiet snort, dipped his head, and almost affectionately nudged his muzzle against her shoulder, as if demanding attention.

“Well, that’s good,” Sakura said. “I hope you slept well.”

Pampli narrowed his eyes at the touch. With her, he was already behaving as if he had decided she belonged to him. As if she always had. But with Gilbert it was different. A little wary. Every time the mage came closer, the beast turned his head slightly and watched his every move. Sakura found herself wondering as she looked from one of them to the other.

“What does he even eat?”

Gilbert looked at Pampli with the expression people usually wore when facing a problem they really did not want to deal with immediately.

“No idea,” he said honestly.

“Very helpful answer.”

Sakura glanced sideways at the beast.

“I hope not people.”

Pampli looked at her with an expression that seemed offended.

“All right,” she said. “That may have been a rude question.”

They got moving again quickly. They had to continue on foot this time, because after that run Pampli needed at least a little rest without extra weight on his back. Fortunately, the beast did not object. He moved beside Sakura softly and almost silently for something his size, sometimes a little ahead of her.

By then night had fully settled in. Only the huge moon gave them enough light to keep from losing the path between the trunks. It struck Sakura at once as too large. Not the moon she remembered from her old world, the one that had shone much more faintly. Here it hung above the forest like a giant pale disk, and under that light the trees looked even taller and the shadows even deeper.

They walked for hours. Sakura stopped keeping track of time. At first they still traded short remarks, then they fell silent again. Sometimes Sakura thought she heard the wind once more, but she still could not make out anything clearly.

The farther they went, the denser the forest became.

And then Sakura noticed the webbing. First one strand between two bushes, thick and silver in the moonlight. Then another stretched above her head. Then more, and more. It was as if the forest itself was slowly becoming overgrown with webs.

Sakura slowed down. A shiver ran over her skin.

“No,” she said quietly.

Gilbert looked back.

“What exactly?”

“Spiders,” she breathed, grimacing. “All this... spider stuff.”

He followed her gaze, looked at the pale nets between the branches, then back at her, and a far too obvious amusement appeared in his eyes.

“And you said that after everything that happened, nothing could scare you anymore.”

Sakura shot him a dark look.

“I didn’t say spiders don’t annoy me.”

“Of course. Completely different thing.”

She only grimaced harder and decided not to answer. The last thing she needed was a philosophical discussion about the nature of fear in the middle of a forest that was starting to look more and more like someone’s grotesque joke.

At last the shapes of rocks appeared ahead between the trees. Low on the outside, black, with dark openings cut into them. Caves. There were several of them, side by side, and each one was wrapped in so much webbing that the moonlight got caught in it as dim white patches. Threads stretched from stone to stone, between outcroppings, along the ground itself. In some places the webs were torn. In others they lay in thick layers.

Gilbert stopped and said with a bad sort of smirk:

“We’re here.”

Even Pampli froze. At first Sakura did not understand what exactly had changed, and then she saw it: the beast had pressed his ears back slightly, stretched his neck forward, and clearly had no desire to go any farther.

Gilbert noticed too, glanced at Pampli, and asked with genuine surprise:

“Seriously?”

Pampli gave a very displeased, quiet growl.

“No, wait,” Gilbert said, openly amused now. “You’re... that,” he said, nodding at the enormous obsidian beast, “and you’re trying to tell me that spiders make you uneasy?”

Pampli gave him a look so sharp that if looks could bite, Gilbert might already have lost a couple of fingers.

Sakura could not help it and let out a quiet laugh. But the laughter died quickly. Because from the largest cave ahead came a sound, like scratching, like dry rustling. The noises were moving closer to the entrance.

Sakura felt the back of her neck go cold at once.

One leg slowly emerged from the darkness of the cave. Then a second. Too long, covered in sparse hairs. Enormous spider legs carefully felt for the edge of the stone outside.
