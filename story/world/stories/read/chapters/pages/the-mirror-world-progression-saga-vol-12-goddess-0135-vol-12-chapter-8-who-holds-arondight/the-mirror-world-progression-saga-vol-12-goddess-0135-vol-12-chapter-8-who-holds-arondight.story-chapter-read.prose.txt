Not long after Samantha returned to Itha Ranon, she got a sudden notice from Ash, and she was a bit upset. “Ana has overextended herself from the most recent summoning — or whatever we’re calling those strange processes with the golems. She's in bed, unconscious, and drained, though I’ve verified she isn’t in a coma. She needed healing from Galahad, or she would’ve died.”

Samantha, alarmed, only replied, “I’m coming,” while rushing off, telling Jeeves to inform Azure.

Along the way, a suddenly guilty Ash said, “Sorry. Should’ve pushed harder for safety, but she swore she could-”

“It’s alright, Ash,” Samantha interrupted gently. “Blame me. Like mother, like daughter.”

“That’s silly. No.”

“At least it's over. We won’t let it happen again, regardless.”

“It isn’t over, though! They haven’t gathered the materials for the last one.”

“Oh. Right. Which is this one?”

“He’s made of mythril. Talk about an expensive golem. Perhaps priceless. Anyway, he’s here. You can meet him yourself. We kept most of them out because they’re huge, but he’s one of the smaller and was particularly insistent.”

“Alright…”

Samantha found her way into the infirmary, where Ana was sequestered in a room. She saw a veritable gaggle of golems hanging out just outside the door, and each of them turned to her in sync, like clockwork, their glowing eyes of fiery power drawn to her like a magnet.

They all went to a knee and bowed their heads, murmuring respectfully, “Your Majesty.” Samantha wasn’t spending time on them right now, though, as she swept right by and into the private room.

Inside, a cluster of healing experts was present, including the troll-stone golem, the first one fashioned, Galahad. Raphael and Ash were also there, as well as an elven healer whose name she forgot. In addition, one other golem was present, a hard-edged sort designed very specifically to look like an iconic armored knight, though without the helmet. He was almost entirely made of mithril, a lustrous metal, that of a rich gray with hints of darker highlights within. It looked essentially like some ‘final evolution’ of steel.

The golem’s fiery eyes were the primary contrast as he looked over at her with a worried expression, before quickly dropping to a knee and bowing his head like the others, murmuring, “Your Majesty.” He had a hand resting on the hilt of a sword, and it was quite a beauty — A variety of blue gemstones decorated the hilt, otherwise intricate in its design. She sensed powerful, otherworldly magic contained in it.

Galahad also knelt. Samantha only spared them a glance before rushing over to Ana’s prone, pale form in the bed under the covers, her face drained of all color.

Samantha put a hand to her cheek and murmured, “Mother is here, dear.” Ana’s eyes and expression may have shifted. “Sorry I was late to help you. It won’t happen again.” After a moment of shifting herself so she sat down on the bed, Samantha added, “Rest, but do come back, okay?”

She leaned down to lightly hug Ana for a little while. She sensed Azure arrive and sit on the other side, murmuring her own comforts to Ana briefly but mostly just there as a physical support.

Finally, Samantha kissed Ana’s forehead before sitting up. Was the elf girl’s expression more peaceful? Perhaps.

She turned her eyes to the others there. Something in her expression made them all pensive. Raphael even backed up a step.

Then again, she did notice the light of her new, likely disconcerting eye bathed them. Yeah, that would probably give anyone pause. “Let’s talk outside.”

This produced even more nervousness.

“I’ll stay with her,” Azure said.

“Thank you, love,” Samantha said with genuine gratitude, before slowly floating out through the door.

Outside, the golems naturally made a semi-circle around her, all of them kneeling as before, with their heads bowed.

“Rise,” Samantha commanded simply. They obeyed promptly, and Samantha studied them all in turn. Eleven golems of varying sizes, each made of a different substance, all with virtually the same fiery eyes. She noticed that the newest one had taken the most prominent position, standing directly across from her.

Samantha sighed and rubbed her forehead. “So… principally? I take the lion’s share of blame for Ana almost dying. I have left her entirely to her own devices to rush you all into existence, giving her whatever she asked for in resources to do it. Obviously, I was not involved enough. But I’m quite aggravated that others closer to this didn’t see fit to warn me. Were you entirely ignorant, or did you sense the danger that was posed to her?”

The large, dull-steel golem raised his hand briefly. With a sober face, he nodded to her. “Though I am not the highest among us, I, Sir Lucan, can speak as the voice of a primary organizer… through the ascending difficulties of the Daughter’s arts. Collectively, we did see the ramping danger to her, Your Majesty. We accept sacrifice as one’s duty, and acknowledged hers in turn. But all that is displeasing to the one we serve is sin, so whatever penance you see fit to assign, we will also gladly accept to obtain honor in your sight.”

Every other golem nodded along to this.

Samantha rolled her neck and took a breath, trying not to be too annoyed with this response. “Your penance is to look after my daughter!” Her voice was a bit higher than she wanted, so she moderated it. “Let me be very clear: she will not kill herself finishing up this gathering. You will not let her, under any circumstances. Understood?”

“Yes,” the mythril golem said with his strong, smooth voice, as if from an ideal hero. “This will be complicated, as we are not complete without our highest leader.”

“And who are you, then? You are the second-highest, correct?”

“Correct, Your Majesty. I am Sir Lancelot.” He bowed low, briefly. “At your service in all things.”

Samantha blinked, which she knew had the effect of turning the ‘lights’ on and off, in a way, so it was especially noticeable. “Why didn’t I anticipate that? You have a special sword. It’s not the one I think it is, is it?”

In a swift motion, Lancelot moved and knelt in front of her, his entire sword and scabbard removed as he presented it up to her in two hands, head bowed. “I present to you Arondight, Your Majesty, to wield for you, or not, as you see fit. As all things must be, it is yours.”

Samantha, somewhat hesitantly, picked it up, which let her feel its power, though it seemed to resist and defy typical System definition. In truth, it was an extension of Lancelot’s will. It represented the precision point of his focus… the sharpest thing perhaps in existence.

The epitome of a duelist is kneeling before me. One more weapon to point at my enemies. We’ve only grown since Matrazere. A frightening thought.

She couldn’t resist unsheathing the sword, which made a very musical sound as it came free. The blade had a glow to it, further indicating its otherworldliness — it was not mythril, it was not enchanted steel. It was more than those things; it was finality made into a point.

She knew that the blade would cut through anything.

There was something… odd about the weapon, though. She couldn’t quite place it, such was its strangeness. “Can anyone else truly wield this, Lancelot?”

The golem looked up at her and nodded. “Any so worthy, Your Majesty, as you’ve proven.”

Samantha sniffed with faint humor as she sheathed the blade and handed it back to him. “You’re answering me in your way, not the way I’m asking the question. I sense more to it. Rise and try again.”

Lancelot swiftly repositioned the weapon back on his belt and stood. “Her Majesty’s senses are truly a wonder. Yes. The sword goes where it is needed, just as I do. That is not always the same place, however.”

On cue, the sword suddenly disappeared and reappeared, blade out, in Samantha’s hand. No sooner had it done so than it disappeared and returned to Lancelot.

Samantha raised an eyebrow. “Huh. I honestly didn’t expect that of you.”

Lancelot seemed puzzled. “Your pardon, Your Majesty?”

“It seems to demonstrate a, ah, pointed lack of ego.” A few of the golems chuckled at her pun.

“Ah. A weapon should have no ego; a weapon simply serves its purpose. Ego dies somewhere lost in our phantasmagoria of eternity. If anything, this blade represents the final edge. In the balance, it is easy, fluid, dividing all states; in the balance, it becomes nothing.”

“Finality.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“And what of the other blade? The one I thought this might be.”

“The weapon of our leader. Certainly, they have no comparison between them. This weapon here has no glory, no honor, no righteousness to speak of. It cuts through all of that; cuts through whatever is necessary, without judgment, without quarter, serving the worthy and the humbled absolutely.”

“And this other sword?” For some reason, in the moment, she just couldn’t bring herself to say the name outright. It felt… wrong and hollow… until it actually existed.

“What this sword has nothing to speak of, that one has in spades… overwhelmingly so.”
