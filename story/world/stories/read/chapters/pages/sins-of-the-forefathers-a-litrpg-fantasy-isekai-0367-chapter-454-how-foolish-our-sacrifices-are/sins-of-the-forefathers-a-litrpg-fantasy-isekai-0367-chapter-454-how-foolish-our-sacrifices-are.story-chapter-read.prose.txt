Freedom.

It really all came back to freedom, didn’t it? Every last second of every last minute of every last hour of every last day I had spent in Vereden…had all revolved around the concept of freedom.

I had lost mine from nearly the moment I had arrived on this planet, only to claw it back with blade in hand.

I had fought in a war not my own to help secure the future and freedom of a people I had barely known at the time.

I had risked life and limb to free an entire murdered complex of tormented ghosts.

Hell, I had even founded an entire Martial Order to help ensure that, not only the people I cared about, but the people of a nation could help retain their freedom.

And here I was, thousands of miles away from the home I had forged for myself, because, in the end, I was going to need to kill the person who had once upon a time…

Been the god of Freedom.

What an irony it was to discover that maybe, just maybe, he might have deserved that title once upon a time.

I’m…not sure what I had expected, all of the gods to have been like, in the long distant past. Nor had I really considered their origins or what might have driven them to the extreme ends they had chosen. I had heard from Travers, who might well have been one of the long-dead Netherim Elders, that the gods had betrayed their people and slaughtered them to the last…and never once questioned it. It was a bit embarrassing to realize that the image I’d had of the gods might have been a bit exaggerated. For all that I’d learned of them, and all I had seen, I had almost built up an image in my head of them as a group of mustache-twirling villains.

But that was ridiculous. People, and the gods had been people at one point, weren’t as irrational as I had been led to believe. They had motives and a history that led to that motive. It…made perfect sense, to me, that they might have suffered to

There was probably even a degree of truth in what the cantankerous had told me. No matter what, I still thought that whoever Lucretia had become as a god, she had been an outright malicious bitch.

Nobody who could inflict such torture upon their once home could be anything but a monster.

Still, if there was a mote of truth in what Travers had told me…it was only that. A mote, and not the whole of it.

I just…didn’t think it was true anymore, that the gods had completely genocided all of the Netherim. The possibility that there had been an entire period of extended unrest among them, culminating in the gods escalating that into outright civil war…

I found that all too familiar.

And if that was the case, then it seemed pretty obvious that the gods had won that war, with the help of the Netherim that had chosen to side with them. From there, the battles must have spread across the entirety of the planetary network. The implication had long been that the Netherim had been upon the other planets as well.

History told us that the gods had ruled all seven planets within the portal network, and they had done it for a very long time indeed. So, they had triumphed over the other Netherim, too.

Supposedly.

Hermus was no help there. His knowledge of everything that had occurred with his former ‘students’ abruptly ended when they had left him here.

Something about that struck me as odd.

In the aftermath of the former Great Spirit of Death’s revelations, we had split up briefly. A contemplative, almost dazed Rafael had told me that the enchantment keeping the door to this cavern closed was his, and he would be happy to lift it. He just needed time to gather some supplies, which was suspicious in and of itself. I didn’t hold it against the Lich, though.

I had a suspicion about just what he was up to, and I welcomed it.

As for Sylvia and Fade, both of them were right next to me. I had needed some time to…adjust to everything I had just learned. Even to fully comprehend the implications of it all. Because everything Hermus had told us was…well, it was important, to say the least. As such, I had retreated to one of the walls, with my love and familiar right at my side. Fade had needed more time to rest, anyway. Hermus’s revelations didn’t change the fact that the copious amounts of Death Aether he emitted was hard on the Spirit Wolf.

Sylvia was…off in her own world, in a way I typically didn’t see from the Sculpted woman.

That suited me just fine. I had…another question that had occurred to me. Something I had puzzled out, sitting against the wall for the last half hour.

I had a suspicion.

I heaved myself to my feet, drawing a flickering glance of acknowledgement from my partner, and nothing at all from the snoozing form of Fade. With a nod, I walked back across the cavern floor and crossed over the barrier onto the platform holding Hermus’s deceased form. When I stood next to the enormous mole skeleton once more, I almost awkwardly cleared my throat. “Ah…Hermus? Are you…still here?”

‘Hmm?’ I heard, the Spirit’s voice sounding oddly even more distant than it had. When next I heard it, though the voice was still whispery, it was at least clearer. ‘Ah, Nathaniel, was it? Was there something you needed?’

I hesitated for one moment more and then decided to go for it. I didn’t think I was going to offend him anyway. “I…had a question,” I started cautiously. At the Spirit’s prompting noise, I bit the bullet. “There’s no way the gods-your students-had enough power to punch a portal straight to the Administrator. Not pre-ascension, at least.”

Silence for one long moment.

Because I was positive I was correct. Teleportation was such an incredibly rare ability on Vereden. There were only a few times I had ever seen it done before. The first had been when that fucker Leonard had used a rare pre-prepared escape artifact to flee rather than lose to Grey at Caer Drarrow. The others had been done by Grey and Azarus, and I was chocking that up to their status as Envoys for their respective Great Spirits. The range for them was limited, though, being roughly a couple of miles at the maximum. I don’t even think it was true teleportation in the first place. Rather, they stepped halfway out of the physical and into the Concord, essentially skipping across the spiritual realm and emerging at a destination point.

I couldn’t even fathom the amount of power that would be needed to truly teleport fourteen fucking people across space and time to some…nebulous location, fixed around a person they had never met, as an anchor point somewhere in the goddamn universe.

Frankly, I think you’d need more energy to do that than had been released during the detonation of the Aetheric Fusion Collider in the heart of Mt. Goryuen.

Like, maybe three or four times that much. And just one of those explosions had been enough to vaporize a mountain larger than Everest back on Earth.

Otherwise, it was a no-go.

‘Very perceptive of you, Nathaniel,’ Hermus eventually responded. ‘And you’re correct. Normally, such a thing would be impossible. Not even if all seven of the original Great Spirits channeled all of their might at once would that be possible. However, there is another method of generating the required power. Something that is related to the very magic that I helped to create. The clue is right before you.’

I blinked and then cast my gaze about at the platform, not understanding for a moment.

And then it clicked.

The only things on this platform were corpses.

Fourteen of them. Fifteen, if you included the skeleton of Hermus’s once corporeal form.

I turned my astonished gaze up to the enormous mole skull above me. “No way…” I whispered. “Are you telling me…everyone ritually sacrificed themselves?”

Sacrifice.

It was a facet of mysticality that I was frankly a bit uncomfortable with. It was possible, if not taboo, to generate enormous amounts of raw Aether by sacrificing a living being in a specially prepared ritual space. It was…acceptable to do so to non-sentient, non-sapient animals in some cases.

It was not acceptable to do so to people. Under all the laws of Vereden, if you were caught doing something like that, you were just flat out executed. Even the damn Principality had stricter laws against ritual sacrifice than you would expect, and they’re slavers.

It was because it had been demonstrably proven that doing so harmed the soul of whatever was being sacrificed. You couldn’t destroy the soul of what was sacrificed, but it did maim them, and the result was almost always a powerful, incredibly angry wild undead. Usually a ghost, from what I understood. The kind that would have created something the creature Harlow, down in the Netherim bunker, had been twisted into, as Akhoroth, Maw of the Wyrm.

And suddenly, several things clicked for me.

“Son of a bitch,” I breathed. “That’s how they did it, isn’t it? All of you were ritually sacrificed, but you resisted the transformation as a Spirit. But…” I looked around wildly, reconsidering the resisting corpses circling Hermus. “They didn’t, did they? And that’s the point. That’s how they were able to overcome the Administrator, when it was likely so much stronger than they were. The gods deliberately sacrificed themselves in a harmful manner, twisting themselves into powerful, angry undead. Fourteen of those things could have probably torn apart a Calamity without even blinking.”

I started pacing rapidly in front of the patient skeleton of the once Spirit, letting my disbelieving gaze settle on each of the corpses surrounding him as I did.

I couldn’t believe it.

There weren’t just the remains of some of Hermus’s acolytes in ages past. These were the once mortal remains of the gods.

So many things about the mystery surrounding the gods now made so much sense to me.

“That many ritual sacrifices would have been more than enough power to transport undead,” I whispered furiously, my mind running a mile a minute. “Things that aren’t alive don’t need extra protections in the runic transportation array to ensure they remain so. And when they got to wherever the Administrator is, they probably tore it apart in a frenzy of rage, and those angry ghosts gorged themselves on its Divinity. That injured the System, and then they came back, this time in newly created, almost resurrected bodies. But the entire process would have been incredibly traumatic to the soul. Personalities would have been different, more extreme. In some cases, probably outright insane. They’d have their power and their Divinity, sure. But…they wouldn’t even be the same people anymore.” I stopped and turned to look at Hermus with an almost pitying expression. “They…weren’t the same people anymore, were they? What came back weren’t really your students.”

‘I believe not,’ Hermus whispered, a note of profound sorrow in his voice. ‘Otherwise, I cannot imagine those children I loved so dearly, would have abandoned me so readily, Not…not after what I sacrificed for them. This is my sin. They were so, so adamant that this was the correct course of action. That this was how they would gain the power to free their people from the grip of the Elders. However, we simply didn’t know enough about the entire sacrificial process. Necromancy was such a young school at the time, and we were all so confident we understood the matter fundamentally. We were wrong, and we all paid the price for our arrogance. My students paid with their sanity, and I paid…with my freedom. I shall remain here, in penance, for what I helped to unleash upon the world. Nay, the worlds. And I shall remain until my very bones crumble away into dust. Considering the power that, even now, they emanate and sustain their existence…that shall be a very long time indeed.’

All I could do was look away in silence. He was…right, in the end. Hermus and his students had jumped headfirst into a solution that ended up having untold consequences for tens of millions of people, over thousands of years.

Everyone involved had paid the price. Hermus. The gods.

And especially the people of Vereden.

Netherim and Genirm alike.

My attention was stolen when Rafael descended from the hole that led to the second floor. The Lich had replaced his impromptu toga with proper clothes, this time. Familiar ones at that. The first time I had met him underneath Elderwyck, the then skeletal Lich had been wearing a surprisingly stylish-looking full-body black robe with red accents. He must have had another one of those somewhere up there, because the dessicated corpse he was inhabiting was once more clad in a direct copy, complete with raised hood from which flaming blue eyes peered. The rest of his leathery features were totally obscured in darkness.

Interestingly enough, he was carrying a staff as well. It was almost a carbon copy of my own, complete with ebony wood and a similar amber capstone. The only thing that differentiated his from mine was the gnarls were different.

His eyes flickered over to me. I received a nod, but Hermus…

The ancient Spirit of Death instead received a deep bow of respect, before the Lich pivoted away and marched straight for the tunnel that led out into the caldera.

My eyebrow twitched. You asshole, you weren’t even going to ask if you could come with us? I had thought you were going to accompany us out of here, but I had at least expected the barest of common courtesies.

Oh, whatever. It’s not like I ever expected to turn him down. The Lich would be very useful on our journey.

I turned my gaze back to the vacant skull of the mole. “Goodbye, Hermus. I’m…not sure if I’ll ever be back. All I can say is…you were very helpful. Rest well.”

‘Goodbye, Nathaniel Hart,’ The disembodied Great Spirit of Death answered me almost absentmindedly. ‘I shall. My rest is not…quite as lonely as you might expect. I suspect we will meet again one day. Far, far into the future…and in a manner you might not expect.’

With those ominous words, the presence that always filled the air when the dead Spirit was present…faded.

I turned away, accepted the hand from the approaching Sylvia, ruffled Fade’s ears, and followed after Rafael with both of them at my side.
