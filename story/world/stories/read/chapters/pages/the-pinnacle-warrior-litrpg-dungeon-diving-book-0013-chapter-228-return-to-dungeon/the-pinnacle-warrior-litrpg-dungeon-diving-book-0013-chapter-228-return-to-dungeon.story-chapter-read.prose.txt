Drawing near to the entrance to the Dungeon proper, the party came to the somewhat begrudging conclusion that they should rest for the night before getting back into the Dungeon. They’d spent a fair amount of time buying materials in Neverwood before getting on the road back to this entrance, and nobody in the town of hidden, amazingly powerful outcasts enjoyed waking up and opening shop at the break of dawn.

Fortunately, each member of the party was now wearing their new pieces of equipment, and, with the exception of Benedict’s newest piece of equipment, which was simply defensive, everybody else had plenty of effects they were looking forward to testing as they got deeper into the Dungeon proper.

“According to the schedule we’ve determined over the last three weeks,” Astrid said as the party finished setting up camp, “tomorrow will be medium tide, then three days of low tide before the wave of new shimmerbugs comes. That should be plenty of time to start to investigate what’s further along, unless the receding of the wave means that the population density will be much higher further along and deeper into the forest.”

“We’ve heard it enough already, Astrid,” Benedict grumbled. “Yes, we have our new Skills, yes, it’s gonna be dangerous, yes, we already know what we’re doing.”

“It may be that I’m repeating the same thing over and over again,” Astrid replied, her voice steady, “but it bears repeating. We don’t know what we’re getting into tomorrow, and making sure we are all on the same page once we go in is important.”

He bowed his head in acknowledgment, muttering an apology as the party all worked together to fully prepare themselves for the delve on the next day.

“I know we never struggled with ’em,” Skandr said as the party talked about the different Skills the various tiers of shimmerbug had, “but it’s strange how easy even the greens are now. You know?”

“The prey has been too easy,” Muti said, seeming to also refer to the tavari. “Felix especially has been unable to grant the influence from Guardian’s Amplification to any of our prey.”

Astrid nodded at the Guardian who had fallen into the same trap that she herself had. After spending time getting trained by the Golden Fists and then insulted, Astrid found it particularly embarrassing that, of the party of five, there were two Skills that had been largely neglected for basically a month after evolving, as well as another that had been neglected. Her own Unconquerable Aura and Felix’s Guardian’s Amplification were basically entirely forgotten and both could have been immensely valuable in the fights in the wyverns’ nest.

Then, Benedict’s Overwhelming Speech had an aspect to it that he’d sometimes forgotten to use. At its core, Overwhelming Speech allowed him to multicast Skills just like Doubletalk had, but it had a secondary aspect that allowed Benedict to dip into his stamina reserves to make his Skills more effective against enemies, regardless of their inherent resistances. That paired with the aggressive aspect of Song of Victory could be quite deadly, the party was sure.

And now, every member of the party had another new Skill that they barely had time to practice with before they had to retreat to Neverwood and then ask for further permission to stay in the country. Fortunately, their meeting with the Grove Warden had gone quickly, and not just by Verdant Sanctuary standards. With a swift accounting of what the Wanderers had done for the sanctuary by clearing the wyvern’s nest, as well as their swift obedience to any commands that they received, the Grove Wardens had taken less than a day to tell Astrid that the Wanderers were welcome to stay until the peak of Steel.

However, you may not evolve to Mithril here. The requirements to receive permission to remain within our borders above Steel tier are much more stringent and demanding than it has been for you so far. You would do well to consider planning on leaving the Sanctuary before you evolve.

“Once people hit Mithril,” Astrid confirmed, “they’re much more difficult to keep where you want them without subjecting them to stringent contracts, is that right?”

Exactly. It is obvious that you are people who desire freedom, so you are given welcome to remain here for an additional twenty levels, however long that may take you, and then, unless you are willing to subject yourselves more completely to the rule of the Verdant Walkers, you will be kindly asked to leave.

“Thank you for your direct answer,” Astrid bowed to the Grove Warden.

Then, as one, the party had gone about preparing to get back to the Dungeon proper.

“I doubt we’re going to be able to keep up the same pace of gaining levels,” Skandr said as the party lay in their cots. “After all, most of the shimmerbugs are going to be lower level than we are now. The greens are about the same as us, and we’re really going to need to start fighting yellows and probably oranges to make sure we’re getting satisfactory Skill offerings moving forward.”

“You’re not satisfied with what you’ve got?” Astrid asked, incredulous.

“No, of course I’m happy with it! I just haven’t had time to experiment with Microburst yet. And,” he continued as Astrid started to open her mouth, “I’m also very excited to continue experimenting with the self-sorting enchantment grimoire. There’s so much I can do with that alone that there’s going to be so much more experimentation ahead of me, especially with higher-capacity and demand spells as my mana sensitivity continues to grow in combination with the spikes to my attributes as well as through the use of Microburst. I’m just saying that if we spend the next ten levels just killing these weak bugs, level 81 is probably gonna be pretty disappointing.”

Astrid chuckled and nodded. “You’re right. But now that we are finally going to be able to move through the Dungeon without getting ourselves killed, we can afford to maybe get into contact with one of the reds. The one yellow we ran into in the beginning of the second week was dangerous enough already, but that was a while ago.”

Skandr grunted his agreement, because the party hadn’t needed to spend time to figure out what the yellow bugs could do. At that time, the party had been level 64, still lower level than the blues, but never worried about them. They should have been. The yellow aura that washed over every one of the sixty shimmerbugs had taken the encounter from one to be taken cautiously to instead one they needed to run away from. The blue and indigos had charge forward, and that Skill crushed and crashed through vine-trees as Felix and Astrid were both smashed back by the force of their charge. Seeing three greens and the yellow behind them closing in faster than the party would have believed when they were already struggling against blues and indigos?

Well, Benedict’s immediate switch to double-layering Voice of Liberation while Skandr loosed his prepared his step of the wind spell had carried them away fast enough that they were able to avoid being locked into potentially deadly combat.

“I’m looking forward to being able to rematch a yellow,” Astrid said. “And then with there still being two more tiers higher than that? Exciting.”

“You’re insane,” Skandr laughed, the words affectionate. “Sleep good.”

Astrid bid her own good night, mentally pulling up the party’s Statuses to make sure that she was prepared to give good directions to them, taking into account their new Skills (with the exception of Felix and herself, who’d chosen to evolve Defender’s Avatar and Body Surge to Steel while hoping for better offerings at level 81) and everything that their equipment could do. She had access to all that information, but she mentally dismissed any piece of equipment whose effect wasn’t unique enough to be noted preparatory to getting into the Dungeon once again.

Everybody had their new Skills, each offering a new or improved way to go deeper and confront the yellows and the horde of shimmerbugs that they would rule. Of course, she was familiar with her own Body Surge and Felix’s upgraded Defender’s Avatar, but there hadn’t been nearly enough time to really experiment with Microburst, Assassin’s Bearing, and Inspiring Song in the past three days since the party had reached level 71. Microburst and Inspiring Song were both basically the same kind of Skill as Defender’s Avatar and Body Surge: massive boosts to turn the tide of battle. Muti was the only one whose selected Skill wasn’t in that same vein as the rest, and she’d selected it specifically because she was fairly certain it would be rolled into her equipment Skill when she evolved.

“I have seen the strength of Immortal Warrior’s Physique,” she said. “I am excited to learn just what I will become capable of with this new Skill, in that same way.”

As for the rest of the party, well, they’d all decided to hold off on incorporating their new pieces of equipment into their fighting style until they reached level 71, given that they weren’t facing anything challenging. That might have turned out badly if they’d come into contact with another yellow, especially if it had trapped them, but in this case, it was a step that they took that didn’t end up causing them trouble.

Skandr had been thumbing through his grimoire long before he had promised to begin using it, and considering how much preparation needed to go into the successful use of the piece of equipment, nobody had begrudged him that. As for the three draconic helms, Astrid had tried out what her helmet could do once and very quickly learned that she shouldn’t be using it outside of either the Dungeon or a fight that needed it. Muti, with the greater flexibility in her own helmet, had been playing around with it for a while. Puffs of mist or shadow were the most common, but Astrid couldn’t help but notice that Muti was also spending more time experimenting with her tattoos, flame and lightning the most common among them. She was looking forward to seeing what Muti was capable with this greater versatility.

Felix’s helmet was of particular interest, given that, instead of using a breath weapon of sorts, it served as an additional barrier. When he once tried it out, Astrid was interested to see what kind of element the helmet’s barrier would carry with it. Instead of Astrid’s, which was always ice, or Muti’s, which was so flexible, Felix’s was basically attribute-less. A shimmering, silvery barrier flashed into existence around him, similar to the barrier he could make with his Boon of the Protector, but the few experiments they’d been able to conduct until now seemed to show that it was a better barrier for protecting from magical attacks.

As Astrid drilled herself again on each of her party members’ Skills and abilities with their equipment, she found herself slowly coming to sleep. And when the morning came, it was time for her to go out with the rest, to finally plumb the depths of the Dungeon proper and see what else was waiting for them. So, with bated breath and an excited grin, she led her party back towards the deep shadows that linked them to the Dungeon proper.
