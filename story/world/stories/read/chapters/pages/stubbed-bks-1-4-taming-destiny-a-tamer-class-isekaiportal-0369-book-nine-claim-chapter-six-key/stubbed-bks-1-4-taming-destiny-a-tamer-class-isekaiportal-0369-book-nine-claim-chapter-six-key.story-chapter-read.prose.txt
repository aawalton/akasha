Even on the edge of the cloud, we have to use shields to protect us from the fist-sized hailstones that plummet from the weather that Josiah and Layton have cooked up together. Apparently, this isn’t the first time they’ve cooperated to create unusual weather, but they’ve always had limits before based on not wanting to cause too much damage. Now, seeing the size of the thing – and the hailstones falling from it – I can only hope that our own shields aren’t as badly depleted as we’re aiming for Torrent’s fleet to become.

But Josiah and Layton have planned the weather system well – it has formed right over the area into which our enemy’s ships are moving. Already, Kyrian’s ship has disappeared, and Bladestrike’s ship is close to doing so. Siren’s ship is trying to take evasive action, breaking away from their original course to try to avoid the thickest part of the storm.

Thunder rumbles overhead and I glance at the cloud above nervously. We’re key targets here – perhaps we should fly lower so the ships are more likely to get struck.

I share that thought with my companions and, as one, we descend to skim over the surface of the increasingly choppy waves.

But, when Noir suddenly roars in panic, I realise that the sea holds other dangers. Ivor, Sirocco, and I quickly turn back, only to see that something has snagged Noir’s tail and is trying to haul him into the deeps. Noir is beating hard with his wings, but he’s quickly losing the fight.

Help him! I snap at my companions as I swoop to deal with whatever is trapping him.

It’s a thick tendril of water, I realise, and I glare at where I last saw Kyrian’s ship. How he can control this without being able to see us, I don’t know. But clearly, he can.

Summoning a thick blade of bright orange fire, I slice through the tendril. On contact with my blade, the water turns immediately to steam and Noir soars free.

But my attack has obviously signalled something to the Water Mage – even as that tendrils smooths back into the waves, a hundred more appear, all moving to converge on me with even more aggression than the one that had captured Noir.

We need to go higher again! I order my companions tightly, hacking my way through the forest of tendrils that has formed around me.

The tendrils seem like a massive underwater beast, a kraken or something similar. They attack from all angles, particularly from above. I’m doing my best to escape, but for every tendril I cut down, ten seem to take its place. I’m harried enough that it’s all I can do to keep up, let alone find a strategy that will actually work.

I know I’m losing when the tendrils begin to make a wall instead of individual strands – it’s trying to encase me in a bubble of water, and, from there, either kill me or bring me helpless to Kyrian.

Suddenly, fire flashes beyond the wall of water. I see enough to know that Sirocco has disobeyed me and has stayed to help. I can’t help but be grateful for that, for all that I wish she had got herself to safety.

Holes form in the wall of water as Sirocco sends Fire Wings at it with impressive frequency. Her efforts alone aren’t enough to get me out of this fix, but they distract the tendrils, and that’s enough to let me save myself.

Activating my tattoo shield, I have a brief window of opportunity. The tendrils battering it are rapidly draining its charge, but all I need is a few seconds.

By the time the shield collapses, I’m just about ready. Fire explodes out of me in ribbons, cutting through the tendrils like a hot knife through butter.

I beat my wings powerfully, rocketing upwards through the falling drops of water – and free of the trap. Below me, the tendrils reconstitute very quickly, and reach angrily for me again, but I’ve escaped their grasp and now I quickly regain height to make sure they’re unable to entrap me once more.

Hail smashes into me – I’ve been drawn closer to Torrent’s fleet and deeper into the storm – so I quickly use a disc of air to deflect the fist-sized balls of ice. Sirocco is battling with the hailstones and isn’t using her tattoo shield – hers must be out of charge too. I swoop closer and grab her.

I’m half-expecting her to rake me with her talons for the unexpected contact, but she seems to grasp what’s going on quickly and sends me gratitude down the Bond as she’s no longer pelted with chunks of ice. She even reduces in size a bit to make it easier for me to carry her, sinking into my arms, her side of the Bond feeling utterly exhausted. I quickly activate my tattoo to grab her and she doesn’t resist.

I’m going through, I warn Noir and Ivor – I’m in the middle of the storm, so I might as well take the shortest route back to my ship rather than heading outwards again. Are you both OK?

The two alcaorises assure me that they’re fine and so I continue flying as quickly as we can. Having learned my lesson, I fly high above the waves, but keeping lightning in mind, I still try to remain below the level of the ship’s masts. I don’t know enough about lightning to guess if the disc of compressed air whirling over our heads is enough to ward it off, but something is obviously working since we clear the edge of the storm without being struck.

And it’s not that there wasn’t any lightning – I was briefly blinded multiple times as bright flashes of light struck shields and briefly outlined them even through the obscurity of the falling ice. And I was also half-deafened as the advent of each lightning strike was marked by the sonorous boom of thunder.

All in all, I’m rather grateful to land heavily on the deck of my ship which is carefully staying out of even the leading edge of the cloud. As planned, my fleet is using the obscuring nature of the hail to reposition, circling the cloud in order to strike at Torrent’s fleet from different sides.

With Water Mages on board our enemy’s fleet, it’s quite possible that they’ll be able to detect where we are, even if they can’t see us, but if the Water Mages on our side are doing their jobs right, the enemy mages shouldn’t be able to see through or control the water within a certain distance of our ships. And if they can’t do that, then, no matter how much damage the hailstorm does, we’re lost.

“How are things?” I ask Loran grimly – as my Bonded manservant, and someone who’s primarily a non-combatant, he’s been delegated the task of managing the Bond network communication across the six ships – not a small challenge. The samurans might have been spread out across the ships to stand with the leaders of each, but they still have to be given messages and have their own messages heard and processed.

But I have faith that Loran is up to the task – from all accounts, he did similar in the battle for Zlona, even directing Nicholas’ ground-based Bonded a good portion of the time. And he knows he can reach out to me or Maxen if he needs guidance himself or advice on strategy.

“Going to plan so far,” Loran answers quickly and confidently. “We’re currently circling the cloud and expect to join in battle as soon as the hail begins to reduce.”

“How long is that likely to be?”

“River says that both Lord Heatwave and Lord Softrain are exhausted. They have stopped feeding magic into the cloud, so it’s likely to begin clearing up soon.” Then Loran grimaces uncertainly. “Or it might get worse. Lord Softrain indicated that weather can be unpredictable.”

I eye the cloud in question. It’s still pretty dark, but, based on the way I’m starting to see the shape of a ship within it, I suspect that the shower is already beginning to lighten.

I squint as I see an odd swirling appear in the clouds above. The next moment I curse as the shower lightens unexpectedly quickly.

“They’ve got an air mage,” I remember. “I’d bet anything Torrent’s getting them to blow away the cloud.”

“It seems so,” Loran agrees grimly.

“We don’t have much time. Is everything else in place?”

“Almost. Lord Forestheart has been more successful than he hoped – the hail floating in the sea offered him an easy way of creating ice chunks.”

“That is good news,” I remark, heartened. With hailstones battering them from above and chunks of sea ice bumping at and scratching them from the sides, hopefully the three ships’ physical shields are all but gone. “What of our Water Mages?”

“They’ve mostly succeeded in establishing a safe area within four body-lengths of each ship,” Loran reports. “There have been two attacks that spiked closer, but they were able to fight them off.” I sigh in relief – at least I don’t need to worry about tendrils like the ones I battled creating a similar bubble around the ship. Not unless the Water Mage wants to put a huge amount of effort into it.

“Do they think they can hold that space?” I check.

“For now. When they tire, they’ll still do their best not to let the enemy mages take control over currents directly around our ships. They have plenty of potions nearby to help bolster their defence.” He hesitates briefly. “They can only protect Captain Sor’az’s ship while it is within four shiplengths, though, and only if there are no enemy mages between.”

I nod – understandable. But Sor’az didn’t want any humans on his ship, Water Mages or not. He said that the damayar have their own methods, though I don’t think that any of them are mages per se. He’s captained his ship successfully for years – it was on land where he met his downfall – so I can only hope that he knows what he’s about.

My eyes fix themselves on the enemy fleet. With the lightening of the cloud cover, it’s time for the next stage of our plan to take place. We don’t want to get too close to the enemy ships – especially not that of Bladestrike – but at the same time, our weapons have limited range meaning that we have to at least get closer. I clench my jaw as worry roils in my stomach.

The hailstones trail off, stopping almost as quickly as they began. The cloud has lightened and, though it remains in existence, it looks much softer than it did before. It’s deposited its load and now will likely disperse completely unless it joins with other natural clouds.

I hear Captain Tolly ordering the ship to turn. Currently, we’re sailing next to the other fleet which, counter to my expectations, doesn’t seem to know we’ve changed position. Correction, two out of three ships don’t know we’ve changed position, I tell myself, eyeing House Siren’s ship that is sailing directly towards Valence’s.

Our ship shifts, the turn ponderous in comparison to a car, but rapid in light of how big this ship is. I fidget, finding the hurry up and wait nature of this naval battle to be more than a little frustrating. I know that my job right now is to rest and recover my resources so I can act later in the battle – as well as ensuring that I’m not injured, killed, or captured – but it’s still difficult just to stand here and watch. I can’t help but worry that I’m in the wrong place; that we’ll lose people because I wasn’t there to help.

Glancing at the water in between each of the enemy ships, I see how it’s littered with chunks of ice. Some are pretty big, too. As I watch, one bumps into Kyrian’s prow. A shield flares weakly.

“Their shields aren’t gone,” I remark grimly.

“Nay, but they’re in a sorry state,” Captain Tolly responds, clearly having overheard me. “A couple o’ broadsides from us and they’ll be done for,” he estimates.

I hope you’re right, I think to myself, and that we can survive a couple of broadsides ourselves. After all, to put ourselves in a position where we can strike the enemies, we’ll have to make ourselves vulnerable. And Kyrian’s ship looks like it has more armaments than mine.

But I’m clearly not the only one with plans aimed at lessening our enemy’s defences.

“Archers!” I hear Maxen call from the deck below. “Take aim!”

Already? I think, startled – we’re still several shiplengths away. Far further than should be possible for non-Classer archers to fire. Unless they have enchanted weaponry, I remind myself – there’s a reason we bought a fair amount of it before leaving Moriax, despite its expense.

“Fire!”
