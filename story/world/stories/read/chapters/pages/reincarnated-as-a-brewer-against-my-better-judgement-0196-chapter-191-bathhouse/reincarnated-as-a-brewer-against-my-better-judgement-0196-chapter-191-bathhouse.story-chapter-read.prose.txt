When Alric finally reached the bathhouse near Merchant’s Row, breakfast at least had settled enough that he did not worry about losing it when someone spoke loudly. The headache still pounded, as did the fuzziness on the tongue. The walk from headquarters and the cool air, however, had helped.

What surprised Alric more was that when he moved with his full guard contingent, people got out of the way very fast. Moving through the main avenue had been quick and easy although it was the cities day off.

“This way, Mister Alric,” Simon said, pointing to a side road off the avenue.

The bathhouse was a lot bigger than Alric expected. It was almost entirely made out of wood and two stories high. Small openings in the roof let out thin trails of steam, but there were no windows at all.

“You guys come here before?” Alric asked; the guards all shook their heads.

“Too pricey, Mister Alric,” Simon explained, looking at the entrance. Alric just shrugged and gestured toward it.

Inside, a broad wooden counter separated the entrance room running along the back. Numerous doors went left and right. There was text above each section, but Alric wasn’t about to risk reading it with his headache. The man behind the counter glanced once and then did a double-take at the number of people as Alric approached.

“Uh, Good day, sir.” The man said quickly, counting the number of people. “Are you a member here?”

“No, and if you ask me to sign something, we're leaving,” Alric said, the thought of his mind bending text made his stomach turn at the thought. The man glanced back at the guards, then at him.

“That won’t be needed, sir; members get discounts.” The man explained; Alric just shook his head.

“Don’t need, how many for all of us? Twenty-three.” Alric said, just wanting to get it over with.

“It’s two large copper per person, sir.” The man said with a small bow.

Alric nodded; it was expensive, but he wasn’t about to argue or quibble. He pulled out four small silver and six large coppers from his item box and handed them over. The man produced an abacus from somewhere, and Alric sighed.

“All correct, sir.” The man said, finally looking up. He gestured to the left doors, “Ladies to the left, Men to the right.” He said with a smile and a bow. That was all Alric needed to hear as the groups split up. Molly gave a cheerful wave as she left.

Alric was one of the last through; the room was full of baskets and attendants. It was explained that they would clean your clothes while you bathed. Alric’s guards could not get undressed fast enough after hearing that.

Alric had, of course, been in changing rooms before; he had showered after sport in high school. He wasn’t embarrassed about himself either. Sure, he wasn’t built like Pavise, but he wasn’t in awful shape. The awkwardness of getting changed and standing naked beside several men who were employed specifically to follow him everywhere was making itself apparent rapidly.

No one else seemed to find anything unusual about it. Clothes went into baskets and were carried off by attendants. Alric eventually stripped and wrapped the given towel around himself. Simon and some of the guards glanced at it but said nothing further, their towels over their shoulders.

They moved through to the next area. It was a long room lined with buckets; each guard split off and approached one. The smell, however, was jarring. It wasn’t unpleasant, but it was humid. Something herbal had been added to the water; it smelt like pine. Each guard would dip their towel into the bucket before wiping themselves down.

Alric copied them; the humidity-induced nausea quickly receded. The hot water against the skin felt fantastic and left a faint scent. He copied the guards' motions: arms, face, hair, and back.

“This is good.” One of the guards said, rubbing the towel over his face. Alric agreed with the sentiment.

“Hmmm. Need these at headquarters.” Came another, rubbing himself down for the second time.

Alric glanced down at his towel; it was getting filthy, as was the water he was using. “You want me to build a bathhouse now?”

The guard glanced around and shrugged. Alric grinned, feeling more himself.

“Since you brought it up, Mister Alric,” came from Simon, who was sitting down and scrubbing the soles of his feet vigorously. That got a round of laughter, which Alric regretted when his head throbbed.

“Ooof, laugh quietly. There is probably a bathhouse guild, and then the king would be upset with me again,” Alric said offhand. Many of the guards struggled after that and giggled quietly.

When they were done, the guards went through the next door. Alric wrapped the towel around himself and followed, eager to see what else the bathhouse offered.

Heat immediately slammed into him; many of the guards recoiled. Alric took two steps inside when his stomach rolled.

The room had tiered wooden benches on each side and baskets of hot rocks. An attendant was scattering water over the rocks, producing all the steam.

“Nope,” Alric said to the sauna at large, heading for the opposite door leading out.

This earned another chuckle and nods from the guards who opened the door and led him through.

The next section was much more promising. A large hall opened up, with wooden slat flooring reminiscent of a wooden deck. Large stone tubs were set into the floor. Six people could sit comfortably in a tub, more if everyone involved accepted their friendship being awkward afterward. Steam rose gently off the water's surface.

The bathhouse had a group to Alric’s left but was otherwise empty. It was morning on the city's day off, after all.

“This.” Alric said, glancing around the space, “This I can do.”

The guards around Alric grinned enthusiastically and pointed to the far section, which had three tubs. That made sense to Alric, as they could watch anyone approach.

Alric lowered himself into a tub with Simon and four others. The water was hot but not unpleasantly so. He sank down until the water went past his shoulders. Someone in the next tub made a happy noise that would either be associated with food or activities Alric never wanted to discuss with the guards. Overall, though, Alric was surrounded by happy guard noises and may have made a few himself.

Since waking in the coffin pretending to be a bed, this was the first time Alric felt genuinely good. Alric closed his eyes and just took it in, feeling his muscles loosen.

“This is worth the money.” One of the guards said, and Alric agreed silently.

He opened his eyes when he heard a wet slapping noise of feet. Two attendants approached: one with a long bronze tool and another with a bucket. Alric watched with interest as the attendant with the tool stepped into the tub, took black rocks, and placed them in the center. There was an immediate hiss. Then it was lowered. Alric had assumed that the center recess was for feet, but apparently not.

Clever. No pipes, boilers, and nonsense. Although I don’t want to think about how often they change the water.

The attendant finally added oil to the water and what looked like a bundle tied to a rock with pine. The water gained a pleasant scent. The attendant then left, heading to the next tub.

Alric leaned back again, letting the heat wash through him, his head tilted backward and closed his eyes. The guards went quiet; conversation dropped off as everyone settled. Someone in the next tub made another deeply inappropriate noise of contentment, which made Alric smile.

For a long moment, they all just rested and said nothing. The sound of wet feet slapping against the wooden slats caused Alric to open his eyes again. An attendant was approaching with a tray. He had opened his eyes too late to see what was on it.

“Master Alric?” The attendant asked, already lowering the tray.

“Yes?” Alric said. As the tray came into view, it held a cup. Alric glared at it as he felt his stomach roll again.

“The gentleman over there wished to send you a cup of wine.” The attendant said, turning slightly and gesturing to the only other group in the room. One man raised his hands in greeting.

“No. No, thank you.” Alric said, putting a hand over his mouth; the smell had just hit him. Around Alric, his guards laughed out loud. “Please take that away.”

The attendant opened their mouth and glanced at the group who had sent the cup.

“Please,” Alric said again, shifting away from it.

The attendant nodded and left with it. Alric felt the blood in his face had drained.

“You alright, Mister Alric?” Simon asked.

Alric nodded slowly, still holding his mouth. “Would you want wine right now, Simon?” Alric asked. Simon recoiled a little, which caused another chuckle, although quieter this time.

Just as Alric was trying to get comfortable again, he saw the waving man from earlier start approaching. He was middle-aged, somewhere in his fifties by Alric’s guess. His hair was thinning and greying around the sides; he had a belly that suggested many big meals had met their match.

A towel hung over one shoulder, but that was all. The man stopped near the tub, clearly unconcerned.

“Was there something wrong with the wine?” he asked.

Alric shook his head slightly. “Nothing like that. I’m sure it’s excellent. I drank enough yesterday that if I tried to drink it, I wouldn’t keep it down.”

The man’s eyebrows shot up, and then he laughed loudly. Alric winced at the sudden noise.

“The kerfuffle at the muster yard?” He asked, tilting his head.

“Yes, that. That was me and a few others.” Alric said, squinting slightly.

“A few others? I heard half the city was there?” The man asked, glancing at Alric’s guards.

“Not half, just a lot of guards, adventurers, and brewers,” Alric said, hoping to end the conversation quickly.

“I see, the wine was definitely the wrong choice then.” He said, rubbing his chin.

Alric gave a weak shrug and shifted deeper into the water. The man seemed friendly and well-meaning, but he certainly didn’t understand why he would send him wine.

“Perhaps a massage would be better?” The man offered with a wide smile glancing at the far end of the room.

Alric was struck dumb for a moment, and then all the pistons fired in his mind at once.

If I ever accept a massage from a middle-aged naked man who just tried to buy me wine, then I need to reconsider my whole life.

“No, no. Thank you.” Alric said, glancing away, “I appreciate the sentiment, truly, but the hot water is doing everything I need right now.”

The man studied him for a moment; Alric smiled politely. The man eventually nodded, “Of course.” He said and cleared his throat, “I don’t believe we’ve been introduced. Oison.” He extended a hand.

Alric took it. “Alric,” then settled back into the water.

The man stood there a moment longer, waiting. Alric waited back, unsure what was expected of him. He gave another polite nod.

“Good to meet you.” Alric probed.

“And you, Master Alric.” Oison nodded, turned, and went back to the other tub.

Alric got comfortable in the water again. He had it all the way to his chin. His headache was finally receding. “Odd fellow,” Alric mumbled. He could feel a nap coming on.

Simon glanced at him tiredly, “Hmm?”

Alric just shook his head and leaned back further.

Oison, meanwhile, was climbing back into the tub he had been in earlier. Three other men had watched the exchange; the youngest glanced at Oison and said: “That looked painful. He sent the wine back?”

Oison nodded, “The wine was a mistake. He is hungover.” He shrugged, glancing over at Alric’s group.

“I see. Did you offer something else?” one of the other men said.

“I did, a massage, apparently a mistake also,” Oison said, frowning; he glanced at the massage room.

“Strange, the massages are the reason we come here?” said the youngest, following his gaze.

Oison just shrugged. “I introduced myself. That’s all. He introduced himself back.”

“Maybe he just doesn’t know who you are?” Came from the second eldest of the group.

Oison took a deep breath, “Well I only gave him my name to see, don't think he knew. The man is a royal factor who no one can properly explain. He owns many businesses now. The king considers him infrastructure, I hear, and he is opening a tailor, bakers, and other businesses. Yet doesn’t know the head of the merchant’s guild? Hells, I’m meant to assess his brandy.”

“Brandy?” came from the youngest, but the whole group had raised brows and glanced across at the brewer and his group.

“We know nothing about him. This is a problem. If he can dismiss us so casually, not even pretending to care. Then he clearly doesn’t need us or thinks he doesn’t.” Oison said, still looking at Alric’s group, “We need to get to know him before whatever he is doing becomes everyone's problem.”
