“We’re going to need more volunteers,” I say, as I review the results of the tests over the last few days with Thessalanor. A loud groan and the liquid sound of insides being evacuated make me wince. I’m so glad we have the sanitation system up because otherwise whoever cleaned the bedpans would hate me almost as much as the volunteers do. At least these ones volunteered because they understand how important this is – enough to suffer personally. However… “I don’t think these ones will want to risk going through this again.”

Thessalanor hums in absent-minded agreement, flicking through the parchment in front of him.

“You could order more of your slaves to volunteer,” he suggests. He looks up at my silence, and sees my forbidding expression. To his credit, he doesn’t quail, though he does visibly swallow. “No? Well, then, perhaps we can offer some sort of incentive.”

“You mean a bribe,” I comment dryly. Thessalanor shrugs. I consider it, then incline my head a little. “It might work.” I sigh. “This is taking forever.”

“We’re developing a whole new treatment here, my lord,” Thessalanor points out. “It’s bound to take time. As it is, we’ve already made a good deal of progress.”

Begrudgingly, I have to admit that he does have a point. We’ve already discovered that the Energy absorption tablet doesn’t have to be in a living body, though it does require the same conditions as a stomach, namely within a certain temperature range, liquid, and highly acidic.

As a result of our trials, we’ve also been forced to test the treatment for Energy poisoning developed by River and her master using local ingredients – fortunately, that works.

After realising that plants used in a potion don’t seem to have the same negative effects as plants eaten raw or cooked normally, I did wonder whether it would be possible to make potions that acted like nutritional smoothies. The answer to that is yes, however the amount of work that would need to go into it – and the low number of Alchemists capable of creating them – makes it unscalable. We could feed a few people like that, no more.

Since Rory, Loran, and Maxen were willing to join the trial, I was also able to see what happened to them from the inside out. I’ve been able to confirm that the excess Energy has an impact on the internal matrices of Maxen and Loran, though I didn’t see any evidence of the same in Rory. As he doesn’t currently have an internal matrix, that might be why. However, that didn’t seem to be the whole story – as can be seen by all three of them suffering negative effects.

It also seems to be something about either the quantity or quality of the Energy here that essentially gives even Maxen, who has a Class that should be capable of processing any amount of Energy, the equivalent of indigestion. In smaller bites, I suspect that his system would process it fine, but as it is, he gets overwhelmed, if more slowly than Loran who in turn can process it more easily than Rory. It’s possible that we will be accustomed to the quality of the Energy here – much like people become accustomed to breathing thicker or thinner atmospheres if they live in different areas – but I can’t assume that we will.

Unfortunately, Elarion hasn’t made much progress towards a stable food supply in his experiments either. Though, I think to myself, he hasn’t cornered the market on Plant Mages…. Perhaps there’s someone I need to speak to.

I consider reaching out to said being through the Bond, but, after a moment of debate about how likely it is he’ll just ignore me on principle given how much I sense he’s concentrating right now, I decide to go and pay him a visit in person. Anyway, I could do with a break – I’ve been practically living in the healers’ rooms. Exercise is supposed to be good for stimulating creative thought.

As I stand up and push the chair back, Thessalanor looks up at me with an inquiring gaze.

“I’ll be back later – I just want to take a walk and talk to someone who might have some ideas on this whole thing.”

“Of course, my lord,” Thessalanor agrees. “Do I have permission to start offering the incentives we discussed?”

I hesitate, then shrug – delegation is probably a good idea.

“Sure. But please discuss with either Sarran or Loran about it before making any announcements – they know best what we’re able to offer.”

A few minutes later, I’m striding through the settlement, towards the gates. I can feel through the Bond that the one I need is outside.

As I walk, I notice the different fashions that are starting to take over. Some people who are very happy with the oversaturation of Energy in local plants are the seamstresses and tailors. The dyers too, I suppose – certainly, the colours are even more vibrant in the clothes here. And the Enchanters – high-Energy fabrics are unsurprisingly easier to enchant.

They’re lighter too – the climate in this place is closer to tropical than temperate and normal wear for Moriax becomes stifling here. People still wear overrobes, but they tend to be of lighter fabric, or with very few layers underneath. The seamstresses and tailors from Elarion’s retinue have definitely been setting trends – they’re very accustomed to humidity. Though, to be fair to them, those from Layton’s area have also had a strong influence – though they’re not used to humidity, they’re certainly accustomed to heat. Personally, I’m grateful for my fire affinity – ever since getting it, I haven’t been bothered nearly as much by either heat or cold.

The climate has caused other problems, of course – as if we didn’t have enough. Fungal growths have been a frequent complaint that have stretched our resources further, and food goes off more easily in the warmth if it’s left out, though at least the areas deep into the cavern tend to be cooler than the surrounding forest.

My thoughts are interrupted as I become abruptly aware that someone is just in front of me. Dodging sideways just in time to avoid collision.

“Tiria!” I exclaim in surprise. The mage has her nose in an old-looking text and doesn’t even seem to have noticed what almost happened. But she does hear my call, lifting her head and twisting her head until she spots me. I see the moment she realises that she’s just walked past me without acknowledgement as her cheeks go bright red and her eyes dart away.

“Markus! Ah, Lord Markus, I mean. Or would you rather–” She cuts herself off, going even redder, as impossible as that seemed a moment ago. “My apologies,” she continues, doing her best to sound calm and collected.

I sigh internally. I thought we’d got past the embarrassment after spending so much time discussing various differences between Moriax and Earth and magic and all of that on the ship. Sure, Keshaka was there for some of it, but we also had periods where it was just us.

“I had assumed you’d be with the Alchemists – I thought you were working beside them to see if a spell would be a better option than a potion for our Energy problem?”

“Oh! I am! At least, that’s where I’m going. And where I was. But I suddenly remembered I’d left this text in my quarters and thought it might be useful.”

She steps closer again and brandishes the text at me as if I’ll be able to determine its contents just from the cramped writing on that double page. To be fair to her, I do see ‘Energy’ written enough times to justify why she might think it would be useful.

“Looks fascinating,” I tell her with a sardonic note. Then, at the way she subtly droops, I quickly add, “And useful too, I’m sure.”

Tiria pulls the book away, looking shy.

“Would you… It’s been a while since we had time for a good discussion like we used to on the ship.” It’s not a question, but the way she looks at me hopefully is enough clue.

“I’m pretty busy at the moment,” I start, and once again see her drooping a little. Unlike her sometimes-contrived upset, I can see that this is sincere. My mouth moves before my brain catches up. “But maybe we can walk around the settlement this evening or something.” She immediately brightens. “Unless some emergency comes up, of course,” I add. It doesn’t quell her sudden enthusiasm.

“That sounds wonderful! Maybe we can go into the forest – I want to compare some of the plants there to the ones that used to be around my tower. And we need to talk about setting up some of my experiments with the local beasts – they might react differently from the ones back on Moriax.”

“We can talk about it later,” I hurriedly interject before she continues chattering. As much as I would like to spend more time with her, this is neither the time nor the place – more than a few eyes are watching us with varying expressions. “I’ll come by to your place at the fourteenth candlemark.”

“I look forward to it!” she enthuses, and I feel her eyes on my back as I turn and continue heading to the forest.

I spend the rest of the trip to the gates wrestling over the question I still haven’t answered – what exactly do I intend with Tiria, and should I really be encouraging this in either her or myself?

Once outside the settlement, I follow the Bond’s direction into the forest. While keeping a wary eye out for potential threats, I can’t help but appreciate the vibrant colours and many melodious sounds of my new environment. The odours are another story – for every beautifully fragrant flower, there’s a nasty whiff of rotting loam or animal waste. And I don’t dare stick my nose into the flowers in case they try to bite it off.

It takes me a while to find Artio, the bear-owl like yelna who Evolved to use a form of Plant-Shaping, but not because he’s far into the forest. It’s mostly because he’s so difficult to spot – even when I have an idea of approximately where he is. He’s covered himself in vines and looks almost like part of the local foliage.

Curious, I try to understand exactly what he’s doing. Sinking my awareness into the bond, I sense that he’s deep in concentration. No, deeper than that. He’s in communion with the world around him.

It reminds me of when I was trying to learn Fire-Shaping, when I had to join the fire in thought as well as in movement. It feels like Artio is both here and not here at the same time.

I can only hope he doesn’t run into the same problem I did – falling so far in that he struggles to come back.

Fortunately, my fears are proven groundless a few minutes later when I feel him surface, rising physically out of the vines as much as he does mentally.

What do you want? he asks shortly, clearly not in the best of moods. But I’m used to that – he still holds the fact that Torrent got away against me. Even the fact that I fought and defeated Torrent junior doesn’t make up for it – not only does Artio not consider ‘revenge against a cub’ to be sufficient, but I didn’t kill Kyrian, or offer Artio the opportunity to do the same. Still, it’s been months and the ornery owl-bear is slowly starting to thaw. In a year’s time, I might have earned his forgiveness. Maybe.

At least, he’s willing to cooperate despite his annoyance. Considering he’s solitary by nature, he displays a surprising amount of understanding about how important it is that we work together.

“I came to ask for help,” I admit. He eyes me dubiously. I relate the issues we’re having with Energy-saturated plants and the lack of progress Elarion has been having. “Do you have any ideas of what we could try?”

Artio gives it some thought.

No, he replies finally. I let out a disappointed breath. I grow and speak with plants that live, not plants that do not live yet. His tone is almost apologetic – the closest to such that I’ve heard it. Perhaps he hasn’t been so lost in his explorations that he’s failed to understand the gravity of our situation.

I take that to mean that he doesn’t have any interest in experimental breeding of plants.

Still, he’s made me curious.

“What are you doing here? It feels like you’re trying to…talk to the plants around us.”

I am, he confirms. Then a wave of grumpiness pulses along the Bond. But they do not wish to talk to me. These plants are stubborn.

I fight back the smile that tugs at my lips. Given how stubborn he himself has proven to be, it feels like the pot calling the kettle black.

“Stubborn, eh? They don’t think you’ve got anything to offer them?”

I don’t, he tells me bluntly. They are old, these plants.

I glance at the vines Artio has wrapped himself in.

“They don’t exactly look old.”

Sensing my confusion, he clarifies.

Not these vines. Their roots.

“There’s a difference?” I ask with a raised eyebrow.

These vines are new growth, he explains, but they grow from something far older. There is a deeper rootstock beneath them, ancient and extensive. Powerful. I am trying to learn what I can from it, but I am less even than a cub. It does not care about me.

How interesting. Is this some greater body of Nature, just like there’s the greater body of Fire in every instance of fire? Or something more like the vine-stranglers? I hope it’s not the latter.

“How wide-ranging is this root-stock?”

Big, Artio answers unhelpfully. Then, perhaps sensing my exasperation, he continues. It underlies everything we touch.

I blink. That is big.

“And, is it only connected to the vines? Or the plants in general?”

Many plants. The vines, some trees, many bushes. It feeds them and receives information in return.

That is more than a little ominous. And also sounds more like the vine-stranglers than the greater body of fire. It also raises another question in my mind.

“Could this be why many of our Plant Mages are having trouble with the local environment?” My question is more of a rhetorical one than something I was expecting him to answer, but Artio responds nonetheless.

Yes. It controls the area and does not appreciate its sprouts being removed. It is too big to consider us a threat, but it will not give up ground easily, he warns.

“Great,” I sigh. Another problem. “What can we do? Are you able to…take control of it or something?”

Artio gives me an unimpressed look.

“What?” I ask him.

Plants cannot be chained. They respond to what they are given. When given light, they stretch towards it. When given water, they spread their leaves joyfully. When given rich soil or mana, they produce tasty food. They live with, not for.

It’s an interesting point, and one that makes me consider the vine-stranglers. We tried to coexist, but it turned on us in the end. I can’t ignore my own role in that, though. I constrained it without building any sort of positive connection with it. When it found a way to break free it was unsurprising that it lashed out.

“Then that is what you’re trying to do? Give it…friendship or something?”

Yes. I am speaking to it. Trying to convince it that we are natural – that we are part of it, and not a threat. But so far, it has rejected my attempts.

“Well,” I murmur, my mind full of new realisations and questions that I will need time to process. “I hope you manage to convince it that we’re benign because I really don’t want to see what happens if it eventually decides we’re a threat.”

It will take everything back, and more, Artio answers ominously.
