Dusk remained kneeling before the broken statue of Selene, his small hands pressed against the dead stone beneath him. His shoulders trembled, but no sound left his mouth. Tears continued falling from his eyes, striking the ashen ground before fading into nothing.

Above them, the sky cracked again.

The sound was quiet, but wrong. Like glass breaking somewhere far away, echoing back toward them.

Veronica looked up. The fractures spread across the dreamscape, thin white lines cutting through the gray sky. The memory was collapsing. Or perhaps Dusk was.

“Dusk,” Veronica said softly.

He didn’t answer.

She stopped a few steps behind him.

The sanctuary around them was no longer the bright, beautiful place from his memories. The flowers were gone. The strange trees had withered. The stone steps were split and crumbling, and the statue of Selene had been broken in half.

Dead. Cursed. Abandoned. Those were the words that immediately came to mind. It was nothing like before.

“Dusk,” she said again.

His fingers tightened against the stone. “She waited,” he whispered.

Veronica’s chest tightened.

“I told Dawn to wait,” he said, his voice barely audible. “I told her I would come back. That once I found Selene… we’d both come back.”

Veronica stayed quiet.

“But I lied.”

There was no way for her to answer him. No spell or clever explanation she could come up with. No arrangement of words she could find.

“I’m sorry,” she said.

Dusk lowered his head further. “Selene is gone. Dawn is gone. Everyone is gone.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you here?” he asked.

“I’m here to bring you back, Dusk,” she answered. “If you stay in here, you’ll die.”

Dusk shook his head. “No. I don’t mean in here. Why… are you in this Valley?”

“I’ve stayed… in this valley for the past thousand years,” he continued. “I hoped to find some clues about Dawn. Hoping to find anything else. Hoping that those people would come back so I could get answers.”

He clenched his fingers. “But they never did.”

Veronica looked up toward the broken statue. For a moment, she didn’t answer.

Why was she here?

At first thought, the answer was simple. She had come to the Valley of Silence to find Selene’s Tear. A treasure. A power that, in her previous life, had allowed one to become far stronger than they were. That was what she sought.

Power.

But now… now that answer felt wrong. Because while it was the truth, it was only half of it.

“I came here because I have people I want to protect. People I want to save,” she answered. “I came here searching for power left behind. Power that I would need to save the people I care about.”

She stepped forward. “But I can’t just stay still as I watch a child die in front of me. As we speak, that demon is outside feeding on us. We are all slowly dying.”

Dusk didn’t move. His eyes were fixed on the stone beneath him.

“So?”

The words came out empty.

Veronica’s gaze lowered back toward him. “So if you stay here, you’ll die. And if that happens, we might all die.”

“I don’t care,” he said. “I don’t trust humans.”

Veronica’s expression fell. “What about me? Or Gwen or Nasha? Do you not trust us?”

Dusk didn’t respond.

Veronica walked closer until she was beside him. She sat down by his side.

“I’ve seen what happened to you. And… it was terrible,” she admitted. “There are a lot of people you can trust, but also a lot that you can’t. People who will lie and trick you.”

Veronica stared at the broken statue of Selene.

“You don’t actually know what happened to Dawn and Selene though, do you?” she asked. “You heard from the crystal that Dawn might have caused this. But there’s no guarantee that’s what happened, right?”

She felt a subtle shift beside her, but she didn’t move her gaze.

“If you die here… then you’ll never know. Maybe Dawn and Selene are dead. But maybe… they are still alive. Maybe you can find out who did all of this. Maybe you can do something to avenge them.”

Dusk’s voice came out quiet. “I searched.”

Veronica looked over at him. She grabbed his hand in hers.

“For a thousand years,” Veronica said. “I know. But dying here means you are giving up. Giving up without a single clue as to what happened. Where Selene had gone. How exactly Dawn disappeared.”

Veronica’s eyes moved up to the sky above her that was continuing to crack.

Sage. Since this is a mental space, can you create something from my memory?

[Yes, I can.]

Veronica held out her hand, and a doll appeared in her palm. The same one they had found back in the village. The one shrouded with spiritual energy.

She handed it to Dusk.

He took it, holding it in his hands, staring at it.

“If you die here,” Veronica said softly, “their connection dies with you. If the demon finishes feeding on you, we may never know what really happened in the Valley.”

Dusk’s eyes trembled.

“I’m not asking you to forgive humans,” Veronica said. “I’m not asking you to fight for me. I’m asking you to wake up and find the truth. If Dawn is gone, then you deserve to know that from something real. Not from the mouths of the people who stole you.”

“What if I wake up,” he whispered, “and there’s still nothing? No Dawn. No Selene. No answers.”

The cracks in the sky widened. A low rumble passed through the sanctuary, and pieces of the broken platform began to lift away, dissolving into pale dust.

[Warning. Memory fragment is growing unstable.]

Veronica didn’t answer immediately. Because she couldn’t promise him otherwise.

“Then you’ll know,” Veronica said plainly.

Dusk’s eyes shifted toward her.

Her grip tightened around his hand. “And if there’s nothing left, then at least you’ll know the truth. You won’t be trapped here forever, reliving the moment you came back too late.”

Dusk’s lips trembled. He held the doll closer to his chest. “I’m scared,” he whispered.

Veronica’s expression softened slightly. “I know.”

Dusk lowered his head. “I thought if I stayed here long enough, maybe I would understand. Maybe I would find something I missed.”

“And did you?”

He didn’t answer.

Veronica slowly stood, still holding his hand. “I can’t guarantee much. But maybe I can help you.”

“Help?”

“I’ll be one of the strongest humans in history in the future. Perhaps by that time, I can find some clue to the truth of what happened in the Valley. And if all else fails, perhaps I can even ask the Goddess of Stories for a revelation, however difficult that may prove to be.”

The air around them darkened at the edges. The nightmare was collapsing.

“Why… would you help me?”

Veronica’s expression didn’t change. “I know you distrust humans after what happened to you. But not everybody is born bad. Not every person out there is evil.”

She rubbed the top of his head, ruffling his hair. “I’ve met evil spirits before. But you aren’t one of those, are you? You’re a nice kid. You went along with everything those slavers said, all to find your precious Selene.”

[Memory fragment is unstable. I will be forcibly ejecting your consciousness in fifteen seconds.]

She gripped his hand tightly. Her eyes moved to the broken statue one last time.

“Did you know? There’s a common saying among us humans,” she said. “Right now, Selene and Dawn live on in your memory. But the moment they are forgotten… that is when they truly die.”

She looked down at him. “Are you going to let that happen? Are you going to let some filthy demon take the very last thing you have?”

Dusk squeezed the doll in his hand.

The space around them rumbled. Everything turned dark as the memory fragment collapsed. Veronica felt Dusk’s fingers slip from hers as her consciousness was forcibly dragged out.

The bubble of demonic miasma was closing in around them. Roughly forty minutes had passed since Veronica dove into Dusk’s consciousness. During that time, whatever energy he had been emitting had grown substantially weaker. Veronica’s own mana barrier had also shrunken.

And the worst part of it all—

“Keep struggling, mortals.”

The voice was raspy, like nails carving through flesh inside a grain mill.

Nasha kept her bow half-drawn, aura infused into one of her last two remaining arrows. She held it pointed at the demon hovering several meters away in the air. Her eyes were locked onto it, unmoving, ready to shoot at any moment.

“The meals that fight back are so much more delectable.” It stared down at them, its clawed nails reminiscent of thousand-legged insects.

“Come on… Wake up, wake up, wake up!” Gwen shouted, shaking Veronica even harder.

If Veronica didn’t wake up soon, the miasma would reach them within minutes. Not to mention, their bodies all felt slightly sluggish. It wasn’t an overwhelming exhaustion, but enough that none of them would be able to perform at their best. Even now, Nasha found it difficult just to keep the bowstring drawn with a modicum of tension.

“Do you really believe in that human so much, little girl?” the demon said, floating closer.

Gwen looked over, glaring at it.

It was hideous. Both intimidating and terrifying. Just looking at it sent shivers down her spine. Before now, Gwen had always thought demons were mostly myths. That they were probably just… special monsters. But the thing in front of her couldn’t even compare to monsters.

“Once she wakes up, you’re going to regret being born!” Gwen shouted.

The demon cackled. “Demons aren’t born, human,” it replied. It flew higher, raising its claws. Beneath it lagged a dark shadow, an ethereal trailing tail instead of legs. Like a lizard without its hind limbs. “We demons are formed. We are created by the concentration of Vex. An omen to all other creatures.”

It lurched toward them, stopping right behind the barrier Sage was maintaining with Veronica’s mana.

“Gwen! Back up!” Nasha shouted.

She drew her bowstring back to full tension, ready to unleash it at any moment. However, she hadn’t recovered enough energy yet, not with the demon siphoning it away. The moment she loosed her arrow, it would be her last.

Gwen jolted, grabbing Veronica’s and Dusk’s ankles before pulling them back closer to Nasha.

“You humans don’t even know it yet, but your world will be ours soon enough.” Its clawed hand pressed against the barrier. It clenched its fingers, driving its clawed tips into the mana protecting them. The barrier began to shudder, sending several pulses outward.

Then, the slightest cracks began to form. Another. Larger and larger.

It was going to break through.

“I must thank you, morsels, for taking care of that guardian. I’ll offer my gratitude by using your life force for a greater purpose,” it said. “Now come. Use up all of that precious energy you have left!”

“Gwen,” Nasha whispered. “Get ready to grab Veronica and run down the sanctuary. Get to the wagon as quickly as possible. I’ll stall it here.”

“What about Dusk?” Gwen asked, looking between Nasha and the demon.

“Forget Dusk. Veronica is more important,” Nasha said, steely-eyed.

“Are you sure?”

“Yes, I’m sure. Dusk isn’t here to save the world.”

The cracks across the barrier grew larger and larger as the demon pressed forward. And right when the mana shattered into fragments—

“Now!” Nasha yelled.

She loosed her aura-filled arrow. A crack of power erupted from her bow, draining the remainder of her stamina to empower the shot.

The sanctuary exploded with noise. The demon was struck directly by the arrow in a flash of red-orange light, detonating the rest of the barrier around it. The demonic miasma scattered from the shockwave.

Gwen turned, prepared to run back and descend the steps below, until she realized the demonic miasma covered nearly the entire sanctuary. The path behind her was completely smothered. There was no way she could make it.

“Nasha!” Gwen turned around. “There’s too much demonic smoke in—”

It was then that Gwen noticed Nasha on the ground, shoulders heaving as the light dissipated. She was completely spent, drained of the last of her energy, unable to help any more than she already had.

Gwen gritted her teeth, clenching her fingers around her staff.

“Shit, shit…” she murmured.

The miasma in front of them had been cleared, but it was rapidly reforming. She couldn’t spot the demon anymore, but it was likely still there.

Gwen turned back around, raising her staff and beginning to circulate her mana. She didn’t know any specific wind spells, but perhaps creating a funnel of fireballs would be enough to make a temporary path.

That was when a burst of wind shook the sanctuary.

Gwen’s hair flew in front of her face, covering her eyes and ears as the gust pushed against her clothes, nearly toppling her over.

“Hope I’m not too late,” a voice called out behind her.

Gwen turned just in time to see Veronica slowly standing. Her hair was beginning to rise, her signature Exalted Form that Gwen had seen a few times before.

Gwen let go of her ankle. “Veronica! You’re back!”

She nodded. “Yeah. We're back.”

Dusk began to stir awake as well. As he lifted his head, the strange aura permeating from him expanded. It wasn’t necessarily a barrier, but his spiritual energy formed a field large enough to keep the miasma at bay. That, alongside Veronica’s wind magic, cleared out most of the sanctuary.

However, at the very edges, the dark mist could still be seen, propagating once more, trying to expand and consume them again.

Up ahead, the demon was several dozen meters away. It floated high in the air, staring down at them, completely unfazed by Nasha’s earlier attack.

It looked at them with intrigue.

“How interesting. You all managed to break free from the nightmare,” it voiced. It laid its palms out, claws pointed upward. “How wonderful. A show worthy of viewing.”

Veronica stood up, completely enveloped in her Exalted Form, the hum of her mana rings spinning at extreme speeds. She turned her head slightly to the side. “You two stay near Dusk. He can maintain a field strong enough to repel the miasma temporarily.”

Nasha huffed, barely managing a glance back up at her. “Got it…”

“What about you?” Gwen asked. “Are you really planning to fight that thing?”

Veronica straightened her back, taking a few steps forward. “I’m going to have to.”

She looked straight up at the demon. “You! What legion do you serve?” she shouted.

While there were several demon ranks, the demons who could speak and show intelligence were most likely real troops, leaders under the control of the demonic army. And the one in front of her seemed especially fond of theatrics. It simply waited there, staring at them. And when given the chance to kill them, it hadn’t. Instead, it had waited to absorb them.

A gleam crossed the demon’s eyes. It leaned forward slightly, tilting its head. “Hooh. You know about the legions?” It cackled to itself. “How intriguing. Since you asked so politely, I am Hevestra. Demon Centurion of the thirty-fourth corps, under the Third Prince.”

Third prince… so it’s not Xertal, who is the fourth prince.

“What are you doing in this valley?” Veronica asked.

Hevestra moved its clawed fingers, making a scraping noise akin to iron swords grinding against stone. “You should already know that by now, don’t you?”

Its body shuddered before releasing an expansive aura of different energies around it.

Veronica narrowed her eyes.

Demonic energy.

Spiritual energy.

Aura.

Mana.

Sage was right. It was using four different kinds of energy.

All at once.
