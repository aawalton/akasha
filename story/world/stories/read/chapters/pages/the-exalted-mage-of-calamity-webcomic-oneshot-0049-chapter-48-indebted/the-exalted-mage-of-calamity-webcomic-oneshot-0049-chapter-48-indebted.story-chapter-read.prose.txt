Arianne raised a brow. “Dear, you’ll have to be a bit more specific.”

Leopold exhaled slowly. “Everything. The covenant’s name. The death toll. Their stated objectives. Veronica also informed me the apparent leader is someone called Virelya… of the Veil.”

“Veronica,” Arianne repeated. “That was the mage visiting Greystone during your inspection, yes? How would she know of this group when we do not?”

Leopold frowned faintly. “I can’t say for certain. She claimed to have learned it during her travels. My guess is one of the cultists revealed the name during her encounters.”

“I don’t mean to sound suspicious,” Arianne said gently, “but knowing the leader’s name? It all feels… convenient, given what you told me this morning.”

“No,” Leopold admitted, “you’re right to question it. There are gaps that don’t align. During our meeting, she gave the impression of being far more familiar with the group than she admitted. Yet it is equally true that she fought them. The reports from Greystone’s square confirm they were actively trying to kill her.”

A soft silence settled across the chamber.

“If this group is larger than it appears,” Arianne said at last, “I fear what that might mean for the other houses.”

“If they’ve taken root elsewhere?” Leopold asked.

She inclined her head. “We vetted every servant and attendant before hiring them. Each had a clear record. Yet Gram was still influenced by them. We don’t even know when he converted. It was because of him they were able to ambush you—and target Claire. If they operate with that level of patience and secrecy… who can say how many others may be compromised?”

Leopold hummed quietly, absorbing her words.

She was right.

Gram had served them faithfully for over five years. Both trusted and reliable. Yet he had orchestrated the ambush, even directing it toward Claire. He had never shown resentment, never displayed bitterness. If anything, he had been one of the more well-treated servants in the household.

And still, in the end, that meant nothing.

Had he been controlled? Or had betrayal taken root in silence long before they ever suspected it?

There was no way to know now. He had been among those who died in Greystone’s battle.

“It may be wise to inform Viscount Berlam,” Arianne said. “You’ve always been on good terms.”

Leopold nodded. “I’ll send him a letter. As for the other viscounts, I would rather leave that to Manevich’s discretion. Announcing that our household was compromised may not be… prudent.”

A knock sounded at the chamber doors.

Both of them looked up.

A servant entered and bowed deeply. “My lord. My lady. Forgive the interruption.”

Leopold straightened in his chair. “Speak.”

“There is word from City Hall. Someone there is requesting your presence—or that of someone you trust.”

“City Hall?” Leopold repeated. “Who is making the request?”

“She carries a map bearing your personal seal,” the servant replied. “She calls herself Veronica Everwells.”

“Veronica?” Arianne said, turning toward her husband. “Why would she request your presence?”

Leopold’s expression tightened slightly. “I have a feeling I know.” He paused. “Did she say anything further?”

“Yes, my lord. She stated it concerns a false commission issued by a pair of… untrustworthy merchants.”

“So it was them,” Leopold said with a weary sigh. “My son should still be at the magistrate’s office. Send word for him to proceed to City Hall and help resolve the matter. Tell him to be fair—but remind him that Veronica is a guest of Ronswick, and that she saved my life.”

The servant bowed. “Yes, my lord.”

He withdrew without another word.

Arianne watched him go, then turned back to her husband. “What was that about?”

Leopold shook his head lightly. “Two swindlers running a false commission. They caused a disturbance at the gates this morning. I already suspected they were planning something, but Veronica accepted the job before I could have them detained and questioned. I imagine she sensed something amiss as well, so I allowed it.”

He pushed his plate forward. A portion of meat remained untouched.

“For now,” he continued, “I’d prefer to let her act within reasonable bounds. She displayed a level of skill in Greystone that is… uncommon for a mage.”

“Isn’t she only Tier-2?” Arianne asked. “Are you considering making her an asset?”

“No,” he replied smoothly. “She was Tier-2. She advanced to Tier-3 during our return.”

Arianne took a final sip of her drink before setting the glass carefully upon the cloth. “That alone isn’t remarkable. We have Tier-4 adventurers within the city. And Nolhan, despite his age, remains formidable. You said he defeated her in a duel, did you not?”

Leopold shook his head. “You heard what I told you—but you needed to see it yourself. During their bout, she used Tier-3 spells. I saw the wings with my own eyes. And yet… she was still a Tier-2 mage at the time.”

“So?” Arianne said. “Now that she is Tier-3, does that mean she can cast Tier-4 spells? Is that enough to warrant investment?”

He frowned slightly. “Ordinarily—no.”

His voice lowered, and he inclined his head closer so the servants along the walls could not overhear.

“She beat a demonic soldier. Alone. Even if she was casting Tier-3 spells, she would still need significant skill or battle experience. But I suspect she’s not even in her twenties yet. I doubt she has as much experience as Nolhan. It was also obvious to me during their bout that she was holding back. She had also been empty-handed in Greystone from the reports Welterman gave me, so there wasn’t any artifact she had to trivialize the fight.

He paused.

“That leaves only one conclusion.”

Arianne leaned forward. “Which is?”

Leopold’s gaze hardened. “To defeat a demonic soldier, even Nolhan would have to fight at his absolute limit. If Veronica prevailed… then she did so with power comparable to his.”

Arianne’s brow lifted, then slowly lowered as understanding settled in.

“You mean…”

He nodded.

“She isn’t merely casting one tier above her level,” he said quietly. “She is likely capable of casting two tiers above.”

The sun outside had sunk toward the horizon; the sky was left washed in dim amber and deepening blue. Dusk had come; the light fixtures outside flickered to life one by one as mana filled them. Their artificial glow lit up the city.

Near the far edge of the district stood City Hall, a three-story structure of pale stone framed with dark timber beams.

Up on the second floor, the lights to a meeting room were turned on, mana-induced currents running through the chandeliers.

Veronica had been waiting nearly fifteen minutes.

That was after another ten spent relaying her information to both the city clerk and a representative from the guild. Apparently, accusations involving falsified commissions were a far more serious matter than she had realized. Whoever those merchants were, they had stirred considerable trouble—especially now that their scheme had failed.

At last, she heard footsteps ascending the stairs. Heavy, impatiently deliberate steps.

The door opened.

It wasn’t the viscount, but someone else. The man who entered, however, had the same unmistakable air of nobility.

He appeared to be in his early twenties, perhaps no older than twenty-three or twenty-four. His dark hair was neatly combed back, but not overly styled, as some strands had begun to unfold, presumably through a day’s busy work. He wore a buttoned vest with a purple tie; a dark jacket was worn over-top with long sleeves and an outer draped cape.

Whoever it was the viscount sent; they definitely had status.

This works, she thought.

“Are you Veronica Everwells?” he asked. His gaze lingered on her in a way she had grown accustomed to since Greystone—much like the guards who had stared after she received her current attire.

Veronica rose and approached him, extending her hand. “Yes. And you are?”

He accepted her hand in a firm handshake. “Tomas Ronswick, heir to Lord Leopold Ronswick.”

Her brow raised just slightly. “I had no idea the viscount had a son. It’s a pleasure to meet you, Lord Tomas,” she said, returning to her seat.

He took the chair opposite her, adjusting his cape before sitting. “Please—call me Tomas,” he said.

His voice was even, yet slightly out of breath. Perhaps he had come here in a rush.

“I’m told you saved my father’s life and protected Claire from a demon. My family’s savior has no need for formalities.”

“Very well,” she replied.

She had never cared much for formalities to begin with.

“Are you aware of why I am here?” she asked.

“I know only that you arrived regarding a commission issued through the adventurer’s guild,” he replied. “I was told it involved some form of misconduct.”

Veronica hummed softly. “Correct… but not entirely.”

Over the next several minutes, she outlined the events: the commotion at the gates that morning; the merchants attempting entry under suspicious circumstances; her journey to the site with Gwen; and the discovery that the area had been rigged as a trap.

She omitted Nasha’s identity, mentioning only that a traveling mercenary had been caught in the ambush prior to her arrival, and that she had assisted in resolving the situation by eliminating the perpetrators.

“The two men used false identification and poisoned weapons,” she said. “They hid inside the wagon and ambushed a mercenary inspecting the cargo. I buried the bodies to prevent scavengers from disturbing the scene. If your guards find freshly turned soil, that is where they will be.”

Tomas’s expression darkened as she spoke. Each item laid upon the table reinforced her account. Though circumstantial on its own, together the evidence was deeply incriminating: poisoned daggers, falsified identification cards, and a sack of hartivel vine powder with a concealed false layer.

“And these men entered through our gates… and are currently lodging within the city?” he asked.

Veronica nodded. “Your father was being kind. He granted them temporary lodging while I went to deal with the goblins that had attacked their wagon.”

“You brought this directly to City Hall,” he observed. “Instead of the adventurer’s guild.”

“I believed that was best,” she replied. “The commission came from individuals granted temporary admittance to the city. And your father was present when I accepted the request.”

His jaw tightened.

“Their plan was to cause a disturbance and rely on sympathy and benevolence,” he said quietly. “Only to exploit it for their own gain.”

He rose to his feet.

Veronica followed suit.

“Thank you for bringing this to my attention first,” he continued. “I will dispatch the city watch to detain the merchants immediately. A full investigation will follow to verify your claims and ensure justice is served.”

He paused before adding, “This may take several days. The offense is severe—not only against the guild, but against the citizens of Ronswick. To profit from death… and to lie in order to secure lodging within our city…”

“It is disappointing,” Veronica said with a measured sigh, slightly more dramatic than her usual tone. “I had hoped to earn some coin and secure the hartivel vines. Instead, this has consumed my entire day.”

She watched his expression carefully.

“As you may have guessed, I do not come from a noble background. I take on work like this to support myself. I also declined payment from your father and instead chose a reward that would advance my mage tiers when I assisted him back in Greystone.”

Tomas’s expression tightened as he absorbed her words.

“Please do not be distressed, Miss Everwells,” he said quickly. “This occurred while you were under our protection, and our lack of oversight allowed it to happen. Our guards should be verifying requests before allowing anyone to cause such a disturbance. It was only through misfortune—and my father’s generosity—that they were allowed entry to carry out this scheme.”

He paused.

“You have prevented further innocent deaths. You will be appropriately compensated.”

“Compensated?” Veronica repeated.

He nodded.

“If the investigation confirms malicious intent, the merchants will face no less than ten years’ imprisonment. Should it be proven they have profited from murder before, execution will be considered.”

His gaze dropped briefly to the evidence on the table.

“I will use my connections to secure the promised hartivel vines,” he continued. “You were expecting two kilograms, yes? Along with one thousand vix? I will ensure you receive three kilograms and five thousand vix. It is the least we can do to set matters right. And even if you’ve already been rewarded by my father—as his son and heir to Ronswick, I believe I should also reward you myself.”

There we go, Veronica thought.

It was likely that this man already knew what Veronica was pushing for, but that didn't necessarily matter. As long as she could still reap the benefits, it didn't matter if he knew her intentions.

“That much? Are you certain?”

“This should not reflect Ronswick or the manner in which we conduct ourselves,” Tomas replied. “Since you are our guest, it is our responsibility that you were placed in danger. Though I was not present at the gates this morning, there is always room for improvement in our procedures. You should never have been put at risk in the first place. For that, I offer my sincere apologies.”

Veronica gave a light smile. “Please, it’s alright. So long as the matter is resolved and no one else is harmed.”

Tomas inclined his head. “We will make this right. I will inform my father about our meeting. If you provide your place of residence, I will send a messenger once the investigation is complete.”

Veronica nodded and gave him the address of the inn where she intended to stay.

“I see,” he said, noting it in a small book drawn from his pocket.

He closed the book, but before he could rise, Veronica spoke again.

“One more thing.”

Tomas paused, brows lifting slightly. “Yes?”

She met his gaze, her expression serious now. “I will need assistance securing official identification records. Not just within Ronswick, but for Vitian.”

The problem of her identity still existed. The fact that Hugh from the magic tower was surprised at her mana cores told her that her past-self might not exist. In the original timeline, she should have been inside Annesheim, studying. Though secluded, her name should still have spread throughout all of Vitian. A mage who survived Medusa’s curse, a rare birth condition that supplied two mana cores—she had become Vitian’s top prodigy.

A commoner might not have known of her—but a person from the magic tower definitely would.

This had always bugged her, but there was no way to even start working toward the answer besides heading to Annesheim herself. At least now, it confirmed she didn’t exist. Although that was based on circumstantial evidence at best. There would’ve been major problems if she simply disappeared from Annesheim, or if there were two copies of her in the world.

He blinked. “Vitian?” he repeated. “You may begin that process here at City Hall. Speak to one of the clerks downstairs. Vitian-wide records will take longer to process, but it is routine.”

“That is precisely why I wish to ask for a favor from your father,” Veronica said evenly. “My situation is… complicated.”

Tomas studied her more carefully now. After a moment, he asked slowly, “Are you asking for a new identity? One different from your own?”

Veronica’s gaze did not waver. “No.”

He had essentially asked if she wanted forged records. In truth, what she sought brushed dangerously close to that—but not entirely.

“I am not seeking to become someone else,” she continued. “I am who I claim to be. I can provide my birthplace. I can provide my parents’ names. Everything I can provide is real. What I require is your father’s authority to ensure the matter proceeds without excessive scrutiny.”

Silence lingered between them.

“And may I ask why such scrutiny would be… problematic?” Tomas pressed. “If this involves something contrary to Vitian law—such as allegiance to another nation—then I fear even my father’s hands would be tied.”

Veronica shook her head. “I am nothing of the sort.”

She brushed her hair back, revealing her ear.

“I am human, but I have some dark Elven blood in me. I was born in Erldrengar, which, as you know, is a neutral nation. I am not a spy, nor am I here with ill intent. I have Vitian’s bests interests in mind, and I can even recite all 87 national laws from memory.” She paused, then added calmly, “My past circumstances are simply a bit… unique. I would prefer they remain private.”

Her tone was steady, but not defensive.

Tomas held her gaze for several long seconds. Finally, he inclined his head. “Very well. I will pass your request to my father. I cannot guarantee the outcome.”

“Just letting him know is enough for me,” she replied.

“Then we will conclude our meeting for today, Miss Everwells.”

He extended his hand.

She accepted it for a final handshake. “Likewise, Tomas. And thank you.”

Veronica left the meeting room shortly afterward. Tomas remained behind to collect the evidence and issue instructions to the City Hall staff.

Whatever came next no longer concerned her.

She walked through the hall and went downstairs. The lobby was emptier now.

When she stepped outside, the sky had darkened fully, the last traces of dusk swallowed by night.

A meal, a bath, and sleep sounded appealing.

“I think tomorrow,” she murmured to herself as she strolled toward the nearest diner, “it’s time to find Galland.”
