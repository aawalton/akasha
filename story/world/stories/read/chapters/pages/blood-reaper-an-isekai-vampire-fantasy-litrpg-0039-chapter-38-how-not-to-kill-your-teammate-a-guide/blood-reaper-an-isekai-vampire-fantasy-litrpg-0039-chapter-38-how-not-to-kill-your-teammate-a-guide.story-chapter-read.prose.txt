The wind whistled across the Highland plains, carrying the distant cries of birds—or maybe something bigger—from deep within the Gloomvale Woods. The perpetual twilight cast long shadows from the jagged rocks Ace had arranged in a rough semicircle for the team's training.

Ace cast a sidelong glance back toward the outcropping where Tara practiced, and a pulse of red light engulfed the towering rocks.

She was making impressive progress.

Good.

Overhead, Olivia balanced gracefully atop the tallest boulder, her slender form silhouetted against the purple-tinged sky. Her movements flowed like water as she practiced her forms, each twist and strike precise despite the uneven surface beneath her feet.

“Need any help?” Ace shouted to her.

The Blood Dancer spun in a full circle, her staff spinning above her head, and she landed  on one foot. Her other leg stretched outward for balance. One palm rested on the top of the boulder, while the other held her staff aloft.

“Not really,” she admitted.

Fair enough.

“We’ll work on hand signals later,” Ace told her.

“That’s fine.”

He gestured for her to continue, and she launched into another form.

Of everyone on the team, Olivia seemed to be adapting the quickest to her new life—and it made him wonder just who she was before this place had swallowed her whole.

It was a mystery he would have to solve later.

“Rachel, ow,” Marcus snapped again.

“I’m sorry!” the analyst said with a shrug. “I can’t control it!”

With a shake of his head, Ace shifted his attention to the two team members who concerned him most. He approached them both, his boots crunching on the rocky soil. Neither possessed combat training or physical strength. In a world where raw power often determined survival, their more cerebral abilities put them at increased risk.

But both had powers beyond what any of the rest of the team could do—including him—and they just needed to see that for themselves.

“I just can’t get it,” Rachel said with a frustrated whimper.

“You will,” Ace said calmly.

“Alright, Sergeant,” Marcus said with a wry grin as he rubbed his hands together. “Boss us around. Make us badass or whatever.”

“That’s…” Ace chuckled as he tried to find the words. “That’s not how this works, Marcus.”

“Sure it is.” Marcus feigned a karate chop as he balanced on one foot. "It’s training montage time. Rocky style. Please tell me we get cool background music. Every good stream needs a soundtrack.”

"That’s not… just… no.” Ace laughed and gestured for them to move to the center of the semicircle of rocks he’d set up as makeshift practice dummies. "C’mon. I want to see what you two can really do."

Rachel's head snapped up. "Us two? I don't—" She swallowed hard. "Combat isn't really my—"

"That's exactly the problem," Ace interrupted. "And it's going to get you killed."

"Harsh, dude," Marcus whispered, his smile faltering.

"It’s true," Ace said with an apologetic shrug. He shifted his full attention to Rachel and paused, trying to choose his words wisely so as to not entirely dishearten her. "We've been fighting as individuals with Tara keeping us alive. That's not enough anymore. Not with what's ahead of us."

“Easy for you to say,” Marcus said with a gesture toward Ace’s armored body. “You’re built like a tank. You’re a fucking Marine, man. We’re just people.”

“No, you’re not.” Ace raised one eyebrow in challenge. “You’re vampires. Marcus, you’re a fucking telepath. Based on what you told us about your Skills, I don’t think you’ve even scratched the surface of what you’re capable of doing.”

“I… I guess.” The man frowned and stuck his hands in his pockets before kicking a small rock down the hillside.

“Look,” Ace continued. “You remember the Carnage Fiend? You two worked together to do something amazing.”

Rachel scoffed. “That was an accident.”

“No, it was a contribution,” Ace corrected. “You contributed to the fight then, and you can do it again. I want you to be able to do that on command.”

The two of them glanced nervously at each other, but neither said anything this time.

Ace stood a little taller and took a steadying breath. He couldn’t act like a Sergeant with these two. They were civilians who needed a gentle, guiding hand rather than an asshole shouting at them.

“You’re capable,” he told them. “Both of you are. You can do so much more than you have thus far. You both have abilities that could turn the tide in a real fight, but you're not using them to their potential.”

“What do you mean?” Marcus asked.

“For starters, Marcus, your Mind Drinker abilities could cripple half a squad before they knew what hit them, but you use them like party tricks. And Rachel—"

"I know, I know,” she interrupted. “I'm a liability."

For a moment, Ace just looked at her in stunned silence.

Rachel held herself tightly, her gaze drifting along the ground as she fought back tears. The edge of her mouth trembled, and she swallowed hard.

“No,” Ace said flatly.

Her brows knit together, and she looked up at him in confusion. “But I’m so… plain compared to the rest of you.”

“I don’t think so," Ace replied, softening his tone slightly. "You see patterns nobody else does. Your Hunter's Resonance could identify critical weaknesses in seconds, but you freeze up when it's time to act on that information."

Rachel looked down at the ground and wrung her hands together. "Analysis paralysis," she murmured. “I can’t control it.”

“Then today we fix that," Ace said firmly. "Both of you. Together."

Marcus raised an eyebrow. "What, you think Rachel and I can suddenly power up because we pissed off a forest Ent once?”

“Basically,” Ace said with a shrug. "That fight proved to me that your abilities complement each other. Mind and data. Perception and analysis. I want to see what happens when you coordinate."

Rachel's eyes narrowed. "This is an experiment."

Ace nodded.

For a moment, silence fell over the clearing. Then Marcus stepped forward with a theatrical bow toward Rachel. "Well then, shall we dance, my lady of the spreadsheets?"

Rachel didn't smile, but something in her posture shifted—a barely perceptible straightening of her spine, coupled with a subtle tilt of her chin. "If we're doing this, we should establish clear operational parameters."

Ace suppressed a smile. They were at least willing to try. That was a start.

“Here’s the drill,” he said. “First, I want you both to summon those orbs you used against the Carnage Fiend. Do it independently, without letting them touch.”

“Here goes nothing,” Marcus said under his breath.

Their telepath closed his eyes. His brow furrowed as he concentrated, and deep lines etched across his forehead as he turned inward. The muscles in his jaw tensed, pulling the skin taut over his cheekbones.

For several moments, nothing happened.

With a flash of light, wisps of violet energy snapped suddenly along his fingertips. The lavender tendrils coiled into a churning mist that rolled across his hands like dry ice. The ethereal haze thickened around his hands, condensing into a pulsating orb that hovered inches above his palms. The orb's glow cast Marcus's face in an eerie light, hollowing his cheeks and turning his eyes into shadowed sockets when he finally opened them. The power pulsed with uncertain rhythm, occasionally sending small arcs of violet electricity skittering across his knuckles.

Ten feet away, meanwhile, Rachel took a nervous breath and shook out her hands. Anxiously, her fingers curled inward. Her shoulders hunched forward as if protecting something fragile within her chest. Blue light flickered between her clenched hands—first just a spark, like a firefly's dying glow. She muttered incoherently under her breath, and with each passing second, the pulsing light grew steadier.

Eventually, her magic pooled in her cupped hands like liquid starlight. It cast a cool glow across the hillside. As her mumbling grew more confident, the light solidified into a perfect sphere that rotated slowly. Around it, a translucent screen materialized—a vertical pane of data hanging in the air beside her head.

Ace couldn’t read it, but her eyes went wide with wonder.

The forest around them seemed to hold its breath. Leaves stopped their rustling, and the flutter of feathers in the trees fell silent. Only the soft electrical hum of power building between these two unlikely partners filled the air.

"Good," Ace said, keeping his voice low and even. "Now, I want you to gradually expand your magic. Don’t take any steps, but use your Skills to expand your circle of influence until the two orbs touch. Stop when you feel the energies start to interact."

Rachel frowned. “Uh…”

“Yeah, Ace, I don’t know,” Marcus admitted, his gaze flicking between the sergeant and the glowing orb in his palms. “That didn’t go super well last time.”

“You didn’t have the space to learn how to control it,” Ace pointed out. “Now, you do.”

Yet again, the two of them glanced at each other anxiously before they ultimately nodded in reluctant agreement.

Inch by inch, the spheres grew. Rachel stopped breathing altogether as she funneled her full focus into the growing orb, while Marcus grinned more and more with each passing second. Their magic crackled and expanded, perfectly controlled this time.

When the two orbs were roughly a foot apart, a violent pulse shot through the air from the space between them. It buffeted Ace like wind from a storm, and he raised one forearm to shield his eyes.

“Whoa!” Marcus said, laughing with sheer childlike delight. “That was awesome!”

“Yeah,” Rachel agreed, a smile tugging at her features as she stared at the orb she was controlling. “Yeah, that was kinda neat.”

“Kinda?!” Marcus replied. “I can feel it resisting. If I could just… maybe we can…”

Though his voice trailed off, Marcus leaned into the orb of energy filling his palms. He tilted slightly toward Rachel, as though trying to force a ball to roll uphill. The longer he pushed, however, the more violently the sparks in his palms fizzled and popped. The orb flickered, and he gritted his teeth from the raw effort of controlling the unstable sphere.

"I can feel it pushing against me,” he said.

"It's like... magnetic resistance," Rachel whispered, her analytical screen wavering. "The data patterns are trying to synchronize."

"Good," Ace said. "Now, instead of fighting it, I want you to guide the energies together. Deliberately."

Marcus frowned. “Boss, what does it look like I’m doing?”

“Resisting,” Ace pointed out with a gesture toward Marcus’s stiff posture. “Think about what it was like back in the forest. You weren’t trying to force anything. Maybe that’s key to better control.”

“I… I mean, I guess.” With an anxious breath, Marcus relaxed his form.

Instantly, the orb in his palm rippled with light. Sparks danced through it again, brighter this time, and it instantly expanded wide enough to touch the blue ball of light radiating from Rachel’s hands.

“Oh, wow,” she said, her eyes wide with awe.

“This isn’t going to blow up, right?” Marcus asked.

“It might,” Ace said.

In unison, Marcus and Rachel glared at Ace.

“Hey, if it does, lesson learned.” Ace chuckled and took a step backward, out of range.

“Cute,” Marcus said under his breath.

“Here goes nothing,” Rachel said.

They extended their arms toward each other. The purple haze and blue light stretched across the gap, tendrils of energy reaching toward each other like curious fingers. Where they met, the colors swirled and merged, engulfing the two of them into a single giant orb of light.

"Holy shit!” Marcus said breathlessly, his eyes wide with wonder.

Rachel's analytical screen expanded, large enough to encompass both energies now. "Your psychic pulses are amplifying my data collection. I'm getting readings I couldn't access before."

"And your analysis is focusing my output," Marcus said, his voice stronger now. "I can feel my control... sharpening."

The combined energy formed a spiral between them, growing more stable with each passing second. What had been two separate, faltering powers was becoming something new—something controlled.

"Now," Ace said, "let's see what happens when you aim it."

He pointed at the nearest rock he’d set up, about twenty feet away. Rachel and Marcus pivoted toward it in unison. A shimmering spiral shot out of the orb like a water spout, enveloping the stone in a cocoon of light.

At first, nothing happened.

With a sudden rush of light, cracks appeared in the rock’s surface. They spread like lightning across weathered wood. It didn't explode or disintegrate—instead, it launched backward, as if it had been kicked fifty feet away in a sudden, violent rush.

Marcus burst out laughing, his focus breaking momentarily as he whooped in joy.

“Holy shit!” he shouted. “That was amazing!”

Rachel smiled—a small, fragile thing, but genuine nonetheless. "I think I can work with this."

As quickly as it had come, however, her smile slowly faltered.

“What’s wrong?” Marcus asked, still elated. “Didn’t you see how badass that was?”

“It’s just…” Rachel stared once more at the ground, her eyes glossing over as she struggled to find the words. “I can do that here, when I have the space to think. But once we’re in a fight… once something tries to kill me… I’m just going to freeze like I always do.”

Marcus relaxed, the excitement leaving his eyes, and he glanced at Ace.

“Look, Rachel,” Ace said softly. “I know this isn't easy. For either of you. But I've watched you both in action, and I'm telling you—there's potential here."

“I’m an executive, Ace, not a fighter,” Rachel swallowed hard. “I’m just going to get you guys hurt. Or worse.”

“You made it this far,” Ace pointed out.

She blinked once in surprise and tilted her head back until she could look him in the eye.

“We’ve fought wolves,” he pointed out. “Fleshstitchers. A Carnage Fiend. The Forgeborn. You survived all of that, Rachel.”

“Barely. I’m only here because you guys looked out for me.”

“True,” Ace admitted. “But it’s not like we dragged you through the battlefield. Imagine how much you can do once you get out of your own way.”

Rachel looked up, surprise flashing across her face, but she ultimately smiled in gratitude.

A moment of silence fell between them as his words sank in. The twilight deepened around them, shadows growing longer with each passing minute.

"So what do you want us to do differently?" Rachel finally asked.

"Talk to each other," Ace replied simply. "Stop thinking of yourselves as separate pieces and start functioning as a unit. Rachel, you see patterns better than anyone. Marcus, you understand how to leverage emotional responses, and you can do more with that brain of yours than you give yourself credit for. Put those together."

Marcus pivoted toward Rachel, and the two of them shrugged in unison.

“Worth a shot,” the telepath said.

For the first time since their transformation, Ace saw something in them beyond fear and forced cooperation.

He saw hope.

Ace nodded, not bothering to mask the hint of pride that shot through him as he studied the two of them. “Let’s try again.”
