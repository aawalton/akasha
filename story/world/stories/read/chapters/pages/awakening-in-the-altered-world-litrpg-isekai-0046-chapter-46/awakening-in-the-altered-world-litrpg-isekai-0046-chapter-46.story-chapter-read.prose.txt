Two Mana Spears CRACK’d against the Frigid Knight, shattering, scarring the ice in its chest and knocking it slightly off its balance.

After the two PING’s! Alex had just heard, he was gratified to see his fake Spears were back to being rather potent weapons.

“Now!” he cried.

Beckett added a hellacious fire-lightning lance, staggering the Boss.

Force surged across Alex’s posterior chain. Force he could now infuse into his body in several-second bursts, as opposed to the instants he’d managed before, and a few feet away Lightning sparked across Beckett’s entire frame, infusing his muscles; his pupils flickered Lightning-white once more.

Beckett cried out and leaped, and as he did, his speartip was wreathed in so much mana Alex could tell he was super-charging Lightning Spear—likely to the maximum degree possible for him.

And Alex willed his Force to infuse his muscles as highly as he’d ever tried, as Swordforce slicked the edge of his blade, a blade fully cleared of ice mana.

At the apex of his leap, the Lightning in Beckett’s upper body peaked, and he thrust.

The spear shattered what was left of the Frigid Knight’s light armor, and pierced deep into its chest, diagonally downward—a golden Lightning-filled line.

The Knight froze as foreign Lightning surged through it, even as Alex surged the Force in his legs, and slashed upward.

A bright-blue blade of Swordforce carved clean across the Frigid Knight’s neck, parallel and higher than the gold.

In that instant, seeing it all, with the sunlight cascading and sparkling over the decapitated, statuesque boss, the two of them pincering it like this—the image was seared in Alex’s mind.

Across the Frigid Knight, Alex saw Beckett’s breathless face, excited but also relieved.

“Alex—” said Beckett; his face froze in a half-grin.

The instant passed.

The Frigid Knight’s decapitated body sharply brightened; at the same time the bodies of its minions brightened as well, and countless seams CRACK’d! across all their lengths—

The Knight, and its minions, all shattered at once.

Alex’s eyes were very wide; shit! He barely had time to Shield, twice—

CRSHH-CRSHH!

His Shields shattered.

And after, dozens of shards of very potent ice surged for the two, from all directions. A few were four feet long, like giant pieces of shattered glass.

Alex had time to inspect them because they slowed.

Two golden domes flickered around Alex and Beckett, and the shards slowed as they passed through, wrapped in that golden hue. Is that—Light mana?

Alex and Beckett blinked; Beckett especially, a four-foot-long shard flew toward him in slow-motion, wrapped in that light, but fading fast; it barely grazed his chest before it dissolved in gold light.

The two of them blinked again, and a third person was standing between them, arms crossed.

“Claire!” said Beckett, with a relieved grin, until he saw Clarissa’s glare.

“Of all the stupid—” she began.

Beckett cried out and collapsed, coughing blood.

“Shit!” Clarissa caught him, and her glare instantly vanished like it was a mask she’d put on, replaced with genuine concern.

She produced a Health potion from her ring, a seemingly more potent one than what Beckett had used. “Start cycling,” she ordered. “It’ll heal your body but it can’t do it half as well if there’s still foreign mana. And you.”

She turned to Alex, who almost flinched.

He was slightly concerned, but to his surprise, she grabbed another Health Potion and said, “Drink.”

Alex was about to protest a bit, almost reflexively—like, he’d probably be fine within half an hour of Pure cycling; he didn’t want to owe anyone—except he saw Clarissa’s face.

He obediently drank.

Whatever initial anger she’d entered with seemed to have softened after she took in the state of the two of them; she still said, “Unbelievable,” but her heart didn’t seem in it as much.

She allowed the two of them a few minutes to recover and cycle. In that time, Clarissa’s other charges also walked over; she stood there, arms crossed, and no-one there seemed like they wanted to say a word.

When she finally spoke—“You’re so raw,” she said to Beckett. “Can you get that? You aren’t who you think you are! You’re just so—”

Beckett blinked, seeming bemused.

Clarissa made a frustrated noise—“God, I sound like my dad.”

She looked away for a little, turned back, and turned her glare back on Beckett, as if now she was sure he could take it. “Let’s get something straight,” she said. “If I wasn’t here, you’d be dead. Pierced through the heart.”

Beckett began saying something, but she cut him off. “And no, you couldn’t have taken it, because the ice hasn’t seen the likes of you before, or whatever idiocy you’re about to say. You think you’re so special. I’ve seen a thousand people just like you. The ones that make it anywhere have the sense to learn. You aren’t—”

“Claire,” said Beckett, voice thick. “I get it.”

Beckett’s eyes were slightly watery; he grinned weakly. “Claire, it beat the shit out of me. It laid me out, and it was less than a second away from taking my head off. Trust me, I get it.”

Alex was a little surprised, if only because Beckett had seemed almost impervious up until this point, even when things were life-and-death. It was pretty difficult to actually get through to him.

Except a few images flashed through Alex’s mind—he recalled Beckett’s surprise when he felt the Frigid Knight up-close, the way it ragdolled him, Beckett’s face changing as it did—almost like, all his instincts were working out until then, and then it was all violently stopped.

He recalled Beckett cringing as it raised the blade, and the anguish and the rage in his stricken face as he got back up.

Alex winced. Beckett was still smiling, but—It makes sense it’d make a deeper impression.

Clarissa seemed to have been ready for a much bigger fight; Beckett seemed to have caught her off-guard as well.

“Well—that’s—we’re fine, then,” she said. “As long as you realize you—we—we’re all very raw, and we’ll all have a lot of growing to do before—well, before we’re there. Drink up.”

She handed Beckett a second improved Health potion, turned to Alex.

“We’ll be here a while,” she said. “At least half an hour, depending on how his cycling is.”

She eyed Beckett’s body critically. “And that’s just to get him to the point where he’ll be able to walk. If you’re feeling a little better, you can check out the chest. It’s yours.”

She nodded across the clearing, where Alex noticed a large chest bound in glimmering silver—he blinked. Was that always there?

“Give me a minute,” said Alex, feeling a little uncertain. He wasn’t 100% sure what his relationship to this Echelon team, and Clarissa, was right now, but she seemed to have decided she was treating him as yet another charge.

“Okay,” she said, and went to address her two rather pale Guild candidates—Alice and Jason, Alex recalled.

“So,” said Beckett, after a pause. “That… sure was something.”

Alex agreed. He frowned—“Are you sure you’re okay?”

“Yeah,” said Beckett; his grin was still weak. “Well, physically—I’ll be just fine. How’s your notifications?”

For the first time since the fight concluded, Alex checked; he kind of already knew, but he was still curious about a few aspects, especially relating to his Skill’s new rarity grade. At least—he assumed that was what he’d experienced.

Skill evolved!

Mana Manipulation [Uncommon] (2/3 -> 3/3)

Allows the user to manipulate claimed mana in accordance with his affinities, structuring all mana the soul manipulates in mana-perfect form.

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Pure mana (2), including slight proficiency in hard constructs (1).

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Force mana (1), including slight proficiency in Swordforce mana (1 -> 2).

Clearing ice off my sword all rift definitely helped there.

Alex also suspected the instant he’d first seen the Knight’s ice arc, and that initial shock of insight, also helped.

Skill evolved!

Skill upgraded!

Mana Manipulation [Uncommon] (3/3)

Allows the user to manipulate claimed mana in accordance with his affinities, structuring all mana the soul manipulates in mana-perfect form.

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Pure mana (2), including slight proficiency in hard constructs (1).

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Force mana (1), including slight proficiency in Swordforce mana (2).

->

Mana Manipulation [Rare] (1/6)

Allows the user to manipulate claimed mana in accordance with his affinities, structuring all mana the soul manipulates in mana-perfect form.

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Pure mana (2), including slight proficiency in hard constructs (1).

This Skill has achieved slight proficiency in Force mana (2), including slight proficiency in Swordforce mana (2).

Yep, sure enough.

His first Rare rarity Skill, accompanied by a surprisingly big across-the-board increases in all things Mana Manipulating.

If Common was 3 supports per grade, Uncommon was also 3, and Rare was 6—would it keep going like that, doubling every two grades? 6 at Epic, 12 at Arcane?

And I think—either Arcane or Legendary’s the highest anyone’s managed to get, so far?

Alex was kind of shocked he was already at rare. But he was almost certain things were supposed to slow down quite a bit as you went up the grades, and not simply because you needed more supports to upgrade.

He was also pretty sure you weren’t really supposed to be at this rarity of Mana Manipulation at Level 5. The situations he’d been put in in this rift had accelerated things quite a bit, but he also wasn’t sure he wanted to cut it quite this close, at least on a regular basis.

“Beckett?” said Alex.

“What’s up?”

“I get what Clarissa said,” said Alex. “And she is right, to be clear.”

Alex smiled. “But you have to admit, four breakthroughs in a single fight’s still pretty sick.”

Which got a genuine, if small smile out of Beckett. “I can’t believe we just did that—I can’t believe you’re at Rare!”

Alex confirmed it.

“I should unbox some random slight support, and beat you to the next one,” said Beckett. Alex was glad to see he was getting a little of his playfulness back.

“Yeah, well—the more distinct supports you have, the harder it is to establish new ones. So I’d be careful with that,” Alex joked. He walked a few steps, glanced back at Beckett—“‘Suppose I’ll be the one to pick this thing up?”

“Sure.”

Alex picked up the giant icy sword, and stashed it in his Interspatial Ring, which was getting a little full. I’ll have to do a sell-off, probably right after this?

And also buy some actually decent gear, because fuck if I’m going into that Rising Stars cup thing in—what, three days?—with a plain sword and a freaking bracer.

Before that, a final bit of looting.

Alex walked over to the giant final chest, injected it with mana, and it opened.

His eyes widened a fraction.

Because within lay a handful of essence stones, arranged on pillows, as well as a crystal a little smaller than his fist, with a single, very intricate sigil of brilliant mana inscribed within.

It was a Skill Shard.
