Magic was incredibly useful.  Despite my inability to find basic, useful spells before I needed them, and my excessive fondness for summoning barnacles, it had become a potent element in my arsenal of tricks.  Unfortunately…  The entire city was covered by a web of detection systems that were monitored by mages who were probably largely skiving off to read naughty magazines whenever they got the chance.  However, there was a slim chance one of those bastards would be on the ball in the event I cast a spell.

Every mage, wizard, shaman, whatever name they preferred, had a unique magical signature, and I was damn sure mine had been fed into whatever magical version of Norton they were running over the city.  I couldn’t use it.  Being the number one most wanted dragon in the empire had been flattering in its way, but now it was an inconvenience.

As the guard limped painfully towards the counter, my eyes watered in sympathy.  Poor guy was just doing his job.  Getting belted in the stones wasn’t covered by most contracts, and I doubted he’d get any sympathy from his supervisor.

“Guys, he’s going for an alarm,” I hissed.

“You need to stop him!” said Pocket.  At the same time, Lughole scowled and waved his hand in a “shut the hell up” gesture.

The dial the safe cracker was fondling so intently continued to spin and turn on multiple axes.  He closed his eyes and put his free hand over his ear so only the one pressed against the metal could hear anything.

“His balls are slowing him down, we’ve got maybe—never mind, there was an alarm on the other side of the desk,” I said.

A shrieking noise rang out, causing Lughole to flinch and cry out in pain.

“Someone get the man some earmuffs,” Burglar ordered.  “Bob, you and me are going to either side of the vault.  The rest of you, clump up in the middle and cover Lugs.”

“We’ve got some time, surely?  They can’t… oh wait.”

Portals, similar to my own, appeared around us, but these were pink.  Out of them spilled a number of heavily armoured and irate-looking gentlemen.

“Guys, can we discuss this?” I asked, unable to suppress the innate optimism that always ran through my heart.  In my mind, Wrath was thrashing around and increasing in size.  How dare these mammals interfere with my completely legitimate enterprise?  There was no theft involved, only… what was it that Pocket had said?  An involuntary reallocation of resources.  That was it, for the good of the community. Yuh huh.

A polearm was levelled in my direction, the amalgamation of axe and ice pick topped with a glowing metal spike, then driven towards me as the man charged.  I dodged to the side and grabbed his head in one hand.  Using a man as a bludgeoning weapon seemed so demeaning to me; I’d rather have died than suffer that indignity, but this chap seemed happy enough with the situation.

I swung him wide, bowling his colleagues backwards.  There was an unpleasant snapping sound as his body hit the wall next to me, and I dropped my now limp weapon.  He was probably not quite so happy as he might have seemed from my perspective, judging from the pained moan that escaped his lips.

“Any of you pricks move, and I’ll… try very hard not to kill you?”  I ought to have got a bunch of levels on the barge trip down to the city.  Explosively converting my victims to poop was not a good idea in an enclosed space like this.

No kill notification appeared, and I stopped myself as I opened my mouth to throw him a heal to be on the safe side.  Magic, dammit!  “Don’t bleed out!” I muttered as his friends recovered and charged me.

One breath was all it would take.  Not a breath, one burst of acid-green and fake-tan-orange flame would clear the corridor, leaving me free to turn and help Burglar, who from the sounds of it was not having a good time behind me.  He would have a lot worse time if I killed a bunch of high-level fighters and blew the resulting biomass out the back of my ninja pyjamas, though.

I’d never been a dancer.  Certainly not back home, and any dancing I’d done with Esme since arriving on Helstat had largely involved frantically trying to distract my mind with mental images of Margaret Thatcher naked on a cold day.  Right now, though, I flowed.  My arms swept out gracefully to intercept cudgels or swords; a bladed edge didn’t worry my armour stat.

A twist and a turn let me pass between the first two before I snatched them up by their metal trousers and tossed them back behind me.  Something slammed into the back of my head, and I won’t lie, it bloody hurt.  It didn’t stop me from backhanding whoever it was away as I turned, sending them flying back into their comrades.

Risking a glance at Burgar, it turned out the local Ronald McDonalds were getting the better of him.  Pocket was ducking and weaving among the baddies.  But all she seemed able to do was swap their tiems of armour and clothing around.

Suddenly finding oneself wearing trousers three sizes too small would be a bit of an inconvenience, and while she made the most of her ability, it wasn’t turning the tide in the burly man’s favour.  We needed something more.

“Swap their tops with their bottoms!” I called as a sword sliced through my left sleeve.  I punched the perpetrator in the face, hard enough that blood splattered, and I worried about nose bones entering brain matter.

This had been intended as a clean job.  No fighting, no killing.  The mob outside of moderately well-paid street walkers had been meant to keep the cops distracted long enough for us to do the deed and escape.

My fist caught another warrior in the side of her head, and she did a flip before landing on her noggin in a way that looked terminal to my unprofessional eyes.  There wasn’t a kill notification, so I was still good.

I exchanged a flurry of blows without making a “your mum” joke with two attackers, then rolled backwards to dodge a spear aiming to take out my eye.  As I staggered to my feet, I bumped into Lughole, who cursed and then let out a yelp of joy.

All the fighting stopped as the heavy door began to swing open.

“Into the vault!” Pocket yelled, following her own advice.

Holding the gap, I let the others retreat first, then stood between the door and the wall like a grumpy dragon who’d been stabbed in the guts once too many times today.  The bad guys weren’t extending me the same non-lethal courtesy that I was giving to them.

Particlics dragged Burglar through, the fighter's face was smeared with crimson, and his breathing was ragged.

“He needs healing!” Particlics said as one of the attackers stabbed me in the liver.

“I need bloody healing!” I growled.

“Kill them!” snapped Pockets.  “You know you can!”

“I’m a bit full up on biomass at the moment,” I muttered.

“What does that even mean?  Kill them, or we’re fucked!” Burglar groaned from the ground.

“I’ll get too full!”  I caught a spear and squeezed my fist to snap the head off, then threw it back out of the opening, earning a satisfying yelp of pain from the mass outside.  “Close the door!”

“Kill them!” the rest yelled at me.

“You’re all behind me!  You don’t want me to get a load of kills right now!”

“Just do it!”

“You’re not my mum!” I yelled.

I needed an alternative.  Five people crammed into a surprisingly large, but unventilated room was fine, assuming I didn’t take a ton's worth of a dump right by the only access we had to fresh air.  Wrath was screaming at me to burn them all, turn them into charcoal, but Greed had noticed something from the corner of my eye that I hadn’t registered.

The goblin in my mind began frantically assaulting my sanity, and my head turned slowly.  The sight was so beautiful.  And it was all mine for the taking.

Shelves marched into the distance, loaded with gold, gems, ornate weapons, armour and everything a young dragon might lust after.

“They should have sent a poet,” I mumbled, catching a mace in the kidney due to my distraction.  I shoved back, pushing the enemy away.  “You lot need to hold the door.  I’ve got shinies to redistribute.”

“Bob, no!”  Lughole shouted, throwing a hand towards the open doorway.  Metal objects flew from his cuff, but I wasn’t paying attention.

It was a perfect insult.  So many gleaming things that didn’t belong to me.  They should be mine; it was my right.

A spear struck me in the back as I walked out into the racks and rows of golden delight.  It didn’t matter.  I waved a hand, and a swath of wealth vanished into my possum pouch.  That skill had been invaluable on any number of occasions, but a certainty settled in my mind that this moment was why IMPS had been gracious enough to gift it to me.

“Thank you, IMPS,” I said softly as more baubles vanished into my private dimension.

“Bob, you asshole!” Burglar snapped.  “Close the door before Lug’s trinkets expire!”

Grunts of pain, frantic orders, howls of anger… none of it meant a thing to me.  So many shelves, so many piles of gold.  I picked up a piece of paper that had “Bearers Bond” written in fancy lettering at the top.  I started loading up on paper as well.

Greed was doing stuff inside my mind that was too unspeakable to comprehend.  I doubted Wrath or Vanity would ever be the same again.

“Bob! Heal us!”

I couldn’t say for sure who’d called out.  It didn’t matter.  Nothing did.

“Fucking dragons!”

Another wave of my hand, and more wealth disappeared from the world.

“Mine.  All mine,” I whispered.

“We fucked up!  He’s insane!”

That might have been Pocket?  Her voice sounded strained, but I’d only gone down one row; there was so much more to ste—reallocate.  I started laughing.  It started low, in my belly, but it quickly became a full-body laugh, my torso shaking as more and more wealth was sucked into my storage space.

“Now this is life!  This is living!  You guys have never slept on gold!”  The madness stopped me from grasping how I sounded.

“He’s flipped!  Mad as a Janglebonk in a ferret farm!”

Janglebonk were tasty.  I didn’t stop redistributing the wealth around me, but I slowed down.  I cleared the second row, then looked back.

They were all bleeding and broken.  Friends was a strong word, and no dragon would ever apply it to thieves, but they were on my side.  My team.  My people.

Pocket was on the ground, clutching her side, but still trying to fight back.  Burglar was a bloody pulp, swaying on his feet and tanking the blows to protect the woman currently wearing his trousers.  I had to admire his dedication.  It was more than I’d expect from a mammal.

I glanced back at the seemingly endless shinies awaiting my liberating embrace.  They needed to come home, back to where they belonged.  With me.  All the shinies belonged with me.

“Bob!”

“Oh, for fucks sake!  Can’t a dragon enjoy a special moment?  You guys are really harshing the vibe!”

I opened a portal beneath the guards in the doorway, dropping them into the river that cut through the centre of Ankmapak.  The rest dodged back, then I opened another blue oval beneath my allies and dumped them onto the farming floor in the dungeon.  They were thieves, so they had to go to the most heavily defended floor.  I was confident they could cope with the unibunnies.
