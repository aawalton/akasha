While Asher took in the rest of his team still hard at work trying to wipe out the remaining two revenants, he couldn’t shake the odd feeling in the back of his mind that something seemed strange about the combat before him.

Moxy and Samantha were fighting near flawlessly beside one another, alternating attacks on the archer and covering for the other’s weak points in an impressive display of teamwork and skill. Samantha would buy Moxy breathing room by letting loose a barrage of arrows, giving the warrior a chance to heal the damage she’d already taken as she went on the offensive. Then, when she eventually ran out of arrows, Moxy would give her the chance to skim over the ground and snatch up more ammunition by launching herself at the archer like a woman possessed, shrugging off the revenant’s projectiles as she managed to deflect or block most of them before trying to blast the woman’s head off.

For her part, the revenant continued relying on that odd, flowing skill of hers, literally bending and shifting like a reed in the wind as she made herself almost impossible to hit. Not even Moxy’s condensed balls of air were able to do anything, as the archer just leapt aside and rode the shockwave, using the explosions of air to put distance between them while she continued firing. It seemed the archer never actually ran out of arrows, which Asher could only assume was another skill of hers as she kept peppering Moxy and Samantha from range.

Similarly, Amanda and Rulfar were having just as difficult a time putting their opponent down for the count.

Unlike the archer’s nimbleness and impressive evasion, the slayer wasn’t quite so quick to dodge. What he was good at, however, was using that Earth or Stone element of his to construct fortifications in the blink of an eye. Rulfar and Amanda were struggling to even get close to the undead. Every time they tried to close the distance or land a hit on him, a pillar of earth erupted out of the ground, either blocking their strike or hiding the slayer as he took advantage of the blind spot to reposition himself. Despite the fact that Amanda’s glowing sword was capable of scything through these surprise pillars with ease, the undead seemed to be focusing purely on fighting a defensive battle. He wasn’t even trying to land his own attacks, which meant he was free to do everything in his power to dodge their combined strikes as he kept them busy.

Asher frowned as he looked between the two battles, watching the revenants continue to dodge and block strikes while barely seeming to land any of their own. The archer was at least firing the occasional arrow, but the longer Asher watched, the more certain he became of that unpleasant feeling he had creeping up the back of his spine. Taking a deep breath, he shouted at his teammates.

“They’re not trying to win! They’re stalling!”

“Stalling?!” Rulfar called back out. With a grunt, he barreled forward and smashed through a wall of solid rock that hadn’t existed a moment before as he tried to reach the evasive slayer. “Why?!”

“I don’t know!” Asher shouted, yanking out his sending stone and pressing the rune for Groln. It took a few seconds, but eventually, the man picked up.

“Asher? Did your team find the lich?”

“No, but we’re currently engaged with three of the revenants,” he said, hearing the sounds of battle coming through from the other side. “What about your team?”

“We are finishing off the third of the three revenants that we ran into as well. It has been a longer battle than we expected, but we managed to pincer them in the end despite their attempts to draw things out.”

“Have you seen any of the greater revenants?” Asher asked, his heart sinking as he realized what was going on.

“No. We assumed the lich was focusing all his might on wiping out one team at a time,” Groln said. “Just like we planned, we were going to take advantage of his mistake to push for the center and finish him off!”

“I don’t think he’s at the center. I think he’s trying to make a break for it!” Asher said, looking up at the crackling storm of blood up above them as he imagined such devastating power being used on a village of innocent people. “We weren’t attacked by any of the greater revenants either, and the undead we are fighting are barely fighting back. I think they’re trying to keep us busy while the lich flees!”

“Two of the Hojon Empire’s greater powers are maintaining the quarantine,” Groln argued, before inhaling sharply as his brain caught up with what Asher was saying. “But if the lich has pulled all three of his greater revenants together to move as one, sacrificing the rest of his minions to buy himself time…”

“He’s making a break for it,” Asher confirmed, hearing Groln swear on the other side of the stone. “Maybe even trying to add to his more-powerful minions as he runs. If he’s capable of taking those two down without losing any of his current minions, he might grow up to five greater revenants before we get to him. Can you check in with the greater powers on quarantine duty and warn them?”

Groln didn’t even answer as the line went dead, and Asher grimaced at the realization that they’d been played. They’d all thought that the lich would take the bait and try to wipe out their attacking forces in order to add to his army, but it seemed the lich had other ideas in mind. Groln had been so sure that the greater powers manning the quarantine were strong enough to hold their own that Asher hadn’t even stopped to consider that wasn’t the case. Deciding they needed to wrap things up here as fast as possible, Asher focused on Samantha and triggered Recall, warping directly beside the warrior while she snatched up more arrows.

“Asher?!” she shouted, one of her wings shooting out and blocking an arrow that would have collided with the side of his head. “What’s wrong?”

“No time to mess around, we’re taking her out now,” Asher said, grabbing an arrow and tossing it to her. “Plan B, hurry!”

Not needing to ask what he had in mind, Samantha nodded, drawing the arrow back before firing it straight for the archer. Just like every other one of Samantha’s shots, the archer nimbly twisted away from it, avoiding the arrow by the slimmest of margins.

But that didn’t stop Asher as he blinked into existence directly behind her.

Yet to Asher’s astonishment, she even managed to avoid him when he tried to slash at her from behind, the woman’s body seeming to move entirely on its own as it automatically dodged his attack. Luckily, Asher wasn’t taking any chances, and he’d already thrown up a Spatial Lock to pin the woman down and keep her from running around the battlefield.

“Moxy, help me out!” Asher shouted, frowning as he tried again and again to cut the woman apart, only for her body to bend and almost slither around his repeated strikes.

In the end, it took Moxy using a sphere of condensed air to blast the woman into the side of Asher’s Spatial Lock. As graceful as the undead was, she wasn’t exactly able to block being hurled into a solid wall, and she wasn’t strong enough to damage the barrier directly. Once the archer was pinned down, Samantha managed to fly through the woman’s legs with Razor Wing, making Asher’s job of beheading the undead all the easier.

“Finally,” Moxy muttered, ripping another few arrows from her body as she grimaced. “Poisoned arrows. If it weren’t for my evolved resistance and healing skills, I’d probably be twitching around on the ground right now. First time I’ve ever fought someone better at dodging attacks than I am.”

“You can be annoyed later, right now we need to finish this up,” Asher said. Tossing Samantha another arrow, he pointed over toward the other battle that was still ongoing. “Give me another lift?”

“I’m going to have to start charging for these if this keeps up,” Samantha teased. Narrowing her eyes, she took careful aim and let the arrow fly. Asher waited a beat as the arrow whistled across the land, trusting in Comprehend to ensure his timing would be perfect. Just before it could strike the undead, a pillar of stone blasted out of the ground and blocked it, but it was still close enough for his purposes. Recalling across the battlefield, Asher slipped into the astral and lunged through the pillar, using the undead’s own terrain to his advantage. The slayer couldn’t have seen him arrive on the other side of the pillar, and Asher’s theory was confirmed as the undead’s eyes widened in shock when he appeared directly before it and went to drive his dagger into the revenant’s eye socket.

He managed to shove his dagger into the revenant’s eye, but just as it had before, the undead’s head snapped back at an impossible angle, narrowly dodging having Asher’s blade punch into its brain. Before Asher could strike again, a sharpened spear of stone erupted out of the ground, piercing through his abdomen and out his back as it locked him in place.

For all of half a second at least.

Slipping through the astral, Asher freed himself from the spear, ignoring the gaping hole in his stomach and back as he pressed the attack. Permanence’s second-tier effect was still on him, which meant blood loss was the least of his worries. He wasn’t the only one going at the revenant with renewed vigor, either. Amanda appeared in a flash of light behind the undead when it tried to put space between them, slicing down with her sword in an attempt to cut the slayer in two. Again, the revenant barely managed to survive as the very ground beneath its feet seemed to pull him out of the way, and he spun with the momentum, driving his warhammer into Amanda’s side so hard that the explosion of force sent the paladin flying.

Asher could only hope that Amanda’s pristine armor wasn’t just for show, because he didn’t have time to worry about her right now. Returning to the material, he stabbed Crescent forward, cursing when a wall of stone erupted from the ground to block his attack. The slayer tried to dart away again, only to suddenly find himself blocked by ten feet of fur and muscle as Rulfar charged into position. Rather than try to attack the undead and receive the same punishment that Amanda had, Rulfar played the role of a moving wall, keeping the revenant from escaping.

“Any day now, Asher!” Rulfar shouted, snarling at the slayer as the revenant tried and failed to drive a stone spear through Rulfar’s chest.

Not wanting to miss his cue, Asher decided enough was enough. Marking one of his daggers, he threw it at the space above the undead’s head while throwing a second one directly at its face. As expected, the slayer blocked the dagger aimed for his face with a pillar of stone, but he ignored the dagger thrown too high. That was exactly what Asher was waiting for, however, and he Recalled to the dagger at the precise moment it went soaring by above him. Twisting around in midair, Asher stabbed down through a tunnel of compressed space, elongating Crescent before driving the mithral blade directly into the top of the undead’s skull.

The slayer crumpled instantly, Sever the Thread cutting through whatever skill was keeping it alive. As Asher landed softly beside it, he grunted, wiping the old, rotting blood off of his daggers before sheathing them.
