“It is your turn next,” James said slowly. “I hope you feel prepared.”

“I could hardly be more so,” Zhang Chāo said in a voice of false calm.

Neither James’s nor Chāo’s emotions were truly settled. For once, neither could be sure of his victory.

There was a palpable tension in the air between them now. Both men felt it. In the next hour, they would learn which one of them was truly the stronger between the two. That might also mean who was the strongest human in the world. James had not encountered or observed anyone in either the melee or the Summit who he was confident overshadowed him or Zhang Chāo. Not even the couple of other human Emperors.

Zhang Chāo was at least one of his primary rivals in the Tournament.

“Do you want to set any ground rules for our fight?” James asked. He recognized even as he spoke that it could be taken as a sign of weakness, bringing that idea up, but he consciously disregarded that. It was a foolish concern in an arena where either or both men could die.

“Like no tossing the other person out of the ring and burying them underground to avoid fighting?” Zhang Chāo teased.

“That wouldn’t have worked on Han Jianguo if he didn’t stop using his Skill,” James replied, shrugging. “He should never have let me use Dominion when he himself wasn’t a Ruler. If he’d just kept it to a physical hand to hand fight… But no, that’s not what I meant.”

“What, then?”

“No using techniques and weapons that we are certain would, if they landed, prove fatal,” James replied. “It’s all right to risk death. We both understand we’re in a life-or-death competition. But the maiming you gave your opponent in an earlier round only lasted until he presumably regenerated. Considering that this isn’t like the melee, and death is permanent here, I think we should at least try to avoid ending our alliance early if we can. That was why I buried Jianguo, by the way. I determined that if I continued fighting him face to face, I might have to kill him.”

“Honestly, that is a dilemma I faced before when I fought him,” Zhang Chāo said quietly. “He is a man of both great strength and strong convictions. He doesn’t give up. Not ever. I had to render him incapable of moving.” He beamed. “Of course, since then, he’s been my most loyal friend and companion.”

You’re so fucking wholesome, Chāo, James thought. Most people would have killed someone who pushed them into a corner like that. But you made him your friend and your best asset.

He felt admiration and even a hint of envy that Zhang Chāo had such a good relationship with such a strong subordinate. James already felt confident that Han Jianguo was more powerful than anyone in the Fisher Empire besides James. Or, at least, Jianguo would win a one on one duel with anyone else in the Empire. There was more than one way to measure power.

“Yes, my congratulations on finding your life partner,” the Emperor said.

Zhang Chāo rolled his eyes and returned to the subject James had raised before.

“So, according to your proposal, if I understand it right, you won’t use Soul Magic?” Chāo said.

“And you won’t use that wicked sword of yours to inflict any potentially lethal wounds,” James replied, nodding.

Zhang Chāo looked down for a few long seconds, then muttered something under his breath. With James’s superhuman senses, he caught the words, “If I can’t win the fight without the sword, then I am unworthy to wield it.” Then Chāo looked back at James. “I will avoid lethal blows from the Mandate of Heaven, yes. No attacks aimed at the neck or the heart. I assume you would survive anything less than full decapitation or having your heart destroyed.”

James nodded. His regenerative powers were completely ridiculous at this point. It was why his lack of too many flashy finishing moves wasn’t as big of a deal as it might be. He could take the overpowered Skills from other people, endure them, and get back up to strangle them to death while they were exhausted. It was rare that someone was actually physically tough enough to overpower him as Jianguo had been.

“Other than those restrictions, we go all out, then, correct?” Zhang Chāo asked.

“Unless either of us has another instant death Skill we’re not remembering right now,” James said, chuckling. He thought of bringing up Roscuro, but the Soul Eater Orb’s powers were not such that they would instantly kill anyone. At least not anyone on Chāo’s level. Roscuro was basically just like any other magical weapon, except for the shapeshifting and the fact that he learned new Skills by consuming souls.

“Prepare yourself, then, James,” Zhang Chāo replied with determination. “I’m going to show you everything I can do.”

The Emperor kept a poker face, but he was pleased that he had at least somewhat neutered Zhang Chāo’s magic sword. It was only by agreement, but Zhang Chāo seemed to be a man who always honored his word. James was aware there was more to worry about. He had been most concerned about the power of the Mandate of Heaven. That sword seemed to be able to cut through anything like butter. But James had also only ever seen Zhang Chāo wield it, never anyone else.

Maybe the exceptional thing in Chāo’s fights wasn’t that he had a magical sword that just destroyed whatever was in its way. Maybe it was just Zhang Chāo.

Nah, I’ll win. Today is the day that I prove I’m the strongest.

“I expect nothing less from you,” James said, nodding to Zhang Chāo’s words. “I intend to pull out all the stops as well.”

Both men smiled.

Then the Emperor nodded to his opponent, walked away, and sat down against a wall to gather his energy and plan his strategy for the next match.

He had thought a lot about how he would fight against both Han Jianguo and Zhang Chāo, and while James hadn’t been forced to show everything he could do in dealing with Jianguo, he felt certain that Chāo would be a different story.

I have to fight the strongest of my allies, and I’m doing it in front of the whole world. The smart thing to do would be to hold back or forfeit or something. At least hide my strength. The rest of the world will still think I’m one of the strongest even if I don’t defeat Zhang Chāo here, especially after that performance against Jianguo. It’s also decently likely that Chāo comes out on top in the whole tournament if I don’t beat him. If I forfeited, he’d go into the next round in perfect condition, while his opponents will have torn each other to rags. There won’t be any more easy wins for anyone at this point. Everyone remaining is very strong, I sense.

There were rational reasons why the Emperor might not go all out. James had much larger problems than winning the tournament. This was more of a matter of ego for him and Zhang Chāo than anything else, even if the prizes for ranking highly in the competition would be valuable to their respective nations. For people who were as resourceful as them, the System’s planned rewards were unlikely to be anything beyond what they could attain on their own.

Maybe it would be wiser to forfeit the match and forget about what was effectively just a dick-measuring contest. Hide his true strength from his real enemies, the monotheistic faction who still posed a risk to James and his family. Then again, maybe that would send them a message of weakness and hasten their attack. Of course, none of the zealot faction remained in the competition—meaning none of them were confident in defeating James or Zora if they had run into them while Zora was still in the competition—but that didn’t matter. The insidious thing about the monotheist faction was that they didn’t have to rely so much on individual strength in any prospective war but on their superior numbers, with which James was not yet equipped to contend.

The Emperor had only quality to depend on.

By now, all of James’s allies had been eliminated from the Victors’ Tournament. Zora had finally lost in this last round, and James didn’t know against whom. He hadn’t given himself the time to think about it.

The bottom line was that he was the only one who had been transported back to the waiting room this time. He was the last man standing. It was obvious that the Emperor himself was the pillar of power in his country, to any discerning person who was paying attention. So he needed to showcase his strength a bit more, didn’t he? To show just how unshakable that pillar was.

But if he stopped fighting, just forfeited without ever losing a fight, his enemies might think he was quitting as a favor to Zhang Chāo. That would still leave their estimate of James’s real strength ambiguous, hopefully.

It was hard to be certain of anything about how other people would think, especially when the real enemies he was worried about were religious zealots whose rationality was likely shaky at best.

“Should I just quit?” he mused aloud, very quietly. “I made it to the round of thirty-two, and that’s pretty good… I’ll likely never run into another human who can take me on my own turf for the rest of my life. The fact that the fight with Jianguo was as close as it was means I need to train harder, but if I keep going, I could actually lose. That would be egg on my face. Maybe it’s better to save myself the potential for embarrassment—”

That is not in your nature, master, came the voice of Roscuro into his mind.

“Oh, no?” James replied.

Oh no, Roscuro deadpanned. You are the kind of egotistical warrior who refuses to ever be second best, even in the eyes of others. For better or for worse, that is who you truly are.

“Yeah,” James said quietly. That was the trouble. “I know.”

Well, the decision is always with the man in the arena, the Soul Eater opined. But if you are worried about what others would think… I think that giving up would signal a lack of resolve. You came to this Tournament. You participated after you had accomplished what you wished to in the melee. You did not have to proceed beyond the first round. That makes it clear enough that you came here wanting to win. If you surrender here, you are as good as saying that you do not believe you can defeat your opponent. Whatever ceiling of power people believe applies to him, they will assume you are noticeably weaker than that.

“You’re excited to fight him, isn’t that it?” James asked, raising an eyebrow.

That does not make my counsel any less accurate, Roscuro replied after a moment.

“Yeah. Well, so am I. So you’ll get what you want.”

Enough thinking. Enough doubt. It was time to settle matters that could only be resolved with strength in the way that was appropriate.

James got to his feet, stretched, and cracked his neck. It was time.

He disappeared from the waiting area and reappeared back in the arena.

Across from him, as expected, stood Zhang Chāo.

The Heavenly King gave the Emperor a nod.

Then James took a step toward Chāo and extended his open palm. James’s opponent gave him a small smile, closed the distance, and took the outstretched hand in his. They shook.

“May the best fighter win,” James said.

“Agreed,” said Zhang Chāo.

Both men grinned, and all James’s doubt and regret faded away.

The two warriors assumed martial arts stances, and then the Emperor and the King began to fight.
