Following the directions Suierrillax provided ended up being quite simple.  As it turned out, she’d organized a place for him within the Courier’s Guild proper.  Holding up his nav bracelet to the door, he expected to see a small, office-like studio apartment.  Instead, an expansive flat opened up before him; including a spacious living room, well-stocked refrigerator, and what looked like a balcony and mediation chamber branching off from there.

A small note rested on the counter near his kitchen, and a quick glance told him it was from Suierrillax.

Kyle,

Glad to have you back.  We’re working on getting some other long-term accommodations figured out, but this should work for now.  Don’t go dying on us again.  It’s a lot of paperwork, and we had to pull enough strings to get all this together.

Seriously.  Don’t die again.

-Suierrillax

At first he chuckled at the note, then stopped to read it over again.  Somehow he could feel the hurt behind the words.  And we’ll see how the others respond, too.  He’d traded a few messages with Jax and Arhades, though both had been pretty busy since his return.  Other than a quick confirmation note that she was available for the lunch that Suierrillax scheduled, he hadn’t heard from Tillienne at all.  They were hurting, too, Kyle thought.

He still intended to connect with Marcus, Garth, and Angela Corthus, but given that the governing body of their piece of the Collective was actively trying to get his citizenship revoked, he deferred to Suierrillax, who told him that it was a bad idea in every conceivable way.  When it was all said and done, Kyle got the sense that he was going to be doing quite a bit of apologizing.

[THIS IS QUITE A NICE APARTMENT,] C.H.A.D.D. remarked, helpfully providing Kyle a small map of the layout.   [YOU COULD GET YOUR CLOTHES CLEANED UP, AND GET A GOOD NIGHT’S REST BEFORE MEETING WITH YOUR FRIENDS.]

“Are you really okay with that, C.H.A.D.D.?  Last I checked, we had some more experiments to do with Graft, not to mention work on repairing the damaged prosthetics.”

[THERE WILL BE TIME FOR THAT, DR. MAYHEW.  I CAN DO SOME OF THE REPAIR WORK WHILE YOU’RE SLEEPING, ASSUMING YOU KEEP ME CONNECTED TO THE SEED AND LEND ME SOME OF YOUR MANA.]

Kyle was a little surprised at the drone’s reaction, given their nearly nonstop studying and experimentation with the new skills, but the thought of sleeping in a real, comfortable bed was too tempting to pass up.  So, after making a quick meal, he showered, got his clothes set aside to clean, and drifted off to a sweet, dreamless sleep.

The next day, clean and refreshed, he followed the directions to the restaurant Suierrillax organized for them; a nice mid-range option with a plethora of private seating.  It had also been vetted by the Courier’s Guild as a safe place to meet and discuss business, only adding to its legitimacy in his eyes.  A few quick trips on a teleporter later, and he was in a Verdant Republic controlled part of the Hub.  Walking toward the restaurant, Kyle felt a subtle shift through Synaptic Barbs, at the same moment C.H.A.D.D. gave a warning through Pack Leader’s Instinct.  With a sigh, Kyle turned to face the person who had not-so-subtly started tailing him.

“What do you want, Casiniax?”  Kyle asked, trying to keep his tone neutral.  There was no way the skrell would be brazen enough to try and have him attacked in Verdant Republic territory, much less after all but accusing their representatives of corruption.  Still, he didn’t want to needlessly escalate, or walk into a trap.

“Why, I’m just here to grab a bite to eat.  So many fine restaurants in this district, after all,” the skrell said, a wide smile on his face.  “Oh, and of course, to ensure that you don’t go about murdering any of these fine people.”

“So you’re here to antagonize me, got it,” Kyle said, turning and making his way toward the restaurant.  He was ready to activate Storm Shelter at a moment’s notice, should it become necessary, and Parasitic Resonance already had tendrils in the air around him, ready to move in at the first signs of trouble.

“Antagonize is a strong word, Mr. Mayhew.  I’d rather think that I’m here to do my diligence.  Take your measure, if you will.”

“Then measure away,” Kyle said, not turning back or breaking his stride.

“Oh, I will,” Casiniax replied.  Kyle could feel the smile behind the words.  “And while I’m here, let me toss out another thought.  You are welcome to choose to self-isolate while the next investigator gets assigned.  Now, no Gray Conglomerate business will bankrupt itself in the ridiculous way your colleague recommended, but it might go a long way towards helping show you take these accusations seriously.”

“That would be well and good if I did take them seriously, which I do not.  They are meritless, and I sincerely hope that the investigator, whoever they are, sees this shakedown for what it is sooner than later.”  Kyle already dropped a quick note to Suierrillax and Jarberry about the unwanted guest, and hoped that they would have some advice to get him gone.

“Truly a pity,” Casiniax said.  “Well then, I’ll leave you to your lunch.  But I wouldn’t expect the young heir to Cain Jewelers to join you today.  From the way I hear it, their family doesn’t much relish the idea of cavorting with murderers.  Ta-ta!”

Can I eat him?

The impression from Phaegos was so unexpected that Kyle almost burst out laughing.

No, no you can’t.  At least, not yet.

Ah well.  Worth asking.

C.H.A.D.D. agreed to keep tabs on the man as they made their way to the restaurant.  Fortunately, Casiniax didn’t follow, and he was quickly taken to his table.  He was the first to arrive, and mere moments after sitting down, he got a message from Jax.

Kyle,

So sorry about this, my family has gotten caught up in this drama.  I thought I’d just be late, now it looks like I won’t be able to come at all.  We’ll rain check when this is all said and done, I promise.

-Jax

Kyle swore, looking out at the restaurant district below.  It was one thing for Casiniax and his ilk to cause trouble for him.  That was frustrating, but expected.  Getting involved with and threatening his friends was something different.  He had only ever been loosely affiliated with Jax, and the fact that they were bringing trouble to him and his family irked Kyle quite a bit.  They called Jarberry and Suierrillax co-conspirators, they got between him and his friends, and he was going to make them answer for it.  At least he planned on it.  Someday.

As the planned arrival time came and went, he was growing concerned that Casiniax got to his other guests as well, until he finally got a notice that somebody was being brought to the private dining room.  He stood to see Tillienne walking in.  Unlike any time they’d met for a meal before, she wore her full combat equipment.  Not altogether unusual for D Grades, but still a marked departure.  Her posture seemed tense, a mix of exhaustion and anticipation.  Even her normally rosy skin looked paler than usual.

“Tillienne, it’s so good to see you,” Kyle began, then stopped as he saw her eyes drift to the tightly tucked sleeve where his left arm had been.  Her fists clenched, and she froze for a moment as her eyes traced the edge of his injury, a bit of the angry red scar still visible beneath his collar.

“Kyle,” she breathed, trying to find her words.  “I… I’m so sorry.”

“Sorry?  For what? Saving a bunch of people from mutant mushrooms?  For fighting C Grades and surviving?” Kyle asked the questions hoping to break the tension, but her reaction told him that was the wrong decision.

“Kyle, I left you behind.  I was too weak to hold my own with you, and you needed to make time for me to escape.  I’m sorry that I couldn’t keep up.  If I’d been strong enough, you wouldn’t be…”

Only now did Kyle remember how his presence felt to others, and he gave her a smile.  “Tillienne, I’m not as broken as I look.  Come on, let’s eat.  I want to hear what you’ve been up to.”

With that, he pushed mana to cycle through his system a little more quickly.  It didn’t have the same impact as a quick activation of Genesis Engine, but it got his point across without causing a panic across the restaurant.  After all, activating boosting skills willy-nilly was considered incredibly rude.

Taking another moment to appraise Kyle, she finally took her seat, and Kyle followed suit.  Technically Arhades had been invited as well, though given the issues with his Tribunal, he expected the velgian would likely not be attending.  He and Tillienne ordered some food, and after some brief small talk, Kyle finally decided to address the elephant in the room.

“Tillienne, what happened to you over the last few years?  I never would have expected to see you in full combat gear coming out to eat.”

A bitter smile crossed her face.  “I was too indulgent back then, Kyle.  The gap was a lot closer during the Practicum, but I let myself fall behind.  So that’s changed.  I will hit C Grade, and I will continue to get more powerful.  I can’t afford to let myself get distracted.  Not anymore.”

“Is it really a distraction to pause every once in a while and enjoy your life?” Kyle pressed.  The moment he said the words, however, he regretted them.  C.H.A.D.D. was already pointing out his hypocrisy through Pack Leader’s Instinct, and TIllienne leveled a fork at him, not a hint of humor to be found in her eyes.

“You certainly never slowed down.  Neither did Arhades.  I barely know Skippy, but I’d wager he didn’t stop, either.  And you know what, Kyle?  If there had been another you, or Arhades, or a second Skippy instead of me on Kertan Six, things might have ended very differently.”

Her voice cracked at the end, and Kyle felt a tearing in his own heart that was entirely non-physical.  She’d been carrying such tremendous guilt since the disaster.  Kyle could almost see a path to ruin ahead of her, but also knew that he was the last person to try and convince her to take a different one.  Still, he was going to try.  He had to.

“Tillienne,” he began, before the door to their room opened once more, just as a powerful C Grade aura washed in.

Arhades strode through the entrance, stopping to give both Kyle and Tillienne respectful nods.  “Tillienne, it has been a while.  It is good to see you well.  And Mr. Mayhew, I am glad to see that you still live.  Our scales would hardly have been balanced if you’d gone off and died after our last meeting.”

Kyle smiled, rising and reaching out to offer his hand to the velgian.  “So, you reached C Grade too.  Not that I’m surprised to see it,” he added hastily, feeling the strength of Arhades’ grip.

“Naturally,” the pale man replied, offering Kyle a warm smile.  “Now is where I find out if my path was worth treading or not.  And then it will be time to put it to the test.”

His smile faltered a bit when he saw Tillienne, and Kyle hoped that he would tell her to slow her pace.  To his dismay, he did the opposite.

“Ms. Tillienne, you’ve grown much more powerful since last we met.  Tell me, have you made any progress in determining the course of your next class?”

Of course he’s driving the obsessive behavior, Kyle growled to himself.

“Not yet, Arhades,” Tillienne said with a shallow bow.  “I would be open to any suggestions either of you would have on the matter, seeing as you’ve both managed to cross the threshold into C Grade.”

“Well, you see,” Arhades began, “there are several key schools of thought if you intend to depart from your clan’s heritage.  Take this, for example…”

As Arhades went on, Kyle smiled, allowing himself to get caught up in the discussion.  He worried for Tillienne, but hoped that by being present he could be an encouragement.  And above all else, it was nice just spending time with these people he’d come to call friends.  As the hours wore on and more food and drink was brought to the table, the worries of the Tribunal melted away as they talked, laughed, and shared.  He knew that there would be a job waiting for him tomorrow, but for now, Kyle was content.  And when this Tribunal is done, we’re all getting together again, he resolved.
