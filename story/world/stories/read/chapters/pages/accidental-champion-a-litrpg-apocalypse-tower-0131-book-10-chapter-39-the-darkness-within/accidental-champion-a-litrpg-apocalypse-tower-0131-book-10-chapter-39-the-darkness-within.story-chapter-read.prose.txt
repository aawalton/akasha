Derrida stood up and stared at the holographic display, eyes wide, head cocked.

She had known of the Void Dungeon for billions of years, and not once had she ever spoken to it. Not once had anyone spoken to it, not as far as she knew. In fact, though she had spoken to other dungeons in the past, she’d thought this being incapable of such communication.

She’d been wrong.

That was something that happened rarely enough for the ancient Wanderer that it gained her attention whenever it did.

Not only that—for a moment, her view of Xavier Collins, her ability to read his mind, had been blocked. She’d managed to circumvent that block and bring the holographic display back online, but not without some effort.

More effort that it should have taken, if she were honest.

The Void Dungeon shouldn’t have been able to block me at all.

“It knows,” Roln whispered. “How? How can it know?”

Derrida shook her head. “Perhaps it remembers…” But even as she said the words she knew they were a lie. The Void Dungeon wasn’t old enough to know such secrets, though of course it could have picked it up somehow over the years, Derrida sensed it wasn’t lying when it said it could smell the connection on Xavier.

Something she herself hadn’t gleaned, even though she already knew of it.

If the secret were to ever get out…

When the Void Dungeon gave Xavier its parting words, saying that not even Wanderers could leave without it allowing it, Derrida stiffened.

That can’t be true…

She concentrated, opening a portal to the Void Dungeon. Just to the entrance, where she’d dropped Xavier off before. Except, the portal wouldn’t open. It was blocked.

She tried again.

And again.

Nothing.

Eventually, Derrida slumped back onto the armchair, a deadened look in her eyes. She was not one who usually worried, but this… this had taken her by surprise. She hadn’t thought the Void Dungeon would be powerful enough to do such a thing. It had never been powerful enough in the past.

I could still destroy it, she thought, sensing she was more than powerful enough to do such a thing. But the fact it could block her entering and leaving it…

It’s a dungeon. Of course it can. It is the master of its domain. How foolish am I to have forgotten such a thing? Just because we’ve left it in the past doesn’t mean that was because of how powerful we are.

The Void Dungeon had allowed all the Wanderers who’d been inside it to leave out of a sense of self-preservation. If they were powerful enough to get to the second last room of the dungeon, just before entering the heart where the Void Core was, then they were likely powerful enough to destroy the core, and like all beings the Void Dungeon didn’t just wish for power—it wished for survival.

So it let us go, because if it trapped us inside itself, the only way to leave would be through.

And yet, she could destroy it. She could probably destroy it with a thought, but there was no way that she’d be able to pluck Xavier Collins out of the Void Dungeon if she did. He would perish along with it.

Maybe I did send him there to die after all.

Sinking into her armchair she knew there was but one thing she could do.

Watch.

Xavier walked through the Void Dungeon exercising a newfound level of caution. He didn’t know if what the Void Dungeon had said was true or not—that the only way he would ever leave was by destroying it—but the more he thought about it, the more he believed it.

He’d already decided he wanted to become powerful enough to complete this dungeon, so did it really matter if he was trapped here or not?

He kept going back and forth on that. More than anything, in that moment, he wished he could talk to Roln. The dead Wanderer might not always know what to do, but at least it was good to have someone to talk to. He could summon Bones, but he wasn’t sure he was ready to reintegrate the soul bound weapon into his life—if he ever was ready at all. And the Personal System, while incredibly helpful, wasn’t exactly the best conversationalist.

And he wished he could speak to Adranial, though he didn’t want to bring her here—he was glad she was safe back on Earth.

Sometimes he would glance at the walls, wondering whether he would see the glowing red eye of the Void Dungeon watching him, but as he made his way through the Ragatni and the other beasts of the first floor of this dungeon, he never saw it again.

He knew it was watching.

It watched through every Void Dungeon-created beast he killed. And it watched through the very walls, whether the eye was there or not. More and more, Xavier got to thinking about the rotten runes on the walls and what they might mean. He’d thought that it meant the dungeon itself was rotten, but the eye he’d seen hadn’t smelled the same.

The beasts he faced became more and more powerful the deeper into the floor he got, but none of them were powerful enough to be a threat to him. He took each of them down with an incredible ease, an ease that would have made him relax if he could relax in his current state. But he felt too wired. Too wired to slow down, and certainly too wired to stop.

I have to destroy the Void Dungeon. There’s simply no other way…

If you’re listening, Derrida, and I survive this, we’re going to have a little chat.

Eventually, tearing his way through every beast he found, moving through the halls and down different sets of stairs, Xavier came upon a closed door. It was the first door he’d come to in the Void Dungeon.

It was a single, massive door that stood roughly twenty feet high and ten feet wide.

The door lacked any sort of handle, but there was a large, brass-looking knocker at his shoulder height. The knocker was in the shape of a fist.

This is the last room of the first floor of the Void Dungeon. If you enter this room, you will not be able to step back out of it. On the other side, you will face the Floor Boss and its minions.

The only way out of the room is down to the stairs to the next floor, and the stairs will only reveal themselves after you slay the Floor Boss.

Xavier took in the Personal System’s notification. He’d had an idea that was what this would be, though it was always good to have confirmation.

The first floor of the Void Dungeon had been wildly easy. Far easier than he’d expected it to be. Once he’d found out the beasts gave him energy, he stopped worrying about conserving it so much, and expanding his senses had given him so much more than he’d expected it to. Through the last several hundred beasts he’d faced he’d honed his ability to manipulate reality. He couldn’t merely create spell patterns on the fly; he created them as easily as he breathed. They barely needed any conscious thought. His magic was driven by pure, deadly instinct.

Whatever was on the other side, he was confident he would be able to face.

And yet, he hesitated.

He knew there was more that he could do. More that he could train. And he essentially had eternity here to do so. He’d spoken to the Personal System a time or two about the rising difficulty of this place, and while it didn’t have access to as much information about this place as Xavier had wished, it knew a great deal.

The first floor was basically a lie. The beasts were significantly weaker than the ones on the floors to come, to the point where they lulled the beings who entered this place into a false sense of security.

The Floor Boss, too, would be far more powerful than the beasts that had preceded it. Despite knowing this, Xavier didn’t lack any confidence about defeating it. His hesitation was purely irrational, and it was something he didn’t usually suffer from.

I’m being watched…

The fact the Void Dungeon was watching him might have unnerved him, considering how much anger it held for him, but it didn’t. Xavier was used to being watched by powerful beings by now. He was sure Derrida, wherever the ancient Wanderer had gotten off to, was intently watching what was happening to him in the Void Dungeon right now.

No, it wasn’t fear or the fact he was being watched that made him hesitate. It was his confidence. It was unshakeable to the point of arrogance. The Void Dungeon locking him in this place had thrown him off kilter for a short time, as had the fact it knew about his connection to the Interdimensional Dragon, but his confidence hadn’t taken long to return. Yes, he might very well be trapped in this place. Yes, he was facing what should have been impossible odds.

But when had that ever stopped him before?

He didn’t believe there was anything this place could throw at him that could defeat him, not if he worked his way through one floor after another and built his strength along the way. He didn’t fear the Floor Boss on the other side of that massive door, and he didn’t fear what he might face on the next floor, or the floor after that, either.

Right now, he didn’t fear anything but himself.

He didn’t want to admit it. Not to anyone. Not even inside his own mind. But he sensed the darkness within him. He felt it. It wasn’t just a lust for power. It was a lust to rule.

Over everything.

And he knew he could do it, too. It would take time, but sooner or later he and the other Xaviers would rule over not just their own universes, but every universe in the entirety of the multiverse. They would defeat the Interdimensional Dragon, and their rule would spread across all of known reality and farther, toward the unknowns that not even the most ancient of Wanderers like Derrida had ever encountered…

Was that what the Void Dungeon sensed from me? Smelled on me?

Xavier didn’t want that—he didn’t really want to rule over everything. The desire was a small one, locked deep inside him. It wasn’t truly him.

At least, that was what he kept telling himself.
