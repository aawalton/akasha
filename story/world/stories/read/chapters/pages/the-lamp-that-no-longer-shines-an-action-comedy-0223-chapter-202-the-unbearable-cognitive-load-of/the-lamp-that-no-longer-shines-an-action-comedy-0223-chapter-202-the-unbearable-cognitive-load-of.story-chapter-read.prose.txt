It had cost Hathaway an entire night of sleepless, sweat-soaked empirical testing to isolate the trigger.

She sat on the edge of the mattress in the grey pre-dawn, eyes bagged, and ran the confirmation pass one final time. She emptied her mind. Pure static. No active processes. A clean idle screen. Behind her, the six enormous red-gold tails pooled across the floorboards lay dead still, as inert and decorative as shed scarves.

Then she deliberately calculated the square root of one hundred and forty-four.

Twelve.

The leftmost tail snapped up and cracked against the bedpost like a bullwhip.

Hathaway buried her face in her hands, her shoulders shaking.

The telemetry is hardwired to cognitive load. Every equation she compiled, every stray memory she pulled from the cache, every hypothesis she so much as glanced at, the tails received the motor command and fanned out like a peacock throwing a tantrum. They were not limbs. They were six aggressive, externally visible CPU-usage monitors bolted onto the base of her spine.

Understanding the bug was, somehow, infinitely worse than suffering it blind.

What kind of sadistic, anti-intellectual firmware ships a body part slaved to the clock speed of the brain? Ask her to stop breathing instead, and she'd have signed that contract on the spot: a lung-compression hex could coast an hour on a single breath. Asking her to stop thinking was asking a processor to voluntarily halt its own clock. It was a death sentence with the paperwork filed under "self-care."

The one identifiable mercy in the whole defective codebase was that the appendages ran a default sleep mode. When she finally crashed into genuine, dreamless unconsciousness, the tails went dark and slumped across the sheets like heavy furniture throws. She had squeezed out three hours. Her spine, which had spent two entire lifetimes learning to lie flat, had no working model for accommodating six additional limbs with the combined mass of a large sofa, and she had spent the night folding herself into aching, un-ergonomic geometries and jolting awake every time she rolled onto her own fluff.

She dragged herself to the mirror. The ears twitched atop her head, swiveling to track a single droplet hitting the bathroom sink two rooms away. The bags under her eyes had bags.

The duration decay is a lie. The buff was holding strong into day two, sublimely indifferent to her suffering. The patch is not coming.

She stood frozen at the wardrobe, running the cost-benefit on a medical leave request. I have a visible, arguably contagious status ailment. The excuse writes itself. I could seal the door, mute comms, and hide until the biology uninstalls.

Then she checked the schedule glowing on her terminal. Thursday. [Doll-Making Basics & Soul Architecture]. Sharon Curie.

Her inner accountant looked at the projected net-worth hemorrhage of skipping a Grandmaster who had personally reverse-engineered the soul, looked at the social cost of walking into a lecture hall as a six-tailed liability, weighed the two for approximately half a second, and dragged her out the door by the collar.
