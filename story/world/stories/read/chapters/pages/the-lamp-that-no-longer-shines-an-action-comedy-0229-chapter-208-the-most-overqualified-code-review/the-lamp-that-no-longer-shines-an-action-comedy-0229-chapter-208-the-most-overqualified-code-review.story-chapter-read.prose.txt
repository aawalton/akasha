[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 7 — 9:18 AM[Location]: Yggdrasil Academy · Dormitory [Golden Bough] · Room 302

Hathaway sat cross-legged in her desk chair with the communication slate balanced on one knee, her directory open on it.

The etiquette here was narrow and unforgiving, and she had spent the walk back from breakfast mapping it. A letter was fatal. A letter arrived by courier, on paper, with a seal, and it said I am formally petitioning a senior for unpaid academic labor, and the recipient would put on her professor face and grade it. A clean, properly rendered diagram was worse. A clean diagram said I prepared this for you, and preparation implied a request, and a request created an obligation.

But a mess. A mess dropped into a chat window at nine in the morning with a self-deprecating one-liner was a girl sharing a funny thing that had happened to her, because she had thought of you, and the fact that the funny thing happened to be a composite spell architecture was purely incidental to the warmth of the gesture.

Never send a question mark, her social engineering thread concluded, with the grim satisfaction of a system architect closing a security hole. A question is an invoice. A statement is a gift. And nobody has ever, in the history of civilization, been able to look at an illegible diagram and not try to read it.

She drafted the payload.

Drew this drunk last night. Woke up and can't read my own handwriting.

She read it twice. The apostrophe was in the correct position. It was the sentence of a woman who had drafted it four times, which she had, and it radiated forethought like a wet dog radiates wet dog.

She deleted the whole line and typed it again from scratch, this time deliberately, letting her thumb land a full key to the left on the word that mattered most.

[Ctrl+S]: drew this dtunk last night woke up and cant read my own handwriting

Perfect. She admired it for a moment, the way she had once admired UI copy that tested nine percent better because somebody removed a comma. This is the work of a woman who has not yet located her boots.

She attached the capture.

The parchment came through raw, all of it, including the bottom third of the page where her own sprawling hand had written, and then underlined twice with enough pressure to emboss the reverse side.

Her thumb found the crop tool and hovered there for a long, agonizing second while her dignity made its case.

Crop it and it looks curated, she thought. Curated is deliberate. Deliberate is a formal request.

She lowered the slate, closed her eyes, and left the margin in.

Routing came next, and routing was where amateurs died.

The rule was Delayed Optimization. You did not fire at your fast responders first, because they would answer immediately and you would spend twenty minutes trapped in a live conversation while your slow responders were still un-contacted, and the whole illusion would collapse into what it actually was: one girl, alone in a room, working her way down a list. You seeded the slow ones early and let their latency do the staggering, and if you got the intervals right, everything landed at once and the terminal lit up like a party.

Broadcast was out. Four of the nine were Ludwigs, and Ludwigs travelled in packs. Four slates chiming on the same beat at the same table and she was finished.

So she typed it nine times, by hand. The words were identical either way. Typing them nine times was more sincere, and she declined to examine that.

The first went to [Still Up Overall].

Mihaye Grün's handle contained no reference whatsoever to quitting, cutting losses, or stepping away, because those were concepts that had never been introduced to her. It was, technically, a claim about aggregate data, made by a woman who studied historical records for a living and had presumably run the numbers. It was also, Hathaway was fairly confident, false.

The status beneath the name changed to typing...

And stayed there.

Hathaway logged the latency and moved on.

Sixth: [Strawberry Parfait ♡].

The Matriarch of the Ludwig bloodline, holder of a scepter, wearer of magma-silk, a woman who kept her eyes closed in public because opening them constituted a localized environmental hazard, had put a heart on it. That's not a joke. The ellipsis is real. She sat there.

Two theatrical cosmologies had met at the property line and neither one of them was going to move.

Seventh: [And I Shall Remember].

Eighth: [Currently Awake].

Alucard had chosen a status rather than a name, and the status was phrased as a temporary condition worth reporting.

Sunday, Alucard. She pressed a hand to her sternum. It's Sunday, and the city is not going to thank you. Somebody has to put a stop to this. It's not going to be me, but somebody.
