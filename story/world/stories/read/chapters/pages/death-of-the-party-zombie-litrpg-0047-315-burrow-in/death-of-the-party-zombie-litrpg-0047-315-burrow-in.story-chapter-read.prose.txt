Skeleton Key whipped around as Sally ate the brains of another Radoch. She barely had time to glance over at the recently reformed wizard before another cockroach lunged toward her with a lance.

Archie was casting a wide wall of flame, blocking off several streets to this area.

Based on how effortlessly he kept pulling out new spells, he was likely the most powerful spellcaster Sally had seen. “Thought you weren’t much for combat?” she growled, twisting away from another assailant.

“It’s just a coincidence that some of what I can create is damaging to these individuals. I could summon flowers and a gentle spring rain, if you prefer.”

The Radochs were really putting their all into trying to stop them. Whether the aliens knew the two Outsiders had recovered the artefact or not, every ground force available was pooling into this town. Above them, one of the Radoch ships was meandering into place over the combat.

Sally was down one hand and out of energy to keep bringing up zombies. While they had achieved their goal, she wasn’t content with getting killed off now. Not when there was still so much damage she could do to the cockroach forces.

As a bug swung a halberd down at her, she sidestepped before turning along the shaft to get closer to the Radoch. With a quick flick of her dagger, she severed one of the attacker’s hands off at the wrist. Healing threads spiraled down her arm, pulling the detached limb close and threading it to her wrist.

A brief pain ran up her arm, and then she flexed her cockroach fingers.

“About time I made better use of my zombie quirks,” she muttered as she drew a flaming skull into her borrowed hand.

White light flashed from the side where Archie was fighting, and half of the Radochs began attacking their closest brethren. “This is all fun, but perhaps we should escalate our displeasure and meet the higher-ups?”

Sally glared at the vessel clouding the sky above. “If you know a way, Arch, I’m all-”

Before she could finish the sentence, the ground shifted beneath her. The aliens tumbled away as the stone rose. A fifteen-foot square chunk of the bloodied square lifted gradually into the air, at first above the throng of attacking bugs, then even taller than the houses.

Sally kicked one last squirming bug from the rising tower and looked up.

They’d reach the ship in no time at all.

Theo yawned. This was taking longer than expected.

The device now flashed a green light to show that it was in the process of scanning the world. Normally, this would only take a couple of minutes. Chuck had decided to do an in-depth scan for some reason, and it was taking a while to complete.

Enough time for the monsters in the area to respawn.

Not that they posed any threat, Theo had dealt with them almost instantly. None of the loot had been especially useful or interesting. A shame, as he had eyed Ren’s sniper rifle with a little envy. If modern weapons could be found on this world… well, that could change a lot.

A gun wouldn’t exactly fit his fighting style, however. He shrugged away the notion and glanced out at the sea. It was just about finding advantages over the Radochs. The invasion troubled him, but it wasn’t necessarily the fighting part that gave him cause for concern.

After all, he was the Aspect of Death. With his full powers, and with Sally and the Outsiders beside him, killing any alien force would be a piece of cake. Especially with their ability to respawn.

The other side of the coin was that the Radochs must know this. Theo had seen… or at least felt, the presence of the fleet heading this way. They could have Aspect-level entities or worse on their side. Chuck and the rest of Sanctuary really had no way of knowing.

And it was that gap in information that bugged him.

//Chuck: Scan is complete.//Chuck: Give me a bit to fully analyze.//Chuck: We have more on our plate than expected.

Theo clicked his tongue and looked over as the scanning device disintegrated. It was safer to leave no trace and no persistent connection between worlds.

You never knew when someone might be looking for a way to piggyback the line.

“Are you some kind of… alien?” Nancy rested up against the wall, fanning her face.

The shadowed man shrugged briefly before turning to face her. While the inside of the wizard’s tower caused him to be even further shrouded in darkness, his glasses still shone bright white. “No question you ask will land on an answer that is not vague.”

“But you’re something.”

“A forest is something, is it not? You can clearly see a berry bush or a tall redwood, but that isn’t the forest. I may look human, but I am not one.” He paused. “Or at least most of me isn’t. Not my whole. We’re talking trace amounts. This form is just… like a familiar memory.”

“A shape of something you used to know.”

The shadow nodded and turned his eyes back to the staircase leading up into the tower. Several piles of ash lay strewn across the stone floor where the defenders had once stood. “I’ll say it again, Nancy. This isn’t safe for you.”

“My good deed for the day is helping you find your way, stranger.” She stood up from the wall and straightened herself out in defiance. “I won’t back down from that.”

“You’ve got principles and spirit.” A hint of a smile emerged from the shadows. “Mortals continue to surprise me.”

“Mortals?” Nancy repeated, but before she could press him further, the odd man had started his way up to the next level of the tower.

Sally wasn’t especially afraid of heights, but she was in no mood to skydive without a parachute. She ground her sharp teeth together as the large vessel above them completely shadowed the rising platform. Archie seemed nonplussed, one hand still held high as the jutting tower continued to grow. Wind whipped around her, ruffling her waitress skirt and cooling the heat of battle from her skin.

It was only a matter of seconds before they reached the underbelly of the ship. The scale of it, now that they were up close, was immense. It dwarfed the Omen several times over. That wasn’t to mention the view of the world from up here, despite trying to ignore it.

“Did you have a plan?” Archie asked. “Or were you just going to knock?”

“I kinda think this was your doing, magic-man.” She shook her head and sighed. “I’m not exactly known for my skills in parlay. So… let’s just go in and murder everyone?”

“If only I were an Aspect of Destruction,” the wizard replied wistfully. “Then I would have smote them from the sky.”

Sally held up the stolen hand and flexed the fingers again. “Looks like I have a disposable hand. I might be able to bring some friends to our little gatecrash party.”

If she could just steal another limb once onboard, then she essentially had a free reload of her Ultimate - even if she lost a hand every time due to energy fatigue. As long as it didn’t work up to explode her entire arm at least.

“We’re here.” Archie lowered his hand so that the dark hull of the ship didn’t flatten his hat. With a click of his fingers, roots grew from the center of the platform, and the barebones of a tree stretched up to press against the bottom of the vessel.

Sally hopped up the branches, getting her footing until she was up against the flat metal. Skeleton key slid into the ship as if it was made of butter, and the zombie slowly carved a circle. Once complete, a hiss of ejected air followed the cut shape jettisoning from the hull. It bounced twice on the platform before spinning off toward the ground.

“Hope that lands on someone,” she murmured before turning her attention to the hole.

“They are currently trying to topple this tower,” Archie stated. “So it is likely. I’d suggest some expediency, as they are about to succeed.”

Sally grumbled to herself before crawling into the space. It wasn’t quite all the way through to a room within the ship, but there was enough space between structural beams for them both to stand. She held a hand down and helped pull the wizard up to join her.

“You know, it’s pretty weird that you’re a man now, instead of a cat.”

Below them, the tower groaned and tipped to the side, falling away to reveal nothing but the long drop down to earth.

“If it’s any consolation, I’m not really human.” Archie gave her a sheepish grin. “In the same way that I was never truly a cat.”

“Yeah, yeah.” She rolled her eyes. “Best stay on my good side unless you want your brain eaten. You’re probably not synced with Sanctuary in this new form, right?”

Archie opened his mouth. His brow then furrowed. “Ah,” he managed.

The zombie was already carving through the next layer of metal. Threats aside, them both falling out of the hole and plummeting to the ground would be a terrible way to end this adventure. They needed to survive long enough to make sure Archie could get back to the Omen, and then worry about Sanctuary.

//Chuck: To keep things brief.//Chuck: Archie’s power increase is noted.//Chuck: I am doing what I can to smooth things out.//Chuck: Make it home safe.

Easier said than done. With a grunt, Sally pushed her new carving up, bending the metal. Just enough space to crawl through. After taking a moment to ensure her footing was secure, she pushed and climbed up into darkness. The back of a storeroom, in the middle of a pack of dark crates. It couldn’t be any more perfect if she tried.

Once she had helped Archie up through the gap, she stepped away from the hole - glad to be away from the looming drop. With her eyes adjusting to the gloom, slim red lights ran along the chamber. Two air vents lined the top of the dark metal walls, and further past all this storage was a wide door to leave the room.

“Could make this a weird mix of Alien and a zombie movie,” she thought out loud. “Or… the direct approach might be better.”

She glanced behind her to see that the wizard was casting something. Symbols of glowing blue hung in the air as he moved his hands back and forth.

“Are you set us up the bomb?” She asked.

“No.” He gave her a brief scowl. “Whatever that means. More of a get out of jail free card. There is still a second ship, so I’d hate to die prematurely.”

Sally bit her tongue. In a way, his death had caused her death - and of all those currently in the System universe. But if Goku could forgive Vegeta, then she could ignore Archie’s prior sins if he was going to use his current power for good.

Once he finished his spell-casting, he gave her a nod.

“Right.” She gestured to the door. “If they have a couple of brain cells between them, they’ll be recalling most of the ground forces back up here. We’d better cover as much ground as we can.”

Sally stepped up to the door and glanced at the lock. It required some form of bio-authentication to open. She reached out with her borrowed hand and pressed it against the screen.

A light just above it turned green, and the door opened with a hiss.
