Tara’s world narrowed to a single objective: reach Ace before he bled out.

Her boots slipped on the blood-slick stone as she sprinted across the battlefield, her Echo Staff clutched in white-knuckled fingers. Through their fragmenting hivemind connection, she felt Ace’s consciousness dimming like a candle guttering in a strong wind. Each faltering heartbeat pushed more blood through the gaping wound in his chest, his life essence spreading across the blackened grass in an expanding crimson pool.

We can’t lose him.

The thought hammered through her mind with the rhythm of her pounding footfalls. She’d watched too many patients die during her paramedic career—good people whose lives slipped away despite her best efforts.

But this was different.

This was Ace, and as much as she wanted to say that the whole team needed him, something pulled on her unconscious mind—a thought that she needed him most.

Raven loomed over Ace’s fallen form, and shadow energy gathered around his talon-tipped appendage for a finishing blow. The corrupted guardian had become barely recognizable as once-human, his body a writhing mass of darkness shaped into a twisted parody of a human silhouette. Even from twenty feet away, Tara felt the chill emanating from him—the cold of the grave, of oblivion itself.

“I’ll make this quick,” Raven’s distorted voice carried across the battlefield. “Then I’ll do the same for your friends. A painless ending. It’s the only kind of mercy I can give.”

Not enough time.

She wasn’t going to make it.

Even with her enhanced vampiric speed, she wouldn’t reach Ace before Raven delivered the killing strike. Conventional tactics wouldn’t work. She needed to try something beyond standard healing. Something drastic.

Something desperate.

Blood Transfusion Protocol was her most powerful and simultaneously dangerous ability, and though she had practiced it as much as possible, she hadn’t been able to use it often in battle. The risk was enormous, the cost to her Soul Meter devastating, but there weren’t exactly any better alternatives at her disposal right now.

So be it.

Tara didn’t hesitate. She slashed her own palm with the sharp edge of her Echo Staff, barely registering the pain as her blood welled against her skin. Instead of allowing it to fall, she channeled her Blood Saint powers, suspending the crimson droplets in mid-air. Her blood transformed, glowing with supernatural energy as it responded to her will.

The hovering blood expanded outward in thread-like strands, weaving themselves into a complex crimson web that shot forward with unnatural speed. The first tendril reached Ace just as Raven’s killing blow began its downward arc toward his exposed neck.

The moment her blood essence made contact with Ace, Tara gasped as their connection solidified. His pain flooded through her—the burning cold of the shadow wound, the desperate struggle for oxygen, the encroaching darkness. Her medical training catalogued his injuries even as her chest constricted at their severity: a punctured right lung filled with blood, three severed major arteries, critically low blood pressure, imminent death.

But she could also feel his determination still flickering beneath the pain—a stubborn refusal to abandon his team even as consciousness slipped away.

I’m here. Hold on. Just hold on.

He couldn’t hear her, of course, but she couldn’t suppress the thought.

Tara poured healing energy through the blood connection, channeling everything she had into keeping his breath going. The crimson thread linking them pulsed with each transfer, growing thicker and more substantial as she fed more of her own life force into the connection.

“No!”

The word tore from Olivia’s throat as she launched herself between Raven and his target. Hydra’s Bane flashed upward, meeting the shadow spike with the grating clatter of metal on metal. The impact drove her to one knee, her body trembling with the effort of holding back Raven’s monstrous strength. Where her blade met his shadow, green light pulsed in hypnotic patterns.

“Deal with Ace,” Olivia called to Tara without taking her eyes off Raven. “I’ll handle this.”

Oh, thank God.

The distraction wouldn’t last. Tara pushed herself harder, doing everything she could to close the distance between her and Ace as fast as possible. As her boots pounded against the ground, step after step, Raven’s amber eyes narrowed, and his attention shifted toward Olivia. Shadow tendrils reformed into a single massive spike aimed directly at the Blood Dancer’s chest.

He took his shot, and he missed.

Olivia twisted out of the way with perfect balance, and her Hydra’s Bane blade flashed through the air. The enchanted metal knocked aside Raven’s spear with an effortless blow before it could strike her or Ace. She did it again, and again, and again, each of her blocking strikes perfectly timed to save Ace from a brutal death.

“You infuriating bitch!” Raven screamed as he launched yet another failed attack.

A cold, mirthless smile spread across Olivia’s face as she pushed back against Raven’s attack. “Flatterer.”

Raven hesitated, his attention momentarily diverted from Ace. The shadow tendrils pulled back, reforming around his distorted body like a cloak of living darkness. His amber eyes studied Olivia with calculating intensity, assessing this new threat.

“The Sisters want your blood,” he growled, his voice distorted by corruption. The sound vibrated through the stone beneath their feet, sending ripples across the black surface of the nearby lake.

“Sounds like they’re going to be disappointed, then.” Olivia’s stance shifted into the opening position of her Blood Dance, and she held Hydra’s Bane at a forty-five-degree angle across her body. “But I promise to make your failure exquisite.”

Marcus crawled toward them, blood streaming from a gash across his temple. Despite his injuries, his eyes burned with determined focus.

Tara knew that expression. He was forming a desperate, dangerous plan.

“Olivia,” Marcus called, his voice strained but steady. “Eastern Tactics, pattern three.”

A flash of recognition crossed Olivia’s face. Without missing a beat, she launched into a complex sequence of movements that carried her to Raven’s left side, and with every step, her blade sliced through the shadow tendrils that attempted to intercept her.

She was baiting him.

The attack wasn’t designed to kill but to provoke—to direct Raven’s focus away from Ace and Tara.

The strategy worked. Raven whirled toward Olivia, shadow tendrils lashing out in furious arcs. The corrupted guardian abandoned his initial target, drawn by the immediate threat of Hydra’s Bane cutting through his very soul.

When Tara finally reached Ace, she slid to her knees beside his limp body. Blood from the stained grass soaked through her clothing instantly, but didn’t care. She dug deep into Essence Perception to detect his injuries.

Once she got a solid reading on him, however, she couldn’t help but wonder how the hell he was still alive.

Up close, his injuries were even more catastrophic than she had feared. The wound in his chest was a ragged hole that bubbled with each labored breath. Shadow corruption leaked from the cavity like a sloughing wound. His skin was terrifyingly cold to the touch.

“Don’t you dare die on me,” she whispered, pressing both hands against the wound. Her fingers slipped on his blood even as she attempted to stabilize the worst of the damage.

Blood Transfusion Protocol still hummed beneath her skin, drawing deeply from her Soul Meter reserves. The sensation was like liquid fire in her veins, painful as hell but serving a greater purpose. She felt her own body weakening as the magic redirected her healing abilities outward, sacrificing her well-being for his survival.

But it wasn’t enough.

With her Soul Meter depleting fast, she used Trauma Conversion instead. Though his wounds were grave, the shadow corruption in his blood fought against her healing efforts. The darkness pushed back against her light like a swirling, insatiable void. Ace’s breathing grew more erratic, the pauses between breaths stretching longer each time.

I need more power.

The shadow corruption in Ace’s wound pushed back against Tara’s healing efforts, the darkness fighting against her light with vicious determination. His breathing grew more erratic, the pauses between breaths stretching longer each time.

Blood Transfusion Protocol was draining her Soul Meter at an alarming rate, and Tara felt her own strength faltering as the magic redirected her life essence into Ace’s failing body.

She kept glancing at Olivia, at Raven, at poor Marcus, and her own breaths quickened. At any moment, a spike could come flying through the air and impale her in the forehead.

It was hardly a conducive atmosphere for healing.

Olivia leaned backward as a spike nearly decapitated her, and it passed close enough that her hair shivered in the rush of displaced air.

“Focus on healing him!” Olivia called, executing a spinning leap that carried her between three shadow tendrils. Her blade carved a perfect figure-eight pattern that severed all three simultaneously. “I’ll keep this asshole busy.”

Marcus joined her defense, his face contorted with effort as blood trickled from his nose. He couldn’t fight directly, but his Mind Drinker powers erupted from his body in sizzling ripples. Each assault manifested as a visible ripple in the air, accompanied by a subsonic thrumming that Tara felt more than heard. When the expanding light hit Raven’s head, it disrupted the man’s concentration with precisely timed attacks.

“Okay,” Tara said under her breath as her attention returned to Ace. “Okay, Tara, just breathe. You can do this.”

Under the others’ protective cover, Tara redoubled her efforts and poured healing energy into Ace’s wound. Her fingers probed the damaged tissue, guiding her power to the most critical areas first—the severed arteries, the punctured lung, the damaged heart muscle. The shadow corruption fought back, but she pushed harder, drawing even more deeply from her Soul Meter reserves.

It still wasn’t enough.

The shadow corruption was too pervasive, too powerful. It seeped back into the wound faster than she could purge it, undoing her work almost as quickly as her magic did its thing.

He was slipping away.

“Fuck,” she hissed under her breath. “Fuckity fuck, fuck fuck!”

Not an eloquent sentiment, maybe, but appropriate nonetheless.

It hit her, then.

What she had to do.

A cold certainty settled in Tara’s chest. This wouldn’t work. As powerful as it was, Blood Transfusion Protocol alone wasn’t sufficient. She needed something more, something that could buff and enhance her magic.

Her gaze fell to the Echo Staff lying beside her, its crystalline core glowing faintly with untapped power. She’d acquired it so recently that she hadn’t fully explored its potential, but desperation sparked invention.

As the cries of pain and shouts of anger echoed across the grassy lakeshore, Tara racked her brain to remember the staff’s powers. It was a level 25 staff, for Christ’s sake. There had to be something useful there.

Sanctuary’s Echo: Strikes against undead or corrupted beings deal 30% additional holy damage.

That explained why Raven had flinched when she neared with the staff, but it wouldn’t help here.

Unbreakable Faith: Staff repairs minor damage to itself over time.

“No, no, no!” She squeezed her eyes shut, trying to remember what else there was.

Healer’s Grace: When used to channel healing abilities, reduces Soul Meter cost by 25%

“Oh, hell yes,” Tara said with a sigh of relief.

Without hesitation, Tara drove the staff into the ground beside Ace’s body. The crystal-tipped end penetrated the grass with surprising ease, standing upright like a beacon. With one hand pressed against Ace’s wound, she gripped the staff with her other and channeled Blood Transfusion Protocol into him.

This had to work.

There was no other option—because if she failed, every last one of them would die.
