Leaves crunched, nettles rasped, and Renner gave a sharp exhale as he lunged. The little witchwood dagger shone like a star in one dark-gloved hand.

And in the blink of an eye, he was stumbling past his mark and Horace was once again perched on the pine bough. I blinked. I hadn’t even seen the Fae move.

“You missed.” The owl’s voice was dry.

Teela snickered from her hiding spot.

“Hey, um… er, sir?” Royce cleared his throat. His face was still notably pale as he stepped towards the tree. “Since you’re, uh, helping?” He glanced at me uncertainly, and I offered a little shrug in response. “Is there a way to free Brin? From her pact?”

“I already asked that,” I murmured, though warmth blossomed in my chest at the question. There he stood, obviously terrified and at a complete loss, yet still putting me first.

“Well,” Horace said wryly, digging one talon into the branch, “I suppose you could always kill the Fae she’s bound to.”

Teela poked her head out. “Yeah, sure, and do you have any suggestions on how we do that?”

“I do not.”

Royce’s shoulders sagged. “Well… is there any other way? Anything we can do to free her?”

“Little one, there is not a thing you can do.”

I pressed both palms against my eyelids. “Horace, I don’t suppose you’re going to be any less cryptic, are you? Offer something a bit more helpful than riddles?”

He just chuffed another laugh. “It’s in my nature to be fickle, Brin. You may take me as I am, or not at all.”

My chest swelled with a sigh. Right. What did I expect, really? I should just be glad he’s here to talk instead of… well, eat one of us.

“Mister, um, Horace. Sir owl.” Royce was turning scarlet. “Can the Thalessians help her? You said there’s nothing we can do, but-”

Renner gave a little growl. “You realize you’re asking a monster these questions, right? We can’t trust a word this thing says. And I’ve already told you-”

“Oh, those paths are too many to count.” Those shining, golden eyes grew luminous. Horace snapped his beak, and the shadows behind him seemed to thicken. “Naming all would take far too long, morsels. I could sing ‘til sunrise of mercy and grace, of veneration and the most noble hearts oathed to guard. Of your bond severed and freedom granted with a flash of gold and fury.”

Royce turned towards me, a smile lighting his features. “There! There! See? We just-”

“Or perhaps,” Horace stared at the deepening purple sky, “I’ll sing of iron chains and blood-soaked snow. Of your screams drowned within a sea of bones.” He smoothed his beak along one wing as the four of us stared up. “Which would you prefer?”

“Fine. I take it back.” Renner’s voice was rough. “Let’s listen to the talking bird.”

I actually laughed. It burst out, hysterical and so raw that it tore my throat. The sound cracked the air, then died as I pressed both hands to my mouth.

“Take heart, little one.” The Fae’s voice was as soft as the evening breeze. “That is only one path, and he will not let you walk it alone.”

I sagged against a tree. Prickly nettles poked at my arms and neck. “Only one path. Right. Because the future is…” What was the word my mom used? I grimaced. “Malleable. It’s… it’s not set in stone.”

“It is not.”

“So anything could happen,” Royce groaned. Broad hands dragged through sandy hair. “That’s what you’re saying? If we ask the paladins for help, tell them what’s going on, they could do anything.” He kicked at a fern. “You know, for someone who came to give advice, you’re really not being very helpful.”

Horace’s tone remained perfectly pleasant as he said, “And for someone who smells like food, you’re really not being very polite.”

“Um.” Teela peeked around the tree again. “But you said you weren’t here to eat anyone.”

“And we’ve established that the future can change, have we not?”

Red eyes narrowed with a glint. She spent a moment looking up at the diminutive Fae with her lips pressed into a pout and her head cocked at a sharp angle, as if debating whether she should politely laugh or turn and flee.

She settled on leaning further out, placing both hands on her hips, and asking, “What’s… uh, that thing you said. Kobolos?”

He shifted a bit, rising up on his talons and swelling with a deep breath. As if savoring the very air. “Ah. An old-blooded beast, little half-Fae. Something that remembered a happier time, when he could walk the earth and revel in its fields. Your sire followed the maddening doom and found himself lost. As many did.”

“The maddening doom?” She crept forwards. Her tail snaked out from beneath the heavy cloak. Its tip twitched, like that of a cat who’d caught sight of a mouse.

“He had many names. Your kind called him the Nightmare.” Feathers gleamed near-black as the Fae’s head tilted back. “I called him… loud. And with a foul taste.” His beak opened wide, then snapped shut with an audible click.

My spine stiffened. I eyed Horace warily, wondering if perhaps that had been a joke of some kind, and couldn’t manage to find my voice. The frozen silence around me seemed to indicate that my friends felt quite the same way.

The owl was quiet for a while, as if the declaration had sent him deep into thought. Then, wings stretching wide and casting shadows to the forest floor, he murmured, “It’s getting late, morsels. I’ve a city to watch, and the four of you should be going.”

A shiver of relief swept through me. Relief and, oddly, something almost like disappointment.

He gave me a knowing look. “Don’t fret, Brin. You’ll see me again soon.”

One side of my mouth twisted up. “Oh? I don’t suppose you’ll be a friendly face at this… event tomorrow, will you?”

To my surprise, he let out a rich laugh. “Oh, little one, I will assuredly be there. Don’t be afraid to say hello.”

“Afraid? Why would I be-”

Sable feathers flapped, catching the breeze and sending the little Fae skywards. Before I could even finish my question, Horace was gliding into darkness with a wink and a coo.

The four of us watched him disappear into the canopy. Once he was out of sight, all the sounds of the evening woods returned in a rush; the hum of crickets, the soft hiss of wind through firs, and the distant rustling of animals amidst fallen leaves.

I was the first to speak. “So… that’s Horace.”

Renner snorted.

Teela fully emerged from her hiding spot, examining the branches above us carefully. “Stars. My skin was itching that whole time. Anyone else get the feeling he was…” she glanced around, then looked upwards with a wince. “I dunno, exactly. Big?”

“Really big,” Royce muttered. He dragged both hands across his face, then gave me a studious frown. “Brin, what was that you said earlier? About your… er, heart?”

I went very still. My gaze fell to the forest floor and my pulse drummed unevenly as I tried to think of some easy explanation, some off-handed response to settle any fears because we had far more important things to worry-

“About it being eaten?” Teela’s voice sounded particularly crisp in the cool air.

“I… that wasn’t…” I searched a patch of ferns near my feet, feeling quite helpless. “Y-you… you heard how he spoke. The riddles and… and he’s not s-straightforward.” Silence. My face grew warm beneath three expectant stares. “And h-he didn’t exactly s-say… that wasn’t what he… It’s n-not… important.”

“Your heart being eaten sounds pretty important, Brin.” Leaves crunched as Rocye moved towards me. Warm hands closed around my shoulders, urging me to look up into a slow, tentative grin. “Hey. C’mon. It can’t be that bad, right? I mean, not when you compare it to all the other spooky stuff he said. To all of us.”

A helpless little laugh clenched my belly. “I guess when you put it like that…”

“At least he hasn’t eaten it. Or eaten you. Or eaten us. Or eaten everyone.” Teela gave an exaggerated shudder. “I got the feeling he… er, could have.”

“Yeah.” Renner was searching the canopy and plum-hued sky. He still held the little silver knife ready, as if he half-expected Horace to dive down with a screech. After a long while he turned his scowl towards me and Royce. “So are you gonna listen to the bird’s ominous warnings? Are you all ready to be done arguing with me?”

I said nothing. Just thought of blood-spattered snow and cold iron as I stared into the evening shadows.

Teela raised both hands palms-up. “Hey, I wasn’t arguing. I believed you the first time.”

Royce made an angry sound in the back of his throat. “I’ll… look, fine. Fine. Maybe we do need to be careful of some Thalessians. Some.”

“All.”

“And maybe we need to be careful of you, too!” he snapped back. The grip on my shoulders tightened. It felt like he wanted to pull me close, and was struggling to stop himself. “Maybe we shouldn’t have a murderer with us!”

I didn’t look up, but I could just hear the sneer in Renner’s tone. “Yeah? Seems to me the murderer is the only one who-”

“Stop.” My voice was soft but, to my surprise, they both fell silent. Crickets sang around us as I took a deep breath. Cold air swept down my throat and nipped my nose. “Saints, you two can keep yelling at each other later. Right now all the matters is helping the city. Keeping people safe. Right?” My gaze lifted to meet despondent blue eyes, then simmering copper ones. Neither of them said anything, though Rocye gave a slow nod.

I pulled free from his grip. “Well, the smoke’s that way. And not too much farther, from the look of it. We should go.”

I drew my cloak tight and, with a quick glance up to catch sight of the pearly plume, began walking. After several paces and, more specifically, the distinct lack of footsteps behind me, my heart began to pound.

Whatever brittle tension was brewing snapped when Teela hissed, “Blood and bones, you two! Be adults! There’s a monster owl flying around and it’s nearly sunset!” Silence. “So glare and walk at the same time!”

Very slowly, three pairs of footsteps sounded from behind.
