Waking up the next morning, Ferris was both excited and nervous. It had been two weeks since Uncle Winton had left him at Scalefort Bastion, and a new team of delvers from Clan Bluereef would be arriving. A group made the trip every two weeks to trade for supplies—including food and delving equipment.

Uncle Winton had said he would volunteer to be part of the team, checking on Ferris and advising him. Ferris would get to introduce Jade to a family member for the first time. How would Uncle Winton react? Hopefully not like Karl…

Ferris also had an appointment to meet Serena in the afternoon to continue work on the research project. The last two times they’d met, Ferris had to apologize for not being able to take part in the first step of the experiments. Today, he would finally be ready.

Breakfast was a quiet affair that morning. Both Brix and Cyrus were absent, likely focusing on their cultivation. Ferris hoped Brix was okay after last night; he knew firsthand how emotions could lead to trouble in the soul. It was rare for Cyrus to miss a meal, but he had also just passed the delver qualification exam. Perhaps he was preparing to delve for the first time.

After eating, Ferris and Jade left to wait for the team from Clan Bluereef to arrive. It was the first time Ferris had left Clan Hollowfield’s compound since arriving, but the process at the gate was simple. His identity token was scanned, and he was sent on his way.

Making his way through Scalefort Bastion, Ferris felt a marked difference compared to within Hollowfield’s domain. The clan had a sense of community, while out here, everyone treated each other as strangers. Shops had guards, and people had set up stands along the street selling all sorts of items. It was more colorful, but also a bit less friendly.

Ferris held Jade close as he slowly made his way through the bastion. No one would harm his familiar on his watch.

Leaving the town’s walls was even easier than the clan compound. There was a line to pay or show citizenship to enter, but nothing stopped Ferris and Jade from leaving. He found a nice piece of grass to sit on and waited near the line of gate portals. A steady stream of delvers entered and exited the gates at the edge of the Wild.

The area between the walls and the Wild always held an eerie danger. Space was a premium inside a bastion, but living too close to the Wild was a death sentence. Eventually, a monster would appear, like the day Ferris lost his leg. As a settlement grew, the buffer between the people and the Wild would slowly shrink.

The closer a city grew to the Wild, the less buffer they had to spot threats. Monsters would sneak by, and people would die. This inevitably continued until a new bastion was found, expensive resources were spent to expand the bastion, or population control was enforced.

Of course, just because the area wasn’t safe to live in didn’t mean it wasn’t used. Groups of people trained outside the walls, as well as a few farms being worked.

Looking out beyond the edge of the bastion into the Wild, Ferris felt there was a similarity to the dome in the slimespace. While Jade’s dome was surrounded by an ocean of acid, the bastions were the only safe place to live surrounded by a metaphorical ocean of monsters. The Wild was too dangerous, even for his uncle. Only tier three cultivators dared to challenge it. The rifts provided a safer place to gather resources and travel, although you couldn’t discover new bastions that way. New bastion stones could only be found in the Wild, making the risky exploration worth it.

There was a morning rush of delver teams queued up to use the line of gates, each team opening up a portal to enter together. Clan Bluereef only needed a single gate, which showed how much smaller his home was. The line was well organized, and soon cleared out enough that the occasional late team could just walk right up to a free gate.

Some of the delvers wore armor, while others appeared dressed casually. Ferris remembered how Uncle Winton, Valda, Dan, and Dean had all magically equipped their armor from soulstorage in an instant. Likely, the ones that appeared unarmed were far more dangerous.

The ones with armor were fascinating to watch. The sheer variety of weapons, armor, and accessories boggled his mind. Back home, almost everyone used matching equipment. Here, for every group that shared similar gear, there was another team with completely different weapons. Additionally, Ferris didn’t see any two teams with the same equipment.

There were far more teams leaving than arriving, but the gates set up to receive would occasionally open a portal. The delvers that exited were usually dirtier, with the occasional bandage, but they typically appeared fine. Mostly, they looked tired and happy to be back in the bastion.

Ferris did see one familiar face: he noticed Cyrus with a group of four others headed out. This must be Cyrus’ first time delving, after passing the examination yesterday. The young man looked much less confident than usual as he listened attentively to another delver in his group. He noticed Ferris, and the two exchanged a casual wave, but they didn’t get a chance to speak before Cyrus had to leave through the gate portal.

After a few hours, Ferris finally saw what he was waiting for. A team of spear-wielding delvers exited a portal, Ferris’ uncle among them. He recognized them all, although he didn’t see Valda, Dan, or Dean this time. Likely, the clan rotated who they sent to Scalefort each trip.

Ferris stood, picked up Jade and his crunch, and headed over to meet them. Uncle Winton immediately noticed him; the second-tier cultivator’s senses heightened above human limits. He grew a huge smile and walked up to pull both Ferris and Jade into a huge hug.

“Ha! I see you made it through,” the man looked down at Jade, “and fully bonded at that. Ferris, introduce me at once!”

“Uncle Winton, I am pleased to introduce my familiar, Jade.” Ferris lifted Jade up higher. “Jade, this is my uncle.” Speaking both out loud and mentally to Jade at the same time took a little practice, but Ferris was able to manage.

“Of all the things, I didn’t expect this. A familiar, possibly, but not a slime,” Winton said, bending down to examine Jade closer. He didn’t sound… unhappy. Just surprised.

Ferris opened his mouth to explain more, but his uncle waved away the conversation.

“Not yet, we have to get someplace a bit more private first,” the man said, glancing around. “Best to always assume someone is listening, damn ears better than a bat.”

Ferris shrugged, then joined the group from home. They made their way to the gate to enter the walled town. Ferris was able to enter without paying the fee by presenting his Hollowfield identity token. Uncle Winton casually chatted about gossip from home, and the familiar names filled a hole in Ferris’ heart.

The rest of the delvers split off, likely to trade for goods the clan would need. Winton took Ferris and Jade to a tea house, and requested a private room. The room was nice, well worth the effort it took to climb up to the third floor. A soft melody of a string instrument hung in the air, drifting in from the hallway. It was tastefully decorated, with carved serpents of some kind flowing in a pattern around the room. Tea and snacks were provided, and then Winton closed the doors with a brief request for privacy.

Sitting at the table, Uncle Winton lifted his bonded spear and closed his eyes. Soon, a light mist filled the edges of the room, somehow circling them while leaving the center clear. All the background noise faded away, including the pleasant music, leaving it eerily quiet.

“There, that will help a bit,” Winton said, opening his eyes again. “Don’t say anything important yet, but we have a bit more privacy than before.”

Ferris set Jade down on the table, then examined the encircling fog. It must be some kind of water magic to deafen sounds.

Meanwhile, Winton had started feeding Jade from the snack trays. Jade was immediately thrilled. The slime demanded Ferris tell them what each item was called, so that it could be properly rated. Winton watched a bit curiously as Ferris dutifully recorded each score in his journal, but the man didn’t say anything.

After the snacks were gone, Uncle Winton pulled something out of his soul storage. Ferris was confused at first, but then recognized the disk-shaped object.

“A spiritual gate device!” Ferris said, excited. It was slightly different than the ones he had used to refine spirit gems, but the basic symbols were the same.

“Ho, so you have learned something since I last saw you. Have you used one?” Winton asked, placing the device on the table.

Ferris nodded.

“Alright then. Enter the device, and I will meet you inside.”

Ferris called Jade over to sit in his lap, and he placed a hand on the device’s gate: a blue gemstone embedded in the disk with a small hand drawn next to it.

Closing his eyes, he tried to meditate to bring himself and Jade into Uncle Winton’s spiritual device. It was hard, as he felt a giddy excitement take hold. He had gotten to see his uncle again! He didn’t know the other delvers very well, but they were from home! He had heard stories from home! So much had happened, he couldn’t wait to tell his uncle everything!

Although… he couldn’t tell his uncle everything, could he? Some things would be considered secrets of Clan Hollowfield. Ferris was forbidden by his oath from simply relaying everything he learned directly to his uncle, but the man was his mentor. Elder Harper had been clear that Ferris couldn’t sell or distribute the teachings, but he could ask for help when he needed. What could he say, and what would break the oath?

Despite the intention to meditate, Ferris found himself considering where he drew the line between his secrets and Hollowfield’s.

Acid Reconstruction and the entire research project to acquire it were a secret of Hollowfield. On the other hand, Ferris had explored the slimespace and discovered how the Consume Pool and Digest River abilities worked himself. Those abilities were his own, and he could discuss them freely… if he wanted to.

Ferris had purchased Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting from the library, but it wasn’t really a secret of the clan. Similar knowledge existed in almost every large organization, and could be purchased on the open market. The contract prevented Ferris from simply giving his uncle the entire thing directly, but he could discuss that he had purchased it and was learning the techniques.

Some people might consider sharing anything at all to violate the oath, even the idea of what technical manuals existed. But the oath was in Ferris’ soul, so it was his intent that mattered. Similarly, if Ferris made discoveries on his own based on Hollowfield’s teachings, he felt they belonged to him.

Of course, Elder Harper might disagree. And if the woman disagreed hard enough, Ferris would not enjoy the consequences. Still, the woman understood that Ferris was a temporary retainer. Hollowfield would never give him access to the truly valuable clan secrets—at least not without something much more binding than this oath contract.

Minor crisis resolved; Ferris finally reached the emptiness of mind required to enter the spiritual gate. Entering through a large doorway, Ferris found himself and Jade in a small, cozy room.

Uncle Winton was waiting for them, sitting in a padded chair in front of a fireplace. The room had the feeling of a warm living room of a modest house, complete with a packed bookshelf surrounded by several paintings on the walls.

“Now,” the man said, “here we can talk more freely. Now, tell me how you’re doing!”

Ferris sighed. Where did he begin?
