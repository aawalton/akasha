The wind blades flew at him fast, so Henry activated Blink to get out of the way, only for the presence of the giant bird to flare out once more and this time, it felt heavier. More targeted. And it had the unfortunate consequence of somehow blowing away his skill, snuffing it out before it could carry him away, while the visible blades of cutting wind were a blink away from cutting him into pieces.

With a panicked, half-uttered curse, Henry threw himself out of the way with Lightning Dash, barely dodging the off-hand attack that would have reduced him to chunks and looked up while arcs of electricity danced along his limbs, remnants of the skill he’d just used. The Windscythe shrieked, its strident call making all those who heard it flinch before it whipped its wings forward, creating a burst of powerful wind that threw Zerathstra away, interrupting his leap. A fraction of a second later, it tucked its head back, smoothly dodging the red-tinged projectile Velistraine had thrown its way, and Henry had to admit; the ease with which the bird was keeping up with the two A-ranks was a little unnerving.

Would he even be able to make a difference?

Even a fraction of a second would help, he reminded himself and shoving the doubt aside, Henry slinked a little closer while the two A-ranks engaged it once more, only for the bird to violently unfurl its wing and blast them all—Henry included—with a powerful gale that sent Henry careening down a few yards and made the giant trees of Thalis sway.

Undeterred, Henry resumed his approach. Though his skin definitely felt a bit more clammy. Hearts hammering in his chest, Henry fought to keep the panic from sending him back into hiding. He felt so… outclassed. And it made sense, this wasn’t just any random A-rank. It was even giving Zerathstra a challenge, while Velistraine was reduced to a supporting role.

What would that even make me, he grimly wondered. Henry hadn’t felt this powerless for a while. But he’d made his decision, and so with a slow breath, Henry continued his approach until he felt he was in range and immediately began calling on his mana. The more he was up here, the more risk of retaliation there’d be, so he might as well make his attempts and get the heck away.

The bird whipped out two more giant wind blades at down at Velistraine, but she couldn’t stop them by herself, which forced Zerathstra to intervene once more lest the attack reaches Thalis while Velistraine took the opportunity to approach from below. In the meantime, Henry pooled the seven Octominds he had available, only sparing the one watching over Maurice.

That would be the only resource he wasn’t burning for this attempt.

Henry poured every ounce of conceptual essence from Thaumaturgy in his Octominds, bolstering each and every one of them until he could feel their presence light up all around him. Next, he tugged on all their attention and activated Telekinesis but before he let the skill reach out into the world, he poured every bit of essence he had left in Predation into its skill nexus. He even added most of what he had left in Dancing Shadows and Watchful Guardian. Predation would give the skill some much needed raw power, and hopefully, the sneaky nature of Dancing Shadows would allow him to slip through some of the bird’s defenses. Finally, the classes’ essence would give the attempt one more push. After all, he was doing all of this to help protect Thalis, and the stopping power of the skill should benefit greatly from Watchful Guardian and the concept the class embodied.

Though now that everything was coming together, Henry realized he might have slightly overdone things and maybe he should have tested this combination in a less dangerous situation. Because deep in his core, Henry felt as if he was holding on to a ball of molten, unstable plasma that just wanted to burn and blow him up along with it. And it also had the unfortunate attention of attracted the bird’s attention, though before it could launch anything his way, Velistraine lunged at it.

Henry used that to slink a little closer and as a precaution, he also suffused his own body and mind with the conceptual energies of Lifeweaving, pouring some of it in Trickster’s Fortitude as well. The cooling, protective magic spread out of his core and down his limbs and while it helped, he knew it wouldn’t make that big of a difference because already, Henry could feel the pressure on his soul grew with every second, and the pulsing flashes of pain were only growing worse with every second. Still, he reminded himself that he’d only get one shot of this.

He had to make it count.

Henry strained to maintain the skill from crumbling away, but he was almost done. And he was ready. So without wasting any more second, he broadcasted his words up to his allies, grimacing as the new skill activation sent another pulse of pain through his head.“[3…2…1…Now!]” he shouted toward Zerathstra and Velistraine, and he activated his magic.

A wave of intense purple burst out of his body, tearing itself out of his soul like a cloud of broken glass while a much larger, vivid haze materialized around the Windscythe king, and if activating the empowered skill had been painful, then it was nothing compared to the feedback he immediately got as his will surrounded the A-rank and clashed against its might.

Henry had known it would be a long shot. But his expectation had still turned to be short. His target had been the wings. Immobilizing those and exposing the monstrous bird's body had been his plan. It was what he activated his skill for. And yes, he’d probably hit his mark, considering the feedback. But Henry had no clue if he had succeeded in immobilizing the creature or not. One moment, he had sensed his own restricting skill wrap around the wings and immobilize them and the next, it was mayhem. Vaguely, Henry registered how the A-rank had flexed its impossibly powerful muscles while it shrieked in outraged. But Henry wasn’t that focus on the external world because as soon as his skill had made contact, one of his eyes had gone blind while an explosion of pain and ache had gone off behind his eyes and down his core. It was as if someone had driven hundreds—no, thousands—of spikes through every square inch of Henry’s body and soul. The pain was—for a second—unbearable. The next moment, Henry’s vision had blinked away. He forgot where he was. He even forgot what had been happening.

Henry floated in nothingness, battered by the mind-numbing pain and aches while he tried and failed to understand what was happening then, just as suddenly as it had been whisked away, his awareness of his surroundings came back. Henry cracked his eyes open and winced from the glaring lights while wind whistled all around him. Groggily, he pulled on his mana and activated another instance of Life Surge, which was painful, but it was needed. Henry believed he wouldn’t have came back to consciousness if it hadn’t been for one of his Octominds who’d already cast the skill and had gotten started on healing him, thanks to the life-saving measure he’d establish with them in the past.

“[Henry, get out of the way!]”

The urgency of Zerathstra's voice jolted him, and Henry’s last moments came back to the forefront. He’d attacked the bird, he realized, and he wasn’t sure of where or which way was up just yet, only that Thalis’s canopy was growing larger in his vision and at the same time, he remembered the clone he’d left with Maurice. Only because the crab was shouting at it so without wasting any more time, Henry triggered his switching ability. But he hadn’t been fast enough.

Right as the skill engulfed his body, a steel-cold pain tore through his limb. He felt it, cut through the first arm, then the second, the shear through the mantle connecting his arms and out the other side. A fraction of a second later, Henry slipped away through the infinitesimally small tunnel connecting him to his clone while through the pain, he sensed the presence of a couple of Octominds weaken at the same time as he lost feeling in five of his arms and then, Henry was back into the light. He was right next to Maurice, somewhere dark. Henry heard the crab saying something, but he still couldn’t see much and while he was bleeding pretty badly, Lifesurge was pretty effective at stopping the flow, and so was Lifeblood Reserve. And just to be safe, Henry popped a Lifeblood Orb into his beak and swallowed it.

That… should help, he believed. His thoughts were like slurry, and while Maurice was saying something, he couldn’t make sense of it. So as the healing energies worked on knitting his limbs back and restoring him, Henry let himself rest for a short minute.

Just enough for the glass shards he could running through his veins and poking into his soul to ease up. He just needed a couple of minutes.

When Henry woke back up, he was in his personal pool at his guest home, underwater. The sky were clear, and the air was quiet.

The birds were gone.

Pushing aside the notifications that clamored for his attention, Henry went to move his arms to readjust his position when he sensed it. The wrongness. Looking down, Henry only saw three of his arms under him. A clear cut had gone through two posterior arms and fully severed part of his mantle and three frontal ones. As he looked down at one of the stumps, he found them sealed. Healed. And when he tried pushing some healing down to one of them, to regenerate his limbs, the magic fizzled away, as if there was nothing to heal.

Huh?

Henry stared dumbly at one of the stumps when a presence bloomed above him, and out came Zerathstra, dropping out from between the branches as if he’d been waiting for him to wake up.

Henry broke his head through the surface and peered about for a moment before he focused on the figure at the edge of his pool. The ape was smiling, and as he sat down with a grunt, he waved an arm toward Henry. “Don’t worry. They’ll regrow. You just gotta flush away the Windsythe’s concepts which… might be a little challenging, considering the difference in rank. But if you go through the evolution, it should be much faster.”

Henry let out a sigh of relief. For a moment, he worried the bird might have somehow done some irreversible damage. But now that Zerathstra had pointed it out, he could feel something foreigns still clinging to where his limbs had been severed. It should be easy to heal once he flushed that out. Plus, now that Henry thought of it, did he really care about permanent damage? He could shapeshift. It was Lesser Shapeshift now, but eventually, he’d be able to take any form he wished.

But speaking of birds…

“What happened to the seagull? And is Maurice alright?”

Clearly, the birds were gone, but while he remembered having teleported back to the clone he’d left with Maurice, he didn’t have eyes on the crab at the moment.

“He’s fine. Training. As for the birds, we didn’t manage to kill the bastard. But that half a second distracted it enough that me and Velistraine managed to land a couple of good hits, and considering the damage done to its flock, it ran away. But it’ll be back.”

That last part sounded heavy, and Henry peered at the ape. “Not the first time it came around?”

“No, it is not,” Zerathstra said with a sigh as he shook his head. Taking a seat at the edge of the pool, he took a moment to dip his hand in the water. “It’s been hounding Thalis for a while, but every time it comes back, it’s a little stronger. And no, I didn’t just let it live for the fun of it. It’s just… really hard to kill an A-rank that actively wants to get away. Especially one that is so mobile. And unfortunately, I can’t chase it away. I gotta sit here and wait for it to come back when it feels ready.”

That sounded like a future problem, but honestly, Henry didn’t have much energy to delve into it.

“The fight looked close,” he said after a moment as he peered inwardly at his Octominds. Two of them seemed to be dormant, as the mini-brains they’d been using as anchor had been severed away. But Henry was glad that didn’t mean they were gone. He could sense them, slowly rousing back to consciousness, though he had no clue which brains they were using, exactly.

Zerathstra nodded. “It had been, yes. Dangerously so. I think… if it hadn’t been for Velistraine and you, I might have had to use some last-resort weapons to chase it away. And the next time it would have attacked, we would have had to just give away the trees and leave.”

That sounded dire.

“In any case. Not to rush or anything, but I’m here to help you flush those concepts away if the evolution process isn’t enough. So if you’re ready, you should get started on it as the longer you take, the more your body would ‘forget’ that you’re supposed to have more arms. And we wouldn’t want that. So. Are you ready to get started?”

Henry looked at his interface and at the many kill notifications waiting for him, then he nodded. “I’ll get started,” Henry said as he read through the prompts. Maybe he wasn’t in the best state to start this, but he’d often felt better after an evolution. Plus, he did affect the fight like he’d hoped. They didn’t kill the bird, but this would do.

It should net him a little extra achievement.

A smile snuck up on him, and Henry found himself chuckling inwardly. He’d managed to affect an A-rank’s fight. He got smacked for it, but still… he had taken another step toward A-rank and with this upgrade, he would take another step at making them his target. Henry was moving up in the world.

“Alright,” he muttered, mostly to himself. “Let’s see what we’re working with.”
