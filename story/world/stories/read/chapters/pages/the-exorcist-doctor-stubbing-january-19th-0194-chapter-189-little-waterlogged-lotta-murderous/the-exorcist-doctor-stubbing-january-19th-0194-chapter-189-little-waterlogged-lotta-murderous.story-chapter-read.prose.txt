The pistol shrimp’s claw snapped, and Maeve moved before she understood what the Myrmur had done. It was just that every instinct she possessed screamed at her to get her head out of the way, so she twisted her entire body sideways and forced the diving suit to follow. The armour, of course, resisted her with all the grace of a locked wardrobe—plates grinding against one another while her weighted boots dragged uselessly beneath her—but somehow, someway, she managed to shift several inches before the water ahead of her compressed into a straight invisible line.

Something screamed past the side of her helmet, and a second later, there was a wet explosion somewhere behind her.

Her eyes widened. She turned her lantern beams just enough to catch a massive shellback flitter spinning through the water… or rather, what remained of one. The fish must’ve almost been as long as she was tall, but the pistol shrimp’s attack had ripped straight through its middle and scattered chunks of flesh, shell, and translucent wing across several meters of dark water.

… Oh.

So that’s why it’s called a ‘pistol’ shrimp.

The Myrmur’s thin legs suddenly uncurling snapped her attention back to it. Whatever stupor her lantern beams had caused was gone now. Its tail folded beneath its body, kicked once, and the entire shrimp shot sideways into the darkness with tremendous speed.

She spun after it. Tried to, at least. The diving suit was still a slow turner, so she lost sight of the shrimp pretty quickly

“Come on,” she hissed, wrenching her torso around while her lantern beams dragged across empty water. “Where’d you—”

Something pulsed red at the edge of her sight, and she threw Mistrender open, the reinforced canopy unfolding in front of her just as another water cannon struck her—but the impact still nearly folded her arms against her chest. Vibrations were stronger underwater, and a brutal one ran down her umbrella’s shaft, through both her gauntlets, and into her metal suit. Every plate around her torso rang. She clenched her teeth as the shock reached her bones, even, leaving her wrists numb even through the armour.

Then another red pulse flashed behind her. She turned and raised Mistrender again, and another shot struck her canopy. Then another came from her right. Then somewhere below at an angle. Before long, she stopped attempting to follow the Myrmur itself and focused on the warning pulses in her vision instead. Whenever red flared somewhere around her, she simply shoved Mistrender towards it and braced with all her might.

Impact. Turn. Impact. Turn again.The pistol shrimp circled her through the dark waters, never remaining in her lantern beams for more than an instant. Sometimes she caught a pale tail disappearing into the black, and sometimes one oversized claw. But most of the time, she saw nothing at all before another compressed blast of water hammered into Mistrender.

“Dearest wife?” Gael’s voice crackled beside her ear. “What’s going on down there? I’m hearing a lot of banging.”

She couldn’t even use her scent latch mutation. Her sealed helmet kept every trace of scent away from her, which, ordinarily, would’ve been a wonderful mutation to be able to use in order to track a Myrmur. Right now, however—another cannon struck from out of sight, and her shoulders jolted as she ate the impact with Mistrender again. This one, however, struck with enough force to knock her several feet backwards before her bloodshackle and air tubes pulled taut again.

She gritted her teeth.

“It’s Mistrender!” she shouted.

“What about it?”

“It doesn’t work properly underwater! My blood cannons have no power, and I’m not disengaging more safety latches than ten when I’m stuck in a hundred kilograms of metal!”

“Why not?”

“Because what if it blows up on me?” she hissed. “You know, you could’ve told me beforehand that you were building a diving suit for me!”

“I thought the surprise would be romantic!”

“What part of this is romantic?”

“Well, technically speaking, we’re alone together on a dark lake—”

“If you’d told me, I would’ve had Arnell make upgrades and adjustments to Mistrender beforehand!” she snapped. “Also, my flexibility’s terrible in this thing! I can barely see anything, and the lantern beams only point where my head points!”

“Yeah, that’s… generally what eyes do too.”

Maeve opened her mouth to bicker again, but then a warning pulse flared too quickly, and she wasn’t fast enough to block with Mistrender this time. The water pressure cannon clipped the left side of her thigh, and suddenly she was spinning violently, her right leg thrown backwards while her suit spun around her.

She lost all sense of direction almost instantly. Her lantern beams wheeled through the dark, her bloodshackle scraped across her waist, and her breathing tubes snapped taut above her. She would curse Gael right about now, but she froze when she felt something cold touch her bare ankles.

Then another freezing drop touched her calves.

Then her knees.

“Oh no,” she whispered.

“What?”

“The suit’s leaking.”

Gael stopped joking,and Maeve looked ‘down’ as best she could. As she’d expected, a thin stream of lake water was forcing itself through a warped seam on her left thigh armor, trickling into the canvas underlayer. It was gathering inside the suit faster than she would’ve liked, first pooling around her boots before creeping steadily upward.

“How bad is it?”

“Not catastrophic, but it is reaching my ankles already.”

She pulled Mistrender close and tried to turn again, but the suit felt even heavier now. She supposed that was what happened when there was water sloshing around her calves while the shrimp continued circling around her, but that didn’t stop her from grimacing anyways. If she were stronger, this would’ve been much easier. Obviously, the diving suit could be better as well, but if she had some means of propelling herself through the water instead of simply hanging here, she might even have been able to chase the Myrmur some time ago.

It’s definitely time to start eating Myrmurs again.

We stopped for six months after we fought the Breeder in Bleakhearth and gave all the points we got to the Saint’s Hands, but… if we’re going to face his Myrmurs underwater, we’ll definitely need more strength.

Still, it was a problem for a later time. Maeve knew it, and Gael knew it too.

“... Use the new move,” Gael urged. “The one we talked about the night before we left for Wraithpier.”

Her face warmed inside the helmet. Of all the times to mention that night. They’d been drinking together on their bed long after everyone else had gone to sleep, and while the both of them were half-sprawled against their pillows, they talked about an interesting potential method of blood circulation through their bloodshackle—among other things she’d never talk about to Liorin and Evelyn. Back then, they’d tossed out new ideas for if they ever encountered a Myrmur neither of them could handle alone, but they were just that. Wild, wild ideas thrown out in a drunken stupor, followed by a bunch of tussling in bed.

But it’s still safer than disengaging more safety latches on Mistrender, isn’t it?

She didn’t have to like it.

Another pulse flared somewhere in front of her, but instead of raising her umbrella this time, she lowered it. She let her arms go slack, and she let her helmet dip forward. The pistol shrimp fired another water cannon, but when it passed several feet beside her head, she remained as still as she could, feigning death.

The water inside her suit was creeping above her knees, but she forced herself to relax. Seconds passed. Then more. Even when the water started to reach her thighs, she held still and waited, waited, and waited some more—until she felt the raw, unbridled bloodlust of the Myrmur swimming straight at her, clearly convinced she’d drowned and was now ripe for devouring.

She waited just a second longer before she jerked Mistrender down and fired a weak blood cannon. As an attack, it’d do almost nothing, but as propulsion, it was just enough for her to clear the shrimp’s head. As the Myrmur passed under her, she seized her bloodshackle with her free hand and threw the chain down, wrapping it around the shrimp’s body.

Got you!

Naturally, the shrimp thrashed violently. Its giant claw snapped again and again, firing water cannons wildly into the surrounding darkness. One tore through a gravehorn eel in the distance. Another blasted apart a cluster of drifting reeds. Maeve clung to the Myrmur’s back while she tightened the bloodshackle around its head, both hands now gripping the chain as the creature twisted beneath her.

Water inside her suit reached her waist.

“I’ve… got it trapped!” she shouted. “Let’s do it!”

Gael cackled through the tube. That was her signal to start pushing toxic blood out of her body and into her bloodshackle. At the same time, Gael forced fresh blood from his own body and into the other end of the bloodshackle, the double circulation roaring through the chain faster than it’d ever been designed to endure.

So as the chain around the shrimp began glowing bright green, the Myrmur began to shriek. It twisted harder, tail snapping, claw firing even wilder than before. Maeve clenched her teeth as the water inside her suit sloshed around her waist.

“More blood!” Gael shouted. “Let’s make it blow!”

Maeve pushed harder in return, and the green glow intensified. The metal links began trembling, and the pistol shrimp screamed one last time before the bloodshackle finally reached its limit.

The chain exploded, and a violent burst of toxic blood erupted from it, swallowing everything around them in a thick emerald cloud.

Gael hauled on the air tubes until even his muscles beneath his coat started burning. The rain had only gotten worse since Maeve went underwater, and between the storm, the creaking boards, and the metal fittings scraping over the railings, he could barely hear anything coming from the speaking tube—barely anything except for bubbling noises, of course.

“Dearest wife?” he called, bracing one boot against the railings and pulling again. “Dearest wife? Gimme a sign if you’re still conscious. I’m willing to eat your curses and insults, and I promise I won’t kill them with the symbiote elixir.”

But only more water gurgled through the speaking tube, which made Gael’s grip tighten. For once, he didn’t have a light-hearted joke ready. Their bloodshackle had exploded exactly as intended—maybe with slightly more force than he’d expected, since even he felt the vibration all the way up here—but her diving suit had already been leaking before them, and the explosion might’ve warped another seam. Maeve could definitely hold her breath longer than the average man, but surely nobody held their breath well while trapped inside a flooded iron coffin sixty meters underwater.

Fergal grabbed one of the thicker hoses beside him. Jin took another without being asked, while Liorin wrapped both arms around a third hose and leaned backwards until his heels skidded across the deck.

“Pull!” Gael shouted.

All four of them hauled together. The tubes rose inch by inch from the black water, dragging heavy loops across the railing. Several connections scraped against the rotten timber hard enough to gouge channels into it, but Gael couldn’t care less about the barge. Rebus, Bran, and Nixy were already scrambling around their legs, keeping the hoses from knotting while Evelyn, Vivi, and Cara shouted whenever one threatened to slip back overboard.

“Something’s coming up!” Vivi cried, staring into the water with her eyes glowing faintly red. “It’s her—it has to be!”

Gael pulled harder. Soon, a round metal helmet broke the surface. Then the shoulders came, and then the rest of the diving suit emerged, hanging vertically beneath the tubes like a drowned iron corpse. His stomach dropped.

“Get her over!”

Fergal and Jin seized an arm each, and together, they dragged Maeve over the railing and dropped her onto the deck. Water immediately began leaking out through the warped seam in her thigh, but not nearly fast enough.

Gael dropped to his knees beside her and grabbed the helmet locks. His fingers slipped once. He swore, tore off one glove with his teeth, and tried again. The first clasp snapped open. Then the second. Then the third.

He twisted the helmet free and ripped it off, allowing a flood of lake water to pour across the deck. Maeve’s head lolled forward with it, her hair plastered over her face—and for one, terrible second, she didn’t move.

Then she coughed. Water sprayed from her mouth, and she bent forward as far as the suit allowed, coughing and spitting while Gael grabbed her shoulders to hold her upright.

“There we go,” he muttered, relief loosening something in his chest. “Keep doing that. Breathing’s one of your better habits.”

Cara and Vivi rushed in with towels. Cara shoved Maeve’s wet hair away from her face while Vivi wiped the water from her eyes and cheeks with careful, nervous movements, but Maeve continued coughing until she finally managed a proper breath. Then she looked up at Gael, and the glare was weak, exhausted, and absolutely murderous.

“You,” she rasped.

Gael gulped. “Yes, dearest wife?”

“... I’m not going underwater again until you upgrade the suit.”

Gael stared at her for a moment before laughing softly.

“Wasn’t planning not to.”

And while the two of them looked at each other for just a bit longer, Rebus, Bran, and Nixy were leaning over the edge of the barge to inspect the waters below. Three heads shifted left and right, left and right, as though they were following something beneath the surface. Finally, Rebus straightened and looked back at the rest of them with glee on his face.

“The Myrmur’s all messed up!” he shouted. “Let’s reel it up!”
