Don tread along the corridor downward in the dungeon. He faced and killed two more lizards in the early parts of the tunnel, but as the vegetation and light grew scant, the attacks stopped. It was pitch black in the dungeon now. Without a light source or Night Vision any person stumbling here would be helpless. Don even considered upgrading his evolution again for a 180 points, but decided against it. He could see reasonably well into the mid range, and his reflexes were fast.

But are they fast enough for a dungeon that has the upper recommendation of level 15?

He supposed he would have to find out. This was the time he would need to decide what kind of a person he wanted to be in this new reality. A coward who backs down, or someone who pushes forward and excels? He wanted to be the latter, so despite his misgivings, he pressed on.

As the vegetation died off, the tunnel became dark, cool and dry. Don tried to listen but there was not a single sound or movement anywhere. So he pushed forward, stemming his anxiety. His Survival Instincts weren’t exactly flaring up, but he felt unease. Soon the drums started. A heavy, slow rhythm of two drums beating together like a heartbeat. Tu-dum…. Tu-dum…. Tu-dum…

Don had the eerie feeling he was the reason the drums started. Whether something had actually scouted ahead and seen him coming, or if the System just had an uncanny flair for drama, Don couldn’t tell. But he’d find out soon.

The air was getting warmer and Don felt a slight breeze come at him from ahead. The air smelled sulphurous, very close to rotten eggs.

There was also something else. Don couldn’t quite describe it, but there was a certain… violence in the air. And as he followed the tunnel downwards that feeling intensified. The tunnel stopped sloping downwards and soon he started to see faint light. Not the blue strange glow of the luminescent flowers. This was torchlight. And yes, soon enough, he saw two braziers at the tunnel’s doorway leading into another cavern.

Don stepped between the two braziers and into cavern.

The cavern was warm, wet and smelled of stagnant water and swamp bubbles. And something else—smoke and old wood. There were hundreds of torches and braziers scattered around the room. It illuminated the whole space, but cast shadows in every corner.

It was a swamp. Patches of murky water broke up the floor into uneven islands of mossy stone and wet soil. The edges were unclear, the water looked shallow, but who knew what foothold truly held and what sucked you in. The water was dark, and Don wondered if there were any monsters inside.

In the middle of the cavern, the swamp seemed converge around a pool of absolutely black water. It bubbled faintly, this little pond of murk and shadows. Don felt an involuntary shudder as he imagined falling into the water.

All the while the drums beat steadily, but Don knew not where the sound came from.

Instinctively he looked up and saw that the swamp was only half the setup.

Above the wetlands, hanging from the walls and ceiling, was a network of structures.

Golden wood. Same as the balcony. Same as the shacks outside. That buttery yellow bark, smoothed and curved into platforms, bridges, and walkways. Everything was connected by wooden ladders, stairs carved straight into the trunks of suspended supports, and rope bridges that swayed faintly as if recently used.

It looked like a village built by people who hated the ground. Dozens of huts and platforms at different heights. No real symmetry as if the architects didn’t care or there was no time.

There were layers to it as the structures spanned dozens of meters into the ceiling.. The lowest platforms were maybe ten feet off the swamp floor. The highest were up in the shadows, impossible to reach without climbing. A few bridges were snapped, hanging loose like wet vines. Others stretched taut across the whole cavern, linking clusters of huts together in strange clusters.

Don stepped forward slowly. Immediately the wet ground bubbled under him and his feet were sucked into the earth.

Immediately Don’s Survival Instincts flared up. It felt exactly the same as having stepped on a spider’s web. The ground started bubbling and Don saw something slither. From the center of the room, perhaps from the direction of the pond, something started to slither towards him. There were dozens of them. Slimy black snakes that seemed to have no tail. Don started running, but one of them caught up to him and coiled around his leg. He felt a sting, as if the snake had bit.

[Health at 95%]

Immediately Don cleaved it off with his claws. He saw that it was not a snake, but a tentacle.

In the next second, three more were already coiling around his legs. Don cut them all in frantic swipes and started running. It was a stumbling race in a terrain that was naturally against him. The tentacles kept chasing him and Don darted from solid looking ground to another. With sheer luck, he managed to avoid landing on false ground and sinking into the swamp, because that would have surely been his doom.

By the time he hit solid rock, a dozen tentacles were nearly on him. His instincts were blaring at him, and Don didn’t have time to think. He jumped upwards and managed to grab the hold of a ledge of these yellow wood platforms. One of the tentacles manages to wrap around his ankle. Don grunted as it bit into him, with whatever barb or suction cup it had.

[Health at 88%]

Whatever it was, Don needed to worry about it later. Other tentacles were coming at him. He heard a deep rumble coming from the pond in the middle of the cavern. Don pulled himself up as the tentacle was trying to force him back down.

While Don struggled in this tug-of-war of life and death, the drums kept beating in a steady rhythm, uncaring and eldritch, as if the dungeon was watching his performance. Tu-dum…. Tu-dum… Tu-dum…

Don struggled, performing the toughest muscle-up of his life. His System enhanced muscles were straining under his skin, and his Energy was plummeting as he fought with the tentacle. It went all the way down to 60% before finally Don felt he could drag his body forward and he won the momentum. The tentacle didn’t snap, but it seemed to give, as if dislodged. It slid off his ankle and Don made sure he was safely deep into the platform before he collapsed on it and laughed in wild relief.

“Holy shit…” Don muttered.

He pulled himself into a sitting position and peered down into the pond from the platform.

A terrible, monstrous face was staring up at him. A dozen pitch-black, bulging eyes watched him with an ancient gaze, as twice as many tentacles waved around the massive face in the air, all black and slick like eels made of ink. The body was green and black, and Don could not say anything of the shape, except that it was large and knobbly, covered in a thick oily membrane. But the most unsettling thing about the monster was its face.

The face was white and completely still like a mask. Completely smooth, except for the wide lamprey maw, which closed and opened rhythmically, revealing a round row of sharp teeth each time.

[Morshoff, Terror of the Deeps level ??? Dungeon Boss Monster]

“Goddamn you are one ugly motherfucker,” Don muttered.

The tentacles waved around on the swamp floor, slithering about, as if feeling for movement. They did not reach for the platforms. When nothing happened, the monster let out another rumbling groan and submerged back into the dark water.

Don shook his head. This was something completely new. He had absolutely no idea how he was going to defeat this enemy.

“But I’ll find a way,” Don muttered. “I have to. I won’t lose. I have to keep evolving.”

Don watched silently at the dark pond which bubbled idly. All the while the drums kept beating, as if satisfied Don had survived the initial encounter with Morshoff.

Tu-dum… Tu-dum… Tu-dum…
