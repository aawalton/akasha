505th Floor Ranked 1 – RECORD HOLDER (Completion Time – 0.0001 sec).

Xavier glanced at the abbreviated titles that popped up in his vision. After receiving so many of these things, he’d asked the System to shorten them. He could have just suppressed them entirely, but he liked seeing the titles—even if he didn’t feel like reading their full descriptions.

The stats he received from the last over two-hundred floors were a nice boon, and he’d added many thousands of points to all his attributes, but they weren’t as satisfying as he’d expected them to be.

Even gaining the record titles for the floors didn’t impress him much anymore. It had become… routine.

Easy.

After breezing through so many floors he was beginning to wonder if there was any challenge left for him in the tower, but god that was an arrogant thought considering he was only halfway through.

Standing in the Staging Room, he chuckled, muttering, “Only halfway,” to himself.

No one in the history of his universe—or his entire corner of the multiverse—had managed to get this far in the tower in such a short time.

It was absolutely insane…

But for Xavier Collins, absolutely insane was par for the course.

He tapped his foot on the floor, sighing after having opened yet another loot box that didn’t contain anything all that interesting. The items in it were plenty powerful and valuable, but as he now had the wealth of two galaxies behind him, and just about all the time in the world to conjure up spirit coins, wealth and items had lost much of the interest they used to hold.

And even after clearing all those floors, he really wasn’t all that much closer to reaching B Grade. Gaining Mastery Points as a C Grade took quite a bit of time, and something told him the System was still restricting him in that area.

He was Level 310. He still had 90 levels to go… Despite all he’d achieved along the way.

If only I’d gained the Mastery Points from killing the World Destroyer… I wonder how much I would have gotten for that.

The System had been too stingy to even offer him a fricken title for killing the World Destroyer. He hadn’t been paying much attention to the System’s notifications for a good long while, but that hadn’t escaped his notice.

Xavier glanced at the ceiling. “You think I’m strong enough already, is that it?” he muttered. “Or it because I keep breaking the rules?” He tilted his head to the side. “I’m breaking the rules to serve your goal, aren’t I?” Think you’d be grateful.

When he’d gained the Immortal Soul lens, still no System notifications had popped up. It didn’t recognise such abilities, despite their obvious power.

Xavier walked back over to the door to the next floor and sighed. The Tower of Champions. He’d been looking forward to returning here. So far, it had been an utter disappointment.

He reached for the doorhandle and hesitated, a thought flickering into his mind.

Not for the first time, he wondered if he should simply push straight to the one-thousandth floor. Roln had warned him against that, but Roln wasn’t here, and Xavier had acquired a new ability or two since then…

How hard could it be? The thousandth floor is the realm of A Grades, but I’ve defeated an A Grade before…

Once more, he felt frustrated at the System’s insistence on banning any and all information about the tower floors he hadn’t been to from him. Oh how he wished he could just ask Adranial what was on that floor.

How bad could it be, really?

Xavier pulled his hand back. The last time he’d stepped through a portal with nothing but arrogance and bluster it had ended with Palini getting killed. As curious as he was, he wasn’t going to let that happen again.

He breathed in.

Patience.

He breathed out.

Cultivate patience.

He breathed in.

Then sighed.

Frack it.

He grabbed the handle.

Welcome to the Tower of Champions!

Please select the floor you wish enter.

Available floors: 1-1,000

Xavier gritted his teeth and willed the floor he wanted.

Entering the 750th floor.

There was no air.

His Force Armour activated instantly, allowing him to breath. Even if he didn’t need it, breathing was a nice thing to be able to do.

Time Alteration was already active.

His heart beat fast, his breath rapid. He activated The All-Seeing Eye and simultaneously pushed out his spiritual sense—looking through the Immortal Soul lens.

In less than a moment, the entire area was mapped out in his head.

The first thing he looked for was the door back to the Tower of Champions.

There was none, which meant the floor needed to be completed before he could return.

Xavier was standing on what appeared to be a barren moon. It reminded him of one of the Hell Moons—that first one he’d been on, before going down the descents—but this was somewhere entirely different.

A massive planet took up a third of the sky. It looked a little like Earth, if the people of Earth had managed to populate every single inch of it. It wasn’t that there were many cities on the planet’s surface—it was more like one vast city had consumed every other one as it continued to grow from one continent to the next. Massive bridges connected the different landmasses, and even those bridges had buildings on either side of them, apartments that had at least two hundred floors each.

Xavier peered past the planet to several objects hovering through space. They looked like ships. Spaceships

Okay, so several objects was putting it lightly.

There must have been millions of ships. An armada. Energy beams shot forth from the lead ships, heading straight for the planet, frozen midstream. If his time dilation field hadn’t been active, those beams would have hit as he’d arrived.

That must happen for other Champions…

He took a step forward. Only then did a notification appear.

Welcome to the 750th floor of the Tower of Champions!

You have entered the middle of an intergalactic war.

For the past three million years, the Empire of Servitude has been waging a war against everyone in their path. Their five-million strong Armada is captained by low-level A Grades, with one-hundred thousand B Grade soldiers on each ship.

Xavier took a second to gulp some air as he read that.

Holy crap. What in the hell did I just walk into?

His heart started beating even faster staring at the millions of ships hovering out there in the black, frozen outside his time dilation field. The All-Seeing Eye allowed him to get a good idea of just how big those ships were. The ships were long—very long—and tapered to a point.

To his eye they looked more like spears than spaceships.

They weren’t powered by technology in the way those from Earth would have imagined. They were powered by energy—powerful runes and spells. They’re shielding was strong, but it wasn’t strong enough to resist his Immortal Soul lens from peering inside the ships.

The planet before you is the world of Galadsea. Ruled by the Queendom of Serrilia for the past two-million years, their society is a rarity within the System. Once they finished expanding upon their planet, they did not continue pushing their rule onto yet more planets in their sector.

Queen Aecia of Galadsea has summoned you to help defend against the Empire of Servitude’s armada.

Xavier slowly inclined his head. Two million years, and they stayed on their own planet?

A peaceful existence… an admirable one.

But a lonely one. He had trouble imagining a civilization that just… stopped striving to reach farther.

He stared off at the three-million strong Armada.

Once again, he looked for an exit back to the Staging Room and failed to find one.

There was more to the notification. He read the last few lines and widened his eyes.

The System has decided Queen Aecia of Galadsea’s cause is unworthy.

Conflict is the main driver in the universe; cowering upon a single planet for millions of years is not.

Therefore, Champion, you are given a choice:

You have 5 seconds to decide.

Xavier released a long breath. “Son of a bitch.” He glanced up at the sky.

The System had never once betrayed someone who’d summoned a Champion. There were times when both sides of a conflict had summoned a Champion, or no one had summoned a Champion, and he was given a choice between the different sides.

But this? It went against everything he knew about the tower floors and the rules around summoning a Champion in the first place.

It felt like the System had lied.

Xavier clenched his fists.

He also couldn’t help but think that this floor was too difficult. Three million ships. Three million A Grades, and countless more B Grades. Did the System expect Champions who’d reached the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor of the tower to be able to deal with these kinds of enemies?

Maybe it does. Maybe if I hadn’t skipped over two-hundred floors I would have found that out before arriving here…

There was so much more information he wanted before he made a decision.

Would this be like the wave floors he’d experienced? Because if it was anything like the fifth floor, where he’d battled against the Endless Horde, then this could be just the opportunity he’d been looking for.

Surely the System wouldn’t simply let the armada crush the planet… but then again, why wouldn’t it?

He tapped his foot on the barren moon’s rocky ground, kicking up dust in the low gravity. He already knew what decision he wanted to make, and he already knew how foolish it was.

There was no exit plan.

Over the past two hundred floors, he’d ran enough experiments with his universe-spanning portal to know that. When he’d first arrived back at the Tower of Champions, he’d thought there was a chance it might work—the System had never restricted any of his powers in the past.

But there was definitely no chance of him simply escaping a floor when the System hadn’t placed an exit there for him.

Well, that’s not entirely true… a little voice inside his mind whispered. If I’m desperate enough, I could always enter the void.

He pushed that thought away and brought the notification back up.

Therefore, Champion, you are given a choice:

You have 5 seconds to decide.

He released a breath. I choose… to fight for Galadsea.

A white light gathered around him, and he was instantly teleported down to the planet. Even now, he felt a slight sense of disorientation whenever the System teleported him.

He was standing inside a grand throne room.

How many times have I found myself standing in a place like this?

This throne room, however, was nothing like the throne rooms he’d encountered in the past. Not even The Collector’s throne room, with that domed ceiling with the vacuum of space on the other side, could hold a candle to this place.

“Whoa,” he said under his breath.

Everyone in the room was frozen outside his time dilation field, so for a moment he didn’t pay any mind to the queen and the others around her. He simply walked across shining silver floor to a vast, wrap-around balcony.

The throne room wasn’t inside a castle. Not a traditional one, anyway. It was atop a tall skyscraper that overlooked a city that stretched on farther than even his natural eye could see, because at the farthest reaches of his vision the world began to curve, and the rest of the city was out of sight.

He shook his head in wonder.

He’d seen all this with The All-Seeing Eye. With that, and his Immortal Soul lens, he’d determined that the population of this world was well over twenty billion. But it was one thing looking down on the planet and ascertaining that, it was another thing entirely seeing the throng of people in the streets that stretched onward seemingly forever.

What struck him most was the fact that down there wasn’t an army, ready to fight for their planet, it was simply… a mass of people.

There must be an army somewhere. How else would this planet have lasted so long? You can’t survive for two million years by accident…

He turned his gaze upward, toward the armada on its way, focusing on them with The All-Seeing Eye. On all the floors he’d been on, all the worlds he’d seen, all the armies he’d decimated—he’d never faced a threat like this before.

Xavier grinned as he felt no small hint of fear within him.

This was what he liked about the Tower of Champions.

This was what he’d been looking forward to.

He turned around and took in the throne room. In the middle of the room was a glowing projection showing the Empire of Servitude’s armada. A beautiful elven woman with green eyes and raven black hair plaited around her golden crown stared at the projection with her hands folded behind her back, a look of great concentration lining her otherwise smooth face.

Powerful looking men and women surrounded her wearing robes and suits of armour with no particular common style. His Immortal Soul lens identified each of them as A Grade.

And each of them was more powerful than Arrack had been.

I wonder how powerful the A Grades in the ship are…

Xavier drew in a breath, then let it out slowly. At the same time, he expanded the time dilation field to encompass the queen and the ten A Grades. The woman’s gaze flicked to him—all their gazes did. She looked him up and down. He wore his black robe with the hood back, his wings expanded behind him, Bones sheathed at his side.

Queen Aecia’s eyebrow rose as she looked around him. “Where are the others?”

“I don’t have a party.”

Queen Aecia blinked. “Solo. Foolish,” she uttered under her breath, but her gaze was still searching the room. “What I meant was, where are the other parties of Champions?”

“Other parties?”
