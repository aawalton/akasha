Day 357, 6:00 AM

The Guide nearly gives me a heart attack. Its message appeared not a second after BSD’s warning vanished. I wait, my muscles tense, ready to jump away left or right from lightning about to strike me, but there’s no attack, nor any further messages from the Guide. I feel like I’m an annoying cicada chirping all night and the sleeping giant has found me. That still means I’m about to get squished.

Tense, I await the imminent mental or physical assault, but all remains quiet. A minute passes, then I head for Edna.

“Edna, something is very wrong with the Guide, it just spoke to me. Again.” The woman nods, her face blank, and for a moment I fear the Guide had possessed her, but then I recognize the hollow look of those who had carried out their revenge. “I have stashed away a couple dozen women and children, as well as three inquisitors who claimed they knew nothing about the church’s involvement with abominations.”

She looks me in the eye, her pain and anger getting swallowed by the helpless confusion.

“What do I do now?”

I don’t know what to tell her. She’s a broken woman who survived for decades in horrible conditions, surviving on the dream of exacting her revenge, and now she got what she wanted. All those years came crashing down on her psyche, and she’s exposed with no more goals left in her life.

“Retire. Go to Hadriuse’s abode, live your life like a healer or an academic, and make the world a better place for the sake of all those you wanted avenged. They would have wanted a legacy of life and prosperity, rather than death and destruction.”

I don’t believe a word of what I preach. If I were to die to a plot, I would want all involved in my demise to suffer fates as gruesome or worse, but telling her that would be like putting out a fire with a bucket of gasoline.

Edna nods, her eyes less hollow, and I’m kind of happy about her naivety. If she picks herself up and becomes a healer after all she has gone through, she has a heart bigger than mine.

“What about the city?” I ask, and she shrugs. “Someone needs to enforce law and order. With the ruling class gone, things will turn ugly quickly.”

Edna still shrugs. Just like she said, she doesn’t give a damn about these people, which goes double for those living so close to the church’s seat of power. They can eat each other as far as she’s concerned.

“We should find some local administrator or someone similar to take up the leadership role before we leave, but before that, we should meet up with Gila and Lucy.”

Edna follows me like a puppy as we leave the city while an army of treants still guards the central citadel.

I sense terrified people staring at us from windows cracked open and from the safety of dark alleys, but we ignore them as we leave the city unchallenged. The rendezvous point is ten minutes away from the gates, and the girls are there waiting. Lucy is a bag of nerves, probably out of genuine worry rather than hormones, and Gila—.

***

Gila stands on a podium addressing a class full of young people, an even twenty. She looks good. She’s older, probably in her mid-thirties, her back straight, her voice solemn, her clothes smart and a perfect fit with a lovely red flower made of frill spiraling above her heart.

“Your job as mages, even as the journeymen you are about to become, is to safeguard humanity from all invading forces and unsavory elements. The path you are about to set your foot on is difficult and treacherous. A slightest miss-step may bring about the downfall of our civilization and the entire world.”

She scans the crowd with a stern gaze, chilling the expectant youths.

“Our ancestors once devised a spell which allowed them to peer into the divine. What they did not know is that the divine looked back and deemed our world a source of nourishment. They tried to enter, but the passage was too narrow, so they influenced what they could, the minds of mages and archmages, until they sparked a conflict between the world’s greatest powers. This event is known as the great wormlord war, which ended with the creation of the dungeons and the birth of the cursed Guideline.”

Gila and the classroom ripple, like a drop had fallen into a calm lake, and her voice grows distant.

“The only reason we and our whole world still live is the heroic sacrifice of Griff Rider and Archmage Edna, who gave their lives and rushed into the tunnel beneath the ruined grand cathedral. I was privileged to follow them a minor part of their way, providing what limited help I could and witnessing their heroic final days—”

***

I gape at Gila, my jaw slack. My shock is so deep, I’m not even certain what shocked me more, having the vision, realizing Gila is someone with a spark of greatness, or seeing her stand all serious, a smart, petite headmistress addressing a gathering of students.

“What’s wrong?” Lucy’s voice trembles. I must look like I’ve just suffered a stroke or a heart attack. Possibly both at the same time.

Your brilliance is no longer concealed from the eldritch. BSD’s message flashes in my memory.

“Edna, I think we’ve been played, me, you, your ancestors, and it’s been going on for hundreds or thousands of years.”

The archmage frowns at me with confusion.

“What if I told you I have just figured out what’s going on?” Her look of confusion remains unchanged. “A group of eldritch monsters from beyond this world are trying to devour it, and they are doing it through the Guide. They have been influencing mages and regular folks alike for generations, and they are probably the ones behind the whole extermination of mages and those magically talented. They eliminated all who could stand in their way, but you survived that purge.”

She hesitates, and I understand her. I’m basically telling her she’s the chosen one, and I myself would never buy that crap.

“We have no other plans, and I know where the answers lie.” I look her in the eye. “I know several archmage level up conditions, and I will share everything I know with you along the way. Then, with all the information, you can choose to come help me fight the monsters plotting to destroy the world, or you can go hide away from the world and become the powerful archmage you have always dreamed of being.”

She lowers her gaze. I’m not really sure what more she would want.

“There’s nothing for you to lose. Either way—”

“Stop, Griff, Fyoor, Aang, whoever you are.” She grits her teeth and meets my gaze. “Yes, I will go with you and see whether you’re telling me the truth or not, but what I’m thinking about right now is how fragile and insignificant we are if others can manipulate us that easily.”

Well, it’s not just otherworldly entities which can change us. All it takes is psychoactive drugs or a nasty fall; hell, even something as basic as pain and infections can completely twist a person’s view of the world.

Again, not what she needs to hear, so I nod.

“Yeah.” A stupid, pointless thing to say, but I get the feeling she wants me to say something.

“So what’s the plan?” Edna asks after several heartbeats of silence.

“We’ll following the tunnel until we find wherever the abominations had spawned from. All the answers should be at the end of that path. The church’s inquisitors I faced mentioned that the wormlords and abominations are a trial from the Holy One meant to cleanse humanity.”

Edna nods. She’s still doubtful, but she trusts me this much at least.

“What about them?” She motions towards the girls with her chin.

“I would appreciate it if you could give Lucy some level of control over the tree soldiers, so she can safely return to Hadriuse’s estate—”

“No,” Lucy screams. “I won’t leave you!”

“You have to keep the baby safe. That’s the most important thing for now.”

Wait, am I really this numb to death? Some cosmic horrors are hiding in the church’s basement, and I need to sacrifice myself to save the world, and I don’t even question the thing, and I’m making a plan to kill myself. Really?

I would like to call myself virtuous, willing to become a martyr to save the world full of sentient life, but that’s not it.

I bleed my inner sorrow into my eyes, pleadingly looking at Lucy. “Please?”

The silence stretches long enough for me to think and to analyze myself. I love life. No matter how insane that may sound coming from a serial suicidee.

Serial suicidee. The absurdity of it is eye-roll-worthy. But yes, I do enjoy life, learning, gaining skills and knowledge, learning the silly rules of the board games in Hadriuse’s abode and playing against myself ate a bit of time for quite a number of loops, and helped me enjoy them.

The direction my thoughts are spiraling in is fascinating. It goes to show I do fear permanent death, and I’m trying to think of anything else.

“All right,” Lucy says, ending my runaway train of thought. “For our baby’s sake.”

Baby… Now, there’s a whole new can of wormlords.
