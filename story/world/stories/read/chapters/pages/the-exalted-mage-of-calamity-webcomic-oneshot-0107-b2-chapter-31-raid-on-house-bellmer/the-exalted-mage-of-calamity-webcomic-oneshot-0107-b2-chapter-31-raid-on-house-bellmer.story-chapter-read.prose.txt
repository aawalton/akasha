Several years ago.

“Tell me, Lord Bellmer…” Charlotte said sweetly. “What do you know of the Lairaka?”

The man in front of her made a strained, choking sound.

He was in his forties, dressed in a night robe now stained blood-red. His hands trembled, fingers grasping at his throat. His eyes flickered from the woman in front of him to the closed door leading out of his chambers, still expecting his guards to come rushing in.

But they never would.

The entire mansion was silent that night. Quieter than a graveyard. Across the first floor, bodies littered the ground. Maids, butlers, the house mage, armored guards. All dead. Slit throats, severed limbs, stab wounds, and every other kind of death one could imagine.

But that wasn’t all. Not only were the servants dead, but so was the family. Lady Tia and her children, her son and two young daughters, had all been slain in their sleep.

None of them had been enemies of Vermillion. Not exactly. They were simply secondary targets. Collateral dressed up as obstacles in the mission. No one could be left alive. Not servants, not witnesses, not even the smallest bud of future consequence.

A mantra of Vermillion: secrecy at all costs.

Nasha stood behind Lord Bellmer, beside a low wooden crate. One boot was planted on the ground while the other rested on top of it. A thin wire ran from both her gloved hands, coiled in several sharp loops around the nobleman’s neck. It was not tight enough to kill him, but only because she didn’t want him dead just yet.

Charlotte stood in front of him with a thin knife in one hand. It wasn’t quite a dagger, yet it was far too small to be used in a kitchen. Why anyone had made a blade so small, no one could guess. Perhaps that was what made the sight of it worse.

“Please,” Lord Bellmer rasped. “I don’t know who sent you, but I can pay you double. Triple. Please. Whatever you want, I’ll give it to you.”

Charlotte smiled. “I know. But I don’t think I need your permission to take what you can offer.” Her eyes flicked toward the girl behind him. “Vania.”

At the mention of her name, the wire tightened around his neck. A choked cough tore from his throat as he clawed at the loops. His skin reddened, and the thin wire was soon slick with blood. After what must have felt like an eternity for him, the wire loosened.

He leaned forward slightly, coughing spit and blood.

“Three nights ago, at the Dareshire auction, you purchased a sealed reliquary,” Charlotte said. “A blackwood box. Silver clasp. No listed origin, and no public appraisal. Something you bought for dirt cheap without knowing its true value.”

Lord Bellmer looked up at her with shaky breath.

Charlotte tilted her head down to meet his eyes. “Where is it?”

“I… I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

The wire tightened again, pulling Lord Bellmer back against the chest as he grasped at his throat.

“I’ll ask you one more time,” Charlotte said. “Where is the reliquary?”

Bellmer fell forward as the wire loosened once more. Blood dripped freely from his neck.

“If… if I tell you, please, leave my family out of this,” he said, the words sputtering through spit and blood. “I’ll pretend you never came here. Please.”

Charlotte looked up at Vania for a brief moment, then back down at him. “Deal. I won’t bother with your family anymore after this.”

The man took a few seconds to steady his breathing. Then he answered her.

“The reliquary is in the east wing,” Lord Bellmer rasped. “Behind the portrait in my study. There’s a false wall behind it. I had it placed inside a warded lockbox.”

Charlotte’s smile returned. “What kind of ward?”

“A family blood ward,” he said quickly. “It only opens to my blood. Take me there and I’ll retrieve it for you.”

Charlotte hummed softly, almost pleased. “Family blood ward, huh? So… any blood from the family would do? Including your wife’s or children’s?”

Lord Bellmer’s face went still. For a moment, he only stared at her, no longer seeming to care about the wounds across his neck. His eyes shifted subtly toward the locked bedchamber door. Toward the silent hall beyond.

“Why?” he whispered.

Charlotte’s expression didn't change.

“Why would you ask that?”

Charlotte straightened and turned the small knife between her fingers. “Your wife is closer to the east wing. That’s all.”

“My wife…” His voice broke. “You said you would leave my family out of this.”

“Oh, I did,” Charlotte replied, looking down at him. “I said I wouldn’t bother with them anymore.” Her gaze flicked toward Vania.

Vania immediately tightened the wire as hard as she could.

“No one will.”

The sounds of struggling, bumping, and thrashing echoed out into the hall. Violent at first, but after barely a dozen seconds, they fell quiet.

Then the door clicked. Two women walked out of the master bedroom, and no one else.

A while later, they arrived at the location the man had described. A painting hung high inside the study. A self-portrait of Lord Bellmer.

Vania walked up and lifted the painting, moving it aside.

As the lord had said, there was a hidden compartment behind it. With a simple push, the thin wall vanished, revealing a small passage half a meter deep that led to a metal safe covered in enchantment.

Vania turned as she heard a thud on the floor behind her.

Charlotte had dropped the dead body of one of the man’s children. A teenage girl, already slain before they ever reached the lord’s chambers. Charlotte had forgotten she killed her. Might as well put the girl's body to use since it was along the way, instead of the wife's.

With a quick slash, Charlotte gathered some of the girl’s blood on her fingers. Vania stepped aside as Charlotte climbed toward the hidden recess and smeared the blood along the safe’s surface.

The enchantment reacted instantly.

Thin lines of pale blue light spread across the metal, tracing the shape of several overlapping circles. The blood seeped into the grooves as though the safe were drinking it in. A second later, the ward pulsed once.

Then the lock clicked open.

Charlotte smiled. “How convenient.”

Inside was a blackwood box no longer than her forearm, polished smooth and bound with a silver clasp. There were no jewels on it. No markings. No obvious value at all. If one did not know better, it looked like something that could have been sold in any old antique shop.

Charlotte lifted it carefully, and for the first time that night, her expression lost some of its playfulness.

“So this is the Lairaka,” she murmured.

Vania looked at the box.

She felt nothing from it. No mana, no special formation, no curse creeping against her skin. It was just a box. A box an entire household had died for.

A shame, really.

Charlotte tucked it under one arm and climbed back down.

The two of them left the study. The dead girl remained on the floor between them as they passed, one arm bent awkwardly beneath her body. Her hair had fallen loose across her face, hiding most of her expression.

“Tarvess will be quite happy about this,” Charlotte said, weighing the blackwood box in her hand. “After months of searching, we finally found it. A clean retrieval, too.”

Vania walked behind her, saying nothing.

Charlotte glanced over her shoulder. “Who knows? Maybe he’ll give us a promotion, too.”

Vania rolled her eyes. “You’re always talking about a promotion. You know that’s not what I want.”

“Yes, yes.” Charlotte waved a hand dismissively. “You’re mainly doing this for your grandmother. I know. Medicine is expensive, Naturia is hard to get, and Vermillion is the only hand generous enough to offer it.”

Vania’s expression didn’t change.

“But a higher position isn’t a bad thing,” she continued. “More coin. Better jobs. Better information. Maybe you’ll even get to meet Varce one day and ask him for more of the Naturia yourself.”

Vania looked away.

They moved through the mansion’s main hall, stepping over the bodies they had left behind. A guard near the stairwell. A maid half-hidden beside a fallen vase. A butler with one hand still stretched toward the bell rope he had never reached. Even the emergency teleportation array had been cracked.

The house had gone quiet. Quiet before, and quiet after.

Charlotte opened the front doors, and cold night air swept in to meet them.

Beyond the mansion, the courtyard rested beneath moonlight. The stone path glimmered faintly from the evening rain, and the trimmed hedges cast long, crooked shadows over the ground. Past the iron gates, Brackmere slept on, unaware of what had happened inside one of its noble estates.

Everyone sleeping soundly. A blissful ignorance.

“You really think he’ll offer me naturia directly?” Vania asked.

Charlotte stopped at the edge of the mansion’s porch, taking in the night air. “Maybe? Who knows. I can’t imagine it would be difficult for him.”

“I can,” Vania said. “Even the royals have a difficult time acquiring naturia.”

Charlotte glanced back at her.

Vania’s gaze remained fixed beyond the courtyard, toward the quiet iron gates. “If it were easy, I wouldn’t need Vermillion.”

For once, Charlotte didn’t laugh immediately.

The blackwood box shifted beneath her arm as she looked up toward the moonlit sky. “Maybe. But that’s why you need to climb higher. The people at the bottom beg for scraps. The people at the top decide who gets fed. Once you’re up there, maybe you can manage your own supply.”

Charlotte stepped down onto the stone path cutting through the courtyard. It had rained recently, and the ground was still wet. The scent of damp stone and rain lingered in the air. Droplets clung to the hedges, reflecting the moonlight like tiny silver beads.

“How long do we do this for, Charlotte?”

Charlotte stopped and turned around.

Vania was still standing on the porch. “All we do is work every day. The jobs are easy enough, but there are other things in life besides commissions. ”

Charlotte held the box in one palm and shrugged. “Like what? All we’re good at is killing.”

Vania stared at her.

Charlotte held the expression for two seconds before chuckling. She stepped closer, moving back up the stairs. Then she looped her arm through Vania’s and leaned against her side, as if they were two ordinary women taking a walk after dinner.

“I’m teasing,” she said. “Don’t look at me like that.”

Vania didn’t pull away. Instead, she tilted her head lower, looking at Charlotte’s hair. “You’re always teasing.”

“Oh? You know me so well,” Charlotte said softly. She leaned back and straightened. “But quitting, huh? I’ve thought about it.”

“You have?”

“Yep,” Charlotte hummed. “About the things I’d do if I quit. Maybe I could raise a family.”

Vania blinked, then raised an eyebrow. “A family?”

Charlotte’s smile widened. “A family of tiny assassins.”

Vania’s shoulders dropped, and she sighed.

“Imagine it,” Charlotte continued, sounding pleased. “Little knives hidden in their sleeves. Poison vials tucked into their shoelaces. If they happen to meet a bully in school, swish!”

“Be serious, Charlotte.”

This time, it was Charlotte’s turn to sigh. “I have dreamed of becoming a model.”

Vania turned to her. “A model? Like… a fashion one? The ones in all those snobby clothiers for nobles?”

“What?” Charlotte looked up at her with a sly grin. “You’ve seen how good I look. And besides, the clothiers do have good tastes. Perhaps I’d even become a designer one of these days. Though I know nothing about sewing.”

Vania rolled her eyes. “I never knew you were one for fashion.”

“I like the attention,” Charlotte said absentmindedly. “Expensive jewelry. The finest dresses. Maybe a little tease for the eyes here and there while men and women alike fawn over me like I’m some siren’s song.”

“You really want that?”

“Maybe.” Charlotte’s smile lingered, but something quieter slipped beneath it. Her words chilled. “But that kind of life isn’t for people like us.”

Vania’s eyes lowered.

Charlotte’s arm remained locked with hers as they walked beneath the trimmed trees in the courtyard. “No one does the things we do and gets to walk away all merry-like. Not really. Even if Vermillion let us, someone else wouldn’t. Rivals. Survivors. Enemies. People who hate seeing monsters pretend they’re ordinary. Why let us experience the good, when all we do is the bad?”

Hearing those words, Vania could say nothing. She only kept her eyes fixed on the path beneath her feet as they walked.

Charlotte looked at her. “Oh, you. You’re thinking about troublesome things again.”

“I’m not. This is just how I usually am.”

“Oh, you can get much wilder than that. Don’t pretend you’re all brooding like this all the time, Miss Two-Faced Assassin.” Charlotte slipped out of her arm and stepped in front of her, studying her face. “Let me guess. You want a little stage somewhere. A small city, perhaps. Not too big like the capital. You want a warm room. Dim lights. A crowd to watch and cheer. A captive audience, yours for the taking. The lights on your face. Your voice echoing through the theater. Time slows, and it all. Belongs. To. You.”

Vania stopped walking, looking at Charlotte with complicated eyes.

Charlotte’s smile softened. “If you really want to be a singer, I’m sure I can pull some strings for you.”

Vania stared at her. “What?”

“A few documents. A clean background. A new name, if you want one.” Charlotte tilted her head. “You could sing in public and still keep working for Vermillion. No one would look too closely. And if they did, they wouldn’t make it far. You can do all of this while taking care of your grandmother.”

Vania searched her face. Truth or lies. Honesty or deceit.

But Charlotte was always like that.

Impossible to read.

“Really?”

“Really.”

Charlotte stepped closer. Her fingers slipped beneath Vania’s chin, lifting it with familiar ease. Dozens of times. Hundreds of times.

Vania let her, though her eyes remained cautious.

Charlotte leaned in and kissed her. It was soft and warm. Like it always had been.

When Charlotte pulled back, she smiled. “Trust me,” she whispered. “I’ll make your dream of becoming a singer come true. A performer by day… an assassin by night. Just stick with me in Vermillion until I can claw my way to the top. Once that happens, I’ll provide all the naturia your grandmother needs.”

Vania didn’t move. Charlotte’s fingers caressed her chin, but she made no attempt to look away.

The life of an assassin was a lonely one. But Charlotte had been with her for nearly two years now, through all of it.

There was no reason to think she would lie.

“Alright,” Vania said after a few moments. “I’ll trust you.”
