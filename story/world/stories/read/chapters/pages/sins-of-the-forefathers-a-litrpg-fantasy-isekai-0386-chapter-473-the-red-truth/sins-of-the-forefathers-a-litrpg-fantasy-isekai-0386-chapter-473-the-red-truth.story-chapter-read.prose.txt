“What happened?” I asked as calmly as I could, some time later.

It was kind of a struggle to manage that, honestly. The anxiety brewing in my gut was urging me to go out there and do something, but…I couldn’t just go charging off.

I needed to know what had happened.

The relieved Varlayans, apparently being led by Van of all people, had ushered my officers and me into the actual interior of the Hagia Dynamis. They had protested when I had insisted the doors stay open, because they were still terrified of whatever Korvoks might still be infesting the city. But it hadn’t been that hard to convince them when I said that not only would the forces outside protect us, but I had more in the other areas of the Varla.

As for those actual forces…I had sent out several different runners after the news Sena had delivered to me. By now, Bleddyn should have linked up with Walter and the men I had left behind, so they could fortify that central plaza for our inevitable push into Karag Korvok. One of my runners had been to inform them that we might be accelerating matters for that, while the other…

The other had been told to make for the Final Line outside the gates and secure our reinforcements for that same purpose. Sylvia should have also received my message via Sprite to finish up her and Bella’s hunt through the city, and make for the plaza as well.

I wanted to find out what had happened and then get moving yesterday.

Once inside the actual building, part of me wanted to stop and appreciate the beauty that was found inside, from the impeccably maintained marble interior and its workmanship, to the brilliantly colorful tapestries upon the walls, and even the full painting down on the domed ceiling far above us.

But I didn’t have time for wonder right now. I needed a briefing.

At my question, the Dwarves who had filled the small dining room we had been led to glanced at each other uncertainly, and didn’t speak.

I fought to maintain my patience, telling myself they had just been the victims of a near-complete slaughter. Who knows how many Varlayan Dwarves even still existed? Eventually, though, I lost my patience. “Van?” I asked tiredly. “Maybe you can answer.”

My old, newfound friend, dressed in much the same way as the rest of the Varlayans, looked up from where he was pouring me and my officers cups of tea. I had tried not to study the corpses of the unfortunates found out in the streets too deeply, so I hadn’t really taken in their somewhat…odd clothing choices, but it was certainly distinct. They seemed to favor long, brightly colored tunics that flowed past their knees, belted in place by broad leather belts. Over that, almost every male in the room wore a short, pale yellow cloak pinned at the shoulder by a brass fitting, displaying a stylized crystal giving off rays not dissimilar to those from the sun. The women, on the other hand, had much longer, form-concealing cloaks.

There was a…disturbing lack of footwear in the room. I tried not to let the sight of so many thick, callused, hairy feet budge me from my determination, but it was truly a struggle.

“Ah,” Van uttered tiredly, handing me my cup. I took it to be polite, but barely paid any attention to it. “Where would you like me to begin, Nathan?”

Sitting in the chair to my left, Olag let a long breath hiss from beneath his clenched teeth. Ever since Van had told us that Azarus had been taken, the Dwarf had been even more on edge than usual.

Not what I had expected, considering the historical animosity between him and his cousin.

“The beginning would be nice, fool.”

I shot him a sharp glance, but Olag was too busy nearly vibrating in place to notice. What I noticed, on the other hand, were a variety of unfriendly looks the previously respectful Varlayans shot the scion of House Florens.

It looked like Van was more popular here than I had expected.

Said Dwarf merely shot Olag a disinterested look in response. “That would be just a tad difficult. Not all of it is relevant, my Lord of Florens.”

I held up a hand to prevent an outburst from Olag, which he finally did notice. He settled down, visibly trying to calm himself with short breaths, and I turned back to Van. “I trust your judgment. We just…need to know what’s fully going on here, Van. From what I’ve gathered, the Korvoks-”

A hiss traveled through the room at that word, echoing out of the lips of dozens of Varlayan Dwarves.

“-seem to have dug a tunnel through the mountain, burst up from the central plaza of the Hold, and then…what, started rampaging through the city, killing anyone they found? And then they captured Azarus? That’s not enough for us to go on, Van.” I paused for a moment. “Also, where’s Gren? I was given to understand that you were traveling with him, as a merchant-agent of the Unchained.”

Van gave me a wan smile in response to my words. “Ah, I’m afraid…Azarus was not the only person that the Skinstealers captured, Nathan. Master Gren was…another of our taken. And they were far from the only two. You were mistaken, I’m sorry to say. Those demons did not invade Blessed Varla for the purposes of mere slaughter. Those whose bodies that you…” He paused, briefly. “Encountered on the streets were merely those who fought back.”

Somewhere in the room, I heard a female voice fail at choking back a sob, followed by the low sound of a male Dwarf's attempt to console her.

“The Skinstealers were here to take captives,” Van continued grimly. “The Legions were caught entirely unprepared when they burst forth from the fountain in the way they did. They sent their rabble, first, to occupy them, led by their Karvokar handlers, one of whom you quite thankfully dealt with.”

I exchanged a baffled look with Renauld, sitting to my right. “Captives?” He asked, audibly puzzled. “What do these crazies want with captives?”

“They’re not enslaving them…are they?”

I swear to fucking God…if these bastards were slavers as well, they might actually be some kind of evil singularity. How much more culturally degenerate do you have to be for both cannibalism and slavery to be things you practice?

“Mmm…not in the way you’re thinking of, Nathan,” Van said with a crooked, mirthless smile. “Not how you experienced it. The Skinstealers, they’re zealots. In their eyes, everything that they do, from the flensing, to the bloodletting, to the cannibalism…it’s all justified, because they believe that they’re searching for, in their own words, ‘Truth’.”

I furrowed my brow, recalling my short confrontation with Djarn. I did remember that the madman had rambled something about truth before I had killed him. I hadn’t put much stock on it at the time, considering I had already mentally sentenced him to death. He was an enemy combatant, and really just…hadn’t mattered much to me. Him or his words.

Maybe I should have listened more closely.

“They call it the ‘Red Truth’,” One of the Varlayans piped in, in a more exhausted tone that I had heard in a long time. “To them, we’re all Hidebound, lied to by our own skin.”

“The bastards think that our own gods-damned skin is holding us back, that it keeps you from ever truly being ‘free’.” Another Dwarf said, spitting off to the side. He just glowered back when others nearby glared at him. “Whatever the hells that means, nobody knows. How are you supposed to be more free when you don’t got no damn skin?”

“Because of that, I’m afraid, they view it as a religious duty to not only relieve other people of their skin,” Van continued. “But to indoctrinate others into this belief. And so, they torture. They mutilate. They flay the very skin off not only their bones, but the bones of others. They have long been a problem for not only the Dwarves of Blessed Varla, but those of the other Holds. However, up until recently, they were a problem well taken care of. The various Legions of the Holds have long worked together to fence them into the boundaries of Karag Korvok-”

“Vandimar,” One of the older greybeards in the room rumbled in a warning tone.

Van winced and nodded contritely to the Dwarf. “Apologies, Dorn. Karag Skinstealer.”

What was that about?

“But that changed recently, within the last few months.”

I put the question out of my mind, and couldn’t help a scowl from crawling onto my lips. I could take a good guess as to what might have caused things to change. It’s not like it hadn’t ruined everything else on Vereden, why not this to?

Damn you, Anguis.

“It was the Skyfall, wasn’t it?”

No fool, Van immediately understood my meaning. “Ah, is that what you’re calling it? For us, the current state of the world outside of the Blessed Holds has come to be known as the ‘Tainting’. But you’re correct, Nathan. When the Tainting happened, the Skinstealers suddenly went quiet. In retrospect, to an ominously suspicious degree. At the time, our belief was that they, too, even in their madness, were dealing with the second-order effects. Even this deep into the heart of the mountain…the Blessed Holds are not immune to what happens in the outside world. There are cracks, and systems, and even some settlements that existed with exposure to the open air. Or, I should say, were. Life in the Holds is a delicate balance with the world that exists outside of it, and that balance was broken.”

“Yes, yes, that’s all understandable,” Olag butted in irritably. “But what about the people who were captured? What about Azarus?”

To my secret delight, it seemed there was something about Vandimar that had changed in the nearly two years since I had seen him.

The Dwarf had finally grown a backbone.

Van looked up sharply and fixed Olag with a glare. “Do not pretend I do not know who you are, my lord of Florens. Azarus, and even Nathan here, have spoken of you in the past. I well know your historical antipathy with your cousin. Why do you even care what happened to Azarus?”

I had to smother a grin as Olag reared back as if he had been slapped. And culturally, he essentially had been. For Velancian Dwarves, the concept of someone from a lower social strata calling them out like this was literally unheard of. You just didn’t do that, and Van very obviously spoke with a clear Velancia accent, marking him as someone not from the Holds.

“I…” Olag blinked a few times before his mood shifted. Not towards anger, like I had expected.

Instead, he almost sounded like he was about to spiral back down into the depression I had snapped him out of.

“Azarus might be…my last remaining family member,” He said quietly, bowing his head. “You cannot know this, whoever you are…but Rhoscara has been lost. Razed to the ground, and the Prince herself captured. She could possibly be…” Olag couldn’t bring himself to say it, but the implication hung in the air.

Dead.

I didn’t like to consider the possibility, but it was true. Ely could be dead. There’s no telling what Anguis had done with her.

A wave of shock rolled through the room, and Vandimar wasn’t immune to it. Still, my old friend had always been one of the most empathetic people I’d ever met. This was a guy who had been trying to buy out slave contracts from under Magnus’s nose, just so he could free them afterwards. “I see,” He said quietly, before sighing. “Well, you’re not the only one then, my lord, to possibly lose a ruler. For you see, the Karvokars and their Skarned were only the first wave to hit Blessed Varla. They were meant to soften resistance through their slaughter and abduction. Following in their wake, the true danger, and the real reason that matters had been so quiet with the Skinstealers. The chaos of the Tainting allowed a new madman to rise to the position of their leader, someone with more ambition. He stormed the Hagia Dynamis with his Council of Rendarchs, and not even Jorvik, the Defiant himself, could stand against their might. So many people were lost, the most powerful of Varla stolen for some unfathomable purpose. Azarus and Master Gren…were among them.”

I exchanged a troubled glance with the recovered Olag before turning back to Van. “Does this madman have a name?”

“Yeah,” A new voice spoke up in a croak, coming from the open door of this room. When I looked in that direction, I was a bit surprised at what I saw. The speaker was a Dwarf who did not look like they should be up and about. They were visibly injured, with bandages curled all across both his abdomen and the right side of his face. There was even some minor blood spotting through the linens, possibly from overexertion, considering he was leaning on a crutch under his left arm.

Still, even with the bandages, there was a visible resemblance to someone else to this Dwarf. Someone I hadn’t seen since before we had departed Blutstein.

Kargath Gravelfoot, the groundskeeper of the Academy.

Come to think of it, hadn’t Azarus left someone on his journey north? I think their name was…

“Torgir Gravelfoot?” Renauld sprang to his feet, already shuffling over towards the injured Dwarf.

“Hey, Renauld,” Torgir coughed, waving the affronted Healer off. Instead, he fixed me with a serious glare. “He has a name already. The bastard announced it to everyone in the hall.”

“He calls himself Ul-Korveth, the Karvokhan.”
