What had begun as a grim necessity to bleed the ambient mana from the broken instance rapidly transformed into an exercise in aggressive timekeeping. The repetitive nature of the Goblin Caves meant the element of surprise was entirely gone. They knew every dark corner, every trapped flagstone, and exactly where every single cluster of monsters would spawn.

Josh and Brett had set the initial benchmark at forty-two minutes. Bhel and Perberos, fuelled entirely by the dwarf's immense indignation and refusal to be beaten by a human, had immediately plunged back into the dark as soon as he was awake and finally returned with a highly contested time of thirty-nine minutes.

By midday, the competitive sickness had spread to the other four parties.

While they were all hovering around level fifteen to twenty, seeing the tier three fighters treat a lethal dungeon like a casual sprint heavily inspired the other adventurers. Thorne, the arrogant human swordsman whose face now bore a fresh pink scar from Carcan’s healing magic, rallied his group and managed a very respectable fifty-two-minute clear.

But the main event remained the fierce rivalry between the two leading pairs.

"Thirty-six minutes!" Brett announced loudly to the entire camp, as he and Josh stepped out of the swirling purple portal for the fifth time that day. The mage did not have a single speck of dirt on his fur cloak. "A new record. And I believe I barely exceeded a walking pace."

"You liar! I can see the sweat coming from Josh!" Bhel roared from his spot near the supply crates, slamming his whetstone down. "Perberos, grab your bow! We are going back in!"

"We literally just sat down to eat," the elven ranger pointed out mildly, though he was already rising to his feet with his usual liquid grace.

The mechanics of their speed runs were terrifyingly simple. Josh did not bother swinging his sword anymore. He kept his heavy steel shield raised and utilised his Adamantine Breach skill to simply bowl through the tightly packed groups of goblins like a heavily armoured siege engine. Anything that survived the crushing impact of his shield was instantly incinerated by Brett.

The mage had completely bypassed the need for complex incantations. He kept his Cinder-Sprite familiar looping happily around his wrist, using his Scorched Veins skill to project continuous, devastating cones of pure thermal energy directly from his bare palms. They were quite literally glassing the tunnels.

By the time the sun began to sink below the western ridges, casting long, bruised shadows across the muddy basin, the sheer volume of slaughter finally achieved the desired result.

Josh was sitting on a crate, accepting a bowl of hot broth from Carcan, when the deep, vibrating hum of the portal suddenly changed pitch. The aggressive, erratic purple light had faded to a dark blue the night before. Throughout the day, it had been brightening with each run that was completed, and finally, the colour shifted seamlessly into a tranquil, deep, mesmerising blue.

A profound, physical wave of relief washed over the camp. The oppressive, heavy feeling of stagnant, broken mana instantly evaporated from the freezing air. The portal had stabilised. It was no longer a bleeding wound in reality, but a standard, dormant dungeon entrance. It would only generate monsters inside the instance now, strictly adhering to the rules of the system.

"It is done," Carcan breathed, her silver eyes reflecting the calm blue light. She lowered her amber-topped staff, the tension finally leaving her pale, exhausted face.

A ragged, spontaneous cheer erupted from the assembled adventurers. Men and women slumped against the rocky walls or simply collapsed into the mud, the adrenaline of the past few days finally, completely leaving their systems.

"Right," Josh called out, his voice cutting through the celebrations. He set his bowl aside and stood up, moving to the centre of the camp near the largest roaring fire. "Gather round. We need a plan for tomorrow."

The five parties clustered together in the firelight. They were battered, filthy, and exhausted, but their eyes held the specific, hardened gleam of veterans who had survived the worst the frontier could throw at them.

"The rift is secure," Josh began, projecting his voice so everyone could hear. "But we cannot simply pack up and abandon the valley. If we leave it completely undefended, wandering beasts could easily claim the cave mouth, and we need to monitor the portal, making sure it doesn’t start breaking again. We need to secure this location permanently."

Thorne stepped forward, his hand resting on the pommel of his broadsword. "My party will stay. We know the layout of the area. We can hold the entrance and continue running the instance to keep the internal numbers down if needed."

"My group will stay as well," another leader volunteered, a sturdy beastkin woman carrying a heavy iron mace. "Two parties is enough to maintain a permanent guard rotation on the portal while the other sleeps."

"That is brave of you both," Brett noted, stepping into the firelight and pushing his non-existent spectacles up his nose. "However, surviving out here requires more than just martial prowess. The palisade walls are destroyed. The barracks are ash. How do you intend to feed yourselves when your travel rations run out? Ashenfall is a long walk for groceries."

A quiet, tentative voice spoke up from the back of the beastkin's party. "I can help with that."

The crowd parted slightly, allowing a young human woman to step forward. She wore simple, earth-toned robes that were heavily stained with mud at the hem, and she carried a staff made of twisted, living oak rather than polished wood or metal.

"My name is Rowan," she introduced herself nervously, intimidated by the sheer physical presence of the higher-levelled fighters. "I am a Druid. My class path focuses heavily on natural remediation and agricultural acceleration."

Josh crossed his arms over his heavy breastplate, instantly intrigued. "What exactly does that mean, Rowan?"

"It means I can encourage plant growth," Rowan explained, gaining a little more confidence as she spoke about her magical specialty. "The soil in this valley has been heavily saturated with the dissolving golden dust of thousands of monsters. That is pure, raw mana soaked directly into the earth. If we clear the ash and the rubble from the old training camp up on the ridge, I can plant fast-yielding crops. Root vegetables, hardy grains. With the mana density in the soil and my daily spells, I can force a harvest in a matter of days, not weeks or months."

A murmur of genuine surprise rippled through the gathered adventurers. Food was a constant, pressing anxiety on the frontier. The ability to generate a sustainable food source turned a temporary military camp into a permanent settlement.

"That changes our entire operation," Brett murmured, his academic mind spinning with the logistical possibilities.

"It does," Josh agreed, a wide smile breaking across his stubbled face. He looked at Thorne and the beastkin. "You will not just be guarding a portal. You will be founding an outpost. If Rowan can guarantee a food supply, this valley becomes a safe haven."

Josh turned to his own party, then looked at the two remaining secondary groups. "The rest of us will head back to Ashenfall at first light. We will report the success of the mission to the Guild. We will tell them the portal is blue, and the valley is secure."

He looked back at Rowan. "More importantly, we will tell them we have a sustainable base of operations. The city is currently bursting at the seams with terrified refugees living in tents in the streets. We will organise a convoy. Bring the trainee adventurers back here so they can level safely in the dungeon, and bring some skilled civilians. Carpenters to rebuild the palisade walls, smiths to forge weapons, and farmers to help Rowan manage the crops. Hopefully we will relieve the pressure on Ashenfall and turn this place into a proper town."

The idea took root instantly. The exhausted adventurers nodded enthusiastically, the prospect of building something permanent offering a much-needed sense of purpose after so much destruction.

They spent the rest of the evening discussing the logistics, drawing crude maps in the dirt, and swapping remaining supplies. When sleep finally claimed them, it was deep, peaceful, and entirely free of nightmares.

The next morning broke with a crisp, biting frost that turned the mud of the valley into solid iron.

Josh's party, along with the two returning groups, packed their gear quickly. They left the bulk of their heavier travel rations with Thorne and Rowan, ensuring the new outpost would not go hungry while they awaited the first convoy from the city.

"Keep the fires burning," Josh instructed Thorne, offering the swordsman a firm, respectful handshake. "Reinforcements and lumber will be here before you know it."

"We will hold the line," Thorne promised, his earlier arrogance replaced by a quiet, determined reliability.

With brief farewells exchanged, the returning parties turned their backs on the blue light of the portal and began the long march west.

As soon as they hit the main frontier road, Perberos separated from the main column. The elven ranger did not say a word, simply melting silently into the dark, towering pine trees flanking the path. Despite the victory in the valley, none of them had forgotten the terrifying native beasts that had ambushed the refugee caravan just a few days prior.

The journey, however, proved to be mercifully, almost boringly uneventful.

A few hours into the march, Perberos dropped effortlessly from a low-hanging pine branch, landing lightly in the frozen mud beside Josh.

"So?" Josh asked, keeping his pace steady.

"The woods are largely empty," Perberos stated, his silver eyes completely calm. "I encountered a few very small, isolated groups of starving goblins. They were too terrified to even draw their rusted blades. I dispatched them quickly. But the larger picture is much more interesting."

"What did you find?" Brett asked, pausing his lecture to the other spellcasters about the theoretical limits of mana compression.

"Tracks," Perberos explained. "Massive, chaotic trails cutting deeply into the forest floor, all heading consistently east towards the deep mountain ranges. The surviving native beasts, and whatever remained of the larger orc packs that escaped the valley, have completely migrated out of the region. There is absolutely no sign of them circling back."

"They learned to fear the road," Bhel grunted approvingly, resting his axes on his broad shoulders. "Good. Let them freeze in the mountains."

They maintained a brisk, highly motivated pace. Without the burden of heavily laden merchant carts or terrified civilians to slow them down, they covered the distance with the enhanced speed of seasoned adventurers.

By midday, the towering walls of Ashenfall finally crested the horizon.

The city looked exactly as they had left it, though the sprawling sea of makeshift tents outside the main gates had grown significantly denser. The refugee crisis was clearly straining the city's resources to the absolute breaking point. The guards at the gate looked exhausted, but they waved the heavily armed parties through without delay, recognising the official crests on their armour.

They marched straight through the bustling, crowded cobblestone streets, making a direct line for the Adventurers' Guild in the central plaza.

The interior of the massive guildhall was a scene of controlled, desperate chaos. Adventurers were crowded around the job boards, shouting over one another, while heavily armed guards attempted to maintain order. The smell of cheap ale, wet leather, and unwashed bodies was overpowering.

Josh led his party expertly through the throng, bypassing the crowded main desks and heading straight for the administrative alcoves at the back of the hall.

Sitting behind a desk piled precariously high with messy, ink-stained parchment was Mich. The blonde elven clerk looked incredibly flustered, an ink smudge completely marring her usually pristine cheek. She was currently arguing heatedly with a stubborn dwarven merchant about the tariff rates for imported iron.

Mich looked up as the heavy, rhythmic clanking of Josh's plate armour approached her desk.

The moment she registered who was standing there, the irritation vanished from her face instantly. Her bright eyes widened, and a massive, genuinely overjoyed smile broke across her elegant features. She practically shoved the confused dwarven merchant aside, standing up and slamming her hands down on the desk.

"You are back!" Mich cheered, her voice cutting clearly through the surrounding din. She quickly scanned the party, her gaze lingering on the scorch marks on Brett's cloak and the deep gouges in Josh's shield. "And you are all in one piece! Mother Tree be praised, I was beginning to think I would have to send a recovery team to scrape you out of the mud."

"It takes more than a few thousand goblins to put us in the mud, Mich," Bhel boasted loudly, leaning heavily against her desk.

"Report, Josh," a stern, commanding voice ordered from behind Mich.

Rochelle, the head clerk, stepped out from a back office. The older elf, with her greying hair pulled into a severe, uncompromising bun, looked at the party with her usual strict, no-nonsense glare, but Josh could see the faint, unmistakable glimmer of relief hiding in her eyes.

"The mission was an absolute success, Rochelle," Josh stated clearly, slipping into his professional, leader persona. "The massive horde in the eastern valley has been completely eradicated. We entered the instance, bled the ambient mana, and killed the boss. The rift has returned to a normal blue colour. It is fully stabilised and dormant."

A profound silence rippled outward from their desk, as nearby adventurers stopped their arguing to listen to the news.

"That is the best news this city has received all week," Rochelle breathed, her rigid posture relaxing just a fraction. She looked at Mich. "Log it immediately. Update the regional threat assessment boards."

"There is more," Josh continued, leaning forward slightly. "We did not abandon the site. Two of the secondary parties volunteered to stay behind and maintain a permanent guard rotation on the portal."

"And it gets better," Brett chimed in, a smug, highly satisfied smirk on his face. "One of the adventurers who remained is a Druid named Rowan. She has evaluated the soil, which is currently saturated with the dense mana of thousands of dissolved monsters. She believes she can force rapid-growth crops and create a highly sustainable food source in a matter of weeks."

Rochelle’s eyes widened, her sharp administrative mind instantly calculating the massive, city-saving implications of that statement. "A sustainable food source. Out there."

"We want to turn it into a permanent outpost, not just a training camp," Josh explained, gesturing towards the crowded, noisy guildhall behind him. "Ashenfall is choking on refugees, and you cannot safely train novices here with the local woods swarming with displaced beasts. We want to organise a massive convoy."

Mich grabbed a fresh sheet of parchment and a quill, her mischievous energy entirely replaced by focused, frantic excitement. "Who do you need?"

"We need the trainee adventurers," Josh listed, ticking the requirements off on his gauntleted fingers. "Send them out there to level safely in the stabilised goblin dungeon. We need skilled civilians from the refugee camps. Carpenters and masons to rebuild the palisade walls into something permanent. Blacksmiths to set up forges. Farmers to assist Rowan with the crops."

"We can do that," Mich said rapidly, her quill flying across the page. "We have hundreds of displaced tradesmen sleeping in the mud outside the gates right now who would jump at the chance to build a new home, especially if it is protected by the Guild."

"I will take this directly to the Guildmaster and the City Council," Rochelle stated, grabbing the parchment from Mich’s desk before the ink was even dry. "We will begin compiling the lists and securing the necessary wagons and draught beasts immediately. This outpost could save Ashenfall from a winter famine."

The older elf paused, looking directly at Josh and his party. "You have done an incredible service to this city today. The Guild will ensure you are heavily, adequately compensated for your foresight."

"We just want to see the people safe," Carcan said softly, leaning on her staff.

With the bureaucratic machinery of the Guild violently grinding into motion around them, Josh let out a long, exhausted breath. The adrenaline was finally, truly gone, leaving only the deep, bone-weary fatigue of a long campaign.

"Right," Josh said, turning to his friends. "The world is saved for another afternoon. I don't know about the rest of you, but I need to get out of this armour. We are heading to the Bull's Head."

"Garrik had better have a barrel of his darkest ale waiting for us," Bhel grunted, already turning towards the exit. "And if Barb doesn't have a hot venison pie in the oven, there will be a dwarven riot."

"I require a bath," Brett announced to no one in particular, pulling his cloak tight. "A real bath. Not a lukewarm puddle in a ruined town, but actual, scalding water and perhaps some aggressively scented soap."

As they turned away from the desk, preparing to navigate the crowded hall towards the comforting familiarity of the Bull's Head Inn, Mich leaned over her desk, a bright, deeply mischievous grin returning to her face.

"Enjoy the ale, boys!" Mich shouted cheerfully over the din of the adventurers, waving a fresh stack of bounty posters in the air. "Get some rest! Don't get too comfortable, though! The frontier never sleeps, and I have plenty of highly lucrative, incredibly dangerous jobs lined up for you in the coming weeks!"

Josh just shook his head, a tired smile touching his lips, and raised a gauntleted hand in a dismissive wave without looking back. They pushed through the heavy oak doors of the guildhall and stepped out into the bright, bustling streets of Ashenfall, finally heading home.
