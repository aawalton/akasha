Both sides exploded into action at the same time, almost like they shared a mind – which they technically did.

Every step left a deep impression into the giant mushroom, their punches and kicks colliding in violent shockwaves that sent faint tremors all the way down to the base of the Fungal Spire.

Percy and Micky’s figures turned into blurs – or at least they would have, to an outside observer, though their sensory domain and superior reflexes allowed them to keep track of each other’s movements fairly easily.

Speaking of their Sage’s Pond, Percy was quite surprised by how differently it worked while sparring against his fellow aspect. His ability to predict the future typically scaled with his understanding of his opponent’s fighting style, as well as how quickly that person could react to Percy’s own decisions.

Usually, he considered himself lucky if he could peer a second or two ahead, as that was more than enough to grant him an insurmountable advantage in most cases. Even that brief window often appeared murky, consisting of at least a handful of branching futures.

Fighting Micky was a whole other story.

On one hand, Percy and his fellow aspect knew each other’s moves and habits inside out, which would have theoretically allowed them to peer several seconds into the future. On the other hand, they both possessed the same ability and knew how to guard against it, which created many more branching paths and littered their vision with countless afterimages, fake projections, and aborted decisions.

‘Interesting…’ Percy thought. ‘This was only meant as a test for my new mutation, but it might not be a bad idea to practice like this in the future.’

Sparring against one another would be the perfect way to iron out the wrinkles in their fighting style and hone their future sight to the absolute limit, because there was nobody better than each other to punish them for their mistakes.

Regardless, that was something to worry about later.

Despite his powerful physique levelling the playing field somewhat, Percy instantly found himself on the back foot. After all, Micky was not only a real beast, but also one with as many cores, the same boosting art, and equally powerful mutations as his own – if not even better ones. Not to mention that his friend also happened to possess twice as many fists to pummel him with.

Consequently, Percy had no choice but to fight defensively, with a focus on guarding his vitals and looking for opportunities to throw the occasional counterattack.

He also had to borrow more of their domain to keep up with Micky. They hadn’t used any external tools or spells, but their willpower was among the few things outside of their raw physical strength that they had allowed into the battle, as it helped them balance their power out.

Percy filtered out the details of the battle and focused on gauging his new limits. Micky also eased up the pressure slightly, to let him concentrate.

Before the ritual, Percy and Micky had been individually strong enough to barely hold their own against a White. In terms of physical power, Percy used to be about equal to a Violet human. His Greater Thess’kalan Physique, two cores and boosting art had been just about enough to bridge the gap.

However, a Violet human was only about as strong as a Green beast if they didn’t cast any spells. Beasts experienced the same passive boosts as sapients with each promotion, in addition to evolving much larger bodies that were constantly fuelled by their unique mana type.

A beast’s bulk was essentially its own version of magic.

Percy used to be about as physically strong as a beast of his grade, but only due to his extra core and the Dance. Without them, he would have struggled to keep up with a Yellow creature.

The new mutation had changed the situation greatly.

Percy felt that he could probably handle a Green beast with his new physique alone. His second core and boosting art should allow him to beat even a Starry Commander with his bare hands. Or well… they would have, if he hadn’t given all the Blue wasps new cores and boosting arts of their own.

Of course, Percy understood that his advantage over beasts of his grade was temporary. A beast’s physical strength scaled much better with their promotions, so they would overtake him long before he reached the peak of the Colour realm. The demigods of the Ancestral Lineage Sect supposedly possessed bodies on par with Blue beasts.

The Twin Heartbeat Physique should eventually bring Percy a little higher than that – perhaps at the level of a Violet beast without the added boosts, and a White if he included them. However, he doubted that he would be able to beat a quasi-divine beast with his bare hands as a demigod.

That wasn’t too bad. The eel that Percy had seen on Ruby Heart had been about as large as a small mountain. Coming within a single grade of competing against a creature like that – and to even do so with a much smaller body and without possessing the beast affinity himself – was nothing to be ashamed of.

Still, he couldn’t help but wonder whether his mutation could be strengthened further.

It wouldn’t be easy. Unless some other faction had produced an ability similar to the Ancestral Lineage Sect’s bloodline, the Heartblood was the only way for him to directly enhance his physique.

He’d probably dug out every drop of power hidden within both his Remiorian lineage and the Thess’kalan ingredients, and he doubted that the ritual would allow him to absorb traits from other, unrelated tribes of demi-humans.

‘Maybe I can try to collect a few weaker, standalone mutations like my Ruby Bones, and then undergo another ritual to assimilate them into my physique.’

Alas, that wasn’t something that he could plan for.

Leaving aside the future, Percy gestured at his fellow aspect to pause, seeing no reason for them to keep beating each other up. His body was already covered in minor cuts and scrapes, though he didn’t really mind them, as they would give him a chance to study his body’s natural regeneration as well.

Sure enough, his wounds rapidly stitched themselves up, restoring his flesh to its unblemished state within seconds. Clearly, he had completely internalized the benefits of his trollsfury ink and marshblood tonic, which were compounded further by the restorative properties of his spiritual blood.

Judging by how quickly his injuries had healed, he wouldn’t be surprised if he could even regrow a severed limb, even without help from a powerful healer like Archibald. Of course, Percy wasn’t going to chop off a hand just to test this, so he’d have to wait for the right opportunity to confirm his suspicions.

Even if he was right, he didn’t think that his regeneration was fast enough to replace a missing leg in the middle of a fight, but at least he wouldn’t be crippled if he found himself stranded at a remote location without access to a proper healer.

Overall, Percy was quite satisfied with the new mutation.

It had improved his sensory domain, greatly strengthened his physical attributes, saved him the headache of brewing more potions in the future, and even lowered the threshold necessary to maintain his artificial promotions.

There was only one thing left to test, albeit the most important one.

‘Nephthys, can you please send me some soul-melting acid?’ he asked his familiar, mentally preparing himself for a world of pain.

Thankfully, the reincarnated goddess started with a relatively small amount of mana, having clearly learned from their previous experiments. Percy still had to be careful to turn the mana corporeal the moment it reached him, as his soul had been a lot more vulnerable to the corrosive resource than his flesh, even before the ritual. In fact, the acid mana had only grown even deadlier after Nephthys’ second core had advanced to Blue.

Phasing the trickle of acid mana into the corresponding channels, he directed it toward his right hand, studying its behaviour.

Some damage was inevitable, but the destruction was a lot milder than he feared.

A few veins near his skin visibly darkened as his flesh swelled slightly, sending a pang of pain through his nerves. Fortunately, Percy could clench and unclench his fist just fine, and even condense his mana into a projectile without suffering any irreversible damage. Better still, his regeneration constantly fought against the destruction, allowing him to handle more acid and restoring his arm to normal mere seconds after launching the purple blob.

Growing bolder, he asked his familiar to slowly increase the quantity she sent him, trying to figure out his upper limit.

Filling more than three-quarters of his channels with mana seemed to be the threshold where the damage accumulated faster than he could heal it, gnawing at his muscles and blood vessels enough to slow his movements down slightly. Even so, he could probably push himself to saturation and hold the mana in for a few minutes without suffering any serious injuries if he really had to, though he would have to take a small break from wielding the exotic resource afterwards.

All things considered, pumping his body full of acid wasn’t a pleasant experience in the slightest, but it was more than bearable. He could finally incorporate his familiar’s mana into his fighting style, and his resistance was bound to climb even higher if she ever turned into a proper aspect.

Opening his Status, Percy’s gaze landed on the description of the upgraded mutation.

___

Mutations:

___

The description had grown a bit long, but that was just a testament to the wide scope of the new mutation. Closing his Status, Percy saw Micky standing by his side, presumably still reading the same information and pondering over its implications.

With another milestone cleared, Percy considered their next moves. Micky might get some free time until the clone from Tenefyre returned, because he didn’t have enough spiritual blood to practice with his sentries, and had to wait for the new blessing before he could try reconstructing his body.

Percy could also take a break from alchemy at last, though he had other things begging for his attention, such as experimenting with his friends’ composite affinities. He still had a cord available as well, since he hadn’t sent out a new clone after retrieving the one from Melodia.

Leaving a cord unused for over a week wasn’t very efficient of him, but he’d wanted to see how the ritual would go before choosing his next destination. The lesser spring that used to own the Elemental Source of the sound affinity was suddenly on the table, as Percy finally had the tools to start working on the storm element.

‘It’ll have to be between that and Mithril… though I suppose that the next auction is also coming up soon, so I might need to send another clone to Gallimus first…’

Fortunately, the clones working on his uncle and grandfather’s second cores were nearly done. Before long, Percy would be able to visit every single one of those interesting places. Then again, there was a good chance that he might buy more karmic compasses at the auction. No matter how much he increased his capacity or how well he planned his adventures, he’d probably never have enough cords.

Sighing, he was about to decide where to invest his current slot when a strange feeling enveloped him. He felt his connection to his own body weaken – almost as if a thin needle had stabbed into every single one of his joints.

Moving seemingly by itself, his hand rose, accumulating a large chunk of phantom mana and shaping it into a dagger. Noticing the movement, Micky raised an eyebrow, an unspoken question hanging in his expression.

Before either of them could react, Percy pointed his palm at his friend and launched the magical projectile directly at the guy’s sternum – one of the only two spots in the Huehuan’s body that could actually kill him!
