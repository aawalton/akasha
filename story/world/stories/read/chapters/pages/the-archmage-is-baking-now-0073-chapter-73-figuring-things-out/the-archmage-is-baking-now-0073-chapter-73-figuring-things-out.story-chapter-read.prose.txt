"Tomorrow?" Bella's eyebrows shot up. "That's soon."

Jane shrugged. "It is. But I think I'm glad, anyway. I've been preparing for this for days. I’d go crazy if I had to wait much longer."

She read the message again, noting the exact time specified. Then she looked around for something to write on. Without being asked, Allen handed her a scrap of paper and a stub of pencil. She quickly scribbled out a list of items she would need from the food market. Flour, eggs, a few specific spices she was running low on. Nothing complicated, just enough to ensure she had everything she needed for the showcase.

"Can you take this to the market for me?" she asked the courier. "You’ll have to go to multiple shops, but they'll know my account."

"Of course, miss." He took the paper and the coin she pressed into his hand. "Right away."

He was gone in an instant.

"So, it's tomorrow." Allen had stopped his tidying and was watching Jane carefully. "Your showcase with the king."

"It appears so."

"Are you nervous?"

"A little."

Bella hopped down from the workbench and stretched. "Well, I should get going. I still have some work to do before my own showcase. If it’s all happening this fast, I might not have as much time as I wanted.”

"I'll walk you home," Brit said, pushing himself up to his full height. "It's getting late, and I could use the chance to stretch my legs."

Bella gave Jane a quick hug and whispered “Good luck” in her ear, then followed Brit out of the workshop.

Jane turned to find Allen still watching her like a hawk.

"What?" she asked.

"Are you all right?"

"Of course I'm all right. Why wouldn't I be?"

“I mean actually all right. I know you've been stressed about this prince business for days. I know you talked with the queen privately. I want to make sure you're fine. Really fine."

Jane opened her mouth to give another dismissive answer, then closed it again. Allen deserved better than that. He had been nothing but supportive through all of this, even where other people might have been jealous. The least she could do was give him an honest answer.

I could have gotten a boyfriend who was jealous, she thought. Someone who would have spent this whole day sulking. Instead, I got one who only cares about whether he can help. How did I get so lucky?

She sat back down on her crate and looked up at him.

"I'm not sure," she said finally. "About being all right, I mean. There's a lot going on."

Allen nodded and found a seat of his own across from her. He didn't say anything.

"I still think the king might have ideas," Jane said. "About me and his son. I don't know for sure, but I can't help but wonder if there's something to it."

"What about the queen?"

"The queen asked me to help teach Brand magic. She said he's been struggling with his training, and she thought a different perspective might help."

Allen considered this. "Do you think she's trying to trick you? Get you to spend time with the prince so something happens?"

"No." Jane shook her head. "I don't think so. It felt like a mother worried about her son, not a queen playing politics. I think she might have different priorities than the king. He seems focused on finding Brand a wife. She seems focused on helping him grow up properly, still. Like he’s younger to her than he is to the king, somehow."

"Are you comfortable doing that? Teaching him, I mean."

"I think so," she said. "I agreed, anyway. Because the queen asked nicely, and because I think I can actually help. If they try to turn it into something else, I'll deal with that when it happens. It feels like it would be my offense to take, not theirs."

Allen nodded slowly. "As long as you're comfortable with it, I'm fine."

"I don't think you need to worry."

She reached out and took his hand, squeezing it gently. Allen squeezed back.

"Should we head home?" he asked eventually.

"Yes. I have a lot of baking to do tomorrow. Well, not a lot. But what I do have is important."

They stood and made their way out of Allen's workspace, joining the stream of people heading away from the market. They didn't talk much. The day had been long and full of words, and Jane found she had used up most of hers.

She slipped her arm through Allen's as they walked, drawing close to him and soaking up his warmth. He smelled like leather and metal, which was a mixed bag of good and odd. Underneath it all, though, he smelled like himself. She leaned her head against his shoulder and let him guide her through the streets.

They reached her bakery as the last light was fading from the sky. Allen walked her to the door and turned her towards him for his parting words.

"You'll do well tomorrow," he said. "At the showcase. I know you will."

"Thank you." She drew close again, throwing her arms around Allen’s waist. "For being there."

"Where else would I be?"

He kissed her goodnight, a soft press of lips that she felt all the way down to her feet. Then he was gone, walking back toward his own home, and Jane was alone with her bakery.

She locked the door behind her and stood for a moment in the dark shop, listening to the silence. She dismissed any idea of cooking outright. Her stomach wasn't particularly interested in food, and the thought of dirtying up her kitchen was about as unappealing as letting a fish bite off her fingers. Instead, she climbed the stairs to her room.

Tomorrow would be busy, but right now, there was nothing to do but rest.

—

Jane woke before dawn, which was normal for her. She lay in bed for a few minutes, staring at the ceiling and waiting for the nerves to hit.

They didn't come. She felt calm. She didn’t know how that could possibly be, but she supposed she’d be a fool to complain about it.

She got up and dressed, deciding to get an early start. The showcase wasn’t until late morning, but dilly-dallying felt dangerous. If everything went well, she could keep the bread warm in the oven until it was time to leave. If there were mistakes, she wanted time to fix them.

Her kitchen was clean and ready to go. Her ingredients had arrived the night before, just as she had requested. Everything was in order for her tasks.

All right. Let's do this.

She started with the burnt-top bread, both because it took the longest and because she knew it best. She had made this bread so many times now that she could probably do it in her sleep. The dough came together easily under her hands, muscle memory taking over where her mind might have stumbled under the pressure of cooking for a king.

Her body knew what to do. As long as she could stay out of her own way, everything would be fine.

She made two batches, just in case. If the first one failed somehow, she would have a backup. If both succeeded, she could choose the better of the two. It seemed like a sensible precaution, and she had the ingredients to spare.

The keln came next. This was trickier, since the herb mixture required a bit more attention and it was important to achieve an exact consistency in the dough. But she had practiced this many times as well. Two batches of keln went into the oven without incident, just like the bread.

She was least confident about the meat buns, so she saved them for last. She wanted to be warmed up by the time she got to that stage.

If you were going to make mistakes, you would have made them earlier, she told herself. You just need to keep up your streak of success.

She didn't make mistakes. The filling came together perfectly, the dough wrapped around it without tearing, and the buns held their shape as she arranged them on the baking sheet. She made two batches of these as well.

Once they were in the oven, she found herself with nothing to do. She had planned time for emergencies, but there were none to resolve. It felt anticlimactic.

The results that came out of the oven were the same. Everything was perfect. Both batches of every item were exactly as she had intended.

Jane made herself a nice cup of tea and sat down for a light breakfast. With all her items staying warm in the ovens, she had plenty of time. She listened to the birds through her open back door as she ate, keeping her mind off the day to come as best she could until a full hour had passed.

She selected the best of the best from the double batches and loaded everything carefully into crates, wrapping each item in clean cloth to keep it fresh. The bread went in one, the keln in another, and finally the buns. She had just finished when she realized she had a problem.

The crates were heavy and bulky, and there were three of them. She couldn’t carry them all across town on her own. It would require multiple trips, and she simply hadn’t budgeted that kind of time.

She stepped outside and looked up and down the street, hoping to spot someone who might be able to help. The street was empty. Everyone had already left for the showcase and the festivities surrounding it. She was alone with her crates and her growing sense of panic.

Of course. The one thing I didn't plan for.

She was just about to go back inside and start making trips anyway when she heard the sound of wheels on cobblestones. She turned and saw Dave coming around the corner, pushing a handcart in front of him.

"Dave!" Relief flooded through her. "If you aren't busy, I could really use your help."

"That's why I'm here." He brought the cart to a stop in front of her door. "Allen mentioned the showcase last night. I figured you'd need a way to move things."

"You figured right." Jane gestured at the crates lined up just inside the door. "I made enough food to feed half the town, and I didn't think about how I was going to carry it."

They loaded the crates onto the cart together, arranging them so the weight was balanced and nothing would shift during the walk. Dave handled his end with competence and not a single sense of trying to play the hero, even though he was just that. Jane found herself grateful once again for his presence in Glenfall.

"I need to apologize to you," she said as they started walking. "I told your father I would make sure you were doing well here. I haven't done a very good job of that."

Dave shrugged. "There's nothing to apologize for. I've been doing fine. I’m finding plenty of work. I’ve tried a dozen different things, Jane. It’s going well."

"Still. I made a promise."

"And you've kept it. You introduced me to people and gave me a place to start. Everything after that is my job."

Jane supposed that was true. Peter had asked her to help Dave get settled, not to manage his entire life. Still, she felt like she could be doing more, or at least checking in every now and again.

"What kind of work have you been finding?" she asked.

"All kinds. Deliveries, mostly. But also some hauling for the craftsmen in the Underbridge Market. And I helped a family move house last week." He pushed the cart around a corner, navigating the turn with practiced ease. "It keeps me busy, and I'm learning the city."

"That's good." Jane thought of the prince, and of the conversation she'd had with the queen about helping him find his way. "Actually, I should introduce you to someone, if the opportunity arises.”

"Who?"

"The prince. Brand."

She saw Dave's eyes widening and hurried to explain.

"Not for any political reason. You're about the same age, I think. And you're both in similar places, in a way. Trying to figure out where you fit. It might be good for both of you to know someone else who's going through the same thing."

Dave was quiet for a moment, digesting this. "I never thought of the prince as someone who might be figuring things out."

"Maybe that's part of the problem." Jane remembered Brand's obvious relief at spending time with normal people in the market. "Having your path decided for you isn't the same as knowing where you fit. If anything, it might make it harder."

Dave nodded thoughtfully, and they continued their journey in companionable silence.

The cart wheels rattled forward over the cobblestones. The streets grew more crowded as they approached the town center, filling with people who were all heading to the same place they were.

Sure enough, the town center was already packed when they arrived. Jane spotted the other two bakers arranging their wares, luckily opting for simpler displays. Suddenly, the nerves she’d been waiting for all morning rose up in a wave.

Then, as quickly as it had come, the feeling passed. She was committed now, after all.

There was nothing to do but see it through.
