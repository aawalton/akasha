Aodhán stared at his status screen in shock.

He had been aware of a few changes—the seals, his advancements, and the evolution of his skills—but everything else was news to him.

The titles he could easily understand, and perhaps the new skills too, seeing as they had been born from his inspirations. But his class had also progressed, meaning he had absorbed energy at some point—a lot of it, judging by how much it had jumped.

That wasn't all.

His understanding of the storm had improved. It was still a force, but it had climbed from ten percent to twelve. That was not a small leap, especially considering how much control he already had.

What interested him most, however, were his skills.

Unsurprisingly, despite all the insight he had gained from Calamity Ghaltris's defensive path, he hadn't gained any truly defensive skills. Even Edict of Reprisal wasn't defensive—but rather a ruinous interpretation of reflection, and a conditional one at that. Authority only held for as long as he could back it.

It made him chuckle, seeing how every insight had been twisted through the lenses of authority and ruin.

He wouldn't have had it any other way.

His ideal wasn't about becoming a shield for others. It was about raising others to stand beside him. It was about becoming strong enough to break every shield.

I gain strength to give it.

If defense decided what may pass… then Ruinous Authority would decide what must fall.

He read through the screen one more time, ensuring he understood each skill, before finally looking up.

The Mythic was still staring at him—fascination now replacing fear—and Aodhán knew it was only a matter of time before the entire sect learned about his immunity. He needed to inform the High Elders as soon as possible.

Right now, however, his entire body itched for something else. Understanding a skill description was one thing. Seeing it function with his eyes was another.

Aodhán wanted to see it.

"Varéc?" he whispered, and the sleeping Fury stirred within his spirit.

He smiled. "I believe it's time to put all our new abilities to the test. Don't you think?"

Varéc perked up immediately, an excited growl echoing through his spirit.

Aodhán chuckled—and a moment later, he rose to his feet, only to be caught off guard by the sheer power behind the motion. Five tiers at once was not something he experienced regularly. The last time he had gained so much power so quickly had been in Abyssos, nearly forty tiers ago.

His power had grown tremendously since then.

So when his body launched nearly seven feet into the air, he was completely unprepared for it. He quickly asserted control, taming the energy roaring through him with a level of finesse many Mythics would have envied.

Inhaling deeply, he flexed his fingers once, twice, feeling the overwhelming strength settle before glancing at the Mythic.

"Are you going to follow me wherever I go?"

"I have to," she replied simply. "I can't let you out of my sight until at least a day has passed. I have to do my due diligence."

Aodhán regarded her for a moment, then nodded. "If that's the case, then I should know your name."

"Vaust," she said with a small smile. "Lilian Vaust."

Aodhán smiled. "Alright, Lilian. How would you like to come delve a rift with me?"

"Wh—what?"

"Come on, it'll be fun. I have a lot of new skills to test, and you need to keep an eye on me. It's a perfect solution. What do you think?"

Lilian stared at him, mouth slightly open, shock written plainly across her face. Then she stuttered, "Of course… if that's what you want."

Aodhán's smile widened into a full grin as massive wings burst from his back.

"That's exactly what I want."

Lilian Vaust stared at the Crown of Ruinous Storms as they flew toward the Rift Verge, wondering what the hell he was thinking.

He flew not on a construct like she was, but with gigantic wings the color of midnight, frost-white hair whipping in the wind. He was beautiful, strong, and commanding—though it wasn't anything he consciously projected.

It was simply his bearing, the way authority gathered around him without effort, leaving everyone nearby to feel the weight of their own inadequacy by comparison.

Lilian knew hers well enough without his help.

She was a member of the null guards—an elite force of Mythics assigned to protecting disciples from the twisted effect. The fact that she had been assigned to a transmigrant half her tier proved what her superiors thought of her strength. The fact that she had accepted without protest confirmed what she thought of it herself.

She straightened her spine anyway, matching his altitude wingbeat for wingbeat.

She had barely made it into the inner sect at all, pushing herself up to the Mythic class through sheer persistence and an abundance of cores rather than any particular gift.

At the 117th tier, she was stronger than any advanced-class cultivator in the outer, inner, and even core sect—though depending on how advanced a core disciple was, even they could pose a problem.

But specials were a different breed of talent entirely.

And though she could most certainly hold her own against any creature in the advanced class, fighting alongside the Crown of Ruinous Storms would be a recipe for shame.

Should she fumble—

"No." She shook the thought away. She wasn't here to fight. She was here to keep an eye on him should he suddenly become deranged.

Pushing aside her fear, she flew closer toward him, the fluid beat of his wings drawing her attention for a moment before she spoke.

"So—how long have you known about this immunity of yours?"

Aodhán glanced at her, crimson-gold irises piercing. "A few months now. About seven to eight months ago."

"Oh." She frowned. "So you haven't always had it, but you still don't know what caused it?"

"No," Aodhán exhaled. "I've spent the last few months researching it, but I haven't found anything yet."

That was true.

He had spent the last five months researching his immunity alongside a host of other things he would never have had access to in Lutia—maps, histories, the Accords, forbidden records, tales of other transmigrants, and the life of the children of ascendants as Az'marthon had suggested.

But while he had found answers to most of his other questions, this one had remained stubbornly elusive, as though the answers he sought were not in any book—but in the Origin Plane itself.

He grimaced. "I wish I knew more. The implications it would have for the Sect would be tremendous."

Lilian regarded him for a moment before nodding. "It would. Many shy away from cultivation because of the twisted effect. Because of the fear of what they might do to their loved ones if they're even slightly negligent." She paused. "If your immunity could be understood… replicated…"

"I know," Aodhán said. He glanced at her briefly, sensing a fragment of her ideal in her words. He really needed to find an answer soon.

The rest of the flight passed in silence until finally, the Rift Verge appeared in the distance. Aodhán teleported immediately, and with an anxious sigh, Lilian did the same.

She appeared before the entrance a moment later and grimaced when she found Aodhán already waiting for her, watching with an expression that gave nothing away.

"Are you ready?" he asked.

Lilian stiffened, certain that her insecurities were laid bare. Her posture straightened instantly, her head lifted a notch, her tone turning crisp.

"I am always ready. The question is—are you?"

Aodhán seemed to consider that for a moment before shrugging. "If I go too far, you can always pull me out of danger."

Lilian felt her stomach churn. She refused to let it show. "Of course. That is why I'm here."

Aodhán grinned in a way that did not inspire much confidence in his supposed lucidity as he walked excitedly into the verge.

Inhaling deeply, Lilian followed him, holding herself rigid as she projected authority.

"Good evening, Elder Lou," she called out before Aodhán could, her head held high. "The Crown of Ruinous Storms would like—"

"I know Aodhán," Elder Lou cut her off with a smile as his gaze landed on them. "Good evening, Lilian. What brings you both here?"

But rather than give her a chance to respond, Elder Lou turned to Aodhán. "It has been a while since I've seen you, boy. I thought perhaps I had chased you away with my talk of arrogance."

"Not at all," Aodhán shook his head with a grin. "I've just been taking things slow since the war."

Elder Lou's expression sobered. "A wise choice. The mind needs time to recover from trauma, the same as the body."

Aodhán nodded in return, and Lilian barely restrained a scowl at their seamless camaraderie. Why couldn't she be like this—calm, insightful, composed?

But she was determined not to be interrupted again.

"Elder Lou." Lilian stepped forward. "The Crown of Ruinous Storms attended the ascension this morning and appears to have experienced no reaction to the twisted effect. I've chosen to observe him for the next twenty-four hours."

"She means I'm immune," Aodhán said. "And call me Aodhán. By the storm, that alias is a mouthful."

Elder Lou's lips twitched in amusement before his expression turned serious. "Did you just say immune? To the twisted effect?"

"Until proven otherwise," Lilian said evenly.

Aodhán sighed. "Can I just delve? I've got a ton of new skills I really want to try."

Elder Lou paused, then glanced at Lilian. "I take it you're going with him?"

"I am."

"Very well," Elder Lou smiled. "The verge is open to you."

"Thank you."

Aodhán raised his sect ring, paid the requisite fee, and began scanning the rifts, his gaze moving from one to another as he searched for something that resonated.

Elder Lou and Lilian followed behind him in silence, watching curiously.

He stopped when he came across the void rift, a memory of the gigantic Super Gnasher appearing in his mind. The memory made him chuckle, but after a moment, he moved on, searching for a different rift.

He wasn't quite sure what he was looking for, but a few minutes later, he found it.

A rift of swirling white and frost blue. An Ice rift.

The rift was no different or more special than the others, but the moment his gaze fell on it, he was reminded of the first dungeon he and Daruk had delved. The dungeon where Daruk had found his legacy.

The thought of Daruk brought a sad smile to his face, and he whispered, "I'll take this one."

"A fine choice," Elder Lou nodded. "You know the drill. Stay alive."

Aodhán nodded, and with an excited smile, he stepped into the swirling portal.

Lilian watched him go, delving straight into a rift so thoroughly unsuited for her path that it was practically a nemesis. She grimaced, inhaled deeply, then glanced at Elder Lou.

"Don't let him intimidate you, Lilian," the elder said, his tone gentler than before. "He may be stronger than you—stronger than us—but in the end, he's just a child."

Lilian held his gaze for a moment. "He's not stronger than me."

And without another word, she stepped into the rift.

Elder Lou watched her disappear, a knowing smile tugging at his lips as he shook his head.

The arrogance of youth, he thought, truly is a remarkable thing.
