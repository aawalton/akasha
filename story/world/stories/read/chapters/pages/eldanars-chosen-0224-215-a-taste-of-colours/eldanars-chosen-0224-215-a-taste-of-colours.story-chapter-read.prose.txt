The dim, flickering light of the corner booth felt like a tiny sanctuary amidst the suffocating dread of the Guildhall. Brett was completely dead to the world, his pale face planted firmly into the crook of his folded arms on the scarred wooden table. His soft, rhythmic snores provided a steady background noise to the quiet, anxious murmur of the remaining adventurers scattered throughout the massive room.

Josh sat comfortably on the hard wooden bench, nursing the last bitter dregs of his terrible dwarven ale. He was acutely aware of the incredible warmth radiating from Carcan sitting just across from him. The intense, terrifying emotional storm that had gripped the elven healer earlier had finally passed, leaving a quiet, vulnerable calm in its wake.

Carcan traced the rim of her wooden tankard with a slender, elegant finger, her luminous silver eyes reflecting the distant, flickering glow of the hearth fires. She remained silent for a long time, simply soaking in the ambient peace of their isolated corner. Eventually, she looked up. Her gaze locked directly onto Josh’s storm-grey eyes. The look carried a sharp, entirely focused intensity that made his heart give a sudden, heavy thud against his steel breastplate.

"What did my brother ask of you?" Carcan asked quietly.

Josh blinked, completely caught off guard by the sudden, piercing question. He shifted uncomfortably on the wooden bench, his armour clanking softly in the quiet booth. He looked down at his tankard, avoiding her eyes. "We were just talking about the scouting route, Carcan. He was explaining the approach to the northern road."

"Do not lie to me, Josh," Carcan reprimanded gently, though her melodic tone left absolutely no room for argument. "I know Perberos better than I know my own soul. I saw the way his posture shifted when he stood in front of you. I saw the way you placed your hand over your heart. That was not a tactical discussion. That was an oath."

Josh looked down at his scarred, dirt-caked gauntlets resting on the table, then at Brett, confirming his friend was still unconscious. He felt a sudden, familiar flush of heat creep up the back of his neck, warming the cold air. He tried desperately to think of a plausible deflection, a way to soften the horrific reality of what Perberos had actually demanded of him. However, looking back into her luminous, waiting silver eyes, he found he simply could not lie to her.

"He told me that Brett and I are anomalies," Josh confessed, his voice dropping into a deep, gravelly whisper so as not to wake the sleeping mage beside him. "He said that I possessed a strength that defied the natural order. And then he gave me an order."

Carcan leaned slightly closer across the table, her breath catching in her throat. "What order?"

"He told me that if the timber walls fall, I am not to stand and die for a doomed city," Josh explained, the memory of the elven scout's desperate, fierce eyes burning freshly in his mind. "He demanded that I take you and run. He told me to use every single ounce of my strength to carve a path through the horde and get you out of Ashenfall. He asked me to protect you with my life."

Carcan’s features softened into an expression of profound, heartbreaking sorrow. She looked away, her eyes glistening with fresh, unshed tears at the sheer weight of her twin brother's love, and his terrifying assumption that the city was already lost.

"That is a terribly heavy burden to place upon you, Josh," Carcan whispered, her voice trembling slightly. "To ask you to abandon the people you have sworn to protect, to abandon your friends, simply to save me."

"Carcan, wait," Josh interrupted quickly.

He leaned forward across the scarred wooden table. He reached out, his heavy, steel-clad hands gently enveloping her slender, pale fingers. The contact sent a sharp jolt of nervous electricity straight up his arm.

"You need to understand something," Josh said, his storm-grey eyes burning with absolute, uncompromising conviction. "It is not a burden. It was an oath I would have made even if Perberos had never spoken a single word to me. I would do absolutely anything for you. I would tear the darkness apart with my bare hands to keep you safe, Carcan. I will protect you with everything I have."

He opened his mouth to say something else. He wanted to finally give a name to the heavy, iron anchor of affection that had been solidifying in his chest for months, to tell her how much she meant to him in this brutal, terrifying world.

He never got the words out.

Carcan did not hesitate. She leaned into him, her pale green robes rustling softly against the grain of the wooden bench, and pressed her lips firmly against his.

For Josh, the entire world instantly and violently ceased to exist.

The deafening, ambient noise of the crowded Guildhall, the crackling of the hearth fires, and the heavy, terrifying dread of the impending siege completely vanished from his perception. The sensation was absolutely explosive. It felt as though a raw, highly concentrated surge of pure magical energy had bypassed his thick steel armour entirely and struck him directly in the soul.

He could taste colours. Brilliant, exploding shades of gold, amber, and vibrant green flooded his senses. He could hear the physical manifestation of light, a beautiful, harmonic ringing that echoed through his very bones, entirely separate from the grim reality of Ashenfall.

The electrical shock of the moment held him completely frozen for a single, stunned second. Then, his instincts finally caught up with his reeling brain.

Josh raised his hands, gently framing her delicate, aristocratic face. He kissed her back, pouring all of his unspoken fear, his deep exhaustion, and his desperate, fierce affection into the connection. Carcan let out a soft, breathless sigh, her hands moving to grip the thick, padded gambeson at his shoulders, completely anchoring herself to his massive frame.

They lingered in that quiet, beautiful bubble of shared warmth, entirely oblivious to the apocalyptic war preparing to break just outside the wooden walls of the city.

A sudden, incredibly loud snort shattered the moment.

"And as I was so eloquently explaining," Brett's slurred, entirely groggy voice announced loudly, his face still glued to the table.

Josh and Carcan broke apart instantly, practically scrambling backward on their respective sides of the wooden bench. They untangled their hands wildly, both of their faces burning with a brilliant, matching shade of bright crimson.

Brett pushed himself slowly up from the scarred wood, rubbing his pale eyes and blinking owlishly at the dim tavern light. He completely ignored their frantic, guilty movements, yawning massively and stretching his long arms.

"The fundamental principle of thermal displacement relies on the ambient pressure of the environment," Brett continued, picking up his complex magical lecture exactly where he had left off. He reached blindly for his empty wooden tankard, frowning deeply when he found nothing left to drink. "Which is why casting plasma in a freezing swamp is exhausting. Did I miss anything important?"

"Nothing at all," Carcan squeaked, her melodic voice cracking slightly. She suddenly found a loose splinter on the wooden table incredibly fascinating, refusing to look up at either of the humans.

"Just resting our eyes," Josh agreed rapidly, his heart still hammering wildly against his breastplate.

Brett nodded slowly as he looked towards the bustling front of the grand hall, feigning deep interest in a group of passing clerks. As Carcan kept her head firmly turned away to hide her glowing, flushed cheeks, Brett slowly turned his head back towards Josh.

The elemental mage offered his best friend a slow, highly exaggerated, and entirely knowing wink.

Josh groaned internally, realising the sneaky mage had likely been awake for the entire heartfelt confession.

Before Josh could formulate a suitable threat to keep Brett quiet, the heavy thud of approaching boots drew their attention.

Torin, the heavily scarred veteran who had led the forward camp, approached their booth. He was accompanied by Kaelen, the broad-shouldered dwarven Stoneweaver who had helped shatter the sinkhole wall. They both looked incredibly grim, carrying a large wooden pitcher and several clean mugs.

"Mind if we share your shadows for a bit?" Torin asked, his gravelly voice low and exhausted. "The hearth is too crowded, and the young recruits are asking too many questions."

"Take a seat," Josh offered, clearing away the empty ale tankards to make room. He was secretly grateful for the interruption, as it allowed the burning heat in his cheeks to slowly subside.

Torin slid into the booth beside Carcan, while Kaelen dropped heavily onto the bench next to Brett, her massive granite staff clattering against the floorboards. The dwarf poured four cups, passing them around.

"The city council has finalised the defensive grid," Torin announced, taking a long drink of water. He wiped his scarred chin, his dark eyes locking onto Josh. "They are splitting the high-tier assets to cover the most vulnerable points. It is not going to be pretty."

Brett leaned forward, all traces of his sleepy, drunken persona completely vanishing. "Where are they putting us?"

"Mages and archers are being stationed on the upper wooden ramparts," Kaelen answered, her gruff voice echoing softly. "They need your fire to light the pitch trenches and break the initial wave. The Stoneweavers are being kept in reserve near the secondary street barricades to build chokepoints if the main timber walls breach."

"And the heavy infantry?" Josh asked, adjusting the strap of his heater shield.

"The absolute meat grinder," Torin stated bluntly. "They are putting you, the rest of the heavy shield-bearers, and the surviving city guard right at the main iron portcullis. If they bring up a battering ram, or if the Bone Goliath’s larger cousins decide to knock, you are the only physical wall keeping them out of the residential district."

Josh felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He was going to be stationed at the very epicentre of the slaughter, standing in the thickest mud.

"Where is the triage centre?" Carcan asked, her voice returning to its usual serene cadence, though her hands were clasped tightly together on the table.

"They have set up the primary medical tents in the central temple square, about three hundred yards behind the main gates," Torin explained, looking at the elven healer with deep respect. "You and the other high-tier clerics will be stationed there. Runners will bring the wounded back to you. They absolutely refuse to risk losing their best healers on the front lines."

Josh looked at Carcan, the horrific reality of the deployment sinking in. He would be holding the main gate, completely surrounded by thousands of undead, while she might as well be half a world away in the city square. If the gates fell and the horde flooded the streets, he would have to fight his way through an ocean of monsters just to reach her, let alone fulfil his oath to Perberos to get her out of the city.

"It is a sound tactical strategy," Brett noted quietly, tracing a finger through a puddle of condensation on the table. "Protect the support assets, use the heavy fighters to bottleneck the breach, and rain area-of-effect spells from the high ground."

"It is the only strategy we have," Kaelen grunted, resting her thick arms on the table. "But if they have as many numbers as we suspect, those timber walls will splinter eventually. We are just buying time for the refugees to hide deeper in the cellars."

They sat together in a heavy, companionable silence for another half hour. The veterans shared quiet, grim stories of past dungeon breaks, finding a strange comfort in the shared camaraderie of the condemned. They finished their drinks, conserved their energy, and prepared their minds for the apocalyptic violence the morning would undoubtedly bring.

Eventually, Torin slapped the scarred wooden table and stood up, his heavy chainmail clinking.

"I need to check my mace and find a floorboard to sleep on," the scarred fighter announced, offering Josh a respectful nod. "It was an honour fighting with you, Josh. I hope to see you standing at the gates when the enemies arrive."

"You will," Josh promised firmly.

Kaelen grunted her goodbyes, following the human fighter back into the crowded, noisy hall.

"Right, well, my theories have exhausted me," Brett announced suddenly, stretching his long arms above his head and cracking his neck loudly. "And the booze is all gone. The night is officially over. We should also find a patch of floorboards to claim before the city guard drafts us for early wall duty."

"We actually have rooms," Carcan corrected softly. She reached into the folds of her pale green robes, pulling out three heavy, brass keys attached to a small wooden block. "Rochelle had a clerk drop these off while you were getting the drinks earlier. Apparently, hitting level twenty-seven officially classifies us as high-tier veterans in the Guild ledger. The administration guarantees us private quarters in the upper dormitories before a major siege."

"Private beds?" Brett gasped, his pale eyes going completely wide with absolute, profound joy. "Actual mattresses? I might genuinely cry. I thought we were going to have to sleep under a table."

The trio gathered their gear. Josh hoisted his massive steel shield onto his back, and they made their way through the shadowed, crowded hall. They climbed the winding, heavy oak stairs to the upper floors of the massive building. The corridors here were quiet and peaceful, lined with thick, expensive carpets that perfectly muffled the heavy clanking of Josh's steel boots.

They found their assigned rooms clustered tightly together at the very end of the long hall, far away from the noise of the anxious adventurers below.

Brett immediately unlocked his door, pushing it open to reveal a small, simple room with solid wooden bed, a small washbasin, and a thick woollen blanket.

"I am going to sleep until the undead physically drag me out of this bed," Brett declared, turning back to his friends. "Goodnight, you two."

The mage disappeared inside, shutting the heavy wooden door with a solid, definitive click.

Carcan stopped in front of her own door, gripping her brass key tightly. She turned to face Josh. The quiet, isolated intimacy of the dimly lit corridor brought a sudden, fresh wave of shy tension back between them, the memory of the booth rushing back to the surface.

"Goodnight, Josh," Carcan said softly, looking up at him through her thick, silver-white eyelashes. A faint blush still painted her cheeks. "Get some sleep. We have a terrifying day ahead of us tomorrow."

"Goodnight, Carcan," Josh replied, his voice a low, comforting rumble in the quiet hall. He offered her a small, genuine smile. "I will see you in the morning. And Carcan? I meant every single word."

She offered a final, lingering look, the warmth of the promise heavily present in the air between them, before stepping into her room and closing the door softly behind her.

Josh stood alone in the quiet corridor for a long moment, simply staring at the polished wood of her door. His mind was completely reeling from the events of the night. The adrenaline of the retreat, the heavy burden of the impending siege, and the explosive, world-altering kiss all warred for dominance in his exhausted brain.

He took a deep, steadying breath, finally turning away to unlock his own assigned quarters directly across the hall.

He pushed his door open and stepped inside, ready to collapse into his armour.

He immediately stopped dead in his tracks.

Brett was not in his own room. The elemental mage was sitting cross-legged right in the middle of Josh’s bed, waiting for him, their adjoining door wide open.

The mage had completely dropped his exhausted, scholarly persona. His blonde hair was wildly messy, and a massive, completely goofy grin was plastered across his pale face. He looked exactly like the student Josh had known back on Earth.

As Josh closed the heavy wooden door behind him, Brett pumped a fist excitedly into the air.

"Yeeeaah, boiii!" Brett cheered loudly, dropping back into the comfortable, ridiculous slang of their old world.
