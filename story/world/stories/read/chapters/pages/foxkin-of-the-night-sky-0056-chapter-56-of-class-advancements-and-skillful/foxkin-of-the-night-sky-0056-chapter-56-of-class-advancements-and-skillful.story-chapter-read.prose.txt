Useless.

Naturally.

Dragonslayer Vanguard.

First impressions:

Stats? Completely feral.

Hard work and high-rarity classes matter.

Good.

Again.

Boring. Cringe. Uninspired.

Especially when compared to my beautiful, elegant, objectively superior Star Aspect.

Therefore—

Also useless.

Tragic, but expected.

First up: Radiant Footfall.

Very aesthetic.

Objectively cool sounding.

Leaving little trails of burning starlight with every step is extremely on-brand for me. Not sure why it's starlight instead of starfire, but I refuse to question it.

Anti-backstab tech is always appreciated.

I already have my gravity sense.

Which means I am already fairly good at detecting assassins, ambushers, and overzealous plant monsters attempting to extract my spine like it’s a worm being evicted by an extremely motivated bird.

—which is to say, most environments—

Including things that may or may not belong to people.

Therefore:

Not great.

Also very aesthetic. Very elegant. Very… redundant.

Which is impressive, except for the small detail that I already do that constantly.

So the System has generously offered me a button that allows me to replicate something I can already achieve through sufficiently chaotic movement and unhinged swordplay.

Very thoughtful.

Into the garbage it goes.

Next: Spiral Sovereignty.

A mild inconvenience disguised as an Epic skill.

Why bother using gravity to cosplay as a kaleidoscopic tornado when I already outspeed everything within my level range?

If something manages to escape my spin radius, the correct solution is clearly to move faster.

Which I can already do.

Garbage bin.

And now—

The final two.

Both Legendary.

Both not just good, but dangerously close to perfect.

My tail swishes harder.

First up: Celestial Crescendo.

At first glance, it doesn’t seem that incredible. It basically boils down to more speed, with an extra powerful attack right at the end. A respectable concept, certainly.

However, the wording is slightly misleading.

After all, it doesn’t say movement speed, like my Running skill does.

It increases Dexterity.

Which means it doesn’t just buff my speed.

It likely buffs everything tied to Dexterity. Movement speed. Reaction time. Attack speed. Likely even my time perception, considering how the stat itself behaves.

Which immediately makes the skill far more interesting than it first appears.

I am, however, still curious about one particular detail.

Stamina.

Specifically, how the stamina interaction works.

If it behaves the way I suspect—which is to say, temporarily raising my Dexterity and therefore increasing my stamina pool alongside it, while also draining stamina to maintain the effect—then something very funny happens.

Because my stamina regeneration is calculated based on my total stamina pool. And my Starborn trait multiplies my regeneration rate.

Which means that if the skill increases my stamina pool through Dexterity scaling, it also indirectly increases my regeneration.

And because I can access Starborn’s regeneration through my Arcana whenever I want…

…this becomes a double buff.

Speed increases.

Regeneration increases.

And because my Star-aspected abilities scale with the amount of Astral Energy I shove into them, the amount of power I can dump into attacks also increases.

Which means my destruction ceiling rises right alongside my regeneration.

Absolutely busted.

Eleven out of ten.

Assuming it works the way I think it does, of course.

And then there’s the other one.

Starfall Sequence.

An active damaging skill.

Normally that would already put it at a disadvantage compared to a scaling buff like Crescendo.

Except there is one extremely important distinction between this and Starlight Sweep.

I cannot replicate this effect.

At all.

From what I can tell, it functions like an echo attack.

Meaning I can slash once…

…and then the same attack arrives again from a different angle.

That alone puts it above an eight out of ten, because echo-type attacks are objectively cool and I refuse to pretend otherwise.

But it gets better the more closely you read the wording.

Specifically:

“Additional trajectories” and “nearby angles.”

Which means the replicated strike does not simply repeat the original slash in the same location.

It can target an entirely different space.

Which conveniently avoids the extremely awkward scenario where the first slash connects perfectly, removes an important body part, and then the echo strike politely arrives half a second later to hit absolutely nothing.

Gold star for that design decision.

But the truly interesting line comes later.

At first glance it looks like a drawback.

“Consumes stamina based on the power and properties of the replicated strike.”

Key word:

Properties.

Which likely means it replicates Starfire as well. Which means it likely replicates my Starfire motes. Which means a single slash could potentially produce multiple flaming orbital murder particles descending from nearby angles.

The restriction to slashing attacks prevents it from interacting with Rending Starfire Line, unfortunately.

However, the Starfire motes alone already push this skill into extremely questionable territory.

And once I eventually invent a new slashing step for Blazing Star Dance?

Stars forbid.

I will vaporize anything I come across.

Also an eleven out of ten.

This is a first.

Both options are optimal.

Aggressively so.
