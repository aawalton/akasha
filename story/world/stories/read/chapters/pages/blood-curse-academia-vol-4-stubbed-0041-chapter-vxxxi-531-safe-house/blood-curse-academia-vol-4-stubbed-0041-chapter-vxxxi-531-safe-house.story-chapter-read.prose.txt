Chapter V.XXXI (5.31) - Safe House

“What do you think you’re doing here?” a grumpy old woman demanded, arms crossed and an unamused expression on her face.

Kizu noted with relief that both of Roba’s hands were their normal shade of skin. Not a speck of glittery blue to be seen. He offered her a shaky smile as he pressed his back against the door leading back into the hallway.

On the other side of the glass windows behind Roba, a massive sea turtle floated by. He extended his neck and snatched a strand of seaweed. Yanking back, the reptile tore the leafy vegetable asunder.

“This is the safest room in the academy,” Kizu replied simply.

“The safest….” The old woman’s posture stiffened. For several seconds she glared at him, her face stoney. Then her nose twitched. That small gesture released the dam. She threw her head back and cackled.

Kizu exchanged a look with Mort. They both shivered. The noise sent him back to his youth under the crone’s tutelage. A cackle like this usually meant a coin flip between very good and very bad news.

When the old academic administrator regained her composure, she simply leaned back in her seat. No hex to turn them into seaweed for the turtle on the other side of the glass. That was a good sign.

Kizu frowned. Was it stereotyping to assume old, belligerent women would transform him into vegetation when vexed? Probably. That rational realization sadly didn’t help put him at ease. He had a decade of experience fearing temperamental old ladies.

“Fine, fine. Take a seat.” Roba waved a hand and a wooden chair appeared in front of her desk. She continued to chortle to herself. “Of course, if the professors break down my door, I won’t protect you. You’ll be at their mercy.”

“I figured.”

“But you also figured that no professor would ever think to look for you in the administrative office during the middle of an academy challenge.”

Kizu nodded and let a smug grin slip through. “Am I wrong?”

“Only time will tell. But I doubt they’ll find you anytime soon. In the meantime, how about we go over that technique we spoke of in our last meeting. I assume you did your due diligence and studied?”

“Is this… is this really the best time for a lesson?” Kizu glanced over his shoulder at the door. He would really like to conserve the little remaining blood in his body. Still, he sat down in the wooden seat across from her. She hadn’t kicked him out or left him paralyzed on the floor so he was counting this as a win. Plus, he was genuinely curious about the lesson.

“Yes,” she replied. “If you’re going to laze about in my office, we might as well knock out this week’s tutoring session.”

Roba rested her elbows on the table and steepled her fingers. Her good humor faded as she eyed him seriously.

“What do you know about mind magic?”

“I know that antimagical barriers around my skull protect me from it,” Kizu offered.

Roba snorted. “I suppose that’s a start at least. Mental magic is extremely complex and any half decent mental mage spends decades dedicated to the art to develop a single worthwhile technique.”

“How long did you spend?” Kizu asked, thinking back on her memory wipe of Inari’s lackeys after his attack on Owl’s Respite.

“Don’t interrupt," she reprimanded him. “And I spent seven years to uncover recent memories and another six to alter their narrative. Even still, after learning the spell combination forty years ago, I cannot touch anything beyond four hours, thirty-minutes and four seconds.”

Kizu blinked. He doubted that Roba had dedicated every moment of every day to the task for thirteen years and she likely was slowed down significantly by both her blood supply at the start and willing participants to experiment on, but even still that was a long time to learn a couple spells.

“May I continue without interruption now?” Roba paused and gave him a reproachful glare.

Kizu mimicked zipping his lips shut with his fingers. A sensation he’d actually physically experienced once firsthand by the town’s constable.

“Good. As I was saying, even should I be willing to teach you the art, I wouldn’t bother. You have a great aptitude for spatial magic and took to it far faster than I expected. However, to use a bit of geographic symbolism, spatial magic is city sized in difficulty while mental magic is in a continent sized. Even should you be a natural genius, it would require rigorous studies.” She paused again and pursed her lips. “Fine. I see the question threatening to burst your insides apart. Speak.”

“I met a witch my age in the Hon Basin who was a mental—”

“Witches use a shortcut to success,” Roba said, not bothering to let him complete the thought. “Though anyone your age is still a terrifying prospect.”

“A shortcut?”

“They cut open the heads of victims, carefully keeping them alive and conscious, and use hands-on methods to study the mechanics of the brain. That level of experimentation is second to none in regards to growth. The covens also have access to several brews designed to assist the blood’s aptitude for specific studies. Though I suggest you avoid looking into the ingredients.”

Kizu winced. He already knew. It was human flesh.

“Regardless, instead of learning how to cast mental spells, I plan to teach you a method of defense.”

Kizu held his breath, mind racing at the prospect of learning a new spell, even a defensive one. Dipping his toes in yet another branch of magic.

“Close your eyes,” she instructed.

He did so, letting the room around him disappear under the cover of his eyelids.

“Now, let’s take a thought or a memory. Something frivolous that you can remember in detail. Deeply ponder it.”

Kizu, scrambling for a usable thought, decided to focus on this morning when Mort stole a bit of his breakfast at Taroe’s house. The monkey had scampered off into a cupboard with his prize. It was an unimportant event that repeated pretty much every other day in his life. Still, this time it had been a slice of pear stolen and the act of treachery had been a moment before Kizu’s chopstick skewered the fruit. He deeply pondered the moment’s event and his momentary feelings of irritation.

“You have the image in mind?” Roba asked, her voice breaking into the memory.

Kizu nodded.

“Good. Now wrap it all up and stuff it in a sack.”

“Wh–what?”

“Imagine a sack. And stuff the memory into it.”

Kizu did his best to follow her instructions. A burlap sack, like the one he’d used to dispose of the blood tests in the first semester, appeared in his mind’s eye. Then he tried dropping it over the vivid memory.

“Next, enhance your head.”

“Enhance? Like… the type of spells I use with my muscles?”

“The brain is a muscle as well. Enhance it.”

Kizu cast the spell. It resulted in a splitting headache that made him wince. But he pushed through the pain and enhanced his brain. Suddenly the image in the bag froze.

“Release it,” Roba instructed. “Then open your eyes.”

Kizu blinked his eyes open. He remained in Roba’s office, the fish swimming in schools behind her.

“Do you remember the memory?”

“I—” Kizu hesitated and concentrated on what he’d just been thinking of. The echoes of a headache were all that remained. “It was… a meal? I think?”

The old woman’s face split into a smile. “Excellent. That memory is now compartmentalized in your mind. I’ll give you exercises where you will learn to pull information from the memory without recalling the exact event. I think you’ll agree that niche enhancement spells are far easier to learn than mental spells.”

Kizu felt his body slacken in relief. “So if someone does unlock my hidden memories….”

“You can seal them away again.”

Not only that, Roba had mentioned a technique in which to extract information. So he could compartmentalize the experience as a whole while retaining the knowledge.

“Different from the arrangement we have,” Zayne said in his mind. “You are currently exposed to the entirety of my memory. Perhaps this way you could block out that aspect so as to not let my personality leak into you.”

Kizu’s eye twitched at the sudden voice in his mind and then quickly coughed to cover the movement. Roba narrowed her eyes but she said nothing.

It disturbed him that he never knew when Zayne was listening in to his conversations. The sliver of soul had only ever helped him, so he wasn’t actively attempting to purge him, but Kizu still didn’t like it.

“Could I break the cocoon around the memory I just forgot?” Kizu asked. “Remember it in its entirety?”

“Yes. Keep in mind that this is different from true mental magic. This is an enhancement technique. A proficient mental mage could easily break both, though you can only access what you personally stored away.”

“So, should I get attacked by a mental assault that releases what the crone hid away, I’ll need to retain enough sanity to seal the memories away,” Kizu mused. “There’s still a chance I lose myself and break.”

“That is a matter of willpower. And, since you are someone known for chopping off your own limb and persevering in extremely dire scenarios, I doubt you lack the willpower required. I’ve noted you are bullheadingly stubborn.” That was nearly a compliment from Roba.

Kizu chewed on his lip. It was easy to say that he would just push through the problem. But there was still the seed of doubt in his mind.

Roba snapped her fingers and a ragged rug appeared on the floor behind Kizu’s chair.

“What’s—”

“Until this competition ends or the other faculty locate you here, I expect you to follow my meditation instructions to the letter,” Roba said. “Now, remove your boots and kneel for meditation.”
