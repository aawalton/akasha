When the timer for the wave the S Grade True Dragon had been a part of finally ticked its end, the System announced that the floor had been completed.

There’d been a part of Xavier that hadn’t wanted to wait the long, long minutes to see if that would even happen, but waiting had been worth it. He was a completionist, and the last thing he wanted was to not have actually completed that floor after how much effort he’d just spent on it.

God, he was glad the System had caught up to the armada having been destroyed.

Returning to the Staging Room of the Tower of Champions was surreal after spending so much time on the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor.

When he’d gone to the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor, he hadn’t expected to be on it for very long. He’d taken quite the leap of faith, jumping forward so many floors in the tower, in the hopes of finding a floor worthy enough to farm until he hit B Grade.

The Empire of Servitude’s armada, with its millions of ships, hadn’t been what he’d expected. There’d been a part of him that had worried he’d gotten in far too deep. Several times he’d thought of abandoning the floor, using the void to escape.

But he was glad he persisted.

Standing in the Staging Room, his mind felt quiet. Incredibly quiet. That, too, was a strange feeling after having shared that strong psychic link with so many other versions of himself. The moment he’d returned through the void it had felt like his mind had shrunk from the size of the universe to the size of a walnut. He found he craved connecting with the other versions of him again. Craved that feeling of being combined, even if it was only through the psychic connection.

They were stronger together.

We’ll make that connection again. Soon.

But he also knew it wasn’t something he would wish to live with all the time. He walked out of the Staging Room and relished the feeling of his own individuality, no longer thinking of himself as “them” or “the Xaviers.”

He was merely Xavier, and that was how he liked it best.

His footsteps echoed down the Tower of Champion’s spiral staircase as he made his way to the bottom. Another Champion was on the stairs, moving in incredibly slow motion. They, like him, were inside a time dilation field, but it was significantly weaker than his own. He noticed the moment the other Champion—a tall, slender man in leather armour with his hair tied in a ponytail—realised he was there. The eyes widening, catching him just as he passed, but not fast enough to turn around.

Xavier: [Hey man, are you there?] He waited until he was on the bottom step, to see if he got a reply. He hesitated there at the bottom step and tilted his head to the side, waiting a little longer. But no reply came. [Xavier Prime?]

Nothing.

Maybe he’s on a tower floor… or maybe his time dilation field is a lot weaker than mine. But no, it can’t be that much weaker…

Xavier pushed open the door and walked through into the tavern. Sam was frozen outside the field behind the bar. He looked around, searching for a familiar face. All the Champions had time dilation fields of their own, but to him it looked like they were barely moving. His power level had already been far, far higher than the average citizen in his sectors, but now that he’d advanced to B Grade, he’d pushed that level even higher.

He smiled when his gaze fell on Adranial. He made his way to the woman. She was sitting at one of the corner tables, slowly sipping a mug of coffee, a habit she’d gotten from him. Her time dilation field was faster than the other Champions in the room, but it still wasn’t fast enough for her to notice him until he was standing right in front of her table.

Xavier enveloped her in his time dilation field just as her eyebrows flicked up her forehead.

A great big smile landed on her face at the sight of him, lighting up her features in a way that only made her more beautiful. “You’re back!” She leapt up from her seat and wrapped her arms around him. A weaker man might have fallen over as her embrace was more akin to a tackle.

“I see you missed me. How long was I gone?”

There was a time or two on that floor when he hadn’t had a time dilation field active at all, though they’d been few and far between. Not only that, he’d spent years in that place. He wasn’t even sure how many had actually passed. His time dilation field might be the strongest around these parts, but he was sure a good bit of time had gone by for everyone else since he’d entered that floor.

“About ten months,” Adranial chided, holding him by the shoulders and getting a good look at him. Her forehead was creased and her eyes were narrowed. “Something’s different.”

Xavier ran a hand through his hair. It had gotten long. Very long. He hadn’t bothered to cut it lately. “Is it the hair? I can cut it.”

She shook her head. “It’s not the hair. Though, yes, please. You can’t pull off the long-haired long.”

Xavier put a hand on his chest and gasped, faux-offended. “I can’t?”

Adranial grinned. “Sorry to break it to you, but no, you really can’t. You should definitely cut it. Don’t you usually get one of your drones to do it?” She studied him a bit longer, her expression shifting as something seemed to dawn on her. Eventually, her eyes widened. “You advanced!”

Xavier blinked. Frowned. “How did you know?”

“Hah! I didn’t know, but that confirms it.” She punched him on the shoulder. “B Grade! Congratulations! So, where the hell have you been, anyway?”

“How about we sit down. I think a nice, hot meal is in order.” Xavier searched his memories for the last time he’d eaten—it had been years. Yes, it wasn’t something he needed to do, not for a very long time. But he didn’t need to drink coffee, either. Not needing to wasn’t going to stop him.

Xavier enveloped Sam into the time dilation field and ordered something wholesome and basic. A go-to, something that had actually popped into his mind not so long ago. Bacon and eggs. He conjured a coffee from his Storage Ring. The stuff they served here was good but sometimes something better was in order.

He savoured the food and took his time with the coffee, drinking it in small sips. It felt good to just enjoy something without the pressure of an entire world—or an entire universe—weighing on his shoulders for a few scant minutes.

It wasn’t until he’d finished his meal that he told Adranial what he’d been up to.

“You defeated the Empire of Servitude’s armada?” Adranial had stood rapidly from the table, shock written over her face. “That’s… that’s…”

“Impossible?”

Adranial shook her head. “I’ve not only read about that floor, I’ve experienced second hand memories of it! Everyone of those ships has an A Grade! Not to mention a hundred thousand B Grades! Then there are the Champions… You’re saying you took them all out?”

“Why don’t you sit back down, and I’ll tell the entire story.”

Adranial frowned at him, huffed, then sat back down and gestured toward him.

As he talked, he could tell she was heavily restraining herself from interrupting and asking questions. He couldn’t help but feel a little enjoyment from the way she kept opening her mouth then forcing it shut again, squirming in her seat the longer the story went on.

When he got to the part about the four A Grade dragonkin Champions turning into an S Grade True Dragon she couldn’t hold it in anymore.

“An S Grade? You’ve got to be kidding me! And a True Dragon, I thought those things were myths!”

When he got to the part about how he’d defeated not only one S Grade, but a hundred of them, he’d stopped to give her a chance to talk, except by that point she’d been rendered speechless, her mouth hanging agape for a moment that stretched awkwardly long.

“Adranial, are you still with me?”

She nodded numbly, her mouth slowly closing. “You’re telling me the truth about all of this.” She put her head in her hands. “The things you’ve done—the things you can do. And this… this Ancestral Soul? That’s insane, Xavier. Some… some ancient, eternal version of yourself has been influencing your every move? Since… since you were born?”

Xavier had wondered if he should tell her about the Ancestral Soul, but there were already other things he was keeping from the woman he loved—he didn’t want that to be one of them. As it was, he wished he could tell her about the revelation about the Interdimensional Dragon, wished he could share the fact that it had, in a way, come from him.

Hell, she still didn’t even know about Roln.

The least he could do was tell her about this.

“I mean, as I understand it, it’s not just me who has an Ancestral Soul, it’s everyone.” He pondered that. “Unless, I suppose, they were a completely unique individual. A first soul. And, considering anything is possible out there in the multiverse, I have to imagine that is, too.”

Xavier frowned, thinking for a moment about the nature of the multiverse, wondering if there were universes out there unaffected by the System… and he imagined there had to be. Not only that, there would be universes where he’d never been born. Where humans had never evolved—maybe dinosaurs still ruled the planet in those places.

Or insects. Hell, maybe even dolphins…

It made him think about what he might do when all this was done. When he’d defeated the Interdimensional Dragon and overthrown the System. Would he Wander the multiverse and discover more of its secrets?

Yes, I rather think I would, though I wouldn’t use all my immortality for such things.

Adranial had gone quiet again, though she looked more contemplative than dumbfounded. “So, there’s some conglomerated soul version of me out there in a hidden phase of reality pulling the strings of fate in my life?”

“Ancestral Souls don’t—”

“Don’t control us, I know, you said that already. Several times.” Adranial sighed. “But they clearly influence us. Some of us more than others.” She eyed him.

“You look worried.”

“I’m just wondering if us… if our being together… is just something your Ancestral Soul influenced you to do. What if it was just somehow the right strategic move in your life? And would you have looked at my twice had otherwise?”

Xavier grinned, his eyes raking her up and down. “Oh, Adranial, I could never stop looking at you.”

Adranial glared. “Xavier, I’m serious.”

Xavier’s grin slipped away. “So am I.” He reached over the table and grabbed her hand, wondering for a moment if this exact conversation was happening in two thousand universes simultaneously, or if some of the other versions of him he’d just created had already diverged. “I’d be a fool to overlook you in any universe. I’m sure I’ve fallen for you every time our paths have crossed, and not because of some outside influence. And if my Ancestral Soul is nudging me toward you? Well, that just means I’ve loved you for longer than either of us, or even this universe, has been around.”

Adranial was gazing into his eyes as he spoke. When he finished, she sighed. “For someone usually so taciturn, you can say a few pretty words from time to time, you know that?”

He squeezed her hands. “And I mean every one of them.”

Adranial slowly started to smile at him. “You know, I actually believe that.” They let go of each other’s hands and leant back in their seats. “So, you’re B Grade now. Which is insane enough as it is, and on top of that, you’ve… you’ve already defeated an S Grade.” She shook her head. That little fact was clearly still sinking in.

If he were honest, it was still sinking in for Xavier too.

She looked away, seemingly staring off into space. “They’re not going to be a match for you, you know that? Not if you can combine your power like that. And that… that was only the power of one world you were using with the other versions of you.”

“Who isn’t going to be a match for me?”

Adranial blinked. “Anyone. The monsters of the inner sectors. The S Grades.” She glanced up at the ceiling. “Hell, even my ancestor.”

Xavier froze for a moment, taking far too long to absorb her words considering just how powerful his mind was. He stared at her. “What? You… you can’t be serious.” He waved a hand. “I defeated one S Grade, an S Grade that only just advanced to that stage, and one that, let’s be honest, wasn’t in a very coherent state of mind. You’re saying I’m ready to face people like Jhanku? And even the Old Man? That… that can’t be right.”

“First, don’t downplay what you just did, Xavier.” Adranial bit her lip. “Second, I’m not saying you should challenge Jhanku today, but think for a moment about what you’ve accomplished. The way you combined your powers. You defeated the S Grade True Dragon then made more of yourself to gang up on the rest. That’s… that’s absolutely crazy! And that psychic link you were talking about? Connecting your mind with other yous? Can you imagine the advantage you’d have in any fight you’re ever in?”

He could imagine it. He had imagined it.

Xavier had been what one would consider overpowered for a long, long time. But even he had to admit that this, what he’d done on the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor, was something else entirely.

And if he was capable of this while he’d only just advanced to B Grade, how powerful would he be when he reached A Grade? How powerful would he be if he managed to recreate that hidden core that the True Dragon had possessed?

“I think it’s time to leave the tower again,” Xavier said. “To expand my empire to other sectors. To solidify it. Strengthen it.” He paused. “And to face any threat that comes our way.”

And create more unfoldings—a lot more—and strengthen the ties between this universe and its brothers.

It was time to take more of this universe under his control.
