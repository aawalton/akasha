Adrian sat behind the stage, his back pressed against the wooden bench as he tried to quiet the restless energy coiling in his chest. The heavy curtain before him muffled the sound of the assembled crowd, but it did nothing to dull the weight of expectations.

He exhaled slowly, adjusting the stiff collar of his uniform. The black disciplinary committee attire felt heavier than it should. This is finally happening.

There were a few others seated around him. All members of the soon to be authorized disciplinary committee, though Adrian didn't really know most of them.

If there was one person he did know, it was Zuri. She sat poised and collected, hands folded neatly over her lap. She didn’t fidget. She didn’t slouch. She was as still and composed as a statue. Composed as always, expected.

To his right, a girl with feline ears and a flicking tail let out a huff, her golden eyes narrowed with impatience. “This waiting is killing me.” Her voice carried an edge of irritation, though the slight twitch of her ears betrayed how alert she was.

She leaned back against a wooden crate, arms crossed, her tail curling lazily behind her. The uniform fit her like it had been made for her specifically, sleeves rolled up to reveal lean, toned arms, the jacket unbuttoned just enough to hint at a rebellious nature.

Liora Emberpaw, she's a senior, though I'm surprised she was chosen to be part of the disciplinary committee given what I've heard about her.  A beastkin with the restless energy of a predator just waiting for the hunt to begin. She had dark brown hair, cropped short and uneven, as if she’d hacked it off herself. Her nails, just slightly sharper than a human’s, tapped against her sleeve. “If they’re gonna throw us in the deep end, they could at least get on with it.”

“Some of us don’t mind a little patience,” A dwarven boy muttered without looking up.

Dain Coppervein, smart guy, don't know much else about him though. The dwarven boy sat on the edge of the bench, feet barely touching the ground. His uniform was mostly identical to the rest of theirs, though his seemed slightly bulkier around the shoulders, as if it had been reinforced for practicality’s sake. His thick auburn hair was neatly combed, but a few strands had already escaped, sticking out at odd angles. Round glasses perched on his nose, catching the low light as he inspected a small, rune-etched coin he rolled between his fingers. His expression was one of quiet calculation, the same look Adrian had seen on scholars deep in thought.

Liora smirked. “Easy for you to say, professor. You’ve been fidgeting with that thing all day.”

Dain pushed his glasses up. “It helps me think.”

“Sure it does.”

The last of their group, a male elf, leaned against the backstage wall, arms folded, his expression one of easy amusement. Unlike the others, he wore his uniform with a casual looseness, his collar undone, his sleeves slightly pushed back. His long, dark auburn hair was pulled into a low, messy ponytail. His sharp silver-blue eyes carried a knowing glint, as if he was already a step ahead of everyone else in the room.

Vaelion Starcrest. Cool-headed, unreadable, comes from a well-to-do family. Though apparently his family made most of their fortunes here in Mistral. Perhaps it's something to ask about later . Where Dain analyzed and Liora bristled, Vaelion observed with an almost lazy detachment, like none of this was particularly surprising to him.

“I’d listen to Dain if I were you,” Vaelion finally said. “Patience is important. You wouldn’t want to embarrass yourself in front of the whole academy, would you?”

Liora’s ears twitched. “Oh, please, I don’t get stage fright.”

“I wasn’t talking about stage fright,” Vaelion said, his lips curling into a faint smirk.

Adrian wasn’t sure if Vaelion was messing with her or genuinely offering advice. Probably both.

He shifted in his seat, rubbing his palms against the stiff fabric of his trousers. It’s almost time.

Adrian stole one last glance at the others, at the black uniforms that bound them together despite their differences. Whatever came next, they would face it as a unit. Whether that was comforting or terrifying, he wasn’t quite sure yet.

Then, a sudden hush swept through the murmur of the crowd, like a great beast drawing in a slow, measured breath. A single set of footsteps echoed through the backstage hall, carrying the quiet authority of someone who knew they never needed to rush.

Adrian turned just as the door behind them opened. Ah, here comes the woman of the hour.

Myrael Faesbane stepped into the dimly lit space, her very presence reshaping the air around them. The wide brim of her hat cast a slight shadow over her face, but her deep crimson eyes gleamed with knowing amusement.

She surveyed them, her lips curving into a slow smile. “Well, well, what a sight you all make, lined up like little soldiers awaiting their fate.” She placed a gloved hand on her hip, tilting her head just slightly. “Tell me, are my newest enforcers ready for their grand debut?”

Liora scoffed, crossing her arms, her tail flicking impatiently behind her. “I’d be more ready if you actually told us what we’re walking into.”

Dain adjusted his glasses, clearing his throat. “Surely, there is a formal procedure—”

Myrael cut him off with a low, musical laugh. “Ah, so eager for structure. You remind me of a young professor I once knew, though, I must say, you wear the uniform better.” She winked, causing Dain to promptly close his mouth, cheeks darkening under his auburn bangs.

Vaelion exhaled through his nose, watching Myrael with a mixture of amusement and wariness. “Let me guess, you enjoy watching us squirm.”

Myrael clapped her hands together, looking positively delighted. “Oh, how astute! It’s always so refreshing to have students with a bit of wit.”

Then, she turned toward Zuri, whose posture had remained perfectly composed the entire time. “And you, Princess, not even a hint of nerves?”

Zuri met Myrael’s gaze without flinching. “There’s no point in wasting energy on what’s already decided."

“How very regal of you.”

Then, Myrael took a step forward, folding her hands behind her back as she addressed them all at once. “Now, my dear enforcers, listen well. When that curtain lifts, you will step onto the stage, one by one, and stand in formation before the entire academy. They will see you. They will judge you. And, most importantly, they will understand exactly who holds the power within these walls.”

Adrian’s stomach twisted. Why does she have to say it like that?

“You are the Disciplinary Committee now. You are my chosen few, entrusted with ensuring order within Mistral Academy. From this moment forward, you will no longer be seen as just students. You will be seen as authority. You will command respect. And where respect falters… fear will suffice.”

Liora made a low, thoughtful hum, as if tasting the words on her tongue. “So basically, we make sure people don’t step out of line.”

Myrael gave a graceful shrug. “Something like that.”

Dain frowned slightly. “And if someone challenges us?”

“Then you remind them why they shouldn’t.”.

“I expect you all to walk onto that stage with confidence. You will carry yourselves as enforcers of the academy’s law. Or at the very least, pretend convincingly.”

Adrian almost flinched when her gaze landed on him. Myrael stepped closer, lifting a single finger and tapping lightly against the insignia on his chest. “And you, dear Adrian, are you ready to be presented before the academy as one of my finest?”

He swallowed hard. No. But instead, he forced himself to meet her gaze. “I’m here, aren’t I?”

A breath of laughter escaped her lips. “That you are.”

With a fluid, almost lazy grace, Myrael pivoted on her heel, the faintest smirk playing at her lips. The air around her practically thrummed with amusement as she strode toward the curtain, black dress swirling behind her like the trailing edge of a storm.

She lifted one gloved hand and gave the faintest flick of her wrist, as if orchestrating the moment with nothing more than an afterthought. The stage crew reacted instantly.

The heavy velvet curtain billowed as it pulled away, a whisper of fabric giving way to a flood of golden light. The murmur of the audience swelled, shifting from scattered voices to a rolling tide of anticipation.

Adrian’s breath caught as the glare of the chandeliers struck his eyes. The once-shadowed backstage vanished, replaced by a vast sea of faces stretching across the grand hall. Every seat was filled, every gaze locked on the five of them as they stepped forward, their black uniforms a stark contrast against the opulence surrounding them. Gods, that’s a lot of people.

His heartbeat pounded against his ribs, his boots moving in perfect step with the others, not by conscious effort, but through sheer force of necessity. His body obeyed, even as his mind reeled.

The wooden stage groaned faintly beneath their weight, but beyond that, silence crashed over the hall like a wave. The audience now held its breath as one.

Thousands of eyes bore into them. Measuring. Weighing. Judging.

Adrian’s fingers twitched at his sides. Are they impressed? Are they waiting for one of us to fail?

Myrael, ever composed, strode to the center of the stage as if she had just stepped into a lavish ballroom rather than a room full of scrutinizing students. Then, with the elegance of a seasoned performer, she lifted her arms. “Ladies and gentlemen, allow me to introduce you to this year’s Disciplinary Committee.”
