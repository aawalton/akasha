Crow frowned as he meditated on what the solution could be, while the floating skull of the Consolidator simply waited. But there was only that dumb rhyme for any sort of clue.

Hmm.

“Ten temples true,” Crow mused aloud. “Eleven in all, one false, one new. Perhaps the false one is thus the opposite of the newest? The oldest.”

“A clever conclusion,” the skull said. “A good guess, but I am bound to caution you that I might’ve made it trickier than that, and such a deduction is a misdirection.”

Crow scowled. “Of course.”

“Again, a good guess. I cannot say for certain, as I do not possess the actual knowledge, like a page of memory blotted out entirely in ink.”

“I suppose I should get on to the other knowledge your slayer, er- liberator asked me about. Your spell knowledge, and certain spells she saw in particular, and your staff.”

“My staff?” the skull asked in confusion. “How in seventy hells was that not looted long before, in the way of thieves braving my traps and such in the catacombs? In any case, it was primarily a defensive instrument.”

“Hmm. That’s not how she described the effects it utilized. A ‘spirit-striker,’ a spell that shot many deadly green beams, and something that created a bunch of sludge orbs.”

“No,” the Consolidator said in firm, dark dismissal. “I’d never design any of that! I let others handle murder so as not to sully my hands so much as my soul. I had no taste for it, you see.”

Crow took a deep breath, nodding slowly. “I see. And so, you wouldn’t know about unlocking this staff? A staff made of gold, with a book at the top?”

“I’ve never heard of this staff at all. It must’ve been the work of this Sage. In lichdom, he may’ve provided it like hand-me-down trash he found somewhere else, to empower my remains. To allow me to guard the halls better. You must understand, I am unlikely to know anything about items you found, as they’d have been looted before your time. Before even the Sage’s time! He’d have to re-equip me somehow. The only other possibility is I wandered other halls and found items elsewhere to use — assuming those weren’t looted by the time the Sage corrupted the place.”

“Curious. And what of the spells, Dirge of the Pitiless King, Time, Enforcing Proper Distance, or Night’s Decrepit March, which created regenerating shadow armor based on mana?”

The skull was silent for a moment. “I did design Enforcing Proper Distance. As for that latter spell, it isn’t directly familiar, but I did have a spell that transmuted the flesh to a stone body, based on continuous mana. A means of protection from physical harm.”

“So the Sage likely corrupted your magic to fashion your lich. Aside from those not enhanced by the corruption.”

“Quite right, I think.”

“Tell me more of your spellcraft. I’m not sure how possible it is considering the limitations, but our liberator wants to learn from you however she can, I believe. She restored an ancient tower of magic and heads a new order of wizardly knowledge.”

“In that case, I suppose I’d be delighted to. My legacy has been tainted for long enough, though I was resigned to it.”

Samantha progressed through a typical day in the life of a goddess — at least, as she knew it. Making decision calls, putting out fires, starting other fires under asses, all that good stuff.

Burnstire had moved out just as reported, with heavy help from her realm to screen their movements as they transitioned to the innate cloak that was the forest. The Daenriar would move swiftly to meet Samanthian forces at Matrazere once the Rivermount armies got beyond the foothills that stretched upriver. Together with Burnstire’s bulk of knights, the Daenriar would wage a warpath that the Dominion would be unlikely to do much against aside from skirmish and annoy, thanks to a lot of their army’s power being in footmen, such as their halberdiers. They could scarcely risk their remaining mobile forces now if they wanted their use for the inevitable siege.

From reports, even their gargoyle numbers were low, though continuously being fed from trickling provinces across their vast territory. Almost all of these gargoyles were essentially ‘conscripted’ guardians of a gazillion estates. According to the intel, the Dominion had doubled and then tripled the price of waiving all types of conscription, in an unpopular move that was borderline contract violation. Coalitions of estate holders were demanding an actual contract about it, to avoid the possibility of getting fleeced with even more additional fee multipliers.

If the Dominion did field an army against the Daenriar-Burnstire alliance, though, Samantha was prepared with a contingency response, just in case. Ultimately, the two large, formidable marching armies were of benefit as a split approach, dividing their enemy’s attention. As such, she kept/moved some of her own forces up that way as support, including primarily cleromages and Servitors. She’d need them anyway, just to properly enchant the heavy knight Burnstirians enough to keep up with the Daenriar, who could jog all day long. Even Burnstire’s light cavalry would need enchantment to some degree.

The bulk of Burnstire’s light cavalry were superb skirmishers, being what were called ‘warder-knights’ that patrolled the massive forest border and had historically been the first line of defense against trolls. They were all skilled composite horse bowmen, with literally nothing but enchanted arrows. Burnstire had a preposterous store of enchanted arrows they’d built up for centuries, the bulk of which were designed to penetrate tough troll flesh to hit vitals. This meant they were also great for penetrating armor.

A smaller number were exploding arrows designed with large fragmenting heads, designed to break ranks and slow down trolls with leg wounds embedded with shards of cold iron, inhibiting their regeneration. In concentrated ranks, these light cavalry could unleash spell-like AoE destruction, basically. Meanwhile, they had what were effectively highly mobile tanks with their heavy cavalry, able to move in and block approaches that aimed to punish the ranged knights.

No wonder the Dominion tried to play nice with them. They’d adapted to fight near horde levels of physically superior enemies. Any invasion would've been deeply costly, even without the trolls themselves despising the Dominion and guaranteed to harry and flank them the entire time.

It was almost a shame that Samantha would likely not see them at their best in a siege situation. Ah well. Perhaps there would be future occasions. One way or another, she’d make use of them, though. Large numbers of the warder-knights were decent-level sharpshooters, whom she could have buffed with her support-heavy forces for even more deadliness.

Effectively, they went pretty well together. The Daenriar were essentially inexorable murder-tanks themselves, sickeningly abusive with buffs and healing ready. The majority were like budget minotaurs, but in the thousands, and with slow regeneration (outside of their forest, anyway). As far as she knew, the Dominion didn’t have a ‘cold iron’ industry. Their superior forged steel didn’t count. No doubt, they’d manage something once they knew about the trolls, but the alliance as a whole was quite scary.

With things satisfactorily synced up and copasetic in the west, meanwhile, the shit was hitting the fan in the Borderlands. Emboldened by their new foreign allies and news of Rivermount’s fall — and honorably legitimized by the ‘excuse’ of the plot to assassinate the rightful Marchioness of Oldaster, Agatha — the entirety of the marches and adjoining counties southeast of Mot Mekess were quick to align with the budding revolutionary forces headed by several renowned knightly orders and their backers. Independence was in the air, it seemed.

The ‘loyal’ barony of Dittany was taken without any bloodshed, Ultaboro was ‘hostilely’ on board by an agreement to return the held-hostage-count, if he agreed to join, and Fallsland was eagerly on board, perhaps out of sheer revenge and vindication for the false allegations and suffering they’d endured.

Forces were marshalling. Mot Mekess was doomed to fall to a siege against too much weight against it, though the ‘acting regent,’ an installed Dominion puppet governor, was dead-set on resisting. Tragically, there was an internal revolt that Samantha had nothing to do with, from a group of young knights stirred by news from the hinterlands. They failed, were overpowered, and hanged, all before anyone could even try to do anything about it. Sadly, though Samantha had spies there yet, Mot Mekess had been left without a strong Samanthian presence due to the supreme danger from witch-hunts. She could ill-afford important members getting caught, so they’d been pulled out early after the troubles there.

She was advised to once again let the Borderlanders take Mot Mekess on their own terms, without any direct and obvious foreign aid… or overt ‘deific’ aid. She reluctantly agreed to the latter, despite how much she reviled what happened to Sir Oliver and her convictions about the preservation of life. Hypocritical, perhaps, but this would cement the legitimacy of the new alliance in the region for good, and they were the ones risking their lives, livelihoods, lands, and families, to achieve some liberty. However she felt, she couldn’t take that away.

Aside from that, deific resurgence was still developing quite fast, and her indirect aid was now quite welcome, along with Redberry. Followers were upticking again after the disaster of Caneboro. ‘The Goddess’ was a symbol of the spirit of their revolution. Something higher but sufficiently vague publicly. An open secret sort of thing, opening cultic doors in private, just as she’d started things with her order to begin with. The right people knew the truth, and there would be evidence for those paying attention, overt or not. Her blessings would still go out. The compromise was good enough — she’d be able to keep people alive.

With all the girls in Itha Ranon, she forced everyone to take a break, too, and have a family meal. Azure got the chance to really do mom stuff by doing a shit ton of baking — seemingly her awkward way of showing affection to them. She went overboard, of course. They didn’t eat much that wasn’t dessert, and both Estara and Ana ate too much and ended up suffering with tummy aches and sugar comas. Twinsies of bloating! They had to have some remedy medicine delivered for that.

Samantha and Azure were asked when the actual full wedding ceremony would be. It got the same answer as just about everything else non-Matrazere related: sometime after Matrazere.
