"To your right, Bastet!" I shout urgently as I see one of the mana-crazed beasts trying to snap at her from behind. Her bright claws flash out and strike it, the lightning created by the contact branching and hitting two others within a split second. They howl, their glowing eyes brightening even further before they suddenly explode in a mess of blood, gore, and shards of bone.

I quickly raise my shield to deflect the projectiles, but I'm not quite fast enough and I'm covered in the mess. Fortunately, my armour is sufficiently reinforced that none of the shards pierce through it, though as I lower the arm I'd thrown up to protect my eyes, I see several sharp bone fragments sticking out of the armour on my forearm.

I glare at Bastet and she looks back at me a little sheepishly.

"Next time, try not to explode them over all of us, would you?" I request, as calmly as I can. I use Flesh-Shaping to pull the blood and guts off my armour into a ball and then drop it on the ground, brushing away the clean bone shards.

At least with those three taken out, the fight is over. The other four lie on the ground, their bodies fortunately more intact. Ninja complains loudly as she begins to clean herself; Lathani seems to have disappeared into her shadow at just the right moment. The others were, fortunately for them, out of range of the explosions at the right moment.

It seems Bastet's fears were not entirely unfounded – we've barely got out of view of the hill and we've already been attacked three times by beasts crazed by the manastorm. I crouch down beside the three intact carcasses and send a touch of magic into them. With them dead, they have no resistance against my examination, and I'm able to inspect their bodies unimpeded.

My magical touch lingers over the obvious mutations in their bodies. Muscles that are oddly grown, organs that are deformed, the presence of multiple misformed Cores, the absence of things that I sense should be there. Energy-warped.

I wonder just how long they would have lasted had they been allowed to live – the damage to their major organs alone should have seen them die already, yet they still managed to live. Perhaps their health pools had been supercharged and they would have collapsed later when those ran out?

The manastorm seems to have had an even worse effect on the creatures’ intelligence. Almost all of the beings we’ve fought so far today had a proper Core inside, clearly marking them as Tier twos – different from the misformed Cores that were also present in some that were obviously Tier ones – yet there was none of the strategy I would expect from Tier two beasts. Instead, they just attacked with no thought for self-preservation.

The previous two groups of attackers were much the same – this is clearly a consequence of being caught in the manastorm and makes me even more glad that we managed to avoid it, both now and previously at sea. However, it makes my fear for Artio and the two deris even greater. I know from our mental connection that they are alive and in a decent state, but they stopped responding verbally a few days ago when they went into a hibernation state to reduce their need for food, water, and oxygen. I can only hope their shelter has protected them sufficiently from the chaos that has obviously run rampant.

I shove the three intact carcasses into my Inventory. Normally I wouldn't, but this isn’t a normal situation. Just like Elarion said before we left him, normally Energy-filled soil would be considered a boon, just as Energy-filled meat is. However, too much of a good thing exists, and if I left the meat out to lose its Energy saturation naturally, It would have probably gone off by the time it was safe to eat. Hopefully at some point we’ll figure out a treatment that makes it possible to balance both risk and benefit.

I'll need to inspect it before handing it to the kitchens, though, because for all I know the mutations caused by the manastorm might operate under different rules from normal Energy saturation of flesh – or cause their own complications. I’ll see what the cooks and healers think about eating these beasts at all, but I dearly hope that we haven’t just lost the forest as a source of food. We really, really can’t afford that right now.

Josiah sent a message over the Alliance Bond shortly after we left Elarion – he has taken a couple of ships out fishing. I hope that the oceans aren’t nearly as tumultuous as the forest has proven to be.

It’s not even just the animals – the plants seem to have gone mad too. Whether it’s the plants that have changed colour in the last tenday, the ones that have started moving unnaturally, or the plants that have abruptly doubled or tripled in size, I’ve regularly found myself surprised. And not usually in a good way, though I do take note of the changes in case it can help us later when we’ve found plants that work to feed our people. After all, doubling or tripling in size in days when farming would be very useful.

I’ve come to know this part of the forest fairly well by this point, but the changes the manastorm have made have turned something that was becoming familiar into something unfamiliar again. It’s more than a little disconcerting, to think I know where I am and then suddenly be blindsided with something new or drastically changed.

Noir, Ivor, and Sirocco are currently trying to see how far it extends too. They haven’t got to the limits yet, but they’re flying slowly, trying to examine exactly what’s changed as well as how far it has.

After tidying up the area, we continue deeper into the forest. On high alert, I send out regular pulses of Inspect to give us warning of anything approaching. It's not infallible, but so far it has flagged one group lying in wait and gave us notice of this most recent group running towards us before we even heard them crashing through the undergrowth.

Reaching the spot where I’m sure I left Artio and the deris, I stop suddenly, staring.

"What in the world is that?" I breathe.

"It looks like some sort of plant," Lathani suggests, starting to move forward

"Don't touch it," I warn her, and she quickly stops, sending me a guilty look. I glare at her. She really should know better than to touch unknown plants by this point. Unknown things in general, really.

"Is it a fungus or a flower?" I wonder aloud, not wanting to get too close. Whatever it is, it's bright yellow and orange, with no obvious leaves, bulbous growths at the bases of its multiple thick stems, and tubular flowers.

I ping off another Inspect – and I curse as the whole thing glows a very deep red.

What is it? Bastet asks, alert.

That thing is a Tier three plant – and it’s right over where our companions are, I tell the whole group, deciding to switch into mental speech since there’s now little chance that we’ll be getting out of this without a fight.

Then we must defeat it, Ninja replies immediately, crouching slightly and glaring at the first plant-form Tier three I’ve seen. We will not abandon our pack. She’s not the only one to feel that way if the determination I feel emanating from our other companions is anything to go by. I don’t blame them – I feel the same way.

We’ll have to be careful, I warn. Since it’s a Tier three, the information I can get from it is limited. It is mobile, and I suspect far quicker than we might expect. It has some sort of spore attack, and it’s got a high health pool. It’s also not nearly as weak to fire as I would like, but you might be able to have some effect, I say with a nod to Aingeal. He rises and falls quickly in the air in acknowledgement. I will try to negotiate with it but…I’m not holding out much hope.

Is it wise to let it know our position? Hunter asks tentatively. Perhaps we should just attack it straight away. I have some exploding runes that might help.

We’ll certainly use those, I agree immediately. And you have a point. The rest of you circle around. I’ll stay here and pull its attention this way. If it doesn’t let us retrieve the others without a fight, you’ll be able to surprise it from behind.

I’ll stay with you, Happy tells me immediately, Fenrir chiming in with a strong sense of agreement.

I think I would be more effective from a distance, Hunter contributes, still hesitant.

Yes, OK, I agree, acknowledging the practicality in their words. Anyway, the others are more stealthy than all three of them, so it’s probably a good idea. Aingeal, you’d better stay with me too. He bobs again in acknowledgement. Given that he’s literally a floating ball of fire, even a plant monster might be able to detect him.

Within seconds, Lathani, Bastet, and Ninja have melted into the forest around. I give them a little more time to get clear and then reach out to the plant monster with an Alliance Bond. I figure that that is the least likely to provoke it, and the best way to discuss a mutually beneficial solution.

The mass of what I can only assume are fungal growths shifts unnaturally in the still air of the forest today. I see the flash of a pinkish interior to some of the tubes, and a green and slimy interior to others.

A moment later, I feel my offer of Alliance has been very firmly rejected.

I breathe out a sigh of disappointment. Expected, but annoying. A fight it is.

Rustling around me and Fenrir’s sudden hiss of warning alerts me to the fact that the fight has actually already started.

I grab Happy and yank her almost off her feet as a tubular growth shoots out of the earth beneath her. I curse – I knew the ground felt slightly more spongy than it should have, but I was so distracted by the weird-looking growths before us to think about it.

Hunter, back away but throw your bombs. Bastet, try to burn its centre with lightning. Aingeal, with fire, though be aware of the dome it’s sitting on. Lathani, Ninja, do what you can to clear a path through to our companions. Happy, get out of range, your weapons will do little here. Fenrir, guard Happy. I’ll see what I can do from below. It has some sort of spore attack – watch out for that.

Even while rattling off my orders, I dodge several more attacks from the stabbing roots, dragging Happy back with me as we try to find the limit of its range. Sinking magic into the ground, I curse again as I feel just how far its roots have extended – and its roots are what are allowing it to spear tubular growths that end in thorn-filled flowers at us. I have no doubt that the fang-like thorns are filled with poison that would do something nasty to us.

Finally reaching its limits, I shove a couple of vials over to Happy.

If you can fill some spear heads with this and stab them into the plant, that might help, I tell her tersely. It’s herbicide so it should work on a plant like this, but no guarantees.

Leave it with me, Happy tells me firmly. Then, with Fenrir keeping a careful eye on our surroundings, I close my eyes and sink my consciousness into the ground.

Immediately, I meet with resistance. The Tier three has extended its domain to the limits of its roots – normal for plant-based Tier threes from what I’ve read, though this is the first time I’ve experienced it for myself – and, just like how a body’s innate resistance makes it more difficult for me to invade their body, this makes it more difficult for me to extend my mana into the area controlled by the plant. However, the difference between this and Flesh-Shaping is that the earth is neutral ground, unlike flesh which most definitely is not.

I sense that I could create spears of stone that could cut through the root network and separate the sections, but I choose not to. All that would do is make me, Fenrir and Happy a little safer. It wouldn’t necessarily free Artio and the two deris from the plant’s dominion. For now, the plant may not know what I’m capable of. When I act, I want it to count.

Trying to keep my presence in the earth as quiet as possible, I work my way towards its centre. Most plant beasts have a central core, though some possess multiple growth-hearts. I prayed this is the former.

Suddenly, fear and alarm shoots through the Bond network.

It’s got me! Lathani exclaims.
