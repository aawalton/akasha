Ethan flashed away in blue-white light, needing a rift to escape the attack of the gargantuan basilisk. Despite its size, the creature was dangerously quick, unquestionably Master rank. Ethan was still in the early stages of Twilight, and he knew instantly that he couldn’t win this fight.

He threw dimensionally charged daggers in every direction, knowing that his own speed simply couldn’t match that of the serpent. Given that he’d abandoned stealth–the creature could clearly locate him through other means–teleporting rapidly around the room appeared to be his only real chance to stay alive.

“What is our plan, Ethan Bishop?” Tomo asked from the Astral. “Should you not retreat?”

Ethan, busy trying to keep the serpent’s coiled, bulky body between him and its jaws, struggled to answer. “The old plan still makes sense,” he said. “The Basilisk would have been a valuable tool at Twilight, but he’s the first Master rank anything that we’ve seen since we arrived. Ignoring the Emperor, I suppose. We can’t let this opportunity slip through our fingers.”

Ethan slipped through a rift a fraction of a second before immense fangs pierced through him, and he had to roll to his feet on the other side of the cave. Tomo made a low growl.

“I am not convinced this is the opportunity you believe it to be, Ethan Bishop. The creature may lack the coordination, and power versatility of a normal Bonded, but its strength and speed eclipse your own.”

“Thank you for that,” Ethan said, flashing away once more, rock exploding where the beast crashed behind him.

The Basilisk let out an odd, hissing roar that echoed through the cave network, and Ethan looked back for just a moment, before flashing away into one of the tunnels. Behind him, intense white light shone, as the creature’s gaze turned everything it landed on into stone. A cloud of dust followed Ethan as the underground lair began to collapse under its own weight.

Running at top speed, Ethan had to rely on his climbing ability to keep his feet beneath him as the tunnel shook beneath him. He struggled to stay in darkness, as any sign of bright light heralded the approach of the massive Basilisk behind him.

He used his rifts as sparingly as he was able, knowing he wanted the beast to chase him, but also that he needed avenues for escape. Each time he appeared next to one of his hidden daggers, he knew he had only moments before the Master rank monster would find him again, and obliterate the entire tunnel.

“I am growing concerned, Ethan Bishop. Should I try to distract the creature for you?”

“Can’t risk you being destroyed,” Ethan said, “I can’t be without a Bond right now. Right now I’m just getting a sense for how this dance is going to play out.”

He cycled to flare, sending out fans of charged knives as he rushed through tunnels. No matter how far ahead of the Basilisk he seemed to get, the beast always seemed to find him. Oddly, its petrifying gaze seemed to allow it to burrow through the cave walls, everything the hot white light fell on becoming dust.

It also had no trouble tracking him, and Ethan could only guess at the mechanism it was using. Still, he was becoming more used to the wild game of cat and mouse, his rifts allowing him to stay just ahead–though with many close calls.

“Are you truly committed to this path, Ethan Bishop?” Tomo asked.

“We need to slow the Followers, Tomo. I learned the hard way that I can’t do it alone–my arm still feels weird, even after all the healing. But a Master rank monster that can turn their Twilights to stone? It’s perfect.”

“I understand what you hope will happen, I merely question the likelihood of success. You had to travel for weeks to reach the Followers. Do you believe you can maintain this pace for that long?”

The question was punctuated by the Basilisk crashing through the tunnel ahead of Ethan for once, and he nearly slid into the creature’s mouth before flashing away at the last moment. This time he appeared on the surface, the red sky above him oddly comforting after the subterranean hell he’d just been in.

With a snap of his fingers, several dozen explosively charged daggers ignited at once. The ground heaved upward, tossing aside trees and stone and launching dirt and dust into the air. Then it sagged downward, as the network of tunnels collapsed beneath him.

“I have to find a way, Tomo,” Ethan said, already sprinting into the forest, heading west. “This is a stupid, desperate move, but that’s where we’re at right now.”

As if hearing his words, the Basilisk erupted from the collapsed tunnels behind him, rising high above the trees with a roar, eyes aglow. It followed Ethan’s path without hesitation, and the chase was back on.

With grim determination, Ethan ran.

Selina should have gone back to the Fable Train hours ago. She knew that, even without Apollo’s increasingly forceful demands that she do just that.

“You’re nearly out of range!” he growled through the communications terminal inside her little mobile bunker. “And it’s getting dark, Selina, already you’ll be traveling into the night just to get back. This isn’t worth the risk!”

He didn’t understand the risk, of course. He didn’t believe she’d seen anything other than more demons out here, hiding among the trees.

She couldn’t blame him, exactly. There were countless types of the corrupted beast on Potentia, and it was possible that some unknown demon had managed to use tactics and abilities that could be mistaken for something else, but Selina didn’t believe it. She knew what she’d seen…and she knew what she hoped.

“I’ll be fine, Apollo,” she said easily. “The relays are working just as planned, and Walter is well under control.”

“Damn it, Selina,” Apollo cursed. “I don’t know what you’re hoping to find out there, but it isn’t worth it!”

“The army will be past the forest tomorrow,” she said. “Walter is too slow to journey in the opposite direction and still catch up. I have to find them now.”

“Them?” Apollo asked. “You have to let this go. Whatever is in that forest attacked you, and you’re giving ‘them’ another chance to finish the job.”

“One more hour,” Selina said. Apollo muttered and complained for another minute, but Selina’s eyes were fixed on the cameras showing the increasingly dark forest around her, and she barely heard him.

There was something in these woods. Something that hunted Twilight rank demons like Walter, and could seemingly manipulate the plants and vines to do so. That was strange, and strange was interesting. After so much time spent in the Fable Train, feeling powerless despite her Dusk rank, she felt compelled to do something meaningful.

And she couldn’t help but feel like there was something meaningful to find out here. Potentia was covered in disturbing flora, and if they could find and recruit demons that could manipulate the world around them, it could be an enormous advantage. There was no shortage of carnivorous trees and vines, and mastering them could mean essentially having access to a second army.

And so she kept moving, even when her hour was up, and night had fallen.

Selina was in the middle of another argument with Apollo when she felt Walter come to a stop. Apollo went silent at the same moment, likely feeling Walter’s movement cease.

“What’s going on?” he asked, concern plain.

Selina wasn’t sure how to answer. She looked at each camera, the green tones of night vision revealing little more than an endless wall of trees surrounding them. She couldn’t see anything, but Walter had clearly stopped for a reason, and he let out a keening sound that Selina would have described as sounding nervous.

“I don’t know what’s happening,” Selina said. “Walter is clearly following some instinct, like when we leave him to fight another demon. But I–”

She cut off as Walter roared, and stumbled from some unseen impact. Selina was tossed from her chair, and quickly regained her feet to search the monitors.

“Vines,” she said, spotting the same kind of attack as the previous ambush. But Vines couldn’t cause an impact like that. The truth was quickly revealed as she spotted an enormous boulder sailing through the air, a brief moment before it crashed into Walter’s raised pincer.

“We’re under attack!” she shouted into the communicator, her eyes never looking away from the cameras.

“Selina, you’re on your own out there! Just run!” Apollo shouted.

Walter shook from another impact, trumpeting his fury as he swiped at the surrounding trees. He staggered once more when dozens of vines, each as thick as Selina’s waist, wrapped around one of his pointed legs. Even more vines were reaching out, and the impacts of hurled boulders–each larger than a car from Earth–were coming faster and faster.

Walter roared and turned, trying to pull himself free, while flailing at unseen attackers. Selina tried raising shields of light to protect him, but the massive thrown stones were moving like bullets, the speed and weight shattering her hastily erected walls of light.

“This might have been a mistake,” she muttered. “Walter, we’re going home!” she said, hammering on the control console.

The lumbering crustacean tried to obey, slowly turning as pincers snipped through an ever-increasing number of vines. Selina still watched the monitors as Walter struggled away from the ambush, hoping for just a glimpse of their unseen attacker.

Suddenly, she felt herself slamming against the side of her bunker, and tumbling uncontrollably as up and down kept changing direction. She groaned as the world stopped moving, struggling to stand as she tried to make sense of what had happened.

The bunker was on its side, and for a moment she tried to wrap her head around how that was possible–the bunker was attached to the center of Walter’s shell, and even if he fell, this shouldn’t be possible. Then she saw that the floor had been torn away, and one wall bent inward, and understood.

One of the boulders had struck the bunker hard enough to rip it from the demon’s back, and send it hurtling into the forest. Bending down and looking through the hole where the floor had been, she could see Walter some distance away, fighting with vines and slowly retreating as more boulders exploded against his shell.

Selina wasted no time, rushing out of the compromised bunker and stumbling through the dark woods, trying to reach Walter–she had no illusions that she could make it back to the army without him.

She’d barely managed a few strides before vines shot out from the darkness, wrapping around her legs. Searing light erupted from her hands, slicing through them and releasing acrid smoke, but more kept coming. She felt her arms bound, and her Nator strength wasn’t enough to save her.

She thrashed and pulled, calling on Pepper to help. The poor creature was wrapped in vines as well, and dragged away into the forest before it could help. She hastily dismissed her Familiar to save it, then looked around desperately. She was completely immobilized, and little more than prey for whatever had so effectively trapped her.

Walter had managed to retreat, but the sounds of battle continued. Selina tried to make her mind work, pushing against the fear that threatened to overwhelm her.

Then figures stepped out of the darkness, surrounding her, and Selina’s eyes widened.

“By the Goddess,” she whispered. These weren’t demons.
