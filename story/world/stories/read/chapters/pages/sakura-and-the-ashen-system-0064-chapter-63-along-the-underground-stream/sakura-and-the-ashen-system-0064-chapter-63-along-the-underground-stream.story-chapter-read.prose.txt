They left the bathhouse before dawn. After the water’s warning, no one suggested staying until morning, even though Lir still had trouble staying on his feet, and Gilbert looked as if a few hours of sleep had only reminded his body how deeply exhausted it was. Sakura would also have gladly returned to the warm stone bench, but now every murmur of water repeated the same thing: Faerd was following their trail. Still far away, but in the right direction.

They gathered their few belongings, put out the fire in the stove, and returned the stone slab with the fish image to its place. They did not manage to hide the passage into the technical tunnels completely. Wet marks remained around the slab, and the freed water still ran through the old gutters. But the bathhouse had changed too noticeably anyway. The blue glow had grown brighter, the currents had filled the channels again, and the map of water collection stations shone on the wall. Anyone who came here after them would understand that someone had recently touched the ancient mechanism.

"Maybe we should block the source again?" Sakura asked, looking around the bathhouse one last time.

Gilbert tightened the strap of his bag and shook his head.

"I don’t think that’s a good idea. The water wanted to be freed. Besides, Faerd doesn’t need a map to understand where we went."

"We’re leaving him a ready-made route map."

"Not exactly. Several streams are marked here, and most of them go in other directions. If we’re lucky, he’ll have to check them one by one."

"When it comes to Faerd, luck usually isn’t on our side."

"That is why we should not waste time discussing how unpleasant he is."

Pampli was the first to leave the bathhouse and stopped on the threshold, sniffing the damp morning air. The green mist was far behind them. Here, ordinary trees grew, covered with moss and drops of dew, and grass pushed up between the roots. Sakura had not yet gotten used to seeing normal colors again, ones not stained by poisonous green.

The underground stream came to the surface a little below the bathhouse. A narrow current ran between the stones, disappeared under roots in places, and then appeared again a few steps farther on. The water was transparent, with a faint blue glow in its depths.

Sakura crouched beside it and touched the surface with her fingers. The voices answered at once. They did not sound as loud as they had at night in the pool, but they became easier to understand. The water showed direction through movement: a cold touch against her palm, the feeling of a turn, brief images of wet stones and a narrow ravine ahead.

"We need to go that way," Sakura said, pointing east.

Gilbert looked in that direction, then at the stream.

"Are you sure?"

"Well, kind of. I’m still learning how to understand the language of water."

"All right. We’ll have to trust you."

They walked along the stream. At first, the path was simple. The current ran down a gentle slope, curving around trees and large boulders, and sometimes disappeared underground, but Sakura still heard its movement. The voices grew more muffled, as if coming through a thick wall, but they continued leading them forward.

Gradually, she began to distinguish separate shades. Fast water spoke hurriedly, interrupting itself. Underground water was quieter and more patient, but its images seemed distant and dark. Small springs repeated only a few simple things: cold, light, roots, stones. Sometimes Sakura tried to ask them a direct question, but the sensations she received in response were so strange that she quickly stopped. Obviously, the voices of water were even more confusing and stranger than the voices of wind, which she had already grown used to over all this time.

When Sakura asked whether the next station was far, the water showed her a fish, three round stones, and a lone tree with an empty nest.

"And what does that mean?" Lir asked after listening to her retelling.

"Maybe we need to pass three stones?"

"Or eat a fish," he suggested.

"Or build a nest in a tree," Gilbert added. "Elements have a very convenient way of giving directions. If we get lost, it will be entirely our fault."

Sakura gave the mage a displeased look, and Lir only chuckled quietly. That sound calmed her a little. After the conversation in the bathhouse at night, he looked better, though he was still walking slowly and limping in places. For the first hours, Lir insisted he was managing perfectly well, but gradually he began to fall behind.

Sakura slowed her steps several times, and Gilbert did the same without commenting on what was happening. Lir noticed it himself every time and tried to walk faster, after which he started limping even more.

The path also became harder. The stream led them to a lowland where the ground had softened and become covered with patches of tall yellow grass. The water split into many small channels, some of which kept flowing, while others lay still between the hummocks as dark, motionless pools.

The voices around Sakura changed. The clear streams still called her forward, but another whisper came from the bog pools. It was louder, clearer, and for some reason much more pleasant. The water there promised dry land, a shorter road, and a safe passage to the right.

Sakura stopped.

"We need to turn there."

She pointed toward the reeds. Gilbert looked in that direction.

"There’s a drop-off there."

"I don’t see it."

The mage picked up a stone from the ground and threw it into the reeds. A dull impact came from below, and the disturbed stalks swayed and parted, revealing the edge of a deep pit. Dark water gleamed at the bottom.

Sakura listened again. The bog kept insistently calling her onward, now promising that the drop was shallow, and the water below was pleasant and warm.

"It might want to get rid of us," Sakura said.

"Finally, an element that thinks practically," Gilbert replied.

"You could have warned me that water can lie."

"You know, I don’t have experience talking to elements."

Gilbert approached the edge of the nearest pool and crouched without touching it.

"Stagnant water keeps too much of what belongs to others. Rotting plants, drowned animals, dirt, the fear of those who got lost here before. I think it lures the living to places where it will be easier for them to become part of the swamp."

Lir carefully stepped back from the dark pool.

"So bog water should not be listened to at all?"

"You can listen if you’re interested in finding out exactly how it wants to drown you."

Sakura focused on the thin transparent stream making its way between the hummocks. Its voice was weaker, but it did not try to sound pleasant. The water showed her a stone ridge to the left and wet roots that could be used to cross the lowland.

"The real path is there," she said. "At least, I hope this is real water and not a swamp that learned to pretend to be a stream."

They moved along the stone ridge. Pampli went first, testing the path with his heavy paws. Several times, the stones disappeared under the water, but the beast easily jumped to the next dry spot. For the humans, the crossing was harder. Sakura slipped once, and Gilbert managed to catch her by the elbow. After that, he himself barely kept his balance, and for several seconds they stood clinging to each other while the wet ground slowly sank under their feet.

"Good thing our sense of balance hasn’t completely failed us yet," Sakura said.

"Better not test it too often."

Behind them came a choked breath. Lir had stumbled at the edge of a hummock and dropped to one knee. He tried to get up right away, but his leg did not hold him. Sakura hurried back to him.

"That’s it. You can’t keep walking on your own."

"I can. If we stop, Faerd only gets closer. And that’s clearly worse than a treacherous swamp."

"That’s why we’re not stopping."

Sakura looked at Pampli. Pampli, standing on the next dry patch, turned his head. For a while, the beast looked at Lir, then gave a heavy sigh, came back to him, and lowered himself onto his front paws.

Lir looked at Sakura in confusion.

"What is he doing?"

"Offering you a ride."

"Seriously?"

Pampli snorted unhappily.

"Judging by his face, he considers you especially slow cargo that would be easier to put on his back."

Lir carefully climbed onto Pampli. The beast waited until he settled between the ridges on his obsidian back, then rose and demonstratively turned his head away, showing that he was not at all interested in the passenger’s condition.

"Hold on tight," Sakura said.

Pampli stepped onto the next stone. Lir swayed, grabbed a ridge with both hands, and no longer tried to prove he could walk on his own.

Beyond the swamp, the land rose again. The stream ran through a shallow ravine hidden between the trees. It would have been almost impossible to notice from the main road. Sometimes remnants of old stonework appeared on the damp ground, confirming that once, a separate path really had run along the water between the stations.

Lir gradually relaxed on Pampli’s back. He still looked tired, but now he could look around instead of only at his feet.

"My father wouldn’t believe it if he saw me riding an obsidian creature," he said suddenly.

Sakura lost her step. Gilbert, walking nearby, also tensed, though his face barely changed. Lir did not notice. He stroked a smooth ridge on Pampli’s back with his palm and continued:

"He didn’t believe a lot of stories about obsidian creatures at all. He said people exaggerate everything when they’re scared."

Sakura stayed silent.

"It’s a shame I can’t go home yet," Lir said. "I understand they’ll be waiting there. But if we go far enough and everything calms down a little, will I be able to return? At least for a short time. Father probably already thinks I died."

Gilbert looked at Sakura with the tense understanding that they could not stay silent any longer. Sakura felt everything inside her tighten. In the bathhouse, she had not been able to tell Lir the truth. After the escape, he had been too exhausted and too happy to be free again. Sakura had convinced herself she would choose the right moment later, when he had rested a little and would be ready to hear it. But there was no right moment.

"Lir," she began.

From her expression alone, the boy immediately understood that this was not about returning home. His smile disappeared.

"What?"

Sakura opened her mouth, but the first words never came. She had imagined this conversation for several days, and none of the phrases had seemed right.

Gilbert silently looked away and walked a little ahead, leaving the conversation to her. After all, Lir was someone she knew first. Her first ally in this new world.

"I should have told you back in the bathhouse," Sakura said, her voice breaking. "Maybe even earlier, right after the escape. I just didn’t know how."

Lir sat motionless. Pampli, sensing the change in his body, slowed down.

"Did something... happen to him? To my father?" the boy asked.

Sakura nodded.

"Was he wounded?"

She tried again to find the right words, but now any delay would only be cruel.

"Your father died, Lir."

For several seconds, there was only the sound of water around them. Lir stared at her without blinking. For a while, his face simply froze. He could not grasp the meaning of what he had heard.

"No," he said at last, quietly. "You must be mistaken."

"I’m not mistaken."

"He could have survived. He always survived. Even when the boat overturned in winter, he got out on his own and then laughed that the water wasn’t even that cold."

Sakura lowered her gaze.

"I’m so sorry."

"How did it happen?"

The question sounded unnaturally even.

"He was badly wounded after they took you. We couldn’t help him."

Lir looked past her for several seconds.

"Did he suffer long?"

Sakura felt her throat tighten.

"No."

She did not know whether that short lie helped or only made everything worse.

"I see."

"Lir..."

"Enough. I understand."

Sakura came closer, but Lir no longer even looked at her.

"I wanted to tell you earlier, I swear."

"It’s all right."

"No, it’s not all right."

"Then I don’t know what else I’m supposed to say."

His voice was still calm. Lir was not crying, and his eyes did not shine with approaching tears. Only his fingers gripped the ridge on Pampli’s back harder.

Sakura felt helpless. She wanted to hug him, stop Pampli, say something about his father, remind him how the man had helped her, but any word right now felt like an attempt to make Lir experience his grief in a way that was convenient for her.

Gilbert turned back and gave a barely noticeable shake of his head, meaning for Sakura to leave him be. Sakura understood, and they continued their path in silence. After that, Lir sat silently on Pampli, head lowered, watching the water. Several times Sakura noticed his lips moving, as if he were repeating something to himself, but she heard no words. Maybe it was a farewell, or maybe a prayer.

Pampli also behaved unusually calmly. He did not try to throw off his passenger, did not stop at every suspicious smell, and even chose a more even path. Once, the beast turned his head and gently touched Lir’s leg with his nose, as if checking that he was still holding on. Lir did not react, but after a while, he placed his palm on Pampli’s neck and gave him a short stroke.

Sakura and Gilbert did not bother the young man until evening.

The voices of the water continued to guide them, but now Sakura no longer trusted every whisper. She listened only to the moving clear streams and tried to ignore the dark backwaters. Several times, the water warned her about washed-out ground or pits hidden under leaves. One small spring led them to an old stone bridge almost completely overgrown with ivy.

When the sun began to sink, the stream went underground again. On the surface, only a shallow gutter remained, with unusually bright grass growing along it. The voices became quieter. They called them farther, showing light, a stone roof, and smoke.

They saw the station when it was already starting to get dark. It stood at the foot of a tall hill, half-built into the slope like the previous bathhouse. The building was larger: two stories, a wide roof of dark tile, and a stone chimney. Moss covered the walls, but the windows were whole.

Warm yellow light burned in one window, and thin smoke rose from the chimney.

Between two trees near the station, someone had stretched a rope where shirts, a long cloak, and several pieces of cloth were drying.

Gilbert raised a hand, stopping the others.

"Someone clearly lives here."

"The map showed the station was active," Sakura reminded him.

"An active source and an inhabited building do not always mean the same thing."

Pampli froze. Lir lifted his head and, for the first time in several hours, looked ahead carefully.

"Maybe it’s a caretaker?"

"Maybe," Gilbert answered. "Or people who found a convenient shelter before us."

They approached carefully. There were no signs of a guard post around. On the damp ground, ordinary boot prints of several different sizes could be seen. A basket of chopped firewood stood by the wall, and bundles of unknown herbs were drying nearby.

A wooden sign hung on the door, carved with a circle crossed by three wavy lines. In the center of the circle, a small metal insert darkened.

Gilbert frowned.

"Do you know what that is?" Sakura asked.

"No."

"You usually know everything suspicious."

"That is not an absolute truth."

Trying to learn more, Sakura listened to the water. A strong spring murmured beneath the station, but its voices suddenly fell silent. They did not disappear completely, but seemed to hide and listen together with her.

Pampli growled quietly. Gilbert gestured for him to be silent, then directed him toward the bushes. The beast did not want to go, so Sakura had to help the mage convince Pampli.

Then Gilbert approached the door. He had not yet managed to raise his hand to knock when three slow knocks sounded from the other side.

Knock. Knock. Knock.

Everyone froze.

Someone had stopped very close, separated from them only by old wood, and then an unfamiliar voice calmly asked:

"Who brought the Obsidian Hierophant here?"
