One, I did.

His name was Adrian. I remembered him from when I’d plotted to have Eric introduce me to Naska’s group, back when I’d pretended I’d taught him how to mine efficiently. Adrian had been the first to get on me for interacting with a member of his group, all loud and accusatory, but had quickly become friendly after Eric’s endorsement.

He’d been a real chatterbox. We hadn’t interacted much, but following our first encounter, he’d always been friendly. He’d constantly been with the calico cat beastkin; the two seemed to be fast friends.

And now he was dead. Hung there like a piece of meat.

It dawned on me, the realisation crawling up my skin, that the association had killed him. Him and the other two here, both girls.

Why? What had they done to deserve execution? Had they tried to steal from the Association? Attacked a staff member?

The other two weren’t from Naska’s group; this didn’t make sense. Nothing like this had happened in the whole time I’d been here.

I left eventually, making a bee-line for my group. Whatever was happening up here, if people were being executed, I needed to see them now. Make sure everyone was still okay.

My group weren’t at their usual site. Where were they? I searched, panic racing through my lungs, but my worries were quickly assuaged.

The whole group was hard at work about a fifteen minute walk from camp, thankfully unsupervised. As I arrived, Maisie turned first. Then Jackal, then Finn, then Ceri, then Marcois.Then Toar.

Each wore a different expression. Surprise. Disbelief. Awe. An ear-splitting grin. Tears.

Jackal was the one crying.

Toar? His face was unreadable for the longest time. While the others came to me, expressing their relief, their questions, and even their anger at me for worrying them so, Toar simply froze.

I wondered if we’d clash over the leadership role. If we’d go right back to fighting. If things were about to become ugly.

When he finally approached, it was with a bowed head.
