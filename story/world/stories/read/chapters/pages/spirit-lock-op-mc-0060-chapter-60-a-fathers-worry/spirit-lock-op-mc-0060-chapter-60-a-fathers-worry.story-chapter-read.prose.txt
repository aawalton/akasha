A few hours later, he confirmed the success of the sisters’ core expansions, waited for them to shower in the gym’s locker room, and then saw them off to Kayline’s dorm before going to his meeting. It was in the South Tower Ballroom. Sound teams worked to wire the venue for the demonstration, checking every detail to ensure there wouldn’t be any mistakes during an event hosting representatives of the Vast Four.

Mayle touched her chest in relief when he arrived. “Thank goodness. I thought you wouldn’t show.”

“I come when my presence is necessary. Tell me about the event.”

Mayle gave him a tour of the ballroom and explained the event's structure.

He nodded at the end. “Good work.”

“Excellent…” she said, trailing off. “So? What now?”

“Now, we wait. For the event to come, and your problems to disappear.”

Mayle eyed him skeptically. “Stella Ingsley is coming. There will also be representatives from the Morine Domain, the Quasar Alliance, and the Sorint Territory. If you embarrass or endanger them, you’ll be cora-shackled in a gas chamber by next week.”

Hayden’s expression didn’t change. “Is there anything else?”

She sucked air through her teeth to temper her rage and said, “Yes. Ritrix is waiting for you.”

He nodded. “Take me to him.”

Ritrix was waiting at the bar in the Sunset Lounge of the South Tower, staring into dead space.

Hayden sat beside him and pointed at a bottle and two glasses. The bartender brought both before walking away.

“Is this your way of saying that I won’t like what you have to say?” Ritrix asked.

Hayden poured the first drink. “I don’t recall you ever enjoying our conversations.”

Ritrix finished off his cocktail and accepted a glass of brandy. He sipped it, set it down, and activated a privacy barrier, casting them into an eerie silence.

“My daughters have been increasingly confident in their abilities,” he began. “I assume you’ve been training them?”

Hayden drank slowly. “As much as I can in secret.”

“When does the rift open?”

“The day after the expo—a time when Stella can prevent the Sickles or other enemies from following us.”

“It’s bold to assume that Stella will help you.”

“Stella does want to keep me, does she not?”

“If she does, will you stay with us?”

“I hope to. Stella is my ideal patron, so I want to stay with her. That said, I won’t stay with anyone who attempts to use this rift to control me. There are no second chances. There are no negotiations. If the Golden Belt plays politics here, I will join the Morine Empire.”

Ritrix narrowed his eyes. “You’re the boldest person I’ve ever met.”

“I’m as bold as my resources allow me to be.” Hayden stared at Ritrix. “Make sure Stella understands that.” Hayden finished his drink and left the bar, leaving Ritrix with a full bottle and a list of reasons to drink.

Stella listened to Ritrix’s report in silence. “Did he sound confident when he threatened us?”

“He sounded absolute. There’s no doubt in my mind that he would follow through.”

She pondered it carefully. “Just to clarify, he didn’t say that he would go to Halkan, right?”

“Correct. He focused on you, specifically. When it came to the Morine Empire, he spoke of them like a concept—an avenue for revenge.”

Stella understood the nature of the proposal, so she nodded. “Is there anything else?”

“Yes. Hayden has solved our transportation strikes, and he’s healed and trained my daughters. I’d view him as a powerful, reasonable ally. That’s my honest assessment of him. As for my debt to him, I ask that you hear him out.”

Stella ruminated over his words. “Ritrix.”

“Yes?”

“Are you really going to let your daughters join him in the rift?”

Ritrix shook his head. “I don’t know.”

***

Later that night, Ritrix went to see his daughters, but they were too exhausted to speak, so he left them to sleep. But first, he hugged them and told them how beautiful they were. He returned to the hotel and fell asleep, feeling bitter that Hayden had drained their energy before a life-or-death event.

That worry proved unfounded. The next morning, the two were waiting for him after his morning meetings, demanding that he go out to breakfast. At the table, the two expressed their excitement, their joys, and their gains. Kayline filled a water glass to show how much control she had over her spirit. Lina could use a spirit, too. It was just the Clear Barrier spirit, but her cora reserves made it unbelievably strong.

Ritrix had never seen his daughters so full of vitality—

—and it was all owed to Hayden Reskco—a man who sought to use them as political meat shields.

There was no doubt that the opportunities Hayden was providing were worth risking their lives over, but the fact that he—someone of considerable age and prestige—was playing their heartstrings felt sinister.

Kayline must have read his expression because that night, once dinner was over and he was walking them back to their dorm, she said, “Hayden is everything you fear he is—and more. He’s not eighteen. He’s ruthless and uses people like a cruel politician. That said… he cares. About us. I know that probably sounds naive, but it’s true.”

Ritrix closed his eyes. “Kay…”

“Don’t ‘Kay’ her,” Lina huffed. “It’s the truth. If we were just political shields, he’d trudge us through the rift with his ground team. Instead, he’s pumping billions into us. You heard us, right? Rank 4 catalysts. Billion credit spirits. Elixirs. Training. And you can call that mitigation all you want, but he’s a lot more powerful than you right now. If we die, they’ll just make us out to be idiots for joining Hayden’s party, and that’ll be that.”

“Which’ll be true,” Ritrix warned.

“Yes, yes, I forgot we were doing this for the love of friendship,” she sassed.

Kayline stared at Ritrix mockingly.

“What?” he asked.

“What do you mean, ‘What?’” Kayline pressed. “Hayden asked if we’d join in exchange for resources and training and we said yes. Since then, he’s lived up to his end of the bargain; now, we have to live up to ours. This is work. So stop treating us like we’re doing this out of the goodness of our hearts.”

Ritrix stared at his shoes, shaking his head slightly. “If you were me, what would you do?” He looked up with sincere eyes.

“I’d stop questioning my adult daughters and enjoy my time with them.”

Lina folded her arms in agreement.

He glanced between them and sighed. “Are you too old for hot chocolate?”

Kayline flashed a smile and looked at her sister.

“Never,” Lina declared.

The three went upstairs and had some hot chocolate. Ritrix did his best to make it a loving moment, but the more time he spent with his daughters, the more he desired to protect them. He didn’t know if he could let them go. Even if he lost his job, he didn’t know if he could let them go.

Lina, Kayline, and Hayden relaxed underneath a large, shady tree in the quad the next morning, trying to ignore the large crowd surrounding them. Their two dozen bodyguards were pushing back the crowd, but it wasn’t enough. Between Hayden’s blindfold boxing and workout videos, and his auction, students were making fortunes just for uploading videos of him. So the crowds grew by the day.

It annoyed the shit out of Lina, who huffed and puffed and raged every fifteen seconds.

“Just enjoy the sunshine,” Hayden said, lying in the grass with his eyes closed.

“I’ll enjoy it when they’re dead,” Lina declared, resisting the urge to flip off the recorders with both hands.

“Don’t do it,” Kayline said.

“You know, when you tell me not to do something I’m not doing, it makes me feel punished for doing the right thing.”

Kayline sighed. “Sorry. It’s just… Can’t you just ignore them?”

“No, I can’t ignore them. Do you even know what those freaks are saying about us?”

Hayden opened his eyes and watched Gluttony fly circles above them. “What are they saying?” he asked dryly.
