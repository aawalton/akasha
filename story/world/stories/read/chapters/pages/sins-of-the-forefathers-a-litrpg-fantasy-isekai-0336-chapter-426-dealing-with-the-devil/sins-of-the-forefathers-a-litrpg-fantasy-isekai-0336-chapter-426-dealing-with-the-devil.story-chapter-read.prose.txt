Breathe in.

Breathe out.

Breathe in, and…

Just barely manage to resist a frantic lunge toward Anguis, activating and unleashing everything I could in an instant.

And judging by the faint, amused smile on the Dwarven Prince’s lips as he studied me, he could tell.

That would be…ill-advised, I’m guessing.

There were a few things I knew about Anguis of House Savoy. I hadn’t made an actual study of the Dwarf, but there were certain things you picked up on while living on Vereden. Especially when you were a student in the Academy of Mystic Arts.

Anguis wasn’t a historical figure by any means, but he wasn’t exactly new to the scene of Velancia. To the best of my knowledge, he had been the head of the House of Savoy for about…a hundred and fifty years or so, at this point. From what I’d learned, he had ascended to his position relatively young, around my age, and had ruled Vittolia and all lands controlled by House Savoy with an iron grip. In contrast with most of the Dwarven nobility, he was also marked to have had a relatively successful career as a Classer, prior to his ascension.

That had been over a century ago. Conservative estimates placed the Dwarf likely to be past the fourth Breakpoint, so likely somewhere in his level four-hundreds. Not a real mover and shaker when it came to the most power people on Vereden, but strong enough to be considered a strategic asset of Velancia.

Notably, I had never found a word on just what his specialization was.

With the revelation that he was likely a Mind Mage…well. I suppose he had kept that secret for a very long time on purpose.

How wonderful for me.

If I tried to attack Anguis, he would stop me cold. I was under half of Anguis’s likely level. Yes, The Scintillant Blade could likely pierce right through whatever mystical defenses he possessed. But what would be the point when he could likely just stop the blade itself cold in midair? With his bare hands? It was pointless to try and instigate a fight right this instant…as much as I wanted to.

And there was the fact that he was openly holding Elysael hostage. I hadn’t missed how his right hand was casually resting on the back of the kneeling, blank-eyed Dwarf’s neck.

I let out the long breath I’d taken in, and carefully folded my legs underneath me as I sank to the floor. Resting my palms on my knees, I did my best to remain calm as I met Anguis’s eyes.

I was familiar enough with Mind Magic from my failures in the course to know…

Eye contact didn’t matter for the discipline.

Anguis’s smile stretched a tad wider, malicious amusement still dancing in his eyes. “I can call in one of the Knights if you like. Those broad steel backs would make a nicer bench than that cold stone floor. Brr,” Anguis shivered dramatically. “Stone just never heats up, you know?”

“Thank you, but I’m fine,” I said, as politely as I could.

The Prince of Serpents shrugged, one hand still resting lightly on the back of Ely’s neck. “Suit yourself. But please, no need for such formality. Since you were once owned by my spawn, that makes us practically family. Feel free to call me Uncle Anguis.”

I opened my mouth and then closed it. Furrowing my brow, I slowly shook my head. “Ah…no. I don’t think I’m going to do that. I…will ask what the point is.”

“Of…?” Anguis drawled, visibly amused.

“Everything,” I said bluntly, gesturing aimlessly with one hand at the world. “Why are you doing any of this? How are you doing this? I…really don’t understand it. You’re already one of the most powerful men-Dwarves in the world. What could you possibly gain by sending the world screaming and trapped into the Skyfall? The Tribulation, as you call it. You say what you’re doing is more than mere Mind Control that let you direct everything at the docks, but how?”

Anguis tapped the index finger of his left hand on his chin thoughtfully, eyes turned up to the ceiling. “Those are a lot of questions,” He mused, before turning a quirked eyebrow on me. “Why exactly should I answer them, Nathan? This isn’t a two-copper stage show some traveling magician puts on. We’re not puppets made of straw and canvas, dancing on a splintered stage for children. I’m not the gloating, stereotypical villain to be cast down by a magic sword in the middle of a monologue.” He laughed, the sound echoing through the cell and out down the hall.

I remained steady. “You wouldn’t be here if you didn’t want to gloat a bit, Anguis. Otherwise, my head would already be rolling.”

“Wrong again!” Anguis tsked, shaking his head. “I like to think of myself as a practical Dwarf, Nathan. What’s the point of gloating, other than to make myself feel better? No… I have a specific reason I’m down here. I’ve even already told you why.”

“You can’t be serious.” I frowned, immediately understanding where he was coming from. “You still want me to join you? After everything you’ve done?”

An eye roll was my answer. “Oh, what have I done so far, Nathan?” Anguis said scornfully. “Place this shrew and her subjects under my thrall? Humiliate you and dear Alveron before a crowd? Pah. Really, you should be more concerned about what I’m going to do, instead of what I’ve already done.”

“…and what are you going to do?”

Anguis’s smile faded, then, and he sat up straight and narrow from his seat on Elysael’s back. Almost involuntarily, I copied him. I could tell that, for nearly the first time since I’d laid eyes on the Dwarf, he was being serious. He wanted me to know he was being serious now.

“I am going to slaughter every man, woman, and child in this city,” Anguis said bluntly. “There will be no battle, no waste of precious military resources in pointless, equal warfare. Instead, there will be a methodical, calculated culling of the populace and its defenders, followed by a razing of Rhoscara. It’s defenders are already suborned, it’s populace undefended. I will not permit a single soul to survive this, nor a single stone to go unshattered. There can be no history of defiance allowed to remain on this world that speaks to there ever having been resistance to the future I am crafting. House Florens and its stronghold will forever stand as a testament to the price of defiance. And unless you want you, and all of your comrades to join the Rhoscarans in the grave, I urge you to consider my proposal with more than immediate, unthinking refusal.”

…

I…

“This is more important than your instinctual aversion to me and my House,” Anguis leaned forward, his intensity so strong I could nearly taste it. “Greater than your noted hatred of involuntary, indentured servitude. Even greater than your admittedly understandable antipathy born from the actions of Magnus. I am informing you, right now, that this conversation has the possibility of influencing not just the futures of Velancia, but of Herztal and Vereden as a whole. The destruction of Rhoscara is a foregone conclusion, as much as that must pain you to realize. It is not a promise or a curse when I say this city is already dead. That path is set in stone, immutable as the mountains. It is the near future, and the future is all that should concern men of import such as ourselves. Now. With that in mind, are you ready for this conversation, Nathaniel?”

I breathed deep once more, almost involuntarily closing my eyes to focus myself.

In.

And out.

This…was not what I had been expecting. Anguis was not what I was expecting, away from the prying eyes of the public.

There was a malice to him, of course there was. But for all of his grandiose demeanor, acting as if we were merely two players upon a stage among others, here he was…

Disturbingly like me. I could see myself doing exactly what he was doing right now.

Switching like this…it was just like earlier. He was changing personalities on the fly, both to probe and to unsettle me, I could see that. But there was a seriousness to his actions that spoke that it was real. He was not playing around.

Anguis meant every word of what he was saying.

…alright. Okay.

I couldn’t do anything but play along…for now.

I opened my eyes and studied the Prince for a moment soberly. “You are an admitted Mind Mage,” I said bluntly. “How can we possibly engage in good faith negotiation when, only a few hours ago, you suborned a similar meeting using your abilities? I could have already fallen to them, and I wouldn’t even know.”

“You would,” Anguis immediately replied, just as blunt. “I promise, you would know, Nathan. Mind Magic is not as subtle as you believe it to be. To dominate the mind of another would require a visible cast Spell. There are no passive domination abilities within the school, no matter what I have researched. And before you ask,” He held up a hand to stop the question already forming on my lips. “There are different circumstances involved in the ritual that took place at the docks. As a show of good faith in these circumstances, I shall reveal a secret.”

He leaned forward, that same similar spark of magenta lighting I had seen earlier returning to his eyes.

“I am only capable of that level of control over Dwarves,” Anguis said coldly. “When Leonard and I ripped Fynneas’s Mantle of Divinity from the bound, slavering form of the Elf, I kept the smallest possible spark of it for myself. This, normally, would not be possible for mortals. I suspect even that single spark would have incinerated the souls of most, with only a Paragon surviving its weight. However, I am not most mortals. House Savoy, and indeed, the purest Houses of Dwarven nobility that exist are directly descended form Yorgun, the Dwarven God of War.”

That…was one suspicion confirmed.

“The Five Ruling Houses all possess a single spark of purity within their respective lines,” Anguis continued. “It grants us strength. Vigor. We rule not simply because we are ancestrally bound to. We rule because we are better. Yorgun had five children, while Fynneas had but the one. With the death of their Father in the War in Heaven, these children sought to break away from the rule of the Jarl’s Council of the Mountain Holds. And they did, after a long, brutal civil war, they did. Velancia. Even this shrew has the self-same spark within her. Not many know this, Nathan,” Here, his series mien cracked for a moment, as Anguis smirked at me. “Be honored. I would never dream of imparting this knowledge to a mere human. But then again…you’re not, are you?”

I remained quiet, merely continuing my study of the Prince. It was one thing for him to know what I was…

It was another thing entirely to offer it up.

Anguis didn’t seem bothered. Instead, he just continued his nearly scholarly lecture. “None but another who possesses a spark of Divinity can accept another. And when I did the same to Fynneas’s smallest fragment…it opened up a world of possibilities for me. This is all rather new to me, you see, but I believe…I have gained the most minor of accesses to the collective soul of Dwarven-kind.”

My jaw slackened without my meaning to, a terribly forboding feeling stealing over me.

“Yes…” Anguis nodded at me regally. “In the same way that Fynneas’s madness propagates out over the collective soul of the Elves, so too can I tap into that of my people’s. I am not nearly so mighty as a true Divinity, of course. But what I can do is enough.” He leaned forward, trying to impart his utter seriousness to me. “There is not a Dwarf alive I cannot influence now, Nathan. There is no defense against this. Not to my understanding, and believe me. Leonard and I have researched this matter in the months since the Tribulation began. I tell you this so you know.”

“It is only Dwarves whom I can claim a level of mastery over.”

I licked my suddenly dry lips, fighting down a surge of panic. “What…” I croaked out, before stopping and clearing my throat uncomfortably. Anguis waited patiently while I collected myself. “What about my comrades? I…distinctly remember some of them looking just as angry as the Dwarves.”

I was a bit startled by the pity in Anguis’s gaze, then, before he shook his head. “That was natural, I’m afraid. Likely, your underlings were simply caught up in the mood around them. And, it is as you say,” He pointed out. “It was only some of them who were considering rebellion. I truly understand, Nathan, how hard it can be rely upon others. Faith in a leader is something so easily tossed aside. Especially for personal gain.”

Oh.

I…guess there had been some disagreements in the past, in the Captain’s circle. Marcel was flat-out an asshole, Gustave was young and inexperienced enough to be easily influenced, and Olag was a Dwarf.

Already vulnerable.

I…guess I could only really count on Nyx and Bella, to a much lesser extent.

And, of course…

Sylvia.

I bowed my head for a moment, still grieved despite myself…and sighed.

“Alright,” I said quietly, not looking at him. “What…is your pitch?”

“It is thus,” Anguis said, suddenly sounding all business once more. “If there is unrest in your ranks, I shall help purge it. When you have firmed your grip upon your forces, you are to depart Velancia with all haste, in whatever manner you see fit. My intelligence suggests you likely arrived by sea, and so I encourage you to re-trace your steps. You will be unmolested on your return journey, I promise you that. No forces of either Velancia nor Lasgach will harry you. Then, I further encourage you to retreat to the safety of the Blutstein wards for the coming months.”

“They are likely to be some of the only surviving when all is said and done.”

My head snapped up to glare at Anguis once more. “So Alveron was right! Global wards are going to be overwhelmed.”

Anguis was unfazed by my ire, instead nodding simply at me. “Partially, if not for the wrong reasons. I did not lie totally, on the docks. Fynneas’s Mantle is purging itself of its accumulated madness, which presents itself as the corrupting poison affecting Vereden’s Aether. In a few months' time, the concentration of this madness-tainted Aether shall grow strong enough that wards will begin to fail en masse. But not,” He said calmly. “Those created by the gods. They are strong enough to survive what is to come. Blutstein, Smaragd, Turchese…these cities will remain safe. Some other major cities with sufficiently strong and ancient enough ward stones will possibly survive the zenith of the cleansing. But we are unsure. If you make haste to Blutstein, it’s possible you could, with the help of the Gnolls and their Gigant, evacuate enough of Herztal into the walls of Blutstein before the wave comes. The country could survive then into what comes next. It is what I shall be doing in Velancia…after I have finished purging Rhoscara personally.”

I slowly shook my head in disbelief. “And so everyone else not in the Gem Cities dies?” At Anguis’s calm nod, I clenched my fists. “Roricia, Kawamara, the Mountain Holds…even the northern tribes…they are all going to die? For what Anguis?” I shot to my feet, barely aware that I was starting to shout, my voice echoing in and out of the cell. “What could possibly justify this level of genocide! And don’t give me that shit about helping the Elves, I don’t believe it for a second!”

Anguis remained calm before my fury. “You’re right not to believe it,” He said with a shrug, slowly standing from his place on Elysael’s back. “It’s untrue. The real answer is far more important and practical. Once the Mantle has been cleansed…I shall claim the whole of it for myself, and ascend into a Divinity.”

I nearly had an aneurysm in my sudden fit of rage. After all of that, it was just about a blatant grab for powe-

“To save us from the death of the System,” Anguis said heavily, shaking his head.

I…stopped. I think even my heart skipped a beat, for a moment. All I could do was gape at Anguis as my rage drained away.

I don’t think he had even noticed. Instead, the Prince of Vittolia had started to pace in the small cell, uncaring at his fellow Prince still kneeling on the stone behind him. “My agents in the Herztalian court informed me you spoke to the Primector briefly, and I expect he informed you of this. It is my belief that he even asked you to fulfill your purpose as a Precursor and slay Fynneas for his Divinity, returning it to the System.”

I gaped harder.

“Yes, as I suspected,” Anguis growled in irritation. “Short-sighted relic. There is no guarantee that doing so will actually repair the damage to the System. And even then, what if a single Mantle of Divinity returned isn’t enough? What if the degradation simply continues? Are you to be expected to track down the two gods that live upon Indiqua and slay them, too? Impossible. They are not so diminished as Fynneas and are well protected. Indiqua has many Paragons to our single remaining fossil. Lynnea’s Court of Iron and Blood alone would slaughter you easily if you sought to slay her. This isn’t even considering the fact that there is no way to access the rest of the portal network, and even track down the rest of the gods to slay in the first place. No…if there is a better path to ensuring our survival, it is to take after the peoples of Indiqua after the collapse.”

He stopped and turned to face me with fanaticism in his gaze. “It is to emulate them. Only a god can protect us when the System eventually perishes, and our Status’s are stripped from our souls. And we have none. Fynneas cannot be trusted to have the Mantle returned to him. There are precious few Divine lineages left upon Vereden to ascend in this manner, and of them, I trust none of them with the responsibility. Certainly not this shrew.” Anguis said, throwing a disdainful look at Elysael. “No. I shall take this burden upon myself and become the shield of Vereden. That’s only the bones of the matter, of course, but-”

Anguis was cut off by the door opening, and a Savoy soldier scurrying in. He leaned in to the Princes ear and murmured something to him before running out, leaving the door open behind him.

In the meantime, Anguis sighed in irritation. “Apologies, Nathan, but this will have to wait. Matters are accelerating out in the city, and I must leave. However,” He fixed me with a heavy stare. “You now know enough to make a decision. I urge you to do so, and when I return, I am afraid I shall expect an answer. I will…illuminate you then, about the further benefits of following my proposed course of action. Good day, Marshal. Come along, shrew.”

At Anguis’s command, Elysael rose robotically and followed after the Dwarf as he left my cell. The heavy door closed behind him, leaving me alone…

With the ocean of doubts his words had left me with.
