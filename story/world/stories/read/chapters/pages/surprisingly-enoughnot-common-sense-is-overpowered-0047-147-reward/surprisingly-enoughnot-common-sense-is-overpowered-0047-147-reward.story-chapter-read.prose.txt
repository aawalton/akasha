Ning was led out of the main hall along with the others who had passed and toward a quiet side chamber carved into the rock wall. The elder had disappeared immediately after announcing the results, leaving an attendant to organize the newly certified disciples.

"Everyone will enter one at a time," the attendant announced.

Ning looked at the line forming in front of the door and sighed inwardly. He was getting rather tired of standing in lines, but apparently bureaucratic procedures transcended worlds.

"Ni Luoxia."

The first disciple entered, and the line slowly moved forward. By the time Ning's name was finally called, he had already gone through several cultivation problems in his head simply to pass the time.

The chamber itself was simple, containing little more than a stone table, an oil lamp, and a jade plaque resting neatly at the center. The elder from earlier sat behind the table.

"Ji Ning. Step forward."

Ning bowed and approached.

"This is your First-Tier Spiritual Plant Certificate. Effective immediately."

The elder slid the jade plaque across the table. When Ning picked it up, the jade warmed slightly in his palm as the inscriptions across its surface shimmered before settling again. They were far more complicated than ordinary identification inscriptions, which made sense considering the certificate had been issued through the alliance itself.

"You should already know the main benefit," the elder continued. "From today onward, the sect will no longer take seventy-five percent of your harvest."

Ning's attention immediately sharpened.

"Instead, certified spiritual farmers pay a fixed quota once every three months. The amount is assessed per acre and depends on the grade of the crop."

The elder gestured toward a stone board beside him. "For low-grade First-Tier spiritual grain, forty-five kilograms per acre every three months. Middle-grade, thirty kilograms. High-grade, including Frozen Breath Grain, eighteen kilograms. Top-grade, fifteen."

Ning's eyes flicked across the numbers as he calculated.

Five acres of low-grade grain meant two hundred and twenty-five kilograms every three months. One acre of Frozen Breath Grain meant another eighteen kilograms.

Compared to losing three-quarters of every harvest, Ning was already liking his new certificate very much.

"As for medicinal herbs and other specialty plants, the quotas are lighter. Thirty, twenty, twelve, and ten kilograms respectively."

The elder moved on. "Fail to meet your quota once and you'll receive a warning. Fail twice and your certificate will be suspended. A third failure..."

He shrugged.

"You return to the old system."

Seventy-five percent.

Ning had absolutely no intention of letting that happen.

"Understood, Elder."

"Good." The elder leaned back slightly. "My name is Luo Hong. Remember it."

Ning paused for the briefest moment before cupping his hands. "Yes, Elder Luo."

Luo Hong studied him before giving a small nod. "Second place on your first certification attempt isn't bad. Don't become complacent because of it."

"This disciple will remember."

"Good. You may go."

Ning bowed once more before leaving the chamber.

By then, the noise outside had begun to die down. Most of the disciples were dispersing in small groups, some happily discussing their results while others left in silence. One figure, however, remained near the stone steps.

"Ji Ning."

Ning turned to find Xin Fu standing there with his hands clasped behind his back. His expression was somewhat complicated, which wasn't particularly surprising. He had fallen just short of qualifying.

"Congratulations," Xin Fu said after a moment. "Second place, and you got the certificate."

"Thank you."

Xin Fu exhaled slowly. "I didn't qualify. I was close, but..."

"Not close enough?"

Xin Fu gave him a dry look. "You didn't have to finish that for me."

Ning smiled.

"I suppose I'll have to try again next year."

Ning considered him for a moment. Xin Fu didn't seem to be looking for comfort so much as venting his frustration.

"Your spell control isn't bad," Ning said. "Your theory is the problem."

"That obvious?" Xin Fu grimaced. "I was mostly taught how to use the spells and apply them properly. I never studied much beyond what I needed."

That explained quite a lot.

"Go to the Spiritual Farming Hall's library," Ning said after thinking for a moment. "Start with Foundations of Spiritual Botany, Volume One. After that, read Annotated Growth Cycles of First-Tier Plants. And don't skip the footnotes."

Xin Fu raised an eyebrow. "The footnotes?"

"That's where half the useful explanations are."

"...You're really recommending books to someone who just lost?"

Ning shrugged. "You didn't fail because you're bad at spiritual farming. You just have gaps in what you know."

Xin Fu stared at him for a moment before chuckling. "Figures. Fine, I'll read them. Next year, I'll pass."

He cupped his fists, and Ning returned the gesture.

"Good luck."

One more friendly face meant one more path in the sect. Ning had given Xin Fu a heads-up; what he did with it was up to him.

With that settled, Ning headed back toward his farm, his thoughts quickly returning to the jade certificate sitting safely in his storage pouch.

The seventy-five-percent system was finally gone. As long as he met the quarterly quota, everything beyond it belonged to him.

Which meant Ning needed to reconsider what he planted on every acre.

Yield, growth time, market price, quota, cultivation difficulty...

He was going to have to min-max his farm.

Fortunately, his timing couldn't have been better. The next harvest was only a few days away, and this time, the majority of it would actually belong to him.

Ning was very much looking forward to seeing the numbers.

...

The outer city was where most early- and mid-stage Qi cultivators lived, people scraping by on commissions, hunting, or low-end crafts.

The inner city was different. Many Foundation Building cultivators resided there, while even the late-stage Qi cultivators who lived within its walls usually possessed some recognized craft or specialized trade.

For Ning, this was his first time entering.

He had simply never needed to come before. His loot was usually sold in the outer city, and after returning from a hunt, he was often too exhausted to wander around looking for somewhere else to spend his spirit stones.

Today, however, Sun Jing had specifically called him here.

Ning approached the inner city gate, where two guards stood watch. Both were clearly in the mid-stage of Qi cultivation, though their aged faces and weathered expressions suggested decades of experience.

One of them stepped forward. "State your purpose. Do you wish to enter the inner city?"

"Yes."

Ning retrieved his jade token and held it out. "Outer sect disciple. Ji Ning."

The moment the guard saw the token, the tension drained from his posture.

"So it's a disciple of the Pure Qi Sect." His tone became noticeably more respectful. "You may enter. The entry fee is two spirit stones."

Entering the inner city normally required five spirit stones unless one purchased permanent residency. Fortunately, outer sect disciples received a discount, while inner sect disciples could enter freely.

Ning handed over two spirit stones, and the gate opened.

The moment he stepped inside, the atmosphere changed.

The streets were wider, the buildings taller, and the spiritual energy noticeably denser. Compared to the outer city, where cultivators seemed focused almost entirely on making a living, this place had clearly been built with convenience and comfort in mind.

Shops lined the streets, their signboards etched with spiritual qi signatures. Cultivators walked openly between them, some wearing refined robes while others carried artifacts without bothering to conceal them.

Before Ning could take more than a few steps, a boy about his age hurried over.

"Guest! Do you need a guide? One spirit stone for the entire day. I know all the shops, markets, and workshops in the inner city!"

Ning blinked.

So even guides had their own market here.

"Sorry. I'm meeting someone."

The boy accepted the refusal easily and moved on in search of another customer.

Ning watched him disappear into the crowd. One spirit stone for an entire day's guidance wasn't particularly unreasonable, but after spending so long counting every spirit stone he earned, paying someone to show him around still felt strangely extravagant.

He had barely continued walking when a familiar voice called out from across the street.

"You're finally here!"

Ning turned to see Sun Jing striding toward him, his robes trimmed with thin golden stripes that caught the light as he moved.

"I am," Ning replied. "Still, I didn't expect you to call me straight into the inner city."

Sun Jing waved a hand dismissively. "Details, details. You were bound to come sooner or later."

Ning didn't comment, though inwardly he recalled the so-called compensation Sun Jing had promised him.

To be honest, Ning had already assumed he'd been quietly fleeced. The man hadn't sent a single message for nearly a month. So Ning had been genuinely surprised when, just as he was preparing to overhaul his fields, a message finally arrived.

Ning glanced around at the bustling streets. "Since we're already here, why don't you show me around?"

Truthfully, he was curious. Now that he was finally here, it felt a little like unlocking a new area after meeting the level requirement.

It would be a waste not to explore.

Sun Jing's face lit up. "Of course! Leave it to me. I practically grew up here. I know which shops are honest, which ones overcharge, and which ones smile while selling you trash."

"...That's reassuring."

"First rule of the inner city: never buy the first thing you see. Second rule, a lot of places exaggerate. Third—"

"Sun Jing."

"Ah. Right." Sun Jing grinned. "Walking first. Talking later."

They moved through the streets, although Sun Jing continued talking anyway.

"That's a formation shop. Don't go in unless you want to lose spirit stones. Over there? Alchemy materials. Smells awful, works great. That inn?" He leaned closer. "Food's amazing. Rooms are terrible."

Ning listened quietly, committing the layout to memory. The inner city really was different: more refined and far more expensive.

Eventually, Sun Jing stopped in front of a shop with a deep-red signboard carved with flowing talisman runes.

"This is it," he announced. "Best talisman shop on this street."

The moment they stepped inside, the man behind the counter looked up and froze.

"Ah, Young Master Sun!"

The shopkeeper hurried out from behind the counter, his earlier laziness vanishing in an instant. His smile was wide, practiced, and perhaps even a little genuine. Clearly, Sun Jing was a valued customer.

"It's been a while!" the shopkeeper said warmly. "I was wondering when you'd come back."

Sun Jing laughed. "Ran out, obviously. Business has been good."

"As expected, as expected." The shopkeeper clapped his hands. "Please, take a look. I've got fresh stock. Defensive talismans, alarm talismans, even a few rare stabilization ones."

Sun Jing immediately began looking through the selection with considerable enthusiasm.

"I'd already finished my talismans in the previous mission," he muttered, rubbing his temples. "Before the next one even properly started. I suffered greatly because of that."

Ning glanced at his expression.

For some reason, an image surfaced in his mind: those distant, hollow eyes people got after surviving something they really didn't want to remember.

A proper flashback face.

This guy had clearly seen things.

"So," Ning asked, "what exactly happened after we parted?"

Sun Jing let out a long breath.

"My mission was simple on paper. Retrieve a jade plaque from the Fan family in Blue Rock Village, east of Redhorn Village."

"So far, normal."

"That's what I thought." Sun Jing's expression darkened. "But I stopped at Redhorn Village to rest, and that's where I ran into Xiao Fan."

Ning nodded.

Of course you did.

"And then we somehow ended up fighting a bandit who claimed to be the younger brother of the bandit leader we'd already dealt with."

"...That escalated quickly."

"They outnumbered us four to one," Sun Jing continued. "We barely escaped. Barely. Then, while fleeing, we stumbled straight into the lair of a Foundation Building monster."

Ning stared at him. "You ran into a what?"

"A Foundation Building bull."

"And you survived?"

"Ran. Again." Sun Jing's expression became complicated. "Don't ask me how. I'm still not entirely sure. Then, somehow, we escaped and finally reached Blue Rock Village. We retrieved the plaque and thought it was finally over."

Ning waited. There clearly was something more to it.

Sun Jing sighed. "It was not over."

On the way back, they encountered the same group of bandits again.

"This time, Xiao Fan's martial arts had improved. Noticeably." Sun Jing looked somewhat disturbed by that fact. "Enough that we actually managed to defeat the bandit leader's brother."

He fell silent for a moment.

"And then we came back. That's it."

Ning stared at him.

"That's rough, buddy."

Sun Jing sighed. "I'm telling you, it felt like the heavens themselves didn't want us to finish that mission."

Ning nodded to himself.

As expected of a cultivation world, once you defeated someone, their relatives showed up immediately. Defeat one person, and sooner or later you would have to deal with the entire lineage.

A classic.

"Next time," Ning said, "bring more talismans."

Sun Jing's shoulders slumped. "Believe me. I learned that lesson."

From the look on his face, Ning suspected Sun Jing wouldn't forget it anytime soon.

"So, which talismans do you want to buy?"

Talismans, like most things, were divided into tiers. The escape talisman and evil-repelling talisman Sun Jing had used before were both mid-grade talismans.

"I want to buy another Fire-Lightning Blast Talisman." Sun Jing's expression became serious. "As long as I have enough firepower, I don't believe I'll end up in that situation again."

Ning thought about it. "I think you should balance your collection more. A top-tier talisman can give you attack power far beyond your cultivation, but that also means it takes longer for you to activate."

He picked up one of the talismans displayed nearby.

"The real advantage of talismans is speed. They're much faster than spells. If you use ones closer to your own cultivation level, you can activate them almost instantly."

Sun Jing was clearly the type to prioritize power over speed.

That wasn't necessarily wrong, but relying entirely on overwhelming power left some rather obvious weaknesses.

Sun Jing considered Ning's advice for a moment before nodding. "That makes sense. But the solution is easy."

He grinned.

"I'll just buy a full set of mid-grade, high-grade, and top-grade talismans. Then I don't believe I'll lose again."

Ning paused.

Even an ordinary mid-grade talisman cost around twenty-five spirit stones. A basic set of attack, defense, and escape talismans meant three per tier, and Sun Jing apparently intended to buy all of them.

The amount of money involved was absurd.

"Is something wrong?" Sun Jing asked, noticing Ning's slightly blank expression.

"Nothing," Ning replied flatly.

Earlier, he had been wondering whether he should refuse such a heavy reward.

Now, he was absolutely certain he shouldn't.

Since he was already here, Ning also intended to buy a few talismans for himself. The sheer power of the ones Sun Jing had used before had certainly left an impression.

Fortunately, Ning was rather well-funded after his recent mission.

...

After purchasing the talismans, Sun Jing continued leading Ning around the inner city.

"Ah." He suddenly stopped in front of a familiar storefront. "Here."

Ning followed his gaze and immediately recognized the name written across the signboard.

Golden Bell Armory

"...Isn't that the same name as the armory inside the sect?"

Sun Jing grinned. "Yep."

"So that means—"

"Our family business." Sun Jing puffed out his chest proudly. "One of them, anyway. We have a few branches."

Ning looked at the shop again, reevaluating it entirely.

"I bought my bow from the sect branch," he said. "A low-grade one, but it was surprisingly sturdy."

Sun Jing's grin widened. "Of course it was."

He leaned closer as though sharing some great family secret. "Our refining focuses on structure first, effects second. Even our low-grade weapons aren't supposed to fail when you need them most. That's our rule."

That explained a lot.

Ning had abused that bow far more than he probably should have, yet it had endured everything he'd put it through.

"...Impressive."

"Right?"

They stepped inside.

The interior was spacious, with weapon racks lining the walls. Swords, spears, sabers, hammers, bows, and several stranger weapons were displayed throughout the shop, while the air carried the faint smell of metal and heated wood.

The shopkeeper looked up and immediately straightened.

"Young Master Sun!"

He hurried out from behind the counter. "You're back!"

"Of course I am." Sun Jing glanced toward Ning. "Keep him company for a moment. I'll go get his reward."

With that, he disappeared into the back room.

The shopkeeper personally led Ning toward the counter and poured him a cup of tea. "Young Master Sun brought a friend today."

"He did."

Small talk had always been somewhat awkward for Ning, so he mostly listened while the shopkeeper talked about the armory and the Sun family's business.

One interesting thing he learned was that Ling Yao, the senior sister he had met earlier, was also related to Sun Jing.

Their surnames were different, so there was probably a story there, but Ning didn't think much of it.

Before long, Sun Jing returned from the back room holding a pair of boots.

"Found them!"

The boots were cloud-gray, with faint wind-patterned inscriptions running along the sides. Even at a glance, Ning could tell they weren't ordinary.

"Wind Cloud Boots," Sun Jing announced. "First-Tier, high-grade. This is your reward."

The shopkeeper nodded approvingly. "A good choice. They're designed to improve short bursts of movement while reducing qi consumption."

Ning examined the boots briefly before accepting them.

"Thanks."

Since he had already decided to accept Sun Jing's compensation, there was no reason to hesitate now. Movement equipment was useful in almost any situation, especially something of this quality.

"We're not done yet," Sun Jing said. "I called someone else here too. I owe him compensation as well."

Before Ning could ask who, a familiar figure appeared at the entrance.

"Over here!" Sun Jing called.

Xiao Fan walked toward them, his expression as calm as ever.

"You're late!"

"You should've been clearer about the directions," Xiao Fan replied before noticing Ning. "Didn't expect to see you here."

Ning nodded. "Same."

Internally, however, he was staring at Sun Jing in disbelief. The man had invited the protagonist too without saying a word.

Completely oblivious to Ning's thoughts, Sun Jing tossed a small bundle toward Xiao Fan. "Here. Compensation for helping with my mission."

Xiao Fan caught it and opened the wrapping.

Inside was another pair of boots, simpler than Ning's and darker in color, but carrying similar wind-patterned inscriptions.

"...Movement boots?"

"Yep."

Xiao Fan looked them over before putting them away. "Thanks."

With the compensation finally distributed, Sun Jing clapped his hands together.

"So!" He looked between the two of them. "Where to next?"

Ning considered the question.

He had Sun Jing with him.

More importantly, he had Xiao Fan with him.

Where should I go with a protagonist in tow?

The answer came almost immediately.

Ning looked at them calmly.

"Gambling."
