The van was unassuming from the outside, just a large white eyesore like many others, but inside it was a marvel of espionage and technology dressed in Latnium and CRC branding.

Vera looked perfectly at home as she sat facing a literal wall of screens, each of them displaying a different angle of the building I was about to infiltrate, or the images from the cameras inside the office floor that was my real target. Some of them were also showing news feeds, livestreams, and monitoring online chatter. Seeing all this activity, even though it was relegated to the side monitors, made me realize just how much stuff was going on each day that we simply didn’t see, teams of Supes doing their job day in and day out.

The more I saw, the more it felt as if the city was becoming more dangerous, the heroes more active. I wondered out loud if that was the case, and Vera said plainly that yes, it was. Most of the calls were no longer cat-in-a-tree rescues. The place was getting hectic, more similar to a big hub like Central City than a quiet coastal town like Halford.

“Eyes on today’s prize, my prince, don’t let the rest of the city distract you,” Nadia said, nodding towards the main screen where we could see the twenty-floor eco-monster of glass and steel we were parked under.

It had been built with superpowered labor in record time a couple years back, a testament of a changing city, and now stood, still mostly empty, overlooking the city center and the old town.

I wondered if Halford was slowly losing its soul, and then caught myself thinking like an old man and thought of Gus.

“Right,” I said, forcing myself back to the here and now. There were a few lit floors in the tower, sterile white lights against the overcast night sky, and our target was the band of light two-thirds of the way up where a company owned an entire story worth of offices.

Nadia put her earpiece on and did a sound check, and Vera followed.

“Loud and clear,” I said with a thumbs up, hearing their voices in my ears.

“Good,” my girlfriend said. “Don’t worry, I’ll be with you every step of the way. Kit check?”

I patted my pocket. “Stopwatch is there,” then the rest of the body. “Earpiece is working, flex cuffs at the ready, the office key, and I have the thumb drive.”

For the office key, Vera had worked it up from a photo of the analog original, built a render, and had Kimberly cut it.

Then Vera took over. “You remember the floor plan, right?” she asked, pulling it up on the main screen on a window hovering above the view of the building. “The elevator drops you here. The camera paths intersect here,” she pointed at red hazes, which were where the cameras could see. “Here, and here. You will have to use the time stop for that. Remember, if we see you in the camera feeds…”

“Then I’m cooked, yeah,” I said. “Nadia and I practiced yesterday. You won’t see me, as long as I time my stops and don’t carry over enough momentum to displace the air or anything nearby.”

“Perfect,” she said. “The take is a scientist’s research notes. Intel says they’re digital, on the firm’s network. Once you are in the master office, you plug in the thumb drive and wait. The program runs by itself.”

“Once it’s done, and not a second later, you get out of there,” Nadia added.

Vera nodded. “We know from intel that they have a guard doing the rounds of the floor. Tonight there’s only one, but we don’t know when he will reach your location until he appears on camera because they don’t keep their schedule anywhere the CRC tools could reach.”

“Did you try your own hacking AIs?” I asked.

She shook her head. “My private tools never touch CRC jobs or software.”

“Reasonably paranoid,” Nadia agreed.

We went over the plan some more. But try as we might, like we had already concluded in the true planning phase the day before, it all came down to a hard limitation we could not circumvent. My time stop was powerful, but once used it entered cooldown and there was nothing I could do about it to make it come online sooner. Forty minutes, and I was going to time it with the stopwatch as not to be caught unawares. That was the best we could do and, with a pinch of luck, the data extraction was going to go smoothly.

That’s what I told myself as I rode the elevator up. The lobby had been empty, and the CRC override code got me into the elevator without a hitch. The camera had been disabled remotely yesterday morning, and nobody had even come to check.

I felt the time stop like an energetic force just beneath my skin, ready to be activated at will. When the elevator doors opened, I triggered it immediately. There was almost no difference, of course, because the whole office was empty and immobile already. Except, I could tell that time had stopped by the smell of the air, and the strange artificiality of the lights (which was rich of me to say, given the fact that I was in an office lit by flickering neons, but I could tell. They didn’t flicker anymore).

I ran, and by the time the stopwatch had barely reached twelve seconds, I had already reached the cameras’ blind spot. I slowed and stopped, arresting my momentum, the last eight seconds spent studying the square open-concept office plan surrounded by massive windows that framed the night lights of the city below, and the frozen drops of faint rain clinging to the glass.

“Clear?” came the question in my ear from Nadia. “Are you at checkpoint-1?”

“Clear,” I replied. “Yeah, I’m there.”

For me, twenty seconds had passed. For them, I had just left the elevator. “Good,” Nadia said. “No trace of you in the cameras. Now you wait.”

I did. Forty minutes trapped in a couple square meters of blind spot, waiting for my power to come back online. And if the guard came right at that moment? I’d be screwed.

I tried to listen for his steps, even though Nadia and Vera would probably see him first on the camera feeds, well before I could hear anything. Indeed, all that reached my ears was the incessant overlapping droning of the lights, and all I saw was the soulless life of chest-high cubicle walls and isolated rooms. One had its door open and I could see a water dispenser, its refrigerator engine humming, and I wondered about the lives of the people working here.

Could I ever be one of them? Or rather, could I have been, had I not awakened or had I kept the lousy power instead of finding Gus’s nanites?

“Situation?”

I winced at Nadia’s voice. “Bored.”

I checked my stopwatch. Shit. I’d been there all of two minutes. I swore they felt much longer.

“Only thirty-eight more minutes to go. We’d keep you company, but…”

“I know,” I whispered.

“Just know we are here, okay?”

It made me smile for a little while, then the silent background noise returned and slowly wormed into my ears. I was alone, utterly alone, in an empty office doing what pretty much amounted to burglary. And I was standing right in the middle of it, or rather, crouching so that the cubicles could hide me from line of sight in case anyone came from the corridor. And I waited, sweat slowly soaking the clothes on my back.

Beep. Fucking finally.

Nadia was barely a second late compared to my stopwatch. “It’s time.”

I didn’t reply. The air lost its color, the lights went quiet and I was spared from the droning. I made a run for the door I had memorized the location of, found it, fumbled with the key and thanked the powers that be that it was an old-school analog key and that it worked in stopped time. Vera had shown me both feeds in the van, and like an idiot I had told myself I could tell from the angles where they would be. Then I was inside.

Seven seconds gone. Shit. I was cutting it close. One swift motion, there was no going around this, and the first camera was right where I had guessed, in the corner of the ceiling, smashed to pieces. Beep. Five seconds left. I checked again.

“Where is the other? Where is it??” My voice carried the tension because I felt the seconds trickle down.

One down, but the other was hiding. I swung my head around the room, and it took me precious seconds to find it. I almost didn’t make it, but I smashed it too.

I wasn’t going to sound any alarms, but if anyone was actively watching… yeah, they’d see two cameras go dark at the same time.

The smells came back, and both crashes landed in my ears at once. Time had resumed. And I’d done it. I found the computer, plugged the thumb drive in and updated the girls on my status.

“Good,” Nadia said. “You’re doing an amazing job, love. Gosh, I wish I could see how cool you look right now.”

Cool? Hmm. Maybe I was a bit cool. “You will.”

“Oh,” she said seductively. “You will show me extensively.” Then in a lower voice. “Bring those cuffs home, by the way. We’ll find a use for them.”

Okay that was hot. I also realized that despite the whispering, Vera was literally plugged on the same band so, if I heard Nadia’s words, then so did she.

Ohwell.

Now I couldn’t wait to be done here. And so, I… Waited.

At least the hard part was done, wasn’t it? Outside, it would be two more time stops and then I’d be back in the elevator and out. But the real hard part had never been the stops, or the waiting. It had been the tension.

And now I knew I could do it. Hell, I’d done it. A smile made its way on my face. I checked the clock. Thirty-four minutes before the stop came back online, but it was no matter, because I’d have to wait for whatever hack tool in the thumb drive to do its job first anyway.

But I’d done it. And I was going to do it again. My time stop really was a strong-as-fuck cheat power, and it was all thanks to Gus’s nanites. And later, at home, I’d give Nadia exactly what she asked for. I couldn’t wait.

Ten long, tense minutes later, I got served a cold shower I wouldn’t forget.

“Adrian,” came Vera’s voice through the earpiece, and despite her attempts I could feel the nervousness. “The notes we need to ‘retrieve’… they are not on the computer network.”
