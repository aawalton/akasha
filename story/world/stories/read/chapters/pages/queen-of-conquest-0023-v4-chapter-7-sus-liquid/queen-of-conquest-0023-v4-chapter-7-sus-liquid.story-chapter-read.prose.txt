Lilith, where are you? I’m coming to find you and bring you home. If you see this, give me a sign.

The message weighed on my mind with a philosophical might, leaving me speechless and frozen, my neck craned toward the marked ceiling.

Until now, I’d only encountered the Demon Progenitor and Orpheus in Graedon. I’d unconsciously assumed the captured progenitor was the demon. But this message… it didn’t make sense. The Demon Progenitor had already found me in the Misty Veils.

Could this be a third one? My gaze stayed locked on the ancient traces of magic etched into each letter. Though many progenitors were rumored to exist, I’d never met another beyond those two. It was rare, extraordinary, even, for a being of such power to meddle with lesser creatures.

Pure malicious intent aside.

Forget progenitors, was there anyone in Ordite who didn't want me dead? Who would go out looking for me in another world?

“What does it say?” Dralos asked, stepping beside me.

Josephine was out of earshot, but I used a the sound communication technique to speak directly into his ears anyways. “It’s asking where I am.”

He raised a scaly brow and mouthed, You?

I nodded.

He responded, his voice like a whisper in my mind. “Who?”

“I don’t know.”

A knight I hadn’t spoken to yet urgently waved us over. He was of average build, clad in golden armor, but his eyes, an unnatural brilliant gold, reflected a light that couldn't have reached this dim basement.

He knelt beside a heap of tangled silver chains dumped in the far corner of the cell. Atop them, a splotch of strange purple-black liquid lay cracked and dried.

But it was the energy lingering in the fluid that really caught my attention. It felt divine and corrupted, both mana and energy, and somehow none of them all at once.

“That’s… strange,” I muttered, crouching to run a finger along the dried substance. A chill skittered through me as heat and cold slammed against me like a tidal wave. My entire body jerked away and shuddered as a feeling of wrongness raced its way up my spine, filling me with a sense of fatigue and weakness.

Neither of my cores are harmed. Not drained. So what did it just do? I probed inward, checking the state of my energies. Everything was perfect, in the exact state it'd been moments ago, so why did I feel weakened?

When the golden-eyed knight reached out to mimic me, I smacked his hand away. “No one touches that until we understand what it is.”

He immediately snapped to attention. “Yes, Duchess.”

I briefly considered storing the chain in my ring, but if it could affect my cores, I wasn’t about to risk the ring and everything inside it. Especially not Orpheus’ core.

“Does anyone have an empty storage ring?” I called out.

A female knight with short black hair cut at the ears and dull-copper eyes stepped forward and saluted, her gauntlet clanging against her breastplate. “Yes, Duchess. We always bring spares.” She handed me a silver ring inlaid with blue topaz, faint mana pulsing at its core. The small pulse of active mana was enough to confirm it was a storage ring.

I took the storage ring from the knight’s outstretched hands and pushed a sliver of Shen energy into it. The topaz gem glowed faintly, then began to tug gently on the grounded chains under my silent command. One by one, they vanished, pulled smoothly into the ring without a sound.

Then I screamed.

The ring scorched my palm like molten iron. I dropped it instantly, and it struck the ground with a metallic clang, eerily similar to the knight’s salute earlier. The stone beneath hissed, steam rising as the ground blackened from the heat.

A raw, circular burn throbbed in my palm. A minor injury like that should have healed within seconds, if it left a mark at all. But it didn’t fade. I instinctively waved my hand around, trying to dispel the lingering burn.

It didn’t work.

Two healer knights rushed over, but I waved them away. “Focus on the search.” If my own regeneration failed, their magic wouldn’t do much either.

Unfortunately, the next few hours proved that there was nothing else to recover in the dungeon or the ruined estate. Beyond the strange fluid, all that remained were scorched bones, rotted food, and charred furniture. I’d copied the message onto parchment provided by Josephine, but I had refused to give her a copy of it, much to her obvious displeasure.

I suspected she could recall the exact script anyway, but I had no intention of helping the priestess in minding my business.

Still, the fluid, now sealed inside the storage ring, was worth the trouble. Since no one could hold the ring without being burned, the knights had secured it between two stone slats and taken turns dragging it behind them as we headed back toward the camp.

It was crude. But it worked.

When we returned to the clearing, two of the four knights we’d left behind jogged toward us. A third followed closely, leading a reined wyvern.

“My lady, welcome back,” the first knight said with a deep bow. It took me a moment, but I recognized him as one of the men who'd helped bring Raphael to the camp. He'd been the larger one with a thick beard that split like a forked tongue in the middle.

Is it Eric? Or Caleb? It's one of them for sure.

He'd barely finished before the second knight, the other one who'd helped move Raphael, blurted out with a glance at the third knight who had not been part of my initial entourage, “Your Grace, we have a message from Lady Nida. The Paragons have completed their missions and await your command.”

“Oh?” I gestured for the knight to rise, eying the wyvern rider. “Where are they? Back in Elyndor?”

The rider nodded and held out a small roll of parchment, tied with a thin red string. “Lady Nida and the others arrived a week ago tonight. She asked whether she should join you here with Vespera or continue waiting in the city proper.”

I unrolled the parchment and scanned it silently.

Mission accomplished. Targets have been taken care of. Pawns acquired. We await your return in Elyndor.

–N.

I burned the message with a flick of Shen energy and turned to the wyvern rider. He looked young, late teens, maybe. The boy had cropped brown curls, soft brown eyes, and a sharp, angular nose. His olive skin marked him as someone from the Sealrite region, particularly uncommon among Elyndor’s pale populace.

“Do you hail from the March?” I asked.

He bowed so low I thought he might kiss the scorched ground. When he rose, his gaze never rose past my boots. He cupped an open hand against his chest in the Lysorian salute. “Lieutenant Cody Summers, First Battalion under General Nida,” he said.

I blinked at the title, still not used to it. Still, I was the one who’d given her that title.

She did not want that responsibility. I resisted a grin as the memory of having to all but force the title upon the tigerkin came to mind.

After taking control of the Alistar Duchy, I’d imposed the same military structure I’d used in Sealrite during Benedict’s invasion.

Not all of my original Field Marshals had survived the Hydra attack, and considering that at the time I hadn't promoted anyone to General, I figured it was time to hand-pick a few of my trusted allies and place them into positions of military influence. Fortunately, no one had argued against my choices. I was confident that the ease with which I nominated the new generals was in no small part thanks to the puppeteering Benedict and Dralos were doing behind the scenes.

Nida was one of the Paragons I’d elevated to General within the Alistar military. Of everyone I’d encountered in Graedon, she was the one I trusted most, and that mattered more to me than experience or raw strength. Trust was rare. Precious. It didn't hurt that Nida had proven herself a natural at war over the past few months, single-handedly repelling any of the small houses within my duchy who'd tried to rise against my rule.

I'd placed the First, Second, and Third Battalions under her command.

The Fourth and Fifth went to Ethan, who had specifically nominated Mirabelle as one of his Field Marshals. I still didn’t know what to make of the former cardinal of the Church of Light, or Ethan’s bond with her, but Ethan had never failed me. I chose to trust his judgment.

Aargorn Delgov, a former Field Marshal, became General of the Sixth and Seventh Battalions. He had the experience to match the rank, having served as third-in-command under Duke Alistar himself. Out of all the Field Marshals, he'd proven himself the most during the Hydra battle. According to my reports, he'd saved nearly a quarter of the duchy's entire military force from the Hydra's attacks with his heart energy until the man had collapsed into a coma from energy deficiency for weeks.

For the Eighth and Ninth Battalions, I'd decided to promote Daenara Mavis. She’d earned that trust well before the Hydra’s attack, obeying every order with precision and unwavering loyalty during the Cael invasion of Sealrite.

I knew less about Link Marell, but both Aargorn and Daenara had vouched for him—his strength, his intelligence, and his cunning loyalty. That'd been enough for me. He became my fifth General, placed in command of the Tenth Battalion. When I'd informed him of his promotion, the man had knelt and all but kissed my feet in thanks. Aarogorn, who'd accompanied, had laughed and clapped my shoulder. It turned out that Link Marell was quite religious, and having the Priestess of your faith show you that amount of trust was worth groveling.

I'd told them both to get to work.

In total, the Alistar military now numbered roughly 9,500 soldiers, 700 knights, and close to 100 Paragons. We had technically exceeded the royal troop limit by several hundred, but I didn’t care. Knights shouldn't count toward military limits anyway. They were sworn to individuals, not crowns or duchies. All 700 were, by law, bound to me personally. Less than half had formally sworn their oaths, but I would correct that in time.

Either way, the King’s decrees held less weight to me than the dung of a three-headed arachnae. Once my lands were secured, I would turn my attention toward the kingdom's capital.

I’d briefly considered placing troops under Marquess Benedict Sharma. He had stood beside me during the Hydra siege, despite his Cael lineage. But Dralos warned that even the appearance of granting a Cael noble authority over the Lysorian military would destroy my reputation. Given my downfall in Ordite, I'd believed him.

So instead, I'd placed Benedict as a silent co-regent alongside Dralos. The people of the Alistar Duchy would continue believing I ruled as their Duchess—which I did, to a degree—but the day-to-day governance would fall to Benedict and Dralos in my absence.

I had far more ambitious plans than spending my time ruling a single duchy.

“How did the hunt go?” I asked the rider, turning away from my thoughts.

He straightened, finally lifting his gaze to meet mine. “Perfectly, Your Grace. The cleanse is proceeding on schedule.”

“Good,” I drawled, shifting my weight and tapping my hip. If the Paragons had gathered the individuals and corpses I’d requested, I needed to return to Elyndor before decomposition set in. Still, I couldn’t shake a faint sense of responsibility for what remained of Lilliana’s hometown. Even if she was satisfied with the Silverwater family’s downfall, the ruins left behind were still her history.

I pointed to Carla and beckoned her forward. She moved immediately, without a hint of hesitation. “Dame Carla, you’ll accompany Lieutenant Summers to the Tenth Battalion. Report to General Marell and give him a full account of what, and who, we discovered.” She saluted, and I continued, “Inform him that I’m assigning him six Paragons and a dozen knights to aid in securing this city and the surrounding territory.”

From the corner of my eye, I caught Dralos frowning, his brows so deeply furrowed they appeared one. Though he didn’t voice his concerns, I knew what ran through his mind.

The Silverwater Barony was nestled in a hornet’s nest—bordered by the Goldenhearts Duchy to the south, Aidelwald County to the north, the Balidore Marquisate to the west, and the Misty Veil Forest to the east. Not a single one of those territories was aligned with Alistar. With Collin Alistar dead, Balidore, Aidelwald, and a few others were beginning to test the duchy’s defenses, probing for weakness. Already, my battalions had engaged with them numerous times at our borders.

If they pushed any further, I’d have my justification to respond with overwhelming force. That, frankly, would be a gift.

Cody began to retreat toward his wyvern, Carla at his side, but I raised a hand and my voice to stop him.

“Rest for now,” I commanded. “The flight to Elyndor is nearly a week, and I believe General Marell is stationed even farther. Eat. Replenish your strength. Let your wyvern do the same.” I stepped toward the beast and pressed a hand to its snout. For most, touching an unbonded wyvern would be suicidal. But this one didn’t flinch, docile under my fingers, likely subdued by the lingering scent of my dragon, Vespera. “I expect the three of you to reach General Marell in under a week,” I added. “So rest while you can. You’ll be flying hard with very little breaks.” The rider saluted again, this time so hard I thought he might have bruised himself. Carla laughed and led him away from the wyvern toward some food the knights were making by the campfire.

A few hours later, Cody and Carla departed.

“Time to head back,” I announced. "Break down the camp. I want us on the move in an hour."

The knights obeyed, though not without a few groans and pointed sighs, especially from Josephine.

I ignored them, already looking forward to my return to Elyndor.

It was time to weave my web through the kingdom.
