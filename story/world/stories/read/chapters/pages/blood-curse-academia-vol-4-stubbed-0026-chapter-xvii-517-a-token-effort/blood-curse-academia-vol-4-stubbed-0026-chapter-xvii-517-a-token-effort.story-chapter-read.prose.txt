Chapter XVII (5.17) - A Token Effort

The next day Kizu sent Charles back his jacket via the post. He didn’t trust handing it over in person. Not when the Tainted boy had already stolen Kizu’s token once. Kizu decided to forswear returning to the third-year dorms entirely until the end of the challenge. Every opportunity he could avoid Charles, he would avoid Charles.

Ione’s mother had taken the appearance of Mort without so much as a twinge of surprise. When Kizu sheepishly admitted what he’d been doing to get his token back, she even seemed to approve of his efforts. Later, Ione explained her mother was like Sene and focused entirely on results. If she’d known what Kizu had been doing in the moment, she might have been annoyed. But since he pulled it off, she approved. It didn’t make much sense to Kizu, but he was still extremely relieved to not have offended the woman.

Now though Kizu felt he was in a stronger position than ever in regards to the tournament challenge. He knew who was hunting him and that knowledge alone granted him a great deal of peace of mind. But as the week ended it meant that he was going to start getting docked points for each day he put off hunting down his own target. Zashima Yugo.

But Kizu hadn’t been dormant this last week.

Under the stairs that led to Shinzou Academy’s World Dungeon entrance, Kizu sat at a dusty table and studied his scrying orb. Nobody disturbed him. In fact, Kizu doubted anyone had set foot in his old study nook since his last visit here. He could still spot chalk marks on the floor from his old divination attempts.

Currently, he was absent from Combat F, but he deemed that a necessary sacrifice to set his plan into motion.

Unlike Charles, who had relied on luck to snatch Kizu’s token, Kizu had meticulously studied Yugo’s schedule. Yugo was currently attending his Rejuvenation and Restoration class. And that just so happened to be the classroom exit that Kizu’s scrying orb was currently displaying on its glassy surface. In five minutes, Yugo would leave that class for lunch. From what Kizu had observed, Yugo liked to eat lunch in one of two different locations.

The bell rang and students filed out of the classroom. Kizu leaned in closer and studied the image displayed on his scrying orb. Then he spotted his mark.

Yugo had deep bags under his eyes and it looked as though his most recent trip to the barber had botched his hair, as it had three jagged chunks missing. The student paused as he exited the classroom and looked left, then right. His gaze lingered on the right, then he stepped in that direction.

Kizu let out a sigh of relief. He was heading for his private study spot outside. If he’d decided to go to the cafeteria, Kizu would have had to navigate through hundreds of other students. Each of whom had the potential to change everything. And every additional unknown variable risked his plan. This was far more preferable. He’d genuinely been considering just skipping class again in the future rather than risk a cafeteria attempt.

With a wave of his hand, Kizu switched viewpoints on the scrying orb and spectated as Yugo trudged through the hallway, his head down. Occasionally, he’d glance over his shoulder, but he never thought to look in the direction of one of Kizu’s divinations.

It had been surprisingly simple to set up divination watch points in the academy’s hallways. Kizu suspected that normal restrictions and wards had been recently dropped to assist students with the challenge as inside classrooms and private areas remained impossibly difficult to divine. At least, not at the level of any student or professor in the academy. Kizu believed that the wards might still crumble under the might of the crone or a coven of united witches.

That all mattered very little, as Kizu had vision of the hallways and that was all he needed.

Kizu had set up a few different scrying points across campus and flipped through them to track Yugo as the student passed through several courtyards.

Then after nervously glancing over his shoulder one final time, he entered his usual gazebo and closed the door behind him. Perfect.

Kizu stood up and dusted himself off. He’d faced down undead necromancers, witch covens, and Dragons, but his heart still fluttered in his chest as he prepared himself for the upcoming confrontation.

Since Yugo had decided to depart to his study spot, it meant Kizu could go with his more brute force plan, rather than the more contrived plot to shuffle the tokens of people in the cafeteria. While that would have likely been a fun challenge, there was a lot less risk in this confrontation.

He lifted his scrying orb, his vision through it detailing the courtyard and letting him know the exact locations of everything within it. Then he jumped.

The cramp and dusty space beneath the stairs was suddenly replaced by the sensation of warm sun and the cool gust of a breeze. He blinked several times, his eyes adjusting to the light after scheming in the dark for the last hour.

His feet left soft indents in the soft soil as he strolled forward to the gazebo’s door. He wiggled the doorknob and found it locked. Unsurprising.

Rather than bother with an attempt at deception to get Yugo to open the door, Kizu pressed his hand against the wood and phased it through the sturdy wood.

His right arm up to his bicep disappeared into the building. A moment of fumbling about, and the lock opened with a click. Someone cried out from inside the gazebo, likely spotting the seemingly disembodied arm reaching through the door.

Withdrawing his arm, Kizu swung the door open and found himself face to face not only with Yugo, but another vaguely familiar Tainted student who clung to him.

Both of whom were in various states of disrobing.

He stared at them. They stared at him.

Then Yugo glanced over at his discarded uniform pants. But Kizu yanked it towards himself with a swift snap of space.

The pants flew through the air and Kizu snatched them as they got close. A quick search of the pockets, and Kizu found not only Yugo’s token, but also that of the half-dressed Tainted girl in front of him.

“That’s…that’s mine!” the girl cried out. Her eyes widened as she met Yugo’s gaze, then her expression twisted into one of fury and she pushed him away. “You ass! That’s where it disappeared to yesterday?”

“Here,” Kizu flipped the token in her direction. “Um. Choose your date better in the future?”

“Don’t tell anyone,” Yugo hurriedly said to Kizu. “I don’t care about the token. I can participate in a few weekend matches to make up for the fall in rankings. Just don’t say any—”

“Oh, you need not worry about Kaga Kizu,” the girl said darkly. She smiled, showing her teeth. “You’ve got an entirely different beast to worry about.”

Then Kizu finally placed the girl’s face. It was one of Emilia’s friends. And, knowing Emilia, she wouldn’t be one to waste her time with friendship to someone not able to advance her position politically. Of course, that was all before the Dragon invasion of Edgeland. Regardless, this girl likely had sharp metaphorical teeth to match her physical ones.

Rather than delve into the political nuances of both the request from Yugo and the threat from the Tainted girl, Kizu decided to take his spoils and depart before something interrupted his victory.

Kizu tossed the pants back to Yugo who flumbled for them, distracted by his date’s sudden onslaught of vicious words. The girl was not letting up as she detailed exactly what she’d have done to him after this betrayal. Lacking the context, it largely went over Kizu’s head, something about an isolation of power and political scandal due to an arranged marriage.

“Wish you the best of luck,” Kizu told both of them. “See you later.”

And with those parting words, Kizu jumped to the academy beacon.
