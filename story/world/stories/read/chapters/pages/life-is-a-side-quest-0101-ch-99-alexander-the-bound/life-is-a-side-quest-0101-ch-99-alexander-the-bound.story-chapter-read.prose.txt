The forest thinned and opened up to long stands of woven moss that all went in the same direction. Alexander activated his glasses as they grew closer. The moss lit up into pulsing lines that moved along the interwoven strands. He looked behind him and could see that the pulsing lines of magic went far behind them as well, as if the moss was pointing toward one central position.

“There is likely something at the center of this that we are heading toward,” Alexander pointed out to Emily.

“I hear it. There is a pleasant humming of power. I feel… drawn to it.”

“Let’s be on guard as we move forward.”

They moved through the moss slowly. The gnome had no trouble navigating it and even had to wait for them as they tried to get their footing.

The moss started to move up a hill, the top of which seemed to be a structure of sorts buried in it. Alexander could see the collected magic swirl up and into the odd structure.

As they finally managed to near the center, the ground shook and the entire hill released a sound. Emily grabbed Alexander’s arm to pull him backward. Then the sound resolved into booming words.

“We greet you, ancient one.” The structure emitted the words in a booming voice that spanned the entire hill as its form lifted out of the moss.

A towering head of roots and wood emerged from the ground. Behind it, wings larger than buildings sprang up, covered in moss and leaves. The twisted form of a dragon appeared in the construct of foliage.

“You, however, we give no welcome to, scourge of the forest.”

The moss around Alexander’s feet spun around his legs and up his chest, lifting him upward and beginning to squeeze the air from his lungs.

“Your type brings nothing but chaos and destruction. I shall have you carry the eons of curses I have built up for your kind.”

“WAIT! Please! Do not harm him!” Emily yelled.

“Ancient one, your words and will are strong, but I am unaffected by your cries for mercy. Yet it has been long since I have heard the voice of the Dunilith. I shall stay my hand for a time, so that we may fellowship.”

Alexander gasped as the pressure weakened and he was able to breathe. As he did, the moss crept up and stuffed his mouth, preventing him from speaking. His arms were wrapped so tightly he couldn’t move them. His mind raced as he looked for a way out of his bonds, but he was thoroughly outmatched.

“If you could be kind to us, we don’t know where we are or who we have offended,” Emily said, putting up a brave front.

“A common story for mortals, though I am surprised to hear you carry this ignorance… I see your lineage, but it is… fainter than I expected.”

“My name is Emily, and this is Alexander. He and I are only looking for a way back to our world. We don’t mean to make enemies—only to get home in peace.”

The face of the dragon froze for a moment, then chuckled.

“You share your name so freely. Your ignorance is great, young Dunilith.” The dragon chuckled even more. “Young Dunilith. That is a title we never thought we would deign to give. First, let me say this: give your name to no one in this land of day and darkness. It merits some submission of your power if given freely.”

“Oh, I wasn’t aware. Thank you for your guidance.” Emily gave a slight bow.

“You are welcome. Your kind and mine are as distant cousins in this land. Though no Dunilith has graced their presence to any within the land’s remaining memory.”

“Um, what happened to them?”

“Their reasons are their own, unknown to us. We are now Queen of this land as a necessity, though we do not choose to wield our authority in any way but to judge.” The monstrous head turned toward Alexander with menace in its eyes. “Your second ignorance is this: you said return to your world, but this is your world.”

“What? Impossible.”

“Long ago, this place of ever day and night and the battles between them were taken and merged to the side of your own. It is but a fraction of what it was. We were not party to this combining—only a leaf flowing in a stream, plucked and added to the world’s weaving.”

“I don’t understand.”

“We are but toys in the hands of great beings. Those who play at gods and suborn us for their own amusements. Your kind may have left because they no longer desired to be a party to this tragic play.”

“I wouldn’t know, but could you find it in your heart to forgive Alexander? He has never abused his power. He is a good man.”

The ground shook as the dragon examined Alexander closely. “There are bonds among bonds on this man. A bond to you, a bond upon a little one, and a bond to an intruder upon the land… Your foolishness knows no bounds. The great magi leave nothing but war and sorrow. You model yourselves as kings and worse. You are the originators of our torment. Your quests for knowledge lack the wisdom to leave the great secrets alone. Planets and galaxies weep under your administrations.”

“Alexander has done nothing but protect me and his friends! He defeated the invaders!”

“Hmm… so many strings tied to this one, including your own. I feel it is not right. You have given to him and he to you… Maybe his power should only be turned to serving you, the Dunilith.”

“I don’t want a servant! I just want a friend,” Emily petitioned.

“Let me see what the little one has to say.” The dragon turned toward the distance to see a small black figure darting from shadow to shadow. It lunged out onto the moss binding Alexander and started clawing and ripping at it unfruitfully.

“Flick!”

The demi-dragon growled as it pulled and spat out small pieces of moss. Seeing its massive opponent, it leapt onto Alexander’s head and growled.

“Little cousin. Do you vouch for this man’s integrity as well? Do you feel he is deserving of your service? You should not allow him to abuse your loyalties so.”

Flick squeaked and chirped ferociously.

“Your crass words will not sway our course,” the dragon rumbled threateningly.

“Alexander asked Flick to protect me!” Emily explained. “Flick was not commanded. He chooses to help Alexander!”

“Mmm.” The dragon rumbled. “Perhaps much has changed. He is a young magi, as are you all. This we find… strange. How are you all as babes in the wilderness? It has been long since a champion has come to reap from my woods.”

“We only just discovered that there was magic and monsters. It has barely been a few months… What champions would have come?”

“Maybe I am wrong? Has our plane been severed and reconnected anew? I know not, but I have not felt it in my slumber…”

Flick squeaked once more and the dragon shook its head. “Maybe we should go to our subject and find what has happened. What mischief has the administrator made for us this time… Magi, what say you in your defense? Speak not your spells or we shall snuff out your light like a candle.”

The moss crept out of Alexander’s mouth and he coughed.

“I… I received my powers almost two months ago. I did not ask for them, but I embraced them when there were threats to my community. I foolishly made a bargain with the widow of the woods, who we thought was the authority here. I beg your forgiveness for the damage I have done.”

“What of your master? What of your training? Is your planet not called Terra?” The dragon seemed agitated. Why was the Queen agitated?

“I had one meeting with a mentor that was a humanoid goat, but he is not on my planet. I received no training, only a notification that I was given powers. As for the name Terra, that was what the Romans called it. It has simply translated to Earth over the years. Why is this surprising?”

The dragon hummed. “You say that it is only in recent months that beings from other planes have invaded, but what of your stories? Are there yet whispers of invasion by other planar beings?”

Alexander looked to Emily. “Only in ancient stories, but every culture has stories of monsters, gods, and dragons.”

“Administrator mischief,” the dragon rumbled.

Alexander’s mind raced as he tried to recall something.

“Who are the administrators?” Emily asked.

The dragon looked to the sky. “There are those that took too much power for themselves and placed upon their own heads crowns to control the universe. They shaped for themselves a great game—like placing bugs in a jar and shaking it to make them fight for their amusements. Those we call administrators. Their hearts are numb and their minds twisted, as they were not made for such power and longevity. I fear for our lands, though we might be grateful for this time of quiet.” The dragon looked at Alexander. “Take the blessed Dunilith and swear to her protection. If any harm befalls her, I shall rip through the barriers of this plane and consume you and your kin.”

Emily looked at Alexander with mixed emotions, and Alexander responded, “I have already decided to protect her and those around me.”

“You do not understand what you have committed to, magi, but we hold you to it nonetheless.” The Queen turned to Emily. “You will find those that recognize you, and they will try to elevate you to rule against your will, just as they have us. Prepare yourself for this. You carry in you the blood of those that established this realm. Use it with cunning and humility.” When she finished, she lowered Alexander to the ground. Flick hopped off his head and made a small bow to the towering dragon.

“Thank you, Your Grace. Does this mean you can help us return home?”

“The portal you entered—we have found it, and we move it here now. Rest easy in this glade. We give you guest right,” the Queen said with a hint of disdain.

Emily walked over and helped Alexander move to a comfortable place to sit in the thick moss. Alexander thanked her before she asked, “Do you feel like you were just pushed out of a plane without a parachute?”
