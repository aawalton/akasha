The Origin Planes are a mystery.

In all of Aeflym, no continent or island could boast of understanding them fully. Nevertheless, the understanding of each continent was unique, shaping not only the lives of each awakened, but also the political and socioeconomic landscape of said continent.

Gordon was no different.

To them, the Origin Planes weren't just realms of magical affinities steeped in chaos, as most of Lutia believed, but living entities that grew and expanded as those they had awakened grew in strength and power.

That understanding was one of the major reasons the sects had been born in the first place. The aim had been simple: power consolidation.

The more powerful the wielders of an affinity became, the more powerful the Origin Plane became, and the larger it expanded, the more awakenings of said affinity could be recorded within the continent.

It was a never-ending loop of growth and power that, if left unchecked, would wreck the delicate balance of magic and power in the continent. The more powerful an Origin Plane became, the weaker its counters became, thus creating a severe need for balance.

Affinities such as Light and Shadow were in near-constant friction. Water and Fire almost always at each other's throats. Life and Death almost wiping each other out multiple times a decade.

Yet, despite the endless wars for balance, many a sect had been wiped out, and the affinity not long after, with no more awakenings of said affinity within the continent. The ceaseless campaigns, therefore, weren't just war for war's sake but a desperate need to pull others down just to remain afloat.

And Aodhán's addition to the storm sect had not just disrupted the balance—it had toppled it. All over the continent, sects were already gathering. In the Serenity Sect, disciples sharpened blades that had been touched too frequently in the last few months. In the Sect of Order, war drums echoed chaotically for the first time in decades. And in the Earth Sect, deep chambers long left untouched were opened once more.

So chaotic had his addition to the sect been that even now, hours after his proclamation, the Origin Plane of storm had yet to stop expanding.

And with the project to help so many in the storm sect gain familiars, that expansion wasn't about to end anytime soon. Even those affinities who weren't counters to the storm would soon begin to feel it.

Action had to be taken.

However, while many of the sects scrambled to put an end to the storm's meteoric expansion, there were a few sects who were scrambling to support it—sects who had a lot to gain from its sudden rise in power.

The Aspects.

All affinities had aspects—other affinities contributing to their makeup. Fire had heat, change, entropy, and light. Serenity had silence, stasis, and negation. But where some affinities only had one or two aspects in their makeup, others, like the storm, had an abundance of aspects, including but not limited to water, wind, sound, lightning, darkness, and entropy.

The abundance of aspects didn't make the storm a higher affinity, but it certainly gave it more allies in any political setting, and these allies, already experiencing the Origin Plane expansion the storm had forced upon them, were more than ready to keep it going for a few centuries at least.

One such ally was the water sect, located deep within the Tidelands of Gordon—a vast lowland basin where three major rivers converged before spilling into a slow, inland sea. And just as the High Elders of Serenity had gathered to discuss a campaign, they too gathered, only for a much different reason.

“We shall send an envoy then.” First Seat Mei Lanyue grinned, her deep blue robes flowing as she took in the twelve High Elders. “The Storm Sect is our ally, and in a time like this, it is important that we rally round them.”

High Elder Hyun-woo smiled. “If only for our own benefit."

"Not ours alone," High Elder Flowing Fist shook his head. "The water sect is one of the most powerful sects in Gordon. Our population is twice that of the Storm Sect. Our alliance is also to their benefit."

The First Seat shook her head. "Whether they benefit or not doesn't matter. What matters is that we do, and we do our best to facilitate it." She smiled, and in the dim light of the chamber, surrounded by the sound of rushing water from the rivers below, her smile looked almost predatory. "If this plays out well, our numbers might as well double by the next decade, and perhaps we might then be able to wipe out our enemies once and for all."

Gazes hardened, and the decision was made.

There was no dramatic debate, no raised voices or clashing wills. The Water Sect had survived long enough to recognize opportunity when it surfaced, and this—this was a tide worth riding.

Far beyond the Tidelands, similar scenes were unfolding.

In quiet halls and chaos-drenched sanctums, sects aligned, sending envoys with their decisions to the storm sect—not out of love or any particular feeling of gratitude, but for the express sake of keeping the storm sect on the rise.

As long as the Origin Plane of the storm continued expanding, it would drag theirs along, forcing them to expand in tandem. There was no reason not to facilitate that. In fact, they would be doing themselves a disservice not to facilitate it.

The expansion of an Origin Plane was a rare thing, and an expansion of this magnitude was even rarer. This was a chance for each aspect to rise alongside the storm, and despite the number of affinities that would no doubt be crushed under the heels of their expansion, the sects barely gave the matter a second thought.

This wasn't a matter of morality but survival.

Balance had never been a universal truth. It was an agreement. One enforced by force. And now, that agreement was fracturing.

Across Gordon, lines were being drawn. Not between good and evil, but between those who sought to pull the storm down and those who sought to ride with it.

And at the center of it all—unaware of the sheer scale of destruction his presence in Gordon would wrought—was Aodhán, his smile wide as he chatted happily with his new friends.

The storm had already begun.

He just didn't know it yet.

Crixis had just made his way into his home in the core sect, eyes wide as he took in the beautiful space. It wasn't large by any standard—a simple two-bedroom bungalow with three other accessory rooms—but it was beautiful, and it had its own little garden.

He had a lot of neighbors, though, and the last word anyone could use to describe the sect was "quiet," but it was certainly quieter than the outer sect with only a couple hundred residents in total.

"I hope you like it," his guide, another core sect disciple—Cyrenna Storm—asked, and Crixis nodded.

"It'll do just fine, Cyrenna. Thank you."

Cyrenna smiled and pulled out a black-striped gold ring from her robes. "This is yours. I trust you know how to make use of it?"

Crixis nodded. His parents had communication rings too. He had always looked forward to getting his own, but now as he accepted the ring, he couldn't help but feel a tinge of disappointment.

No doubt, his parents would be proud of him when he eventually told them. Perhaps he should be proud of himself too. He had made it into the core sect on his first try. That was a feat worthy of admiration.

And yet he wasn't satisfied.

He’d watched Aodhán climb that mountain, watched him overcome limits that should have broken him. And him? He’d retreated at the first sign of a limit.

He may have made it into the core sect like his parents expected, but he had broken no boundaries. Overcome no limits.

His achievement lacked substance.

Cyrenna noticed his expression and chuckled. "Don't sweat it, Crixis. This is the peak for us ordinary folks. Specials are a different breed entirely. We're never making it there no matter how hard we try. If anyone is leaving the core sect, then they must be heading straight to the elder sect."

Crixis grimaced, fully aware of the way the promotional trials worked. "I understand."

Cyrenna smiled. "Good. You're also entitled to a monthly allowance of a thousand sect coins. It's not as much as Specials get, but trust me when I say it's a decent amount."

Crixis chuckled. "Thank you."

She regarded him for a moment, her expression tight as if she had a ton of questions she wanted to ask. She didn't ask them, though, and instead smiled. "I'll take my leave then."

She turned toward the exit without a backward glance. However, just as she reached it, an obsidian-robed man arrived in a gust of wind. Crixis recognized him as the judge who had led Aodhán away from the mountain and immediately bowed. "Elder ThunderWind, have you come to see if I'm settling in well? Because I am." He glanced at Cyrenna. "Core disciple Cyrenna Storm has been very good to me."

Elder ThunderWind chuckled. "Rise, disciples. And no, Crixis. I have not come here to check on you. My presence here is for a different reason entirely." His smile widened. "I'm very glad I caught you before you began settling down because I have a proposal for you."

Crixis exchanged a glance with a confused Cyrenna and frowned. "What is the proposal?"

Elder ThunderWind's expression sobered, and he stepped closer to Crixis, his voice dropping to a whisper. "Special disciple Aodhán Brystion has selected you to join him in his mansion at the special sect. Should you say yes, then you will become his ally, his brother, his closest friend, and more importantly, his shield. You will guard him and protect him with your life. In return, you will enjoy all the benefits of the special sect and get the chance to learn from one of the brightest talents in the entire sect." He smiled. "What do you say, Crixis?"

Crixis exchanged an awed glance with Cyrenna, his eyes wide. He hadn't expected this. But Crixis didn't need to think about it too long before a smile bloomed on his face in excitement.

"Yes, Elder ThunderWind. My answer is yes."

Elder ThunderWind grinned. "Congratulations to you then. You have just become a special sect disciple in all but title. You should be proud."

Crixis glanced at Cyrenna once again, and though he didn't say it, the thought was obvious on his expression.

Ordinary people really could make it into the special sect after all.

It took Elder ThunderWind a while to track down Rhetta and her friends.

After leaving Crixis's core sect apartment, he'd headed down to the outer sect to deliver the same news. However, as one would expect, searching for a single core signature in the outer sect proved to be vastly harder than in the core sect.

He'd found them an hour later—sweating and bleeding as they raced out of a rift far above their power level. Teleporting to them, Elder ThunderWind delivered his news, expecting a swift acceptance of his proposal. After all, no one could say no to such an opportunity.

To move from the core sect to the special sect was an amazing opportunity, but to move from the outer sect to the special sect was a once-in-a-hundred-lifetimes opportunity. He expected jubilation at the least.

Yerxa and Zarion hadn't disappointed him, squealing and screaming as they jumped for joy and hugged each other in excitement. Rhetta, however, had a very different reaction to the news.

Her expression fell, and it almost seemed he'd just delivered devastating news.

"Rhetta?" he questioned in confusion. "Did you understand my proposal?"

"I did," she responded soberly, bringing the excitement of the others crashing down.

"Then what is the problem?" Elder ThunderWind asked.

"I…" She hesitated and shook her head. Had this proposal come yesterday, she would have jumped at it like her friends, but not now. Not after all she'd witnessed at the trial mountain that morning. Not after the oath she'd sworn to herself in that moment as she'd watched Aodhán from below.

Oh, how great it would be to simply skip it all and lounge in the special sect, but Aodhán hadn't attained his current power by skipping steps and receiving handouts. He had accumulated his strength through sheer determination and will—pushing himself past the limit of his advantages, and Rhetta had resolved to do the same.

She would push herself too—forging her own path to glory so that the next time she stood beside him, it would be as an equal, or at least an equal of sorts.

The words formed in her mouth—yes, yes, of course, yes—but she couldn't speak them. Because she'd seen Aodhán's eyes when he reached the summit. Not triumph. Not relief. Just hunger for more.

If she took the easy path now, she'd never see that hunger in her own reflection.

The resolve in her heart tightened once more, and Rhetta shook her head. "I'm sorry. My answer is no."

Elder ThunderWind paused and glanced up at the other two, who were frozen mid-celebration. "What about you two?"

He asked after a moment, and Rhetta looked back. "You can leave me, guys. This is a lifetime opportunity. You don't have to stay behind because of me."

"This is a lifetime opportunity for you too, Rhetta." Yerxa spoke quietly. "We could grow so much stronger."

Rhetta shook her head. "I'm sorry, but my path is different."

"And that is admirable," Elder ThunderWind cut in, his gaze respectful. He glanced back at Yerxa and Zarion. "Will you be doing the same?"

Zarion hesitated, glanced at Yerxa, then slowly nodded. "We'll stay. We won't leave our friend behind. Tell Aodhán we love him and appreciate his offer."

Rhetta's expression sobered. "Guys, please, don't do this."

"It's already done." Yerxa shook her head with a sad smile. "I see that look in your eyes, and I know I can't convince you. And I refuse to leave you. So it's settled." She looked up at Elder ThunderWind. "Our answer is no, Elder ThunderWind."

Elder ThunderWind regarded them for a moment before nodding. "Then I wish you all the best on your path. Know that I respect it and have the highest of hopes for you. If you ever need anything—anything at all—you know how to find me."

Zarion nodded, and Elder ThunderWind smiled. "Storm bless you, disciples. And may it illuminate your hearts with inspirations."

He left an instant later, and Rhetta turned to look at her friends with teary eyes. "You've saddled me with a responsibility, and I won't fail you." She glanced at the rift behind them and exhaled. "We'll either get to the top on our own or die trying. Since you've chosen to stick with me, then our paths have become the same. I fear we'll have to pave it with our sweat and blood."

Yerxa grimaced in disgust. "Ugh, I hate those hideous bodily fluids."

But even as she complained, they headed back into the rift that had almost just killed them. They weren't the only ones, though. All over the sect, disciples pushed themselves past their limits. In training halls, blood pooled on stone floors. In rift entrances, broken parties limped out only to turn back in. In meditation chambers, cultivators sat until their cores burned.

All inspired by one man.

And his dragon.
