The horrible stench of thick, lingering smoke assaulted Eik’s nostrils the moment he stepped through the portal into Gohkamoria.

Unfortunately for the Gohkamorians, their world was more than old enough to support an X-ranked presence. In fact, on a cosmic scale, it didn't really take very long to get to that point. Earth had kind of been forced to adapt more quickly, but that was a different matter.

He had landed on what appeared to be a major pedestrian street in the middle of a large city. For a moment, he almost thought that he was back on one of the those worlds overrun by miasma, the smoke permeated so terribly.

Countless chimneys spouting black trails and the incessant noise of metal striking metal from the buildings all around hinted at the existence of an artisan’s district in the vicinity, but for all Eik knew, this was how the entirety of the Gohkamorian city was set up. Maybe the ugly bastards just liked living in a depression factory like this.

“And you guys,” Eik said as he sauntered forward lazily, hands stuck in his pockets. “You got a problem or something?” At least ten thousand Gohkamorian soldiers were quickly surrounding him on all sides, polished armor and brandished weapons glittering in the sunlight that only barely peeked through the partial clouds and smoke-filled air.

Within seconds, the clangs and rattles that had previously echoed unceasingly all came to a stop as the roofs filled out with more and more Gohkamorians. They were gathering like ants.

“Eik Magnasen!” a loud voice shouted as the new largest Gohkamorian Eik had ever seen elbowed his way past the flocking warriors. “My name is Mukhathar of the Second Gohkamorian Armada, and I am hereby officially informing you that you are illegally trespassing on Gohkamoria without permission. You have been strictly forbidden from setting foot upon our worlds and any land under direct Gohkamorian rule or ownership. I demand that you heed my warning and immediately return from where you came. If you do not, we will be forced to remove you by force.”

The man was a high-grade S-ranker, judging by his aura signature and movements. Probably about as strong as Andihar Dayarunar, if he had to guess.

Eik hadn’t arrived in Rorgherd, the capital city of the Gohkamorian civilization, through any of the official fractures. Given his permanent and strictly enforced ban on travel to the world, alternative means had been necessary. Luckily, Eik had no shortage of alternatives.

Eik’s Great Group of Guys had three A-ranked fracture specialists on retainer, so he hadn’t even had to hire outside help.

Professionals as they were, two of them had even been there before and had plenty of appropriate coordinates ready to open a portal for their leader. For his specific purposes, there was no need for subtlety or hiding. As a matter of fact, the more people noticed him, the better.

With a hanging lower lip and an extended jaw, Eik gave the man the stink eye as he wandered up to look up and into his eyes. For a couple of seconds, he held his gaze before pushing past him and through the horde of troops.

A wedge of unseen aura in front of Eik kept them at bay. More so than an actual, physical blockade, the aura functioned like a localized gravitational field that would crush them both in spirit and in body if they got too close.

“Get back here!” Mukhathar shouted, a blast of energy from a swing of his blade carving a deep groove in the pavement in front of Eik’s feet. “You do not have permission to move around in our territory! Any movement of yours on Gohkamoria will be considered an act of hostility and reported to the Nidafjeld Alliance as such!”

“Yeah, why don’t you do that, you blubbery bastard?”

“I— What?” the man asked with hesitation.

“I’ll be back home before a report like that becomes anything. And what do you actually think they’re going to do about it, huh? You’ve taken advantage of that as well, haven’t you? If you’re strong enough, you can get your way in this cruel, cruel world. It’s just that, now, the one getting their way is me. Do you see the Alliance here to defend you? That is the exact problem with the Alliance—it is an entirely self-serving entity. And by now, they know well what master hands out the best scraps.” Eik's eyes swirled with ice.

There still hadn’t been actual physical retaliation to his presence yet, but Mukhathar still had a few more things to try. “Do you think we don’t have troops ready to enter Earth as we speak? Your family is there while you’re here. Are you really willing to take that risk?”

In a moment, the entire world seemed to grow heavy as the horizons darkened. Each and every word from Eik’s mouth struck the army of ambushers like sledge hammers. He chuckled. “Any soldiers sent to Earth are already dead. There are a million Profound Toxic beasts in Forest alone. Any Gohkamorian who sets foot there is ripped apart, and should anybody make it to my family, there are over a hundred S-ranked summons waiting for them, as well as another very grumpy X-ranker with a short fuse.”

Hushed whispers erupted among the warriors. Mukhathar tried but failed to hide his nerves as he swallowed hard. “What… What are your intentions in coming here?”

For such a warlike people, always eager to start something with those weaker than them, they were being surprisingly timorous in the face of an apex predator. On the other hand, Eik was finding himself struggling to keep his blood thirst under control.

Each inhalation delivered more than just that filthy stench of black smoke, the almost palpable fear in the air provoking the primordial beast inside him. If his restraint lapsed even just for the moment it took to blink one’s eye, it could become a genocide.

He spoke through gritted teeth and a flexed jaw. “I come in peace… for now,” he told them ominously, his aura fluctuating violently in emphasis of each word. “I have simply come to deliver a message to your people. I assume you know of the demands I made yesterday.”

“I am aware,” Mukhathar hissed. “You made your point yesterday, so I don’t understand what you could hope to gain by coming here, except to scare us.”

Eik smirked. “Well, I can’t deny that that is a welcome bonus, but I’m just here to make sure that all of your people got the message.”

“You think we would withhold information from our own citizens?”

Eik arched a brow and snorted a derisive laugh of incredulity. “Of course I do. The Gohkamorian elite is a bunch of disgusting, greedy, cowardly degenerates and I wouldn’t trust them to burn their used toilet paper to keep their own children warm.”

“Why, you pes—”

“Anyway,” Eik interrupted. “Don’t bother me, all right? Then I’ll just go home afterward. If you really want to make a big deal out of this, then you will be the first to die,” he warned, stabbing a finger at the S-ranker. “Capiche, Mortadella, or whatever the hell your name was?”

The enormous man looked ready to explode with frustration as he ground his teeth, staring at Eik with eyes that just wished he had the power to fight back. But reality wasn’t always what you wished it was. “We will be keeping a close eye on you!” he snarled.

“Whatever, loser,” Eik muttered and sauntered off. As the soldiers parted before him like a school of krill in front of a shark, the Worldbreaker hocked a mean loogie onto the street. “This town sucks ass anyway. I want to go home.”

He continued through the city for a good fifteen minutes before arriving at a large plaza. At least a couple of hundred meters across, it would be perfect for what he wanted to do. Very visible and all that.

At this point, Mukhathar had dismissed about half of the troops that had eventually gathered, leaving only about six thousand, although they were now scattered around him in a considerably larger area than before. As far as he had noticed, Eik hadn’t met a single civilian since he got there, so they had likely been herded away by soldiers moving ahead of him.

The buildings were squat and mostly devoid of decorative features. Here and there, there would be the occasional ornamental carving above the doorway of a building or in a particularly large paving stone. It was mostly made to resemble the intimidating visage of a snarling Gohkamorian.

Surrounded by the boring ass, brown and gray architecture, Eik just wanted to get this crap over with so he could back to his kids. Although, to be fair, while the main interior of their buildings appeared to usually be restricted to a single story—an occasional second floor here and there—they did have quite a few tall, thin spires dotting the skyline.

It looked good even if it did feel entirely out of place among the rest of it. A large building dominated the northern edge of the plaza, complete with thick columns of stone supporting a heavy entry gate that took up half of the front facade. By way of its design alone, it looked important.

“Hey, what’s this building?” Eik shouted into the air, not caring who heard and answered.

“You don’t need to know that!” came the answer. Eik could feel their presence. It was Mukhathar.

Eik shrugged with nonchalance. “All right, suit yourselves, I suppose. But I will destroy it if you don’t tell me.”

Silence reigned for a handful of second before Mukhathar cleared his throat. “It’s the seat of the Ministry of Economic Development.”

In that same instant, the seat of the Ministry of Economic Development exploded violently from within, debris tumbling across the plaza and tearing up the pavement like butter.

Just two seconds after Mukhathar had given in and offered Eik the answer he wanted, the massive structure was nothing but crumbled, smoking ruin.

Eik picked his nose and flicked it away. “It was blocking my view. Thank the gods, it was important.”

The rustling noise of subordinates trying to hold back their furiously indignant leader from running head first into his own death reached Eik’s ears. And he could keep a smirk from crossing his lips.

Standing there, in the heart of the enemy’s territory, Eik raised an arm toward the sky without another word and without a single worry for his safety. With his index finger, he pointed upward as a small but terrifyingly bright droplet of ultra potent Profound Toxin manifested at the tip. It glowed with such ferocious luminescence that one could do nothing but be in awe.

“Apocalypse Canvas,” he muttered under his breath and unleashed the droplet, sending it rocketing into the light cloud cover. A second later, the sky erupted into a glittering sheet of azure that spread and spread, ever outward, until it covered the sky entirely from horizon to horizon.

Ominously, it wobbled in places, as if something enormous undulated beneath the inverse surface.

At a snap of Eik’s fingers, titanic letters appeared beneath the blanket. Nearly anybody would be able to read the message spelled out across the sky in Alliance common.

Dear citizens of Gohkamoria,

It’s not you. It’s me.

No, that’s not right. It’s not me, but it’s definitely some of you.

For years, my world—Earth—was assaulted, courtesy of certain individuals among you. Those sitting comfortably on the top.

The time has come for retribution.

But, unlike them, I am not an indiscriminate murderer, and I’m willing to give you a chance. Deliver to me those responsible. All I need are identities. I’ll take care of the rest.

Fail to live up to this, and the scope of my wrath will broaden.

Conceal a guilty party and I’ll know.

Frame an innocent Gohkamorian and I’ll know.

Families of the guilty will be spared.

Do as I request and the rest of you will survive to rebuild.

Think carefully. This is your chance to live.

Take it, or die.
