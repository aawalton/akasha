The rest of the reception was eventful with plenty of dancing, conversation, and good cheer. It was also, however, exhausting. Even for two Summoned whose stats prevented them from getting physically tired as easily as regular people, the mental toll was fairly hefty. In a good way, of course. Trevor and Adabelle would look back at this day with fondness, even the embarrassing parts.

It wasn’t until the sun started to dip below the horizon and lanterns were turned on that the guests began to leave. There were plenty of hands left to clean up, and they worked on that as the newlyweds were told to sit down and let it happen. Both were the types to help out if there was a need, but Klarkartar and Griffin were there to keep them busy.

“I’ve been all over the world,” the merchant prince boasted with a grin as he sat across from Trevor and Adabelle. “Whether I’m setting up roots myself or hiring some local talent to do it for me, I’ve got stores in almost every major city on the continent, and even traveled beyond that. That’s why your first destination is… drum roll please?”

Griffin, who was beside him, daintily struck the table with her fingers in a slow pattern. If this bothered Klar, he didn’t show it. He was too excited.

“Todarro!” the minotaur announced, the coins hanging from his horns clinking together as he raised his arms.

Trevor and Adabelle politely clapped. He had already told her this, but it seemed as though Klar didn’t remember spilling the beans during the courier’s bachelor party. It was a harmless secret, though, and they played along so he wouldn't get in trouble with Griffin.

“Wow, that’s quite far from Tosa,” the demonkin remarked. “What’s in Todarro that’s so special it has to be our first destination?”

“I have a little getaway at the edge of the country,” Klar said proudly. “Nothing too extravagant, but it’s yours for two days and, let me tell you, the view is to die for. It’s situated not just on a cliff, but in it, and it overlooks almost the entire Wildlands. If you have binoculars, which there will be, you could even see the cliffs on the other side.”

Trevor nodded appreciatively at that detail. He hadn’t known about it but it certainly sounded impressive.

“We figured you two would want some time for yourself,” Griffin explained, her voice the polar opposite of Klar’s effortless volume. “Lai and I both remember what it was like after our wedding, and it was smaller than this one. Todarro will give you time to rest and recuperate before heading off to your next big destination.”

“Which is?” Trevor probed.

“Paladona,” Klar answered immediately.

“Which was supposed to be another surprise,” Griffin said pointedly.

“I know, but to not tell Trevor would be doing a disservice as his best man,” the merchant prince replied, putting a hand over his heart. “He had been so clearly reluctant to visit a spirit dungeon, even if we believed he would be able to use his Power to great effect, and I would be remiss not to warn him so he’s prepared.”

“Adabelle and I talked it over quite thoroughly,” the courier said. “I still have some misgivings—because ghosts, obviously—but I trust her when she says that it’ll be like running the earth elemental dungeon again. Just, a little more spooky.”

“We promise that you’ll be fine,” Griffin assured. “Ghosts are much less scary in this world because they actually exist here.”

Trevor tilted his head in confusion. “Not sure I follow the logic.”

“What I mean is, they weren’t real where you came from. Sure, there were stories, urban legends, and everything in between, but they didn’t actually exist outside of the imagination. Most Earths don’t have the kind of power required for actual manifestation,” she explained. “Here, they’re basically a solved equation because we have the tools, means, and knowledge to figure things out as well as known phenomena to study. Also, you’re going to be accompanied by Adabelle. Her blade is enchanted to strike incorporeal foes.”

“So, you’ve basically scienced the scary out of ghosts?” he asked, arching an eyebrow.

“That’s right,” Adabelle said, patting his hand affectionately. “That’s also why I’m not worried about their plan for you to take the ghost’s armor, which they’ll be bonded to if I remember correctly.”

“You do, Lady Adabelle,” Klar confirmed with a nod. “Injuring the armor is enough to cause the ghosts to flee, so taking it will have the same effect. They follow a very strict set of rules, you see. Very well documented by the adventurers of Paladona. In fact, I’ll make sure a memo goes out about getting you some purification salt. Clay can absorb it into his body and act as another line of defense.”

“Oh, they’ll probably really like that, actually,” Trevor said. There were lots of stories about ghosts and salt back on his world, and it was a cultural bridge he could latch on to. “Adabelle wants to go, anyway, so I’ll enjoy being in her care.”

“Thank you, Trevor.” Adabelle squeezed his hand with a smile. “I really am excited to go adventuring with you. Lord Gabin talked you up quite a bit, and I’m ready to see you in action.”

“Right, cool. Thanks, Lord Gabin of Vilderchtif,” the courier said with a self-deprecating chuckle. “I’m looking forward to it, too. You’ll likely be a lot more proactive than Wayne.”

“It’s not like it’s all dungeon, all the time, either,” Klar stated sagely as he rapped his knuckles on the table. “The Magrovan Peninsula is a wonderful place surrounded by ocean. Its temperature remains relatively warm all year round, so its beaches are always open. The people are friendly, and you’ll be there during the Fustavele de la Cruon.”

“That sounds exciting, what is it?” Adabelle asked.

“It’s a five night festival that gives thanks to a local sea goddess, Cruon,” the minotaur explained. “You’ll be there for the last three since it actually starts tonight, but you’ll have a good time. I promise. You’re going to adore it.”

“I believe you,” Trevor said, more excited about this than the dungeon. He had been to the Festival of Golden Lights twice now and it never failed to delight. If this was even half of that, he knew he’d have a good time.

“And what comes after Paladona?” Adabelle asked, leaning forward. “There is another dungeon as well, right? I distinctly half-remember something about the Froslan Peaks.”

“To be announced later,” Griffin said with a cryptic smile. When her sword sister pouted, she shook her head. While her face returned to a more stoic expression, her three fox tails swished happily. She was clearly very excited. “We want you to focus on one thing at a time, and relax in the moment. You can look forward to things without knowing what they’re about, but this is your honeymoon. Take your time. Enjoy each other. It won’t last as long as you think it will.”

“That’s good advice, Griffin, thank you,” Trevor replied, putting his arm around Adabelle’s shoulder.

“You’re welcome,” the kitsune replied before turning to Klar. “It should almost be time, right?”

The minotaur retrieved a polished silver pocket watch from inside of his suit and opened it. Trevor smiled. That had been his gift to both Klar and Thimbleden for agreeing to join him in the wedding party. It was good that it was already useful.

After a moment of consideration, the merchant prince nodded. “Yes, she should be arriving in three… two… one.”

Klar pointed towards the gazebo when he finished counting, and a human woman appeared at its center. Her attire reminded Trevor of a butler from the black suit and white gloves, though she did have a vibrant green ribbon tied to her belt. The newcomer patted herself down as if to make sure she didn’t forget anything, lingering on that last accessory. It almost seemed like she was still getting used to the clothes.

She had short hair that ranged from orange in the front to green in the back, and was incredibly slender for her height. The expression she wore as she looked around was one of wonder. Her eyes, which were light blue with star-like pupils, looked around before coming to a stop on Klar.

“Boss!” she yelled, her voice having the ghost of a British accent to it, as she rushed down the gazebo stairs. The woman, who no longer seemed completely human to Trevor, skidded to a stop in front of the minotaur and bowed. “Asalorana, reporting for duty, boss!”

Klar stood up, and the others followed suit. “Lora, I’m glad you made it on time.”

“I couldn’t be late today of all days, especially since— Oh! Your dress is so pretty!” the woman exclaimed as she caught sight of Adabelle. She moved to the table, putting her hands on it as she leaned over to get a better look. “The lace is exquisite, and the mermaid cut really looks good on you. White instead of ivory was a good choice, and I absolutely adore your horn band. Those scales are so shiny! And that green on your vest, Mr. Groom, is quite dashing. Really nice against the black suit.”

“Thank you. I like your eyes, they're very pretty,” the bride said as she reached up and gently brushed the ribbon on her horn. The bell attached to it jingled as she did, and Lora beamed at the praise. “Our good friend Corrin made both my dress and Trevor’s suit. I’ll be sure to pass on your enthusiastic praise.”

“Definitely, yes,” Lora replied with a nod. “I’m Asalorana, but you two can call me Lora. I’ll be your transportation for the next however long this is going to take. But, don’t worry! I have a calendar, so even if I don’t remember, I will definitely be there for you. I swear on the boss’ name.”

“We appreciate that, Lora,” Trevor said, giving Klar and Griffin a quick glance. They both nodded. With a smile, he bowed to the woman. “I’m Trevor, and this is my wife, Adabelle. Thank you for agreeing to do this.”

“It’s no problem, and it’s good to meet you!” Lora greeted with earnest enthusiasm. She reached up without looking and patted the minotaur’s chest. “Honestly, I’m just glad that I get to use some of these destinations the big guy’s sent me to. I will be out of your hair most of the time, though. We go, I leave, you get up to couples stuff, and then I meet back up with you when it’s time to go. Promise. Though, you may see me out and about. Some of these places sound really fun, like—”

“Lora,” Klar interrupted. The woman paused for a split second.

“—Nowhere in particular,” she recovered smoothly. “Boring trip, honestly. You can definitely do better. But not really. That’s a lie that I absolutely can’t keep up. You’re both going to have so much fun!”

The strange woman’s enthusiasm was infectious, and Trevor found himself starting to become more excited. “I take it it’s time to leave, then?”

“I won’t presume to tell the bride and groom when they can leave their own reception,” Klar said with a chuckle. “Lora is here when you feel like it.”

“That’s right!” she agreed, nodding hard enough that her hair shook and revealed more colors underneath the outer layer.

Trevor and Adabelle gave one another a questioning glance. He took her hand and squeezed it, while her tail wrapped around his wrist. She tapped him gently with the spade at the end.

“I do believe we’ll take you up on that,” the noblewoman replied with a smile. “Once we’ve said our goodbyes, of course. Most of our guests had already left, but Rashie would never let us hear the end of it if we left without telling her.”

“No rush, I’ll be here when you get back,” Lora said, giving them two thumbs up.

Hand in hand, the newlyweds left the odd trio behind as they walked to the gifts table. Most of the cleanup was already done, and Wayne had folded up the entire dance floor and left with it. He had been one of the first to say goodbye, so they weren’t expecting him to be around.

Adabelle waved Rashie and Lailah over, giving them both a tight hug as Trevor added the gifts they were taking with them to his inventory. Clay jumped onto the table and asked to get picked up. Their master obliged, setting them on his shoulder.

“Thank you both for today,” he said as he walked around and joined his wife. “Especially you, Lailah. This was a great wedding, everything I could have hoped for.”

“It was nothing, messenger boy,” the Scottish elf replied, brushing it off. “It was fun. I enjoyed myself, that’s why it came out so well.”

“She’s being humble,” Trevor said, leaning towards Adabelle. “Should we be worried?”

“I think so,” the demonkin answered, matching her husband. “But I think she’s just tired. I think she needs to crash into Griffin’s fluffy tails for a while.”

“Oi,” Lailah warned as she crossed her arms. “I’ll have you know that… sounds pretty good, actually. This was great, but you’re right that I’m beat. You two are probably feeling the same way, am I right?”

“I’m definitely feeling it,” Trevor chuckled. “As nice as today was, what I need right now is to get this suit off, put my feet up, and take a load off.”

“And I will have to agree,” Adabelle said.

Rashie reached up and tugged on the courier’s sleeve. “Uncle Trevor?”

“Yes, my darling niece?”

“You’re not peeking at the gifts, right?” she asked, looking both worried and serious.

“I wouldn’t dream of it,” he promised as he pulled her into a hug.

That put a smile on her face. “Good. Then do you think you’ll open them tonight? Or tomorrow?”

Trevor looked at Adabelle since he had no preference. “It’s most likely we’ll open them tomorrow, shark,” she answered.

“Okay, good,” Rashie said with a nod. “Just remember to use mine right away, okay? I had to pay a lot of money for it.”

“You didn’t spend too much, did you?” the courier asked. He remembered what she had said back on the Floating Isles about money, but he wanted to make sure.

“Nope. It was in the budget dad set for me,” Rashie answered before pouting. “I wanted to spend more, but I gotta learn fiscal responsibility. Spending money is way more fun, but I gotta.”

“You do gotta, shark,” he replied with a laugh. “We’re going to take off, then. Make sure to keep Tosa safe for us, okay?”

“Can do,” the girl replied, giving them both a tight hug. Lailah was dragged into it as well, not that she complained.

They did the rounds to thank those who had come one last time before returning to Lora, who squealed at the sight of Clay.

“Oh, aren’t they adorable!” the Summoned preened, reaching their hand out. Clay hesitated at the bubbly energy before allowing her to pet them. “I especially like the sapphire eyes, but the gold whiskers are a nice touch, too. Unfortunately, you’ll have to ditch them for the time being. Sorry, small friend.”

The familiar looked at Trevor, who nodded. They jumped into his hands, and the stone husk that made them look like a stoat disappeared into his inventory. That left the courier with a black marble, which he held onto tightly.

Both he and Adabelle had been warned that Lora had some heavy limitations, and she couldn’t take too much in the way of magical items. It was the biggest obstacle for using the Summoned as anything other than personal transport since she had a size limit and couldn’t take extradimensional bags. Clay’s husk was just over the threshold. The sphere was their actual body, though, so that was fair game.

And Trevor was a Summoned with the Power of Inventory. They had done extensive testing with Lailah, Griffin, and others to make sure that there wouldn’t be any problems moving people who could perform magic. The limitations may have been heavy, but there were ways around it.

With one last wave goodbye, Lora put her hands on Trevor and Adabelle’s shoulders, scrunched her face up tight with concentration, and then they were gone on their honeymoon.
