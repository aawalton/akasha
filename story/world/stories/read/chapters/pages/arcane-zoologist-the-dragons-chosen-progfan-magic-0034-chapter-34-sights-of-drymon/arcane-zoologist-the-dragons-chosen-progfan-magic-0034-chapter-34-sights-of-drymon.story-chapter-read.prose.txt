The journey to Drymon turned out to be far longer than Callie had thought. Or perhaps the rest of the way felt long because the cheerful atmosphere from before was completely gone. Mom looked mad, likely because Callie approached the thunderfoxes carelessly.

Callie couldn't help worrying about the little kits. They were so scared, yet now they have been captured by these horrible elite hunters and taken to heaven knows where. To be sold as fur? Eaten as a delicacy by the dragonbloods? Or perhaps even worse.

Her heart ached for the little creatures, so she couldn't bring herself to join into the conversation. Callen and Silas both tried to keep the atmosphere light, but when their attempts to involve Callie or Mom into the conversation were returned with a brisk nod or a dismissive shrug, they stopped.

For the longest while, the silence in the carriage was so swiftling that Callie began to count the number of times Gale's hooves clattered against the ground. The sound was so repetitive that Callie found herself drowsing.

"Callie, wake up! We're here," Callen gently shook her shoulder.

She hadn't even noticed falling asleep or that she had slumped against her brother, who had been acting like a warm and comfortable cushion. Rubbing the sleep out of her eyes, Callie peered out of the wagon.

"Welcome to Drymon," Silas announced.

Callie's mouth fell open. She knew that Drymon was an actual town and not a village like Greyfen. She knew it was supposed to be bigger, but she never quite imagined how big it was.

Unlike their little village, where houses stood a good distance from each other and there was only one road stretching roughly through the middle of the village, here the houses were very close to each other. Not to mention that they were much bigger.

Most houses had two stories, just like the chief's house and they had solid rock foundations. They looked… sturdier than the simple village houses of Greyfen. There were roads weaving every which way and before long, their wagon got onto the solid stone road which seemed to stretch through the center of town. Gale's hooves clattered so loudly, but at the same time, the noise was satisfying.

"Wow, the road is solid stone, not just dirt," Callie exclaimed in awe.

"Yeah, it makes it easier for wagons to move. It's not shaking as much anymore, is it?" Silas noted.

Callie nodded. While the shaking really hadn't bothered her that much before since the cushion within the wagon was nice and soft, she instantly noticed a difference when they got onto the stone road. It was still a little shaky, but Callie didn't have to fear falling over anymore.

"Look. Over there is the town hall and the building next to it is the library," Silas pointed.

Callie's jaw dropped. The building was huge! It had not one, not two but three stories and all the walls were made out of stone. The library building next to the town hall was two stories high and not quite as big, but its walls were also made of stone.

"It must have taken forever to build a house out of stone," Callie marveled.

"Wait until you see an actual castle. It's at least a hundred times bigger than this place," Silas noted.

"You're exaggerating," Callen scoffed.

"Not even a little! I think I might be actually underestimating it," the merchant chuckled.

"No way something that big could exist!" Callie protested.

Callen shook his head. "I'll only believe it once I see it."

"I suppose you have no choice but to become a travelling merchant and check for yourself," Silas noted.

The air in the wagon seemed to grow heavy as Mom peered at Callen. Callie couldn't tell what she was thinking. Was she mad? Or sad? Perhaps both?

But she didn't have time to worry about that. Instead she was greeted with an array of colors she had never seen before. No, not just colors, but smells and sounds too.

The street got increasingly busier, more and more people appearing seemingly out of nowhere, to the point that the wagon was barely able to move forward. But Callie wasn't sad about it. Instead, she stared into the street, admiring the various stalls that lined up on both sides.

They were similar to the stalls that merchants set up in Greyfen, but there were hundreds of them, and each seemed to be selling something different. Fabrics in colors Callie had never even seen before, fruits of all shapes and sizes, delicate but beautiful dishware that looked like it would break at the first gust of wind. Callie felt herself growing dizzy with all the variety available.

Although her mind did occasionally wander back to thunderfox kits. Even all the new things that she was seeing weren't quite enough to push the encounter from her mind.

In fact, when they passed by some stalls which had animal furs put on display, Callie's worry only deepened. Would this happen to the little kits? She wished so desperately to do something to help, but by now the rangers were long gone. Even if they weren't, what could Callie do as a child?

"This is the marketplace," Callen said, excitement clear on his face. "It's amazing, isn't it?"

"Wh-Where do all those things come from? It's amazing how much there is."

"Despite being a small town, Drymon is the central trade hub in the north. As such, the marketplace is quite impressive and it can have places such small towns normally wouldn't have. Like the library for example, and a large inn where we are heading now."

"An inn?" Callie questioned. She had heard of these places, where supposedly a lot of people stayed during the night while travelling, but she could never quite picture one.

However, once they got through the dizzying variety of the marketplace, the wagon stopped before a building even larger than town hall. At least in width. It had two stories, but it was so wide, Callie could barely see the end of it. It had a solid rock foundation, and the corners also seemed to be held up by solid stone beams. How did they even get those here? They must have been incredibly heavy.

"Okay, here we are. I'm going to go and set down the wagon and put the horse into the stables. Feel free to go ahead and get yourself rooms in the inn," Silas said.

Callie, Callen and Mom all climbed out of the wagon. Callie stared at everything around her. She tapped her feet against the stone road, feeling its solidity. She looked at the buildings around her, especially the inn.

Everything was so… overwhelming. There were also so many people passing by, and they were all strangers. In the village, she knew everyone. But seeing all these unknown people scared Callie, so she scuttled closer to her brother.

"Don't worry. It's going to be fun," Callen said. "Let's head inside. I can show you how to book a room."

"Don't make fun of your mom. You really think I've never been in an inn before?" Mom smiled mischievously. "I've seen far more of the world than you could even imagine, young man."

Mom took the lead, so they followed after her into the inn. Callie didn't even know what to look at. The bright red carpet at the entrance, the beautiful wooden counter, the comfortable looking furniture where some people were lounging about and chatting with each other. Or perhaps the delicious smells coming from the adjacent room.

That first evening in the inn had felt world-changing for Callie. She explored everything she could (with her brother, of course), asked questions about things she'd never seen before, wondered at food that looked completely alien to her. It felt like she hadn't seen anything at all when Mom had already announced that it was time to sleep.

In the morning, after eating the most heavenly breakfast Callie had ever eaten, that is to say waffles, Mom and Callie headed to the library. If it were possible to stay and eat more of the crunchy sweet goodness, Callie would have done that. How can something so delicious exist and be allowed to be eaten for breakfast? Surely food that tasty should be saved for special occasions.

Perhaps Mom had treated this trip as a special occasion, smiling happily as Callie scarfed down the waffles. She had received the library card from Silas over their breakfast table and promised to return it in the afternoon. So they would have until then to explore the library and find books about magic.

Callie's heart hammered in her chest as they approached the grand stone building. Not many people were going there, unlike the market. Getting through was an absolute nightmare. Callie thought she would be trampled. But here it was peaceful and even as they entered the building, there was this deep sense of quiet and peace.

The woman at the counter scrunched up her nose when Mom and Callie walked in. As if they had done something wrong by coming. How rude!

"Do you have a library card? You are not allowed inside without one!" The woman smoothed her immaculate dress and glared at Mom.

Callie didn't like this lady at all. As pretty as she was in that dress, nobody could treat her mom that way. It didn't matter how rich they were.

"We do have a card," Mom replied triumphantly, pulling a little bundle out of her pocket. Removing the cloth, she revealed the library card. It was a small rectangle made out of some very fancy looking wood with a writing "Library Card" in the middle and a little golden coin embedded at the bottom.

The woman skeptically took the card and examined it from all sides, as if not believing it was real. "It looks real… Very well, you may enter, but the child must stay outside."

"Wh-What?" Callie exclaimed.

"Are children not allowed into the library?" Mom narrowed her eyes on the woman.

"A well-behaved child is allowed inside, but I cannot admit a child who doesn't understand the value of books. Besides, each person requires a library card to enter. One card cannot admit two people," she said with a satisfied smirk.

"No…" Callie despaired. She had really been looking forward to this. A place filled with books. Surely it was filled with precious knowledge about magic and arcane beasts alike. Yet she couldn't go.

"One card per person… Callie, you go inside. I'll wait for you," Mom said, nudging Callie forward.

The librarian sputtered, clearly not expecting this. "Excuse me!"

"Callie can read very well, and it was she that wanted to come here and study books, not me. If children are allowed inside as long as they understand the value of books, then I believe my little Callie qualifies without an issue," Mom announced proudly.

"This child can read?" The librarian blinked at Callie, then pulled out a thick looking journal and pointed at the cover. "Read this!"

"Drymon Library Admittance Records," Callie read without an issue.

"I… My apologies," the librarian's expression softened considerably. "I never would have thought that with your appearance… I guess I shouldn't have judged the book by its cover."

"Is it alright for me to just leave her here in your care?" Mom asked. "I'd like to visit a few places in the meanwhile."

Callie looked nervously at Mom. She didn't want to be left alone in this unfamiliar place.

"Yes. As long as she remains quiet and browses books peacefully, I do not have an issue with the child staying here," the librarian said.

"Wonderful! I'll be back to get you in the afternoon," Mom said and left before Callie could object.

"I rarely see children your age that can read. Who taught you?" The librarian asked with a smile.

Callie found it odd that she was suddenly treating her so kindly. Was reading really such a big deal? Well, Callie knew that not even all adults in the village could read, but it wasn't actually such a difficult thing in her opinion.

"Granny Ethel, our village healer, taught me how to read. I'm her apprentice," Callie explained.

"A healer. A truly noble vocation. Healers are always needed. Did you come here to learn more about healing?" The librarian said, and motioned Callie to follow deeper into the library.

"I…I suppose. I wanted to see what kind of books you had and what I could learn from them," Callie muttered nervously.

"I believe you'll find quite a few topics of interest here," the librarian had a mischievous spark in her eyes as she pushed open the large door.

Callie's breath hitched. Beyond it was a huge room, larger than any Callie had ever seen before. In the middle of the room there were rows of tables and chairs, likely meant for people to sit down and read.

But all around those tables were countless shelves and each one was filled to the brim with books. Callie couldn't believe her eyes. The most books she had seen at one given time could be counted on a single hand. But here there were hundreds, no, likely thousands of books.

"Wow…" Callie stared.

"I believe the afternoon will come all too soon for you," the librarian chuckled.
