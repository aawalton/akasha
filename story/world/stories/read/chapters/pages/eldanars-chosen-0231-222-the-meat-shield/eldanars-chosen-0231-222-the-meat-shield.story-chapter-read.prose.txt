The dark stain cresting the distant northern hill did not stop. It continued to pour over the horizon, rank after endless rank, until the entire rolling landscape was painted in sickly shades of rusted iron and rotting bone.

The visual impact was staggering. It defied comprehension, an endless sea that the eye simply could not process. The suffocating, oily stench of stagnant grave water, decaying flesh, and old copper rolled across the open plains, hitting the defenders on the timber walls like a physical blow.

Thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights formed the vanguard. They marched shoulder to shoulder in perfect, terrifying lockstep, their rusted halberds held upright, creating a dense, moving forest of deadly steel points. The tattered, rotting banners of long-forgotten, fallen kingdoms hung limply from their spears, offering a silent testament to the ancient horrors that had been raised from the dark.

Behind the heavy vanguard came the endless, shuffling ranks of the lesser infantry. This was a chaotic, writhing mass of decaying flesh. Crypt Ghouls and rotting zombies dragged their feet through the freezing mud, their bodies a bruised, purple mass of decay. Their movements were entirely devoid of the sharp military precision of the skeletons, lurching and swaying as they followed the relentless pace of the vanguard.

And towering high above the sea of lesser infantry were the heavy siege assets. Two dozen massive Bone Goliaths, constructed of hundreds of fused skeletons, lumbered forward like moving siege towers. The sheer weight of their iron-plated boots shook the frozen earth.

The horde poured over the hill like a slow, dark tidal wave of rot. The stream of monsters simply never ended. It continued to flow, entirely covering the frost-covered road and spilling out into the open plains, completely swallowing the natural landscape.

A profound, suffocating panic crashed over the entire length of the Ashenfall timber walls. The sheer scale of the extinction event marching towards them shattered the minds of the defenders. The psychological weight of seeing ten thousand monsters in the pale morning light was entirely different from simply hearing their marching boots in the dark.

"Light preserve us," a seasoned guard captain whispered, his face draining of all colour as he stared at the endless sea of bone.

Further down the ramparts, the fragile discipline of the conscripted militia finally broke. A group of young merchants and terrified farm boys dropped their crude spears. They turned and attempted to bolt for the steep wooden stairs leading back down into the city, their faces twisted in masks of pure, unadulterated terror.

"Hold the line!" Torin roared, stepping directly into their path. The heavily scarred veteran swung his iron mace, smashing it violently against the wooden handrail of the stairs to block their escape. "If you run now, you’ll just die tired! The cellars will not save you if these walls fall! Pick up your weapons!"

The panic was not contained to the walls. Word of the horde's size spread like a physical sickness down into the city below. The massive, crowded plazas and winding streets of Ashenfall erupted into a chaotic symphony of wailing and despair. Tens of thousands of trapped refugees, hearing the terrified shouts of the militia on the ramparts, began to scream. The sound drifted up to the defenders on the wall, a heartbreaking, desperate chorus of weeping mothers, terrified children, and praying elders. The noise was a horrific reminder of exactly what was at stake.

Josh tightened his grip on the leather handle of his shield. He felt the familiar, heavy rush of his class anchor deep within his chest. The latent magical energy hummed powerfully beneath his skin, burning away the freezing cold of the morning and the suffocating fear trying to take root in his mind.

He looked at the trembling bakers and blacksmiths standing near him. He needed to project absolute, unbreakable strength.

"They die just like anything else!" Josh bellowed, his deep, gravelly voice carrying a commanding, uncompromising authority that cut cleanly through the wailing of the city. He slammed the flat of his heavy longsword against his steel shield, the deafening metallic clang snapping the attention of the nearby militia. "They are just rotting meat and brittle bone! We hold this wall! We do not take a single step backward!"

Bhel enthusiastically joined the display, smashing his axe against the wooden parapet. "Mountain and stone! Let them come and break their teeth on our iron!"

The display of raw, veteran confidence worked. The terrified militia members slowly stopped backing away. They picked up their dropped spears with trembling hands, drawing a fragile, desperate courage from the towering human and the gruff dwarf standing at the epicentre of the danger.

As the militia settled, Josh turned his storm-grey eyes away from the approaching horde. He looked at Carcan.

The elven healer was standing behind him, her pristine pale green robes billowing in the freezing wind. Her luminous silver eyes were locked on the distant, corrupted treeline, her face a mask of serene, terrified stone.

The heavy, uncompromising oath Josh had sworn to Perberos in the Guildhall echoed loudly in his mind. He was supposed to protect her. He was supposed to ensure she survived, no matter the cost. Standing on the wooden rampart directly above the main gates, at the absolute epicentre of the impending slaughter, was the worst possible place for her to be.

"Carcan," Josh said, his voice dropping into a low, urgent tone meant only for her. He took a heavy step toward her, his armour clanking. "You need to leave the wall right now."

Carcan blinked, tearing her gaze away from the sea of bone to look up at the towering heavy fighter. "I beg your pardon?"

"I need you to fall back to the central temple square," Josh instructed firmly, his expression entirely serious. "Torin said the primary triage centre is set up there. That is where the high-tier clerics are supposed to be. If this gate buckles, this wooden platform is going to become a meat grinder. You are too vulnerable up here."

Carcan’s aristocratic features tightened. The serene composure melted away, replaced instantly by a fierce, brilliant spark of defiance. She gripped her amber-topped staff tighter, planting the wooden base firmly against the floorboards of the rampart.

"I am not going anywhere, Josh," Carcan replied, her melodic voice ringing with absolute, unyielding stubbornness.

"Carcan, please," Josh pressed, stepping closer, desperately wanting to physically shield her from the horrors marching towards them. "I promised your brother..."

"My brother does not command me," Carcan interrupted sharply, her silver eyes flashing. "And neither do you, Josh. I am a healer of the Mother Tree, but I am also a member of this party."

She stepped forward, refusing to be intimidated by his massive, steel-clad bulk.

"Do you remember the Kobold invasion?" Carcan asked, her voice trembling slightly with the suppressed emotion of the memory. "Do you remember where I was?"

Josh frowned, remembering the chaotic, bloody night they had fought through the streets of Ashenfall. "You were locked inside the triage tent with the civilians and injured."

"Yes," Carcan nodded, her eyes shining fiercely. "I was locked in a dark room. I was completely blind to the battle. I could hear the screaming, I could hear the clash of steel, and I had absolutely no idea if you, or Perberos, or Brett, or Bhel were lying dead in an alleyway. The terror of not knowing was the most agonising torture I have ever experienced. I spent hours pacing the floorboards, feeling utterly useless while my friends bled."

She reached out, her slender, elegant fingers gently touching the cold steel of his heavy breastplate.

"I am never doing that again," Carcan vowed, her voice softening into a tone of profound, unbreakable devotion. "I am not hiding in a medical tent waiting for runners to bring me your shattered bodies. I am going to stand right here. I am going to weave the golden thread in the mud and the blood, and I am going to fight beside my friends. If we fall, we fall together."

Josh looked down into her luminous, determined eyes. He felt a massive surge of protective fear, warring instantly with an overwhelming, profound pride. He knew there was absolutely no force in this world that could move her from this spot.

He slowly placed his heavy, gauntleted hand over her delicate fingers resting on his chest.

"Then stay behind my shield," Josh murmured, accepting her choice with a slow, solemn nod. "Do not step past the parapet line."

"I will keep you breathing, Guardian," Carcan promised softly.

Out on the corrupted plains, the undead horde finally reached the invisible boundary of extreme bow range.

The massive, terrifying army did not blindly charge the walls. With chilling, coordinated precision, the entire ten-thousand-strong force ground to a sudden, perfect halt. The deafening thud of their marching boots ceased instantly, leaving a heavy, unnatural silence hanging over the battlefield.

They stood perfectly still in the pale morning light, a massive, unified block of pure slaughter, simply staring at the timber walls of Ashenfall.

The sudden silence was infinitely worse than the marching. It was a psychological weapon, allowing the terrified militia members to truly comprehend the impossible numbers arrayed against them. The wind howled mournfully between the silent ranks of the dead, carrying the nauseating stench of the grave directly into the faces of the defenders.

"Why are they stopping?" a nervous archer asked, his hands shaking so badly his arrow rattled against the wooden frame of his bow.

Brett murmured, his pale eyes darting across the static lines, tracing the flow of the necrotic fog. "Lyra was right. They are completely leash-bound to a central intelligence."

Brett pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, and triggered his enhanced magical vision, sensing the mana within their ranks. He narrowed his eyes, squinting through the ambient dark magic radiating from the horde. His gaze swept past the towering Bone Goliaths and the pristine ranks of the skeletal vanguard. Deep within the rear lines of the horde, surrounded by a dense, protective ring of massive Crypt Weavers, he finally spotted the anomaly.

It was a figure clad entirely in ancient, blackened plate armour, riding atop a massive, rotting skeletal warhorse. The commanding entity radiated an aura of dark magic so dense it physically warped the air around it, creating a swirling vortex of freezing shadows.

"I see the general," Brett announced quietly, his voice tight. "He is keeping his distance, staying well behind his front lines. He knows we have high-tier archers on the ramparts. He is not going to expose himself until the walls are breached."

"Then Perberos has a long walk ahead of him," Carcan whispered sorrowfully, her silver eyes scanning the distant shadows for any sign of her twin brother.

The eerie silence on the battlefield lasted for three agonizing minutes.

Then, the dark general raised a massive, rusted broadsword high into the freezing air.

The order was entirely silent, transmitted through the dark magical tether binding the horde, but the reaction was instantaneous.

The perfect, static formation of the army fractured. The heavy, disciplined ranks of the skeletal knights remained firmly in place, acting as an impenetrable rearguard. The towering Bone Goliaths did not take a single step forward. Instead, the chaotic, writhing mass of lesser infantry was thrust to the front.

Thousands of shambling zombies, Crypt Ghouls, and other disfigured, rotting corpses let out a unified, hollow shriek. They lurched forward, breaking into a clumsy, heavy-footed run, charging directly towards the pitch-filled trenches and the timber walls of the city.

Torin leaned over the parapet a few yards away, a grim scowl crossing his scarred face. "They are sending the trash first."

Brett watched the chaotic, screaming wave of rotting meat rush across the frost. His brilliant, analytical mind rapidly processed the strategic deployment. The enemy commander was purposefully holding back the heavily armoured elites and the siege assets.

"It is a battle of attrition!" Brett shouted, his voice cracking with sudden, urgent realization.

The elemental mage spun away from the parapet, looking down the line of tense, ready casters and archers preparing to unleash their opening volleys. He drew upon his magical reserves, amplifying his voice to cut clearly through the rising panic.

"Do not burn your cores on the fodder!" Brett roared, his words echoing across the wooden ramparts. "They are using the zombies as a meat shield! The general wants us to exhaust our mana and empty our quivers on the trash! Mages, pace yourselves! Conserve your heavy spells for the Goliaths and the knights!"

Caistina Tharistrar, standing further down the wall, heard the young mage's warning. The elven trainer instantly recognized the flawless logic.

"The boy is correct!" Caistina’s magically amplified voice thundered in agreement, carrying absolute authority. "Archers, loose at will! Mages, use standard strikes! Do not overextend your reserves! We bleed them slowly!"

The massive wave of rotting meat rushed across the frost-covered plains. They did not carry ladders or siege towers. They were exactly what Caistina had predicted hours ago: a mindless wave of bodies intended to pile up against the walls until they created a physical ramp of flesh.

"Loose!" a guard captain roared, dropping his red pennant.

The sky above Ashenfall turned black. Thousands of heavy, iron-tipped arrows were released simultaneously from the wooden ramparts. The deadly volley arched gracefully through the pale morning sky, falling like a devastating rain of steel directly into the charging mass of zombies.

The physical impact was brutal. Hundreds of the rotting monsters were pinned to the freezing earth, their bodies pierced by multiple shafts.

The mages on the wall followed suit. They did not unleash apocalyptic firestorms or massive kinetic shockwaves. Instead, they maintained a steady, measured bombardment. Balls of searing fire, jagged bolts of lightning, and sharp shards of conjured ice rained down upon the plains, blowing craters in the charging lines and sending shattered limbs spinning through the freezing air.

Josh watched the slaughter from his position above the main gates, his shield raised slightly against the blowback of the magical explosions.

However, as the dust and smoke from the first measured volley cleared, the terrifying reality of the enemy they were facing became horribly apparent. The magical and physical bombardment had been devastating, but it had yielded a vastly mixed bag of results.

In several craters, the bodies of the destroyed ghouls shimmered brightly for a fraction of a second before dissolving entirely into piles of fine, golden dust. These were true dungeon spawns, creatures born entirely of ambient mana, and they were permanently dead once their magical cores were shattered.

But a terrifying percentage of the horde did not dissolve.

These were natural corpses, twisted and reanimated by the dark magic flooding the plains. Unless their brains were completely destroyed or their bodies were hacked apart, the necrotic energy simply kept animating the ruined meat.

Dozens of the heavily injured zombies simply pushed themselves back up from the freezing mud. They ignored the arrows protruding from their chests. Some were missing entire limbs, yet they continued dragging their shattered, burning bodies forward with relentless, mindless determination.

They simply kept coming.

"It is not enough," Brett muttered, his pale face reflecting the glow of the distant fires. He gripped the wooden rail of the parapet. "It is like throwing a bucket of water at a forest fire. We are barely thinning their ranks, and the front line is untouched."

The sheer numbers of the horde were effortlessly absorbing the punishment. For every zombie that fell permanently, three more scrambled wildly over its corpse to continue the charge. The leading edge of the swarm was rapidly closing the distance to the city walls, their hollow shrieks growing deafeningly loud.

"Ignite the trenches!" Captain Aris screamed from the lower tier, seeing the massive wave of bodies approaching the jagged pit of alchemical pitch.

Brett did not wait for the lower-tier mages to fumble with their fire spells. He stepped right up to the edge of the wooden parapet, pushing his crimson Ember-silk sleeves back.

He closed his eyes, drawing deeply upon his vast, humming mana reserves. The air around him instantly warped with intense, rippling heat. The time for measured conservation was over. He opened his hands, preparing to unleash a massive, continuous torrent of living thermal energy to set the entire defensive perimeter ablaze, fully committing to the brutal, exhausting grind of the siege.

The battle for Ashenfall had truly begun.
