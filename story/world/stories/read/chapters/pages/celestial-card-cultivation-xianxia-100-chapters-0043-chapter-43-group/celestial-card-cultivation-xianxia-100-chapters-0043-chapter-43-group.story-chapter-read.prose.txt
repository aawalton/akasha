Jianyi measured a small cup of rice and rinsed it thoroughly. The elder liked simple dishes, so that’s what he usually made for breakfast.

With practiced movements, he swiped the rice into a pot and used the same measuring cup to add ten times as much water as rice and put it on the stove.

The formations that were spread throughout the compound were a luxury. Not having to carry firewood into the kitchen was one of those.

The water started to boil quickly.

He cleaned a bundle of scallions, removed the ends, and cut them into thin rings.

Minghao entered the kitchen, greeted him, and started to prepare tea for them.

In a small skillet, Jianyi heated some oil and fried the scallions while stirring the rice.

“Some of my best and worst memories are associated with congee,” Minghao said wistfully. “It is what you eat nine out of ten days for breakfast on the frontlines. So many jokes, so much camaraderie—so many friends lost, so much pain to swallow down just like the too-thin rice porridge.”

Minghao was usually upbeat and cheerful, but mornings were the most likely time of the day for him to be in a mood.

Jianyi had found that the best way to deal with it was to smile and listen.

The scallions smelled wonderful, the rice was slowly falling apart, and most of the water had been absorbed or evaporated.

He measured out the congee into three shallow cups, heaped the fried scallions on top, and finished with a few drops of soy sauce.

One of Elder Sun’s favorite dishes, ready to deliver to his room.

After having eaten breakfast himself, he wandered downhill to the outer sect. Despite the fun he had had convincing Jianyi to open his dantians manually, the elder was still preoccupied with whatever the demonic cultists did. Combined with the rather solemn mood that Minghao had displayed this morning, and the nightmares that plagued him again, despite the progress he had made with unlocking his will, things felt a bit… gloomy.

In that context, his decision to participate in the upcoming session seemed even more questionable. He didn’t think he would have agreed if the latest letter his mother had sent him hadn’t been so insistent on making friends. He did not understand why she couldn’t see what a distraction that was.

Anyway, he had made the decision; now he would have to live with it. At least for today. No need to repeat it afterwards.

He entered the training field, the morning sun still low, reflecting on the sect’s blue pagoda roof tiles.

The Liu cousins were waiting for him, and he greeted them with a nod. Jun jumped towards him, clapping him on the back. “This is exciting. Thanks for inviting us.”

“Indeed, many thanks,” Hong added.

“No need to thank me. It was not my idea to train in a group,” Jianyi looked up and saw Shuhua and Miyao coming towards them.

“This is very exciting!” Shuhua said as a greeting.

“See!” Jun clapped him again on the shoulder. “This is what I said as well.”

Introductions were quickly made, then awkward silence; nobody seemed eager to take the lead.

Jianyi squinted at the group. Time was too precious for this. “Shuhua, you …”

“Shushu!” she grinned at him.

“Shuhua, you had the idea for this. What did you want us to do?” Her smile dimmed slightly as he ignored her attempt to get him to use her nickname.

“Well, I don’t know. Miyao and I both had the same experience of training for years alone with an instructor, and while the sessions with Cai Fangyin and the elder are very good, we thought it would be even more fun and help us get an edge if we trained with friends.”

Friends. There was this word again. People did not seem to appreciate how busy he was. He sighed.

“You haven’t started sparring, though, right?”

Miyao, who seemed a lot quieter than Shuhua, shook her head. “No, we are on the fourth form.”

“Oh, we are still on the second form,” Jun said, disappointedly.

“So are we,” Jianyi added, “we are a bit behind because of… whatever.” He did not want to discuss the week of running again. But he did have an idea.

“My teacher actually started training me on the eight movements that make up the five forms of the Fivefold Spear Art. Given that Elder Fang does not teach them, maybe that could be a worthwhile use of our time?”

Miyao nodded. “I was taught based on the movements as well; it should help anybody, independent of which form they are learning.”

“It wasn’t core to what I was taught, but I touched upon it with my instructor; I am open to it,” Shuhua agreed.

The cousins had not explicitly learned about the movements in school and were keen to understand more.

Jianyi looked at Miyao to see whether she wanted to demonstrate, but she waved for him to go first. He rubbed his nose for a moment, thinking about how to convey this best.

“So we all have learned Piercing Fang…” he flowed through the form at normal speed. The others nodded.

“There are eight movements that make up all the forms. For example, Piercing Fang consists of Rise.” He lifted from the ankles upward, straightening his knees as his arms rose in tandem.

“Advance.” He stepped forward, heel first, arms flowing forward.

“Close.” His shoulders and arms moved inward.

“Retreat.” His rear leg moved backwards while his hips stayed square.

He returned to the Rooted Stance automatically and looked up at the others. Shuhua clapped, and the cousins grinned.

“Very impressive, Jianyi,” Hong said with a bow of the head.

Jianyi was slightly flustered and didn’t know how to respond. Instead, he focused on the matter at hand. “The eight movements are…”

After an hour of training Open and Close, they had to end their session as they needed to get ready for classes.

“This was really helpful, thank you for suggesting this session, Shuhua, and thank you for sharing the movements, Jianyi. We are grateful,” Hong said, hand over heart. Jun nodded beside him.

“So, we'll see each other next week?” Miyao asked, looking slightly uncertain.

Before Jianyi could respond, Shuhua confirmed. “Sure. Same time? We can increase the frequency once we get closer to the mid-year assessment!”

Whenever Shuhua got enthusiastic, Jianyi felt as if a caravan wagon was rolling over him. As comfortable as he felt explaining the movements, he did not know how to respond. Instead, he murmured something about chores and time and fled the scene.

In truth, it hadn’t been bad, and helping the others had also helped him think even more consciously about certain movements. So, it hadn't been a complete waste of time. He would have to see how things turned out next week, given that he would visit the tailor later that day to see whether the offer to help out there was still on the table.

Tian Ruo, the old tailor who had invited Jianyi to return and potentially work with them, smiled as he entered the workshop.

“Welcome back. How can I help?”

“Senior Tian, you kindly offered that I might be able to learn and work here, and I wanted to see whether that offer was still on the table? The only caveat is that beyond my studies, I already have two jobs, washing dishes in the kitchen and as a servant for my teacher, Elder Sun.”

“Oh, Elder Sun. A legendary figure,” the tailor said, eyes wide.

“Do you know him?

“Know him?” the tailor laughed. “Certainly not. Know of him? Yes! I assume some of it might be forgotten amongst the younger generation, but when I was fresh in the sect, some of his legends were still told frequently.”

“What kind of stories?” he asked eagerly. This was the first time in a long time that he had a chance to learn more about his teacher.

“Well… there were, of course, many stories about his prowess on the frontlines, but one thing that moved people for a long time was, of course, his declining to become patriarch.”

“What?!” Jianyi almost shouted. Completely flatfooted by that revelation.

The tailor chuckled. “Yes, it was told—and mind, this was gossip amongst us mortals—that a few hundred years ago, Elder Sun had been elected to succeed the previous patriarch, but he felt that he could serve the Empire best by continuing to focus on the frontlines and fighting against the demons.”

Jianyi stared at the worktable in front of him without seeing anything.

He had long suspected that Elder Sun was more than a regular elder in the sect; the only thing that had made him question this from time to time was that he spent time with him and was so engaged in the fight against demonic cultists in the city.

But now it sort of made sense. If he had always prioritized the fight against the Demonic Tide and their human helpers above all else, it could explain why he behaved the way he did.

Still, declining to become patriarch of a prestigious sect was not something that Jianyi had ever heard in the stories.

Maybe he could ask the elder at some—distant—point.

“So why do you want yet another job?” the tailor asked, pulling Jianyi out of his musings.

“Doing something that builds on my old life while learning something new sounds interesting. Also, I can already sense many different ways to spend contribution points coming up.”

Tian Ruo laughed. “Mortal or cultivator, gold or contribution points, you can never have too many.” He scratched the stubble on his chin. “Mhh, if you have just a few hours each week, how about you help cut cloth? We usually start preparations for next year’s intake a bit later, but given how few we currently are, getting ahead of things can only be good, and if you cut the cloth, we can get to the stitching in our own time. Should you have more time later, we can get you to tailor some of the more elaborate work we do for elders or special occasions.”

Jianyi nodded. “Thank you, that sounds good. Maybe things will calm down in the future, and I can dedicate more time to this.”

The tailor chuckled. “It would be the first time that I have seen things slowing down for a cultivator, but we can only hope. I can pay you ten contribution points. Does that work?”

That was double what he was making washing dishes. If he was totally honest with himself it would probably make sense to ditch that job and focus his energy on either tailoring or his own studies and training. But… first of all, it would feel like abandoning the kitchen staff, who had been kind to him and whose contribution points had helped him get to the Martial Meridian Art quicker. And second, giving up on a source of income felt anathema to him. Money had been short too many times in his childhood to easily forget.

Something to decide at another time. He needed to hurry back to the compound and help Minghao prune the bamboo garden in the courtyard. He had promised to help earlier that day and could not be late; that was, after all, his main job.
