Jacques the Hunter checked his quiver before he moved closer to the camp. He huddled between the bushes and pulled his black hood on. It was a chilly morning, and Jacques hoped they couldn’t see the mist of his breath.

That tall man who had saved that girl… Jacques couldn’t shake him out of his mind. It wasn’t just that the man had managed to escape him— something nobody in this new reality had managed to do. It was the look they had shared. That level confidence, that calmness. Jacques remembered that moment fondly. The man hadn’t even been angry at him for shooting at him. He had only looked to see who he was dealing with.

Oh, and they would deal again. Jacques would wait. Every suppressed emotion and instinct that had been allowed to bloom in this new wonderful reality was urging Jacques to attack that man immediately when he exited the camp. But he had orders from the Hungerer.

Jacques had been deeply depressed in the old world. It was a world of charades, and Jacques had hated it. He had to create all kinds of personas for work and family. Every second of every interaction had worn him down. No longer. Now he was free. Free to be himself.

The whole world had always touted that you should be yourself. Jacques never could. He had tried as a kid, and it had almost gotten him jailed and medicated. No, he had to become a chameleon. But that was no longer necessary. Everyone he had ever known was probably dead already. They would not survive in this new wonderful reality. But that man…. That tall man. He was a predator, just like Jacques. Jacques knew it. It was in his eyes. That tall man had also buried his emotions in the old world. If Jacques would not be allowed to kill him, he would befriend him. Maybe this man was someone to build a common understanding with.

But when the tall man came to the shoddy gates of the camp with that blonde teenager, Jacques’s bloodlust arose again. His breath quickened and a rush filled his mind.

“Why can’t I kill him?” Jacques whispered.

There was a silence, but then a voice answered. A slithering, soft, feminine. Like that of a snake.

“He is special…. Just like you…. I have plans for you both. You will have a chance to kill him. But only when I think the time is right. He is going to level. Let him. Go level yourself and I will permit you to meet him later.”

“Yes, Great Hungerer,” Jacques whispered reverently. He watched the man hungrily as he talked with the teenager. His bloodlust flared again. The tall man flinched and looked around. His eyes lingered around the bush Jacques was hiding in, but his Stealth was 7 and he trusted it would be enough. But the man had sensed him— somehow.

“So you are special too…” Jacques whispered.

“I’m really sorry about Vance, dude,” Charlie said as they stood at the gates of the camp.

“Not your fault,” Don said. “That night of sleep did wonders for me. All worth it.”

“You do look great,” Charlie said, then she got all flustered. “I mean the sleep sure worked out for you.”

Don was feeling great. Uncannily so. He wasn’t sure if it was a System thing or his Evolutor race thing. But he felt like he could go for a while without sleep now.

“So you’re going back out there?” Charlie said. “To level?”

“Yeah,” Don said. “You should do that too. This camp thing you guys have going…”

Don was conflicted. He understood that not everyone could do what he was doing. But Vance was not a good leader. He didn’t understand that if they stayed huddled up in here, they would get weak and be eaten. The Hunter or some beast would come for them.

“We keep each other safe,” Charlie said weakly.

Don grunted. “I see the value in numbers, I do. But you should take anyone willing and set up your own thing.”

“I’m just a kid,” Charlie said.

“Time to adult up,” Don said. “This new world doesn’t care if you’re fifteen or fifty. I say you setup a camp somewhere else and be realistic about this thing. This is the tutorial, you know. It’s not going to get easier.”

“Will you join the camp if I do?”

Don didn’t say anything first. He really didn’t like the idea of being bogged down by weaklings. It was a harsh thought, but it was the truth. “I’ll hunt food for you and protect you. But that’s it.”

“We actually talked about it with Michael…” Charlie said. “Setting up our own camp. But now he’s gone and…”

Charlie trailed off. Don squeezed her shoulder. “You’ll be alright.”

She sniffled and nodded. Then she collected herself and looked at Don with that fierce level look again. “We talked about setting a camp near the dispenser in the forest. But it’s guarded by that demon bear.”

“Dispenser? Demon bear?”

“Oh, you don’t know?” Charlie said, genuinely surprised. “There are dispensers in the forest. These white platforms where you can exchange System points for items. But they’re apparently all guarded by a boss monster. The bear, Blazeclaw, is level fifteen. We tried telling Vance that we should kill it, but he doesn’t want anything to do with it.”

“Figures…” Don said. “What can you buy with the System Points?”

“Who knows,” Charlie said and shrugged. “But maybe it's weapons and gear. Or stuff like ovens and showers. Dude… I would kill a thousand bears for a hot shower right now.”

Now that was a compelling thought. Not the showers, but acquiring equipment from the dispensers. This was not an environment to bank your resources, and the System points were doing nothing for Don in his inventory.

“I’ll go kill the bear.”

“Dude…” Charlie said quietly. “I know you’re strong and all. But we saw that bear. I don’t think you can take it out alone.”

“I better get some levels then,” Don said and smiled. “You’ll be alright.”

“Yeah…” Charlie said.

Before Don could answer, he sensed something. It was his Survival Instincts. The sixth sense flared up. Something was close and it wanted to kill. Don looked around and felt as if there had been slight movement in one of the bushes by the treeline twenty yards away. Maybe there was something in the bush? But it was all a bit blurry and weird and Don couldn’t be sure. He would have to be wary when he left the camp.

“Look,” he said and turned back to Charlie. “Think about what I said. I’ll go kill the bear and you can set up a fresh group around it. Don’t trust Vance. He is weak.”

“Alright…” Charlie said. “You’ll come back, right? After you’ve killed the boss?”

“I will. Get your shit together by then.”

“Hah,” Charlie said. “I’ll try. Stay safe big guy.”

“Thanks,” Don said and turned to leave. “You too.”

When Don entered the forest in the cool morning mist, he felt a weight shrug off his shoulders. He looked back at the camp. Politicking, negotiating and all kinds of bullshit. He was not going back there. Charlie was a good kid, and if she took Don’s advice, he would help her set up a base with proper leadership, but that was a worry for another time.

The more immediate goals were more exciting. The demon bear was definitely a challenge Don wanted to take. After defeating the boar, he found he had an appetite for bigger game. He wanted to top that last achievement.

This might develop into a whole ass thing…

Not the worst fixation to have. But there was clear benefit to defeating Blazeclaw. Unlocking the System Point dispenser could hold who knows what resources. And Don needed every advantage against the environment and against the myriad assholes of this new world like Vance and the Hunter.

So checking out the bear boss was definitely on his bucket list. Charlie had shown him the direction the boss was in. But Don had other plans too. He would surely need to get stronger to fight the boss anyway. He figured he would need to find a few more Barbtail Gibbons to unlock their evolutionary trait. In addition to that, it would be great to find a cave or some place he could sleep once he needed to again. But he figured he was good to hunt and fight for at least the next twenty four hours, if not more.

It’s time to go use up all this energy. First stop, bear boss. Next gibbons, and then we look for new monsters to grind on. It’s going to be a great day.
