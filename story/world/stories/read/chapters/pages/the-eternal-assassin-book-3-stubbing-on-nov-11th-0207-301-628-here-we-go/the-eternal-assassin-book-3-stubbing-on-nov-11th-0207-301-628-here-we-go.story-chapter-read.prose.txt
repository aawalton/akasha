While Asher had quite a number of complaints whenever it came to Richard, he had to admit, the wizard did good work when he actually put his mind to it. By the time the team was ready to head back into the cave system the following morning, Richard had already come through with his promise.

And in the form of a dagger of all things.

[Gemstone Dagger] - Excellent quality. Soul Shock enchantment.

“Are you serious?” Asher asked, staring at the gleaming blade as he raised an eyebrow. “A dagger?”

“What? I figured you were more adept at stabbing stuff compared to any other method of application,” Richard argued, crossing his arms defiantly. “You’re sending a magical shock through their very soul, it shouldn’t come as a surprise to you that getting the artifact deep into them would be the best method of application. All you need to do is stab them close to the center of the chest, the artifact will take care of the rest.”

“What exactly does ‘Soul Shock’ do?” Rulfar asked, blinking at the surprised looks he got from everyone. “What? My Scent skill’s second-tier effect is basically the same thing as Asher’s Identify, if a bit weaker. If it’s close enough I can smell it, I can generally figure out what something is.”

“Soul Shock will do exactly what it sounds like,” Richard explained. “Stab the dagger into their chest, and a pulse of soul magic will snap them out of whatever state The Lost Lady has them in. Fair warning, you’re welcome to keep the dagger after you’re done with all this, but the enchantment doesn’t really do anything detrimental to people with a normal soul. Stab a normal person in the chest and trigger the enchantment and… well, I was going to say they’d be fine, but you’ll have stabbed them in the chest, so they’ll probably die anyway.”

“I could have told you that one,” Moxy snorted. “And you really think Runt here is going to give that dagger up? You know he’s got a collection of them, right? Gemstone is a new one. Good luck prying that from anything other than his cold, dying hands.”

“Thank you, Richard, you’ve seriously outdone yourself,” Asher admitted, giving the wizard a genuine smile. “You may have just saved a few people’s lives with this.”

“Good for them,” Richard grumbled, shooting a brief glare toward Kroult despite the lich’s silence before shaking his head and walking away. “Try not to die out there.”

“I think he’s actually starting to care about us,” Moxy grinned, snickering to herself as Richard threw her a rather rude gesture as he turned the corner. “Someone remind me to run that sparring golem idea past him after we finish off this exhibitionist.”

“Richard really has seemed to turn over a new leaf,” Samantha muttered, ignoring Moxy’s comment completely. “I’ve been hesitant to go talk to him about cashing in my favor he owes me after our initial encounter, but maybe I’ll give it a shot.”

“Already have something in mind?” Asher asked, more than a little curious as to the answer. Having a blanket favor from a wizard was a pretty valuable token.

“Maybe,” she muttered, smiling as she held out her hand. “Though that’s a problem for later. Ready to take down another lich?”

“More than ready,” Asher nodded, taking her hand and pulling her into his Personal Rift. Once the others had followed suit, Asher focused on the Eternal Mark he’d left at some random part of the cave system and brought them all back. They’d debated whether or not to simply Recall directly to the arm, but the risk of The Lost Lady having set up some sort of trap outside of the area he could view was too great.

Instead, they were going to have to take the long way around.

“Ughhhh more cave walking,” Moxy groaned as they downed Darkvision potions and started walking. “How did I let you guys talk me into this again?”

“Probably due to the fact that we think we’ll be fighting the actual lich and her minions within the next few hours,” Asher reminded her. “If you want to sit that one out, by all means, I’ll bring you back to the palace. I wouldn’t want to ask you to fight anyone you couldn’t handle.”

After dodging a few air blasts that cracked portions of the wall and floor wherever they landed, Asher led the team toward Grant’s severed arm. Due to the fact that he couldn’t sense where the tunnels led and was just playing hot or cold with his Eternal Mark they had to back track a couple of times, but they managed to find the arm quickly enough.

Along with the deadly trap set around it.

“There’s definitely something going on there…” Rulfar muttered, breathing in deeply through his nose before shaking his head. “It’s well hidden, but I can smell the gemstone dust. The only problem is I don’t know what it’s intended to do.”

“I believe it is soul magic,” Kroult spoke up, his flaming eyes dancing as he stared past them at the lone arm lying motionlessly on the ground. “I would assume it is designed to attack the soul directly.”

“Alright… how are we going to use the arm to track them down if we can’t pick it up?” Moxy asked.

“I did not say we could not pick it up,” Kroult stated, surprising them all by walking forward and gingerly lifting the arm off the ground. “I stated this before. As a lich, my soul is protected, as it is not in my immediate body. Even The Lost Lady would have to go to rather impressive extremes in order to harm my soul directly. It is one of the reasons why Asher’s skill is so unique and deadly to us liches. The magic is busy trying to attack me, Rulfar. You are free to use your skill.”

“Maybe that’s why she risked one of her minions to try and steal Kroult away from us,” Asher said as Rulfar stepped forward and carefully took a deep breath in over the arm, making sure he didn’t touch it regardless of what Kroult claimed. “It makes sense. I’m a direct counter to her physically, and he’s a direct counter to her primary choice of magic. We’re just about the worst team of people she could ever hope to have hunting her down.”

“I’ve got his scent,” Rulfar confirmed, unsheathing his battle axe as he took the lead. “Let’s go.”

After that, there was a good bit more aimless walking down the twisting, winding tunnels making up the complex cave system. Asher was glad Rulfar’s tracking skill allowed him to know what tunnel to take, because they would have been there for days otherwise as they tried to figure out where his Eternal Mark was located if it were still on Grant. After a solid hour of jogging through the cave, Rulfar pulled up short and stopped them with a raised hand.

“We’re getting close,” he warned, closing his eyes and breathing in deeply through his nose. “I’m picking up other scents. Other people, presumably more of her minions. They’re tough to differentiate, but I’m getting at least a dozen or so people.”

“Now you’re talking,” Moxy grinned, cracking her knuckles as she got ready for a beatdown. “Take down without killing, right? Simple enough.”

“I’ll shout out anyone I see who doesn’t have an aura,” Asher nodded. “Those ones are fine to lay to rest. But yeah, do your best to keep the rest alive. Assuming you have the chance. None of us are dying here today because we took an unnecessary risk. I don’t like it, but obviously if it comes down to our lives or the lives of only a few others, ours are more important. We’re literally pruning the continent of the Council of Death.”

“Not to mention these guys are sort of already dead, even if they don’t know it yet,” Moxy pointed out.

“That too,” Asher agreed. Everyone took a minute to make sure their weapons were at the ready before assuming their usual formation. Kroult took the middle, ready to assist with buffs wherever he could. Samantha was in the back, where she could provide ranged support without getting in too much danger. Moxy and Asher were on the sides, ready to attack the front or the rear depending on what hit them. And Rulfar took the lead, shifting into his monstrous battle form in preparation for whatever was about to come their way.

“I am going to start with a simple spell to improve reaction time,” Kroult told them, gently laying a hand on one arm after another. “It tends to make people rather twitchy outside of combat, but I believe it will be beneficial.”

“Thanks, Kroult. Let’s go,” Asher said, holding Crescent and Nail at the ready as they moved forward. He wanted to try out his newest dagger, but Richard’s warning echoed in the back of his mind. Until they took out The Lost Lady, there wasn’t any point in striking any of the captured men with it.

Rulfar proved yet again that his nose was the real best member of the team, because less than a minute later they turned down a tunnel and found themselves standing in a large cavern that seemed partially hollowed out. Asher was pretty certain they were close to a quarter mile underground at this point, which meant they never would have come even close to finding this place without Rulfar’s assistance.

The cavern reached up three or four stories in height, with some strange, stringy plant growing down from the ceiling all over the place like thin strands of ivy. There were eight minions of varying ages moving about the cavern and tending to what looked like a hidden garden of all things, growing produce and sowing seeds like they were still ordinary farmers and not horrifying abominations of tumorous muscles and glowing runestones. The sight surprised Asher at first, before he realized it made sense. If none of the men were technically dead, their bodies would in fact still need nutrients in order to maintain their mass. He was a little worried about exactly where The Lost Lady was sourcing all the protein needed to create such gigantic muscles, and he decided then and there he’d rather not find out.

“They haven’t noticed us yet,” Rulfar muttered, hunkering down as best as his ten-foot-tall frame would allow him. “Samantha, Asher, can the two of you take any out before they do? And Asher, what are we looking at?”

“The two younger ones we might be able to save,” Asher whispered, pointing out one man with a mustache who looked to be in his late twenties and another man with a nasty scar traveling down his torso who looked like he was around thirty-five. Seeing as all their bodies were the same grotesque musculature regardless of age, their faces were the only thing he really had to go off of to guess their ages. “We can take out the others. None of them have classes or auras.”

“I’ll take out the far right,” Samantha whispered as she readied an arrow. “Go for the one next to him?”

“Make sure it’s a head shot. They’ll shrug off anything else, but an arrow through the brain will probably put them down,” Asher said, doing some quick math in his head as he looked at the distance between him and the man Samantha suggested. There was a good few hundred feet between them and the minions, which meant he wouldn’t be able to get close before being forced to drop Astral Dip. “He’s way too far. I think we need to run plan B.”

“Been a while,” Samantha grinned, holding her arrow out for him to touch. “Ready?”

“As always,” Asher said, getting nods from the rest of their team as well.

“Come on, I’ve been waiting ages for this!” Moxy said.

“You’re sure you can hit him in the head from this distance?” Rulfar asked, frowning as he tightened his grip on his axe. “On a moving target, that’s one hell of a shot.”

Rather than even bother to answer him, Samantha narrowed her eyes at her target. Taking a deep breath, she held perfectly still for almost three, long seconds, before letting her arrow fly. The glowing arrow streaked across the cavern, covering the hundreds of feet between them and her target in a single second. The blank-faced man busy pulling carrots from the ground only had time to twist his head toward the sound before her arrow punched straight between his eyes, sending him toppling over and ending his life in an instant. Before the others could move so much as a muscle, Asher Recalled to the arrow and popped into existence directly over the corpse, using his elongated dagger to cleave straight through compressed space at the second minion who had been working close by.

Those were the only free shots they’d get however, as the remaining six minions all threw aside whatever they were doing and began sprinting straight for him. Whatever had caused the previous minions to flee from them farther up the tunnels seemed to be over and done with, but that was alright with him.

The sooner they finished off the chaff, the sooner they could take down The Lost Lady herself.
