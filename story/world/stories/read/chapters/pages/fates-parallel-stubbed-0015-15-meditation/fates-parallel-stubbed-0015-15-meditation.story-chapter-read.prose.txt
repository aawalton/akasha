Jia and Eui returned home after being lectured at length about the importance of consistent meditation. The one unifying factor between all three disciplines was meditation—not only did they all cultivate mana in their own ways, but meditation was also important to solidify lessons learned through practice, combat, or classes.

Most practitioners meditated daily as a matter of course, and those who were at bottlenecks were known to meditate for days or weeks at a time just to attempt to break through to the next stage. It was apparently a legend that Qin’s progenitor meditated behind closed doors for a century before emerging reborn as the God-Emperor and founding the nation that was now one of the most dominant forces on the continent.

Feeling chastened by Takeda and Guan Yi’s rebukes, the pair immediately began meditating once they had arrived home. No sooner were they positioned comfortably on opposite couches in the lotus position, than Eui pointed out a problem.

“Which meditation technique do we use? We know like four at this point.”

Jia frowned and puzzled over the problem.

“Well, the—what did Ienaga call it?—the combat meditation thing only works if we’re exhausted, so I think we can rule that one out. And I still can’t make heads or tails of Qin’s jade slip.”

“We’re supposed to open a path to our dantian through our meridians.”

“Great! What’s a dantian? Where is it? The meridians are those weird channels I sense when I use Qin’s meditation technique, but how do we open a path through them?”

Eui shrugged.

“Beats me. I managed to force some of that weird qi in my meridians out, but it took forever and it was exhausting. Unless there’s something I’m missing—and I am sure there is—it’s gonna take years to awaken that way.”

Jia’s frown deepened. Was the reason Yan Zhihao was so confident that there was a secret to awakening the other disciplines that he knew that Elder Qin was withholding such a secret himself?

“Let’s just rule that one out for now. That leaves ki meditation or mana meditation. We’ve just been practicing how to empower our movements with ki, so probably that one, right?”

Eui nodded, but her gaze was distant and she seemed to be lost in thought. Her face contorted into a sort of frustrated looking scowl. Jia was quietly amused that she was learning how to tell different kinds of scowls apart.

“What’s on your mind? If you have another idea I’m open to it.”

Eui groaned and rubbed her face.

“Ugh, I don’t really know. I’m trying to think of a way to say it without sounding weird. You know when we awakened and shared memories, and then when you did it again to show me the combat meditation technique?”

Jia nodded.

“Of course. Kind of hard to forget.”

“Right. Well uh, the second time the memories were a lot more vivid. I don’t know what you saw, but I saw exactly what I needed to do. But more than that, I was pretty much completely out of mana when you did that. Even with the technique you showed me, I wouldn’t have been able to keep going.”

Jia tilted her head, confused.

“You did, though. We both practiced that technique until we awakened.”

“Yeah, because when we—I dunno, connected or whatever—I suddenly got a whole bunch of mana. For that split second we were greater than the sum of our parts, and when we separated, some of that stayed with me.”

Jia mulled that thought over. She was pretty sure she saw where Eui was going with it, and understood her hesitation.

“So you want to try doing that again?”

“I—I don’t know, maybe? I’m kind of weirded out by the idea of letting someone else into my mind like that. But if my guess is right, it might be more effective than regular meditation and we need all the advantages we can get—especially you right now, right?”

“True. I guess I am ok with it if you are. Though the side effects the first time were pretty unpleasant.”

Eui seemed momentarily shocked before she seemed to come to a realization and sullenly hugged her knees to her chest.

“I...I guess it would be. Of course. Sorry I mentioned it, never mind.”

“Eui wait, that’s not what I meant! I just wanted to make sure you were ok with the side effects. We both got them, right? The weird thoughts?”

Eui glanced up and chuckled in that strange mirthless way of hers.

“Heh, yeah, I guess we did. Except they were unpleasant for you. Obviously they would be, they were my thoughts after all. For me it was actually kinda nice—you’re so optimistic.”

Eui’s face was bright red as she stared down at her knees, unwilling to meet Jia’s eyes. Jia rose to walk around the tea table and plopped herself down next to Eui before wrapping her surprised friend in a hug.

“That settles it then! We’re definitely trying this joint meditation thing! If hearing my thoughts makes you feel better, great! Hearing your thoughts will just help me support you better as a friend. It’s win-win!”

Eui looked askance at Jia before giggling and shaking her head.

“There’s that optimism I was talking about. You’re not worried that my shitty, depressed thoughts are going to just bring you down with me?”

Jia shrugged.

“Not really? The shared thoughts and memories feel...different, don’t they? Like when I saw the memory of you, um, k-killing someone. I knew you were scared, but I didn’t feel scared.”

Eui tilted her head back and forth as she considered before nodding.

“You’re right. I hadn’t really thought about it, but it is like that. Also, how much of that memory did you see? That’s super embarrassing.”

For some reason Eui was covering her face and blushing all the way to her ears. Jia tried to reassure her.

“Uh, not much. It was really fragmented. I think any memory from before our awakening is going to be like that. Dae was explaining something about it to me before, but it was kind of confusing.”

Eui took a deep breath and seemed to calm down.

“Ok good. I mean, it’s not like it would have been that bad. I’ll probably tell you about it someday, I just wasn’t really ready to share that yet.”

“I understand. Should we get started, then?”

Eui nodded and a few moments later they sat cross-legged, facing each other on the same couch. They joined hands as they meditated. That probably wasn’t necessary for what they were trying to do, but Jia thought it felt right. As they meditated, Jia felt the now-familiar feeling of the physical falling away and becoming distant. In her senses, there was now only her aura and her awareness within it. Faintly, she could sense a familiar presence nearby, and with a bit of focus the presence lit up in her senses to reveal Eui’s aura right in front of her own.

From this point on was somewhat unfamiliar territory. They had done this twice before, but the first time was an accident and the second had been an act of desperation while they were both exhausted. Tentatively, Jia reached out with her aura and found that the mana in Eui’s aura would simply flow around hers as if it was a rock in a stream. Eui’s own attempts to reach out were met by the same result.

Jia refocused on her breathing. Her aura constantly had mana flowing into and out of it, and each inhalation drew a small amount of mana into her awareness, expanding it imperceptibly and shrinking her aura by a corresponding amount. Then, each exhalation slightly expanded her aura to make up for the lost area. When their minds had connected on the training field, they had first formed some sort of bridge between their auras through which their awarenesses were able to come into contact.

The metaphysical nature of mana and auras meant that there wasn’t something as simple as a point from which her aura drew in mana. Nonetheless, Jia made an effort to visualize it as such, attempting to will her aura to accept the mana from Eui’s—after several failed attempts and some intense concentration, a tiny trickle of Eui’s mana began to flow into her aura. Excitedly, Jia tried to move her awareness through the connection, but not only was it a one-way connection, her lapse in concentration broke it immediately.

Jia took a steadying breath and resumed her concentration. A few moments later, she saw that Eui had also managed to form a small connection, her own mana trickling slowly into Eui’s aura. Rather than getting overeager, Jia decided to try building on that bridge to form a more solid connection. This time, she focused on the mana that naturally flowed away from her aura, trying to redirect it to reinforce the trickle of mana going to Eui. Soon, that trickle had expanded until all of the mana flowing out of Jia’s aura was redirected to Eui’s.

A connection had been formed, but intuitively Jia knew it was incomplete. The last time they had done this, only Eui had benefited from it. Jia suspected that the connection they had formed then had been a lot like this one. She wasn’t sure why it was so difficult to figure out this time—there must have been some conditions that made it easier before.

Setting those thoughts aside, Jia worked on creating a second connection, this time once again trying to draw mana from Eui’s aura into her own. The process was more familiar now, and she could sense Eui working with her to help form the connection. Thanks to Eui’s help the process went smoothly and they had managed to create a circuit of mana between their auras.

Once the circuit was established, several things happened very quickly. First, the two connections merged together to form a two-way bridge between their auras. Next, that bridge expanded rapidly until their auras were no longer two distinct clouds of mana, but one that enveloped them both. Finally, within their newly merged aura their awarenesses were inexorably drawn together before either of them had time to prepare for it.

Jia is staring at her, terrified. There’s blood on her hands and Eui knows it’s her fault. Eui wonders if it’s a portent of things to come. Will she continue to drag Jia down to her level? Will the blood on Jia’s hands be figurative or literal next time? Everyone is looking at Eui and she can feel the accusation in their eyes. When she speaks, it’s without thought. It barely even feels like it’s her own voice.

“I guess I lost, huh?”

The memory ended, but the connection remained. Every thought felt confusing and jumbled. It was difficult to determine where Jia ended and Eui began.

“This feels weird.”

She wasn’t sure whether the thought had come from Jia or Eui, or which she even was for that matter. The more she tried to think about it the more chaotic and confusing her own mind became.

“Were you afraid of me?”

The thoughts that followed were completely incomprehensible. She gave up on that line of questioning and focused instead on the original goal of meditating to improve her mana sense and internalize her lessons. Jia and Eui focused on their breathing, finding it had now become perfectly synchronized.

She allowed herself to get lost in the meditation, each breath slowly empowering her awareness and replenishing her aura. Her mind began to recall Jia’s lessons with Dae, Eui’s experience with reading and writing, and everything that either of them had learned in the past week. As it did, the new lessons and previous knowledge of both girls combined and consolidated into a single new foundation of knowledge.

The flood of information soon became too much to process, and she simply let it wash over her, hoping that she’d later be able to recall the important parts when it counted. Eventually, her thoughts quieted once again and she was left with only her aura and her linked awareness floating within.

Her awareness had grown significantly during the meditation session, but she felt intuitively that she had reached a point of diminishing returns. The mana she took in with each breath was reduced to a tiny trickle compared to the steady stream it had been when she started.

Slowly, as she returned her breathing to normal and felt her body come back into focus, her awareness extricated itself from the linked aura and returned to her body. As their auras began to separate, Jia felt like herself again. When she opened her eyes, she saw Eui staring back at her. When Eui spoke, Jia knew what she was going to say before she even said it.

“That was weird.”

Jia giggled at the absurdity of it. It was such a bizarre and transcendent experience that such a mundane statement didn’t feel like it did it justice.

“You already said that didn’t you? Or was that me? It was hard to tell.”

Eui shook her head.

“I have no idea, it kinda hurts my head to think about it. I think the meditation was effective, though. Actually, I’m pretty sure I know a bunch of stuff I didn’t know before—can you really get away with stealing fruit by knocking over a basket and giving most of it back? Wouldn’t that just make you look more suspicious?”

Jia scratched her head and chuckled.

“Well it works better if you can blame someone else for knocking it over, but people are less suspicious if you help them. I think you filled in the blanks on how to read, but I am not sure how I feel about knowing which places bleed the most when cut.”

Eui blanched slightly, but shook it off quickly.

“Sorry. That might be useful information one day, though! If only so that you know to avoid getting hit in vital places. What about the mana gathering, was it more effective?”

Jia focused on her aura for a moment. Her mana sense felt more acute than it had before, and she could feel that her awareness had expanded significantly within her aura. Unfortunately—

“I don’t really have anything to compare it to. It feels like I’ve progressed, but I don’t know if regular meditation would have been better or not.”

Eui deflated slightly as Jia spoke, burying her face in a palm.

“Of course. We got ahead of ourselves, I guess.”

She was so stupid—Jia frowned momentarily at the thought. She didn’t think that was hers, though it stood to reason that the intrusive thoughts would come back, now that she thought about it. She tried to console her friend.

“Don’t worry about it, we’ll have plenty of time to test it later. Sharing knowledge like that is already amazing enough, isn’t it?”

Jia stretched and looked out the window. They had meditated all night and it was now morning—no wonder she felt so stiff! It hadn’t really felt like that long to her, and she wondered idly how immortal practitioners kept appointments if their meditation could take so long. She supposed that was part of why the schedule in the academy was so loose.

Jia noticed Eui staring at her and belatedly realized that she had been stretching her back out like a cat. She immediately sat up straight, blushing.

“U-um, anyway, what do you think we should do today, Eui? I’m planning to meet Dae for more tutoring later. You’re welcome to come along, if you like.”

Eui’s expression was reluctant—she was jealous—and after a moment, she shook her head.

“No thanks. I kind of want to try working on writing some of those talismans, and if we can share our lessons it’s probably better if we split our efforts.”

Besides, she didn’t want to get in between the two lovebirds—Jia frowned even as she felt her cheeks reddening.

“It’s not like that! I wish you would stop teasing me about it!”

Eui looked confused.

“But I didn’t say—oh! Wow, they’re really strong right now, aren’t they?”

Jia shook her head, realizing that she had responded to something Eui hadn’t actually said.

“Ugh, yes. Sorry.”

“It’s fine. We knew it was going to happen. Just another reason to spend a bit of time apart to let it cool down, right?”

Jia sighed. She wasn’t sure if that was the right approach, but she wasn’t sure about much of anything at the moment. Her mind was still reeling a bit—this was a lot to take in all at once.

“Alright. I guess I’d better get going then, but next time you should join us, ok?”

Eui grumbled in response.

“I’ll think about it.”

Jia knew that was the best she was going to get out of Eui for now. As she walked towards the lecture hall, her thoughts dwelled on Eui and one strange idea kept repeating itself over and over.

She couldn’t let Jia become another Sun Jaehwa.

Jia didn’t know who Sun Jaehwa was, but whenever she thought about the name it brought back the fragmented flashes of Eui’s dark past, and the first person she had killed. It felt like a violation of Eui’s privacy, so Jia tried to distract herself as much as she could from that thought. Unfortunately, the paradox of trying not to think about something brought her mind back to the same question every time.

Who was Sun Jaehwa?
