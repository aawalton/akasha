It had been a long time since Luke had last allowed himself a moment to rest and just chill in bed. Events were coming together into a total clusterfuck of a shitstorm, and it was drawing close enough that droplets would start falling soon as the wind picked up. Still, staying in bed was something he'd earned. Or perhaps not. Still, either way, with her pressed against him, he saw no pressing need to get out.

"I have to go," Hannah whispered without opening her eyes.

"Already?"

"Luke," she mumbled, a smile creeping onto her face. "We've been in bed for hours."

"Oh."

She opened her eyes, kissed him, then sat up, the covers falling down to bunch around her hips.

"Nice," Luke said.

She just laughed and stood.

"Nice," he repeated, watching her dress in another faded T-shirt with the band name 'Dance Macabre' printed on it, the white letters on the brink of falling off.

"I'm going to the thing," she said, covering herself by putting on underwear and pants, much to his disappointment. "You coming?"

"Milla's thing?"

"Yeah."

He shook his head. "I'll try to reach Level 40 today. Hoping it'll give me a nice boost before the Singers get here."

"Good idea. Got band practice later." She looked up at him from where she sat, tying her shoes, with an expression he hadn't seen on her before. Vulnerability. Hesitation. "See you tonight?"

"Of course," Luke said.

Her smile lit up the room, and the shadows in her hair wriggled, as if dancing. Hannah nodded and stood. "Tonight."

He waited a moment, but she just stood there, arms crossed, looking at him.

"What?" Luke asked.

Tapping her foot against the floor, she shook her head. "I have to go, and the Shelter is coming with me."

"Oh," he said, catching her meaning. Luke sat up, then slid out of bed.

Hannah nodded and spoke, changing her voice to sound more like Luke. "Nice."

Once they'd said their goodbyes, Hannah headed over to guild headquarters next door, disappearing as soon as she touched the plaque. A slight chill clung to the morning air, and Luke was glad for the comfortable robe he wore at almost all times, since it provided a bit of extra warmth. A nearby bench looked inviting enough, and he grabbed a bite to eat and drink as he sent out a few messages.

Luke: "Want to head into a dungeon?"

Ray: "In one already. Your sister pushed me into another party. Said I need to learn to work with others? Can you believe that? If she weren't your sister, I'd call her names."

Luke: "Who are you with?"

Ray: "Elijah, that guy Martin and his buddies, and one of those chicks from Chicago, the one with the hexes."

Luke: "You're in a dungeon. You're literally able to see their names by inspecting them."

Ray: "Eh."

Luke: "Maybe Milla is right."

Ray: "You're hurting my feelings now."

Ray: "Fight coming. Got to go save the noobs."

Curtis wouldn't go into another dungeon either, saying he was trying to get the hang of whatever it was he'd attempted with the goblins, and that he needed peace and quiet for it. Then he turned off his communication gem. Classic Curtis.

Luke: "Hey Lemira. Are you in a party already?"

Lemira: "I am. Veronica and I are dealing with monsters roaming the streets of this city. It is a good start for my new companions."

Luke: "Who's Veronica?"

Lemira: "The dark-skinned woman with a bow."

Luke hadn't heard of her.

Lemira: "If you are looking for party members, you should do so in the guild interface. Guildmistress Milla should have a task set for you in there as well."

Luke: "Oh, right. Good idea. Thanks. Be careful out there."

Lemira: "You as well."

The guild interface. Being in a guild was still new to Luke. Well, not new, maybe, since he'd been in ISG before, but utilizing the different aspects of a guild was something he hadn't put much time into. To remedy this, Luke popped open that part of the interface and took a look around. A long list of members popped up, their roles and party compositions added right next to their names and levels. Luke's task, as it turned out, was 'Level up.' Every single member in the guild was doing something, most of them clearing dungeons.

"Definition of you snooze, you lose," Luke grumbled, seeing there would be no one available to join him.

Luke: "Are you saying I need to solo dungeons today?"

Milla: "Thought you'd be coming with me to the meeting. You weren't there this morning when everyone set out. I'm sure you'll do fine. Just be careful."

Luke: "Meh."

Going into a dungeon by himself was not something new, but the difficulty of the last dungeon he'd been the first to enter gave Luke pause. Could he solo a dungeon? He considered the question for a while as he stared straight ahead at nothing in particular. It would be dangerous, but if it came to that, Luke was confident he would be able to get away from most dangers. Being able to heal himself was an advantage, but solo delving into danger...

Checking the interface showed an experience bar that teased him with how close he was to leveling up. He wanted to reach Level 40 before the Singers arrived. It wasn't even a sure thing that they would come once the countdown ended. Sooner than that wasn't impossible. Perhaps it was even probable.

"I'll do it," Luke said to the empty air.

Before that, he needed to see if contacting Guide was a possibility. To that end, Luke pulled up his feet on the bench and sat cross-legged as he let his mind drift away. Any hope of finding Guide would be in the weave. The recent practice proved useful, and the threads appeared in moments. This time, however, Luke needed to go beyond. Guide was not a being of this system, but of The Greater System. It was there Luke would find the one he was looking for.

Like so many times before, the weave of The Greater System proved elusive. No matter what he tried, moving beyond the weave of his own system just wouldn't work, not in his current state. Fine, then. If he needed to be half-dead to even glimpse the damn thing, so be it.

Guardian Weave was a good option for lowering his mana, but even after wrapping every organ and even a bunch of bones in a protective layer of mana, dropping his max mana to a mere ten percent of his maximum, the expected effects didn't occur. No dizziness, no headache, nothing. The skill didn't count as having lost mana, for some reason.

"Stupid," Luke muttered, removing the protection from everything except his heart, Meta-Heart, Lungs, throat, and brain. Instead, wasting mana, he weavestepped back and forth while storing a good amount of healing mana through Bountiful Recovery. That did the trick.

Groaning, he sat back down on the bench, hovering around fifteen percent mana left. What a pain. Even as he submerged his mind into that pool of calm that was meditation, mana regeneration forced him to keep using Weaver's Rupture, or it would rise too high again before he even glimpsed The Greater System Weave.

There it was. Brilliant and awe-inspiring, with threads shining like the sun compared to the pale local weave. Beyond it, the void spread in all directions, going on forever. Ignoring that black nothingness, Luke set off to search The Greater System Weave, a task he soon found was impossible.

While the weave and its thread appeared before his senses, there was little information he could glean from it all. Perhaps if he touched a thread, that would change, but he couldn't predict that with any certainty. Also, it would force another brush with death, and Luke had stuff to do. Even focusing on The Greater System Weave or the threads therein made his head hurt, and his attention kept going to the small areas between the threads, through which the void beckoned. At first, the being in there looked on with interest, but when Luke just sat there trying to read the weave, it seemed to lose interest. But there was something else in that emptiness, too, like movement at the edge of his vision.

Luke's attention kept being dragged from threads and back into that blackness, no matter how many times he tried focusing, as if whatever was out there wanted him to see something. Since it was the first time he'd ever sensed something out there that wasn't the void being, he couldn't help but feel a tinge of interest, despite knowing that stepping too close to the void would bring about disaster. Then again... That was how he'd come face-to-face with Guide the last time. Would it work again? Perhaps the weave wasn't what he needed. The void did feel inviting.

Luke snapped out of it through sheer force of will. Being pulled into that again was a bad idea, and that was putting it mildly. Guide rescued him last time, but he got the sense that it was a one-time thing, and he'd come close to destroying everything and everyone on his last brush with the void. Best not risk it. Even so, there was something out there. Something... hungry?

It wasn't hungry like the void being. That thing wanted to end everything, even itself. No, this was just plain hunger, Luke thought.

"Smudge?" he asked, projecting the question through The Greater System Weave. If someone had asked him how he did that, Luke wouldn't have been able to provide a decent answer, but project the question he did.

There was no answer.

Luke brought out a monster core, holding it up with a thread of mana. "Smudge?"

Excitement. The thing in the void radiated excitement.

"Smudge!"

The presence disappeared. It was just gone, replaced by the void being. That bastard returned when it found out what was happening.

"Bastard!" Luke yelled, this time using both his will and his mouth. The void being didn't even stir, but the effort crumpled Luke's focus and brought him out of meditation just as something hit the side of his head. It felt like a small stone. Had someone thrown something at him?

"Whu?" he asked, trying to bring a hand up. The arm wouldn't work. Luke was on the ground. Huh, that was strange. Darkness crept into his vision, and he saw something spreading out in front of his face. Blood. His blood.
