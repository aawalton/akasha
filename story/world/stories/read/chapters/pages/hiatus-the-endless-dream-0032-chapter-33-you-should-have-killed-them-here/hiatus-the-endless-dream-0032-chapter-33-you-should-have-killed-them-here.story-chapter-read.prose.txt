Krevith

Quiet. Externally, at least.

My mind was still in chaos. I could not parse how much was from the small statue of the Fiend and how much was from the visions of the forest. The thing in the forest … it was not a Spirit of Judgment. I could say that confidently. A helper, a lesser spirit, perhaps—but the line between Spirit and monster blurred much at their meager power. Frightening power for mortals such as I, but meager when drawn against the true Spirits, and even lesser still when weighed against even the gaze of God.

The Spirit in the darkness of the forest said much in little time. I was thankful that my mind was such a mess. If it had not been, I may have given in to the cycle of rebirth.

I could not yet do so.

The Spirit had made one mistake—it pulled too hard on the fights of Gladiators in the visions it showed me.

Even when suppressed in violent joy, I still felt betrayal and shame. The hand of God no longer reached me. My emotions were free to take hold here, so the shame stopped my steps forward into rebirth. I did not deserve escape, but there were those here who did.

The … Fiend. No. The name didn't fit. The boy was terrifying. But a Fiend he was not. A good one he would make, but that did not change his nature. Yet, he was not of the priesthood, nor like the Huntress Paltra—an outlier on the fringes of society. He had come from seemingly nowhere, much like the man claiming to be a Prophet.

Perhaps tricks, or additional punishment from God. It mattered. All did, but for now, it was acceptable. Or it might have been if the boy had not pulled the actual Fiend from his pocket with intent. It made one thing clear. He was not an ally, not in any true sense.

My thoughts were stifled as a hamlet came into view. My sight had drifted. I had not noticed the broken stones of lime that littered the trail behind us, and so was surprised when the canopy gave way to a clearing.

I stared at the moon. A beautiful thing, and something I was only first seeing. Today was a day of many firsts.

The words of "Prophet" Jeren communicated through the "Fiend" were not lost on me. The Huntress Paltra would fire a warning shot at any moment. We would talk. Then they would turn us away or not. I feared what might happen if they turned the boy away. So, when he strode confidently towards the center of the hamlet—which held an untouched church of stained glass and other finery—I followed.

We paused as we passed the line of destroyed buildings, and the boy turned to me with a frown. I made my eye scarce from his gaze. We stood there, and I was unsure of why, when I realized that a warning shot had yet to be fired.

I had no great affection for hearing of my death in a vision. Yet, somehow, there was some small piece of it in the knowledge that the Huntress and Maerin looked after it. So when no warning shot was fired like was promised, worry swelled in my soul. I had not paid the price of death that Jeren had said existed. I wished not for it to rebound on others. I lengthened my stride to the church and pushed open the door, hoping an arrow would appear beside me.

None came. Yet my worries vanished. The moonlight pierced through the stained glass, coloring the church. Bulbs of light dimly illuminated swathes of space, and by the altar at the front, bathed in candlelight light was young Maerin, not much older than the supposed Fiend. She still looked sickly, if only through the white tipped hair, the reds of which she still tried to use to cover the burn scar there.

She smiled, but I did not mistake the anger underneath. It was directed at no one, and so ended up stabbed into all.

"This is her?" The boy asked.

I nodded and turned my gaze back to Maerin. To her, I gave a deeper nod, one that touched my shoulders as well.

She gave a diplomats smile and spoke. "Good tidings. I recognize our dear Gladiator, but I have to say, from the batch of sinners I heard about, none fit your description. Perhaps you have more to say on that Krevith." The voice was demanding. A definition of the girl, yet not one completely unearned.

I did not know who he was. Nothing had been given and nothing asked, yet I could not outright say that. "He is," I started, taking great care to construct my words. I found better ones to start with. "A Prophet has called him a Fiend."

Yes. That would do nicely. I was not claiming belief myself that I did not have.

If Maerin caught my meaning, I did not know, although I liked to think that she might have. She had been involved in petty politics from a young age, once she married.

Disgust twisted my gut. The man was dead now. It was not fine. I noted the anger and bottled it. Labeled it for use. It would have to be uncorked soon, lest it explode at random. Not in front of Maerin. It was a selfish anger; she did not ask it of me.

"Well." She said, "Come forward. There are plenty of seats."

I hadn't yet taken a step when the Fiend spoke, "Not until whoever is aiming at me comes out of hiding."

Ah. Yes. The Huntress. She would be here. I hoped that at least. If she had taken the death rightfully owed by me, my heart could not handle that mountainous weight.

She stepped out from behind a pillar, and I was relieved. Not so relieved, I did not tense at the sight of a drawn crossbow. She lowered it to the ground, but did not discharge it with a blank or otherwise disengage the weapon.

An unwise choice. She had no way to know. Warning her would not look good on either of us, and if denied, build tension unnecessarily. So I said nothing.

"No warning shot?" The boy asked.

"Not for you. No." And I did not know if a threat had been spoken. The Huntress was only known to me by name and reputation, but naught else. Why she was here, I was still unsure. She lived on the outskirts. No one would accuse her of heresy there, even if true.

A question for if we lived through the night. She looked harsh, with her half snarl and form covered in furs, but someone cruel would not protect another. It was decided then.

We would become friends if we got through the night.

The Huntress walked towards Maerin, and I followed closely behind. The boy followed shortly after. We stood at the front of the church. There was no silence as Maerin filled it. "What do we owe this visit? If Krevith were not here, I do admit I may have run you off, yet you do not seem afraid that I would do so anyways."

The boy shrugged and answered. It became obvious he was no great auditor. "You know the situation we're in. Ten Survivors. Four of you need to survive for another four hours and fifty-three minutes. You two don't survive past hour three on your own."

Too blunt. Yet too wordy. His voice was layered in strange magicks, and it made me wonder if I had missed the death of a Survivor somewhere. He had said ten, but there was more meaning behind those words. Perhaps time worked differently for him, and he really was a Fiend seeing into the future.

"Why?" it was the Huntress who asked. She hadn't taken her eyes off the boy, and her eyes were not gentle things.

They looked between Maerin and the Huntress. "It's what the Prophet said. I seem to be lacking details. So, don't ask."

Yes. There was something there. He had more information than he was telling any of us. There was also a reason he did not want us to know. I thought back to the statue of the Fiend and its horrid visage. Then back further to the paste of red that littered that underground arena. Certainly, without doubt, he was hiding something, and not being sly about that fact.

"We have warning now. I doubt we'll see the fate your Prophet has promised." the Huntress Platra shot back.

The Fiend gave up, "Krevith," he directed at me, "you handle negotiations. I'm gonna look around."

He left unceremoniously. Heading up towards a staircase. It seemed to lead to the bell tower.

Several seconds passed, and both women relaxed greatly, and the mask Maerin wore disappeared in part.

There were no hugs or further warm greetings.

"I am … glad you are safe Krevith. The forest is dangerous. There's something living in it. More than one."

I nodded, "My joy is sincere, and entirely my own, to hear that Lady Maerin." She flinched at my words. Ah. Right. "Sorry. Just Maerin."

I directed my next statement at Paltra, "We met one such creature. A lesser spirit of judgment, surely."

She nodded. Her hand waved my words away. Something else interested her, "This Prophet. Do you believe he is a true one?"

I shook my head. "I have doubts. He is self-proclaimed, and his words bring much comfort to the others, but the others are … used to softer things. The words … hit on exacting fears and soothe them."

I debated speaking further, but they deserved to know the routes my brain took. "If either is a Fiend it would be the proclaimed Prophet."

"You do not think the boy is one?" Paltra asked.

I shook my head, "No. Perhaps he may be. I lean not. A danger? No doubt, yet his nature is not of Fiend."

No more words should be said.

"He reminds me of you, Maerin." I held my breath after the statement, afraid that she might order Paltra to spray my blood on the grounds of this holy place. She did not order it, though I could tell she wanted to. No sharp words came for me either.

"The others. They are part of this group. Orlan, Alder, and Niro?" She asked instead.

I nodded and hoped that she was not considering options of extreme measures. To my… relief, Paltra spoke reason, "Maerin. It's best we stay here."

"You heard the boy. If we stay we die. I prefer living. I need not more convincing."

"I do." When directed at Maerin, the Huntress' words were not as harsh as before, and the candlelight in her eyes looked like it danced.

That seemed to give Maerin pause. A good sign, that. The Lady negotiated, "What would you require then?"

"Assurances," the answer came instantly. "That you will not make attempts at any of their lives until the end of the trial."

A pause. Then, voice low:

"Are you fucking kidding me." It was not a question when Maerin spoke. She stepped towards the Huntress, shoving a finger into the other woman's sternum. "Those are not terms you get to dictate."

No more words. Maerin's breathing grew tense, heavy, but not erratic. I didn't dare move, in fear that attention and anger would shift towards me.

The Huntress winced and briefly looked away. "I know it is unfair, but I ask it for them anyway."

"You ask for me to choke and drown in my own bile. To be cowed in the presence of monsters. If these are the assurances you need, you can stay here." Maerin was a flurry of movement, of large motions and outrage. She wheeled briskly on a heel and sat back on the low altar, glaring at Paltra. "I will be going back with the Fiend and Gladiator.

The courage to speak found me, and I started softly, "The others hold no great affection for the boy. We may have the best of both options. Protection and separation. Close, but not part of." I made care to refer to … I needed to learn their name. I dared not call him Fiend, it was not what I truly thought, and I wished to impress upon these two the same.

Maerin's head tilted slowly towards me, "This seems skewed in Paltra's favor, not mine Krevith."

I shouldn't have brought it up, but it might be the only chance I had at getting both to come to safety. Paltra was a Huntress. She lived not in society. No help, yet she was healthier than many. Formidable, but formidable against mortal. She was here for Maerin, entirely and utterly, but if Maerin overstepped, Paltra would leave. I opened my mouth. At the last moment, wisdom hit me, and I opted to remain silent.

It did not go unnoticed.

"Speak," the Lady commanded. I listened.

"The boy is not a Fiend, but they have one with them. Dormant and docile as it is." I saw Maerin's eyes gleam at the information, and I knew she needed no more convincing.

Paltra paled, much as I had when I had seen the creature. "How can a Fiend be docile?" she hissed out, looking towards the staircase that led to the bell tower.

"I know not. I lack understanding, but it is truth." I looked at Paltra. "Your worry is that the trial will fail, not that Maerin has her revenge?"

She hesitated, not wanting to answer, but that was answer enough for me, so I continued. "Then this much will be fine."

I turned back to Maerin, "I will not promise anything on his behalf, yet I would like a promise of temporary companionship from you two until the trial is done."

"Amenable." Maerin snapped. She stood and began walking towards the bell tower. She stopped and glared at Paltra as she passed---waited until agreement also came from the Huntress' lips.

She stopped once more at the base of the stairs and turned to me. "This is the second time I find myself owing you, Krevith. I thank you and find myself having nothing to thank you with. I am in your debt and that will not be forgotten, but do not think for a moment that means I am at your mercy."

I shook my head. "It never crossed my mind La– Maerin."

She nodded and headed up the steps, leaving the Huntress and me in the dimly lit church. We listened to her footsteps ascending the belltower.
