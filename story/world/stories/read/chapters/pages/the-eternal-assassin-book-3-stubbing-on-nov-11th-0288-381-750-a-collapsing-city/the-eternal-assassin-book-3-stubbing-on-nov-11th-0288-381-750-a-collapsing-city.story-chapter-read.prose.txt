While Leighann darted off into what looked like a large supply room connected to her main workshop, Asher stood there, face-to-face with the final greater power of the Noala Kingdom. Before he could offer so much as a tentative ‘hello’, the dragon opened its mouth, and a deep, rumbling voice echoed forth.

“Reaper,” she said, a few thin flames trickling from her nose as she snarled. “Why are you going after Leighann again? I was promised by Donvath himself that was a one-time thing.”

“I’m not trying to kill her. I need her help,” Asher said, not backing down as he faced off against the beast. “We can spend the next few hours fighting the endless zombie horde until the entire city falls, or we can take out the lich responsible and stop things here once and for all. I need Leighann to find him for me.”

“I see,” Elnor rumbled. Her large eyes slowly blinked while she thought over his words. It seemed dragons had an extra set of eyelids that were entirely transparent, perhaps to help keep the organs safe from drying out when they breathed dragon fire. “Very well. I will help you both locate the lich.”

“Wait, seriously? Just like that?” Asher asked, taken aback by how easy that was.

“I must have incinerated thousands of zombies over the past hour, but there appears to be no end to them,” Elnor explained. “More of them keep coming from all directions, climbing out of the ground or running from the nearby forests. I happen to know that Whikoga was not built on a mass grave the likes of which the world has never seen, which means these undead are the results of a skill. Either being created here, or more likely, transported from somewhere else. Either way, killing the lich responsible will stop new ones from coming, and then we can finish off those already within the city’s walls.”

Leighann chose that moment to burst back into the room, laughing manically as she heaved a saddle over Elnor’s large back and got to work connecting the straps along her scaled belly. Elnor’s dragon form was large enough that Leighann barely even had to duck in order to walk underneath her, and Asher could only shake his head at how unfair certain elements were. Sure, the Dragon element was supposedly one of the rarest ones out there, but even so, the sheer amount of power it gave Elnor was ridiculous.

“Alright, let’s go save the city!” Leighann said, climbing up the side of the saddle and securing her feet as she sat down. “Some of my favorite shops are located here, I’d hate to see them destroyed.”

“All of your favorite shops are located here, you idiot,” Elnor huffed, flickering her head in Asher’s direction. “Don’t just stand there, get on. Though the saddle she commissioned is only for one. You’ll just have to hold on tight.”

I can’t believe I’m actually doing this, Asher thought, using Distortion to hop up onto Elnor’s back. Placing an Eternal Mark on the saddle just to be safe, he plopped himself down behind Leighann, grabbing a hold of two rings on either side of the saddle and making sure he had a firm grip. Never in a million years would he have thought he’d be riding a dragon at any point during his new life, and yet here he was.

Not bothering to ask if he was ready, Elnor lowered her body into a crouch, before leaping up off the ground with a powerful sweep of her wings. If Asher hadn’t had hours of practice holding onto Samantha’s back when she took off with Rapid Flight, he probably would have tumbled backwards and off the dragon, regardless of his handholds. Elnor was fast, and the three of them rocketed into the sky.

Having flown on a pegasus, his fiancé’s back, and now a dragon, Asher could say with absolute certainty that, weirdly enough, the dragon was the most nerve-wracking experience of the three. He quickly discovered why Leighann’s custom saddle had a part for her legs to literally lock into place, because when Elnor banked to the side for the first time, he nearly fell off yet again. Dragons were fast, but so were pegasi and the Wing element. The main difference was the power. A single flap of Elnor’s wings rocketed them forward like Samantha using Rapid Flight all over again, and Asher was quickly forced to get used to jerking across the sky as they would go from intense acceleration to a gentle glide, back to intense acceleration again. He was glad he hadn’t eaten yet for the day, because he probably would have lost his lunch after only a few minutes of flying in such a manner.

“Focus,” Elnor called back, and he did his best to do just that as he peered down at the ground.

From up in the sky, Whikoga looked to be in even worse shape than he’d thought.

The lands directly outside the city were still burning from the last gout of dragon fire, but that didn’t stop the hordes of undead from rushing through the scattered flames and scampering over the outermost wall. The first ring of the city was in utter ruin, and Asher’s heart went out to the thousands of people who must have fallen to the horde already, and the thousands more running for their lives. Barely a fifth of the buildings within the first ring were still standing, the large zombies having clearly smashed their way through everything in their mission to find and kill everything that drew breath. And yet even with all that devastation, that wasn’t what caused a spike of fear to shoot down his back.

The second ring was under attack.

Asher doubted Bordan and Clare would have lost to any number of zombies, but there were more entrances than just the main gate leading in between the rings. Hell, from the sheer number of zombies forcing their way into the city, enough of them may have piled up against the wall that the others just climbed over them and dropped into the second ring. Regardless of how it had happened, he watched as the very first city he’d ever entered in this new world slowly fell to the horde of zombie-plant hybrids. Already they were spreading through the outer portion of the second ring, pushing their way closer to the center of the city with every passing minute. He gasped when he picked out a spot he was more than a little familiar with, his eyes widening as he spotted the tiny black specks battling it out in the streets.

That’s Draken’s manor! Rosh and the others are in danger!

Yet as much as Asher wanted to Recall down there and protect his friend, he knew the best thing for him to do was to be ready to leap into action the moment Leighann spotted the lich. Rosh had finally built a life he enjoyed living here in the city, and Asher didn’t want that ripped away from him anytime soon. Turning toward the woman in front of him, he shouted to be heard over the roar of the wind.

“Anything?!”

“What, you think he’s just going to be sitting up here on one of the clouds?” Leighann shouted back. Cackling to herself, she shook her head and kept scanning the ground. “I’m looking as fast as I can, but I’m going to need a few minutes! There are a lot of nobles fighting down there who don’t like having people look too closely at them any more than you do. Each one is like a little bubble of hidden space, and I need to search through all of them one after another!”

Asher clicked his tongue as he realized this little plan of his was going to take some time. He’d hoped Secret Seeker and Identify would somehow allow him to help, but it was clear that he was worthless up here after all. Just as he was about to tell her he was going down to help and would be back in a minute, Asher froze when he felt the unmistakable sensation he’d been waiting for all this time.

Deep, searing pain radiating through the Eternal Mark he’d left on Rosh.

Without missing a beat, Asher triggered Recall, twisting space around him and appearing in that same bedroom he’d been in only a little while before. Half of the wall had been smashed through, and Rosh’s broken, unmoving body was lying against the far wall with blood dripping down his head. Based on the large undead that was lunging toward him to finish the job, it looked as though the abomination had smashed straight through the side of Draken’s manor and hit Rosh before he even knew what was happening. Right before the undead could reach him, Asher sliced through it with an elongated Crescent, chopping the thing in two and watching as the pieces fell on either side of him. Satisfied the zombie wouldn’t be getting back up, he threw up a Spatial Lock before turning to his friend.

Rosh lay crumpled on the ground in a heap, his neck broken from the looks of things and with jagged shards of wood and stone perforating his body in dozens of places. As Rosh’s blood pooled out across the floor, Asher threw Permanence’s second-tier effect onto the man, ensuring that he wouldn’t die from blood loss anytime soon.

“You’re not dying that easily,” Asher said, frowning as he tried to figure out what to do. Even if he wasn’t going to bleed to death, Rosh was currently out cold. The damage was too severe for a potion of Lesser Restoration, and he wasn’t willing to risk dumping multiple down the man’s throat and hoping for the best. Every potion a person drank lowered the efficacy of the next one taken that same day, and he wasn’t willing to risk making a mistake. Not when Rosh already had the shards contained within his soul needed to heal himself. Donvath had made sure of that, even if he didn’t recall giving Asher the instruction all those months ago. He just needed a way to wake Rosh up to get him to heal himself. Some sort of powerful jolt.

“Thunder!” he shouted, doing his best to carefully drag Rosh into his Personal Rift before turning to slice though a zombie slamming itself against his Spatial Lock. “Where are you?!”

Leaping through the hole in the side of Draken’s manor, Asher’s eyes widened when he took in the battlefield that the second ring had become. Gang members belonging to both Draken’s crew and a few others he recognized were doing their damndest to wipe out the zombies just as fast as the undead were rushing them. A squad of people with bright-red hair were working together to incinerate the hordes before they could get too close, and rugged thugs with weapons crafted from bone of all things were defending their flank, making sure they wouldn’t be attacked from behind. Some of the gangs had even teamed up with the guard, the two forces fighting as one for presumably the first time in history as they screamed and hacked at the unending stream of corpses. Before Asher could start running around and hunting for Thunder, his own shadow flickered under his feet.

And out stepped an old friend.

“Kree!” Asher laughed, slowly lowering the dagger he’d been preparing to plunge into what he’d thought was an assassin coming after him. “I’m glad to see you’re alright!”

“You too!” Kree grinned, before narrowing his eyes as a trio of zombies rushed them from the side. Spinning around, Kree’s own shadow flowed up his body and onto his arm, where it formed a wicked blade that stretched out a good three feet from the tips of his fingers. With a quick slash, Kree decapitated all three zombies in a single swing, smiling proudly as their unmoving bodies fell at his feet. “It’s been a while!”

“No kidding,” Asher said. He whistled at not only the impressive strike, but the strange confidence coming from the boy. When he’d first met Kree, he’d been a scrawny thief, using his Shadow element to scrape by and sticking to the shadows to hide from anything even remotely dangerous. Now, he barely even recognized him. Months of working with Draken’s gang, actually getting three square meals a day and taking care of himself, had done wonders for the boy’s complexion and physique. He wouldn’t be winning any body building contests anytime soon, but he didn’t look like he was one missing meal away from starving to death any longer. “Kree, do you think you can help me?”

“Of course, anything you need!” Snapping him a crisp salute with his shadow blade, Kree stood up straighter. “What is it?”

“I need to find Thunder, but I don’t see anyone smashing heads in with an electrified mace anywhere,” Asher said, scanning the messy crowd while he looked for her. “In fact, I don’t see any of Draken’s elites defending the manor. What’s going on?”

“Draken and the three top elites left a bit ago. They’re trying to slow down the main horde before it can reach us in order to give everyone time to flee,” Kree explained as he scythed through another wayward zombie. Tossing a grin over his shoulder, Kree motioned for Asher to follow him. “Come on, I can bring you to them!”
