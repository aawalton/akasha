However, I may have leaked a bit of your plan to Corthian Mining and the other investors that were impacted by Kertan Six, and they all signed documents indicating that you’re welcome to access the planet and improve the quality of the world prior to its sale.  The best way for them to help stay solvent with the shareholders, after all.  Casiniax was livid when those documents made it to the hearing.”

The downside, if you would even call it one, is that your humanitarian efforts have to bear out now.  There’s an expectation that you continue to support the planet’s restoration after the auction, at least for a while.”

You’re approved for two full days, after which you’ll need to apply for additional approvals each time.  Your primary objective for this trip will need to be reclaiming any of your outstanding resources.”

I’m not going to be able to upload any of the old schematics, but I’m betting that C.H.A.D.D. has some map data saved.”

[OF COURSE I DO.  IT WOULD HAVE BEEN IRRESPONSIBLE TO DESTROY THE RECORDS PRIOR TO THE RETENTION POLICY, AFTER ALL.]

“What is the Collective’s retention policy?” Kyle asked, not knowing there was one.

[I AM UNCERTAIN, DR. MAYHEW.  HOWEVER, BETTER TO BE SAFE THAN SORRY.]

“I mean, I’m glad you have the maps, but that is some flawed logic you’re operating under, C.H.A.D.D.”

[‘THANK YOU C.H.A.D.D.’ WOULD HAVE BEEN A MORE APPROPRIATE RESPONSE, DR. MAYHEW.]

“You two are ridiculous,” Suierrillax said, interrupting their banter.  “In any event, the teleporters are ready for you.  They’ll get you to a nearby station, and then you’ll be flown down in a shuttle.  And before you ask, the pilots have been vetted, and the Mercenary’s Guild is acting as a guarantor, sending one of their own for both security and to discourage anything untoward from any outside factions.”

“In fact, it is.  She also has a vested interest in returning to Kertan Six, in fact.”

“Then it gives you a loose end to clean up.  Look, Kyle, I’m not going to pretend to understand the nuances of power differences between C Grades, but everything I’ve seed indicates that you haven’t been a lightweight for a long time.  That job Jarberry got you hooked up with?  It was too dangerous for most of our early or mid-tier C Grades in the Courier’s Guild.  Jarberry was so confident in your abilities after reaching C Grade that he had no issues recommending you for it.  You were a D Grade that nearly got killed by a C Grade.  The guy was punching down and still couldn’t take you out.”

Kyle stopped for a moment, remembering his talk with Iberious, and then the battle for Er’Mithren.  Suierrillax was right, he wasn’t a lightweight at all.  When he considered his interactions against forces around his own level and grade, there were only a handful that proved truly dangerous.  Those memories were just colored by the fact he’d been forced to fight above his weight class most of the time after reaching D Grade.  I really do have to work on my mindset, Kyle thought.

“So, if I find anything about Valentine or DeRosa, am I able to act on it?”

“You certainly have a right to,” Suierrillax said.  “Now get moving.  Your nav bracelet should have access to all the systems inside the ruins, but you’ll need to move quickly.”

“Will do.  And thank you.”

Ending the call, Kyle stood up, then began the process of tying the shirt sleeve on his left side.

[DO YOU THINK THIS IS A GOOD TIME TO TRY THE NEW PROSTHESIS, DR. MAYHEW?]

Kyle looked down at the edge of the gnarled wood visible beneath his shirt collar.  “Not yet,” he finally said.

[THERE IS NOTHING WRONG WITH CHOOSING TO WAIT,] C.H.A.D.D. assured him.  [HOWEVER, I WOULD ENCOURAGE YOU TO CHOOSE A CASUAL ENVIRONMENT FOR IT FIRST.]

“I think I’ll be more comfortable giving it a try once we get more bandages to wrap it,” Kyle said, surprised at the feeling of self-consciousness at the idea.

[IF YOU PLACE AN ORDER NOW, THEY WILL ARRIVE BY THE TIME YOU RETURN.]

Pulling up his nav bracelet, Kyle did just that.  With the advent of Aspect of the Tree: Branch, Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. started working on an everyday prosthesis, one designed for day-to-day activity rather than combat.  It would take time to get used to, but it was a tool that he very much looked forward to.  Just… not yet.  He wanted to get dedicated time to practice with it and gain confidence before using it out and about, as well as getting something to cover up the clearly plant-based components.

A couple of quick teleports later, and the duo found themselves on a small space station near Kertan Six.  From their view, they could see the largely-devastated planet, a single patch of green representing where Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. had lived prior to their return to the Hub.

“Hard to believe it’s the same planet, isn’t it?”  A feminine voice behind them asked.

Kyle turned to see Tillienne approaching, having already felt her presence through Synaptic Barbs.  The fae woman still looked tired, though she’d gained some life in her features since they’d last met.  More than anything else, she had a soft smile on her face – something she’d struggled to do when they shared their meal.

“It really is,” he said.  “It’s good to see you, Tillienne.  Thank you for coming.”

“Of course,” she replied, stepping up alongside him as she looked at the planet.  “It was billed as a simple job, you know?  Low-ranked planet, a handful of D Grade enemies, and support for the company’s own forces.  It really turned into a mess.”

“Through no fault of any of ours,” Kyle said, looking back out at the planet.  “Sometimes things just go sideways.”

“That’s putting it lightly,” she said with a chuckle, the soft ringing laugh a welcome melody to Kyle’s ears.  “I really don’t know how you do it.  How any of you do.  I’ve fought a handful of C Grades now, you know?  And each time, it’s been a close thing, a life-altering thing.  You, Arhades, and Skippy just go on like it’s just another day.”

Kyle shook his head.  “In that case, we all should have gone into acting instead of the Guilds.  Tillienne, every encounter I’ve had against a C Grade was massively threatening.  I can’t speak for Skippy, but I know Arhades was in the same boat.  You’re no worse off than we were, trust me.”

“I hope that’s true,” she said.  “In any event, let’s hope there aren’t any C Grades waiting for us today, hm?”

Before Kyle could reply, a ding rang out from the space station’s speakers, followed by a message.

Passengers for Kertan Six, your shuttle is ready.  You will be dropped off at the previous extraction location, and a shuttle will be coming to pick you up using your nav bracelet coordinates in forty-six hours.  You are invited to reclaim any resources that you left behind, as well as assess the damage from the skirmish before your forced evacuation.  Please note that significantly shifting that scope may have consequences, to be determined by the Collective.

“That’s our cue,” Tillienne said, turning away from the planet.  “I suppose we’ll get a closer look here in just a little bit.”

Kyle followed the four-armed woman to the shuttle, and they rode in amicable silence as the small blot of green on the desolate planet expanded to greet them.  They landed near the exposed water of the cenote, and Kyle looked down at the water with a wide smile, feeling his connection to the aquatic chaddlings restored with proximity.

“So, what all do you have to retrieve?” Tillienne asked, looking around at the fallen trees and path of destruction carved out by the B Grade’s attacks.

Without a word, Kyle gestured to the water.  The surface began to bubble as the aquatic chaddlings surfaced, long strands of algae trailing behind them as they tottered on tiny legs out of the water and toward him.

“These, for starters,” Kyle said, looking at the pristine and clear water they left behind.  The chaddlings had really outdone themselves with the passive filtration, Identify showing him that the water was incredibly pure.  Even as he watched, a large chink of ash fell into the water from the lake’s edge, only to be nearly instantly broken apart by a dozen tendrils of algae floating nearby.

The sight reminded him of the warring fire and ice during the job for the Pillar; a swarm of smaller attacks neutralizing a single, stronger one.  And suddenly, something he’d been working on fell into place in his mind.  The problem with using Graft with a higher-quality seed and a mundane one was that the mundane one always gave out before the process could complete.  In his mind, he had to use the skill to combine two seeds.  But what if he balanced quality with quantity?  Using a larger number of the mundane seeds to match the inherent mana and intent of the stronger?

Blocking out the world around him, not wanting to lose the moment of inspiration, Kyle shifted his perspective into the World Tree Seed and activated Graft, this time bringing out seven of the bamboo seeds for one of the seeds from Er’Mithren.  The process was running smoothly, until he accidentally pushed too much power through two of the bamboo seeds, breaking the equilibrium.  Undeterred he tried again.  And again.  Finally, on the fourth attempt, the fusion worked.  There was a crackling sound as the entire construct melded together, and soon only a single, small seed was left.

With anticipation, he activated Cultivate.  The seed grew with explosive force, a bundle of thick reeds springing out with razor-sharp edges giving off the concept of sharpness itself.  With a grin, he pushed the skill further, creating more of the seeds which he then grew into another set.  He felt C.H.A.D.D.’s elation, though it was tempered with a note of warning.

Turning his attention back to the outside, he saw Tillienne standing in the middle of dozens of aquatic chaddlings with her arms crossed, tapping her feet.  “Mind telling me what the heck that was?”
