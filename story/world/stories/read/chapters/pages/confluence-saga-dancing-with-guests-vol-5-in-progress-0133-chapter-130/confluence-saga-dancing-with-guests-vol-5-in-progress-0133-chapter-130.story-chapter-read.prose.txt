On The Decline of Family Lineages.

We are the pre-eminent. We have been a staple in the lists of Families of Significance for longer than your line has existed. We earned our place through the talents of our ancestors who showed their worth for all to recognize.

You are new, you are the upstarts. You think minor success, a turn of chance, a novel idea, is sufficient to place you on our level? You are nothing but a fleeting disaster waiting to happen.

It matters not that you can fund whatever endeavour you please, that you can find the best of trainers. They are not the best, they are merely those available for hire. The best are already claimed, already busy doing what they have done for eons, training those whose pedigree is beyond reproach.

Remember that you are merely tolerated, that others have been where you are and yet only the records remember their names. Our records, we were there and still remain. Do not compare yourselves to us, for you are not.

Anya stared at Gwen in confusion. Had she grown up among humans? What sort of question was that. Of course she had, hadn't everyone? Where else would she have…

"I grew up Heir to Holding Lys," she answered.

"That's- I mean- I haven't heard of that Holding."

"I used to think it was a larger Holding, that we were important. I'd been to the Annual Convention when I fully Awakened and saw all the others but didn't realize most of them had Sovereigns, or even retainers who had passed the wall."

"Hadn't passed the wall? You grew up in a Holding where that wasn't normal?" Gwen asked, her fingertips shifting back and forth on the table.

"No, Mother hadn't. She finally did a few months after we did."

"So your mother was the Sovereign?"

"Yes, I was her first. My younger sister is the Heir now."

"You have a sister." Bern said.

Anya turned to glance at him, only to find him still staring at his food, giving her no attention whatsoever.

"Almost seven years younger. Why?"

"If they look alike then there is something unusual going on," he said.

"I'm sure someone would know something, there is no way," Morgan agreed.

Are they really? she sent.

She felt Seb's mixture of confusion and frustration echoing across the band. This made no sense, the questions were prying without sharing any details in return, the fact that they spoke as though she were not there unless asking her something was infuriating.

She felt a technique press against her hand and smiled. The table was wide enough that reaching across to squeeze hands would look awkward in the moment, the pressure was Seb's attempt at the next best thing.

Thank you.

"Does she?" Gwen asked.

Anya looked back at her, into unblinking eyes whose glow seemed so familiar yet at the same time so unusual.

"What?"

"Look like you. Your sister."

"No, she looks like our mother."

"Only one then," Morgan said.

"Still concerning," Bern said.

"Will you two stop that?" Gwen snarled. "I get that it is our way. That conversations happen despite the presence of those spoken of. We were told this would be seen as impolite. That we would be Guests and expected to follow form."

Anya saw the entire group next to her stiffen as Gwen spoke. She felt something in the air, a tension that peaked for a moment before fading. She felt Seb's confusion before his focus sharpened. Something had happened there.

Affinity? she sent.

No… he answered. Gwen wasn't the source, it wasn't her Energy… It- I don't understand.

"Apologies," Morgan said, drawing Anya's attention as he set down his utensils. "I doubt Bern will admit it but we were wrong in how we acted. Gwen is right, our actions were not proper."

"The rules apply to how we act with regards to the Hall," Bern said stiffly. "She is not an instructor, she is merely a-"

"Obernyn!" Gwen said and once more Anya felt as though the world around them shivered.

Seb?

That was her, but… no… it wasn't her.

"I'll apologize for my brother," Gwen continued. "We didn't expect to meet anyone here who clearly was-"

"Gwen?" Morgan said. "You've already said enough, done enough with your words in this hall."

"I know Morgan, I know. But you have to admit this is a surprise!" Gwen said, her tone shifting nearly as quickly as her facial expressions did.

They talk out loud, they argue out loud, they don't care if we hear? she sent.

I wonder if they even care if any of us are here. Well other than you.

But why?

Well you're beautiful, smart, competitive, stubborn-

Seb! she sent, blushing despite her attempts to appear composed.

She knew he was teasing her. Not that he didn't mean every single word he'd said. He rarely said the words, he rarely called out the details that way, he rarely had to, she knew how he felt, what he thought.

You look like them, or they look like you, he sent more seriously. Maybe relatives of some sort?

But they look like me, not my mother.

So the only way that makes sense is if-

-my father?

~ * ~

By the time they got back to their suite Seb was nearly as jittery as Anya. Gwen had said nothing more to them, going so far as to turn her chair slightly so she wouldn't even look at them accidentally. Bern clearly wasn't going to say anything more to them so they'd finished their meal in silence.

If there'd been dessert things might have settled slightly. A good dessert was always a distraction, especially when there was no good way to express irritation. They'd been the second group out the door, taking the first departure as an invitation to do the same.

Seb?

Yes?

What do you think they were asking?

I think it was about your father. That they either know something or could at least make a guess?

He shut the door behind them, leaning back against it as she turned to press against him. They stood there, tension and stress slowly draining from the band. They'd barely talked to anyone other than their unusual dorm mates, but that one conversation had been enough. Having close to another hundred people arguing, boasting, celebrating or even simply talking too loudly hadn't helped.

We can go ask them, he sent.

And have them either ignore us or talk around us again? No.

Then what?

Her answer was not in words, nor entirely body language. The bond between them made her answer clear. They could deal with whatever had been going on in the morning. There was no reason to let something they couldn't solve distract them.

They were just barely through the bedroom door, still perfectly in lockstep, when someone knocked. Seb paused and felt as much as heard Anya grumble. When there was no immediate repetition they took another step, only to be stopped by more rapping.

Seb...

If they keep knocking…

Fine!

He didn't even try to pretend he didn't feel as frustrated as she did as he went to the door. They'd gone through the induction and then to dinner, they'd suffered through the strange interrogation, and now that they were done for the day they were being interrupted again?

He pulled the door open a crack, his leg braced against it so it would go no further.

"Yes?"

The last of the strange group stood in the hallway. She'd said nothing during the meal, at least nothing he'd heard. Despite having clearly seemed confident and prepared at dinner she seemed more hesitant now, her blonde hair seeming almost as dull as she took a step back.

"Gw-," she began before seeming to choke on the words, her hair falling limp as she did. "That is to say, Mistress Gwen, wondered if she might have a moment of both your time? She wishes to make amends for what was said and done at dinner, and to do her best to explain."

Seb could feel Anya at his shoulder, frustration warring with curiosity.

"Tonight?" he asked.

"If she might. She realized her questions were inappropriate, that she took without giving in return. My words cannot bind her but she did say she would answer what questions she could, explain what she could, for whatever knowledge you might share in return."

Anya?

Answers? Not more questions?

Hopefully.

"Is she going to continue to pry?" Anya asked from behind him.

"Yes- I- Mistress!"

"Cara. What did you say?" Gwen said as she stepped off the stairwell and into sight.

"I apologize. I did not mean. She asked and I-" Cara answered, her words coming faster as she started to pant.

"Bother," Gwen said. "That answers that question. Cara, you did nothing wrong, she's family."

Seb wasn't sure whose reaction to the word was strongest. Anya's thought turned triumphant, she had an answer, even if one that left more questions while Cara nearly deflated.

"Thank you," Cara said with a gasp, her hair visibly brighter once more. "Gwen, I told you that this might be a mistake."

"You did, and maybe it is. But we have our answer now," Gwen said before stepping past her companion. "Might we come in? We mean you no harm and as she said I will do my best to answer your questions."

"All of them?" Anya asked.

"No. Even if I wanted to I could not. Cara wouldn't let me, wouldn't be able to."

"I wouldn't-" Cara began.

"You would," Gwen interrupted. "We both know you'd have no choice. We're not yet at the point where either of us can make those decisions without his Eminence's permission. Not even in a case like this."

Seb took a step back, Anya still glued to his side as he let the door swing open.

"Come in then."

"Thank you, ..?" Gwen said.

"Seb. Sebastian if you must."

"If I must? Is that how things are to be Sebastian?"

If Anya had not tensed he might not have noticed anything. He'd heard the words, felt them, and then felt Anya bristle.

"No, it is not," she said. "You may call him Seb."

"Oh!" Gwen said. "Did you notice?"

"Yes Mistress."

"Notice what?" he asked.

"I'll answer before we leave tonight. I promise," Gwen said.

"That's not answering our questions," Anya said.

"No, but I can't answer that one without context. Please call me Gwen, and this is Cara, my lifesworn."

"Anya."

Seb felt the pressure of Gwen's gaze as Anya named herself. He felt her attention shift to him as she smiled, her teeth peaking past her lips.

"You chose that name, didn't you."

"He did," Anya said.

"How close were you, are you?"

"Newly wed," Seb said.

"Partners, co-rulers," Anya added.

"Doubly-"

"-bonded." they finished then glanced at one another.

He hadn't meant to add that, he knew Anya had not either. Yet it was the answer to the question, but what did it matter. They'd told Sarna, but she had guessed.

"Cara," Gwen nearly snapped.

"Yes?"

"He's family."

"But he's human, he must be."

"Then he is to be treated as family. I don't want any mishaps."

"Oh, yes. As you say."

"Will you please explain what you mean?" Anya said.

Seb's thoughts refused to settle, refused to accept the conclusion he was coming to. It made no sense, it couldn't be. They kept referring to human as though they were not. Which mean they likely were not.

"We, even Keldren, sort of, are all Fae."
