Our horses were tired, but they only had to survive a few more minutes. If all went well, this wouldn’t take long.

I locked the gate-breaching party in my sights. They readied their arrows, we held our shields out to guard ourselves and our mounts.

The Blood Tear Cultists didn’t have many archers. Either my city guards had picked them all off (which was unlikely) or the cultists preferred not to engage as archers. They weren’t a proper army, after all.

I blocked an arrow as it raced toward my chest. It cracked into my basic shield, ripping the outer boards and punching a hole in the outside. My shield, no matter how weak, took away enough momentum, though. When the arrow reached my chest, it left only a scratch on my scale mail before bouncing off.

That had been an enhanced arrow of some kind. We were up against Path-walkers, so perhaps it was some equivalent kind of resonance Skill.

But Burr didn’t have armour. If that arrow had hit her instead, it would’ve killed her.

Another one raced toward us, but I triggered my Art, catching it in a well of gravity and dragging it down to the ground.

Then we were upon the cultists.

I led my squad around the back of a mantlet, spear pointed at the nearest cultist. Using a resonance Skill, I skewered a Titanium’s head, and Burr’s hooves trampled a Steel before he could duck out of the way.

As the rest of my group of charging Dupes smashed through the cultists, trampling them or dismounting and fighting on the ground, I turned my attention to the bulky cultist behind the cart of bodies. He was only a Titanium, but according to my Perception Skill, he was the biggest threat here.

And the markings on his head were blazing bright crimson. Even from fifty feet away, I could feel a pull on my blood.

“Brawl, stay back!” I yelled, pulling Burr to a stop and holding my spear out to the side.

I whipped my crossbow off my back and aimed it at the cultist, then fired a resonance-Skill-enhanced bolt.

Before it hit, a pulse of red energy washed off the Path-walker like a supernova. It blasted the arrow out of the air and sent rippling tendrils of energy washing across the ground like a blood-red crater.

I was expecting a transformation, like I’d seen with Jaruwain. Instead, this man’s runes simply glowed brighter, locked in that state. The energy rippled through the markings, disintegrating the flesh around it and causing it to lift in floating chunks. Rippling, red-tinged fields encircled his limbs, like a wicca’s brawling abilities. However, the sacrifice had enhanced them far beyond their tier.

Now, the cultist gave off the pressure of a Silver.

The bodies in the wagon were nothing more than a heap of ash, now. It wasn’t the slow and efficient harvesting of a single body that I’d seen the cultists pull off at the temple, but it was still enough to empower the man for his transformation.

We’d arrived just in time. They had been gathering up the necessary biomass to pull this off for as long as they’d been besieging the city, and only now did they have the capacity to wield it.

The cultist looked at me with wild eyes, then glanced at his falling comrades. Most of them were dead already as my riders swarmed over the gate-breaching party. Though there had to be about forty cultists, it wasn’t a contest when it came to our Sapper training, and our force being mostly Titaniums.

I expected the cultist to make a desperate charge at me. I locked him in my vision, preparing to meet his charge with my shield. A Silver against a Silver.

He lowered himself to all fours and bounded forward, leaping along the trail toward the town gate. Swerving around me, he moved faster than I anticipated, energy and flesh trailing behind him. He wasn’t after me at all.

When he reached the gate, he turned his shoulder into it, then rammed the center. The boards splintered under the force of the impact, and the gate swung inward, its cross-beam shattering.

“After him!” I yelled. “Yesman, guard the gate! Stop whatever you can from getting into the city!”

I snapped Burr’s reins. I couldn’t afford to stay with my squad right now. We had to stop the cultist.

That was the trick up their sleeve. If we hadn’t arrived, they would’ve busted that gate down. The cultists would’ve rampaged through the city, and although my defenders would have put up a good fight, the city would have fallen.

Still, it seemed slightly too easy. Was this the best Hinlaw could muster? Was his grip on the cultists slipping?

I’d deal with it as it came, then. Burr galloped forward, creating a tunnel of wind around me. It pulled at my hair and rippled through the holes in my helmet as we charged.

The cultist leapt at the nearest gate guard, an unfortunate mortal man. Ripping the man in half with his bare hands, the cultist let out an animalistic screech. The man’s upper torso was still screaming, begging for help as it flew over the city.

The cultist continued inward, smashing through the walls of a house near the gate. The plaster and wattle walls imploded on impact, and the already charred facade of the house collapsed.

The cultist grabbed a mortal civilian who had been carrying a bucket of water. He pinned her to the ground and slammed her head down hard enough to shatter her skull, and she was dead before I arrived.

I slammed my spear into the cultist’s back, swinging it downward like a club while using my Art to enhance its speed. Bone shattered, and the cultist fell face-first into the muddy street, teeth grinding against gravel.

He tried pushing himself up again, but his legs didn’t work. I whirled my spear around, then stabbed through the back of his neck with a resonance Skill, and finally, he fell still. The markings on his bald skull dimmed.

Three other cultists made it through the gate before Yesman’s squad blocked it off. I raced to stop them. I took down a Steel-tier with my crossbow, nailing him to the inside of the palisade wall through his neck.

One of the cultists aimed straight for a civilian—a man with a bundle of spears in his arms, who was rushing to help the defenders of the gate.

The cultists were just trying to do as much damage as they could. This was intended as a punishment for unruly cities.

Burr galloped toward him, and I skewered the cultist through the chest with my spear, driving him to the ground and holding him there. My speartip embedded in the ground, and I let it stay there until he died. He wouldn’t be punishing anyone today.

The last cultist must have been a sorcerer. He held his arm out to the side and created a whip of blood, then cracked it on the ground, creating a swell of dust. I turned to the civilian with the spears, who I’d just saved, then held out a hand. The man placed a spear in my hand.

I threw it. Though I didn’t have much practice, Moonhand had taught me a few spear-throwing techniques. The cultist knocked the first spear out of the air with a whip of blood, but I took a second one from the man and launched it moments later.

It impaled the cultist through the neck. He fell to the ground, grasping at the weapon, before falling still.

I ripped my spear out of the ground, then rejoined my squad outside. We left one group to guard the gate, then, with the rest of my column, we circled around the outside of the city, trampling through the camp. When we were halfway around, the remaining cultists, maybe twenty of them, retreated into the field.

It really was that easy…

But that didn’t help the churning in my stomach. Something was wrong, if Hinlaw’s control of the cultists was slipping.

“Should we run them down, sir?” a Dupe from behind me asked.

“Let them go. We don’t need to lose anyone else today.” I shook my head. Although we had caught them by surprise, we still suffered a few casualties, and I didn’t need to make it worse.

Next, I glanced over at the other half of the battlefield where Lord Ceonwin had attacked. “Besides, we still have some cleanup to do.”

Lord Ceonwin dealt with the cultists on that side of the castle, and now, he held the three commanders Hinlaw had sent captive.

Riding across the battlefield, I approached Lord Ceonwin. “Sir, we’ve cleaned up our side of the castle.”

“Excellent work.” He motioned behind him, toward his captured commanders. Three Path-walkers in yellow gambesons knelt in the snow, surrounded by sappers. “We’ve captured their leader. Hinlaw sent his second son, Sir Hinbern, to lead this force. He is a Silver, and he slew a few of our men.”

I chewed my lip. “Hinbern.” It was obvious who he was—he had a similar face to Hinfic, but his blond hair was much shorter, and he had a tattoo on his cheek.

I couldn’t let him go alive. He was still the heir to Hinlaw’s domain. Alis was right.

In my mind, it was harder. But I’d just seen what he was directly responsible for. Two brutal civilian deaths was enough to erase any sympathy I may have had for him. He’d chosen his side.

Dismounting, I approached him. My desire to spare the ‘innocent’ heirs of Hinlaw faded by the minute. “You led the cultists here,” I told him. “They slew innocents within the city. You knew what would happen if they breached the gate, and yet you led them here anyway.”

Hinbern scowled at me with an icy gaze. He said nothing.

“You are not innocent in this.”

Finally, Hinbern spat, “My father will—”

“I’m not worried about your father,” I said. “I’m worried about what you did, and doing what’s right.” I didn’t know what everyone else knew of my plan, and the less they knew, the better. I wasn’t planning on publicly duelling Hinlaw, after all, and I didn’t need people worried about my disloyalty to the Warlord.

Any more than they potentially were. I didn’t know what Habit had said. Did he warn Hinlaw?

Hinbern’s eyes narrowed, but he said nothing.

With the help of my resonance Skill and my Art, I drove my spear through his eye. His body fell limp, and he flopped to the side, dead on the spot.
