Chapter 71

November 23, 2099

Mai squeezed the dual triggers of her arch rifle once more, with her lips pressed tightly together.

During the following weeks, the Siberian air cut into her skin like needles as the freezing winter cold wrapped around her body. A tangible threat against her face that reminded her how fragile life was across those vast snow-covered plains. Despite being encased in the exoskeleton, the freezing temperatures had become worse as the days went by.

Mai watched from her forward position on the battlefield. The scene before her and the squadron was something out of a nightmare.

The energy shield that formed the foundation of the unit's defenses flickered weakly, threatening to collapse under the constant assault. All around them, the thunder of railgun fire, electric arch rifles, and voices mixed with the crunch of snow and the unnatural sounds of the Fractus. The daylight was already fading, and darkness spread across the battlefield like an endless nightmare.

The shield flickered one last time before disappearing completely.

They were at the mercy of the freezing blizzard and the enemy.

It was a battle with no hope of victory.

Everyone knew it.

But resisting was not about winning. It was about surviving, about holding on until their last breath.

They had all reached that point.

Yes.

The sphere had moved. Good.

There were no celebrations, but they had managed to provoke a reaction in the distortion.

During those days, Stepnye Volki had advanced alongside the rest of the units, positioned among the rear forces.

Perhaps that meant something would change for the better.

No.

What happened afterward crushed their hopes as quickly as breath vanished into the cold air.

The sphere stopped and small spherical portals began emerging from the main sphere and positioned themselves around the circle of millions of combatants, forming a thin ring of death that slowly expanded toward them from every direction.

Death was coming from the sphere.

Death was coming from the portals that had appeared behind the encirclement.

The battle that followed over the next few days had brought them to this point.

Reinforcements were constantly arriving from China, India, Mongolia, and every country that could send troops. But this was the end.

Now that weeks had passed since that attack, everyone felt that all they had done was throw a stone into a beehive and hope they would not be attacked afterward.

On November 22, more than ten thousand souls had fallen in just a few hours.

And when noon came on the twenty-third, the chaos of trying to understand what had happened on the main front ceased to matter when no more communications came through. The millions who had been mobilized had all perished before the tide that now advanced, spreading like a drop of oil in water.

Stepnye Volki was thirty kilometers from the sphere. But they were surrounded as well. The few remaining units were being attacked from every direction, and rumors began to circulate that even farther east, enormous Fractus had begun appearing, gigantic creatures that had only been seen a few times near Cape Town three years earlier.

But as long as those monsters did not reach them, it was fine.

They already had more than enough to deal with.

Weeks had passed, and they had been fighting without rest, against the Fractus, against the cold, against exhaustion, and against despair.

Natalya had even been evacuated only a few hours earlier on the last transport that withdrew the wounded. She had suffered a terrible abdominal injury that had nearly cost her her life.

Only Sasha, Ludovic, Eun-ji, Soo-ah, Jiho, and Mai were there, along with the few units holding out against the advance.

"Stepnye Volki, one more round?" Sasha asked, with a smile that tried to hide his nervousness.

His deep, firm voice cut through the chaos surrounding them. Mai glanced at the Ukrainian commander and noticed the exhaustion etched across his face. They had been fighting for weeks, and the tide of Fractus refused to give way.

In the distance, a Xe-res collapsed, its twisted form exploding into a cloud of fragments, but it was not enough. There were hundreds more, perhaps thousands, an unstoppable tide of monsters emerging from the depths of the distortion.

The Shrikes circled above them, with their dark and unsettling forms cutting through the air as they dove toward the ground.

Stepnye Volki, along with the other seventy squadrons deployed in the area, formed a defensive line in the middle of the snowstorm. Each unit fought for its life, for its future, for one more moment in a world that seemed to be falling apart.

Mai clenched her teeth once more as a Shrike lunged toward her. She deployed her rifle and, with a quick, practiced movement, fired a charged burst that pierced the creature before it even touched the ground.

The Fractus plummeted, its shriek tearing through the air as it dissolved.

But that was nothing more than a drop in an ocean of destruction.

After all, the unit formed by the three squads no longer existed either.

The few who remained had died early that morning. Of the more than forty who had started, only fifteen remained. And now they were dying.

They had lost contact with Raven two hours earlier.

Lamy, the fey from Unitas, had disappeared the previous night as her screams faded into the heights. Abducted by a Shrike.

Dead.

She had dissolved along with the rest of her team and squad, who had fallen a week earlier. She joined Raven but the lost of her companions left her very unsettled. And now she had joined the ranks of the fallen.

"Hold the line!" Ludovic shouted. His voice echoed through the speakers of his helmet.

Mai saw him with his heavy weapon braced against his shoulder, firing relentlessly at the Xe-res advancing toward the extermination zone. His eyes, filled with fury and exhaustion, reflected the desperation of someone who had seen too many battles and yet had never stopped fighting.

Beside him, Eun-ji maintained a constant stream of fire. Her face remained serious, devoid of emotion at that moment, but Mai could see the weight she carried on her shoulders.

Her eyes were dry.

She had no tears left.

During the past few days, she had spent hours tending to the wounded and, despite her abilities, she had not been able to save any of them. With no one left to save, she had done nothing but shoot and shoot since early that morning.

The battle spared no one. And now there was almost no one left.

Only them and a few other scattered teams.

Nothing had come from the front for hours. There were no communications.

"Mai, cover the left flank," Sasha ordered without turning around. His voice remained firm despite the tension.

Mai nodded and ran toward the designated position while picking up a couple of rifles from two bodies lying in the snow.

The ground was covered by a thick layer of snow, but she did not slow down. The sound of nearby explosions made her bones vibrate, and she could feel the earth trembling beneath her feet with every impact.

Some nearby hills had platforms for drones and other Crawler-type machines equipped with rail cannons, but they were also being attacked by the Shrikes.

The snow was stained with black and purple tears dripping from the Fractus, but the number of enemies did not decrease.

It was not enough. Not nearly enough.

In the distance, a roar erupted, a guttural bellow that made every soldier tremble.

Shrikes.

Xe-res.

More Fractus.

The entire battlefield had become a single organism, a mass of death that advanced and adapted. They moved in groups, attacking ferociously and reconfiguring themselves as they fell.

It was a macabre dance, a choreography of horror without pause.

"Don't fall behind!" Jiho shouted while remaining on the firing line with her magic, commanding enormous elemental beasts of ice and fire. At that point they were nothing more than distractions, but they bought them enough seconds to fire. Even so, Jiho was already exhausted. Her magic was becoming less and less stable, the elementals were getting smaller and smaller, and she only had a few left, which would run out within minutes.

"One step back and we're finished!"

The mages from the nearby units unleashed what little energy they had left, raising walls of shields and summoning localized storms of snow and fire, trying to slow the tide while their organs burst from the strain.

But the Fractus did not stop. They kept advancing.

"Careful!" Mai heard Ludovic's warning.

A Xe-res had flanked the line. Its twisted form lunged toward her at terrifying speed.

Before she could react, a shot rang out beside her and the creature fell, its distorted body crumbling into fragments.

Mai turned toward Sasha, who gave her a grim nod.

The unit's defensive line was beginning to give way more and more as screams and flashes from rifles and explosions began to diminish everywhere. The Fractus advanced with unstoppable force, and the terrain itself became more dangerous with every passing second.

In the distance, other units were being surrounded.

The sound of battle mixed with the screams and moans of the wounded.

The situation was completely out of control.

Mai took a deep breath, feeling the pressure closing around her chest.

Then another roar rose above the chaos.

A gigantic Shrike, its body forged from crystalline material, crashed into the lines, annihilating several nearby squads within seconds.

Mai saw it from a distance and her heart stopped for an instant.

The already weak and broken communications began to fail, and the few remaining squads became scattered. Contact between the units grew scarce, like the muffled gasps of dying men, fragments of voices swallowed by static, the sounds of the Fractus, the fury of the storm and the wind.

For Mai, time seemed to stretch. Seconds became minutes. Snow fell over the battlefield, but beneath it the ground was soaked with blood. The echo of explosions throbbed inside her head. Her squad was surrounded and fear spread among them like a freezing that they could not shake off.

There were hundreds. No, thousands heading toward them.

The battle had ceased to be a confrontation between armies.

Now it was simply a massacre. Stepnye Volki watched as the units fell, one after another, swallowed by the vortex of destruction, with no hope of returning. The snow, which had once been white, had become a dark shroud stained with the blood of those who had stood beside them.

Ludovic was the first to fall.

A Xe-res lunged from his left flank, attacking him without warning. The sergeant was dragged away by one of its limbs and, before Mai could reach him, the creature's jaws tore him apart. His body was scattered across the snow, his eyes staring toward the sky.

Then it was Eun-ji's.

She had healed dozens of people during the battles, but she could not save herself. The shock of seeing Ludovic dead had frozen her in place. Her lips trembled as she looked at his body in the snow.

Aside from Nikolai and Soo-ah, in a way, everyone had always felt that there had been something between Ludovic and Eun-ji that went far beyond watching each other's backs and sharing food, campfires and shifts. But neither of them had ever taken a step toward acknowledging that it was a serious relationship, not even a platonic one. Sasha had asked them more than once if they were high school teenagers.

Whether there had been love between them or not, that would remain an enigma forever. Just another untold story.

The squad was thrown several meters through the air as a blast of wind swept down upon them.

A Shrike caught Eun-ji, lifting her from the ground and then crushing her beneath its weight. The sound of her bones breaking was drowned out by the deafening wind. Mai barely managed to distinguish the flash of her final moment, her face looking strangely serene amid the horror.

Her body disappeared from the ground, being reabsorbed into the Fractus.

Then she reappeared atop the Shrike, which had taken on a more animalistic form. Eun-ji's corpse crowned its head like a fleshly diadem before dissolving.

They had barely been able to react to the speed at which everything was happening.

Soo-ah was next.

A Xe-res attacked from the rear just a few seconds later, breaking through her defenses and destroying her mechanical augmentations with a deadly blow that split her torso in two. Her body fell to the ground, lifeless, and the snow soon swallowed it, erasing her sacrifice beneath its cold whiteness.

Mai's face had frozen. In just a few seconds, three of her comrades, friends, and already a family she had found during that last stretch of her life had vanished.

They had been through so much that, lately, she had almost come to believe they were too good at what they did. They had experience and worked well together. But in reality, they were just as fragile as anyone else.

Death came for Sasha without poetry. It did not even bother to announce itself.

The Ukrainian commander was retreating alongside Jiho toward the last defensive line, half-buried in snow, while the two of them covered Mai and the formation crumbled like a salt castle beneath the rain.

Only the three of them remained.

Their breaths were fleeting clouds, and every shot disappeared into the roar of the endless horde.

Then the sky seemed to tear open.

A Greater Shrike, one of those born only when the distortion bled, plunged from the heights like a hawk made of black crystal. Its wings folded with a dry snap and its crystalline body absorbed the light of the gray dusk.

Sasha managed to push Jiho backward.

"Run!" he shouted, although his voice was already an echo lost in the blizzard.

The Shrike descended upon him with an almost ceremonial movement. Its claw pierced his abdomen with surgical precision, lifting him from the ground like a living banner.

Mai saw the impact.

She felt the world tilt, spin, and shatter.

Sasha did not scream.

He simply gritted his teeth, looked toward where she stood... and nodded.

A tiny gesture.

A keep going.

A don't stop.

The creature tore him in half with a single movement, quick, clean, almost elegant, and his remains were absorbed by the Shrike.

Jiho responded with fury, desperation, with whatever scraps of magic she had left. Her body trembled and her fingers bled as she tried to summon another of her elemental colossi, ice and fire intertwined, rising behind her with a roar.

"Dammit...! MAI, DON'T LOOK BACK!"

But the spherical portal, one and a half meters in diameter, opened and grew beneath her feet.

Perfect.

Mai opened her eyes at the sight.

A hole in three-dimensional space that connected to a place far beyond the reach of any being belonging to a third dimension.

Jiho fell through it before she could even understand what was happening. Her magic collapsed in a flash of blue light.

Mai fired three consecutive bursts at the Shrike that had killed Sasha, erasing it, but she could not reach Jiho.

Her scream was trapped in a point where sound could not exist.

Mai extended a hand.

The opening closed with a whisper and the snow became smooth again, untouched.

So simple.

So unfair.

So final.

Mai was alone.

The silence that followed was not real. The battle continued to roar, but to her everything became a tunnel. An instant suspended in time, just a frozen breath in which breathing hurt, in which every heartbeat was a stab, and her head lowered without her deciding to do so.

She walked a few steps toward the place where Sasha had fallen.

The snow was almost smooth again. The blizzard had already covered the blood and the cores. The Fractus had consumed everything.

There was not even ash left.

But here and there, reddish and pinkish stains speckled the snow like flowers blooming across a frozen funeral shroud.

Mai wanted to scream.

She wanted to cry.

She tightened her hands around the rifle, although she could barely feel her fingers inside her ruined suit. The cold burned. The magic in the air seared and cut like two invisible blades.

The Fractus were approaching, watching the last survivor of Stepnye Volki.

Mai took a deep breath, an inhalation that tasted of iron and old snow.

"It's okay..." she whispered, barely audible, as if she were speaking before a freshly dug grave. "It's okay. I'm still here."

The wind rose, dragging currents of snow like nameless spirits. She raised her rifle, trembling, and ignited one last spark in the midst of the growing darkness.

Detonations and screams echoed in the distance. She was not the only one living through a tragedy in that place. Probably tens or perhaps hundreds of thousands of people were feeling the same thing at that very moment as their lives were extinguished one by one.

They were the condemned few remaining in that place.

It seemed that everything was over.

Alone.

Surrounded.

Exhausted beyond any limit.

But alive.

Still alive.

And in that white hell, that was enough to keep killing.

Then a colossal Shrike appeared before her, its crystalline body glowing with an unnatural light, beautiful and lethal at the same time.

That creature was not simply another monster.

It was the embodiment of the end itself, the tangible darkness and death that had come to claim her soul.

Mai tried to fire, but her hands were trembling uncontrollably. The exhaustion and wounds she had suffered throughout the battle were taking their toll. She could barely remain standing and, when the Shrike lunged at her, it seized her by the torso with claws as sharp as blades.

It lifted her from the ground, leaving her body trapped in its grip, and Mai screamed as the creature reconfigured itself and a set of jaws appeared, closing around her body.

The pain was indescribable.

Her ribs cracked under the pressure of something that felt freezing cold, and yet at the same time as though someone had poured a bucket of needles into her body. Her body was torn apart by the fury of the beast.

The Shrike hurled her through the air and she crashed heavily into the snow. She felt the ice seep into her skin, mixing with the blood pouring from her wounds.

She knew the next thing she would feel would be the spikes piercing her.

She knew she would die staring at that gray sky, turned into a crown when she was absorbed.

But that did not happen.

She was caught once again.

And thrown.

She did not know how many meters she had traveled, but her body spun through the air like a rag doll before falling into the snow.

What happened? she wondered as she felt her life slipping away.

Mai could no longer move.

Her breathing grew heavier with every passing second, each inhalation a battle she was losing. There was no strength left in her to resist.

Life slowly drained from her body, like a river slipping toward the ocean, and darkness began to swallow her completely.

The sound of the battle faded away, leaving behind an overwhelming silence.

Mai closed her eyes, feeling her body falling apart, like a leaf carried away by the wind.

She thought of her comrades, her friends, everything they had done and everything they had fought for to make it this far.

And then a thought slipped into her mind, something deep and distant:

"Is this the end?"

She did not know. She could not know.

What had happened to the Shrike?

Where had it gone?

It had disappeared, and where it had attacked, there was nothing left but a large crater.

Mai looked around. The Fractus had passed her by, advancing westward, moving farther and farther away from where she lay.

Could it be possible?

Could she still escape? With that wound and all the blood she was losing?

She could not stay there.

What hope did she have left?

She did not want to die.

Despite all the danger she had faced throughout the years, her will to live was still there.

She was not going to die in that place.

Fuck no, she thought, and, with her teeth chattering, she began to crawl.

For two long hours, Mai dragged her broken body through the snow, covering herself with the blood of her fallen comrades and her own. The snow turned red, and the traces of her struggle slowly disappeared, as if they had never existed.

The cold pierced all the way to her bones. Every movement was torture. Snow clung to her skin, the ice mixing with blood as the weight of death pressed harder and harder upon her shoulders.

Every time she saw a fallen comrade, a familiar face, she crawled toward them, searching for some sign, some answer, some way to understand why all of this had happened.

But there were no answers. Only the echo of despair.

The cold swallowed her completely.

She could no longer feel her legs. She could no longer feel anything.

Only the emptiness and the endless wait for death that still had not come.

She stopped, her eyes fixed on the dark, cloud-covered sky.

And in that moment, when she had no strength left to continue, when life itself seemed to be slipping from her body, a voice whispered inside her mind.

Continue.

Continue.

She looked around.

She saw the faces of soldiers from other units, the few who had survived until that day. They were from Raven.

Jasmiel, the freckled girl. Dead.

Tara. Dead.

Vladimir, dead too.

Where was Ludovic's body, or at least the scattered remains that had been left behind?

She must have left him behind while crawling.

She looked around again and, among the fallen, thought she could make out the faces of the others.

She was already hallucinating.

Soo-ah, Sasha, Ludovic, Eun-ji...

Jiho?

Right.

She was gone too.

Natalya?

Right.

She had been evacuated.

She hoped she was still alive.

Nikolai?

Ah... right.

That had happened days ago.

She hoped he would not take their deaths too badly.

Knowing him, he would blame himself for not having been there.

The faces of her friends and the memories of her life floated through her mind.

Miho's face appeared in her thoughts, with her eternal patience and youthful spirit. It had been more than a century since she had met her. And although they had only been together for a short time, the truth was that Miho had always remained in her mind. She had been the one who took her into her home, gave her a roof over her head, food, clothes, and work. They had only been together for a short time, but Mai had truly felt as though she were her sister.

Then many other faces and people had come into her life.

Aya, who had known her parents and with whom she had formed a long friendship until the passage of time separated them.

Rein and Noki, the Tempest team. Leon and Oxy, with his strange manias.

The face of Mimi appeared in her mind like a flash that made her though in why she was thinking about her.

Jinat, who, while seeking help to take revenge and help other feys, had led her to join Whisper. That was where she learned about the darkest side of the global fey world.

That was also where she had met Milena and Sari.

The two little sirens who, despite being so fierce, had always been so sweet to her. Seeing them together had always made her heart clench. Almost as if she had wanted to be with them. But they were so close that it almost felt like a crime to come between those two feys who had been together for so long.

Maybe she should have done it.

It was a little late for that now.

So many people had passed through her life.

But she had never truly opened herself to a serious relationship.

She thought of Takashi. She had almost done it with that man.

Too bad she had met him when he was already in his final moments.

So many people had passed before her. But now, at the end, it felt especially bitter on a personal level.

She liked saving lives. It was what she had learned to do, even if she had to get her hands dirty.

That was simply how the world was.

There were no such things as fairy tales. At least not the kind from legends.

And there she was, bleeding to death while regretting that she had not enjoyed her life more. But if she could do it all over again, would she change anything?

She could not turn back time. She had shot people when she had needed to.

She had saved others when she had needed to.

Maybe it was a little foolish to think about that at that moment. But there she was, her vision clouded and almost on the verge of tears.

Even if the world had no place for fairy tales or happy endings, like living happily ever after.

She wished something had been different.

She wished someone had been there beside her, telling her that everything would be alright.

Or had there been someone at some point?

Perhaps before she became a fey? Or in between?

A phrase from somewhere far away echoed inside her mind as she began to feel warmth.

"Thus, I surrender myself to you, all I am belongs to you, just as all that is yours shall be ours."

She did not know where it had come from.

But that phrase, spoken by three voices, resonated in the deepest part of her mind.

A promise.

To whom?

Where?

One of those voices was hers.

Whose were the other two?

A sudden flash of a green land with palaces crossed her mind.

Surely just an hallucination.

The wind carried screams and explosions from battles taking place somewhere far away. They had to be the last voices she would ever hear.

The cold was leaving her body as she felt her strength continuing to fade.

After a few minutes, Mai's vision began to blur, and she stopped moving.

She did not want to die, but she no longer had the strength to move a single muscle.

"Despite everything you have suffered, do you want to remain in this world?"

I don't want to give up, Mai thought.

So these would be her final moments, talking to herself.

What did it matter?

There was no prince or anything of the sort at the end of her story. There was no castle with children running around and calling for her. There was no husband or someone with whom she could put the final period to a story that ended with them living happily ever after.

At least her consciousness would not leave her alone.

"Wouldn't it be easier to transfer you into a new body and start over, leaving all the pain behind?"

Confused, Mai struggled to concentrate.

Where had that thought come from?

The voice asking the question seemed to come from a part of herself, as though a fragment of her essence were trying to communicate with another.

But it felt strange.

It was not coming from within her.

It was something foreign to her.

The voice spoke to her, expressing its thoughts calmly and blending with her senses, yet everything manifested as a perfectly comprehensible dialogue.

Perhaps it was nothing more than a hallucination brought on by her mind during its final moments, Mai thought.

An illusion created by her subconscious to deceive her and make her feel that, at least, she would not die alone.

That a ghost had been there during those final moments so she could die in peace. She would have liked to entertain her mind with something beautiful, a warm place filled with light where she could be with the people she loved.

On the other hand, she was beginning to feel less cold.

As the voice continued speaking, the pain in her body momentarily faded, as if the presence of that voice had given her a spark of energy.

It was true that when someone froze to death, they experienced a sensation of warmth during their final moments.

"What do you say?" the voice asked.

Transfer me where? If this place falls, there will be no hope.

"You don't have to transfer into a body that shares this same form. Life has many forms."

As though that phrase were flickering to life in some hidden corner of her mind, it appeared once again.

"Thus, I surrender myself to you, all I am belongs to you, just as all that is yours shall be ours."

I can't die.

No.

I still have something to do. I can't die yet. I made a promise that I don't want to break.

To who? Where?

The voice remained silent for a few moments.

Then it asked in a different tone.

"If you were given the opportunity to end this war, would you do it?"

Why do you think I'm here?

"Even knowing that greater challenges await you in the future?"

What are you talking about? That's life.

"You will be like a ghost for most of your life. Knowing that, do you want to continue?"

I've been a ghost for most of my life.

"Even ghosts have responsibilities."

Spending eternity haunting people isn't exactly in my plans.

"Will you let me enter your body?"

Mai's eyes were blurred, but with the little strength she had left, she looked around.

She did not have the strength to use her antennae, but that last question had awakened her senses.

Just as she had thought, that voice was not hers.

She was not having a conversation with herself.

Something was trying to enter her.

Into her mind.

It was trying to reveal every part of her being.

"There isn't much time left, but the decision is entirely yours, little one. You have the choice, and I cannot force you."

Little one...

It had been a long time since anyone had called her that.

Miho's face appeared in her thoughts.

Mai tried to understand what was happening and, with the strength she had left, decided to continue the conversation.

What difference did it make at this point?

Who are you?

"I am someone who has been on this planet for a very long time."

What do you want?

"To offer you the opportunity to change the course of this war."

What could that be?

An elemental?

In recent years, some elementals with consciousness had been discovered, but none possessed anything remotely close to the power necessary to do what that voice claimed.

Something trying to possess her, perhaps?

Almost losing consciousness, Mai asked:

If you can do it, why now? Why didn't you do it years ago, before all of this began?

"It would be difficult to explain. But difficult trials are part of evolution. An enemy of this kind was necessary for the world to change. And if this war ends, the world will have to face even greater trials to prevent it from falling back into the madness that existed before the war. Humans seem to be a species that struggles to coexist with itself, but they have hope."

As those words intertwined with her consciousness, Mai felt an impulse.

The need to get up.

She had heard shrieks not far away.

There were probably some Shrikes nearby.

Perhaps another group.

Mai thought about it.

What difference did it make in that situation?

What do I have to do?

"Will you let me enter your core?"

Mai was breathing with difficulty.

At most, she probably had one or two minutes left.

The light had disappeared from her eyes.

The voice inside Mai emerged as a whisper as she felt her being fading away.

Yes...

Mai thought she was dying.

That was when she felt as though her heart had exploded.

A sharp pain erupted inside her and she felt it spreading into every bone and every muscle of her weakened body.

"I'm sorry, this is necessary. It will only take a moment."

Mai felt every muscle in her body twisting, every cell and every neuron being shaken. But she also saw something she had not expected. It was as if she could see inside her own body. She could not explain it. She was aware of every bone, vein, artery, and organ in her body, seeing them as if she had ripped out her own eyes and placed them inside herself, watching them travel through every nerve from the top of her head to the tips of her toes. She could smell her own blood, taste her insides, hear it flowing through her blood vessels, and feel every part of her body as though she were touching them with her own hands.

She saw the faint life coursing through her entire body.

And around her heart, a crystalline green shape.

Her Shy Organ, the FAE core.

It was truly beautiful. That was her heart, with that strange shape growing around it.

So many names had been given to that thing that disappeared whenever someone tried to study it directly. Despite the impression it gave her at that moment, it also felt good. That was what had saved her so many times. Even if it was weak compared to those of other feys, it was her own core.

Mai felt her body beginning to warm as her core extended beyond her heart. It was as if the core were developing extensions and spreading beyond the heart. Little by little, her strength returned. The bleeding had stopped, and the wound in her stomach burned from the regeneration.

Then Mai sensed something else.

Memories that were not hers.

They stretched far back in time. Different faces, different souls, all seen from a perspective almost like that of a bird.

Castles in flames.

Wars.

Armies fighting.

Scholars in their laboratories attempting to create elixirs.

Young women being burned at the stake.

Battles in deserts, where armies had fought creatures that had never appeared in any history book.

But beyond those bloody scenes, Mai also saw peaceful ones.

People coming and going, trying to live as best they could in a hostile world.

Mai could feel that whoever this entity was, it had seen an enormous amount during its lifetime. But beyond the scenes of violence, it had also seen hope.

Humans seem to be a species that struggles to coexist with itself, but they have hope, she remembered.

Those memories were not hers. She was certain of that. But then, whose were they?

What space and time had those visions come from?

Were they coming from the entity communicating with her?

"W-what are you?" Mai asked as she regained her strength and used her voice. "What am I seeing? What are you doing to me?"

On the other hand, the entity's voice could now be heard inside her.

Your perception of the world is three-dimensional and linear. What you are seeing now is your interior, because I am entering your body. You are perceiving what you would experience beyond the three spatial dimensions. Your sensory abilities are slightly different because you possess certain lepidopteran traits incorporated into your genetics.

"I mean these images too. What are they?"

I cannot answer that.

"You can see my secrets, but I can't see yours?"

The entity did not answer.

After a few seconds, she stopped perceiving things that way. Instead, everything was replaced by a strange sensation across her skin.

Even with some pain, she crawled forward and tried to stand. That was when she noticed the glow surrounding her, shimmering with iridescent tones.

She felt incredibly warm.

Mai stared in astonishment as the clothes she was wearing began to disintegrate. Although the equipment still had some capacity to absorb damage, the garments peeled away from her body as if something incredibly sharp, yet invisible, had torn them apart.

Then she noticed the change in color spreading across her arms and the rest of her body.

Her skin had taken on a darker tone, as though she had spent far too long under the sun.

Her moth antennae regained their strength and rose above her head, emitting an iridescent glow.

But that was not all.

Something had just appeared across her skin that Mai had only seen once in her life.

She had seen it that night, when she had returned from the Other Side to Earth in 2004.

Patterns resembling some kind of plant motif had appeared across her skin and in several parts of her body. Almost like a tattoo.

But unlike a normal tattoo, those patterns glowed across her skin as though they were some kind of bioluminescence.

"What have you done?"

I only opened and reconfigured the pattern of your core. I think you know that the reason your core is different from everyone else's is probably because you spent very little time on the Other Side. But at the same time, it is your core that makes you unique among the feys. You came into contact with material from a higher dimension on the Other Side, and that is what makes your core unique.

"Define 'very little time'..."

Probably more than forty years, but less than a hundred.

The Other Side.

"What do you know about the Other Side?"

Only stories...

Mai wondered if giving someone she did not know at all permission to do something like this had been a good idea, but she knew that if she had not done it, she would have died several minutes ago.

If she had not accepted, everything would have been over.

What did it matter to sacrifice herself if it meant she could change the course of the war? If that decision allowed her to fulfill the promises and hazy memories from the Other Side, it did not matter.

If it meant putting an end to this horror, then so be it.

Whether it was true or just a hallucination as she was dying, what did it matter?

With renewed determination, Mai slowly stood up.

She remained completely naked in the middle of the desolate night. The plain, covered in snow and horror, stretched before her feet like a red and white canvas.

Her hair hung loose, and the blood that had stained it was gone.

"What should I do?"

Are you ready?

Mai nodded.

Can you promise me something?

"What?"

I will be with you for a very long time, from your perspective, but at some point in the future you will have to let me go. When I ask you to do it, will you? Without asking when or why, will you do it?

Mai did not answer for a while, but eventually nodded.

Your favorite weapon.

"Huh?"

I am bound to you through your core. I will take on a physical form through you. I would recommend making it the weapon you feel most comfortable with.

"Even if it's outdated?"

A weapon never goes out of style. It is the way you use it that gives it meaning.

"What can you do?"

I will help you put an end to this war, but only you can do it. In its current state, there is no one else who can. Through you, I can manifest in the three-dimensional world. Only you can use my physical form.

Mai thought.

"A bow. Can you take the form of a bow?"

Are you sure?

Mai thought about it for a few seconds, then nodded again.

So be it. Extend your arm and relax your body.

Mai obeyed. She extended her left arm and held her hand as if she were gripping something invisible.

The structure of space around her hand distorted, as if she had just shattered some kind of invisible glass.

The first thing Mai saw was a sphere, followed by a rhomboid surface containing it.

That geometric shape moved toward the palm of her hand, and Mai began to feel a tingling sensation running through her entire arm.

From that structure, more distortions resembling shattered glass emerged on both sides, their color shifting between emerald and turquoise.

The wind across the plain had begun to blow furiously from the east.

Mai felt a new burning sensation in her back.

Something was bothering her shoulder blades.

She stopped feeling the ground beneath her feet.

She was rising.

"What is this?!"

Resist it. Your core is changing to adapt to my existence.

"What does that mean?!"

Mai looked over her shoulder and, for a moment, could not understand what she was seeing.

What looked like translucent green and turquoise wings had emerged from her back.

But those could not be wings.

As far as she knew, she did not have wings.

They were ridiculously large.

They stretched toward the west and had to be three times the size of her own body.

She felt the same burning sensation in her lower back. From the area closest to the gluteus medius muscle, two smaller wings had emerged on either side of her body.

"What is this?"

Your true form. But I cannot control their size. It must be because your core is like a diamond in the rough. It has not been polished. As it fuses with your core, it is taking on a form in an additional spatial dimension.

"I don't understand what you mean."

Those wings should be part of you, just like your antennae, but because you spent so little time on the Other Side, they never developed properly. This is forcing your evolution toward what you should have become if you had remained on the Other Side for at least a thousand years.

"A thousand years?!"

Don't worry about that now. Focus on your hand.

Mai took a trembling breath, but obeyed. This did not seem like the best time to ask questions.

In her hand, the structure had taken the shape of a bow.

But it did not resemble the bows Mai was accustomed to using.

It was now black, with a polished surface that reflected its surroundings like an obsidian mirror, and the same pattern that covered Mai's skin appeared across its surface.

It had a much more modern shape, along with other parts whose purpose Mai could barely distinguish.

On the other hand, its size was something she had not expected.

That thing was practically the same size as she was and had to weigh at least as much as her.

But despite that, it felt light and cold in her hand.

The string was red, made from a material she could not identify.

Let's go even higher.

At that point, Mai said nothing more. There was no point in continuing to ask questions.

Less than five minutes ago, she had been on the verge of death, and now she was soaring naked through the sky, holding a ridiculously large bow in her hands.

What name do you want to give me?

"What?"

I need a name, just like you. A word that will allow me to manifest in your hand whenever you want.

"Why?"

I don't make the rules. Names are important. They function as magical calculation circuits.

The wind struck Mai's naked body as she continued to rise, but she did not feel cold at all.

She had to be more than a thousand meters above the ground, and from there she could see the full extent of the devastation caused by the war.

Thirty kilometers away, the gray sky was crossed by a dark mass that concealed the landscape.

The sphere.

Could it really be possible?

Could she end everything there?

Six years of war, only for everything to be resolved in that place?

"You're going to help me put an end to this, right?"

Yes. Just one arrow, and it will all be over.

"Really?"

I have no intention of lying to you. Just give me a name and the pact will be sealed.

Mai thought for a few seconds.

A pact.

Like a sorcerer?

"You're a bow. How about the name Azusa?"

The answer came immediately.

I like it.

For the first time since that voice had begun speaking to her, Mai noticed a change in its tone.

She could not say that it was exactly happiness, but it sounded different.

"My secret name is Rei. My second name is Mai. And you are Azusa."

The name is accepted. This bow will have the function of closing distortions in space-time, just like that sphere.

"What does that mean?"

That mouth through which this planet is being invaded has disturbed the structure of space-time for a very long time. Even when we close it, others will open.

"Others?"

I will explain later. For now, focus only on what you are holding.

At four thousand meters above the ground, Mai stopped in midair.

She looked over her shoulder, but those membranous wings, resembling those of a butterfly or a moth, did not flap at all.

They remained completely still.

She focused on the bow again.

Just shoot.

"Shoot what? I don't have any arrows."

You don't need any.

"How?"

Just imagine that you are about to shoot an arrow and draw the bowstring as far as you can. Imagine it as if it were real. As if you actually had an arrow in your hands, as if it were part of your own body and you were firing it.

"I can't use magic. How am I supposed to materialize something out of nothing?"

Don't try to understand it. Just imagine it.

Mai took a deep breath and tried to calm herself.

She closed her eyes and tensed her left arm while her right hand gripped the anchor point of the bowstring.

It was very similar to a compound bow, but on a gigantic scale.

As she drew the string, she could hear internal mechanisms moving.

She carefully aimed at the shadow several kilometers away.

It seemed impossible.

Was all of this nothing more than an illusion created by her mind during the final moments of her death?

Perhaps she was still down there on the ground, bleeding to death, and all of this was simply the product of her brain shutting down.

It's not an illusion. Concentrate.

Mai drew the bowstring and felt it brushing against her naked body.

She adjusted her position, trying to protect her chest from the string.

At that moment, she was glad she didn't have much of a chest.

That string, whatever material it was made from, was so thin that accidentally releasing it could probably turn her into an Amazon.

She had practiced archery for years, but aiming was very different when her feet had no point of support and the bow was as large as she was.

On the other hand, the bow seemed so light that it gave her the impression that it was made of glass.

Perhaps it really was, despite its appearance.

With the string fully drawn, Mai tried to imagine an arrow where there was absolutely nothing.

Seconds passed, but nothing happened.

She decided to relax her breathing and think about the bow.

About how she had seen it form.

Crystal.

And about what she had seen inside her body.

She thought about both things at the same time.

The material of the bow and a part of herself.

Just as Azusa had said.

She had said that her body was enough to house Azusa's existence, so if that was true, she had to be some kind of medium between her and the physical world.

She began to feel warmth in her fingers.

And that began to grow.

It was like growing crystals, except this time she was creating them from her fingers.

Light.

Weightless.

The crystals took on a linear shape that extended from her fingers to the point where the bow was supposed to hold the arrow.

The crystal arrow had a spiral shape along its central body, ending in a green tip.

Mai concentrated as hard as she could, trying to perfect the image.

If she could create it through her mind, then she had to form it correctly.

Concentrate all the tension on the target and release it.

Mai barely paid attention to the instruction.

She was completely focused on the arrow and the black spot in the distance, and it seemed that even the wind was beginning to die down.

She released the arrow.

A flash of green erupted from the bow.

The impact seemed instantaneous to Mai.

There was no resistance from the wind or anything of the sort.

The arrow disappeared from her sight after traveling a hundred meters, but the path it had carved toward its target did not disappear.

Thirty kilometers.

It seemed impossible, but if that entity really came from another dimension, perhaps space had folded or distorted itself somehow, making movement through three dimensions something direct.

For the first few seconds, Mai saw no result and lowered the bow slightly while holding her breath.

Then, in the distance, there was a flash of white light.

And the next thing she saw was the sphere contracting in on itself.

The following instant, there was an implosion.

Or, at least, that was how Mai could interpret it.

The sphere truly contracted until it disappeared, but it released an energy wave so powerful that, for a moment, Mai thought she had done something wrong.

The snow on the mountains and plains near where the sphere had been was swept away within a radius of at least five kilometers and, a few seconds later, the shockwave reached Mai.

Her body lost its balance and was pushed backward by the brutal wind.

Mai suddenly felt exhausted, and the bioluminescent tattoos on her body had just disappeared. At the same time, she felt the cold again.

The wings on her back dematerialized, leaving only the bow in her hand.

She was falling.

She saw the ground very close below.

Perhaps the snow would save her, but if she lost consciousness in that state, she would die within minutes from the cold.

As she spun through the air, she looked toward the horizon.

There was no doubt.

A gigantic cloud of snow had risen and was spreading in every direction from the epicenter where the sphere had been.

It was no longer there.

It had disappeared.

Mai managed a faint smile and lost consciousness.

The rest of what happened was history.

But it was part of a hidden history.
