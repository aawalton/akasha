“Hmm…” I mumbled, rubbing my chin as I stared at the definitely deceased corpse of Rhogann. I couldn’t help but wince at the feeling of blood rubbing against the rough beard I’d grown on my cheeks in the last weeks.

It’d been a while. It wasn’t my blood, of course. Like everyone else, I was covered near head to toe in the sticky liquid.

But it was an unpleasant sensation nonetheless.

“Yup,” I eventually nodded firmly. “He’s dead all right.”

Sylvia didn’t even look up from cleaning her black blade, sitting on one of the chunks of stone from the broken candle shelves. “A brilliant observation, Nathan.”

A low chuckle escaped me, but I couldn’t maintain it for long. It transformed into a long sigh almost without me noticing.

“Probably…should have held back a bit,” I mumbled to myself.

Not far from where I was standing, Walter winced.

The battle in this spire had wrapped up pretty quickly after my squire had halfway bisected Rhogann. The Skarned had long since been dealt with, and with Sylvia, Walter, and my help, we managed to finish off the rest of the Karvokar in this hall.

Which was a bit surprising itself, really. The Karvokar we had fought in here were a bit weak in comparison to some of the ones we’d encountered out in the Hold proper. My working theory was that Rhogann had been as much of a teacher as it had been an apparent religious leader. I think these had been the next generation of the flayed monstrosities.

But in the end, that didn’t matter all that much. They were dead, and we were not.

Mostly.

We’d suffered two casualties in the fighting. I didn’t know their names, and nobody could tell me anything significant about them. The most my people could tell me was that they had been members of small-time Orders from back in Herztal. But they had been members of the expedition, and every death suffered under my command was my responsibility.

Sadly, like every other loss we’d suffered through the fighting here in Karag Korvok, we couldn’t take them with us. The best we could do for the two of them was arrange their bodies neatly and cover them with some banners we’d torn off the walls.

It would have to be enough.

The fighting had only ended about fifteen minutes ago, and most of that had been spent recuperating. Renauld was nearly done with Healing the last of the wounds from the fight, and it was nearly time to move on. This was only the first of the three spires that our group was meant to handle. I hadn’t forgotten my responsibility to the rest of our scattered forces, around this stinking, bubbling lake of literal blood.

We had two more of these Halls to assault, and I had to assume that they were guarded by Rendarchs as well.

Powerful opponents, but not insurmountable.

The problem was, we were doing this for a reason. The entire point of clearing these spires was to search for either our missing captives or to find a way into the central tower that rose all the way up to the roof of the mountain.

There were obviously no captives in here. No Azarus. Hell, there weren’t even any rooms. It was just one big worship hall.

As for finding a way into the central spire…

I turned to face Walter with a patient look on my face. “Walter, please don’t get me wrong…but we had that handled. I’m proud of your progress, but I was hoping to interrogate that guy. These Rendarchs, they’re probably second only to this Ul-Korveth guy here in the Hold. He, it, whatever, might have been able to tell us how to get in there. We need to figure out how to progress, and we can’t spare any more time.”

Walter bowed his head, a grimace on his lips. “I’m sorry, sir. I just…”

“You saw a chance, and you took it,” I finished for him. I sighed. “I don’t blame you for that. I trained you to do that.”

“Frankly, Nathan,” Sylvia piped up from behind us, in a remarkably mild tone. “I’m not sure we can get anything out of these…things.”

I turned to her with a frown already growing on my face. “Explain.”

Sylvia finished cleaning her blades and sheathed them, standing from her rock. “They’re zealots, Nathan,” She said bluntly. “There is nothing more dangerous than a zealot convinced they’re in the right. Look how far they go to mutilate their own bodies in their search for a higher ‘Truth’. What could we possibly do to them that would make them volunteer information? Torture?” She chuckled dryly. “What tortures can we inflict on them that they haven’t already done to themselves?”

I saw Walter bite his lip at her words, while I just balled my hands into fists. I knew she was right. She often was.

I just hated how impotent it made me feel, because I knew there was something that could have gotten around this problem. Something I had explicitly failed at, that was the weapon of the person who was my greatest enemy.

Mind Magic.

“We still have to try, goddamnit,” I hissed, nearly shaking. Sylvia wasn’t fazed by my anger, merely raising an eyebrow at me.

She knew I wasn’t angry at her.

I was angry at everything else.

I…I wasn’t afraid to say that the stress of it all was getting to me a bit. The literal life-or-death fate of Vereden was resting on our success. Every time I failed, every death I let happen, and every fucking setback that fell in our path…

It threatened to cause our entire fragile tower of cards to come crashing down.

There had to be more that I could be doing.

“…I think I can do something about that,” I heard, piercing through the haze of frustration that had nearly blinded me. I blinked and looked off to the side.

There, Fade sat on his haunches with Sena at his side. Both of them were remarkably calm, for all of the slaughter they’d been in the midst of. All of the blood down here threatened to dye Fade in its color, but Sena was already most red. Still, the proof of their kills was evident all over their furry bodies, matting their fury to their muscled frames.

“What?” I said aloud, causing Sylvia and Walter to blink at me. They followed my gaze over to the two Spirit Beasts, and immediately understood. “What do you mean, you can do something?”

Fade stood up and padded over to stand in front of me, angling his head up to meet my eyes. “I mean that I think I can force these Rend-something-or-others into telling us what they know. There’s a lot of weird stuff that Taran taught me, you know. Very…situational.”

I blinked rapidly. “So, what? You can just read someone’s mind if you need to?”

“Ah…not exactly?” Fade hedged. “Look, if you can just immobilize the next one of these guys we run into, you’ll see what I mean. But I need him alive to do it, okay? Don’t kill them and I’ll do the rest.”

Furrowing my brow, relayed my Familiar’s words to Walter and Sylvia. Now it was their turn to stare at Fade with confused looks on their respective faces.

Oh, whatever. “I’ll keep that in mind,” I said before shaking my head. I looked out into the crowd of my soldiers. Some were picking their way through the masses of the dead and making sure they actually were, while a majority were taking a rare moment to rest. I almost felt sorry about my next command, as I raised my voice. “Form up! We’re moving out to the next sp-”

I didn’t get the chance to finish my words.

A loud, distinct cracking sound echoed out over what seemed to be the entire mountain interior. The noise was so loud that near physical waves of sound were bursting down the hallway that led to the exterior of the spire. I couldn’t hear them, but many of my men looked like they were shouting in shock at the cacophony, and not a few were clutching their ears in pain. I wanted to join them, because it nearly felt like my eardrums were bursting.

Instead, I took off in a dead sprint down the hallway. I was only vaguely aware that everyone else was following along in my wake, including my love and my squire.

I had to find out what was happening.

I burst out into the air of the mountain cavern just in time for the show. Across the lake of bubbling blood, one of the other spires…

Was crumbling.

Great cracks were visible in its exterior surface, growing every second that I watched. Slowly, chunks of rock that were larger than my home back in Herztal began to slide out of place, and before long, the entire structure began to collapse. However, it wasn’t collapsing inward, in a controlled manner.

No, instead, the spire was collapsing toward not only the lake…

But toward the spire I was in.

My eyes widened and my heart skipped a beat before sense reasserted itself. “OUT!” I bellowed. “EVERYONE OUT NOW! WE MAKE FOR THE NEXT SPIRE!”

Thankfully, my people followed my orders. All of us broke out into outright sprints as we stumbled out of the doorway, all the while the entire area began to tremble. The collapsing spire that had only moments ago stretched all the way up to the roof of the mountain, but now had slipped from the Calamity’s flesh, stretched a long shadow across the lake basin. It only grew larger and larger as a cacophonous screech of pain reached out across the entire Hold, originating from the far ceiling above. A massive spray of blood rained down around us as the bulk of the massive serpent overhead abruptly sagged from the loss of one of the spires holding it in place, gouging the flesh on either adjacent tower from the new weight placed upon them. My heart beat faster and faster in my chest as we sprinted towards the only source of safety I could think of.

The door to the next tower loomed in my vision…

Just in time to hear the falling tower crash into the one we had vacated only moments before. The resulting thunderous blast was loud, deep, and near enough that I felt my eyeballs vibrate in my skull from the sound. We only barely managed to stumble through the open doorway of the tower before a massive wave of fast-moving dust from the collision blew past the entrance in a near-solid wall. Moments later, a near tsunami of blood from the disturbed lake surged through the comparatively narrow entryway in a highly pressurized spray. More than one of my battle-group lost their footing and was nearly swept away in a grisly crimson tide. We only managed to hold against the wave by clinging not only to each other, but to nearly all fixtures embedded into the stone walls of the spire we had found ourselves inside. I myself was the attachment point to an entire chain of people desperately clinging to some kind of hook. I screamed from the effort as my right arm was nearly pulled from its socket, entirely inaudible over the roar of the blood tide. Equally unheard was the changing pitch of my scream as I transformed into my fully monstrous self, just to survive for a moment longer.

Then…it finally ended.

All of us to a man flopped to the wet and bloody floor, alternatively gasping for breath and coughing up blood that had rushed into our lungs.

That couldn’t be good for us; I was positive about that.

I barely had the strength to stagger to my feet when a dread-inducing sound reached my ears.

A second cracking noise, coming from right next door.

The tower we had just left couldn’t withstand all of the extra weight that had just slammed into it.

It was going to fall as well.

There was nowhere to run, not in the time we had. The door we had burst through only moments ago looked to be blocked by a boulder that had been swept towards us by the blood wave.

The only thing I could do…was huddle together with the rest of my men, and pray.

As the world outside crashed down around us.
