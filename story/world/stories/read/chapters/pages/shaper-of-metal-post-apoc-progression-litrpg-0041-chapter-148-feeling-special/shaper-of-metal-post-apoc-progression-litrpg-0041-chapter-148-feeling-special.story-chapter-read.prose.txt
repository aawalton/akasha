Chapter 148: Feeling Special

The group took a more collective break after everyone practiced with the ‘juice-ups,’ mostly due to fatigue strain on the juicers. They ate snacks or had lunch, as suited the person, while otherwise hanging out in the breakroom. People chatted in pockets, mostly bullshitting and talking about everything other than work. This was the habit of ANPs, and the veterans enforced and maintained it as if it were sacred.

Crash made it crystal clear, eyes intense on them, before anyone sat down. “Rooks, break time is break time, work time is work time, and don’t you forget it. Shop talk stays outside this holy sanctum.”

Highfive had no trouble with it. He immediately dove into sports, and then went on and on about sports and more sports. He and Crash were like two peas in a pod in that regard, soon firing off heated yet perfectly normal arguments about the most promising throwbacks, overrated stars, who would win what upcoming ‘battle’ out of the dozens of upcoming events, and all such scintillating minutiae of sports. A few others sporadically contributed, but nothing compared to the spirited takes of the duo. If Jack had to characterize them, Highfive was usually on the ‘side’ of popular consensus, while Crash was faintly mocking him with risky edge takes.

Jack had seen this relationship multiple times in his life: the conservative caller would be right more often, but the other guy would have a mountain of excuses lined up, such as bad referee calls, dirty plays from ‘hitmen,’ and so on. Meanwhile, when the edgier guy called it right with his underdogs, he’d be insufferable for days. Highfive and Crash would be exactly this.

This talk made a majority of the girls fog over in the eyes, and invariably they gravitated to another pocket to chat about what Jack assumed was ‘anything but this.’ The only exceptions were Lighthouse — who argued intermittently with Highfive, primarily — and Neex, who was listening and absorbing intently. That was until Bo more or less dragged her over to the newly-minted ‘girl’s table.’

And so it went. Jack mostly just laughed at the antics of everyone else, choosing his few comments specifically to stir the pot and keep the slight entertainment going.

Spiral sat and ate in silence, but he didn’t do it alone. He sat next to them, though whether he was listening or not wasn’t entirely clear. Jack guessed he was, though.

The rest of the practice was working in coordination as a team, and continuing to pump up their familiarity with each other. They fought enemy sims of various stripes and traded off amplification focus in differing scenarios. Jack got the tap to work primarily on shifting barrier walls, sometimes with ceilings, to close or open up space as needed. To properly leverage his Collect technique, he had this structure always interconnected in some fashion.

They practiced against enemy hordes, including mutants, but not sticking to them exclusively, so as to keep diversifying their strategies. One group of foes, derogatorily called ‘mermooks,’ was apparently common in the islands and caves. They were somehow able to be controlled by the Muties and pressed into service mostly as cannon fodder, but were nonetheless dangerous if they got close and in numbers. Amphibious humanoids with faces a cross between a frog and a fish, they had very nasty teeth and spikes growing out from their forearms. They were exceptionally fast swimmers. Out of the water, they could spit a dart-like glob of dissolving fluids as much as six or seven meters, and had a very good aim. They liked to aim for the face.

What lovely creatures.

Because Jack was more flexible on the defense than Lattice, he was the ‘forward,’ lighter protection for the others, but often had the chance to counterattack. His best moves entangled, trapped, wounded, and/or distracted enemies for the others to finish off, mostly because he had to multitask and remain aware of the whole battlefield. It was significantly easier when they weren’t surrounded and had a mostly forward engagement, as this allowed his improved eyesight to help process things.

A bit of flanking pressure was alright when he could use Fieldsense to cover those flanks, but when Solvent made the enemy hordes ramp up and surround them, he had serious trouble keeping up with everything. Lattice was more than up to the task of covering for failures, blocking off routes by generating concentrated crystal around anyone under threat. It slowed down their ability to perform offense the way they wanted to, but she allowed pivots back into a stronger showing.

Jack experimented with heating metal, too. The ability to passively damage was great and often easy to apply, but he had to watch out for friendly fire. As such, he made very glaring ‘hot spots’ to work from and ensured his allies were aware of them. Though he could hide the heat to some degree with Transmute when he wanted to, the danger to his allies was too great, so in most situations in team battles, he’d let the natural red glow propagate so it was obvious.

Overall, Jack felt both like he did well and he was scrambling to keep up with the lifelong veteran elites he was basking in the presence and often the wisdom of. He got advice a lot, especially from the ‘big three’ war heroes of Solvent, Lattice, and Crash. From tips about multitasking to pep talks about ‘going for the throat’ more and committing when he saw the opportunity, it was at times bewildering, and he had to keep telling himself they were focusing on him because he listened seriously and acted on their advice, not because he was screwing up.

Project gratitude, Jack. Project it! He truly was grateful; it was simply a bit of whiplash to adjust to the experience gap now in play. He was the experienced one during the PACCs, at least when it came to general experience and some actual field experience… that was totally reversed overnight. He might as well be running with three or four Chrome Giants at this point.

A bright point in the practices was getting double-boosted with Highfive and going to town. He literally let out a heartfelt exultant cry when they pulled off their old, practiced move, but magnified to giant size: the Slap Five move, performed on a big, squishy bug thing. A giant metal hand met with Highfive’s charging slap, and splat! It sprayed everywhere, though the crew had thankfully been protected by Lattice’s encasements, and of course, Highfive was already within his sheath.

Some of the girls gave a loud ‘eww’ anyway, but the majority cheered and laughed at the spectacle.

Solvent nodded approvingly. “Effective and coordinated force. Well done.”

He also got the chance to coordinate with Lighthouse a bit, which was fun. She could electrify his metal and even force it down one direction, ensuring it found the enemy, sometimes even if just close to the metal, as she could make it arc short distances. He basically just gave her new angles to work with, and the power was intensified due to bypassing the more difficult medium that was propagating bolts through the air.

They did a bit of simulation with Lighthouse ‘under the sky,’ as aided by the power of the chamber. The environment wasn’t real, but showed a very realistic black-and-blue bruised sky above their heads, just itching for a storm to brew.

Jack had just wound metal cording around a huge mutant ogre monster, with two ugly heads and three ugly mouths, when the tap was made to Lighthouse. She just stood there, but the sense of power in the area was discernibly amplified. Thunder rolled above, and a charged feeling enclosed them. Lighthouse herself began to glow with power, and so did the area where the mutant target was struggling in its aggregate bonds. That energy then flashed brighter and wider.

The pause was enough for anyone to dodge by then, if they had the will and the ability. This target couldn’t.

A giant bolt of lightning arced from the false sky and down, blasting the ground, popping eardrums, and sending so much force it concussed the air slightly, causing many gathered around to yelp and back up a step.

Jack’s vision was good enough to see a brief shift in the connected bolt, as it angrily swayed above the blighting bright core multiple times before the whole of it finally disappeared. He was sure it was still a fraction of a second, but she’d caused multiple stages of damage in that time.

When the flash was over, there was nothing left of the mutant at all. There was scorched ground, superheated metal, and a puff of ash.

Daaaamn.

Applause resounded. Jack joined them eagerly. Highfive whistled, amplifying it with his fingers.

Lighthouse did a stage bow and a tiny wave with one hand. “Thank you, thank you… but especially Ferro, Solvent, and Conflux. Thanks for that. I feel special.”

“Indeed you are,” Solvent said with a grin. “They may see you from a mile away, but as a team, we can make that matter very little.”

“Not just with Jack, either,” Permafrost said, perhaps a touch sullenly. She hadn’t been able to participate much for a while now. “I can stick people in place with ice.”

“I can make a crystal weak on top,” Lattice mused idly.

“I can hypnotize, for that matter,” Délight added. “In some cases.”

“Very true,” Solvent said to Délight. “The more easily dumbfounded are a good target for a boosted you, once you learn control. In fact, if we encountered mermooks, they could probably be turned completely around.”

Délight smiled. “An army of dull-witted fishmen under my command? Don’t tempt me.” Glancing at Neex, she cleared her throat. “Er, no offense.”

Neex appeared puzzled as she looked up from the notebook she’d been writing in. “None taken, naturally. Fish are very dumb, and these unfortunate devolved remnants are perhaps even dumber.”

A gaggle of chuckles answered this.

Due to the high intensity practice, it wasn’t long before Bo and Conflux were getting seriously fatigued, and many of the rest weren’t far behind, including Solvent. Jack was kinda getting there, though the efforts were more mentally draining in the conventional sense than anything. He was instructed to keep to a low baseline, as his later fatigue states were more dangerous than the others, because he had to avoid getting knocked out at all costs. On top of this, he’d need to keep reserves for the true emergency moments.

Solvent finally called a halt. “Alright, everyone. Let’s put a break on it and meet back up in two hours. We should be fresh for it by then, and we’ll finish out the evening when we putter out again.”

Jack said his farewells quickly to try to head home. Before he did, Solvent called him and took him off to the side.

Patting his shoulder, Solvent looked him in the eyes with a serious cast, though also with a mitigating smile. “Agent Ferro — Jack — I just wanted to say, sorry you got poked so much by us oldies out there today. I can confidently speak for all of us when I say it’s only done so collectively and constantly like that because we all have the same conclusion, and that’s quite simply that we’re in the midst of someone with talent that we have the… urge…” He looked off and took a breath, shrugging. “The urge to cultivate. Maybe when your skills get all crusty like ours, you sense that better. Gets hard to resist when the subject doesn’t get chippy about it, nor underperform because of it.”

Jack nodded soberly. “Absolutely, I understand, Agent Solvent. I’m honored; grateful. Really, I am. Please continue, and that goes for everyone else who has advice, too. Whoever they are.”

Solvent nodded slowly and then clapped Jack’s shoulder again before releasing him. “You’ll be right in our shoes one day, I guarantee it. Then you’ll understand it on a whole new level. And, it might be rare, but call me Owen off-duty.”

Jack blinked. “Ah, really?”

Solvent gave him an amused, yet incredulous look. “You think my family calls me anything else? Come on.”

“I doubt your kids call you Owen.”

“No, not them, at least, but they won’t be dropping that handle on me, either.”

“Ha! Called it. I knew you were a dad.”

“Oh, that obvious, is it?” Shaking his head and chuckling, Solvent flicked his head and said in a faux movie-influenced accent, “Get outta here, kid, ya bother me!” He turned away at that point.

Grinning at the exchange, Jack departed. He was looking forward to a shower and a nap before hitting up with some more training. He well understood they had to cram in everything they could before the trip, to be sharp for whatever might come, fish-headed or otherwise.

For nearly a decade in New Babylon, the fashion for a classy outfit for men was a turtleneck with a simple one-button, suit-style coat over it. Hats were common, but he wasn’t really a hat guy. Regardless, one could get custom tailoring done very quickly via automation if you shelled out the bennies for it. He had them to spare, to say the least.

Jack went with a silver suit over a dark turtleneck and called it a day. He even got his hair trimmed — slightly. When he checked it out in the mirror in the tailoring shop, he really didn’t think he’d ever looked better in his life.

Sharp as a tack, ohhh yeah.

He had to arrive early to get all the human elements out of the way first, because the arranged, singular room was otherwise not to be disturbed except by blind levitation protocols. Though the place was ostensibly private — and the bill would make that crystal clear later, he knew — Central Processing itself governed the security, so it was total and absolute. It was a place a step above the one he’d met with Inkblot once for tea. This was for private dinners between those of the upper crust, and so obvious was this that he was asked if he had any ‘authorized personal assistants’ to vet so they could be present.

So that’s how they get human service, eh? Ridiculous. I guess there aren’t any other options. It’s not like there are high clearance waiters out there looking for work.

Jack was so anxiously ready to see Neex that she communicated with him mentally before she even arrived, alarmed. “Are you okay, Jack?”

“Yeah!” He sent back immediately. “Sorry. I’m just, ah… ready for you to get here.”

“Oh!” A mix of emotions translated across the bond, but mostly profound pleasure, contrasting with answering anxiousness. “I’ll be there very shortly!”

“No, no, please, just take your time!”

Neex got rather stubborn. “If you’re feeling that way, I most certainly will not, Jack. I’m coming!”

Jack sighed in annoyance at himself and didn’t reply. Putting his hands in his pockets, he just stood by the door and waited. The room was small and gorgeous, mood-lit and quite romantic in an understated way, with a focus on the central table and chairs, ornate wood, but the chairs were well-cushioned for comfort.

No clue when we’ll be able to do this again. Gotta go all out with the Skyman charm.

The door finally opened, and in walked his breath taken away.

Neex wore a mostly form-fitting, stylishly cut white evening dress, with a diagonal wrap from one shoulder down under the arm on the other side, and a slit at the leg. The white color contrasted quite beautifully with her blue skin and black spots. Meanwhile, her head tentacles had gone full ‘human,’ as it were, completely transformed into a purple updo. She wore matching sleek shoes and had dangling diamond-studded earrings.

If anyone had ever gotten this dressed up for him, or even attempted to look this good, the memory had just been banished to the ether forever, because this blew it all away like a nuke.

‘Ka-blooey’ went Jack’s brain, essentially.

Neex evidently forgot her own nerves as she also seemed to be staring at Jack with wide eyes. “You look… incredible,” she said breathlessly, her expression one of appreciative awe, her skin rippling slightly, and her pupils thinning, drooping at the edges, and coming close to forming a ring — something he’d never seen before.

Jack cleared his throat. “Uh. That’s my line. Heh. Seriously, you are... absolutely gorgeous. You’re outdoing mere humans today.”

They both more or less became more self-aware and got rather awkward, and then got even more awkward as they felt mutual attraction quite clearly through the bond. Nonetheless, they smiled right through it at each other, happy despite it all.
