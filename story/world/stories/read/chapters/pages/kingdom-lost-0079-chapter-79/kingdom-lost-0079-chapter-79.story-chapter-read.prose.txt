The camp sat quiet beneath the trees once the last echoes of soldiers working faded. Wisps of smoke still drifted from the distant compound, carrying the sharp bite of tar and scorched wood. Soldiers busied themselves with, tending wounds or sharpening blades by firelight. No one spoke much. The aftermath of the battle pressed on every shoulder.

Hooves thudded softly against the earth as the additional troops arrived hours later. Dozens of horses followed them in a loose column, saddles empty and ready for the long ride back. Men dismounted without fanfare, leading the animals into the clearing.

Riley watched them approach from the edge of the fire. She wiped her hands on her trousers and stepped forward to meet the lead rider. "Start with Valrik and as many of the others as you can carry," she said, voice even. "Get them to the hospital."

The soldier nodded once and turned to relay the order. Teams formed quickly, lifting dead bodies and securing them across broad horse backs. Others helped the walking wounded mount up, careful with bandaged limbs and shallow breaths.

Riley stood apart, arms crossed, and studied the scene. This was not something she had expected. In the game, troops simply reappeared in the hospital after a fight, no matter how far the battle took them from the tower. Valrik had been a soldier, an important one, but still just a soldier, so like all soldiers he should be revived by the hospital.

Her gaze shifted to Thorne. He stood near one of the horses, staring at Valrik's still form as soldiers lashed his body into place. He reached out and adjusted the straps with a gentleness that did not match the man who had charged the burning gate hours earlier.

Riley frowned in contemplation. Thorne had survived the last iteration of the tower. He would have seen the healing process before, watched soldiers return whole after impossible wounds. Yet here he was, reacting as though these men had truly died and would not come back. His shoulders carried a heaviness she had not seen in him until now.

She turned away before he noticed her watching and looked instead toward the horses being loaded. The camp stirred with new activity, but the quiet between the men remained. The road home stretched long ahead, and the hospital waited at its end.

The soldiers continued their work around her, lifting bodies onto the horses with quiet efficiency. Riley paused, arms still crossed, and let her gaze drift across the camp. She had spent every day since arriving to this strange place, simply trying not to die. Monsters, the Clawborn, and raiders had kept her focused on the next meal, the next raid, the next fight. Survival left little room for deeper thoughts.

Yet the questions rose anyway. Were these soldiers truly alive? Were the Hoshin villagers alive, or Zelgra, or even the Clawborn themselves? She had generated these troops from a building, the same way players summoned units in the game. Back home, the soldiers had stood idle until given orders. What did they do once the workday ended? They retired to the bunkhouse each night. What happened inside those walls? In the earliest days they had labored without pause, twenty-four hours at a stretch, yet their output had slowly improved once rest became part of their routine.

The bigger question pressed harder now than it had on the start of her time in this place. Where was she, really? To stay alive she had shoved those thoughts aside. Except for that reckless return to her starting point, she had given the world and its mechanics little attention beyond what they could provide. It had been easy to watch events from a distance, like a player mocking poor choices made by others on a screen. Good sleep and a full stomach made judgment simple. Here she had nearly starved, slept on cold stone floors for only a few hours when she slept at all, and faced decisions while exhaustion clouded every thought. How could she expect the clear head she once took for granted back in her own world?

The old saying came to her then with new force. Every fighter had a plan until they were punched in the face. She had not merely been punched. She had been run over by a car sixteen times in a row. The wonder settled in her chest. How the hell was she still alive?

She drew a slow breath and straightened her shoulders. The horses stamped softly, ready for the journey. Around her the soldiers finished their tasks and looked to her for the signal to move. Riley met Thorne’s eyes across the fire and gave a single nod. The column began to form. The journey back to the settlement stretched ahead through the trees, carrying the dead, and the wounded. This left Riley to carry the weight of these new questions.

The quiet of her thoughts shattered when Thorne spoke.

"Riley, it seems the fire has burned down. Shall we take a look in the compound for the main stash of loot?"

Riley jerked back to reality? She stared at Thorne and squinted for a moment. The way she saw this world had shifted, just a little.

"Yeah," she said. "Grab Rayan and a few soldiers and lets move out."

Thorne paused. "You alright?"

Riley kept her voice flat. "You have your orders."

Thorne snapped to attention and turned toward a group of soldiers waiting a short distance away. Riley watched them. One of the soldiers glanced back at her and raised a hand in a small wave. She hesitated, then lifted her own hand halfway in return before she caught herself. She lowered it quickly and stormed off, confusion pressing against her ribs with every step.

She felt the ground under her boots as she headed toward the gates. Smoke still curled from the blackened timbers. Behind her, boots crunched on dry leaves as Thorne and the chosen men fell into step, the young prisoner Rayan walking between them with his hands bound. Riley did not look back again. She kept her eyes fixed on the blackened buildings ahead, jaw tight, and tried to push the strange new weight in her chest down where it belonged. The questions could wait. Loot first and then the ride home.

Thorne turned his head to look at Riley. She kept her own head straight and swiveled her eyes as far to the left as they would go, enough to acknowledge him without meeting his gaze directly.

"You, take us to the spoils," Riley commanded, turning her head toward Rayan.

With a soldier gripping his arm, Rayan led the group across the compound. They moved toward an adjacent building that stood half-burned, its timbers blackened. The structure sat directly across from the spot where the soldiers had found the boy earlier.

"We need the key to access the inventory," Rayan said. A smile spread across his face.

Thorne walked up to the charred door and kicked it open with one sharp motion. The wood splintered and swung inward. Rayan’s smile faded into a sudden, frozen look.

"Real secure," Thorne said. "After you."

Rayan went through the door without hesitation.

"Apparently no traps on the door," Riley said in a matter of fact way as the group entered after her.

The room was quite large. Several shelves lined the walls and stood in the middle of the floor, all stocked with goods. Charred wooden beams showed where the recent fire had licked across the ceiling and down one corner. Black soot streaked the planks in uneven patches. A few small windows with thick solid shutters and iron bars on the outside let in narrow slits of daylight. The bars and shutters had clearly been placed to keep people out. The fire didn't burn this building as much as some of the others.

Rayan advanced and opened a few of the shutters. Light flooded the room. The group looked the space up and down. Then they fanned out and examined the loot more closely. Potion bottles lined one shelf. Neat rolls of fabric sat beside stacks of clothes. Clay jars and folded blankets filled several crates. Riley’s mind started to reel as she took it all in, and then it stopped all at once.

"Wait. Where is it?" Riley turned and looked at Rayan.

"What?" Rayan asked.

"This isn’t the real stash," Riley continued. "These are the items they raided that their masters didn’t want. But they would have built up the valuable loot before delivering it. Corvessa wouldn’t take a few handfuls of gold or whatever. That would be a waste of their time. They would take delivery of a trunk or wagon full. So where is it?"

Rayan’s face tensed up. He looked as if he needed to use the washroom really bad two hours ago.

"Um, well that is exactly what I was going to take you to next…" Rayan smiled nervously.

He broke free from the soldier’s grip. Riley shook her head as the soldier looked to her for direction. Rayan walked between two of the shelves and pulled a rug on the floor away to reveal a trap door. Then he reached down and exposed the handle for opening it.

"Here is where it is," Rayan said.

Riley stared at Rayan for a long moment. Rayan just stared back.

“Well? Open it,” Riley commanded.

Rayan slowly reached for the handle, then hesitated.

“No, you deserve to open it. It is yours for the taking,” Rayan said.

Thorne stepped forward and said in a commanding voice, “Open it now.”

Rayan smiled nervously, then reached for the handle and gave it a pull.
