If Gary wasn’t paralyzed already, he would have frozen. A senior inspect from the clan had shown up. In his cabin. At night. A person who seemed aware of Gary’s illegal activities, and whose cultivation was at a level Gary couldn’t even fathom.

“I will unfreeze you now. Please don’t wake your friend,” Senior Inspector Whitefang said. The air relaxed around Gary. He could move again, but he only took a step away from the door.

He couldn’t run, so standing close to the door would only make him seem guilty. “How can I help you, inspector?” he asked, trying to play it cool. One wrong word could doom him. Everything he’d ever worked for, including revenge against the Network, would be just gone.

On the plus side, the stress had evaporated Gary’s exhaustion.

“Are you aware that producing or distributing Muscle Sap, whether for free or for a price, is against Imperial Law?” the inspector asked.

“I am,” Gary replied.

“Are you aware of a Muscle Sap distribution ring acting on this floor?”

Gary opened his mouth to say no.

Wait. Even if he’s not godlike enough to read my mind, his aura perception is all over me. He can probably read my heart rate. Shit. He can tell whether I’m lying. Even if he can’t, I have to assume he can.

It would be silly if a vastly superhuman detective couldn’t detect lies.

Why is he asking these questions, anyway? Isn’t he here to arrest me? Maybe he doesn’t have enough evidence. Can I escape this?

All these thoughts happened in a split-second. Gary’s mouth was still opening. “I am aware,” he said.

The inspector’s green eyes were piercing. If he was surprised by Gary’s answer, he didn’t show it.

“How are you aware?” he asked.

“Through some friends,” Gary replied. I must not lie. I must not lie.

“Have you consumed Muscle Sap?”

“Well, yes. The clan gives us some.”

The inspector’s eyes sharpened, sending goosebumps all over Gary’s body. He felt seen.

“Have you ever bought Muscle Sap?” the inspector asked.

Gary’s heart jumped with elation. He thinks I’m just a buyer! Thank God. Thank fucking God.

“Never,” he replied truthfully.

“So, you’ve never consumed Muscle Sap outside what the clan has rightfully given you?”

“I have consumed more,” Gary said.

“Oh?”

“I completed a quest at the Floor of Trials and earned five drops of Muscle Sap. I… I shared two of those with a friend because she saved my life. I was aware that it was illegal. I’m deeply sorry.”

Gary held his breath. Admitting to a smaller lie was a great way to get people off his scent—or, at least, that’s how it worked on Earth. It was a risk, but he was fairly certain that giving two drops to a friend was something the inspector wouldn’t care about. He was investigating a thousand drops.

The inspector’s gaze remained hard. Gary kept his cool by constantly reassuring himself that if the inspector knew the truth, he would have arrested Gary already, or whatever they did here.

He was deeply aware that one accurate question could doom him.

“Did you make your friend pay for those drops?” the inspector asked.

“No. They were a gift.”

“Do you know anyone who buys or has bought Muscle Sap?”

“Some friends.”

“Do you know anyone who sells, has sold, or plans to sell Muscle Sap?”

Gary’s heart sank to the pit of his stomach. Of course I know him. He’s me. And also Chloe, Asim, Bob the Cultist… He was trapped between a rock and a hard place. Revealing any of them would not only doom his friends, but also doom himself, since the inspector would press whoever Gary named for more names. On the other hand, if he didn’t answer, that obviously wouldn’t work.

A light bulb went on over his head.

“I know some people…” he replied hesitantly. “However, I’m afraid of them...”

“Who are they?”

“They call themselves the Brothers. There’s four of them—well, three now—and I know they were looking to sell some boss balls they got from the Floor of Trials. I heard those balls contain the primary ingredient for Muscle Sap. If you ask the brothers, they’ll probably know more.”

They knew Bob, who knew the rest. However, the brothers dabbled on various illegal activities, were highly competent, and they hid at the city, not in the Heaven Reaching clan. Most importantly, they came from the Tower, so they knew how law enforcement worked. Gary hoped they could evade the inspector for long enough. Without looking away, he opened his chat and thought-typed two messages: the first went to Bob, the other to Chloe.

Gary: Bob, message the brothers that an inspector from the Heaven Reaching clan is after them. They must hide immediately. If he finds them, they’ll rat us out.

Gary: Chloe, if you’re awake, go wake up Bob this instant. Tell him to read my message. We’re in trouble.

Bob didn’t reply. True to Gary’s instinct, however, Chloe did.

Chloe: On it.

Whitefang’s eyes shone. “Where are those brothers?”

“I don’t know,” Gary replied.

“What do they look like?”

Gary described the four brothers—vaguely.

“You seem to know them,” the inspector said regardless.

“We met at the Floor of Trials.”

“How do you know they wanted to sell their boss balls?”

“I heard about it from an acquaintance.” When the brothers approached the transaction warehouse, Torga had informed them—that was the first moment Gary knew about their involvement, so he wasn’t lying.

Of course, it all depended on how much the inspector pushed. Gary couldn’t avoid the truth forever.

The inspector narrowed his eyes at Gary.

“Are these brothers the people that your friends bought from?”

Gary’s heart was flooded with glee. “Yes!”

“I understand.” The inspect scribbled something on a scratch pad. “Thank you for your cooperation, Gary Steel. Is there anything you’d like to ask me?”

“Why me?” Gary asked.

“The ongoing tournament has seen a rise in the number of powerful disciples. There have been some upsets in the rankings, leading to complaints. We suspected Muscle Sap distribution. It happens occasionally, but the rings are always struck down.”

“Then, you’re asking everyone who’s winning in the tournament?”

Finally, Inspector Whitefang cracked a smile. “Not everyone. I can’t be too disruptive, but you, Gary, are the single most prominent rising star outside the families. Your growth is abnormal. Everyone has their secrets, but if there was someone buying Muscle Sap, you were a most likely suspect. Personally, I’m glad that’s not the case. We need people like you. Keep up the good work.”

“Thank you, Inspector…” Gary replied numbly.

“Now then, I have some criminals to look for. My apologies for disturbing your night, Gary Steel. I wish you a pleasant rest.”

With that, the inspector disappeared. The pressure went with him. Gary deflated, falling onto his bed like a dead balloon, but he struggled not to show too much. Maybe the inspector was still around. Cog snored as normal, despite the conversation that had just taken place over his head.

It was our fault, he thought. We wanted to put the families in their place, but we went too far. The Muscle Sap had a real effect. The families complained, and they summoned an investigator from the clan headquarters.

Fuck that. If we lost, nobody would call foul. We didn’t even cause a major upset. People just lost a round or two early. How corrupt are they, that the second the families are slightly inconvenienced, the entire system moves to justify them?

Despite his seething rage, Gary found himself drifting to sleep. He resisted long enough to text Chloe and Asim in their group chat.

Gary: An inspector from the Heaven Reaching headquarters questioned me hard. He could tell lies, but I think I fooled him. He’s after the brothers now.

Chloe: How did he know?

Gary: It was the tournament. All those Heaven Reaching disciples we sold to, who were beating family cultivators left and right, caused suspicion. He told me he’s not allowed to question disciples without proof. I’m an exception.

Chloe: This sucks, but it’s why we used obfuscation. Everyone knows Bob and only Bob. I will tell him to get out of the city immediately and hide until the invasion quest. The only other people who know who we are are ourselves, Torga, and Marla.

Gary: There’s also Alicia and Cog. They’re winning in the tournament, and they know us.

Chloe: They must resign the tournament and go into hiding.

Gary: I can’t ask them that. If they disappear mid-tournament, they’ll be screaming guilt. The clan will banish them. They’ll be wanted. If they ever try to climb, they’ll be found and captured immediately.

Chloe: It’s the same for Bob, Gary.

Gary: Bob is doing his job. He knew the risk he was taking. These two didn’t. It was our fault for bringing them into this for no fucking reason.

Chloe: What are you suggesting? That we take a massive risk to protect them?

Gary: Isn’t that what friends do?

Chloe: Call me selfish. I want them out.

Gary: They aren’t going anywhere. This was our fault, and we’re sharing the risk with them.

The reply took some time to arrive.

Chloe: I can’t believe you, Gary… Didn’t you want to make the Network pay? Didn’t you want revenge? If we’re caught, it all goes up in smoke.

Gary: You keep using revenge to convince me. Fuck that. I’m not sacrificing my friends to get it, especially when I was the one who put them at risk in the first place. I’ll make them pay, but I’ll do it right. Cog and Alicia stay. I’ll warn them about the questioning first thing tomorrow, and we’ll work together to find a story that works. If they’re questioned—which is unlikely, since there are literally dozens of people at their level in the tournament—we’ll just hope for the best. The inspector wasn’t too hard on me. He’ll be even laxer on them.

Chloe: You’re dooming us, Gary. As you wish. Find a story that works and message it to them immediately, so they can read it if they’re awoken by the inspector.

Gary: I’ll do the same for Caramel, Pop Ear, and Helen, since I was the one who referred them to Bob.

Chloe: Understood.

Gary’s eyes were closing despite the tension, but he struggled to fabricate stories and message them to everyone. It ended up taking him over an hour. By the time he was done, he was so drowsy he didn’t even care about getting caught. He was asleep before his head hit the pillow.
