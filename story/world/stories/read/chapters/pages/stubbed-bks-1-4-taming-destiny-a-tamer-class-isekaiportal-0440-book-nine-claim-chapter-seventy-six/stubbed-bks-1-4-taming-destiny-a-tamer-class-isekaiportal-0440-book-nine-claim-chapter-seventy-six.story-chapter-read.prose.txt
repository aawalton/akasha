The first thing I do when I get to the healing rooms is confirm that the woman has been restrained in a way that ensures she and everyone else are safe. She’s lying on one of the beds, her arms chained to the headboard above her, her legs pinned spread-eagle by more chains. In her mouth is a ball gag made of leather that doesn’t allow her to fully close her jaws, though it doesn’t hold them open painfully wide. Drool dribbles down the sides of her mouth as she growls at me. Oddly, I notice just how unfocused her eyes are – she’s clearly aware of my presence, but she’s not actually looking at me.

I test the manacles, noting that they are tight but not cruelly so. Nonetheless, they’re leaving red marks on her skin due to just how much she’s fighting them.

Hesitating for a moment, I reach for the release of the gag that blocks her mouth. She snaps at my fingers as soon as I pull it away, and only my quick reactions save me from being bitten. Given how unfocused her eyes are, that was surprisingly quick and accurate.

I step back, out of reach.

“What is your name?” I ask her gently.

She just growls at me, fighting the restraints harder.

“Can you understand me?” I ask in the same tone. “Nod once for yes, shake once for no.”

No response except more growling that increases in volume as I get closer and then dies down as I step away again.

When I’m three steps away, she seems to lose interest in me completely, perhaps because I’m too far out of her reach for her to bite. Instead, she turns her attention to her restraints.

I watch in horror as she begins gnawing at her own flesh, showing no signs of pain or concern for herself even when blood begins to spill onto the white sheets below her head.

Swallowing, I approach her again. The moment I step forward, her head snaps to me like an animal’s might, and the growl begins growing in volume again. Hating myself a little for having to do this to her, I use my superior speed and strength to grab her hair to pin her head in place and force the gag back into her resisting mouth. She tosses her head despite my grip on her hair, but I manage to do up the gag’s ties.

Eyeing the lacerations to her wrist, I pull a vial of healing balm out of my Inventory, smearing it on the new wounds. Then, heaviness weighing on my shoulders, I retreat to the room where Healer Thessalanor indicated I would find the eyewitness accounts that had been delivered.

Unfortunately, the healer himself was too busy dealing with the injuries the woman caused to exchange more than terse directions with me, but I saw the worry in the lines of his face at this new development. Since I feel that exact same worry, I don't waste time and instead start poring quickly through the accounts, searching for what might have triggered the attack.

A quick perusal of the eyewitness accounts offers no real clues. They describe a normal day in the marketplace. No loud bangs, no sudden violence that might trigger an episode, nothing I can see that might set off someone who is already traumatised. Though of course I don't know what she's been through. It's possible she should never have been sent to the market at all. Who knows what she's suffered in the past? And the last week has been stressful for everyone.

I sigh and lean back in my chair, rubbing my eyes.

"Lord Markus?" Healer Thessalanor's voice comes from the doorway.

I turn.

"Thessalanor," I greet him tiredly, with no little frustration. "Is everyone healed from the attack?"

Healer Thessalanor nods, but his eyes are wary.

"What happened?" he asks urgently.

I shake my head.

"That's exactly what I'm still trying to find out." I gesture at the pile of parchment. "By all accounts, she just snapped. One moment she was buying something, and the next she lunged at the shopkeeper and tried to bite his face. And then when he backpedalled, she just went wild – snapping and scratching at anyone who even came near."

"Troubling," Healer Thessalanor comments – an understatement.

"I'll say. If the pressure is starting to get to people like this, then who knows who will be next to snap?" I think privately of just how many traumatised people there are in the settlement, for one reason or another. This is an aspect to bringing slaves that I hadn’t considered, though they haven’t cornered the market on trauma.

"Have you inspected the patient yet?" Healer Thessalanor asks.

"Not yet," I admit. "I tried asking her questions, but she seems incapable of understanding anything at the moment, let alone replying. She’s also a danger to herself,” I warn. “When I took the gag away, she started gnawing at her own flesh, like an animal with a trapped paw."

"Troubling,” Thessalanor repeats, his eyes shadowed. “Would you like me to inspect her?"

I push myself to my feet. I'm not making any progress here. Perhaps being more hands-on will reveal some more clues.

"Why don't we do it together?" I suggest. "Your skills are different from mine. Maybe you'll see something I miss, or vice versa."

Healer Thessalanor nods briskly and we hurry down the corridor into the room where the woman is still restrained. He doesn't seem put off by the growling that rises in volume as we enter.

"I see what you mean," he murmurs.

I nod grimly. I hesitate over leaving the gag in place, but then decide that it would be unwise to remove it. It pains me to treat her this way, but I have no desire to be regrowing my fingers because I got too close.

Working silently together, Healer Thessalanor and I begin inspecting the patient with our respective skills. I dig as deeply as I can into her body – which is limited, given that she isn't Bonded to me – and what I find makes me frown. I surface from my trance and wait until Healer Thessalanor does the same.

"Are you seeing what I'm seeing?" I ask. "Odd traces in her body, like scarring?"

"Yes," Healer Thessalanor confirms.

"Can you tell what the scarring is from?” I ask. “It doesn't look like anything I've seen before.” Being a slave, I might have suspected past punishments – many of the slaves I've treated show evidence of their prior treatment in their body – but this isn't like that.

"I haven't seen anything like this before," he admits, which tells me just how unusual it is, given his experience. "It's almost like something has been eating through her body – like a worm through a fruit."

I nod slowly, able to see the comparison now he's made it. Closing my eyes, I dig back into the woman's body. He's right – it does look very much like the trails a worm might leave in an apple. Yet I can't detect any sort of creature inside her at all.

"Perhaps it's a parasite she picked up somewhere," I suggest, opening my eyes. "Parasites can be tricky to detect."

"Could this be the cause of her behaviour?" Healer Thessalanor asks.

I hesitate.

"I can't see any traces near her brain. Can you?"

He shakes his head.

"I would have thought that something producing this kind of effect would need to affect the brain," I suggest.

"We can see the trails," Healer Thessalanor points out, "but not the creature itself. However, there is no entry or exit point that I can detect. "

"Nor I,” I admit. “Which implies that it might be there – and capable of concealing its existence. But all that raises the question of how we intend to find it, given that bending all of our magic to the task hasn't worked so far." I pause, torn on what to do next. If it's not the cause of this after all, we'll have wasted our time for little gain. But if it is the cause, and we don't pursue it, we won't be able to help her.

Worse, if it is a parasite, she may not be the only one suffering. I think uneasily about the samurans, and the parasite several of them had been infected with. Parasites spread. The cause of her psychological breakdown or not, that scarring doesn’t look healthy. If others are suffering too, we need to find some way of dealing with it.

The problem is, I can only see one way to gain more precise inspection capabilities, a way I’ve used effectively many times before – and it's a way I'm reluctant to use without her consent unless absolutely necessary.

"Do you have anyone else who has more precise detection skills, or better abilities for finding things that are hidden?" I ask hopefully.

My hopes are quickly dashed when Healer Thessalanor shakes his head.

"I am the most experienced healer we have," he says – unnecessarily, since that's precisely why he's the head healer, "and even some of my colleagues with very precise detection skills are not as capable as I am in that domain."

I sigh. He eyes me shrewdly. "You have an idea."

I raise my brow, surprised he's seen through me so easily.

"If I Dominate her, I gain far more access to her body and would be able to inspect her significantly more closely," I admit. Privately, I consider that I'd also be able to see her internal matrix – which might be relevant, because for all I know this is a mutation caused by the manastorm, and the parasite theory is a red herring.

"Then why not go ahead with it?" Healer Thessalanor asks.

I hesitate.

"I don't like using it on people who haven't consented. If they pose a direct danger to me and mine, I can bring myself to force it – but this woman is hardly a threat."

"The people she bit in the marketplace would disagree," he points out.

"She's not a threat now," I argue.

"Not as long as she's restrained," he retorts, fixing me with an unimpressed look. "Lord Markus…if she got free, what do you think would happen?" I don't answer, and he nods as if my silence is answer enough. "And if this is a parasite, she may not be the only one affected. We need to get to the bottom of this or risk it repeating itself."

I hold his gaze for a moment, then incline my head. His words echo my own thoughts. This is a matter of safety for everyone in my settlement. And beyond that, if there's a way I can help this woman regain her sanity, I shouldn't shy away from it, no matter how distasteful I find forcing a Dominate Bond on someone.

"Alright," I agree. "I'll do it."

Just as I lean forward to catch her eyes with my own, my ears pick up the sound of running footsteps.

"Lord Markus?" Healer Thessalanor says.

I hold up my hand, listening. The footsteps are getting closer and faster. Soon even Healer Thessalanor can hear them, and he turns toward the door – perhaps with the same instinct I have that something must be very wrong for someone to be running with such urgency.

A man dressed in a messenger's clothing bursts into the room, his eyes wild.

"Lord Markus," he gasps. "There's been another attack. In the warehouse." His eyes drift to the restrained woman. “Two people this time – they’re biting and scratching like she did!”

Thessalanor and I both exchange wide-eyed glances for a moment and then we quickly rise from our seats.

“I’ll order everyone to prepare. A team of healers will be with you in a few flames,” he tells me curtly, then sweeps out of the room.

I nod unnecessarily, fixing my eyes on the messenger.

“Take me to it. Now.”
