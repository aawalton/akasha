Turning away from the colosseum without so much as a second glance, Viktor made his way to the other great project that occupied the fifth floor, the existence of which, unlike that stone leviathan of a building’s, served a purpose he could actually appreciate.

The trebuchet.

An engine of war that dominated the surroundings with the sort of quiet authority that shrank everything beneath its vast, reaching shadow. A titan of wood and iron, its long throwing arm hanging motionless above the massive counterweight that loomed just off the ground like a slumbering beast, waiting to be unleashed at whatever its master desired.

At its foot stood Kazyk, his soot-blackened apron hanging crookedly over his wiry frame. A collection of tools whose purpose Viktor had long since stopped trying to identify protruded from every available pocket, as though the gremlin had started this morning by simply grabbing whatever metal object happened to be within reach and shoving it wherever it could go.

The instant Kazyk spotted him, he snapped upright with the eagerness of someone who had been waiting all day for this exact moment. “Master!” he called, flashing that signature grin full of too many teeth.

Viktor let his gaze travel slowly over the war machine from top to bottom, lingering for a moment upon the sling, then the release hook, then the axle, and finally the counterweight itself. He was fairly sure everything was fine, but he would rather not discover otherwise mid-shot. Finding no obvious problems, he turned back to its overseer.

“How is it?”

Kazyk’s pointed ears twitched enthusiastically. “Perfect, Master. We finished the latest inspection less than an hour ago, checking every last component with our own hands. Every bolt is tight, every rope is properly tensioned, every bearing has been greased. And we’ve also loaded the projectile. If you gave the order this very moment, she’d throw just as true as she did yesterday.”

“How were the test shots, then?”

“All is well, Master. The throwing arm swung cleanly through its full arc, and the release pin behaved as intended every single time, disengaging at exactly when it was meant to. We also checked the frame after each shot. No structural damage. The counterweight guides remain aligned. The axle shows almost no wear despite the repeated firings.”

“Good,” Viktor said with a smile, before casting a glance at the boulder resting within the cradle. “You said it’s ready to fire, right?”

“Yes, Master. Ready this very instant, should you wish it.”

“So that’s the one? It’s not a test rock?”

“It is the one, Master. What you intended for the real thing.”

Celeste, he called his Dungeon Core. Have the Iskora team reached the mortuary complex yet?

[Soon, Master. They are about two ruins away from it.]

That’s close enough, Viktor thought. He turned to Kazyk and gave a firm nod.

“Let’s do it.”

At his chief gremlin’s prompt, the trebuchet crew began to retreat from the siege weapon in an orderly rush, each one of them taking up a position well clear of the colossal arm’s projected trajectory, while a pair of hulking Ettins moved to release the final locking restraints holding the counterweight in place before they too lumbered backward with surprising haste for creatures of their size.

Everything grew still.

The whole chamber seemed to hold its breath.

Viktor closed his hand around the lever connected to the release mechanism, feeling the engine’s entire waiting weight pressing back through the wood into his palm.

He pushed it.

A sharp metallic clack echoed across the fifth floor as the catch disengaged, and the counterweight dropped. In an instant, the enormous throwing arm sprang upward with astonishing speed, transforming the immense reserve of stored potential into violent motion. The thick timber groaned under tremendous stress as the sling whipped through the air in a blur almost too fast to follow, a pale streak against the dim light of the chamber.

For the briefest instant, the boulder remained cradled within the pouch, suspended at the very apex of the arm’s furious arc. And then, the release hook let it fly.

The massive stone tore free from the sling with a deafening crack that reverberated across the place like distant thunder rolling over some vast, empty plain. It hurtled upward in a graceful arc as it climbed higher and higher.

As the minions cheered for the successful shot, Viktor closed his eyes and sent his vision forward. Just like with the bolts fired from the ballista, he “flew” alongside the boulder, nudging it toward its destination—a gap in the ceiling. Through this tunnel, he made the rock go up and up, until it broke into the Domain of Light on the fourth floor.

After that, it passed through the Domain of Water, before finally arriving in the Domain of Earth.

Out over Khenemhotep’s kingdom of sand the stone went, and once it neared the mortuary complex, Viktor had it circle lazily overhead while he turned his attention downward, scanning the sprawl of ruins and sand for any sign of the Iskora team.

At the edge of his vision, he spotted a group of people crossing the desert, though not toward the great tomb at all, but rather toward the edge of the empty expanse that lay beyond it. That had to be the Daelin team, then. They must have picked up the pace after they had reached the second floor, since the two teams were now committed to two entirely different routes through this domain.

Once they arrived at the other end of the desert, they would find a hole gouged into the ground, one so massive they must be blind to miss—the very same opening this boulder had just come sailing out of moments before. And that would be their means of descending to the third floor. There was no staircase for them there, of course, but between their aeromancer and their Riftwalker, overcoming such a minor inconvenience shouldn’t pose much of a challenge.

So he paid them no further heed and went back to hunting, and it was not long before he found what he was looking for: the five figures moving through the ruins below. He guided the boulder until it hovered directly above their position, before at last giving it the order to drop—straight down.

His vision rode the stone as it plummeted. The wind screamed in a rising howl that seemed to swallow every other sound in the world as the sand rushed up to meet him at terrifying speed.

The five silhouettes grew bigger and bigger with each passing instant, changing from indistinct specks into recognizable shapes. Then shapes became people, and people became—well, for lack of a more delicate term—targets. Did anyone manage to tilt their head upward as the shadow fell over them, catching some fleeting glimpse of the death now bearing down from above? Well, he didn’t really care. He had eyes for only one single person: the unassuming figure in a plain robe, who was strolling along at the rear of the column like a man in no particular hurry to reach anywhere.

The Apostle.

Viktor willed the rock to come for his head. He willed it straight, and straight it went.

The result was a BOOM that sent tremors through the entire second floor. Earth trembled. Sand erupted into a golden cloud. A shockwave rolled outward in a visible ring, flattening dunes and scattering debris in every direction. The three Iskora adventurers, lacking the luxury of a spectral state to shield them, were thrown clean off their feet, tumbling helplessly across the scorched ground.

As dust and debris rained down for several long seconds afterward, the boulder sat at the crater’s center, half-buried in the churned earth, its underside crushed against the compacted sand, fractures spiderwebbing across its battered surface from the force of the impact.

Then it rolled.

Yes, it rolled. The boulder left the crater of its own accord, as though shoved aside by some invisible hand. It moved a good twenty paces before finally coming to a stop, very nearly crushing Rowan in the process, the former soldier scrambling out of its path with a yelp and precious little grace to spare.

And, at the spot where the stone had once rested, the Apostle leisurely rose to his feet, brushing the dust from his robes with the mild irritation of a man whose outdoor party had been ruined by rain rather than someone who had just had a mountain dropped onto his head.

He walked toward the rock without any particular hurry and tapped his knuckles against its cracked surface as though inspecting a piece of furniture.

“A boulder, huh?” he said, his voice carrying that same smooth, unbothered cadence it always did.

The three Iskora adventurers, who had been standing frozen in place, staring at him with expressions somewhere between disbelief and outright horror, finally found their voices again.

“How...?”

“It’s impossible...”

“How the fuck are you still alive?”

Funny, Viktor thought, I was about to ask the same thing.

“Just hit it harder,” that woman had told him through Ekon. Yes, that was it, the Eternal Flame’s endless wisdom on how to deal with this human Reliquary, offered up like she was doing him a favor. Hit it harder. Right. What a marvellous advice. He would love to know how exactly he was supposed to hit harder than this.

Well, actually...

As it happened, he had one more trick up his sleeve.

Celeste, he called his Dungeon Core. Is that spider-woman in position yet?

[Yes, Master. She is awaiting your order.]

This was an arrangement he had prepared in advance. One of the attendants serving Khenemhotep within the great tomb, a Jorogami—I hope I’ve finally gotten their name right—had been sent out to the edge of the mortuary complex on his order, carrying with her a triggerstone, one half of a fire gem split in two. Which meant, she should be able to use it to create a fire at the location of its corresponding powerstone.

And where, one might reasonably ask, was that other stone?

Inside the boulder, of course. Buried deep within its stony heart, packed in tight with a genuinely excessive amount of black powder.

“Do it,” Viktor said.
