As the three bird people and the naked creature reached us, I could barely avert my gaze from the soft, bare creature. It was too cute for me to handle!

“Hello, bug player!” said a dark blue-furred bird. “We’re always happy to have your kind in our town. Did you come here for trade?”

But all I could think about was the unbelievably adorable soft thing in front of me.

“Can I touch you, please?” I asked before I could stop myself.

“What? No!” the creature said and took a step back.

“You scared the Naked-Nula,” said the dark blue bird. “You may be the first bug people she’s met!”

“Um... Naked-Nula?” I asked, also confused about the whole "bug people" thing.

“I’m guessing you’re new to this,” said the bird man. “Look, the system translates whatever we call our own species into ‘human.’ So, just to make things clear—we’re the Bird People, you’re the Bug People, and they—” he gestured to the naked creature, “—are the Naked-Nula.”

“Oh. Cool,” I said, though I found the term "Bug People" mildly offensive.

“And what is this thing?!” he asked, now pointing at Uul. “I travel a lot. I swear I’ve never seen something like that.”

Oh no. Now I needed to lie. I had never been good at lying.

“This is... Uul,” I mumbled. “He’s... uh... tame.”

“Oh...” The bird man clearly wasn’t satisfied with my answer, but that was the best lie I could manage.

“But why are we standing here?” the bird man asked. “You must be tired from your long journey. Please, come with us to our house. You can rest, eat, and we’ll introduce ourselves properly. Our guild always welcomes the chance to meet another player.”

He walked a short distance to a pleasant-looking two-story building, and we followed him inside.

We sat in comfy chairs around a large wooden table, with drinks and fruit baskets in front of us. It was a nice place. And the fruit—delicious.

“So,” began the dark blue bird, “My name is Trilyni. This is Ulan and Varashka,” he gestured to his two bird friends, “and we’re from a small guild based in this town. We're called... ‘The Sparkly Sparkly Guild!’”

Then the cute Naked-Nula creature spoke. “Hi,” she said. “I’m Freya. I’m from the Order of the Phoenix. We’re the largest Naked-Nula group, based in Liliverth—the capital of the Naked-Nula kingdom of Lithuron. I’m currently running a trade survey for the Order across small towns in this area.”

She paused, looking thoughtful. “I’m sorry I got scared before,” she said. “It must’ve been the first time you saw a Naked-Nula.” Then she extended her arm to me and said, to my utter delight, “Yes, you can feel me.”

“Is this...” I murmured, unsure. “Really okay?”

“Yes,” she said, making a short bark-like sound that might have been this creature chuckle.

I brought my hand forward, excited, and touched her arm. I brushed my rough carapace fingers against her soft skin, pressing just slightly, feeling the bones beneath. Up close, I could even see the tiniest white hairs on her arm. I was just about to melt from cuteness overload when Freya pulled her arm back and bark-laughed again.

“That really tickled,” she said. “I think that’s enough for now.”

“Thank you...” I said, blinking dreamily.

“And how about yourself?” asked Trilyni, politely. “Could you introduce yourself as well?”

“I’m... Shooya,” I mumbled. “I’m here to, uh, trade. Yes. I come from a guild... It’s called... Also Sparkly Sparkly Guild!”

Yeah. When I said I was a bad liar, I meant really bad.

“Oh,” said Trilyni, clearly confused. “What a... coincidence.”

I glanced at Uul. It wasn’t like I could read his expression, but I was sure that if he had palms, he’d be face-palming hard.

“So, Shooya,” said Freya, turning to me, “we’re actually here for the same reason—to check the value of products and goods in different shops. We could go together!”

“Ah...”

Then Uul gave me a subtle slap with his tail. Fine, I get it.

“Okay,” I said. I would actually love spending time with the cute creature.

We sat some more and ate more fruit. They talked and laughed. I was mostly quiet. But they seemed like decent people. They were kind to me. I liked them.

Then Freya stood up. “It’s time,” she said. “I have many shops to visit today. We’d better get going.”

She then offered her hand—to help me up, apparently. I didn’t think twice. I literally grabbed the opportunity to touch her softness again. She pulled me up, and then I reluctantly let go.

“To the general store?” Freya asked.

“Okay...” I mumbled.

We said our goodbyes to the bird people and left the house.

Freya walked ahead, gracefully weaving through the street, and I stumbled along behind her.

As we walked, Uul came closer and whispered in a low voice so Freya couldn’t hear.

“In the general store, tell Freya you suddenly feel weak and retire to a side room. She will follow. If not, ask her to.”

“But this...” I mumbled. “This...”

“Don’t answer me,” he said, voice low and icy. “You do exactly as you’re told.”

“What? Did you say something?” Freya asked.

“No...”

“You’re funny!” Freya giggled and—to my absolute shock—put her arm around my neck.

“You’re, like, so shy and quiet!” she said. “Are all your people like that?”

“Eh... not really,” I admitted. “Just me, I guess...”

“Wow! I guess all species have all sorts of people.” Freya was full of energy. “So you’re shy. But what else? Tell me more about yourself.”

“Ah... I’m also dumb,” I admitted.

Freya stopped walking for a moment, looking at me strangely. Then she made a loud, bark-like laugh.

“You’re so funny, Shooya!” Freya said. “I have a feeling you’re not dumb at all!”

“Th... Thank you,” I said.

“So... how about a boyfriend? Got any?”

“No...”

“Any cute bug boy you’ve got your eyes—or antennae—on?”

“I... There’s a bug girl, actually. That I like.” I admitted, twisting my antennae nervously.

I thought about Jeeraha, back in my town. I’d probably never see her again. The thought made me sad.

“She must be really cool if you like her!” Freya said warmly. “You could tell me about her. But... oh! Looks like we’re here!”

We were standing in front of the general shop.

And suddenly, a horrible realization fell on me.

I really liked this person.

And in a moment, I would betray her.

I trembled at the thought.

“Are you okay, Shooya?” Freya asked. “I really don’t know how to read your kind, but...”

“I’m fine,” I lied.

We entered. A bird NPC greeted us.

I saw a small room off to the side, with a bench and table—a waiting area. This was it. I had to pretend to feel sick... and go sit there.

“I... I...” I looked at Freya.

“What is it?” she asked, concerned.

“Maybe I need to...” I couldn’t say it. I didn’t want to. I had to. But I couldn’t. I trembled. “Maybe...”

Then Freya’s eyes began to close. She suddenly looked drowsy and was about to collapse.

I caught her before she hit the ground.

“Useless dumb girl,” muttered Uul. “Can’t do anything right. Now lay her on the bench! Right now!”

I was shaking. My antennae quivered. I had nowhere else to put the sleeping Freya. I was terrified of Uul.

I laid her gently on the wooden bench.

“I damn hope that NPC over there won’t make trouble for us,” Uul muttered. “Because of you!”

“Please... don’t hurt her,” I pleaded.

“So someone’s nice to you for five seconds, and you get all attached?” Uul sneered. “It’s really embarrassing. Have some self-respect.”

I kept looking at him.

“I’m not hurting her,” he finally said. “I’m taking control. That’s the point. She’ll even be happier, okay?”

I nodded.

I looked down at Freya. She looked so peaceful. So calm. So soft. So vulnerable.

And now, we were going to control her. Rob her of her freedom.

I felt like utter garbage. A worthless piece of crap. There was no way around it.

“I’m sorry,” I whispered to her.

Uul began... doing something. Hovering over her body, his fur sometimes vibrating. He made this low “Ammmm” sound. Sometimes he smiled, satisfied.

“Do you know why I chose the Naked-Nula instead of the Bird People?” he asked.

I gave it some thought. “You... think they build airships better?”

Uul smiled. “Yes. From the data my masters gathered, the Naked-Nula are similar to the builder species from their home world. They believe they’re better engineers. But the real reason, is because of what that Naked-Nula said — She is part of a very large group in their capital — the Order of the Phoenix. A large group implies substantial resources. We need a group with manufacturing capability to complete our mission. The fact we encountered an individual from such a group is a stroke of luck.”

He beamed with satisfaction. “The path from controlling this Naked-Nula to her group’s leaders is relatively short and simple. It may be easier than I expected to complete my mission.”

“So…” I began hesitantly. “After you get control of the... leader?”

“Yes,” Uul sighed. “I will release Freya. I can’t maintain control over too many highly intelligent individuals at once. Naturally, I won’t waste energy on low-ranking individuals like her.”

“Oh... okay.”

Uul continued doing... whatever it was he was doing to Freya. After about half an hour, he seemed satisfied.

“Now I will wake her,” he said. “Not fully awake — she will be in a dreamlike state. That is important for some spore testing and adjustments. Do not speak a single word while she is under.”

I nodded silently.

After a moment, Freya opened her eyes—but didn’t move much. Just sat there.

“Sit,” Uul instructed.

Freya immediately obeyed and sat upright.

“Your name?”

“Freya Svensson.”

“Tell me facts about yourself,” said Uul. “Move your hands up and down while doing so.”

“I’m 19 years old,” she said, raising and lowering her arms. “I love tulip and lily flowers. I want a boyfriend, but I don’t have one. I like cars... and also robots. I think I’m pretty—”

“Enough,” Uul cut in, locking eyes with her. “Ammmm... Yes, yes... I think all is well. Now lay back and sleep again.”

Without hesitation, Freya lay down and returned to sleep.

Watching her like that—so empty, so robotic—was terrifying. I hated Uul for doing that to her. I also hated myself.

“All those long tests and procedures,” Uul explained, “were only because Freya is the first Naked-Nula I’ve taken control of. The next ones will be much quicker.”

“I’m going to fully awaken her now,” he continued. “She’ll be confused. We’ll need to help her relax. Can you manage that, at least?”

“Yes...” I said softly.

It took a few moments, but Freya stirred, rubbed her eyes, and sat up, looking around in a daze.

“What...?” she said. “Did I just fall asleep or something?”

“Something like that,” I said, doing my best to smile.

“Damn, how silly of me,” she laughed. “I’m feeling weird now. Like... so calm all of a sudden. Guess I needed that nap.”

“Hello, Freya,” said Uul.

“Whoa—wait! You can talk?!” Freya’s eyes lit up. “That’s awesome! Why didn’t you say anything before?”

“Because we weren’t friends yet,” Uul replied smoothly. “But now I’d like to be your friend. Is that something you’d like?”

“Yes!” Freya grinned. “You look cool. And fluffy! I’d love to be your friend.”

Fluffy? Maybe. But cool? He was definitely not.

“I’m glad to hear that,” said Uul. “The truth is, Shooya and I aren’t actually part of any guild. We’re on a very important mission. We need to bring my masters here from another continent—so they can bring peace and prosperity to your world. We’d like to ask for your help. Would you be willing to help us, Freya?”

“Yeah!” she said, eyes shining. “It sounds like a really important mission! I’d be happy to help. We’re friends, after all!”

“Very good, Freya,” said Uul. “I think you’ll be an important part of our team.”

“I hope so,” Freya said, blushing. “I’ll definitely do my best.”

Freya was obedient now—too obedient—but she also seemed genuinely calm and happy. I guess Uul hadn’t lied about that. I could only hope he would finish whatever he was doing with her quickly... and release her without causing any lasting harm.

“Could you tell us more about your group?” Uul asked. “The Order of the Phoenix?”

“Sure,” Freya said brightly. “We have around 150 members. Our leader is John Smith—he’s really smart and also very nice. His second-in-command is Felix—he’s super cool and also single.” Freya blinked, catching herself drifting off. “I focus more on trade and finance, so I can tell you more about that side of things.”

She shifted slightly, slipping into a more serious tone. “Our income comes mostly from trade and quest rewards, though part of it is generated from investments and small side ventures. Our weekly cash flow is around 500 gold. Expenses run about half of that on salaries and supplies, while the rest goes to costly magical equipment, weapons, and investments. We hope to expand trade into the Rishuan kingdom, which is currently weak, but we’ve identified substantial opportunities — primarily in pelts, furniture and—”

“Thank you,” Uul interrupted. “And what about military power?”

“Oh,” Freya blushed slightly. “I don’t know all the details, but from what I understand, the core fighting force includes around 40 members at levels 3 and 4. They specialize in ranged combat, melee, or magic. Most are equipped with rare or custom magic gear and are considered extremely powerful.”

“But we don’t operate as a standing army. Most military actions are done in cooperation with the king’s forces. We’re cautious and strategic—only engaging in battles we know we can win.”

“So far, we’ve been very successful. Thanks to our efforts, King Azumuth has expanded his territory by nearly 50%. The Order of the Phoenix has now been formally recognized as part of the king’s elite task forces. Felix was even ranked as a general recently.”

“Very good,” said Uul. “I’ve been thinking that we should all go to Liliverth for a visit. We could meet this John Smith and Felix. They too could become my friends. I believe they could be most helpful in accomplishing our mission. So tell me, Freya—what do you think of my plan?”

Freya considered this for a long moment.

“It’s a bad idea,” she finally said. “A very very bad idea. You should not go to Liliverth.”

“What!?” Uul’s voice cracked with genuine surprise. “Explain.”

“Your masters—they’re the Controller species from the continent north of here, right?”

“Yes, they are...” Uul sounded off-balance. I’d never heard him speak without a mountain of self-confidence. “How the hell did you know that?”

“John Smith said you’d come,” Freya said, a little offended by Uul’s tone. “He knew. He said the Scorpion Dogs were just the first wave—and that another invasion by Controller species was inevitable.”

“What!? Expecting us?” Uul was now furious. “How can this be!?”

I was secretly delighted to see him lose his composure—but then I looked at Freya. She looked miserable, like she had failed him somehow, and that weighed heavy on her. Yet she pushed through.

“You shouldn’t go to Liliverth, Mr. Uul,” Freya said, her voice trembling. “They’re ready. They have systems and procedures in place. Surveillance methods. They’d detect you the moment you entered. They’d suspect you immediately. You wouldn’t even reach the palace—they’d shoot you from afar.”

Uul went silent. He was thinking.

“I have a calm spore I can spread,” he said quietly. “It’ll make everyone who breathes it more relaxed, trusting... less suspicious.”

“They’ve prepared for that too,” Freya said. “If anyone notices an unusual change in group mood, they trigger a special alarm. Every member goes on high alert, and they’ll hunt down the source.”

“It seems...” Uul said slowly, “my masters may have... miscalculated the intelligence of certain individuals.”

He fell into deep thought for a while.

“Freya,” he finally said, “I need to make friends with a large group of Naked-Nula. A group with manufacturing capabilities—but not too powerful and vigilant like the Order of the Phoenix.”

“There are more Naked-Nula groups,” Freya replied, “but most are small and disorganized. Not suited for large-scale projects. Except... maybe the Band  of the Stormcrows. They're based in a small town, in another Naked-Nula kingdom farther south. They’re definitely resourceful, and they got this policy against monster hunting for XP—so they’re all relatively low-level.”

“Resourceful and low-level?” Uul echoed, interest piqued. “That sounds ideal.”

“But...” Freya hesitated, “I’m not sure it’s a good idea.”

“Why not!?” Uul snapped. “What now?”

“There are rumors,” she said. “Stories. Despite their low level, they’re said to be scarily clever. There’s talk of a terrifying poison gas mage who can turn air to poison and wipe out soldiers in seconds. Their leader is supposed to be a ruthless killer girl. Some say they train monsters and work with goblin armies. Honestly, I don’t know what’s true and what’s just myth.”

“Ammmmm,” Uul hummed. “Specific powerful individuals aren’t necessarily a problem... if I take control early. We will be calculated, alert, and careful. Nevertheless, we will proceed.

Besides, I want that poison-gas mage under my control.”
