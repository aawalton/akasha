Josephine stumbled back, her face pale. One of the knights caught her, doing his best to hold her steady, though her trembling legs made it a challenge.

“W-w-why does a Sanctum have traps?” she muttered, more to herself than anyone else. “This is a holy place. Who… who would do such a thing?”

I chuckled softly, brushing a loose strand of brown hair out of my face and tucking it back into the ponytail I’d tied. “You forget who ruled the city this Sanctum belonged to.” The flame of lunar energy floating above my palm drifted forward, lighting the room with cold moonlight as it sank into the shadows.

The priestess gasped. Several others shuddered audibly.

Hundreds of bodies hung from the vaulted ceiling, swaying faintly in the stale air. Others were chained to the walls, their wrists and ankles nailed through bone and flesh in cruel and unnatural positions. Nothing moved, save for the occasional drop of blood falling with a quiet splash into the water pooling around our feet.

Not water, I realized. Blood.

I looked down at my feet, where I had thought I stood in a shallow puddle, and lifted my boot. A dark scarlet smear clung to the sole. In the gloom, the blood had appeared black, easily mistaken for water.

“By the Gods,” I whispered. There was death, and then there was madness. This scene… this was madness made manifest.

“Do you think the Silverwaters did this?” Dralos asked, covering his nose with a handkerchief, his brows knit together in a look of horror.

I shook my head. “Unlikely. The Baron was cruel, but he wasn’t... this.” I gestured toward the corpses, unable to find the right words.

“The progenitor, then?” he whispered low enough that Josephine wouldn't be able to hear, though I doubted she was paying attention to our words amidst the horrific depiction of death.

“Just as unlikely. Progenitors see us as insects," I responded in a similarly hushed voice. "This looks like a feeding ground for something that feeds on the living.”

Dralos stepped back involuntarily, eyes wide. I didn’t. Instead, I took a breath, shuddered at the smell, and stepped into the chamber. Flashes of what had happened in Ordite, what we'd done to the town of Albridge flashed before me, nearly causing me to miss a step and trip. I violently shook the memory away, locking it into the corners of mind mind with other similiar memories and focused. My heart and mana cores ignited, roaring to life as they fused and emitted powerful Shen energy into a sphere of Authority around me.

Arrows tipped with purple fluid shot at me from every direction, but within my Authority’s sphere they crumpled and dissolved before coming close. Energy blasts and mana strikes, enough to threaten bronze-realm knights, fizzled to nothing against my protection. While the others at the entrance heaved or clutched their mouths, I walked toward the tome, ignoring the chaos as best I could and focusing on the Cultivation Doctrine.

First, retrieve the Doctrine. Then I can consider whether to deal with whatever did this.

The tome lay atop a stone pedestal, bound shut with a thick chain etched in prayer script, just like the metal that lined the entry's chained columns. It clinked softly as I lifted it. I slipped the book into an inner pocket of my cloak, careful not to let the chains brush against my skin. I didn’t want them draining my mana like the column chains had.

“Lady Lilliana, is that it?” Dralos called out, still not having left the relative safety of the entrance.

“I don’t feel any energy or mana from it. Hard to say without studying it further.” I looked around with a grimace. “But I don’t intend to study anything in here.”

The draconian nodded. “Agreed.”

I had taken only a few steps when a voice rasped from the depths of the gore-soaked chamber. It was faint, nearly lost beneath the steady drip of blood and the sticky squelching of my boots.

But I heard it.

And something in me, some part of Lilliana, reacted. Recognized it.

“...Lilly?”

An emotion stirred within Lilliana that I didn't understand. It felt similar to pity, but not exactly. I shook my head, banishing the thoughts of emotion, and searched the chamber until the voice came again.

“...Lilly? Is... that... you?”

Pinned to the far wall was a man, nailed there in brutal fashion. Nearly ten mana-embedded stakes held him in place. Only his head remained free, lolling weakly to one side as life drained from him.

A name surfaced on my tongue. “Raphael.”

Then a word.

Brother.

I didn't recognize him in any personal sense, having never seen him before. I’d never even seen a painting of Lilliana’s siblings. But as I stepped closer, the emotion within Lilliana’s soul swelled, confirming his identity.

He even looked like her. Or, at least, how she had looked before our merge.

His thick brown hair was matted and damp, framing sharp, bloodied features. His body—bare and broken—hung limp, sweat and blood soaking every inch. His eyes remained shut, his lips barely parting as he whispered her name again and again like a prayer.

He was young. No older than his mid-twenties. Perhaps younger.

“Does anyone here have healing abilities?” I called out to the knights. Some were still recovering from the chamber’s stench, but the rest stood ready to help. Two golden-clad knights stepped forward. I pointed to Raphael. “Heal him.”

The first was a woman with bright blue eyes and a grim expression, lips pulled into a tight grimace. The second, a lithe man with refined features, moved with the grace of a noble predator, a stark contrast to his relatively soft, rounded features of a rich upbringing.

I approached the first spike, driven through Raphael’s right hand, and pulled. He didn’t so much as flinch.

That's not a good sign, I thought, frown deepening.

My jaw flexed as I tamped down Lilliana’s growing rage and yanked another from his left hand. One by one, I removed the stakes. His arms. Torso. Legs. Feet. Nearly a dozen in total.

Green and white healing light flowed from the knights, bathing Raphael’s wounds. They worked in silence, pouring mana into him even as I peeled away the metal holding him together. When the final spike clattered to the floor, Raphael's full weight sagged into me. I brought him down gently, his body weighing half what it should.

The medical knights collapsed, exhausted from mana use. I resisted a flinch as they knelt in what they likely had not yet realized was blood.

“Good work,” I said, shifting Raphael's weight and draping one of his arms over my shoulder. I took only a few steps before two broad-shouldered knights entered the chamber, easing him from me and helping the healers to their feet. If they'd realized they'd been kneeling or sitting in blood, neither of the med-knights said anything. “Let’s get out of here.”

Once we exited the Sanctum’s underground, I ordered the entrance resealed. An earth-core knight pushed energy into the ground, burying the entrance beneath four feet of solid earth. With luck, it would keep the creature from returning. Or, if it was down there, keep it from increasing its kill count.

We traveled about a mile from the Sanctum before stopping at a flat patch of land between a ruined building.

This must have been some type of courtyard, I figured.

“Clear the area,” I ordered. “We make camp here. Set up tents. Once everything’s in place, we’ll rotate watch in groups of three.”

The knights saluted and got to work. The clearing was mostly free of larger debris, though the smaller rubble needed removal to make way for the tents.

“Carla and Devon," Dralos said, motioning to Raphael. “Keep him stable the best you can. Caleb, Eric, lay him here.” He pointed to a thick sleeping bag he pulled from a storage ring. It wasn’t much, but it would do until we had a proper medical tent. If Raphael survived that long. The two burly knights who'd taken Raphael from me in the Sanctum's underground laid Raphael on the sleeping bag even as another pair of knights began to set up a tent next to him.

“What do you think that thing was?” Carla asked, glancing at Devon. They were both already kneeling beside Raphael, their palms outstretched with white and green light.

“No idea,” Devon replied. “Caleb?”

The burly knight stretched, rotating his neck with a sharp cracking sound. "Ain't got a clue."

“Hard to say," Caleb said, grunting as he repositioned Raphae's body to be within the sleeping bag. "With the city in ruins, any number of beasts could have wandered in from the Misty Veils.” His voice was gravelly, deep-set brown eyes flat and exhausted, devoid of the usual golden glow that Alistar Knights normally had.

“I heard a Beast King lives in the Veils,” Devon added as he ran his glowing hands over Raphael’s body. “Could’ve been that.”

“The Beast King is dead,” I interjected, and I sat on the damp earth beside Raphael. “It couldn't have been that.”

An awkward silence followed until Carla finally broke it, leaning back with a tired sigh. “My lady, who is this man?”

I exhaled slowly, staring up at the early afternoon's overcast sky. “My brother. Raphael Silverwater.” Their shocked faces earned only a faint smile from me. “We’re in House Silverwater’s capital. Finding a Silverwater heir in its Sanctum shouldn’t be a surprise. I would even venture to say it was the least surprising aspect of our day so far."

But…” Devon began, his hands stilling above Raphael’s chest, the torrent of healing mana being poured into the Silverwater boy never ceasing, even as he looked up at me questioningly.

“Isn’t he the heir?” Carla asked, straightening herself to rejoin the healing effort.

I raised an eyebrow and gave a dry laugh. “I’m not sure he is anymore. If he were, what is he doing here?”

The question settled over us, heavy and unanswered.

“What’s wrong with him?” I finally asked.

Carla leaned over him, waving her hand over different parts of his body as she spoke. “We’ve mended most of the bones,” she said. “But he still has a lot of internal bleeding. Blood in his lungs. Tears in nearly every muscle. If we’d found him even thirty minutes later, he would have drowned in his own blood.” She hesitated, then furrowed her brow in concentration. “And… there’s a spell. On his mind. We ignored it earlier to address his physical wounds, but I don’t think he’ll ever fully wake while it’s active.”

“Tell me how to break it,” I said, shifting closer.

Carla stopped me with an immediate shake of her head. “No offense, my lady, but if you force it, you’ll shatter his mind. Right now, his mind is like fractured glass, and even under normal circumstances, mind magic requires finesse. We need to identify the pattern in the spell and unravel it without damaging him.” She gave a small, reassuring smile. “Fortunately, that’s why I’m here.”

I blinked. "You're a mind mage?"

She nodded. “Yes, Duchess. More accurately, I am a healer who specializes in mind magic." She smiled wryly. "I may not be the most powerful mind mage in Lysoria, but I am second to none with it comes to the Alistar Duchy. I was assigned to your troop in case we encountered any mental warfare."

I didn’t return the smile. I only gestured for her to continue Raphael's treatment.

Mental magic.

I’d need to speak with her more about that once Raphael was stabilized. Perhaps she could help Nasq as well.
