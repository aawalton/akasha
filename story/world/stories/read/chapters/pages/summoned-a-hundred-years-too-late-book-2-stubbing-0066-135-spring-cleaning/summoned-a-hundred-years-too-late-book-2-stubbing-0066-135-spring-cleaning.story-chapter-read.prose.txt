Alexandra wasn’t too sure how to approach this one other than sheer repetition. She stood up, borrowed a few small apples from Zeynep, and got to work.

She started with two. Two was easy. She’d never juggled before, but it would take more than two measly apples to stop her.

Three took longer. The first two throws and catches went well, but she didn’t understand how to keep going. Her mind froze when she caught the last one. She was getting lost, stopping mid-action. Holding that pattern for five minutes was going to be difficult.

Still, the good news was that her high dexterity made it hard for her to actually miss. She was catching the apples. It was just a matter of focus.

With that in mind, she added another apple. Then, when she noticed it wasn’t harder, a fifth.

Five apples went up fine, and she could still catch them, though she needed to throw harder, allowing for a longer airtime.

She tried again. And again. Each time, the same thing happened. She'd get three or four rotations in, feel good about it, and the moment she thought I’m doing it, her hands stopped listening to her. An apple would hang in the air a beat too long before she remembered to catch it. Another would stay in her hand for a second, breaking her rhythm.

She was forgetting to keep going.

Alexandra picked the apples up, checked for bruises, and stood there a moment before starting over. This time, instead of watching her hands, she started counting out loud. One, two, three, four, five, one, two. She matched each number to a throw. No thinking about whether she was doing it right. Just the count.

The apples flew in an arc over her head.

She lost track of how long she’d been going, but the number in her head kept cycling.

“What are you doing?” Willow’s question threw her for a loop, and her sequence collapsed.

The house they settled on was a narrow two-story building, a five-minute walk away from the town gate. Mostly made out of wood, it had a polished stone base and was adjacent to the walls that surrounded Balrum.

It was small. A single room downstairs that had to serve as kitchen, a ladder leading up to a loft where three beds had been shoved against the slanted ceiling. The furniture was old, but it had the merit of being there. There was a table with four mismatched chairs, a stone oven, and shelves already lined with dusty plates.

“We really could stay with Zeynep,” Willow said, passing a finger on the dust-covered table with a grimace.

“You could,” Zeynep said. “I really don’t mind. Renting in Balrum isn’t cheap. Most people here own. This is a whole haul of elemental sources down the drain.”

After a short trip to the market to buy supplies, Alexandra rolled up her sleeves and grabbed a rag.

She started with the table, working through the dust in even strokes, rinsing the rag in a bucket when it turned gray, wringing it out, and going back over the same boards until the water ran off clear.
