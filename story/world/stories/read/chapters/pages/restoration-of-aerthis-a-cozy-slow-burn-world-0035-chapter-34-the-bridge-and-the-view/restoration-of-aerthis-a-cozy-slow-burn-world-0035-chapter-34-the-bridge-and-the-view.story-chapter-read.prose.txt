The road stretched quietly beneath Elynara’s boots, each step a steady drumbeat against hard-packed earth.

Her thoughts turned from worries to relief.

The walls of Oltenar had vanished hours ago, leaving only open fields and the slow roll of distant hills. Wind pressed at her sleeves, clean and cool, and for the first time in weeks there was no errand list tucked in her mind. No delivery work, or requests waiting to fill.

Just the walk. Just her.

At first her thoughts circled back to cracks she had been charting – back to the thin threads of mana, the shrinking clusters of spirits, the weary places on the map she couldn’t stop marking. The image hovered at the edge of her mind, all dim trails and scattered dots.

She let it all drift by.

The air smelled of damp earth and the faint sweetness of new grass. Sunlight pressed warm against her shoulders.

With each breath, the tension eased enough for another thought to surface: not what was broken, but what could be fixed.

Her Plan, as it stood, was still small enough to fit on a single page. Tend spirits. Help Mina grow into her gift. Teach others when the chance came. Nothing flashy. No grand theories. Just three lines and a lot of walking.

Yet even that tiny plan gave shape to her days already and promised more to come.

She tapped her staff lightly on the road to match her stride.

The rhythm steadied her, pulled her back to the simple act of moving forward. Each sound echoed against the stillness as her thoughts churned.

Spirits needed mana. So did everything else. Even her. Every flicker of flame, every shaping exercise she practiced was a withdrawal from a dwindling well.

Necessary, yes. But still a cost.

Maybe that was the point.

Spirits didn’t only shine because of what was poured into them; they fed it back, changed it into more. Mana into soil, into plants, and back into the land again. In that exchange, the world stayed alive.

If the land grew stronger, she suspected the mana would too.

It was only a guess. The System’s tiny lines and numbers hinted at the same pattern. Her own cycling practice confirmed much the same however. And it made obvious to her how even her own body, her life, consumed some of the mana she produced. Not much. Just enough to sustain itself.

Elynara might have made more. But as for the others?

It would rarely be enough to share.

Her oldest memories stirred faintly at the edge of thought, nudging her towards something. Scribbles about saturation points, thresholds of growth, margins she had once studied, in her life before this life.

But the pieces were too scattered to use. Their details blurred beyond cognition.

Elynara exhaled sharply through her nose and shifted her grip on the staff. Another puzzle. Another half-answer she couldn’t apply yet.

She needed another idea.

A shadow flicked across her face. She looked up.

Starpuff drifted just above her head, moving through the air in slow pulses. The puffball’s glow was muted in daylight but still visible, a soft glimmer weaving lazy arcs. It tilted once in her direction, then spiraled ahead before dipping back to hover by her shoulder.

“You’re enjoying the quiet too?” she asked.

It trilled softly and spun once, a single tight loop.

Elynara smiled.

“Good. Keep watch, then.”

The road narrowed as it rose toward a low ridge. Wildflowers lined the ditch, tiny splashes of color against the green. She slowed enough to run her fingers over a spray of blue petals in passing, then continued up.

At the top of the rise, the ground leveled. A wooden bridge crossed a shallow stream ahead, its railings silvered from weather. The sturdy boards creaked faintly under her first step, the sound of it sharp in the quiet. She stopped halfway across and rested her staff against her shoulder.

Below, the water ran clear over polished stones. Tiny darting shapes flashed between them. Mana threads shimmered faintly at the edges of her vision, flowing along with the current. Not much. But alive.

She breathed in, slow and deliberate. Just enjoying the sight for a moment.

Starpuff floated down and perched on the bridge rail, bouncing up and down in unrestrained joy. Its glow reflected faintly off the water.

“See anything?” she asked quietly.

It tilted its head, then bobbed once more, giving no answer she could read.

Typical.

She stayed a moment longer, letting the cool air from the stream wash over her skin. Her fingers brushed the railing. The wood was rough and warm under her palm, worn by countless crossings before hers.

Then she straightened up, stretched long enough to feel the shiver run down her legs and arms, and moved on.

The path beyond the bridge curved gently to the left, climbing again toward a stretch of scrub and low trees.

Beyond that, more horizon. More quiet. More chances to think.

Her staff struck a steady beat against the dirt. Her heart matched it.

The Plan was small. But it existed. Each step here was part of it, even if it didn’t feel like progress. The shrines would need tending. The land would need mending. And when she reached the ruins at the edge of the Deadzone, she would be walking in with open eyes.

Another idea would come. It always did when she kept moving.

Starpuff zipped ahead again, leaving a faint curl of light in its wake.

She followed.

And just as her thoughts started wandering places again, something soft and fast smacked into her temple. A bright trill followed, far too smug for coincidence.

“Rude,” she muttered, swatting at the air.

Starpuff bobbed just ahead on the empty road, a faint shimmer of little puff pulses. It hovered there, daring her to follow. With no carts or hoofbeats around, and only grass whispering in the wind–

The temptation to give chase rose before she could stop it.

“Fine,” she said. “But only for a bit.”

Mana gathered under her palm with the ease of practice. She shaped it into a crude lasso and flicked it forward. The loop snapped wide, then wavered. Starpuff zipped neatly around it, trilling something that could only be laughter.

“Show-off,” she hissed, drawing another breath.

This time she tried fire. A butterfly of flame bloomed between her fingers, wings flickering unevenly. It dissolved before the shape could hold, but Starpuff dove after it anyway, twisting into a tight spiral. Sparks scattered harmlessly across the dirt.

She tried mist next. It curled into translucent wings, wobbly but delicate. She pushed the shape forward, coaxing it to flutter.

Starpuff shot through with a triumphant pop and spun a circle around her head.

“Seriously?” she called.

It trilled again, unbothered.

Her next few shapes grew stranger – arches, double loops, a spinning ring of water droplets. None lasted long. Starpuff slipped through every single one, faster each time. The road filled with her small spells and its gleeful warbles, two sets of play weaving down the long, empty stretch.

She laughed in spite of herself.

The sound carried far, unbroken by walls or roofs. Each breath pulled cool air into her chest, cleared the last weight of her earlier thoughts.

Another arc. Another dodge. Another spark scattering across the dry earth.

Her staff clattered gently as she leaned it against her hip, then tried a more complex pattern, weaving both water and fire threads into a single spiral. It flickered, folded, then held a heartbeat longer than the rest.

Starpuff swooped and burst through the center with a flare of green light.

Elynara gave up.

Her knees bent, and she dropped onto the grass at the road’s edge, satchel sliding to rest against her hip. She pressed a palm to her forehead, breath coming faster now.

“Enough… enough,” she wheezed between laughs. “You win.”

Starpuff hovered, spinning one last slow loop before drifting down. It landed squarely in her hair, sloping just across her forehead. Warmth spread faintly from its small body, a subtle hum against her scalp.

“You’re impossible,” she said, still smiling.

The puff trilled once, softer this time, and settled in.

The wind shifted. Grass brushed against her boots. Far off, a bird called across the fields. The long road stretched empty ahead, but it no longer felt lonely. Her pulse steadied, and the hum of mana inside her calmed with it.

She pulled her satchel closer and opened the flap. Her fingers found her notebook. The cover bore slight smudges from too many hurried sketches. She flipped it open to a blank page, pencil poised.

A few quick lines captured the road, the bridge she had just crossed, and a tiny puffball scribbled jumping through loops far too large. Next to it she wrote: “Shaping drills in motion – improving control?”

Starpuff peeked at the drawing, tilted its head, then made a sound she could only interpret as a snort.

“It’s accurate,” she told it.

Another soft trill followed, and she decided to put the notebook away for now.

A moment later, Elynara stretched out in the grass, arms propped behind her as she leaned back, gaze wandering toward the pale blue sky.

Her chest rose calmly, the edge of worry had softened now. The laughter made it fade away, and the earth under her palms felt steady again.

The fields around her spread in soft swells of green. Not lush, though. The stalks were thinner than she liked. Than what her memories told her was healthy.

Flower patches dotted the hillside but looked too sparse, petals dulled around the edges. Even the trees at the distant curve of the rise carried tired leaves – green enough, but not full. Everything was stretched, and a little too pale. Still living, but strained.

Her fingers curled slightly into the grass.

This wasn't new.

She had seen it in other places too. The same dull tone. The same thinning where mana should be feeding the land. But seeing it here, where the road had felt almost free from worry... it pinched more than she expected.

Starpuff hovered near her knees now, wobbling gently, no longer smug or spiraling. Its glow stayed faint, steady. Even it seemed to feel the shift in the air.

Or just followed her mood like it was a game of copycat.

Silly. It made her smile return however.

So perhaps not so silly after all.

Elynara sat up again. Her eyes scanned the treetops, the rise, the brush beyond the ditch. No spirits to be found. Not even a flicker.

And then the thought landed with unusual clarity.

Sharp, and entirely hers.

What if she could do what spirits did?

The question hit with the force of a spell, cutting past the usual tangle. She pondered it before, if briefly.

But what if it wasn’t only about giving mana to spirits… but about feeding the land directly? Without going through the spirit intermediaries? Just pushing energy into the soil beneath her boots, into roots and stems and seeds waiting in the dark.

What if she could help it grow?

Her mind spun. Spirits gave mana and the world bloomed. She had seen it, again and again. And in turn, the world fed mana back. A loop. An exchange. Was there a way to insert herself into it?

Not just as a supplier of mana, but as a part of the pattern?

She didn’t know.

And that excited her.

The idea burned brighter the longer she sat with it. She could barely shape a mist with a lot of focus now. Her fire butterflies fared much better, moving and staying as long as she kept her focus sharp.

But this felt different. Less about perfection, or elemental tricks, and more about intent.

Wasn’t that what spirits responded to most anyway? Intention? Connection?

Her hand pressed flat against the grass. It barely stirred under her palm. No pulse or hum of mana she could feel. But her mana stirred in response to her thoughts.

She sat still, focused on the breath, and let the mana pool behind her fingertips.

One drop.

She released it into the ground.

Yet nothing happened.

She didn’t expect it to. It might have come easily to spirits. But plant growth magic, or whatever it was they did, seemed… a little… well, a little more than a little… more complex than the magics she was used to using so far.

But the effort left her smiling regardless.

It wasn’t a mistake to try.

She stood, brushing the flattened blades of grass from her skirt. Her legs felt steady again. The ache in her back from the long walk had faded into the background. Her shoulders pulled square.

She had two things now.

Two goals she had no idea how to reach.

Learn to nourish the world, find whatever that saturation point was. And trace how that mana flow could circle back, grow, build. Learn to do what spirits do themselves.

She reached for her satchel, unhooked the flap, and pulled her notebook free again.

It landed in her hand with the familiar weight of promise. The page from earlier still showed loops and misty spell sketches, barely useful. She flipped it, pencil scratching quick words into the new margin:

“Mana to nourish the land? Possible? Start small. Seed, drop, breath, anything.”

She underlined the word seed, then circled it once. It sounded poetic, but the idea was more than that.

Elynara closed the notebook and hooked it back in place.

Then she looked back down the road. It stretched toward a rise lined with low trees. Beyond that, a shadow she knew marked the edge of her next stop.

The horizon seemed closer now.

The thought steadied her. Work lay ahead. Shrines. Villages. But for this brief space of road, there had only been play, and the hum of her own mana moving faster than she could track.

She tucked the notebook away, pulled her staff back into her hand, and rose. Her legs felt looser, and a little lighter.

“Alright,” she said to the puff still nested in her hair. “Let’s keep moving.”

It chirped once and stayed put.

Step by step, the rhythm returned.

The wind shifted behind her and pushed at her shoulders, carrying the scent of running water from somewhere beyond the trees. She glanced at the horizon once more and quickened her pace.

The rhythm of her boots met the rhythm of her breath, and somewhere in between, the flow of mana took hold.

Elynara didn’t mean to fall into training – it simply started. A familiar companion to her daily life. Her staff tapped steady beats against the road. Her fingers flexed. Mana gathered and shifted, moving in slow, deliberate pulses.

One step. One breath. One push of energy.

It felt natural. Easier than sitting still. Her feet carried her forward while her focus turned inward.

She lifted her free hand and split the flow. Half to the staff. Half to her palm. A small flame curled at the tip of her staff, no more than a candle flicker. In her hand, a pale sphere began to form, soft light cradled between half-closed fingers.

Both wobbled.

She adjusted the flow, shaping by instinct and habit. The flame twitched. The sphere buckled at its edge, threatening collapse. Her pace didn’t break.

Starpuff drifted in lazy spirals overhead, trilling approvingly.

Then the flame sputtered, smoke rising in its place. The sphere blinked out a second later with a quiet pop.

She let out a slow breath.

“Better than the last time.”

And it was. Doing many things on the move at once was just that much harder to do without issues.

So Elynara tried again.

This time she began with water, coaxing moisture from the air. A bead of mist formed above her palm, wobbling with the movement of her stride. She layered heat beneath it, just a touch, enough to catch it at the edge of transformation.

And if it worked…

It would mean warm water to drink or wash her hands with anytime.

It lasted four steps before falling apart.

She didn’t mind.

The road gave her space to fail.

Insects buzzed in the grass nearby, a low hum beneath the quiet sky. The wind had gone still, but the light pressed gently from above, filtered through slow-moving clouds. Somewhere behind her, a bird called once, then vanished into silence again.

The path bent slightly and rose, giving her a clearer view of the shallow valley beyond. More thin trees. More tired fields.

But open and broad. With enough space to breathe.

And so, she did.

Breathed in, and out.

In. And out.

Then tried again.

The next spell lasted longer.

Fire to her left. Water to her right. She pulled mana evenly between the two, focused on keeping each stable without letting either break the other. Her arms stretched slightly for balance, her staff a counterweight across her shoulders.

Starpuff dipped low, trilling again. Judging her.

“You’re not helping,” she said.

The puff zipped past her ear and left a faint spiral trail in its wake.

Yet both spells held.

She blinked, startled. Lost focus.

Water collapsed. Then the flame dispersed.

She let out a bark of laughter.

“Still counts,” she told the wind.

The next attempt came slightly easier again. Far from perfect, obviously. Or graceful. But just that little bit smoother.

Her steps remained firm. Her thoughts stayed clear. She didn’t expect perfection. She expected movement.

That would be enough for now.

The wind returned slowly, brushing against her back, tugging lightly at the hem of her robe. A line of trees whispered together in the distance, too far yet to pick individual trees out. The sun had shifted lower in the sky, light turning softer, shadows stretching ahead across the road.

She reached into her satchel without stopping and pulled out a ration full of dried berries from her provisions pouch. Chewed it while shaping another pair of spells, this time alternating between them instead of holding both.

Left. Right. Left. Right.

Flame. Mist. Flame. Mist.

Her staff tapped out the beat, footfalls matching it step for step.

Starpuff twirled again, then flipped upside down and stayed that way, dangling midair in clear disapproval.

Elynara snorted.

“Try it while walking.”

The puff flipped upright and wobbled dramatically. A clear message. You walk. I supervise.

She shook her head, not even bothering to respond.

The next hour blurred in repetition. Shapes formed. Shapes broke. Some lasted half a minute. Some barely blinked into existence. Each one carved a path through her reserves. Not enough to drain her, but enough to feel the edge of effort again.

She reached the next bend of the road with sweat at her brow and her mana lower than she expected. A good feeling. A clean kind of tired.

The grass ahead looked softer, so she stepped off the path for a break. Sat down and let the quiet return.

Starpuff drifted to her shoulder and curled up, pulsing once.

She drank from her waterskin and didn’t speak.

The spells would come easier, in time. The shaping would sharpen. The balance would hold. One step at a time. One road. One flame. One drop of mist.

Elynara leaned back on her hands and let the breeze cool her face.

Then she stood again before continuing.

And as she approached the treeline, the road ahead narrowed.

Trees crept in close, tall and leaning, their branches scraping faint lines of shadow across the dirt. Elynara slowed, senses stretched thin. Her head ached faintly from the spellwork, the weight of her earlier attempts still pressing behind her eyes. She rubbed her temple, then shifted her grip on the staff, settling its length comfortably against her spine.

The underbrush rustled and she felt more than heard the approaching thuds.

She froze.

A crack of branches snapped the quiet. Then another. She turned fast, staff lifted, and caught the first blur breaking from the tree line.

Bonefang.

Her breath caught, but her body moved faster.

The beast lunged, yellow fangs flashing. Elynara twisted her weight, planted her feet, and shoved mana forward. The explosion of flame burst at the tip of her staff, lancing into the creature’s shoulder. The spell struck hard, heat cracking in the air as sound followed.

It yelped and tumbled sideways, smoke curling from its fur. Didn’t move again.

A second shape bolted in behind it. She pivoted low, dragging her staff across the earth. Mana surged through her heel, up her spine, and into the ground.

“Rise!”

Fire erupted upward. The burst caught the second beast mid-pounce, the motion of her staff sending it flying up with a hiss and something that tried to be a snarl. Until the pressure from the spell crushed it before it began.

Too close.

Elynara raised her staff again, pulse hammering now.

But they didn’t move again.

Still, Elynara sent two more small spells at them. Just to make sure.

One yelped, then stilled. The other simply shook with the spell.

Yes.

It was best to make sure indeed.

Elynara stayed tense. Waited. Listened.

But nothing else followed.

Her breath escaped in a sharp exhale. Her shoulders dropped.

Starpuff hovered near her shoulder, trilling softly. She turned her head slowly and narrowed her eyes at it.

“You didn’t help. Or warn me.”

It blinked once, then spun in a smug little spiral. Not even pretending to deny it.

She huffed.

“You’re impossible.”

The puff darted ahead, bouncing slightly in the air as it passed through a low branch without care.

She glanced back once toward the underbrush. There were no more eyes watching. No growl in the distance. But her muscles still buzzed.

Bonefangs weren’t the worst thing she could’ve run into out here, but they were still predators.

And she had been distracted. Her grip tightened slightly at the thought. Training was good. Training without watching the trees was not.

She shifted her weight, rolled her shoulders once, and began walking again. Staff ready. Steps deliberate.

“First fight since leaving town,” she murmured aloud.

The wind moved again, brushing leaves against each other. The rustling sounded too much like whispers.

Elynara focused on the path ahead, running the encounter through her head.

She reacted well. And fast.

Took care of the threats quickly and efficiently, with spells forming at her command.

Practice really made perfect, it seemed.

Barely bothering to push her would-be-ambushers away from the road, Elynara continued on her way.

They were not worth dismantling, no matter what Marcus would say. And she earned more than enough doing her errands lately.

The next stretch of the road wound tighter through the woods. Thicker roots pushed up through the earth in places, forcing her to step wide or balance carefully across them. The canopy shaded more of the light, but beams still cut through where leaves thinned.

Every so often, her staff tapped against stone instead of soil. She followed each sound like a note in a song, letting it pull her focus forward.

Her pace slowed naturally, her senses tuned sharper now.

No more rustling followed however.

But the treeline still loomed close. Trees taller. Bark darker. The leaves had a stiffness to them here, less sway in the wind, more silence where sound should linger.

Starpuff kept ahead, but closer now, tracing loops that fell tighter and more focused. It trilled low once, and Elynara reacted.

…Yet it just wanted attention. Silly puff.

She passed an old stone marker half-buried by moss, then another. The path had once been marked. And that meant she wasn’t far from the bridge Alicia mentioned. She hoped it was still standing.

She didn’t trust the forest to be generous a second time.

The bend came gradual, hidden behind thick scrub and stubborn roots that clawed at the edge of the trail. But past it, the forest peeled back, and the path dipped gently toward worn stone.

A bridge.

It looked old, but solid. Its sides were etched faintly with lines that might have once been carvings. Now they were softened by age, barely more than shadow against the stone.

Grass and moss curled at its edges, but none yet claimed the center. The river beneath whispered low and even, shallow in places, winding past the rocks without fuss.

Elynara slowed her steps, then stopped at the highest point. One palm settled over the top of her staff. The other lifted to shield her eyes.

The air seemed to flow and breathe here.

It felt quite pleasant.

The tension she had carried since the Bonefang encounter thinned under it, lightened by the sound of water and the sudden sky above.

She leaned forward slightly, elbows on the bridge rail. Starpuff drifted down beside her, bobbing once before tucking into a lazy float.

Even it had quieted. For once.

The river below ran clear. Stones winked through the surface, catching glints of sunlight between ripples. Every so often, she saw a flick of movement – small fish darting, their scales flashing silver.

“I never even thought to ask where the town got its water from,” she murmured.

It seemed obvious now. A river, slow and steady, fed by mountains far off. Flowing through the land, into the fields. Holding everything together without being asked to.

Yet another thing feeding into the larger cycle of things.

She smiled, just a little.

Then turned to look beyond.

Looking from one side of the bridge, the forest gathered itself again. Branches reached close, the trees pressed tight in whispers. Behind them, far past the clustered green, the jagged teeth of mountain ranges broke the sky. Their peaks looked distant enough to be imagined.

But the other side opened wide.

Plains unfolded in soft curves. The grass rolled gently, pulled by the breeze. Somewhere far ahead, the green faded into sharp contrast. Pale streaks. Dry patches. Even from here, the shape of the land changed – as if something had clawed the color out.

Hills, maybe. Or broken stone. But most importantly – no signs of life.

A scratch against the horizon, brown and white, too stubborn for grass to cling to.

She exhaled.

The sight pulled a thread of worry through her chest, but didn’t tighten it. The silence here was clear, not empty.

This land wasn’t dead. Just tired.

Her hand rested against the edge of the bridge, fingers brushing stone. It felt sturdy and cool under her fingers.

Maybe that’s what the world needed.

No miracle fixes.

Just hands that held it steady, long enough for it to remember how to breathe on its own again.

Her gaze drifted back to the far-off scarred land.

She would go there too, one day. Not now. But the knowledge settled in her gut without fear.

There was time.

There was always time, if you made it.

Her fingers drummed gently against the stone. A familiar rhythm. Half thought, half spell practice.

Starpuff twirled once in the corner of her vision and chirped. Then dove into the river air, spiraling just above the surface until a fish jumped – startled it – and vanished again.

Elynara grinned at the puff being surprised for once.

“Menace.”

The puff flew higher, looping back toward her. She caught it lightly with one hand, tapping its side. It wobbled once, satisfied, and nestled into her shoulder.

Her smile lingered as she stepped off the bridge.

The forest waited again ahead, denser now. The road curved and narrowed once more. But a warmth of certainty had settled inside her.

She had left Oltenar behind with questions. Doubts. That buzzing in her head, always chasing the next answer.

But here, halfway across old stone, caught between forest shadow and open sky, she found something steadier.

Hope didn’t need to be loud.

It only needed to last.
