Surviving in the Grand Arcana affected people differently. The one commonality was that sooner or later it always twisted people in some way. Some started collecting weapons, others collected ways to kill people, others still became obsessed with a certain type of monster.

Pup had acquired a fascination with the undead in a way that was far from healthy. When he wasn’t running after me and Avon in an attempt to impress us, he’d ponder the nature of the undead, analyzing species as if they were his personal butterfly collection.

Still, among the crap, there were some interesting things he had discovered. Each type of undead retained part of the abilities of its native species, while also acquiring the “undead constants,” as Pup liked to call them. In more cases than not, the combination made the new creature tougher, stronger, though also less agile. When it came to aelves, they were possibly the only partial exception. For some reason, they had retained most of their speed and hand dexterity, allowing them to shoot arrows and be athletic in their combat as before. However, being undead, they still succumbed to the common weaknesses of their kind.

Thread strike!

I performed another strike to thin the mass of undead elves that filled the chamber. Dozens of them filled the small space, though that didn’t make things particularly easier. Each creature I cut up seemed to regrow at least two more times. The only way to permanently destroy them was to use fire and Paladin skills. With my fire magic weak, and the Paladin card undeveloped, I had to resort to my divine blades. That meant that roughly one in ten strikes took hold. To make matters worse, Eleonor wasn’t particularly helpful, either. As destructive as her skills were, she had reached her limit.

“Careful!” I performed a multi-attack on an undead aelf that leaped at her.

Tens of strikes ripped through the creature’s clothes, slicing it up to shreds. One managed to affect the thread that kept it moving.

“Stay!” I ordered the aelves, using my Gravedigger card.

They didn’t. The power that compelled them was stronger than my skill. The only benefit I had gained from all of this was that the undead masses considered me one of them. Sadly, that had caused them to focus all their attention on the goth.

Just leave her here, Serp said. She’ll be fine.

Seeing that the woman was outright panting for air, I didn’t put much faith in his words.

I cast another fire mega spell. The circle covered a large part of the floor, then erupted in a burst of flames. The smell of burning flesh struck my nostrils. Unfortunately, all it managed to achieve was to burn off the top layers of the creatures’ skin—not something they needed to function.

“Go,” Eleonor shouted. “I’ll catch up.”

“Shut up and stay behind me!” I performed a butterfly strike, severing the arms and heads of several creatures in front of me.

“Always so stubborn,” she snapped. “You must reach the chamber. I’ll find a way to join you.”

Before I could say a word, she had vanished.

“Ele!” I shouted, but it was too late. Even the Observer card wouldn’t help me find her now.

Anger flickered within me for a moment before fading away. It was her life, so she could lose it any way she wished.

The aelves instantly froze in place. With no target to attack, they remained there like trees in autumn, unsure how to react. Their instructions must have been to attack any intruders who entered the tower. The power of the Gravedigger card overruled that. In their eyes, I wasn’t an intruder, just another one of them, regardless of my behavior.

That also works, Serp said.

I ignored him. Divine blades still in hand, I made my way through the mass of undead aelves. None of them attacked me, yet they didn’t move away either. Walking through them was like walking through a static dance club, only with the knowledge that they could turn on me at any point.

If only I had leveled up my Paladin, I could kill all of them here and now. Instead, I had to settle for second best.

Shoving my way through the living decay, I reached the next staircase. The main tower of the castle had been built so that there was no direct path to the dungeons. Whether that was due to practical reasons or some fancy of the rule-masters, it was impossible to tell.

Undead aelves mixed with skeletons were there every step of the way. Barely noticing me, they let me pass as if I wasn’t there.

This wasn’t right. A single thought was needed for all these creatures to descend on me like crows on a fresh corpse. It was as if their master had created them, then set off somewhere, leaving them behind. Thanks to the Observer card, I could tell that the undead, especially the aelves, were fresh. They must have been turned a day ago or less, which roughly matched the time Eleonor had led me into the well.

It stood to reason that the entity that had broken the rules would do anything in its power to save its skin, even complete the trial. I wouldn’t be surprised if right now, the rest of the group were facing the last surviving aelves in the area. The guards, the armies, even the nobles and their servants had been reduced to these wretches. Once the jungle elves and their human allies reached the castle, the fight was effectively over. Could this also have been part of the Stoneweaver’s plan? If the trial was complete with him still alive, there was a good chance that everything would restart.

Can you feed on those? I asked Serp.

No! the blade quickly said. And I’m not talking about taste. Undead flesh isn’t flesh.

What about the blood?

Seriously? You’d want me to pick up crumbs off a toilet?

I took that to mean a maybe; if given a chance, I wasn’t going to resort to it, but nothing was off the table when it came to survival.

Another five levels down, I finally reached the bottom. The corridor was wider, with prison cells on either side. All of them were empty. Whatever occupants there were, now roamed the corridors and staircases as skeletons or rotting undead.

I glanced over my shoulder. No undead were visible on this level. The floors above were a different matter. Even an elite group of participants would need a while to pass through. For all intents and purposes, I was on my own.

Is this another betrayal, Harlequin? I asked.

Had I been the only one with the necessary strength and luck to get here, or had I been the one sent as a sacrifice? Time would tell.

A large, iron-framed oak door awaited me at the end of the corridor. At some point in the past, it had been double-barred. Now, it was left cracked open, tempting me to walk inside.

I put my swords away. If this were a trap, I needed to have the flexibility of choosing my weapon.

Here we go. A spell circle formed over my chest, releasing a torrent of wind. Both sides of the door creaked as they swung open, revealing a wide circular room full of lit candles. In many aspects, it reminded me of the Hyena’s chamber. Fine blue sand covered the floor, filling the space between an altar of black marble and five massive Anubis statues. It would have been nice to call them decorations; sadly, each of them had a status box.

“You actually made it,” a man said from the altar.

Every muscle in my body tensed up.

Maximillian Swansen, Chosen of Anubis

ALIGNMENT: Anubis

Level: 25

Strength: LOW

Reflexes: LOW+

Memory: LOW

Resistance: LOW

Perception: LOW+

Magic: NONE

Divinity: LOW

Cards: Stoneweaver, Architect, Nine of Eyes, Ten of Eyes

Specials: Dark Vision, Sand Walking

Weapon: Thread Gloves

Several alarming things instantly stood out, but none gave me a bad feeling as his name did.

“Swansen,” I repeated, keeping my eyes fixed on him. “I knew a Swansen.”

The man started clapping. He was sitting on the edge of the altar, like a delinquent at school. Judging by his smug expression and the way he was dressed, he might very well have been before entering the Grand Arcana. Skinny, with short brown hair, he epitomized the stereotypical image of an eighties playboy: a loose, unbuttoned sleeves shirt over a blue t-shirt, neither of which was tucked into his torn skinny jeans. Even his shoes seemed rather ordinary—a pair of sneakers with a brand logo that didn’t mean anything to me. Looking at him, one might think that he had freshly joined the Arcana. Yet, there was no way a new recruit could have obtained a Grand Arcana just like that.

“You’re Carl’s brother,” I said, fully aware of the implication.

“I thought the goth would be the first to notice,” he said. “Guess he was right about you. There’s a first.”

What was going on? Part of me suspected that Carl wasn’t what he pretended to be, but not once in two runs had I suspected that he might be going against Harlequin. The weasel had been very clever about it. All the cases of incompetence were nothing more than a well-scripted performance. He had never survived by accident; he had never gotten others in trouble by chance, either. All had been planned from the very start.

“Was it your idea?” I asked, taking a step forward. Spell circles appeared beneath my feet, ensuring that at any point there was a layer of stone between me and the sand.

“You’ll have to be more specific.” Maximillian tilted his head to the side in amusement.

“Having Carl kill off the potential threats.”

“My idea?” the man laughed. “And just after I praised you. You really are an idiot, like the rest. A naturally gifted idiot, I must admit. I always knew you’d kill Hyena. That dog was too arrogant for his own good. Thought that rules didn’t apply just because he was a demigod.”

“Aren’t you the same?” I kept on walking. Upon halving the distance between us, I stopped. “I don’t think you’re supposed to be telling me this.”

“Why not? The cat’s out of the bag. And it’s not like you could kill me.”

I summoned Serp into my hand.

“Scary,” Max laughed. “Maybe you think that just because you’ve completed five trials you stand a chance?”

“Others said that before,” I said, starting to hum a tune.

A single strand of aether emerged from my mouth, making its way towards Maximilian. Just before it reached his ear, the man grabbed it with his left hand, causing the entire thread to dissolve.

“They aren’t me.” His expression hardened. “But you’re right. You’ve caused a lot of annoyance. That’s the problem with you anomalies, you always attract attention. If it were up to me, I’d have killed you during the first trial.”

As if you could.

Not even a Grand Arcana holder could barge into an early trial without consequences. Hyena knew that, which was why he had settled for a few standard Major cards.

Still, there was no point in deluding myself. Even a slight mistake against such an enemy could well turn out to be my last. Not only did he have a Grand Arcana, but three other cards belonging to a house that we weren’t supposed to know about. I couldn’t remember what its Minor Arcana cards did, but the Major assisted with inanimate manipulation.

I blanked my mind.

Dashing forward, I threw Serp right at the man.

At the very same time, the sand shot up, solidifying into a wall.

I suspected that was his game. The sand was native to the desert reaches, not any place within this trial.

Spell circles kept forming beneath my feet at every step. I unsummoned the serpent blade, then summoned it again as I leaped into the air. This time, pillars of ice emerged from beneath my feet, allowing me to rise above the wall of sand.

Thread slash!

Max leaped off the altar, leaving the attack to crack its surface.

An evasion type, I thought.

Everything considered, that was a pretty good matchup. Nine times out of ten, such opponents would forsake any direct combat skills in order to obtain such that they manipulated the environment. Judging by his cards, I already knew that he would rely on the statues in the room as well as the sand. The Architect allowed him to construct structures which, given that we were in a room of sand, placed us in his playground.

Air! I used a spell circle to boost the distance of my jump, landing on the altar. From there, I cast a mega spell.

A massive circle covered the entire floor. Moments later it erupted like a geyser.

Keeping my guard up, I combined a butterfly strike with a thread strike, slashing the surrounding water. Entire sections of it were split like sheets as my attacks went through. It was naive to think that would be enough, though. Even before the water fell back down, I knew that Max had blocked it, though I didn’t expect to see the full effect of the Stoneweaver card in action.

“Fancy,” he laughed, twenty feet away. All the sand had retreated from the floor, creating a single entity that had extended a shield before it. It was composed of multiple stone sections, resembling a cross between a futuristic sci-fi robot and a suit of armor. That was not all. Its whole surface had turned as solid as polished marble.
