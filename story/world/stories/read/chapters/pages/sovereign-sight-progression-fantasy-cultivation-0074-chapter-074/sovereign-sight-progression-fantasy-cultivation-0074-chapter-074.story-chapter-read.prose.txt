Chartermark Leaderboard - Phaar Region

Adhira. A rush of blood made Harker suddenly dizzy. He reached out, resting his hand against the whorled surface of the nearest Charterstone. She's still alive.

Ever since waking in that blighted tub, the thought of her had infested him like Aberrants. Intended or not, he’d left her alone on the Gnarl with only mercenaries and monsters for company. It was a relief to know that she’d evaded the Stitcher and Grim Company both. Hopefully, with Harker out of her hair, she wouldn’t encounter them again.

Not only that, but she had improved her ranking. A single rank wouldn’t be much to brag about, but climbing from 4th to 3rd was likely more difficult than rank 100 to rank 80. At that level, the competition was beyond fierce—evidenced by the fact that the top five had only jumbled around, and the top two remained locked in place. Whoever Temra and Janovan were, they clearly had to be monsters of their own.

His gaze flicked up the list, moving toward the 100th ranking. He spotted a few familiar names.

…

…

The trio from Creston had survived. Had they come through the city as well? Or did they carry through with their climb up the mountain? Perhaps they even ran into Adhira on the way. Harker grinned at the thought. That would be awkward for her—after all that time giving them the slip.

Somewhat more surprising was Sean Weaver, someone from Vale like him. Harker hadn’t seen the guy since the Charterstone ceremony. With the Stitcher’s attack, he wasn’t sure what had happened to all the Talents headed to the Travelspire.

At least some of them must have made it. Reaching rank 67 is impressive too. Harker chewed on his lip. What was his Talent again?

He couldn’t remember. I suppose I’ll just ask when I reach the Spires with him. And Sven, I suppose.

Harker wasn’t too thrilled about meeting that hothead again. At least, for all the changes in his life, his luck was still trash.

“Bah.” He pushed at the list, scrolling further up. “Where am I—Oh!”

…

“Oswick!” Harker smiled. “I thought I recognized that name. But he’s only at rank 96?”

The man had felt sturdy to every one of Harker’s senses, and though he’d only seen his Talent twice, it appeared decently potent. If the man had even the beginnings of a refined body and a decent Talent, he’d be ranked higher. Harker could only assume it had something to do with why he hadn’t left for the Travelspire yet.

“Wait.” He flicked through the list, bile rising up his throat. “Where am I?”

He endured a heady rush that stabbed a hot poker of queasy panic through his gut. He forced himself to adjust the list slowly, but he didn’t find his name anywhere between 100 and 60.

Had he fallen off the leaderboard completely?

He scrolled down, further and further, a dread certainty gripping him. “Sacred Depths. That can't be.”

…

…

Eyes dry, throat aching, Harker stood frozen before the Charterstones. Dread was banished before the onslaught of giddy disbelief. Rank 35.

It had to be some sort of elaborate prank. When he was at the top of the mountain, Harker was at rank 98. He’d leaped up sixty-three spots—an absolutely mad leap through the leaderboard.

The Boons. They have to be the reason why. There was no other explanation that made even half as much sense.

He still had six Boons left to earn, too. How high will his ranking go if he can get them all? Adding an even partially refined body on top of that—could he get into the top ten? The top five?

Could he join Adhira at the top of the heap?

When the Ordeals were over, the top five on the leaderboard were promised a Boon of their own. For the first time, Harker allowed himself to imagine what that might be…

Stop. Harker pulled back, attempting to cool his enthusiasm. Adhira was third, but that was by virtue of Dual Talents and a powerfully refined body. There wasn’t a world where he could match her Talents, and the others in the top five were no doubt just as impressive. Law 1B—Want Nothing. There was only pain when you reached for the impossible.

But… I’ve already done the impossible.

Dual Talents manifested in less than a fraction of a fraction of the population across all of Teyr, and the leaderboard was only for this region. The chance that the others in the top five had such an advantage was vanishingly small. That meant they had found alternative avenues toward the peak, such as facing challenges worthy of their rank. Challenges like taking down true Aberrants in the Southern Reach.

He was a Knack at rank 35. What else could he do?

Harker huffed a breath. "Thanks for the tip, Clarice."

His Talent twinged, twinged, the grimy stain of unwashed flesh crawling across his nose and skin alike. Fingers twitched all too close to his hip…and his few remaining pouches. Harker blinked.

He was being robbed.

Perfect.

Dropping his hip and turning slightly away from the pickpocket forced their fingers to grasp onto nothing, even as they tripped on the uneven cobblestones. Harker stretched, making a show of not noticing as the thief melted back into the crowd.

A child. He’d heard that most of the pickpockets in large cities were young, but he hadn’t expected what looked like a ten year old to be slipping through people like an otter through seaweed. They’re experienced too, otherwise they wouldn’t have recovered so well. How long have they been doing this?

He dismissed the leaderboard and focused on his Talent. It expanded around him, quickly reaching the five foot maximum and then further as Harker compressed the sphere into a cone. He swept the crowd, subduing and filtering out the chaos as best he could until it pinged off the slender shape of the thief.

Found you.

It wasn’t dressed in rags, but the shirt and trousers were threadbare and more than a little dirty around the knees and elbows. Very similar to those around them, in fact, save the wealthier merchants that clustered around the badger-flagged inn. It let them blend in, their smaller size all but ensuring their invisibility. The one that had stolen from Harker was slim with matted hair and skinny fingers—more wraith than child—and all of it proved useful. A small knife, no bigger than her thumb, sheared through the purse strings of a passing beer bellied sailor. It dropped, cradled by her other hand and vanished into her tunic.

She’s good.

The purse contained little of interest—Harker could all but feel its contents against the probing end of his Talent. A few copper coins, an Abyssal carved from bone, and a half-empty jar of waterproofing ointment. More details crowded him, shouldering past his filter to bombard his mind with the scent of ale-breath and the sound of hobnailed boots against cobbles. Wincing, Harker pulled back, reducing the size of his cone at the same time he monitored his reservoir.

Around two-thirds left. I should keep this short-ranged. Harker let his Talent settle around himself in his standard sphere, once again reducing the drain to a trickle. I got her now, anyway.

Even better, now that he’d cottoned onto her movements, Harker immediately saw three others working the crowd. Where others ambled, strutted, or stumbled, these little ones loped. They prowled, like rats in a cage.

Harker leaned back against the Charterstone, enjoying the chill thrum of its presence. He still wasn’t sure if it was aware in any real way, but the half-real cold that emanated across his Talent was like a stiff breeze, and it almost counteracted the humid wash of bodies and cavern-warmed air. Most people were still avoiding it, which let Harker watch the crowd flow around the square with impunity.

Sovereign Sight’s ability to enhance his sight was immediately useful. He spotted and tracked six thieves now, all of them working within the eastern quadrant of the square, and let his Talent draw forth details on them. A scar across the back of a neck. A pair of striking eyes that were smudged with dirt. Footwraps too thin and marred with old blood.

One of them broke away, stepping closer to the inn toward another thief. They were talking, but Harker couldn’t hear or even see them through the flapping of a badger-banner—not without pushing his Talent to the limit and draining his Water.

What if I…? Harker clenched his spiritual muscles, gathering the sphere of his Talent and compressing it. This time, instead of letting it spread outward in a cone or line, he held onto all of it. His Talent shrank, smaller and smaller, but stopped when it was four feet across in front of him. It refused to compress any further.

Why not? I can pull my Talent inward to observe myself, so why can’t I hold it externally in front of my eyes? If he could, he was sure he could enhance the spyglass nature of his vision. He pressed again, but it was like trying to crush a stone with his bare hands.

The Water has volume. Is that why? Harker frowned, following a third thief as they drifted closer to the whispering pair. But it doesn’t take that much Water. I should be able to squeeze down that little amount, right?

There was an element he wasn’t accounting for—he knew it.

The trio of thieves cut aside, disappearing down a side alley. The others in the crowd were vanishing too.

It was time.

Harker stood up, slipping himself into the press of bodies. He could explore his Talent later.

His mother had given him many lessons about surviving in the world. Some were about combat, some about staying invisible, and others were about gathering information.

Law 2A—Watch The Angles.

He’d evaded the mercenaries and the Stitcher. They likely thought he was dead too, which was a huge benefit to him, but his injuries and recouperation left him woefully behind. At his wrist, the countdown showed time slipping by deceptively slowly. He had a lot of ground to make up, and just over a week to do it in.

If he wanted to keep his head above water, he needed a reliable source of intel. Thieves needed the same thing, especially if they worked in groups like this. Someone, somewhere was pulling their strings.

He just had to follow them.

C’mon now rats. Lead me to the ratlord.
