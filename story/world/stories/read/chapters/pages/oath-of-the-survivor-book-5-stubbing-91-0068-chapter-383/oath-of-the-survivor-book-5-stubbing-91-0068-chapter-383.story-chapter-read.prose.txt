[YOU ARE VEERING NEARLY A FULL DEGREE OFF COURSE, DR. MAYHEW.]

“I don’t think that it will make much of a difference in an orchard this size, C.H.A.D.D.”

[BUT IT IS STILL INCORRECT.  AND YOU CAN FIX IT RIGHT NOW WITH MINIMAL EFFORT.  OTHERWISE IF YOU MOVE ON, I WILL HAVE TO LIVE WITH THE KNOWLEDGE THAT IT WAS A POORLY COMPLETED PROJECT.]

Kyle gave an exaggerated sigh as he flexed Aura Force, refilling his most recently dug holes and realigning them with the drone’s projections.  Once placed to C.H.A.D.D.’s satisfaction, Kyle pulled small saplings from the World Tree Seed and carefully lowered them into their new homes.  These were a unique fruit created by Graft, which combined the resilience of fig and pomegranate trees with the nutrient-dense guava fruits.  The resulting plants grew well in Kertan Six’s soil, had a hardy root system to stabilize the ground, and provided another local source of easily accessible resources for the colonists.

After all the saplings had been planted, Kyle connected to each tree using Devourer’s Domain, and activated Cultivate.  Soon he was standing inside a shaded grove with trees nearly seven meters high.  A fragrant breeze blew by, and he smiled as he took in the scent of the newly-blossoming flowers on the trees.  It had been nearly nine months since he and C.H.A.D.D. had started their work in earnest on Kertan Six, and Kyle found the simple work to be deeply rewarding.  Compared to when he first left the planet, and with his new skills, he could comfortably clear, plant, and grow fields a couple hundred meters long in just a few minutes.  Watching the barren waste erupt with vibrant life was cathartic.  From death comes life, he reminded himself, thinking back to Phaegos’ words during his last round of skill upgrades.

“What’s next on our list?” Kyle asked after taking another moment to appreciate his handiwork.

[THAT IS ALL FOR NOW, DR. MAYHEW.  MR. KULHAVEY IS STILL PREPARING THE PARCELS FOR OUR NEXT PROJECT.]

“Done already?  Time sure flies,” Kyle mused.

[YOU WILL EVEN HAVE TIME TO CLEAN UP BEFORE THE PARTY.  I AM SURE THAT AMALIA WILL APPRECIATE THAT.]

Kyle grinned as he took to the sky, lifting himself with a wave of power from Aura Force before launching himself back toward his new home.  At Marcus’ direction, he and C.H.A.D.D. had planted and recovered nearly a full third of the planet’s surface already.  They could have completed more, but Marcus was nothing if not precise in his instructions.  The man found the most critical points to reinforce for his earliest mining efforts, and the first wave of workers were brought in with gainful employment just a month after he’d begun.

Among that group was the Santos family.  Frank was getting older, now, but he still served as a community leader and guide for many of the people who decided to resettle in the early waves.  Though Kyle hadn’t had a chance to spend time with them when he’d first returned to the Hub from Kertan Six, he’d heard Frank continued to serve a similar role during the slow decline of Corthian Mining; doing everything he could to encourage a sense of community among the people who were struggling.  And today, they were going to celebrate his seventieth birthday.

“I’d prefer hearing comments about how I smell like dirt than having to explain to Frank why she’s off in the woods alone.  Again.”

[IN MY DEFENSE, DR. MAYHEW, SHE ASKED TO SEE SOME OF THE NEW DEVELOPMENTS AROUND YOUR RESIDENCE.  IT SEEMED A REASONABLE REQUEST AT THE TIME.]

“C.H.A.D.D., there are razorpine variants planted near the residence.  If one of those had fired on her-”

[AND SHE HAD A LEGIONNAIRE WITH HER TO ENSURE SHE WAS KEPT SAFE.  COUPLING THAT WITH HER OWN HEALING ABILITIES, I DID NOT THINK IT AN UNDUE RISK.]

“Which is why I had to apologize to Frank, because he very much thought it was,” Kyle shot back with a sigh.  The old man had been gracious, but when he heard that she’d wandered off toward Kyle’s cabin he’d certainly been stressed.  There was a reason it was clearly marked as a no-trespassing danger zone, after all.

Unfortunately, that language didn’t seem to mean much to teenagers with variant classes.  That was ultimately the biggest unexpected wrench in their plan, and a large reason for Marcus’ caution.  The children that had escaped Earth’s demise were awakening at an astonishingly high rate, and many of them were getting variant E Grade classes as a result.  As secrets went, it was kept relatively well, but there were plenty within the Gray Conglomerate that could piece together the common thread.  Moreover, that also led to the issue of foolhardiness.  It was hard enough being a teen, much less one that just learned that they had access to power.  They needed structure and guidance, and Marcus wanted to ensure that infrastructure was developed and in place before their resettlement and industrial efforts expanded too far.

Those thoughts were put aside as Kyle saw the familiar landscape around his cabin appear on the horizon, and burgeoning city of Novum Aurora expanding around it.  Mechanical drones were already hard at work expanding the web of railways from the heart of the small town, cutting through the large spaces Marcus asked Kyle to leave between sets of trees and farmlands.  A few large storehouses stood near the rail stations, each concealing a mine shaft that descended into the caverns below.

Marcus’ vision was to build similar settlements and structures all across the planet near the most valuable resources, which would allow efficient access to the minerals, all while keeping the surface relatively free for nature preserves and other expansion.  Pragmus Meat Purveyors were currently in talks to set up some small-scale projects, focused on free-range meats, and Kyle knew that Marcus, or rather Marcus’ team, were courting several other large enterprises to get involved.

A warning flashed through his nav bracelet as he crossed the border into the forest that separated his home from the settlement.  Marcus had insisted on keeping some distance and setting up deterrents, and despite initially disagreeing Kyle finally came around to the idea.  With as much time as he spent traveling across the planet and away from Kertan Six, it was prudent to have some security measures in place.

So, he made a security system that increased in intensity closer to his cabin, including a single chaddling legionnaire which led a small group of the standard melee versions in patrols.With a handful of other traps and variant plants, such as the modified razorpines, the forest grew progressively more dangerous as one approached the heart where his cabin stood.  Most E Grades and even early D Grades would have a difficult time making it all the way to the cabin, and if they did, they’d be met by another obstacle.

As Kyle landed, he smiled at the sight of his home.  The two-story structure was encased in a thick, armor-like gray bark, sealing off all doors and windows.  The stonebloom material had been further reinforced with the concept of Immutability, and improved with multiple rounds of Graft.  The result was a fortress that Kyle expected could withstand attacks from even early C Grades, though he hoped that wouldn’t be put to the test.  Reaching out with Devourer’s Domain, Kyle gave the bark a gentle nudge.  It flowed seamlessly away from the windows and doors, revealing the entrance to his home.

[CAN WE CHECK ON THE VARIANT GROWTH PROJECT, DR. MAYHEW?]

Checking the time, Kyle nodded.  “We should have enough time for a quick cycle.”

Walking into the basement of the cabin, Kyle looked at a small, egg-like object and the plants naturally growing nearby.  This was naturally the core from the hodag Kyle defeated on Guancious.  Though it was still lacking quite a bit of energy, courtesy of the draining technique Mangalica deployed, it was recovering.  Kyle still couldn’t draw it into the World Tree Seed’s inner space, but he could bring in plants that had benefited from its unique properties.

Kyle had purchased a set of small nourishing arrays to help the treasure recover, and then simply allowed it to exert passive influence on the plant seeds nearby.  At least, when he wasn’t around.  With slow, deliberate steps, Kyle connected the egg to the plants and seeds nearby.  Taking care not to draw any power out of the egg, Kyle simply allowed the concepts to more deeply connect to the plants in the room while gently pushing some of his own energy into the egg.  As he did, he activated Infuse, incorporating the concept of Adaptability into the plants to further improve the egg’s influence.  Finally, he activated Cultivate, growing more of the improved seeds and drawing the existing plants back into the World Tree Seed.

It was a slow and arduous process, but it was starting to show results.  Some of the plants were beginning to show early signs of the mana-nullifying properties the egg promoted.  The stonebloom in particular found a nice synergy, which was something Kyle intended to put to use when it was mature.  After all, if they could have a C Grade chaddling, why settle for any but the absolute best parts?

Unfortunately, they didn’t have the luxury to do a full analysis of each individual plant before the party.  Kyle cleaned up, put on some fresh clothes, and took off for Novum Aurora.  He was more mindful of his travel speed this time, and restrained his aura as much as possible while using his skills actively.  Even though the Santos family and other guests wouldn’t be bothered, he knew his presence still made some of the other Earthlings uncomfortable.  Their discomfort was only somewhat mollified by the blessing of one of the Originators and personal support of their leadership.

He arrived at the Santos residence right on time, and Amalia opened the door with a disappointed pout.

“I was hoping you’d be late, Uncle Kyle.”

“Oh?  And why is that?”  Kyle asked, suppressing a smile.

“Because then I’d be able to ask you to take me flying to make up for being tardy.”

“We can ask your grandfather, I bet he would be fine with us taking a quick trip through the air.”

“But I don’t want to go for a quick trip.  I want to really soar.  At least until I get my own flight skill.”

Kyle admired the girl’s enthusiasm, and didn’t want to burst her bubble about the likelihood of a Healer gaining a skill like his.  He sincerely hoped she wouldn’t have to walk down the same painful roads he had to get where he was.  He also understood the hypocrisy that his wish carried with it.  After all, hadn’t he been given an opportunity for a quiet, safe life?  An opportunity he quickly shelved in favor of his own pursuits.

“Every flight skill works differently,” Kyle finally said, ignoring the inner dialogue.  “I don’t know how helpful mine will be as you develop your own.”

“Well yours is the only one I’ve seen.  And no matter what Arianna says, hers doesn’t count.”

“I tell you what, next time I’m back at the Hub I’ll see if I can pick up some literature on the most common flight skills, how does that sound?”

“You’d better,” she said with all the sass he’d come to expect from the thirteen year old.

“It’s a deal,” Kyle said, walking past her to the kitchen.

The gentle aroma of slow-roasted vegetables and meats guided him to a large pot of simmering stew, where Frank was leaning against a counter, talking to a familiar muscular young man and shorter woman.

“Happy birthday, Frank,” Kyle said, shaking the older man’s hand.  It felt frailer than he remembered, and an unbidden memory came to mind as he remembered his own grandfather’s condition toward the end.  Pushing the thoughts aside, he then turned to the other two and nodded.  “Joseph, Mary Ellen, I’m glad that you could both make it, too.”

“We wouldn’t miss it,” Mary Ellen said with a warm smile, sliding her arm across Joseph’s back and pulling herself closer to him.

“I’m more surprised you had the time to come.  You’ve been working yourself to the bone, Kyle,” Joseph said, a warm look on his face.

Kyle couldn’t stop the smile as he remembered the pudgy Fire Mage from Garth’s team when they first met all those years ago.  And now, he was standing here as a freshly evolved D Grade, and one of the most powerful individuals left from Earth.

“I wouldn’t miss it either,” Kyle finally said.  “Now, that stew smells delicious.  Can somebody pass me a bowl?”
