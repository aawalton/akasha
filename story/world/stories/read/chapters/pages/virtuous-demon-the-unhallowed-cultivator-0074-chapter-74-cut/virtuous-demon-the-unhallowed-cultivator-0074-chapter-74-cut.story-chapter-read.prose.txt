“You want to know how I sliced that mountain in two?” The Divine Sword Saint stood on Zhenren’s Throat, showing Shuren the landmark he made at the age of twenty.

It was a grand mountain, towering over a dozen thousand metres high. Now, it had formed into a canyon in the Crestfell Continent, leading into a hidden underground region.

“Well, I found out my enemies had made a hidden base beneath the mountain. I was tired of chasing them around, so I went straight to them. What were they called again? Marataen?” Fidgeting with his beard, he scoured his mind for his old memories. “Whatever, they’re all dead. Irrelevant dregs. Now where was I?”

Pointing towards the split mountain, he gave the answer to Shuren. “If you want to make a cut like that, you need to be aware of every move. So that Heavenly Blessing is actually a detriment, basically giving you the insight of the blade without your personal understanding.”

“I didn’t use any techniques. No special abilities, or anything of the like. I learnt this move after hearing of a wayfarer telling me this profound saying. An outlandish statement in our world of Immortals, but all the more true.”

The Divine Sword Saint raised his simple sword up, cleaving it down with a casual swing.

All the clouds in the atmosphere of the world split in two.

“I fear not the man who has practiced 10,000 techniques, but I fear the man who has practiced one technique 10,000 times.”

“So what do you think of the man who has practiced a sword slash over a million times?”

Liu Shuren did not use a technique.

No esoteric inheritance of the Sword Saint.

He didn’t even control the flow of his Qi in a unique way.

There was only one thing on his mind.

To break the barrier between a Rank 1 and Rank 3.

And land an injury on Lixi.

How many times had he swung the sword?

Every morning, every single day of Shuren’s life, ever since he had been cursed, he would always train.

Before he would practice any of his other techniques, he would swing the old sword gifted to him by his grandfather.

A simple steel sword of no value.

A single cut.

Over, and over, and over, and over.

How many times had he swung the sword by now?

A thousand times? A hundred thousand times? A million times?

Perhaps even more?

No matter.

Shuren felt the warmth of the sheathe, bloodied by Ming’s injuries.

One with small cracks in the middle from Lixi’s snake.

Yet holding the blade, Shuren forgot about his injuries.

His cracked ribs. His bruised internal organs.

And the blossoming of blades that revelled in ravaging his body in a river of blood.

The name of this move was…

“Cut!”

He did a basic horizontal slash.

His sword moved from the left to the right.

That was all.

All the rocks and boulders in the canyon were cut in two.

The dust clouds of dirt vanished from the swing.

A burst of wind was formed from pushing the air itself.

But Shuren’s sword halted in the air near the end.

Caught by Lixi’s hand.

The playful demeanour in Lixi’s face had disappeared.

His fingers shook, barely able to contain the blade's force. His fingers scalded from the friction of metal.

He gazed at the face of the man who did this blow.

Someone at Rank 1, yet his eyes didn’t even consider Lixi’s presence. It was a hollow silver, with no light in them.

As if everything those eyes saw was nothing but the sword.

And a realisation dawned on Lixi. Is it talent? Is he even a random disciple of the Seven Clouds? Somehow, I feel that’s not the case. But…

Thinking back to the sorts of people he had met throughout his life, those who were righteous, those who were demonic, those who were vagrants. And those who were free, uncaring of it all.

No wonder he reminded me of Reizou.

He’s obsessed. A lunatic, avaricious obsession over the sword. This is beyond just a gifted talent.

“Too bad. In the end, you weren’t able to injure me. The gap between a Rank 1 and Rank 3 is too large for you to overcome.”

There was no response. Don’t tell me he’s unconscious?

The sword let itself go from Lixi’s grasp, lifting high up in the sky.

Lixi watched in expectation, seeing the exhausted body of Shuren act once more.

With both arms raised up, Shuren once more cut.

A vertical cleave, to sunder the mountains.

Before his sword could complete its swing, it had shattered into innumerable fragments.

This time, Lixi dodged to the side. He could have blocked the attack head on, but he observed as Shuren’s blade shattered, while his body continued with the slash.

The earth split, with a deep cut forming in the canyon.

Shuren stood with nothing but a bladeless hilt in his hand. Rooted to his spot like an impervious monolith.

“The winner of the bet is me. In the end, you-”

Lixi lifted up his left hand. A shard of a blade stabbed into his fingernail, causing a minor cut.

That was all Shuren’s efforts amounted to.

His entire life of practicing a single blow could only amount to chipping the fingernail of Lixi.

That was as far as he could go, to deal damage to someone two realms above him.

But Lixi spent a while gazing at Shuren.

In the end, clapped his hands. “You win.”

“Hei Xin, I would personally recommend you to become a disciple under my master.” Lixi said with sincerity. “What you seek isn’t the veil of righteousness or the façade of the demonic. What you desire is the blade. If you want to be true to yourself, if you want to defy Heaven, you need someone to guide that obsession of yours.”

“You will not find that in the Seven Clouds.”

There was no response.

Lixi realised Shuren was standing, yet he was unconscious.

His body refused to fall.

“No matter. Knowing your hypocritical heart, I know we will meet again soon. So long, Hei Xin.”

Amongst the shadows, Lixi had long disappeared.

And only until he left did the pain kick in.

From the blades that bloomed from his body.

Shuren regained consciousness, only to yell in agony amidst the empty canyon.

More blades jutted from his chest, even to the point of appearing on his arms.

The price of using the blade was to be paid in blood.

That was his sin. Of defying providence.

Lixi stood on the other side of the canyon, observing towards the Unhallowed Monolith. “The meeting point should be towards there. Then the exchange should happen.”

He once more looked at his left finger, at the minor cut.

The thought of even killing Shuren came to his mind for a moment.

That would be so incredibly boring, and such a waste of an intriguing man.

But most important of all, was the command of his Sect Leader.

It’s a shame he was of the Seven Clouds Sect. I’ve already been ordered to not kill even a single one of their disciples. All because of the reputation of their leader, Aether Ul-Regen. Even though he’s of the same rank as our leader.

Lixi saw the seven mountains looming in the distance. As if they had stood there for time immemorial.

They were not always there.

One time, over just a single mortal servant, he destroyed a city of cultivators. He equates the lives of cultivators to less than that of a slave. He is an utter absurdity. That man should not even exist in this world as he is.

Lixi sighed. He had finally found an interesting toy, but he could only watch it perform from afar.

He knew things were going to get a lot busier now.

“Ah, I’m getting a bit hungry. Maybe I shoul-”

The sensation of death loomed over every part of his body and soul.

Lixi turned into a blur, running as far as he could.

Yet when he moved in the light, he emerged.

With one less arm.

He looked back, seeing a tiny figure in the far distance.

A man with black hair and deep amber eyes staring right at him.

With an ornate sword by his side.

“Impossible! There’s no way that girl managed to find someone this quick!”

Lixi used everything in his arsenal to get away as far as possible.

Yet even he knew that man was going easy on him, letting him run back.

“There’s only one reason then. He’s been there this entire time, observing from the distance!”

Snakes writhed at the stem of his arm. Losing an arm was a small price to leave.

Ming limped back, looking for Shuren. He barely stabilised his injuries, slowing down his bleeding.

Soon enough, he had seen a figure standing in the distance.

Ignoring the incessant pain around his entire body, Ming continued.

“What happened? I lost consciousness for a moment. I have to quickly get back!”

But when he finally reached Shuren, he could only stop in shock.

Blood had formed all around Shuren, covering the canyon into a shade of crimson. Like it was the sight of the gruesome murder of a dozen people.

Shuren’s shirt was torn, revealing the numerous scars beneath.

Yet it was only him, standing alone. He gripped the hiltless blade, with only the whites of his eyes showing.

He refused to fall. Not in front of a demon.

Even in the face of death, he refused to accept defeat against his own curse!

This was Liu Shuren’s defiance against his fate!
