Mother,

I am aware this message may take some time to reach you and situations may have evolved prior to your reading it. School is as expected, albeit less pleasant than it should be due to it being a human one.

We are all well, everyone behaving as they should. Our instructors are surprisingly competent. Well other than Lord Percivus who is exactly as stringent as should be expected when he finds time for us.

His attention on our pair of dorm mates, I see no need to name them, has more than once of late resulted in our consultations with him being delayed or done in their presence. Whether they listened or even were aware of us is of no matter, the fact that they have become his priority, simply because the male can use Bladesong without the decades of training, and the female is possibly of the folk? I know you can do nothing, that he is above us to a degree that makes complaining pointless, but something needed to be said, she thinks them to be great friends already.

Anya watched the candle flicker as Seb stared at it from maybe ten feet away. Watched as he took a measured step forward before the flicker repeated, then repeated again. Finally at six and a half feet by the measurements on the ground the flame vanished, then at five feet the candle went flying as though punched.

"No improvement." Percivus said from his usual position overseeing their training.

"It felt smoother," Seb answered. "Easier to maintain the compression, to take advantage of the distance."

"Something then. And when relaxed?"

"Maybe slightly further, or at least clearer at the extreme distance. Not enough to-"

"Every change is worth noting, every improvement a benefit."

Anya tried not to groan. She didn't need to be here for this training, she couldn't do nearly as much as Seb did with his aura, at best she could affect a candle flame a few inches from her skin. It wouldn't serve her any purpose, the distance hardly enough to change the effect of a technique.

Lord Percivus seemed to love tiny improvements. A week spent on managing to get one more lap before exhaustion, one more repetition of a form during the lull. A week where every practical class was torment, every duel they were forced into a challenge that she'd never have expected. Facing off against anyone when she could barely keep her head up wasn't something she ever wanted to do again, even if they'd managed to beat Peter, Paul, and Marie that day.

A second week, this one focused almost entirely on Energy techniques and manipulation, on forcing them to refine their efforts until they reached some level of precision that only Percivus and her Awakening Gift recognized. No wasted Energy, no flaws in the form. Nothing but the technique as expected, over and over until she felt as mentally drained as she'd ever felt physically.

Finally the third week had been the incorporation of it all into a more complete routine. Switching between Energy and Physical, then into the combination of the two, repetition and exhaustion blurring until she'd forgotten what it felt like to think clearly.

Despite expectations to the contrary he hadn't allowed them even a day of rest, their one day off turned into further training, the only concession was being allowed to stop two hours before dinner. Three weeks of pushing themselves, of being pushed and Anya had been ready to quit, ready to tell Lord Percivus and Mistresses Elienne and Sarna that she was done. She knew Seb had felt the same.

The fourth week hadn't changed, except that it had. The expectations were relaxed by the slightest amount and suddenly they could think again, they could be themselves again. The demands had only increased, new tests, new experiments, new drills. But what had felt impossible was now something Anya pushed herself towards rather than felt demanded of her.

Even this newest drill to test the distance Seb could leverage actual force out of his techniques, seemed reasonable. The paired routine, alternating between compressed and expanded ranges to bring power to bear without losing awareness was one she was glad he only occasionally shared with her. The flickering visibility was nearly as disorienting as the original sensations had been.

Her specialized training was both simpler and trickier. Elienne was a Weapons Master. She might have begun as a Monk but she was not limited to unarmed combat. Instead she'd start each lesson with a different weapon, or different style, and push Anya until she adapted, until she found herself able to predict and match the scaling challenge. The goal was not to teach her the styles, but to teach her how to learn, how to shorten the time before she could hold her own against a stranger.

At least they have us focusing on our own Gifts, she sent as Seb sank to the ground next to her.

Don't think that, they might start.

You don't think they've thought about it?

She felt Seb's resignation as he leaned against her. Not firmly enough to be seen to slump, but pressing against her in a brief moment of connection. They hadn't many chances until a day or so ago. They hadn't had the energy left by the time they fell onto the bed to do more than get comfortable, even with Gwen's ongoing help at dinner.

They'd woken up still connected by the bond more often than not, been aware of their shared dreams almost every night, yet not once had it threatened to be more than comfort. They hadn't felt themselves overwhelmed, or even pushed by the feeling. It wasn't their merger, it wasn't the combination that Anya knew they both were leaning on to keep up with Percivus's demands, it was simply a mark of who they were to one another.

"Enough rest," Percivus said. "I have seen you fight others, I have seen you practice alone. I have yet to see you face one another, even as a way to test yourselves as you progress."

Simple? she sent.

Start simple, start precise. Push until he says stop?

As long as you can keep up.

~ * ~

Seb spun, narrowly avoiding Anya's fist before nearly folding himself in half as her follow through predicted his movement. They'd been sparring for over ten minutes and had long stopped pretending to be treating it as a warm up.

Neither of them was hesitating, his blows aimed where he knew she'd go, his blade slicing through each avenue of escape when she tried to reset the moment. No robes to complicate matters, no Bladesong to give him an advantage that might otherwise have handed him victory.

Lord Percivus had pushed him to become more aggressive, to ensure each strike, regardless of whether by blade or by hand, was a threat that could not be ignored. The extra reach was all he had going for him, Anya's movements more precise, more measured, more dangerous. He knew she wasn't holding back, that he'd be lucky to escape with a bruise if she landed a real blow. The point wasn't actually to land a real blow, it wasn't to win through power or technique.

His techniques flickered around her, invisible strikes mixed with crystallized Energy to keep pressure on her even as he fought to regain distance. Most were harmless, too weak to do more than distract, especially when they were melded so completely that she could feel the intent behind each. Those that were dangerous she dealt with, her movements fluid enough to block or deflect even the most impossibly angled blow.

It wasn't fair. He couldn't win if they kept things in close quarters, not without turning the fight into something it wasn't. A technique clipped him, not enough to do more than startle him but that was all Anya needed. Her hand hadn't been close enough, she shouldn't have been able to hit him just then. His aura let him… she'd been complaining about how useless it was because of the limited distance.

He fell onto his back in a desperate attempt to avoid an unseen blow, shoving himself along the ground before scrambling to his feet, still close enough for Anya to threaten, to keep him from leveraging his mobility, but far enough to have an instant of safety.

Cheater! he sent.

The extra range is just enough. Gets me past expected defense.

Not if I-

He didn't get a chance to finish the thought, his awareness filling with volley after volley of techniques that were as much his as hers. He realized he'd stopped moving, that Anya was just as still, her hands holding his wrists as they stood locked in a battle of barely visible techniques. Each flicker countered by another, each shard of Energy shattering as one of them formed a matching one in the same location.

He tried shifting his angles, tried leveraging the additional range and winced as a shard sliced his cheek. He wasn't fast enough to do all three, to counter, to pressure and to strike from range. He had no way to break the stalemate, no way to take advantage. He knew she couldn't either, that her focus was on matching, on keeping his entire attention on their techniques. They weren't standing still entirely by choice, moving would be a distraction.

"Enough."

The word sank into their joint awareness, meaningless compared to the challenge they threw at one another, the desire for victory facing the ongoing pursuit of a path that would flow through their balance.

"Enough," Percivus said again as they both fell to the ground, the discordant tones of Bladesong cutting them down.

It still took a moment for Seb to focus, to release the ongoing duel, to feel Anya's retreat in the same heartbeat. The pressure didn't let up, the notes disrupting their thoughts until all that was left was fragments of their meld.

"I have seen what I need," Percivus said once silence returned. "You may have the rest of the day."
