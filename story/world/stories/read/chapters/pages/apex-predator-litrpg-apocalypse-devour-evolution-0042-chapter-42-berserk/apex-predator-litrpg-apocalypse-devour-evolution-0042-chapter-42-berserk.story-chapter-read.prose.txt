Don was in a haze of red and black. He vaguely remembered who he was. Don Damon. Former construction worker, current apocalypse enjoyer. A fighter. Someone who had interested an eldritch god. Someone who was currently in a race against a psycho killer to fight a giant monster bear.

None of that shit mattered.

There was only the primal urge to fullfill. A boundless rage that demanded to expand. A ceaseless HUNGER that had to be fed. A violence boiled inside Don and he gave into it.

The king of Grimoids didn’t recover from the shock of losing its hand when Don struck him.

Half of that was because Don was just. That. fast.

No claws, no nothing. Only a fist. A goddamn haymaker thrown over the shoulder. Somehow Don had the presence of mind to accelerate it with his psychics.

The king spat out a pitcherful of blood when the punch connected. Don felt a satisfying crunch of the ribcage breaking. The punch threw Homass ten feet backwards.

In a blink, Don was on it.

He jumped on his chest and pounded. A barrage of anger, pain and pure unadulterated savagery that had only a singular intent. Pulverize. Destroy. Annihilate. CONSUME.

Don’s fists crashed down again and again. He couldn’t hear himself scream. Couldn’t feel the bruises forming on his knuckles. Couldn’t care.

Each punch sank deeper into the thick meat of the king’s chest. Bone cracked. Muscle split. Blood fountained up, slick and hot. Homass tried to raise a hand—no, his stump—but Don slammed it down with a knee and kept going.

There was no technique now. No strategy. Just violence. Violence as answer. Violence as statement.

This was the ultimate function of evolution. Don understood now. Through his haze of red and black he understood something.

Violence was the ground-level reality. Violence and pain.

You could bullshit yourself from Sunday to Tuesday, go through your life living in a bubble. Tell yourself all the stories, cope and intellectualize however you liked.

But you couldn’t escape pain. Pain was at the baseline of reality. And so was violence.

When shit really threw down, and you didn’t have the capability for violence, you were prey.

So Don exacted violence. He had to know. He had to know who was prey here. It needed to be conclusive. He would only accept absolute victory or absolute defeat.

Homass struggled. It swung at Don.

Don tanked the punch,  but he was thrown off the king. It got up on wobbly legs and snarled before it charged.

Don dodged sideways and delivered a brutal kick to the king’s already messed up ribs. The monster bent forwards.

Don grabbed the beast’s throat, twisted his hips, and flung the king sideways into a wall with a thud that shook the entire platform. Wood splintered. One of the pillars cracked. Homass bounced off and dropped to all fours, wheezing, blood pouring from his mouth like a broken sink.

Don stalked forward.

Not sprinting. Not charging. Just walking. He wanted this to last.

The king tried to stand. One leg wobbled. The other buckled. He reached for his club with his good arm and slammed it on the ground. But when Don stalked forward, it took a backstep.

Don was watching. He grinned.

Homass noticed it. It roared in anger and attacked.

Don didn’t dodge.

He caught the swing mid-torso, absorbed it with pure stat advantage, then drove an elbow into fingers of the king’s weapon hand. The club dropped. Don followed up with a savage fist to the nose, flattening it, then kicked the beast’s kneecap in with a clean crunch. Homass screamed as he toppled to the side.

Don didn’t stop.

He grabbed the king by the hair and yanked his head back, dragging the creature by the face like a sack of meat, leaving a trail of blood and broken teeth.

Then he slammed Homass into the floorboards.

Once.

Twice.

A third time for good measure.

The king twitched like a downed ox.

Don put a foot on his chest, leaned down, claws gleaming and wet. The berserker haze made his vision pulse, but he saw clearly now. The king was afraid. It had no fight left. It was just waiting for it to end.

That angered Don.

It wasn’t enough. Don wanted more! More blood. But the king was no good sport. It was weak.

“FIGHT ME!” Don roared.

The roar echoed through the hall. Deep, guttural, like something ancient had climbed out of Don’s chest and spoken through him.

Homass flinched.

Then it breathed.

The Grimoid king didn’t beg. Didn’t cry. It didn’t even try to run.

It moved.

Slowly. Groggily. But it moved.

Don stepped back.

His breath was still ragged. Rage still coiled in every muscle. He wanted to fight, destroy, savage and CONSUME. But he waited. He wanted a proper fight with a proper foe.

Homass grunted and rolled to one side. It crawled on all fours, heaving for breath. It reached for its club with the stump of its ruined arm, but couldn’t grip. So it leaned on its knee, pulled the club up with the other hand, and rose.

One foot. Then the next.

It stood.

The king of Grimoids was a ruin. Blood poured from its side. Its eye was swollen shut. Its jaw hung off-center, and its chest caved slightly inward. But it stood.

Don watched.

Didn’t move. Didn’t strike.

Homass looked at Don. They stared at each other for a long second. No words. Just breath and presence and understanding.

Then the king raised the club.

A final stance. A final challenge.

Don nodded once.

He charged.

The floor cracked beneath him. His claws came out, gleaming with blood and purpose. Homass raised the club high, bringing it down in a wide arc.

Don ducked low, surged forward.

And jumped.

He rose with the power of every stat still burning through his limbs. Psychic force kicked in mid-leap, boosting the final lunge. Don moved in an uncanny straight line in the air.

He went over the club.

Spun in the air, somersaulting for acceleration

And came down, claws first.

He landed on the king’s shoulders. Sank both hands into the neck.

And pulled.

The flesh didn’t resist. His claws met bone. Cut through it.

In one clean, final rip, the king’s head came off.

Don landed behind the body. The head hit the floor. The club dropped. Homass’s body stood for a moment longer.

Then it collapsed forward, heavy and still. Don’s vision was tunneling. Suddenly it was hard to breathe.

Silence.

Then, the notification.

[You have slain Dungeon Boss: Homass, King of Grimoids level 14 - You have gained massive amount of experience for defeating a boss enemy. 140 System Points earned.]

[’DING!’ Race: [Evolutor (Dominator)] has reached level 13 – Stat points allocated, +3 Free Attribute Point]

[Title earned: Solo Killer]

[Solo Killer: You were the first in your tutorial to kill a dungeon boss alone! +1 to Power.]

Don brushed the notifications off. He would deal with them later. Right now his head felt like it was exploding. He couldn’t see. He couldn’t move. He could only lay on the floor.

[Berserk Mode: expired]

All stats temporarily reduced by 90%. You are suffering Post-Berserk Exhaustion. Movement severely impaired. Strength severely impaired. Duration: 900 seconds.

“Ah, fuck,” Don muttered.
