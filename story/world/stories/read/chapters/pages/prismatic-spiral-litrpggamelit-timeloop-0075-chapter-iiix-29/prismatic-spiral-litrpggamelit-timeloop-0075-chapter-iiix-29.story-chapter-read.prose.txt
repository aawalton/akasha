Chapter II.IX (2.9)

“You need to exercise further caution when engaging with foes who wield electricity,” Sterling lectured. “Your last encounter eviscerated your head, but if the attack had been weaker you could have very well had your heart stopped by such an attack.”

Mitsuko stood on The Selcouth Sable’s deck again. Mitsuko had repeated the last loop and the ship sailed away in one piece with everyone still alive. The main difference this time was her companion.

As were the terms of their deal, Sterling acted as her guide in this loop. Her friend had reclaimed his usual body. The black cat with brilliant green eyes sat on his haunches and licked a paw while he lectured Mitsuko on the dangers of electricity.

“How was I supposed to know it was electric?” Mitsuko muttered.

“It glowed yellow? That’s usually a key indicator of a lightning attack. Or has lightning changed colors in the last thousand years?”

“Lightning is normally white and blue,” Mitsuko said indignantly. “I’ve even seen pink and red lightning before! It can be, like, every color!”

“Well, here, it is yellow. If something glows yellow, assume it is electrically related.”

“Good to know.” Mitsuko sighed and tossed an ice sword into the sea. It landed in the frothing water the ship left in its wake. Before it submerged, Mitsuko pulled it back with a Mend.

“Why did you not use your new spell during combat last loop?” Sterling asked.

“I didn’t think to. I saw no obvious openings.”

“My spell, while superior to the others, should not be the only one in your arsenal. You must not rely solely on Mend. Find opportunities to engage foes with Deteriorate. Combat is the fastest means in which to level our spells.”

Mitsuko considered that. While she lacked the ability to cast Deteriorate on other living creatures, now that she had an aura to it she could use it on their clothes like she often used Mend. Could that pull them into difficult positions? Create openings in a sword fight?

“I need someone to practice fighting with,” Mitsuko said.

“Well do not look in my direction. I am a guide, not a sparring partner.”

Obviously not Sterling. Besides, she didn’t want a spar. She needed a fight. A conflict where the stakes were real. Or, as real as they ever were in a timeloop. In a way, the kraken at the start of every loop proved a nice challenge for her. Eventually, she would devise a way to kill it. Not this loop. And likely not next. But eventually. Still, that was more a force of nature than a normal fight where she could truly engage with her foe.

Like the previous loop, the captain and crew were incredibly grateful and a bit in awe of the power she’d displayed. She’d managed to put off the conversation with Holly, promising to explain everything in a bit. For the moment, she needed to sort out her thoughts. She looked to the sea, as if hoping it might provide answers to her questions.

“I don’t suppose you can tell me what that eye-whale was?” Mitsuko asked Sterling.

The cat shrugged. “Not really related to the Prismatic Spiral or even the archipelago. An ocular leviathan. I’m surprised you’ve never encountered one before. They’re known to roam the seas in predictable migration patterns. They travel beyond Hon all the way to Edgeland and back.”

“They…do?” Mitsuko asked. She’d heard stories about leviathans. Though, like krakens, she’d never personally encountered one. There were plenty of dangers at sea, but both leviathans and krakens were mythical. “Are there a lot of different types of leviathan?” “I am hardly an expert on marine biology,” Sterling replied. “Though I know ocular leviathans do tend to be the species most often encountered. Because of their vision, they like to live closer to the surface while others prefer the darkness of the deep depths of sea valleys. Ocular leviathans are the most passive and least dangerous as well. Merfolk often fight grand battles against their more lethal cousins. Fighting back one of the behemoth leviathans is the stuff of true legends. They’re said to be the size of cities.”

Mitsuko processed that information. She’d met a few merfolk before but never discussed leviathans with them. Perhaps that was still accurate or perhaps Sterling’s information was out of date. She had no way of knowing.

“Strange coincidence that I’ve encountered two very rare sea monsters while in the Prismatic Spiral,” Mitsuko commented.

“Coincidential indeed,” Sterling replied noncommittally.

He said nothing else. Mitsuko sighed. It was clear there was more here than he was letting on.

“I leveled up twice, which means two more questions and puts my total reserve at 5,” Mitsuko said. “Can you tell me if it really is a coincidence?”

“It’s not,” Sterling said dryly. “And since you’ve used one of your questions, I’ll briefly expound on that. Crusts are notorious for their talent at hunting sea monsters. They travel across the world and dive into the deepest chasms for a chance to face off against monsters. It’s an ingrained part of their culture.”

“For elite monster hunters, they didn’t pose much of a challenge,” Mitsuko replied, recalling how she’d skewered the crab man through his neck in the last loop.

“You are a human. Not a sea monster. Face them in the water and you might find them a bit more of a handful.” Sterling flicked his tail. “I digress. I do find it odd that the crusts have allowed the beasts to live in their territory. I suspect that they might have set up a breeding program in which to help train their warriors. It seems a plausible suspicion. Though please keep in mind that it remains simply a theory of mine until further evidence presents itself.”

Mitsuko had heard of a group of sea monster hunters that originated in the Prismatic Archipelago. She knew very little of them though beyond the fact that the Sailor’s Guild sent word to them whenever a beast blocked trade routes.

“You haven’t given me enough to make a question worth it. What else can you tell me about these elite sea monster slayers?” Mitsuko asked.

“They’re exclusively crusts. They work in groups, like the ones you saw in the last loop. And they’ve existed for over four thousand years. If you haven’t heard of any krakens causing havoc in your day and age, then they’ve expanded their power and spread their influence far. They were also the chief peacekeepers within crust society.”

“Organization’s name?”

“The Xanthidae.”

“Xanthidae,” Mitsuko repeated. The foreign word’s pronunciation felt stiff in her mouth.

“I rarely dealt with the monster hunters myself. It never seemed relevant in my life. The information I told you is what I absorbed through the osmosis of living my life. Common knowledge for an individual of my station. And outdated.”

Mitsuko nodded. Still, it was an informative conversation. She knew very little about crust society. And what she knew of the Xanthidae was just that people sent notice to the Prismatic Archipelago’s Sailor’s Guild when particularly dangerous sea monsters arose.

Deciding to leave the open sky behind, Mitsuko retreated back to her cabin. She needed to discuss her situation with Holly and get her friend caught up to speed. It would be tedious repeating the conversation every loop, but Holly proved invaluable not only with her personality and talent for divination spells, but her enthusiasm and inventive thinking helped Mitsuko develop novel ways to test out her temporal spells.

An hour later, this proved true once again as Holly suggested that Mitsuko experiment more with gases.

“You mean…like the state of matter?” Mitsuko asked, bemused by the suggestion.

“Yeah! Like, I saw you command liquids and solids. Have you done anything with gases?”

“I… can’t say I have.”

Holly waved a hand and a small cloud appeared in the cabin between them. A very minor elemental spell that even Holly, with no real skill in the field could cast with ease.

Mitsuko reached out and caressed the white fluff. Droplets of water clung to her open palm. She cast Mend on the water in her hand and watched it dissipate back into the cloud. Then she cast Deteriorate on the space in front of her. The cloud winked out of existence in an instant.

Holly waved a hand through the air, as if testing to see if Mitsuko had made her spell go invisible. But her hand came back as dry as ever. “Amazing! So you can cast on the three basic states of matter. What about light? Can you send light back at the sun?”

“Do not mess with the sun!” Sterling butted in immediately. “In fact, try not to mess with light in general. It’s just going to bounce around until the energy gets absorbed by some color. Nearly impossible to control. Both Mend and Deteriorate will result in nothing more than obscured vision and a headache.”

Mitsuko furrowed her brow and glanced at the cat.

“Why should I not mess with the sun?”

“Because I said so!” Sterling snapped.

“That is a terrible reason.”

“I am the Sage of Mending! One of the most powerful and long lived beings to ever walk this planet! Heed my warning, mortal!”

“You’re one of the most long lived creatures on the planet?” Mitsuko asked. “Are you counting all the time you were dormant within the Prismatic Spiral?”

“Without that I’d still be in the top one percent.”

“I hate this!” Holly declared. “Is this how you feel when I scry someone privately? I only get half the conversation and it’s all the questions with none of the answers!”

“Sorry, Holly,” Mitsuko apologized. “Sterling just warned me not to cast Mend or Deteriorate on the sun. And he won’t say why. So now I’m tempted to try it out.”

“Don’t!” Sterling yelled. “Use one of your questions. I beg you!”

Rather than listen to the sage, Mitsuko leaned over to her circular window. Then she cast Deteriorate on the sunlight streaming into the cabin.

A flash of light blinded her and she heard cries from above. She stopped casting nearly instantly. Her eyes now only saw white and she fumbled around and collapsed on her bed. Overhead, the sailors swore loudly asking one another what had just happened to them.

“How big of a radius did I just cast on?” Mitsuko asked Sterling.

“Less than a few meters. That’s likely the maximum area of light you can pull at level 2. I warned you not to do that. Satisfied now?”

After a few minutes of complete blindness, Mitsuko cast Mend on her eyes to repair them. Holly complained as well, but her sight had returned by the time Mitsuko Mended herself, as the gnome hadn’t been looking directly out the window when Mitsuko cast the spell.

She stood and paced back and forth. This could be a genuinely useful spell. All she needed to do to make this viable in a fight was cover her eyes in preparation. Especially since she could instantly Mend her eyes. Yes…the more she considered it, the more she realized this was a very useful ability. The spell seemed to pull down light from above at an accelerated speed. It made her curious about what the other spell could do.

“What about Mend?” Mitsuko wondered out loud.

“Don’t!” Sterling cried out.

Curiosity eating at her, Mitsuko reached out, her fingers illuminated by the streams of sunlight through the small window. And she cast her new spell. For a second, nothing. Then darkness. But only for a split second. Then the sun returned. And with a vengeance. A blast of brilliant red light filled the sky.

“Incompetience and foolishness and a stubborn ass’s dropping!” For the first time since meeting him, Mitsuko heard Sterling swear.

The red light brightened.

Holly leaned in closer. “It looks like the sun is getting closer?”

Mitsuko’s body perspirated. In mere seconds, her clothes were drenched in sweat as the heat increased tenfold. The water bubbled. The sea itself boiled.

Holly coughed. Mitsuko glanced over and saw her friend’s normally pale skin red and blistering. The gnome fell to the floor, wheezing as she desperately attempted to suck in the hot, dry air.

“Ending loop now!” Sterling shouted.
