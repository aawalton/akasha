With my heightened perception, my ears picked up something from my right. I caught movement and noticed footprints in the sand. I unleashed a heated breath, my flame roaring in the direction of the invisible intruder. Instantly, something appeared, or should I say, someone?

Wait a minute, that looked like my cloak!

Once the blurriness in my vision passed, I was able to see our dire situation more clearly. My Cloak of Despair was draped on someone else’s shoulders! She was hooded, with a half mask covering the bottom part of her face. I could tell she was young, and pretty short too, about my height when I was human. She held that cloak over her body, enhanced with something else to protect herself from my flames. I stopped pouring heat on her, confused with what was happening. A bright glow was coming off of the crown of a long stick she had in her grip. Not only did she have my cloak, but she also managed to get her filthy hands on the scepter I’d taken from lizard boy!

Okay, now it was time to panic. I had to check my codex, and when I did, I swear my chest stopped beating. She cleared out my inventory! Every… last… item!

‘Thief!’ I cried out like she could understand me. Surprisingly enough, she could, because judging by the look on Nolan’s face, she’d looted him, too!

Heck, she even stole the cigarette right out of Nolan’s lips!

“Ah, fucking great. A klepto,” Nolan hissed. “Been a hot minute since I crossed paths with one of them. Pain in my ass.” He reached for his gun, but his hand grasped at empty air. The girl flashed Scarlet at us, twirling it between her fingers with ease. “Looking for this?”

“Stealing a man’s gun?” Nolan scoffed. “Now, that’s just low.”

I snarled, smoke curling from my nostrils. My evolution was interrupted, my stone was gone, and this... this random thief had emptied my inventory in seconds. How was that even possible?!

“Hand it over,” Nolan demanded, his voice dangerously low. “All of it.”

The blond girl’s eyes crinkled above her mask—she was smiling. I could tell from the way her cheeks lifted that she was having a blast. She tucked Nolan’s gun into her belt and asked, “Hand it over? Now why would I do that?” Her voice was light, but condescending, with a hint of punk. It was then I noticed something shiny reflecting the sunlight, a bright band around her finger. “Finders keepers, old man.”

I lunged forward a step, my claws digging into the sand. ‘That stone is my evolution! I need it!’

She tilted her head, studying me with curious eyes. “Evolution, huh?” She squinted. “Wait a minute, were you just going to stand there and watch that dragon evolve?” she questioned Nolan.

“That’s none of your damn business,” he hissed. “Now, I’m above hitting kids, but I’m not afraid to make an exception for a grimy one like you.” He took a step forward, but the girl didn’t flinch.

Kid? Heck, she looked like she was my age! Erm, I mean, my Earthian age! Yeah! Kick her ass!

“I’ll take my chances,” she said, twirling my evolution stone in her other hand. The sunlight caught its surface, making the completed relic shimmer with untapped potential—my untapped potential!

I growled, my patience wearing thin. ‘Look, I don't know who you are or how you managed to steal from us without being seen, but that stone is important to me. Important to the fate of this world! It’s not just some trinket you can pawn off.’

The girl’s eyes narrowed as she studied me. “You’re pretty chatty for a baby dragon. A real bookworm, aren’t you?”

Nolan’s eyes darted between us, his jaw clenched tight and his hands balling up into fists. “You’re making a big mistake. That dragon isn’t just any dragon.”

The thief’s eyebrows rose. “Oh? Is she special or something?” She tossed the stone up and caught it casually, making me heat up with anxiety. “Ah, right. Fate of the world and all of that haze. Well, if this stone is so important, then why don’t you come over here and get it?”

‘You keep out of this fight, short fry,’ Nolan ordered me telepathically through our [Private Link]. ‘While she doesn’t seem like the killing spree type, we don’t know what else she snatched on her way here.’

‘Is she a dungeon thief? Waiting for parties who finish raiding, lurking just outside of dungeons to jump them for all of their loot?!?!’

‘That’s not unlikely. But honestly, I don’t give two shits why she’s here. We need our crap back.’

“Well?” she prompted, tapping her foot impatiently in the sand. “I don’t have all day, grandpa. Some of us have places to be.”

Nolan took a step forward, his posture shifting subtly. “Last chance to hand it over peacefully.”

The thief rolled her eyes. “Booooriiiing….” And just like that, she snapped! Klepto Girl tucked the scepter away and then b-lined right into Nolan, the gunslinger suddenly finding himself pinned in hand-to-hand combat with a girl half his age and size. He was on the defensive, barely avoiding her quick strikes as she came at him with a flurry of punches and kicks. He wasn’t even trying to hit her back!

‘What are you doing?!’ I projected frantically. ‘Hit her, already!’

‘I’m trying, for fuck’s sake!’ Nolan grunted back, dodging a kick aimed at his head.

She feinted left, then struck right, an after image chasing her moves. Oh, she was cheating! Of course, Klepto Girl had an arsenal of items, and right now, she was using one that boosted her agility and speed.

I wouldn’t be surprised if she had one for strength, too, with the way she had Nolan by the ropes.

As Nolan struggled, I couldn’t just stand around anymore. We were losing. While Nolan was keeping her occupied, I could try to snatch back my evolution stone. I launched myself forward, hoping to flank. The stone glinted in Klepto Girl’s pocket as she dodged a swing from Nolan. I swear the girl had a pair of eyes in the back of her head, because before I could get within arm’s length of her, she spun around and roundhouse kicked me right in the face. I went tumbling back, the taste of sand filling my mouth before I came to a stop.

‘Such a cheat,’ I snarled, struggling back to my feet. ‘How can you hit a baby like that?!’

“Oh please, don’t play that card,” she scoffed, dodging another one of Nolan’s punches with infuriating ease. She then followed her strike with a dirty uppercut, launching the gunslinger backward.

We were outplayed. How could we win against an entire tool shed of core items?

“You were about to evolve into something bigger and badder,” she continued, as she walked over to me. “Dragons, such a filthy, destructive race. Out for only themselves, paying no regard to the chaos in their wake.”

She was spewing nonsense. What did this pint-sized thief know about me? My claws sank into the sand as rage built inside me. This wasn’t just about my inventory anymore—she was making it personal.

“Human traitors like that old guy over there, you’re programmed to deceive them, aren’t you? Buddy up to them until you’re in your prime, then turn around and rip them into shreds. Or worse, make them your slaves. You got lucky. He doesn’t look very smart, but don’t think for a second that because you level up, you’ll be safe.”

She crouched down to my level, pretty brave too knowing what I could do at such close range. She wasn’t confident that I wouldn’t hurt her, only confident in whatever she had under her belt to deal with my breath weapon. “Dragons think they are unstoppable,” she said, and then chuckled. “Such egos. But if they were as good as they say they are, then why do their numbers continue to decrease?” She gave me a dark stoic look with those blue eyes. “I could kill you with one hand,” she taunted, lifting her fingerless gloved hand. “See? This one hand,” she said, pointing at it as black aura began to grow from it. “I’d do everyone a favor by taking one down. One less dragon scum to worry about, Right?” The girl smiled. “…But I don’t like getting my hands bloody.” She shrugged, and then straightened up on her feet with a haughty sigh. “I’ll just take your stone, and your pride, and leave you to your little adventure, lizard.”

As soon as she turned around, Nolan swung at her with a hook jab that froze midair. It was like there was some type of force-field in between them, stopping Nolan’s momentum completely.

Klepto Girl tapped the bracelet on her wrist. “Barrier relic. Expensive little thing I picked up from a merchant. Dumb mouth-breather didn’t know what hit him.” She then expanded the barrier, causing Nolan to literally fly across the field. He nearly banged his head against a rock, Nolan struggling to pick himself up as he cursed under his breath.

“Such a brat,” he hissed as he rubbed his head. “But I can’t really blame you now, can I? You’re probably a pick-pocketer who got lucky one night and turned your entire life around for the better,” Nolan taunted. “It’s a high when you get your first good score, ain’t it? Makes you feel powerful, untouchable. But you only feel that way because you have nothing else going in your pathetic life. No foundation, no friends, no family.”

Oh, that last word touched a nerve. All of a sudden klepto girl’s eyes widened, then narrowed dangerously. Nolan continued to mess with her, but she responded in a way I didn’t expect her to. Instead of bashing Nolan’s face in, she fled!

She spun on her heel and dashed away, leaping from pockets of spiraling wind plates inches off the ground. ‘Stop!’ I roared. I charged after her, my feet kicking up sand, but it was no good. ‘Please!’ I begged. ‘Plleeeeease! Don’t do this!’ I cried. ‘I’m still tiiiinyyyy!’

“Forget it, she’s gone,” Nolan said.

We were screwed….
