Lira did not go back to the library right away.

She walked the long way around, through the outer gardens and past the laundry courtyard, letting the noise of the castle wash over her while her thoughts refused to settle. Lira was not much of a drinker, so she didn’t partake in the wine much more than a sip.

Aric's last words followed her with every step.

I hope you make the right choice, Lira.

It was a generous offer. That was the worst part. Aric had not threatened her, had not cornered her, and had not brought guards. He had looked at a maid who had been lying to him for months and offered her a place at his side, provided she was willing to lay everything on the table.

Everything...

She tried, honestly, to picture it. Sitting across from him, somewhere in the Castle Main Hall, with an active truth spell. She would begin at the beginning.

My name is Lira Velora. My father was executed six years ago on Lord Kelroth's orders, and I have been forging documents since I was twelve. I have learned magic through forgotten primers, then have started crafting spells using the lexicon my father had died for.

I have killed Thadwick, and left him hanging from the ceiling, and forged his confession. I have planted the seal in a way Hesta would find, so she would hang even if it was for the wrong crimes. Then I have written six letters in Salvek's drunken hand, and used Deren's sister as bait to give the knight an opportunity to avenge the maids.

And at the very end, I would admit that the real reason I have walked through the castle gates was to kill the man you just named Master of Coin.

Lira almost laughed. She could not tell that story to anyone and expect a favorable response, especially not a dutiful prince who spent his whole life upholding the letter of the law. Aric was a good man, she could admit that now after watching him nearly collapse healing a child. But a good man who valued order would hear that confession and see a murderer, a spy, and an unlicensed mage.

She did not think he would have her killed on the spot for refusing to confess to him. That was not who he was. But she did not expect him to let it go either. She watched him push the Thadwick investigation to its limit, going as far as to recover the maids' bodies. She watched him cast his perception spell on Salvek’s letter, examining it thoroughly. A man like that did not leave an unreliable variable undefined. If she refused the heart-to-heart conversation, he would, within his right as prince, investigate her, fully and properly, and she had no illusions about what a full investigation would find. Either she would be exposed as a mage, or she would be exposed as a murderer, and then she would be executed anyway.

So there were two paths ahead for her.

She could join him, and hand him the whole truth, and hope that her usefulness would outweigh his adherence to the law.

Or she could disappear.

She could hide in one of the safe houses for a while. She had enough silver stored away for a fresh start, maybe in the Magari Republic half a world away.

It would also mean leaving Pallan, Tessa, Maren, Lyth, and Celene, leaving the library she had rebuilt from the ground up, and it would mean leaving the castle at the exact moment Lord Kenth Kelroth was about to walk into it.

No, if she needed to disappear and flee, she would Fireball Kelroth before she went away.

She sighed and turned back toward the library, her thoughts unresolved.

She had been sitting down behind her desk for thirty minutes when she heard voices outside the doors.

One of them she knew immediately. "...and I believe you will find it a great deal more presentable than the last time you saw it, my lord."

The doors opened, and Harwin... no, Sir Harwin entered with Lord Taren at his side.

Lira rose at once and dropped into a curtsy, keeping her hands flat on her dress since Harwin had a habit of pressing coins into open palms, and she was in no mood to test herself against his magic.

"Lord Steward. Sir Harwin." She kept her eyes lowered. "Welcome to the library."

"Ah, there she is." Harwin's smile widened, as if her presence were a personal pleasure to him. "Lord Steward, allow me to introduce the library's caretaker, Miss Lira Tenlan. I have mentioned her to you before, I believe."

"Have you?" Taren squinted at her without much interest. His face was already flushed, though if it was from too much walking or something else... she couldn’t tell.

"She is quite remarkable," Harwin continued. "When she took over, this place was a ruin, and she had to clear and reorganize the entire first floor in a matter of months. A task that would have taken most people years to do. I confess that when she first came to my tower, I thought she was the most resourceful young woman."

"You flatter me, Sir Harwin."

"I speak only the truth." His eyes crinkled.

Harwin turned back to the Lord Steward. "Shall we walk around, my lord? I would like you to see the reading areas."

They wandered off between the shelves, with Lira trailing a few paces behind them, and she listened to the two men trade remarks about the curtains, the benches, and the general air of respectability the library had acquired. Harwin did most of the talking while Taren mostly made small noises of agreement.

Every so often, from the corner of her eye, Lira watched Taren slip a silver flask out of his coat and take a long swig. Even from where she stood, the sharp smell of strong spirits reached her nose. He would wipe his mouth with the back of his hand and return the flask to his pocket.

Harwin did not so much as glance at it. He kept his eyes on the shelves, his smile perfectly in place. He was clearly accustomed to his superior's habits.

"Is there anything the library lacks?" Harwin asked, as they paused near the history shelves. "Now that the season is in full swing, I imagine you must have needs. Books, furniture, staff? Tell me, and I will see it done."

"I wouldn’t mind if both Maid Sera and Maid Tessa were permanently assigned here, but that is not an urgent matter. What is most needed is an update to the collection, Sir Harwin, Lord Steward," she said. "Most of what we have is between twenty and eighty years old. Nobles who come to read want to read what their peers are discussing. If I could request a few contemporary authors, Baron Sulevant on modern political history, Lady Bethany of Greenfield on estate economics, Mr. Elam Croft for the historical surveys, it would go a long way."

"Write them down for me," Harwin said, nodding. "I will have Cedric handle the purchase."

By the time they finished the tour of the ground floor, the Lord Steward's face had gone from flushed to florid, and he was leaning heavily on the corner of a bookshelf. He waved a hand toward the nearest cushioned bench.

"I will sit," he announced. "My feet are tired."

"Of course, my lord." Harwin helped him down like a dutiful valet, a smile always fixed on his face. Then he straightened and turned to Lira with a small bow that was, somehow, an apology for the entire situation. "Forgive us the imposition, Miss Lira. It has been a long morning. I must go and see how Cedric is faring alone with the tower. I will leave His Lordship in your care for a short while."

"Of course, Sir Harwin."

He left, and the doors closed behind him.

Lira let out a slow breath, and then she went back to her desk and pretended to work, while keeping an eye on the drunk lord. That lasted about ten minutes, until Tessa stepped in through the entrance, carrying sandwiches for Lira’s lunch and humming under her breath.

"Tessa." Lira beckoned her closer and lowered her voice. "Do you see the gentleman on the bench near the window?"

Tessa peeked around and hissed. "That's the new Lord Steward!"

"He's had a bit too much to drink. Could you bring a pitcher of water? Cold, if the kitchen has it. Maybe it will help him sober up before someone important sees him."

"Sure." Tessa shrugged, cheerful as ever. "I'll be back in a moment."

She returned a few minutes later, a pitcher of water in one hand and a tall glass in the other, her steps light and cheerful. And headed directly to the older noble.

"My lord? Some water, if you please?"

The man looked up. His eyes traveled slowly over the sixteen-year-old girl, from her face to her chest to her waist.

Lira felt something in her own chest go cold and started moving toward them. She was about to call the girl away from the old pervert as Tessa set a cup on the small table beside him and bent to pour.

Thwack! Lord Taren slapped her on the bottom. The sound cracked across the library.

Tessa gasped and jerked away, the pitcher slipping out of her hands and shattering against the floor, water and glass spraying across the polished stone. She stumbled back, both hands flying to her mouth, eyes wide with a look of fear Lira had only seen once before, the day Hesta had cornered her in the corridor and punished her with library duty back when the library was freezing, dusty, and absolutely miserable.

Taren chuckled, leaning back with a look of pleasant satisfaction.

Lira did not remember reaching the man. She found herself standing over the bench, blood roaring in her ears, so close she could smell the liquor on his breath.

"Never do that again," she said in a cold voice.

"Hm?" Taren blinked up at her, unfocused. "Or what?"

"Or I will ask Sir Deren to challenge you for her honor." Her voice came out flat and low. "To the death."

"What honor? She is a commoner," he slurred.

"He would do it, my lord," said Lira, her voice even colder. "You know he would."

The old man's face changed. It was small, only a flicker, a tightening around the eyes and a brief shift in his posture. He had probably watched the duel that ended Salvek. Despite the drink, he didn’t lose his self-preservation instinct.

"I only meant to have a bit of fun," he muttered, looking away.

"Then I suggest you find your fun elsewhere."

She stepped back before she did something she would regret, then turned her back on him and walked toward Tessa.

The girl was already on her knees, trying to gather the larger shards with shaking hands.

"I'm sorry," Tessa whispered, not looking up. "I'm so sorry, Lira. I broke it. It was a crystal pitcher for the nobles, not regular glass..."

"Stop." Lira crouched beside her and gently pulled her hands away from the shards. "Don't touch it, you'll cut yourself. It is just a pitcher. It wasn't your fault."

"I am sorry..."

"I am the one who is sorry. I should have never sent you near him. I promise you he will never do it again."

"He won't?" the girl asked.

"Never." Lira met her eyes and made herself smile. "Go and sit down for a minute. I'll finish this."

Tessa did not move at first, but then she nodded and got shakily to her feet, heading to one of the private reading areas behind the romance section.

Behind them, Taren had gone very quiet, sunk into the cushions with his arms folded and his gaze fixed on the ceiling.

Lira fetched a broom and a dustpan and began sweeping up the broken glass, one sharp fragment at a time.

Taren did back down because of the threat of Sir Deren, and not out of respect for the laws of the kingdom. No, the kingdom's laws did not apply to Taren, only another noble's sword did.

That was the problem with the kingdom that Aric didn't understand, if she was honest. He could sit in that chair and speak of making Halthar a better place for nobles and commoners alike, and he would probably mean every word of it. But that betterment would come out of a few noblemen's sense of decency and charity rather than any systemic change.

Sadly, most of the nobles around him were Tarens, in one shape or another. Kavard, and Salvek, and the thousand other aristocrats who took what they liked because nobody had ever told them 'No'. Aric's way, as far as she could see, meant patiently working within a rotten structure, using kid gloves to make minor changes and stopgap measures, healing a child here, promoting a noble to a place where he would do less harm there. It was all well and good, but this doesn't prevent another famine from rising in another place. Nor does it punish Taren for his failure.

No, Lira had no patience left for that. She had seen so many people die in the street to believe the current structure could ever be mended from within. Unlike Aric, she chose to cut off the rot rather than quarantine it.

As the last shard of glass fell into the dustpan with a small chime, her eyes went to Lord Taren, who was now snoring like he hadn't just traumatized a girl.

She could not accept Aric's offer.

Their ways of cleaning this kingdom were simply not compatible. He was too soft on the nobles, like Kavard and Taren, even when they had caused too much suffering, while she wouldn't blink twice at killing them both. She feared that one day, sooner or later, she and Aric would end up standing on opposite sides.
