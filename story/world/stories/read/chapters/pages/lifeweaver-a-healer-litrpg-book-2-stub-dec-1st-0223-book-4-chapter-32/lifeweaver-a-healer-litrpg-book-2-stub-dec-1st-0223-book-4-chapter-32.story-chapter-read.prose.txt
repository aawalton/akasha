Perhaps staying intertwined there on the grass for another hour wasn't the nicest thing to do when Ella was waiting for him, but in that moment, Luke didn't care. He was due a little peace, quiet, and time to unwind. This at least meant he was feeling good and eager to get to work once they got off their asses and prepared to leave.

"Ready?" Luke asked.

Hannah squeezed his hand. "Ready."

The weave was all around him at all times now, as long as he cared to look for it. Reaching out to grab it was as simple as picking up a rock. Well, maybe that was a bit of an overstatement, but it had gotten easier, at least. The echo thread they needed glowed as it made its way to him, or perhaps it was he who moved in the weave towards it. In truth, it was both and neither. Reaching out to grab it with a thread of mana, he stayed his hand when Hannah spoke.

"I think I see it."

"The weave?"

"My moonscape, yes, but there's a specific one right in front of us."

Impressed, Luke turned to her. "Can you touch it?"

"I thought we had to leave?" replied with a sly smile.

"Hey!" he said, unable to keep laughter out of his voice. "Focus, Vesper!"

"Vesper Champion, if you please."

"Huh?"

"Yeah. Didn't I tell you about that? My class changed. Well, not changed, but the name of it did. Maybe a few other things, but I'm not sure yet. Need time to figure it out."

"After that Other made you stronger?"

She nodded. "Yup."

"People changing classes all over the place."

"Who else?" she asked, raising an eyebrow.

"Curtis."

"Aaw. Did he get stronger, too?"

After a slight pause, Luke said, "Yeah."

"Dammit. I was going to rub his face in how much stronger my shadows are compared to his darkness."

Luke looked over at her, frowning. "Aren't shadows and darkness the same thing?"

She narrowed her eyes. "Careful, mister."

Laughing, he returned their attention to the echo thread. "So?"

A shadow extended from Hannah's finger, almost as thin as a thread of mana, and she swiped at it without any luck. "Nope."

"Maybe it'll come to you. For now, let's get going."

After having taken hold of so many echo threads at that point, it had almost become second nature to him, interacting with the weave and its threads. Holding one was still an uncomfortable experience, but his body had adapted to it somehow. That was a good thing, since it meant blood didn't shower out of his eyes and ears each time, and his internal organs didn't turn to mush. While healing himself from that damage had become a simple enough thing, not having to was a nice quality of life improvement.

The thread brought them all the way from wherever this place was to Norway, and they appeared right behind Ella, startling a large group of people. Weapons appeared, and spellweaves formed all over in the air before the young Rusher raised her hands. "Ro dere ned alle sammen, det er den jeg har fortalt dere om!"

Ella turned and frowned at Hannah. "I don't know you, though."

"This is my girlfriend, Hannah," Luke said.

Hannah glanced up at him from under her uneven bangs, a teasing smile on her lips as her cheeks flushed. "I am?"

"Yup," Luke said with conviction.

"Hannah," Hannah said, extending a hand to Ella, who took it. "The girlfriend."

"Ella."

"Hey, everyone!" Luke then said, raising his voice so the fifty or so people in the room could hear him. "Thank you all for coming to help us with this. Sorry, we're late!"

No one said a word. They just stared at him.

He leaned over toward Ella. "Do they speak English?"

"Of course they speak English," she huffed. "Everyone here speaks English. Do you speak Norwegian?"

"Ja," Luke said with his best mock-German accent.

Ella rolled her eyes. "They have been waiting. We have a lot to do. If you hadn't noticed, the Singers are occupying our country. This better be important."

Looking around, he saw no Singers. He saw the sea stretching as far as the eye could see in all directions except one, where he spotted land in the far distance. They were high up on an island. Farther down a long slope, a small town rested against the side of a mountain, near the water. Not a portal as far as the eye could see. That was the resistance's strategy in this country, then. They kept themselves away and isolated. Good plan.

"I think it is," Luke said. "Let's not waste any more time, then. If you would ask your friends to hold hands."

"We're not going to sing, are we?"

Luke gave her a bemused look. "We might."

Once everyone had gathered and linked hands, it was time to get started. Luke extended a thread of mana to the closest person, a woman in her thirties who looked like she was going hiking, complete with a huge backpack and sturdy boots. She flinched at first, but then settled down. That one thread continued through her, to the next person, and the next, and the next, all the way to the end. This was more people than he needed, but it was better to err on the safe side.

Like in the dungeon, Luke settled into deep meditation and let the weave come to him. Now that he knew it was possible, taking the threads and forming the spellweave of a portal proved a simple enough task. Simple did not equal easy, though, and by the time it formed into the physical realm, motes of light dancing in front of it, Luke's new T-shirt was drenched in sweat. Several of the Integrated had fainted, and some more swayed on their feet, but they would all be fine given a couple of minutes of rest.

"That is impressive, not going to lie," Hannah said, stepping up to the portal. "Where does it go?"

"That's the next step," Luke said, looking over at Ella. "Hey, speedster. Why don't you head down to the town, and we'll save these good people the trek back."

The outline of Ella blurred, and then she was gone.

This next part was far easier. Pulling on her echo thread, Luke fastened it to the portal, wincing at the discomfort of holding a system weave. No key was necessary this time.

"There," he said, waving to the group. "You can go through now!"

They didn't move.

Sighing, Luke stood. "It's safe, guys. Here, I'll show you."

Stepping through, he found himself on a small, cobbled street, surrounded by wooden cottages with small, lush yards. Ella stood in front of what looked like a private clinic, the building built with brick. He waved at her, then headed back through and onto the mountainous slope.

"See?" he said. "It's fine."

Ella came through and waved at them. "Kom dere vekk herfra, tisper!"

That did the trick. Soon, it was only the three of them left.

"What now?" Ella asked.

Luke grinned. "Now you run until you see the thread coming from the portal."

"That's it?" she asked, looking doubtful.

"Maybe," he said with a shrug. "You needed to be running to see the weave. Err, starry sky, was it? So run."

Ella ran.

Milla: "What are you doing, Luke? I don't like you running off right before we're going through with your plan."

Luke: "I'm training resistance fighters in Norway."

Luke: "Also, Hannah is back."

Milla: "I know, she checked in."

Milla: "Something you should do more often."

Milla: "Also, Ray has found a couple of portals. They are grouped together."

Luke: "Really? He hasn't said anything to me."

Milla: "Did you ask? I asked. I asked a lot, Luke. Also, could you get Curtis to come back to NY? We'll need him for this. Penny is here already."

Luke: "I'll see what I can do. When do you need us back?"

Milla: "A couple of hours ago."

Milla: "The EDP is ready."

Luke: "Be right there."

Hannah walked up to stand right next to him, pressing her side into his. "Milla?"

"Yeah. We should get going soon. There'll be hell to pay if we're late."

"You're lucky to have a sister who cares so much."

"Sure," he scoffed, then thought better of it. "You're right."

"I can see it, you know."

He turned to her. "See what? The thread?"

"Yep."

"You want to try breaking it?"

"I am," she said. "Look at it."

Taking a closer look, he saw it shudder. Once, then twice.

"That's you?"

"Behold my might," she said with a sigh, making it shudder again. "I can thump its moonlight like a drum. It's not breaking, though. Guess I've got more training to do."

"Are you using your shadows?"

She shook her head. "They can't pass through to that other side, no matter how hard I try. This," she focused and made it shudder again, "is all will."

"Not bad," he said.

Luke: "Do you see it?"

Ella: "I see it."

Luke: "So break it already! We have to go. It's time to do this for real!"

Ella: "How do I break it?"

Ella: "I ran through. That did nothing."

Luke: "You have to want it to break. That want is important. Use your will to shift yourself to the metaphysical realm and destroy it!"

Ella: "You sound like a deranged philosophy professor."

Luke: "Thank you."

Ella: "That was not a compliment."

Luke: "You never met my professor. Now break that star!"

Ella: "It's a streak in the starry sky."

Luke: "So break that streak!"

After a couple of minutes of silence, Ella appeared in front of him, panting hard. Her stomach growled like an actual predator. It sounded like the Rusher needed a good, hearty meal. She shook her head. "I can't do it."

"Well. Good on you for trying. You'll get there," he said, turning to Hannah. "Both of you will."

Neither of them looked convinced.

Milla: "It's time. Let's go!"

Luke sliced through the thread and then destabilized the portal itself, closing it. That done, he clapped his hands once. "Let's go! It's time we won this war of ours!"

"We're going to the same place," Ella said. "Race you there!"
