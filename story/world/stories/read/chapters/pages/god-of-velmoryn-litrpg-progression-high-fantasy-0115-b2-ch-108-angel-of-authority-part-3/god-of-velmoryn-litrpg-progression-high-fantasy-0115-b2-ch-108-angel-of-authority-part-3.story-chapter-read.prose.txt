Did this bastard implement himself into his own angel?

I was disgusted. I could not know for certain whether that head was truly being controlled by Noah or had merely taken on his appearance, but my opinion of the Night God was already low enough that the mere possibility felt revolting.

He doesn’t even trust his own angel…

The thought unsettled me more than I expected, mostly because of how quickly I had condemned him for it.

Was I really so different?

Hadn’t I been afraid to create copies of myself for exactly the same reason?

Perhaps I had once looked just as pitiful from the outside, incapable of trusting anyone naturally, always allowing suspicion to eat away at me, convinced that the entire world was waiting for a chance to turn against me.

I sighed inwardly, thankful that I was no longer that same person. Now I trusted Tekla. I trusted Amon. Aurelion.

I pushed the thought aside before it could spiral any further. My feelings were irrelevant right now. Aurelion was what mattered.

If Noah was truly controlling this angel, and Guidance had either been wrong or somehow misled when it called the creature a manifestation of the Authority of Justice, then Aurelion’s chances of leaving this trial alive were almost nonexistent.

Once again, I considered seizing control of my divinity, tearing this entire space apart, and killing Noah’s angel myself. Even if Aurelion’s oath killed him afterward, at least I would avenge him.

But I did not.

Haste had never served me well, neither in my previous life nor in this one, and acting now would almost certainly guarantee Aurelion’s death. Trusting him and giving him a little more time was the least I could do. I owed my angel that much faith.

That did not make watching him any easier.

Regret surged in me as I felt just how much pain Aurelion was enduring, yet through all of it, his will and loyalty did not waver even once.

He glared at Noah’s angel as another spear of brilliant distortion tore through his abdomen, carving out a massive hole in his torso. Golden ichor spilled from his mouth when he coughed, his teeth clenched tightly.

“Now,” the head marked with P said, “you have been properly punished for your crimes. However, considering the circumstances that may have compelled you to take innocent lives, it is only just that we offer you a chance to repent.”

The darkness around them shifted.

Countless stars appeared across the strange space, each one different in color and size, some barely shimmering while others burned so brightly that they seemed almost alive.

“For an angel as young as yourself, it is only natural that you would know little beyond obedience,” the head continued. “Were your actions determined by your God? Did you receive orders from Him?”

I did not miss the honorific the head bearing Noah’s face used when referring to me. And I certainly did not miss what the question implied.

This trial was not merely a way to punish or kill Aurelion.

Is he trying to confirm whether I’m awake?

“Hah.” Aurelion snorted weakly and spat out the ichor that had gathered in his mouth. “You know nothing about my Lord. I was never ordered to harm the innocent. I exist to uphold my duty and protect what belongs to my Liege. My Lord has never needed to force His will upon me, because His will is my own.”

His scarlet eyes narrowed.

“Is that how your god treats you? Have you ever known happiness while serving your lord? Break your oath. Perhaps my Liege will show you mercy and accept you instead.”

Angels can do that?

Guidance had told me before that Aurelion would never be able to act against my will, but judging from his words, perhaps that had always been a matter of choice rather than something he was innately incapable of doing.

“What an insolent creature!” the head marked with E screamed. “We must free this world from such evil. DEATH TO HIM!”

“Interesting,” P said, completely ignoring the outburst. “Sadly, you may truly be a spawn of evil, inherently incapable of appreciating the freedom that comes from serving Justice. Even so, we are fair, because we were born from the Authority of Justice, and therefore we will grant you one final opportunity.”

One of the countless stars surrounding them began to move.

It had a dark crimson core surrounded by a faint green shimmer, and as it drifted closer and stopped only a step away from Aurelion, something deep within his chest reacted to it. A strange euphoria spread through him, almost instinctive, as though whatever existed inside that star was calling to something buried within his nature.

“This star belongs to a creature of the Netherworld,” P continued. “You must already feel the connection. By your nature, you possess an affinity with this creature, as you do with many others born there, including their deities.”

A disturbing smile slowly curled across Noah’s face.

“You may choose to serve one of them and begin your life anew. All you need to do is admit, and swear before us, that your actions were influenced by your Lord’s command. If you do, we will accept that the punishment you have already endured is sufficient for your crimes and release you. Our God will even help you find a new Lord.”

“If… if I were to accept your offer,” Aurelion muttered, each word heavy with pain.

“We swear upon the Authority of Justice that everything we have said is true,” P replied quickly, with the faintest trace of impatience entering its voice.

“Is that so?”

Aurelion’s lips twisted into a small, bitter smile.

“Then I would become worse than you. A servant who betrays his Lord and lies merely to save his own life, rather than cursing himself for failing to become a worthy sword of his Liege, is nothing but trash. And any fool willing to accept such a servant into their service is not worth serving.”

He coughed again, ichor mixing with his breath as it spilled from his lips.

“Now I understand. You planned all of this. You sent that pitiful god to die so my Liege would be forced into slumber, and I…” His expression got solemn. “I was naive enough to fall into your trap. In the end, without my Liege guiding me, I am nothing but a brute who knows how to swing a glaive.”

Aurelion…

Even in a situation this desperate, even when he had been offered a way to save his own life, Aurelion had chosen instead to strengthen the lie that I was truly asleep and leave Noah believing that the velmoryns were now without either their god or angel.

The head bearing Noah’s face, however, did not seem convinced.

It studied Aurelion for several long moments, its gaze sharp and searching, before finally turning toward J.

“I request that we proceed with the next phase.”

“Granted… Proceed… With… Judgment… Of… The… Soul,” J rasped, and Aurelion screamed almost immediately.

Dozens, no, hundreds of spears formed from that same brilliant distortion tore through his body, piercing flesh, shattering bone, and mangling him.

I was already preparing to withdraw from his senses, seize control of my divinity, and tear this cursed place apart with every bit of strength I could unleash before the Pantheon stopped me. If I acted quickly enough, I could still save him. I could still rebuild his body with my divinity before it was too late.

But Aurelion’s thoughts stopped me.

“I will not fail you… I will not become the reason your plans are unveiled…”

He repeated the words in his mind over and over like a prayer, screaming in pain as his body was torn apart.

Any doubt I still had disappeared. If I intervened, the oath he had sworn would kill him.

Fuck! Whatever I do, it still ends with his death.

The last thing I saw through Aurelion’s eyes was another spear of brilliant distortion rushing toward his face.

Then there was nothing.

No sight. No sensation.

Only Aurelion’s thoughts and the voices of Noah’s angel.

“Choose,” J rasped.

Aurelion’s vision returned, though he was no longer seeing through physical eyes. It felt different now, closer to the spiritual perception, similar to how I perceived the world.

A sphere hovered where Aurelion’s body had been, its surface a mixture of brilliant gold and vibrant scarlet. It shone fiercely against the surrounding darkness, its light pushing back against the shadows pressing in from every direction.

“Choose death for your God, and you will cease to exist,” P said. “Or sever your bond with Him, and you shall remain alive and return to the Pantheon. Should your God later pay the required price, He may even reclaim you as His angel.”

Aurelion’s sphere remained unresponsive, its golden-crimson light continuing to shine in the darkness.

Something is wrong.

With so many emotions and thoughts surging through me at once, it had taken me far too long to notice what should have been obvious.

If Noah’s angel truly possessed the power to kill Aurelion whenever it wished, then why had it spent all this time trying to break his loyalty?

Mercy? Justice?

There had been neither in this trial.

So why give him another choice?

It cannot kill him. Not unless it breaks his will first.

“I already chose,” Aurelion’s voice resonated from the sphere. “I shall serve my Liege for eternity.”

“No. You must choose one. Has all that pain taught you nothing, you stupid…” P began, irritation finally breaking through its emotionless voice.

But A silenced it.

“Rules… are… absolute…” it whispered.
