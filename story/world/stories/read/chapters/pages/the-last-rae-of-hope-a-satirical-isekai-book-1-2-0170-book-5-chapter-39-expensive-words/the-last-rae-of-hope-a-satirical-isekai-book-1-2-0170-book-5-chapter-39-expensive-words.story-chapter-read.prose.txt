Delta XII was a small, circular room covered in glowing red runes. Its center display formed a magic circle about six feet in diameter.

“Set him in the center,” Galenus instructed. He appeared rather pleased with himself, and all things considered, I had to admit his smugness was earned. Without his portals, we never would have won so decisively.

“Alright.” I reached out with Ragnerus’s core, and a gentle wind caught it, merrily tossing the ball back and forth for a moment before having it settle at about eye height. Then dark mist flared from the circle's humming outer rings, swirling and covering the demon core like a tucked blanket.

“Approximately twelve hours,” Galenus said before frowning slightly. “Possibly less, given his constitution. I would not be surprised if he reforms early into his irritating self.”

“Then what?”

His ears flattened. “Then he becomes your problem once more.”

“Is this about the General dragging you around by the horns earlier as his personal mobile transportation device, or does it go beyond that?”

“My horns are my best feature, delicate though they be!” Galenus brayed. “Do you know how long I studied to perfect their shape?!”

“They are very handsome,” I agreed.

“Beautiful,” he corrected sharply, even as he appeared a bit mollified.

“Pardon. I misspoke. Beautiful.”

Olethros’s horns have a more appealing curvature, though.

“And they are most certainly not for touching.”

Was that a demon thing in general, or just for him?

“Certainly not…” I agreed quietly.

True horns on animals have blood vessels…Would demon horns feel warm?

Maybe he'd let me find out—

“While I cannot sense your mental ruminations, you’re emanating a strange animus.” Galenus stared at me.

I let out an embarrassed laugh and picked off a bit of sod I had stuck to my shoulder. “Sorry, uh, just thinking I should clean up a little while I have the time.”

Galenus nodded, polite enough to ignore my blatant lie. “You do seem to collect dirt at an alarming rate.”

The shower was the best reward. I stood under it until the water ran clear, and then some, preparing myself for my next confrontation.

Nora’s room was not what I had been expecting. Her bedroom back home served as the foundation even here, but that’s as far as the simulation went.

Everything else in it was different.

Holographic maps of Speranza had claimed most of the walls, with translucent scribble in Nora’s handwriting adding that personal touch. A three-dimensional model of Naught now floated, rotating slightly as it cast an ethereal blue glow over the organized chaos of her desk. Her phone was there, along with open notebooks, a half-eaten something, and a bunch of tiny gemstones of varying qualities.

Plants only found in Speranza sat in her window.

At least her bed was the same, except Chester had hollowed out his own loaf-shaped divot in her bedspread.

He opened one green eye, assessed me in silence, then closed it again.

Glad I meet your approval.

“Much better,” Nora approved as she swiveled her desk chair to face me. “Have a seat.”

It was the beanbag I always sat in.

I glanced around, locating the map with the holy city circled on it. “We should probably start talking about how we’re going to approach Chairo. Ragnerus laid siege to it decades ago, and he was preparing to do it again before all of this. He might have insight we don’t.”

“Which is why we should save this talk for when he wakes up,” Nora advised.

“You’re right, of course,” I agreed. “I was just looking for a place to start.”

Nora grimaced. “A lot of his old subordinates are watching us. He might be able to get them to fully commit to our side, but as it stands, our numbers would be hard for a full-on siege. Even then, I’m not sure we have the time to dedicate.”

I could smell the dark reveal coming.

You can’t hide your concerns any longer, Nora!

“Why is time a factor?” I asked, feigning a bit of vapidness.

She sighed, reached for her phone, and clutched it tightly. “Relias is still pulling Paradise toward Chairo, just slower now. I don’t know exactly why, and I don’t want to speculate too much, mostly because none of my ideas on it are pleasant.” She paused, looking at the Naught hologram twirling above her desk. “What we do know is that Chairo is amassing amity right now. How? I can’t say—though I suspect the leylines play a part in it. But for what Purpose? I don’t think we want to find out either, but I think we will, if we let him reach his goal.”

“Makes sense…” I trailed off, making a show of settling into the beanbag chair a little more before putting my hands casually behind my head. “So, what else have you been up to while I go out on my assigned side quests?”

“I beg your pardon?”

I kept my eyes on the ceiling so I couldn’t make eye contact. “Elenora Beatrice Perez. I know you. So, let’s not beat around the bush. You did necessary things and kept me out of them as a necessity. But I think it’s time to tell me what you’ve had a hand in. I don’t want any surprises.”

The room was quiet for a moment.

Then Nora sighed. “You remember Mother Liora? Crazy priestess and mass murderer?”

I sat up with a jolt. “How could I possibly forget her?!”

Nora held up a finger and opened her mouth, but then thought better of it. “Anyway. She’s been propagangelizing for Relias ever since her exorcism.”

“She’s been what?”

“I’m coining this one, Rae. Verb. Present participle. Definition. The act of disseminating a strategically crafted message that one also genuinely believes, particularly when the goal is to disrupt an existing faith structure or dominant narrative. Do you approve?”

“The definition only! The actual act sounds bad for business. Also, what is this about an exorcism?

“Oh yeah… I kept that part from you, too. So… Relias hasn’t been on any of the broadcasts for weeks now. He didn’t look very good on his last one—I’d say someone gave him a shiner, but who’d be around to punch a priest up there? Anyway, Liora was officially absolved of sin in the same breath that pinned the murder of all those people in Chairo on Olethros. Oh, and me, I guess.”

“Oh, Nora…”

She shrugged, her lower lip pursing. “I know it’s a lie, so it doesn’t bother me. But whatever. She’s been giving sermons left and right, denouncing you and us for all sorts of things that aren’t true.”

“Like plotting against Chairo?” I asked, my voice falling low. “Because that one’s going to come true, which is going to make everything else she said seem to be true as well…”

Even a bigger reason not to go for a traditional siege. We’d need to do it a bit differently.

“Well… It doesn’t help with all the new murders.”

“New murders?!”

Nora visibly winced. “A whole platoon of Silver was killed in Amantia Town.”

“But… I thought we gave everyone time to get out of the castle before the partial collapse!”

“That wasn’t it,” Nora said, shaking her head sadly. “Rae, he sent angels to kill one of the outer patrols, just so Mother Liora—”

“Had something to propagangelize about,” I finished for her. “Oh…”

“I know I shouldn’t have kept it a secret,” Nora murmured. “But I didn’t want it to affect your abilities. I’m sorry—”

"No."

"No?"

I shook my head. “We talked about keeping secrets, didn’t we?”

I was ruining it, but it couldn’t be helped. This may even be our last girl talk ever, but that made it all the more important for me to make my point.

“You can apologize all you want, but I’m not going to accept it. You absolutely, positively, cannot keep things from me anymore, even if you think it’s for my own good. We are moving into dangerous territory. If something is going to upset me, then so be it. I can’t control how I feel about anything, but it’s my duty to take the appropriate action in response. Understood?”

Nora hung her head. “Yes...”

I waited for a bit, making the silence as uncomfortable as possible.

“This is the part where you say, ‘I won’t do it again,’ but you have to mean it this time, too.”

“But what if—”

“No.”

“You didn’t let me finish—”

“Uh huh.”

Nora began to mutter. “Olethros was right...”

“So this is your second scolding for withholding the same information?”

Nora let out a sullen squeak.

“Louder and more articulated.”

“I won’t do it again.”

“I’m holding you to that,” I warned. “And if I ask you something, I want the truth, even if it’s ugly.”

“Alright.”

Another long moment of silence.

“So… You… and Olethros, huh?” she said awkwardly.

I squinted at her. “What about us?”

“I…” She trailed off. “Congratulations?”

Oh my god. Did he tell her about the head pat thing?!

No... She just probably...

I mean, it wasn’t like I was keeping secrets—anymore, that is.

I inhaled deeply for a moment, then let it out with a slow, tiny sigh. “...So you approve?”

Nora wiggled her nose. “I don’t disapprove.”

“I’ll take what I can get.”

There. A nice, calming thought. We’d be back to talking normal girl talk any time now—

“Have you said I love you yet?”

“Nora!”

“What?!”

I glared at her, but she returned it with a look so cold and steely that I shivered.

“Not in so many words,” I admitted begrudgingly.

Damnit, why?! This was supposed to be about her shortcomings, not mine!

“It’s only three words.”

“They’re expensive, okay?! Especially that second one! I’ll… When the time is right… I’ll be sure to say—”

“Make sure that you do,” she said in a voice that was much older-sounding than it should have been. “So you don’t have any regrets.”

The precise way she said it sat heavily with me, and so our third moment of silence stretched into minutes. She knew my long-term goal without me having to admit it. She was also saying she didn’t disapprove of that, either.

At least not officially.

“You’ll let me be the one to say it, right? I think I deserve the right to explain myself to him—”

“I’m not going to interfere, promise. As long as you keep your side of the deal.”

I’ll make sure he knows it’s not him.

It’s me.

“Of course.”

Chester stood up with a stretch, his gaze finding mine.

“You better not say anything either.”

His eyes narrowed ever so slightly, but before I could secure his unspoken agreement, Galenus’s voice cut through the room.

“The General wishes to speak with you, Captain Lightbringer.”
