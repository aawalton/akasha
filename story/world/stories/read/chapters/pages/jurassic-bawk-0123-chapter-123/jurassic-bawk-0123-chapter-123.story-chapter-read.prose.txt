“This is ludicrous,” the Empress of the Forest muttered as she floated back and reassessed the group in front of her.  “You, Squire, explain how you were able to gain a Momentum Core at such a low level.”

“I might be motivated to offer this information, should you offer commensurate compensation,” the pangolin offered.

“Hmmm,” the Empress mused.  “Perhaps I could give you some pointers on how to manipulate temperature by changing the inherent kinetic energy of atoms.”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne interrupted.

“Please, allow us to speak for a while,” Phatagin begged.  “So long as we can be assured that our adversary is not preparing some sort of ambush, what harm would it cause to wait?”

“Bawwk!” the rooster rebutted.

“Yes, I can understand that this conversation might bore you, but please listen carefully; it might give you ideas about your own growth as well.”

The rooster had to admit that his Squire had a point.  The Empress’ body structure was quite different than anything he had encountered before.  And, although her core was less efficient at storing mana than his own, it seemed to allow for surprising versatility.  Different types of mana danced around each other in an endless whirl, avoiding direct collisions of opposing types by interposing neutral elements between them.  Charlemagne had already learned the “flavor” of fire and ice-aspected mana.  Now that he had experienced the power of lightning, he could also recognize the electrically charged torrents of mana that circulated in strange fits and starts, darting around the slower moving elements in a never-ending race.

But there were other mana types heavily represented inside the Empress’ Core.  One felt heavy and ponderous, slower to react and more difficult to separate, but powerful.  Another flowed smoothly, separating momentarily to dodge obstacles before coming back together into a cohesive unit once again.  Yet another was almost impossible to detect, its distinctive signature fading as soon as Charlemagne noticed it for the first time.  There were hints of even more, each with their own particular attributes, and Charlemagne fell into a trance studying them.  Phatagin, not receiving any negative response to his plea, decided that silence was acquiescence and carried on with the conversation.

“Well, to be totally honest, I actually do not know how I acquired my Momentum Core.  I suspect it had something to do with my Patron, who had gifted me with the prerequisite Skills required to obtain it.”

“You have a Patron deity?” the Empress gasped, shocked by the revelation.  “But you’re…well that is…the only way that could be possible is…you are a First Gen!”

Josephine nodded, appearing to confirm the other woman’s suspicion.

“What is a ‘First Gen’?” the pangolin asked.

“You know, one of the first generation.  Those who have experienced an Integration.  Man, that response really clinches it.  You’re totally First Gens.”

“Indeed we are,” the pangolin agreed.

“We?  Oh, you must mean your Knight, not the other one.”

“My name’s Josephine, thanks,” the Attendant added.

The Empress of the Forest put a finger to her lips in thought as she considered the other woman, as if seeing her for the first time.

“There’s something off about you, too,” she said after a moment.  “But I can’t put my finger on it.”

“I’m an Attendant.  Ser Charlemagne dragged me into danger despite the rules clearly stating that chivalrous behavior would be rewarded.  I still can’t believe the System let him do it.”

“Huh, that’s interesting.  Not as interesting as a First Gen whose mana control is so advanced that people would destroy entire star systems for a chance to learn his secrets, but interesting.”

“Bawk?” the rooster asked, perking up at the compliment and feeling proud of himself.

“Yes, yes, but don’t let it go to your head.  Your race is clearly well suited for the task.  You’re practically a Vessarian yourself internally.”

Charlemagne wasn’t sure what the word “Vessarian” meant, but through the literally magical translation of the System, he understood that the Empress was referring to her own species.  With that in mind, he took a closer look at the internal structure of her body, moving away from the Core to look at how everything fit together.

The first thing that the rooster noticed was that the Empress had a spine.  His Vertebrate Anatomy Skill told him as much before falling mute, as it was still far too low level to provide useful information about a species never before encountered on Earth.  The second thing Charlemagne noticed was that the Empress’ entire biology seemed to be designed around her Core, which was located at the exact center of her mass, in her abdomen.  Thick mana veins radiated outward into her limbs, which were formed from large blobs of protoplasm.  The limbs were reinforced both by a hardened external layer that acted like a suit of armor and by crystalline rods that were held together by a strange force.

More mana veins were protected by the spinal column, which formed a double helix that ran through her torso, before branching off into four identical connectors.  These were where the Empress’ limbs connected to the rest of her body.

But the most shocking thing that Charlemagne discovered about the Empress of the Forest was that her head was largely for show.  Yes, she had eyes, a face, and ears, but these appeared to be easily movable, being attached to her spine by long strands of flexible connective tissue.  She also did not appear to have a brain: that is, her neural tissues were distributed into small nodules spread throughout her body.

“Bawk,” he denied, having completed his examination.  He realized enough to know that his body and the Empress’ operated differently, despite their external similarities.

“How fascinating,” she answered.  “And how do you know that?”

“Bawk bawk, bawk bawwak,” he expounded, happy to talk about himself at length.

When he finished his explanation, Charlemagne expected the Empress to be impressed.  But she appeared angry instead.  An icy wind whipped up around her, causing both Phatagin and Josephine to shudder with cold.

“I have dealt honestly with you, mister Knight.  If you cannot treat me with the same courtesy, the time for words has passed.”

Charlemagne was confused but willing to put words aside and commence combat.  But, just as he was about to rush forward and attack, Phatagin’s angry yell stopped him short.

“Hey now!  You dare call Charlemagne a liar?  Every word that he spoke was one hundred percent truthful!”

The Empress of the Forest pulled back, her eyes wide with shock.  The whistling wind died out as quickly as it had sprung up.

“Attendant!  You have no reason to lie.  Is what your Knight said the truth?”

Josephine looked thoughtful for a moment as she considered the question.  Finally, she shrugged.

“As far as I can tell, it probably is true.  I haven’t travelled with Ser Charlemagne and Squire Phatagin for very long, but I have yet to see them flat out lie to anyone.”

“I am convinced that Ser Charlemagne is, by nature, prone to be truthful,” Phatagin added, not knowing that the rooster had recently lied about possessing a Pain Resistance Skill.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne concluded.

“If you were not born with such a fiery nature, then that means…wait, how did you obtain your Core?  It’s not a Mana Core, is it?  Because it doesn’t feel like one.”

The rooster explained in detail how he had obtained his Mana Core and upgraded it to an Ember Core.  To her credit, Phasima listened to the unlikely tale without interrupting.  When he was finished, the Empress literally sat down in the air and put her head between her hands.

“So you not only managed to brute force your way into obtaining a Mana Core the first time you were exposed to mana, you then upgraded it by eating a priceless System treasure and combined it with a Skill that a First Gen never should have obtained, let alone mastered.  Then you somehow obtained the prerequisites for one of the most potent fire-based physique Skills known across the multiverse.  I feel like I lost some IQ points listening to all that, but it’s just so dumb that it can’t possibly be made up.  Ser Charlemagne, you might just be the most unique existence in the entire multiverse.  I want to join you in your travels.”

Charlemagne thought for a moment, wondering how long it would take until something else killed the Empress so that he could consume her corpse and wishing that the Squiggles would stop robbing him of meals in these make-believe worlds.  Then he reflected on the fact that he had already filled up on human fingers and rings.  Perhaps it really would be best to wait.

“Bawk,” he finally assented.

“Great!” the Empress said with a huge smile.  “Hopefully this will work then.  Follow me.”

The Empress turned, seemingly unworried about a surprise attack from the rear, and regally floated over to one of the many icy columns that rose from the icy floor to support the icy roof.  She pressed a hidden button, causing a section of the floor to slide back to reveal a winding staircase.

“Come,” the Empress said as she began to float down the staircase.  Charlemagne followed, hoping that their destination was not far.  He didn’t really enjoy being underground.  Phatagin and Josephine brought up the rear.

Thankfully for Charlemagne, the quartet quickly left the stairs behind, exchanging them for a long, dimly lit hallway flanked by statues sculpted from ice.  Phatagin looked thoughtfully at the jagged sculptures, somehow sensing the aggression that oozed from the inanimate objects.

“I’ve disabled the ice golems in this room, so they won’t attack you,” the Empress of the Forest assured the trio as she led them down the hallway.  “In fact, I have issued orders for all of my minions to stand down.  There is really no point in sending them to their deaths, after all.”

At the end of the hallway stood an enormous pair of double doors, which swung open ponderously at the approach of their mistress to reveal an enormous circular chamber.  In the center of the room stood a large golden altar with coals glowing brightly within.  Ominous runes were etched into the stone walls and ceiling, while strange geometric shapes covered the floor.

“This is the ritual chamber, where I am supposed to complete the Ritual of Severing three nights hence,” the Empress explained.

“What is the function of the Ritual of Severing?” Phatagin asked, not understanding the function of anything within the room.

“Yeah, this doesn’t seem like any sort of Ritual I’ve ever heard of,” Josephine added as she looked around.

“That’s because it’s all made up.  The System decided that the Ritual’s completion marks the end of this scenario, or ‘room’ as I think it’s calling this little charade.  All I have to do is stand by the altar and channel mana into it until all the runes are fully lit.  There are several sites scattered throughout the area that are supposed to make it easier to complete, which you could have destroyed if you had found them before coming here.”

“Well, we are here now,” Phatagin said, stating the obvious.  “What now?”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne answered.

“That makes no sense,” the pangolin objected.  “Why would you fight now?”

The Empress of the Forest turned and faced her guests before laughing.

“Oh no, good Squire.  Your Knight is correct.  I can’t just give up and allow you to stop me from doing my job.  This was always going to end in us duking it out.  But I do have a suggestion, if you’re open to it.”

“Bawk,” the rooster answered.

“I thought as much.  Well, here’s my suggestion…we fight until one of us is no longer able to continue, but refrain from any lethal blows if at all possible.  If you’re able to vanquish me, you invite me to your Party, if you’re in one.  Or I can invite you if you’re comfortable with that.  If I win, then you must surrender so I do not have to slaughter your Squire and Attendant.”

“Bawk!” the rooster readily agreed.

“Then prepare yourself,” The Empress stated as she flew up higher into the air, raw power gathering into the palms of both her hands.  “You might be the greatest savant at using mana that I’ve ever seen, but I’ve got more than a few tricks up my sleeve!”

With that, a burst of icy shards lanced towards Charlemagne, sending Phatagin and Josephine running for cover.
