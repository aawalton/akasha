Chapter 372

It was lucky that the rest of the group had stepped all the way to the back of the laboratory and in behind a durable protective screen before Eik had actually sent any of his aura into the mixture.

The foundation had detonated like a road side bomb, but, with his aura, Eik had shut down the actual destructive outburst from his own ass flesh utterly. That meant that, in addition to his own blood, Eik’s big, expensive laboratory was now absolutely covered in all kinds of mashed plant matter and mushy animal parts.

For a few moments, he watched the many slimy substances slowly slide down the walls and drip from the ceiling in viscous, gooey drops. One by one, the alchemists stepped out from the cover of the screen and tip-toed through the mess that was slathered across the floor as well.

Heath was last to follow them out of the perceived safety, trauma still fresh from the time Eik had tested Death Star, basically leveled a continent outright, and then, with a grin, conjured up another one after almost killing every single other person present at the demonstration.

Eik cleared his throat as he threw several brief glances at the guild master. “Uuh, right, so… As you may have already figured out, that was just a little test. The real attempt comes next.”

“Uh huh,” Meri grumbled. “And have I also correctly figured out that you’re going to need me to find all the correct materials again and my grandpa to read the instructions out to you again while you work?”

“… Maybe.”

“But I must admit, Mr. Magnasen,” Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak piped up, picking up the notebook in which the order and specificities of the mixing were written and flipping back to the beginning of the recipe. “I’m really rather surprised that a magical ingredient, that is essentially you, would react so violently when the one who tried to influence it was its origin.”

Eik sighed. “Yeah, tell me about it. That was actually the exact reason why I thought it would be a piece of cake. Unfortunately, as it turns out, a remnant of my will still resides within even that small piece of me, and I would absolutely hate to be reduced to a liquid in a bottle.”

“So you’re saying it’s impossible?” Meri questioned, dark despair slashing through her face. “You can’t go against yourself?”

Eik blinked. “Impossible? Of course it’s not impossible. If there’s anyone who can get me to submit and obey, it’s Ihasu, but after her it’s my kids or my dad, but after them, it’s me. It might not be as easy as I had initially hoped, but if anybody is going to beat my ass, it’s going to be me.”

Hope returning to her features just as quickly as it had faded, Meri was suddenly all too eager to gather up the ingredients for the second attempt. She rushed around the laboratory and opened a crate stuffed full of leafy stalks here and unzipped a pouch of unknown powder there.

Along with the rest of the team of alchemists she must really have been working hard, because she barely threw a single glance at the list of materials for the mixture as she flew from drawer to drawer like a whirlwind, rapidly building a new heap on the cloth next to Eik’s cauldron.

Cauldron already clean, Eik took a deep breath and reached for the flat-leafed white root and crushed it at the bottom. “Please guide me, Guild Master.”

***

It took Eik another seven attempts to get the mixture to a point where it stopped lashing out violently before it had even been attacked. And after that, it was eight more to get it to calm down enough to actually be workable.

Gradually overcome by the urge to get back to his own project again, Heath eventually left to work on his exam blade. Eik didn’t blame him. Watching a guy mash plants into a cauldron until it exploded in his face was pretty funny for the first five or maybe six times, but without an existing interest in alchemy, it would get old after a while, even if the completed work would be perhaps the most powerful object in history.

“All right, all right. Okay,” Eik breathed through a deep sigh, although he didn’t for a moment let his vigilance waver as he continued to watch the mixture warily. “I think it’s stable now. Finally. What now?”

Ganyak hir Gawak left the safety of the protective screen and inched slowly closer to the cauldron until he could risk a peek at the stable ingredients. “It does seem to be stable, but as you can surely see as well, it has no power to grant to the imbiber.”

“Why is that anyway?”

The guild master pursed his lips. “Well, there is simply more to successful alchemy than simply putting the ingredients together. What you have done, as you know, is pacified the active agent. But that alone doesn’t unify the materials into a coherent harmony. To put it simply, the reason alchemists need immaculate aura control is in order to persuade these quasi-sentient forces to take a form according to our wills.”

“So I just do what I always do?”

“Pretty much, yeah. Do whatever works in order to get the energies to obey.”

“I can do that,” Eik said resolutely.

Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak hurried to retreat back behind the protective screen where he practically squished his face against the transparent surface to get the very best view of what would happen next.

Taking a few seconds to psych himself up, Eik took a firm hold of his aura, keeping it in a steady, controlled grip like an expert surgeon holding a scalpel. One wrong move right now would result in an instant failure.

Even before making contact, he could feel the power of his own physical remnant floating around in the mixture. Naturally, he could tell that the origin of its power was his own, but at the same time, it had indeed become separate from him and he couldn’t just take control over it.

Like a wary animal, it swirled about, permeating the entire mixture, as if just waiting for him to go in for the first strike.

And with an abrupt explosion of speed, he did.

Immediately, it put up a defensive mass of aura in order to deflect his first lunge, while simultaneously sending a spike of hostile energy back against him. He seized the spike with ease and shattered it like a window pane against an anvil. In terms of raw might, the mixture was completely impotent in front of X-ranker Eik Magnasen.

Unfortunately, this was one of those times where raw might wasn’t the only thing that mattered. Yes, Eik could have eradicated this mixture and the remnants of power within with half a thought, but this thing had to come out on the other side even more intact than it was right now.

When you had to both incapacitate and completely preserve the enemy, even just a fraction more power in the opponent’s hand made it a thousand times more difficult to succeed.

But fuck that.

Eik might well be the least deserving Elixir King in the history of all time, and it was definitely true that he should have worked another hundred years to be worthy of the kind of things he had achieved with his alchemy.

But while he might not have theoretical knowledge worth the single piece of paper it was scribbled on, or the ability to tell his medicinal plants from his own ass, if there was one thing he was good at, it was brute forcing his way to the top of the food chain.

And he wasn’t about to let this tiny-ass version of himself outdo him.

Hundreds of small aura clashes were exchanged in the span of a few seconds, each of them absolutely powerless to touch Eik at all, but slowly and surely shaving away at the spiritual integrity of the mixture. He felt it lose strength in real time.

As its coherence unraveled and its lashings grew weaker, less coordinated, and less frequent, Eik saw his chances of completing the potion dwindle to something that resembled zero.

That was, unless he did something creative.

Changing the flow and demeanor of his aura completely in an instant, Eik heard the gathered alchemists behind the screen gasp at his insane aura proficiency.

Even as he had rocketed up through the power ranks, the fact that the sensations of controlling Profound Toxin and his aura were nearly identical never changed. That meant that his skill with Profound Toxin farmed automatic expertise in aura control as well.

And as an X-ranked Worldbreaker, Eik was very good with Profound Toxin.

Where even a legitimate expert alchemist like Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak was certainly considered a master of aura control, and someone like Andihar possessed nearly unrivaled aural potency, they were nothing next to Eik.

Capable of basically flooding an entire continent with killing energy at will and performing feats of aura manipulation that should have been impossible, he was quite simply operating at magnitudes above others.

In a one hundred and eighty, Eik completely halted his aggressive efforts and switched into a mixture of harmless suppression and revitalization. After all, if the mixture was dying, then all he would have to do was revive the bastard.

That made perfect sense, right?

“It’s working,” he muttered under his breath, fascinated, as the life force of the quasi-sentient alchemical mixture rapidly intensified. And intensified, seemingly without end. Maybe this was working just a little bit too well. “Oh, crap.”

With a thought, he cut the energizing flow of power short, but it was far too late to save what he had already caused.

The mixture exploded in his face.

“Fuckin’…” he hissed as he physically resisted the urge to crush the cauldron between his fingers like a freshly made wad of sourdough.

“What happened, Mr. Magnasen?” the guild master asked as he weaved through drops of viscous goop falling from the ceiling.

“I fucked it up, is what happened,” he sighed. “It was dying and I thought ‘Hey, that’s more or less my own energy, so all I have to do is pump some more of that energy into it.’”

“Ah…”

“My words exactly, homie. My words exactly. It kind of makes sense, though, doesn’t it?”

“Yes, but the capacity of a formless mixture like that to hold energy is so vastly inferior to your own, that it would be almost impossible to do without the explosive consequences you just felt on your own body.”

Eik frowned at that. “Of course it’s possible.”

Taken aback, the guild master was joined by Meri and most of the other alchemists. “No, I’m not quite sure you understand what I’m saying, Mr. Magnasen. Supplying power from a vast reservoir such as yours to a vessel so much smaller—and one that does not even possess a proper body to help regulate it—is… a recipe for destruction, not a potion.”

“No, you’re the one who doesn’t understand. I already did it back there. When I said I fucked it up, I didn’t mean that I tried something I couldn’t do. I just got a little surprised.”

“Mr. Magnasen…” Ganyak hir Gawak trailed off, looking reluctant to argue further with Eik but clearly not convinced by the X-ranker’s words.

“Just help me recreate the mixture, all right? Then I’ll show you what I mean.”

With a sigh, the guild master did as requested. Now used to the process, they quickly got the mixture to a stable condition and Eik could move on to his next attempt.

“Be careful,” the guild master said.

“You just watch from over there,” Eik told him and took a deep breath as he reached inward for his aura.

Again, he ground down the spiritual integrity of the mixture until it was on the threshold of collapse only to ram new energy in there to bring it back. But this time, he knew exactly how it would react, and instead of simply cutting the injection of energy off completely, he instead reversed half of the flow to run a simultaneous supply and drain.

Now he could control the mixture’s energy supply down to the individual mote.

And then, from one moment to the next, it quieted. Reacting instantly, Eik froze the process and watched.

Yes.

“I’ve done it.” The announcement was met with gasps as the alchemists rushed out from behind the protective screens, but before they could make it halfway across the laboratory, a sudden blast of energy threw them backward with force.

Spectral limbs reached out of the cauldron. The potion was warping.
