ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia, Mental Supporter Lv. 3, seeks a moment of peace at the Nexus Bazaar but is immediately ambushed by a notorious player hacker named LiviaFan69. The fanboy, who hacked the server seeking a waifu selfie, is intercepted by the Bloodbath & Beyond guild—but not before Livia has to reckon with his unexpected vulnerability. The encounter forces her to draw a line between compassion and enabling, and defines her new role as the guild’s fiercely guarded anchor.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 3 and accidental server celebrity, had made a decision.

She was going outside.

Not far. Just away.

The guild’s base camp had been, for the past several days, the entirety of her world—a contained ecosystem of neuroses, plushies, philosophical basket-weaving, and one wooden heart from a slime that she had moved to the back of her notepad and was not thinking about. She needed air. Or the fantasy equivalent of air. Whatever this world’s atmosphere was.

The Nexus Bazaar was the MMORPG world’s answer to a city center: a sprawling marketplace hub where players traded legendary gear, NPCs spammed holographic quest boards with the enthusiasm of people who had never been told no, and a glitched wyvern hologram the size of a house advertised “Epic Loot Crates: 99% Off!” in rotating neon letters. A bard-bot near the entrance strummed a jingle that was technically a song about adventure but structurally identical to a furniture store commercial.

Livia found a stone bench near a fountain, sat down, and opened her notepad to a blank page.

This is nice, she thought. This is genuinely nice. Nobody here knows who I am.

A player three benches over leaned toward his friend and whispered, loudly, “That’s the NPC from r/LiviaWaifuHub.”

Right.

[Achievement Unlocked: Bazaar Escapee. +10 Serenity Points (Temporary)]

“Temporary’s right,” Livia muttered. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 3: 2900/3000 EXP. One hundred away from the next level. Her Empath Surge was running in the background, a constant low hum of other people’s feelings that she was learning, slowly, to manage like background noise.

She scribbled: Issue: Can’t escape fame. Even at the Bazaar. Approach: Accept this is permanent. Mourn briefly. Move on.

She had just started on the mourning section when she heard it.

Running footsteps. Garish armor. Heart emojis.

He was a Level 45 Rogue, which meant he was strong enough to hack a server but not strong enough to do much else. His name tag read LiviaFan69, and he was holding a Livia plushie above his head like a torch.

“Empath Eternal!” He skidded to a stop in front of her bench, breathing hard. “I hacked the server to find you! Sign my plushie! And can you say—” he produced a digital recorder from somewhere, —“breathe in for four— for my ASMR channel?”

He was already brandishing a sketchpad. The art on it was detailed. Technically accomplished. Very much NSFW. Livia recognized the black lace from the basement shrine.

Of course he has the shrine version.

“You hacked the server,” Livia said, very carefully, “to find me.”

“Forty-seven attempts!” he said, with the pride of someone reporting a personal best. “The firewall your guild put up is insane. Seventeen layers! I had to route through three separate—”

“Please stop,” Livia said.

[Warning: Server Breach Detected. Achievement Unlocked: Stalked by a Superfan]

She opened her notepad: Patient: LiviaFan69. Issue: Hacked game. Brought sketchpad. Approach: Unclear. Do not look at sketchpad again.

Fan69 was still talking, the words tumbling out with the velocity of someone who had rehearsed this and was now running the rehearsal at double speed.

“I know it’s weird, okay, I know that, but your ASMR video— the ten-hour one— I’ve listened to it every night for two months. Every single night.” He paused. “I went through a really bad stretch. Like, bad bad. Lost my job. My apartment. My…” He stopped himself. Started again at a different angle. “I’d put it on and you’d say— breathe in for four— and I could. I actually could.”

His voice had lost the manic edge.

[Patient: LiviaFan69. Surface: Desperation. Beneath: Genuine loneliness. Prolonged isolation. Latching onto parasocial connection as substitute for real support. Severity: High.]

He’s not a creep who became lonely, Livia thought. He’s lonely and it came out as this.

She closed her notepad.

“That sounds like a really hard time,” she said, carefully. “I’m genuinely sorry.”

Fan69 blinked. Like he hadn’t expected that.

“But—” she continued, —“what you’re describing—the job, the apartment, the bad stretch—that’s real. What I am is not.”

“You feel real,” he said. “In the subreddit, we’re all watching you and it’s like— you actually listen. You don’t judge. Out there—” he gestured vaguely upward, toward whatever sky existed above the game, —“I’m invisible. Just another guy. But you see people.”

Livia looked at him for a moment.

He’s not wrong about what he felt. He had listened to her voice every night for two months because it was the only thing that helped him breathe. That was real. His pain was real.

And he had hacked a server, brought NSFW fanart, and was holding a plushie of her face.

Both things were true at the same time.

“I hear you,” she said. “Really. And what you felt during those nights was real. If those sessions helped, that matters.”

His expression opened slightly.

“But I’m not what you need,” she said, and kept her voice even. “An NPC who helped you breathe is not a substitute for the real thing. A subreddit of people watching me isn’t the same as people watching out for you. And hacking a server to find me—” she gestured at the sketchpad, —“this isn’t connection. This is you being so alone that you aimed all of it at the nearest available thing.”

Fan69 stared at her.

“You deserve actual support,” Livia said. “Real people. Real help. Not a ten-hour loop of an NPC telling you to breathe.”

His face did something complicated.

“What if I can’t find that?”

“Then you look harder,” she said. “And you start by going back to your world. Post your memes if you want. But put the sketchpad away and log off and call someone. Anyone.”

A pause.

Then, from behind a vendor stall, a pink beam swept the ground.

Bloodbath & Beyond had, apparently, been tracking her.

Bron arrived first, armor clanking, face carrying the expression of a man who had sprinted from the base camp and was prepared to continue sprinting through this person if required. “Nobody creeps on our Buffer!” he announced, to the Bazaar at large.

Phina came next, Glimmer pulsing with arcane light that was significantly more threatening than a slime had any right to produce. “Away, defiler of the Empath’s grace,” she said, with the serenity of someone who had already decided the outcome.

Nyx materialized from behind a vendor stall, flashlight sweeping, daggers drawn. “Uh… back off,” he said, and despite the delivery it was somehow the most menacing of the three.

Miles came last, as he always did, stepping in front of Livia with his psychoblade humming at his hip. He looked at Fan69 with the specific expression of a man evaluating a problem he had already solved.

“You’ve got guts, Fan69,” Miles said. “Hacking our server. Coming after our therapist.” He tilted his head. “Bad move.”

Fan69’s bravado, already deflated by Livia’s words, flickered out entirely under five Level 90+ players staring at him.

Livia stood up. “Stop.”

The guild paused.

She looked at Fan69. “I meant what I said. Go home.”

He looked at her for a long moment. The manic joy from his entrance was completely gone. He looked, underneath it, very tired.

“Okay,” he said. Quietly. “Yeah. Okay.”

Miles snapped his fingers. A portal opened behind Fan69 with a sound like a disappointed sigh.

Fan69 stepped through it.

The portal closed.

The Bazaar cheered. Several players had been recording.

Compassion did not mean saying yes, she thought.

It didn’t mean understanding someone so completely that their behavior stopped having consequences.

She could understand why someone crossed a line.

She could still tell them they had crossed it.

[You drew a boundary with compassion and held it. +1000 EXP. Level Up! Mental Supporter Lv. 4: 900/4000 EXP. Stat Unlocked: Mental Defense +10.]

[System Note: The difference between helping someone and enabling their avoidance is knowing when to do which. You knew. The system is taking notes.]

Why are you recording that? Livia thought.

The system did not answer.

It never did, when she asked questions like that. She’d noticed. She was starting to keep a separate list.

Livia sat back down on her bench.

The wooden-heart notepad was still in her hands.

He looked tired, she thought. Underneath all of it. Just tired and alone and he aimed it at me because I was there.

I hope he calls someone.

I have no way of knowing if he will.

That’s the part they don’t tell you in the textbooks.

The guild dispersed back into the Bazaar with varying degrees of satisfaction—Bron loudly recounting the encounter to a group of players who had not asked, Phina composing what appeared to be a short verse about justice and slime solidarity, Nyx already retreating toward a shadowed corner with his flashlight.

Miles stayed.

He sat on the bench beside her—not close, a professional distance, which she noticed because he usually didn’t observe professional distances—and looked out at the Bazaar.

“You okay?” he asked.

Not “solid work, rookie.” Not “nobody messes with our therapist.” Just:

“You okay?”

Livia considered the question.

“He said I saved his life,” she said. “With the ASMR. That I helped him through something really bad.”

“Maybe you did,” Miles said.

“But I couldn’t save him from…” she gestured vaguely at the space where Fan69 had stood, —“whatever made him think hacking a game and bringing fanart was love. Or connection. Or whatever he thought it was.”

Miles was quiet for a moment.

“That’s not your job,” he said.

“Isn’t it? I’m a therapist.”

“You’re a person first,” Miles said. The smirk was entirely absent. “You don’t owe anyone yourself. Not him, not the subreddit, not…” He stopped.

Livia looked at him.

He was looking at the Bazaar.

Not the guild, she thought. Not the fanbase. He said: not the subreddit. Not anyone.

[Patient: Miles. Surface: Measured. Deliberate. Beneath: He is saying something he has needed someone to say to him. Duration: ongoing.]

She didn’t push it.

She had learned, from Nyx, that some things needed to arrive at their own speed.

“Okay,” she said.

Miles nodded once. “Okay.”

They sat there for a moment, the Bazaar moving around them.

Then Bron’s voice carried across the plaza: “BUFFER! Some guy’s selling a signed Livia plushie! Is that legal?”

Livia closed her eyes briefly.

“No,” she called back.

“He says it’s certified authentic!”

“It’s not!”

“He has a certificate!”

Miles stood, smirk returning in full. “I’ll handle it.”

He walked toward the commotion with the easy confidence of a man who had handled worse.

Livia watched him go.

You don’t owe anyone yourself.

She wrote it in the margin of her notepad, small.

Filed it next to: Miles. Not joking. Much later.

She sat on her bench until the Bazaar’s noise settled back into its usual frequency, the cheering crowd dispersed, the players who had been recording moved on to the next thing.

Her notepad was open to the Fan69 entry:

LiviaFan69: Hacked server. Brought fanart. Said I saved his life. I set the boundary. I held it. He looked tired. I hope he calls someone.

Note: Helping someone and enabling their avoidance are different things. The difference is knowing when to do which.

She had written that last line before the system had said it.

She was getting better at this.

Her old life had taught her to be a shadow, invisible, taking up as little space as possible. Here she was a beacon—not because she’d tried to be, but because she had kept showing up, kept listening, kept being present when it was easier not to be.

I am their linchpin, she thought, and for the first time it did not feel like a burden.

It felt like a choice.

She had chosen Surge.

She was still choosing it.

[Achievement Unlocked: Anchor. +10 Resolve Points]

[System Note: The system does not usually comment on subject’s personal history.]

[The system is making an exception.]

[Noted, without further elaboration: this is not the first time you have said something you needed to hear yourself.]

Livia stared at that notification for a long time.

Then she closed her notepad, tucked it under her arm, and went to go deal with the certified authentic plushie situation.

Some things, you process later.

Some things, you process by showing up anyway.

[END OF CHAPTER 9]
