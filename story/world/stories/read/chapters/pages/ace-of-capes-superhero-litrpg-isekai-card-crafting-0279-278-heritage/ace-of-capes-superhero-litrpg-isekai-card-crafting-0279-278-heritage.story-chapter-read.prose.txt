Lexie paused, considering the gaping darkness in front of her.

She’d simply assumed at first that all the lights were out, but now that she thought about it, the room didn't feel like just another hidden room.

The walls were echoey, and a cold draft emitted from nowhere, almost like an icy wind. Not to mention the voice she'd just heard.

It wasn’t a system announcement, nor was it necessarily a voice in her head, really.

It was like someone or something in the space had spoken directly to her.

It was a Guardian's temple. Specifically, the Temple of Janus.

Okay, and what was that doing here on Governor Azure’s property?

As Lexie walked in, she waited to see if the voice would come again, but nothing happened.

“Hello?” she called out.

There was no response.

Interesting. She looked behind her to see that the doorway to the office was still open, but it was covered in some kind of filmic substance that seemed to separate her from the rest of the world. What was that?  Should she try touching it? Probably not.

"The Temple of Janus, Guardian of the Keys." Lexie paused on that thought.

Did this have anything to do with the Key that the Mountain was referring to?

Was the Key here?

No, it couldn’t be that easy. Not that Lexie had to get the Key anymore, considering that she’d killed Vulcan, but now she was kind of curious about why the Mountain wanted the keys in the first place. And also why she wanted Vulcan dead.

She'd seemed confident that Lexie.

It probably wasn’t because she cared that he was using his skill for evil. The Mountain seemed to suggest that it was because he was using it too much, and he wasn't paying the price for it.

Everything Lexie had learned about Oracles seemed to discourage rampant usage of future sight, and the damage to the human body and its pathways was likely a mechanism to support this.

So why wasn't Vulcan damaged? With how much he used his powers, he should have been dead long before.

Was it because of the Alchemist? Perhaps the Alchemist had created a golem for him that would bear the brunt of the backlash and the pathway damage whenever he looked into the future.

That actually made a lot of sense, and that was probably what pissed the Mountain off, because this way, Vulcan was using up all her sight juice without any consequences.

Was backlash necessary for some kind of cosmic balance?

So hypothetically, if Lexie could do the same thing for Dewie, not with a golem per se, but with a card which she was working on, if she could make it such that Dewie didn't need to bear the brunt of the backlash for his vision, would the Mountain then want to get rid of Dewie?

That was something to think about.

But also, back to the Keys. The Mountain wanted the same key that Yasycht also wanted to free himself from whatever realm he was trapped in.

Yeah, Lexie wasn't going to do that.

But hypothetically, if she were to do that…where would she even look?

The Key had to be somewhere really hidden, where neither Yasycht nor Neqal nor any of their associates could find it.

That intrigued Lexie.

She had so many follow-up questions, and the Temple of the Guardian of the Keys would be a great place to start her search.

Lexie kept walking, and she heard the echoing sound of gongs being struck.

One.

Step, Step.

Two.

Step, Step.

Three.

The sound was anticipatory, almost like a ritual in preparation for something. Lexie tried to summon her cards, but it didn’t work.

She paused.

Strange. She was cut off from her system inventory, where she'd stored all her cards except for her soul card.

Interesting.

This had never happened to her before. Not even in the heart of the dungeon had she been cut off from her system inventory. Not even when she'd been inside the soul of an Eldritch during a soul touch.

Several features of the system hadn't worked anymore, but her card storage had always been there, so much so that she'd even taken it for granted and forgotten it was technically part of the system.

But here, it was like that was entirely gone. Which spooked Lexie as much as it fascinated her.

She kept walking, the gongs getting louder. She couldn’t summon Pvilycht or any of her cards now that she was here, which should make her feel unsafe, but she didn’t feel like she was in danger.

On the contrary, there was something a little familiar about this place. She wanted to sit, close her eyes, and think about exactly what it was. But she kept walking. There was something on the other side, something she needed to discover. Something at the end of the darkness.

Then suddenly, she was out.

She blinked rapidly against the glaring light piercing through the large windows of the Governor's study.

She had somehow, without knowing it, walked out of the darkness and ended up back in the office, staring at the secret entrance in front of her.

She frowned.

What the heck just happened? Had she somehow walked in a complete circle without realizing it? No. She was standing right at the spot she entered from, so that must mea––

“What are you doing in here?”

Lexie spun around, realizing just then that she wasn’t alone. She’d been so focused on the dungeon entrance that she didn’t realize that Mayor Azure was standing right behind her, looking incredibly annoyed.

Well, furious was more the word for it, but he'd done a good job of tempering that fury into mere annoyance.

Lexie pointed at the secret entrance. “Did you see me come out of there? Or did I not even go in in the first place?” Lexie’s alternate theory was that she'd never actually walked into the space, and she’d simply been standing there in a trance-like state.

The Mayor wasn’t interested in answering her question.

He moved to press a button that caused the bookshelf to slide back in place, blocking out the secret entrance once more.

Lexie frowned. She wasn't done. She wanted to try going back in, this time with her cards in her hands. She was sure DECODE could help her figure out how that space operated and where the voices were coming from.

“Open it,” she told the Mayor. “I want to go back in.”

“Are you seriously trying to tell me what to do in my own house? After I just caught you snooping around? Do you know how much trouble I could get you into?”

“You won’t, though,” she told him, and his gaze sharpened.

Lexie's initial instinct was to rise to the challenge, but she pushed it down.

Come on. Let’s lead with diplomacy for a change.

She took a breath and said as sincerely as she could manage, “I’m sorry about snooping in your office. I was just curious."

“Yes," he said sarcastically. "That’s typically the case when someone repeatedly violates others’ boundaries.”

She accepted that with a nod of the head. “I’m also sorry that I broke into your office that one time, even though breaking in was what helped me save Dewie.”

The top of his lip curled in distaste, and Lexie cocked her head. Sometimes she was unsure of how the Mayor actually felt about Dewie. Lately, it felt like he cared about Dewie because he treated him well enough, even though they barely interacted. But Lexie couldn’t forget that in the past, Dewie had once told her that his father hated him for causing the death of his mother.

While she’d brushed it off at the time and put it down to childhood angst, she now knew that Dewie was very good at seeing things as they actually were. As socially awkward as he was, he typically read situations very well, and hence, there was something to be said for the fact that the Mayor might not care as much about Dewie’s life being saved as he cared about Lexie overstepping her bounds.

In which case, Lexie had to figure out the one thing the mayor did care about. His dead wife, probably.

He definitely wouldn't want her to bring it up, though. Hmm.

How about now that she’d apologized, she just tried asking him nicely to see what would happen?

“Can you please tell me what that place is?” she asked. “Who is Janus, and why is his temple in your office? Pretty please?"

He cocked an eyebrow, then shook his head. "Just get out, before I have security remove you."

Lexie sighed.

Well, that didn't work. Maybe she was approaching this too much as a human. She decided to take a page out of Yasycht’s book. “If you tell me the truth, I’ll owe you a favor.”

“A favor?”

“Yeah. I don’t know if you noticed, but I’m really popular now. Like super popular. And super powerful as well." She cocked her head. “You’re up for reelection soon, aren’t you?”

She saw the realization sink into his gaze, and Lexie held it.

Now, morally, she didn’t really want to endorse Dewie’s father or get involved in politics at all. But she decided the Governor hadn't done a terrible job for the past few years, at least from the little she knew, and she could sell her soul a little, just to fulfil her curiosity.

Her PR team might have a different opinion, though.

As he considered it, Lexie thought of an important caveat.

“I’m not going to kill any of your political rivals for you or anything,” she clarified. “Just to let you know.”

“Of course not." He looked offended. "I’ve never killed a political rival in my life.”

“Good. Glad we’re on the same page. But I can do plenty of other favors that will help you with your political aspirations.”

“And how can I be sure that you won’t go back on your word, or that you won’t try to use whatever information I give you to harm me?”

"I wouldn't harm you. You're Dewie's dad, and if I harmed you, he would be sad, and I don't want him to be sad."

He didn't appear convinced.

“I suppose you’ll just have to trust me,” she said. She certainly wasn’t signing any kind of soul contract with him, but she told him, “How about I give you something incriminating, as leverage.”

“Oh?”

“Yes. You’re the only one I’m telling this to, so if the information gets out, I’ll know you’re the one who did it.”

He crossed his arms over his chest, trying hard not to look spooked by the threat.

“I killed Vulcan.” Lexie was banking on him not being in the inner sanctum of the association, which already knew what had happened with Vulcan. So to him, this would be valuable news.

It worked. His eyes became the size of saucers, his face paling.

"I can let you record my confession if you want," Lexie said. "And if I don't keep my end of the deal, then you can take that right to the association, and they'll fix me with tilling bands."

"Will those actually work on you? After all, they didn't exactly work on your father, nor did they apparently work on the Alchemist."

Lexie shrugged. "Who knows? So do you want to record it?"

"No need," he said with a smirk. "This conversation is already being surveilled."

"Oh." Lexie just noticed the fixed PHORBs in the corners. “Yes. Now, can you tell me how a temple of Janus exists in your walls? I couldn’t access the system at all, using it, not even my inventory. It was like a space completely devoid of system access, and it's just here in your office."

The Governor didn't respond instantly. Instead, he walked closer to the window, staring out at the lush garden outside.

“The temple used to be an access point, where the worshippers of Janus would meet, to pray for smooth journeys and transitions. Usually at the end of every year, but also before important trips."

“Why is it here?”

He stared at the shelf. “Because for years, the Azure’s and a few other families served the Guardian Janus. We protected his temple, enacted his will. Some of us also gained special powers as a result."

“Oh.” Lexie nodded. “So you were one of the historic noble families that had deals with the Guardians.”

He raised his chin with a sort of defensive pride. "More or less."

“But your name is Azure.” That name didn’t sound like the family name of a historic noble family. Typically, they had names like Calmpotter or Firebringer. Or even Sparrowfoot. Not something as common-sounding as Azure.

Not all ancient noble families dealt with the Guardians. But typically, anyone who did have something to do with the Guardians was a noble family and had a name and a power to match.

But Governor Azure’s power was turning people’s vision blue or something like that. Xena even remarked on how lame it was, which was why he’d quickly pivoted from being an unmemorable hero into politics.

“We weren’t always the Azures,” he said. “We were once the Waystriders, and we were in charge of giving access to the temple, ensuring that everyone who was there was a true worshipper. We also worked to enact the will of Janus, working in proximity with the Oracles to ensure the good of the Empire. But someone in our lineage made a mistake. We were betrayed, and our family was tossed out and stripped of our post as a result." His jaw clenched. "We lost much of our authority then, and even our soul ability. This was before the invasion of the Fae made it worse."

“I see.” Interesting that he would call the Fae an invasion when some see that more as a rescue.

“The Waystrider name came with too much stigma,” he said. "Those who hated the Guardians saw us as traitors of humanity. The other Janus worshippers also scorned us and treated us as a mockery. We had no choice but to change the name and start over somewhere new." He coughed and cleared his throat. "I grew up ashamed of who I was, until one person taught me not to be anymore."

It took Lexie a second to realize. “Dewie’s mother.”

“Yes." An almost smile touched his lip. "She was an Oracle, and she could always sense who I was even before I said anything. With her, I learned the secrets my family never spoke about, about who we truly were, and how powerful we were meant to be."

"Hold on, if you were stripped of your access to the temple, then how come it's still here?"

He turned back. His eyes glittered. "That part I can't tell you."

However, Lexie could make a few intelligent guesses.

"How did the temple end up here? Is it actually here, or is it in a pocket dimension?" She raised an eyebrow. “Because if it is a pocket dimension, only one person could be responsible for that.”

He didn't answer, but he didn't need to.

It had to be the Alchemist.

Which meant that somehow, Dewie's father was working with the Alchemist.

"I think, Ms. Sparrowfoot," Governor Azure said. "That it's time for you to go."

The next few days were pretty busy for Lexie.

Apart from crafting cards, she went to check in on the people of Indigo, particularly Gunther, who expressed effusive praise when he saw her at the healing center. His home was destroyed and his livelihood gone, but he was extremely happy that Lexie had saved his wife and child.

"Thank you." He basically sobbed while speaking, holding her hands with his shaking ones. "Thank you."

"It's alright." Lexie felt awkward about receiving such intense gratitude, especially because she didn't feel like she'd done much to deserve it. Saving him and his family had been easy peasy.

But it also felt nice to know that everything had worked out for them. She didn't stick around long at the healing center, because the press got word that she was there and she was instantly swarmed by PHORBs, but she did later get Stanley and Doyle to set up a charity fund so she could donate money to Gunther and other people affected by the destruction.

She would have given it to him directly, but she knew he wouldn't take it, and this way was better. And though it wasn't really Stanley's job, he helped link her to the people who could do it and handled all the logistical aspects, also marketing it as an open fund so that Lexie's fans could donate if they wanted to.

Stella was right, in that the heroes seemed to have temporarily forgotten about Lexie, thanks to the incoming war, and so Lexie had freedom to pursue her individual interests.

She crafted. She plotted. She spoke to Elvira–whose number she'd gotten from Aiden–about what cards to choose to be part of the deck.

Elvira wasn't offended that Lexie hadn't contacted her in at least a few months, and Lexie wasn't offended that Elvira hadn't thought to check in on her. Now that they were back in contact, it was like old times, and they discussed everything with the ease of two kindred souls and former partners.

Elvira let Lexie know the types of questions she should ask herself as she created the deck, and also told her she needed to have an archetype for the ideal user.

'Everyone' wasn't a good starting point.

She needed to go out and do actual research, or have a team that did that, and they would collect data on different identities that different decks would serve.

Which was a lot of work.

But at least the work distracted Lexie from the fact that Xena still wasn't talking to her. Lexie had gone back the next morning to see her, only to discover that Xena had left Emma a note and gone on a trip with Lionel.

That felt a little petty, but Lexie was still giving her her space and hoping she would come around. She'd sent texts, apologizing for everything, but the texts went unread and unanswered.

Lexie also spent some time with Pvilycht and Tate, who had given her the list of Developers. The question now was finding out how to make them want to help her.

Eldritch Lexie would have thought about just threatening them, but she knew that wouldn't work in the long term. Neither would Pvilycht's vague suggestion of 'appealing to their personhood' nor Tate's suggestion of offering them lots of money.

She needed to really get them to invest in this, so she had to appeal to the things that a Developer was interested in. She had to prove that her theory was a viable alternative to the current system.

Which meant she had to wait for Aiden and his golems.

She'd tried to ask him about his golem earlier, but he'd cut her off, basically hinting to Lexie that the Fae were listening to their conversation. So she saved that and the conversation about the Alchemist for when he got back.

She anxiously waited for his return, trying to keep her thoughts busy so as not to think about it. She knew it would at least be a few more days.

Until one day, when she was eating cereal, something popped behind her and wrapped its hands over her eyes.

"Guess who?"

Lexie inhaled sharply. Overwhelming joy flooded her so much that she jumped out of her seat and launched herself at him, wrapping her arms tightly around his neck, even though it made her hang in the air.

"Dad!"

"Yes, honeybee," he laughed. "It's me. I'm home."
