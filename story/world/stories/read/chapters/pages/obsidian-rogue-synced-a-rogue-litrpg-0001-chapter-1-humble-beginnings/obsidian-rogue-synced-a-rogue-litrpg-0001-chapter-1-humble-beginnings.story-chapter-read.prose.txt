Syncing day.

Levi had worked very hard to get a shot at syncing. He'd had to do more than his fair share of shady dealings before Killian had agreed to sponsor his syncing without a contract. For him, it was worth it. The lack of a contract allowed him to retain his freedom, and his years of dealings with Killian had paid off the cost for today.

At twenty years of age, the slightly taller-than-average young man was considered a bit old for syncing, but he barely cared, paying little attention to the stares and glares he received as he stood in line at the Diamond Hall.

Dressed in his ragged clothes, he was an eyesore to the people occupying the hall. Most of them turned their noses at him, some whispered behind his back, but all things considered, he couldn't care less, especially today.

Plus, he'd expected it; a part of him encouraged it. Being constantly looked down upon had only made him tougher and stronger. Strong enough to get a sync ticket without signing what was basically a glorified slave contract—a gamble on his part, but one that had paid off in time.

"Name and ticket," A priest clad in pristine white robes said in a bored tone as Levi's turn in the line finally came.

"Levi Tate," he said as he handed over his ticket.

As the priest scanned the ticket, Levi glanced at the wooden doors behind the man that led down into the sync tub, then back to the robed man. His eyes watched as the priest's face twitched slightly in disapproval. The man was fighting the urge to turn his nose up at the street urchin that was before him. Levi smirked defiantly. The priest could only judge him, nothing more.

"Sponsored by Killian?" The priest asked

"Correct," Levi said, eager to move the conversation along.

"Hmm, one of Killian's," The priest murmured, a tight smile on his face. "It's fine, you can take the door behind me, someone will attend to you."

Nodding in response, Levi made his way to the backroom, a smile threatening to rip from his face as he moved past the priest. His entire being shook from excitement, and how could it not? He'd been waiting so long for this. He'd spied, pickpocketed, and done so many odd things just so Killian would pay for his sync ticket, contract-free.

So it was only natural that as he opened the door to the backroom and was greeted by the sight of multiple priests and potential synced milling about, he smiled. Already a young adult, he couldn't help the giddiness that threatened to overtake him. After all, he'd seen most of the other street kids like him, sync one after another. Leaving him behind to do little jobs while they moved onto bigger things, now it'd be his turn.

"Good morning," a voice coughed politely beside him. "Are you here to sync?"

Levi looked around to see who'd spoken, and the face of a young priest around his age came into focus. A priest who he'd heard walk up to him, but paid no attention to, as he'd been distracted by his own thoughts. He stared at the face of the priest, who was like a brother to him, one of the few other kids who hadn't picked on him when they were much younger.

"No, Conor, I'm here to get a steak," Levi laughed and shook his head.

"Well, right this way, sir," Conor said, pointing to the door Levi had just come through.

"Oh, sod off, Conor," Levi laughed.

Conor smiled, then chuckled, pulling his hand from his white robes and offering it to Levi, who eagerly clasped it, both young men genuinely happy to see each other.

"Alright, enough of the joking around." The priest said with a grin."It's time to get you into a tub before my supervisor chews me out for lazing about."

Levi nodded in agreement. After all, he himself was eager to sync. He couldn't wait to get his class and then dip out of this shitty town. Luckily for him, he was on the right track to achieving his goals, closer than ever before, at least that was what it felt like as he wordlessly followed behind Conor. The priest casually exchanged words with a few other priests on duty as he led him to an empty tub.

For the love of Cerpital, this place is crowded.

The thought hadn't come unbidden; if anything, it was appropriate for the situation. The backroom he'd been ushered into qualified to be a hall—it was certainly bigger than the town hall, and for good reason too. For one, the entire place was filled with tubs; he’d counted at least thirty tubs, with multiple people—priests and potential synced—swarming the hall. Not really surprising the amount of traffic since Sync day was only held once every two months in their little town."

"We're here," Conor said, stopping in front of an ordinary tub filled with water. "You ready?"

"Born ready," he replied. "What do I have to do?"

"Simple, strip to your underwear and get in. I'll hold you down underwater for ten seconds while chanti—"

"Are you trying to drown me?"

"No, nothing like that," Conor chuckled. "Once you're underwater, I will begin to chant. Rest assured, no time will pass for you once you're offered the choice. I'll pull you back up once you're done."

"How will you know when I'm done?"

"Trust me, I will. Now undress and hop in. I don't have all day."

Levi rolled his eyes at the priest before ultimately doing as he was told. He pushed away the feeling of fear as he submerged his body into the water in the tub, and a chill ran through his spine as he settled in. His head poked out of the tub, and he smiled tightly at Conor.

"Anytime now," The priest said exasperatedly.

Levi chuckled nervously as he stared down at the water; it seemed to have a little bit of glow to it, but he couldn't be too sure. Maybe he was just being paranoid, the same paranoia that screamed out at him that he was making himself vulnerable by agreeing to go underwater. Conor could try to drown him, but he highly doubted that it would come to that, and by the look of things, Conor wouldn't be able to match him in a contest of strength.

Convinced in his ability to survive an attempt on his life, Levi nodded to the priest, sucked in a lungful of air, and then fully submerged himself, closing his eyes at the sound of Conor's chanting.

Hopefully, I don't die accidentally.

The morbid thought wasn't unwarranted, as there were a handful of cases of people actually drowning when syncing. While it was rare for such a thing to happen, it wasn't one that was unheard of. Sure, it wasn't fair to not trust Conor, but Levi had learned not to trust anyone, and it was something that had borne fruit for him—repeatedly. With nothing else to do, Levi decided to keep his mind preoccupied by timing himself. Conor had told him ten seconds, so he'd give the cheeky priest his ten before trying to leave.

Here goes nothing. Ten, Nine, Eight, Seven, Six, Five, Four, Three, Two, One. Whelps, I can sh—

At the end of the day, the answer to Cerpital's question was a no-brainer.

In the absence of any further notification, Levi opened his eyes and emerged from the tub, the water sliding off his frame easily, too easily if you asked him, and that was when Levi realized that he wasn't even wet, a hand to his underpants and the other to his hair confirmed his suspicion, neither was wet. Shrugging off the weird sensation, he completely stepped out of the tub to the sight of a grinning Conor.

"Congratulations, Levi, you're now one of us. You're now synced," Conor said, a genuine smile on his face. "Well, what class did you pick? You know what, don't tell me."

Levi wanted to tell him anyway, but paused at the intense expression on Conor's face as the priest stared at him, disbelief crossing his friend's features at whatever he'd seen.

"Rogue? I thought you would've gone for mage," Conor asked.

"Come on, Conor, you know the class doesn't suit me," Levi said. "I prefer freedom and a bit of anonymity."

"Whatever you say, man." Conor scratched his chin, then pointed at some items beside the tub. "Congratulations on syncing! Those are your stuff. They appeared when you were in the tub."

Ahh, so Cerpital did receive my last answer.

Levi nodded as he gathered up the items, confirming they were what he was looking for before he slipped into his new cloak, put on his new boots, and put his daggers within his new cloak, discarding his old clothes as he prepared to head out. The pair walked silently to the exit—a different door from the one he'd walked through initially. The priest glanced at him skeptically, and even though Levi suspected it was because of his class choice, he refused to revisit the matter.

"It was nice seeing you, Levi."

"Same, Conor."

The two young men clasped hands and called it a day. Conor returned to his duties as a priest, and Levi took to the streets as he made his way back home. The feeling of becoming a newly minted rogue was exhilarating, and it showed as Levi now had a spring to his step that hadn't been there when he'd arrived at the hall. Whistling a half-forgotten tune, he walked through the streets of Gantus, the small town that had molded him. He smiled—not at any of the tens of people milling about on the dusty cobblestones, no; he smiled because he'd soon be out of this little town; his life was far from here.

First order of business as a newly synced, I need to see Silas and Bree.

The thought of the pair caused his smile to falter, but Levi brushed off the uncertainty. It was a poorly kept secret that Levi intended to leave after syncing, maybe not immediately, but it'd be sooner rather than later. For that reason, he hesitated to decide whether he wanted to break the news to them immediately. Shrugging his shoulders at the awkward encounter that lay ahead, he pushed the thoughts to the back of his mind, opting to focus on the road ahead of him.

The spring in his steps seemed to vanish into thin air as Levi was now close to the slums of Gantus. The contrast between the living quarters for the poor and those of the rich in Gantus was clear to all. The Diamond Hall was placed in the area reserved for the rich and prospering, so Levi had a fresh enough mental image to compare to the filth that was the slum that he'd just walked into. The newly minted rogue took the twists and turns that led him back to the run-down apartment that he called home.

The smell that assaulted him was as familiar as it was repugnant. Litters of rubbish covered the floor, beggars hugged the sidewalks, miscreants just like him lurked behind buildings just waiting for who'd provide their next meal, but all through the side looks he received, Levi walked through unfazed as he headed for his apartment just around the corner.

"Alright, this is it," Levi breathed as he came to his door. The cracked walls and uneven door caused him to sigh as he walked in.

"Levi," Bree cheered as he walked in the door.

His adopted sister rushed to hug him, with a smile on her face. Silas, who was on the other side of the room, flashed a toothy grin and a thumbs-up. Levi patted Brees's hair as he soaked in the moment. It seemed like walking into the house in a beginner rogue's clothing was all the announcement he needed to make. Plus, it was obvious from the looks of things that the pair had already known beforehand, judging by the cake lying on the stool.

With a childish smile, Bree detached from him, wrapped her little hands around his wrist, and led him to the cake. It didn't have any write-ups on it, and it had parts of it missing —with the prime culprit being Bree, who had icing smeared around her lips —but to Levi, the cake was perfect.

"Congratulations, Levi!" Silas and Bree said in unison.

"Thank you… Both of you. I really appreciate this." Levi said.

"Blahh blahhh, time to dig in !" Bree yelled as she fetched a handful of cake, stuffing it into her mouth, with Silas joining in.

Who needs cutlery when you have siblings like these?

Levi grinned as he dug in as well, the trio stuffing their mouths with the glorious-tasting cake. The silence that enveloped the room was broken only by the occasional laughter of the trio. Each one laughing at the other because of how their faces looked smeared with cake and icing. Throughout the laughter, he and Silas had wordlessly agreed to leave the last piece of cake to Bree.

Their ten-year-old sister reached for the last piece of what was left of the cake before suddenly pausing and nudging Levi.

"The last one's for you, big brother. You deserve it.” Bree said. “Thanks for all you do."

"Don't worry about it, kiddo," Levi said before devouring the last piece.

"So what now?" Silas asked after he'd finished with the cake.

"I have to go see Killian,"

"Why?" Silas asked.

"Yeah, why?" Bree pouted, "I thought you hated him."

"I do," Levi chuckled. "But he's been giving me jobs for years, and he's the only one who let me sync without signing a service contract. "

Even if he worked me like a beast.

To be fair to the cunning taskmaster, there were very few people who'd be willing to sponsor a sync ticket for a slum rat even if said slum rat offered to work in advance for it, and there were even fewer people like that in a town like Gantus. That was why Levi worked day and night for Killian, liberating unsuspecting visitors and residents of their little town of their valued possessions.

Besides, he wasn't just a random street rat; he was smart, smart enough to have never been caught. A trait that was worth a lot to someone like Killian.

"That checks out," Silas said. “So when are you leaving ?”

"Now," he answered. "Hopefully, he'll have a job available. I'm almost about to meet my coin target before I…"

His voice trailed off at the last part mainly because of the way their faces soured at his words. The topic of him leaving was one that was rarely discussed, but one that they'd had in the past. It was a decision that was logical for Levi; he'd sworn to himself that he'd keep them away from the streets and pay for their syncing ticket when the time came. With Silas turning fifteen next year, time was running out for Levi to find good jobs, and his only options required him to leave the small town and get to the city where he could find a better task master or hit the dungeons.

"It's fine," Silas said with a half grin. "We're with you all the way."

"Agreed," Bree said.

"Alright, I'll be out late," Levi said. " Don't wait up"

"Sure thing, just make sure to keep safe," Bree said.

With a mock salute towards his adopted siblings, Levi left through the door. He needed to see Killian even if to say his thanks. To an outsider, it might have been a one-sided transaction, but he'd gotten something very valuable in exchange. Freedom.

And power too.

Levi shook his head as he exited his apartment. Killian's was his destination. The crooked taskmaster's lair was tucked in between the slums and the area reserved for the prosperous people in Gantus. The perfect area for someone who did business with both sets of people.

"Smart crook." He whispered. The rogue was already on the move, his feet leading him through the numerous twists and turns needed until he was right where he needed to be, Killian's office.

The old taskmaster had only one attendant, Claire. She was a bit older than him, ruder, and a little bit condescending, but Levi paid her no heed, especially since he was in it for the money— and Killian did pay well, if you knew how to negotiate.

With a sigh, he walked through the front door. Claire raised her head from the desk to stare at him. Her eyes shone with disapproval, but her lips sported a thinly veiled smile. Levi knew he wouldn't have to wait long for whatever insult or jab Claire had for him today.

"He's busy," She said dismissively, her head dropping back to whatever was on her desk before shooting back up. "Huh… you've synced, eh?"

"Hmm, it seems like they’re just allowing anyone to sync these days,” She teased.” Congratulations on syncing, but you still can’t see him. He's having a meeting with a valued asset, and he's asked not to be disturbed."

The wooden door sported multiple runes, mostly for privacy and protection, according to what Killian had said, but one could never be too sure with the old crook.

He hadn't even registered his presence, even though the rogue had been in plain sight. More alarming was the fact that Levi had difficulty keeping his eyes on the rogue.
