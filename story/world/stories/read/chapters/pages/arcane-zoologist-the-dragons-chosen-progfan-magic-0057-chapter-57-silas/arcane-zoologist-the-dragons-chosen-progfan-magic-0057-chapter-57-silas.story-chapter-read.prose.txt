For a tense moment, it seemed like Callen and Silas would fight in earnest. Callie felt terrified. She didn't want her brother to get hurt. All she wanted was to return home.

All of sudden, Silas smiled, loosening his grip on the blade. "Callen, my young friend!"

"I'm no friend of yours, you snake!" Callen snarled, his knuckles turning white from how tightly he was clutching the sword.

"There, there. No need to solve everything with violence. We can talk this out like adults, can't we?" Silas smiled.

"You tried to kidnap my little sister!" Callen growled, waving the sword.

Silas didn't appear bothered. He actually took a step closer. "I think it's pretty safe to say that your little sister is a little special is she not? Do you really think this place is right for her?"

"This is our home!" Callen said firmly.

"Yes, yes, I understand. This is all you two know. This is your whole world, but does it have to be that way. Callie has an unusual gift. A gift that could change this very world, and you just want to keep hiding her in that village? Never let her utilize her potential?"

"Callie's place is with our family," Callie said firmly, and Callie nodded.

"I understand. Of course children would cling to their parents. It is natural. I was that way too, long, long ago. Until I understood that my parents were holding me back. I already told you that they didn't want me to become a merchant."

"You're not just a merchant are you though? You're a hunter!" Callen said.

"No, I'm no hunter. I can't do what your mother does. I merely take advantage of opportunities when they present themselves to me. And sometimes, I create those opportunities. Tell me, Callen and Callie, do you really want to spend your lives in this dirt hole? Don't you want to see the wider world? You don't have to go alone, you know. You two siblings could remain together."

"What nonsense are you spouting? You're trying to sell Callie to dragonbloods aren't you?"

"Sell her? Heavens no! I'm trying to give her a better opportunity at life. She would never amount to anything here. But if she is given the chance to learn proper magic, just imagine what she will be able to do," Silas said, taking another step forward.

Callen lunged and swiped in the air with his sword, giving a clear warning to stay back. "You always had a way with words didn't you? I may be young, but I'm not a fool. I will always protect my family, especially Callie. I will not give her to you, nor will I go with you. Just leave!"

Silas sighed dramatically, throwing his dagger in the air and catching it again. "I was really hoping that we could come to an agreement. I suppose I'm going to have to do this the hard way. Last chance, Callen. Become my apprentice. Travel with me. You don't need your family to become something more."

"No," Callen snarled.

At that moment, Silas threw his dagger at Callen. Callen raised his arm, and the dagger pierced it. Had he not done that, it would have struck his chest.

"Brother!" Callie screamed.

He grunted, but yanked the knife out of his, blood spilling onto the snow. Callie felt herself grow queasy. Callen staggered towards Silas, readying to strike with his sword. But his hands trembled.

Silas noticed that and laughed. "You've never even harmed a human before. You can't do anything against me, can you?"

The man pulled two more daggers out of seemingly nowhere. Was he going to throw them at Callen again? No, no, no.

"STOP!" Callie screamed. She could feel her magic going completely haywire. Despite having almost no energy left, she pushed as hard as she could. She had to protect her brother.

Not with the destructive fire magic. No, she needed something that would leave Silas unable to attack. Callie remembered the vines that she can grow around glauxis's and Callen's feet when they were about to attack each other. She tried hard to make the vines grow around Silas.

But they had to be strong. These couldn't be as small as before. Callie pushed her magic as far as she could to trap Silas. It hurt. It felt like she was ripping a part of herself out. But she had to protect her brother.

Just as Silas raised his hand to throw, thick vines erupted from ground around him and tightly wrapped around him. His daggers fell into the snow, and he was rendered completely immobile.

"Callie!" Callen ran up to her. She could feel her blood steadily dripping down her nose. But it didn't matter. What mattered was that Callen was safe.

Utterly exhausted, Callie allowed the darkness to engulf her.

Callen hurriedly tossed his sword aside and grabbed Callie before she could hit the ground. Her nose was bleeding, a clear sign that she had pushed herself past her limit.

"This is amazing! Even though she was exhausted, she still managed to grow these vines. This child's potential is astounding. Do you know what she'll be able to do as an adult? Do you know how much dragonbloods will pay for her? You would never have to worry about going hungry again for the rest of your life? Heck, you could bring your whole village out of poverty!"

"Shut up!" Callen snarled, gently picking up his sister with his uninjured arm. His other arms hurt like hell and were still bleeding. He had to hurry back home.

"Think about it, Callen! Don't be a fool! Your sister is worth her weight in gold. No, even more!" Silas struggled, trying to get free of the vines, but they were just too tough.

Callen lowered Callie to the ground for a moment, and picked up his sword. This man was completely obsessed with Callie. Or rather he was obsessed with money. Letting him go would be too dangerous. He would pursue Callie. He would call for dragonbloods.

Silas laughed, as Callen approached. "What? Are you going to kill me? You don't have the guts!"

Callen raised his sword. He could do it. He could.

All of a sudden, smoke exploded around them. Silas had thrown a smoke bomb.

Callen's first instinct was to get back to Callie. He whistled, calling for Gale, and retreated out of the smoke cloud. Or at least he tried, but it was expanding rapidly and making Callen's eyes water.

I have to get Callie out of here. We will be safe back in the village.

Callen abandoned the sword, grabbing Callie. Gale came galloping towards them. It was only thanks to his hooves that he could tell where the horse was in the smoke cloud. He grabbed on the reins and put Callie on his back. Then he jumped up himself and commanded the horse to run.

Just a moment later, he heard something whizz in the air right behind them. Another dagger. Silas had somehow gotten free of the vines. He must have cut himself free.

"HYAH!" Callen spurred Gale, and the horse went running through the snow.

Silas didn't pursue. Callen knew that he didn't have to. He had witnessed Callie's magic. He knew what she was capable of. He would be back, but he wouldn't be alone. He would bring the dragonbloods with him next time he came.

If only I didn't hesitate, I could have killed him, and we would be safe…

But the idea of killing a human, even if they were as horrible as Silas, made Callen sick to his stomach. He couldn't even kill an animal, much less, a person.

But his weak resolve meant that his whole family would suffer. Callen hated himself for that weakness.

When Callie woke up, she found herself back in her bed. Had that whole thing with Silas been a dream? Was it because she knew that he had been the one behind all those bad incidents with arcane beasts?

But as Callie tried to sit up, she realized that she couldn't. Her limbs felt heavy as if made of metal, and she had no strength whatsoever. As if she had used up all of her energy.

So that encounter… the kidnapping… Callen getting stabbed? Was it all real?

"CALLEN!" Callie called out.

A moment later, Mom rushed into Callie's room. "Callie, you're awake?"

"Where's Callen?" Callie asked.

"He's at granny Ethel's getting patched up. It was a deep cut, so he'll need stitches, but he'll be fine," Mom said, patting Callie's hair. Her hand was slightly trembling. "How are you feeling?"

Callie relaxed a little. "Very tired. Wh-What happened to Silas?"

Mom's lips pursed into a thin line. "He got away."

Callie wasn't sure what that meant, but the way Mom said made it sound like it was the worst thing that could have happened.

"But everyone is safe. That's all that matters right?" Callie said hopefully, although she already knew that things weren't fine. Those things would never be the same as before.

Mom tried to force a smile, opened her mouth to say something then closed it, her gaze dropping down. "I'm sorry, Callie, but we're going to have to leave the village. Silas will undoubtedly alert the dragonbloods about your abilities. They'll be here as soon as they are able to. Dad went to explain everything to the chief. We'll leave tomorrow morning."

"What?" Callie gasped. They were… leaving their home. But what about other villagers? What about Wiggles and Snowglow? The mireling would wake up in spring and find that Callie was gone.

But the heaviest was the knowledge that it was Callie's fault that their family had to leave.
