Luna Deane was on a mission, and it was going rather… poorly. She leapt from yet another first-storey rooftop, landing with not so much as a scuff on the cobblestone. Silent and precise, just as she was trained. But once again, the thing she was hunting wasn’t here either.

She scanned the street ahead. Only a handful of houses remained. Seven, to be exact. Seven doors, err… windows, between her and the bitter truth she dreaded. What in the Twin Moons’ name would she tell the captain if she came back empty-handed again? She was supposed to be decoding that damned book long before it had apparently decided to up and flee from her own bag.

That diary. That rotting, stubborn, paper-turned-artefact bastard. A black, ancient cover that looked like it would crumble into dust if someone sneezed on it, yet somehow resilient enough to run away. Run. In that state. The thing shouldn’t have been able to twitch, let alone wander off. Even Luna had to turn its pages like she was handling poisoned glass shards.

ARGHH!

She’d already taken the risk of a dream divination and gotten a blurry, lucky glimpse of this very street. That was the only reason she was here, pounding the shadows before the captain realised she’d bungled yet another “simple” task. She needed to retrieve that diary before it caused actual chaos. Before someone else realised what it could do. Before the captain realised she’d lost it in the first place.

Luna exhaled a sharp, bitter sigh. Why did fate always insist on being a cruel bastard? She shook her fist at the twin moons hanging above, only to drop it again with a weary groan. She touched her eyes once in a short ritual and muttered a prayer to the Twin Moon Goddesses.

“Crimson Mother, Keeper of the Open Gates, whose sight pierces all shadow,” she breathed, letting her voice hum with mana. She pulled a ceremonial dagger from her belt, channelling more mana into it until the air shimmered, forming a thin veil that cut her off from the world, rendering her invisible. Verdant Mother, Keeper of the Closed Gates, Mistress of the Unspoken Secrets.” Her tone grew heavier, resonating in her throat with each invocation. “Hear your blessed child. Lend your gaze to my seeking; bless this attempt to pry truth from fate.”

With that, she let the dagger slip from her hand. It didn’t fall outright, hovered an inch above the ground, suspended in the invisible pull of her working. She whispered into the stillness:

“Location of the Diary of Lik’Ara.”

The blade floated for another heartbeat… then dropped with the gentlest of thumps.

Luna’s stomach sank. She almost wanted to cry again.

It didn’t work.

That cursed thing had anti-divination wards as well. She’d already used the Moon Goddesses’ grace once today to track it, using the prayer empowered divination and even that had only worked enough to give her a fuzzy dream-vision. The damned artefact had interfered mid-divination, like it had deliberately spat in her eye.

Normally, the diary was harmless. Mostly dormant. That was why it was stored away and officially classified by the Moon Church as a Delta-class artefact, bottom rung, little real danger. Sure, it left behind traces of its own mana, which was enough to qualify it as an artefact at all, but otherwise it just sat there.

Until now.

Until it had up and vanished on its own.

FUCK. FUCK. FUCK. WHY?!

Her frustration was entirely earned. The artefact that was supposed to be inert had, without warning, decided to start acting up, while it was in her possession.

Should she just call the captain and tell him what had happened?

The thought crossed her mind, but she immediately imagined the result: a loss of credit points, a stern lecture, and maybe a few weeks of the entire team making jokes about her. No. Not yet. She braced herself. She’d investigate on her own first, and only when she was certain it was truly beyond her would she bring the captain into it.

And she did have a lead. The diary was somewhere in this neighbourhood, her dream divination had at least confirmed that much. Since it was blocking her attempts at divination, she’d just have to hunt it down the good old-fashioned way.

Yes! I can do this!

Luna nodded to herself and moved on to the next house, her eyes flicking over the building’s edges and seams. She worked a combination of ritual magic and small detection charms, searching for traces of mana leakage, anything that might indicate something supernatural lurking inside.

Mundane locks were laughable to a Moon Aspect cultivator. The path’s rituals had answers for just about any such obstacle. She glanced at the window, muttered under her breath, and waved her hand. A charm shimmered briefly in the air before the latch clicked open from the inside.

Charms or Hexes, in the strictest sense, were simply low-level rituals. They behaved like spells but had an important distinction, spells required a structured weave of mana, along with the caster’s precise knowledge of that structure. Charms, however, were more instinctive; they didn’t require full spellweaving to execute. And ever since she had taken her first steps into the [Shaman] aspect, charms had been her saving grace.

She didn’t know many… yet. The few she had mastered were already invaluable, and she was certain that as she advanced along her aspect, she’d unlock stranger, rarer, and more potent ones.

The captain, for example, had some really mind-bending charms. Granted he was at Tier 3, and rumours said he had already fully refined all his Tier 3 organs, meaning Tier 4 was within reach. Luna had never even seen a Tier 4 cultivator before, much less met one. The captain was already frighteningly capable at Tier 3, so she couldn’t imagine what he’d be like after ascending. She was almost looking forward to the day he made the official announcement to the entire team of Twin Moon Knights, The Order of Moon Church that operated within Crieffton.

Almost...

She shook her head, forcing herself back to the present. The hunt mattered now. Before long, she found herself in the very last section of the block.

Please don’t tell me this thing has anti-detection magic on top of everything else. If it does, I am so screwed.

She crossed her fingers for luck and scaled the wall of the next house. Crouching just below the first-floor window, she worked her charm again. The latch gave a soft click.

A grin tugged at her lips. She pulled a small mirror from her pocket and angled it just enough to peek inside without exposing herself.

Empty room. No occupants. Good.

She climbed onto the sill and slipped inside, but nearly shrieked when her foot slid out from under her. She teetered forward, skull on a direct collision course with the bedpost. At the last second she twisted, caught the window frame, and swallowed the yelp that had built in her throat.

Heart hammering against her ribs, she looked down at the treacherous, oily sheen glistening on the floor right where she’d landed. Why in the name of the Twin Mothers was there a puddle of oil directly inside a windowsill? How utterly irresponsible were the owners of this place? This was nothing short of attempted murder!

…At least, it was for innocent, unsuspecting thieves.

Luna took a slow, steady breath, then cast another charm, one designed to detect the presence of anything supernatural. The moment it activated, her senses were slammed with an overwhelming surge of mana radiating from everywhere around her.

Holy Mothers!

Her eyes went wide, and she froze mid-step.

That was… wrong. She’d been running this charm practically every other minute while scouting the street, and nothing had registered from outside. With mana this dense, there should’ve been leakage bleeding into the street.

Which meant one of two things.

Option one: the entire building was under a secrecy enchantment. An architectural-level veil designed to mask the area from outside detection and reduce supernatural interference.

Option two: someone inside had cast a secrecy ward manually, feeding it with their own mana.

Her gaze flicked to the walls around her. Could it be coincidence? Of all the last three houses her earlier dream divination had shown her, this one ended up radiating mana like a beacon wrapped in shadow.

She considered it for a long second, then made her choice, she jumped back out the window. If there was one thing she’d learned, it was to clear all other possibilities before barging into a magical hornet’s nest.

She checked the remaining houses. Just as she’d expected, no supernatural traces, no mana spikes.

That left exactly one building. The singular house that might be housing a cultivator.

Luna swallowed hard, then fished a pendant from her pocket, a silver chain with an opal set in its center. She wasn’t walking in blind. She’d do a quick danger-read before stepping back inside.

“This house houses danger,” she murmured, beginning the steps of the simple ritual.

If the pendant spun clockwise, it meant yes. Reverse meant no.

It trembled, started to turn clockwise— her stomach tensed — then abruptly reversed direction, spinning counterclockwise with steady speed.

Luna let out a slow breath of relief. The speed of the spin usually reflected the intensity of the answer. If it had whipped clockwise like this, she’d have been halfway down the street already. As it stood, the reading meant the inside wasn’t dangerous at all.

Which meant all that mana might be from the diary itself.

She didn’t know its full capabilities. For all she knew, the artefact could project secrecy wards on its own. And if that was the case, it had been hiding from her this whole time.

Oh, just you wait. You’re getting sealed for life, you paper-coated little bastard.

Still, she wanted to be absolutely sure. She reset the pendant, ran the ritual again, and spoke clearly:

“The Diary of Lik’Ara is inside this house.”

The opal shivered in place, then went still. The divination attempt was clearly blocked again. But that didn’t bother her. The fact it was blocking a reading on a random house practically confirmed it.

Luna grinned and climbed back through the window, this time stepping over the slick patch of oil on the smooth marble floor. Once inside, she triggered her detection magic again.

The place was drenched in mana, making it nearly impossible to get a clean read. But she could feel a gradient in the flow, like currents in water.

She followed the pull, step by step, toward where the energy felt thickest.

The room around her was mostly dark, but her vision didn’t care. She could see perfectly in the absence of light, a base trait of her current tier. Soon, at Tier 2, it would evolve into something far more profound. A thought that was a constant, motivating itch in the back of her mind. She was so close, lacking only a few more credit points. The fact that her Tier 1 organs weren't fully refined yet was a minor detail; she would get there. Just knowing the next advancement was within her grasp was a potent morale boost in itself.

After a few more casts of her detection magic, she felt the mana intensity steadily increasing as she made her way toward the hallway.

Hmm.

Luna moved with deliberate care, re-wrapping herself in a veil of stealth. The last charm had almost worn out, and she wasn’t about to get sloppy now. The layer once again masked her footsteps, muffled the faint rasp of fabric against her skin, and even suppressed her scent.

The hallway door was slightly ajar, a faint wash of yellow gaslight bleeding into the darkness through a narrow gap. She eased forward and widened the opening just enough to peek through.

Mistake.

A heavy, round object dropped directly onto her head the instant she moved it. Even braced for trouble, she staggered a step and letting out an involuntary yelp—

—and her boot slid across something slick. Oil. Again.

Her balance went out from under her and she lurched toward the stairs. She tried to recover, turning the stumble into a controlled hop, only to feel sudden resistance around her ankle.

Her eyes flicked down.

A taut rope.

In that dizzying moment, her eyes locked onto the other end of the rope. A small, white, fluffy fox held the taut line firmly in its mouth, its clever crimson eyes watching her tumble with unmistakable, glittering amusement.

What the actual fuck?

That was all she had time to think before momentum took over. She pitched backward, her body cartwheeling uncontrollably down the hard stairs in a series of painful, jarring impacts that knocked the wind from her lungs and any semblance of dignity from her mission.

Julia paused mid-cultivation, ears twitching at the faint sound of movement upstairs.

Hmm. Clover, maybe? If she needed something, she’d come down. Or perhaps she’d missed her nightly cuddles and was sulking, that would explain a grumpy Clover voice.

Julia sighed and turned her attention back to the golden, pulsing heart before her. Its rhythmic glow was marred by a creeping wash of azure, almost halfway across the surface, around forty-five percent complete.

Her lips curved into a grin. Faster than Agnes had predicted. At this rate, she could finish in just two days.

A flicker of pride warmed her. She had been pushing herself harder than usual, but once she’d started, the supposedly dull and repetitive process had become… exhilarating. The silent contest of wills between her and the stubborn, beating heart felt like a battle all its own. For all its stillness, it had begun to grow on her.

Well, just a little more. The clock on the wall, wooden face lit by the steady glow of a gas lamp, showed it was nearly one in the morning. She could push to fifty percent before resting. Two or three hours of sleep would be enough. Not optimal, but far from her worst nights. A potion could handle the rest. For now, cultivation took priority.

She closed her eyes, ready to dive back in—

A scream ripped through the house.

Julia’s eyes flew open.

That wasn’t Clover’s voice.

She was on her feet instantly, snatching a mana potion from the table and downing it in a single gulp before sprinting toward the sound.

The noise grew louder, crashing, thudding, the unmistakable clamor of something meaty tumbling down the stairs.

Julia channeled mana into the opal pendant resting against her chest, activating the enchantment bound within. A spell circle flared to life above her head, and from it surged a massive tendril of shadowed mana, coiling in the air like a summoned predator.

She rounded the corner…

…and froze at the sight before her.

A hooded figure lay crumpled at the base of the stairs, groaning and cursing under their breath. They lifted their head, locking eyes with Julia.

“Oh shit!” The voice was female.

The mana tendril struck in the same instant. It lashed out and violently constricted around the intruder’s torso, pinning her arms to her sides and lifting her a few inches off the floor.
