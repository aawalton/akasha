Chapter 29: The Song of Blood

Amongst the two corpses of my former allies, I sat down to meditate on what I had become. The stone floor was cold beneath me, made colder by the blood that had pooled around Zoey's bisected form. Her sap had darkened as it dried, turning from bright green to something the color of old bruises. Vathwin's mechanical fluids had mixed with his blood to create patterns on the rock that looked almost artistic, if you could ignore what they represented.

Pain was a constant companion in life, that was clear now. But many aspects the Nyxen praised, and meditated upon, had more of a presence than I first thought. They weren't just abstract concepts to be studied in books or recited in training halls. They were living forces that shaped every moment, every choice, every breath.

Shadow was a companion of the light. Most of us know that intellectually. We don't think about it too much, but we know it. That meant more than just shining a flashlight on your hands and creating shadow puppets on the wall for your children on a lazy Sunday afternoon. The memory of doing exactly that with Jackson struck me like a physical blow, but I pushed it aside. That man was dead. Those moments belonged to someone else.

Shadow was in every choice you made, every decision that revealed who you really were beneath the masks we all wore. When you smile at a coworker despite the dark thoughts crawling through your mind, the cloying exhaustion that made you want to just go home and not deal with the world. The fleeting consideration to punch that same coworker right in the face when they made some stupid comment about your work. The momentary fantasy of what it would feel like to watch them crumple.

Shadow thoughts like that don't always dog the heels of every good impulse, but they are there. The doubt that worms its way into your mind when you're trying to do the right thing. The whisper that asks why you should bother being good when the universe clearly doesn't reward virtue. The voice that gets a little louder each time life beats you down.

Even truth requires lies, for without them, how would we know its ring? How could we recognize honesty if we had never been deceived? The lies we tell ourselves are just as important as the truths we discover, because they give weight to both.

I had been walking the path of truth for a while now, even when that truth was ugly. Justifications ran through my mind as sweet, filtered air flowed through the respirator's processors. For Elisa, for revenge, to survive, all lies wrapped around kernels of truth. But they needed to be there, they gave shape to the reality I was creating. Without the comfortable fictions, the naked truth would be too sharp to bear.

The truth was simple: I wanted power, and I was willing to kill for it. Everything else was just decoration.

My bloodline was within me now, stronger than it had ever been, singing in my veins with each heartbeat. I could feel it more than ever before, a presence that was both part of me and separate from me. It allowed me to use my abilities far easier than I think any Initiate had a right to, gave me command of aspects I was sure wasn't the norm for someone of my supposed level.

The Nexus bloodline was an inheritance I was only beginning to understand. It had awakened fully during the essence trial, when I had made that bargain with the voice in the starlight. Whatever price I would eventually pay for that power, it had already proven its worth. Without it, I would be dead, poisoned and forgotten in a hotel room that existed between realities.

My blood sang a gentle song now, questing out from me to feel the Flux aspects around me with an authority that was not at all gentle beneath its deceptive softness. I could feel the vital aspects in the trees that grew in impossible places throughout the dungeon, their life force pulsing in rhythm with something greater than themselves. The void in the patches of space between the stones, empty areas that existed but contained nothing. The shadow within the light that filtered down through cracks in the ceiling, darkness given definition by illumination.

I could feel the primal aspects in the Flux beasts that roamed outside what had been our little camp, what was now my temporary stronghold. Their hunger, their rage, their simple drives distilled into pure emotion. They called to something in my bloodline, recognized me as either predator or prey. From their sudden stillness in the corridors beyond, I suspected they had decided I was the former.

Most disturbing of all, I could feel it in the corpses of my former allies I had slain. Death had not severed their connection to the Flux entirely. Echoes remained, fading slowly like the last notes of a song. Zoey's life force still clung to her remains, confused and lost. Vathwin's mechanical augmentations continued to broadcast faint signals, his cyborg nature making death a more complicated process than it was for purely organic beings.

The notification that had been waiting patiently in my peripheral vision finally demanded attention.

You are now Flux Level 17 and have 7 attribute points to assign.

Ah yes, the experience I had gotten for killing my former teammates. The reward for betrayal, measured in cold numbers that translated to real power. There was something beautifully honest about the Flux's refusal to judge my actions. It simply recorded what had happened and granted advancement accordingly. Kill or be killed. Grow stronger or die weak. Everything else was just noise.

I opened my eyes, willing the Resonance Flux to pull up my status page. The familiar blue text materialized in my vision, displaying the sum total of what I had become.

[STATUS] Name: Varus Thorne Age: 31 Flux Level (FL): 17 Race: Human (Awakened) Profession: Essence Crafter (Initiate): 9 Faction: Nyxen (Initiate)

[PROGRESSION] Flux Experience (FEXP): 2100 Path: Path of The Nexus Eclipse

[TEMPERING] Mind: Awakened Initiate Body: Awakened Initiate Spirit: Awakened Initiate Bloodline: Nexus Bloodline (Apprentice)

[ATTRIBUTES] Resonance: 21 Projection: 18 Containment: 7 Perception: 4 Attunement: 4

[ASPECT ABILITIES] Vital: None Primal: Flux Surge (Initiate)

Flux surges within you in response to your anger, granting you enhanced strength and speed. Deepen your connection to the Primal Aspect to enhance the strength of this ability.

Flux Crush (Initiate)

Your rage surges through the Flux, allowing you to use it to crush opponents with Containment lesser than your resonance.

Flux: Flux Sense (Initiate)

Your awareness of life, and objects connected to Resonance Flux. Can currently sense the strength of other initiate tier life forms and objects. Deepen your connection with the Flux on a neutral level to increase the power of this ability.

Void: Consume Flux (Initiate)

You can concentrate, tapping into the void aspect of the Flux and consume an initiate level Flux ability.

Echo: None

[Bloodline Abilities] Regeneration (Apprentice)

Through your Bloodline, you are granted a passive regeneration ability with the Resonance Flux. You will regenerate from wounds, toxins, and other harmful effects passively at the apprentice level. Regeneration is slow at first, can be increased with meditation and connection to the Flux.

Command Presence (Initiate)

Through your Bloodline you are granted some measure of authority over all aspects of the Flux. Small at first, you can project a commanding aura through your connection to the Flux.

The numbers told a story of transformation, of a man becoming something else through violence and necessity. Each point represented choices made, paths taken, lines crossed. I was no longer the person who had entered this dungeon weeks ago. That version of myself wouldn't have recognized the creature staring back from these statistics.

Thus far, I had been trying to shore up my Containment, building my defenses against the hostile reality I found myself in. I no longer felt any particular need to make that a priority. The best defense was overwhelming offense, the ability to crush your enemies before they could threaten you. I would still put points into Containment, but Resonance and Projection were feeling far more important now. They were what had won me the fight just now, after all. Even Vathwin's considerable defenses had not been high enough to resist my enhanced Resonance.

I wasn't surprised. My bloodline was an unfair advantage, a genetic lottery win that gave me access to power others could only dream of. I often heard people say life wasn't fair, but I don't think one truly understands that particular flavor of pain until they lose someone important to them. Until the universe reveals its true nature as a place where random chance determines who lives and who dies, where no amount of goodness or virtue guarantees safety.

The irony wasn't lost on me. I had been on the receiving end of life's unfairness when monsters tore through my front door. Now I was the unfair advantage, the unexpected variable that would ruin someone else's carefully laid plans.

I noted the consumption ability I hadn't yet used, the Void aspect power that would let me steal and destroy enemy abilities. I knew I had it, could feel it lurking in my consciousness like a loaded weapon waiting to be fired. There hadn't been a need to use it yet, but the time would come. Everything was a tool to be used when the situation demanded it.

I assigned three points to Resonance, three to Projection, and one to Containment. The moment I confirmed the allocation, Flux surged within me like electricity through copper wire. Power flooded through my body, settling into my bones and organs and nervous network. I rolled my neck as the energy found its new channels, feeling stronger, more dangerous, more complete than I had moments before.

Next, I checked my initiate points, the currency that measured my progress through this deadly competition.

Varus Thorne: 58 points

My newly acquired respirator made every breath sound like the cold whisper of dead branches in an ancient graveyard. The mechanical undertones gave my breathing an ominous quality that I found oddly satisfying. Not a lot of points compared to what I could have earned, but that was my fault. I had been playing this game too cautiously, hiding away here, biding time instead of actively hunting.

It was time to change that approach, and I knew just how to do it. The dungeon was full of other initiates, all of them carrying points that could be mine. Some would be stronger than Vathwin and Zoey, but many would be weaker. All of them represented opportunities for advancement, chances to prove that I had truly embraced what I was becoming.

I stood up from my meditation, looking down at the corpses that had once been my allies. Their deaths had taught me something important about myself, about the choices I was willing to make when survival was at stake. There was no going back now, no pretending I was still the man who had built houses and worried about mortgage payments.

That man was dead. What had taken his place was something harder, colder, infinitely more dangerous. Something that breathed through a machine and saw other people as resources to be harvested.

Soon, very soon, I would be ready to claim what was mine.

But first, I needed to find more prey.
