By the time I get back to the compound, it’s late in the afternoon. While the Gallery might lack windows, I can tell Morgan is still inside. Her apprentices are nowhere to be seen, mostly likely having gone to bed. The both of us can get dragged into things pretty easily. For her, it’s magical research. Few can match her dedication—some would call it obsession—to magic.

“Hi, Morgan. Still working? When was the last time you slept?”

“I can sleep later. Besides, I need even less sleep than I used to. Being an Ascended comes with many perks, you know? How was your dive?”

“Good. Very interesting. Came across a boss with intelligence. It had some minor tactical capabilities and was ordering around a few of the fodder monsters. Also, I brought you something.”

Interesting loot is one of the few things that’ll pull Morgan away from whatever her current fixation is.

“Gimmie gimmie!” she says, turning around with hints of greed and excitement in her eyes.

I drop the flame thrower on the ground with a thunk. I can hear a faint sloshing inside.

“The boss was using a flame thrower. I wondered if you could do something with it.”

“Mach, I appreciate the vote of confidence, but I’m not a chemical engineer.”

“Well, but it’s magic, right? That’s your thing.”

She laughs.

“Oh, Mach. You’re precious, you know that?”

“Um.”

“I’ll take a look at it, I promise. Actually, now that I think about it, I have a few ideas…”

She drifts off for a moment, lost in her own world. Her fingers twitch and runes briefly float around her.

“If I… Yes, that could… I’d need…”

Half formed ideas slip from her lips, and I watch as a dozen illusionary Opuses form before disappearing like morning mist under the sun. I do my best to quietly slip out, but I don’t even make it to the door.

“Where are you going?” Morgan asks.

“I didn’t want to bother you. I’d just get in the way.”

I gesture to the warehouse sized gallery, full of desks covered in half built Opuses, materials, and monster parts. A dozen ritual circles fill one half of the massive laboratory, some of them incomplete. A huge smelter sits in one corner, placed to melt Electrum or other metals into liquid form to be casted into components, or into ritual circles themselves.

In the center lie the crown jewels of the entire magical laboratory, a dozen paintings. Each of them is covered in a series of small runes so fine they look like a single brush stroke when viewed together. Each painting resembles a circuit board, gold runes on black backing. The runes used an electrum based paint, while the black backing uses a mineral sourced from Fractures that acts as a magical insulator.

“I can’t really help with any of this, you know? Magic doesn’t work for me.”

“Well, who’s going to lift big heavy objects for me?” She says, pointing to a four ton block of Mithril.

“Morgan, you don’t need me to lift that.”

Despite Morgan’s rather lithe frame, her strength is still beyond superhuman. Being an Ascended comes with many perks, as Morgan likes to say. One of those perks is immense physical strength, even though her powerset doesn’t have anything to do with it. She could punch out a 3-Star if she really wanted to, I’m sure.

Of course, that doesn’t count her telekinesis spells, which could also lift such a heavy object. With ease, even. I’ve seen her lift fifty ton tanks without a problem.

“Look, Mach, you don’t have to go anywhere. Really, I don’t mind it when you’re around. Do you have something else to do?”

“I was just going to go to the gym. Do some more training. Matt’s asleep, but I can work the controls well enough on my own.”

“You’ve been training with Matt for almost a week straight. When you’re not in there, you’re in the air or in a Fracture. You need to slow down.”

“Like you?”

A tired smile breezes over her face.

“I’m not spending every moment of every day in near constant combat, Mach. It’d wear anyone down, even you. You’ve got limits too. Remember after Amiens? You said you’d slow down.”

“It’s just that…” I trail off, fists clenching.

“I get it, Mach. I do. It feels like you’re spinning your wheels. That you’re not doing anything. You feel that resting is a waste of time, that you should be working. Well, let me tell you that rest isn’t a waste of time. You can’t help anyone if you’re a wreck.”

“I’m fine.” My reply is instant and instinctive.

I’m fine because I have to be.

“No, Mach. You’re not. Fuck, I doubt there’s a single person on this planet who is!”

“You seem to be doing alright.”

“I promise I’m not. If you think working for forty-eight hours straight and downing a gallon of energy drinks a day is healthy, I have bad news for you. Look. Just sit for a bit. If it makes you feel any better, having you around is helpful for me. I could use someone to bounce ideas off of.”

“Morgan, I don’t know how magic works in the slightest.”

“Even better. It means you’ll ask more questions.”

She snaps her fingers, and strips of Mithril slough off the cube, flying through the air. They quickly form a huge chair, one sized for me. I slump into it with a sigh.

“Fine. You win. What are those, then?” I point at the circuit board-like paintings.

“Those are the beginnings of some wards. Very, very complex. And very early prototypes.”

“Well, what are they supposed to do?”

“Theoretically, anything. These designs are supposed to keep monsters out of a designated area. If I can just figure out the targeting schema… Well, like I said. Early prototypes.”

“What’s wrong with them?”

“Well, I haven’t figured out how to differentiate between monsters or Empowered. Both are magical.”

I scan the closest ward, and I can make absolutely no sense of it. Each individual rune is miniscule, just millimeters in size. The entire painting if made of hundreds, thousands of tiny concentric circles connected by strings of more runes, all traced in gold. Each trace reminds me of a pond during a rainstorm, countless tiny ripples racing across the surface of the water.

“Well, they’re insanely impressive. How do you even paint something like that? It’s so fine!”

With a hand on one hip and a smug grin, a paintbrush coalesces in her hand. The tip is as thin as a human hair. With another snap of her fingers, a cup of Electrum-based paint flies off from a shelf and floats nearby. A careful dip leaves the brush perfectly coated.

“Even before my Ascension, I was a really, really good painter.”

She picks up where she left off on one of the wards, and makes a tiny, barely perceptible movement. The brushstroke is immaculate. Only a machine could produce anything close to it.

“And now? My hands never waver.”

She steps back, holding out the brush in my direction.

“Want to try? I’m sure you’ve got the precision.”

“I’d just mess it up, I’m sure. I’m not an artist like you.”

“Come on, Mach,” she says while rolling her eyes. “It’s a prototype. I have literally four others just like it. Give it a go, would you?”

She shoves the brush into my hands. It’s comically small in my fingers, comparable to a toothpick.

“Here, you see that rune? Yeah, just copy that one and put it there.”

I do my best to replicate the exact rune she pointed out, but even with perfect precision it’s extremely difficult. I leave a tiny dribble of extra paint. Despite the miniscule amount, it’s enough to drip down, and blends with a rune below.

“Oh, hell, I’m sorry.”

Morgan pats my shoulder, as I took a knee to paint on the much shorter easel.

“A good first attempt! Really, nice job. Come on, try again. See this rune? Yeah, put it right there…”

Dawn breaks, though neither of us can see from inside the bunker-like Gallery. Morgan yawns as we finish one of the ward prototypes together.

“Whew, what time is it?”

“6:30 in the morning. The next day. You should probably go to bed.”

Morgan goes to say something before another yawn steals her words. Instead she resorts to a nod, and exhaustedly marches towards her quarters not far from the gallery. I move off towards the gym, intent on continuing my training. Matt might be busy with other tasks today, but like I told Morgan earlier, I can work the controls well enough on my own.

Not sleeping is very strange, and not just in the sense of losing something core to the life of a human. Even Morgan, for having left most of mortality behind, still must sleep. To sleep is to set an end for the day, to divide yesterday, today, and tomorrow into discrete segments. Not only is sleep a required aspect of staying alive—going too long without sleep can kill—but also a key part of keeping track of time.

Plenty of times during college it still didn’t count as “tomorrow” despite the day turning over into the early morning. It was still today, the same day, because I hadn’t gone to bed yet.

I haven’t slept in the last two months.

The apocalypse has been one long, unbroken string of events, many of them terrible. A singular terrible day. A horrible today I wish could end and become tomorrow. It disconnects me from the world I knew more deeply than anything else, I think.

Walking is still walking, though I have to be more careful. While flying out is more complicated, it didn’t fundamentally change. I still have to get on a plane, it’s just a different kind. Learning to see in the entire EM spectrum did have a learning curve attached, but it’s still seeing. Just more of the same. Not sleeping, though?

A fundamental change. Something I never really expected, at least. I did my time as a sleep deprived, overworked engineering student. At the time, I would have thought never needing to sleep would have been amazing. Now I’m not so sure. I think the only reason I’m able to keep track of the days is because of my perfect memory, though that can be disorienting in its own way.

A front gate camera picks up a convoy of vehicles heading in our direction. It’s a black SUV with diplomatic plates escorted by a handful of armed Humvees, their gunners eyeing the open terrain with well deserved paranoia.

I change course, shutting down my threads of thought. The plates belong to the ambassador of the Peoples’ Republic of China.

I wonder what they want with us. Probably nothing good. I’m sure it would take a lot for them to ask for help.
