"What?"

Aodhán straightened, the word slipping out before he could stop it. For one unsteady heartbeat, his mind raced far ahead of the First Seat's explanation—already bracing, already rehearsing the ways he might argue, might prove himself worth keeping.

Then the First Seat kept talking, and the ground beneath that thought quietly gave way.

"We need you to find that legacy first," the First Seat continued, his runic gaze intense. "If there truly are enemies searching for it, we would be remiss to delay."

Something in Aodhán's chest loosened—slower than relief should come from something so small. He nodded in agreement, feeling his earlier worry melt away, only to be replaced by another—one with sharper edges. Enemies searching for a legacy meant enemies willing to kill for it, and willing to kill whoever stood in their way.

Back in Lutia, he'd never heard of people intentionally hunting down legacies. It seemed that custom wasn't one Gordon shared.

Exchanging a glance with Sophran, he asked, "When do we leave?"

"At first light," Calamity Amoz responded, his tone grave. "Take a nap and refresh yourself. You'll need it."

"Thank you, Elder." Aodhán nodded carefully, then glanced back at the High Elders. "I'll do my best."

"That is all we can ask," the First Seat said, his expression grim. "Be careful, Aodhán," he added a moment later. "We do not know who these enemies are or how powerful they may be. You must be on alert."

His gaze shifted to Calamity Amoz. "You must all be on alert."

"We will be, First Seat," Calamity Amoz replied. "I'll be going with a team of Mythics. Cephas and Rezin will also be with me. We will protect the Crown of Ruinous Storms and his underling."

The First Seat nodded. "Then it's settled." He glanced at Aodhán. "Get some rest, disciple. You leave for Alcatraz by dawn."

Aodhán nodded, then paused, his brows furrowing. "Apologies, High Elders. I have a question."

The First Seat gestured for him to speak, and Aodhán stepped forward.

"Should we find the legacy seed," he began carefully, looking the First Seat in the eye, "does the sect already have someone in mind? Someone they'd like to give it to?"

The High Elders exchanged glances, and a moment later, High Elder Mystral spoke.

"We have consulted Magnus Ghaltris and his family, and they have stated the seed should go to any candidate we desire. There are a few candidates we have in mind, unless…" She glanced back at Aodhán. "You have someone in mind."

"Me?"

Aodhán blinked, surprised.

The First Seat nodded. "If you successfully find the seed, this would be a fitting way to say thank you. Not only for this, but for everything you have done for the sect so far."

Synové's face flashed in Aodhán's mind as he remembered the promise he'd made to her and Unrid all those months ago, after they'd first returned from the academy.

He'd told her he would teleport her over should he ever find another legacy seed. And now, from nowhere, the High Elders were handing that opportunity to him.

An opportunity to awaken his own mother.

Gratitude pooled in his chest like a river, and he bowed immediately. "Thank you so much, High Elders. I am beyond grateful."

"You deserve it," the First Seat said with a smile. "Who do you have in mind?"

"My mother," Aodhán replied without hesitation. "My adopted mother. She's unawakened."

A somber expression passed across the High Elders' faces, and High Elder Zarxan slowly nodded.

"That's a noble desire, Aodhán. We wish you all the best in this expedition, for the sake of the sect and that of your mother."

"Thank you, High Elders," Aodhán said again, unable to help himself.

The First Seat nodded. "Now, go. Take a nap. And do your best. You're our only hope of finding this seed first, as it is."

Aodhán nodded firmly, determination gleaming in his eyes. "I will not fail you, First Seat."

I will not fail you, Mother.

"It is settled, then," Calamity Amoz said quietly. "We'll meet here at the elder hall by dawn tomorrow. Don't be late."

"I won't be."

Aodhán bowed again, a grin forming on his face as he turned to leave. But as he did, his gaze caught Zarxan's worried expression.

Their gazes met, and the High Elder smiled wryly. "Be careful, Aodhán. And come back safe."

"I will."

They left the hall a moment later, and as the door shut behind them, Aodhán nearly squealed.

"Haaa! I can't believe it!"

Crixis laughed. "I'm proud of you, elder brother. You deserve it."

"Indeed," Sophran chuckled. "Your actions alone have led to the widespread gaining of familiars in the sect and our growing strength. I couldn't have been happier."

Aodhán breathed, just soaking in the wonder of it all. He would find this seed even if it was the last thing he did. He would help Synové awaken, and then he would never have to worry about her mortality again.

No more counting the years she had left. No more wondering if she would fall ill to some random sickness and die before her time.

She would live to watch him become a Mythic. To watch her children become Calamities.

She would live to see all of it.

He smiled widely as he imagined it, before slowly shaking his head. "I must find this seed first. I don't know how, but I must."

Crixis grimaced. "But what would anyone outside the Storm Sect need a Storm legacy seed for?"

"Something sinister, I'm sure." Sophran scowled. "Perhaps they plan to infiltrate the Storm Sect."

Aodhán frowned. "If that's their plan, then it's no longer viable. We already know of it and will be guarded against it."

"Or we'll make the mistake of turning away the next double inheritor that arises out of paranoia." Sophran shook his head. "It's a slippery slope to head down, but let's face it. There are probably still legacies out there that predate the formation of sects that no one has found yet. What if a double inheritor arrives right now? Will we turn them away, or will we accept them?"

Aodhán grimaced, not liking the doubt that stirred within him at Sophran's question.

It was indeed a slippery slope. This singular action would make it hard for them to trust any inheritor or double inheritor who arose from that moment onward. Anyone who came now would automatically be suspected of infiltration. Because if their enemies were already searching for one legacy, who's to say they weren't searching for more?

Who's to say they hadn't found any?

Who's to say there wasn't already a spy among them?

No!

Aodhán shook his head violently to dispel the thoughts. That kind of thinking led nowhere good, and spreading it would only cause panic.

He sighed. "How about we don't let the enemy win by thinking too much about this? The less people know, the better."

"Indeed," Sophran nodded. "For all intents and purposes, it could just be a sleeper searching for the legacy."

Aodhán latched onto the idea immediately, nodding in agreement. But even as he did, he knew it was unlikely. The actions of the system in protecting the seed weren't targeted at sleepers.

It was targeted at cultivators. And not just any cultivator—a dangerous one. He himself had found a legacy seed before, back when core sense hadn't even fully developed, and he hadn't experienced anything like this.

Perhaps it was because he hadn't actively been looking for it, though. He had just… stumbled upon it as though—

He frowned.

As though he'd been led toward it.

The thought gave him pause, and his frown deepened as he thought through the Raventhorn accident with a new perspective.

He had always considered it a brutal accident, as it had claimed the lives of so many people. But after all he had learned today about legacy seeds being fated to certain people, and the system itself acting as a protector over them, he was forced to wonder if it could have been fate instead.

Because what were the odds that a Storm Origin Breach opened up right above him in the middle of an endless sea?

What were the odds that they had crashed onto the very island that held the legacy and simply strolled toward it? Was finding that inheritor's trial really luck, or did something else have a hand in it?

Aodhán was inclined to go with the latter.

After all he had learned about his life, the manipulations of fate over him were no longer a surprise.

But did that mean there truly were people fated for legacies?

He shook his head, refusing to believe it, and spoke aloud. "It doesn't matter whether there are enemies or not because we'll find it first."

Crixis glanced at him and nodded. "Hopefully, we are not too late."

They returned to the special sect after that, and when they arrived at the mansion, Sophran spoke, declining to come in.

"I think I should head home and give you time to rest and prepare for tomorrow," he said and smiled. "Come back safely, both of you."

"We will," Aodhán replied, and a few minutes later, he and Crixis walked into the living room and shut the door behind them.

Crixis sighed immediately. "I should probably tell my parents I'm leaving the sect."

Aodhán nodded. "Send them my regards. And tell them I said I'll keep you safe."

Crixis snorted, leaving to do just that. When he left, Aodhán let himself collapse into a chair with a smile, worried about the adventure ahead yet unable to keep himself from smiling.

Within his spirit, Varéc purred in anticipation, images of roiling clouds and wide cities flashing through his mind.

Aodhán cracked a small smile. "Not happening, buddy. No way am I unleashing you on Sleeper City with your size. Once again, that size-altering perk would have come in handy."

Varéc growled in disappointment, but after a few minutes of back and forth, he eventually allowed himself to see the logic in Aodhán's words and began sulking within his spirit.

Aodhán ignored him.

A storm was brewing. He could feel it.

But unlike the Balance War, this wasn't a storm he wanted to avoid. No, it was one he wanted to run towards, if not for the Storm Sect, then for Synové.

He sighed once more, yawning tiredly.

He hadn't slept in over three days now, and though he knew he needed rest, Aodhán couldn't rest with the adventure now before him. If he was to find this legacy faster than their enemies, then he needed his sole ability to become as sharp as a blade.

He needed core sense to become as precise as inevitability itself.

But one could only cheat nature for so long before the repercussions came. And Aodhán, it seemed, had cheated it long enough to finally pay the price.

The hour before dawn found Aodhán passed out on the couch, a half-eaten tray of vegetables and roasted meat on the table before him.

He had fallen asleep barely two hours into training core sense, but after three whole days of constant wakefulness, his body had finally had enough.

But even in his dreams, Aodhán hadn't stopped training, because while his body was tired, his mind was not.

Scenes of every major instance in which he had used core sense flashed through his mind, right from the first time he'd sensed Councilman Balor's limited core in the Warren Forest all the way to the ascension that had happened just a few months ago.

With each one, the growth and evolution of the ability were obvious.

At first, he'd only been able to gauge the tier range of those around him. Then it had grown more specific, so much so that he had begun identifying energy signatures and sensing the node clusters of awakened plants.

It had evolved once more after that, allowing him to filter and choose which affinities he wanted to see—a development that had helped him greatly during herb-gathering exercises.

But core sense hadn't stopped there.

It had grown wider and deeper after that, enlarging both in scale and depth until he could finally see the world even with his eyes closed.

It wasn't true sight by any means. But after the ascension, he could now see the energies clinging to the things around him, giving everything a faint, grainy outline.

It was far from precise and nowhere near reliable yet, but it was still a massive improvement from where he'd started. And as the scenes churned in his mind, insights and snippets rose—ideas on what direction to evolve the ability in for this particular mission.

A legacy seed hidden by the system itself would not be an easy find, and with all the talk of fate choosing its own inheritors, Aodhán doubted it would be on his side this time.

But Aodhán didn't care. He would find that legacy one way or the other and hand it to Synové, even if he had to go back to Lutia to do so.

He woke up a few minutes later, just an hour before dawn, and immediately began preparing for the day. Crixis was already awake, and fifteen minutes later, as they both dressed, Crixis asked,

"Have you thought of the implications of giving this seed to your mother?"

Aodhán glanced at him, halfway into his robe, then sighed. "I have. I thought about it last night."

Crixis pursed his lips. "She'll have to come to the Storm Sect, leaving her husband and your brothers behind. Do you think she can do that?"

Aodhán's expression tightened, and for a moment, he stared at the half-fastened robe in his hands. "I don't know. But the rest of us are awakened. She's not. If I don't do something, she'll die one day and I can't—"

His mind flashed to Master Gyatso, and his fingers tightened around the fabric. He shook his head. "I can't lose anyone else."

Crixis's expression sobered. "I understand. Hopefully, your family will too."

Aodhán stayed silent for a long moment, then looked away. "We should get going."

They left the house a few minutes later and began making their way to the Elder Sect. However, just as they reached the administrative building, Aodhán sensed a duo of familiar cores approaching from behind.

He glanced back sharply, his eyes narrowing as two figures swept through the morning sky, their robes fluttering in the wind.

"Is that who I think it is?" Crixis asked, his eyes widening.

Aodhán chuckled. "It sure is. What I don't understand is what he thinks he's doing, though."

"I'm coming with you. I can't let you go alone."

"I'm sure it is," Tianyu said with a smile. "But Elder Brother Sophran hasn't been out of the sect in a long time. This could be good for him."

"You'll have to inform the High Elders," he said with a chuckle.

"Definitely." Sophran smiled. "Now, let's go find that legacy."

They bowed immediately, and when they lifted their heads, Calamity Amoz raised an eyebrow.

"I wasn't aware the Undying Saint and his underling would be joining us for this expedition."

Calamity Amoz exchanged a glance with the other Calamities, then let out a deep breath. "As long as the High Elders approve it, I'm fine with it." He gestured toward the hall. "I doubt they are all seated at this hour, but High Elder Mystral and Tharn are both in there. Make your case to them."

Sophran nodded and stepped into the elder hall with Tianyu to do just that.

After the door shut behind him, Aodhán glanced back at the gathered group, taking the opportunity to observe his comrades fully.

Aside from the two Calamities Calamity Amoz had introduced as Rezin and Cephas the night before, there was one other Calamity present, draped from head to toe in what could only be called liquid sky.

She smiled warmly at Amoz. "Well, it seems I'm not the only addition crashing your party this morning, Amoz."

"Very hard, trust me. Vaymoz Alcatraz may be a mere sleeper, but he's also the ruler of one of the largest human settlements in all of Gordon."

"Incredibly. And his people respect him for it." Rezin scowled. "He knows exactly what a Calamity is capable of—better than most sleepers ever will—and he defies us anyway." He shook his head before glancing at Amoz. "Say... don't you two have some history?"

Calamity Amoz nodded. "Thank you, Remaris. You have spoken well."

The system won't allow any search skills to function in Alcatraz, but we are hoping a search ability should be exempt. If it is, then we shall begin our search immediately after seeking Vaymoz's permission. It is our hope that your ability can immediately point us in the right direction."

He lifted his head. "I'll do my best to return safely."

"Then let the storm guide you," High Elder Mystral whispered, her gaze darting meaningfully to Selia for a moment before she stepped back into the hall.

Aodhán followed her gaze to the Calamity, but Selia's expression gave nothing away—just that same easy, unbothered smile she'd worn since she'd arrived. Whatever silent exchange had just passed between the two women, it wasn't meant for him.

"Are we ready?" Calamity Amoz asked, and immediately, the Calamities, the Mythics—everyone—turned questioningly to Aodhán.

Aodhán wasn't surprised. He was the anchor of this mission, after all.

He nodded. "I am."

Reality blurred immediately, and several seconds later, they arrived above a sprawling city that stretched as far as the eye could see.

Aodhán stared.

The city was unlike anything he had ever seen before, filled with towering structures, intricate architecture, and strange inventions whose mere presence seemed to defy the very essence of cultivation, as though declaring without words that humans didn't need cultivation to be great.

It was stubborn humanity laid bare, a city built by people who couldn't fly, warp reality, or command storms.

And yet, even in the darkness of pre-dawn, the city was beautiful.

Vast districts stretched across the landscape in every direction, broken only by towering skyscrapers, glittering avenues, and the occasional airstrip dancing through the skies.

Aodhán smiled.

Somewhere in this vast city, Edrick Ghaltris's legacy was hidden, and it was time to narrow it down.

Core sense spread out immediately, expanding across the sleeping city until it covered several miles.

Aodhán closed his eyes then, inhaling deeply as the world lit up behind his eyes, energy clinging desperately to every surface. But even in a city supposedly built for sleepers, several clumps of brightness filled his vision—awakened plants, buried treasures, magical items, and a host of other energy signatures.

He would never find the legacy in this mess.

Fortunately, core sense came with an affinity filter. His smile widened, and without hesitation, he whispered.

"Storm."
