Before he headed in for a chat, and before he turned his attention to a particularly interesting Avatar of his, Henry took a look at his interface. It hadn’t been working while they’d been in space, but now it was back and funnily enough, it almost looked like it lagged for a moment. It stalled, for less than half a second in which Henry sensed the briefest, lightest touch of magic on his soul, and then it came up.

It didn’t take Henry long to go through everything, and pretty quickly, a few items caught his attention. First, it was the stats. There was more of them than he had expected for being level 9. He had felt the change as he leveled, and it made sense that he’d gained a couple extra stat points with all the energies and powers he had been constantly channeling, but still. There was a little more of them than he had expected. Henry guessed he’d have a better idea of his rate of growth once he got a few more levels and had the detailed breakdown of the System to make sense of it. For now, he wasn’t complaining.

The second item was seeing the results of the last two weeks of managing thousands of Avatars, day in and day out, while funneling the power of thousands of cores into Unfettered Echoes. Power which he had gained through detonating his clones and poisoning the space mantises. This had pushed Telepathic Orchestration to its limit, which now made it the largest element of the A-Rank Mind Weaver’s Tongue. Predation of the Trickster had also hit A-rank, with Trickster’s Toxic Concoction quickly joining as well. Its subparts, which included Henry’s toxin creation, storage, and deployment skills, now included Spiteful Detonation as well, and all of these elements had been pushed and empowered equally. Though it was interesting to see that Predation and its cornerstone, Bite, hadn’t gotten too big of an upgrade, aside from the sheer power the Bite and Henry’s unique poison could inflict. Then again, the System and its nudges were a lot less effective, and Henry could likely implement whatever he wanted if given enough time and resources. For now, he was pretty happy with the capabilities of Bite, and a good boost to their effectiveness was good enough for him.

Lastly, Henry had a decent idea of what he wanted to work on next. Being stuck in the void of space, dealing with creatures that loved to screech their maddening screams right into his soul, Henry had mostly relied on his Toxic Rays and his detonating Avatars, which he could somewhat tune out when he sent them to die. But he couldn’t get too close himself, and by the nature of their telepathic abilities, the mantises were much more of a pain to deal with from up close. So Henry hadn’t tried as much. Now though, he wanted to work on his close-ranged abilities a little more, and also bring Telekinesis to the next level. It was pretty close to A-rank, and he’d used it plenty to defend and crush the occasional space-bug, but it was time to take it to the next levels. Same went for Lightning Discharge and his healing abilities and between all of those, he needed to keep pushing Unfettered Echoes, as that was his bread and butter.

Should be easy enough, he thought as he dismissed the stat screen and took note of what his thousands of Avatars were telling him. Nowadays, he had to use his Octominds to filter out the input, as his own attention could only take him so far. So Henry, while his messenger Avatar took in the lake and Wolf Den’s simultaneously from above while Maurice and Velistraine likely took a moment to check their own interface, an Octomind brought something to Henry’s attention. One Avatar in particular was having an oddly weak connection to him, even if it was well within its optimal range. It was still a Lifeblood one, but even those could handle the distance well, nowadays. In fact, it was below the lake, and looking through its eyes, Henry found himself in an opulent, fully submerged room, with glowing bulbs tastefully bathing the space in clear blue lights, while here and there, algae-like plant curled and twisted in the water along the walls.

This looked like an office that was commonly used to receive people, and at the end of it, there was an odd-looking desk that curved upwards like a smile. It was clamped to the stone floor, but it also had some sort of enchantment that prevented what was placed on its surface from floating away.

Behind it, a familiar, gold-scaled figure looked up at the Avatar, right as Henry took control of it. Quickly enough, Henry sensed the magic stifling and suppressing him. Four half-columns inscribed with runes surrounded him, engraved with glowing symbols and out of them, four chains rattled as he attempted to move his arms. Trying to activate a simple Telekinesis skill, he felt it slowly come apart before it was ready to activate, but Henry had a feeling he might be able to force it through.

“Ah. The kraken’s awake, I see. It took you a while.”

“I am,” Henry simply said, gazing back at the Merfolk while he simultaneously played around with the suppressing magic, prodding at it by crafting a few Telekinesis skill-nexuses and observing the results. “I had a bug problem to deal with. Kind of you to have kept my… summon alive and well.”

“There were bugs in the hole you crawled into? I wouldn’t know. I don’t tend to scurry away under the attentions of my betters.”

Henry had glanced away, half of his attention having been fixed on crafting more and more skill-nexuses. The more of them there was, the slower the suppression’s effect seemed to be at destabilizing them. Henry wasn’t putting his back into it just yet. Though that might change soon.

“So you’re still intent on being hostile?” Henry asked, and in response the Merfolk chuckled.

“Well, you defied me, and you did kill one of my best people,” he said with a shrug. Then the needle-teethed smile slipped away, while the Merfolk’s eyes glimmered dangerously. “I’ve killed people for less. Much less.”

The Merfolk’s aura crept out with the calmness of still water. The lights dimmed, and the water suddenly felt heavy and difficult to breathe. Like what might a human feel under the crushing weight of the depths. Physically, Henry was not capable of feeling such a sensation anymore as his body was very well adapted for the abyssal depths. But his spirit still felt the pressure smothering his spirit, crushing him under its weight, dimming his mind and pulling him down to a deeper, deathly quiet place.

Until Henry let Unfettered Echoes off of its leash.

Henry’s aura didn’t surge out with force, nor did it creep out. It just… unveiled itself, like it had always been there just a step out of reality, watching. Waiting. The weight of the Merfolk’s aura was immediately shunted away, and Henry had the pleasure of seeing the predatory smile slip. Unfettered Echoes might not be at the top just yet, but it was a twice-fused Aspect. It had a certain weight to it that wasn’t easy to match, and Henry would bet it would only grow stronger as he attuned himself more to it through time and battle. It was still relatively new, after all, though even if it was not as tested as whatever the Merfolk had deployed, it could match it easily enough.

“Let me cut to the chase,” Henry said, pulling his Octominds for a brief moment, then having each activated multiple instance of Telekinesis, each done through Rupture Casting. The script on the pillars boxing him in shone brightly, suppressing most of them. But not all. And not everything.

Four arms, and four Evershifting Strikes activated, each accelerated and weighed by the skill, and four pillars were pulverized instantly.

Looking back up, Henry found the Merfolk holding on to a spear, eyes glinting, and Henry just lifted his arm and shrunk them just enough to have the metal manacles drop.

“I’m not going to repeat myself, and I’m not going to apologize for defending myself and my autonomy. So here’s the plan: there’s a bigger threat than you coming our way. From outside of this world. You can either help willingly, or you’ll help when I convert your core to levels. Yours and everyone you send to waste my time.”

With that, Henry let Predation add its voice to his aura, and now, all the echoes, all the invisible eyes were suddenly a lot more ravenous. A bit more murderous. Enough that the Merfolk’s eyes flitted for half a second before he clamped down on his reaction. The Merfolk’s eyes narrowed, and Henry noted the grip tightening around the spear shaft. But Henry didn’t focus on it. Instead, he just leisurely hovered, enjoying the sensation of being in water once more. He wasn’t sure he could win in a straight up fight against this one, but if he were to go for the kill… he couldn’t see himself losing a battle of attrition either. Henry’s Avatars could hit as hard as he did, and how many could the Merfolk dispatch before they drained him out of blood and mana? Less than a thousand, Henry would bet. And it seemed that the Merfolk might suspect it.

“So?” Henry asked, swimming close enough to loom over the Merfolk, skin rippling ominously with blacks and yellow lines. “What will it be?”
