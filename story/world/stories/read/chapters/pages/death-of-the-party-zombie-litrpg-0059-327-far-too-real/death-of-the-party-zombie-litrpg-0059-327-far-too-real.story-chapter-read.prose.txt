Sally lay in the warm grass, arms behind her head as the sun shone down on the calm field. The content look on her face soured as a shadow passed over her. She cracked open her right eye to see who the intruder was.

“Good morning, Sally,” Archie said.

She closed her eyes again. “It’s alright.”

There was a brief moment of silence as the wizard considered his approach. “I have noticed you’ve been… somewhat idle since the return from my planet a few weeks ago.”

“What is there to be energetic about?” Sally sighed deeply. “Max, Ren, and their world were safely transferred to Sanctuary. The dwarven retinue spends its time happily mining away or in the tavern. My aptitude for magic is improving, even though my System hasn’t settled yet.”

“All true, but that’s not…”

“Ignoring, of course, the assault on our planet soon. We have no idea if Hunter survived or is able to find us. And Theo… well, I’m sure my wayward husband will return to us when he is ready.” Her brow furrowed as her eyes peeked open again.

Archie nodded politely. Theo’s sacrifice was ill-conceived, but had won the battle against the Guardian. The magicians cleaned up and teleported away before the pocket of reality could implode. One unintended benefit was that the pair absorbed the power of the Amalgamation and had become Aspects themselves.

If Theo had still been there, that might not be the case. Whether this was foresight or dumb-luck, nobody cared to deliberate. Of course, that wasn’t the issue.

The vampire hadn’t returned to Sanctuary. Archie was sure he wasn’t actually dead, but they had no idea where he was. More troubling was when he could be, as well.

“I’m sure you didn’t just come here to prod me about my worries, Arch,” Sally said, pushing herself up. “What’s up?”

“Care to walk and talk?”

She grumbled, but made it to her feet and fell in line with the wizard.

“Unfortunately, I’m here to labor you with more.”

“Should have led with that; I wouldn’t have gotten up.” Sally groaned.

“Exactly.”

The pair moved through the field toward a gentle stream over by a small grouping of wide-canopy trees. It wasn’t the sort of place she liked to hang out usually, but she didn’t have the appetite for hanging around the village lately. It felt… empty without the vampire there.

“Spoken with your father recently?” Archie asked.

Sally shook her head. “I gave him my blessing to become a Guardian, but he still feels weird about not being able to travel with the Outsiders.”

“It’s a stark change, and I wish it could be different.” Archie offered her an apologetic shrug. “Five empowered allies that can stay on Sanctuary to protect it is all we can do while our efforts are pointed toward the invasion.”

Wolf had offered to become the Guardian of the Forest area. The large bear had taken to the new world like a duck to water and was thriving. Both Humphrey and Norah were in contention for Guardian of the Wastelands, with Edward edging in purely for the sake of being a thorn. Dent had taken up the mantle, but the other two positions weren’t decided yet. It was another boost toward defending the world against the bugs.

“Guardians, plus the Aspects of Chaos and Order.” Sally wrinkled her nose. “Even without Theo, we are in much better standing. But you wanted to pick my brains about our defenses, maybe?”

Before the wizard could confirm, there was a flash of blue light. The Architect appeared beside them, blinking his eyes a couple of times before focusing on the pair.

Sally crossed her arms. “Ah. Are we worthy enough for the original, or is this another projection?”

Chuck grimaced. “Were I not in the middle of something important, we’d be meeting in the tower. You’ll have to make do with this version of me.”

“Go on, then. If you’re both here to talk my ears off, it must be something dire.”

The two men exchanged a sheepish look before Archie nodded. “It’s about the potential new System.”

“It’s… the possibilities are overwhelming,” Chuck continued. “We are talking about a much more immersive and detailed existence. You saw how the System-created were on Archie’s planet. That could be the default. No more game-like, soulless creations.”

The wizard gestured out into the open field. “A fully realized replication of reality.”

“Fully.” Sally narrowed her eyes. “So there are downsides, right?”

Chuck pulled a face. “Much like real life, sure. It changes everything, and we’re not saying it’s better or worse… but there are moral decisions involved.”

“Explain.”

“Sentience is a big thing to grant,” Archie offered. “No more mindless drones; everything will be a living, breathing soul given the right of existence. That makes a lot of the adventuring side of things here more complicated.”

Sally rubbed the side of her head. “But they could just respawn if that was the case?”

“That’s the crux of it.” Archie cleared his throat. “We would have to get rid of that here.”

That seemed unthinkable. Being able to respawn was one of the defining factors of Sanctuary and its inhabitants. Permanent death would only end in heartbreak, not to mention…

She raised an eyebrow. “The Radochs won’t desire us as much without that ability, huh?”

“Possibly.” The Architect shrugged. “If we continue to grasp at our immortality, I am sure the roaches won’t be the last to come and fight us for it. Resurrection will still be possible, through spells and items… so we aren’t going full cold turkey.”

The zombie took a moment to think. Making themselves less of a galactic target would be beneficial in the long run. Death didn’t have to be the end, but it would just be more awkward to come back. As the Aspect of Life, she surely would have some options there with a little training. That said…

“More natural world,” she stated. “Are we saying what I think we are?”

“The other side of our immortality is our lack of aging.” Archie stroked his gray beard idly. “So yes, the passage of time will start to flow again. People will be born, raised in this reality, and eventually die.”

“Resurrection doesn’t mean you can live forever,” Chuck added. “A body only endures so much, and dying of old age can’t be reversed.”

“I’ll grow old,” Sally whispered, staring out at the horizon.

“Technically, as you are both undead and an Aspect, your time isn’t coming soon.” The wizard smiled. “You still have much to do for Sanctuary.”

“The thought of losing my mortal friends isn’t so great, though.” She frowned down at the grass. “Surely living in this untouchable utopia is better than inviting in the flaws of reality. Constant threats or not, we can beat anyone.”

“To be fair, that was my first thought as well.” Chuck flickered slightly. “But after the Omen returned, and we sifted through the data Archie was able to retrieve from the Radoch vessels, well, permanent death could be a more viable alternative.”

With a wave of his hand, a blue screen appeared in front of them. Detailed in white lines like a blueprint, the monitor went through several imagined scenarios the invasion might bring.

Most of them involved overloading the respawning mechanic. Saturating the atmosphere with poisonous gas. Turning landmass to molten slag. Several kinds of plagues. Worst of all was a method only alluded to, but involved the spaceships in orbit slowly stripping away the protective layer keeping Sanctuary as a planet. Inviting the Sea to wash over the world.

“As much as my ego would like to think they’d never get the upper hand, it’s something that could easily snowball.” Sally sighed. “So postponing the System change until the invasion isn’t really an option?”

Chuck shrugged. “These strategies would work either way. It’s just that the current way things work, people would be stuck in an endless loop of torment. Dying to revive to die again. At a certain point, something has to break.”

“There are other benefits, aside from the sweet release of oblivion,” Archie objected, giving the blue wireframe man a brief scowl. “The new System does not abide by the current level and ability mechanics. You have seen how applying yourself can result in new skills learned. Power is determined by effort and actual experience. Those currently held back by the way things are now will be able to flourish in the new.”

“If they’re not too busy having babies and getting arthritis.” Sally huffed and rubbed at her eyes. “Why you gotta break my balls about this sort of thing? I’m supposed to be the immature goofball, and you’ve… you’ve allowed me to become too stable. It’s harshing my chi.”

The Architect rolled his eyes. “You’re a pillar of this world. Helped save and form it. Have put your life on the line and seen and done more than more will ever do. One day you’ll leave us behind and I want you to be happy about what you’ve done.”

Sally deflated and looked away dismissively. Somehow she didn’t think Chuck meant dying when it came to leaving. Sure, she had been to other worlds more than most, but Archie had been hinting at a greater purpose for a while now. If this ultimate STAR System could be propagated throughout the Sea, well, that meant a lot more people needed saving from many more threats.

Naturally, she wanted to sink her teeth into that. She couldn’t avoid it.

“So…” she sighed again. “You’re seeking my approval?”

“Not exactly.” Chuck gestured for them to start walking. “While having you on board is non-negotiable, the weight of this change doesn’t sit on your shoulders alone. Archie will have to give up a certain amount of his power.”

The zombie turned a frown toward the wizard. “What?”

“I can’t continue as I am now. Power has been leaking from me like a spilled pot. Part of the switchover is fueled by some sacrifice, but I will still remain as an Aspect after.”

“Even if we do this right now, there’s not enough time for everyone to train to make up the strength difference.” Sally threw up her hands in frustration.

“Plus, we can’t do it until Theo returns.” Chuck gave the zombie an apologetic grin. “Everyone has to be on-world.”

Other than growling to herself, Sally had nothing to add. With the looming threat of the Radoch invasion at their doorstep, this was a terrible time for any sort of monumental change - even if the benefits outweighed the downsides. The war needed winning either way, and this threw a spanner in the works.

Plus, where was that damnable vampire?

Theo lay on the cool grass with his eyes wide open. His form shifted, blending in with the shadows of the early evening. With his brain slowly ticking over, he looked down at the bloody page.

One of the Bone Wardens had put a hole through his heart, and thus the detail of the folder paper had been ruined. That said…

He looked up from the marred sketch to the stone archway in front of him.

It was definitely the same thing. Why it was stuck here in someone’s garden like an ornament was a question he briefly considered, but struggled to keep a hold of. His body kept shifting to shadow, or fuzzy gray-scale, as if he wasn’t properly tuned.

With a click of his tongue, he put his limbs in proper order and stood to his feet. With this portal finally found, his task was clear. Idly, his eyes drifted to the backdoor of the house, where he had spotted an extensive personal library of occult matters.

He just had to find a better copy of the page, and someone capable of performing magic.
