Sakura settled a little apart from Gilbert and Pampli. After the swamp training, her whole body still ached from the strain, and the arm with the new petals pulsed unpleasantly with the aftermath of the transformation. The strength of the new levels was slowly spreading through her body.

Pampli lay down nearby, but he still kept one eye on her, as if worried that the power of self-knowledge might harm his mistress or send her somewhere else again. Gilbert stood a little farther off, one shoulder braced against a tree, watching in silence. Sakura had already realized that Gilbert was not the kind of person who would interfere, rush her, or ask unnecessary questions, and that made her much calmer.

There weren’t many rules written in the scroll, but each one sounded unpleasantly precise:

DO NOT SUBSTITUTE YOUR TRUE ANSWER WITH THE ONE YOU WISH FOR.ACCEPT WHAT YOU SEE.

And the last line, very small and the strangest of all:

LIES LEAD TO SILENCE. TRUTH AWAKENS ECHO.

This scroll was nothing like the other magical scrolls Sakura had used before. She felt that on an instinctive level, that it demanded some other kind of handling. The only problem was that she still didn’t understand what kind. She was only about to find out.

Strange gray lines came together at the center of the scroll, and her hand almost reached for that spot on its own. So Sakura steadied her breathing, laid the scroll across her knees, and pressed her fingers to the center of the parchment. It didn’t feel quite like paper. It was more like thin mica or the dried petal of some enormous plant.

Sakura slowly fed a little mana into it and waited.

At first, nothing happened. Then the lines trembled. It looked like ripples spreading somewhere beneath the upper layer of the parchment itself. The gray lines began to shift, folding into a new pattern that was still too blurred and unfinished for Sakura to recognize clearly.

She frowned. The air around her grew duller, as if the scroll itself were swallowing extra sound. A line from the System flashed before her eyes.

SCROLL OF SELF-KNOWLEDGE ACTIVATED

Sakura had only just blinked when the gray lines finally settled into a clear pattern that looked like an abstract eye inside a flower. She stared into that strange symbol and felt it pull in her attention and concentration. It was like falling into its depth.

And then Sakura saw the subway. Not the entire day she had lived through shortly before dying, but the core of it: the fire, the crush of bodies, the choking smoke, the panicking crowd, the fear. And that sticky, animal feeling that you controlled nothing. You couldn’t get out, couldn’t help anyone, couldn’t change any of it. You couldn’t even pull yourself free.

Her chest tightened sharply, and her fingers twitched on the edge of the scroll.

Sakura didn’t want to look any farther, and the scroll seemed to hear that wish. It didn’t force her to relive everything all over again. It pulled out the very core of the memory instead. The essence of that fatal day. What had been worse than the fire, the crowd, and even the possibility of dying itself was being trapped and having no power to change anything.

After that, the symbol drawn on the parchment changed. It seemed to grow another layer of sharp petals.

And right after that distortion, Sakura saw not her past life, but her present self.

She saw the battle in the swamp, among the obsidian fragments and the black sludge. She saw the flash of [Devourer], and then the crack of another body breaking under crimson petals. She felt that moment again, when you are still breathing hard after a fight, and yet inside something is already rising that is not the simple relief of a survivor, but something darker and sweeter. Almost shameful.

Power.

How good it feels to carry it inside yourself. How much easier it becomes to breathe when you are the one pressing the world down instead of the world pressing you down. How much you want another level, another successful strike, and another proof that you are no longer helpless.

Sakura tensed even more.

She had already thought about this after that fight, and even then she had understood how dangerous that feeling and those thoughts were. But now the scroll had taken her by the chin and forced her to look straight at it.

And instinctively, she could feel that the magic hidden inside this strange scroll was not trying to accuse her. It was not trying to call Sakura an evil, ruined killer. It was saying something else.

That her pull toward power existed because power was protection from that first fear. From that day in the subway.

Sakura closed her eyes for a second, trying to accept that strange thought. A foreign thought being handed to her by the energy of this enchanted object. And it was one of the strangest things that had happened to her lately.

Sakura truly did not want to be weak. She could not allow herself to become that person again, the one who simply stood in a trap and waited for everything to be decided for her. And if power gave her a chance to never feel that old helplessness again, then of course she would be drawn to it.

After that realization, the symbol on the parchment changed again. It grew another layer of petals, but this time they were not sharp. They were rounded. Of course, Sakura had no idea what that meant, but she was ready for another magical revelation from Veyr.

What she saw, however, was not Veyr, and not even the smoke-filled subway.

It was another place, as if stolen from the oldest memories that had once been locked away.

A bright room with a long corridor, painted walls, a clean floor, and many children’s voices somewhere very close. It was children’s noise, something Sakura definitely recognized from her past. Cozy. Homelike.

But in the middle of that noise, in the middle of those voices, she suddenly felt a terrible, cold loneliness.

Sakura saw herself as a child. Not tiny, but still very much a child, seen from the outside. The little girl was sitting on a step by the wall, a little apart from the others, with a bowl of rice on her lap. The white rice had clumped together and gone cold, but she was still eating it carefully and with deep concentration.

There were many children around her. They existed nearby, but as if in another world, one Sakura did not fit into at all. The little girl sat apart, as if she had either been forgotten or simply never been fully accepted.

That feeling struck harder than Sakura expected. It was the oldest trap she had ever lived through: when you are among people and still alone. Maybe no one is screaming at you or hitting you, and maybe you should be grateful for that, but it still feels as if an invisible wall stands between you and everyone else. As if you are only a ghost at the edge of other people’s vision.

Little Sakura kept eating her rice slowly, looking down, because she knew that if she watched the groups of children, it would hurt even more.

Then she heard the voices of two women behind her. Caretakers, probably. They were not speaking loudly, but not quietly enough for the child on the step to hear nothing. Maybe they didn’t think she was listening, or maybe they simply didn’t think it mattered.

“She’s sitting alone again,” one woman said, without malice. “All the other kids are like kids, and this one is always off by herself.”

“She’s been like that from the start,” the second answered. “Like she’s not really here. Sometimes you look at her and it feels like she doesn’t even hear when people talk to her.”

“She hears. It’s not like she’s deaf.”

“Maybe she hears. But her eyes... it’s like she’s always somewhere else.”

The first woman was silent for a moment, then sighed softly.

“She even frightens me a little. She’s just too quiet. Children usually cling, cry, demand attention. But this one has been acting like this ever since she appeared here.”

Sakura felt something tighten inside her. Now she remembered that moment too clearly. That conversation, which little Sakura had definitely heard, and which had sunk much deeper into her than she had ever realized.

Strange. Quiet. Not of this world.

Little Sakura on the step did not react at all. She only tightened her grip on the chopsticks a little and kept eating her rice.

But the scroll once again was not merely showing her a scene. It was pulling out the core of it. Not the loneliness itself, but what stood behind it. The fear of being alien. Always alien.

To be near people and still never reach them. To not know how to enter their world properly. To not understand why it seems so easy for everyone else while for you it feels as if there is glass between you and them.

Sakura slowly drew in a breath.

This third part of the flower drawn on the parchment held no power, no pain, and no fear of death, but it might have been the foundation. The thing that had grown everything else. Her pull toward connection did not come simply from some natural kindness. Not just because Sakura liked animals, children, or suffering monsters.

It came because somewhere very deep inside, she knew too well what it felt like to sit on a step with a bowl of rice, hear people behind you, and understand that they were looking at you as someone separate, strange, wrong.

And maybe that was exactly why Sakura reached so stubbornly for those she did not have to break, but could hear instead. Those she did not need to defeat, but to connect with.

The memory slowly faded, and the children’s voices finally dissolved in her head, leaving behind only the aftertaste of that scene from childhood.

She needed several seconds to hear the forest again and feel the damp air. To become aware of the heaviness of her own body, the pain in her arm, Pampli’s presence nearby, and Gilbert standing only a few steps away.

But something inside her had already changed.

It had become harder to lie to herself about where all of this came from. And somewhere deep inside, Sakura felt sorry for that little girl on the step.

Gilbert, who had not interrupted her with a single word until then, finally shifted a little and looked at her more closely.

“Are you all right?”

Maybe she really had gone pale from what she had just lived through. Sakura did not answer at once. She needed a few more seconds to fully come back to herself. Slowly, she lifted her hand from the parchment, rolled up the scroll, and only then nodded.

“Yes,” she said more quietly than she had meant to. “I’m all right.”

Her voice sounded tired, but not completely broken. And that, apparently, satisfied Gilbert more than any long answer would have.

Sakura exhaled and looked down at the scroll in her hands.

“It’s just... this thing helps you understand certain feelings.”

Gilbert narrowed his eyes slightly.

“Digging through your own feelings can be painful. But often useful.”

“Yeah,” Sakura agreed. Then she gave a faint smile. “But I’ve had enough self-knowledge for one day. A little more, and I’ll become way too enlightened.”

She carefully put the scroll back into its case, and the case back into her Inventory. Right now, she didn’t even want to look at it anymore.

Sakura dragged a hand over her face, trying to shake off the lingering heaviness. Then she lifted her head and only then realized how much the light around them had changed.

Or rather, how little of it was left. The forest had gone completely dark, and even the sky above the treetops had turned deep, cold, and bluish-black. Somewhere far away in the thick growth, night creatures had started calling to one another. The air had grown colder, sharper. Evening had long since turned into full night, and she had not even noticed.

Sakura blinked.

“Wow.”

“What?” Gilbert asked.

She looked around once more, almost as if she hoped she was mistaken.

“It’s completely dark already.”

And it really was. Self-knowledge had cut her out of time and dragged her so deep inside herself that time had stopped feeling real or making any sense. It felt as if she had only just sat down, only just touched the scroll, only just seen the first lines. But in reality much more time had passed. It was like a dream, when you think you only slept for five minutes and wake up to find that several hours are gone.

Sakura let out an almost bewildered huff.

“Time went way too fast with that thing.”

She looked once more toward the place where the world had almost dissolved into the dark trees and thought that she really had had enough for one day. Swamps. Levels. Power. Memories. Truth.

That was enough for tonight.
