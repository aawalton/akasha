Chapter 110: A Daunting Foe

The too-brief captured moment of bliss with Neex was interrupted by a glaringly unavoidable mem-text notice through the System.

<Congratulations, Team High Life! You have been assigned your second match of the Powers Applied Coordination Courses, aka, the PACCs. This match will begin tomorrow at 1400 sharp inside Arena Chamber Bravo. Both teams are required to report to the central entrance at 1330, and will be allowed to their team exclusive areas at 1340 for final preparations. Entrance into the actual arena chamber will begin at 1353.

It’s advisable to practice light tomorrow, leading up to the match, and enter at 100% efficiency. Today, you otherwise have full Sim Chamber access up to 2100, and will not have it tomorrow.

The match is against Team Irresistible Style. The match type will be Fortress Defense — Protect the Leader, and your team is the defense. Both groups must designate a leader whose defeat spells automatic loss. Furthermore, the defending leader is automatically defeated if they leave the fortress walls. The map is: Rocky Desert in the Afternoon.

To celebrate, you may have your choice of a free dinner tonight at 1800, available in the mess hall!*

Good luck, and May Humanity Prevail!

(*attendance for dinner is mandatory)>

Jack sat up in the bed, as Neex — regrettably — faded things out with her ‘ocean-emotion-bomb’ after a final mental hug, suddenly a tad shy about it.

“Here we go again!” she said, nervously breaking the ice. “I bet your group is about to blow up about it.”

Jack sighed. “Yeah, pretty mu-”

“Who the hell is that team?!” Highfive shot into the team gestalt.

Almost right on top of him came Bo. “I’m already digging, Fiver! Chill!”

“Huh? You guys don’t know?” Augur, his voice ragged in quality from lack of use, and shifting from surprise to smug satisfaction. “Wow, you guys haven’t been paying attention to shit since you’ve been training!”

“Watch the language, Augur,” Inkblot said firmly. “But go ahead and tell us.”

“Pff, I don’t have to tell you shit, chalkbrain!”

“Excuse me?”

“Hey!” Bo cut in insistently. “Augur, we really need to know for the good of our team, as soon as possible, so please cut it out and spill the damn beans already!”

“Not exactly a polite request there, either, hmmmm…”

“Augur, as their unofficial teammate,” Ira chimed in, neutral in tone, “it is only fair to provide intel when you have it, especially considering how much insider information you’re being allowed in kind. Like this alert, for instance? Kindly do ‘spill the food’ for your comrades.”

“Haha… Alright, since you asked so politely, I will.” A brief, dramatic pause with everyone likely holding their breath. “It’s Délight’s team! They added a tank recently. Changed the old name. Not exactly a secret, considering who it is, now is it?”

Dead silence for a moment.

“Greeeat,” Jack threw out with a sigh, as he physically ran a hand over his face. She was definitely in his top five of people he didn’t want to have to deal with. She was already on missions. Couldn’t they just raise her up already? But then again, he was doing missions, too.

“Well, this sucks ass,” Highfive said, nearly a growl. “Hiccup and Chime. Frag me! I hate fighting them.”

An annoyed Bo began, “Who the hell is the boy toy she-”

Augur was talking right over her excitedly. “It’s not even the worst of it! The tank is a rising superstar that she’s getting freaky-teaky with!” Another dramatic pause, waiting for reactions.

“Augur, I swear, if you don’t-”

“The one called Defiant!” His voice was an excited whisper, like he’d started a ghost story. “They say he’s got a Conceptual powerset! As a tank! What’s more, it’s broad! That’s what they say. Apparently, he’s completely invulnerable and can barely even be moved! And he’s fast! And his fatigue… it’s like he doesn’t have any! He can’t be worn out!”

Highfive seemed stunned. “What the hell? How could that even exist?” He sounded downright offended and crestfallen. “Conceptual isn’t broad…”

“Exactly, you big dummy,” Bo said dismissively. She followed it up with a loud scoff. “Augur is falling for the advertising, buying the merchandise, and even drinking the damn kool-aid. I’ve heard of him. He’s like a typical strong body, just off different principles. But I heard he has no offense and is slow.”

“That’s because he got his big mutation relatively recently, Bo,” Augur said with annoyed, mocking emphasis. “Not that it’s public knowledge, but even a cursory analysis with a little pattern recognition could determine that. He went from a solid average to hyper-desirable after a relatively known, mediocre Level Four and Five stint on two different teams. What else could get him on her team? All I know is, they went down four-to-two, and he outlasted their opponents, who obviously weren’t pushovers.”

Bo sighed and left a long pause. “This isn’t good. If even a portion of this is true, he could be major trouble. Shutting off an equal-level Conceptual user would’ve been impossible for me days ago. Now? Maybe, but I might pass out. Wouldn’t leave much margin for error.”

“Let’s not consign ourselves to that,” Jack said firmly. “We don’t know enough, and we have the day to dig, right? You have sources to corroborate, Bo?”

“Even you don’t believe me, Jack?” Augur exclaimed. “Wow.”

“Sorry, Augur — I don’t disbelieve you got the intel, but it doesn’t mean we have to believe every part of it. I’d rather you try and help us figure out what to do. If he’s a badass, then we need to find his weaknesses.”

“I’ll dig around,” Bo replied. “Even if I have to call in a favor.”

“You know there’s another problem with this team, right?” Highfive asked. “Délight. She comes to know everything about an enemy team pretty much every time. She already knows most things about Bo and me. Odds are, she’ll find a way to get the rest. That’s just her wheelhouse, man.”

“Shit on a fraggin’ shit sandwich!” Bo cursed emphatically. “She’s gonna get it from that piece of trash, Corroline, isn’t she?! She is. One-hundred-percent. Frag my fraggin’ life!”

“Calm down, Bo,” Jack tried soothingly. “We don’t know that, and I don’t think there’s a damn thing we can do about it even if it were true. It’s simple, eh? We make sure to do something they can’t possibly prepare for. Something no one has seen.” He had zero clue what that would be, but that hardly mattered. He just needed them to cool it down a notch.

“Yeah…”

Augur exclaimed with a gleeful sound. “Ohhh, this is gonna be fun!”

“Also,” Inkblot added, “Augur is under lock-and-key or escort until this is over. No exceptions.”

“What?! No! What the-”

“That girl will have you singing like a canary in a heartbeat, you little narc. You can’t help yourself, and we can’t have that.”

“Pfff, that's- but I-”

“I agree,” Jack replied.

“Guys?! I won’t-”

“I also agree,” Highfive said.

“I agree with gusto,” Bo said.

Augur seemed stunned to silence briefly. “Ira?” he squeaked hopefully.

Ira made the mental equivalent of clearing her throat. “I’m afraid I must bow to the consensus, Augur.”

Augur was all petulance. “S’not fair…”

“Actually,” Jack added, “it’s kind of questionable that the majority of us can deal with her, right?”

“We’ll have to,” Highfive replied with a grunt. “Mandatory dinner. Guaranteed access, even discounting that we gotta go from A to B in many cases. But I’m confident I can say she isn’t going to crack someone that hates her guts like me and Bo.”

Ah. So that’s what the dinner is about. Pfft.

“I might not be immune,” Inkblot said darkly, “but I don’t narc on nobody.”

“I see,” Jack said in some amusement. “Well, she won’t get me, either. I guess we are all potential escorts then.”

“I request Ira!” Augur shouted quickly.

“I’ll be happy to assist as needed,” Ira replied a touch wryly. “However, I can’t push it lest it be seen as favoritism. We’d need good cover. I definitely can’t go to the dinner.”

We’ll figure it out, don’t worry,” Jack offered assuringly. “So, question: does this resistance against her we have happen to extend to the battle time stuff she does?”

“Hell no,” Highfive replied. “She likely won’t bother with all-out charms. She goes for distraction and hypnotism. Stunning people, capturing them in fascination. And, ah… more sinister shit, if she can get away with it. She wears you down over time.”

“She’s like a debuffer most of the time,” Bo added. “With a side of buffer because she can intangibly raise the spirits of her allies, or even charm them to get them through pain and injury. And she’s also the sort of debuffer that can eventually totally debilitate you after first softening you up.”

“So she’s perfectly suited to work with someone supposedly invulnerable,” Jack said disfavorably. “Who doesn’t tire, especially.”

“Exactly!” Highfive exclaimed with an emphatic sigh. “Ugh. This sucks. And we’re on defense. This is a nightmare.”

“Well, so far, it sounds challenging, I admit. Why this for our second match?”

“We punched way, way up, I think. Here’s an acid test trying to knock us back down.”

“On the plus side,” Bo said, “if we win, we’d be like superstars, and make Miss Sleeparound’s team be exposed as pyrite.”

“Sleeparound?” Ira asked quizzically.

Highfive spoke over her, “Wait, what? What the hell does that mean? Pyrite?”

Bo replied, “Fool’s gold. In other words, revealed as less than golden.”

“Pff, they’re already not that. Délight is rotten to the core, and that power couple she totes around aren’t much better. Chime used to be alright. I think the incident kinda warped him, you know? Yeah, he just goes along with whatever Délight says now.”

“Hardly surprising, considering what she does and is,” Jack said. “What about Hiccup?”

“Pushy mean-girl bully behind a smile, pretty much. If anything, Délight holds her in check. Chime used to, somewhat, but not anymore. But, hell, my info might be outdated. Maybe they’ve all matured?”

“Nope,” Bo replied. “Not enough, anyway.”

“Well, I’m dying to know their powers, at this point,” Jack said.

“Hiccup’s tool name leaked a long time ago. ‘Instant Repositioner.’ She’s a Metamorphic Stalker that can suddenly displace herself a small distance, for a fraction of a second. In that time, she can move her body into a different position. Doesn’t have typical Stalker stealth; instead, she gets in melee and devastates with setups, flanks, and so on. Post-displacement hits are nasty. Really deadly when your attention divides. You have to stay focused on her, or you’re toast. Not very tough, but like a poor man’s Acrobat defensively. And, yeah, she’s been training her entire life obsessively in martial arts. One good thing? She usually uses reinforced metal gauntlets and boots. I doubt her replacement material will be as good, especially with how a tiny thing different at that level of specialized training is massive.”

Jack frowned as he pondered her powers. “No ranged potential?”

“Nope.”

“Another with some synergy with the tank, if they’re both engaging.”

“Yep. Can’t let them gang up on Highfive, for instance. You can bet your ass she can hurt him if she gets a good angle. And getting angles is essentially her specialty.”

“Alright, what about Chime? Is that clever or self-explanatory?”

“The latter — he’s not creative enough for a clever name. He’s a sound-based blaster that has to use percussion instruments.”

Jack laughed. “What, like cowbells and triangles?”

“Yep. Literally, yes. He loves cowbells.”

“You won’t be laughing when he hits you with that shit,” Highfive said. “We’re almost guaranteed to go deaf, by the way. It’ll heal, but it sucks. And that’s secondary, because the sound is way versatile, creating big shockwaves, stabbing force, or even targeted super-vibration. The last bit takes constant effort, though.”

“I see,” Jack said, still fighting off a grin at the image of someone ‘murdering’ him with a cowbell. “Wait… do I debuff him automatically by existing, too? No metal instruments?”

“To a degree,” Bo said slowly, leaving a pause as she considered it. “Mmn, yeah, I think he trains with a lot of wood items — and fiberglass, ceramic type shit, whatever — for just about everything. He’s trained up for something like this.”

“Alright. So… it sounds like a strong team. Tank, support debuffer, versatile ranged damage dealer, survivable melee damage dealer. Seems like dealer’s choice about who to take out first, with Chime the most desirable priority, and then Délight, before her shit starts getting worse.”

“Bingo. Yes. Hiccup is way more manageable — in theory. By the way, we’ll want as heavy a tint on our faceplates as possible without being unable to see. Thankfully, it’ll be super bright for the map type. But it helps a little bit against Délight.”

“Does going completely blind help against her, by the way?”

“You can’t stop it, bro,” Highfive answered. “Even without eyes, she’s told me she can do shit. It helps to block her off as much as possible, though. Just like armor protects but doesn’t make you immune, you know?”

“Without eyes? Seriously?”

“Eyes just feed information to the brain, after all,” Bo replied. “Her source domain type is Alchemical, which means, by default, it's transmutational. If you were blind, she’d give you her false brand of light all the same, dipping you into and doping you with her alchemy. Her shit just works better when you’re already giving her your eyes.”

“Holy shitsticks, I’m spooked about her all over again. Anyway, I can handle a bit more tint than the rest of us. In fact, I’d better, or that desert sun will dish out some pain.”

“Just take note that Chime likes to target fragile shit like faceplates, if he can’t wipe you out otherwise. That opens you to head shots as well as weakens you against Délight.”

“Great. Noted.”

“I suppose I’ll have to suffer my plastic eyewear,” Inkblot said with a hint of a sigh.

Jack got up and stretched, making his way to the kitchen for a drink. Despite all the negative things they had to face with the team, at least they had a lot of intel. In contrast, there probably wasn’t a lot on Jack or Inkblot, and Bo had changed a lot with her new mutational ability. Sadly, everyone was expecting that to get leaked to Délight.

“Guys, I know a trick,” Augur said conspiratorily in the gestalt. “Well, actually, it’s just a theory, but it's worth a shot.”

“What are you on about?” Bo asked. “Spit it out.”

“Soooo, Délight makes use of colors as part of the enhancement vision gives her. She normally expects eye feed to be giving the expected full spectrum to both eyes.”

“How do you know this?”

“Actually, that’s true,” Highfive said. “I remember her playing with that for mood effects. And she’d make it super warm and red when we- er, well…ahem.”

Bo scoffed loudly in disgust, as Jack and Augur burst out in laughter.

“Anyway,” Augur continued, “you could filter out one core color, maybe red or blue, to diminish her for at least a little bit, for a little while. Even better? I think? Filter one eye red, one eye blue. It’ll frag up the balance she depends on, making for more easily-ignored junk information and such. I think she’ll also have trouble figuring out what you’re doing, thinking you did one or the other first. So she’ll overcompensate and fail again at least once more. Yeah… I think this will throw her off. I’m already forming figurines of everyone to stage a mock battle, and I’m certain I can verify this, at the least.”

There was a long pause from everyone.

“Damn, Augur,” Highfive said finally, humor-tinted amazement in his voice. “That’s some killer work! Hell yeah, we’ll do that!”

“Really?!” Augur exclaimed, then immediately cleared his throat. “I mean, really, yes. It’s gonna do great work and make a huge difference.”

“What about no color at all?” Jack asked.

“Eh. She’d figure it out quickly and compensate. Hmm. Not a bad play for after she figures out the other thing. I wonder if a programmable lens could be arranged?”

“Yes,” Inkblot said. “The operational faceplate can do that if modified with the right optional firmware, with whatever filters you want. My eyewear is more reduced to avoid surface area, but I’ve already looked into these sorts of things.”

“Rad!”

“I’m still curious how you figured this out,” Bo said suspiciously. “Délight must’ve been keeping this under wraps in more recent history.”

“Well, for one thing, it’s kinda obvious. Mood and color have links? Light and color can hypnotize? Aside from that, I- er, well. I’d rather not say…”

“Oh, you’re not teasing and then failing to spill, brat. Out with it. Or do I need to come over there and bully it out of you?”

There was a slight pause. “Yes. I think you need to come to my room and bully me, actually.”

Everyone aside from Bo laughed at that — even Inkblot huffed in amusement.

Bo groaned. “I forgot the age you were at — look, just spill already! No judgment, no persecution. Right, guys? Say it: no judgment.”

After amused protestations of ‘no judgment,’ Augur finally sighed and said, “Fiiine. Délight is a Linewalker. She has a room in the hall, you know? She tends to leave it open because… well, that’s just how she is, I guess. Anyway, I just happened to hear a few things, by chance, and put disparate pieces together to form a strong hypothesis. I then obtained various corroborating evidences, some from verified, revisited past observations, that made it effectively ironclad. There. Happy?”

There was a stunned silence initially, and then everyone spoke into the gestalt at once.

Jack coughed a laugh. “Eavesdropping again, Augur? Wow.”

“Augur!” Ira admonished sharply, scandalized. “You’re not supposed to do that!”

“Did you do this after your ban?” Inkblot asked with a note of barely restrained consternation.

Bo was mildly disgusted. “You are such a little creepoid.”

Highfive was the most amused. “You’re gonna be even more of a handful in a few years.”

“Hey!” Augur shouted over everyone, sounding deeply offended. “Hey, no! No! You said no judgment or persecution, guys! You promised! Stop it!”

“We did,” Inkblot admitted grudgingly. “Mmph.”

Bo snorted. “Yeah, well, I’m doing my best, brat. Try making it easier in the future. Anyway, your explanation makes sense. You might be a creepoid, but you’re useful. Our little, spying, useful creepoid.”

“Gee, thanks,” Augur replied sarcastically. “Bye now, you bunch of liars! I need to focus on this mock battle, not that any of you deserve the results…”

“Alright, everyone,” Jack concluded as he began getting his own breakfast ready, “for now, we go our separate ways while some people dig for info and so forth. If you have an angle, now’s the time to seek it. Let’s make sure to get our faceplates and such ready for this modification, though. We’ll do some training later on. Sounds good?”

Agreements resounded, and the gestalt was soon dropped, as ‘Operation: Infogathering & Social Engineering’ began.
