"You know, I've been meaning to ask, but why is there music?" Ning asked.

And yet, there was music.

More importantly, it wasn't bad.

Maybe this really is boss-battle music, Ning thought as his legs finally gave out beneath him. He dropped onto the stone floor with a tired groan. The day had not been kind to him.

"Oh. Right."

"I should double-tap him and make sure he stays dead."

His fire ignite talisman.

The guy had fumbled terribly.

It had hurt him on a spiritual level.

Seal of Ten Thousand Melodies?

There really was a seal for everything.

A seal could perform functions comparable to formations, yet could be prepared and activated with the convenience of a talisman. It was the whole damn package.

Wait.

Was the seal actually adjusting the music to match the mood?

He wanted one so bad.

A supernatural talent.

The problem had never been memorizing the scripture. Ning had committed the words to memory within a day. Even the spiritual circulation wasn't particularly difficult; with Know Thyself, he could clearly feel the intended route, how qi gathered around the palms, passed through the heart, and finally converged near the forehead.

Almost.

Every attempt failed at the final step because the scripture demanded something that couldn't be achieved through circulation alone: the Ferrying Mind. Compassionate without becoming attached to the outcome of that compassion, present without being consumed by another's suffering, and self-aware enough to touch another soul without losing sight of one's own.

Ning drew a slow breath and returned to the question he had been considering ever since receiving the inheritance.

What did it actually mean to ferry a soul?

It wasn't about destroying one. If that were the purpose, it might as well have been called the Soul Slaying Scripture. Yet neither was it simply about saving one. Ferrying implied something different. You didn't destroy what entered your boat, nor could you decide whether it deserved to reach the opposite shore. You simply carried it from one place to another.

Until now, Ning had approached the Soul Ferrying Scripture like every other technique he had learned. Understand the qi route, identify the trigger, study the mechanism, visualize the intended result, then repeat until comprehension followed.

That worked for spells and martial arts, but a soul wasn't something so easily reduced to structure.

A soul had weight.

Regret made it sink, obsession made it cling, fear made it scatter, and desire made it sink its teeth into the world and refuse to let go. To touch another soul meant allowing yourself to feel some part of that weight.

If Ning tried to drag it along by force, he would merely become another chain holding it down. If he rejected its emotions entirely, he would never truly touch it. Yet if he allowed himself to drown in them, he would lose the very stability needed to guide it.

That was the Ferrying Mind.

It wasn't cold detachment, blind compassion, or some grand desire to save every suffering soul beneath heaven. It was simply the ability to come close enough to bear another's weight while remaining steady enough not to be buried beneath it; to let another person's fear, hatred, regret, and pain enter his awareness without ever mistaking those feelings for his own.

A clear heart could stand unmoved upon the riverbank.

But a ferryman had to enter the river.

He had to feel its current, carry another through it, and still remember which way led back to shore.

Ning's breathing gradually settled as the final piece clicked into place.

"So that's what I was missing."

Xiao Fan's struggle pressed against his awareness.

Lei Shen's hatred followed after it, vast, bitter, rotten with decades of unwillingness.

His own fear rose alongside them.

This time, Ning did not retreat. Instead, he tried to understand them, to feel them, and let himself become the anchor.

The moment Ning understood that, the Soul Ferrying Scripture finally responded.

The qi that had always faltered at the final step flowed onward naturally, and Ning immediately understood why every previous attempt had failed. He had learned the words, memorized the circulation, and analyzed the technique from every angle, but he had always approached the Ferrying Mind as another problem to solve.

He had never actually tried to ferry a soul.

Now, with Xiao Fan's soul being invaded before his eyes, the distinction was no longer theoretical. Ning wasn't trying to destroy Lei Shen, nor was he trying to forcibly drag Xiao Fan to safety. He simply wanted to reach Xiao Fan, understand the struggle taking place within his soul, and give him the guidance needed to find his own way back, all while ensuring he himself wasn't dragged into it.

For the first time, his state of mind matched the scripture.

I may have discovered another use for the protagonist halo. Ning thought.

Learning a technique in the middle of a crisis, precisely when that technique was needed most, with comprehension suddenly becoming clear enough to break through a bottleneck that had troubled him for days? Ning had certainly never experienced anything so conveniently timed before.

Yet the first time it happened was when the Son of Luck was in mortal danger.

Suspicious.

Still, Ning wasn't about to complain about convenient enlightenment when it was finally happening in his favor.

He had work to do.

Ning looked down at his hands and immediately noticed the first problem. "Senior, how much spiritual energy does Soul Ferrying normally consume?"

The Tower Spirit hesitated. "For the soul of an ordinary Qi Condensation cultivator, not much. Lei Shen is different. He was once at the Purple Mansion Realm, and his remnant soul has been refined through the Blood Infant for years. Even in this state, ferrying him would require considerably more spiritual energy than you currently possess."

"Right." Ning nodded. "Because I have basically none."

The Tower Spirit opened its mouth, only to fall awkwardly silent.

"Do you have a seal for that?"

"There are seals capable of gathering and refining spiritual energy, but normally the cultivator still needs to process the gathered qi themselves. I can't simply pour someone else's spiritual energy directly into your meridians."

Ning stared at it for a moment before his gaze drifted toward the enormous ritual formation beneath them. The Tower Spirit followed his eyes, and its expression slowly changed.

Although the ritual had been destroyed, blood-red light still pulsed weakly through its channels. Lei Shen had spent years preparing it to steal power from the entire sect, and much of the spiritual energy it had gathered had yet to completely disperse.

"Well..." The Tower Spirit floated toward the center of the formation and rolled up its tiny sleeves. "I suppose I can't remain useless until the very end."

"Your words, not mine."

"Shut up."

The Tower Spirit pressed both palms against the ritual. Golden runes immediately spread from its hands, crawling through the blood-red formation and forcibly reversing what remained of its circulation. "This ritual was built upon seals in the first place, so I can redirect some of its remaining spiritual energy toward you. The problem is that your body won't survive taking it directly."

Ning's face immediately scrunched up.

Don't tell me that, after everything I've done, I'm still somehow going to end up sacrificed for the protagonist.

"Which is why I'll use myself as the final seal. I'll filter the spiritual energy before sending it into you and limit the flow to the absolute maximum your meridians can withstand." The Tower Spirit continued after the pause.

At this point, Ning didn't have spare strength to complain about this sacrifice play bait.

"Fair enough. Do it."

The first surge struck him before he could properly brace himself.

Ning's entire body seized as spiritual energy flooded through his meridians, and a strangled curse escaped his throat. It felt as though someone had poured a raging river through channels that had already been cracked, repaired, and then cracked again for good measure. His meridians burned, his muscles spasmed, and every recently healed injury seemed to remember at once that it had been injured.

The Tower Spirit grimaced as its translucent body flickered under the strain. "I'm giving you as much as your body can bear!"

"Then my body and I have very different definitions of bear!"

Pain or not, Ning forced the Soul Ferrying Scripture into circulation.

Warmth gathered within his palms. A faint golden mark appeared at the center of his forehead, shaped like a small diamond crossed by a single thin line, while pale ripples spread around it like moonlight reflected upon dark water. Ning pressed his hands against Xiao Fan and began the scripture.

[Heaven bears the breath; earth receives the bone.]

Golden threads passed through Xiao Fan's flesh and sank into his spiritual sea. For an instant, Ning saw only darkness, but then that darkness opened before him and revealed the struggle raging within.

Xiao Fan stood at the center of his spiritual consciousness, his body formed from golden soul-light. Opposite him was Lei Shen, no longer wearing Lei Dong's ruined flesh but appearing as a tall man with long purple hair and thunder robes, his eyes burning with centuries of accumulated resentment.

The two were already fighting.

"You think this body belongs to you merely because you were born in it?" Lei Shen snarled as purple thunder erupted from his fist. "Once your soul is erased, everything you possess will become mine!"

Xiao Fan crossed his arms and took the blow, golden light exploding around him as he slid backward. "My body, my soul, my fate. You seem awfully fond of claiming things that don't belong to you."

Outside, Ning continued the circulation despite the spiritual energy burning through his damaged meridians.

[Lost souls, see the river; bound souls, loosen the chain.]

As the golden threads sank deeper, Ning finally came into contact with Lei Shen's soul.

The hatred almost overwhelmed him.

It was far more than simple anger. Ning felt centuries of confinement, humiliation, ambition, vengeance, and memories replayed so many times that grievance had become indistinguishable from identity. Lei Shen had fed that hatred for so long that even he might no longer have remembered what he had originally wanted before revenge consumed everything else.

Ning nearly recoiled, but he caught himself.

This was what it meant to ferry a soul. He couldn't remain safely outside and pull Lei Shen away like an object. He had to touch enough of that burden to understand what held the soul here, while keeping his own mind clear enough that Lei Shen's hatred never became his.

Ning steadied his breathing and continued.

[The living return to self; the dead return to dust.]

Golden light appeared within Xiao Fan's spiritual sea, causing Lei Shen to look upward in alarm.

"What is this?"

The Tower Spirit understood immediately. Even while filtering the ritual's remaining energy through its own body, it extended a strand of golden light toward Xiao Fan's forehead. "Xiao Fan! Ning is trying to ferry Lei Shen from outside! Keep weakening his hold on your soul. The less stable his connection becomes, the easier it will be for Ning to separate him!"

Understanding flashed through Xiao Fan's golden eyes. "So that's what he's doing."

Lei Shen's face twisted. "You dare-"

Xiao Fan's fist smashed into his face before he could finish.

Outside, Ning gritted his teeth and continued. The Tower Spirit was supplying spiritual energy as quickly as his body could endure it, but even purified qi felt like fire passing through his exhausted meridians. Blood slowly began leaking from the corner of his mouth, while the Tower Spirit itself had grown so faint that Ning could almost see through it.

Neither stopped.

[Clear heart becomes the bank; ferrying mind becomes the lamp.]

The golden threads within Xiao Fan's spiritual sea thickened into luminous chains. Wherever they touched Lei Shen, purple thunder peeled from his soul in ragged strands, revealing the battered remnant hidden beneath.

For the first time, genuine fear entered Lei Shen's eyes.

"No! I have not taken my revenge! I have not reclaimed what was stolen from me! I endured all those years for this! I will not disappear here!"

His spiritual sea erupted.

Lei Shen abandoned his fight with Xiao Fan and seized the golden chains with both hands. Purple-black lightning exploded outward as his remaining consciousness compressed upon itself, every scrap of hatred and unwillingness becoming fuel for one final struggle.

Outside, Ning's body jerked violently, and the golden mark on his forehead flickered.

"He's resisting!" the Tower Spirit shouted.

"I noticed!"

Ning forced more qi into the scripture. The ritual beneath them was already growing dark as the Tower Spirit drained the last remnants of usable spiritual energy from it, and the little spirit's body flickered dangerously with every breath. There would be no second attempt. If Ning lost his connection now, he didn't have enough strength to establish another.

The final circulation was close. He could feel the scripture approaching completion, with Lei Shen's soul already beginning to loosen from Xiao Fan's.

Then Lei Shen roared.

Purple-black light erupted from his soul with enough force to shake the entire spiritual sea. One golden chain cracked beneath his hands, followed by another, and the phantom river beneath him began to churn violently as Lei Shen forced himself back toward Xiao Fan.

I WILL NOT GO!

The roar shook Ning's consciousness.

Blood spilled from his nose as the backlash struck him. He tried desperately to maintain the scripture, but the connection was collapsing faster than he could stabilize it. Lei Shen had spent years refining the Blood Infant specifically to seize another body; now, with annihilation before him, every remaining fragment of his soul was clinging to Xiao Fan with terrifying desperation.

The Tower Spirit's face went pale. "Ning, he's breaking free!"

The Tower Spirit had almost nothing left to give him; Ning's meridians were already at their limit, and the golden threads connecting him to Xiao Fan were snapping one after another. He tried to force the final circulation through, only for Lei Shen's resistance to drag it apart before it could close.

For the first time since beginning the scripture, Ning knew with absolute certainty that he didn't have enough.

It's going to fail.

Through the collapsing connection, however, Xiao Fan's voice reached him.

“Who said we're finished?”

Ning couldn't help but let out a weak smile; he knew the protagonist was going to work his magic yet again.

Inside the spiritual sea, Xiao Fan had been driven to one knee. His golden soul-light had dimmed, Lei Shen's purple thunder had spread throughout the surrounding darkness, and by every reasonable measure, he should have been just as spent as Ning.

Then the seal on Xiao Fan's right hand began to shine.

The mark that had remained dormant throughout the battle suddenly blazed with golden light, and ancient lines spread from Xiao Fan's palm as he deliberately poured the last of his strength into the Original Seal.

Lei Shen's expression changed for the first time from rage to disbelief.

"What is that?"

Xiao Fan raised his right hand, the Original Seal blazing across his palm.

"You've been trying to take everything from me since the moment you entered my body." His fingers slowly closed into a fist. "Now let me show you what this seal does."

Golden light flooded the spiritual sea.

"Original Seal: True Soul Lock."

The seal erupted.

Golden chains burst from Xiao Fan's palm and wrapped around Lei Shen from within, catching his remnant soul just as Ning's ferrying chains were beginning to collapse. Lei Shen roared and struggled, but Xiao Fan tightened his fist, and the chains pulled taut.

"You wanted my body?" Xiao Fan's golden eyes burned through the storm. "Then you should've made sure I was finished first."

The Original Seal pulled.

Lei Shen's soul tore free.

Outside, Ning suddenly felt all resistance disappear, and the Soul Ferrying Scripture that had been on the verge of collapse surged forward as though a blocked river had finally broken through its dam.

Ning didn't waste the opportunity; he continued the chant.

[By scripture and crossing, beneath heaven's witness and above earth's embrace, the wandering soul is ferried beyond the river of life and death.]

Golden light flooded the chamber as Lei Shen's soul separated from Xiao Fan. The instant the connection broke, Ning's scripture caught the wandering remnant, and the phantom river manifested beneath him.

Lei Shen struggled desperately against the current.

"No! I am Lei Shen! I have endured centuries! I will not-"

The river carried him away.

His voice disappeared into the distance, and the purple thunder surrounding his soul scattered into countless motes of fading light.

The chamber went still.

Ning's hands dropped, the mark on his forehead losing its glow.

The Tower Spirit collapsed out of the air like a dropped doll, catching itself only inches above the floor, as the ritual seal beneath it finally turned fully gold and settled into a quiet, stable glow. Xiao Fan's body stopped convulsing and lay still.

Ning swayed. Then both of them collapsed at the same time.

For a while there was only breathing. Ning lay on his side with one cheek against cold stone and decided, with total sincerity, that he had never appreciated cold stone more in his life.

Xiao Fan coughed. "It's finally-"

Ning's hand shot out and clamped weakly over his mouth, weak in strength, but backed by an enormous amount of hell-the-fuck-not energy.

Xiao Fan blinked.

Ning opened one bloodshot eye. "Don't speak another word."

Xiao Fan could only nod at the sheer pain Ning promised if he did otherwise.

Only then did Ning remove his hand.

The Tower Spirit floated between them, looking as though it had aged several centuries, despite being, in essence, a tower.

"It's over," the Tower Spirit said quietly. "The ritual has been broken. Lei Shen's remnant soul has been ferried away, and the formation has returned to my control."

It looked at the two of them.

"You two saved the sect. Stand proud."

Ning slowly closed his eyes and let out a long breath. "Good."

Silence settled over the chamber.

Then Xiao Fan cleared his throat. "Ning... I think I may have awakened my spiritual sense after all this."

Ning let his own senses stretch out. Xiao Fan was still only at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation, but he'd already unlocked a spiritual sense befitting the seventh.

At this point, why the fuck not?

Ning said, letting his eyes fall shut again, "I don't care anymore."

Xiao Fan wisely fell silent once again, not getting the reaction he wanted.

Then they heard a series of footsteps.

Ning's expression immediately worsened as a woman emerged into the chamber, her robes still remarkably neat despite the chaos outside and her dark hair pinned with the same deliberate care as before. He recognized her at once: Mo Lian, the prisoner who had been calmly playing Go against herself while the entire prison collapsed around her.

She took in the ruined chamber with obvious interest, her gaze passing over the shattered ritual, the damaged battlefield, and finally the two disciples who looked as though surviving another strong breeze might prove challenging.

"My," she murmured. "It seems quite a few interesting things happened while I was locked away."

Her gaze passed over the two battered disciples lying on the ground, and her lips curved into a faint smile.

"Stand."

Ning's body moved before his mind could even register the command. His palms struck the floor, his legs forced themselves beneath him, and despite every exhausted muscle screaming in protest, he rose unsteadily to his feet. Beside him, Xiao Fan did the same, his body trembling as it obeyed a will that wasn't his own.

Pain immediately tore through Ning's body. His legs had already been trembling beneath him, his meridians were a mess, and every instinct was screaming at him to lie down and preferably remain there for the next several days. Instead, Mo Lian's command forced his battered body to remain perfectly upright, muscles straining against injuries that should have dropped him to the floor.

Ning tried to move a finger and received nothing in response.

Know Thyself, meanwhile, was more than happy to inform him exactly how badly his muscles were straining and how the strange influence carried by Mo Lian's voice had settled over his body and consciousness.

You know, for something called Know Thyself, helping me actually control myself once in a while would be appreciated.

Mo Lian approached at an unhurried pace. "You're the little disciple who passed my cell earlier."

Her gaze lingered on Ning, and she smiled as she came closer. "I wondered what became of you. To think you were involved in all of this... and such a cute little thing too."

Ning decided that being called cute by the centuries-old mind-controlling prison lady was probably somewhere near the bottom of today's experiences, which was impressive considering the competition.

At this point, Ning was legit about to cry at the non-stop escalation.

Before Mo Lian could say anything more, however, the scrape of a boot against stone caught her attention.

Xiao Fan had moved. It was barely half a step, but there was no mistaking it. Mo Lian's command had forced him to stand exactly where she wanted him, and somehow, through sheer force of will, Xiao Fan had moved anyway.

Mo Lian's attention immediately shifted away from Ning. "Oh?"

Xiao Fan's jaw clenched as he forced his shoulder to twitch next, golden qi flickering weakly around his body before fading almost immediately.

Mo Lian walked toward him, genuine curiosity replacing the casual amusement in her eyes. "You're actually trying to break through my command."

Xiao Fan's jaw strained before he managed to force out, "T-trying? I moved."

Mo Lian laughed softly. Her command restrained their bodies, not their tongues; after all, there was little amusement in breaking someone if they couldn't speak back.

"Kneel."

The command struck with considerably more force.

Xiao Fan's knees buckled, but instead of falling completely, his body stopped halfway down. His legs shook violently as he fought against the command, and blood began seeping from the injuries his newly restored body had accumulated all over again during the battles that followed. There was barely any spiritual energy left for him to rely upon and no technique supporting him, only sheer will forcing his body against an order it desperately wanted to obey.

Mo Lian watched him struggle, and something unsettling slowly entered her smile.

"Interesting. Your cultivation is mediocre, your spiritual energy is exhausted, and your body has already reached its limit, yet your will still refuses to bend." She stepped closer until she stood directly before him. "I've met cultivators far stronger than you who broke much sooner."

Xiao Fan raised his head with considerable effort. "Should I... congratulate you?"

Her eyebrow rose.

"Perhaps... give you a reward?"

For a moment, Mo Lian simply stared at him before laughing genuinely.

"I like you."

"...The feelings not mutual." Xiao Fan struggled to say those words.

"Even better."

She reached down and took his chin between her fingers, forcing his face upward. "I've shattered Dao hearts that endured centuries. I've watched proud men forget their own names because I asked them to, and I've made cultivators beg me to return thoughts that were once their own."

Her thumb slowly passed across his cheek.

"But you..." Her smile widened. "I think I'll take my time. Let's see how much of that will remain when I'm finished."

Xiao Fan stared back at her. He was exhausted, immobilized, and apparently moments away from having his mind torn apart, yet somehow his mouth remained committed to making the situation worse. "You really... talk too much."

Mo Lian's fingers tightened around his chin. "Perhaps I'll begin with that mouth."

Then she noticed Ning.

The amusement in Mo Lian's eyes faded slightly.

Ning was staring past her.

For the first time since she had entered the chamber, there was unmistakable fear on his face.

Mo Lian's smile returned. "Oh? So you finally understand your situation?"

Ning said nothing.

His eyes remained fixed on something directly behind her.

Mo Lian's smile slowly disappeared.

She turned.

A pale hand clamped over her mouth.

Mo Lian's eyes flew wide as a torrent of water forced its way between her lips before she could utter a single sound. Her cheeks bulged, and her throat convulsed as she instinctively tried to pull away, but the hand held her with terrifying strength, denying her even the breath needed to form another command.

Dark qi erupted wildly from Mo Lian's body, but it accomplished nothing. Water continued pouring down her throat, forcing her to swallow again and again until her stomach began to distend beneath her robes. For the first time, genuine panic shattered the composure that had survived years of imprisonment. She clawed desperately at the hand covering her mouth, but before she could tear herself free, water exploded outward and enclosed her entire body within a perfectly round sphere.

Only then did Ning properly see the girl standing behind her.

White hair drifted gently around a beautiful young face, and Xiao Qing was smiling.

There was nothing remotely warm about that smile.

Her eyes weren't even on Mo Lian's face. They were fixed upon the fingers still resting against Xiao Fan's cheek.

The water sphere began to compress.

Mo Lian thrashed violently inside it, dark qi lashing against the surrounding water, but every impact vanished uselessly into its depths. She tried to open her mouth, perhaps to issue another command, only for water to immediately rush in. Whatever words she intended to speak dissolved into a stream of bubbles as the sphere denied her both voice and breath.

Xiao Qing's smile never changed.

She reached through the surface of the sphere, took Mo Lian's wrist, and carefully removed the woman's hand from Xiao Fan's face.

One finger at a time.

Only after the last finger had been peeled away did Xiao Qing finally look directly at her.

"You touched Brother Xiao Fan."

The sphere contracted.

Mo Lian's body folded under the sudden pressure, her eyes bulging as the water around her compressed hard enough to make the stone beneath them crack.

Ning felt the command holding his body disappear and immediately sagged, barely catching himself before hitting the floor. He looked at Mo Lian struggling helplessly inside the sphere, then at the smiling white-haired girl controlling it.

Xiao Fan himself stared at the girl in disbelief. "...Xiao Qing?"

The horrifying expression vanished from her face almost instantly.

Xiao Qing turned toward him, and the coldness in her eyes melted so completely that Ning briefly wondered whether he'd imagined the entire thing.

"Brother!"

She rushed toward Xiao Fan as though she hadn't just appeared from nowhere, force-fed a dangerous mind cultivator enough water to inflate her stomach, and then started crushing her inside a sphere for touching him.

"...Why are you here?" Xiao Fan asked, surprised.

Xiao Qing threw her arms around him.

"It's because the formation has been restored. We can enter the prison now." She looked at him with teary eyes before smiling brightly. "Brother... you did it."

"I see." Xiao Fan smiled weakly as he slowly closed his eyes.

Not far away, Ning finally understood.

Everything was truly over.

Normally, he would've made some sarcastic remark about how a jade beauty arriving at the last possible moment to save the protagonist was the most cliché ending imaginable. He probably would've even complained about how dramatic her entrance had been.

But Ning no longer had the mental energy for any of that.

So he finally let himself feel what he'd been through: a nightmare walk through a dungeon, a battle to the death, his first kill, his first soul ferrying, his first true boss fight.

Ning's knees gave out beneath him.

Fortunately, someone caught him before he could collapse onto the stone floor.

Ling Xin.

She steadied him with one arm, effortlessly holding him upright.

"You fought and saved the sect," she said lightly. "Don't you think it'd be a little embarrassing to face-plant now, hmm?"

Her tone was teasing, but the concern in her eyes was impossible to hide.

"Master..."

Feeling, at long last, that he was in safe hands, Ning finally let the exhaustion claim him.
