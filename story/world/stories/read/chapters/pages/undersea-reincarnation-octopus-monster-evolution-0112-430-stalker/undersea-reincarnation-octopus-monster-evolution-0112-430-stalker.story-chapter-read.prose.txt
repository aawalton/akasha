As the Avatar violently exploded in a cloud of blood and wild mana, Henry narrowed his eyes. While the toxin-addled blood managed to get a good spread, it did clear out pretty fast. He did not have a way of turning his blood and the toxins within into gas, or having it remain somehow suspended mid-air for maximum coverage. Sure, the detonation alone had been enough to kill a few of the creatures, but there was definitely room for improvement. The explosion packed enough force, but what if he could make it a little more effective at spreading his toxins?

Well, that’s a project for another time, he mused, turning his attention to another phenomenon taking place. Something that told him that quite a few of the creatures below were dead. Henry didn’t get any notifications, but felt the core form somewhere deep in his soul. He knew instinctively he could materialize it, consume it, or turn into his tokens, but for now, he left it alone. It came in waves, some larger than others and he had to admit, the cores were middling at best, considering that many of the creatures seemed to be in the mid B-rank, but every little bit counted. And Henry knew he was about to receive more, considering the scene below.

The pillbug ship hadn’t taken as much damage from his explosion, having been sent rolling a couple of times down the road, but it did spray a decent amount of his blood about, and it did send a few of the Void Lords flying out into the emptiness of space and just like those still attempting to leave the ship, it was as if they were moving through a slurry. Their limbs jerked in halting movements that were growing weaker by the second as Henry’s poison took root. It was effective, especially on creatures weaker than he was.

But not all of the invaders were of the same kind. And not all were dead.

The screech that came out of the ship was loud, and more importantly, it seemed to reach both his physical ears and his mind. All three of them flinched and shared a look. That would be the alarm that Velistraine had warned them about, and so without a word, Henry carried them down. For a moment, everything was quiet, and then with the sound of metal grinding against metal, a large, darker bladed-arm punctured into the ship’s chitinous hull and slid down. Purple blood sprayed out, while the legs of the ship itself twitched in pain as the bladed arm tore through the large ship like a sharp knife through paper.

“[Wait, that thing’s alive?]” Maurice exclaimed, while Velistraine kept her eyes on the towering creature that was tearing its way out of the ship. It didn’t take it long to carve itself a new exit, and all things considered, it wasn’t looking too different from the chaff they’d just eliminated.

But it sure seemed more dangerous.

While the regular B-rank Void Lords had a gray chitin that veered a little blue, this one was nearly black, with not much else to break the color. If Henry wasn’t looking at it from above, it might have been difficult to keep track of it. Not to mention, something about it made his gaze want to slip to the side. A mental compulsion of some sort.

Henry would need to keep an eye out for it, he thought as Velistraine’s feet hit the ground while he cast Identify. By itself it was easy enough to track. But if there were to be a couple dozen? That might get tough.

Not a shiny, poetic name, Henry noted, but he couldn’t dwell on it because as soon as the text appeared in the corner of his vision, the creature in question blurred. There was a shing of metal impacting metal, and Velistraine was sent skidding back a few feet as she held her two swords in front of her. The Stalker reappeared a few paces away, its antennas twitching as it rotated its head from side to side, its compound eyes gleaming in the dark. Then it slowly looked up at Henry while translucent, barely visible wings unfurled out of its back and Henry had a strong guess about what was about to happen.

On some level, it was a little insulting.

The creature blurred once more, this time aiming to dispatch what it assumed to be the weaker prey. But Henry had been ready and for a moment, he considered teleporting out of the way, but that would stretch the fight longer than it needed. Plus, this thing came into his preferred combat range. Why would he let the opportunity be wasted?

With his Octominds accelerating his cognition, Henry stilled, watching and sensing his surroundings carefully, so he noticed it when the mantis-alien appeared out of a fold in space, accelerating toward him with its bladed arm buzzing with an ominous purple that strongly reminded Henry of the dark bubble Maurice had just used, which meant tanking the attack was out.

Instantly, eight Octominds, along with Henry grasped for the creature with Telekinesis, while a couple of dozen Avatars layered panels of Telekinetic walls between them. Unsurprisingly, Henry’s Telekinesis fizzled out when it came in contact with the bladed arm. But the rest of the creature wasn’t as difficult to grasp and unfortunately, Henry’s grip only slowed the Stalker for a beat as before he could slice it in half with his own arm, it teleported out of his grip, appearing behind him before stabbing the back of his head, only for the arm to cut into the Avatar that took Henry’s place.

Henry had begun channeling the switch as soon as the Stalker had slipped out of its cage.

“[It’s fast,]” Maurice sent to Henry, while below, Velistraine kept glancing worriedly between the Stalker and the remaining creatures making their way out of the pillbug. As for the Stalker, it hovered in place behind the Avatar it had just killed. Henry had considered detonating it, but he wanted to keep his eyes on the A-rank, and he didn’t believe a detonation would do enough damage anyway.

It rotated its head, its attention crawling over Henry’s skin like a thousand ants trying to burrow into him—no, into his soul. It was trying to do something to him, but he couldn’t tell what, and he didn’t really want to figure it out. Glancing down to Velistraine, he spoke to her through an Avatar before he Blinked on top of the Stalker and whipped an arm out.

“[I’ll have my Avatars work on the pillbug. Help us with this one and let’s scram,]” he sent while his arm blurred toward the Stalker, but the creature just Blinked out of the way. They had to move as already, one of his spread out Avatars had picked up on another pillbug ship making its way toward them. For something as bulky and tall as this creature was, it was incredibly fast. Maurice’s bubbles couldn’t appear or move fast enough, and Henry’s arms kept missing. He could attempt a Dispelling Pulse and catch it before it teleported out of the way, but he’d rather keep that in reserve. Henry had the strong suspicion that the dispelling strategy would work once and only once. He couldn’t mess it up.

Without wasting any time, Velistraine bent her knees for a fraction of a second, then exploded up toward the creature, a nimbus of red surrounding her form, leaving two of Henry’s Avatars behind her, which slipped into the pillbug ship, a haze of toxin surrounding them as he boosted Toxic Shroud with some of his Predation.

Naturally, the Stalker wasn’t about to just fly there and do nothing, but with his “easy” prey not turning out to be as easy as it seemed, it turned its attention back to the Velistraine who was already winding her right sword back.

Henry dashed forward to capitalize on whatever was about to happen. He wasn’t sure if the Stalker was about to counter Velistraine, or slip away, or turn its attention back to him, but whatever happened, Henry had a Dispelling Pulse channeled and ready. And considering it has been nearly a day since he’d arrived here, Henry had had time to recover a decent portion of his conceptual essences.

Instead of defending against any of them, the Stalker shrieked. It was, Henry guessed, what it might feel like to be a human and get run over by a truck. The shriek, aside from being unbearably loud and painful, hit both Henry’s body and soul like a freight train and immediately, Henry sensed a decent chunk of his nearby Avatars die out. The sound was like hundreds of nails on hundreds of chalkboards, the wailing of all sorts of animals being slaughtered, and the ringing, ear-piercing chittering of a nightmarish plane of insects combined.
