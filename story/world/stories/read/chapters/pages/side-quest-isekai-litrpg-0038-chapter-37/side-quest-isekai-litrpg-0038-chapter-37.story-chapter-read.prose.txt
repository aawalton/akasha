Dejected, Logan slumped against the base of the dragon sage statue. Even though his stamina was full, he was emotionally and mentally drained. He aggressively rubbed the heels of his palms into his eyelids and, not for the first time, shouted at himself in frustration.

“Stupid! Why would you use the egg for light?”

On the ground beside him sat a cold, severed finger. Not his, of course. But during the past two hours as he cataloged every single item in his inventory, he had found the stupid glowing finger of the stupid undead wight of the stupid-stupid-stupid dumb Kalashi brothers.

Granted, the egg had only given off a small glow, and Radiance’s finger hardly emitted even a fraction of that… but so what? If he had just slowed down instead of getting distracted by the brightest, flashiest solution…

Now, the egg had fallen. And no matter what he had tried to think up, he couldn’t figure a way to descend that pit. He had actually tried lowering himself down, but even hanging from the ledge, his toes couldn’t find purchase.

For all he knew, it truly was bottomless. Eldertread might have dampened his fall from the ceiling to the ground here, but he wasn’t about to test the limits with the pit.

The only thing that gave him hope was that every few minutes, when he checked his Quest Board, the quest to save dragonkind was still active. The egg was supposed to be indestructible. And quests were supposed to gray out if you couldn’t complete them. So, there still had to be some hope. Right?

What he really needed was for the sun to come up so he could get his ass out of here and either hatch a plan with the others or else get in touch with Razor. He didn’t like either option.

For one, if he pulled the others into this, he would have to explain how he crossed the Boundary. Telling them about his legendary Aetherlens skill had been one thing. But sharing that he had an extinct, hidden, secondary race and came from a completely different world so the Boundary didn’t apply to him? That just didn’t sound right.

But the other option was getting back to Razor. Even if Alden had secured a wagon ride back to Gnashridge, it would take a full day to get back. Then he’d have to survive Razor’s wrath, which wasn’t a guaranteed thing. An image of a shredded prowler carcass flitted to the front of his mind.

All this assumed the team had even stayed in Farrowstead to wait for him. It was now his second time going missing with them in how many days?

Stupid.

He groaned as he lifted his bowed head and let it slap back down against the statue supporting him. He had to stop the negative self-talk. As long as another swarm of gobles didn’t invade the cave, Logan had all night to come up with a solution once the sun rose and he could safely make his way back to Farrowstead. Whether it was by bringing the others here or by returning to Gnashridge first, he simply couldn’t do it on his own.

The next morning, the moment dawn broke, Logan trudged his way back to town.

With his mind racing, he pushed open the tavern door before he even realized he was back.

“Logan?” It was Cassandra. She lifted a hand to her lips. “Are you okay? You look terrible. Where were you last night?”

“Hm? Oh. Yeah, I’m fine. Uh, hey, I think we all need to talk.”

“The others are upstairs… Why don’t you come up?”

Logan followed her upstairs. He had decided to give them another part-truth by telling about the gobles, but he still was going to hold back on the whole Boundary topic. Rather than spill his secrets to the team, he would suck it up, tell Razor what happened, and suffer her anger.

But he still needed to let the others know that the trip back to Gnashridge could be dangerous. If the gobles truly had some telepathic communication like he suspected, then what happened last night was no secret to the main goble horde. And for all Logan knew, Gnashridge might be burning this very moment.

No. Gnashridge is fine. His quest board had quests linked to the town, too, and those weren’t grayed out either.

After the others all gathered into a single, cramped room, Logan told the story as truthfully as he could, starting with the bard’s request outside the Farrowstead smithy, his walk through the forest, and his encounter with the gobles—both inside and outside the cave. He did not mention how far into the cave he had ventured, letting them think he had just stumbled on a hidden goble encampment.

He waited with bated breath for their reactions.

Alden folded his arms. “Really? Gobles? And not just anywhere, but in the Cave of Cursed Music? Logan, that place is a joke. That’s just a place for silly children to make up stories and dare each other to touch the dwarf statue’s tiny—”

“Hey, laddy, careful what you say there.” Bromlin’s stern glower at Alden broke a mere second later as he chortled.

“Yes,” said Logan, trying to stay patient. He had just told them a pretty outlandish story, after all.

“Very well. So you’re telling me that you fought off all those gobles just to fetch musical instruments for some bards so you could… get a medallion?”

Logan sighed. “From the drummer and fiddler, yes. I don’t know what the lute-player is going to give me. But can we get back on topic? I’m worried that the goble group I ran into warned the rest of their people. Is it true they can communicate with each other from afar?”

Although Alden still scrutinized Logan, Cassandra nodded to Logan, but she also raised a settling hand to the elven mage. Logan wasn’t entirely sure why. “Yes,” she said. “They call it a hive mind.”

“As does everyone else in the world,” Alden added.

Ahh, that explained it. They—or at least Alden—didn’t believe him. And now Logan sounded suspicious for questioning something rather common sense.

“I get it. You don’t believe me. But what reason would I have to lie about this? I’m just saying, we have to stay alert on our way back to Gnashridge Heights.”

“Perhaps,” said Alden. He peered at Logan’s gear bag. “Or, perhaps you foolishly listened to a bard’s tale about all the alleged treasures in the Cave of Cursed Music and then even though everybody knows the stories aren’t real, you somehow walked in there with a fancy legendary skill and found out it was true after all, and rather than inviting your team to join you on a once-in-a-lifetime adventure to explore something so incredible that, I don’t know, maybe someone like me could even earn an affinity while exploring, you, you—” he finally paused to take a deep breath “— you probably just want to keep it all to yourself!”

Logan stared, dumbstruck. It wasn’t that Alden didn’t believe Logan’s story because it sounded so dangerous. It was just because the elf had missed out on opportunity to get an affinity.

“That’s ten bronze coins, Brummy. Told you he was jealous.”

“Bah, fine.”

Cassandra laid a hand on Alden’s shoulder. “Alden, even if Logan found some loot, if it was his quest, he earned it all himself.”

“What? N-no,” Logan sputtered. He opened his guild-issued storage. “And actually, yeah, I did find some loot.” He pulled out the assortment of monster cores and set them on the ground. Half a dozen orbs spilled onto the floor. “Honestly, you can have it. In fact, you should use some now if it will help you stay safe. I just wanted to let you all know the danger we might be in, regardless of whether you stay here or come with me back to Gnashridge.”

It wasn’t until after he said it that he remembered he was the only one who could gain skills directly from monster cores. The others would need a dozen, and even then to distill it.

But nobody even focused on that because they were too busy gawking at the trove Logan had just revealed.

Senna’s voice was just above a whisper. “Logan. Really? You. Are. The. Best.” She gasped. “Brummy! Do you have your axe with you?”

“Of course I have me axe, lass! What kind of dwarf do you think I am?”

Logan’s collar was getting itchy. “Sorry guys, but do you think we can discuss this all on the road?”

“Oh, we are going to Gnashridge all right,” said Senna. She was clearly already making plans in her head. “Do you know how much these are worth? Logan, I’m clearing off your tab, and that includes all the interest.”

Alden still hadn’t looked away from the cores, his shining eyes big enough to rival the orbs themselves.

That changed, though, when a tremor shook the room, followed by bloodcurdling shrieks from outside.

Everybody rushed to the room’s one tall window and smashed their faces against its pane.

Well, almost everyone.

“What is it?” asked Bromlin, who was the only one who couldn’t reach and was instead jumping at everybody’s waistline.

“I think,” Logan whispered, “it’s the start of that danger I was talking about.”

Even from their sheltered position indoors, the air itself seemed to contract, like a great inhalation before an earth-shattering scream.

Then came the rumble.

A deep, seismic groan made the worn planks at their feet shudder. Outside, jagged fissures split the land like veins of shadow, tearing gaping wounds into the terrain. Each one stretched at least fifteen feet across, its edges crumbling as if the earth had rotted from the inside out. A pitch-black abyss churned within.

Darkness rose from it in coils, like tendrils of inky energy slithering upward as though seeking something to ensnare. The unfurling mist twisted unnaturally, trailing steadily upward despite the wind. The outline of the shadows buzzed and bounced, almost as if they were caught between two realities.

Senna whistled. “This seems like something a lot worse than just gobles.”

Something about the way the dark energy moved and bled into the world seemed familiar to Logan. It carried the same bouncing sensation he felt when wielding the Void Affinity, like when Unrelenting Hunt flooded his senses, or when Null Pulse pulled against the world to launch him forward.

This was an unbridled breach of Void energy far beyond mere mana, and it was coming from somewhere deep within the earth.

Logan scooped up the bag of cores and tossed them into his storage. “We have to get back to Gnashridge. Now!”

This time, nobody questioned him.

Hurrying out of the inn’s private room and into the open air, they sprinted along the town’s outskirts, weaving through narrow alleys and dodging startled townsfolk who had emerged to witness the growing calamity. The dense Void wall loomed, rising higher by the second. Eventually, it stopped expanding, but the darkness continued to churn in place.

Every time they took a differnt route, though, a wall of the humming energy blocked their path.

After trying seven different routes to get back on the path to Gnashridge Heights, a worried realization struck Logan. “There’s no way back, is there?”

Everybody was too at a loss for words to speak. They were stuck in Farrowstead.

“Maybe,” Cassandra eventually said, “we can at least try to protect the town here?”

The others nodded distractedly, but nobody suggested how. Their wide eyes stayed fixed on the menacing walls of energy.

Logan pressed a fist to his forehead. If they were going to save any place, they wouldn’t be able to do it from here. He just needed some quiet to think.

Meditate!

Everything slowed. His thoughts stretched outward, and the distractions peeled away. New as he was here, the peculiarities of this world were a messy jumble to him, ranging from blights to monsters to hidden chasms.

Instead of trying to force himself to form a connection between all the pieces, he let them all slosh together in his mind and considered them from a removed perspective.

Just when he was about to give up, a few key things struck him as sharing similarities.

There was the abyssal pit in the Cave of Cursed Music, where the egg had fallen down. He didn’t know how deep it went, but it had to have a bottom, and the egg had to be safe, otherwise the quest would have failed by now.

That wasn’t the only massive subterranean place he had, seen though. Beneath Gnashridge Heights’ inn, he had seen that massive pillar of mana that descended into the unfathomable depths. The Boundary had been present there, too.

He recalled that uncanny feeling of being watched, which hadn’t made given the team’s information that nothing and nobody was on the other side of the Boundary. But he had seen the special totems the gobles had used, charged against the Dragon Sage statue’s gemstone eyes, to pass through the Boundary.

Logan’s eyes snapped open.

There was a way others could pass the Boundary, too, just like him. But explaining that meant he would have to share his secret.

To his surprise, Alden was squeezing Cassandra’s and Senna’s shoulders in an attempt at encouragement, even though he looked more frightened than either of them. His eyes fell on Logan, and in that stare Logan saw someone who was prepared to die, even if they hadn’t fulfilled everything they wanted in life.

Alden opened his mouth, closed it, and then just gave Logan a small, resigned nod.

In return, Logan offered a sad smile.

These people were his team. Even with… well, with whatever the heck this looming calamity was, they were still ready to face it with him. Even Alden’s sense of injustice wasn’t deep enough to make him abandon the others.

Getting to Gnashridge wasn’t an option. But maybe, as a team, scaling that pit beyond the Boundary was. As much as he hadn’t wanted to reveal his secrets, he didn’t think he had a choice anymore.

He met each teammate’s eye and cleared his throat.

“You can pass through the Boundary? Like, the Boundary.”

“Yes,” Logan said to answer Senna’s question. “And I think I can get you across too. The gobles all used totems to pass it, and their bodies are still in the Cave of Cursed Music. If I loot them and charge their totems using the same method, you can cross the Boundary just as easily as me.”

“So that’s why you were missing for so long beneath the Gnashridge inn,” said Cassandra.

Logan nodded. He had still glossed over some details, but this time it wasn’t out of secrecy. There just wasn’t enough time to explain it all. Bromlin, for example, kept miscalling Earth Dirt, and Logan had stopped trying to correct him.

To their credit, they didn’t seem to question his revelation as much as they had his initial story about the gobles and the cave. Not even Alden. Perhaps the appearance of the massive void ruptures had given him a bit more credibility.

“Okay, okay,” said Senna, waving her hands. “But before we go barreling into certain death, I think we could all use a little upgrade. And in all our running through the city, I couldn’t help but notice the forge is still standing.” A gleam entered her eye as she looked at Bromlin’s axe, and then Logan’s guild-issued storage pack. “So, can we talk about those gajillion monster cores again?”
