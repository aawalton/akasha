I’m before an entire pantheon of giant gods. It’s just some mind realm they use. I don’t think this is a first in actuality, not with all the past lives, but it's new to me, and boy, oh boy, my compliments on the inspiring awe aura thing they got going — even I’m feeling it, and I know it’s just for the effect!

Ashawein solemnly took the wineskin, though Samantha was actually stalling a bit.

Why the hell am I left with the choice?! Ashawein has to become a deity. But I need a draught to complete the challenges! Son of a-...

Ashawein cleared her throat and glanced at the waiting, sober-faced giant deities gathered around, like living monuments with eyes that all burned into your soul in their own special way. “Excuse me, but, ah, I don’t suppose I can have two?” Her voice became a little more squeaky at the end than she wanted. “Two draughts, that is. I have need of a second.”

The Guardian of Rituals glanced at the Ancient Father — who was frowning disapprovingly — and then shook his head sadly. “This is not possible. Our rules are sacrosanct. To attain it is precious, and not to be squandered. If you do not take this as seriously as you should, perhaps we-”

Ashawein quickly scooped the wineskin into the bowl to fill it with the Nectar and pull it away. The liquid, possessing a ‘heaviness’ to it, did not spill, drip, or wet the surface of the wineskin, remaining altogether still throughout the process.

Immediately after she filled it, the servants rose and turned away, proceeding back down the steps.

Staring at the top of the wineskin in her hand, she felt the eyes of the gods burning into her. The Guardian glowered at her, certainly. Another — a female deity — in hunter's leathers, huffed a laugh and wore a small smile.

The Ancient Father, dead forward to her view, pulled her eyes with his own, and when Ashawein finally met them, she saw thoughtfulness, consideration, and something underneath, like profound weariness. He asked softly, “Will you drink it, oh brash Alamadyrian?”

Samantha-as-Ashawein opened her mouth, ready to spew some bullshit answer, but she swallowed it and sighed. Ashawein wouldn’t lie to the gods, and odds are they’ll see through them anyway. Finally, she replied, “I don’t know. The completion of a very important quest requires this. I came back to these lands to seek it out, already — not to drink it, but to carry it back to the Dragon Queen.”

Gasps, dark mutterings, and scoffing noises resounded around the thrones.

The Guardian of Rituals hissed and leaned forward, scowling. “Heresy! Do you think we will allow-”

“Why would you consider that?” the Ancient Father queried loudly, drowning out the other, though his tone was more confused than angry. “You hold immortality and unthinkable power in your hands, and you’d give it away? To what end?”

“To ally the world,” Ashawein replied, her eyes sweeping left and right over the seated deities. Little do they know it, but they’re all doomed to pass away, and soon. “For the same reason that I think you wish to raise up more deities: the Pale Prince is a menace that will take everything the world has to defeat. The Indigowood is vast and composed of numerous, varied entities of power, all consolidated under the queen. More will be needed, but it’s a start. So, I ask you all, do you wish for an ally to Yarune, or not?”

A long, uncomfortable silence stretched. The Ancient Father sighed wearily, and his eyes shifted over to an empty throne.

Hmm. Who are the missing, and why? Oh, shit. Are they dead already?

Samantha scanned over the gods present — though she didn’t know their names, their appearances said everything about them already. By the process of elimination, she could probably determine the archetypes missing…

Ah. Their oracle was struck first. Typical, and now things are making even more sense. These gods must have some sense of how connected I am to Fate.

Ashawein continued, “I know what you lack. Trust that I am what I am, regardless of this or that title, Ancient Father. Trust that Fate will play out precisely as it needs to.”

Even if this world is long ago doomed, and you utterly consigned to oblivion even before it died.

The Ancient Father’s head snapped back to hers as he gazed at her in shock. “How could you know-” He cut himself off, took a deep breath, and finally nodded slowly and thoughtfully at her. “Very well, Ashawein of the Load Bearing Clouds. You suggest a painful sacrifice to our wishes and your own potential, but even the gods must bow to the sacred choices of mortals. Yours is motivated by nobility and rooted in humility. Taken into your gut or not, you’ve earned that draught as none have before. Go, now, and pray that your choice is the correct one.”

Ashawein bowed low. “Thank you. I will.”

When Ashawein returned to the Dragon Queen’s palace and presented the wineskin to Othenia, the draconic beauty’s eyes bugged out in disbelief. She reached for it as if to a mirage she was sure would disappear in an instant. “A Blessed Wineskin… the Nectar within, I feel it…”

Before even touching it, she met Ashawein’s eyes in wonder, her eyes wet with some unreadable emotion. “How? How could you possibly be this powerful? Why would you not drink it by now?”

Ashawein beamed at her reaction. “Because then I could not ask for the boon.”

“What is it?” Othenia asked with breathless insistence, shaking her head, searching Ashawein’s face like a puzzle she couldn’t solve. “What could possibly be more important than immortality? Godhood!? Tell me!”

Ashawein blinked at this thoughtfully, taking her time, taking a breath, in a way that had so often frustrated Othenia, and now had her nearly ready to erupt. Finally, eyes looking away, Ashawein said, “I had another answer, initially, but now it-it’s… something else entirely, something rather scary, even as I know it must be done.”

With Othenia hanging on her every word, Ashawein finally met her eyes, and, her face hot, nonetheless smiled and declared boldly through her anxiety and fear, “I’ll ask for her daughter’s hand in marriage.”

Othenia’s reaction was certainly one for the record books. Stunned disbelief disintegrated into some rawer emotion as her eyes went wet, and two tears fell down her cheeks as she blinked them away. “That’s absurd,” she muttered. “Completely absurd.”

“Will you deny it, then, as would be your right? Tell me now, please.”

Othenia stared at her, mouth opening without words coming out.

Ashawein nodded to herself. “I love you, Othenia. I believe you love me. Will you deny this, either?”

Othenia took a deeper breath, as if she’d answer, but once again, her breath caught, and she kept staring.

Ashawein shrugged, even as she fought her own butterflies, and her beating heart was in her ears. “See? It’s cut and dry in the end. This is why my people are direct. Well, okay, you want a practical explanation… it’s more tangential, but think of the long-term: we both have influence, we can probably bend your mother’s will to the ends we seek-”

Othenia suddenly interrupted her by kissing her, and the rest was utterly forgotten.

After a while, a breathless Othenia took her hand and darted off, tugging and muttering, “Come,” with her eyes focused afar off, her face cast over with a dark shadow. “It’s time you met her.”

Ashawein gasped. “Really?! The queen’s audience? Is it a throne room?”

Othenia scoffed. “Of course not, silly giantess — it’s a lair.”

“Wow!” Ashawein giggled, though mostly this was due to her giddiness after profusely kissing her long-time crush. Then she remembered she was about to ask her mom to marry her, and she quickly sobered up.

She was taken into an iconic dragon’s lair, a massive chamber absolutely piled full of enough gold and jewels to buy an empire.

Othenia let go of her hand and hurried ahead, gradually slowing. With her back still turned, she gestured vaguely. “Put down the wineskin. You need to complete the quest, after all…”

Ashawein did so, as she was otherwise tromping through gold coins, eyes flashing around, looking intently for a dragon. “Is she not here at the moment?”

Othenia didn’t answer and seemed almost pensive, despite her profound and endless pride. Her wings fidgeted, her tail flicked around low. “I have quite a confession to make, Ashawein.”

Squinting her eyes, Ashawein frowned. “Hmm? Now?”

Othenia, back still turned, seemed to bark a hysterical laugh, throwing up her hands. “It should’ve been obvious to me — why you were randomly insensitive to this, when sensitive to all other things! I’ve heard it said you are the blindest to what you are the weakest — true in both our cases. I thought I was so very clever, so devious! In the beginning. And then, like a fool, I abandoned caution when I should’ve stopped following you around at the first inklings. The point of no return, to love as a mortal — it strikes quickly, doesn’t it? Like lightning, and when it hits you, you are well and truly done.”

By now, Ashawein had her hands on her hips. “Othenia, tell me what you mean, please. Are you already married, perhaps? It isn’t ideal, but-”

Riotous, bitter laughter cut her off, as Othenia finally turned around. “Fool! You’re a fool!” Her features began to shift, becoming more draconic. “And so am I. Here, giantess, let me show you the truth.”

More and more draconic she became as her body extended, her neck grew out, as her tail thickened, and lengthened. Her whole body grew… and grew… and grew.

A massive black dragon buffeted its wings once at Ashawein, almost knocking her over, and then hopped up onto the top of the massive treasure pile, turning herself and ‘perching’ there, as she looked down at Ashawein. Finally, a grand voice erupted, “I am the Dragon Queen, Ashawein, and I have no daughter, though the identity is a tool of mine, as is the name. It was an old, old name I once used for myself, one precious few would recall. But regardless…” — the dragon puffed out her chest and pointed at herself with a claw, proud and majestic — “It was me all along, fool!”
