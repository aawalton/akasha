A deafening silence hung over the room in the wake of Tae-seok’s proclamation. In synchronized slow motion, the mouths of both Lieutenant Shin and Mu-cheon fell open as they turned to stare at Chae-ha, unable to believe what they had just heard. The young girl looked away from their combined gaze a little too quickly, letting out a disappointed puff of held breath as she did so.

Mu-cheon whirled around to face Elder Eom.

“What insanity is this, Elder? She’s been cooped up in here for almost two weeks!”

“Calm yourself, son. It happened when we were still on the battlefield. Lieutenant, do you remember when she helped you send that ‘urgent message’ to Commander Mok, telling him to flee?”

“Of course,” Shin replied, completely bewildered. “We were retreating. He couldn’t continue with his part of the plan…”

“Yeah, that’s right. But the Meng-shan didn’t even know he was there. He didn’t need to run right away; in fact, it probably would have been safer for him to stay put, given what happened afterwards. Isn’t that right, girl?”

“I’m sure I don’t know what you mean,” Chae-ha muttered, still looking away.

“I think you know exactly what I mean. You sent Commander Mok a message urging him to flee ‘immediately,’ and then led an army of Meng-shan down into the same hunting trails he used to sneak behind their lines. You even convinced the naïve Lieutenant over there not to warn him you were doing that. You deliberately engineered a collision between the two forces.”

“She said…he would be mad if he thought…”

Lieutenant Shin’s voice trailed off as the man struggled to process the reality of what had happened. After a moment, something within him snapped. He slammed his fist into the same side table he’d struck earlier, and this time one of the legs buckled, causing the entire thing to come crashing down noisily onto the wooden floor.

“…it was necessary,” Chae-ha finally acknowledged, unable to find a way to avoid admitting the truth. “Lieutenant Shin, the plan I presented earlier is the only one that will work. But you and I both know that Commander Mok would never have approved it. He would have insisted on throwing lives away trying to reclaim the lost mine. We had to choose between losing him and losing the war. I…chose the former.”

Tae-seok crossed his arms. “And you figured you’d replace him with me. Not because you think I’m the best suited for the job, but because I’m beholden to the Young Master, and he listens to you. So by putting me in charge…you thought you’d be putting yourself in charge. Isn’t that right, young lady?”

“How could I not have seen this?” Lieutenant Shin almost seethed, rage, confusion, and despair all warring within his eyes.

“You would have noticed if you’d stopped to think about it,” Chae-ha whispered. “It’s not that you’re stupid, Lieutenant. Quite the contrary. It’s just that most people become so focused on the moment-to-moment difficulties they face, they never stop to step back and look at the big picture.”

The young girl glanced over at Tae-seok. “In retrospect, I was the one who was foolish. I should have realized that was the exact skillset a retired general would have. I’m sorry I tried to fool you, Elder. You deserved better.”

Mu-cheon’s mind began to race. If word of this got out, Chae-ha could be executed for treason. The young man’s mind still hadn’t finished processing everything he’d heard, but the part of him that had sworn to protect her moved almost instinctively. He placed himself in front of the only door to the room and blocked it with his body.

“Chae-ha saved the lives of nearly four hundred warriors,” he reminded the room. “And if she wasn’t there, we all would have died when that second column arrived. Including Mok.”

“You think that justifies what she did?” Shin responded, whirling on Mu-cheon.

“Honestly…no, I don’t. But the war effort right now needs two things: a good plan, and a strong leader. If we walk out of this room with neither of those while yelling about mutinies and calling for executions, everything falls apart. And how will we ever beat the Meng-shan after that?”

“So you’re saying I should just…accept what happened? Give up and give in? Stamp her bloody letter, because in the end it’s all for the greater good?!”

“No, son,” Tae-seok replied, putting his hand on the man’s shoulder. “There are some good things about the girl’s plan, yes, but I’m overruling the last bit. The Young Master was right when he said we need a strong leader. But in my mind, that doesn’t mean replacing you – it means elevating you. The young lady will write a letter to the Sect Leader asking for you to be promoted to Vanguard Commander, and I will co-sign it.”

The old man glanced over at Chae-ha, his blind eye cloudy and white but his gaze still piercing. “I refuse to be manipulated into replacing you, boy. But I’ll stand by your side and help as much as I can, as a mentor and an advisor. Together, we can turn this war around.”

The Lieutenant looked down at his hands, still sore from where they had struck the table. His eyes stayed locked on the crumpled, splintered piece of furniture as he tried to consider what to do next. Loyalty to his commander warred with loyalty to his sect in an obvious internal struggle. The room grew quiet as everyone waited for him to finish digesting the offer.

“How do I know she won’t do the same thing to me one day?” he eventually managed, weakly.

Chae-ha paused before responding, aware that everything depended on how she handled this final question. After a few heartbeats, she decided there had been enough deception, and it was time to reply with the simple truth.

“I’ve never once doubted your loyalty, Lieutenant. I know you’re a man who puts the well-being of the sect above his own pride. So, I ask you: should you lose your way one day, and become a threat to the war effort, but refuse to step down…what would you prefer I do?”

Shin stared at her, unable to believe she’d actually asked that. His mouth worked soundlessly for a while as he tried to figure out how to respond. Eventually, his resolve hardened.

“I would rather die than be the reason we lost this war.”

Tae-seok slapped him on the back. “You and me both, son. But don’t worry. In my experience, men like you, who care deeply about their comrades, make great commanders. Besides, we need more of your type to balance out people like her. She’s clever, but that young lady…”

Tae-seok paused, looking at the unassuming, injured Inspector propped up in her medical bed.

“Well, let’s just say it’s a good thing she’s on our side.”

The next day, all of Laiwu was abuzz with rumors of a new construction project, as Lieutenant Shin tried to source the necessary specialists on short notice. Most people assumed the Tai-shan soldiers were telling the truth when they said a new ore vein had been discovered, and they needed men to work on infrastructure for a new mine. Groups of lumberjacks and engineers found themselves being herded out of town by Tai-shan ‘escorts,’ who effectively press-ganged them into service deep within the woods. It was an unpleasant approach, but necessary, as the details of the actual project needed to remain hidden.

While Lieutenant Shin and Elder Eom were out marshalling the resources needed to execute Chae-ha’s plan, Mu-cheon found himself knocking on the door to her room. After entering, the young man pulled up a chair alongside her bed, where she was propped into a sitting position by a large array of small pillows.

“Little sister, something’s been on my mind since yesterday, and…I think we should talk.”

Chae-ha looked away from him, and down at the floor, before eventually replying.

“…okay.”

“You almost died out there. It…scared me, you know? I know you’re worried about standing out from the crowd, but I think it’s time we assigned you a few personal guards. Experienced men, who can get used to working with you as a team, and to your…quirky way of fighting.”

Chae-ha looked up at him in surprise, her eyes wide with disbelief. She resembled a disheveled owl. “That’s what you wanted to talk to me about?”

“Well, yeah. What did you think I was gonna say?”

“I was expecting a lecture. And for you to be…upset. About what happened with Commander Mok.”

Mu-cheon sighed, closing his eyes and wincing as if in pain. “I am. Or…I was. I drank the worst of it out of my system last night. The man was an idiot, but he was doing the best he could for our sect. I’ll…make sure he gets remembered that way.”

The Young Master opened his eyes. “When I woke up this morning, I told myself that I had no more time for worrying about the past. I have enough problems that still need dealing with here and now. And one of those problems is how to make sure my little sister doesn’t get herself killed.”

“The guards are…a good idea. When we were out there on the road, I was only able to get as far as I did because I convinced a few soldiers to come along with me. So, yes, I’ll take you up on that offer. But it won’t be enough to solve the root problem.”

“Well, no solution is perfect. The only way to completely avoid danger is to not be on the battlefield. Honestly, that was also something that I thought about, but…even if I sent you away, I know you’d just find your way back. At least if I keep you with me, I can keep an eye on you. And…you can keep an eye on me.”

The young man shrugged. “Let’s be honest; we both need watching over.”

Chae-ha rewarded his effort with a small smile. “That’s true, senior brother. But even with the guards, the only way I’ll ever be able to stay close to you is if I can take care of myself. In the event that I get targeted, I need to be able to fight back. I can’t afford to be outmaneuvered like that again.”

“So…does that mean you’re going to try and improve your Phantom Steps? To better hide where you’re stepping, in case they target your legs again?”

Chae-ha shook her head, before looking down at her hands. The wound in her side throbbed as she thought about the sequence of events that had led to her getting stabbed.

“No, the problem wasn’t really that they targeted my legs. My panels can already disguise the locations of my feet, at least from certain angles. The problem was that there were several attackers, and they came at me from multiple directions, all at once. What I need against that kind of strategy isn’t better martial arts; it’s the ability to more effectively control space.”

Mu-cheon scratched his head. “I’m not really following. Are you saying you’ve figured out a way to do that?”

“I have,” Chae-ha responded, flipping through her stack of papers. After a moment, she held up a new sheet with several sketches on it. Mu-cheon’s eyes grew wide as he recognized the objects, which were depicted as being carried in quick-release pouches under her robes. He let out a low whistle.

“Well…that’s one way to do it.”

That evening, Mu-cheon found himself walking up and down the industrial district of Laiwu, an area where stinging smoke and hot air seemed to always be swirling together. The combination made every breath distinctly unpleasant. The Young Master was searching for the sign of a particular blacksmith; a man named Master Tak, who had come with a strong recommendation from several of the soldiers. He sighed with relief when he finally spied the man’s shop, thankful for the opportunity to retreat indoors.

His gratitude did not last long. As soon as he opened the door, a blast of even hotter air hit him in the face. The interior of the smithy was a sweltering mess of chaotic clanging, running assistants, and glowing metal bars. A row of blacksmiths, likely apprentices studying under Master Tak, were each running a separate forge. The discordant clanging of hammers against heated iron made it hard to hear what they were shouting at each other.

After a moment, one of the shop assistants approached the Young Master, whose identity as an Inner Disciple was abundantly clear from the silver embroidery on his oxblood robes. The boy’s eyes landed on the handle of Mu-cheon’s Dae-jik-do, which was peeking out over his shoulder.

“My lord, what brings you to our shop? Looking for a new sword?”

“No, I’m here on behalf of a…friend. Is Master Tak around?” Mu-cheon asked, scanning over the boy’s head for the smith.

“I need to order a few hundred caltrops.”
