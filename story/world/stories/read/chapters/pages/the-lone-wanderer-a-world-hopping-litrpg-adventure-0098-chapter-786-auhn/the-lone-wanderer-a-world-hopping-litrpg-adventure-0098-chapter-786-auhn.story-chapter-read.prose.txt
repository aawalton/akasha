“Faster!” Auhn ordered his great grandson.

They opened portal after portal, skipping vast cosmic distances with each jump. There was no air in the vacuum of space to carry their voices, but they could easily release some from their internal worlds, or communicate in a hundred other ways.

Baphomet’s furious outburst had unsettled Auhn. Especially since he understood what the unstable Eighteenth Circle mage was capable of. However, he’d trusted their mutual interests to rein the bastard’s worst impulses in.

That opinion hadn’t changed until the horned freak lost most of his zealots and suffered serious injuries. It was only then that the Arahnians had finally decided to flee the scene, and they’d done so in a rush. They hadn’t even dared to retrieve their puppets. The equipment wasn’t cheap, but Baphomet had mostly destroyed it anyway, so they’d be better off buying new ones. Their lives were more important than a pair of broken tools.

As was the message that they had to bring back to Arahnis at all costs.

“Are you sure he’s going to send people after us?” Kauhn asked, clearly having yet to grasp the severity of their predicament.

“I’d be shocked if they aren’t behind us already,” Auhn replied.

It wouldn’t be too difficult for Baphomet to replace his slaughtered subordinates, but his injuries were a different story. Gods only aged like that when their internal world got seriously damaged, and that sort of thing usually took several millennia to heal completely.

Auhn had no idea what obstacle had driven the mind deity to such extremes. Baphomet usually had no problem terrorizing any faction in the universe unfortunate enough to cross his sight. Regardless of its cause, Auhn knew exactly what this outcome meant for him and his descendant.

“Catharis isn’t as powerful of a faction as its cosmic rankings would have you believe. They’re only respected due to Baphomet’s mind control spell,” Auhn explained. “What do you think will happen if word spreads that he can no longer cast it?”

Blood visibly drained from his great grandson’s face as the younger deity finally understood why they were fleeing.

This was no longer about the secret of the cyan powder, nor about punishing the people of some ignorant, newly-risen greater spring. It was about Baphomet’s very survival, as well as a priceless opportunity for every other major player in the cosmos.

An Elemental Source of a rare affinity was suddenly up for grabs!

Auhn and Kauhn were possibly the only people in the universe who knew about this, so Baphomet and his fellow Catharian deities had to silence the two of them at all costs!

Well… the people that Baphomet had attacked on Auhn’s behalf might have also learned about this – assuming that the horned freak’s spell had left any survivors behind in the first place – but Auhn had no idea if the mysterious faction was even capable of capitalizing on the information.

“What are we going to do?!” Kauhn asked in a panic. “We’ll never outrun them! I’m sure they have plenty of Fifteenth and Sixteenth Circle gods to send after us, if not a couple of space affinity deities as well.”

“It’s not just about speed,” Auhn replied, trying to maintain his composure. “They know where we’re going, so they can easily intercept us long before we reach Arahnis.”

He fell silent for a few seconds, considering their options.

“Do you know where the Freinian system is?” he eventually asked his great grandson.

“Isn’t that where we grow all our mind and time affinity reagents?” Kauhn asked.

Auhn nodded. “Go there. Those are recently established resource worlds, so I doubt other factions have heard about them. The system is far enough from Arahnis that the Catharian deities are unlikely to stumble upon you by accident, and we have several gods stronger than me stationed at our outposts. Tell them what happened and ask them to escort you home.”

“What about you?”

“Someone needs to stay behind and disrupt our spatial trail, otherwise they’ll follow us,” Auhn replied with a sigh.

“Great Grandfather… let me do it,” Kauhn offered, his eight eyes gleaming resolutely. “You’re more valuable than me.”

“You know that’s not true,” Auhn said, shaking his head. “A Fifteenth Circle karmic deity is a million times more precious than a Sixteenth Circle mage who’s only mastered common elements. Besides, you need to keep disrupting your karmic trail as well, otherwise they might find you. I can’t do that.”

Kauhn clenched his fists but didn’t argue, likely having realized that his ancestor was right. He looked at Auhn one final time before opening another portal.

“Make sure to crush those bastards after reuniting with the rest of our gods,” Auhn instructed him. “My life is a small price to pay if we can get our hands on an Elemental Source and eliminate a rival faction. Especially if you manage to pull this off without any outsiders realizing what happened.”

Kauhn nodded as a film of liquid coated his beady eyes. “Farewell, Ancestor. Your sacrifice will never be forgotten,” the younger deity muttered as the spatial tear closed between them.

Auhn proudly held the soft breeze that had carried his descendant’s last words to him around his body, allowing himself to appreciate the warmth pressing against his skin for a single moment. He’d sired countless offspring in his long life, each of which had sired countless offspring of their own, stretching Auhn’s linage across Arahnis and half their other planets.

Kauhn was the most talented of them all.

Auhn turned around to greet his pursuers. As he had suspected, they’d been right behind them.

Three new portals opened about a lake’s width away, revealing just as many deities. They resembled Baphomet slightly, though their furry coats sported different colours. The two males were grey- and brown-furred, while the final mage was a female with a leaner body, much shorter horns and darker fur.

Auhn didn’t know their elements yet, though he could tell by the quality of their portals that none of them possessed a space affinity at least. That ought to make things a little easier. Auhn didn’t doubt that Baphomet had a couple of those at his service, but it had probably been difficult to reach them in such short notice. The trio were all Sixteenth Circle mages like Auhn. He would have never managed to beat them, even if he had kept his descendant by his side, but that was fine.

He didn’t need to kill them – or even survive.

Auhn projected his internal world outwards, causing a pale star larger than the tallest mountain on Arahnis to swallow his body. Every time it pulsed, deafening shockwaves carried by thick currents of scorching air rippled outwards. Its blinding rays shone upon his enemies, nearly incinerating their robes and fur – at least until they shrouded themselves within their own internal worlds.

Auhn had never regretted his failure in comprehending the final auxiliary concept of the conflagration affinity as much as he did at that very moment. It could have made all the difference.

He shrugged.

It wasn’t like he’d slacked all those years. Incorporating new concepts into one’s internal world simply grew exponentially more difficult with every additional element. Mortals thought that attaining divinity was hard, but the gulf separating the Self-Discovery and Elemental Acquisition stages was just as wide, and every subsequent advancement even more challenging.

“Don’t do anything stupid,” the Catharian goddess warned. “We were ordered to capture the two of you alive. You’ll just have to spend a few thousand years in custody – until Lord Baphomet recovers. We’ll allow you to go after that.”

“If you cooperate with us, we’ll even consider letting you practice your magic while waiting,” the grey-furred god added. “Think of this as a slightly inconvenient vacation.”

Auhn failed to suppress a chuckle, not believing their honeyed words for an instant. Everyone with half a brain could tell that killing him and Kauhn would be the safest way to silence them.

“I’ve always wondered… how does it feel to spend tens of thousands of years following that madman’s orders?” he asked, mostly to buy his great grandson a few more seconds.

The brown-furred deity sighed. “It’s no secret that Lord Baphomet can be a little… eccentric. But he’s done a lot for our world. At the end of the day, the survival and prosperity of our people is all that matters. Isn’t it the same for you?”

“Indeed,” Auhn admitted.

Without warning, the white star shrank to a third of its size, causing the superheated plasma to grow even denser and more unstable. The brilliance of the miniature sun grew unbearable for everyone present, including its owner, burning Auhn’s skin off and causing the blood inside his veins to boil. The construct’s rhythmic pulses grew more frequent and violent, but he stopped the shockwaves from rippling outwards, using them to further boost the star’s internal pressure.

The void cracked along with Auhn’s bones as his own mana swiftly slipped away from his control.

It didn’t matter.

He wouldn’t need bones, nor mana after today.

His eyes had melted out of their sockets already, but he could still just about tell what his opponents were doing through his magical senses. They had put some distance from him, shrouding themselves within their own magic for protection.

They didn’t dare approach, but they couldn’t leave this area either, because this was where Kauhn had opened his portal from. Unfortunately for them, Auhn wasn’t going to leave any evidence of his descendant’s escape behind.

‘Erosion, rust, and cataclysm…’

The last two were decent elements defensively, but Auhn might still take one of the bastards down with him if they failed to gauge the distance properly. Or maybe not. Nobody talented and experienced enough to reach the Sixteenth Circle would be dumb enough to get caught up in the world’s most obvious explosion. Preventing them from chasing after his descendant would have to be enough.

Unable to keep the star contained any longer, he finally let go.

The dazzling sun swelled to over a hundred times its original size, growing as large as a small moon in an instant. Auhn would never get the chance to reach the Ancient realm and turn into a proper planet, but his final act would still reverberate across the galaxy for thousands of years: a short-lived star that would blink in every planet’s sky for just a single moment.

The residents of the nearest solar systems would experience that moment in a couple of years, while the most distant ones would only see it several millennia later. Everyone, with very few exceptions, would be oblivious to the fact that this brief flash represented the fall of a powerful deity.

As Auhn’s body shattered, burned and got devoured by his own attack, he wondered whether this was his punishment for keeping lesser worlds down – for trying to punish somebody for the crime of sharing a magical secret with others.

If Auhn was being honest with himself, it was a nasty thing he’d tried to do. A cruel thing. Collaboration would have been a nobler goal, had they lived in a kinder universe.

But they didn’t, so he had no regrets.

He’d hunted the seller down to secure his people’s future.

He’d sought out Baphomet for Arahnis’ sake.

He’d fled from the mind deity to give his descendant and his colleagues the chance to seize an unprecedented opportunity.

And he’d gladly die for them, too.
