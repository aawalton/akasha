I did my best to keep my troubled thoughts off my face after I had finished my meeting with Isolde. In my wake, I left my Commander looking especially grim, and I didn’t blame her for feeling that way.

I wasn’t the one who was going to have to pitch the mission to find Grey and his expedition, not only to the Gnolls themselves, but to Wenzel. It was going to be a hard sell, trying to convince the Regent Lord to sign off on a mission that could risk the Gigant. Not only that, but even if the Throng’s central pillar ended up being fine, it would still be away from Blutstein and it’s hundreds and hundreds of thousands of hungry mouths for weeks to months.

Isolde and I had agreed, the only way he would sign off on something like that was if we disclosed the coming intensification of the Skyfall to him.

If we were going to potentially die either way, if we didn’t resolve this apocalypse…it was worth the risk.

But after my tough meeting with Isolde, I did my best to absorb as much of Aveline’s presence as I could. I didn’t know when next I would be able to speak to her, and that was if this Sprite would even survive when I left it. Sometimes, my Sprites had a tendency to explode, after I had vacated one of my consciousnesses from them. Not violently, of course. Just a little pop, and they were gone. It was entirely possible this line of communication would be gone when I returned to Rhoscara, and then that would be that. I hadn’t known about this limitation when we were leaving Blutstein, all those months ago. The evolved version of the Skill had been very new to me then, and I hadn’t known all of its limits. I wished I had known to leave another Sprite down here in the south, but if I hadn’t. The closest Sprite from Blutstein had been left at a fishing village by the name of Maypole, a few miles up the coast from the Capital, along with a sack of APD’s. If this Sprite burst when I left…

I wanted these last few hours to mean something.

To both of us.

And so I spent a pleasant afternoon with my daughter and the still confused form of the woman I sometimes thought of as a sister. Rachel was still a bit confused by my abrupt appearance, she rolled with it when she saw how happy Aveline appeared to be. We didn’t do all that, to be truthful. All we did was sit around and simply…talk. Nothing heavy, of course, and nothing that could potentially upset Aveline. Sights I’d seen on the journey that might interest my daughter, interesting people I’d spoken to, and sometimes…simple day-to-day happenings.

Of the battle that loomed over me like a cloud, I said nothing. This was a time for family, not the blood and misery I would no doubt be immersed in soon.

All too soon…

My time was up. My goodbyes with Rachel and Avelin were decidedly tearful, on one of their halfs. Rachel was a very stoic woman and could handle a parting with an air of grim understanding. But Aveline was still a little girl, and I…was having to leave her once again. This time, on her birthday.

I didn’t begrudge her a few tears.

When my time with my family was up, I checked in one last time with Isolde, exchanging nods heavy with meaning, and then…

Reversed the connection binding me to this Sprite.

I experienced a decidedly odd sensation when I did, as if my consciousness was being suctioned up along a giant straw. My vision blanked out for a moment, and a sense of vertigo overtook me. When it passed, I dizzily opened my eyes to find that I was back in Rhoscara once more. To my quiet, routine consternation, the world looked the same as it had when I’d flung my mind down south.

Still grey, still cloudy, and still the same level of unending, dim illumination. If I didn’t have my watch, I’d have no way to tell just how long I’d been in Blutstein for Aveline’s birthday.

Luckily, I did.

About five hours, it seemed. It was about lunchtime now.

Best to go check in with my people and see if there were any fires for me to put out.

I groaned as I sat up from the impossibly still position I’d been sitting in for the past five hours. Every joint in my body snapped, crackled, and popped as I did so, sounding nothing less than a bundle of twigs being snapped over a knee. Cracking my back, I braced myself…

And bounded across the roofs of Rhoscara, bound for the tannery district.

………………………………..

Luckily, there were no actual fires for me to extinguish back at the expedition base camp. That would have been a level of cruel irony fit to make me believe the universe was not only sapient but malicious.

Instead, I just had metaphorical fires to put out.

Turns out, not long after I had returned to the city, the Rangers atop the wall-based watch towers of the city had spotted something, far off into the distance. Something that had set a clamor ringing throughout the city. Quite literally, in fact.

The myriad belltowers that dotted the crimson-roofed city of Rhoscara rang loud and constantly, signaling what we had all expected…and dreaded.

The Savoy were near.

According to the messenger Dwarf that had addressed the leadership of the Herztalian expedition, sails had been sighted over the horizon.

Gold and green sails, bearing the image of a coiled serpent, rearing back to strike.

The quality of Rhoscara’s far-eyes meant that those benighted sails were just barely poking over the curve of Vereden in the distance. That meant they were at least four hours away form us, at this point.

More than enough time to bring everyone within the city to battle-readiness.

I was lucky enough that the bells only started ringing while I was halfway back to the encampment. The sound of them had both caused my heart to start beating wildly within my chest and to abruptly accelerate. When I got back to the camp set up around the stationary form of the Ashen Bride, it was to find the leadership fully gathered on the deck, barking orders to our people as they put all of our preparations into action.

Including, apparently, a runner to go and fetch me from wherever I was out in the city.

I think in normal times, I would have startled everyone from how I dropped out of the sky to land beside them.

Instead, I just received an acknowledging nod and was brought up to speed on the situation.

“Four hours out,” Bella told me tightly, a few minutes later. Everyone in a position to make decisions of import among the leadership had changed position upon my return. While our orders were being carried out, we had migrated up to the section of Rhoscara’s wall we were set up close to, and the accompanying watch tower. The Rangers on duty inside, long used to us, had greeted us with sharp nods before resuming their own watch alongside ours. “That’s what I’m guessin’, judgin’ by their position.”

Bella had pulled out her own far-eye and was busy inspecting the coming enemy fleet. It was still beyond even Perception boosted eyesight, but not a far-eye. After a moment, she handed it to me wordlessly so I could see myself. Raising it to eye level, I grimaced.

Yup, that sure was a lot of ships. Far more than I had ever seen gathered in one place.

And I truly meant that.

I was familiar with major port cities at this point. I’d spent months in Elderwyck operating as a spy. I had lived within the walls of Hinaga for just as long, simply recovering from my ordeals in that place. And I had ultimately put down roots in another port city, this time Blutstein. I’d seen the endless tide of sails coming and going at all hours. I had thought I knew what naval might looked like.

I was wrong.

The Fiume d’Oro was packed from bank to bank with warships. Big ones, too. Massive hulking troop transports with nearly a dozen sails propelling their massive, iron-clad bulks. They dominated the horizon painted by the far-eye, with only a small amount of space between each one. Even one of those ships was twice the size of the Ashen Bride.

And it’s not like those in front were alone. Behind them I could see that they were accompanied by more of the same. From this distance, it was hard to tell just how many…

But I’m guessing the procession stretched back for miles upon miles on the river.

I lowered the far-eye with a shaky breath. No wonder the Rangers had looked decidedly grim when we’d gotten up here.

That force was far larger than I think anyone had been expecting.

Trying to put on a brave face, I turned to the rest of the leadership and offered up a weak smile. “Well. No one ever thought this would be easy.”

My joke fell predictably flat, with most of my peers ignoring me in favor of appraising the Savoy themselves.

I did get a dirty look from Marcel, though, so I counted that as a win.

Nyx lowered her own far-eye and turned to look at me with a grim look on her obsidian features. “Tens of thousands of troops, at the very least,” She said darkly. “Likely upwards of forty. That many ships, that large? We could probably fit the entire expedition on one and a half of those beasts.”

She wasn’t wrong.

And there were dozens of them.

Forty thousand was just an estimate, anyway. A lower, eyeballed estimate. It was entirely possible that number of Dwarven soldiers on those ships was more than that.

Much more.

After all, if you were aiming to ransack one of the largest, most important cities within Velancia...

Why wouldn’t you bring nearly everything you had to bear?

Our observations were interrupted when another Ranger burst through the hatch leading downwards from this tower. He poked his head through the opening and looked around wildly for a moment before his gaze lit upon us standing near the railing. His eyes lit up in relief. “You’re the Herztalians, correct?” Before I could even confirm, he continued hurriedly. “Prince Elysael has called a last-minute meeting before the arrival of the Savoy. All Generals and leaders of attached military forces are required to attend at the Palace.”

And then he let go of the lip of the hatch, immediately falling away out of sight before I could get a word in.

Silence fell over the tower for a moment before Olag let out a satisfied sound. He turned to face me with a raised eyebrow. “Well, you heard him, Marshal. The time has come.”

I nodded back at him. “Right, right. Olag, Alveron,” Said Dwarf gave me his attention, while the Elf continued staring off into the distance, apparently lost in thought. I did see a twitch under his hood at about ear height, so I assumed he was listening. “You two with me. We’ll go see what the Prince and her Generals have to say. Nyx, Bella, Marcel, Gustave, you stay behind and coordinate our preparations with the Rangers. I trust you to handle this in my absence. I’ll keep you updated on anything important.”

At their nods, I held up a hand and created a Sprite meant to act as a communications relay. Long used to this from me by now, Nyx didn’t even blink before accepting the limp construct and stuffing it in a pocket. With that done, I turned away from the expanse of river and the distant, oncoming ships. Olag was already clambering down the ladder through the hatch, while the rest of the Captains were apparently still considering the fleet. When I didn’t hear Alveron immediately following after Olag and me, I turned around to find the elderly Elf still staring out in the direction the Savoy were coming from.

And yet, he wasn’t looking out with a far-eye. Instead, he was staring motionlessly at the horizon with a furrowed brow and a contemplative look in his eye, leaning on his staff.

“Alveron?” I prodded, causing the last Paragon of Vereden to finally blink and turn his head to look at me. “If…you need help getting down, I can go fetch a rope?”

That caused him to blink at me uncomprehendingly for a moment before a dry chuckle escaped the Elder. “I’m not so old and feeble that I can’t climb down a ladder yet, Nathaniel. No, it was…something else…”

“Yeah?” I prodded, after he went quiet again.

“I thought I felt something…” He whispered, almost like he had forgotten I was even here. “Something over the horizon that…I…” Alveron abruptly shook his head, ruffling his pulled-up hood. “No, it doesn’t matter right now. Let us go and see what the authorities of this city want.” With that, he firmed his shoulders and stalked past me to follow after Olag.

I shook my head, and with one last look over to the river, I did the same.

It was time to finalize our defense for the Crimson City.
