This war had raged for a total of fifteen days.

Fifteen days of chaos, destruction, and death.

But never had Aodhán felt the energy as high as he did the moment he stepped onto the battlefield this time. It was as if the presence of the enemy transmigrants had tripled the brutality of their enemies, and suddenly their own formations began cracking.

He understood why immediately.

Hope was a weapon. And their enemies had just been handed four of them.

The shift was palpable in a way that had nothing to do with power and everything to do with will. Enemy Mythics who had been fighting defensively for two weeks were now pressing forward with reckless, furious abandon, and formations that had held for days were suddenly buckling under the weight of renewed conviction.

It was the transmigrant effect.

Aodhán had seen it from the other side—the way enemy ranks flinched when he and the others deployed, the way their formations tightened with fear. He'd never fully appreciated what it meant until now, standing on the receiving end of it.

His gaze landed on the four transmigrants an instant later, and a shuddering breath escaped his lips as core sense fed him information on their power, path, and strength.

They were all between the 70th and 80th tier of advancement, far too powerful for him to engage with in direct combat. But Aodhán feared he had little choice in the matter.

These transmigrants had been carefully chosen to counter them all. He could feel it in the distinct weight they each radiated, in the intentional way their gazes roamed the battlefield.

And when serene gray irises locked with his a moment later, Aodhán wasn't surprised.

Ruin against Absolute Equilibrium. Did he even stand a chance?

But fleeing was not an option he was willing to explore. Doing so would only force one of the others to pick up the slack and fight one against two.

No. Whether he could or not, he had to hold his own.

Swallowing hard, he whispered, "Come out, Varéc. I need you."

Varéc rushed out of his spirit with a thunderous roar, midnight scales rippling with electricity, and Aodhán swallowed again. He glanced back up at the serenity transmigrant who had waited patiently for him to gather himself and asked, "What is your name?"

The bronze-skinned man stared at him for a moment before responding. "My name is Alaric."

Aodhán nodded, gathered his strength, and glared at the man. "Then let's do this, Alaric. Come at me."

Alaric didn't hesitate.

And an instant later, the battlefield around them stilled—not from silence, but from something far worse. Correction.

And Aodhán finally understood what it felt like to be balanced against his will.

***

Back in the elder hall, the High-Elders and several other Calamities watched the tide with uneasy expressions.

"This is bad," High-Elder Mystral noted as she watched Aodhán prepare himself for a fight he had no logical hope of winning.

He had progressed much in the last two weeks, even gaining three more tiers to push himself to the 45th tier. But this opponent was nearly twice his tier, with a spirit just as strong as his and a willpower pool to match.

There were no two ways about it. Aodhán's chances of victory were slim at best, and if they didn't do something, he would lose his life.

Beside her, High-Elder Velora grimaced. "He has surprised us many times before. Perhaps he will surprise us again."

"Or perhaps he has no more surprises left," High-Elder Tharn muttered quietly. "We can't afford to gamble with his life like this. He's too promising." He glanced at the First Seat, expression resolved. "I say we do it."

The First Seat gave no reaction to his words, but it was obvious he was considering it. And after a second of thought, he nodded. "I was hoping we could hold that card for a few more weeks. But if our enemies want to escalate this war, then it's time we did the same."

He smiled and glanced at the High-Elders. "Who wants to go?"

"I'll go," Zarxan spoke before anyone else could, his muscles already braced to stand.

The First Seat glanced at him, understanding flashing in his eyes as he nodded. "Fine. But you must not allow yourself to be pulled into the war. You must return."

Zarxan stood to his feet. "I will."

The First Seat smiled. "Then go. Save as many as you can."

Zarxan nodded, and an instant later, he vanished, reappearing above the battlefield in a burst of chaos and shuddering reality. Prior to his arrival, he had leashed his aura and cloaked his dominion tightly.

Nevertheless, he knew his presence on the battlefield wouldn't go unnoticed for long. The senses of Calamities were too keen. Fortunately, Zarxan didn't need much time to do what he had come for.

Aodhán's miracle rain might have consisted of multiple steps for his tier, but Zarxan had managed to condense and enhance it into a simple decree. Without hesitation, he raised an arm to the storm and commanded, "Bless my allies with a tempest of transformation. Grant them clarity beyond doubt. Grant them speed beyond hesitation. Grant them power beyond recklessness. Withhold your inspirations, yet be not miserly with your wrath."

Energy and willpower drained from his inner world as the decree formed, quintessence and chaos rushing through his spirit to fuel it and strengthen its potency.

But Zarxan wasn't quite done yet. His gaze found Aodhán in the chaos, just as he and the serenity cultivator clashed—equilibrium canceling out ruin without so much as a flicker.

His gaze hardened, and in a whisper quieter than the others, he added, "Bless him with defiance."

And then he released it, thunder booming as all that energy and power he'd gathered into the decree instantly rushed into the storm, so potent and overwhelming it set the heavens on fire.

The immense release of energy drew the attention of everyone present, and Calamities rushed toward him immediately, decrees forming on their lips in an attempt to pull him into the war and trap him.

But Zarxan had completed his mission, and after one last glance in Aodhán's direction, he vanished, reappearing in the elder hall.

"You cut it close." High-Elder Velora glanced at him. "Too close."

Zarxan nodded, his gaze fixed on the screen as the storm suddenly stilled with the effect of his decree. "What matters is that it is done." He glanced at the screen showing Aodhán and released a slow breath.

"It is done."

Hundreds of miles away, on a battlefield that didn't know yet what had just been given to it, Sophran was dying.

***

Sophran had felt pain before.

His path was practically built on the concept of it—of endurance and regeneration.

In the last two weeks alone, he'd had to pull himself back from the brink of death far more times than he could count.

Pain had become an ally. A comrade. An old friend.

This current pain, however, wasn't one Sophran was familiar with.

Seraphine hadn't just sliced him in two. She'd sliced straight through his spirit too—a targeted, clinical attack designed to separate him from his path.

And had he not stumbled, she would have succeeded in slicing him apart.

Despite his survival, his spirit trembled in agony, barely held together by strings of shimmering spirit essence. But it was more than enough to help him activate Ancestral Continuance, vitality surging within him to pull both spirit and body back together.

But Seraphine did not wait to watch that happen. Neither did she hesitate.

Eyes flashing cold and merciless, she moved again, blade carving a perfect arc meant to finish what she had begun.

But Sophran refused to let that happen. Gritting his teeth in agony, he forced another skill activation through his wounded spirit, his body dissolving into a cloud of living mist to avoid the attack.

The sudden change in his appearance and composition caused Seraphine to hesitate, just for a moment, before following through with her attack anyway.

But that moment of hesitation was all Sophran needed to force another skill activation through his agonized spirit, groaning as energy and willpower rushed through reforming pathways.

He glanced at the warring Mythics around him—bloodied, fractured, burning through their reserves—and every trace of hesitation vanished from his heart.

They'd already given him their permission.

Now it was time to bare all those fangs he had been growing.

He glanced back at Seraphine, whose blade was now only inches from his unbound form, and sneered, lightning bursting out of him in an overwhelming wave as the skill activated.

[Living Convergence]

The explosion blasted Seraphine backward, her blade and armor shattering as lightning ravaged through her form.

But Seraphine had never been the true target of that skill.

The battlefield had.

And from within the explosion, branching tendrils of living storm lashed outward—hundreds of luminous strands that bypassed Mythic defenses as though they were mist. They pierced through armor, through aura, through resistance—

—and connected directly to the spirit.

Hundreds of tendrils.

Hundreds of Mythics.

All connected to Sophran, spiritual strength pouring into his spirit like rivers into a starving sea. Power flooded him like a dam broken open. His spirit expanded violently, reforging itself in a brilliance so blinding the very air around him shook with the weight of it.

But unlike with Verdant Thunder, he wasn't draining them to exhaustion. He was simply borrowing their strength—an action that would no doubt leave them weakened while he was being strengthened.

But Sophran wasn't just taking. That wasn't the path of the Living Storm.

The path of the Living Storm was life. It was vitality. And as power flooded Sophran's spirit, vitality flowed out immensely, filling the bodies of every connected Mythic with so much life essence that killing them became practically impossible.

He was a keystone. He was a nexus. He was an ecosystem.

And when he locked gazes with Seraphine this time, it was she who shuddered with dread, realizing that she was no longer fighting a single man but an entire army of the undying.

And as if that wasn't enough, the heavens chose that moment to burn, the storm booming with a clap of thunder so loud it deafened everyone for miles.

And then it exploded in a shower of golden rain.

A rain that avoided her. Yet sought out her opponent.

A rain that caused the power of her opponent to triple as if they weren't terrifying enough already.

A rain that caused the gaze of her opponent to burn with fearsome light.

The man rose into the air then, his levitation fluid, and Seraphine instantly knew this wasn't a battle she could win.

She'd barely finished the thought before verdant-gold eyes were before her and an iron grip on her neck.

Miles away, Aodhán found himself in a similar situation, only his was more sinister.

***

Aodhán choked, not from an iron grip on his neck, but from a collar of suppression that worked dangerously like a band of null metal.

Gritting in frustration, he tried his hardest to rip the collar apart. He tried to embody lightning—to phase, to teleport. He used his essence thread technique—but his efforts only caused the collar to tighten further.

Varéc rushed at Alaric once more, drawing an immense amount of willpower from Aodhán's core to unleash a blistering beam of electricity at the serenity awakened. But the attack barely crossed half the distance to the serenity awakened before it simply fizzled out, corrected to a mere bolt of lightning incapable of hurting a fly.

All the while, the collar tightened.

Alaric wasn't even fighting him. He simply watched, waiting for Aodhán to tire. For the band to choke him to death.

It was terrifying. But beyond that, it was deeply unsettling.

All his life, Aodhán had had to respond to power with more power. More attacks. More lightning. More destruction.

The counter had always been more.

But against Alaric, Aodhán was learning that more power was not the way to go. Alaric responded to power by making it less. It didn't matter how much chaos or willpower he pushed into an attack—Alaric corrected it to baseline. To mediocrity.

All while holding onto his own full power.

Fighting him was like fighting uphill—a moth against a god.

He was nearly impossible to counter, even with him and Varéc working together.

But nearly impossible didn't mean impossible, and as his vision began to blur and his limbs weaken, Aodhán realized he'd been fighting the wrong battle entirely.

The collar wasn't choking him because Alaric was stronger—even though he was. It was choking him because every time he pushed against it, the collar used his own extremities against him.

It was tightening in response to struggle.

Which meant the collar wasn't a constraint. It was a correction. And you couldn't fight correction with more extremities.

You fought it with agreement.

The realization struck him like a bucket of ice water, and he stilled immediately, eyes widening.

There was only one skill in his arsenal that agreed with Serenity.

{Eye of the Storm}

The skill wasn't a combat skill, and he only ever used it in times of extreme stress and emotional turbulence. To calm himself. Once upon a time, he'd also used it every time he wanted to create the miracle rain, but ever since he created Tempest of Transformation, doing so was no longer necessary.

And to that end, the skill had become a bit redundant.

And it would have remained that way had the prospect of death not sparked his memory, forcing him to remember that there was more to the skill than peace and quiet.

The skill's effect flashed in his mind, and his eyes widened further as he realized how utterly foolish he had been.

{Eye of the Storm—Passive}: You have entered a heightened state of awareness and perception that can be tweaked as you wish, enabling you to see clearly through chaotic weather and magical disruptions. A deeper sense of tranquility can be achieved by immersing your mind deeply into the effects of the skill.

Extra effects:

Grants a minor ability to identify enemy weak points.

Effect can be imbued into some skills.

Ever since the creation of the Miracle Rain, he had forgotten about the extra effects Eye of the Storm provided. Aside from {Storm Manipulation}, he had never imbued the tranquil effects of Eye of the Storm into his other skills. He hadn't even tried.

Somehow, someway, he had simply forgotten about it—forgotten that he had a center, a bastion of tranquility and serenity that could never be corrected back to the center when it had never left.

He realized then that what he needed wasn't more power. It was cold, serene, centered control.

And without hesitation, he channeled all he could—energy, willpower, chaos, quintessence—into Eye of the Storm and sank his mind deeply into it.

The effect was instantaneous.

The world dimmed—sound, light, color, chaos—everything dimmed as his emotions died. Rage vanished, fear disappeared, desire dissolved, until all that was left was a cold emptiness cloaked in a peace so complete it felt borderline sociopathic.

Aodhán felt his entire body settle as that peace engulfed him in a cold embrace, and with a slow, blissful breath, he lifted his gaze to Alaric, feeling no pleasure at the flicker of surprise in his gaze.

That flicker, however, was what let him know that he wasn't being choked anymore. He'd been corrected, and he had agreed. He was centered now. Balanced.

And from where he stood, it seemed to him that Alaric was now the one who needed correction.

A small, lifeless smile touched his face at the realization, and with the hyper-focus of a predator watching prey, Aodhán activated {Lightning Body—Silver}.

Only this time, he channeled it through that cold, lifeless tranquility he was feeling.

Alaric's gray eyes narrowed almost imperceptibly, and Aodhán's smile widened as the skill came alive within him like a pyre.

He vanished immediately, phasing straight through the heart of Alaric's equilibrium field—and found, to his cold satisfaction, that Lightning Body imbued with Eye of the Storm had nothing to be corrected.

He wasn't extremity passing through a moderating force.

He was a center passing through another center.

And centers didn't correct each other. They collided.

And so did his palms an instant later, slapping hard against Alaric's rib cage before he could even think of dodging.

It wasn't a devastating blow. But it had landed—the first attack to do so since this battle began.

Aodhán's smile widened further.

Alaric, however, wasn't so pleased. And with the hint of a snarl, he raised a hand to the air, gray light surging out of him in a shallow dome of absolute suppression.

Before the dome could trap Aodhán, though, the heavens caught fire. Thunder boomed so loud it threatened to split the earth apart. The storm exploded with rain, and from within the deluge, thousands of golden drops fell, flowing on invisible currents to find their targets.

One such drop raced toward Aodhán.

Alaric rushed to stop it, a dome of serenity rushing upward to block the attack.

The Fury attacked immediately, razor-sharp claws slicing toward him, but Alaric barely spared it a glance. The claws raked against his skin without impact, and a hand slammed the Fury into the earth just as it moved to attack again.

Having succeeded, he glanced back at his opponent, only to find Aodhán missing.

He looked up quickly, eyes widening in shock as he found a being of silver lightning floating high above his construct just as a drop of golden rain splashed against his forehead.

"No!" His gaze hardened, his aura surging outward in a smothering wave even as he rushed toward Aodhán, Suppression Dome surging to engulf them both.

But it was too late.

Aodhán opened his eyes an instant later, crimson-gold irises gleaming with a defiance they had lacked only moments ago.

His suppression hadn't worked.

Aodhán smiled again, that same lifeless smile that made him immune to his suppression, and an instant later, Alaric was sent flying, eyes wide, as he heard a word he couldn't believe—a declaration that should have had no place in his domain.

"Ruin."
