Lira woke up in a foul mood. She had spent the previous night and day with Maren before returning to the palace around the same time she had left it, just in time for a full night's sleep.

She had hoped that a day spent with a friend would dull the anger she carried back from Salvek's establishment, but after a good night's rest, the memories felt even clearer. The crying girl. The casual way Salvek had referred to women as goods. And the matter-of-fact order to send a terrified girl to another noble because he preferred them crying.

It was enough to make her stomach churn.

She silently folded her blankets, changed into a fresh maid's dress, and walked to the servant baths. Normally, she found the routine relaxing. Heating the water, washing her hair, and scrubbing the dust from her hands usually helped clear her thoughts. Today, it accomplished nothing. She should have stuck to casting the cleansing spell instead.

She wasn't in a better mood even by the time she reached the library, and the familiar smell of parchment welcomed her inside.

Without bothering to open the library to visitors yet, she collected the book containing the Mana Obfuscation bookmarks, surrounded herself with the magical interference, and reached for a spell.

"Ladar. Linasara. Oshkan. Cognar. Turat."

Thousands of books bloomed inside her mind.

This time, she searched for murders. Specifically, investigations involving dead nobles.

Most of the glowing lights disappeared until only a handful remained. One of them rested on a shelf near the legal records. Lira walked over and absorbed the contents.

"Pika. Linasara. Cognar. Coopar. Nagla."

Information flooded into her mind.

The book contained the memoir of a senior guardsman. It turned out that noble murder investigations followed surprisingly rigid procedures. If the victim was a commoner, the city guard usually handled the matter themselves. If the victim belonged to the nobility, however, the investigation became far more complicated. The captain in charge first gathered every ordinary piece of evidence available before contacting the Mage Guild and requesting a certified diviner.

Divination magic worked poorly without context. A diviner couldn't simply ask who killed Lord Whoever and expect the spell to answer. They first needed detailed information about the victim, recent movements, relationships, habits, enemies, financial disputes, family connections, and anything else the investigators managed to uncover. Only after being supplied with that information would they cast an investigative spell.

One spell appeared again and again throughout the records.

Detect Hate.

The spell apparently produced a list of everyone harboring strong hostility toward the victim.

Lira frowned.

That... was inconvenient.

The investigators then questioned every person on that list until someone confessed or enough evidence accumulated to justify an arrest. She rubbed her temple.

If Salvek suddenly died tomorrow, she would almost certainly appear somewhere on that list.

Perhaps not at the very top. The man was a piece of shit, after all.

She imagined a diviner announcing, There is one palace librarian who secretly despises him.

That alone would be enough to bring guards knocking at her door.

Lira let out a slow sigh.

She doubted that, if no culprit were ever found, whoever occupied the lowest rung of society among the suspects would become an extremely attractive scapegoat. A palace maid would lose that contest every time.

She dismissed the thought and cast the spell again.

"Ladar. Linasara. Oshkan. Cognar. Turat."

This time, she adjusted the question.

When were noble deaths not investigated?

Again, only a handful of books remained highlighted.

After a few readings, most of the death circumstances proved unsurprising: old age, disease, battle, execution, and accidents witnessed by enough respectable people.

Then another category caught her attention.

Formal duels.

Lira paused.

Apparently, once a duel had been properly witnessed and recognized under noble law, the loser accepted responsibility for their own death. There was no murder investigation because the Crown considered the matter settled.

Her thoughts immediately wandered toward Sir Deren. He was, without a doubt, the strongest duelist in the castle, and with the Sword Saint manual she had provided him, rumor had it he had become almost undefeatable, with only Aric able to keep up with him.

Could she somehow convince him to challenge Salvek?

She discarded the idea almost instantly.

"Yes, Sir Deren," she muttered quietly. "Would you kindly risk your life to kill your former mentor just because I said so?"

No.

That conversation wasn't happening.

Still... Salvek wasn't exactly the sort of man who inspired loyalty. Surely he had offended enough people throughout his career that someone else might eventually challenge him.

That thought inspired another question.

"Ladar. Linasara. Oshkan. Cognar. Turat."

This time, she searched for documents written by Salvek that might provoke someone into demanding satisfaction. It felt like an absurdly specific request.

She fully expected the spell to return nothing.

Instead… One ping.

The only light remaining in the constellation of written texts.

Lira blinked.

"...Really?"

The highlighted document wasn't even inside the library. It rested somewhere among the guest residences reserved for visiting nobles.

She left the library, her curiosity immediately replacing her frustration.

Once there, she climbed the stairs toward where the spell led her when she happened to hear familiar voices.

Dira and Juliet were already changing the bed linens after the previous night's visitors had left the room.

"You girls are working hard."

"Lira!"

Dira waved enthusiastically.

"What are you doing here?"

"With Hesta gone," Lira said, "I thought I'd replace her by handing out unreasonable punishments."

"Don't even joke about that!" Dira protested.

Lira stepped inside and started helping with the sheets.

"You make a terrible Hesta," Juliet laughed. "She never joined in with the work."

The three women worked together for several minutes, chatting idly as they stripped the beds and replaced the linens with fresh ones. Compared to plotting murder, ordinary maid work felt strangely relaxing.

"So," Dira asked while shaking out a blanket, "I heard Prince Lyth is always in the library now?"

"I heard Prince Aric spends a lot of time there too," Juliet added.

"Lyth will probably inherit the library one day," Lira said. "That kid is a future scholar. As for Aric, he only shows up from time to time to read about the Lost Age of Magic."

"I thought he took a liking to you," Dira said.

"With my freckles and my orange hair?" Lira scoffed. "Not a chance."

The conversation drifted naturally while they moved from room to room. Eventually, they reached the suite indicated by her divination spell.

While Juliet collected towels and Dira remade the bed, Lira casually opened the indicated drawer.

Exactly where the spell predicted lay a single folded letter.

She waited until neither maid was looking before whispering,

"Pika. Linasara. Cognar. Coopar. Nagla."

The contents unfolded inside her memory.

Lady Vanira,

Allow me to offer my condolences regarding your husband's unfortunate passing. I understand that his gambling debts have left you in difficult financial circumstances despite Her Majesty's generosity in allowing you to remain within the palace.

Should you desire an opportunity to restore your financial independence, I happen to know many distinguished gentlemen who would generously compensate a woman of your beauty for discreet companionship.

Everything would remain confidential and entirely at your own convenience.

If interested, simply write back.

Sir Salvek

Unbelievable...

Asking a grieving widow to sell herself before her husband's body had even gone cold. The man truly possessed no sense of shame.

She quietly closed the drawer and joined the others.

"What do you know about the lady staying here?" Lira asked as she helped Juliet spread a fresh bedsheet over the mattress.

Dira sighed immediately.

"Poor Lady Vanira."

"Her husband ruined them," Juliet explained. "He lost everything gambling."

"His ancestral estate, their savings, and even her jewelry," Dira added while fluffing one of the pillows. "Then, after realizing what he'd done, he hanged himself."

The room fell quiet for a moment.

"And her family?" Lira asked.

"Her brother wouldn't take her back," Juliet said.

"The Queen offered her rooms here indefinitely," Dira added.

Lira nodded slowly as she tucked one corner of the blanket beneath the mattress.

"So she truly has nothing."

"Pretty much."

"I don't think I've met her."

"You probably have," Juliet replied. "She spends most mornings walking in the gardens."

"She's considered one of the beauties of the capital," Dira continued. "Long blond hair, blue eyes, and enormous bre..."

Juliet smacked her lover lightly across the shoulder.

"You really have only one way of describing women."

"I'm just saying, the woman turns heads wherever she walks."

Lira merely smiled while they finished making the bed, but her thoughts had already wandered elsewhere.

She had known how repulsive Salvek was after what she had witnessed the previous night, but she hadn't expected him to be quite this brazen. More than the proposition itself, it was the timing that disgusted her. The Queen had looked at a grieving widow and offered her sanctuary until she could rebuild her life. Salvek had looked at the exact same woman and seen nothing more than a business opportunity.

Lira doubted Lady Vanira had accepted the offer, but she also doubted she had forgotten receiving it. A letter like that lingered in the mind long after it had been burned. It also made her wonder how many similar letters existed. How many desperate noblewomen, widows, daughters burdened by debt, or wives trapped in unhappy marriages had quietly received invitations from Salvek? Sooner or later, one of those letters might find the wrong husband, father, or brother.

Or perhaps, she could make it happen, leading to a duel.

The thought lingered with her as she said goodbye to Dira and Juliet before making her way back to the library.

The morning passed quietly until Prince Lyth arrived at his usual time.

Only this time, he wasn't alone.

After Sir Henry, his bodyguard, Sir Deren entered next, dressed in a simple blue doublet rather than his usual armor, with Prince Aric following half a step behind. Walking between them was a young woman about Lira's own age.

Sir Deren smiled the moment he spotted Lira.

"Miss Lira," he called happily. "Allow me to introduce my sister."

The young woman gave a small, practiced curtsy.

"Lady Luxia Valis. She has come to the capital for her debut," the knight explained.

"It is a pleasure to meet you, my lady," Lira replied with a respectful bow.

But deep down, she was thinking,

I shouldn't.

The thought came immediately.

Deren had been nothing but courteous to her. Using him as a pawn simply because he probably could easily kill Salvek felt wrong.

But this is far too convenient.
