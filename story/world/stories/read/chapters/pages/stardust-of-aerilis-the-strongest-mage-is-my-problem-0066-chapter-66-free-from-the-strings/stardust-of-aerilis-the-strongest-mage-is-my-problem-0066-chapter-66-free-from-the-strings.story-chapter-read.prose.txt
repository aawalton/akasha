"Another deal?" Aiven asked, his eyes narrowing. He noticed Cyria’s facial expression—the sharp, predatory smile and the glare of absolute confidence.

Before Cyria could answer, Virelle surged forward. She didn't just drift; she lunged, hovering just inches above the marble table, positioning herself as a living shield between Aiven and the Fox.

"How dare you?" Virelle spat, her violet eyes flashing with a lethal light. "How dare you ask for another deal after admitting to deceiving us from the very beginning? You were his instrument! There is no way to know if a single syllable that has escaped your mouth is anything more than a fabricated hook to drag us deeper into your greed!"

Aiven, however, remained still. His mind was racing.

It’s weird, Aiven thought. Why is she this confident? Cyria had just admitted to betraying Lucien. She had divulged her true motives to them, essentially admitting that she was a traitor to the only person more powerful than her. Based on what he had seen at the Sunken Fane, Lucien was plenty powerful. If Lucien decided to attack Cyria for her double-crossing, Vulpine would suffer immense losses.

And at this moment, Aiven realized the truth: Cyria didn't have the leverage. They did. Virelle and Aiven were the only ones capable of shielding her from Lucien’s wrath. Cyria needed them for her own survival, not the other way around.

"Sure," Aiven said, his voice quiet but remarkably firm. "I’ll make a deal."

Virelle snapped her head around, her silver hair whipping. "Master! Do not play into the hands of this cunning fox!"

"Virelle, it’s okay," Aiven said, looking her in the eye. "Trust me."

Cyria’s fox ears twitched, and her sharp grin widened. "Excellent. A man of vision. I was about to propose—"

"I’ll set the terms," Aiven interrupted.

Cyria’s smile faltered for a fraction of a second, her golden slit-pupils sharpening with a sudden, genuine interest.

"You will tell us the truth about Virelle’s origin," Aiven stated, leaning forward. "All of it. Second, you will have someone teach me exactly how to use the Loom-Breaker. And third,I will not allow the Loom-Breaker to be used on Virelle. She is no longer a piece in your alignment."

He paused, letting the silence hang in the air. "In exchange, when I meet Lucien again, I will try to persuade him not to harm you or Vulpine, but of course, there is no guarantee of success.."

Cyria watched him for a long beat, her tail giving a slow, rhythmic flick. Finally, she let out a short, airy chuckle and leaned back into her chair.

"The moment I told you about my original plan, Aiven, I had already abandoned the idea of using the Loom-Breaker on her," Cyria admitted, her golden eyes shimmering with a rare, honest approval. "I knew you wouldn't let me.'"

She then looked at Aiven, “But I would still need the Loom-Breaker. It’s the only instrument for me to protect Vulpine with. Unless the two of you are willing to join me long-term?”

Cyria was gambling. She knew that to ensure Vulpine’s livelihood and her own survival, she desperately needed either the artifact or Aiven and Virelle firmly on her side. But as Aiven looked at his obsidian arm, he realized the situation was not boding well for the Fox. In the worst-case scenario for her, he could simply take the Loom-Breaker and go to Lucien. Lucien would teach him, protect him, and provide the answers he sought. At this point, Cyria had almost zero leverage.

"I'm still thinking about it," Aiven said, his voice quiet but remarkably firm. "But at this point, Cyria, you don't have much to bargain with. You need us. We don't necessarily need you."

Cyria watched him for a long beat, her long orange tail coming to a complete standstill. The predatory confidence in her eyes flickered, replaced by a momentary, sharp-edged vulnerability. Slowly, she let out a heavy, weary sigh and leaned back in her reclining chair.

"That's correct," Cyria murmured, looking at the ceiling. "At this point, I am somewhat... powerless. My original plan was to let you get the Loom-Breaker and then use it on Virelle to secure my own future. I did consider your potential to be powerful, Aiven—that’s why I agreed to let you use the artifact—but I didn't calculate that you were that powerful, to the extent that Lucien came to you."

"Does this mean you're giving up on making deals?" Aiven asked, his voice quiet but remarkably firm.

Cyria watched him for a long beat, her long orange tail coming to a complete standstill. "No," she murmured, looking at him with a mix of frustration and genuine, reluctant admiration. "But there’s no deal left to be made, is there? Lucien’s little appearance at the Sunken Fane was unpredicted. It made my entire board crumble."

She stood up, her golden eyes turning sharper, losing every trace of corporate warmth. "I, however, can and should probably do one thing." She stepped closer, her posture radiating a sudden, lethal authority. "Which is to utilize every resource Vulpine has to take that Loom-Breaker from you right now."

BOOM.

Virelle didn't wait. She unleashed a massive, circular blast of violet mana. The shockwave didn't target Cyria, but it slammed into the reinforced aether-glass walls of the penthouse. The massive floor-to-ceiling windows shattered instantly, raining crystalline shards down into the Fangreach streets below.

A violent, high-altitude wind roared into the office, whipping Cyria’s hair and scattering documents across the room like autumn leaves.

WEE-OOO! WEE-OOO!

Alarms began to scream throughout the Spire, red lights flashing in rhythm with the howling wind.

Cyria stood in the center of the wreckage, her clothes flapping in the gale. She looked at the jagged remains of her windows, then back at the mage who was now glowing with a terrifying, incandescent light.

"I should have probably kept that thought in my head," Cyria admitted dryly, her voice barely audible over the alarms.

Virelle looked her dead in the eye, her prismatic orb screeching with a predatory whistle. "There is more where that came from, Fox," she hissed. "Do not mistake my Master’s patience for my mercy."

Cyria sighed, raising her hands in a gesture of surrender. "Resorting to such force would cause far too much damage to Vulpine with a vanishingly low chance of success," she said, her voice regaining its pragmatic cool. "I won't do anything."

The heavy double doors burst open. Vane and a dozen armed Vulpine security officers rushed into the room.

Cyria raised her arm, a sharp, commanding gesture.

The security team hesitated, looking at the destruction, but they slowly lowered their weapons.

Cyria turned back to Aiven. Her expression was no longer that of a CEO or a predator. She looked tired, her golden eyes reflecting the vast, open sky behind her.

"I hope you can think things through properly, Aiven," Cyria said softly. "Sure, I can let you use the Loom-Breaker. I’ll even tell you everything I know about Virelle once we have more time. But understand this... I have many mouths to feed at Vulpine. I am selfish, yes, but I am also responsible for every life on this island."

She looked out at the city below, then back at him. "Lucien wants to reset the world. He wants to burn the greedy parts away to save a planet that's already half-dead. I just want to keep this spire standing. I hope you can make the correct choice."

Cyria gestured toward the open doors where the guards were still dispersing. "Now, I suppose you should get some rest. As you can see, I have a great deal of cleaning up to do."

As Aiven and Virelle walked toward the elevator, leaving Cyria standing amidst the wreckage of her empire, Aiven realized that he was finally the one holding the pen, and the story he intended to write was one where neither he, nor the girl beside him, would ever be anyone's puppet again.
