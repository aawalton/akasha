Lyselle stared at the open doorway after her daughter left. She didn't sit, she didn't pace, she didn't even breathe. Anya had walked out without saying farewell, had walked out mid sentence, not even bothering to finish speaking before turning to leave.

They were in her Holding, in her study and her daughter had simply decided to leave without so much as a farewell. She'd known there was a wedding coming, she'd have had to have been deaf not to have heard Sandra tell everyone about it, or more specifically about her dress.

She took a deep breath, almost gasping as she realized how long she'd paused. Her eyes turned to Tomas and narrowed. He'd known something and not told her, he was involved in the wedding and she hadn't heard?

"What did you accept?"

"My Lady?"

"Seb thanked you for accepting," she clarified. "Holding Lys has not been approached about the wedding. I'm certain of this because I haven't been approached about the wedding. I'm doubly certain because my daughter just made it clear I would have to wait for a guest invite to find out more. Yet you accepted something."

"Elle-" he began

"No" she snapped, cutting him off. "This is Holding business."

"With respect Elle, it is not."

She blinked. Everything Tomas did was Holding business. The only thing that wasn't was personal business and she couldn't remember the last time he'd had any of importance. For him to disagree with her about this affair, to tell her she was wrong meant-

"He asked me to stand with him," he explained. "As his first mentor after Awakening."

"That was Holding business."

"It was. We made mistakes with him, but we didn't know better. We couldn't have predicted any of this. He didn't request that Seneschal Lys stand with him, he asked Tomas."

"Did you ever tell him your name?"

"You did. I called you by name at their house, you answered the same."

That was right, she had done that. The day had been a strange one. He'd used her name, not in public but certainly not in private, and she'd done the same. It had been a much lighter day than today, a much more pleasant occasion.

"Why you?"

"I don't know," Tomas admitted. "They're always Diving it seems, and they don't exactly have a multitude of retainers to pick from."

"You accepted."

Not a question. She knew the answer. They had gone behind her back, asked her Seneschal, her closest friend despite their roles, and she hadn't known.

"I did."

"Did you know I was excluded?"

"I had not considered your personal business. Sandra has been quite vocal, she speaks to you as often as I do of late."

"She argues with me you mean."

"She's young and as stubborn as her mother."

She had to smile at that. Her youngest was very much her daughter. They both were. Headstrong, determined, willful. She'd tried to treat them as she had been, she'd tried to be a good mother. Those might not have been the same thing, especially not now that Anya was independent and could fend for herself.

"Does she hate me?" she asked.

"Anya's hurt," Tomas answered, following her train of thought almost perfectly.

"She stormed out."

"You used to do that to your father. He took it worse."

"What do I do?"

She didn't expect Tomas to answer. She couldn't begin to answer the question herself.

"Everything would be better if everyone would just do as they're told," Mahk muttered.

"What was that my Prince?" the maid asked.

"Nothing girl my sweet," he answered, barely even aware of the exertion he put into disrupting her thoughts.

"Of course my Lord."

He couldn't remember who she once was, it didn't matter. He'd forgotten she was there, now she'd just forget what he'd said. That was the beauty of compliant staff, they did their job and they forgot everything you wanted them to. They didn't argue, they didn't disagree, they didn't hesitate. They did their job and that was that.

Outside of the private Velli homes things were messier. Employees had opinions, they had interests, they had needs. And they had to be allowed to do so, they couldn't simply be compliant otherwise someone would notice. The Arbiters, the Seers, the other Families.

They suspected too much as it was. There had been a few slips over the years, mistakes that were dealt with promptly, but not without leaving a trace, a hint. No records, those could be dealt with, but memories were trickier. He knew they were watching, that they were just waiting for him to slip, for the Family to end up vulnerable.

Didn't they understand what Velli provided? Structure, progress, control. Everything worked out for the best when people listened to those who knew to make the hard decisions. The rabble complaining? They'd stop once they had a new target. Complaints were already down, interviews with aggrieved families were being passed over in favour of new scandals.

Affiliation rates were still up. They weren't where his analysts had projected, but they were better than they had been in over a century. Public displays were fading away, the fad had passed, but not without serving its purpose.

Things hadn't worked out perfectly, some might even argue they hadn't worked out well, but they didn't see the whole picture. The Patriarch and Matriarch did, he did. He'd once thought his sister did, history now showed otherwise.

Awakened Humanity needed guidance. It needed to be pointed in the right direction. Someday soon it would be.

Clyde panted as he stood over the last of the rabbits. He'd managed it, a third pack in the same day. The club he'd been given dropped from his hand as he looked for the guards, looked for the approval at his efforts.

He hated them, hated that he looked to them. He hated them almost as much as he hated that slip of a thing, that girl. He'd show her, he'd take his club to her and she'd beg for his mercy. He often found himself dreaming of that day. Of standing over her as she admitted she was wrong, as she sought forgiveness.

Forty rabbits. A year ago he'd been happy to finally kill ten, that he'd managed to graduate away from slimes and finally take on something less disgusting. He'd tried the long-eared green vermin once, he'd managed to take one down. He might even manage to kill two or three at a time, without anyone distracting the rest.

"Well done Clyde. A fine haul," the guard said before flitting about and collecting the Energy crystals. "Six for watching you, two for your meal and bed, the rest are yours. You must nearly be level 8, keep going and you'll be there soon."

He knew he was being mocked. He knew they didn't need anything he could gather. Either guard would have cleared the room without blinking, would have gone down another four or five levels and faced something they might actually see as a threat. He knew but he couldn't stop them.
