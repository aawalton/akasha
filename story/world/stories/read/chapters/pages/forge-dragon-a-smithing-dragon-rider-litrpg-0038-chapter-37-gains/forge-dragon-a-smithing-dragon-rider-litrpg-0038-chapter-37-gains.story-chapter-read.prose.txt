The invading horde broke at the fall of their champion. They’d seen Caleb’s whirlwind of blades and wanted nothing to do with it.

They didn’t know that Caleb was exhausted and lacked the mana or stamina to put up much more of a fight. Caleb looked around at the blood-soaked ground, found a clean patch of snow, and collapsed.

The sound of armored men moving quickly filled the air as the legion moved to follow the Frigidi in case their retreat was a ruse. The collared warrior had recovered by then and went on ahead with a mad glee in his eyes. With the Frigidi hero revealed and slain, there was no reason for the man to hold back and hide—no reason save for the desire not to execute fleeing men.

Some of the less than upstanding members of the community joined the charge against the rout.

“What am I going to do with you to,” an exacerbated voice called from nearby.

Caleb rolled over to the a Centurion of the Thousand Blade Legion looking down on himself and his brother shaking his head.

“Ummm, excuse me?” Caleb asked at the same time Grisim spoke.

“A triumph would be nice, but laurels are okay too.”

***

The legion’s camp followers and auxiliaries along with the whole of the village came out to clean up the aftermath of the battle. Caleb, Grisim and Bog were “escorted” to a tent and where they received medical care from the army’s surgeons, but they were under no illusion that them leaving the tent would be allowed.

As they walked, the whole of the village watched them eyes wide after the display they—but mostly Caleb—had put on. Bog earned a mix of confused and worried glances and people self consciously clutched at their knives and spears when the “iron salamander” came through. Caleb was relieved to see both his father and Master Dimel in the crowd, but there was no sign of Kelia’s father.

The thought of Kelia’s father stirred a mix of emotions. For one, it was the first time he’d thought of his wife’s father since they’d gotten married. Kelrin had always liked Caleb and approved of their match, but he wasn’t sure how the older man would take the sudden marriage.

His thoughts turned to Kelia and he hoped she was safe in the wyvern nest.

The pair were left for hours to stew in isolation. Whenever they asked when the Centurion would see them, they were told soon.

The Thousand Blade Legion’s name was deceptive. They were technically the 1,111 blade legion, but that was a bit of a mouthful. The core of the legion was the titular 1000 blades. Each blade, when picked up by an uninitialized individual would automatically and involuntarily be given the common class Legionnaire. These men were given strict instructions on how to allocate their stats and how to train their skills. The path of the Legionnaire was well trodden, and if one lived long enough, they could see themselves to level 70 before being forced to “retire.”

The 1000 Legionnaire’s were directed by 100 uncommon Decurions. These men were also strictly governed in how to progress their levels, but were given more mental stats to help them better command on the field. Their skills were focused on improving the efficiency of their commands.

10 rare Centurions each commanded a century of 100 legionnaires and their accompanying Decurions.

The legion as a whole was led by the legendary Legate.

All this was to say that when they were told the Centurion had ordered them to stay in place, they listened.

“What happened out there?” Caleb asked his brother once they were alone, a guard outside.

“I should be asking you as much! What was that?”

Caleb grew suddenly embarrassed to have his brother so impressed with him. He thought back to the ease at which he’d killed all the men on his way to the keep and his embarrassment turned to regret.

It had been too easy. Yes, the ritualists and the giant had been hard fought, but all the men in between had been like nothing to him. He’d always wanted to be initialized for the benefits to his forging. He’d never given much thought to the rest of it, but now he saw the darker side to the initialization.

The responsibility to defend the village was a heavy one.

“I learned a new skill,” Caleb said.

“A skill, or a skill?” Grisim asked.

Caleb checked his notifications.

Immutable Metal has increased to level 3.

Caleb read his notifications out to his brother who then asked for descriptions.

Jump: Use your stamina to jump. Jumping distance increases with your strength and level in this skill.

Iron Vortex: Call upon the power of the wind and metal to protect you and disrupt your enemies. Create a vortex of air and metal around you. The size and speed of the vortex increase with your willpower and levels in this skill and its governing affinity.

“Now tell me what happened,” Caleb insisted after sharing his details.

“Well… that sword I picked up apparently belonged to a Decurion… a dead one,” Grisim admitted, finally showing some humility. “My class evolved from Frost Dragon Hunter to Imperial Monster Hunter. It knocked me down to level 4.”

“Your class changed?!” Caleb asked. “And it went down?”

“Yeah!” Grisim said. “Because it’s rare now.”

Caleb thought to what he knew of the Thousand Blade Legion and suddenly feared for his brother’s life. He’d taken up one of the Decurion blades and not become a Decurion. They would have to tread carefully if he were to survive this day.

“That’s… really bad,” Caleb said in a whisper.

“What do you mean?” Grisim asked, not seeing any downsides.

“You took the place of a Decurion! What are they going to do? Just let you leave? This is bad!”

“Oh… I hadn’t thought of that,” Grisim said, he too now seeing his situation clearly. “I got a new skill though, Magis Cadunt—stupid high tongue name—I have increased strength when fighting something larger than me.”

“So… if you lose some of that ‘baby fat’ you’ll get stronger?” Caleb asked, teasing his brother.

They argued like brothers do for a while then the discussion returned to their predicament. Grisim vowed to be humble and contrite in hopes of getting spared.
