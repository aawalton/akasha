Well, Chloe wasn't really sure what she was supposed to say right now. On one hand, it was great that they'd finally found Greg, and judging by the way he was looking at her, he seemed at least kinda happy to see her too. On the other hand... Chloe had absolutely no idea who this guy was. She felt a little bad about that.

Apparently, Greg knew her, or at least remembered her face and here she was standing in front of him with absolutely nothing. There wasn't even the tiniest little spark of Oh yeah, that's that guy! It was kind of embarrassing, actually.

The whole thing reminded her of this one time back in school when a classmate she'd apparently known for almost an entire year walked up to her and said, "Hey, Chloe!" and she smiled, waved, and confidently replied, "Hey!" before immediately turning around and whispering to her friend, "Who was that?" The worst part was that she'd spent the next five minutes pretending she totally knew him, because she'd been too embarrassed to ask, and somehow that had only made things worse when he started talking about a group project they'd supposedly done together.

So... yeah, this felt kind of like that. Except this time, instead of forgetting some random classmate, she'd apparently forgotten a pretty important person who seemed to know her. Which, honestly, felt a little worse. But hey, if Mom told her anything, it was that honesty was the best policy.

"Umm, Greg, right? Do you really know me?"

Greg stared at her for a few seconds before letting out a short snort. "Yeah, I know you. Although, judging by the look on your face, I'm guessing you don't remember me at all."

Chloe winced. "Well... not really."

"Don't worry about it. You were a kid, and I wasn't exactly someone you would've had much reason to remember."

"But why wouldn't I remember you? Were you, like, secretly really boring?"

"Listen, I was one of the chauffeurs on standby at Divinity Inc. I spent most of my time driving executives around whenever they needed a car, so unless you were particularly interested in the people behind the wheel, there wasn't much reason for you to remember me."

Z suddenly spoke up from beside them. "That would explain why Chloe doesn't remember you, and also why I didn't really remember you either."

Greg glanced toward her. "Because I'm a driver?"

"Partially," Z replied.

Greg raised an eyebrow. "What's the other part?"

"Well… I never used the company car while I was still working at Divinity Inc."

There was a brief silence before Greg snorted and shook his head. "Well, that would certainly explain it. I suppose it's difficult to remember a chauffeur when you never actually had to use the chauffeur."

Chloe frowned thoughtfully before her eyes suddenly brightened. "Wait, so you drove Mom around?"

Greg looked at her as though the answer should have been painfully obvious. "Of course I did. She was the CEO, so she had somewhere to be almost constantly, and I was one of the people whose job it was to make sure she actually got there."

"Oh! What was she like?"

"Your mother? She was kind of a pain in the ass, if you want the honest answer."

"Whaaat? No way, Mom is like the best person ever, she couldn't possibly be annoying."

Greg smirked a little. "She was just terrible at sitting still, and she had this habit of treating the back seat of my car like a second office. I'd pull up to the building, she'd climb inside carrying half a dozen documents, answer three phone calls before we'd even left the parking lot, and then spend the entire drive reading reports while telling me where to turn without ever actually looking up."

Chloe giggled. "Oh, nevermind then, that sounds exactly like her!"

"Yeah, there were plenty of times when she'd get into the car without telling me where she wanted to go because she'd be so absorbed in whatever she was working on. I'd ask her where we were headed, and she'd tell me she'd figure it out once we were moving, which was not particularly helpful when I was the one sitting in the driver's seat."

Chloe covered her mouth, trying not to laugh. "Did you ever just drive somewhere random?"

"I was tempted, but no. I needed the job, and well, your Mom was pretty high up the ladder."

That made Chloe let out a hearty laugh. She was having a lot of fun just listening to Greg talk about Mom.

Greg shook his head, though the faint smile on his face remained. "She also had a habit of forgetting to eat whenever she was busy. I'd occasionally get halfway through a trip before she'd suddenly realize she'd been working for six hours without having anything besides coffee. Then she'd insist she was perfectly fine, somehow."

"Mom drinks way too much coffee," Chloe said knowingly.

"I noticed. Ah, and there was one time when she asked me to stop at a bakery because she'd decided she wanted something sweet. I pulled over, she went inside, and a few minutes later she came back with a box of pastries. We drove for about thirty seconds before she suddenly looked down at the box and realized she hadn't paid for it."

Chloe's mouth dropped open. "She forgot to pay?!"

"Completely forgot. She made me turn the car around, marched back inside, apologized to the cashier, paid for everything, and then spent the next ten minutes complaining about how embarrassing the whole thing was."

"Did she at least give you a pastry?"

"She did, actually, said she'd wasted my time making me turn around, so she gave me one of the pastries as an apology."

Chloe grinned. "See? Mom's nice."

"She was," Greg admitted. "For all the ridiculous things she did, your mother was actually very considerate toward the people working around her. She remembered everyone's names, no matter how small their role was in the company."

Hearing that, Chloe frowned a little. "Oh, sorry again for forgetting who you were."

"Eh, it's all water under the bridge, so don't worry about it," Greg said with a shrug. "Besides, considering everything that's happened, I think you've got bigger things to worry about than whether you remember some guy who used to drive your mother around."

Before Chloe could answer, Liz casually joined the conversation. "After the new CEO took over, Greg quit Divinity Inc. A lot happened after that, and Percy and I eventually ran into him."

Greg snorted. "Yeah, and I've learned a surprising amount from those two since then. Liz taught me how to deal with all that fancy computer stuff, especially the software for spreadsheets and whatever else you people use to make numbers look important."

Chloe blinked. "Spreadsheets?"

"Apparently, being a chauffeur wasn't enough anymore. Somehow I ended up doing a bunch of administrative work, and suddenly I was expected to know how to use all these programs I'd never even heard of."

"Geez, what did you need spreadsheets for anyway?"

"To track uh… stuff, listen let's worry about the details later yea?"

Chloe was pretty sure that Greg, Liz, and Percy must have been involved in some shady stuff to make money, but they were also helping her out so… it's all probably all good. Besides, Mom did tell her a long time ago about this saying that was like, ignorance is good or something like that.

Greg then coughed into his hand. "Anyway, I also learned painting from Percy over there."

Chloe glanced toward Percy, who immediately looked away as though he'd suddenly become very interested in something on the opposite side of the room. She then looked back to Greg. "Wait, Percy taught you how to paint?"

"Yeah, it was alright, but I think Percy liked it a lot more than I did." Greg said.

"So you're a painter now?"

"Apparently. I'm a chauffeur, an administrative assistant, and an artist now. Quite the career progression, if you ask me."

Chloe giggled again, but her smile slowly faded as another thought came to her. There was something she'd been wanting to ask ever since they'd found him, something that suddenly felt much more important than spreadsheets or painting. She looked at Greg with a much more serious look on her face.

"Greg... this might be a long shot, but do you know where my mom is?"

Greg scratched his chin and thought about it for a moment before shaking his head. "Honestly? Not anymore. I've been locked up in this place for quite a while, so unless she's been hiding in a cell somewhere nearby, I don't have much idea where she is or what she's been doing."

Chloe's shoulders sank slightly. "But..."

Greg raised a finger. "Hold on, if you've got a phone or something I could borrow, I might be able to make a call."

Chloe looked up again. "To who?"

"The former CEO, duh, who else?"

"Wait just one moment, as far as I am aware, Lady Ishgria has been uncontactable for a very long time." Z said.

"Yeah, I figured that might be a problem, but it doesn't necessarily mean we can't reach her." Greg said.

Z looked at him skeptically. "And how exactly do you intend to accomplish that?"

Greg shrugged. "She has a lot of numbers here in Tartarus. Private ones, mostly. Numbers she only gives to people she actually trusts. I happen to have one of them."

Chloe's eyes widened. "You have Mom's number?!"

Greg gave her a tired smirk. "Among other things. Being a chauffeur has its perks."

Chloe stared at him for a moment before her excitement returned. "Greg, you might be the most useful chauffeur ever!"

"Hold your horses," Greg replied, raising a hand before Chloe could get carried away. "We're going to need a phone first."

"Oh. Right."

Z reached into her belongings and pulled out a small flip phone, holding it out toward Greg. "You can use mine, and don't worry, this one's a burner."

"Heh, someone like you has a burner phone?"

"I have my reasons."

"Sure, everybody does."

Despite his words, Greg took Z's burner phone, flipped it open, and squinted at the tiny screen for a moment before nodding. "Yeah, this should work."

Chloe glanced around at the others. Sierra was sitting quietly nearby, Raenof was leaning against the wall with his arms folded, Percy was still just kinda standing there, and Liz seemed perfectly content to watch Greg struggle with a phone. Chloe figured they might as well make sure nobody minded waiting.

"Are you guys all okay with hanging around for a little while? I mean, if Greg really can contact Mom, we probably shouldn't leave before we find out what she knows."

Sierra nodded almost immediately, her ears perking up with renewed interest. Raenof gave a lazy shrug, while Percy grunted. Liz folded her arms. "At this point, we're all kind of too invested to walk away now. Besides, after everything we've been through today, watching Greg try to remember a phone number is probably the most relaxing thing we've done."

Chloe giggled before turning back toward Greg, who was already hunched over Z's phone with an expression of intense concentration. "Okay, Greg, go ahead!"

"I am going ahead," he muttered. "I'm just trying to remember which number belongs to the Boss."

Chloe watched as Greg carefully punched in a series of numbers and pressed the call button. Everyone grew quiet as they waited. The phone rang for a few seconds, and then then someone answered.

"Hello?" said the voice from the phone.

Greg straightened slightly. "Boss?"

"Who is this?"

"It's Greg."

"Greg who?"

"The chauffeur."

"Umm, I'm sorry, but I think you've got the wrong number."

The call ended, and Chloe just shot a look at Greg. "Did you call the wrong number by accident?"

"Yeah, probably missed a number or two, I'll try again."

Greg looked down at the phone with a faint scowl before trying another number, and this one connected much faster. "Hello?"

"Boss?"

"What? Is this one of those scam calls I've been hearing about?" Said the voice from the phone, and Chloe guessed this one came from some older lady.

Greg closed his eyes for a moment before ending the call. Chloe's shoulders shook as she tried desperately not to laugh. "Greg, I don't think you're very good at this."

"Listen, I got it alright, lemme try again."

He scrolled through the phone again, muttering under his breath as he tried to reconstruct the number from memory. Another attempt resulted in someone asking whether he was trying to order a delivery, and the next somehow reached a very confused old man who insisted he had never heard of any "Ishgria" in her life. Chloe, however, was starting to get a little worried.

She leaned toward Greg and peered at the phone. "Greg, do you actually remember my mom's number? Because you've just been calling random people, and I'm starting to think we're going to run out of people in Tartarus before you find her."

Greg slowly lowered the phone and stared at the screen. For a while, he said nothing until his eyes finally widened a little. "Wait, wait... I think I've got it. I remember Boss's number."
