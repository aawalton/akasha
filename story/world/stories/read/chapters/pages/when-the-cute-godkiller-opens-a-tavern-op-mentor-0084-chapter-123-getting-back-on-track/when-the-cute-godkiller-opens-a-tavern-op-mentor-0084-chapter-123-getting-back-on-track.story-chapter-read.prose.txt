Chapter 123 - Getting Back On Track

”Why didn’t you tell us before?” Grace asked. Back then, she would’ve been more accusatory with her tone, but now the question was genuinely more to do with her curiosity, rather than throwing a tantrum about being left out. “About the hidden skills and spells, I mean.”

“Same reason why there isn’t a list or a guide that tells you exactly which ones to take as you progress your classes,” Shallies answered simply. “You’ll end up choosing skills you don’t actually want, and it’ll ruin your class. That’s why I give you as much time as you need to choose your skills, even if it takes a while. But you certainly didn’t take as long this time.”

With dinner done, the Chef, as always, was at the back of the bar, cleaning. She wasn’t even subtle about her mana-control this time around, utilizing it to such a level that it looked like she had invisible helpers. Still, Grace found it endearing that the Godkiller would still leave a few tasks for her hands to work on.

“I think knowing where I want to go helps a lot with deciding which to choose,” said the half-orc, keeping the rest of her thoughts to herself.

“Just don’t forget that the weaves you don’t use on yourself, you can use on other people, if you’re working with them.”

“Right. I figured that one out at the sub-raid. I gave Ren Strength and Tayin Focus. I’ll keep Mana-Affinity, Constitution, and Agility for myself.”

“Offensive and defensive. Very good,” Shallies said, holding up a finger. “Now imagine if I had told you about the hidden skills before you even got enough points to learn the weaves for each Attribute?”

Grace immediately answered that the Godkiller wasn’t casting any spell, rather than replying to the actual question. It was Shallies’ constant test to her students to exercise their awareness and alertness to gestures. The half-orc received the equivalent Good Job Experience points for answering correctly. The other two were too busy discussing something else, but the gesture got their attention.

“I might’ve skipped on those two weaves,” she said, answering honestly. “I get your point.”

“I would’ve probably skipped some of my passive skills too,” Ren admitted, joining the conversation.

“I got lucky, so I don’t have that problem,” Tayin added, smiling smugly. “Can’t wait to try this spell out, though.”

Arun’dul simply shrugged. He was in the middle of meditation. The traditional one. He sighed, opening one eye. “Please don’t expect me to contribute every time you say things in turn. It’s a bit tiring. And no, before you ask, I can barely meditate, let alone attempt the Perpetual Method.”

The three gave up, laughing their disappointment.

Grace turned back to Shallies. “Now what? We’re preparing for the tournament, right?”

“Yep. But it’s not going to happen so soon, since I’ll need to pull a lot of strings to organize this event. We have a few months to prepare.”

The half-orc gave her full attention. Tayin followed suit, and Ren even took out his trusty notebook.

“First things first, you need to take a challenging quest with Arun’dul. He can’t just join you in name, and you all need to re-align your teamwork as a four-man team.”

“We can close the dungeon in Rullzia Town,” Ren suggested.

“My thoughts exactly,” Grace added.

“Would that be challenging, though?” Tayin asked.

“It’ll be challenging enough,” Shallies said, nodding. “By now, the monsters inside that dungeon will have respawned. You can hunt everything, or go straight to the boss. I’ll leave that up to you. I won’t be joining this time, anyway. But do make sure to grab all the chuchilis you can get. Wouldn’t want to waste those.”

Almost immediately, Grace felt a switch click. Everyone was quick to make plans. Even Arun’dul stopped meditating and participated in the discussion. One of them would make a suggestion, then another would either clarify or create a counter-suggestion, but none of the topics ever degraded into an argument.

The half-orc only noticed she was holding a cup of hot chocolate in her hands half an hour into the discussion. She hadn’t even realized when Shallies had gone through each of them and had given them something to drink. By then, Arun’dul was in the middle of discussing his choice of skills and what he could offer to the group. He would always precede one of his Hero-tier skills with a disclaimer, that he would only use them if their survival depended on it, but he wasn’t afraid to share them all.

The details were ironed out by the time night had fully embraced Cinterbrim Province. Now that they had something to look forward to, the lulls that had appeared in the evenings since the end of the raid were gone once more. She felt anticipation and excitement for another adventure.

When she got home, her parents were there, looking somber. And her anticipation was quickly washed down with a wave of cold.

“You’re not going to talk me out of it, are you?” she asked, staying at the foyer.

“No, sweetheart,” Elesia said quickly. She rose from her chair and approached for an embrace.

“It’s just that the entire landscape has changed, ever since Shallies revealed herself,” Schooner added, joining them.

“You do know that mentioning her name brings her attention to you, right?” Grace asked, not resisting either embrace.

“I’m sure she’s honorable enough to let us keep our privacy.”

They invited her over to the living room. There were no drinks, since it was so late already.

“I’m fine, really,” Grace said, but she obeyed anyway. “We’ll be fine.”

“Any plans on the way, then?” her father inquired.

“We just need something to feel that she’s not throwing you into danger after danger, dear,” Elesia added. “Ironic, we know, but… There are steps to it, right?”

They were clearly worried. Grace did her best to understand that, and decided to humor them.

“Well, remember the thing about the kraken?” she started. “We haven’t actually closed that dungeon, yet. It was growing a bunch of chuchilis. It’s an ingredient that Shallies can use so that we can gain experience from defeating monsters quicker. We’ll be going back there to close it after the Gaiamaxims Guild’s farewell party. Other than that, we’re just going to continue with our training. Oh, and Arun’dul’s officially joining our party from now on. Nothing else besides those.”

The two looked at each other. They were clearly relieved, but Grace could feel like they were still hiding something.

“Is something wrong?”

“No, dear,” Elesia said. “After participating in a sub-raid, we thought Shallies might send you to even more dangerous places right away. But it looks like she has it all figured out. I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised…”

“We actually suggested that we close the dungeon,” Grace corrected, tilting her head. “But I guess she did plan for it ahead of time, now that you mention it…”

“Sorry for suddenly putting you on the spot like that, Grace,” Schooner said. “We’re very proud of you. If Shallies didn’t make us swear secrecy, the whole of Cinterbrim Province—no, the entire El’doron Kingdom would know about your mentorship with her by now. And the entire world by tomorrow!”

Grace shyly scratched the back of her head. “Geez, Dad. You’re overreacting.”

Schooner’s hearty, celebratory laugh was contagious. And Grace could finally feel the heavy atmosphere start to lighten. She couldn’t help but join him.

“Alright, that’s enough heart-to-heart talk,” said the Mayor, getting up from his chair. “The farewell party’s in two days, and we have a lot of things to prepare for the Gaiamaxims. We can’t just simply let them go after doing their best to protect our province.”

“Good night, honey,” Elesia said, joining her husband. “Don’t stay up too late.”

“Good night.”

Just like that, quiet returned to the room. Grace wasn’t unfamiliar with the silence. She had experienced all sorts of it. The good, the bad, and a lot of them in between. But this one felt warmer than most. She felt giddy at her father’s words, at her mother’s worry, and the fact that they were far from the constricting parents from when she was still a kid.

They were willing to talk now, to understand her side of things, her way of thinking. And even though it looked like they weren’t fully in agreement still, they were willing to let her prove to them that she could bear the burden.

In that silence, Grace felt like an adult.

A responsible one? Maybe not yet…

She wasn’t alone with her thoughts for very long. As if to respond to her question, a fiery creature flew into the living room. Chippy. The phoenix’s wings were oddly silent, as if he had been learning skills of his own. His chirps, however, were full of vigor and joy.

“You were busy all day, huh?” Grace said, giving her cradle companion some much missed snuggles.

Guildmaster Robert was finishing up some last minute papers, scattered documents and various reports that revolved around the Gaiamaxims Guild and the Thrush’ven Alir Raid. He wanted to be done with them before the farewell celebration, just to avoid any headaches.

Robert was old. Not ancient, but he was married to his job. And while his physique could easily match any veteran adventurer out there, at least for an hour or two, it was his features currently buried in stacks of papers that could tell stories of his vast experience.

As a normal adventurer and Guildmaster, of course.

His tired eyes settled on reports regarding three particular individuals. Four, technically, but reports regarding Shallies could never amount to the actual truth behind the person. For now, he reviewed those from Grace, Ren, and Tayin.

Robert hadn’t expected for the half-orc to join the original trio that included the giantkin, Gar. He had held great, but tempered expectations for them, up until the noldzwjak poisoning had almost killed that dream. He regretted not being there for them when it had happened.

At the same time, he was thankful of Shallies’ timing. And fast forwarding to now, within just a few months, he could barely recognize the new trio.

“Guess that’s the Godkiller’s mentorship for you,” he mumbled.

As he signed off the confirmation for the reports, his System Console opened up with an urgent notification.

[You’ve received an urgent message from Godkiller Bernadette. Please see to it immediately.]

With a sigh, and even though he already knew it was coming, Robert stopped what he was doing and read the message.

Guildmasters,

It is with a heavy heart that I bring you this message today. Upon settling the events of Thrush’ven Alir, it has been discovered that an evil goddess has been plotting to return to Ysvil.

Thus, I shall be calling the Pantheon to sponsor the next tournament for our future heroes, and prepare them for the Divine War.

I trust that you will uphold your oaths and vows imposed upon you by your duties, and that this information will be strictly forbidden from spreading upon further notice. Should you have suggestions of adventurers of your own, there are many gods and goddesses willing to take on last minute representations. Take the necessary steps as you see fit.

Further details of the tournament, including the name, time, and location, will be sent to you soon, once I’ve ironed out the details with the other sponsors. It may take three months, or as much as half a year. Preparation regardless of time constraints will be part of the challenge. And I expect that all of you will be available to help out.

As for when the actual Divine War will happen, there’s no guarantee. Only know that we’re pressed for time.

Until then,B.

“Can’t believe I’ll have two in my lifetime…” Robert sighed.

While he had lived through the last Divine War, he had never been directly involved with it. Still, there were preparations on his end that must be done to accommodate the tournament or anything else leading up to the war.

After deleting the Godkiller’s message, he started making one for his assistant.

“Rio. I don’t suppose you can stand-in for me, no?” he mumbled to himself. He quickly shook his head and rewrote his message. “Maybe not…”
