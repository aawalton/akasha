The dream of recent events felt too real. Acrypha really didn’t need to rewind the past yet again, but everything was murky. She could hardly remember the now. The past was all she had.

Curiosity was said to have killed the cat. Acrypha had envied cats, always believing that cats had it so easy.

She hadn’t envied their fatal impulses, however, yet that was what she got, apparently.

The priestess had never truly intended to help her. Oh, she hadn’t forced anything on Acrypha or had tried to kidnap her forcefully or anything of the sort. Otherwise, she’d have fought back. And with the help of [Afterlife Guardians], she’d have won handily too.

But the woman had been smart. She had gone after Acrypha’s emotions.

“You see the truth now, Acrypha?” the priestess had asked. “This is why I was so interested. Why I wanted to call you over. Because…”

“Because I might be the only one who can help you.”

The priestess had quickly shaken her head. “You may be the only one who can help this strange situation.”

Acrypha hadn’t needed to agree. She wasn’t at all obligated to assist some random woman in a world where civilization was collapsing. Unlike Viren, her heart wasn’t made of solid gold.

But there had been a sweetener she had been unable to refuse. The mystery of how and why Living Mana had infused with her spell, with the guardians that Ryland called Manifestations, was something she had been hoping to solve for a long time now. And if she helped this woman and everything going on with the Living Mana, she could find the answer.

She might just be able to finally let her parents’ spirits rest in peace.

Of course, Acrypha had only agreed to help once she was let in on the whole thing. The way everything was being kept secret, it all appeared too suspicious and almost conspiratorial. It turned out the truth was even worse.

“He’s going to do what?” Acrypha asked in sheer disbelief.

“Yes.” The priestess was nodding in devout conviction. “The Great Demiurge, Kindly Mavar the Eternal, is determined to end the threat of Living Mana in the entire cosmos. It’s why he’s emptying Rhel as we speak, so that any fallout from his sacrifice doesn’t affect the poor, innocent Rhellians.”

Acrypha’s mind boggled at the revelation. That their entire trip to Rhel had all been part of a plan to not only get the Rhellians start to integrate with people from other Realms after their centuries-long isolation was one thing.

That its actual reasoning was the potential destruction of Rhel itself as a sacrifice that would end Living Mana as well was…

She wasn’t sure what to think of it. Acrypha had pondered about Living Mana for a while now, and there were some troubling realizations she couldn’t fully gel with. Even now. Living Mana was, well, a living thing. Sort of. And like most living things, it wanted to keep living. Keep surviving. It contained the primal drive to ensure the continuation of its species.

No wonder, then, that it despised mages who used mana as fuel for their magic. No wonder it always tended to attack its perceived enemy.

But at the same time, thinking of it as a mere living creature wasn’t quite correct. It wasn’t a rational, thinking being. Not at all. Its existence went beyond entities like humans or even manabeasts.

Living Mana was a catastrophe given a mockery of life.

Whatever the case, though Acrypha had agreed to help the priestess, she wasn’t stupid. Before she went anywhere or did anything, the priestess and any related authorities would need to inform the Arcoryx contingent of her whereabouts, especially Marionys Ryland. They really wouldn’t want Ryland to come hunting after her in worry.

The priestess had readily agreed. She had already started using Glyphs of Communication she possessed to contact everyone related to the business. Everyone she had to speak with was on the woman’s list of communiques.

Except, there was a slight issue. The very first person the priestess contacted turned out to be the highest authority in the Realm of Rhel.

The Demiurge.

Acrypha was minorly alarmed that Mavar Val Arona himself was not only invested in the matter—which made sense, she supposed—but that he had it so far up in his list of priorities that this random priestess would inform him of this random strangeness with Living Mana with some random girl.

Her alarm then turned to outright dread when the Demiurge chose to appear himself. Acrypha could do nothing but stare for a moment.

“You’re here… in person?” she asked. Her etiquette training kicked in, and she quickly remembered her manners. “Your Eternity?”

She couldn’t figure out how exactly he had come into their midst. At least with Ryland, it was very evident in how he used his rifts. But Mavar Val Arona had just chosen to appear out of nowhere. She hadn’t even seen the flash of a spell circle.

Wait… no, it couldn’t be. Had he used his infamous Soul Glyph somehow?

He smiled down at her as the priestess fell to her knees in supplication. There was no malice or avarice or anything actually concerning in his expression. Yet Acrypha had a feeling this meeting didn’t exactly bode well for her. “As soon as I received that intriguing communique, I knew I had to visit in person.”

There was so much she could have said in response to that, especially at seeing his avid interest. His Realm was literally collapsing. The civilization this ancient mage had supposedly been working towards for centuries now was on the brink of destruction.

And it was all because of him. Because he was a controlling freak who had tricked an entire populace into believing he needed a backdoor into their very souls.

That was it. That was the same thing she saw in his eyes. Acrypha might have gotten too motivated by the lure of discovering just what was happening with her parents and the Living Mana infesting them, but that didn’t mean she was going to fall for whatever the Demiurge was intent on doing.

“You had to visit someone like me,” Acrypha said, summoning her courage in the face of someone who could probably wipe her out with next to no effort. It was strange how much it felt like standing before Ryland, except with him, she never recalled needing to feel any true fear. “Right now, Your Eternity? When Rhel is falling apart?”

“You do not mince your words, do you?”

She recalled saying something that had everyone at the Manabreak mine staring at her like she had just committed treason. “I don’t have a good track record, no.”

He laughed. It didn’t really set her at ease, despite his seeming affability. “Let me see if I understand correctly. Your parents left a bit of their manasouls with you when they Inscribed their Soul Glyph of Legacy upon you. And now, that Soul Glyph is infested with Living Mana.”

Acrypha slowly nodded. “That’s right, Your Eternity.”

“Hmm.” He took on an academic cast, his look turning far-away and thoughtful. “Have you considered that what the Living Mana actually infested was your parents’ partial manasouls?”

“You mean like the manasoul fragments created by the Living Mana you’re conducting through the Crypts?”

The priestess gasped a little at those words. Right. Acrypha had a reputation to maintain.

“Indeed,” Mavar said. The way he spoke sent a shiver down Acrypha’s spine that she was barely able to suppress. It had sounded so eerily like Ryland himself speaking. Now she understood what Viren meant whenever he said that she sounded like Ryland. “I am amazed you’ve already figured it out.”

“It wasn’t me, really, Your Eternity. It was Marionys Ryland.”

There. She had brought up her insurance policy. That should keep the Demiurge off her back.

Acrypha was well aware by now that Ryland had a rather complicated history on Rhel. But more than that, his reputation was far more renowned here than it had been on Vyrd. The people here knew him for the inconceivably powerful mage that he was. That meant they’d be wary of doing anything to offend him, like whisking someone like Acrypha away.

But Acrypha’s words only made the Demiurge look at her even more acutely. “Thank you for the confirmation… Acrypha, yes? I had suspected as much, since the last incident, but I wasn’t quite certain. This proves it, however.”

Alarm bells gonged in Acrypha’s head to signal the approach of a catastrophe. She didn’t need her training as a noble’s daughter to figure that she had made a wild miscalculation. Informing the Demiurge that she had Ryland’s backing was all well and good.

But confirming what sounded like his fears had just convinced the calculating part of him that he needed to act.

That he needed his own insurance, of a sort.

“[After—”

Acrypha’s cast froze. Her lips and tongue wouldn’t move, her chest felt like it was trying to draw breath while submerged underwater, and of the entirety of her body, her eyelids were the only thing she could move freely.

A brief flash had indicated the Demiurge’s spell going off. She had only been able to note in horror that it was Tier [25] at least.

Mavar Val Arona tutted. “You need not fear anything, Acrypha. Haven’t you always wanted to unearth the mysteries of Living Mana? Haven’t you always wished for it to stop eradicating mages all across the Realms?” His pearlescent gaze hardened a little. “Well, I do.”

The pressure lessened, enough for Acrypha to speak. She knew if she tried casting any spell again, she’d suffer the same. Silence was all she’d get. Maybe permanently this time. Panic was trying to claw up her throat like bile, but she forced it down to think.

To act rationally.

“What do you want with me, Your Eternity?” she asked. It was easy to show fear. She couldn’t win against him, and though she might have summoned the courage to face him anyway, losing pointlessly wouldn’t help her in the long run. What she needed was to try to salvage something from this. “Please, I’ve done nothing to anyone. I never asked for this.”

For a moment, the Demiurge’s expression broke into pity and sympathy. It didn’t look fake. Then he very overtly steeled himself against it. “We all have roles to play, Acrypha. When it came down to it, did your parents not sacrifice themselves for the greater good? When it became inevitable, did I not surrender my entire civilization?”

Acrypha swallowed. “That’s—”

“You of all people should know and understand, Acrypha. The world, the cosmos itself, advances on the backs of those who can sacrifice all they hold dear.”

“But that requires consent! That requires—”

“That requires an understanding. Which is what I’m trying to do right this moment. Even now, when I could simply abduct you away this instant, I am trying to get you onboard while taking up what little time we have left. So please, Acrypha, come with us. Help us stop the Living Mana doing to others what it has to you. To your life. To your family.”

Acrypha swallowed again. Poisoned honey. That was essentially what he was feeding her. “Then why won’t you let me inform anyone?”

“Because doing so will end up with us losing what little time we have left. I’m afraid I’ve coddled you for long enough, Acrypha. It’s time we left.”

“So you’re alright with killing me, just like that?”

“I have killed far more and far worse.”

She managed to twist her head to look at the kneeling priestess with her head bowed. The woman hadn’t said a single thing yet. Not even once. Maybe it made sense, considering the priests and priestesses might still be under the Demiurge’s thrall, but Acrypha failed to think about it just then. “And you’re perfectly fine with that?”

“If that is what His Eternity deems necessary,” the woman said. “Then that is what shall be done.”

“Good,” Mavar said. “Then let us depart.”

He did cast a spell then, but Acrypha couldn’t remember what it was. Her dream collapsed, and reality returned.

The world had changed. Everything really was murky and distorted. She felt full in a way she could barely fathom just then, her heart and her lungs working so tremendously slowly that she felt as though she should have died. Just like when that cast-less spell from the Demiurge hit her, she still couldn’t move.

But one thing steadied her against fully giving into the panic.

Viren.

She couldn’t fathom how or when or why—well, no, the why was pretty obvious because this was Viren—not when she was barely capable of considering herself still living and sentient. But Viren and his flames were eating away at whatever strangeness was keeping her trapped.

His words reached her, bubbling and jumbled and barely audible.

“Don’t worry,” he said. “I found some schematics that show how to break you open. I just need to break this extra locking Glyph and then you’ll be free!”

Acrypha barely understood all that he had said. She had no choice but to put her faith in Viren, but all things told, she couldn’t really think of anyone else save Ryland she’d have believed in just then.

Viren was proficient at best when compared to Acrypha, but his willpower went far above her own.

To her great surprise, and great relief, Viren managed it. Her strange liquid cage suddenly began steaming. It grew unbearably hot and her whole body was wracked with burning shivers, but there was a sound accompanying it that tamped down her rising panic. Something was sliding open and Viren’s excited voice came in unfiltered.

And then she was collapsing on the floor with a flood of the strange liquid surrounding her, Viren desperately trying to prop her up while trying not to succumb to the little deluge overwhelming them both.

“I’ve got you,” he said over and over. “Don’t you worry. You’re safe now.”

Acrypha’s thoughts were almost completely concentrated on the fact that she was somehow still alive. “Th-thank y-you.”

“You’re welcome. Now, you wouldn’t happen to know the way out, would you?”

Acrypha managed a shake of her head. It took a good few minutes for her to start feeling like herself. Every moment, she felt like hurling. She had thought she’d be wet, that her hair and clothes would cling to her and leave her a mess, but the strange liquid had sloughed off without leaving any real mark.

Apart from the psychological one where she had felt like she had been drowning constantly. She hated it, but she couldn’t stop thinking about it either, so she was of little to no help to Viren, which just made her feel even worse.

How could she allow herself to be so useless?

“…can’t believe it wasn’t harder,” Viren was saying. From what she gathered, he was talking about how he had freed her. “Maybe he had expected the barrier outside the moon would be the main thing preventing anyone from getting this far inside, so he didn’t bother with a more complicated Glyph or Inscription.”

It was bothersome that she had escaped rather easily. Acrypha was managing to get to her feet now. She was trying desperately not to think about her experiences from a few minutes ago. It was difficult, but she’d manage once she figured out how to—

Everything shook hard. Cracks started fracturing across the surface of the enormous chamber they were in.

“Great,” Acrypha said. “Just great.”

Out of the frying pan and into the fire. At this point, after what she had just gone through, she really couldn’t muster up any further panic and worry. If she died now, she’d at least die on her feet this time.

Of course, the same couldn’t be said for Viren. He was running around everywhere, cursing and panicking, fingers pointing at the sealed doorway he was trying to cast his spells against.

“Do you know if you triggered some sort of Inscription or Glyph?” Acrypha asked when she dragged herself over to him.

“I’m not the brilliant magical academy student,” he said, desperately trying to melt his way through the door. It wasn’t working. “You are!”

A large chunk of debris crashed down next to them, and they both jumped.

“We’re going to die!” Viren said.

Acrypha didn’t think it was that bad, all things considered. Her perspective had probably shifted extremely wildly over the last day or however long she had been trapped by the Demiurge. Speaking of whom, where was—

Her eyes caught the strange liquid moving. On its own.

“Viren…”

The warning in Acrypha’s voice drew his attention away, and his mouth gaped as he stared at what she had indicated.

The Living Mana was climbing and taking over the entire chamber. It really was alive. It was the Living Mana itself that was destroying the moon.

For the first time since her “resurrection”, Acrypha almost felt awful. Her entrapment had been keeping the Living Mana at bay somehow. And now that she was free, now that Viren had broken whatever locking Inscription keeping that pod-like cage closed, the Living Mana was free to act as it saw fit too.

Right that moment, it saw fit to end the moon itself.

“[Afterlife Guardians],” she cast.

Magic sizzled out of Acrypha as her manasoul turned into a churning generator inside her. Had it somehow evolved and grown even stronger?

The Living Mana surged at the spell, as if Acrypha had empowered it too, somehow. She scowled. Wasn’t whatever the Demiurge was trying to do supposed to have freed her parents’ spirits? But where were they? All she saw was the Living Mana gathering closer, surging around her in a storm of energetic power.

“Acrypha…” It was Viren’s turn to send in a warning.

Acrypha blinked several times. The Living Mana… She looked up, noted the form the surging pearlescent colours were taking. Her parents’ spirits in the form of protective guardians were back.

Except, they were gigantic. Monstrous. Evolved.
