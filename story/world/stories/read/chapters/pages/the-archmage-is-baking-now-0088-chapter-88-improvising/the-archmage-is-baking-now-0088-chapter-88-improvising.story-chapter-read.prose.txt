The royal carriage was nothing like the wagon Jane had taken to Willowbrook. Four horses pulled it instead of one, and they moved at a pace that ate up the miles. Combined with the wider downhill road, they made good time.

Jane sat across from Captain Harris, watching the landscape for signs of the missing duo. They had been on the road for two hours now. The guards who accompanied them rode in formation behind the carriage, a significant military force that Jane hoped she wouldn’t need.

The captain had been quiet for most of the journey. Now he leaned in, finally breaking the silence with yet unanswered questions.

"Miss Jane," he said. "I have to admit, I know little of magic. I don't understand how it can help us here. Is there some spell that finds people?"

Jane shook her head. "It's not that simple. There's no spell I can cast that will simply tell us where they are."

"Then how can you help?"

"I'll have to improvise." Jane looked out the window at the passing trees. There was nothing obvious to indicate anything had passed through them. If there had been, she supposed, the prince would already be found. "We'll need to stop occasionally. I can try to look for traces of their passing, I think. I might be able to find something your riders missed."

Jane turned her attention inward, thinking about what tools she actually had available for this kind of search. Telling the captain there might be a way was a lot different than actually devising a method. To do that, she had to look deep into all the tools available to her, and then see what might fit.

There were spells that looked for echoes of people, though they were vastly different than what she needed here. She had used something similar once before, back when she was first learning to make keln. The shade of the baker who had invented the recipe had appeared to her, summoned by Jane's intent and the woman's lingering connection to the home she had lived in.

That spell wasn't really meant for this. It was a specifically powerful spell meant to last a long time. She’d need just as much power, focused in a different way and looking for an echo of passage much weaker than that left in a home by its occupant. She’d have to improvise.

The design for the spell began to take shape in her mind as the miles rolled by, built of bits and pieces of this and that. This was, in the end, a big part of what made her an archmage and not simply a very good mage. She was beyond the need for books, in most cases, on to writing new magic of her own.

The design wasn’t the only difficulty. It would also be draining. A spell like this would be messy and would pull heavily on her reserves, which put her at risk for using all her magical power before she found anything useful. It was going to be a slapped together, dicey sort of thing, but it might work. It was the best idea she had, anyway.

Finally, when the sun had begun its descent toward the horizon, Jane called for the carriage to stop.

"Here?" The captain looked out at the plain, unremarkable road. "Why here?"

"If something had happened any closer to Glenfall, they would have just walked back by now." Jane opened the carriage door and stepped down onto the packed dirt. "This is roughly the point where they would have been too far to turn around. I know. I walked this same road on foot. If something went wrong, it likely happened here or further."

The captain followed her out of the carriage, watching as she walked from the road and knelt in the grass. The guards formed a loose perimeter around them, as if Jane was the most important person in the kingdom. She supposed at that moment she just might be.

Jane pulled small steel tool from her pouch and began to etch the ground. It didn’t take long. She modified and edited as she went, making what small adjustments occurred to her as she worked as quickly as her skills would allow. When the circle was complete, she placed her palms flat against its edge and began to push magic into it.

The drain was immediate and severe. Jane felt the power rushing out, and though she had been intellectually prepared for the drain, it was nothing compared to feeling it. She gritted her teeth and held on. It was only a matter of waiting, now, and of tolerating the discomfort.

Finally, the circle charged in earnest, and the world above the road flickered.

Suddenly, the road was alive with motion. Ghostly figures moved along it, overlapping and blurring into each other. Jane saw merchants with carts, farmers, and even families walking together toward destinations she couldn't see. They moved in all directions at once, a jumble of motion and faces it was hard to see anything distinct in.

And there, among the whirl of activity, she saw them.

Dave's carriage was familiar. She had spent so much time in it, the vehicle could hardly be missed. He sat in the driver's seat while Brand sat beside him. Brand was laughing at something. They were heading away from Glenfall, toward the merchant's destination. Nothing about them suggested they had seen any trouble.

"Miss Jane?" The captain's voice pulled her out of her reverie as the image finally faded. "Are you all right?"

"I'm fine." Jane forced herself to stand. "It's not here. Whatever happened to them, it happened further down the road. They passed this point safely."

The captain had seen the same vision she had seen. It wasn’t a private vision. There was little to disagree with.

He nodded. "Then we keep going."

"I'll need to rest before I can do that again. The spell is draining. But we should cover as much ground as we can in the next half hour."

The captain nodded again and began issuing orders to his men. They were back on the road within minutes.

Jane sat by the captain again, each of them wordlessly taking a side of the road to monitor. The drain from the spell was still burning her, making every one of her muscles ache slightly as her headache began to support that pain behind like a rear-guard. She knew it would pass, but for now, she had to do her best to refill her magic and get ready for the next round

"Where did you learn to do something like that? Was it the academy?" The captain scratched his chin. “I haven’t seen them do anything like that in their demonstrations.”

It was a reasonable thing to ask, Jane supposed. It wasn’t quite a sensical question if someone knew enough about magic, but in the whole kingdom there were only a handful of people who knew that much. For anyone else, ‘Did you learn it from your teachers?’ was close enough to a valid question that it was hard to explain why it wasn’t an exact match.

"Sort of.”

The captain raised an eyebrow. "Sort of?"

"The academy taught me everything I know about magic, in some ways. Every fundamental bit of knowledge I have came from them, with just a few very specific exceptions." Jane shifted in her seat, trying to find a more comfortable position that put less strain on her magic-abused body. "Without that foundation, I couldn't do anything at all. So in that sense, yes, it was the academy."

"It seems like there’s more coming.”

There was. In search of a way to explain things, Jane's eyes drifted to the captain's waist, where his sword hung in its scabbard. It was a simple thing, a soldier’s sword with little in the way of decoration on it. It looked like he used it frequently.

"Do you spar?" she asked. “With your sword, I mean.”

The captain jolted a bit at the change in subject but seemed content to follow along..

"Yes. Regularly."

"Where did you learn to fight? Originally, I mean."

"An older guard taught me when I was young. He'd been protecting the royal family for thirty years by the time I joined the guards. I built on what he taught me, sparring and learning from anyone else that could spare the time. Other guards, mercenaries. That sort.”

"That's a lot of practice."

"Thousands of hours. Everything I am as a fighter came from that time."

"Is that all you need to know, then?" Jane nudged the conversation in the direction she had in mind. "To beat someone new? Say you sparred with someone who fights in an odd way.”

The captain thought about it. Jane could almost see him cycling through memories of fights long past, comparing his impressions with his actual recollections.

"No," he said finally. "I have to improvise, then. It’s like fighting a very large man. You have to adjust, or he’ll beat you with his reach and strength alone.”

"And if you couldn't do that, I’d bet you’d lose." Jane really knew nothing about swordfighting, but she bet it was close enough to other things she did know about to fit some patterns. “You probably wouldn’t be the captain of the guard.”

"Oh, I don’t know. There's a lot to guarding the king that doesn't involve fighting. Logistics and command skills, that sort of thing. But the guards who can't improvise in an actual fight tend to stay pretty low-ranked, yes. More often than not, that reveals a simple mind.”

"Magic is a lot like that. There are many very good mages who do exactly what they were taught to do. They learn a spell from a book, they practice it, and they make a living casting it. It’s simple."

"That doesn't sound simple to me."

"It isn't really. I don't mean to dismiss it. Some of those mages are masters of a niche. They can do things I can’t, or do them better, sometimes." Jane paused, organizing her thoughts. "But most of what being an archmage requires involves either overwhelming power, an ability to improvise, or both. It’s like your sparring. There might be people who can do things you can’t, but it’s the man who adjusts that wins most often."

"You have both?" the captain asked. “The power and the flexibility?”

"It’s not quite that simple, but yes. My aunt, for instance, is much more powerful than I am. She’d be an archmage by virtue of that alone, if it came down to it. I’m a bit more able to improvise than she is, I think. Not so powerful, but close, where she’s not so flexible, but quite flexible compared to some." Jane smiled before looking out the window again, renewing her watch for her friend and her nation’s only prince. “That’s why I can make up a spell like this, and also why I have trouble coming up with the wherewithal to actually cast it.”

"How are you feeling, by the way?” I know you said the spell was draining."

"Better than I was, but not fully recovered yet. I should be able to cast again soon."

"Don't hurt yourself, if that’s possible. We need you."

"I know."

She went back to watching the road for a while, and was gratified when it only took a few more minutes to recover enough to be ready for another casting. She had been closer than she thought, and would be able to cast the spell a little more often than she had planned.

"Stop here."

The carriage slowed and came to rest. The captain offered her a hand, which she took gratefully, using it as balance as she let herself down to the ground. Her legs were steadier than they had been, but she still felt wobbly enough that twisting an ankle or tripping was a real possibility.

She drew the circle more quickly this time. She was a bit more familiar with how the spell felt, now, and was able to meter her power a little better as she let it loose. The drain was still unpleasant, but it hurt less, and even if the spell seemed a bit weaker, it reached acceptable levels with much less personal cost.

The images were hazier this time, but she spotted what she was looking for. Dave's carriage rolled past at a healthy pace, not looking distressed or in any way like it was under attack.

They made it past this point too, and they were just fine.

Jane released the spell and let it fade, then pushed herself back to her full height.

"They came through here," she said. "Still traveling safely, as far as I can tell. Whatever happened to them, it happened further along."

"Then we keep moving."

"We keep moving."

The captain helped her back into the carriage, and then they were rolling again. Jane focused on her recovery. Improving her control over the spell didn’t mean she’d be using less magic, really, just using it more often. She needed to concentrate on keeping her strength up.”

Hold on, Dave. Hold on, Brand. I'm coming.

The pattern repeated itself three more times over the next hour. Each time she learned a little about the spell, which was just enough to keep her from becoming actively nauseous from the building effort of mustering that much power.

"Miss Jane?" The captain's voice was concerned as he helped her to her feet the third time. "Are you all right?"

"Fine." She wasn't fine, but she would be. "I just need a moment."

The moment stretched into several minutes. When Jane finally felt steady enough to stand under her own power, the sun was close to the horizon. They had maybe an hour of daylight left, give or take, and she wanted to make the most of every moment.

"We should keep moving," Jane said. "I'll be ready to cast again soon."

She started toward the carriage, but the captain stepped into her path.

"Miss Jane." His voice was gentle but firm. "You’ve hit your limit. Even I can see that.”

Jane felt a flash of irritation, not helped at all by her fatigue. "I think I can manage."

"No," the captain said. "You can't. And even if you could, searching at night is a fool's errand. We’ll miss things.”

"It's urgent," Jane said. "Every hour matters, doesn’t it?"

“Urgent and careful have to live in balance with each other, Jane.” The captain was gentle, but she could tell he was speaking from experience. It was hard to deny he had lived much more life than she had. “If it's important enough to be urgent, it's also important to do it right. We'll rest. I have to insist.”

The captain was at least right that she couldn't keep this up all night, no matter how much she wanted to believe otherwise. Taking an honest look at her condition drove the point in even more. She was dangerously close to a more severe kind of magical over-draw.

"Fine," she said. "But we start again at first light."

"Of course. There's an inn not far from here," the captain walked towards the carriage, waiting patiently to help Jane back into it. "We’ll be there in some minutes. In the meantime, please rest.”
