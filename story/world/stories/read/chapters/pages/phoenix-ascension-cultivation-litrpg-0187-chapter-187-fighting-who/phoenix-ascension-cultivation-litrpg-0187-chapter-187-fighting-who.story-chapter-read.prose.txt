Powerful swords had spirits. To put it better, powerful things had a spirit. A spirit could be naturally born within, honed by the item as time passed, or it could have been nurtured by the cultivator.

Spirits were used to control the weapon, or realm, or both in this case. If a sword had a spirit, then it was that much more valuable to the average cultivator.

Of course, not all spirits were made or born equal. Just as the stubborn sword within my dantian had a spirit, so did this one

Apparently its name was the Yin Despair Sword, which was… a name.

I wasn’t going to judge, considering what mine was called.

Anyway, that wasn’t the point here. The point was, the spirit had a large amount of control over the thing it possessed, so to put it bluntly, I was standing face to face with the God of this realm.

A fat rat.

“Hello,” I said without much excitement. I never liked dealing with these things; it was always annoying, and most of the time they were whiny little shits.

“Hello, uhm, Phoenix? I… This wasn’t a situation the automatic spirit was trained for, so I had to step in. Please forgive my rudeness, but how did you get here?”

“It’s a long story; it doesn’t matter. Let’s just get this over with. I’ll give you the short of it. I am a Phoenix; I can cultivate; I have used a Yin cultivation technique; I gained a spot; I am here for Yin stuff for someone else.”

“O…kay… Well, I can’t detect any lies from you, and you do indeed have a cultivation base. There isn’t much I can say to that; you’ve entered here fair and square, although…” The fat rat looked back at the islands, and then at my wings.

“It’s not cheating, it’s a technicality,” I explained simply, proudly waving my wings.

“You… are way too smug.” The sword spirit said, but didn’t refute the facts. “Alright, with a completion time of five seconds, that puts you… in second place!”

“What?!” I was flabbergasted to hear that. “Then what time did the first place have? Who was that guy?”

“Ah, that doesn’t matter anymore. It’s a time long gone, but he’d become the owner of this sword you’ve entered.”

“I thought he was the one who had grown this sword.”

“Well, he had grown with the sword, but the sword was already powerful. It did improve by leaps and bounds once he caught up… That’s not important. This second place won’t matter much except that it will be a point multiplier at the end of the realm, if you make it that far.”

“Huh? What do you mean? This place and the overall realm are connected?”

“But of course. Now, this first trial wasn’t conceived for a completion time shorter than ten seconds because it was made for humans, not freaks like that guy, or Spirit Beasts that cheat!”

He was clearly still pretty pissed at the fact that I’d flown over it, and that was exactly the kind of behaviour I was tired of from these spirits.

“Right, so can I go? There’s much time left, and I want as many things as I can so I can finish the qu… questionnaire.”

“Questionnaire? Do you think I was born yesterday- I-.” The rat sighed. “Sure, go forward.” He stepped out of the way and allowed me to go through the second door, and I caught the first of his annoyed grumblings before disappearing into the next trial.

“Would my next master be that bird… The heavens have truly abandoned me…”

That was a rude comment as far as I was concerned, but it didn’t matter.

The next trial was a small, cozy room, filled to the brim with Yin-type plants.

In the middle of the room was a giant furnace, and there was a spot specifically designed for someone to sit at.

I did as much, and a recipe appeared in my mind alongside the monotone, mechanical voice of the sword spirit.

“Welcome to the second trial! Any cultivator needs to have a good foundation in Qi and Spirit, but knowledge is just as, if not more, important! Any respectable cultivator should be able to refine a pill in case of an emergency, and… this is the trial! Your test will be refining a Qi Condensation pill specifically designed for Ghost cultivators using the recipe given to you. The final score will be rated based on technique, accuracy, and purity. Good luck!”

Well, refinement was something I was good at. But for it to say that any cultivator should also double as an alchemist… It was laughable. Even when I was at the peak, there still weren’t that many alchemists per thousand cultivators.

It was a ruthless, money-eating, penny-pinching, life-throwing profession that only a few could pursue.

It made me wonder if this sword was made before even the world I used to live in.

The world I’d failed in…

Putting those thoughts aside, I paid attention to the recipe. It was similar to a normal Qi Condensation Pill recipe, but instead of a mix of Yin and Yang plants, there were only Yin plants.

Which was fair enough.

On top of that, though, one also had to use something called a ‘Ghost Flame’, which I did not have, but was kindly provided underneath the furnace. I just had to control it with Yin Qi.

I couldn’t lie, there was a bit of excitement in me, as refining pills was a guilty pleasure of mine.

And now I had the chance of trying it out using a new technique I’d never used before.

There were a total of three batches, so three tries to get it right.

Well, I only needed one! I was pretty sure, anyway.

Without going too deep into the details, I first played with the flame and got the inside of the furnace to an adequate temperature, and then maintained it for five minutes.

This first temperature was for the plants that needed a higher one to properly break down, and I would lower it as the plants' needs changed.

After the first batch of Yin plants turned into some sort of silver liquid, I held it into a ball form and used the second ingredient, which turned into a golden liquid.

All of the impurities escaped in the form of smoke; my control over the fire was so precise that it only attacked the parts I didn’t need.

After lowering the temperature a third time, it was now cool enough for the liquids to become viscous, which meant that it was almost time to combine them all into a pill.

Throwing in the last hand of ingredients, I made it so they mixed together with the first two right after becoming pure liquid.

The cooler and hotter liquids mixed into a pill of a beautiful, cold silver color, about as big as a baby’s fist.

However, just as they came together, they split apart into harmonious, equally sized pills. There were about six of them, all perfect.

The refinement was a success!

I’d taken them out with care and placed them inside a provided jade bottle, the fragrance escaping before it was fully sealed.

The automatic voice sounded out once again as I’d placed the cap over the bottle, and the entire thing disappeared.

“Congratulations! You have scored full marks for speed, quality, quantity, technique and purity! You may move forward with this trial.”

That was quick, at least. It analyzed the pills extremely fast, though, and the only thing I regretted was the fact that it didn’t return them.

But now I knew how to refine these types of pills, and it wouldn’t be too far-fetched to modify the other recipes as well.

Too bad I didn’t have them in my memory anymore…

Regardless, I got up and entered through another door.

When my eyes saw this place, I almost couldn’t believe it.

It was deep in the middle of the void, a space I’d never been to before.

In front of me, probably thousands of kilometers away, were two figures.

One was yall, thin and lean, wearing a fur cloak that was open just enough to show his abs, and his long hair flowed down, tied in a ponytail.

He was wielding a long spear, and some sort of purple crystal was floating around him.

The other person was who I assumed to be the former owner of this sword. A man of average stature with a bulky build and short hair. He didn’t look like a ghost at all!

In his hand was a smaller version of the very same sword I was in, or maybe they were just that big; who was to say?

It mattered little.

I’d guessed the point of the trial before the announcer ever told me.

“This third trial will be one of the mind! This is a sequence from the owner of this sword, who had fought against a Calamity, and won! Allegedly. This is just what the sword could capture in that moment.

“The first strike, and arguably, the most important one. If you can get through the after-effects of the attack without fainting, you would be considered as having passed.

“The intensity has been reduced, of course, since the shockwave from that attack alone was enough to destroy the universe they were in. Oops…”

And with that, the voice disappeared, and my heart was racing with excitement.

This was a level of power I’d had before; otherwise, I would not have dared to do what I’d done, but there have been very few instances where I could gain enlightenment from it.

And now this opportunity was handed to me on a silver platter! For all intents and purposes, I was still at the beginning of my cultivation journey, which meant that I still had a lot of places where I could improve.

Being able to watch such a clash was an honor that was hard to describe in words.

I waited, and soon, the two moved. The actions were slowed down to an incredible degree, just enough for me to see just how simply they moved. The concept of daos had long since disappeared from their being; they were now the embodiment of it. Each movement they made created a new done, and each new one destroyed them.

The universe bent to their will as space disappeared, and a distance of several hundred thousand kilometers became nothing as they charged and…

Finally, they clashed. Sword and spear imbued to the peak of their respective daos, and the resulting explosion was magnificent.

I couldn’t even see one hundredth of the consequences. Laws bent, worlds were created and died. What must have been billions of years for them was nothing but a fraction of a second for the two clashing cultivators.

Information flooded my mind as I took everything in. Yang and Yin clashed, and the universe around them responded to their call.

My eyes widened, my heart raced, but strangely, there was no pressure at all. The only thing I’d felt was pure bliss. I was feeling extremely lucky to get to enjoy this from a spectator’s viewpoint.

Those were things I did on a few occasions before, but besides that one annoying guy, there was nobody that I could challenge to get stronger.

And maybe that was my downfall. I wasn’t ready. I should have been ready, could have been ready, but my range was too shallow. I’d never thought of moving outside the Great Realms, of seeing other universes and realities.

I was a frog in a well. Now, I saw the truth. Had I met those guys back then… Maybe things would have been different.

Calamities… I would remember that title for as long as I lived.

Sadly, the projection ended just as the shockwave was getting to me, and in that final moment I felt… Death.

More than death, it was the absence of it, or anything. There was nothing anymore.

Everything had ceased to exist.

“Congratulations! You have successfully passed the third trial. You will be teleported away shortly. The rewards will be delivered at the end of the realm!”

And with that, the world turned dark, and I was taken away via a power different from the doors.

When I came to, I looked around in wonder.

This next trial took place inside a large, empty room, with only what looked to be a giant arena in the middle.

Walking forward, I climbed up, and a silver powder manifested at the other end, condensing into a tiny blob…

That then shape-shifted into me!
