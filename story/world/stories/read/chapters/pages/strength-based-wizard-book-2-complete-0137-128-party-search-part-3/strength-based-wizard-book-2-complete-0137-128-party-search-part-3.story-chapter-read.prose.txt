Chapter 128

Party Search, Part 3

“We should probably establish a baseline,” I say.

Charlotte’s body loosens slightly, and the thin coils of light around her fingers disappear. “A baseline?”

“Yeah, to know how well my new Skill actually works. Hit me once without my aura active. Then we test it with the aura. You know, like science.”

“Science?” she says.

Jerome’s voice comes from the side. “That is a crude but acceptable testing structure. Assuming you’ve recovered enough Health to take the hit straight on.”

Labonte grins. “I love science when it involves my Guild’s insurance premiums! You sure about this Joe?”

Charlotte studies me. “Yeah, you sure about that?”

“Definitely,” I say, nodding to her. I turn to Jerome. “I’ve recovered enough to take at least one hit and recover quickly from it. Heck, if I’m lucky, who knows? Maybe I’ll gain a new Resistance from it.”

Jerome shrugs.

Charlotte gives me a look. It is a very specific look. The kind of look women give men immediately after hearing an idea that technically contains words but sounds like it was assembled by a raccoon in a hard hat. Which I learned through hard-earned experience is the best approach for gaining Resistances.

I consider another moment. “Just don’t use anything too strong for this first test. Maybe a Level 2 Spell?”

“Reasonable,” Jerome says.

“Boring,” Labonte says.

Charlotte lifts her hand again. Tiny red-gold sparks curl return between her fingers, but then shift, brightening into something pearly and iridescent.

“Got it. I’ll use a second-level Spell,” she says. “This one is called [Glitter Bomb].”

Labonte claps once, delighted. “Oh, I adore that one.”

I just blink. “Er… Bomb?”

“Don’t worry about it!” Charlotte’s expression turns professionally neutral, which makes the whole thing worse. “Ready?”

I square my stance and tap my chest with one fist. “Send it.”

She casts her Spell. A sparkling sphere forms in front of her hand, baseball-sized and packed with swirling pink, gold, and blue glitter. It spins for half a second, then it launches. The projectile hurtles across the training room, leaving a sparkling trail behind it.

I don’t dodge or block. Instead, I let it hit me square in the chest. The impact thumps against my sternum. My [Bludgeoning Resistance] eats it immediately. The hit barely makes a blip on my Health bar, which appears in the bottom right corner of my HUD, registering that the hit did something. Ten points of Health, to be exact.

Then the ball explodes.

The world becomes glitter and light. The flash blinds me for a moment, white-hot and sudden. My eyes clamp shut, but the light burns through anyway, searing across my vision in jagged starbursts. The explosion itself carries heat and force. Not fire exactly, but energetic, prickling, vicious little bites that rake across my upper body. Thankfully, I was training shirtless, as I fear any shirt would be torn to shreds with the small explosion.

My skin stings. Tiny welts and scratches bloom over my chest, shoulders, neck, and face. My Health bar dips more meaningfully this time. Another thirty points gone.  Then my body starts patching itself together. The scratches close, the welts flatten, and the sting fades into a warm itch.

The glitter cloud disperses. I look down, only to discover that I am sparkling. My entire upper body glimmers with pink-gold-blue radiance. I lift one arm, and the glitter catches the training room lights in a dazzling wave.

“The fuck?” I say.

Labonte bursts out laughing. “That’s a good look for you, kid!”

Even Jerome’s mouth twitches. I think they knew this would happen when they heard the name of the Spell Charlotte intended on casting.

Charlotte, to her credit, is not laughing. She’s just smiling confidently. “It’s a secondary effect,” she says.

I slowly turn my sparkling torso toward her. “You glitter-bombed me.”

“Yes.”

“As an attack.”

“Yes.”

“I look like something a unicorn sneezed!”

“That means it worked… And speaking of—”

I hold up both hands, which shimmer with enough reflected light to guide aircraft. “You mean this is what the spell is meant to do?”

Charlotte folds her arms, looking entirely too pleased with herself. “Targets hit by [Glitter Bomb] become easier to hit on subsequent uses of the Spell. The glitter clings to the target’s signature. It also makes them easier targets in general, especially in low visibility.”

I pause, considering. “That’s actually awesome.”

She nods. “It’s very practical.”

“It has bludgeoning damage from the impact,” I say, ticking it off with one sparkling finger. “Energy damage from the explosion. Quick blinding effect. Then a secondary effect that helps you and your allies track and hit the target afterward.”

“That’s the idea.”

“And it makes enemies look ridiculous.”

“That’s not in the description, but yes.”

I look down at myself again. The glitter is still aggressively sparkling. “How long does it last?”

“Not too long, but long enough. A little less than a minute.”

“This is a great spell.”

Charlotte’s smile widens a little. “It’s excellent in dark Gate Realms. Caves especially. Tunnels. Underground complexes. Anywhere visibility is bad, really. Once you hit something, it sticks out like a sore thumb.”

“Useful,” I say.

“Sadly,” she says, “the secondary effect doesn’t apply when the spell misses its target. And it doesn’t work on random surfaces or objects.”

I brush at my chest. The glitter does not come off, though it’s already starting to fade and dim. “All right,” I say. “Baseline established. Let’s test it with the aura this time.”

Charlotte’s expression sharpens again. “Sounds good,” she says.

I reach inward and activate [Magic Sapper]. The aura unfurls from my body in a smooth pulse, invisible but unmistakable. I focus on my [Aura Sense] and the air around me changes texture. A sphere radiates out from me, wide and smooth, filling the air with faint white energy. It isn’t bright, but more like the ghost of light. The aura’s edge is perfectly round, which is weirdly satisfying.

Inside it, everything feels… scrubbed. It’s hard to explain, but the surface of my skin feels tight, like I’ve just walked out of a steaming hot sauna.

Charlotte raises her hand again.

“Same spell,” she says.

“Same spell,” I confirm.

She casts [Glitter Bomb] again.

The baseball-sized orb of glitter and light forms in front of her palm. It spins, sparkles, and launches at me with the same aggressively festive menace as before. The projectile fires across the room. The moment it enters my aura, I feel it. There’s a slight draggin sensation in my mind. The glitter ball flickers for a fraction of a second, its colors dimming a half shade as it passes through the white haze of [Magic Sapper].

I let it hit my chest again. The bludgeoning force is the same pathetic little thump as before. My Health bar reappears in the corner of my vision, revealing the truth of my aura. Only nine points of damage this time.

Then the explosion goes off.

The flash blinds me again, just as sharp and obnoxious as before. White eats my vision for a heartbeat, and my eyes water. The energetic sting bites across my chest and shoulders, but this time the heat is softer. Like someone turned the volume down on the pain.

My Health bar dips again. I watch it closely. Twenty-seven points of damage. A reduction of exactly ten percent, just as the description of the new Skill promised.

The glitter settles over me again. The secondary effect still takes hold. Though this time I can feel it cling to me, a little magical marker settling over my aura. I make a mental note that the aura seems to enhance certain aspects of [Aura Sense] and [Perception].

I blink away the spots in my vision.

“Okay,” I say, wiping at my eyes. “The aura doesn’t stop the flash, or the glitter effect.”

Charlotte nods, already studying me like I’m an especially loud lab specimen. “Nope.”

“But it reduced the damage by ten percent exactly,” I say.

Jerome’s voice comes from the side. “I’m glad to see your new aura works as expected.”

“Ten percent isn’t a huge difference, though,” I say.

“It is huge,” Jerome says. “In a real fight, across repeated spell impacts, it could be the difference between standing and falling. Between finishing a cast and losing concentration. Between surviving a final attack and dying. Don’t discount it.”

Labonte nods enthusiastically. “And it may get stronger as you do.”

I nod. They both make very good points.

Charlotte lowers her hand. “The aura feels strange from this side.”

“How strange?” I ask.

“It’s hard to put into words,” she says. “Everything gets filtered. I can feel the drain.”

That tracks. [Clean] didn’t become a shield with this new Skill. It became a sanitizer for incoming magic.

My Stamina bar is ticking down slowly while the aura stays active. Not free, but close enough that I could run it during a fight without feeling like I’m bleeding myself dry. Of course, this is without me burning Stamina for my own Spells and Skills.

I still need to test [Cleanse Magic], I think.

“Again,” I say.

Charlotte arches an eyebrow. “You want another one?”

“This time I’m going to try the counter Skill that came with my aura.”

Charlotte rolls her shoulders. The sparks along her fingertips shift back into pearly iridescence. “Ready?”

I widen my stance.

[Magic Sapper] still hums around me, faint white sphere steady in my [Aura Sense]. I focus on the feeling of the aura, on the way it scrubs at the air. Then I reach for [Cleanse Magic]. The Spell sits adjacent to the aura in my mind, connected to it like a tool mounted on a wall. I prepare myself to cast.

“Ready,” I say.

Charlotte casts her spell for the third time.

The [Glitter Bomb] forms and fires. It streaks toward me, spinning and sparkling.

My [Perception] sharpens the moment.

The ball crosses the outer edge of my aura.

There!

I trigger [Cleanse Magic].

The effect is instant.

A pulse snaps outward from my chest through the [Magic Sapper] sphere. It feels like a wet cloth dragged across a dirty window. The baseball-sized lump of glitter and light unravels in midair. One second, it’s there. The next, it dissolves. The projectile comes apart into thin motes of colorless dust that vanish before reaching me.

The training room goes quiet. For one beautiful second, nobody says anything.

A stupid grin spreads across my face. “Success.”

“You erased it,” she says. She seems a bit shaken by the mental feedback she received from my spell.

“Cleaned it,” I correct. “Scrubbed it away using the aura.”

Labonte throws both hands in the air. “Ha! Well done!”

Jerome’s mouth forms the smallest approving line. From anyone else, it would be nothing. From him, it’s a standing ovation.

Charlotte smiles. “That’s going to be annoying to fight against.”

“Good,” I say. “Let’s go again!”

Charlotte’s eyebrows lift.

“I need reps,” I explain. “And need to see what my current max is with this Spell.”

Labonte beams with pride. “That’s my boy.”

“Sure thing,” says Charlotte.

“Can you use something stronger?” I ask.

“Stronger than [Glitter Bomb]?”

“Yeah. Mayb not kill-me stronger. I think we should ratchet it up one level at a time. How about a Level 3 Spell?”

Charlotte studies me for a moment.  “I can use a Level Three Spell,” she says. “[Earth Thrust]. It will come from the ground, and I can focus its conjuration point to anywhere within my range.”

Jerome nods approvingly at her suggestion. “I was going to suggest something similar.”

I look at him. “Really?”

“Yes, we should test and confirm whether or not your new aura is a sphere that extends underground. If not, it may be a huge weak spot that you’ll need to be aware of.”

“That… That’s really smart,” I say. I then look back to Charlotte. “Sounds good!”

I widen my stance and activate [Magic Sapper] again. The aura unfurls from me in that clean white sphere, smooth and faintly glowing to my [Aura Sense]. It expands through the air, down through the floor, around my body in all directions. The low Stamina drain begins immediately, a tiny steady leak in the background, like leaving a faucet on in the next room.

“All right,” I say. “Ready.”

Charlotte casts her spell. The floor beneath me trembles. Just a deep, low shudder that rolls through my boots and up my legs. The tile around my feet bulges. A jagged point of stone and compressed earth punches upward from beneath me, splitting the training room floor with a cracking sound like knuckles breaking in slow motion.

It’s slower than I anticipated, which is helpful as I need to react and properly time [Cleanse Magic]. I trigger [Cleanse Magic]. The feedback hits my mind like a hard click. The white energy of my aura pulses downward, trying to scrub the spell out of existence before it finishes forming.

It doesn’t stop it. The spike keeps coming. But it weakens. The jagged earth turns brittle in places mid-rise. The edges crack.  I twist and swipe down with one hand. My palm smashes through the weakened spike, crushing it before it can land a direct hit. Earth and stone fragments burst outward across the floor, pelting my boots and shins. A chunk bounces off my thigh and skitters into the pile of robot wreckage.

I look down at the broken stump where the spike almost impaled me. “Okay… Does this mean my aura is a dome and not a sphere?”

Charlotte shakes her head, eyes bright. “It disrupted the structure of my spell while it was still underground.”

That brings my grin back. “Nice!” I shake dust from my arm. “That’s huge.”

“Definitely,” Jerome says.

Charlotte rolls her shoulders. “Want to go higher? Level 4?”

“Yup! What’s next?”

“[Unicorn Smash].”

I stare at her.

“Unicorn Smash?”

“Yes.”

“That’s a real spell?”

“Yes.”

“Of course it is…”

Charlotte gives a little shrug. “Funfetti cake gets me [Glitter Bomb] and [Unicorn Smash]. Usually.”

I blink.

“Usually?”

“Ate it with sushi once… The results were… Different.”

“I told you, Jerome,” says Labonte.

“No…” Jerome grumbles.

“A tactical bakery.”

“Absolutely not. Our kitchens are already top-of-the-line and more than sufficient!”

I ignore them and look back at Charlotte. “What does [Unicorn Smash] do?”

“It summons a spectral unicorn made of the same glitter and light as [Glitter Bomb],” she says. “It charges in a straight line. Horn first. Then detonates.”

I wait.

“That’s it?”

“That’s it.”

“A glitter unicorn charges me and explodes.”

“Yes.”

“All right,” I say. “Let’s do it.”

Charlotte lifts both hands this time. The air in front of her changes, as glittery light gathers and begins to take shape. The spectral unicorn manifests in full, about the size of an actual horse but much more alarming. Its mane is made of streaming glitter. Its eyes burn with pink light. Its horn is long, spiraled, and pointed directly at my sternum.

Charlotte flicks her fingers. The unicorn charges.

It moves fast. Much faster than [Glitter Bomb]. Its hooves strike the training room floor without sound, each impact bursting into little sprays of light. It crosses the room in a straight line, horn lowered, mane streaming behind it like a rave turned comet.

It enters my aura.

Nothing happens.

No dragging sensation, flicker or slowing.

My stomach drops.

I frantically trigger [Cleanse Magic]. The feedback slams into my brain. It’s jarring. A hard mental rebound, like I just tried to erase a brick wall with a wet napkin. Failure. My thoughts stutter. For one terrible half second, I’m stunned.

The unicorn keeps coming.

“Oh shit,” I blurt.

I throw myself sideways. Almost too late. The horn passes close enough to leave a long burn across my ribs from where the stream of sparkling lights coming off the unicorn’s body grazed me. The unicorn’s body clips me, spinning me off my feet. I hit the floor hard.

The unicorn continues in a perfectly straight line, running another thirty yards or so before it explodes. The detonation is three times larger than [Glitter Bomb]. A massive cloud of light and sparkles erupts outward, engulfing the far side of the room. The flash is blinding even though I’m not at the center of it. Glitter shoots across the training floor in a rolling wave, bouncing off the walls, coating wreckage, and drifting through the air. A piece of robot torso, previously minding its own business, gets launched six feet and clanks against the wall.

Labonte cheers.

I lie on my back, blinking spots from my vision.

My Health bar has dipped from the glancing impact and the shockwave. Not badly, but enough to remind me that Level Four spells are not something I will be casually tanking anytime soon.

Charlotte jogs closer, concern flashing across her face. “You okay?”

I lift one thumb from the floor. “Unicorns are bullshit!”

Labonte laughs so hard he has to lean on Jerome’s arm. Jerome does not look amused, but he also doesn’t move away.

Charlotte stops a few feet away. “Didn’t work? I didn’t get any feedback this time.”

“Nope,” I say, sitting up. Glitter cascades off my shoulders. “What’s worse is I get stunned when it fails.”

“So, Level 3 is the limit of your new counterspell ability, and anything stronger should be avoided,” Jerome says. “The risk is your enemies won’t be announcing the level of the spells they are casting. You’ll need to be careful.”

“Aye aye,” I say. I’m grinning like an idiot, still sprawled on the floor. Because overall? I’m happy. [Magic Sapper] works. [Cleanse Magic] can completely wipe lower-level direct spells, weaken stronger ones, and teach me very quickly when I’m in over my head.

And Charlotte? Charlotte is impressive. Hell, she’s more than impressive. She’s Level 32, but her Class is versatile as hell.

I sit up and look to Jerome. I trust Jerome’s assessment, too. If he selected Charlotte as a top candidate to be in my Party, then it’s probably the right thing to do.

I turn back to Charlotte. “You still want in?” I ask.

Her eyebrows lift.  “Into your Party?”

I nod. “I want to see what your Healing and Utility packages look like, but I’m sold enough.”

Charlotte straightens. Some of the softness in her face remains, but there is steel under it now.  “I do,” she says. “I mean… I’d be happy to join.”

I study her for another second.

“Alrighty then,” I say. “Welcome to the Party!”

As I leave Harvest HQ, I decide I want to take a beat. Instead of conjuring my Full Metal Staff, or any of my gear, I remain in street clothes and opt to make the walk home.

Downtown Cleveland hums around me. Cars roll by, tires hissing over pavement. A bus exhales at the curb. People move in little currents along the sidewalk, no one sparing me more than a glance. I suppose without the wizard hat, cape and jorts, I’m just a random meathead on the streets.

I stop at a small coffee shop on Euclid Avenue, a couple of blocks away from my apartment building. Cleveland got punched by hell and invaded by fish people in the same twenty-four-hour period, and somehow people are still lining up for coffee. That’s humanity, I guess. The world almost ends, and someone still needs their oat milk latte.

I approach the coffee shop. The sign is black with gold letters, the windows big and clean, the inside all exposed brick and plants nobody has managed to kill yet. The line doesn’t look too long. I step in. The bell above the door jingles.

The barista is a woman with purple hair and a septum ring, and if she recognizes me, she does an admirable job of not reacting to the fact that a System-powered magical gym cryptid just walked into the store during her shift.

“What can I get started for you?” she asks.

“Cold brew,” I say. “Large.”

“Room for cream?”

“Black is fine, thank you.”

She nods. “Name?”

I pause. “Joe,” I say.

Her mouth twitches. “Joe,” she repeats, writing it on the cup.

I pay.  While I wait, I stare very hard at a chalkboard menu advertising seasonal drinks. My phone buzzes in my pocket. I ignore it. I miss when my phone was silent most of the time and easy to ignore, I think.

A barista calls my name.

“Joe.”

I grab the cold brew and turn away from the counter.

That’s when I see her.

Through the large storefront window of the café.

Standing on the sidewalk.

Looking right at me.

I nearly drop the coffee.

My fingers clamp down at the last second, plastic cup flexing. Thankfully, I don’t tap into my Strength, saving the cold brew from utter destruction.

The café noise seems to fade and intensify all at once. The espresso machine hisses somewhere behind me. Someone laughs at a table by the wall. A spoon clinks against ceramic. But all of it feels distant.

Because I can’t help but stare back at the woman standing on the other side of the window. A woman I expected—no, hoped—to never see again.
