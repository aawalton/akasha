I cannot channel my power into the mortal realm anymore…

My mind stilled as I realized the scale of the catastrophe. Forget advancing the Velmoryn civilization; unless I was willing to burn 500 Divinity every time I reached down, I wouldn't be able to directly intervene in the coming war against the Night God.

That was impossible. I lacked the reserves to afford such a cost.

Wait. Did that bastard set me up? Did he plan not only the collapse of the Goddess’s barrier but my ascension as well?

If I had remained in the mortal realm, I could have wielded my power freely. It would have been a decisive advantage. Now, the odds, which already seemed stacked against me, looked even more hopeless.

No! Nothing is lost yet. I spent my previous life giving up the moment things got difficult. Never again.

I pushed away negative thoughts and activated Guidance. If the God of Night could use his divinity so frequently, there had to be a way to mitigate the cost.

[The Restriction]

The Mortal Realm is designed to remain free from divine influence. Once a deity gains enough power to defy mortal logic, they ascend, and are no longer able to directly intervene. However, if a deity is willing to pay the heavy price, there is no force that can stop their will.

Aside from that, while direct channeling of divinity is restricted, gods are permitted to lend their power to their devoted believers. Apostles, Priests, or Inquisitors may borrow your strength without penalty. Furthermore, Relics or fragments of a deity may serve as vessels to store divine power.

So that’s how it is…

I already had a Priest and an Inquisitor, but I had no idea what distinguished them from an Apostle. I didn't even know how to appoint one.

[Apostles]

Each deity requires three pillars: an Angel to guarantee safety and pass the will, a Priest to preach, and an Apostle whose devotion is absolute.

An Apostle is a believer who will never question, doubt, or challenge their god. To them, there is nothing more important than serving their deity - even at the cost of their life. You may allow an Apostle to channel as much divinity as their physical form can withstand.

Furthermore, if specific conditions are met, a deity may descend into an Apostle’s body. However, the cost is absolute: once the deity departs, the mortal vessel will disintegrate.

Requirement: To appoint an Apostle, you must find a believer who blindly follows your will and shares all your beliefs.

Current Status: You have no believers who qualify.

Even without Guidance’s additional information, I already knew just how difficult appointing an Apostle would be. Finding someone with completely aligned beliefs might have sounded simple on paper, but for me, it was nearly impossible.

Not only was I from Earth, raised on values alien to this world, but I was also in constant flux, forever debating my own morality.

The only way to align a Velmoryn with my beliefs would be total transparency. I would have to reveal my true self. And even if I did, success wasn't guaranteed. The risk was too high.

I refused to show more than I already had. My visit to the Pantheon had taught me a harsh lesson: this world was brutal. Being a god didn't make me safer than a mortal. If anything, it made me a bigger target.

Besides, I had already found a better solution. While other deities relied on mortal servants to bypass restrictions, I had my Astral Rebirth. The ability to create vessels at will was my greatest advantage.

But I wouldn't rush. I would follow the plan I had already set in motion. Only after achieving my initial goals would I start experimenting with the vessels.

I felt a weight lift as I shifted my focus to Mel’s reward. The restriction, which had initially seemed catastrophic, now looked like a mere inconvenience. It was a constraint that might have actually pushed me in a better direction, one I probably wouldn’t have chosen on my own.

I willed a cyan crystal into existence, envisioning a blue as vibrant as the morning sky. I watched it form - speck by speck, particle by particle - my Divinity shaping it exactly as I imagined.

Then, I focused on its purpose. It needed to store mana and release it on command. But there was a condition: it had to be bound to Mel alone.

Intricate white runes etched themselves deep within the crystal. I couldn't read them, nor did I know what they stood for, yet I perfectly understood their combined function.

This is new.

I had never felt this sensation of instinctive knowledge before. But for once, I welcomed it.

[Warning: Creation consumed 2 Divinity Points!]

The cyan crystal hovered before me, no larger than an eye, waiting to be sent to the mortal realm. But first, I added a delicate gold setting and a chain. What had started as a concept for a simple stone had evolved into a proper amulet.

I was satisfied with the result. The act of creation brought a strange, deep sense of fulfillment.

Perhaps I should learn smithing and craft artifacts myself.

With my unique skill, I could engrave a sliver of my own consiousness into the artifact, achieving effects others couldn't even dream of. Without the ability to regenerate their astral bodies, no craftsman would ever dare sacrifice a part of their very being.

But while the thought was amusing, it was far too early to consider such a path. I had a colossal obstacle to overcome. The conflict with the Night God was far too close, and anything that didn’t serve that purpose was nothing but a distraction.

Focusing on the crystal, I attempted to send it to the mortal realm. My divine energy swirled around it, scarlet light wrapping the stone in a cocoon.

But as its glow brightened, a red window flashed before my eyes.

[Are you willing to pay 500 Divinity Points?]

I had already expected that I wouldn’t be able to move items into the mortal realm. Luckily, I knew the solution.

“Aurelion,” I called to my Angel, who was staring up into the shimmering stars.

I had noticed that just like how I could locate my believers and grasp their emotions, Aurelion could do the same. He couldn’t hear their thoughts as vividly as I could; he only felt their intent - but it was still impressive.

“My Liege?” He dropped to one knee in front of me instantly.

I decided to talk to him later about stopping this kneeling thing every time I called his name.

“Take this amulet to my believer; I wish to reward them.”

“The priestess?” he asked, gently grabbing the stone as it hovered toward him.

Unbeknownst to him, Aurelion’s question made me realize I needed to reward Tekla as well. Even if preaching and educating were her duties, she was performing far beyond my expectations. That kind of dedication required recognition.

Besides, it would completely dispel her fears that I was dissatisfied with her.

I had the perfect reward in mind.

Ever since I viewed the Goddess’s memory about Freya - how dignified the Elf looked riding the griffin - I knew I wanted that imagery to be part of Velmoryn culture. The beast fit perfectly into my lore as a wild god.

But I didn’t want a griffin identical to existing species. Not for Tekla. She was defined by her stark whiteness, far more so than any normal Velmoryn, and I wanted her companion to complement that divine aura. The image of my Priestess astride a white griffin should become legendary among believers and enemies alike.

I began to shape the raw energy of my realm.

I let my intent guide the process, molding the skeletal structure of a lion's hindquarters and merging with an eagle's torso. Muscle and sinew knitted together under my command, followed by skin. Finally, I covered the form in flawless, snow-white fur and feathers that gleamed even in the dim light of my domain.

The result hung suspended before me, a magnificent beast even in its infancy.

As I finished shaping the creature, it was time to give it a soul. It needed a small part of me that would not be tied to my will - a spark that wouldn’t turn into a vessel, but would instead allow it to live as an extraordinary beast, similar to how the kelvarins lived as birds rather than extensions of my will.

I split a tiny fragment of my essence. The process, which had once required intense focus, now came naturally. I guided the shining scarlet light toward the creature’s chest and gently pushed it inside.

The connection was severed instantly. I felt intent and desire bloom in what had been an empty husk just moments before.

[Warning: Creation consumed 138 Divinity Points!]

I looked at the griffin cub hovering in the air, still in slumber. It was very young, yet already larger than an adult dog. I focused on its mind to verify if the process had been a success, checking if the creature could exist independent of my will.

I saw its primitive urges - hunger, the desire to sleep, the sensation of cold. But strongest of all was the bond it had already developed for Tekla.

“He is beautiful, my King!” Aurelion approached the griffin, a warm smile on his lips. “May I?”

I nodded.

He plucked a feather from his own wing, a red plume that looked like a lick of solid fire, and brought it close to the cub.

The feather disintegrated into red energy, identical to mine, and spilled into the cub’s body.

[Griffin gained a new skill - Steel Feathers.]

“My King, should I… should we name him?” Aurelion asked.

I sensed his excitement, but I shook my head.

“Let Tekla name it.”

Aurelion bowed and lifted the griffin gently, cradling it as if he was afraid it would crumble at the slightest touch.

My divine energy rushed and swirled around him until he disappeared in a flash.

Let’s see how you will react.

I allowed myself a moment of excitement and shifted my view to the mortal realm.
