"Looking for the dragon?" Mom repeated Callie's request, looking nervously at Dad.

"Who else would understand the magic better than the dragon? Surely, if there is somebody who could help get this under control, then it is the very creature that gave me magic, no?" Callie said.

Dad crossed his arms over his chest. "What happened to temper your heart? Didn't you say that it was working?"

"I did, and I think it does… but I don't really understand it fully…"

"That is a rather vague concept," Callen nodded. "But how would we even go about finding the dragon? Clearly, it has remained hidden on purpose, otherwise somebody from the village surely would have noticed it by now."

Callie shrank. It was what she worried about, too. How to find somebody that doesn't want to be found?

"I was hoping it would respond to me…" Callie said, although she didn't feel very confident about it.

"I'm going to be honest, Callie," Mom said. "Now isn't a good time. We're going to have to start harvesting soon. There won't be much time to search for anything once that begins."

"Maybe we could search a bit before it begins," Callie suggested.

Mom and Dad shared a glance. Callie had no clue how, but it seemed like the two of them had agreed without even sharing a word aloud.

"We'll try to get it under control on our own. What you found out in the library has given me some ideas," Dad said. "If that fails, then we can try searching for the dragon."

Callie opened her mouth to object, but Mom shook her head.

"No, dear. Seeking out a large, dangerous arcane beast should be our last resort. We don't understand what this creature was thinking when it gave you this magic power. We don't know what it is even capable of or how it would react should anyone seek it out on purpose," Mom said firmly.

That was the end of that discussion. Callie understood her parents' caution, but she really felt like she had to meet the dragon at this point. But sneaking behind their backs wasn't something she wanted to do. She would try to convince them again later.

For the time being, Callie went to the one arcane beast she was allowed to meet and whom she hadn't met in a long while.

"Wiggles!" Callie called as she entered the chicken coop.

The hens clucked and walked up to her eagerly, demanding their feed, which Callie spread in the wooden troughs. But there was no sign of the mireling.

"Wiggles?"

A few minutes without a response, and Callie began to worry, but then the mireling appeared out of the usual hole in the corner of the coop.

Friend?

"Hey, Wiggles… Are you alright?" Callie asked. The mireling looked unusually sluggish, and his slime appeared more… wobbly. It wasn't as firm as normal.

Fine. Just heavy.

The mireling suddenly expanded, filling up a good bit of the coop and making the chickens cluck nervously.

"Wow, you're so big. Have you been eating a lot?"

Eat lots. Need slime for cold sleep.

"Cold sleep?" Callie cocked her head curiously. She then realized that while mirelings were abundant during summer, even if extremely shy and elusive, she had never seen a single trace of a mireling. What did these creatures do during the winter?

"Do you sleep the whole winter?" Callie asked.

Yes, sleep.

It made sense. Mirelings were made up of mostly slime. It wasn't difficult to conclude that it froze if the temperatures got low enough. So mirelings likely hibernated during the winter, much like snakes and frogs did.

Callie reached down and patted her enlarged mireling friend. "I'm going to miss you… Won't you be lonely?"

Wiggles extended two tendrils, much larger than normal, and wrapped them around Callie.

Sleep with kin. Not alone.

Callie hugged Wiggles and felt a little better. It was a good thing Wiggles wouldn't be alone. Frankly, she had almost forgotten there were other mirelings around. After all, Wiggles was the only one who approached her. Maybe she should make an effort to meet other mirelings too. After all, not every mireling was the same.

"Wiggles, could I meet your kin sometime?" Callie asked.

Wiggles released her from the hug, withdrawing his tendrils. He appeared to be deep in thought for a moment.

Okay. Need food.

"Food?"

Kin eat lots.

Callie thought about it. Wiggles was quite happy eating the scraps from their compost pile—vegetable peels, leaves, and whatever other food residue they had. Not that there was much.

But more mireling required more food. Perhaps, she could offer them some leftovers from the harvest? Stems from vegetables, leaves, bean stalks, and other greens that her family couldn't eat.

"I'll prepare some food for you and your kin, okay?" Callie said. "Just wait a little, okay?"

Okay.

Just as Mom had said, the harvest season began in earnest not even a week later. It seemed a bit early in Callie's opinion; some vegetables looked like they could ripen a bit more. But perhaps it was just due to the rather cold summer that they didn't have time to do so.

In either case, all their neighbors had begun the harvest, and the chief announced that the Harvest Festival would be held next week. Another oddity, in Callie's opinion.

Each year, the harvest festival was held at a different time. The chief usually chose the time that would be convenient for everyone. When most of the things were harvested, they still had time before the winter preparations.

But this year, the chief had given them all very little time. Callie had no clue how they would get everything done in time for the harvest festival. The whole family was working hard to harvest and prepare the vegetables and fruits. Tomatoes, cucumbers, potatoes, onions, garlic, beans, cabbages, pumpkins, apples, and pears.

"Can I give these stalks to Wiggles and other mirelings?" Callie asked Mom once they were done harvesting beans.

"The mirelings?" Mom took a step back. "There are multiple now? Callie, I warned you not to meet any more of them."

"It's mirelings just the same as Wiggle. And I haven't met them yet, but I would like to. They are preparing to hibernate for the winter, so I figured I might give them stuff we can't eat."

Mom narrowed her eyes, clearly not pleased by this proposition. "What if another petal appears on your mark? You can barely keep control of your magic as is. Not to mention bringing a bunch of arcane monsters to the same place…"

"Arcane beasts," Callie corrected.

"Arcane beasts. Yes. But my point stands, bringing a bunch of arcane beasts to one place and feeding them can't be good. If you feed a wild animal, an arcane beast or not, they will come back and expect more. What if they start eating our vegetables?" Mom pointed out. "No, I cannot permit you to do this."

Callie slumped. "Mom, please! I won't even talk to them. I'll just watch from afar, okay?"

"Even if you do not talk to them, it is too risky. What if they start eating the crops from our neighbors? How will that be any good for anyone?"

Callie wanted to object, but thinking about it, Mom was right. If the mirelings believed that the village was a safe place where they could come and eat, it wouldn't take long for them to clash with some of the villagers. They already ate some of the vegetables on occasion, making the people regard them as pests.

"But… What should I do? I really want to study them. To understand mirelings as a whole, not just Wiggles alone. Please, Mom! I'll just watch from afar. I won't do anything that would let another petal appear. It shouldn't even appear if it's just mireling."

"How do you know that?"

"I… It's just a feeling I have," Callie said. It was nothing more than an instinct. Perhaps it was wrong, but Callie really felt like the mark wouldn't grow if she got close to other mirelings.

Mom narrowed her eyes, then rubbed at her temples and finally sighed. "You can take some of these greens into the forest, well away from the village, and give them to mirelings there. Go together with Callen and don't go close to those mirelings. Observe from afar!"

"Yay!" Callie cheered. "Thanks, Mom!"

Without any hesitation, she informed Callen of her plan, who looked just as exasperated as Mom, but agreed to go with her anyway, promising to keep her a good distance away from any mirelings. The next morning, Callie explained her plan to Wiggles.

"Can you take me somewhere deeper in the forest? Where your kin might find these?" Callie asked.

Okay.

The mireling wobbled slowly, weighed down by its slime. Callen was looking distrustfully at him the whole time, but didn't say anything. After a while, they reached a place beyond the blueberry grove where lots of sun could get through the relatively sparse tree canopy. Some of the trees in the area were old and dried up.

Callie and Callen placed the greens by one of the trees and retreated a good distance away to observe. Callie wanted to remain a bit closer, but Callen remained firm and pulled her further away.

Why hide?

Wiggles seemed confused by this behaviour.

"Mom is scared my magic might grow stronger. Don't mind me. Call your friend to eat," Callie said, and eagerly withdrew her notebook.

Wiggles wobbled over to the green and began… jiggling? The sight was rather bizarre as the jiggling continued for a good while. Was this a way of communication? But Callie couldn't hear any noise being produced by this.

Nonetheless, after a short while, Callie saw other mirelings appear out of the oddest of places—from beneath the tree roots, out of tree hollows, large rocks, and bushes. Before long, there were at least a dozen mirelings gathered around the greens.

Food.

Food.

Food.

They all chanted and hurried to eat the greenery Callie and Callen had brought while they hid in the bushes.

Callie observed the sight in amazement. Each mireling was different. All of them had the characteristic greenish slime, but their sizes varied, as did the color of their core. Some had a pinkish core, some had brown, some had blue. Wiggles was the only one with such an intense red core, and he was the largest one to boot.

"Could he be the leader of the group?" Callie whispered excitedly and began sketching out the scene in her notebook and supplementing the notes.

Name: Mireling

Appearance:

Ball of green slime with colorful core (red, pink, blue, brown)

Behaviour:

A shy creature that will usually hide and avoid humans. However, if it feels threatened, it will use its acidic slime for defense.

They appear to live in family groups that hibernate together during winter.

Abilities:

Dissolve in slime

Slime tendrils

Acid slime (controlled at will)

Change size (the more mireling has eaten, the more they can grow)

Warnings:

Its acid can leave nasty burns on the skin that are difficult to heal

Notes:

Loves eating flowers and greenery, incredibly fast

Callie doesn't know how long they remained there, just watching as the mirelings ate. It was fascinating to observe that they would let the smallest ones eat the most of the greenery, while the larger ones, especially Wiggles, would hang back and eat the leftovers. To think that mirelings would be such social creatures.

After a while, Callen would poke, urging her to head home. She really didn't want to since the mirelings were still eating, but she knew that they both still had a lot of work to do. Callie would have to content herself for now.

"Wait, Callie! Sit still! There's something on your head," Callen whispered.

She went still, expecting it to be a spider or bug. Callen hurriedly took the sudden visitor off and tossed it on the ground. At first glance, it looked like a large white caterpillar, so Callie didn't think much of it.

But just as they turned to leave, Callie paused. She looked back at the caterpillar. Large, white, and covered in gentle fuzz. Could it be? No, it should be too early for that.

Callie had to check. She removed her vambrace and reached out for the caterpillar.

"What are you doing?" Callen asked.

When Callie's fingers touched the caterpillar, her mark lit up.

No touch!

A little voice complained, and the caterpillar curled up into a ball.

"It's an arcane beast?" Callen blinked in surprise.

"Oh no…" Callie pulled her hand back. This was bad. This was undoubtedly a frost caterpillar, a small, arcane beast that could be found everywhere during winter.

Hence, it was such a problem that it had appeared now. Frost caterpillars appeared only after the first frost. If one had appeared now, it meant that the cold would soon follow.

"We have to warn everyone in the village!"
