Keeping the fish captured in her mouth, as it thrashed about, the girl scanned the chamber. Only after making sure she couldn't hear any strange noises did she begin to eat.

The spacious chamber was filled with the constant rush of falling water. Reliefs and inscriptions in unfamiliar languages covered the walls, while several openings were concealed behind curtains of water. The streams poured into a channel nearly a meter deep, its swift current carrying the water into dark passageways that vanished into the depths of the pyramid.

The girl remained by the water's edge, alert to her surroundings as she devoured her catch. When she finished, she tossed the remains aside.

The girl angrily ran a finger along her pointed teeth. After making a few faces, she spat a fish a small fish bone onto the stone.

Satisfied, she licked her lips, wiped the back of her hand across her mouth, and looked around attentively.

Her pointed ears twitched slightly, trying to catch any sound.

The only thing she could hear was water flowing through a small river in a chamber made of stone as black as obsidian. When she was certain there was nothing else to hear, she finally moved, slipping away on all fours like an animal.

She no longer knew how long she had been living inside the pyramid.

The days had lost their shape a long time ago.

There were no sunrises or sunsets down there. Only a constant gloom, interrupted by the ancient lights in some chambers that never went out, and by the absolute darkness of the deepest tunnels she had already explored so many times.

Time inside the pyramid, without light or with permanent light depending on the place, was measured differently.

By hunger. By sleep. By the slow growth of moss along the damp walls.

Those were the real measures of time.

She had arrived there at some point in the distant past and had learned to live that way.

Now she moved through the passageways like a silent shadow, crawling or running.

Her hair was long and tangled, falling over her shoulders in a dark mass that hid its true blonde color.

It wasn't her fault that she was so dirty. She had spent several hours crawling through a new passage. She could clean herself a little later.

But the truth was that the dirt helped hide her from the other predators in that place.

Her ears had become sharper over time, growing slightly pointed, and her teeth had developed a faint edge, enough to tear apart whatever she needed to survive.

Her permanently naked body had adapted to life beneath water and stone: light steps, a flexible back, and lemon-yellow eyes accustomed to distinguishing shapes where there was almost no light.

She had learned every path in that part of the pyramid. Narrow tunnels that descended like roots into the earth. Enormous caverns supported by ancient pillars that sometimes creaked with a distant sound. Underground rivers that ran through the pyramid like hidden veins, carrying dark water from one place to another.

She had wandered through those places for a very long time.

Sometimes they changed.

That was enough to break the monotony of her life down there.

There wasn't much to do. Not really.

Sometimes she walked. Sometimes she almost crawled. Other times she swam or slept curled up near the water to escape danger.

She was very fast underwater.

The cold river water was familiar to her.

She moved through it with ease, slipping silently through the currents, emerging into caverns or chambers where small silver fish swam through slow-moving water.

When she was lucky, she caught one with her hands, quickly and without thinking too much.

When she wasn't, she settled for insects, larvae hidden in the dampness of the stone, or moss torn from the walls.

Other times she could hunt larger prey, though it was risky.

It wasn't exactly a beautiful life. But she had survived that way, adapting to the labyrinth from which she had never escaped.

From time to time, she heard sounds in the depths of the tunnels.

Something dragging itself along. Something breathing. Weird echoes that weren't always echoes.

Then she hid, slipping into cracks or submerging herself in the water, until the world became silent again.

Safe enough for her to come out.

It was always the same.

Find food. Eat. Avoid danger. Sleep.

Nothing more.

That time, she had finished feeding and was walking slowly through one of the corridors bordering an underground river.

Her bare feet barely made a sound against the damp stone.

Her breathing was calm.

She thought about finding some dry corner where she could curl up, but it would be better to do it in the water, where she could keep her head above the surface, while letting the rest of her body rest.

The water acted like a blanket, regulating her temperature.

Sleep. That was all she was thinking about now.

Then she heard it.

A voice.

She stopped dead.

It wasn't a strange echo. It wasn't the wet sound of some river creature or insects either.

It was… a voice.

Her mouth trembled, as if she had forgotten how to react to something like that.

Then she heard another. Two voices.

They spoke to each other, sometimes in low tones, sometimes raising their voices slightly. The sound bounced through the stone vaults, becoming distorted, but never losing its essence.

Her heart began to beat faster.

People.

The thought took a moment to settle in. As if it were impossible.

How many years had it been?

She didn't know.

She had stopped counting a long time ago.

But that… That didn't matter.

She moved.

She ran through passageways and corridors, following the voices as they grew closer.

At first, she hid, pressing herself against an uneven wall and watching toward the source of the sound. Her eyes adjusted, searching for shapes, lights, any indication of what was ahead. She slipped along the wall with remarkable agility and peered into the chamber.

Oh.

She knew that place.

The parallel bridges.

A trap, because anyone crossing them was exposed across a wide open section, leaving them vulnerable to attack.

The trick was to cross while crouched low like the mice and make no noise.

The faster, the better.

And then she saw it.

It was one of the ancient flames from those chambers where the light never went out.

She didn't like them very much.

Some creatures used those rooms as traps to catch prey such as mice and other species.

The place where the flame came from was on the opposite side, separated from her by a bridge. But that wasn't a problem for her.

Keeping close to the wall, she climbed and crawled effortlessly over the stone.

Her breathing became shorter.

Was it real? Could it really be possible?

She didn't know whether she should approach. Or just run away. But her legs didn't retreat.

There was something stronger than fear. Something she hadn't felt in a very long time.

Curiosity.

She took one step, then another.

Drawing closer to the voices that shouldn't have existed in that place.

She didn't know.

She couldn't remember the last time she had heard a voice that wasn't her own. Her own voice was the only thing she had heard since she had arrived. It had been so long that she had wondered whether she was the only one left in that world.

It almost felt like a hallucination.

She advanced cautiously, keeping close to the wall, moving with a silence that was no longer an effort, but a habit. Her feet barely touched the ground as she descended toward the source of the voices.

Each step measured and breath held.

The instinct to hide was still there, urging her to remain crouched among shadows, columns, and cracks she knew better than anything else. But something inside her chest was trembling. It wasn't fear.

When she could finally see them more clearly, she froze.

There were two of them.

A tall man and a small woman with long hair.

The long-haired woman held the clay lamp, its warm light breaking through the gloom with a softness that seemed almost unreal in that place.

The woman passed the lamp to the tall man.

Its light reflected off the damp stone of the bridge, drawing contours that seemed to move with every small gesture.

The dark-haired woman leaned near the edge, looking down at the narrow stream running along the side of the bridge.

The dark-haired woman knelt. And she carefully brought her hands to the water, then lifted them to her lips.

From her hiding place, the blonde-haired woman felt something break inside her.

Her eyes filled with tears before she could stop them.

People.

Alive.

They were there!

She wasn't the only one, just as she had thought for so long.

For so long that, at some point, she had stopped caring and simply contented herself with surviving there.

They were breathing. They were moving.

For an instant, everything else disappeared.

The constant non-physical cold that never left her. The hunger that always returned. The years of silence that had erased any sense of time.

None of it mattered.

Only that moment.

But then the water beneath the bridge moved.

At first, it was a slight ripple, barely perceptible.

Something was rising from the depths of the river, distorting the surface as it came.

The water burst open violently, and the creature emerged with a brutal hiss that made the entire cavern tremble.

Its head, covered in red and white scales, rose above the bridge, casting a shadow that swallowed the light of the lamp.

It attacked without hesitation. Straight at the two of them.

The blonde-haired girl hiding in the darkness reacted before she could think.

Her body moved on its own.

She had been alone for too long.

Far too long listening only to the echo of her own footsteps, the sound of the water, the murmurs of things that had no voice, and the sobs of her own being.

And although she didn't fully understand why—she didn't want to see them die.

Not now.

Not after all that time.

Not after finding people again.

So she stepped out of the darkness.

The attack was so fast that the air itself seemed to break as the enormous creature, twisting across the stone bridge, lunged at them.

Its scales reflected the light of the lamp in fragmented flashes, and its hiss filled the underground cavern like living thunder reverberating through the rock.

"Back!" Shin shouted, dropping the water-filled stone heads and the torches he had been carrying under his left arm. He quickly handed Mai the lamp as he tried to protect her.

She immediately backed away, clutching the lamp against her chest with both hands.

The flame trembled, but it didn't go out.

At that moment, that tiny light was worth more than any weapon.

Without it, the darkness of the pyramid would devour them without resistance.

Shin didn't have that luxury.

He raised his arm, and the armor responded.

There was no hesitation or surprise.

The dark matter burst from his skin as if it had always been waiting, covering him in a living layer that sealed itself over his body with precision.

Plates formed across his shoulders and descended over his torso, while his right arm elongated, sharpening until it became a dark blade.

The creature recoiled in surprise at the transformation. But after a second of hesitation, it attacked again.

Shin stepped forward to meet it.

The impact shook the bridge so violently that Mai feared the entire structure would collapse.

Shin had caught the creature's jaws with the dark blade.

The edge descended with all his strength, striking the serpent's head in mid-charge.

The blade cut through from the jaw to the neck, opening the flesh in a clean slash.

Dark liquid splattered across the bridge, and the gigantic body writhed violently, its tail striking the stone with a sharp crack.

For an instant, it seemed like enough.

The serpent's body, which he thought was lifeless, contracted. It tensed.

"What?!" Shin didn't understand.

The freshly opened wound began to bubble, the flesh foaming like a snail producing a protective layer against a predator. Shin stepped back. Amid the foul-smelling bubbling, the creature's flesh twisted and split apart.

After a few seconds—barely enough time for them to understand what they had just witnessed—they found themselves facing two enemies.

Where there had been one serpent, there were now two.

Smaller, yes. But not small enough to be any less dangerous.

Their bodies were still thick, their movements fast, and their jaws could easily engulf an entire man.

Shin clicked his tongue and clenched his teeth. He hadn't expected that trick.

One of the creatures turned toward him. The other fixed its eyes on Mai.

She didn't move. She had been paralyzed by surprise.

Her hands were locked around the lamp, and the trembling light cast violent shadows that made everything look even worse than it already was.

Shin could withstand it. The armor protected him.

But Mai couldn't. If that thing reached her...

The serpent lunged toward her while Shin intercepted the other.

And then—

something fell from the darkness.

A figure descended from above the bridge with a leap.

Her feet touched the stone for an instant before she rolled to absorb the impact, then pushed herself forward, landing directly on the back of the serpent advancing toward Mai.

The two barely had time to react. Everything happened so quickly that they couldn't even tell whether the thing in front of them was a person or an animal.

She moved so fast that they barely had time to realize she was a woman.

The impact wasn't particularly strong, but it was more than precise enough to stop the attack. Even so, the structure trembled again.

The creature writhed, trying to throw her off. It couldn't.

The newcomer was already mounted on its neck. With one hand, she forced its mouth open.

An arm plunged into the serpent's open jaws.

Without hesitation, without fear. All the way in.

The beast's roar turned into a thick gurgle as its jaws tried to close, missing her by mere centimeters.

The newcomer pulled on something. With a wet, unpleasant sound, she tore something out from inside the creature.

It wasn't blood that came out. It was something else.

The girl jumped away at precisely the right moment.

The serpent came apart. It didn't fall. It didn't collapse.

Its form simply lost consistency and dissolved into wisps of dark smoke and particles that scattered through the air, as if it had never been solid in the first place.

Shin barely had time to process what he had seen.

The second serpent was already upon him with another charge.

It lunged with its jaws open, trying to close them around his torso.

But now he understood. Not completely. But enough.

He didn't retreat or dodge the new attack. He simply let it approach. At the last instant, he thrust his armored arm into the creature's mouth.

Its fangs struck the armor with a sharp crack, unable to pierce it. The creature twisted its head, trying to lift Shin, but he had clung to the ground. Without realising it, the claws on the armour’s feet had gripped the stone floor, thereby preventing him from being lifted and hurled off the side of the bridge into the abyss below.

Shin took another step forward, forcing his arm deeper inside.

He searched blindly until he felt it.

Something solid near the roof of the mouth. Something that didn't seem to belong to the rest of its body.

He grabbed it and pulled with all his strength.

The reaction was immediate.

The serpent's body tensed and vibrated. Then it disintegrated in the same way as the other, turning into black smoke that dispersed over the bridge and river like ash carried away by an invisible current.

The silence returned, broken only by the water roaring beneath the bridge.

Shin slowly lowered his arm as the armor began to dissolve into particles that scattered through the air.

Then he looked at his hand.

Between his fingers, he held an irregular fragment that looked like a kind of rock composed of geometric, triangular formations.

Dark and cold stone.

When he raised his eyes—

he saw her more clearly.

The figure who had appeared out of nowhere to help them.

She was a young woman.

He didn't know where she had come from, but she had saved Mai and knew where to attack the serpent.

Her hair was long and tangled, falling wildly over her shoulders and back. Her skin was covered in mud and dirt, with small scars marking her arms and legs.

And her posture...

It wasn't entirely human.

Her ears were slightly pointed. Her sharp teeth barely showed as she breathed.

But what caught Shin's attention most—

was that she was crying.

She had one hand pressed against her arm. The serpent had bitten her.

Mai ran toward her. "Wait! Don't move!"

Shin followed immediately, never taking his eyes off the wound.

As they approached, the girl took a small step back, her feet moving awkwardly over the stone, as if she didn't know whether she should flee or defend herself. Her breathing was uneven, her lemon-yellow eyes wide open… but they weren't looking at the wound.

They were looking at them.

At the contact.

Mai didn't stop.

She approached slowly and carefully, then gently took the girl's arm.

The girl immediately tensed. As if such a simple gesture were something unfamiliar to her—or something she had forgotten long ago. She touched the tips of Mai's fingers and stared at her.

Strange. Too close.

"Easy…" Mai murmured, lowering her voice slightly, trying to make it sound warm. "It's okay… it's over now. Thank you for saving me."

The girl was still trembling. But she didn't pull away or try to free her arm.

She just… stayed there. As if she didn't know how to react.

It was then that Shin raised the lamp, its light illuminating her pale face clearly.

Shin went still. Mai did too.

She was beautiful.

Not in a delicate or fragile way. There was something rawer about her. Something wilder. Her beauty didn't seem made to be observed, but to survive. Her eyes gleamed in the darkness with a strange, profound clarity, as if they had spent far too long staring into the dark of that twisted place.

Shin felt something stir in his chest.

Like a brief but intense tug.

[You know this feeling.]

An absurd, inexplicable sensation.

As if he already knew her. As if those eyes weren't new to him. But it made no sense. He couldn't remember her. Those lemon-coloured eyes, those little pointed teeth. Her neck was a bit long but delicate. Her breasts, her stomach.

Shin swallowed hard as he felt a tug at the back of his neck. He recognised her – but from where?

Mai lowered her gaze toward the girl's arm, focusing on the wound and frowned. "Shin… look."

He followed her gaze.

The wound was disappearing.

The torn skin slowly closed before their eyes, as if her body knew exactly how to rebuild itself. There was no flowing blood, nor any swelling.

Only a rapid, unnatural recovery.

Within a few seconds, barely a mark remained.

Shin said nothing and lowered his gaze to his hand.

The fragment he had torn from the serpent was still there, dark, cold, irregular.

Then he noticed something else.

The girl was holding something too.

Another fragment.

Without saying anything the girl raised her hand, showing the fragment, and Shin brought his hand closer to hers.

He hesitated for a moment, but continued.

The fragments touched and pulled toward each other like magnets. They fit together perfectly, without the slightest resistance. As if they had always been one.

Shin thought about it. Originally, the serpent had been a single creature that had split in two. Could the same thing have happened with these fragments?

But the resulting shape was unmistakable.

A pyramid.

It was a pyramid composed of other fragments.

Five triangles. Four at the base, supporting the fifth. Those triangles themselves contained smaller ones.

Shin stared at the shape in confusion.

Something about it told him he knew what it was called, but he couldn't bring the idea to mind.

Imperfect around the edges, but complete.

It was the same symbol.

One of the same ones they had seen again and again on the walls of the pyramid.
