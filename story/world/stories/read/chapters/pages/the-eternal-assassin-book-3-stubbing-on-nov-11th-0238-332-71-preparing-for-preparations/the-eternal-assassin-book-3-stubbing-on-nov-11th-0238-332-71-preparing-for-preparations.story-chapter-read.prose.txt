Asher leaned back against the old oak tree, catching his breath as he waited for his energy to recover from his back-to-back uses of Recall. He watched Samantha shoot through the sky on her emerald wings, the hunter’s movements so quick and fluid it was hard to believe she’d only had the Wing element for a few months now. Moxy was doing her damndest to predict where she was going to go and blast her out of the air with her Air Blasts, but not a single punch managed to connect.

Rulfar was off to the side, the seven-foot-tall bloodhound absolutely dwarfing Kroult as the poor lich did what he could to try and keep up with Rulfar’s training. After witnessing how ineffective Kroult was when it came to physical combat, Rulfar had demanded that Kroult allow him to teach him at least the basics of fighting. Asher thought it a tad pointless, seeing as Kroult had at least a few close-range defensive spells he could cast if things ever actually got to that point, but Rulfar was a hard man to argue against when he set his mind on something.

It has been almost a week since they’d taken down Jormon halfway across the continent, reducing the Council of Death to a paltry eight members. Of the original fourteen, four of the liches had fallen by Asher’s own hand, most of those four thanks only to the hard work of his teammates supporting him. One of the liches, Zygrul, had vanished after betraying the rest of the Council. With its Void element, the lich had made itself public enemy number one of the entire world. Despite that, Asher knew there wasn’t a single thing anyone could do about it just yet. The void realm was a deadly place where nothing should be able to survive. Other than Zygrul, anyone who dared venture into it would be eaten alive in seconds by the realm itself. At the very least, the lich was no longer part of the Council, so they didn’t have to worry about it showing up to help its fellow liches out.

And last but not least, was Kroult. Asher had discovered the ‘young’ lich entombed in a coffin of soulsteel deep within the earth where he’d been kept prisoner for the last six hundred and thirty-two years. Kroult hadn’t even wanted to become a lich in the first place. While trying to stop his master from going through with the ritual, the poor hedge wizard had accidentally been transformed into a lich in his stead. When the Council had demanded his phylactery to work toward their plans to create an Eternity element, he had refused, and suffered greatly for it.

Six hundred years of isolation, without being able to move so much as a muscle, had taken its toll on Kroult. The lich wanted nothing more than to end his own existence, but with Zygrul having possession of his phylactery, he was trapped. Because of that, Kroult and Asher had come to a tentative arrangement. Kroult would help them take down the rest of the Council, but the moment the job was done, Asher would use his Sever the Thread skill to finally kill him and allow him to pass on.

At the time, Asher had been fine with the idea. But as the weeks passed and Kroult continued helping them time and time again…

A tiny part of him held out hope that the lich would change his mind down the road.

“Yo, Runt!” Moxy called. Asher startled back to the present, reflexively slipping into the astral realm as a fist-sized bullet of air blasted apart the bark of the tree he’d been leaning against. “Look alive! We’re supposed to be training, not daydreaming!”

“I’m recovering my energy!” Asher snapped as he returned to the material realm, frowning at the too-wide grin on the warrior’s face. “I just spent the last few minutes Recalling again and again to Samantha while she flew around! The skill takes a lot of tritessence to work!”

“Moxy, I know Asher can heal anything you do to him, but could you please avoid damaging trees for no reason?” Samantha asked as she landed next to him. Laying a hand on the damaged bark, she used Tree Mender to repair the damage. Samantha’s Forest element wasn’t one she got to use too often during their lich hunts, but it was quite powerful when she was in the right location.

“I think you’ve been hanging out with that druid too much,” Moxy snorted. “Where is Sedrick anyway? Too good to spar with the rest of us?”

“Sedrick is over a hundred years old,” Asher drawled. “Maybe over two or even three hundred for all we know. I don’t think he’s doing much sparring with anyone. Have you even seen what his training with Samantha consists of? Most of it is meditating.”

During their last lich hunt, the team had stumbled upon a lone druid living in the middle of a dangerous forest. Samantha’s Natural Guide skill allowed her to head straight for the very few structures contained within the forest, the first one of which ended up being Sedrick’s cabin. The older druid had taken one look at Samantha’s origin element and all but demanded she let him train her in the ways of the forest. She’d been a bit on the fence at first, but Asher’s Judgement skill had allowed him to easily tell just how powerful Sedrick truly was. With his encouragement, she’d become Sedrick’s pupil. The old druid was capable of teleporting vast distances simply by walking in and out of trees, so he showed up every so often to train with Samantha before retreating to his quiet abode.

“I think we should stop here for the day,” Rulfar said sheepishly, walking over to join them with Kroult at his side. The poor lich’s arm was bent at the wrong angle, and Asher could practically see the annoyance in the way the dark green flames burning in the lich’s eye sockets flickered. “Kroult needs… not healing, but the undead version.”

“I told you I did not need to know hand-to-hand combat techniques,” Kroult said bluntly, his flaming eyes flaring as he glared up at Rulfar. “I can repair my arm on my own, I will just need some time.”

“Unhealing?” Moxy asked, looking curious at the lich before shrugging. “Eh, whatever. So, Runt. What’s the news? Does the coalition have a target yet?”

“Moxy, I told you, the first meeting is today,” Asher repeated, trying to contain his sigh as he shook his head. “As soon as I know something, I’ll inform the rest of you. It took some time for the chosen representatives of the countries that were willing to lend their aid to actually get here. Even pegasi can only fly so fast.”

“Not that I want to sit in on some stuffy meeting in the first place, but the fact that Donvath said we aren’t allowed to makes me want to,” Moxy admitted, punching a fist into her hand. “Think I can just pop in for fun? I bet fighting the guards would be a pretty fun way to spend an afternoon.”

“Moxy, please don’t pick a fight with a gathering of the strongest people in the entire continent,” Asher said, giving up on keeping his exasperation to himself as he stared at her. “I’m sure plenty of them will be willing to spar with you after the coalition meeting. This is serious.”

Just as Donvath had promised him what felt like ages ago now, a number of countries had agreed to come forth and finally work together to wipe out what remained of the Council of Death. A group of fourteen ancient liches, each one the keeper of deadly magics, was a serious threat to any individual nation. Nobody had really dared to go against the Council, as while taking down one lich was more than doable in theory, the remaining thirteen threatened to retaliate in kind, working together to annihilate whatever country threw the first blow. Because of that, the kingdoms of this continent had been too nervous to really do anything about the rot within their midst.

At least they had been. Right up until he had started killing them one after another.

Asher was in a unique situation where, despite staying in the Noala Kingdom where he’d been summoned, he wasn’t technically a citizen of any kingdom. It had been the Council itself that had yanked him from his life back on Earth and dragged him over to this world, so they only had themselves to blame. Now that Asher had proven the Council could bleed, the tide was beginning to turn in their favor.

“How many countries have joined the coalition already?” Rulfar asked. “Last I heard it was only four.”

“It’s up to five now,” Asher confirmed, ticking through them on his fingers. “The Noala Kingdom, obviously. The Hojon Empire, which is huge, seeing as they’re one of the three superpowers on the continent. The Eight Crests. The Nunait Kingdom. And… the Lambit Republic.”

“You don’t sound too excited about that last one,” Moxy snorted. “Aren’t those the guys that sent the Shadow Squad after you?”

“Yep. There’s a good chance they’ll try their luck at taking me out again during one of the missions, so I need to keep my guard up. It’s not like they can even keep me down for good unless they go after my soul directly, but I’d really prefer avoiding spending another few weeks drifting around within empty space while waiting for Eternal Regeneration’s third-tier effect to bring me back.”

After bringing his Eternal Regeneration to the third tier, at the cost of a million shards, Asher had taken a huge step toward being truly immortal. He already didn’t age, but now, even if his body was utterly disintegrated, a new one would reform over time for him. He still had to be careful of things that could harm his soul directly, but other than that, he was unkillable.

Which was a really nice feeling, seeing as he had plenty of enemies out there who wanted nothing more than to see him dead.

“You know… with all these powerful people converging in the capital, do you think the Head might make a move after all this time?” Samantha asked, looking a tad worried. “They’ve been silent ever since you took out their Knuckles, but that was months ago. If they’re operating out of the capital, now might be the time for them to try something. You’ve killed three of their Fingers by now. I can’t imagine them just taking that lying down.”

“They’re welcome to try,” Asher shrugged, not really all that worried about the Head at this point. Sure, the fact that the mysterious figure had a skill that conjured up ten master assassins made of shadows was a little concerning, but he knew how to handle them now. Not to mention he’d come a long way since their last battle. They had far bigger fish to fry than an angry Head of the Assassin’s Guild.

“Look at that, you gotta admire the confidence,” Moxy grinned, punching him none-too-gently on the arm. “To think I remember when you were still running around like a chicken without a head, desperately trying not to have a hole punched through your chest.”

“You and I think back to our first days together very differently,” Asher muttered, looking up and checking the position of the sun. “Alright, the meeting should be starting before long. You guys enjoy the training, I don’t want to be late.”

“Be sure to pay attention and take notes. I want to know everything that was talked about once you’re done,” Rulfar instructed. Anyone who didn’t know the man would probably be surprised to learn that the wall of muscle clad in thick leathers with a bearskin wrapped around his waist was in fact the smartest and most studious of their entire team.

“I’ll do what I can,” Asher promised, giving Samantha a quick kiss before waving at the others and focusing on his Eternal Mark back in the palace. “Wish me luck!”
