The funny-numbered courtyard was quite large. In fact, calling it a courtyard was something of an understatement, considering that it came with an entire spiritual field.

As a registered spiritual farmer, Ning had been assigned this particular residence for precisely that reason. The field contained genuine first-grade spiritual soil, something he had spent years trying to produce back in the Outer Sect.

He had poured countless hours into Soil Refining, carefully improving his sub-first-grade field, only for the soil to stubbornly refuse to cross that final threshold. Yet here he was, having received an entire first-grade field simply by becoming an Inner Sect disciple.

Ning had rather mixed feelings about that.

So all those years of hard work were worth... one promotion?

Well, he certainly wasn't going to complain about receiving better land.

He approached the entrance and channeled a little qi into his jade token. The formation covering the courtyard rippled briefly before opening a passage for him.

According to the handbook that had come with his Inner Sect robes, every residence was protected by a basic repelling array. Its defensive capabilities were limited, but it could keep ordinary intruders away and alert the owner whenever someone attempted to enter without permission.

The handbook contained quite a lot of useful information, actually, including instructions for operating the various formations and facilities provided by the sect. It was probably why Elder Shu had been so comfortable abandoning them after handing out their residence tokens.

Well, it's not like anyone would ignore the handbook and end up stuck outside their own courtyard, right?

Somewhere in the Inner Sect, a certain protagonist might have disagreed.

Ning put the thought aside and began inspecting his new home, which would likely serve as his residence for the foreseeable future.

The house itself was modest compared to the surrounding land, though more comfortable than anything he had owned in the Outer Sect. It contained a bedroom, a dedicated cultivation room, and a washroom, with the basic furniture and daily necessities already prepared.

And most importantly, there was a bed.

A proper bed.

"Uff."

Ning collapsed onto the mattress without the slightest hesitation.

The soft bedding practically swallowed him whole, and for several blissful seconds, he experienced what could only be described as a profound state of enlightenment.

The unity of man and bed.

A terrifyingly profound technique. He doubted even the sect's Golden Core Patriarch had comprehended such a Dao.

"So comfy," Ning muttered, stretching his arms across the mattress.

His old hut had only come with a simple sleeping mat, and any additional furniture had to be purchased with his own spirit stones. Considering how carefully he had managed his expenses, luxury had always been rather low on his list of priorities.

There was also another improvement that deserved recognition.

Not a single mosquito.

Ning lay still for a moment, appreciating the silence.

Those accursed bloodsuckers had somehow managed to trouble even that bald guy who could defeat practically anything with a single punch. Naturally, Ning had no intention of underestimating them.

Between the spiritual field, the comfortable bed, and the apparent absence of mosquitoes, his new residence had already exceeded his expectations.

Of course, cultivation remained his main priority, but he saw no reason to make his life unnecessarily miserable. Spending every waking moment cultivating, fighting, and chasing opportunities sounded like an excellent way to reach immortality only to discover that he had forgotten how to enjoy being alive.

Besides, one's state of mind could influence cultivation. A calm, content heart was surely preferable to one constantly burdened by stress and exhaustion.

At least, that was the argument Ning had settled on.

Whether it was a profound cultivation insight or simply an excuse to enjoy his new bed was a question he had no intention of investigating.

By the time the evening sun began disappearing beyond the distant peaks, Ning had grown so comfortable that the thought of getting up seemed increasingly unreasonable.

He closed his eyes, intending to rest for just a little while.

Naturally, that little while lasted until morning.

...

The following morning, sunlight slipped through the window and fell across Ning's face.

He stirred beneath the blankets, opened one eye, and immediately regretted it.

Five more minutes.

Unfortunately, he had already slept through the entire night, and there was a lot he needed to accomplish.

For a brief moment, Ning considered how wonderful it would be to remain in this warm cocoon forever, without ever having to wake up again.

...Wait. That's a slightly concerning thought.

He reluctantly climbed out of bed and made his way to the washroom.

While bathing, Ning used a drop of Evergreen Morning Dew, allowing the spiritual liquid to cleanse the sweat and impurities accumulated from his regular training.

He had never been particularly fond of neglecting personal hygiene, especially considering how much physical conditioning he performed every day. There were few things more unpleasant than spending hours training and then having nowhere comfortable to wash afterward.

And after a certain traumatic incident during his college days, when one of his roommates had entered the communal shower while still wearing socks, Ning had developed a rather firm opinion on the importance of having one's own bathroom.

Some things simply couldn't be unseen.

Once he had finished washing and eaten a simple breakfast, Ning finally turned his attention to the day's work.

Although he had yet to receive his assigned mentor and technically had no immediate obligations, there was plenty to keep him occupied.

The first matter was reorganizing his spiritual farming arrangements.

His new field contained genuine first-grade spiritual soil, opening up possibilities that had previously been beyond his reach. Frozen Breath Grain had served him well for a long time, but its effectiveness had gradually diminished as his cultivation advanced, and he needed to begin considering more suitable crops.

He also had several cultivation techniques to practice, future expenses to calculate, and a longer-term plan for reaching the later stages of Qi Condensation.

For now, though, he decided to begin with something familiar.

Dragon Qi Grass.

Its effectiveness had also diminished somewhat after repeated use, but it remained useful during the middle stages of Qi Condensation, and Ning already understood its growing requirements well enough to cultivate it without much difficulty.

Ning spent the morning preparing the soil, arranging the planting rows, and sowing his first batch of Dragon Qi Grass. By midday, he had finished the work and was considering whether to start another row when the courtyard formation sent a faint warning through his jade token.

Someone was waiting outside.

Ning made his way to the entrance, where he found a middle-aged man dressed in the black-striped robes of an Inner Sect disciple. The man appeared to be in his forties, and although Ning couldn't accurately judge his cultivation, his relaxed bearing suggested that he had been in the Inner Sect for quite some time.

"Are you Junior Brother Ji Ning?" the man asked, giving him a polite nod.

"That's me. May I ask who Senior Brother is?" Ning replied, cupping his fists in greeting.

"Ho Fushin, from the General Affairs Hall. I've come to inform you that the sect has arranged a mentor for you." He reached into his storage pouch and produced a jade token engraved with delicate patterns. "Your assigned mentor is Senior Ling Xin, an alchemy elder residing on Spirit Flower Peak. You'll be her first personal disciple, and this token will allow you to enter her residence."

Ning accepted the token, somewhat surprised by how quickly the arrangements had been completed.

Elder Shu had warned him that his lack of background might make finding a suitable alchemy mentor difficult. After all, the Inner Sect had plenty of disciples with better connections, and an elder could hardly be expected to accept someone simply because the sect had recommended them.

He had been prepared to wait another week or two.

"That was quicker than I expected," Ning remarked with a smile. "I suppose that's to be expected of the General Affairs Hall."

"Ah, yes. Well, the arrangements went rather smoothly this time," Ho Fushin replied, though his smile seemed a little strained.

Ning noticed it immediately.

There was something peculiar about the man's expression, almost as though he wanted to say something but had decided against it. Having spent years navigating the Outer Sect, Ning had become quite good at recognizing when someone was withholding information.

Of course, it could simply be his imagination. There was no reason to assume the worst merely because someone smiled a little strangely.

Still, a few questions wouldn't hurt.

"Senior Brother has gone to the trouble of coming all this way, so please accept this small token of appreciation," Ning said, taking out a jar of Frozen Breath Spiritual Wine and offering it to him.

"Oh, there's really no need for that," Ho Fushin replied, although his hand had already reached for the jar.

He loosened the lid and took a small sniff, his expression brightening at once.

"Frozen Breath Spiritual Wine? This is quite good stuff. Where did you buy it?"

"I brewed it myself, with some guidance from an elder back in the Outer Sect."

"Really?" Ho Fushin looked at him with renewed interest. "I didn't expect Junior Brother to have experience with spiritual brewing as well."

"Just a hobby," Ning replied modestly. "Speaking of alchemy, though, I was hoping Senior Brother could tell me a little about my new mentor. I know her name, but that's about it."

Ho Fushin carefully put the wine away before answering.

"Senior Ling Xin is an extremely accomplished alchemist. Her understanding of medicinal properties is among the finest in the Inner Sect, and even some of the other elders occasionally consult her when they encounter difficulties. She's particularly well-known for researching new pill formulas, though she rarely involves herself in ordinary sect affairs."

That sounded rather promising to Ning. Someone with such extensive knowledge would certainly be able to teach him a great deal, especially since he had only recently begun studying alchemy seriously.

"Then I've been quite fortunate," Ning said. "Although, if she's that accomplished, I'm surprised she hasn't accepted any disciples before. Is she particularly strict when it comes to choosing students?"

"Strict?" Ho Fushin repeated, looking momentarily taken aback. "Ah, I suppose you could say that. Senior Ling Xin has always preferred to concentrate on her research, and her methods can be rather... unconventional."

"Unconventional?"

"Well, she's an exceptionally talented alchemist, but she has a habit of pursuing ideas that other people might consider a little too ambitious. You know how some cultivators become completely absorbed in their work? Senior Ling Xin is something like that, only much more enthusiastic."

Ho Fushin gave a small cough before continuing.

"Of course, there's nothing wrong with that. Many great discoveries have come from cultivators who dared to experiment, and Senior Ling Xin's accomplishments speak for themselves. I'm sure you'll find studying under her quite enlightening."

Ning looked at him for a moment.

Why does that sound less reassuring the more he explains it?

"Has something happened during her experiments before?" he asked.

Ho Fushin's expression stiffened slightly.

"Junior Brother, I'm merely a disciple of the General Affairs Hall. It wouldn't be appropriate for me to discuss an elder's personal affairs, especially when I haven't witnessed most of these things myself." He paused, then added in a more encouraging tone, "Besides, every alchemist has their own methods. You should judge Senior Ling Xin after meeting her rather than listening to rumors."

Well, that was a perfectly reasonable answer.

Unfortunately, Ning found it even more concerning.

He had asked a fairly innocent question, yet the man had somehow managed to answer it without actually answering anything.

Still, pressing him further would probably be pointless, and Ning wasn't about to start his Inner Sect career by forcing a senior brother to gossip about an elder.

"I understand. Thank you for the advice, Senior Brother Ho."

"Think nothing of it. Just remember to report to Spirit Flower Peak as soon as possible. Senior Ling Xin should already be expecting you."

With his duties completed, Ho Fushin cupped his fists in farewell and departed, leaving Ning standing outside his courtyard with the jade token in hand.

Ning watched him disappear down the path before looking at the token again.

An accomplished alchemist with no disciples, a fondness for unconventional experiments, and a reputation that apparently made people reluctant to discuss her.

Hmm.

Perhaps he was overthinking things. After all, talented cultivators were often eccentric, and it wasn't as though experimenting with pills was inherently dangerous.

Then again, he had read enough cultivation novels to know that the words unconventional alchemist could cover a rather alarming range of possibilities.

Ning glanced toward the distant mountain peaks.

Well, better make a few preparations before going.

He returned to his courtyard, deciding that meeting his new master was probably something he ought to approach with a little caution.

...

Every Inner Sect elder was granted a mountain peak to serve as their personal residence, with considerable freedom to develop the land as they pleased, provided they didn't violate the sect's regulations.

Ning had expected Spirit Flower Peak to be relatively easy to find. After all, the jade token provided a guiding thread of qi that would lead him directly to his destination.

What he hadn't expected was how far away it would be.

He followed the winding mountain paths for quite some time, passing several inhabited peaks along the way. The farther he traveled, however, the quieter his surroundings became, until even the occasional disciples he encountered had disappeared.

Well, at least it's peaceful.

Ning continued onward, keeping his senses alert as he approached the entrance.

A wooden sign stood beside the path, bearing three neatly carved characters.

Spiritual Flower Peak.

An elegant name, certainly. Ning had imagined something rather picturesque when he first heard it, perhaps a mountain covered in spiritual flowers and medicinal herbs.

From where he stood, however, the most noticeable feature was a large building farther up the slope.

He followed the guiding thread toward it, and upon reaching the entrance, the thread faded away.

Ning glanced at the jade token.

So this is the place.

He straightened his robes and approached the door.

Considering what Ho Fushin had told him, he felt it would be wise to make a good first impression.

Ning took a breath and stepped inside.

"Excuse me, Senior Ling Xin. My name is Ji Ning, and I've been assigned as your-"

BOOM!

A thunderous explosion shook the building.

Ning barely had time to register the sound before a wave of hot air and thick smoke burst through the room, carrying such an overwhelming medicinal fragrance that his nose immediately began to sting.

The shockwave struck him squarely in the face.

For one brief, bewildered moment, Ning could only stare into the smoke.

...An assassination attempt?

His knees buckled.

Somewhere beyond the ringing in his ears, he heard someone shouting, followed by hurried footsteps rushing toward him.

Then everything went black.

...

"An unfamiliar ceiling," Ning muttered as he stared upward.

His head felt heavy, and for a few moments, he couldn't quite remember how he had ended up here. The last thing he recalled was arriving at Spirit Flower Peak, introducing himself to his new master, and then...

BOOM.

Ah, right. The explosion.

Before Ning could properly gather his thoughts, a face suddenly appeared directly above him.

"Whoa! Who?!"

He jerked upright, nearly colliding with the person leaning over him.

"Oh! You're finally awake!"

The owner of the face hurriedly stepped back, revealing a young girl who looked no older than fourteen. She wore simple alchemist robes, and her long hair was loosely tied behind her back. Her bright eyes followed Ning with obvious relief as he sat up.

Ning blinked at her, then looked down at himself.

Bandages.

A whole lot of bandages.

Apart from his head, practically his entire body had been wrapped in white cloth, and judging by the uneven layers and the way several strips were already coming loose, whoever had done the bandaging clearly had very little experience.

What am I, a mummy?

"Um... what exactly happened to me?" Ning asked, examining one of the bandages hanging from his arm.

The girl clasped her hands behind her back, looking a little embarrassed.

"Well, you see, I was conducting an experiment with a new pill formula, and there was a small accident with the furnace. A few medicinal properties reacted unexpectedly with the fire qi, and the resulting explosion released a cloud of medicinal smoke. Unfortunately, you happened to walk in at the exact moment it happened."

She glanced at his bandages before adding, "The smoke contained a rather potent mixture of medicinal properties, and your body couldn't withstand it, so you lost consciousness. But don't worry! I've already treated you, and there shouldn't be any lasting problems."

Ning stared at her.

A small accident?

He had barely stepped inside the building before being blasted unconscious, and now he was lying on a strange bed, wrapped up like a dumpling.

Heavens.

So this was what Ho Fushin had been trying to warn him about.

Suddenly, the location of Spiritual Flower Peak made perfect sense. No wonder the sect had placed it so far away from the other peaks.

"Well, as long as there's no permanent damage," Ning replied, forcing a smile.

The girl visibly relaxed. "Good! I was worried you'd be angry. It was an unfortunate accident, after all, and I certainly didn't expect someone to come walking through the door at that exact moment."

Ah, so it's my fault for having terrible timing?

Ning decided to keep that thought to himself.

Instead, he straightened his posture and cupped his fists as best he could with his bandaged arms.

"You must be Elder Ling Xin. This disciple pays his respects."

"Oh?" The girl's eyes widened, and she looked genuinely pleased. "You recognized me?"

Due to her youthful appearance, people often mistook her for an ordinary young disciple, and some even questioned whether she was old enough to practice alchemy. Having someone recognize her immediately was a pleasant surprise.

"Yes, Elder. I was assigned to become your disciple after the Outer Sect Competition," Ning explained, producing the jade token Ho Fushin had given him.

Ling Xin looked at the token for a moment before snapping her fingers.

"Ah! That matter! I knew there was something I was supposed to remember today."

She took the token from Ning and examined it.

"The old fellows from the sect have been pestering me about accepting a disciple ever since I finished stabilizing my cultivation. Apparently, someone with my accomplishments ought to contribute more to the next generation, and spending all my time researching pills doesn't count as fulfilling my responsibilities."

She shook her head with a sigh.

"Honestly, what do they expect? Do they think new pill formulas simply fall from the heavens? Every great discovery requires time, dedication, and the occasional sacrifice of a perfectly good furnace!"

Ning glanced at his bandages.

And apparently the occasional disciple.

"Anyway, you're the one they sent me?" Ling Xin asked, checking the token one last time.

"Yes, Elder."

"Hmm. Good, good."

She returned the token before leaning closer to examine him.

Now that she wasn't occupied with treating his injuries, Ling Xin finally took a proper look at her prospective disciple. Her eyes moved from his face to his robes, then back to his face again.

"Well, at least you're good-looking."

"...Thank you?"

"And you recognized me immediately, too. That's another point in your favor." She nodded with satisfaction and gave his shoulder a friendly pat. "Not bad, young man. I was worried they'd send me some arrogant little brat who'd spend the entire day questioning my methods."

Ning maintained his polite smile.

I'm beginning to understand why nobody else volunteered.

Still, he couldn't deny that Ling Xin was an accomplished alchemist. Ho Fushin had made that much clear, and if the sect had assigned him to her, there had to be something worth learning.

Hopefully.

Ling Xin suddenly straightened her back and clasped her hands behind her, her cheerful expression disappearing as she adopted the dignified bearing of an elder.

"Now then, Ji Ning. Since you wish to study under me, there's something I must ask you first."

Her voice had grown serious enough that Ning instinctively sat straighter.

"Tell me, what is alchemy?"

Hmm. Ning had a feeling he knew where this was going.

Still, it was a perfectly reasonable question for a master to ask a prospective disciple, so he gave the answer he had learned during his studies.

"Alchemy is the art of refining medicinal materials into pills and elixirs through the controlled application of fire and spiritual qi. By combining ingredients with different medicinal properties, an alchemist can produce medicines capable of assisting cultivation, treating injuries, and achieving various other effects."

It was a proper answer, one that would probably have earned him full marks during an ordinary assessment.

Unfortunately, Ling Xin's expression suggested otherwise.

She frowned slightly. "That's all?"

Ning paused.

Ah, so we're doing this kind of examination.

He had encountered enough eccentric cultivators to recognize when someone was looking for a philosophical answer rather than a technical explanation.

Fortunately, Ning happened to possess much experience in the Dao of Bullshitting.

He cleared his throat and continued.

"Of course, Elder, that's merely the conventional understanding of alchemy. Personally, I believe there's something much deeper to it."

Ling Xin raised an eyebrow, and Ning knew he had caught her attention.

"Every medicinal herb, every mineral, and every spiritual material possesses its own nature, yet through alchemy, we can bring these different properties together and create something that never existed before. A pill can possess effects that none of its individual ingredients could achieve on their own. In that sense, alchemy is the study of transformation, the exploration of countless possibilities hidden within heaven and earth."

Ning paused briefly before delivering the finishing blow.

"If cultivation is the pursuit of the Dao, then alchemy is one of the ways we explore the Dao of Creation itself."

There. A little flowery language, some Daoist philosophy, and a generous application of the Dao of Bullshitting.

Ning felt he had performed quite well.

He looked at Ling Xin, waiting for her response.

However, the young-looking elder remained silent.

Her eyes were fixed on him, and for several seconds, she didn't move.

...Did I overdo it?

Just as Ning was beginning to reconsider his choice of words, Ling Xin suddenly burst into laughter.

"HAHAHAHA! Good! Very good!"

She threw her head back, laughing so hard that she had to wipe the corner of her eye.

"Excellent! I never expected the sect to send me someone who could understand such a thing! Those old fossils have finally done something useful!"

Ning blinked.

It worked?

Ling Xin rose from her seat, and with a dramatic sweep of her sleeves, she began pacing across the room.

"You're absolutely right, Ji Ning! Alchemy is the pursuit of creation! Tell me, why should we be satisfied with refining the same old pills that our predecessors developed hundreds or even thousands of years ago? Why must every alchemist spend their entire life following recipes written by people who have long since turned to dust?"

Her voice grew louder as she spoke, and her eyes shone with an almost frightening enthusiasm.

"Every medicinal herb beneath heaven and earth possesses its own mysteries! Why do some materials nourish one another while others reject each other? Why can a poisonous herb become a life-saving medicine when combined with the right ingredients? There are countless mysteries waiting to be uncovered, yet most alchemists are content to spend their entire lives following recipes!"

Ling Xin raised one hand toward the ceiling, her voice growing louder with every word.

"If a formula fails, investigate why! If two medicinal properties reject each other, find a way to reconcile them! And if a thousand attempts end in failure, then make the thousand-and-first attempt! That is the spirit of a true alchemist!"

She clenched her fist, looking as though she were addressing an assembly of ten thousand disciples instead of a single boy wrapped in bandages.

"Even if the heavens themselves declare something impossible, I shall refine a pill and make them swallow their words!"

For a moment, Ning could almost imagine a beam of heavenly light shining down behind her, accompanied by the sound of celestial drums.

She would have looked like a true prophet of alchemy.

If only there weren't several suspicious scorch marks on her sleeves.

Ning watched his new master with a complicated expression.

...What have I gotten myself into?

When he first decided to become a drug lord alchemist, he hadn't thought too deeply about the philosophical implications of the profession.

After all, the quintessential cultivation experience involved taking drugs, swallowing pills, chewing mysterious herbs, and consuming all sorts of dubious substances to reach a new high stage of existence.

And since practically everyone in this world consumed pills, the market was enormous.

Want to break through? Take a pill.

Injured? Take a pill.

Want to live longer? Take a pill.

Going bald? There's probably a pill for that, too.

Having trouble controlling your temper? Take a chill pill.

Ning had even wondered whether the reason so many cultivators were either batshit insane, incredibly stupid, or some unfortunate combination of the two was because they were all druggies and alcoholics.

After all, most of them started cultivating as children, and from that point onward, they spent their entire lives consuming increasingly powerful medicinal substances.

Have any of these people ever been sober?

The more he thought about it, the more plausible the theory became.

Naturally, a profession with such promising prospects had caught Ning's attention. The opportunity to manufacture pills, sell them to cultivators, and help people reach higher states of existence without suffering any legal repercussions was simply too amusing to ignore.

But now, looking at the young girl enthusiastically proclaiming her intention to challenge the heavens through pill refinement, Ning was beginning to wonder whether he had underestimated the occupational hazards.

I wanted to become a drug lord, not an assistant to a mad scientist.

And considering that his first encounter with her had ended in an explosion, he had a feeling that the difference might become painfully clear in the near future.

Having finally finished her passionate declaration, Ling Xin turned toward Ning, her face flushed with excitement.

"Well, Ji Ning? What do you think? Surely you understand now why merely following old pill formulas will never allow someone to reach the true heights of alchemy!"

Judging by the expectant look on her face, she was clearly waiting for him to acknowledge her profound insights.

Ning, of course, knew better than to disappoint her.

He widened his eyes slightly, displaying just the right amount of admiration, before nodding solemnly.

"I see now, Elder Ling. Alchemy is truly… profound."

"Exactly! You've finally understood!" Ling Xin nodded enthusiastically, looking quite pleased with herself. However, she soon noticed something amiss. "Wait, why are you still calling me Elder? Shouldn't you be calling me Master by now?"

Ning's lips twitched as he looked at her.

Although she hadn't said it outright, the expression on her face practically screamed, Why haven't you acknowledged me as your master yet?

If he were being honest, his expectations for his new master had already taken quite a beating.

Still, Ling Xin was an accomplished alchemist, and he had already been assigned to her. Besides, it wasn't as though he could return to the General Affairs Hall and request a new master simply because the current one had accidentally blown him up.

At least, he hoped that wasn't a common enough occurrence for the sect to have an established procedure.

With that thought, Ning cupped his fists and bowed respectfully.

"Disciple Ji Ning greets Master. I hope Master will guide me in the Dao of Alchemy from this day forward."

"Hehe! Good, good!" Ling Xin broke into a delighted grin, looking immensely satisfied with his response. "Then it's settled! From today onward, you are my first disciple!"

She gave him an approving nod before adding with great confidence, "Don't worry! Since you've become my disciple, I'll make sure to teach you everything I know about alchemy!"

Ning smiled politely.

For some reason, that sounded more like a threat than a promise.
