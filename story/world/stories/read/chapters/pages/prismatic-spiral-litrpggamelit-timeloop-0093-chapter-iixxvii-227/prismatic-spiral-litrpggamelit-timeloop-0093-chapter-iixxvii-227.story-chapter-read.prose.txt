Chapter II.XXVII (2.27)

“Quite the adventure without me, last loop,” Sterling accused her.

Mitsuko was examining her arm. She tested its dexterity by twisting it and spreading her fingers. During her brief battle with the kraken in this loop it had managed to crush her right side and nearly removed her arm after it latched on with a sucker. But now she was back to full strength. Even after over 20 loops, she still wasn’t used to the causal powerful healing she had at her disposal. It was a spell that fixed damage unlike anything she’d ever seen. And, as a friend to the Emperor of Hon, she’d seen some of the best medics in the world at work.

“You wouldn’t have been much help,” Mitsuko pointed out. “You can’t even swim in that body, let alone fly between locations. You would have either drowned in the sea or stayed with Holly on the ship for the entire loop.”

Sterling stuck his nose in the air and sniffed. “My talent for swimming far exceeds your own.”

“That’s not saying much. I grew up in a jungle where swimming in a pond was akin to covering myself in bacon grease and throwing myself in a snakepit.”

“Snakes don’t eat bacon,” Sterling replied haughtily. “Your village was a barbaric society. How did you wash yourselves in such a place? Complete reliance on well water and magic?”

“Mostly in the fresh streams that came down from the mountains. Not deep enough for us to swim in.”

“Hmm.”

Mitsuko leaned over the gunwales and peered down at the placid sea. “You know, the moment before I died I saw something interesting?”

“Pray tell.”

“The door into the library about Azure Island had a hole in it. I got a glimpse of the inside.”

“And, since you mentioned it was of interest, I assume there weren't simply books within?”

“A massive statue of a mermaid. You realize what that implies, don’t you?”

Sterling remained silent. She glanced down at the black cat and then realized her mistake. Sterling was, thankfully, not leaping at the opportunity to seize the question from her lips.

“Azure Island is submerged,” Mitsuko concluded. “And that would also explain where all of these crusts are coming from. It could be a subnautical society with mermaids and crusts.”

“An interesting theory,” Sterling replied.

Time passed and Mitsuko continued to stare at the sea, fruitlessly willing her eyes to pierce the surface and reveal the secrets beneath. Alas, she lacked that ability.

“Can I cast Retrospection on the entire sea?”

“Certainly, if you want to view the history of it one second ago. You spread yourself too thin to see far into the past.”

“Could I use that as a technique to see across the ocean? Like a divination clairvoyance spell?”

Sterling considered that. “An interesting use of Retrospection. I am not overly familiar with the spell’s nuances having never cast it myself. However, I do not believe that would function as you hope. The sea is too vast to comprehend with a mortal mind.”

“I thought you told me I was no longer mortal,” Mitsuko pointed out.

“Physically. Mentally you’re… well, you’re not entirely following the rules of mortality with your mind anymore either. But this degree of comprehension will be too much. Though I suppose you’re welcome to attempt it.”

Retrospection, while it did have an aura the same as her other two spells, worked best when Mitsuko made physical contact. She would need to plunge her hand into the sea for the clearest results.

“What did Juniper discover in the last loop before I died?” Mitsuk asked, changing the subject. “And yes, that’s a question from me.”

“Likely best if you question her yourself,” Sterling replied. “My answer won’t be satisfactory.”

“Aren’t you linked to the Prismatic Spiral?” Mitsuko pressed. “You should know everything she does.”

“Similiar to if you attempted to cast Retrospection on the sea, my perception of everything in the Prismatic Spiral is extremely limited. I do have a connection, though it is hardly all knowing. I told you before that I am unable to tell you exactly who the guardians are. There are swathes of knowledge about the modern goings-on of the archipelago that I remain ignorant to.”

Mitsuko sighed. Nothing could ever be easy. She supposed she could renege on her schedule and let Juniper appear in the next loop. After spending time with the sage, she wasn’t quite as angry about her mistreatment of Sterling and the waste of several of her loops caught in a mental wipe cycle as she first had been. But… no, actually now that she thought about it, the anger immediately rekindled. She stomped it down. Perhaps she was too stubborn but at the moment she wasn’t desperate enough to care. The secrets of Amber Island could wait for a future loop.

“I need to gain access to Porcelain earlier in the loop without getting stranded on the corpse of a leviathan.”

“A very astute observation,” Sterling praised. “And a worthy objective.”

Mitsuko paced up and down the deck, thinking up dozens of strategies and dismissing them nearly as fast as they popped in her head. “In one of my first loops, I remember the sailors on The Terror yelling at the crusts about safe passage.”

“Didn’t you tell me they attacked your ship and attempted to kill you?”

“I think there was some sort of miscommunication with them at the time.” Mitsuko stopped her pacing and faced the ship’s bow. “I’d like to get to the stage where I can kill or repel any monsters that come my way. However, if there’s another way around the problem, I’d have to be stupid not to look into it. I’m not going to stubbornly grind myself away at the problem, even if I am caught in a timeloop. Time is still not an endless resource.”

“So you plan to investigate The Terror in this loop? Purchase passage through them? How will you gather the necessary funds?”

“I want to speak to the Sailor’s Guild first. They have members on other islands so that implies they also traverse the nearby seas. As for funding my expedition. Hmm.” She crossed her arms and considered. While she and Holly had money stored in their cabin, it definitely wouldn’t be enough to convince Captain Alina to set sail. She might be able to convince Sett to take her to Porcelain Island as a favor if she got rid of his captain. But that idea left a foul taste in her mouth. Even if it was in a timeloop and her actions would be erased in a few days, she was still too proud to become that man’s assassin. “I’ll find money somewhere.”

“You could always just rob someone,” Sterling suggested. “You’re more than capable of pulling off a simple heist with your current arsenal of spells. It’s not like you’ll need to face any consequences.”

Mitsuko shook her head. “Burglary for this amount? I wouldn’t even know where to start. I’d need both a mark and a fence. Plus, I don’t like the idea of taking from a bunch of innocents. The only person I know with any money in town is Wan and the last time I tried to infiltrate his rooms I ended up on a wanted poster and hobbling around with only one foot for the rest of the loop.”

“Surely he’s not the only one you know with a reasonable amount of money. Also, don’t you have your fancy ring?”

Mitsuko opened her mouth to snap at him and dismiss the idea of her selling her artifact. Then she closed it and frowned. She thought back to a comment her pa had made when he visited her in prison. Something about an expensive ring that Akuto had wanted to give her. While Mitsuko was loath to give up her own artifact, she wasn’t above selling loot taken from the dead.
