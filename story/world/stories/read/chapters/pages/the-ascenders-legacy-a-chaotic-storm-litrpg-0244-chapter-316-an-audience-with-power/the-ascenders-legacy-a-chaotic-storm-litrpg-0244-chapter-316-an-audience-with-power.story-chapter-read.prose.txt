Aodhán had never really considered what it would be like to be surrounded by friends who were transmigrants and double inheritors. But it turned out it was just like having normal friends—albeit without all the fawning, awe, and admiration.

That lack of admiration was immediately evident in their reaction to Varéc the moment they walked into the living room. Rather than freeze in fear and reverence like most people did, they rushed forward instead, huge smiles on their faces.

"By the storm," Mirelle, the azure-haired girl, cooed as she placed a hand on Varéc's towering form as if he were a mere pet. "He's much bigger than I envisioned."

"And more beautiful," Darien muttered. He then glanced at Aodhán. "What kind of dragon is he?"

"He's a Fury," Aodhán said with a smile. "A creation of fiction from my home world."

The double inheritors paused at that, while Sophran simply nodded and raised a hand to stroke Varéc's neck. "He's beautiful."

Varéc growled at the attention—uncomfortable, yet clearly loving the ministrations of his neighbors.

Aodhán stepped closer and raised a hand to scratch beneath Varéc's snout. Varéc purred immediately, becoming pliable in their hands as they all stroked, scratched, and patted him.

Silence reigned for a heartbeat, then Darien asked, "What world are you from?"

Aodhán glanced up at the raven-haired boy, feeling that familiar pulse of connection spark between them once again. He smiled. "I'm from Earth. A beautiful blue world with one sun, one moon, and billions of amazing people."

"Oh, that can't be true," Seamus grunted in amusement. "Billions of amazing people? You don't have jerks on Earth?"

"Or psychopaths?" Mirelle added, and Aodhán chuckled.

"Well, we do. Perhaps a few million amazing people, then."

Sophran smiled and gestured toward himself. "I am a transmigrant too. These three are double inheritors. My home world is almost no different from yours. It is called Pica."

Aodhán nodded, struck by how strange—and wonderful—it was that this was the first real conversation he'd ever had with another transmigrant. "How many moons does Pica have?"

"Just two," Sophran said with a nod. He then turned a teasing gaze toward Seamus. "What about you, Seamus? What world are you from?"

Seamus glared at him from behind Varéc's purring form. "I'm a freaking double inheritor, Sophran. I'm from the world of Aeflym."

They all laughed, and the sound filled the space with genuine warmth. It was such a simple thing—laughter shared among friends—but Aodhán realized with a small start that he'd missed this. The easy camaraderie. The simple pleasure.

Darien moved toward the cake they'd brought, cutting generous slices for everyone. They settled into the expanded living room, scattered across various couches as the conversation continued.

For the next several minutes, they slowly got to know one another. Sophran shared his transmigration story—a tale the others clearly had heard a thousand times before, if their groans and eye rolls were any indication. He'd stumbled onto his patron's legacy at the ripe age of nineteen and had promptly been transmigrated to one of the mundane cities in Gordon, who had referred him to the sect.

It was a sweet story—almost sickeningly so. Not only had Sophran not been hunted, but he’d been celebrated since his arrival, lauded as an heir of divinity by all who came across him. His path to the sect had literally been paved in gold, unlike Aodhán’s, which had been paved in blood.

Memories flashed unbidden—blood pooling on deck planks, the weight of chains, Daruk's lifeless eyes—and he immediately shook his head, banishing them before they could take root. He smiled as Sophran continued his story, knowing that very soon, the specials would ask for his own story too.

But he was ready, and when Sophran finally finished, ending the story on a note of riotous laughter, Aodhán was asked to share his own.

His smile wobbled for a heartbeat, but he stabilized it and sighed. "I fear my story isn't quite as light as Sophran's."

"Tell us anyway," Mirelle urged. "Unless you don't feel comfortable."

Aodhán didn't feel comfortable. Not because his story was so brutally dark, but because telling them about all the people he'd accidentally killed on the Raventhorn while running from the Warren village to avoid conscription to the war, or how he'd been kidnapped by cultists and forced to flee his continent, would only warrant a pity party.

And this wasn't a pity party. He wasn't about to chase away his new friends by telling them just how damaged he really was.

So instead, he stripped the story of all its dark elements, keeping his account as light as possible—arriving in Lutia, discovering his affinity, and joining the academy.

He couldn't avoid it completely, however. A few minutes after finishing his carefully sanitized version, Mirelle leaned forward, her expression curious. "So how did you end up in Gordon, then? I heard High Elder Zarxan was the one who brought you to the sect himself."

Aodhán opened his mouth to answer, then shook his head; memories of the family he'd left behind flashed across his mind. No, that part of his story was too dark for a first-time conversation.

"I'm sorry," he smiled sadly. "I'd rather not talk about that now."

"That's alright." Mirelle smiled. "You can tell us whenever you're ready."

The other specials nodded in support, and Aodhán smiled. "Thanks. You all are very gracious."

Seamus nodded somberly. "Yes, we are. It's such a heavy burden to carry, being so gracious all the time."

They all laughed, and the conversation simply flowed to other topics.

They talked about the sect, about the elders, and finally, about the other Specials who were apparently out of the sect on missions within the world rift.

The mention of a world rift piqued Aodhán's attention, and he frowned. "What's a world rift?"

"It's exactly what it sounds like." Seamus leaned back in his chair and smiled. "It's a rift, but permanent. Stable. And the size of an entire world. It's impossible to delve completely because it's constantly shifting and evolving. New territories emerge; old ones collapse."

"It's also incredibly dangerous," Mirelle added, her usual brightness dimming slightly. "The creatures there aren't like normal rift monsters. They've had centuries to grow, to adapt, to become… wiser."

Aodhán felt a thrill run down his spine—not fear, but excitement. A world-sized rift, constantly evolving? That sounded like exactly the kind of challenge he'd been built for. Then he caught himself. Maybe Lutia really had damaged him if his first response to "incredibly dangerous" was anticipation.

"And two of our fellow specials are there right now?" he asked.

"Nymera and Dravion can handle it," Sophran said with confidence. "They're the strongest of us. And if they can't, they've got their familiars to help them out."

"Right," Darien nodded. "It would be much safer to delve the rift with a familiar by your side. Did we mention that you can't delve the world rift as a group?"

Seamus grunted in annoyance. "Infuriating limitations. I can't wait to gain my familiar and finally push past the outer regions of the rift. It keeps stumping me." He sighed heavily. "I wish my luck would come already."

"And mine too," Sophran groaned. He glanced at Aodhán and frowned. "You know, Aodhán, when they first told me you had a familiar, I couldn't believe it. Part of me still can't. You'll have to tell me how you achieved it."

"And me," Seamus cut in. "If I can gain a familiar before Sophran does, then I've truly won at life."

Sophran scoffed. "The odds aren't in your favor, pretty boy. There are four transmigrants in the special sect, and three of them already have familiars. It's obviously my turn."

Mirelle rolled her eyes and gave Varéc one last pat before moving toward the cake on the center table. "I doubt there's a queue for familiars, Sophran—and if there is, I'm willing to bet you're not on it."

"Oh, I'm on it," Sophran glared. "Nymera and Dravion have explained the process to me countless times. It all boils down to luck, and my luck these past few days has been outstanding."

Darien shook his head. "I still find it hard to believe it all comes down to dumb luck." He glanced at Aodhán. "Did you do something different during yours, or was it luck too?"

Aodhán's mind flashed back to the moment he'd gained Varéc—choosing him out of thousands of possibilities. He slowly shook his head. "It wasn't luck exactly. But luck did play a part."

All three boys froze.

"So, you did something?" Seamus whispered.

Aodhán nodded. "Yeah. A few simple steps, really—"

His words trailed off as realization struck him. For some reason, he'd never really thought of what had happened that day in detail. But now, as he thought of the exact steps he'd taken and ways to perhaps replicate them, he realized something he hadn't truly understood before.

As much as his transmigrant status had helped him gain Varéc, his lightning pendant had played an equally vital—perhaps an even more significant—role.

The greatest obstacle within the origin plane was the inability to think clearly or to think beyond the inspiration the plane itself tried to impose. The whispers of chaos never allowed anything else. But he had shut those whispers out with his pendant and, with the strength of his spirit, accessed what only Mythics should have been capable of.

No wonder the other transmigrants called it luck.

They must have experienced a brief window of clarity within the plane and stumbled into gaining a familiar. Perhaps that was how most people ever managed it.

But with his knowledge, that could change.

His breath caught. This wasn't just personal knowledge—this was leverage. Power. The kind of information that could shift the balance of an entire war.

And he was going to give it away.

The thought should have scared him. In Lutia, hoarding advantages had been survival. But his ideal thrummed within him, warm and certain: I GAIN STRENGTH TO GIVE IT.

Maybe that was the difference between who he'd been and who he was becoming.

If his pendant could somehow be replicated, there was a very real chance that many within the elder, special, and perhaps even core sect could gain familiars. With this, he could double the strength of the Storm Sect in a single week.

And more than that—Aodhán didn't mind at all.

Excitement bubbling in his chest, he looked at Sophran. "I think I just stumbled onto a goldmine. Is there any way I can speak to a High Elder right now?"

The boys exchanged glances.

"Will it help the sect?" Sophran asked carefully.

"Yes," Aodhán replied without hesitation. "Far more than you can imagine."

Sophran's eyes widened. "Then come with me. I'll lead you to them."

Without hesitation, they rushed out the door, the others hot on their heels. Barely a week into the sect, and Aodhán was already about to turn the entire place on its head.

Within him, his ideal hummed—a mantra of power and upliftment.

I GAIN STRENGTH TO GIVE IT.

Sophran didn't have a direct line to the High Elders, but he had the next best thing—a relationship with an elder who did. The moment they stepped out of the mansion, he began contacting her.

He had no idea what grand revelation Aodhán had just stumbled upon, but after the display of power at Trial Mountain only hours ago, Sophran had no doubt it was enormous—perhaps even revolutionary.

He had seen the look in Aodhán's eyes: pure realization.

If arranging a meeting with the High Elders was the fastest way to learn what that realization was, then Sophran was more than willing to commit. Especially since it almost certainly involved familiars.

Despite his witty, self-deprecating exterior, his lack of a familiar was something that kept him up at night. As the only transmigrant in the special sect without one, he had worked tirelessly to ensure none of the double inheritors reached that milestone before him.

He would never live it down if that happened.

They arrived at the administration building a few minutes later, and Sophran immediately hurried toward the reception desk. "Good afternoon, Elder Finch. I've just set up an appointment with Elder Vasmos. She asked us to come up."

The elder tapped at a holographic screen, likely checking Elder Vasmos's schedule. But it turned out to be unnecessary, as a moment later, Elder Vasmos herself stepped out of her office, a powerful aura radiating from her.

She was tall—perhaps seven feet—with snow-white hair, eyes, and skin. At a glance, she resembled a marble statue brought to life, but the blazing intensity of her core shattered that illusion immediately. She was the most powerful Mythic Aodhán had ever seen—or perhaps, the most powerful he had ever been able to clearly identify.

At Tier 174, she stood on the very precipice of the calamity tier, her core blazing with blinding intensity. When she spotted Sophran, she smiled and approached them, her obsidian cloak rippling softly behind her.

"Elder Vasmos," Sophran said, bowing deeply. Aodhán quickly mimicked the gesture.

"Rise. Storm bless you all." Her gaze settled on Aodhán, and her smile widened slightly. "You must be our newest member of the special sect. I have heard great things about you—truly outstanding."

"Thank you, Elder," Aodhán replied with a smile. She nodded in acknowledgment.

Her attention shifted back to Sophran. "You requested to see me urgently. What is so important that you felt the need to drag our newest member along with you?"

Sophran glanced at Aodhán, a subtle signal that this was his moment.

Aodhán stepped forward. "I know the time of the High Elders is precious, but I have knowledge that needs to be shared with them—knowledge that could greatly impact the sect. I would be happy if there was a way you could connect me to them."

Elder Vasmos frowned. "I'm afraid that may be a bit tricky, as everything involving Calamities usually is." She studied him for a moment. "Are you certain you cannot discuss this with me instead? I promise to act appropriately on whatever information you present. That is why we are here—to assist and help."

Aodhán shook his head. "I'm sorry, Elder Vasmos. It's nothing against you, but I'm still warming up to the sect. There is only one person I trust right now—High Elder Al'ruhn. I would prefer to share this knowledge with him first."

"Elder Vasmos, please," Sophran said quickly. "Help us this once, and we'll owe you."

Her expression softened, though hesitation still lingered. "I can't simply take you before the High Elders without knowing what this is about, Sophran. Tell me what it is, and if it's beyond my ability to handle, I give you my word that I will take you to them."

All eyes turned to Aodhán once more.

He grimaced. He didn't blame her—she was only doing her job. Still, he couldn't yet bring himself to trust anyone in a position of authority. Lutia had taught him that lesson the hard way.

She was right, though.

He had to give her something.

His gaze slid to her core—that blazing mass of storm energy, impossibly dense and powerful, yet lacking something vital.

"You do not have a familiar, Elder Vasmos," he said calmly. "What if I told you that the knowledge I possess could change that? Not just for you—but for the entire sect. What if I told you that if you allow us to see the High Elders, you could have a familiar by the end of the week?"

Elder Vasmos stared at him, eyes wide.

Her hands trembled slightly as she reached for the communication crystal on her desk—just a tremble, quickly suppressed, but Aodhán caught it. Hope. Raw and desperate and barely contained.

He understood that feeling perfectly.

After a brief moment of hesitation, she said, "I would say… let's go, Special Disciple Aodhán."

Aodhán smiled. "Then lead us to them, Elder Vasmos. Things are about to change—drastically."

Behind him, Sophran shuddered with anticipation. Seven failed attempts. Seven times he'd entered the origin plane and come out empty-handed while watching others succeed.

But that was about to change.

He could already imagine the future—and it was glorious indeed.
