Jessie Gerald, the improbable sixteen-year-old leader of the fastest-rising guild in Phoenix, would rather be at school.

Civics class was so much better than this. Heck, even trigonometry was probably better than this. But she’d been talking with Ellen a lot, and one thing her brother’s girlfriend had been teaching her was how to act. She wasn’t the best actor in the room; Ellen was much better than she’d ever be, and the man standing behind Terrel Young was, according to Kade, a professional one. But she’d been thinking about this role for…since she read the Guardians’ internal communications for the first time. She knew what part she was supposed to play.

Thanks to Yreanne and User295, she already knew pretty much how this conversation was going to go. After Yreanne had breached the Guardians’ communications network, ‘J-Dawg’ had asked User295 to do the same thing to the Governing Council itself. She’d wanted to know the GC’s relationships with the other guilds.

What he’d found had been fascinating.

The GC’s inner council made most of the decisions in Phoenix. The mayor existed, but he existed as more of a figurehead. That was all…well, not common knowledge, but Jessie had known it for a while. The interesting thing was, in reality, only seven voices mattered on the Inner Council. The five guild leaders—three, at the moment—Myers, and Anders did almost all of the policy creation. Everyone else went along with it.

And there were constantly-shifting internal divisions within that group of seven. The Coyotes and Roadrunners were currently rivals, but hadn’t had an open conflict in…ever, as far as User295 could tell. The Guardians were reclusive, hoarding power and concentrating it in their territory. The Portal Tyrants and Roadrunners were firm friends. The Iron Falcons only ever weighed in if it mattered for Tucson.

And the Governing Council—meaning Councilwoman Harriet Myers—manipulated all five to get what it wanted: stability in Phoenix, with her at the top.

The two big issues were exactly what Myers had tried to start with. Phoenix’s defense infrastructure, and the Carlsbad Portal. With the Carlsbad Portal closed, though, the only issue that mattered was defense, and the Guardians mattered a lot more for that.

Jessie was pretty sure she had a ready-made crisis she could deploy any time she wanted—one that’d make the Carlsbad Portal look like an E-Rank portal out in the Sonoran Desert.

Tanat.

And the Portal Tyrants’ actor and recruiter had given her the perfect window to deploy her.

“Tanat is a friend of mine,” I said into the room’s silence.

Jessie nodded, standing up. I put a hand on her back, between her shoulder blades, to hold her steady. She nodded just the tiniest bit, and I let her go. “Tanat represents the Desert Wind Guild’s potential third S-Rank delver. She is from another world, but is nevertheless an S-Ranker, and is not a monster. When combined with Kade and Ellen Traynor, we should have more S-Rankers than any two guilds in Phoenix. Isn’t that right, Councilwoman Myers?”

Even more silence, but over the silence, the metaphorical sound of gears grinding in a whole lot of minds filled the air. The whole room was calculating.

“As of one minute ago, the Desert Wind has a verbal alliance with the Portal Tyrants. That, in theory, lets us field…” Jessie made a show of counting on her fingers. “Four S-Rankers. I’m just a schoolgirl, but four is a significant amount of firepower no matter how you do the math.”

Councilwoman Myers’s eyes narrowed. She opened her mouth to say something, then closed it. “Anders, thoughts?”

Nathan Anders, the Phoenix Reborn, laughed. “They’d have Sarah Cullman, too. She’s got a soft spot for Noelstra and his sister. And they might have me on top of that. Haven’t decided yet.”

I relaxed. We’d won. It was one thing to see the checkmate on the board, but it was another to watch your opponent realize that she had no moves left, and Councilwoman Myers wasn’t used to being outmaneuvered like this. The fight left her eyes slowly.

Before it could fully leave, I cleared my throat. “There’s another problem, and it’s a big one. The kind of problem we all need to be aware of. I need your help with—“

The door opened, and a man walked in.

Gold shield emblem on his shoulder. Military dress uniform, in navy blue. Graying hair, cut tight. Eyes every bit as predatory as Councilwoman Myers’s, but in a more wolflike way. Or maybe a big cat. Longspear on his back, pieces of armor plating on his wrists, chest, and legs.

Vern Kellen, the Guardians’ leader.

“I see we’re having a Governing Council meeting,” he said, voice loud but not aggressive. “I wasn’t invited. That’s disappointing, given the current political situation. I would think maintaining good relations with the Wickenberg Portal Break and our farmland would be critical to the Governing Council’s long-term strategy.”

Hmmmm. I didn’t miss what he’d said. Not the Guardians, but the portal break.

“A lesser commander would almost think you were trying to work behind our backs,” Vern finished.

“We were,” Jessie said.

His eyes flicked to her. “Explain. Now.”

“Why don’t you explain, Councilwoman Myers?” Jessie asked.

“Fine. The Desert Wind Guild brought credible information regarding a power grab by your guild and proposed a counter maneuver to maintain Phoenix’s political stability. The Portal Tyrants accepted their offer, and the Governing Council was in the process of debating it. Nothing is decided yet.”

“It’s decided,” Terrel Young growled. “We want that information on core breaks. It could be revolutionary. And if they’ve got a—“

“Nothing is decided,” Myers said again.

Anders coughed. When Vern Kellen turned his way, he nodded. “Councilwoman Myers is right. Nothing’s officially decided. That being said, the Desert Wind’s offer is strong.”

“And what is that, exactly?” Kellen asked.

Jessie cleared her throat. “We’re offering to put out public support behind the Governing Council—that is, both the S-Rankers associated with the guild, plus Eleanor Traynor, who should hit S-Rank in the next few days. That includes my brother. You’ve read your briefings, right?”

“More of them than you,” he said.

“Great. Then you know Kade’s the hero who won the Fallen Delver’s Tournament, broke the Siege of Phoenix—“

“With Ellen’s help,” I added.

“—and defeated the Carlsbad Portal Break’s boss pretty much single-handedly,” Jessie finished. “That’s a lot of reputation, and Phoenix knows all of that. It’s been all over the news.”

“That’s it? Reputation?” Kellen asked.

“Yep! Sometimes, that’s all you need.” Jessie sat down heavily, and I touched her shoulder. She glanced back and shook her head.

I braced myself.

Sometimes, reputation was all you needed, but sometimes, it wasn’t anywhere near enough—and in this case, we’d discussed Vern Kellen pretty heavily on the drive over. He had a reputation, too—as a rational man, a man of clear boundaries, and a man who’d do whatever it took to position himself where he and his people could benefit the most. But he was also a man with an ego, a planner, and someone who could be manipulated.

His arrival hadn’t been expected, but we had discussed what to do if he did show up mid-meeting. Jessie was executing the plan perfectly. Now, all I had to do was bait the trap.

“Are we done here?” I asked. “I’ve got a dozen other, more important battles to fight.”

Vern Kellen’s eyes locked onto me.

Then he nodded. “You and me. Outside. I want to see if your reputation is earned or overinflated.”

“Fine. But let’s make it fast. Like I said, more important battles.”

His expressionless face broke into a smile for a moment. “Oh, I will,” he said.

Kade was being stupid again, and Ellen was incredibly jealous.

Not that she couldn’t fight. Oh, could she fight now! Her whole body and core screamed to let loose with every second, and S-Rank was so incredibly close she could taste it. A fight—any fight—would get her closer.

But this was a waste of Kade’s time, and Jessie, Ellen, and Kade himself all knew it.

Whatever he’d done to his core when he’d founded his new Path, he was just better than Vern Kellen. Faster. More agile. He hit as hard as the fighter and had access to far more magic. The arena far below Ellen’s vantage point was covered in electrical burns and scorch marks, and cut and smoldering vines littered the ground; Kellen had a magic spear.

But even that wasn’t enough. Ellen was…bored.

She yawned, then turned to face Councilwoman Myers. “While Kade is taking apart one of the strongest S-Rankers in the city, we need to talk about one other detail. He’s going to need your help. All of your help.”

“How so?” Harriet Myers asked. The spark of defiance she’d gotten when the Guardians’ guild leader crashed the meeting had faded to nothing. She still wanted to resist, and that was normal. No CEO ever wanted the Traynor Corporation to complete its hostile takeover, and like it or not, that was exactly what Jessie was doing.

“Kade’s operating at a level no one else on Earth will be able to compete with. He’s pretty sure, as of last night, that he could beat Angelo Lawrence at least half the time, and most of the other S-Rankers in the city more than that. But there are other powerful entities out there. You remember the Crone, right?” Ellen asked Nathan Anders.

“Yeah, I remember the Crone. And that tentacle during the Craftsman fight. That’s what killed Bernard the Wall.” Nathan shivered. “I’d had to see what’d happen if it attacked here.”

“Kade has enemies at high ranks,” Ellen said simply. “Part of our condition for helping you with Phoenix’s political predicament is—“

“That we help him with the Crone?” Nathan asked.

“No. Kade doesn’t want to put Earth in any more danger than it already is, and his enemies are the Placid King—the mastermind behind the Siege of Phoenix—and the God of Thunder, his former mentor. The Crone, to the best of our knowledge, has nothing to do with this.

“With that said, we don’t need your help with the direct fight. Instead, we need your help with Robert Traynor.”

Myers snorted. “With your dad? Can’t you take care of that yourself?”

“No. We need Traynor Overholz Cannons. As many as we can get our hands on. I can’t negotiate with…Bob…because I only have one thing he wants, and I refuse to give it to him. I guarantee he won’t talk to the Desert Wind Guild, either. But he’d be happy to talk to the Governing Council. So, once Kade’s done beating Vern Kellen into submission, we need you to act as the middleman for us. Buy the cannons, get them delivered as soon as possible, and we’ll take care of the rest.”

Nathan’s eyebrows furrowed. He shook his head. “I’m not in the habit of letting someone else fight my battles. The Placid King is behind the Siege? I want a shot at it.”

“Talk to Kade about that,” Jessie said. She’d found a bench, and her eyes were locked on the fight. Ellen winced in sympathy; Kade’s little sister was moving as little as possible, and every move she did make looked painful. She sat next to the girl and joined her in watching the fight.

Not that it was much of a fight.

I’d gone into the fight with Vern Kellen with only three rules. They were big ones, though.

First, no Stormbreak. We fought in a combat room, yes, but we were in the middle of downtown Phoenix. I couldn’t risk hurting people with my most powerful single move.

Second, no Hand of the Thunder God combo. Same problem, but on a more focused scale. With Jessie and Ellen—not to mention the GC’s Inner Council—watching from above, I couldn’t risk an errant lightning beam catching someone important.

And third, both Vern Kellen and I would walk out of this arena when I was done.

But god damn, was the Guardians’ guildmaster making that third rule hard to break!

He was just fast enough, and his ridiculous vine spear just long enough, to keep the Storm’s Blade at bay. I shifted stances. Torrentborne. Lightning Dance. Reposition. Into Thunderlord. The spear whipped around, vine shaft bending wildly and tearing the air over my head as it broke the sound barrier. Slow. But fast enough.

His aura slammed out. It caught my Laminar Blade and Stormfall combo, acting like a magical shield in front of him. I’d wondered why a spearfighter wouldn’t have a shield as an option, and now I understood. His aura was all the defense he needed.

Granted, he wasn’t unscathed. I’d hit him a dozen times. Glancing blows. Burns covered his breastplate and vambraces, and a cauterized line from ear to lip showed just how close I’d gotten to ending the fight permanently.

“You’re good, Kade Noelstra,” Kellen admitted. He backpedaled, and his spear thrust out, extending impossibly far as the vines grew. I pressed against the wall of aura and against the hail of vine-strikes. The Storm’s Blade cut through the vines as fast as they rained down on me.

Lightning Dance again. This time, no stance-shifting. The Storm’s Blade lashed out. Kellen vanished. He reappeared behind me, a puddle of liquid forming by his feet as the vine-spear stabbed toward my chest. I spun, and my lightning-and-maelstrom armor took the blow. Lightning Chain. I pulled myself toward the guild leader. My sword flashed.

Blood.

I’d caught something. Kellen grunted. He turned, a new cut down his shoulder. Not deep enough to stop him, or to be lethal, but enough to slow him down. “You’re very good,” he said.

“I’m not good enough. Not yet,” I replied.

His eyes locked on mine. “So, you admit you’re beaten?” His spear hung in the air between us, almost menacingly.

Almost.

I shook my head. “Not even close.”

Then, before he could react, I used my own aura, and the Stormkin Avatar erupted forth to do battle.

I had things to do, and none of my rules said anything about asking Dad for help.
