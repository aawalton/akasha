We arrived at the village at the foot of the Sunless Reach around noon on Fireday, having spent Waterday traveling through the mountain pass and the plains beyond it before camping during the long night.

I was honestly glad Ja'a had purchased those mammoth-fur cloaks.

The temperature had continued to drop the closer we traveled to the Kurco Range. It felt strange. We were technically crossing the planet's equator, yet every day seemed colder than the last. The temperature only began rising slightly once the mountains were behind us.

Maybe altitude was the deciding factor, or maybe it was the distance from the ocean. Then again, trying to understand this world's climate was probably a fool's errand.

The cosmology alone was enough to give any Earth scientist a headache. Two satellites acted as suns while the actual sun remained little more than a bright star in the sky. Days and nights were determined by celestial bodies that shouldn't even exist according to several laws of physics.

For all I knew, temperature might be partially controlled by mana. Maybe cold climates formed where ice-aspected mana accumulated. Maybe there was an entire branch of magical meteorology waiting to be invented.

I would need years of research before I could even begin forming theories, and I doubted I was the right person to tackle that problem anyway.

As we drove through the village, several people recognized me.

"Hey, Alice!" called an older man from outside a workshop.

I waved.

"Do you need more chickens?" shouted another old man.

I laughed.

"Not today, Bobby!" I called back. "My team and I are having moon hare for dinner."

"Good luck catching those little buggers!"

"Well, if we fail, we can always eat your chickens!"

The old man barked out a laugh.

"You are popular," Vena observed as several villagers smiled or waved in my direction.

The cleric was finally done with her vow of silence and could speak normally again.

"I may have thrown a few silvers around every time I visited," I admitted.

"That's generous."

"I am just stimulating the economy."

"My girl," said Ja'a, offering a fist bump.

I returned it.

Kan snorted from the driver's seat, with Raik sitting beside her.

"The inn is that way," I said, pointing toward a familiar building near the edge of the village.

The settlement itself wasn't particularly large.

Most of the buildings had been constructed from stone hauled down from the mountains. Slanted roofs protected them from snow, while stacks of firewood were piled against nearly every wall.

Unlike the mammoth village, the slime village, or Weavershall, this settlement was smaller and rougher, with no defining industry beyond serving as a staging point for freelancers hunting sky mana cores.

As we entered the stable yard, a boy of around ten came sprinting toward us. He had white hair and a pair of goat horns protruding from his forehead.

"Alice! You're back!" the kid said, beaming. "And you brought friends!"

"I also brought work for you."

I tossed him a bronze coin.

"Take care of these for us, will you?" Then I tossed him a second one for good measure.

"Of course, Alice!" His grin somehow widened even further.

He immediately moved toward the Burdrases and began unhooking them from the carts.

"Are you staying for lunch?" he asked.

"Not a chance."

"Why not?" The boy acted offended.

"Because your dad's cooking is terrible."

He burst out laughing.

"You can't just say that," Vena protested, clearly disapproving of how rude I was being.

"Oh, don't worry. It's not exactly a secret."

"It's true," the boy admitted. "Everyone says that."

"He should let you cook instead."

"I tried asking."

"And?"

"The old man is as stubborn as a goat."

"Sounds hereditary," I said, glancing at his horns.

The boy groaned, and I laughed lightheartedly.

"Oh well, good luck with him," I said. "The animals might be staying here for a day or two. Take good care of them."

He placed a hand over his chest.

"I will."

Once the carts were unloaded and the animals settled, we left the stable and headed toward the eastern trail. The village quickly disappeared behind us as we began climbing toward the mountains.

"This path is mostly empty of monsters," I explained. "It's too close to the village. Nothing here survives long enough to become dangerous."

"If you want to collect sky cores, it's better to circle the mountain and climb from the western side."

"What happens if something dangerous migrates here?" Kan asked.

"Freelancers get called," I shrugged.

"Although most of the mountain keeps getting picked clean. This is one of the few places where sky mana cores can be reliably harvested."

"This is such a waste," said Ja'a. "Someone should establish a proper monster-farming operation. The sky mana here is incredibly dense, but I can't sense anything stronger than a child."

"How would you even do that?" asked Calr. "Most sky monsters eventually learn to fly."

"Lightning field mice should work," said Raik. "They could import some from the Living Storm Dead Zone."

"That could actually work," Ja'a immediately nodded, "Maybe I should sponsor someone to try it."

Her eyes lit up when she said that.

As the conversation continued, we finally reached the edge of the phenomenon.

I was walking at the front of the group, so I turned around and started moving backward. I wanted to watch their reactions.

The moment I crossed the boundary, the world abruptly changed.

Day became night in a heartbeat. One small step was all it took, and the sunlight vanished as if it had never existed.

What made it truly strange was that from outside the disturbance, the mountain looked completely normal. You could even see sunlight shining on the slopes ahead. But from inside the phenomenon, the sun was completely negated, as though something had erased it from reality.

A second later, the rest of the team stepped through one by one.

Everyone stopped.

Each reacted in their own way.

Calr did exactly what I had done when I first experienced the phenomenon. He immediately began stepping backward and forward across the boundary.

Day, night, day, then night again.

"The sun just disappears," he muttered. "Like it was never there."

"Even the temperature changes," said Raik, extending his senses while mana bled from his body in gentle waves of heat.

"The sky is beautiful," Kan whispered.

She stared upward as countless stars spread across the heavens. Without sunlight, the night sky revealed itself in impossible detail, rivers of starlight stretching from horizon to horizon.

Shingo was looking around with a sense of wonder in his eyes, and even Katar appeared unsettled by the abrupt shift. His hand rested on his sword as though he expected an ambush to emerge from the darkness at any moment.

I watched their reactions with a smile. I remembered feeling exactly the same the first time I came here. It was nice, for once, to be the person with the knowledge and experience. Usually, I was the stranger in a strange land.

Not today.

As everyone regained their composure, we resumed climbing.

"Keep an eye out for moon hares," I said. "It will be at least another hour before we reach my camp near the tower."

"I can sense a few living things nearby," said Ja'a. "Most of them are hiding underground. Nothing particularly strong."

"There should be normal animals here too," I said. "I've seen foxes and birds before. The only magical creatures I've personally encountered were moon hares and celestial owls."

"That, and the sky elemental we're here to slay," I added.

As I spoke, I heard the sharp clang of a crossbow firing.

I turned just in time to see Calr's bolt streak toward a patch of magenta fur.

A fraction of a second before impact, the rabbit vanished. The bolt passed harmlessly through empty air. A moment later, the creature reappeared several meters away.

"No way," said Calr. "I thought I had it."

"They're hard to catch," I laughed. "Let's keep moving. That one knows you're here now. It'll keep blinking away every time you attack it."

"No, no. I think I can get it." Calr was already reloading.

He fired a second bolt.

Just like before, the moon hare vanished at the last possible moment and reappeared farther away.

"I see." Calr nodded slowly as he began reloading again.

"I think I understand now."

"Don't waste your arrows," I groaned. "It's way too alert now."

"Last attempt. Promise."

This time, instead of firing his crossbow, he suddenly threw one of his old throwing knives.

The moon hare immediately teleported away, exactly as expected.

But that was according to Calr's plan. The instant the creature reappeared, calr was already in position.

Thwack. His crossbow snapped up, and the bolt flew straight, hitting the moon hare directly on the neck.

Calr pumped a fist into the air in celebration.

"Oh wow, you actually got it," said Ja'a.

"I guess I underestimated you," I admitted with a smile. "You proved me wrong."

"Now try catching a few more for dinner," said Ja'a. "I've heard they taste incredible."

"They do," I said.

"Katar, skin the fur," said Ja'a. "It should fetch a good price."

"Fine," grumbled Katar.

The swordsman walked over toward the fallen rabbit while the rest of us continued scanning the darkness for more flashes of magenta fur.

By the time we reached my camp, we had five hares skinned, cleaned, and packed away.

My campsite was little more than a stone fire ring beside the ruined remains of an old domed structure barely larger than a single room. Similar ruins dotted the mountainside, leading all the way toward the tower at the summit.

As soon as we arrived, everyone slipped naturally into their tasks.

We expanded the camp by unfolding chairs and setting up a portable table. Calr and Kan gathered around it to butcher the hares while Vena and I prepared herbs and seasonings. Raik and the rest of the team handled the tents and camp preparations.

One of the more bizarre features of the Sunless Reach was its weather. The sky was always clear with no clouds in sight. Despite the perpetual night and the cold temperatures, the weather never seemed to change. That meant no rain and no snow. Maybe that's why vegetation was sparse.

We decided to roast the hind legs for lunch while the rest of the meat went into a stew pot. Soon, the smell of roasting meat filled the campsite. By the time the food was ready, everyone was hungry enough that conversation largely stopped in favor of eating.

Each person received one leg, while Shingo received three. The giant kindred needed the extra protein the most, after all. Moon hare tasted somewhat like rabbit mixed with pheasant, flavorful, lean meat that wasn't chewy; whatever magical adaptations the creatures possessed seemed to keep it tender.

The Sunless Reach had a strange tranquility to it. As we ate, our attention naturally drifted upward. The sky above was breathtaking. Countless stars stretched across the darkness, interrupted every few minutes by another shooting star streaking across the heavens. Each passing star filled me with mana, adding to the feeling of comfort and peace that always settled over me whenever I visited this place.

"It really is beautiful here," Kan said softly.

The starlight reflected in her eyes as she stared upward.

"Yeah," smiled Raik, looking at her instead of the sky.

He slipped an arm around her shoulders. The blonde girl leaned against him without complaint.

For a few minutes, nobody spoke. We simply sat around the fire, eating roasted moon hare and watching the stars drift overhead.

Once I was done eating, I stood and retrieved my telescope from my bag of holding.

Calr immediately noticed.

"What's that?"

"You're in for a little surprise," I grinned.

I spent a few moments setting up the tripod and calibrating the lenses before aiming it toward one particularly bright object.

When I was satisfied, I stepped aside.

"Calr, meet your sun for the first time."

He leaned forward and pressed his eye against the telescope.

A moment later, he gasped. The expression on his face alone made reinventing the device in another world worth it.

As he stepped back, Ja'a immediately floated over and claimed the eyepiece for herself.

Meanwhile, Calr's eyes fogged over as he activated Analyse.

"We have a third sun," he finally said. "Something similar to the suns of the Bloodline and Mythic Realms."

"It would be more accurate to say that you only have one sun," I corrected. "The things you see each day are actually a second and third moon."

His eyes immediately fogged over again.

I could practically see his worldview rearranging itself.

Pulling out my astronomy notes, I unfolded several pages and showed him my rough heliocentric model of the local system.

"And you've located all the planets?" he asked.

"No. Only the two closest ones."

His excitement somehow increased.

For the next few hours, we played with the telescope. I showed the team both planets I had already identified, the outermost planet and the gas giant with its spectacular rings.

Everyone wanted a turn. Even Katar eventually looked through it after enough encouragement.

Watching people see planets for the first time never got old. Still, nobody was nearly as invested in the discovery as Calr.

For him, this wasn't just curiosity. This was a puzzle. Something that could be solved.

After a while, I let him take over the telescope while I settled into my chaise longue to watch the shooting stars.

I barely managed five minutes of relaxation before hearing an excited shout.

"Alice! I found it! I found another planet!"

I sat upright.

"How?"

Calr turned toward me, practically vibrating with excitement.

"I have a perfect memory. I used Analyse to compare every night sky I've ever seen throughout my life. Then I looked for the stars that drifted the most."

I blinked.

That was actually brilliant.

I hurried over and looked through the telescope, and sure enough, there it was.

Another gas giant, or maybe an ice giant. Smaller than the first one I'd found, though whether that meant it was physically smaller or simply farther away remained impossible to tell.

"I think I can find the others," he said.

"As long as they're in the correct phase and not hidden behind the sun," I corrected.

He nodded immediately, but that didn't seem to discourage him in the slightest. In fact, it only made him more determined.

I pulled out fresh paper and began recording his observations while he continued his search.

Eventually, everyone drifted off to their own activities.

Vena started sparring with Katar, the swordsman, instructing her on how to improve her use of the claymore.

Raik was helping Kan train her cold resistance by freezing her inside the bathtub.

Shingo had fallen asleep beside the fire and was now snoring loudly enough to scare away the few nearby animals.

Ja'a sat near one of the tents, performing inventory and making notes about everything she planned to sell once we returned to Hano.

Meanwhile, Calr and I remained glued to the telescope. Hours passed as we mapped the heavens. We found several more planets, sketched their approximate locations, and even identified a handful of asteroids drifting against the background stars. Calr's Analyse ability turned what would have taken me months into a single afternoon.

We only stopped when the smell of rabbit stew finally dragged us back to reality.

As we approached the fire, I saw Kan and Raik manning the pot. Ja’a was sitting near them. She lifted her head and narrowed her eyes at us.

"Your souls have grown again."

Calr and I exchanged glances.

"Both of you," she continued, "though his more than yours."

"Seriously?" Kan groaned. "I have to freeze myself alive to get stronger, and you two get stronger by staring through a tube."

"Staring through a tube?" Calr protested. "We were uncovering the secrets of the universe."

"Does it matter?" scoffed Katar.

"When you freeze yourself, you get stronger against ice users. When they stare through a tube, they only get better at staring through more tubes."

"That last part made absolutely no sense," Calr complained.

"It made perfect sense."

"No, it didn't."

"It did."

"It really didn't."

While the two continued bickering like children, I sat next to Vena.

"So that's what you do whenever you teleport here," Vena said, looking at me.

"Partly," I shrugged. "That and I use my teleportation magic. A lot."

"What do you use it for?" asked Ja'a.

"Something like this!" I said.

Reaching into my bag of holding, I pulled out my trusty shovel, then leaned back and looked toward the sky. I had to awkwardly wait a whole minute for a shooting star to cross the heavens.

"I wish I were safely standing on top of buried treasure that has remained undisturbed for at least a hundred years," I wish out loud.

The world blurred, and I vanished.

Five minutes later, I returned with a heavy canvas sack. I threw it toward Ja'a, and it landed on the ground beside her with a metallic clatter.

The merchant opened it with anticipation. Inside, she found an old jewelry set: a silver necklace, two bracelets, a pair of earrings, and a ring. Each piece was adorned with a brilliant blue sapphire.

Ja'a's eyes widened.

"You could do that the entire time?"

"I've been doing that the entire time." I shrugged. "How do you think I got rich?"

The merchant stared at the jewelry, then at me.

"You're telling me your financial strategy is literally wishing for treasure?"

"That, and investing in a couple of businesses," I shrugged.

"Although I've probably picked most of this region clean by now," I added. "I can't seem to find gold coins anymore."

"Why the hundred-year restriction?" Calr asked. He was also looking at the jewelry with an unreadable expression.

"I am not a thief," I shrugged. "I don't want to accidentally steal somebody's savings."

"That is well thought out and responsible," Vena nodded approvingly.

"I have my moments." I shrugged.

"Which makes me question why you became a freelancer," asked Kan. "If you don't need money, why are you adventuring?"

I thought about it.

"It's fun," I smiled. "But if I'm being honest, I only joined this expedition because Raik and Katar saved me from that spider matriarch a while ago."

"So this entire adventure is to repay us?" Raik grinned.

"Partly. I also wanted to see places other than Hano."

"I would love to have a power like yours," Vena said wistfully.

I looked at her as she stared at the stars.

"Why?"

"I could teleport wherever I was most needed."

The answer came instantly, without hesitation, confirming my earlier conclusion.

I wasn't a saint.

I wasn't a hero.

But you know what? I don't need to be! I can always support the actual heroes instead.

"Wanna try?" I offered.

"Seriously?" She blinked.

"Yeah. Seriously."

Everyone stopped what they were doing. Even Ja'a looked up from inspecting the new jewellery.

"Is that wise?" she asked. "I thought you had trouble teleporting with other people."

"Mostly because I run out of mana," I said. "My spear can only hold so much, but here in the Sunless Reach itself, my star mana is nearly endless."

I turned toward Kan. She was currently guarding the stirring stew pot.

"How long until dinner is ready?"

"Around thirty minutes."

"Perfect."

I looked up and waited until, eventually, another shooting star crossed the sky.

"I wish we would safely return here in thirty minutes."

The magic settled around me, banking the wish. Some of my personal mana was also consumed, but not as much as I expected.

Then I stood and walked over to Vena.

"Are we actually doing this?"

"Of course."

I placed an arm around her shoulders.

"Ready?"

"I guess."

"Good enough."

A few moments later, another shooting star streaked overhead.

"I wish we would safely teleport to wherever Vena would be the most useful."

We both vanished.

Vena and I appeared inside a small wooden house.

The sudden transition made Vena stumble a bit. I quickly helped her regain her balance and looked around.

The room was modest even by village standards. Rough wooden walls surrounded a single bed and a small table, while a clay burner glowed with embers, keeping the room warm and adding a faint haze of smoke to an already overwhelming smell of rot, sweat, and sickness.

A teenage girl lay on the bed. She was curled into a ball, clutching her left arm against her chest while trembling from pain.

Without hesitation, Vena rushed to her side and pulled back the blanket covering the girl.

I immediately recoiled at the sight.

"Is she rotting?" I blurted out.

The girl's arm looked horrifying. Her skin had turned gray and black from her fingertips to her shoulder. Swollen veins bulged beneath the flesh, while patches of dead skin hung loose. That wasn't even the worst of it. I could see movement beneath the surface as a maggot emerged from a crack in the flesh before disappearing back inside.

"No," Vena said grimly. "This is a curse."

Before I could ask anything else, the front door burst open.

A boy around the girl's age stormed into the room carrying a wooden club. The moment he saw two strangers standing over his friend, he raised the weapon.

"Who are you?" he shouted. "What are you doing to Viva?"

"Calm down," I said, raising both hands.

The boy looked ready to swing anyway. Still, I had been training and fighting nonstop since I arrived in this world, and I could tell he was no real threat from his posture and the way he held his weapon. So I decided to de-escalate the situation.

"She is a cleric," I continued, pointing at Vena. "She's here to help your friend."

"A cleric?" His grip on the club loosened slightly.

"Yeah," I reiterated. "A cleric of the Holy."

Vena barely glanced at him.

"What did she steal?"

"What?" The boy blinked.

"She clearly stole from a shrine," Vena said in a voice that remained calm and matter-of-fact. "Which god?"

The boy's face immediately darkened. For a few seconds, he remained silent, then he looked away.

"She took a silver necklace."

"Explain," Vena sighed.

"There's this smith from Verraden..." The boy swallowed, then continued. "Every year, he places a silver necklace nearby. We live on the road to Ectamal, you see."

"He leaves it on a tombstone marker," the boy added. "Viva watched him do it and took it after he left."

"That wasn't a tombstone marker." Vena closed her eyes.

"What?"

"It was a shrine to Rokh," the cleric rubbed her forehead. "Pantheonic God of Death, at least the current one."

"Oh."

The color drained from the boy's face.

"Those offerings are placed there to ward against the undead and guide off wandering spirits."

"We didn't know."

I frowned at that.

"Do the gods really curse people for an honest mistake?" I asked.

"Some do." Vena's expression shifted into a grimace. "But Rokh is known to be one of the reasonable gods. He is not Shana, after all."

"My guess is this is not the first time she did it," she suggested.

"I only know about one," said the boy.

"Three times," spoke the girl in a harsh, scratchy voice. "I took a necklace three years in a row."

The cleric nodded, checking the girl's throat with her hand for signs of inflammation.

"Alright. Here's what we're going to do," she said. "I will try to remove the curse."

Hope immediately appeared on their faces.

"Meanwhile, you are going to return the necklace." She pointed at the boy.

"I only… have… last one," spoke the girl, breathing heavily. "Sold the others."

"It will do." Vena pointed toward the door. "Then go."

The boy hesitated.

"But..."

"Now."

A moment later, he picked up a thick silver chain from a closet and started running down the road.

The cleric waited until he was gone before turning back toward the girl.

"Alice."

"Yeah?"

"Help me hold her down."

That was slightly ominous. Still, I stepped forward to help.

Vena gently removed the girl's shirt to expose the infected arm.

The flesh looked even worse than before, the corruption having spread all the way to her shoulder, with dark veins pulsing beneath the skin like living worms.

"Alice."

"Yes?"

"Do you have salt?"

"Salt?" I paused.

Of course, she needed salt. Purification rituals. It made sense.

"Yeah, sure."

I reached into my bag of holding and handed her a medium sack, around two or three kilos, that I had brought for cooking and the occasional ghost slaying.

Vena opened the bag, then hesitated.

"Do you have a leather belt?" she asked.

"Sure." I nodded.

At this point, my bag of holding was a hoarder's dream.

Vena placed the leather between the girl's teeth and whispered, "Bite on this. This is going to be painful. I am sorry."

The girl nodded.

Without hesitation, Vena began pouring salt over the diseased arm.

The reaction was painful to hear.

The girl screamed a gut-wrenching scream. It was the sort of raw, desperate sound that came from true agony.

Her entire body convulsed, and she tried to pull away, but I was holding her too tightly.

The cleric continued packing salt into every crack and wound until the entire arm was covered in a thick white layer.

Then she began to pray.

"Oh, Holy Light, safeguard us from the pettiness of people and the tyranny of gods."

Golden light filled the room, and the girl's screaming suddenly intensified despite the leather covering her mouth.

The salt started darkening, turning gray and then black, the layer hardening in the process into something resembling ceramic.

The smell was awful, like rotten meat left in the sun.

Vena continued her prayer.

"Oh, Holy Lady, have mercy on this child. Shelter her under your wing."

The golden light seeped through cracks forming across the blackened crust, piercing through it.

Finally, it cracked, and pieces began falling away.

The girl's screams weakened as the corruption retreated. And like a chick hatching from an egg, healthy flesh appeared beneath the crumbling dark crust of calcified salt.

Eventually, the teenager collapsed unconscious from exhaustion, her face covered in sweat and her breathing shallow but steady.

Vena carefully lifted the blackened chunks of cursed salt and carried them to the coal burner. The moment they touched the hot coals, dark smoke erupted upward before dissolving into nothing.

"Wasn't that curse a bit extreme for a stolen piece of jewelry?"

Vena sighed.

"As much as I don't want to defend the gods of the Pantheon, the value of the necklace was more important than its price on the market. This far from his base of power, Rokh probably used it to anchor a miracle, and this silly girl stole it... she stole a fragment of divine power."

"Would the god be angry at you for dispelling its curse?"

"Maybe," she said again. "We need an appeasement offering."

"Would you happen to have any silver jewelry you are willing to part with? Something from Verraden, if possible?" Vena asked.

I immediately removed one of my bracelets.

"Will this work?" I asked. "I bought it to ward off the undead."

"Perfect."

She turned toward the unconscious girl and slid the silver bracelet onto her newly healed wrist.

Only then did Vena place a hand on the teenager's forehead.

"Rise and shine in the name of Mary."

The prayer was spoken in a soothing voice, almost like a lullaby.

The girl slowly opened her eyes.

Confusion gave way to disbelief as she stared at her restored arm.

Vena smiled.

"You should be fine now."

Tears immediately appeared in the girl's eyes.

Before she could speak, the cleric raised a hand.

"Listen carefully."

The girl nodded.

"You see this bracelet?"

She tapped the silver band.

The teenager nodded again.

"You will wear it for a full year."

"Okay."

"Next year, you will place it on the shrine where you stole the necklaces."

"I understand," the girl swallowed.

"Good."

Vena's smile returned.

"Now rest."

The cleric placed her hand on the girl's forehead one final time, and golden light shimmered.

The girl's eyes closed, and within seconds she was peacefully asleep. It was probably the first truly peaceful sleep she had experienced in days.

As silence settled over the room, I looked at Vena. The cleric looked exhausted. Yet there was also a quiet satisfaction on her face.

"Can we do it again?" she said.

"Do what?" I asked.

"Teleport again and see if other people need help."

And people call me a saint...

If there were really a saint, it would be her.

"Maybe tomorrow, after we kill the sky elemental," I said.

The thirty minutes ended before the boy returned. I held Vena by one arm, and we both disappeared.
