When Edge bent down to pick up a rock, he found himself staring at generous cleavage formed from sparkling white stone.

It took him a few seconds to process the full implications of the unexpected development. He was piloting a clone, but it wasn’t his clone. The sentinels look like their avatar, not like me. The body swap wasn’t too disorienting—the constructs didn’t have organs, and their features were merely cosmetic.

That being said, he could already hear Jumo’s laughter. The crew was going to tease him about this for quite some time… or more likely, forever. At least they’re wearing armor. I’d die of embarrassment if the clones were nude, and their originals would be less than pleased.

While he had decidedly mixed feelings about this situation, he had more important matters to attend to than staring at the slice of stone breasts visible in the periphery of his view. He shelved the issue for the time being and focused on making the most of his five-minute test drive, exploring the skill’s ins and outs before tomorrow’s match.

Edge couldn’t talk, but his fingers were responsive, which meant he could communicate via hunters’ hand signals. He couldn’t access the gear strapped to his body, which had also been phased out, but he could reach into his vault. It was a pleasant surprise, since it meant he could still use consumables, change weapons, and the like.

As the description had warned him, he couldn’t cast spells while Sentinel was active, and his passive powers didn’t boost the clones’ performance. On the plus side, he had left Mantle running, and while it didn’t affect the construct, the aura could still benefit his friends.

Next, he bent down, picked up a baseball-sized rock, and hurled it as hard as he could. The stone went streaking from his hand at incredible speed, shattering when it slammed into the boulder he had used for target practice. His Reflex and Speed were worse in this form, but his Power, Endurance, and Durability were way higher. He had a hunch that each construct had unique advantages and drawbacks, and this clone was specialized for weathering a direct assault.

He grabbed another stone and squeezed, grinning when the rock was reduced to gravel with no appreciable effort. Edge wanted to try out the other constructs before he ran out of time, so he ordered the stone sentinel to dig the deepest hole it could and sent his awareness into the clone made of ash and wind.

The next thing he knew, he found himself hovering a dozen feet off the ground. His body felt light as a feather, although it still had a decent amount of mass thanks to the ash.

This sentinel was way faster than the stone clone. Its manual dexterity was better than his body’s, which meant it could manipulate objects freely and perform precision tasks. He spent a wonderful minute flying, soaring above the treetops, enjoying the view from on high before moving on.

The ash body could pick up rocks too, although it couldn’t throw them very hard. He ran a few tests and decided that it could carry about fifty pounds, although the burden slowed his movements considerably. While the stone clone was perfectly capable of brawling barehanded, if he wanted to fight in this body, he needed to arm it with something appropriate.

Light blades that target vital points would be perfect, along with poisons, weapon oils, and consumables. On that note, ranged weapons that don’t rely on the body’s strength should work well too, like boltcasters and crossbows. I could try to engulf my enemy’s head to blind them with ash, although I’ll have to contend with their Disruption field if I do.

The ash sentinel’s shape was malleable like his shadow form, which meant it could squeeze through tight spaces. He ordered it to gather every stick it could find and pile them beside the hole the stone clone was digging, then slipped into the clay construct for the final phase of his trial run.

This form split the difference between the other two as far as physical attributes were concerned—an even balance that was superior to what his flesh-and-blood body could manage. The clay had a lot of mass but was relatively soft, which meant most physical attacks would simply pass through. He could firm up parts at will, so he could still wield weapons and the like. He could probably suffocate enemies by pulling them inside, though attempting it while controlling the construct would be rather unpleasant.

Edge would have to learn more details later, because his five minutes were up. The clones vanished in a flash of light, and he found himself standing in his own skin. There was a massive mound of sticks and a deep hole beside him. His clones had made considerable progress for a few minutes of labor, which meant the Epic power had some utility outside of combat.

With that out of the way, it was time to steal a skill and take Tri-State Sentinel to rank two. While he looked for a beast to hunt, he considered what he’d learned, merging his direct observations with the information provided by his vision.

The spell was incredibly expensive to cast, but it didn’t cost any energy to maintain past that point. The power had some significant drawbacks, but it offered incredible benefits, including the ability to phase out of the way of an attack he couldn’t survive.

The clones were immune to venom, poison, suffocation, and drowning, along with magic that altered a living body. He was vulnerable to skills that were effective against each clone’s specific composition, and being hit with Disruption would shave some time off the clock, although only a powerful surge could negate the massive amount of mana powering the potent magic.

He had considered trying to get around the movement restriction by casting Sentinel while riding in a wagon, but now he was certain that he would remain floating in place if he tried. He wasn’t sure what would happen if someone moved a physical object into the same space his phased form occupied, although he would try his hardest never to find out. If that happens, I’ll switch on Shadow Play the instant I rematerialize, which should prevent a worst-case scenario.

He hadn’t gotten a chance to evaluate his clones’ combat prowess and hoped they would be decent fighters in automatic mode. Even if they weren’t, he could control one directly and use the others to launch a pincer attack to keep his opponent off-balance.

Technique aside, their attributes are great, and equipping them with the right weapons will make them even stronger. They make decent laborers, and working together, they should be able to move some insanely heavy objects.

All in all, Edge was thrilled by what he’d learned. He knew he had only scratched the surface of Sentinel’s potential, some of which he was about to unlock here and now. He had spotted a stage-two boar napping in a sunbeam while mulling the matter over, which would provide a delicious dinner for his friends on top of advancing his skill.

He used his go-to ambush of sneaking up in shadow form and freezing the beast with an iceblade, then Extracted three skills back-to-back. He fed two to Wind Ward, which was sufficient to rank it up thanks to the experience-generating training sessions Chibime and Sea conducted in his village. He gave the third to Sentinel, which meant he had two skills to advance instead of one.

Smiling like a fool, he stepped into his inner world, praised the pair of avatars shining with transformative energy, and called up his Guide to provide the details.

The skill Wind Ward has advanced to Rank 3.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Wind Ward is a triggered skill. As such, its mana cost is fixed and may not be flared. Instead, the skill’s cooldown has been reduced from 5 minutes to 2 minutes.

The upgrade was in line with his expectations, as most triggered skills received a reduced cooldown or the ability to fine-tune their activation conditions after hitting rank three. Since Wind Ward was extremely effective at deflecting incoming projectiles and he could communicate with the avatar directly if he wanted to tweak its behavior, being able to use it more often was the best possible outcome.

Two minutes was a long time in the middle of combat, but it was a considerable improvement, dramatically shrinking the window where he was vulnerable to being sniped and making him more effective when engaging ranged specialists. Wind Ward would come in handy during the Duos’ Clash, and he was happy it had advanced in time.

When the frenetic tornado stopped shining, it was bigger and blusterier than ever. Edge showered the excited avatar with praise as he asked his Guide to display the next update.

The skill Tri-State Sentinel has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

Since rank-two upgrades determined a power’s developmental path in addition to providing immediate benefits, Edge spent quite a while pondering each option. Epic powers came few and far between, and he wanted to make sure he was making the most of its potential before committing to a course.

The first upgrade was interesting. It would enhance his perception by removing the dulled senses he’d experienced during his test run. He could also speak while the skill was running, which wasn’t vital but would certainly come in handy.

He might have been tempted if he were using the constructs over a considerable distance or their duration was longer, letting him perform complex tasks with ease. However, since he planned to use Sentinel primarily as a finishing move or a form of emergency defense, the path wasn’t as tempting as the other two, and he crossed it off the list.

The duration upgrade was simple, straightforward, and generally useful. He was certain that it would grow considerably longer as he progressed down that path. Three more minutes of clone combat was a decent upgrade in itself and, just as critically, would extend the period during which his body was invulnerable. However, Edge wanted to take the Epic skill down a path that would maximize its power, which led him to pick the second option on the list.

While it didn’t provide a direct boost, empowering the clones with his passive skills and auras was a massive upgrade, along with any auras his friends provided. The Speed bonus from Fear the Seasons and the damage from Dragon’s Greed were crazy powerful, as was the perceptual enhancement from All-Seeing Gaze. Warlord’s Mantle provided a nice attribute bonus too—plus some defense against physical and magical attacks—and Aegis would shield the constructs from corrosion and elemental spells.

When you took into consideration the fact that he planned to pick up quite a few more passives when he evolved and that each skill would effectively triple its benefit since it affected all three sentinels at once, this was clearly the best path for him, regardless of what was on the table when he advanced it to rank four.

When he made his selection, Mud Mel, Ash Lilly, and Stone Sash, who were glowing so bright it was hard to look at them, changed form. The details of their features became more intricate, and the avatars doubled in size until they were just a little shorter than Chibime. Everyone was thrilled by the accomplishment. Edge and Rue threw an impromptu party to celebrate both avatars’ achievements, dancing and playing until his time was up.

He stepped back into his body and hiked to World’s End with a spring in his step, fantasizing about fighting by Mel’s side in front of a roaring crowd.
