Freya

I didn’t get much sleep after that prayer session with Zaiden. When we went back to Alterus, I spent the next few hours until morning trying to figure out why I heard my father’s voice in the realm of The Infinity Tree. By the time I woke up, I came up with the only theory that made sense—my father was a god.

As much as I wanted to dismiss the entire bizarre idea, the evidence supporting that theory was too strong. First, my reincarnation. If we were going with the whole being part god theory, I should have stayed dead after Nyx blasted everyone in that diner. Yet, here I was, in a dragon body, doing dragon things in a world that wasn’t my own. Second, my ability to activate dungeons. I remembered Nolan telling me that only gods could do that. I had the special touch, and it was no coincidence that I did it so easily, either. Then, there was that time I summoned The Dark One, and how he called me master. Again, more godly play at work.

And let’s not forget my infernal bloodline… yeah, that made sense, too. My special codex assignment given these necro powers and my ability to level up like nobody’s business. Dad was a god, which one? Now that, I had no clue.

Well, maybe not no clue. It was obvious that he was one of the bad ones. Either one of the big six, or a demi, or titan. The gods were trapped inside of that realm, and ever since I heard them, I couldn’t get my mind off of how to free them.

I doubt it’d be easy. It had been an entire decade, after all. But if I was part god, why wasn’t I trapped in the realm of the tree, too? Isn’t that how it worked?

I planned on telling Nolan everything, but as soon as he came into my cabin and woke me up this morning, I couldn’t utter a peep. He was pretty quiet too, for his own reasons, until we met up with Lun by a farm.

He was standing at the doorway of this quaint farmhouse with a gorgeous fae woman with long braided jet black hair. She had these beautiful pair of emerald eyes, and a type of smile that matched the radiance of the sun. Her giggling was infectious, a goofy dragon smile stretching between my cheeks as I walked beside Nolan. As she handed Lun a carton of eggs, she spotted us, her attention immediately drawing to me. That smile on her froze, and she was looking at me like she just saw a ghost.

To be honest, she caught me off guard as well. I couldn’t understand why she felt familiar. I’d never seen her before….

Nolan waved at them while I tried to get the butterflies in my stomach to stop Salsa dancing. Once we reached the porch, he greeted them and introduced us to the woman.

“Name’s Nolan, and this here is my sidekick, Freya,” he said with the friendliest tone I’d heard from him thus far.

“She’s… adorable,” the gentle-faced fae woman complimented. “Freya is a lovely name. My name is Fiona.”

“Fiona here is an amazing cook!” Lun praised. “She makes the best honey cakes in all of Alterus. You should try some!”

Fiona’s cheeks flushed pink at the compliment, but her eyes never left mine. There was something in her gaze—recognition mixed with confusion, like she was trying to solve a puzzle that had pieces missing.

“Freya,” she repeated softly, almost like she was testing how the name felt on her tongue. “It’s strange, but... have we met before?”

My dragon heart skipped a beat. The butterflies in my stomach turned into full-blown acrobats.

“No, you couldn’t have,” Nolan said for me. “And she can’t talk directly to you without opening a private link. Dragon rules, remember?”

“Oh, yes, right! Of course, It’s… just that I rarely see them so young! Or at all.”

“Dragons don’t make it to Alterus at all.” Lun chuckled. “Same as most fae regions.”

“Would you like to come inside? I just finished baking,” Fiona offered, though her gaze kept flickering back to me with that same perplexed expression.

I nodded eagerly, my dragon snout practically twitching at the heavenly scent wafting from inside the farmhouse. Sweet honey mixed with something spiced and warm.

I nudged Nolan to accept the invite, but he kindly denied it. “We’ll take a sample to go, if you don’t mind. We are in a rush out.”

‘We can’t stay for a little bit?’ I insisted.

‘We need to get to Esme, remember?’

‘She’s not going anywhere. The shop will still be there.’

‘We aren’t staying. She is staring at you too hard. She might have bad intentions. You are a dragon, still young, and still very much a prize for a lot of people. And she was playing dumb like she didn’t know she couldn’t talk directly to you. I doubt she had a dragon scale to communicate with you.’

‘Honest mistake. Why are you being so harsh to this sweet woman?’

“Oh, sure! Let me go inside and grab you some!” she said as she vanished inside.

‘And we aren’t eating that shit either. Could be poisonous.’

I rolled my eyes.

Zaiden was right. Nolan was acting more and more fatherly the longer I hung out with him. Which honestly, felt pretty damn good. We vibed well, we got along, and even when we didn’t, our relationship always bounced back. He was like my best friend and my biggest supporter.

“So, I see you two are ready to head on out?” Lun asked curiously.

Nolan smiled. “I see you’re ready to do the same.”

“This small town is sweet, but my heart belongs somewhere else.”

‘Hey, ask him if he wants to stay in our party!’ I suggested to Nolan. ‘Lun’s an amazing mage!’

Nolan sighed and relayed my request, but Lun denied. He insisted on running solo. After being with Varg, and then getting wrapped up with Zev, he was done with team ups. Not only that, but he double-crossed the king, and he was worried about his safety. He was bent on keeping a very low profile.

“You know, you’re not half bad, lizard boy,” Nolan said to Lun. “I was planning on hog tying you up again, but after the adventure we had, I’d say, we are practically brothers.”

Lun deadpanned him. “That comment wasn’t an ounce bit heartwarming,” he said, then fixed his face to a more playful tone. “But I guess the same could be said about you, gunslinger. As I could have easily tied you up and claimed the bounty on your head, run off to a nice quiet island with the money, and sip Grenoose with my darling bunny for the rest of my life, but our little survival trial was a real eye-opener. I was never one for second chances. Raised in a cut-throat society, the concept was unheard of. Though, in light of these changes, I might change my perspective on it. If it weren’t for you and Freya, I wouldn’t be standing here right now. Not only did you help my people, you two saved my life. And for that, I am forever grateful.” He offered Nolan his scaly hand, the gunslinger taking it confidently.

“Same,” Nolan said with a smile. “Hey, do yourself a favor and get out of bad business.”

“Oh, after this, I’m finding myself a nice hideout until the king and his men are dead!”

“If things go the way I hope they will, you won’t find yourself hiding for long.”

“Here you go,” Fiona said as she came back, offering Nolan a cloth-wrapped bundle. The sweet aroma that wafted from it made my mouth water instantly. “I added a little extra, just in case you get hungry on your journey.”

Nolan accepted it with a nod, though I could tell from his stiff posture he was still suspicious. “Much obliged.”

“It was lovely meeting you both,” Fiona said, her eyes lingering on me again. “Especially you, Freya. If you’re ever passing through again, my door is always open.”

‘Tell her thank you!’ I urged Nolan.

He relayed my message before he said goodbye, Lun walking down the short flight of stairs with us. She stood at the doorway waving still as we continued to walk away, her eyes on me like a hawk!

“Heh, the amazement when you see a dragon for the first time!” Lun chuckled, glancing back at Fiona who was still watching us from her porch. “I remember the first time I saw one back in Lacrym. Nearly pissed myself.”

“Not surprising. Dragons are rare enough that most folks stare. But I don’t like how she couldn’t take her eyes off Freya.”

“That’s the protective side of you glowing, gunslinger,” Lun said as we reached the golden wheat fields. “Fiona wouldn’t hurt a fly! She runs her own orphanage, you know? A really sweet gal. Occasionally helps out in the farm, makes sweet cakes in the morning, and—”

“Knits these funny looking sweaters for the chickens in winterfall,” someone said from our left, and when I turned around, I saw Typha and Zaiden. She crossed her arms over her chest and raised her eyebrow at us. “I hope you weren’t planning on leaving without saying goodbye.”

“Where did you two come from?” Nolan asked.

“Shadow portal,” Zaiden said as he flexed his shadow arm. “Just one of the many tricks I’m learning from this thing. And being Freya’s thrall has its perks,” he added with a smirk.

Huh, so he could track me too? What a treat!

“We were about to come find you,” Nolan replied. “Got distracted by honey cakes.”

“So, this is where we part ways?” Typha asked us.

Nolan nodded. “Seems like it.”

“Well, I can’t say it’s been fun, but it was certainly an experience.”

“We have a lot to be thankful for,” Zaiden added. “Fate clearly had other plans for us, so let’s not waste it by being idle. There’s a lot of work to be done,” he said before he looked at me. ‘We could really use you in Alterus, but I understand you have bigger plans out there. Maybe you can stop by and visit us sometime? I can portal over and pick you up, just say the word,’ he told me privately.

‘Sure! This fae town seems friendly enough!’

‘And it’ll be reinforced by the time you do visit. That bastard isn’t going to catch us like he did last time.’

‘With your new powers, I’m sure you’ll give Zev hell!’

“Can you two cut that out?” Typha teased. “It’s rude to have your own secret conversation going while we are standing right here.”

“Heh, someone’s clearly jealous,” Zaiden poked at his younger sister.

“So the both of you are staying in Alterus,” Lun summarized, “and Freya and Nolan, you are headed east. More dungeon raiding I presume?”

“That’s the plan.” Nolan didn’t tell anyone we were going to fix up a cure for King Khunum’s curse, and I didn’t either. It was better that way, just in case things in Alterus got messy again.

“You two ought to consider a break! No one would fault you for a little rest and relaxation before diving into it again.”

“It’s something to consider when time allows, but for now, we hammer on, right short fry?” he said, looking down at me like I was still a baby dragon. I gave him a sassy look. I bet to Nolan, I’d always be the baby dragon who found him in the forest that night.

“Then, safe travels to you both,” Lun said, giving us a respectful bow. “May fortune favor your endeavors.”

“Likewise,” Nolan replied. “Keep your head down out there.”

“I wish I could give you something where you two could reach me if you ever need any help, but nothing I could whip up could be as potent as you’d need it to be.”

“Oh, but I thought you were the strongest mage of the Empora District!” Nolan teased. “Trained under the guidance of nobles, mastering every arcane discipline there is!”

Lun deadpanned Nolan. “Don’t test me, gunslinger!”

Nolan chuckled. “You’ll be missed, mage. Take care, Lun.”

“Thank you, Lun,” Typha said, and we all waved him goodbye.

He saluted us as he got ready to take off, magical energy swirling around the crown of his staff. “Good luck!” And just like that, Lun vanished.

I was kinda bummed out he didn’t want to be in our party, but I understood why he couldn’t stay.

“There aren’t enough words,” Typha said to Nolan and I, her eyes beginning to mist with tears. “Everything you’ve done for Zai and I…”

“No need to thank me. Like I said, it was all Freya’s idea.”

“But you helped. Give yourself some credit. I know I gave the both of you a hard time, and even after all of my bitching and complaining—”

“Yeah,” Nolan snickered. “There was definitely a lot of that.”

“Even after all of the noise I made, you both still stuck by us. You didn’t have to, but you did,” Typha finished, her voice softening with genuine gratitude.

Zaiden stepped forward and offered Nolan his hand. “What my sister is trying to say is thank you. For everything. For not giving up on us when you could have.”

Nolan shook his hand firmly and nodded slightly. “The little one here has a soft spot for strays. Can’t say the both of you didn’t help, too. We couldn’t have gotten out of that dungeon alive without you.” He glanced down at me with that half-smile that always made me feel safe. “But, I’m disbanding the party. I don’t do guilds.”

Zaiden chuckled. “Hey, no hard feelings! I already have a place to stay in Freya’s domain. Not the best view from out the window, but it’s home.”

Such a goofball.

“If you ever need a place to crash that isn’t crawling with monsters trying to eat you, our door will always be open,” Typha added, offering Nolan a handshake as well. I snuck my paw in that shake, too, getting a giggle out of Typha.

“Alright, enough of the mushy stuff,” Nolan said, though his tone was warm. “We’ve got daylight burning.” He adjusted the cloth bundle of honey cakes under his arm before giving them a final salute. “You two stay out of trouble, alright?”
