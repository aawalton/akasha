Ringtail looked like she had come prepared for a journey. Dark scaled cloak to keep out the rain, a stuffed bag slung over her shoulder, and a very noticeable absence of her shield or halberd. No trace of military steel on someone who’d never had much love for the military.

“What are you doing here?” Kalsin asked, gently pushing Jack out of the way. She let him, too focused on tying her half-shaved hair back into a bun with long, annoyed yanks.

“Deserting,” Ringtail said. She squeezed the letter into a ball and tossed it up in the air, catching it lightly. “You have a way with words. I especially liked the part about sticking it to the Republic.” She stuck her nose up, looking at Jack. “But when it comes to him, I call dibs on being first.”

Jack finished bundling her hair. She then made an irritated shooing motion at Ringtail.

“You have no idea what you’re talking about, Rin,” Kalsin said.

Ringtail gave the letter another twirl. “Don’t you wave at me, sidecuts. You may be a big gal, but I jumped around towers thrice your size since I was but a gutterling. You don’t stand a chance.”

Jack rebutted with a threatening step forward, coupled with a very clear sign—one that could be interpreted as go fornicate with yourself.

“Who else did you tell?” Kalsin asked.

“Nope. I’m not going anywhere. Not much has mattered in my life, but I can tell this shit is different. You don’t just piss away a chance like this. I will be on the right side of history—his side.”

Having enough of being ignored, Kalsin stormed up to Ringtail and swept away the soaked letter she had been tossing. He stuffed it back inside his pocket where it damn well belonged. He’d have to burn it the next chance he got.

He said, “This is not the time nor the place for your stupid games, Ringtail. Jack and I must leave, and we must leave tonight. The Clenn recruits she arrived with are going back toward the Inner Line within days.” He lowered his voice. “I healed you, okay, I cleansed you because I figured I might as well leave with a single fucking good deed in this whole military charade my life has become lately. And now that I had, I need to vanish. Jack has a plan to make us vanish.”

“Ooh, lookit. I have a talent for making things vanish.” Ringtail wagged her eyebrows at Kalsin’s groin.

The voices from behind her grew louder.

Kalsin had to hold himself from reaching out and shaking her by the shoulders like a ragdoll.

“Who else. Did—you—tell?”

For the first time tonight, Ringtail had the courtesy to look miffed. “Uh. Just Dweller.”

Seconds passed. Uncomfortable silence. Then, the people who crested the basin turned out to be not ‘just Dweller’.

“Oi, guvnor! The storm is sweeping in mighty quick over yonder horizon,” Fals said with a hand shading her eyes. “We’re about to get soaked, me thinks.”

Shoreline came next, with Crux just one step behind and hoarding a gods-forsaken chest strapped to his back. “Rain already got through my boots, Fals. I worry more about the wind.”

“You picked an inconvenient time for an exit plan, Ghost,” Fals stopped next to them, then ducked deeper underneath the pocket of the cliff. She settled in like a bear ready for hibernation. “Pony!” She reached out to scratch Sap under the chin.

Over the next wind-blown moments, a total of six more figures appeared from the dark. Pike, Littleguy and Cyanfish all brought their full military gear, with the latter leading his own mare by the leash. Tall and willowy Dweller was easy to spot even in the dark, and Quiescent was almost invisible as he stood eerily still a step away from the others. Wardoll rounded out the entourage, dabbing at her cheeks as her cosmetics turned runny from the rain. She kept shooting betrayed looks at Fals.

Kalsin tried very, very hard not to lose his temper.

Ringtail didn’t exactly try to make herself small, but she was shifting from one foot to the other as Kalsin stared her down. She lightly tugged on Dweller’s sleeve. “C’mon. Admit it’s all your fault, you gossipy fuck,” she muttered from the corner of her mouth.

Dweller shrugged at first, then folded her arms in a way to keep her coat closed when an errant gust of wind caught it open. “Right. I did tell Shoreline. Sorry. Was I supposed to just ignore the fact that my bunkmate’s scar mysteriously disappeared from her stomach overnight?”

What? Jack’s face turned to Kalsin in shock.

“What?” Kalsin asked his bodyguard right back.

“What?” Fals added for some reason.

With no hesitation whatsoever, Jack pulled Kalsin close, grabbed his tunic, and yanked it up sharply. Only once their audience got an eyeful did he regain his composure enough to pull it back down.

“Shit, Jack! I…” he started. Her frown didn’t ease.

Kalsin took a deep breath. Damn it all. “Look. You were right. Being stuck here was the worst thing I could be doing. So I chose to finally do things my way.” He reached up to scratch at her old scar now sitting on his jawline. “As you pointed it out, using this newfound talent of mine for healing was a valid option. Try and use what I have to unfuck some of this fucked country—or uh, however else the noble would phrase it. Right? So that’s what I intend to do. Take some Scarrd folk, make them Clenn folk. Simple enough,” he said, part of him defiant in the face of her scrutiny. “Only I… might’ve accelerated the timeline a bit.”

He could practically feel the disapproval ooze from his bodyguard’s posture. Yes, she had encouraged him to take action—but there was a fine line between calculated decisions and reckless flailing. This was uncharacteristic of him, and she knew it.

Jack reached out to flick a finger against his scar, then drill that same finger into his chest, right above his heart. Her face was angry still. But questioning too, now.

“Yes. No,” Kalsin said. He closed his eyes for a moment, wishing they didn’t have an audience. “I’m not okay with it. But I’ve come to terms with it.” He gave a little helpless motion. “It’s the best I can do under the circumstances. And now that things have started rolling, it has to be enough.”

Jack watched him for a moment longer, than huffed once. Her hand went back to the hilt of her sword and she resumed her position at his side, watching the half-circle of men around them. ‘This is not over.’ her posture stated. ‘But I understand part of why you did it.’

Maybe she understood because she herself felt the same way about some other things related to him. Accepting, but grudgingly so. Because that’s simply the way some things have to be.

The newest batch of soldiers of the Outer Line of Defense watched in silence.

“If that’s real,” Pike said slowly, looking around until his eyes returned back to Kalsin. He took a step toward him. “Then let’s not waste any time.” He untucked his shirt from his beltline. “It sits at the small of my back and a bit to the right. You can just reach in and take it off me, yes? Simple as that, and I’m Clenn again?”

Cyanfish lurched forward just a heartbeat after him. “I was prism, if anyone’s to be healed first, it’s me!” A note of desperation and uneasy hope colored his voice. “It’ll return, right, Ghost? My surge will be restored to overworld when you take my scar away?”

“Woah, woah!” Ringtail quickly got in their way. Jack had half-drawn her sword, sneering at the oncoming figures with a look that dared them to come in touching distance from her charge. “Let’s just wait a minute, fellas,” Ringtail said. “Nobody is touching anything tonight.”

“Easy for you to say,” Pike said. “You already got what you wanted, ‘Tail.” He looked past the shorter woman and nodded at Kalsin. “We’re on your side, mate, and I’ll gladly leave the military behind if you give us back our dignity.” He spread his hands. “Isn't that what this is all about?”

“This, was never supposed to happen,” Kalsin said with a pointed look around the gathering.

“You were gonna run, is what you’re trying to say. Before any of us got a chance to get fixed up.”

“Oh shut the hell up, you ungrateful fuck,” Ringtail cut in. She stared up at Pike with a furious look on her face. “Stop for a moment and maybe consider what it costs him to do what you’re asking for. He took up my scar, yes. But it was ugly and painful and too draining for all of you bastards to swarm him all at once.” She looked from Pike to Cyanfish, then to the rest of the company. “Ghost—Kalsin, is now set on a course to change the world. He will get to each and every one of you. In time. Once we’re safe behind some thick walls with some food in our bellies. Right now, our only priority is to safely extract the most damn important person in the Republic from the borderlands.”

Kalsin couldn’t help but shudder at the thought. It had become something of a recurring nightmare since the revelation about his power—men with revolting scars crowding him like sharks, his flesh chipped away bit by bit as they descended on him. He thought he saw a mirror of that same hunger reflected in his crewmates’ eyes as they stared at him. Or maybe it was all in his head.

Pike had retreated from the two women in his path. He reached up to scratch his jaw awkwardly. Wardoll started pacing up and down the edge of the basin, boots leaving muddy footprints in the softening ground.

A thump sounded as Crux dropped the chest he’d been lugging around, stretching his back. “I suppose that makes you something of a revolutionary, huh?”

“I suppose that makes me something of a revolutionary,” Kalsin muttered.

“Beats being pressganged into soldiering, I think,” Crux said. He looked at Shoreline with a raised brow.

She chewed on her lips, eyes drooping as if she was napping upright. “It does. Now that I’ve seen it, too.” She pointed at Kalsin’s midsection while looking at Dweller. “I would’ve bet four coral rings you were bullshitting me, sailor.”

“So who did you tell?” Cyanfish asked. The dressing covering his one eye looked cleaner than it did right after the battle. When Shoreline just looked lazily at Crux, Cyanfish laughed quietly. “Ah. Of course.”

Crux fiddled with the lock on the chest, crouched, angling his face downward to avoid the droplets of rain. “You’re all fools if you thought news as big as this would stay under wraps for longer than a couple of hours. It’s crazy talk. Ridiculous. I still don’t believe it, and I’ve just seen it with my own two fucking eyes.” The lock popped open, and he raised the lid. Metal and chainlinks jingled in the wind.

“I assume these ridiculous news didn’t die with you,” Kalsin said wryly.

“Nope. Fals was in shouting distance when Shoreline gave me the news.”

Everyone turned to look at the bard in a soldier's disguise. She glanced back with a hopeful expression, hands tucked under her armpits for warmth and hunched under her nook.

Nobody bothered to ask who she told. They’d be lucky if the rumors weren’t all over the camp by now.

“Fuck,” Kalsin said.

He was already running through the scenarios in his head. If the Civil House agents knew he was their seb hordozó, how much time would they need to ready their contingency? Perhaps until now they had only branded him a heretic—and were only just realizing the true danger he posed to the Republic.

He had healed Ringtail this morning, and left their hut sometime early afternoon. She had likely gotten up not long after. The chain reaction that is word of mouth was harder to gauge, but even an hour would have been enough for the agents to set actionable plans in motion.

The Outer Line of Defense was a robust place. There were rules to follow and systems ripe for exploiting. By now, the agents have had plenty of opportunities to find some ways to muddy the waters.

Kalsin looked at the stormy sky about to descend on them. This reunion had been cut short on too many fronts.

“Hey,” Ringtail said, siding up to him. Opposite from Jack, notably. “Hey. Kaslin, I’m… I should’ve kept this to myself, I know, but…” She bit her lips. “Are you mad at me?”

He wasn’t, Gods help him.

Well, he was, but for another reason entirely.

“That letter was private, Ringtail. Everything that was said—it was between me and Jack.”

“Right.” Kalsin didn’t have to look to know Ringtail just shot a frown over his shoulder at his bodyguard. A look that was likely mutual. “And I don’t dispute that. I don’t. But without that letter, I wouldn’t have known where to find you. And it’s not like I rummaged through your stuff to find it!” She held up her hands defensively. “It fell out of your pocket, I swear.”

“It did, eh?”

“Yeeeeah. Mmhm. It sure did!”

Ringtail was saved from further interrogation by Littleguy silently passing them toward the gear Crux was handing out from the chest. He caught the army-issue sword and scabbard and started untangling the belt around them without a word. The mess kits, pots and utensils packaged in a compact bundle, went over to Cyanfish. He had even crammed several blankets inside the thing, though likely not enough for all people present.

Which made Kalsin’s next question all the more pressing.

“Your plan,” he told Jack. “Is it still a realistic option with the increased headcount of these fools?”

Jack, still gripping her sword, had her eyes clouded for just a fraction of a second with a root’s surge. She then gave him a little shake of her head.

Kalsin took a deep breath, watching his mates jibe and curse as they tied knots, pulled on hardy boots, and complained about the steadily increasing fervor of the downpour.

“What did you have in mind?”

Jack thought for a moment. She then pulled a little box from her bag, and Kalsin had to lean close in the dark to see that it was a compass. She tapped the southern marker, then made a motion as if rowing a boat.

Kalsin chewed on the inside of his mouth. “Maybe. Yeah, maybe. If we’re to return to the Republic, Seven Fallter is the best place to do it. We could cobble something together that floats, hug the shore along Grenstel, and then…” he muttered. He was aware of Ringtail standing close and listening in, but they were well past that small concern. “But how do we reach the gulf? We can’t stay between the two lines of defense now that men will be looking for us.”

Jack shot him a look that growled ‘and whose fault is that?’.

“If you’re mad at someone, be mad at me,” Ringtail spoke up. She stuck her nose up in the air as if to compensate for the height difference. “Kalsin did something noble. The last thing he deserves is blame.”

Jack snarled down at the little woman, and Kalsin spoke before Ringtail could dig herself deeper under her skin. He knew from personal experience how good she was at that. “You planned us a route inside the Old?”

Jack slowly relaxed her posture and turned back to Kalsin. She shrugged, then nodded. She weakly gestured at the two of them, then at Hollow and Sap.

True enough. Two soldiers on horseback would have attracted little attention, and would have covered the distance quickly. And with their waterborne mounts, there would’ve been no need to make or otherwise steal a boat.

Kalsin rubbed a finger against his temple. “Mh. The more people around, the more your problems multiply.” Story of his damn life.

He watched Quiescent test a strip of dried jerky with his teeth, then spit it right out with a scowl at a grinning Fals. Much as he liked the bunch, he wished there was a way to give them the slip.

“So I know all this brooding fits the weather perfectly and all,” Ringtail said, poking Kalsin in the arm. “But why are we acting as if we’re already being hunted?” She looked from Kalsin to Jack, then back. “I mean, I caught a bit of your history from, uh, from what I’ve read. But… nobody knows about you being our savior. Right? Not yet, anyways. Couldn’t we just waltz right back into the Republic through the Inner Line?”

Jack let out a small noise of derision.

“The authorities have been aware of the rumors for many years now, about someone healing scars. They’ve got their ears to the ground.” Kalsin said. “More importantly, they also have my scar prints for identification. I can get new ones, but I can’t remove the ones I already have. There’s no chance they’d let me pass. The whole reason we’re here is because we’ve already been accused of being heretics.”

Which, ironically enough, was a whole legend that originated from a certain kid being ambushed in a dark alleyway on one tragic evening. And they say dogs chasing their own tails is supposed to be amusing.

Not from the dog’s perspective, it isn’t.

Ringtail slicked a wet strand of her short hair out of her eyes. “Huh. Heretics. Can’t say I ever heard the phrase down the rumor mills.”

Speaking of, Kalsin remembered Áron and Erazza were supposed to follow them to the Old, eventually. Poor fellas. They had just barely missed their elusive seb hordozó.

“Oh. Actually. I meant to ask,” Kalsin said, looking at Jack. “When is Cinder supposed to be joining us? ”

His sister was quick on her feet and better at hiding than the both of them combined, so Kalsin wasn’t too worried about her traveling separately during the harder stretch of the journey. But he missed her. There was no reason she couldn’t have joined them, just for tonight.

A flare of lightning lit up the emotions passing through Jack’s face at his question. Confusion, worry, dread. None of which made him feel any better.

“Look, I know you two don’t get on well,” Kalsin said, exasperated. “But I’m gonna be surrounded by Clenn for possibly the rest of my life. So I ask again, and please extend the basic courtesy of telling me; where is my sister?”

Jack’s mouth opened and closed. She shot a pleading glance at the nearest person, who happened to be a frowning Ringtail.

Worry dug its claws into Kalsin’s gut. Why wasn’t she answering him?

“Did something happen?” Kalsin said, grabbing onto Jack’s shoulders desperately. “Jack. Jackie. Please, tell me she’s not in trouble. Tell me she’s fine.”

Kalsin was aware of his rising panic starting to become the focus of the company around them. He was aware of the winds blowing louder, the rain biting harder, and the storm arriving to plunge the borderline into wet and inky darkness. He was aware of Jack’s sorrowful expression as she stared down at him, lips trembling with something she didn’t want him to know, something that she kept unsaid for his sake.

Kalsin felt his hand tighten into a fist, his inability to act, to do something turning into rage. He’d been through this nightmare once before. And he refused even the suggestion of losing his sister again.

Just as he was about to speak—the deep, heart-thrumming wail of a horn blew through the night. Another followed before the first could even finish, this one from a different lookout spot farther down the border. Then another from farther still, until the rumbling thunder rolled over the distant calls for attention.

The Outer Line of Defense had just been mobilized to full alert.
