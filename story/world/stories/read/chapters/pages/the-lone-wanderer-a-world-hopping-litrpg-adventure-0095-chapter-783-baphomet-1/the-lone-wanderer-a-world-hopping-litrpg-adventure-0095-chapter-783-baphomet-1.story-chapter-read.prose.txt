Several minutes earlier

Baphomet was surprised to see a pair of Arahnian deities flying toward his planet. It wasn’t every day he received visitors, and certainly not from a faction that knew what they would be getting themselves into. If he recalled correctly, it had been tens of thousands of years since gods from that particular world had sought him out last.

He was tempted to kill the weaker deities for a chance to harvest a couple of affinity-granting treasures for his descendants. Catharis was more than wealthy enough to afford all the common treasures they might need, but a Fifteenth Circle karmic god might produce a rare treasure upon death, and those were precious everywhere.

In the end, Baphomet held himself back. In his experience, those daring enough to approach him usually had a good reason to do so.

After receiving his thread-weaving guests – or their puppets really, since the Arahnians’ true bodies were still floating in the vacuum above the atmosphere of the planet, unwilling to encroach on the Ancient’s body – he calmly listened to their explanation.

“Do you see why we came to you?” the Sixteenth Circle Arahnian who had introduced himself as Auhn asked. “Fools openly auctioning the secret of the cyan powder off are a problem for us all. You’re the only one who can help us punish the culprit in a timely manner.”

“And what’s in it for me?” Baphomet asked back.

He was sitting cross-legged at the centre of the blood-painted pentagram, the dim candlelight in his chamber illuminating the ugly faces, beady eyes and strange mouths of his visitors. He hadn’t offered them a seat, partly because neither puppets nor deities tired from just standing, partly because the Arahnians’ bulbous lower bodies didn’t exactly allow them to sit, but mostly because Baphomet had, frankly, never been the most hospitable host in the first place.

“We’ll provide the karmic connection to our target, and you get to keep whatever knowledge or resources you can from their planet. All we ask is that you hand us the bastard’s head when you’re done – preferably in time for the next auction. The whole point of this is to deter other worlds from pulling similar stunts off in the future,” Auhn explained.

Baphomet stroked his long beard, occasionally rolling or rubbing the soft strands of pale hair between his bony fingers, pretending to seriously consider the offer while secretly feeling glad that he hadn’t done anything rash.

The truth was that he would have helped the Arahnians even for free, as he didn’t want thousands of greater springs to start popping up out of nowhere and challenging his cosmic standing. In fact, he’d already sent word to his zealots to start gathering as soon as he’d spotted the deities flying toward Catharis, fully aware that whatever they wanted from him would have inevitably involved his unique branch of mind magic.

But there was no harm in extorting a few additional benefits from the Arahnians. The weaker deities probably understood that too, so they pretended to wait patiently for his response.

Before long, his Violets, Whites and demigods started to gather inside the vast chamber. Each found their usual spot along the blood-red lines of the enormous symbol and prostrated themselves while facing him.

The pentagram was an enchantment, though not a very complex one. Baphomet had never bothered to study runecrafting deeply, yet he’d found that arranging his minions in the shape of a simple “unity” rune helped him gather and harness their cumulative power more effectively.

The mortals wore bright crimson robes that contrasted with his own black. Their fur sported various shades of grey or brown, while their black eyes reflected the candlelight like polished obsidian.

Baphomet was one of only a handful of members of his species to have ever been born pale-haired and red-eyed. His unique colours sadly hadn’t come with any hidden abilities or cultural significance – the others like him had all lived short and uninteresting lives. However, his compatriots had always found his gaze somewhat intimidating, even in his youth.

That didn’t apply to his zealots, of course.

The order of powerful mind users that he had painstakingly cultivated held nothing but unwavering loyalty and absolute devotion towards him. They pressed their faces against the dark, bloodstained marble, causing the stumps of the curved horns that the males had voluntarily broken off in a display of submission to scrape against the floor as they waited for the ritual to commence.

The karmic deity – the one who hadn’t spoken much – tossed a fist-sized sphere at Baphomet. He grabbed it with a swift motion, scoffing upon realizing that its lively yellow and orange colours didn’t match the style of his living quarters in the slightest.

“The colours and direction of the needle keep shifting,” the younger deity said. “They’re too unreliable to point us at our target, but I’ve made sure that they’re linked to the right thread at least.”

Baphomet nodded. “Kauhn, was it? Is it safe to assume that we’re dealing with a rather powerful deity?”

“Most likely, yes,” Auhn answered instead of his subordinate, seemingly unwilling to let the younger god talk more than necessary. “We don’t know how else to explain the difficulty in tracking them. That’s why we came to you – we were hoping that you can redirect your spell to the nearest mortal and gather enough information to lead us to their planet.”

“Very well,” Baphomet said, dismissively gesturing at his guests to move their puppets to some corner of the chamber, out of his sight.

He unfolded his internal world, allowing vast quantities of mind mana to spill into the chamber as he aligned a projection of an identical symbol over the one in the real world.

Holding the karmic compass with both hands, he carefully channelled his power through the ethereal thread it represented, linking himself to the poor fool on the other side.

To his surprise, it wasn’t a deity like his guests had speculated. Even for a mortal, his target was shockingly weak.

‘A mere Green? Are the Arahnians messing with me?’ he wondered, glaring at the puppets.

Their expressions remained tense but otherwise clueless. Either they hadn’t known, or they were excellent actors.

Whichever it was, it didn’t really change much. They couldn’t harm Baphomet anyway, and the fact that his target was a weak mortal would only make him easier to control.

Peering through the Green’s eyes, Baphomet found himself standing atop some kind of giant mushroom, beneath a clear blue sky and surrounded by colourful motes. The mana density wasn’t bad, but most greater springs had something better.

‘Bipedal, four-limbed, mostly hairless and pink-skinned…’ he summarized.

He’d never met that species before, lending credence to the Arahnians’ hypothesis that this was some newly-risen greater spring. Baphomet would have to fully seize control of the mortal’s faculties to learn more about him.

His zealots moved without being told, each condensing their mana into a thin red needle and shooting it directly into the compass. Taking deep breaths one after the other, they kept sending whatever mana they regenerated into their constructs, forming a faint thread behind each needle.

Hushed whispers filled the room as Baphomet’s minions sang their all-too-familiar hymn, causing the bloody pentagram beneath everyone’s hooves to glow in an ominous red light, and the sweet scent of iron in the air – Baphomet’s favourite – to thicken.

He directed the needles into the mortal’s mind, seizing his body before the latter even registered what was happening.

That was when Baphomet noticed something that gave him pause.

‘Two cores?! How is this possible? And is that a Ring of Sacrilege on his finger?!’

Unless the Void Hand had recently invited new member-factions to their alliance without anybody hearing about it, Baphomet had no idea how to explain this. Had some unknown world lucked out with a bloodline or mutation that replicated the effect of one of the most powerful Decrees in existence?

Another thing that stood out was that the mortal’s mind was rather odd. It was closely connected to several others, almost as if Baphomet was dealing with some kind of hive mind rather than an individual. He’d tested his spell on eusocial beasts before, of course, but this was his first time stumbling upon a sapient with a mind structured like this.

‘No wonder the Arahnians’ failed to pin him down… There was never a single person, nor location.’

This… was a problem for Baphomet too.

His spell normally allowed him to take complete control over a victim’s body, allowing him to use them however he pleased without anybody realizing. However, the mortal’s allies would undoubtedly detect the intrusion within seconds in this case.

One solution would be to try possessing the others too. Their minds were already linked, so it should technically be possible. Unfortunately, hijacking the mind of even a single mortal was already a challenge. Every person was essentially the ultimate authority within their own head, so it generally took a lot of effort to overrule them.

Despite his power and experience, Baphomet could only enforce his will by borrowing the help of over a hundred other minds. Possessing two mortals at the same time might be doable if he strained himself, but there were several more connected to his target.

‘If I can’t control them, I’ll need to find and eliminate them all before anybody realizes what’s going on. The people who share a mind with him are the only ones capable of getting in my way,’ he decided.

Luckily, the strongest connection belonged to a person standing right next to Baphomet’s target. They had two cores as well, and Baphomet couldn’t recall ever meeting this particular species of four-armed, beaked people either.

Gathering his target’s mana, Baphomet fired a projectile right at the second mortal’s sternum. He had no idea what either species was capable of, but mana cores were universal weak spots for pretty much every race in the universe.

Somehow, the four-armed Blue managed to sidestep the point-blank attack, almost like he had predicted the future. The spell still landed, though it sank about halfway into the man’s left breast rather than his core, before exploding into grey motes.

Baphomet took solace from the fact that he had at least wounded his opponent deeply, though he was shocked to see the narrow slit in the creature’s chest mend itself rapidly.

‘How the hell is he doing that?!’

Baphomet was pretty sure that the projectile he had just fired had consisted of some variant of the soul affinity. He’d never seen a mortal regenerate a spectral injury so quickly.

“Percy! What the fuck man?!” the Blue yelled.

The four-armed man’s body practically melted into grey sludge, hugging the Green and pressing him against the giant mushroom.

Baphomet’s surprises didn’t end there.

It wasn’t that strange that the two-cored Blue possessed a domain, but the ocean of willpower wrapping around the possessed Green felt way too strong – almost as tight as a demigod’s!

If that wasn’t difficult enough to believe, Baphomet felt a part of the willpower being drained directly from his captive’s body. The two mortals could apparently share their willpower freely – an ability that Baphomet had never heard of. The Blue was clearly borrowing the entirety of their domain to restrain his friend.

‘Just what is this place? And what in the seven hells is a Percy?!’
