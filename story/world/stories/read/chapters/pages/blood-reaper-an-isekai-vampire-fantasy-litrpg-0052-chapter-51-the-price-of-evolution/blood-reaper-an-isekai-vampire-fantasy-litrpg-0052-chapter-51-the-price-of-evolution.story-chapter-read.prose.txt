Seven heroes had been summoned to this world, and now only four were left.

At any moment, the others might run—and if that was what they wanted, Ace would let them go.

With his back still to the others, he knelt and gathered the stones for Rachel’s grave with mechanical efficiency. He didn’t think. He didn’t let his mind wander. Instead, he simply picked up rock after rock and tested each one's weight before adding it to the collection in the crook of his arm.

The stones’ sharp edges bit into his skin, drawing pinpricks of blood that healed almost instantly thanks to his enhanced healing. Some rocks weren’t good enough, and he tossed them aside—too light, too brittle, too unworthy of Rachel's memorial. The pile grew against his chest until he couldn’t hold any more. He could have made multiple trips with smaller loads, but something in him needed the weight.

The guilt.

To his surprise, no one left. No one ran. No one bolted toward the treeline as though their very lives depended on getting as far away from him as possible.

When he finally returned to the group, none of them had moved. The stones shifted against each other as he walked, creating a soft grinding chorus that reminded him of Rachel’s mumbling.

Once more, he knelt.

And once more, Tara helped him lay the rocks.

As the pile grew, his mind wandered. He had questions only they could answer.

“I need to know if you all can handle being around me after what I did to him,” he said flatly. “If you can't, now's the time to say it. Before someone else dies because we're not trusting each other.”

Marcus dropped his gaze. “It's not—” he started, then stopped. “I know why you did it. Doesn't make it easier to watch someone you know get killed.”

“Nothing about this place is easy,” Ace replied as he reached for another stone. “In Afghanistan, I lost three friends in an ambush. Good Marines. Good people.”

Tara and Marcus stared at him in stunned silence.

“I was beside myself,” he continued. “This was early in my service, and I couldn’t cope. It was the first time I’d seen death, and my commanding officer told me something I never forgot. 'You honor the dead by protecting the living.'“ Ace positioned another stone carefully. “That's what I've been trying to do here. Not always succeeding. But trying.”

Olivia took a step closer, as if drawn in by the talk of death. Her eyes followed Ace's movements with new appreciation—though for what, he couldn’t tell.

“Rachel was always so worried about her clothes getting dirty,” Tara said with a sad smile, echoing her earlier comment. “Remember how she used to perch on rocks instead of sitting on the ground during our rests?”

“And the way she would sigh when she was about to spew nonsense we could barely understand,” Marcus added, a ghost of a smile touching his lips. “Which was basically all the time.”

As they continued building the cairn, memories of Rachel mingled with the stones. Each recollection was another piece of the memorial, preserving more than just her remains—but also who she had been.

Olivia finally stepped forward, reaching down to select a smooth, flat stone. She placed it carefully on top of the growing pile.

“She called me a 'bloodthirsty ballerina' once,” Olivia said, her voice unusually soft.

Ace glanced up at their Blood Dancer. This was the most he’d heard her speak since they got here.

“I guess, in a way, she was right,” Olivia continued as she selected another stone. “I kind of admired her honesty.”

Silently, with her feet barely making a sound, Olivia crossed the distance between them. She knelt on Ace’s other side, until he was sandwiched between her and Tara, and their bloodthirsty ballerina reached for a stone. She joined them in placing the rocks across Rachel’s grave, filling in the gaps he and Tara had missed. Each of her movements had a certain grace to them—nothing wasted, each gesture flowing into the next. The three of them formed a triangle around Rachel's remains, working in a silence that felt almost like rhythm.

“Was your unit structured like we are?” Olivia asked suddenly, her eyes on the stones rather than Ace. “Clear hierarchy, defined roles, tactical unity.”

The question surprised him. Most people asked about how many people he had killed, not organization.

“Similar.” He adjusted a rock that was about to roll out of place. “More formal. Chain of command was everything. When you know exactly who's responsible for what, you build trust. Once you have trust, everything else comes together.”

She nodded, and her dark eyes met his. “Do you trust us?”

A good question.

Ace paused and furrowed his brow as he lost himself briefly in thought. He wanted to tell them the truth.

“Yes,” he answered. “Combat does something to you. Creates bonds stronger than family.”

Tara looked up at that. Dirt smudged her cheek where she'd absently brushed hair from her face. “Because of the shared trauma?”

“Kind of,” Ace confessed as he reached for another stone. “It's knowing someone will die for you, and you for them.” He paused and tried to stuff down a memory, but it surfaced anyway. “My squad knew me better than my own family did.”

“What happened to them?” Olivia asked, her voice uncharacteristically gentle.

He swallowed hard, but this time, he didn’t answer.

“You lost them,” Olivia said quietly.

She leaned in closer, those dark eyes of her widening slightly as her gaze roamed his face. Her normally impassive face showed something new—understanding, perhaps, or recognition. Her shoulder brushed against his, her attention completely focused on him rather than the memorial.

“And now we’ve lost Rachel, too,” she added softly.

“Yeah,” he said. “We’ve lost another one.”

Tara set down her stone and sat back on her heels. “I took an oath as a paramedic. The Paramedic’s Promise. To preserve life. To act with ethics. To do no harm. Now, I have to kill to survive. Drain blood to heal. Sometimes, I dream about the patients I lost, and I wake up wondering if there's anything left of the person I was.”

Her confession broke something open between them—a dam of unspoken fears, let loose by the brutality of this new life.

“I'm terrified I'll forget,” Marcus said from where he still knelt several yards away. His voice cracked as he spoke, and he cleared his throat. “I’m afraid I’ll forget what it was like to be human. What it felt like to just have fun. To be stupid on stream and make people laugh.”

Everyone shifted their attention toward him. He tried to say something else, but his mouth opened and shut as he fought back more tears. His eyes squeezed shut, and he took a deep breath to steady himself.

The silence weighed on the air as they waited for him to continue.

Marcus dug his fingers into the dirt, and he shook his head in numb shock. “My channel had over a million followers. The people who watched me, knew me. I had a thriving community of people who loved fucking around together and joking in the chat. Now, I'm becoming something they would run from.”

In the silence that followed, the rocks continued to pile on Rachel’s grave.

Olivia ran her fingertips over the stone she held, tracing its contours with her slender finger. “I was a museum curator. I preserved beautiful things. Protected them from decay.” A faint smile touched her lips. “Now I find beauty in decay itself. In death. In blood.” Her eyes met Ace's. “I never saw it before. The elegance in endings. The art in death.”

The confession sent a chill through Ace that had nothing to do with the gusts of wind tearing past them. There was something disturbing in her honesty—and yet he respected it.

“Ace,” Marcus said in a terrified whisper. “What are we? I mean, what are we really? What’s the point of going on?”

For a moment, Ace didn’t respond. He continued to place the rocks he had gathered, and he wondered if it was a question he could even answer.

Maybe there wasn’t one.

Maybe this place would erase them entirely.

“Look,” he eventually said. “I’m not going to lie to you. The fact is, we’re killers now. That’s who we have to be if we want to keep going.”

“How?” Marcus asked, his voice breaking yet again.

Ace shrugged. “Marines compartmentalize, if that helps.”

“What do you mean?” Tara prodded.

“The mission comes first. Feelings come later.” He arranged another stone as he fought to find the right words. “I separate the vampire—the hunger, the power—from the man. Lock the monster in a box until I need it.”

He was met with silence.

Ace glanced up to find them all watching him intently, and he figured he needed to be honest here.

“Sometimes the box leaks,” he confessed.

This time, something shifted in the air between them. The tension that had wound so tight since Rachel's death—since Victor's—loosened a fraction.

Just a bit, but enough to lighten the mood.

“That's why I’m still here,” Tara said quietly. “You make the hard calls, but you don't forget the cost.”

Ace felt a weight settle on his shoulders—different from the burden of failure that had been crushing him since Rachel's death. Something heavier. Something he had carried with him into battle plenty of times before.

“Does any of it even matter?” Marcus asked suddenly, his voice cutting through the quiet. “All this talk of humanity, of who we were. The System doesn't care. This world doesn't care. We're just her playthings.”

“Marcus,” Tara said gently. “We’re not—”

“We are,” he interrupted. The man stood, his body tense and shaking. “Rachel analyzed everything, planned for every contingency, and look where it got her!” He kicked a small stone, sending it skittering across the ground. “What’s the point of staying alive, here? It’s what the System wants. Why give her the satisfaction of watching us suffer?”

The challenge hung in the air. Ace felt something stir inside him—not just anger or determination, but something deeper. The shadows at the edges of the crater darkened, pulsing slightly in rhythm with his heartbeat. He hadn't called them consciously, but his connection to the Shadow Realm responded to his presence.

To his power.

“It matters more than ever,” Ace said, his voice low. The shadows stretched towards him, curling around the edges of the stones like ghostly fingers. “In a world this brutal, surviving is rebellion. It’s rubbing her own magic in her face.”

Marcus scoffed.

“I’m serious.” Ace stood, and the shadows darkened the ground around his feet. “The System wants us to become monsters. To turn on each other. To forget. Every memory we keep, every line we don't cross—that's a victory she can't take from us.”

Everyone went silent, and all eyes drifted upward toward him. No one moved, save for the steady rise and fall of their chests with each shallow breath.

“Rachel deserved better than what she got,” Ace continued. “But she's not just a bloodstain on the ground. We honor the dead by remembering them and by protecting the living. By remembering who we are.”

Only one stone was left in the crook of his arm, and he placed it on the pile with the others. The shadows at his feet faded completely, leaving only the cairn and the four of them in the Highlands’ eternal twilight.

But something had changed between them—a new understanding, a different kind of bond that formed from the broken shards of what they’d been.

The weight of Ace's words hung in the air, filling the space between them. For several heartbeats, no one moved.

With a heavy sigh, Marcus stood. He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand, as if bracing himself for what had to be done. He bent down, selected a smooth stone from the ground at his feet, and placed it deliberately on the growing pile.

“At least together we have a chance,” the man said.

The admission cost him. Ace could see it in the tight line of his jaw, the flush creeping up his neck. Pride surrendering to pragmatism. Fear yielding to necessity.

After that, no one spoke for quite a while.

The four of them worked in silence, each finding their place in the rhythm of laying rocks on Rachel’s memorial. Tara carefully arranged smaller stones to fill gaps. Olivia selected pieces with an artist's eye for balance and form. Marcus focused on the outline of the mound, ensuring stability. Ace handled the larger rocks, his enhanced strength serving a gentler purpose than combat for once.

As the cairn grew, it transformed from random pile to deliberate monument. The last visible fragments of Rachel disappeared beneath their careful arrangement. Each piece of her they covered felt like sealing away a memory, both preserving it and letting it go.

When only one stone remained—a flat, smooth piece Ace had set aside specifically for this purpose—he held it out between them.

“Together,” he said simply.

Four hands reached for the stone—Tara's small, steady fingers; Marcus's trembling grip; Olivia's graceful touch; Ace's calloused palm. For a moment, they all connected through that final piece of Rachel's memorial, skin against skin, a physical manifestation of what now bound them.

Not blind trust as before, but something more deliberate.

Something forged, rather than forced.

Ace became acutely aware of Tara's gentle presence beside him, the warmth of her fingers brushing his, how her breath caught slightly at the contact. On his other side, Olivia's intense gaze burned into him, her fingertips lingering against his knuckles for a breath longer than necessary.

Together, they placed the last stone on Rachel's memorial.

Ace stepped back first, surveying not just the cairn but the devastation surrounding it. The Thornhide Behemoth's death had carved a wound across the hillside. Massive trees lay splintered like matchsticks. Blood stained the soil in expanding rings of crimson.

In the distance, barely visible now, the faint dust outline where Victor's body had dissolved into the air served as a stark reminder of their double loss. Ace's jaw tightened.

Seven of them had started this journey together.

Now, only four remained.

“The blood will draw monsters,” he announced, his voice cutting through the solemn silence. “We need rest. Let’s find a new cave for the night. Somewhere else.”

With deliberate effort, he turned his back on the carnage, ready for whatever came next in this endless nightmare.

“What do we do after that?” Marcus asked.

“We hunt,” Ace answered. “We’ll start with the slugs, just like Rachel suggested. We become the killers, and we focus our attention on getting stronger. We survive.”

His gaze met each of theirs in turn—Tara's compassionate expression, still red-rimmed from tears she'd tried to hide; Olivia's intense stare, which catalogued his every movement with unsettling focus; Marcus's tear-streaked face, the remnants of fear now mingling with something that looked almost like resolve.

In those three faces, Ace saw not just grief, but the first flickers of determination rekindling. It wasn't hope, since this world had beaten that out of them already. But there was a sense of purpose, and that was enough.

For now, anyway—because the System always had other plans.
