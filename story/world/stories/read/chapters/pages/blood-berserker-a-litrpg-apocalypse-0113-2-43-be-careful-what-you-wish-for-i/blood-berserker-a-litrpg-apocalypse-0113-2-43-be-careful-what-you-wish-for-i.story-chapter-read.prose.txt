Two hours had passed since the maze had popped out from nowhere and blocked his view of the berserker, and Tristan couldn’t help but grit his teeth in frustration as he loosed an arrow.

Of course, the ranger had already known that fighting demons was going to take a lot out of him, but he hadn’t expected to have to take on an endless wave of monsters.

Still, he continued to move through the path that the death maze offered with his senses on high alert, even as he loosed another arrow, ignoring the growing ache in his palms from the repetitive action. His eyes locked onto his next target just as the arrow he fired pierced through the chest of his previous target, killing the imp instantly.

This is just a waste of my time. Tristan mused.

So far, the wave had lacked variety—mostly just low-level monsters that did nothing but frustrate the ranger—And the mana draw for having to refill the arrows in his quiver seemed negligible, but when done in the amount that he was doing it, it was inevitable that his body was going to feel the strain it was currently feeling.

Even worse was the fact that he’d only leveled up once despite the number of demons that he’d killed.

Tristan grunted at the thought before pushing it away. There was no point complaining about the level of the monsters, as there was nothing he could do that was going to improve the quality of monsters in the wave he was currently dealing with. But what he could do was to use as little movement as possible to take care of the fodder wave, so that he could keep his energy level up.

With a clear mind, he moved towards the corner of the path, ignoring the wet gurgling sound coming from the dead imp behind him.

A brief flash of annoyance flashed through his eyes at the constant noise that the imp was making. So he turned back and fired an arrow straight between its eyes, ending its struggles, and returning to his chosen corner of the path—as he continued to wait in ambush for the next unlucky monster to cross his path.

That had been his tactic—lie in wait and pray that a monster was dumb enough to get into his line of sight.

So far, it had worked, but Tristan had started to become annoyed with the quality of demons that entered his path. It seems I’ll have to go deeper in the maze if I aim to get any demon worth my time and arrows. Hopefully, it’ll grant me more levels as well.

With careful steps, he walked around the corner, bow held in a firm grip with an arrow already notched, ready to fly on a whim.

Tristan wasn’t afraid of anything—In fact, it was quite the opposite with the ranger. It seemed that he was more attracted to the possibility of death than a boring life, a thought supported by his choice to hunt whatever made his blood rush—and at that moment, that was the demon prince.

He’d finally found something worth fighting in this cursed apocalypse, and he’d be damned if he let the berserker beat him to killing the demon.

Then again, I suppose the demon prince is far above me in level if the sight of it almost pulling apart the arms of that monster of a berserker’s is anything to go by.

It was an honest assessment by the ranger, but it didn’t do much to shift his conviction.

From the very moment he understood the apocalypse in the tutorial tower, he made peace with the fact that he wasn’t going to live long by hiding. Neither was he going to live long by being in a faction and having to take orders from anyone.

He wasn’t a social person—evidenced by the way he’d split ways with his initial squad a few days after the tutorial had ended.

Tristan already had it at the back of his mind that he was going to have to go through the apocalypse by himself, and if that was what it was going to take to survive, then he had no qualms with it, and to be honest, he wasn’t sure he would’ve preferred it any other way.

Which was why he ignored the voice at the back of his head, warning him that the system preferred for humans to split up and make themselves easier to kill. And as interesting as that theory sounded at the back of his head, Tristan still believed in his decision to fly solo.

That was why he was in a rush to level up as fast as possible, and the reliable way he could do that was by finding and defeating the biggest monsters available.

At the moment, the demon prince Kabash fit the bill to a tee, and he was so excited by the opportunity to kill the demon that he could almost forget that fighting the demon was the closest he could come to committing suicide without using his own hands.

Morbid thought aside, this was a way for him to do some good. And as much as he didn’t like to admit it, his best–and probably his only–shot at avenging those sacrificed to the demon prince lay with the berserker, and he could only hope that they wouldn't die while trying to avenge the dead.

The ranger quickly dispelled his errant thoughts as the first of the monsters came into the path he lay in wait in.

The master imps were almost the same level as him, and while that might have troubled another person, Tristan didn’t even flinch. As far as he was concerned, he had everything needed to kill them. Yet he couldn't afford to take any chances, so he locked in on the closest of the five master imps charging their way at him, and then he let his arrow fly.

Fuck me!

The ranger resisted the urge to wince as his arrow was swatted out of the air by the master imp he’d targeted.

[Quickfire III]

Muttering a curse, Tristan activated his skill and released three arrows at once, his hands moving in a blur as his eyes picked the target.

Two out of his three arrows manage to make it past the swatting of the charging master imps before hitting one in the throat, and another in its kneecap.

A wet gurgling sound from the imp with an arrow in its throat was accompanied by a horrifying shriek that ripped free from the throat of the master imp with an arrow in its kneecap as it fell face down, unable to keep its footing.

The master imp with an arrow in its kneecap had also managed to trip one of its kin on its way to the floor, which brought a flash of relief to the ranger’s eyes as he now only had to focus on going toe to toe with the two other master imps still charging at him.

Tristan remained calm even as the first of the master imps crossed beyond the point that would make it awkward for the ranger to fire at it.

The odor wafting from its unwashed body mildly irritated the ranger's eyes and nose as it hit him in the face. But the ranger ignored it as he was more concerned about the charging demons rapidly closing the distance between him and them.

[Quickfire III] can only be used four times a day, and I still have to keep its ten-second cooldown in mind. Tristan mused.

[Quickfire III] needing to cool down before he could activate the skill again was a bit inconvenient. But the good part was that he’d killed one master imp and sidelined two temporarily, which meant that he only had to focus his attention on the two master imps invading his personal space. Luckily for Tristan, he’d never been one to depend on special skills to fight.

If anything, the o’l reliable draw and fire seemed to be the only thing he had to use in most cases, and this seemed to be one of those cases. So in one smooth motion, Tristan drew and fired an arrow straight at the master imp closest to him, knowing fully well that he’d have to deal with the monster imp following closely behind that one.

The fired arrow found its mark. Its metal tip went through the master imp's left eye and down to its brain, with an audible squelch, but the ranger barely acknowledged the strike and the wave of euphoria that passed through him—his focus already on the second master imp charging at him.

The master imp trampled upon the body of its dead kin in its rush. And as close as they were, Tristan knew that he was neither going to be able to notch and fire an arrow at the master imp, nor could he turn and flee, as he would also be leaving his back exposed to the master imps.

Ah, fuck it! Clamping down on the human instincts that warned him to flee, he pulled his bow close to his chest and dived through the legs of the charging demon, taking advantage of its momentum to avoid the demon clattering into him.

His face scrunched up in disgust as the monster imp's privates flashed in his eyes, before he hit the floor hard on the other side.

Doing his best to forget the disturbing image, he quickly pushed up from the ground. The ranger tightly clamped down on the urge to empty the contents of his stomach on the floor as he pulled an arrow from his quiver, quickly notching it and letting loose as soon as he could. Unfortunately for the ranger, the demon turned to face him, righting its course and making the arrow meant for the nape of its neck end up in its left shoulder.

Tristan let the disappointment of his missed shot roll down his shoulders as he prepared another arrow to fire at the pissed-off master imp heading in his direction.

He hadn’t even notched on the next arrow when his gut screamed at him to move out of the way.

The ranger didn’t hesitate to move to the side as soon as he registered feeling, and he did so right in time, as the master imp that had been tripped earlier swiped at where he’d stood a heartbeat ago.

For the second time within a couple of breaths, he was trapped between two master imps charging at him, and Tristan did not like the thought of having his skin shredded by either of their claws.

Tristan literally didn't have a second to spare, but he’d have to buy himself that time if he had any hope of taking down the three imps that remained. But before that, he was going to pick between one of the two master imps charging at him to kill, and at the same time, he also had to avoid getting fatally wounded by the other. And that was what the ranger did in that millisecond.

With as much quickness as he could muster, he pulled out his bow and fired an arrow towards the master imp with an arrow embedded in its left shoulder.

Tristan's arrow flew true as it hit the master imp in its throat with an audible thunk!, but the ranger wasn’t watching as he’d turned his attention to the other master imp charging at him.

This time, the ranger didn’t have a clever ploy in mind to escape the imp charging at him, so he settled for the next best thing. He dived in the other direction.

White-hot pain blossomed in his side as the master imp's claws caught him in his side mid dive, the sharp appendages tearing through his clothes and into his skin with frightening ease as it threw him off course, and into the maze wall.

The ranger muttered a curse under his breath before pushing up against the maze wall, his bow going up as he aimed at the incoming master imp, a devilish smile on his face. “Say hello to the devil for me. [Quick fire III].”

A heavy thud echoed in the path as the master imp dropped to the floor, an arrow protruding from each eye as it lay dead on the floor.

The other master imp, dragging itself on the dirt floor towards the ranger with an arrow in its kneecap, wasn’t spared as it received an arrow to its throat, killing it instantly.
