Derrida sat in her library, in her grand mansion on the planet she’d chosen to live on for the last few million years. When she’d stepped inside Xavier Collins’s library, she couldn’t have helped but see the similarities between his space and her own, though her library was so large she needed to manipulate the space inside it to fit more walls and shelves. Her library stretched on for many thousands of miles—it was more akin to a country than a room.

She’d been collecting for a lot longer, after all.

The ancient Wanderer was not currently reading a book—she’d read everything on these shelves several thousand times over, and those not worth a reread had been discarded—instead, she was looking at a holographic display showing Xavier’s progress through the dungeon.

Sitting beside her was a Wanderer she’d given a true death to not so long ago. Well, it wasn’t the true Wanderer, it was the mental construct that had been inside Xavier’s head. She hadn’t just blocked the use of it, she’d removed it. She still wondered if she should place it back in his head or not. Though Xavier’s progress had been even beyond her imaginings, she wasn’t sure the construct was providing the benefits she’d wished it would.

She glanced over at Roln, who, like her, had been intently watching the holographic display. It wasn’t just the holographic display they had access to, either—they were reading Xavier’s mind. To her, it was laid bare, the words of his thoughts clear, just like one of her books, and she provided that same access to the construct.

“Why did you lie to him?” Roln asked. “I still don’t understand.”

Derrida cocked her head to one side, peering into the man’s eyes. Though he wasn’t really there, of course. And he wasn’t even a spirit. He lacked a soul. Lacked a body. He was nothing more than a program, running after death. Yet there did seem to be more personality to him than that, she tried to remember he wasn’t real.

If she were one burdened with such regrets, she might feel sorry for giving the man a true death. What he’d done, interfering with Xavier’s life, was far less than what she had done since. Not only that, without his intervention, Xavier might never have come this far.

But she was not burdened by regrets. At least, not ones such as that. When you lived as long as her you found there were things you must let go of, else they will never stop living inside you.

She raised an eyebrow, considering Roln’s question. “It almost sounds as though you care for him.”

Roln blinked, looked at her. “I’ve been living inside his head for many years now. Of course I care about him.”

The conviction in his voice surprised her. Not what she’d expected from such a man.

“Lies are useful, you know that more than most,” Derrida replied.

Roln grunted. “Still, it’s dangerous. To send him into the Void Dungeon… there is no way he can survive that place. Not at his level of power. And yet you told him you’d come for him.” He stared at her. “You said you would come, but you never intend to.”

Derrida smiled thinly. “He must face this alone. He must push himself to his limits. Again and again and again. Until either he dies of the Interdimensional Dragon dies. Only then can he truly rest. I will lie to him if I need to. I will kill. I will do anything in my power to ensure he remains on this path. I will not allow him to falter.”

Roln locked eyes with her. “If he does not die, there will come a time when he is more powerful than even you, and you know that. How do you think he will feel when he unravels the lies?”

“He will be grateful,” Derrida said, but in her own thoughts—thoughts that unlike Xavier, she was able to easily block the mental construct from reading—she wasn’t quite as sure.

She still didn’t know how this man would truly turn out. Didn’t know what type of person he would be when he possessed ultimate power. There would come a time when he would be able to read her mind as easily as she read his now. Even with her ability to see inside his mind she still didn’t know what would happen.

And neither does Xavier. He fights for good. He wants a multiverse where everyone has the right and the means to live the type of life they wish, as long as that life does not harm others, and yet deep down he doubts himself. Doubts that, in the end, he will stop reaching for more power.

Perhaps that was why she’d sent him to the Void Dungeon, rather than the Multiversal Academy. She’d intended to bring him to the academy, hadn’t she? Intended to put him forth as a new student in those hallowed halls—halls that had stood the test of time, halls that even she, oh so many years ago, had once walked through.

Long before she’d even been alive, the Multiversal Academy had stood. A place where Wanderers and those yet to become them could train and learn and fight. The Multiversal Academy was a safe haven, free from the politics and wars that were fought between the different Wanderer factions. Though that wasn’t to say it was safe.

Still, she was certain Xavier would have thrived in such a place. There was much for him to learn, and the teachers there were some of the most gifted in the universe. The headmaster was a Wanderer that had been old when she’d first stepped into the multiverse herself. One of the few Wanderers to have survived the war with the Interdimensional Dragon that had wiped out most of her generation.

And several generations before us.

Though of course there were rumours that there were more survivors out there, some who might even be older than the Headmaster of the Multiversal Academy. The multiverse was big place, so big that it was easy to get lost in, easy to hide in.

Yet she hadn’t sent Xavier Collins to the Multiversal Academy. She’d sent him to the Void Dungeon. And she’d lied to him, saying she would get him when he’d passed enough floors, yet that wasn’t something she intended to do.

She wanted him to finish it. A feat that shouldn’t have been possible for one such as him.

She’d told the man that he it wasn’t possible for him to complete the dungeon at his level of power—and that was true enough. There was no way for him to complete it. No way that she could think of, at least. Yet this man had defied even her ideas of what was possible many times in the past, and she needed him to keep doing that. To keep honing that edge he somehow had.

Going through the dungeon, reaching the end… that would do that for him.

If he completed the dungeon, well, sending him to the Multiversal Academy afterward was a no brainer…

Maybe there’s also a part of me that sent him to the Void Dungeon to die, because I don’t know what he’ll become, because I don’t know if one day he might be worse than the Interdimensional Dragon.

And if he dies, well, I’ll never have to find out.

Derrida turned her attention back to the holographic display, where Xavier Collins was showing skills he’d never possessed before entering this place. He’d barely started in the dungeon, and already he’d progressed faster than she’d assumed.

Whether he lived or died, she knew watching his progress through the dungeon would be far more interesting than rereading one of her countless books.

Xavier could sense them.

The Void Dungeon-created beasts were no longer able to hide from him. He couldn’t just sense their outline, like that first Ragatni he’d faced back near the start of the dungeon, he could feel far more of them than that. He could push his senses down the Dark Tunnel and identify everything that stood there.

The Personal System that Derrida, the ancient Wanderer who’d sent him here, had given him had now gained that same ability.

There are five Class C7 Ragatni around the bend. The cavern is larger than the Dark Tunnel. They will fight you in a pack. They are smarter than you think.

I don’t fear them, Xavier replied, and it was the truth.

He’d faced another dozen Ragatni since that first one, and his skills had only been increasing with each one he defeated. Even the prospect of facing five of these at once—quite a jump in difficulty, he might add—didn’t bother him, but he’d just descended down at set of stairs, and while this wasn’t the second actual floor of the dungeon, he knew whatever he faced down here would be harder to defeat than what he’d faced in the Dark Tunnel.

You should still exercise a heathy degree of caution.

Xavier smiled.

He extended his wings, a hand on the side of the wall. The All-Seeing Eye was still useless at detecting these things, so he couldn’t see them, only sense them with his spiritual sense, hear them and, worst of all, smell them. He’d known there were five of these things without the Personal System having to tell him, and he’d been able to discern that they were at the same level of power as the ones he’d already faced. He could even tell the exact distance they were from where he stood.

He was going to be cautious. There were things at work in this place that he didn’t understand, and these beasts—even though he was sure he could defeat them—were to be respected.

But that didn’t mean he couldn’t have a little fun as he fought them…

He leapt forward, his wings turning him in the air as he came around the corner. Another thing he could feel, with both his hearing and his spiritual sense, was just how large that cavern around that corner was.

It felt like a while since he’d actually been able to fly. The Dark Tunnel’s ceiling had been oppressively short. The cavern’s ceiling was a good ten times higher than the cavern had been.

It was good to feel the wind through his wings again.

It was barely a moment before he was flying over the Ragatni, looking down at them from on high. These Ragatni weren’t any larger than the ones he’d faced in the Dark Tunnel, and from his vantage point, out in the open, they looked smaller. He knew that was simply a lie of perspective, however, and he wasn’t about to assume these things were any weaker than the others he’d faced.

The fact that there were five of these things to face all at once did make him wary. Not exactly for this fight, but for the fights that he might have as he descended farther down into the Void Dungeon. If the Void Dungeon was willing to make a fight literally five times as hard as the last on the same floor, then the jump in difficulty between the actual floors was likely much larger than he’d imagined.

He supposed that only made sense, given he was told he wasn’t powerful enough to complete the Void Dungeon. It made him wonder, just when did Derrida intend to come get him? When he’d completed the first floor? The third? The fifth?

He was still inclined to go farther than she expected. To push back when she came to get him. He needed to become stronger, and he’d already honed his abilities more than he’d expected here.

The Ragatnis instantly clocked his appearance. Every single one of them began to glow a fiery red. Not only that, he could sense their use of Roots of the Grave—hands were about to emerge not from the ground beneath him, but from the walls and the ceiling above. This piqued his interest, as it was something he hadn’t seen from the other Ragatni he’d faced.

But Xavier wasn’t about to let any one of his enemies lay a finger on him—especially not an undead one.
