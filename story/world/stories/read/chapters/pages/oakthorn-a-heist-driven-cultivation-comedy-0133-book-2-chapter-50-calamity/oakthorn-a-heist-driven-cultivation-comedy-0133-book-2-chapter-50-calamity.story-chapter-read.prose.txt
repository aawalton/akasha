Gods could bleed. Destruction knew that better than most.

And today marked the beginning of a river of divine blood.

Three Remnants pulsed against Destruction’s palm as she emerged from the Descent. These crystalline fragments of godhood throbbed with ancient memories and forgotten power. Blood and ichor caked her skin in grotesque patterns, mapping her journey through memories never meant for mortal minds. The volcanic tunnel behind her sensed her exit and retreated back into the molten earth, having surrendered its most precious treasures to her unyielding will.

The chamber awaited her return in reverent silence. Volcanic heat billowed through cathedral-high ceilings of obsidian and basalt. Rivers of magma carved their paths through ancient stone and bathed everything in a hellish amber glow that transformed shadows into living nightmares. The air itself seemed to hold its breath, heavy with anticipation and the scent of brimstone.

Destruction stumbled forward on unsteady legs. Her once-regal attire hung in charred tatters around her form. The fabric drifted and disintegrated with each movement, revealing flesh scored with wounds that glowed from within. Raw power leaked from these injuries and sizzled against the stone floor where it fell like burning rain.

Her hair floated around her head in a corona of dark energy, defying gravity’s petty constraints. Dirt, blood, and ash formed a macabre war paint across features too perfect to be human yet too fierce to be divine. Her eyes burned with feverish intensity—twin infernos that had witnessed the birth and death of stars, galaxies extinguished and reborn through her will alone.

Tyric, her current high priest, stood frozen at the chamber’s edge. A lifetime of devoted training had taught him to know what signs to look for when dealing with her. She could see him attempting to read the subtle shifts in the air, through the tenor of thunder beyond her fortress walls. But this—this was something he had never witnessed in all his long years. The goddess before him radiated untethered power and moved with the dangerous grace of a predator pushed beyond reason, beyond restraint. Madness and purpose warred within her gaze, and neither seemed likely to surrender.

He took an involuntary step backward. She could see how the primal survival instincts screamed warnings through his aged body and urged him to flee, to hide, to throw himself into the magma rather than face what stood before him. The goddess who returned from the Descent was not the one who had entered it. Something fundamental had changed.

The careful restraint she normally exercised around her faithful had burned away. He seemed to sense that a wrong word or a misplaced glance might result in his obliteration—collateral damage to her newfound purpose.

Destruction tracked his movement with predatory focus. Her nostrils flared as she detected his fear, savoring it briefly before remembering why he mattered, why he lived while so many others had perished beneath her wrath.

“PREPARE THE ANVIL,” she commanded. Her whisper magnified throughout the volcanic chamber and shook loose obsidian shards from the ceiling.

IT IS TIME FOR DESTRUCTION TO CREATE.

Tyric bowed deeply, obedience unquestioning despite the terror that threatened to paralyze him. He recognized the terrible anticipation hanging in the chamber—history itself holding its breath before a pivotal moment. The goddess strode forward across the obsidian platform, her footprints leaving molten impressions in solid stone. The volcanic bowl that served as her throne room stretched before her, connected to her fortress by a single bridge of blackened stone.

Opposite her stood the Oakthorn Throne—an immense monstrosity of living wood paradoxically bound with eternal flame. Black thorns covered its surface and twisted and undulated toward her presence like serpents yearning for their mother’s touch.

The throne’s core remained vaguely oaken in appearance, yet predominantly composed of sentient magma with obsidian veins for structural reinforcement and dramatic effect.

She raised one hand toward her seat of power. The throne’s backrest split open with a thunderous crack and revealed a forgefire so intense it could dissolve enchanted steel through proximity alone. The heat rippled outward in visible waves and distorted the air throughout the chamber.

Tyric returned leading twenty Endwalkers in solemn procession. They carried her sacred anvil between them, a massive block of metal unknown to mortal smiths. Their movements were perfectly synchronized, each step taken with religious reverence as they positioned the anvil at the center of her diamond-shaped island.

The high priest approached with caution, his weathered face glistening with perspiration that had nothing to do with the chamber’s heat.

He cleared his throat. “My goddess, what manner of creation do you intend? Such preparations have not been made since—”

“THREE REMNANTS REQUIRE A VESSEL,” she interrupted, her voice echoing across the volcanic chamber. “SOMETHING WORTHY OF THEIR POWER.”

“The Remnants from the Descent?” Tyric whispered, eyes widening as understanding dawned. “You succeeded in retrieving them all?”

“I DID.” She turned her burning gaze to the assembled Endwalkers. “LEAVE NOW. I AM NOT TO BE DISTURBED UNTIL MY WORK IS COMPLETE. BE SURE NO ONE ENTERS THIS CHAMBER IF THEY VALUE THEIR LIVES.”

She paused, an unusual consideration flickering across her features. “SEEK SHELTER DEEP WITHIN THE FORTRESS. WHAT COMES NEXT MAY SHAKE THE VERY FOUNDATIONS OF THIS REALM.”

The old mortal bowed deeply and gestured for the Endwalkers to retreat. “The goddess speaks! She will not be disturbed upon pain of death. Away to the lower sanctum, all of you—quickly now!” When they hesitated, he added in a reverent whisper, “Our lady prepares to reshape the world itself. We must make ourselves ready for what follows.”

With a dismissive wave of her hand, Destruction commanded their final departure. None dared question her will.

Her high priest relayed her order to the procession. They departed with grateful haste, but Destruction noticed fresh garments left behind after they vanished from sight. A tiny flare of gratitude warmed her chest, unexpected and almost unwelcome in her current state. She incinerated what remained of her old clothing with a thought and donned the new attire—ancient yet finely crafted, imbued with enchantments to withstand impossible heat.

She withdrew the three Remnants from within her being and held them to the light. Each crystal pulsed with memory.

Here in her hands lay the history of her failures. Her defeats. Her shame.

No longer.

Destruction cast the three crystals into the forgefire without ceremony. The Oakthorn Throne shuddered and wailed, a sound beyond mortal comprehension that reverberated through stone and spirit alike. Within moments, the crystals liquefied into a metallic viscosity.

Their essence flowed around each other in radiant streams of power, swirling with colors no human tongue had names for. Using her flames, she gathered the seething liquid into an ancient casting mold worn smooth by eons of use.

Power erupted from the mold in a geyser of primal energy. Storm clouds materialized overhead and swirled in tight formation, drawn by the working of divine will. Thunder rolled across the sky in continuous waves that rattled the bones of all who heard them. Entranced, Destruction lifted an unadorned hammer from beside the anvil and began her work.

She folded the molten crystal again and again, adjusting temperatures with divine instinct, working with the metal rather than forcing it to her will. The three Remnants remained distinct yet interwoven—separate melodies combining into a harmonious symphony of destruction. Each fold created patterns through the material, whorls and eddies of concentrated power that resembled starlight reflected on midnight waters or the last moment of a dying sun.

The goddess hammered with purpose and fury. She poured her malice into each strike and infused her envy with every fold. Her despair flowed through the metal with each rhythmic blow, and her rage echoed in the thunder that answered her hammer’s call. The crystalline metal responded to her emotions, drinking them in, transforming them into something both beautiful and terrible.

Each impact sent magma spilling over the edges of her volcanic domain and down the mountainside below. Lightning pierced the churning clouds and arced downward to dance across the forming blade, leaving trails of blue-white fire in its wake. Destruction continued without pause, though she felt her nearly infinite core draining at a dangerous rate. The work demanded everything, and she would give it.

She refused respite. Memory fueled her rage. Her hammer rose and fell, each strike resonating through the mountain.

Thunder split the air. The metal screamed beneath her blows, resisting transformation. She struck harder.

Destruction remembered her sister’s betrayals—Creation’s jealousy, her manipulation, her attempts to steal what wasn’t hers to take. The blade drank in her fury and glowed brighter, edges sharpening to impossible keenness.

Molten sparks erupted with each impact, raining down onto the obsidian floor. Where they landed, the stone cracked and hissed. The magma pools surrounding her island rose and fell in rhythm with her hammer, as if the volcano itself breathed in time with her labors.

She remembered Death’s lies—promises broken, alliances abandoned, affection proven false at the moment of greatest need. She folded the metal again, hammering with such force that the anvil sank deeper into the platform. Cracks spread outward in fractal patterns.

Outside her fortress, clouds swirled from clear skies, drawn by the power she was channeling. Lightning struck the peak repeatedly, attracted to the concentration of primal energy. Her followers huddled in the lower chambers, feeling the mountain shake beneath their feet.

She remembered Life’s sadism—the pleasure that pathetic goddess took in watching others suffer while pretending benevolence, the smug superiority in her eyes. The goddess twisted the forming blade, working star-metal into the edge—a material that could slice through spirit and flesh with an equal disregard for defenses. Her sweat sizzled against the metal, adding her essence to the weapon.

The forgefire roared higher. Flames licked the ceiling in hungry tongues. The heat grew so intense that the air itself began to burn.

She remembered the mortals who forgot her—those who turned from allegiance when prosperity made her protection seem unnecessary, their prayers falling silent when their bellies were full. She beat rhythms of vengeance into the sword’s spine, songs of retribution that would echo with every swing.

Volcanic ash and cinders swirled around her in a cyclone. The anvil glowed white-hot beneath her work. Any mortal smith would have been incinerated hours ago, yet still she hammered.

She remembered her shame—the heartbreaking moment she slew Creation’s seven-year-old son, Lucien. The memory burned worst of all. Her hammer hesitated mid-strike. One solitary tear fell upon the blade. It sizzled, leaving a distinctive ripple pattern that would never fade. She forced that pain into the metal, letting it temper with grief turned to purpose.

Blood appeared on her palms where the hammer’s handle had worn through her skin. It dripped onto the forming weapon, divine ichor absorbing into the metal with hungry eagerness. The sword pulsed, already alive in some unspeakable way.

Finally, she remembered all the Progenitors she’d killed for her sister, for the ungrateful mortals who now walked the world oblivious to her sacrifices. What had she received in return?

A box.

Imprisonment.

A world that hadn’t bothered searching for her when she vanished.

She plunged the nearly-complete blade into the heart of the forgefire, holding it there as energies of this world and another surged through metal and maker alike.

“FUCK THEM,” she growled. Sparks erupted from her lips and scattered across the anvil where they hissed and died. “FUCK THEM ALL.”

Hours melted into days.

She barely noticed time’s passage as the work consumed her. The Oakthorn Throne sizzled beside her and vibrated with anticipation, sensing the magnitude of what was being born. When the sword neared completion, she tore a single twisted branch from the fiery throne, using it to fashion the handle and crossguard.

With divine craftsmanship, she fused Oakthorn’s power into the blade. The crystalline metal accepted them hungrily, power flowing through every fiber of its being.

Destruction raised her hammer high above her head. The chamber fell silent, even the bubbling magma stilling in anticipation.

“I WILL BURN IT ALL DOWN.”

Her vow echoed across creation as she delivered the final strike. The hammer fell with perfect precision, and the sword sang in response—a note of such perfect destruction that reality itself shuddered.

Rain began pouring through the volcano’s open ceiling, sweeping away the sweat that coated her molten body. Her clothing, incapable of withstanding the sustained punishment of heat and power, hung in charred tatters around her form.

She stood unmoved, uncaring of her exposure or the elements that dared touch her skin.

She had succeeded.

Calamity was born.

The sword radiated vengeance and destruction beyond anything this world had witnessed. It hummed with malevolent purpose in her hand, eager to taste blood and anguish. Dark satisfaction filled her as she launched herself upward, shooting through the volcano’s opening into the storm-wracked sky beyond. She landed gracefully on the empty beach beyond her fortress, feeling the sand turn to glass beneath her feet. The ocean stretched before her, and beyond that, the snow-tipped mountains of the Grayspires rose against the horizon.

She swung Calamity once. It was a casual gesture, almost an afterthought.

In the distance, the sea heaved upward in response. A wall of water rose impossibly high and began its inexorable journey toward land, a tsunami that would devastate whatever coastline it eventually reached. A smile curved her lips as she watched destruction bloom from her merest thought.

She had visits to make. Certain cowards had been on their thrones unchallenged for far too long.

And after that?

Her sister’s time to die had arrived. Maybe she’d kill that bastard, Stormhollow, for taking too long in escorting her.

Yes, he would die too.
