The fracture that Shoreline chose as an ideal fishing spot was a good distance away from their company, maybe two or three miles down along the wall. Kalsin and Ringtail stopped at the fracture as wide as his forearm, and listened to Shoreline meticulously going through the tools of the trade. Angling, she had explained, had several techniques that consisted of this and that, all of which quickly escaped Kalsin's head as soon as they marched through his earholes. He got the important bits; tie the knots with the gorge buried in the bait—the gorge being some sort of hook—then anchor the whole thing, and finally cast the line in the water.

The lines had to be more than fifteen feet in length to reach the edge of what people called 'deep-fracture', home of underwater life forms that blossomed in the sunless depths of the abyss. Back in the Republic proper, fractures were wide enough for men to dive in and use nets, mine minerals, collect corals and whatever else riches the abyss held beneath. But not out here. Fishing from above was the way of things in the east.

As Ringtail had predicted, it was monotone work. Eventually, Shoreline moved farther down the fracture after enough of her knowledge rubbed off on Kalsin and Ringtail, both of whom knelt at the opposite sides of the thin stream, hunched over, hands busy. Or in Kalsin's case, hands fumbling and trying to avoid using his fingertips, which had quickly turned tingly and irritated.

"Is that the quickest you can go?" Ringtail said, breaking the blessed quiet that had descended over them.

Kalsin didn't look up. "That's right."

"Well, try to speed up. I'm doing double the work over here."

"I'll try."

They worked in silence for another minute.

"Caught anything yet?" Ringtail asked.

Kalsin looked across the row of fishing lines they had already set up. "Nope."

"Hmph."

The whistle of a slight breeze. People talking in the distance. Grass rustling.

Kalsin held back his frustration when the string slipped from between his awkwardly pinched fingers again. He saw that Ringtail had already filled her part of the fracture and was ready to move farther down. But instead, she just sat there with that perpetual frown of hers, fingers rapping against the ground, clearly wound up about something.

"What?" Kalsin asked.

Ringtail's expression cleared for a moment. "Hmm? What do you mean 'what'?"

"Nothing. I just thought you wanted to say something. You looked like you were going to."

"And you look like you're a seventy-summer retiree who can't even tie a knot," she snapped.

Kalsin decided to focus on the work instead. He finally maneuvered a string to its proper place when he heard her mutter something. He could've asked again, but learned experience and all that. So he kept his mouth shut instead.

"I said I'm sorry."

Kalsin didn't pause in his work. "I'm sure you are."

He nearly jumped when a splash broke the quiet as Ringtail hurled something in the water. "Damn it! I’m— Please just stop deflecting for a moment and listen to me!"

Kalsin made a show of setting down his half-baked contraption. He sat back, hands resting on his knees, and looked her in the eye. "Okay. I'm listening."

Ringtail's voice caught in her throat. She blinked at Kalsin once, twice, then as if getting over some invisible hurdle, finally spoke. "I’m sorry for what I just said and also for snitching on you to Brownweather and what happened to your hands and it was never my intention for you to get hurt, and I’m sorry for being a self-absorbed and overly competitive pain in the ass sometimes who can’t stomach even the smallest idea of losing." Her words rolled over each other like an earthquake-shaken mound of oiled corn, a flush of color blooming on her cheeks that could've been from embarrassment, or anger, or both. "I’m an idiot and I don’t know how to— Like, damn it Kalsin. I’m so sorry for your hands. I just wasn’t thinking, I never do, and it fucking killed me watching you have to suffer all these past days. My stupid pride never let me apologize and all this was ultimately my fault, even if you sometimes make things hard by being so... so fucking... infuriatingly you."

Kalsin couldn't hold her gaze for the whole thing, and he didn't know how to respond when she ran out of breath. There was a lot to unpack in her words, and her being uncomfortable made him uncomfortable, which left his feelings in a confused mess and his tongue heavy. He suddenly felt like that shy kid back from Parlest, hanging onto his mother’s skirts.

"Uh. You're not that bad."

She probably was that bad, but he was off balance and just said something for the sake of saying something.

Or maybe, for the first time in a very long stretch, he let his walls down enough for a rare glimpse of honesty to slip out.

As for Ringtail, with the burning confession out of her system and no longer fueling her, she surveyed a rock on the ground as an awkward silence crept in. Tap, tap, tap, her hand kept poking the ground.

Kalsin, with some effort, continued. "And I owe you an apology as well. I should not have evaded you as I did. There's been lots of things going on lately and sometimes it's easier to delay issues, skirt around them, rather than confront them. Say a word and mean another, you know? Never a good idea, but always easier. And lately I've said a lot of words to you that I shouldn't have."

Ringtail’s tapping had stilled. She stayed quiet for a while, but slowly the air grew lighter. From the edge of his vision, Kalsin saw her watch him with a slightly tilted head. "You meant part of it, though, didn't you?"

"Part of what?"

"When you got upset about me covering my scar."

Kalsin couldn't help but let loose a rueful laugh. "Upset? That's one way to call a near psychotic outburst. I lost it over absolutely nothing."

A small smirk tugged at Ringtail's lips. "Neh, upset and psychotic, sorta the same thing. I’m just dampening the blow, ya know, since I'm such an accomplished diplomat. But really, tell me. I'm curious. Why is covering my scar such a big deal for you?"

"I grew up in Araz."

"Oh. Oh."

Kalsin didn't feel sharp having to admit it, but he said it anyway. "I can't hide behind the excuse, I'm aware, but a residue of upbringing remained with me even against my best efforts. I still find it... strange. Upsetting, even. When I meet folk, my eyes instinctively seek out their scars, and having found none, part of me files them off as Clenn. Then I realize the logic doesn't apply in most parts of the Republic, and then I blame the person rather than myself to make me feel better.” He took a low breath. “And I guess I'm jealous too, because, I mean… look at me." The last part slipped out without intention.

Ringtail's eyes quickly slipped down to his jaw, taking in his scars, then back. "Eh. It's not that bad."

"Cheap, using my own lines against me."

"Seriously. I’m not squeamish about scars. They can look intimidating, actually. Combined with your silver eyes, it’s kind of stunni—” She bit off her words, turning away with a cough. “Uuuuh, I mean. It’s like, you know. It’s alright."

Kalsin shook his head, exasperated. "Look, we can agree to disagree, but—"

The sound of a horn pierced through Kalsin's voice, coming from their group's lookout that was now a good distance to the south of them, having moved away to the fracture. They stood, both of them tensing, both hoping it was just a Veiled sighting and nothing more. Kalsin turned in the direction Shoreline had gone and saw her hurry back toward them. "We should head back, now!" she yelled.

She said more, but they couldn't hear as a second horn swept across the plains. The Veiled were coming.

But before the distant lookout near their company could finish its second horn, the one now closer to them also blew a signal, then another quickly after. Multiple locations were going to be attacked, all at once.

Men Kalsin didn't recognize ran like denizens of a roughed up anthill, some rushing for their weapons, others already armed and taking up position at the walls. People shouted, hoarded shields to those who had none at hand, and kicked out flames underneath brewing tin pots. Everyone carried swords at all times, and so Kalsin quickly found himself having drawn his blade and holding a shield someone had shoved in his left.

Two horns from two different lookouts next to one another. Maybe more, down toward the south and too far away for him to hear. The Veiled were attacking multiple positions at once; and Kalsin was at the wrong one.

Him, Ringtail, and Shoreline would not have time to return to their fellow recruits. They'd have to face the Veiled, for the first time, with strangers by their sides.
