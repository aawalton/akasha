Viktor peered down upon the devastation he had wrought.

Everything had gone much better than anticipated. When he sent the water serpent after the mutated giant, he had only intended to wound it. The creature was enormous, after all, so killing it shouldn’t have been easy. His plan had been to inflict enough damage to force it away from the island and back into open water, where he could gradually wear it down through repeated attacks. He had fully expected a prolonged engagement. But instead, he had killed the thing outright by blowing off its face.

When he returned to the Folded Chamber, he saw the giant anchoring itself against the edge of the main island as it tore down the barricade of vines, allowing the smaller monsters to pour through the gaps.

Well, at least that meant Ekon was still kicking. Which, all things considered, was rather impressive. The bald man had been trapped there with that monstrosity for a while and somehow managed to avoid being turned into a smear on the landscape. Viktor wondered how much of that was his own doing and how much came from the aid of that mysterious woman, the Eternal Flame.

Either way, he should hurry. Every moment he spent observing was another moment Ekon moved closer to becoming fish food.

He attempted to lift himself into the air, and the winds responded without issue. In other words, the suppression effect was not active throughout the entire Folded Chamber. The phenomenon must therefore have been localized in some fashion. Perhaps it only functioned within a certain distance of the giant. Perhaps the creature needed line of sight. Or perhaps, it had to consciously focus on a target.

So, instead of flying straight toward the island, he circled around it in a wide arc. Once he reached the far end of the chamber, he descended until he was skimming just above the water. The stone structure itself now served as cover, preventing the giant and the other monsters from noticing his approach.

After reaching land, he immediately conjured the water serpent with his Thauma. The living construct flowed across the ground, slithering along the wall of the building as he rose to the top. On the way up, he took out the wooden case he had brought and tossed it through one of the windows. He arrived at the roof just in time to see the giant lifting a massive boulder, and it didn’t take a genius to figure out what was going to happen next.

There was not enough time to stop the giant before it released the projectile. By the time he reached it, the rock would already be in the air. So he needed to do two things at the same time.

He formed an ice core within the serpent’s body and sent it surging ahead. The construct got to the shoreline just as the giant threw the boulder. He jumped from the roof, using his buckler to stop the flying rock, all the while keeping his attention on his snake—or, to be exact, on the ice core hidden within it—to prevent it from detonating prematurely.

The timing had been dangerously tight. One small deviation, and it would have all gone wrong. If he had dropped a second later to meet the boulder, the three people who were hovering behind him right now would have been reduced to mincemeat. For the briefest of moments, he found himself wondering if any of them had lost control of their bladder at that instant of impact. Well, better not to ask. In fact, better not to turn around, or he might see something no one wanted to be seen.

In any case, the giant problem had been resolved. What remained was the shark problem.

The flood was slowly receding from the island as the sea around it grew calm once more. The shark-men, having been briefly scattered, began to regroup, dragging themselves back toward the shore in relentless waves, seemingly unaffected by the death of the giant.

Well, yes.

Throwing underwater monsters back into the water did not solve anything; it merely delayed the unpleasantness for a few fleeting moments.

He could, of course, stay in the air and pick them off one by one. That would be easy enough. But then, after taking enough casualties, the remaining monsters would simply retreat into the depths, and going down there to hunt them would be a massive waste of time. On the other hand, leaving them alive was not an option either. No, all hostile forces within the Folded Chamber must be eliminated before he could proceed to the next step of the plan. And to do that, he needed to gather them in one place and wipe them out in one decisive strike.

With nothing left to consider, Viktor moved.

He flew out over the open water, then he dropped. At the same time, he activated “Water Walk,” a Thauma he had crafted based on the spell the Acolytes of the Deep had used. The moment he touched down, the surface hardened under his feet just enough to bear his weight.

There he stood, waves below him, waves all around him. No shore, no shelter, no protection.

Come at me, you bastards. This is your home turf, right?

It didn’t take long for the first shark-man to launch itself out of the churning water. The creature came from his left, bursting from the surface with its jaws spread wide, revealing rows of serrated teeth that glistened with saliva.

Without even moving his feet, he blew it away with a blast of wind. No, not back into the water, but up into the air. And while the shark-man was suspended helplessly overhead, he conjured an ice lance directly from the water surrounding him, and hurled it right into the creature’s gaping mouth. The projectile penetrated deep into its throat before he triggered the detonation. In an instant, flesh and bone and tooth fragments exploded in a wet cloud as the headless body dropped back to the sea, and its frenzied kin fell upon it before the ripples even spread.

More shark-men were now coming at him from every direction, so he started moving as well. He darted across the surface as he waited for his opponents to commit to their leaps. Another shark-man lunged upward from the depths, this one wielding a jagged polearm. The weapon swept in a wide arc, aimed at his neck, but before the blade could reach him, it struck something invisible. The impact jarred its arms, and the dazed creature fell back into the waves with a confused thrash.

Well, yes.

There were indeed invisible things scattered around the area. Because he had been casting “Wall of Air,” a Thauma inspired by those aeromancers who had attempted to trap him during the battle above the outpost, while he was running around. Compared to theirs, his walls were smaller and couldn’t move, but they didn’t need to. He was merely placing them here as unseen obstacles whose locations were known only to him.

The shark-faced monsters kept coming. Some tried to bite, some tried to stab, some tried to throw projectiles. He didn’t care. He just ran around his walls, moving in an unpredictable path so that nothing could rise directly from under his feet. And whenever he found a clear line of sight? Another ice lance, another burst of gore, another corpse for the feast.

There were too many of them now. He could feel the creatures gathering beneath the surface, drawn not only by the target he had painted on his own back, but also by all the meat and blood that had been raining down for the past several minutes. He glanced at the island while sidestepping lunging predators, and found the shoreline completely empty. Good. Every last one of them had come to join this dance.

It’s time.

The wind gems tied to his legs fired off simultaneously, shooting him high into the air. He took out a small bag that contained a handful of round objects. He threw them all into the water, scattering them in a ring around him.

He flicked his fingers.

The next second, profound and echoing thunder exploded under the surface as colossal columns of water shot upward like geysers that breached the sky, before crashing back down in a torrential deluge.

Then, after the waters finally calmed and the rain subsided, corpses began to resurface. First in twos and threes, then in dozens, then in a floating carpet of pale bellies and sightless eyes and torn flesh.

Viktor chuckled as he looked at the carnage. Alycia’s explosives had never failed to impress.

Once he had realized the Folded Chamber was a water realm, he immediately instructed Celeste to have the gremlins haul a fresh batch of explosives to the Core Room. So, after his puppet sank and he found himself back in his room, he had teleported to the dungeon to collect the bombs before flying here. He had grabbed the medallion as well, the Reliquary that allowed him to move freely underwater. Thankfully, he didn’t have to actually use it.

As for the explosives, they followed the same principle as the ones he had used to destroy Iskora’s portal. All of them had the same detonator, so he only needed to spread them out, then trigger them all at the same time.

With his work complete, Viktor drifted back to the island, where Ekon and his companions stood in frozen awe, their gazes flicking repeatedly between him and the field of floating carcasses offshore.

Then, Mandragora snorted. “Do we even need to be here? You just single-handedly killed everything.”

She had a point, he supposed. Had he known everything about this Folded Chamber from the start, then yes, he could have just come here and cleared it by himself. But he hadn’t had that information. So someone else needed to go in first, to probe the defenses, to draw attention away from him.

Suddenly, a flash of light flared beside him, before condensing into the shape of a woman.

Galatea, of course.

After Celeste informed him that Mandragora and Renee could open the rift from within, he figured keeping Galatea outside the Folded Chamber was no longer necessary. So he had ordered her to enter and scout the interior of the stone building while staying invisible.

“There are only three men inside,” she began her report. “All of them have been restrained by the vines.”

Viktor arched a brow. “Three?”

“Yes.”

Including the woman who had gone out earlier and the man he had killed, there were a total of five people in that massive building. Surely there had been more, until he slaughtered dozens of them in the ruins. But that was weeks ago. Why hadn’t they received reinforcements?

Oh, well. That was a problem for later.

For now, the fact remained that all significant threats had been neutralized, and the Folded Chamber had been secure. There might be some stragglers still hiding somewhere, but Ekon’s party should be able to handle them without problem.

“I’m going back now,” Viktor said. “You stay here with them to investigate the building. Report your findings to me tomorrow morning.”

“Understood.”

All that remained was mere grunt work, which could and should be delegated to other people. He would just head home and sleep. After all, lingering here any further meant he might need to recast “Dark Veil” and “Change Voice,” but his Focus was a bit low now after all the fighting.

Besides, Claire said kids his age shouldn’t stay up late at night.
