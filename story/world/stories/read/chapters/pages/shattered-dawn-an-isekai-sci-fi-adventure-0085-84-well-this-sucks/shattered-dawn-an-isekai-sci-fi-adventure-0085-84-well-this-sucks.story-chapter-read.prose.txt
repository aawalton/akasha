Laserarms fired nearby, the splatter-thump of each shot joined by a flash of light. Projectile rifles responded, a rhythmic pop-pop-popping of gunpowder detonations.

The fight for New Kairn Tol had moved inside the walls.

Elion breathed deeply to clear his head. Things had not gone as planned. Did that change his next steps? No. He still needed to get his knife out of the swordinator. He still needed to help Keyla rescue Selna.

The current situation just made all those things feel more difficult. Surely Venya would be coming back to this camp with her warlocks, as soon as they realized what was going on. Dave was probably on his way to whine to her, though Elion doubted he would move very fast with an injured leg.

“Step one,” Elion said as he stood. “Get that knife back.”

He returned to the swordinator and inspected it. He’d smashed the lid off of the top with the sledge hammer, and his knife lay on the shimmering white plate, like a display in a museum.

Inspecting the control panel, Elion tried to decipher the instruments. Dave hadn’t labelled anything, so Praxis couldn’t help with translations.

“I need Keyla’s help,” he said.

Yaret looked up. “She’s not well.”

“I can help,” Keyla protested, trying to rise and wincing. “What do you need?”

“Can you glitch out this machine?” Elion asked.

“I can try.”

Elion and Yaret carefully helped Keyla over to the swordinator. She sat on the ground and leaned against the arachnatronic. Blood stained the makeshift bandagages Yaret had wrapped around her waist, and her face paled.

Reaching out, Keyla placed her hand on the side of the swordinator. Teal light flickered, crackling between her fingers and the machine. Elion reached for his blade, trying to summon it to his hand.

“What’s happening?” he asked.

“There are several elements overlayed here,” Keyla said. “I need to find the right one, the one that’s holding your blade.”

She closed her eyes.

Shouts sounded from down the street. Elion rose and ventured out toward the main road. An arachnatronic headed their way, black robed figures riding atop it.

He ran back to Keyla. “They’re coming back,” he said. “Can you do it?”

“That’s interesting. It’s running Desmian and Silvestrian circuits, just using the Artefin elements for power.”

“Can you glitch them out?” Elion asked.

“I can, but it might take a few minutes,” Keyla said.

“We don’t have a few minutes. Yaret, help me carry it.”

Yaret grabbed the carrying handle on one side of it. The other handle lay on the ground, knocked off when Elion had bludgeoned the box with his sledge hammer.

Elion crouched, wrapping his arms beneath the bulk of it and lifting. It was heavy and bulky, like maneuvering a laundry machine. Together they heaved it off of the arachnatronic.

Yaret grunted as they moved.

As they shuffled toward the edge of the camp, Elion searched for a hiding place.

“There!” he called. A large boulder stood at the edge of the chasm, providing a sheltered space that would obscure them from view of anyone in the warlock's camp. Hopefully that would buy Keyla some time.

They shuffled over to the the boulder and set the machine down. The heavy contraption caught Elion’s finger, smashing it against the ground.

“Gah,” Elion exclaimed. “Lift it back up!”

Yaret pushed, tilting the heavy machine, and Elion slipped his finger out. He ran back into the camp and found Keyla, leaning on the damaged arachnatronic as she tried to climb to her feet.

Just beyond a nearby tent, Elion heard warlocks shouting as they unloaded from their vehicle.

“Where’s Elfron! What happened here, where’s the girl!”

Elion grabbed Keyla’s arm and pulled it over his shoulder. They stumbled through the camp, keeping tents between them and the warlocks as best they could.

Excited shouts rang out behind them, as the warlocks discovered their dead sentry and the signs of fighting.

“He must be here somewhere!” someone shouted.

Elion and Keyla reached the boulder, and rounded it. A deep overhang and a crevasse in the rock provided a hidden place for them; Elion couldn’t make out Yaret in the darkness until he was practically on top of him.

The edge of the cliff lay a dozen feet away. A slight shifting of the wind carried a wave of acerbic, acid pit scent across them.

“Okay that might have bought us a minute or two,” Elion said, helping Keyla to sit beside the machine.

“What happened to Hol?” Yaret asked.

“I don’t know,” Elion said. “I didn’t see any sign of them. I don’t think we can count on any support.”

Keyla placed her hand on the machine again, teal sparks crackling once more. “It’s such a strange construction,” she said. “It doesn’t seem like it needs to be actively powered.”

“Hurry Keyla!” Elion said, bouncing on his toes.

“If I just glitch out the power source, I don’t think it’ll do anything. I need to power it in the right way so that it unlocks.”

She closed her eyes, continuing to use her Artefin skill to investigate the machine.

Anxious, Elion pulled loose parts of the machine away. The door at the top covering the shallow interior came away easily. The long white tray in the top of the machine glowed softly in the darkness. His butter knife lay there, still firmly welded in place.

He tried pulling on it but it did not budge.

“Try here!” he said, pointing to the tray.

Keyla moved around, fingers crackling with turquoise electricity. She planted her hand on the white tray, splayed fingers over the knife. Power surged through her. The knife held firm.

“That’s Silvestrian,” she said. “Not Artefin.”

A warlock rounded the corner of the stone.

The silver-white glow of the swordinator revealed their hiding place immediately.

“Over here!” he shouted, running toward them. He fired Zelian darts at them, spraying them wildly across their rock hiding place. Elion ducked behind the machine, as Yaret sheltered in a crevasse of the rock and Keyla sprawled flat on the ground.

Elion cursed. He grabbed the machine, wrapping his arms around it. Using all of his strength, he heaved it up to his chest and stumbled to the edge of the chasm.

One of the warlock’s darts struck the machine, knocking Elion to the side and nearly sending him over the edge. The swordinator slipped from his grasp, crashing down.

Elion gave it one strong push as it fell, sending it tumbling down into the pit.

The machine bounced once on the steep incline then splashed into a pool of acid.

Elion tried summoning his blade. He still sensed it, down in the acid pool below. It did not come to him.

A half dozen warlocks rounded the stone and quickly cast binding spells, knocking Elion to the ground. Purple ribbons of light twisted themselves around his arms and legs. Keyla and Yaret were also bound.

Elion struggled against the bonds as the warlocks grabbed them and pulled them into their camp.

"Somebody get Venya!" one of the warlocks ordered. "This is them!”

A discussion broke out.

“Wasn’t she in town?”

“She was going to get that girl.”

I think that the fighting caught up with her."

"Send a runner!”

“We have to get out of here!”

“We won't get involved in that fighting. These two are why we're here!"

The magical bonds on Elion began to loosen, weakening as the magic faded. He fought one arm free and punched a nearby warlock in the shoulder.

He wanted to use ‘Manifest Armaments’ but was afraid that the Ascendency fatigue would only make the situation worse for him.

The warlocks quickly recast their spell, binding him again.

"Somebody get cords!" another warlock said. "Our spells are weakening.”

Ropes were produced and hands and legs were tied. Elion, Keyla and Yaret were pushed into a tent. Keyla cried out, clutching the injury in her side as blood drained from her face.

“Careful with her!” Elion shouted at the warlocks. “She’s injured!”

Keyla’s face was pale and waxy in the dim light of the tent. One of the warlocks, a woman, bent and examined Keyla’s wound.

“Who did this?” she demanded. “Venya will want her alive.”

When nobody fessed up, the warlock turned back to Keyla. “Adequately bandaged, I suppose,” she said. “Where in Kylios is Elfron?” Again she received no answer. Grumbling to herself, she produced her long wand and prodded Keyla’s side with it.

She slid the wand under the bandage Yaret had applied.

Keyla gasped. Violet lightning crackled down the wand, and the wound hissed, the smell of burned flesh filling the room.

“Should help it stop bleeding,” the warlock said. She rose, then assigned two warlocks to remain behind as guards.

“We’ve got what we came for,” the woman said. “We need to find Venya as soon as possible and get out of here.”

The warlocks left, leaving only the two guards in the tent to watch over their prisoners.

"Well, this sucks," Elion grumbled. "I didn't even get my blade back and now we're trapped."

"You said this handkerchief belonged to my mother?" Keyla asked.

“Yes,” Elion said. “It did! You don’t remember her at all?”

“I must have had a mom,” Keyla said. “Everyone does, right? I don’t remember though.”

Elion frowned. He started at the ground, twisting against his bonds. He could have prevented this. This was all his fault. He should have come up with a better plan. He should have fought harder, run faster, done everything better.

“Yes,” Elion said.

“It looked like there was fighting going on down there,” Keyla said. “In the center of the town.”

“I’m sorry,” Yaret said. “I should have been faster.” His face reflected Elion’s feelings as they both gazed at Keyla.

“You did what you could,” Elion said. “It’s my fault.”

“What’s your fault?” Keyla asked.

“What is our next move?” Yaret asked.

Elion gritted his teeth. “Our objectives haven’t changed,” he said. “They might have gotten a bit more difficult to reach, but they are the same.”

He eyed the two warlocks guarding them. They sat nearer the entrance of the tent, playing a card game at a low table.

Elion felt a strange sensation. He had felt it before, but had never noticed it. His bond to the blade… Changed. He could no longer sense the knife, buried down in the Acid Pits. Instead, it rested near him; within his reach. Like all he had to do was reach out and grab it.

He did.

Cold silver formed in his hand.

The acid had destroyed the machine and freed his blade.

“It’s time to get out of here,” he said. “Let’s do it before Venya gets back.”
