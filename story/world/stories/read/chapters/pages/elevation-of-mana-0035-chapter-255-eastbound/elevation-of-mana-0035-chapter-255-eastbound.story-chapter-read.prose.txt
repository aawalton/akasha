The day came to go and Iru was at my side.

“You weren't assigned here,” I said, sighing.

“No, but I'm going anyway.”“...”“Don't give me that look, there are different plants up in those mountains, and I for one plan to see them. Also, if I left you alone out there you'd be in a world of trouble before getting back. You seem the sort to find it,” he said sagely.

“Never, not once ever have I been in trouble Iru.”“Yes, but like I said, you seem the sort for it. Anyway I want to go.”“And your duties here?”“It's fine, things will be fine, I'm leaving a message about where I'm going.”The rest of the group wasn't large. We had only a few people capable of magic and it wasn't like we really could spare many. There were a couple people who were stronger and faster than normal, a pair of twins named Ray and Roy, which were probably the most normal names of any humans I knew. There was also a girl who could throw fire, an elf, Lia who now spoke up for him.

“We could use the help if it's monsters, none of you have seen them before and we don't have enough of my people powerful enough to be much help.”“True I guess miss Lia, but we'll be in trouble when we get back.”“No, Adia is old and strong enough to handle things here.”“Surprised she's not coming with us,” Ray said, his brother nodding.

“She wants to,” Lia replied. “But she has other duties here, ones that she can't miss without major issue. Believe me too, nobody wants his Majesty to have to come find us,” she said with a shiver.

“He that strong?” Roy inquired.

“I've only heard the stories from my grandfather, but if half of them are true about the things he did in his younger days, terrifyingly destructive.”

“Don't suppose you'll share?” I asked as we finished packing our bags, Iru had already prepared his. They were simple leather things, with a very basic wooden frame around them, but functional, and moderately comfortable, if a bit heavy.

“Those are not my stories to share,” she replied with a soft smile.

“Fair enough,” I conceded, though some of the other boys looked a bit disappointed by that.

We had pretty vague directions, East, maybe a bit North, there were no maps as such. Which was unfortunate, but not unexpected, the elves hadn't used their airships to fully map the region, yet, though I was of the understanding that they wanted to. Not sure how I felt about that, being that it would give them even more power over the area, which while they weren't doing anything untoward yet certainly wasn't theirs.

Even as the sun rose we set off, for it was best to begin such things before daybreak. We had a long way to go and a short time to get there, so there was up the edge of the valley, then eastbound and down, heading towards the next.

Ray and Roy had the heaviest packs, though each of us bore one, and myself and Iru were in charge of making sure that blisters, soreness, and the like stayed at bay. This might seem a simple job, but the amount of time and effort it saved even as we began was nothing to scoff at.

Without feet wearing out, without the discomfort of a pack carried too long in one position, and with the fatigue that built up in muscles through effort or lactic acid mitigated through light spells we made better time than any normal group would make.

“Could have used this before,” said Ray, stretching out under a tree as we ate a simple lunch of dried fruits and nuts, good, long lasting foods that would hold us until we got to the area we were going. “Feet feel great, even if I am hungry as a bear.”“Yeah,” I explained. “The hunger is a side effect of any real healing.”“Wonder why?” his brother asked.

“Because the energy and stuff to fix things has to come from somewhere,” I laughed.

“Never thought about that,” said Iru with a shrug. “Just always assumed it did because it did.”

“Wait, why is it energy? Like fire is energy? I think the teacher spoke about that,” Ray inquired.

“Lots of things are energy, but like, when you're food you feel more energetic right?” I returned, secretly hoping I'd not made a critical misstep here.

“Oh, I suppose that's true.”“Never heard it put like that, but I suppose it's true,” Lia answered with a shrug. “I do feel more ready to go about things when I'm not hungry. Anyway, we should get on with the trip, our lunch break has been long enough as it is.”“Yeah, and we should think about asking Iru to add a bit of fresh fruit to our diet if we find anything he can grow,” I laughed.

“Why?” the young man in question asked.

“You've never eaten nothing but dried fruits and meat before have you?”Roy chuckled, and Lia made a face, it seemed they understood.

“Should I have?”“No, we'll all be having a horrid time when we break to relieve ourselves if we don't though,” I laughed.
