Two days before the rift opened, Halkan Morine—sovereign of the Morine Domain—instructed diplomats to tease out Hayden’s political skill and temperament. He planned to steal Hayden’s technology regardless of the auction; his interest was in the person himself. The next day, that changed when his daughter’s bodyguards flew into his chambers and said, Sir! There’s an emergency!

What happened? he had roared, expecting an assassination attempt on his daughter’s life.

A student’s spirit lost control. It spread a coran blast through the room and… it damaged your daughter’s core.

What do you mean “damaged it?”

Sir… she has tyrexis. Her channels are out of control. We have her stable, but… she’s in bad condition.

Tyrexis was a disease that spread corrupted cora through someone’s body. Its effects were minor at first, but it made someone’s muscles rot without frequent doses of cleansing elixirs. It was better to remove someone’s core, but that would condemn his daughter to a depressingly short life.

Later that night, Halkan spoke to the doctor. Isn’t there something? I have unlimited resources.

Nothing conventional. There’s no cure or… experimental treatment. That said… there is something… or rather someone who may be able to help.

Who?

Hayden Reskco.

As in the man holding the tech expo?

Yes. During his Junolyx entrance test, he boldly declared that Haeta Core Syndrome wasn’t a disease. Six weeks later, independent sources have confirmed that Lina Iska’s channels are healed, and her core is exhibiting unprecedented breathing patterns. If Hayden Reskco came from the future, he may know a cure for your daughter’s illness.

Halkan took that information to his aides, who returned with a report saying that Hayden was notoriously uncooperative and unlikely to help him, especially if it was politically unwise. Halkan was convinced that he could bring him under his wing, but time was a factor. He needed Hayden’s help immediately—it was non-negotiable.

Unfortunately, Hayden was untouchable—or so he thought.

Not a day later, the rift crisis presented itself. Hayden was illegally entering the rift to obtain something—something he desperately wanted. If Halkan could get what Hayden wanted first, he had a ticket to protect his daughter.

Chasing Hayden was a politically sensitive move, but Halkan believed it was the right one. No, he was sure of it.

Halkan was a superstitious man who believed in luck, fortune, and punishment. He followed his superstitions and gut feelings his entire life and spread his domain over three billion galaxies. To him, signs and omens were the key to success, and this one couldn’t be more blatant.

Fate demanded he bring Hayden Reskco into his fold—to the point that it would kill his daughter if he failed. This was the trial with ultimate stakes, and Halkan would obtain him—no matter the cost. It was personal. It was universal. It was his destiny.

Stella wasn’t superstitious—she was furious. She approached the Morine Domain’s representatives in the South Tower Ballroom and asked the question on everyone’s mind: “Is the Morine Domain declaring war?”

Hayden wondered the same as he watched Carian’s ship follow him. He could barely see them due to the rain splattering on the camera, but they were there, tailing them as Hayden’s team approached the reflective mercury drop in the sky.

“I thought you said you got shit takin’ care of,” Fringe—a burly rifter on his team—snapped. He and the others were clearly on edge after realizing they still had to deal with the Sickles.

“I said no such thing,” Hayden said. “I said that I spoke to Stella personally.”

“Well that wasn’t good enough. If the Sickles get through that rift, we’re dead.”

“I don’t recall Stella’s patronage ever being part of this agreement. I hired you to protect me against the Sickles; the fact we can enter ahead of schedule is a blessing.”

Fringe pointed at Hayden. “You better temp that attitude. Because if you don’t—”

“Fringe!” Kalia snapped. She was the team's hired lead, and she was in a foul mood with the rest of them, but she remained calm. “This is the job. Now be quiet. Lent’s trying to ask a question.”

Hayden turned to the pilot. Lent—the team’s pilot and treasure hunter—turned back. “How certain are you that this is a launcher? If we launch through this and hit a cave, we’re done for.”

Rifts had unclear openings. They could lead to an open world or to a cave. If someone entered with a shuttle, the impact could kill them on impact. That was why the first team always went in via parachute and “body warped” the entrance.

“I’m certain,” Hayden said.

“Okay, how should we—”

Hayden received a call from Ritrix Iska. “One minute.”

“One minute?” Fringe yelled. “We don’t have a—”

“Be quiet.” Hayden ordered. His tone was so authoritative that Fringe complied. Then, he answered the call, lifting his green bracelet to let everyone know he was on the line despite the silence. He was using thought communications.

What’s the situation? Hayden asked when Ritrix’s face popped up in his mind. He had made Mayle and the other spies think that Ritrix had betrayed him—but he hadn't. He was just playing a part, and he did it well.

The Morine Domain tricked us into letting the Crimson Sickles into the rift with you. The Crimson Sickles are denying any involvement with the deal, and we believe them. That said, you should prepare for intervention. The Morine Domain is being extremely aggressive.

Do you know why?

The sovereign’s daughter developed tyrexis after an accident yesterday. He’s convinced you can save her.

Hayden closed his eyes, remembering back to what the old man had told him at the shore of Riaka. It will make your journey substantially more difficult, especially as you reach major inflection points. That’s what he said, and this was the most consequential inflection point in his journey.

It’s only going to get worse, he thought.

Hayden? Ritrix asked with the inflection of a question.

Tell him that there’s no cure for tyrexis, Hayden responded, but I will teach someone to treat his daughter if he stops his interventions. I will show no mercy or compassion for anyone who tries to affect me.

Ritrix nodded. Understood.

Good. What about your daughters?

It was twenty minutes into Kayline and Lina’s flight when their ship suddenly lost altitude. Kayline gripped her armrest, looking out the rain-splattered window as it lowered. “What’s happening?” she asked.

“You’re linking up with your team,” Kernant said. Kayline stared at him in confusion as the ship reached the ground, exposing another shuttle.

“Team?” Lina asked. “As in…”

“You’re entering the rift,” he said. “If you’re too foolish to back out.”

Lina’s lips curved into a grin. “I couldn’t be more stupid.”

Kayline smiled as well. “Neither could I.”

Kernant sighed. “If that’s the case, then listen up. You have three bodyguards—the rest are mercenaries on a mission. You are not to intervene. You are not to fight. You are patsies whose only role is to provide Hayden with resources, and protect yourselves. That’s it. Do you understand?”

Kayline nodded and said, “Understood,” but she didn’t understand the gravity of their role until she ran to the other ship and saw twenty hardened adults in tactical gear glaring at them. This was a mission for jaded adult rifters, not children—or guards.

In fact, she wasn’t even sure if it was a job for rifters. Their callous faces brought only one word to mind: Ambush.

Hayden hung up his call and addressed his team. “The Crimson Sickles are coming, but not because they want to. There’s aggressive politics at play—but that doesn’t change anything. Our goal remains the same: enter the rift and obtain what I’m looking for. That’s what I paid you to do, so we’re going to do it—at any cost. It doesn’t matter if people pursue us, attack us, chase us, or try to steal our prize—that’s our goal. So keep focused and launch the rift.”

Fringe was furious about his nonchalant attitude, but the other team members gave their assent, and Lent hit the thrusters and launched into the rift, swallowed whole by the colossal drop of mercury swirling above Planet Heline.
