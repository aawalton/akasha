I opened my eyes inside my dream sanctuary. The familiar college apartment greeted me.

The old couch sat exactly where I remembered it, with a bookshelf to its left and my desk with my laptop to its right. The tiny kitchenette filled the corner nearest the exit door, while the bed sat on the opposite side. Finally, a black tarot table full of zodiac signs and other mystic symbols, the only thing that existed solely inside the dream.

For several moments, I simply sat on the couch and enjoyed the silence. This was only the third time I had managed to enter my sanctuary entirely on my own, rather than starting from Nina's sanctuary.

Dreamers were supposed to navigate dreams naturally. Nina wandered through the Dreaming almost as easily as most people walked. Meanwhile, I struggled to reach my personal space consistently.

I leaned back against the couch, wondering why it was so much easier when Nina was involved.

When I slept wrapped inside her wings, entering the Dreaming felt effortless. I would close my eyes beside her and realize I was dreaming right away. Yet when I slept alone, I spent most of the time wandering through half-formed dreams.

Maybe it was her Dreamer gift helping me unconsciously. I also remembered being able to find my way to my sanctuary when Obora put me to sleep as a form of attack.

Before I could think about it any further, a knock sounded at the apartment door.

A smile immediately spread across my face. Crossing the room, I opened the door and found Nina standing on the other side.

Beyond her stretched the impossible landscape of her own sanctuary. Massive floating islands drifted through the endless sky, each one a workshop dedicated to a different craft the inventor had pursued.

Nina clapped enthusiastically.

"Alice, you made it on your own!"

"I did."

She immediately stepped inside.

As soon as she crossed the threshold, the floating islands vanished, replaced by a starry void. Then the apartment door closed behind her, sealing us inside my sanctuary.

Nina looked around the room once more. Her eyes traveled over the computer, the refrigerator, the microwave, the electric lamps, and several framed photographs sitting on nearby shelves.

I had brought her here before, yet she never seemed to grow tired of examining everything. I remembered her gutting the bed just to marvel at the springs.

"Your sanctuary is as weird as ever," she finally declared.

I laughed.

"That is because I am not from the Seven Realms."

Nina blinked. Then her expression transformed into one of pure satisfaction.

"I knew it."

"You knew what?"

"All the glimpses I keep seeing in your dreams." She pointed dramatically at me. "They feel like stories written by an insane author."

Honestly, that was not an unreasonable description of Earth from an outsider's perspective.

"Well, my world is completely different from yours," I admitted as I dropped back onto the couch.

Nina immediately joined me, settling onto the cushion beside me. She sat close enough for our shoulders to touch, and her wings extended to engulf me in a hug. She had always been a touchy person, so I did not mind.

"So tell me about it," she said.

"Earth?"

"Yes, Earth. What does that word mean?" she asked eagerly.

I realized I had been using English when referring to my world.

"It means 'earth,' like the element," I said, switching to the Common Language equivalent. "Or maybe more like earth as in the ground," I added, using another word that carried a similar meaning.

For a while, we talked about home. As an inventor, Nina was particularly interested in our advanced technology.

I explained planes, trains, and telephones. Showing her my phone as a prop led to a conversation about satellites, computers, and the internet.

The similarity between the internet and the Great Dream was not lost on her.

"That is why you were able to navigate the Great Dream with ease." Her eyes widened with excitement.

"If finding memes and starting new ones counts as navigation, then call me captain," I said, laughing at my own joke.

"And you managed to do all that without magic?" she asked.

"Yes. Just combustion and harnessed lightning."

"I always found our reliance on Mana, Souls, and Monster Cores inconvenient. Maybe I should try to harness lightning, too."

"You already understand how electric circuits work after helping with my taser spear. You only need to find a way to make and store lightning that does not rely on mana." I shrugged.

As the conversation continued, I found myself talking about things I had not thought about in months. I described my university and how heavily we focused on education. Nina's mind was blown when I told her that most people underwent at least ten years of schooling, while some spent twenty years or more pursuing their education.

I described cafés crowded with students. I described traffic jams, supermarkets, libraries, cinemas, and apartment buildings taller than most castles.

"I wish I could show you," I finally said.

"Show me what?"

"My world… My memories."

Nina tilted her head.

"Why can't you?"

I blinked.

She pointed toward the tarot table. The Fool card rested in the center. On one side sat a deck of cards, and I knew the topmost card was The Star while the rest remained blank.

Two cards. Two rooms.

"It has been a while since you created your first extra room," Nina observed.

"Since then, you have acquired a new Soulbook. You have traveled through the countryside and visited multiple towns. You have been inside a Dead Zone, and you have fought undead and cultists."

A smile spread across her face.

"Your Inspiration must be bursting at the seams."

"You think I can make another one?" I asked excitedly.

"I think you should."

My eyes remained fixed on the cards.

The Fool represented Home, the beginning.

The Star represented Hope, my wish for a world with more magic.

An idea crossed my mind. It was mostly inspired by the dream Obora had tried to trap me in.

Nina saw the realization happen, and her wings fluttered with excitement.

"Oh." A grin spread across her face. "You already know what to make."

I slowly smiled.

I reached toward the deck and shuffled the cards slightly. Then I drew the topmost card and found myself holding The Moon. The card showed my face shedding a tear while looking over a dog and a wolf with a narrow path between them, while a crayfish emerged from the water. It was a card of memory, illusion, reflection, and dreams.

The moment my fingers touched it, I knew it was the right choice.

Slowly, I placed it in the center of the table, replacing The Fool.

The entire sanctuary shifted.

The college apartment dissolved around us, and a heartbeat later, we stood in a completely different space.

Rows of comfortable couches, loveseats, and beanbags were scattered across a fluffy white carpet. A giant home cinema screen occupied most of the far wall, while a popcorn machine stood near a doorway leading deeper into the apartment. Heavy blackout curtains covered the windows opposite the exit door, sealing the room away from the outside world.

I recognized every detail.

It was a perfect recreation of my father's home cinema in his high-rise condo, the same place I dreamed about back when Obora tried to trap me.

This time, I was here of my own volition, having integrated the space into my own sanctuary.

The screen was already active, with something playing across its surface.

Twelve-year-old Alice appeared holding a hunting rifle almost as tall as herself.

"Not this video…" I immediately groaned.

The video had always been my father's favorite, and he would show it to anyone who visited him.

The younger version of me stood in a field wearing oversized camouflage clothing while trying very hard to look composed. A deer wandered into view several dozen meters away.

Then came the shot, and the deer simply dropped.

Young Alice stared in complete shock while the man holding the camera started shaking with excitement. It was my father, laughing proudly as his young daughter managed to bring down a deer with her first shot.

The screen captured the exact moment twelve-year-old me turned toward him with wide eyes, desperately searching for his approval.

The memory hit me harder than I expected.

Back then, I would have done almost anything to make that man proud.

My father was always traveling somewhere for business, and after the divorce, he lived in a completely separate house, making him a rare presence in my life. Yet whenever he did appear, he spoiled me rotten with toys, gifts, vacation trips, and unique experiences.

Meanwhile, my mother had been the complete opposite.

She was always there, but she was cold, strict, and emotionally distant.

As a child, I chased every scrap of attention my father offered.

As an adult, I became more cynical. I started seeing his gifts as a way to one-up his ex-wife. The conflict between my parents became the defining feature of how I viewed them. Since both were wealthy, I even leveraged their mutual dislike of one another to finance my research trips from time to time.

It would probably be a long time before I saw either of them again, if I ever did.

They probably think I am dead.

I need to send them another dream message before I wake up.

As I was lost in thought, Nina's voice pulled me back.

"What was that weapon?"

I blinked.

Nina was staring at the screen, where my younger self was still jumping in celebration while holding the rifle over her head.

"Yeah, I am not opening that Pandora's box."

With a thought, I paused the memory. The image froze on my younger face.

I took Nina's hand and gently pulled her away from the screen toward the curtains.

"Come here."

Walking across the room, I pressed a button mounted beside the wall. The curtains slowly opened, and Nina gasped in amazement.

Beyond the glass stretched a nighttime cityscape. Thousands upon thousands of lights illuminated the darkness. Roads glowed like rivers of gold. Towering skyscrapers stretched into the sky. Cars moved continuously along the roadways while illuminated advertisements covered entire buildings. Far above, aircraft moved through the night, including a few helicopters and a couple of airplanes.

These were things that would have looked more magical than magic to anyone born in the Seven Realms.

Nina pressed both hands against the glass. Her eyes widened.

"This is much bigger than Hano."

"It is."

"Your city gets this big?"

"Only a couple of dozen cities on Earth are this large," I admitted. "Though this is not even close to the biggest."

Nina slowly turned toward me.

"It isn't?"

"You should see Tokyo," I said with a grin.

Well, she could see Tokyo. That was the whole point of creating this room.

I turned toward the giant screen and examined it. Finally, I spotted what I was looking for.

"Alexa, play drone footage of Tokyo at night."

A familiar voice answered immediately.

"Playing Tokyo, Japan in 4K Drone at Night."

Nina nearly jumped.

The screen behind us shifted. A moment later, an aerial view of Tokyo filled it.

The camera soared above endless oceans of lights. Entire districts stretched to the horizon. Trains moved between skyscrapers. Neon signs illuminated streets crowded with millions of people.

Nina stared in complete silence. The inventor looked genuinely overwhelmed.

Finally, she pointed at the speaker.

"Is your moving painting sentient?"

"No."

"It understood you."

"It follows simple instructions."

"It spoke."

"It is still not sentient."

Nina looked unconvinced.

I decided not to pursue the argument. Instead, another idea occurred to me.

"Alexa, show me a performance of Beethoven's Ninth Symphony."

If there was one thing Nina loved above anything else, it was musical instruments. They were what had put her on the path of invention.

The screen changed once again as a massive orchestra appeared. Dozens of musicians occupied the stage: Violins, Cellos, Trumpets, Flutes, and Clarinets. Instruments from nearly every family of music.

Nina immediately leaned forward. The performance had not even begun, yet she already looked fascinated.

Then the musicians started playing, and the girl's mouth slowly fell open. She watched every movement, staring at every instrument as though she were trying to commit them to memory.

Eventually, she pointed toward a musician seated near the center.

"What is that one called?"

I narrowed my eyes at the instrument.

"That is a bassoon."

"What does it do?"

"It makes sounds," I said in a deadpan voice.

"You know what I mean." Nina rolled her eyes. "How is it made? What sound does it make?"

I laughed.

"Alexa, show me a video of somebody crafting a bassoon."

The video changed again, but unfortunately, the result was less educational than I had hoped. The screen was filled with industrial machinery. Precision cutters moved automatically. Computer-guided equipment shaped wood with microscopic precision.

Nina looked increasingly confused.

"What is happening?"

"Yeah..." I scratched the back of my head. "This is not particularly helpful."

The machinery continued carving.

"Back on Earth, we automate a lot of manufacturing."

"Automate?"

I considered how to explain it.

"Imagine creating a golem that only performs one task. Maybe carving a piece of wood."

Nina nodded.

"Then imagine another golem that only drills holes."

She nodded again.

"And another that polishes the wood."

Understanding slowly appeared on her face.

"So instead of one craftsman doing everything," she summarized, "you have dozens of specialized golems, each doing one tiny part."

Nina watched the machinery again.

"But there is no art in that."

"There can be."

She looked skeptical.

"The art is often in designing the original object. Somebody still had to invent the instrument. Somebody still had to determine the dimensions. Somebody still had to figure out how to make it sound beautiful."

"Though plenty of people still prefer things made by hand." I shrugged.

That answer seemed to satisfy her.

The conversation quickly spiraled from there.

We abandoned videos in favor of diagrams, illustrations, and photographs. I showed her violins, harps, pianos, saxophones, clarinets, and dozens of other instruments. More importantly, I let her hear them individually rather than buried beneath the sound of an entire orchestra.

For the next hour, Nina sat completely captivated while Earth unfolded before her one memory at a time.

The funny thing was that she rarely focused on the things I expected.

Most people from the Seven Realms would probably have been distracted by the skyscrapers, airplanes, or computers. Nina certainly appreciated those things, but her attention always drifted toward the inventions themselves and how people had arrived at each idea.

Take the screen in front of us, for example. Nina did not ask about the television itself, but about the concept of a screen.

That led me all the way back to the seventeenth century, where I ended up explaining the first projector, the Magic Lantern. The Magic Lantern led to photography, photography led to film, film led to moving pictures, and moving pictures somehow turned into a discussion about frame rates and why a rapid sequence of still images could trick the human brain into perceiving motion.

The entire conversation felt absurd, but Nina followed every step of the chain with genuine enthusiasm.

"So animation is just thousands of drawings shown very quickly?" she asked.

"More or less."

"Somebody actually draws all of those?" The inventor looked mildly horrified.

"It used to be done that way, although modern methods have been developed since then."

At some point, I realized explaining animation would take longer than simply showing it to her.

The giant screen dominated the room. It had been created for exactly this purpose.

"Alexa, play episode one of Hibike! Euphonium."

The screen immediately responded.

The first few minutes revealed a slight problem. The characters spoke Japanese, a language Nina could not understand, while I understood only about half of what they were saying.

Fortunately, the anime was technically just a dream, and the Moon room was part of my sanctuary.

"Alexa, enable Common Language subtitles."

A strange sensation tugged at the edge of my awareness. It felt similar to spending mana, except the resource being consumed was the stuff of dreams, my Inspiration.

The subtitles appeared anyway. I checked my phone, and just as I had expected, the battery had dipped by a sliver. I honestly thought it would be more. When I tried accessing the internet before from inside my dorm room, the cost was always higher.

After some consideration, I realized that the Moon room could recreate anything already stored in my memory for almost no cost. Since I had already watched the anime, the sanctuary could reconstruct it effortlessly. Translating the dialogue into Common Language, however, required creating something new. The Moon room could still accomplish the task, but doing so consumed Inspiration. The more faithfully I recreated something, the cheaper it was. The more I demanded from the sanctuary, the more Inspiration it consumed.

As I was lost in my revelation, Nina was completely absorbed by the episode. Although the characters and story were largely lost on her, she focused on everything else instead.

Every few minutes, she asked another question. What was a euphonium? How did valves function? Why did schools have music clubs? Why did students all wear matching uniforms? How many children could fit inside a single school?

Each answer led to three more questions.

Fortunately, at the cost of a little Inspiration, I was able to split the screen, willing several windows to pop up.

Whenever I wanted to explain something, diagrams appeared beside the episode. Cross-sections of instruments, historical photographs, and audio recordings playing individual notes materialized on demand.

The screen started to feel less like a cinema and more like a sci-fi spacecraft bridge.

By the time we finished the third episode, Nina looked mentally exhausted.

The poor girl had probably experienced more new concepts during the last few hours of dreaming than I had during my first week after arriving in Hano.

She sat quietly on the couch, staring at the blank screen. Her wings shifted occasionally as she processed everything she had just learned.

Finally, she turned toward me.

"Alice?"

"Yeah?"

"Can I bring a guest next time?"

The question caught me completely off guard.

"A guest?"

She nodded.

"I mean, next time. Can I bring someone into your sanctuary?"

"You can do that?" I asked.

"You still do not have a gate connected to the Great Dream," she explained. "You would probably need to create a special room for that."

"But it should be doable if I invite people into my own sanctuary first," Nina continued. "From there, I could bring them here."

"Who do you plan on inviting?" I asked hesitantly.

"My childhood friend."

Nina immediately brightened as she started talking about her. The girl was apparently the same Songstress who had received Nina's first Creation-State mandolin. The way Nina described her made it obvious she cared about her a great deal.

"I want her to hear your world's music," Nina explained. "There are hundreds of instruments here that have never existed in the Seven Realms. She could pick one for me to make."

I hesitated.

My sanctuary contained much more than music.

It contained my memories of Earth. Some of that knowledge could become dangerous in the wrong hands.

"As long as you trust her, I trust her," I finally said. "I just do not want people hunting me because of what I know."

Nina's expression immediately softened.

"I would never allow that."

The certainty in her voice reassured me more than I expected.

The conversation reminded me of a promise I had made earlier that day.

"Speaking of friends," I said, "I promised Sergeant Yon I would ask you to work on something for us."

The inventor immediately sat up straighter. Nothing captured her attention faster than the possibility of designing something new.

I pointed toward the giant screen.

"Alexa, show me deadlift exercises."

The image changed instantly.

A heavily muscled man appeared, lifting a loaded barbell from the floor.

Nina stared for several seconds before slowly leaning forward.

As the video progressed, I explained deadlifts, squats, bench presses, shoulder presses, and rows. Unlike many Earth concepts, these were ideas Nina immediately understood. Strength training was universal, and the equipment itself was remarkably simple.

The challenge was scale. Those tools were designed for humans, but some Kindred people could wrestle the Hulk.

"Those are the designs we need," I explained. "The problem is the weight."

Nina studied the equipment for a few moments before nodding thoughtfully.

"The problem would be the bar. There is a limit to how much weight steel can carry before it bends or breaks."

The inventor remained silent for nearly a minute.

Then her eyes lit up.

"I know somebody who might be able to do it."

Nina apparently knew an enchanter who specialized in gravity monster cores harvested from creatures living inside the Colossus Dead Zone. Normally, the man created equipment that became lighter while keeping its other characteristics intact, such as sturdiness, but the enchantments could probably be reversed.

"Rather than adding weight to the sides, the whole bar would become heavier, making it even better balanced," she suggested.

We spent the next half hour sketching designs while discussing weight ranges, durability requirements, and safety mechanisms. Most of the equipment was surprisingly straightforward.

The difficulty came from machines like the lat pulldown, whose pulley mechanism would not survive users capable of casually lifting a ton.

"We can probably make the normal weights easily enough. The rest would need enchanted materials."

The confidence in her voice immediately reassured me.

"I will leave a few gold coins with you when we wake up. I want a working prototype as fast as possible, please," I said.

"I am always surprised by how casually you throw around gold," Nina chuckled.

"Most of it I found lying on the ground, literally," I said with a shrug.

"Seriously?"

"Yeah, seriously." I laughed. "I dug out a thousand-year-old cache of gold from some ruins. I even found fist-sized ancient fire cores buried with it."

"I completely forgot about those," I added thoughtfully. "Maybe Raik could use them."

"Some people have all the luck," she grumbled.

I just grinned.

Then I pointed toward the giant screen.

"Come on. That is enough work for tonight."

"So what are we doing?" Nina tilted her head.

My grin widened.

"If we are going to spend the night inside a dream cinema, then I should probably show you one of the greatest movies ever made."
