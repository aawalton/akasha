Despite everything that he had been told or felt about the old Knight, there was a part of Ezra that couldn’t help but hesitate to try to hit Dominick. He was a true old man, and if he wasn’t wearing armor, he could have fit in beside any other geezer who was too old to work and spent his days sitting outside of his home. All his life, Ezra had seen similar old men and women, and he’d also seen how fragile their lives were. On a particularly cold night or after receiving an ordinary fall, these men and women could end up terribly sick, permanently bedbound, or even dead.

So, when Ezra approached Dominick the first time, despite the constant screams of fear coming from his inner world, feeling the overwhelming pressure of the old man’s aura, Ezra could only bring himself to go about half as hard as he was capable of. Three steps ahead of him, Dominick’s shroud burst into existence, and Ezra couldn’t help but feel a certain familiarity towards that shroud. Just like what he had seen months ago, or whenever Jormun gave him visions, Dominick had a metal affinity.

When the yooperlite axe swung towards Dominick, the stone clanged ineffectually off of the shroud. Ezra’s hand ached, even with the half-assed swing, and a half-meter-long spike of metallic aura shot out towards Ezra’s weapon.

“I figured you’d do that,” Dominick’s voice didn’t carry any frustration or disappointment, which was all that kept Ezra from losing himself in a series of apologies, questions, and incoherent excuses. “I appreciate that you’re the type of man who isn’t going to try to beat up a random old guy, so I gave you this opportunity. You attacked me without using any aura, which, if you’re trying to not kill a defenseless old man, is a good start. However, I’m not a defenseless old man. I’m a Knight, and I have been for decades. My body is failing me, but I still have many years left in me before I have to worry at all about some young buck Adept like you.

“Now,” he continued, settling back into his chair and gesturing for Ezra to do the same, “one last thing. How much do you actually understand about how the aural shroud works?”

“It protects you and makes all of your attacks stronger,” Ezra answered.

“That’s true, but you need to better understand the specifics,” Dominick said. His aura hadn’t faded, and his shroud remained shining around him. “For as long as your inner world’s reserves can continue to maintain your shroud, you are entirely invulnerable to any attacks that are not fueled by aura or magic. Any attacks you sustain will drain your inner world’s reserves, but they won’t do anything to you until you either release your hold on your shroud or your inner world is exhausted. Considering how long I’ve been a Knight and how much I’ve developed my inner world, my reserves would hold up to you attacking as hard as you can until your hands are bloody, and I’ll  still be just fine.”

“So what happens if somebody attacks you with aura, then?” Ezra asked, coming closer to observe the Knight’s shroud. Unlike the ones he had seen from others, Michel, Chloe, and Basil, there was no fluctuation in Dominick’s shroud. It appeared almost like a spiritual form of the man himself, made of faintly shining aura.

“Then it’s better that I defend myself, if I can.” As Dominick spoke, a smile quirked at the corner of his lips. “If you were to attack me with aura, I could still defend myself and keep you from harming me, even without raising a finger. However, that would become very demanding on my inner world’s reserves, and on top of that, that’s largely only possible because I’m two tiers above you and my inner world’s affinity is steel. My aura, by nature, is defensive, and would stop you from being able to pierce my shroud. If it were someone with flame, light, poison, or darkness affinity, then you’d probably be able to land a blow on them, even if it wouldn’t do much damage.”

Ezra blinked in surprise and couldn’t help but glance at Jormun, who floated just beside Dominick. The grizzled ghost nodded once in agreement as Dominick continued speaking.

“This is the last thing before we start your training. To make sure that you believe and understand me, I want you, right now, to try your absolute best to land a hit on me by using your aura.”

The hesitation that Ezra had felt towards attacking Dominick had bled away by now, and he nodded once as he took his shield off, took his axe in both hands, and stepped closer to the still-sitting Dominick. At his command, aura flooded his body, pushing him to move, to do anything, and he didn’t hesitate to do so. Twisting with his whole body, Ezra slammed the head of his axe towards Dominick. The glowing flecks within the stone head glowed brightly and left trails in the air as the weapon smashed into Dominick’s left side.

This time, the Knight’s shroud reacted before Ezra’s weapon made contact. A shining section of aura, about the size of a palm, jutted out about ten centimeters away from the calmly sitting old man’s body. Ezra’s axe smacked against the aural defense, and both of his hands went numb as his fingers suddenly stopped following his will. His axe fell from his fingers, and, with the greater familiarity with his own body that Ezra had gained over the past months, he quickly realized that it wasn’t just the force of the rebound that caused it to happen. Something about the protections that Dominick’s aura provided to him also harmed Ezra.

“So, you understand why I’m so willing to let you fight me.” Again, Dominick’s grin was frustratingly self-assured, but instead of upsetting Ezra, the thought of himself being able to do that brought a wide smile to his face.

“Now, try it again,” Dominick said, a certain tone to his voice that Ezra didn’t understand.

He followed the command and very quickly came to understand what Dominick was getting at.

As Ezra swung at the older man, Dominick stood up and swept his practice, blunted halberd through the air. The head of the weapon caught under the head of Ezra’s axe and yanked it from his hands. Then Dominick stepped forward and kicked Ezra in the gut. Through his heavy armor, the blow struck like a club, and Ezra felt his breakfast threaten to come out as he tumbled across the ground.

“Come on, hit me again.”

At that, Ezra understood that they were, finally, entering the training that Chloe had tried to prepare him for. Instead of rushing forward without any second thought, Ezra kept a close eye on the old man as he reached down and grabbed his shield. As he strapped it onto his left arm, Dominick’s devious smile became ever so slightly more appreciative and he kicked the axe towards the younger man. It was almost worse that he was being so permissive, but Ezra was allowed to equip himself in peace. Then Dominick gestured for him to approach again.

Remembering Chloe’s counsel to be cautious, but decisive, Ezra waited a second, took two steps forward as a feint, and paused a half beat. Though Dominick didn’t react to the feint, Ezra swept his path to the right while keeping his shield at the ready and his axe prepared to help defend himself. Dominick held his polearm at the ready, and as Ezra grew closer, swept it down decisively. The trained Adept raised his shield to deflect the attack, which he managed to do successfully.

“Good!” Dominick smiled widely. “You at least know how to use the shield.”

As he spoke, the old Knight twisted his grip as he took a large step back. The bottom hook of his weapon caught the edge of Ezra’s shield, even as the Adept adjusted his grip to try to get the halberd to slip off. As soon as the old man had sunk his grip in deep, he further adjusted his stance to allow him to slide his weapon down to the bottom edge of the circular shield. Then, as Ezra desperately tried to escape the lock, the more experienced man bodily lifted him into the air before smashing him into the ground.

A large cloud of dust flew up all around as the breath was forcefully pushed from Ezra’s lungs. He wheezed in pain as Dominick looked down at him.

“The longer you stay down, the more push-ups I’m having you do,” was the closest thing to pity that his trainer gave him.

With a pained groan, Ezra forced himself to his feet as the old man gestured for him to come at him once again. Hopefully, he’d be able to land a blow on Dominick before too long.

Despite using a shield, Ezra never got close to actually blocking any of the old man’s attacks. Whenever he let the axehead fall on his shield, Dominick reacted perfectly to slide his weapon into the next attack, whether that was with the spearhead on the top, the axehead on the side, or even the butt end of the haft. Regardless of what Ezra prepared himself for, he met only frustration.

Once, Dominick let him get close, close enough to think that he could at least swing at the Knight.

Instead, Ezra quickly learned just how quick the old man was. The halberd slipped past his shield as Ezra tried to press the attack. The old man let go with one hand, letting the weapon slip down to the side. Then, he swung a painfully fast jab into Ezra’s ribs, the metallic aura protecting the old man’s fist as he did so. Ezra‘s ribs groaned in response to the hit, but the old man was too capable to accidentally break the younger trainee.

With wheezing breaths and while fighting to  keep from puking, Ezra stepped back, having learned not to simply take the hits that came his way. If he didn’t react, then he was going to keep getting hit. The whipping sound of Dominick’s leg through the air towards his stomach was a further reminder that the old man wasn’t going to pull his punches. Or kicks, as the case may be.

Finally, frustrated, Ezra created his pseudo-shroud, his movements growing sharper and faster as he pretended to be more wounded than he actually was. He stepped forward, his shield at the ready, and as soon as he was just outside of Dominick’s reach, he hurried forward. Wind whipped around him and sent sand flying in every direction. With as fast as he could go, Ezra sidestepped Dominic’s first attack as he swung his axe at the old man’s exposed side. The weapon grew close, but the shroud reacted, smashing away the attack and, with the strange aspect of the aura, made Ezra‘s arm go numb from his elbow to his fingertips.

“This is bullshit,” he grunted, unable to keep his mouth closed. By the way that Dominick‘s smile grew in response to his outburst, that was the wrong thing to say.

“You think that the shroud is unfair when sparring against me. So, let’s not use any aura.”

Ezra started to say something about how it was unsafe for him to spar with the old man without using aura, but Dominick wasn’t having it. The Knight stood squarely in the center of the sand pit, holding his weapon with both hands and his face severe.

“We will spar using the same equipment we already have. You will not use aura and I will only use the barest hint of aura to ensure that I don’t accidentally hurt myself. However, in exchange for my lack of reliance upon my inner world and shroud, I will stop going easy on you. You need to understand just how important training is.”

Going off of how Jormun was laughing at his expense, and the fact that Ezra already suspected he’d royally screwed himself over, he wasn’t looking forward to the way that the old man was carrying himself. Then, the practice resumed.

Moving just as cautiously as before, Ezra tried to approach carefully, stopping just outside of Dominick’s reach. He tried to stop there, but, different to before, Dominick didn’t simply wait for his approach. With a single step, Dominick was close enough to start swinging, and the halberd swept towards Ezra’s head. He raised his shield, but Dominick was prepared and pulled his weapon back. Ezra’d over committed, and Dominick smashed the practice weapon into Ezra’s knee. It popped painfully and Ezra’s weight betrayed him. He fell in a clatter of metal and pain, and Dominick grunted in disappointment.

“Sorry. Put your knee out. Fortunately, I had Sylvie set the morning aside.”

Sylvie was, it turned out, a healer, and after a few short seconds tending to Ezra’s knee, Dominick nodded.

“Now, again.”
