Ryland’s priority at the surprising development of their current circumstance was ensuring that they all remained safe no matter what happened. He had not foreseen the arrival of the Demiurge, but it certainly made sense in hindsight.

The Guardian had repeatedly said that Ryland wouldn’t be necessary here. Now, he knew why.

“[Eternal Bastion],” Ryland incanted.

The spell circle with his Soul Glyph of Aegis as the Centreglyph activated on everyone. They all looked down in surprise, a transparent barricade erecting itself around them all.

Well, all save Harker, who almost looked affronted at Ryland. But the man had nothing to complain about. He had chosen his destiny already, sealed his Fate with the actions he had taken and the choices he had made thus far. Regret wasn’t going to change the past.

There was no time for him to complain, however. The Demiurge really was appearing amidst them.

“Is it really him?” Ophelia shouted. The incredibly powerful figure coming down had fomented a storm, gale-force winds ripping away all sounds before they could travel to their targets. “Why is he here? What’s going on, Ryland?”

She wasn’t necessarily sure what exactly Mavar had come down here for. In not so many words, Ophelia was confused why the ruler of Rhel had chosen to act now instead of earlier.

A valid question for any outside observer.

“Because he was afraid,” Ryland said.

Zevo turned to him with a glare almost as heavy as Harker’s. “The Demiurge is never afraid. Please do not insinuate such mundane emotions to Kindly Mavar, sub-Academ Ryland.”

“Your naked fanaticism does not do your position any favours, High Academ,” Ryland said.

“Naked fanaticism? Your mischaracterization of me standing against falsehoods doesn’t do you any favours, sub-Academ.”

Ryland shook his head with a tut. “This isn’t the time for argument. But the fact remains that the Demiurge chose this specific moment to act. Not a moment earlier. Think on that instead of arguing.”

“He has his reasons.”

“Indeed, he does.”

The insinuation didn’t escape Zevo, who continued to glower, much as he had done when tackling Ryland in the Crypts that day. It was almost wondrous the effect Mavar’s Glyph of Unity had on people. Zevo had seemed like he was starting to favour Ryland, to see him in a positive, respectful light.

All of which had to take a back seat because the Demiurge was almost among them. The effect of his arrival would normally have made existing in the same region basically untenable, but Ryland’s barrier was ensuring that they all remained almost completely unaffected.

“I sort of understand what you mean,” Ophelia said in the same loud voice. “But I still don’t understand why the Demiurge is acting now. Surely you could take care of things, Ryland.” She hurriedly looked at him, her eyes beginning to squint in the growing intensity of the descending light. “Not that I’m asking you to, of course.”

Ryland smiled at her, though he wondered if she could see it in their current conditions. “Kindly Mavar clearly wishes to enact something far more… final than what I probably would have gone for. You see, the trap wasn’t simply meant for the High Academ or even the Guardian of Rhel. It certainly wasn’t meant for me, considering the invitation to leave that I received.”

Ophelia’s eyes widened. “Then, this whole time, he was trying to capture the Demiurge?”

She whirled to Goodman Harker, who was emulating everyone by staring straight up at the power about to join them. Nevertheless, he was paying enough attention to his surroundings to have heard what was going on.

“Yes,” he said. “Yes, I was targeting the Demiurge. You all are nothing next to him. And he deserves it more than anyone else. That tyrant, that monster, that—”

High Academ Zevo had whirled at the name-calling with murderous rage, though Suthor was once again blocking him from acting. Yet again, they never got the chance to resolve their actual situation.

The Demiurge landed.

Pillars of burning light lashed down from the high atmosphere, spreading farther and farther across the entire continent-sized shelf of ice. The sky split apart with the noise of a hundred thousand lightning bolts flashing at once, unveiling the arrival of a gargantuan orb of power that truly could have birthed a small solar system with its irradiating heat and magnificence.

“That thing is going to kill us all,” Harker said mutely. “It will wipe out this entire side of Rhel…”

“No, it won’t,” Zevo said. He turned, almost reluctantly, to Ryland. “Because he knows we’re protected.”

Ophelia’s expression wasn’t satisfied. It was clear she still had a great deal of questions and the like. But her sheer awe at the power on display was taking her ability to talk away. “I’m almost starting to wonder if we finally found someone who can give you a run for your money, Ryland.”

Zevo looked between the two of them like the idea of Ryland standing against the Demiurge was preposterous. But he too said nothing. For, like the rest of them present, he had seen just what Ryland was capable of.

For his part, Ryland was just curiously observing Mavar’s spell. [Descending Sun] right out of the gate. Mavar was definitely not taking any prisoners here.

Unfortunately—or fortunately, Ryland wasn’t certain—the Demiurge’s spell never made landfall. The gargantuan orb of burning light never reached the surface of Rhel, though it certainly got close enough with how everything was turning to over-pressurized steam that had churned into a condensed yet continental storm.

Just before Mavar’s spell could reach them, the mastermind behind this whole operation had decided to show himself. The storm of steam turned so incandescent that it seemed as though the sun had reached them.

Then it rapidly shifted into a titanic giant rising higher than a mountain, ascending with the rapidity of a reverse lightning bolt. Its gargantuan arms rose high, stopping the descending star like a man lifting a boulder. Except, the man was big enough to crush mountains underfoot and the boulder would put most moons to shame.

“I think I’d be getting vertigo,” Ophelia murmured, slack-jawed with disbelief at the scale of powers on display. “If I could actually see through all this blasted light.”

“Just be thankful we aren’t being eradicated entirely…” Zevo murmured.

He had a very fair point. Everything around them was a riot of destruction. Where the continent-spanning shelf of ice had been was nothing but mana taken to a livid stage, churning with untameable ferocity and determined to end in pure annihilation. Pillars of light continued crashing down from the heavens like the Great Gods smiting Rhel itself.

All the while, none of them were affected. Ryland had extended [Eternal Bastion] over Harker too, but with a few additional Subglyphs at the same time. Now, even though he remained protected, he was also entirely frozen in place.

Something he couldn’t really complain about when Ryland was saving his life.

Ryland himself was busy observing the second spell. He had no trouble recognizing Mavar’s handiwork, having experienced it himself beforehand. But the second spell…

“It’s actually stopping him,” Zevo said with shocked wonder. “That titanic being has actually stopped the Demiurge’s power! A-a Transcendent’s power!”

Ryland didn’t blame him for his disbelief. It was no small secret that the Demiurge of Rhel was not just the strongest figure on this Realm, but one of the most powerful across the entirety of the Realms. This proved they were facing supremely powerful opposition.

But that last bit caught even Ryland off-guard just a little.

Transcendent.

Ryland had no doubt that Mavar was strong enough to be considered at the peak of what the Grand Frame allowed individuals to achieve. Of course, he was one of those rare beings who had climbed to the very pinnacle of power offered by the Grand Frame. There were only a mere handful of those beings in all of the cosmos. Not a single one of them were on Vyrd.

Rhel possessing one of their own was why the other allied Realms were keen to bring them into the fold of the Accords. Under their charter, the wildcard would be tamed. Would no longer be a threat to their politics. And, in worst-case-scenarios, he’d be an asset for their arsenal.

Of course, the truth was that the Grand Frame was limiting. Just as Ryland himself knew that reaching the limit of the Grand Frame didn’t mean his actual power ended there, so too was he aware that Transcendent was only the bare minimum of what Mavar could be considered as.

The real question, the one that was bugging Ryland, was just how long had the Grand Frame been on Rhel? For it certainly hadn’t existed the last time he had been here.

But Zevo was speaking of it with incredible familiarity. In fact, no less familiarity than a Vyrdian or anyone else from the Accorded Realms, who had known about the Grand Frame practically from their births. So, had it been present on Rhel for decades now as well, if not a century or more?

The clash between the Demiurge and their adversary grew titanic enough to hook even Ryland’s attention for the time being.

With [Descending Sun] at least temporarily stopped, Mavar had to figure out an alternative to destroy his opponent. This turned out to be an attempt to wrest control of the storm that the clash of powers had unleashed.

The attempt reminded Ryland of facing Sokolo Dyron, whose soul magic tried to instantiate a simulacrum of a soul into anything he wished to manipulate. Mavar’s tactic wasn’t dissimilar. Just instead of a Soul Glyph of Spirit, the Demiurge was using his Soul Glyph of Unity to bring the very atmosphere under his heel.

In a blink, they were surrounded by a storm of not air, but Glyphs. Millions of Soul Glyphs populated the space around them, each one trying to control a pocket of reality to force out the mage behind the colossal titan.

Air twisted faster and faster into drilling cyclones. Lightning formed jagged bolts that seared across the space. Water condensed into projectiles that could puncture metal with ease.

And through that maelstrom, Ryland could see it. Could see them.

Gracious.

It could be no one else, going by that splash of colour traversing through space high above. Ryland had to give them props for it. Even he hadn’t spotted Gracious’s ascent through the chaos at first, not even with [Omniscience] active. He wondered whose idea it was. Gracious the First wasn’t as bold as Gracious the Second.

For it was quite obvious they had no intention of conducting a long-ranged battle against the Demiurge and wanted to take the battle straight to Mavar.

Ryland wished them luck.

Admittedly, they were capable of quite a few things. This was evidenced when Gracious’s climb turned bright and intense, cutting through the storm that Mavar had unleashed like a reverse meteor.

Only for a gigantic arm of white and gold energy to materialize in an instant, catching and stopping the meteor with an inescapable grip.

“He played him!” Zevo said with near fanatical wonder. “The Great Demiurge showed that stupid monster that mere might is nothing in the face of true ingenuity.”

They certainly couldn’t disagree that Mavar had tricked Gracious. The mages from the Flourinus Order were now trapped for good. Mavar raised the gargantuan, mountain-sized arm higher, punching it through the upper atmosphere of Rhel, tearing a hole that revealed the distant stars.

“It’s over!” Zevo said.

“Th-this cannot be,” Harker said. “No!”

Riotous energy was writhing through the arm to assault the mage it had trapped within it. Ryland wasn’t quite sure what Subglyphs Mavar was using, but he had clearly studied his opponent. Whatever was in there prevented Gracious from calling forth a Wound Corridor to escape.

The energy started flashing. Lightning larger than country-spanning rivers coursed up the humongous arm’s length. They flashed blue and red, their brilliance standing out starkly against the revelation of the cosmos’s eternal night-sky.

Then, the lightning struck.

If [Descending Sun] had been blindingly brilliant so far, if Harker had glowed like a star when he had channelled all that stolen mana, then this was an order of magnitude more intense.

Power shrieked out with an echo that had to reverberate across the whole expanse of the planet of Rhel, buffeting Ryland and his party where they floated near its surface. Light flared out strong enough to turn everything so white, Ryland was forced to place [Omniscience] on the rest of his little group to stop them from screaming at potentially going blind.

Waves of power curtained out of the point of impact in destructive auroras, wiping out the churning storm, eradicating the last of the Continental Icedrift, turning the planet itself inhospitable for scores of leagues all around them.

Which it already was, in all honesty.

Through the shrieking of Mavar’s power, another noise joined the fray. Gracious’s pained cry added to the din, adding another layer of distortion to the air around them. The death throes of the Order mages were unfortunately never not dramatic.

“I should be dead a hundred times over by now,” Ophelia murmured.

Ryland had to admit that his barrier spell was putting in a good deal of work. And it was precisely because of Ryland’s barriers that Mavar could carelessly withhold all restraint when it came to dealing with Gracious.

Harker fell to his knees, staring with wide, frightened eyes at the insanity above them. He looked a little comical since there was no longer any ground for them to stand on. His body slowly started tipping over and his centre of gravity shifted. “If I could have claimed even a fraction of that sort of power…”

“Save your sacrilegious talk for later, mongrel,” Zevo said. “You aren’t even worth witnessing the magnificence taking place.”

Ryland ignored them, frowning up at the continued detonation of world-ending bolts that the Demiurge was casting. His attack hadn’t ended yet, which meant Gracious was still alive. Still holding on. Which meant he was expecting some form of relief.

Like the reinforcements that materialized a moment later.

Ryland thought about intercepting the new arrival with [Translocate], but the truth was that the new power had appeared right in the exact same spot that Gracious occupied. Right on top of them, there was a second Order mage.

One who immediately proceeded to simply consume the magic that Mavar was blasting out.

The Demiurge was clearly infuriated by the interruption. Renewed sorcery assaulted Gracious and the second Order mage, an avalanche of sky-shattering magic beaming through the space.

“Wait here,” Ryland said.

“Where are you going?” Ophelia asked with some alarm.

“There’s another one of them up there.” It was Zevo who had answered. “But I don’t understand where the second one came from…”

“Indeed. So before anything occurs—”

“It isn’t needed!” Zevo turned to Ryland with the same imperious look that the Guardian had sported. “The Great Demiurge can’t be bested just because there’s one more of these curs!”

“Is that really your opinion, High Academ? Or are you simply parroting what you must?”

“I—” Zevo’s face underwent a wild flood of emotions before he settled back on anger. “How dare you level such insinuations against me? My thoughts and feelings are my own!”

Ryland tutted, sensing the pointlessness of an argument. “Don’t worry. I’ve made sure you will remain safe with or without me. [Everlife Spectre]. [Translocate].”

Zevo tried to say something, but Ryland had already taken action. He didn’t actually head into that maelstrom of power himself. That would be foolish. Instead, he shoved his shining skeletal arm through the rift he had created and grabbed the gargantuan mouth attached to Gracious’s shoulder.

“Chivalrous,” he said. “You seem smaller than the last time. Are you a new one?”

The Order mage just kept eating magic. Well, new or old, some things just never seemed to change.

Ryland was about to rip the enormous maw away from the battle, but he was stopped. Strands of burning, flickering, electrified gold wrapped around his limb, then began pushing him away.

Hmm. It seemed the Demiurge really didn’t want him to participate.
