Fighting a team of Wayfarers was frustrating. Even with a cohort of specters running interference for her, Ves found it impossible to put any of her foes down for good. Her blood whips could batter through their defenses, but every time any of them started to falter, one of their healers would restore them. Going for the healers didn't work, either. Even Ves's impressive speed wasn't enough to get her past the elven archer guarding their flanks, and charging through the tank just wasn't an option. Life magic. Ves thought, clicking her tongue in annoyance and launching a trio of sanguine projectiles at the elven woman in the back. Disgusting.

Life force existed to be consumed, not channeled. No other energy could let one advance so far, so quickly. Mortals shrunk away from those ideas, but demons did not. That was something her people and Vylith's had in common. Maybe that was why the Blood Queen had so readily allied with them.

Two of her blood shards shattered against the elf's arcane defenses but the third one penetrated. It slammed into her shoulder, ejecting a spray of blood that quickly flowed through the air to join the crimson serpents that spun around the vampire's arms in thick cords. Before she could follow up with another attack, though, the armored man appeared between them. He moved with a speed that belied his bulk, warding her away with wide sweeps of his curved blade. Ves was forced to back off, having learned early in the battle not to underestimate his strength. He didn't pursue, forced to turn his attention back to the specters that were moving to harass his companions.

It was a stalemate. How boring. The vampire could have taken any two of them even without her summoned minions, but four was too many. She could hold her own against them, but Ves wasn't going to be taking them down. She'd just have to wait for Vylith to dispose of the succubus. After that, they'd be able to take the rest of her party apart at their leisure.

But what was taking her so long? The battle had lasted for several minutes now. Vylith was an elite wraith demon, a tek'kalla mage and a C-rank Wayfarer shouldn't be giving her any trouble at all. Annoyed, she used a length of blood to whip a pair of arrows out of the air and then dodged a bolt of fire shot by the bard. The edge of the blast caught her side, searing through her and charring her flesh. The vampire snarled, her eyes flashing red. That miserable little-

The world shook. This wasn't one of the dimensional tremors echoing through the floor from the far reaches of the Labyrinth, but something much closer. So close that merely detecting the massive surge of magical energy with her aura sent a lance of white-hot pain lancing through her temples. There was a flash of violet light, a rush of immense power, and a sound like a dozen thunderclaps. It was over as quickly as it had begun. The dark forest fell still, the battle momentarily forgotten. Ves's vampiric eyes easily pierced the gloom, and the sight before her made her stop dead in her tracks.

Part of the forest was gone.

There was no other way to describe it. What had moments before been a dense copse of trees and thick underbrush was now simply… gone. In its place now lay scorched, blackened earth, mirror smooth and covered by a light dusting of fine, sand-like powder, as if everything that had once occupied that space had been reduced to its constituent particles. And standing in front of the swath of devastation, crackling energy flowing around her palm, was Jade.

That sight, and the echo of power still reverberating through her aura, frightened Ves like nothing she'd ever felt.

All thoughts of vengeance left her mind. She was running before her concious mind even realized what was happening. Never before had she seen such destructive power, and she definitely wasn't waiting around to find out what having it directed at her felt like. It was time to withdraw. Trees passed in a dark blur as she darted through the forest, rushing in the direction of the nearest node as fast as her legs could-

There was a sharp crack, a flash of pain, and everything went black.

Once Tara went down, Fenrin knew Jade was going to use the divine relic. That didn't make the sudden explosion any less of a surprise. The effect created a shockwave that sent him crashing into Naomi, causing them both to fall to the ground in a confused tumble. The half-elven archer regained his feet as quickly as he could, grabbing his bow and notching another arrow, ready to take aim. Only, the attackers were gone. He could just make out the rapidly retreating forms of the two surviving specters, neither of which seemed interested in sticking around after the eruption of divine power. As for the vampire…

"There!" Luis shouted, pointing with his curved sword.

His sharp, elven eyes cut through the layered shadows beneath the moonlit forest canopy better than his companions, so it only took him a moment to discern Ves's retreating figure. She moved like a shadow, barely visible against the curtain of night surrounding her.

"Fenrin, if she gets away…"

Luis didn't have to finish the sentence. If the vampire escaped, and her collusion with the wraith demon stretched further than tonight's battle, they would be in serious trouble. For their plan to confront Lord Vastrozun with the relic of Destruction to work, they needed secrecy. She couldn't be allowed to tell anyone.

Fenrin grunted, overdrawing his bow and pouring mana into the shaft. Lightning arced along the arrow's length and a sheath of wind coalesced around it, coming to a pointed tip on the arrowhead.

"No. You. Don't." Fenrin grunted, doing his utmost to resist the sudden fatigue of low mana. He could barely see the vampire. Only moments had passed, and she was already more than fifty paces out of the small clearing that had been their battlefield. If she made it any further, they'd never catch her.

He wasn't going to let that happen.

The bowstring snapped, lightning erupted from his bow, and the arrow exploded towards his target as the spell took hold.

Hurricane Bolt

Rating: C level spell

Cost: Moderate mana cost

Duration: Instant

Cooldown: One hour

Effect: Imbue an arrow with wind and storm magic, unleashing it upon any target you can see. The more lightning energy you store in the arrow, the higher the damage. The more wind energy you store in the arrow, the better the guidance.

Base damage: High

Maximum damage: Extreme

Base guidance: Moderate

Maximum guidance: Unerring

Fenrin's mana pool wasn't the largest, but most of his spells and talents consumed little of it. His newest spell was the exception, and he poured everything he had left into it. The lightning arrow disappeared into the darkness, a streak of blue-white light twisting its way through a maze of trees and branches. He held his breath, waiting for the-

Ding.

An experience notification popped up in his combat log. Fenrin breathed a sigh of relief.

"Get her?" Luis asked. He had moved to stand guard over Siora, who was already tending to Tara's wounds.

"I did. Was a little worried for a-"

He was interrupted by another notification.

Quest Complete: You Had Time to Say That?

You could have put some more effort into that one.

Objective: Deliver a snappy one-liner immediately before defeating an enemy. Rewards scale with the strength of the enemy and the quality of the one-liner.

Reward: 5 crescent coins.

Fenrin blinked in surprise, automatically catching the handful of coins that appeared in front of him along with the message. When had he…

"Ugh." He groaned, pinching the bridge of his nose. "I forgot about that."

Five coins? Five? He'd never even heard of such a meager quest reward.

"I don't suppose I could get a do over?" He asked into the air. Who knew, maybe Lady Elaria was listening.

"What was that?" Luis asked.

"Never mind…"

The Labyrinth had a strange sense of humor.

To Jade's relief, Siora had Tara back on her feet in no time.

"I'm fine. Really." The tek'kalla assured them, waving away Naomi's hand and hauling herself back to her feet. The white panels of her conjured armor were cracked and streaked with blood, but her face had regained its normal color. At least, Jade thought it had, it was a little hard to tell in the torch light.

After a brief search and a few minutes of tense waiting, it became clear that no one else was waiting to attack them. That only left the question of how they'd been found. As best as they could determine, it was probably some kind of advanced tracking magic. A fair number of powerful divination spells existed, and exactly what magic powerful demons could perform was anyone's guess. If it had been the wraith demon who'd found them, though, she wouldn't have another chance. Jade surveyed the devastated forest, a chill crawling up her spine.

I did that.

The divine relic pulsed in her hand. Jade looked down at it, surprised to see the dark crystal shard still clutched in her fist. Violet flashes arced within its shadowy depths, as if eager to be unleashed again. She quickly tucked it away, wiping her hands on her cloak as if to rid them of something distasteful.

"Everything okay?"

It was Naomi. The bard approached, still plucking out a healing melody on her guitar. The soft heat of life energy filled Jade, washing away the chill. She sighed.

"I'm fine. It's just…"

She looked back at the patch of ground that, a few minutes ago, had been a thriving forest. Of the wraith demon, nothing remained.

"Did it hurt you?"

"What? Oh, no." Jade shook her head. "The goddess was right, the relic is stable. It's…" she was about to say 'safe to use', but the words died on her lips. Pure, cosmic power was not something she should have access to. Not something anyone should have access to. But here she was, a splinter of divinity held in her pocket. And with it, the authority to decide what did and did not deserve to exist.

"I like that part of you."

"Huh?" It took Jade a moment to realize she'd trailed off and had been blankly staring through Naomi for the past several seconds. She shook her head. "What?"

"You don't like having too much power."

"I don't like anyone having too much power."

"You do better with it than most."

"I'm not so-"

"Trust me, Jade." Naomi stopped playing and gently placed her hand on Jade's. Only then did she notice it was trembling. "You've been given a lot of dangerous powers over the past few months. That scared me, at first."

"Are you saying I'm not intimidating?" She tried to say it as a joke. It came out more as a plea.

"You can be intimidating, but you aren't frightening. The Labyrinth could've done a lot worse when it decided to offer you that race."

"Naomi…"

"It could have done a lot better, of course, but we can't have everything."

Jade punched her lightly in the shoulder and they both laughed. There was tension in it, the gravity of their task hung over them like a guillotine, but there was warmth, too. The weight on Jade's shoulders eased somewhat. Her friends were with her. They trusted her. They would do this together.

Assuming no more assassins were waiting for them.

Four more uses. Jade thought, focusing on the relic once more. No… that would be pushing it. More like three.

There was no way to recharge the divine relic. They needed to ration it. There was no telling if one discharge would be enough when they confronted Vastrozun. But at least she now knew how it worked.

"It was just the two of them, plus their summons." Tara's words broke into her train of through. She swiped away a screen projected from her suit that looked sort of like an old-fashioned radar display. "No one else is within a few miles. Not even any monsters."

"We might not know how they found us, but they won't be coming back." Luis said. "The battle log says we got both of them."

"We should get out of here anyway." Jade suggested. "In case they have more friends somewhere."

"Tara's home?" Naomi asked.

"We should stop by Illysport before we leave." Siora suggested, sounding a little shaken. They'd emerged from the battle whole, but it had been an unpleasant surprise. "Stock up on supplies."

"Good idea."

Jade took a deep, relaxing breath as the others conversed. When she finished, her hands were still again.

Keep looking forward.

That's what she had to do. The finish line was in sight. One last push, and then it would be over. They could all go home. Part of her wanted to do that right now, consequences be damned. But she couldn't. She wouldn't leave the fate of her family and friends in the hands of someone else. Not when she had the power to change it herself.

As they departed, traversing the nighttime woodlands towards the nearest node, a blinking icon finally caught Jade's attention. It was one last system notification. She'd finally hit level 30.
