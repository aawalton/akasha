There were a number of problems that needed to be solved, not the least of which was packing everything and finding somewhere to go. After a brief flight south to secure that I returned to Icehome, to find what looked like a hive of activity. People were not simply sitting in their warm room but rushing about, putting packs together, stacking sleds, and deciding what they were taking.

Not everything was going of course, not everything could go. There was a simple fact that a lot of things were simply too large to be practical, or unneeded, or simply not worth the effort. Some of it was being put into rooms in case anyone ever came back, some was being broken down for materials for more sleds, because man these people had a lot of stuff. The buildup of generations was being wiped away before my eyes.

“We've got some things we're not sure on,” Liet said.

“What's that?” I inquired.

“The heating crystals, the big ones. Frankly we're not sure if we'll need them where we're going, and there's no good way to move them, but people are hesitant to leave them behind. It's... they've been with us for so long, it's like losing an old friend in a way, the very foundation of our society.”“You won't need the main one, and it's massive. I'd encourage people to take the smaller ones, call them family heirlooms or something, but the main one needs to stay. Not even sure how you'd get it out as I'm pretty sure much of Icehome was built around it,” I said, nodding. “Or built up after it was put in place, who knows.”

I joined him as we moved through the halls and tunnels in the ice, moving through the pale corridors as we made it to the actual heart of the hive. There a number of elves were gathered around, some getting warm, many others looking around the room, this way and that, trying to find a way to move the gigantic crystal.

“Elder!” one of them called. “Have you and his Majesty come with a solution?”“We're leaving it,” Liet said in response.

This was not the words that the people here wanted to hear, and immediately there was argument. Men and women began to gripe and yell, hurling a mix of insults and pleas.

“It's been with us forever, and it's so beautiful, we can't just leave it,” a woman shouted, eyes almost watering. “No more than we can leave parents or children here!”“Enough,” I finally said, voice empowered until it sent a wave through the room. “It is too large, there is no place for it, no way to move it, and no reason to do so. It has lived here for as long as anyone knows, and I suspect it was here when Icehome was founded. This is its place, we should let it have this one. We must respect the old ways, but as well find new ones, new methods to live and prosper.”Reading on this site? This novel is published elsewhere. “Then tell me about it,” I told them. “Not now, before we leave, we all gather here and everyone shares their memories of this place. Speak of it as if it were a lost relative, remember it, share those with others. Yet like those who've died we must move on, we must continue, for this place will no longer work for you and all of you know it, all of you agree with it. When it's over we'll leave it here, sealed in this chamber so that should any return in the future they may find it again. Perhaps we should write those memories down for anyone who finds it, so that they won't ever be lost, even to those who've never met any of us.”

“That...” began the woman who'd complained, but caught herself. “I would like that.”

As they disappeared the elder leaned closer to me.

“Surprised anyone agreed to that.”“Basically a funeral for your longtime home, ritual helps people sometimes.”There was some conversation as things were packed and it was decided that the stories people told would be carved into the benches and tiers around the crystal itself, which I thought was a lovely touch. Perhaps in a few thousand years someone would find them, and if we were lucky, they would still speak out language. If not, well, I took a few moments to carve a primer for it on one wall, with magic and the fact that I could perfectly visualize it there was no reason not to.

“Why are you doing that?” Liet asked.

“In case the language should change, this shows what it is, at least the basics,” I explained.

“Why would it change?”“Things change, it is the nature of the world. If we want these stories to be known, then we should see that they remain in a way someone can find them.”

The time for what I considered the funeral for Icehome came. People were ready, ready to leave, bags packed, sleds loaded, things prepared and put away. More old tunnels were sealed off, and when we left even the heating room would be sealed off. Pieces were in place for just that, stoppers for the airflow vents to keep animals from creeping in, though who knew how long that would last.

“I was born here, in this very room...” Elder Liet began, and we all paid attention carefully, the designated carvers began their work, starting close to the crystal itself.

One after another men and women spoke, each had a story, a memory they wished to share. Some took a bit of time, rambling a little, some were very fast, keeping short stories while they tried not to weep. Others did weep, for they'd known nothing but this place, and to see it go hurt them. One by one they went, people telling stories about love, or family, or even simple funny memories that had happened.

Then it ended, the residents finished, and they all looked to me. That I'd only half expected, but I was ready for it.

“Unlike many of you, I was not born here, but far, far away to the south. My first time came well after I'd reached adulthood, and my reason for coming was to deliver news...” I gave a few short stories, how we'd arrived, how we'd waited for days, giving our strength to heat this place, the names of those who'd passed that were no longer here to tell their stories.

When it was over people filed out, one by one, eyes wet as the last of the lights were removed and the source of heat, so long blazing no longer had mana to feed it. It wasn't cold, wouldn't be for a long time, but as the entrances were sealed it stopped giving off that life-saving heat, fell dark in the center where it rested, like a candle guttering down, or the last embers of an old fire.

Liet himself moved a stone to seal the final opening, the eldest of this place his duty was to take care of all of it, and as we turned to leave, the tunnel cooling around us I patted his shoulder, for though he had not wept like others I could see the pain on his face. There were no words, for I had none that would truly help with what he was feeling, what so many were feeling.

In that companionable silence we helped the last of the people onto their sleds, wrapped warm blankets around children as they left the great doors, and sealed them behind us. Perhaps one day someone would return here, and if they did, they would see what had been, and hopefully appreciate it as deeply as those who'd lived here did.
