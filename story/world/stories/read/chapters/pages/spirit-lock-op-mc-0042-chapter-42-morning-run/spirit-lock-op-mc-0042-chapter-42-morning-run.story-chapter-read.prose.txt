The next morning, Hayden locked away his wind catalyst and cleansing elixir and went for a serious run. He only wanted to clear his acne between executing his plans, but he quickly found that even running would provide him no respite. He was a celebrity now, so as he ran, he heard people screaming, “Hey! It’s Hayden!”

Hayden passed them with a brisk pace, but it wasn’t a minute later when he ran into the cross-country team, and their competitive spirits flared up.

“Can we join you?” one asked as they ran.

“If you keep pace,” Hayden said. “I’m not slowing down.”

A woman on the team rolled her eyes. “I think we’ll be fine.”

They weren’t fine—and the whole school knew it. Someone captured the despair on the cross-country team’s faces when they finished the first lap. Their clothing was soaked with sweat, and their faces were as light red and raw as blanched tomatoes. By contrast, Hayden’s chest rose and fell with powerful breaths, but he didn’t look the slightest bit winded. It was shameful—

Especially since he was running against scholarship team members.

Hayden was deeply ambivalent about this gossip—he just didn’t know it yet. Or, rather, he chose not to know about it. He felt pride seeing visual admiration, fear, reverence, and respect—whispers and rumors meant nothing to him. That said, he forgot how powerful group communication could be even for small events.

The next morning, there were twenty people lined up on the quad, waiting for him to run past.

There were third-year scholarship students present, so they did much better. That said, they didn’t make it three laps at his speed before slowing to half speed. To their credit, they did another five laps.

Not everyone lost gracefully. One casual runner snapped, saying, “Dude, I don’t have time for this!” while throwing their sweatband on video. A GIF of the clip quickly became known as the “hateband” meme, and it spread like wildfire in private chats.

That GIF pissed off so many men that they demanded top runners put Hayden in his place. It was necessary, they explained, because it would put an end to the toxic “Hayden’s the best at everything” movement festering on campus. So the next morning, thirty of the top elites from all clubs decided to challenge him.

-

Hayden didn’t increase his pace—but the number of spectators skyrocketed. Large crowds gathered in various parts of the campus to cheer Hayden on—or simply watch elites humiliate themselves. It was only on that day that he understood the frivolous power of his reputation.

There was a group of women cheering, wearing professionally made “Hugs to Millions” shirts. He waved, and they swooned.

It was a stark contrast to how citizens treated him during triumphs. There was no reverence, just simple mixed emotions of joyful adoration and aimless lust. It was a bizarre experience.

The next day brought larger crowds—and greater competitors. Curious teachers and professional rifters joined in. Surely, a B-rank rifter would be able to outlast Hayden, right?

Not on his watch.

Hayden couldn’t compare with their speed; many lapped Hayden three times with prideful smirks. Yet their jubilation soon turned to alarm when Hayden didn’t stop running—and started to lap them when they slowed down. He ran two hours straight for sheer spite, ensuring that no one would claim victory over him prematurely again. He also broke their spirits to stop the challenges from weak, arrogant people.

The strong wouldn’t risk their reputations on something so frivolous—and Hayden respected that.

It wasn’t like Hayden was challenging anyone—

He had to run like that. The primary means of infusing facial muscles with cora was through perspiration. Unfortunately, his coran infusion was so strong that he could work out for an hour without adequate sweating. To succeed, he had to run at full performance—in the summer heat.

It was a requirement—the competitions were just a needless breeding ground for jealousy and resentment.

That said, he didn’t shy away from it. He didn’t care what demigods thought—let alone children. As for drawing attention, that wasn’t a concern. Belia was suppressing information on Hayden so heavily that he regularly heard statements like, “Yeah, it was taken down, too. It’s bullshit.” It wasn’t a problem—

Or so he thought.

Hayden had a cult of online followers who ripped videos of him before Golden Hawk took them down, then posted them on torrent sites. The trend was insignificant at first, but it wouldn’t be long before it hit the mainstream and attracted his competitors. That was unacceptable.

Sairan called his assistant. “Cancel all of my appointments and get me on a shuttle to Junolyx.”

“But sir, you’re meeting with Gira Ew—”

“It doesn’t matter who I have a meeting with. If we don’t get Hayden on this team, someone else will. And if they do—they’ll destroy us. Now get me on a shuttle—now!”

Sairan had already been traveling to Heline since he obtained the video of Hayden, so he only needed to hop two galaxies via hypergates to reach it. It took eighteen hours, most of which he slept, so it didn’t feel like long before Mayle welcomed him to Junolyx University. She was understandably stressed—so she warned him about Hayden’s behavior and reminded him that he already turned down the Monks. Sairan said that it was fine and enjoyed a good sleep. He would need it.

“Oh, those are the people challenging Hayden, today. He’s a… student here. He goes on a run every morning, and people’ve taken to running with him.”

“Not for another fifteen minutes.”

A smile crept on Sairan’s lips. “Thank you.” He called Mayle. “Something’s come up. I’ll have to skip breakfast.”

Mayle tried to object, citing enigmatic arguments, but he waved them aside. Fifteen minutes later, he was stretching outside, preparing to make a little splash. He was famous, but not well known. So while someone using the face-scanning feature could quickly bring him up, he remained innocuous while he waited.

A group of women gathered around the automatic doors as Hayden walked out.

“You like our new shirts?” one asked. The shirts had a tasteful silhouette of his face. Below it read: “Hotter Every Day.”

Hayden flashed them a magnetic smile. “I think they’re charming. Thank you.” The women blushed and skipped backward, preparing their recording devices.

They’re serious about this, Sairan thought. But he has so little cora…

Sairan wasn’t the strongest rifter by any stretch. At his peak, he was a B-rank, and he earned that through tests and spirit evolutions, not direct fighting. That said, he was one of the best seers in the universe. He had two seer spirits, and he didn’t need them for most tasks. As a blindfold boxer, he could see coran channels just by how people breathed, and he could clearly see that Hayden wasn’t significant. In fact, his subconscious breathing was almost nonexistent.

There’s no way that’s right… Sairan thought. I’ll try Eyes of Life.
