Chapter 119: Dueling Paranoias

Jack rushed next to the currently open fortress gate, as everyone else burst in different directions on their own tasks. He lined up a long series of hard, steel spikes with super-sharp tips in a square shape immediately in front of the door. He drove them down in quick succession, utilizing Throw point-blank and explosively, making clean penetrations in the stone as he had dozens of times before. Fortunately, the floor wasn’t quite so tough, thick, or heavy as the walls of the place. He knew from a glance that they’d pierced exactly as he needed them to, all the way into the dungeon’s cramped interiors.

Making most of them fall below and come up under to hug and suspend the compromised floor/ceiling, he controlled two spikes on opposite ends, and turned them into rough sawblades on one side. Then he cut out the remaining gaps between the spikes until the stone was completely cut out, and it was already falling, causing his metal ‘catcher’ to sink. He moved all his power output to lowering it as quietly as he could.

The next part was perhaps the easiest, because it was already made. He moved a pre-finished, contoured slab of metal into the cutout, now with a texture and general appearance indistinguishable from the rest of the floor. He just had to make some smudgy alterations at the edges, and it was done in under a minute!

Particulate field cover around the sandy yard was next up. Priorities.

Jack didn’t so much as pause after finishing with the false floor, pulling out a sack of metal powder he had with him, as he rushed out the gate into the glare of the false sun. The walls around the fortress greeted him. He dumped the sack out and seized the dull metal dust with memorite, ‘dust to dust’ as bits collapsed into bits that had to be spread out and out and out until the diffuse clouds were good enough to be invisible. Then, they were blown out even more, so he could cover a wide-enough area — from the fortress to the wall for this sack, but including three zones of higher concentration at the entrance and the corners.

Once done like he wanted it, he locked it all in place as an Anchor iteration of fairly minimal mass and then ran over to the other sacks nearby. He got to work moving the greater mass to each side of the four corners of the fortress, not bothering with the finer, perfecting work just yet. Instead, he began burying needles, wires, and other small metal bits — already dumped out and ready for him — first handling the front section, and then going around the fortress to each side in turn. In each section, he fine-tuned the particulate field as he passed, ensuring it was not visible and had thicker zones ready in the corners. All fields flowed up the sheer wall of the fortress, too, just in case they were dumb enough to try climbing.

Once back around to the front, Jack had to deal with the portcullis gate in the wall. He didn’t bother with much here, just re-made the steel for strength and put Fragile Spike tips on the ends, so they became formidable spears.

Next, he moved on to the Fortress gate, which was a heavy wooden gate bound in steel. He fed more metal through the bands, essentially impregnating the whole thing with cord hidden behind the banding.

After this, he went through the final checklist inside the fortress and on the top. Materials hidden everywhere in plain sight, as pre-fabricated, disguised pieces that blended into the existing environment. Mostly, he picked up metal pieces and wire waiting for him, and fused them into the walls and ceiling, with some in the floor. It was all to ensure that any fighting inside would not know where attacks were coming from. Endless blindsides; Ferronaut’s deathtrap.

Up top were a variety of heavier metal implements he could drop down wherever the enemy ended up on the outside. Spheres, darts, cords. He included one sphere as actually a realistic replica of Bo’s helmeted head, which he affectionately dubbed ‘Dark Helmet.’ The decoy tactic might be known about, but that hardly meant they couldn’t still be thrown off. Maybe it would end up irrelevant, but eh. He never failed to amuse himself.

Time was winding down by now. Jack rushed into Inkblot’s box-like alcove, where Inkblot was already waiting, and filled in the final metal layer around him, then moved out and fused the door section shut. He then rushed into the room where Highfive and Bo were waiting, Highfive just finishing up the final foam applications. Jack and Bo began cooperating to place the final panels, Highfive soon following, and Jack finally sealed them all inside with a final fitted door piece, fusing the metal together-

Mini chimed. <A ten-second countdown has begun before the start of the match.>

In the artificial light of the sealed steel room, the three of them took a moment to do quick high-fives. They’d done it! All those drills had paid off.

A moment later, they were moving their focus to the camera feeds and getting serious. The drill was actually the easy part. Now it was showtime; the proving ground. A thousand more simulations might not have helped with the final real deal, because they were missing information. Hopefully, it wasn’t enough to screw them.

And it started. There was nothing explosive that happened immediately. Exactly what they’d expected occurred: they picked up weird and mixed harmonics, so much like what Augur’s simulation had delivered that it was uncanny. He’d gotten that dead-ass right, at least. The camera feeds flickered erratically, some going static temporarily.

One of them up top on the fortress took an intense vibration passing through it and went out completely. It didn’t turn back on.

Shit.

Jack struggled not to groan out loud. The feeds were like a deadly weapon. Getting them taken away would suck after all their hard work.

But other cameras didn’t go out, didn’t get hit just right. They survived the informational onslaught. It had just been a lucky hit, bad wires, some shit like that. Chime didn’t know he’d done it, or about the cameras. He was just trying to find them. Hard.

Jack breathed a controlled sigh of relief.

In the team gestalt, Bo chided, “Save our oxygen, Jack. You don’t hardly breathe to begin with.”

“Sorry,” Jack replied. “Just glad we’re still in business.”

“Yeah.”

“Actually,” Highfive said nerdily, “We’re good for hours in here. I looked it up. What kills is CO2 poisoning. Before oxygen depletion, anyway. And as Homo Superior, we’ve got even longer.”

Bo had a constrained sigh in her voice. “Fiver, you looking up stuff is starting to scare me a little bit.”

“You’ve created a monster,” Jack quipped.

There was a change in the feeds. All of them began to pick up intense, strobing light distortions coming from all directions.

“Holy shit!” Jack exclaimed. “That’s not normal.”

“The hell is that?!” Highfive exclaimed right over top of Jack.

“Must be Délight’s new toy,” Inkblot said darkly.

“It is,” Bo said confidently. “It’s not a debuff. It’s some kind of wide-scale buff hitting a radius bigger than this whole fraggin’ fortress. At least it ramps up. If I disrupt it, it’ll take time to ramp back up. In a direct clash, she probably couldn’t afford to do that in the action economy. We can’t take advantage from this distance, so it’s pointless right now, but I can stop it whenever. It’s gonna be a little draining when it's already ramped like this, but not as big of a deal after my mods. I’ll have plenty of juice after.”

“If we need to at all,” Jack added. It wasn’t a good thing if they did, because Bo needed to save the lion’s share of herself to disrupt Defiant. Once. By no means did Jack want her already drained when she had to pull that shit.

He couldn’t spare much thought for it, but the upgrade could even help explain Délight’s perspective shifts. Before Level Six, she was mostly all debuff. Something this widescale as a buff — if it was good — could radically alter her role and value. Maybe ‘playing nice’ made more sense now. If she was already teetering in her outlook before this happened, it could be like suddenly getting a sign from heaven.

Food for thought. But enough about that.

The giant aura was like being placed in a new world with a new sky. The brightness was dimmed, and the strobing was continuous, like a very faded, light multicolor field resonating through all of their little reality. Looking from multiple angles in the cameras, it felt like it was centered on a certain spot where she was and radiated out in all directions in a sphere, continual exploding patterns of not-quite-light and not-quite-matter. It was passing through everything, he was sure, but it was too diffuse to detect where they were, as opposed to looking out at the sky. There were no clear effects, but it was all quite disturbing nonetheless.

“Chime to front!” Bo called, though Jack was already looking through the feeds. It was the same place the strobe was coming from.

Chime moved with fluid quickness from behind rocky cover, and began blasting the wall dead in the middle on the west side with his concentrated soundwaves. It was a typical, conservative spot, and rather advantageous for letting Jack pelt them with shit as they moved around toward the front. The least advantageous was coming in right next to the portcullis, and going for the gate right off, but the enemy didn’t know that. In simulations, sometimes they came that way, and sometimes from the side.

If the side way said anything, it was that they were paranoid and going to send Defiant in to scout once the wall was down.

One blast, and Chime moved back behind cover. But he was going to pop right back up.

Jack wasn’t planning on doing nothing from range this time. The only reason to do that was to make the enemy less paranoid. But there was no helping that now that they had previous knowledge from their last match. They were super paranoid, and nothing was going to change it. In some sims, they were that way by default.

Jack almost prepped to fire on Chime as he popped out. But his movement…

Instead, he watched Chime pop back out and fire, super-fast. Way too fast, and seemingly more fluid, snake-like, in erratic bursts, timed with the strobe.

“That damn aura improves their speed,” Jack reported in annoyance.

“I think you’re right,” Bo replied.

Jack took hold of a bucket’s worth of darts from the roof and timed a ‘rain down’ right when the strobe pulsed. He never saw Chime pop out, and that was about as expected. The most intense time of the strobe was every second, and Chime wasn’t trying to hit every pulse, which would’ve made timing too easy.

A cone of sound knocked the darts out of the air, but Jack was already launching another volley. As these rained down and were defended against again, Jack took hold of a dart, split it into three with his technique, and zipped them around the cover, aiming at about leg height, and what he could glean was the center of the strobing.

He didn’t spend much time on it, simply grabbed another dart and did the same. And again, and again, and again, sometimes moving his target spot slightly.

He couldn’t see, and he wasn’t willing to move a field out to have ‘feelers’ because it would reveal too much to Chime, but he didn’t care. It was incoming fire they had to deal with, and he could do it for hours if he wanted.

The real question was whether that big aura cost relevant fatigue. If it did, she was on a quicker timetable than he was.

It was something less than a minute before they seemed to get their shit together, and Chime stopped wasting his time blocking darts. He popped up over the rock cover he was previously stepping out from and blasted the wall spot again, this time for a more sustained and damaging blast, before he ducked away and moved — just as Jack shot nearby darts at him and whiffed.

The top of the wall crumbled and fell. As a whole, it was heavily damaged. It wouldn’t take much more before it collapsed.

I can’t save it anyway. Best to sacrifice it for a shot at injury.

Keeping half a mind for the darts, Jack picked an apparent blindspot from the other side of the wall to drop an entire basketball-sized metal sphere over and around. He couldn’t see the rock cover clearly enough to remotely move it very far from the wall, but he could try a sharp angle from the cover of the wall to the front, charging around the precise cover the enemy group was using. They were so focused on not getting bled by ‘shin-darts,’ they might not see the sphere before it was right on them.

This was a ‘special’ sphere of two layers. Inside was a solid metal core, but around it were fitted pieces of jagged projectiles. Hundreds of them ready to go. If he could fire a cone at them in ambush and avoid Chime’s block, someone was getting injured, regardless of Defiant lurking around.

Jack timed it as best he could. Chime popped up over a new rock section, blasting the wall, ducking back, dodging darts. Right then, the sphere zoomed across the space on the flank. Being likely his best chance so far, Jack pumped all of his output into this.

He bumped a rock along the way.

Damn it.

Oh well. He immediately fired a cone at them from that spot. He followed that up with even more material blasted at them, and then grabbed aggregate mass and spun it around chaotically. There was now a ton of it around them, with Jack even pulling out the remains of darts and so on, so this was overall dangerous and hard to defend.

Chime certainly went into overdrive on defense, by appearances. Jack saw his sphere go flying at one point, though he arrested it and sent it right back. The whole group could be seen in a brief flash, moving to another section of rock cover into a discovered terrain decline, quickly out of his perception. And then the aura disappeared.

Ha! Gotcha. Yeah, turns out having a big bullseye on your ass is bad when hiding!

Jack fired randomly around for a while before figuring it was fruitless without knowing exactly where they were. He moved the remains of his sphere where he could see it on the cameras. He readied other metal in the vicinity to fire as suppression once he located them. Though he’d certainly spent a bit of fatigue to utilize the sphere, he might’ve scored a wound or two. If not, it was worth a shot, and the cost was ultimately very little. His team remained cozy in their hidey-holes.

There was no warning when the soundwaves hit the wall and began to break it apart. Jack didn’t even have eyes on Chime — he’d found a perfect spot under cover, not at good angles to where their cameras were.

Jack could still see where it was coming from. He pelted it with ammo. Nonetheless, another soundwave hit the wall spot and collapsed it into rubble. Jack immediately moved his sphere right into the apparent hollow area the enemy team was in and branched out tendrils with blades on the ends of them, swiping wildly.

The sphere went flying and bouncing with a huge dent in it. He moved it back in, but the group seemed to have moved again.

The effect, from way back in the beginning — the deep, resonating sound, blasted the wall — section by section, and intensely. Jack had no idea where it was coming from.

“Damn it!” Bo exclaimed.

“He knows about the cameras,” Inkblot declared.

“Well, isn’t he just Smarty McSmartpants?” Jack said idly. “He can’t get them all from here, though. Wanna bet?”

“Hmm, no,” Bo answered hesitantly. “Actually, he’s guessing rather than detecting them, right? Otherwise, he wouldn’t be doing that section by section.”

“Bingo, Cupcake of Darkness.”

“Alright, that does it. I need a ridiculous pet name for you, too.”

“I eagerly await it! We are an item, after all. Right, Fiver?”

“Bro,” Highfive admonished in consternation, “It was never true, and you can’t even play like that now.”

“Why’s that?”

Radio silence was all that answered him. Jack scowled sullenly inside his mask.

The western wall was battered by vibrations strong enough to even feel them slightly in their shielded cell. The two primary camera feeds were killed after ten total blasts, and then the fortress had another blown out up top. That was it, and things just stopped. They retained the majority of their cameras, including one that faced almost directly at where the breach was, from an arrow slit. That feed was damaged and full of static, but functional. Meanwhile, another feed crisscrossed from the far corner, so they didn’t need it once the enemy team actually went past the walls.

“He spent energy to do that,” Inkblot said, “and he’s far from done here.”

This was proved correct, as Chime started blasting out even the rubble concussively, to make a clear way through. Jack held off, knowing they had to cross at some point. The less they thought he could do, at this point, the better. Though it was tempting to bait Chime into draining himself in trying to batter every section of the entire grounds to eliminate the cameras, their enemy could literally fall back and wait out the fatigue, assuming they didn’t have a nasty enough bleeding wound that forced them forward. Which he was pretty sure they didn’t. Nons didn’t easily get those at random. You had to target arteries, or the wound would remain manageable.

Once the rubble cleared, a slightly different tone of sound came blasting through the cleared spot, more intense than ever. It hit the wall of the fortress like a giant hammer and shook it like a tree. Shook everything inside, as Jack felt their entire room vibrate, though definitely less than the rest of the place.

Jack was stunned by the action.

Can he bring down the whole damn fortress?!
