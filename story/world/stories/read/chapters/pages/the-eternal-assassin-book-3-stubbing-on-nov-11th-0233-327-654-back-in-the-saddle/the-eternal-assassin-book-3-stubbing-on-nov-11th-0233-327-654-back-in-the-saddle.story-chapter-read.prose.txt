Asher spent the rest of the day lying in bed, staring up at the ceiling as he worked to process everything that had just happened to him. World-shattering revelations weren’t exactly new to him by any means, but receiving one after another in such short order like the one-two punch of a professional boxer meant his head was reeling something fierce.

“Of course Donvath wasn’t hiding anything truly crazy… he’s just a man desperate to save his father from the same raving insanity that he’s destined to enter into himself one day,” Asher muttered, shaking his head as he thought back to how suspicious he’d been about the basement he’d been forbidden from entering. The fact that he’d immediately started hypothesizing all sorts of wild things that Donvath could potentially be getting up to down there spoke more to his own lack of trust than anything Donvath had done. Yes, the High Prince had killed him. A fact he still wasn’t entirely over just yet. But if what Gran said was true, he had done it at her suggestion, for his own good.

Usually, Asher absolutely hated it when people tried to do things ‘for his own good’ without asking, but Gran had such a strange, unique aura about her that he couldn’t help but nod along and imagine she was entirely justified. And he was pretty certain it wasn’t any sort of magical source creating such an aura, but the simple sense that came with having walked on this world for century after century. He was all but certain Gran was far older than Helpha or even Wilna, which was pretty wild to think about. He didn’t know how she compared to say the Council of Death, but he wouldn’t even be surprised if she was older than a few of the liches.

“Tier-three skills can do some crazy stuff,” he sighed, thinking back to the strange sensations he’d felt when Donvath triggered his ultimate skill. Asher swore he could feel time changing for a moment, along with Ophello’s mind being restored to him. Despite the fact that it clearly wasn’t a combat-based skill Donvath had developed, there was no doubt in his mind that it was more powerful than any skill he’d ever witnessed before.

Upon hearing Donvath admit that his father would only be around for a couple of minutes before losing his lucidity once more, Asher had briefly considered trying to use Permanence’s second-tier effect on Donvath’s ultimate skill, forcing it to remain active and keeping Donvath’s father ‘awake’. But without even having to reach out and try, he’d somehow known deep in his core that his own skill wasn’t strong enough to accomplish such a feat. He wasn’t sure if it was because Donvath’s skill was a whole tier above his own, or due to the strange nature of these new ultimate skills he was only just now learning about, but either way, there hadn’t been anything Asher could do to help.

The fact that Donvath hadn’t already asked him to try was proof enough of that. The High Prince had devoted his whole life to bringing his father back, even if only for short bursts here and there. If there was even the slimmest chance Asher’s Permanence could have worked, Donvath would have begged, threatened, or maybe even tried to force him to give it a shot. Though who knew…

Maybe once Permanence evolved into its third tier things would be different.

Putting that matter aside for the moment, Asher went back to his internal musings about the potential pact he might have to swear to join one day. On the one hand, he wasn’t exactly the biggest fan of fighting monsters. But on the other hand, if these monsters were truly as dangerous as Gran alluded to, maybe they would be a bit more interesting than what he was used to.

Eventually, the bedroom door swung open, and Asher sat up to find his entire team filter into the room. Even Kroult was with them, the lich’s flickering green flames shining as he followed the rest of the team before closing the door behind him.

“Would you look at that, he really did come back!” Moxy snorted, walking over and grinning as she slapped him on the shoulder. “Don’t you worry, I socked that High Prince in the face for you already. I figured you might be too tired to do so once you were back.”

“I still can’t believe you did that,” Rulfar grunted, sniffing cautiously as he looked Asher over. “How do you feel? Did you seriously come back from being disintegrated? Donvath told us what happened, but I’ll admit I didn’t quite believe it.”

“Yep, good as new,” Asher said, raising an arm and flexing as he forced a smile onto his face. As nice as it was to see his team again, he had too much on his mind at the moment to fall right back into the old routine. “Sorry for the unexpected pause in the lich hunt. I can’t say I was expecting Donvath to kill me.”

“We forgive you,” Moxy said, plopping herself down at one of the chairs as she cracked her back on it. “Anyway, you didn’t miss much these past few weeks. I started working part-time as an instructor for the royal guard, thanks to Christian vouching for me. Carrot snagged a couple of bloodhound contracts here in the capital to keep himself busy. And Wings here has been training like nobody’s business. Sedrick’s got her running ragged.”

“You’re working with the druid already?” Asher asked, not sure why he was surprised to hear the news. He’d been gone for over three weeks. It would have been silly to expect anyone to sit around waiting for him, doubly so when Donvath had promised he’d be back by the end of the month.

“Yes… though ‘working’ is a stretch,” Samantha admitted with a pained smile. “Our time is largely split between meditating in the nearby forest and hunting down monsters and bandits that threaten to upset the balance of nature. Sedrick is both powerful and wise, he’s made that quite clear. Though he’s almost Donvath levels of eccentric. He seems to have the ability to walk through large trees, using them to teleport regardless of distance. So he’s actually been coming all the way out here to help train me before withdrawing back to his cabin we found him in.”

“Wow… that’s a powerful skill alright,” Asher said, wondering if Samantha would ever end up being able to do something similar.

“And Kroult here has been working with Richard, if you can believe it,” Moxy laughed. “What was it Richard told you when you asked to work with him?”

“That he would be better off digging up an old corpse, reanimating it, and teaching it magic from scratch than he would be getting anything useful from having me as an apprentice,” Kroult said blankly. “Even so, we have been working together. Much of his time is now spent caring for his new pet, so I handle a lot of the simpler grunt work.”

“Well, glad to hear Mutae is doing well,” Asher said, feeling a genuine smile on his face for the first time in hours as he thought back to the weird pet. “That reminds me, I still need to Recall over to Scritchems and give him the news one of these days.”

“Not before we finish off that disease-riddled lich!” Moxy said, punching a first into her hand as she gave him a manic grin. “We’ve been waiting for you to come back long enough! Now that our ride is here, there’s no sense in waiting any longer!”

“You guys really want to risk coming with us?” Asher asked, glancing between the four of them. “I know it’s been a few weeks for the rest of you, but surely you remember how fast Rulfar almost died.”

“All fights come with risk,” Moxy shrugged. “And according to Donvath, if you try to fight this monster without us, you get bitten in half and digested. Sounds like you need someone who’s actually good at fighting.”

“We’ll take whatever help we can get,” Samantha smiled, interjecting before Asher could toss a dagger Moxy’s way. “But Asher, are you really good to jump right back into things? You’re sure you don’t need any more time? You didn’t have anything… weird happen that you need to digest?”

Asher realized quickly enough what she was alluding to, and he gave her what he hoped was a reassuring smile as he shook his head. He’d have to tell her about the progression in his communication with the deity of Eternity, but right now, he just wanted to put the whole thing behind him. The knowledge that a deity was watching his every move and was concerned for him was a little unnerving, but he wasn’t about to change how he lived his life just because of it.

“I think the faster I get back into the saddle, the better I’ll feel,” Asher admitted. “Not to mention Donvath said as soon as we take down this latest lich, he’s going to try and pull the trigger on the creation of the coalition of countries. I’m eager to get some much-needed help to take down the eight that will remain after Jormon. And we’re apparently going to be getting a rather ridiculous sum of shards for all that we’ve already done.”

“It’s about time,” Moxy snorted, placing her hands behind her head as she leaned back. “I’ve got a few skills sitting at level 40 just waiting for a windfall of shards to come my way. I’ll take whatever those pathetic kings and queens are willing to shill out, so long as I’ve earned it.”

“I also have skills ready for their third-tier evolution,” Rulfar said, scratching at his stubble as he glanced toward the ceiling. “To think we might stand a real chance at reaching the Stage of Power… I believe last I read the number of people in the Stage of Power was estimated to be only a few thousand total across the entire continent, and that’s including those who are rulers of the different nations. Obtaining eighteen million shards is no easy feat.”

“Potentially nineteen,” Asher corrected, getting a look of surprise from Rulfar. “Turns out there’s another blended skill that we didn’t know about. One that incorporates all three of a person’s elements in its creation. With fifteen base skills, three blended skills, and one ultimate skill, that makes nineteen. I just don’t know if this ultimate skill is earned before or after reaching the Stage of Power.”

“I had no idea,” Rulfar muttered, frowning as he glanced down at his hands. “Strength, Bear, and Blood… I can’t even begin to imagine what such a combination would create.”

“Try Pelts, Air, and Life,” Moxy snorted, raising a glowing violet finger as she lazily waved it about in the air. “That said, I’d bet I’m pretty far from that. I still need my Pelts and Air blended skill.”

“I don’t have any of my blended skills yet,” Samantha pointed out with a frown. “I was hoping the last few weeks would help me catch up a little, and while I’m sure they did, I’m still lagging behind all of you. I have a few skills left to evolve before I even hit the Stage of Bleed.”

“Don’t worry, I’m sure you’ll get there in no time,” Asher reassured her. With every minute that passed chatting with his team like nothing had happened, he felt a little bit better than before. It wasn’t as if he could do anything about Gran's and Donvath’s strange pact before he hit the Stage of Power himself, so there really wasn’t any point in worrying about any of that.

Far more important was finishing off the rest of the Council of Death before they could do the same to him. He wasn’t sure if losing Zygrul had put them in something of a state of disarray or if they didn’t even know that the lich had betrayed them yet, but he knew the moment he let up and gave them a chance to figure things out, it would only be a matter of time before they came after him again with the intention of ripping the element of Eternity from his soul. They needed to keep pressing the attack, and that meant picking up right where they left off.

“This has been a strange couple of weeks for me, followed by an even stranger few hours since coming back to life,” Asher admitted, looking around the room at his waiting team. “That said, Moxy is right. We need to leap right back into it. Let’s all get some rest. Come morning, we’ll bring the fight straight to Jormon, with all of us working together. If we’re lucky, that monster he’s hiding is the only real threat, and we’ll be able to take down the lich without breaking a sweat.”

“That’s what we like to hear!” Moxy grinned, hopping up off her chair and rubbing her hands together eagerly. “I’m going to destroy that thing and then hang its head up above Christian’s mantlepiece.”

“It's probably disease ridden,” Rulfar pointed out as he followed her out of the room. “You’d risk killing everyone in the capital in some sort of horrific plague!”

“Enjoy your rest, Asher,” Kroult said as he went to leave. Pausing in the entryway, the lich threw a glance over his shoulder, the fires dancing in his eyes burning low for a moment. “And do not forget what you promised me. As soon as the last lich falls, I wish to be laid to rest.”

Asher nodded, his stomach twisting into a slight knot as Kroult closed the door behind him, leaving him alone with Samantha. He’d really grown to like Kroult over these past few weeks. It was easy to let himself forget that the hedge wizard was only aiding them because he wanted Asher to help him reach his eternal rest.

“Hey, don’t worry about that right now,” Samantha said gently, sliding into bed beside him and pulling his head onto her chest. “That’s still a long way out. Just worry about the things you can control in the here and now.”

“You’re right,” he sighed, closing his eyes as he snuggled up closer to her and wrapped his arms around her. As always, feeling his fiancé in his arms brought him a serene peace he couldn’t get anywhere else, and he smiled as he finally let his tense body relax. “One lich at a time.”
