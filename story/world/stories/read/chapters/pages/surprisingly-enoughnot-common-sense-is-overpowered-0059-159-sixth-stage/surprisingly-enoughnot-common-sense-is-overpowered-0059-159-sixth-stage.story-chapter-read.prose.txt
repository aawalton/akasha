Twenty disciples remained after the preliminaries, and from this point onward, the competition shifted to one-on-one matches.

Each disciple effectively had two lives. One defeat was allowed, but a second meant elimination. After each round, the remaining disciples were paired according to their records: undefeated disciples fought other undefeated disciples, while those carrying one loss were matched against others with one loss. When the numbers were uneven, the leftover disciple either received a bye, which counted as a win, or was matched against the closest available record. No two disciples ever fought each other twice.

It wasn't a standard bracket where winning or losing a particular match immediately decided your placement. The disciples would continue being reshuffled according to their records until only ten remained, officially becoming the Top Ten. From there, the same system continued until the final rankings were settled.

Placement was determined primarily by losses. Fewer losses meant a higher rank, and if two disciples finished with the same number of losses, whoever had won their direct match would be placed above the other. If the two had never fought, the one whose losses had come against higher-ranked opponents was placed higher. The competition ended once every disciple's placement was settled.

Ning had to admit, it was a rather strange system.

Then he looked at Xiao Fan and suddenly understood.

Ah. It's probably like this so the protagonist can conveniently fight every high-grade spiritual root.

Xiao Hong and Zhou Sheng had already fallen to him. At this rate, Jin Shu and Qiu Han would probably find their way onto his schedule sooner or later.

Ning decided not to question it.

As far as he was concerned, the rules were simple: fewer losses meant a higher rank, and if the number of losses was the same, whoever had won their match ranked higher.

So he just needed to keep winning.

He managed exactly that for the next stretch, defeating a Fifth Stage cultivator in a straightforward and uneventful match. It was the sort of match that would probably be skipped over in a single line if it were a novel.

His third match, however, was a different story.

"Next up: Jin Shu versus Ji Ning."

The formation barrier rose slowly, humming with a heavy vibration that rattled the loose gravel around the ring.

Across the stone platform stood Jin Shu, currently ranked second among the newcomers. She kept one hand resting lightly on the hilt of her curved blade, her posture relaxed despite the focused look in her eyes. At her heels, a massive gray wolf prowled across the bluestone.

The beast was huge, easily outsizing most wild spirit beasts at the same stage. Its iron-gray fur was thick, with faint dark patterns shimmering beneath it whenever qi circulated through its body, while its golden eyes remained fixed on Ning.

Jin Shu grinned. "I've been looking forward to this. Your matches actually had some meat to them, unlike most of mine, so don't disappoint me."

Beside her, the gray wolf let out a low growl without taking its eyes off Ning.

Ning exhaled quietly. "Let's both do our best."

His tone was perfectly polite, but inwardly he was holding back a sigh. Why did absolute battle junkies seem to populate half this sect?

The presiding elder raised his sleeve, waited until both sides were ready, then brought his arm down.

"Begin!"

Ning didn't waste a second. Cold qi surged from his dantian as his fingers formed the familiar seals, and Hidden Ice Mist flooded across the platform, rapidly swallowing both contestants in dense white fog.

Ning backed into the mist while drawing Love from his shoulder. Turtle Breathing suppressed his presence as Shadow Steps carried him quietly along the perimeter to find a good firing position.

Hei Ya's nose twitched, and without the slightest hesitation, the beast changed direction and charged straight toward him.

Ning caught the approaching movement through his Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity and loosed his first arrow. Hei Ya twisted aside at the last moment, but the iron tip still tore across its flank, drawing a thin line of blood without slowing it down.

"Did you think Hei Ya hunts with his eyes?" Jin Shu called from somewhere behind the beast.

A crescent of compressed wind tore through the fog toward the direction of Ning's shot. He pivoted aside, and the attack passed close to his head before striking the empty stone behind him.

So much for hiding.

His second arrow was already on the string, but this time Ning ignored Hei Ya and aimed directly at Jin Shu.

The arrow tore through the mist toward her shoulder. Jin Shu caught sight of it at the last moment and swept her curved blade across her body, knocking the shaft aside with a sharp clang.

Ning had already released another, this one aimed at her thigh while her blade was still recovering. Jin Shu shifted her footing and twisted aside, but Ning adjusted the arrow's path slightly with a thin thread of qi.

Her eyes sharpened.

Before the arrow could reach her, Hei Ya lunged across its path. The shaft scraped along the wolf's foreleg before spinning away into the mist.

He fired twice more while circling along the edge of the platform. Jin Shu deflected the first with her blade and slipped past the second herself, immediately answering with another crescent of wind that forced Ning to abandon his position.

Jin Shu clearly hadn't exaggerated her family's beast-taming methods. She could defend herself perfectly well, but whenever Ning caught her at an awkward angle or forced her blade out of position, Hei Ya was there to cover the opening. Jin Shu returned the favor whenever Ning managed to land a clean hit on the wolf, attacking before he could follow up.

Their coordination made attacking either one of them troublesome, though Ning was already beginning to see how he might turn that against them.

Jin Shu emerged through the mist and closed the remaining distance with frightening speed. Her curved blade swept upward with wind qi gathered along its edge, forcing Ning to abandon his firing position.

He circled away and managed one final shot toward Hei Ya, embedding the arrow near the wolf's ribs, but Jin Shu immediately answered with a wide slash that carved a path through the fog and forced him back.

This thing is faster than expected.

Ning had done his homework before the one-on-one matches began. The gray wolf bloodline was the foundation of the Jin family's influence as beast tamers, refined through generations of selective breeding until their physical abilities far exceeded those of ordinary spirit beasts at the same stage.

According to the information he had purchased, Hei Ya was considered the finest of their current generation.

Now that Ning was actually fighting it, he believed the rumors.

Hei Ya burst through the mist and launched itself toward his chest. Ning dropped low and rolled beneath it as its jaws snapped shut just above his shoulder, but before he could regain his footing, Jin Shu was already there to cover her companion's landing.

Her curved blade came down, and Ning released the bow before bringing his spear up with both hands.

Clang!

The impact traveled through the shaft and into his palms. Jin Shu followed with another heavy cut almost immediately, then flowed straight into a third before Ning had fully recovered from the second. Her attacks weren't particularly elaborate, but every strike carried the momentum of her entire body behind it.

Unlike the beast tamers Ning remembered from games, Jin Shu apparently hadn't received the memo that she was supposed to stand somewhere safe while her pet did all the work.

"You're awfully quiet," she said as her blade struck his spear again. "I thought you'd be more fun than this."

Ning didn't say anything as he blocked another slash and immediately retaliated, turning the momentum of the parry into a thrust toward her ribs. Jin Shu knocked the spearhead aside with the flat of her blade, stepped around the shaft, and slashed toward his shoulder.

Ning pulled back just enough to avoid the edge before thrusting again. She deflected that attack as well, but he let the force carry his spear around in a circle and attacked from the opposite side.

Jin Shu leaned away, caught the shaft against her sword, and shoved it off course before stepping forward with another attack of her own.

For several exchanges, neither managed to land anything cleanly.

Jin Shu's swordsmanship was also better than Ning had expected. She certainly had the aggression of someone who enjoyed fighting far too much, but she wasn't simply relying on brute force.

Her foundation was solid, and whenever Ning tried to exploit the momentum of one strike, she adjusted quickly enough to stop him from turning it against her.

A warning prickled at the edge of Ning's perception. He shifted his rear leg just before Hei Ya's claws swept through the space behind his calf, then turned and thrust his spear toward Jin Shu's exposed side.

She caught the spearhead on her blade.

Ning immediately changed direction and drove it toward her shoulder instead. This time her sword was out of position, and Hei Ya rushed in, slamming into the side of the spear before it could reach her. The blade scraped across the wolf's shoulder instead of piercing deeply into its flesh.

Another shallow wound joined the rest along Hei Ya's flank.

Ning twisted the spear as he withdrew it.

[Scorching Purge]

A concentrated stream of crimson fire erupted from the spearhead and struck the same injured shoulder. Hei Ya recoiled with a pained snarl as its fur blackened beneath the heat, but Jin Shu was already attacking from the other side, forcing Ning to withdraw his spear and block.

"That was good." Jin Shu grinned and attacked again.

Battle junkies really are a pain.

The exchange grew increasingly difficult. Jin Shu parried Ning's spear whenever she had the angle and avoided the attacks she couldn't block cleanly, while Hei Ya covered the rare openings Ning managed to create. In return, Jin Shu protected the wolf whenever Ning tried to capitalize on one of his attacks.

When Jin Shu forced Ning's spear high, Hei Ya attacked his legs. When Ning turned to deal with the wolf, her blade came for his exposed side. Even when he managed to create distance, Hei Ya could track him through the mist and lead Jin Shu straight toward him.

Pure Eyes helped enormously. With Clear Sight active, Ning could follow the movement of Jin Shu's blade, the placement of Hei Ya's paws, and even predict some of their attacks.

Unfortunately, seeing an attack coming didn't necessarily mean he could avoid it.

Jin Shu suddenly changed her rhythm and stepped inside his guard. Instead of another heavy swing, her blade cut sharply across his upper arm while Hei Ya attacked from the opposite side. Ning twisted away, but there wasn't enough room to avoid both, and the wolf's claws tore across his ribs a moment later.

Sharp pain flared through his side as blood began staining his robe.

Jin Shu noticed immediately. "Got you."

Ning ignored her and drove the butt of his spear toward her stomach. She knocked it aside with the pommel of her sword, but Ning used the contact to reverse his grip and send the spearhead toward her shoulder.

She caught it on her blade, only for Ning to change direction again and slip the spear past her guard toward her ribs.

Hei Ya lunged between them.

Ning pulled back enough to keep the strike shallow, leaving another cut across the wolf's foreleg before retreating from Jin Shu's immediate counterattack.

By now, Hei Ya was bleeding from several places, although none of the wounds were particularly serious for a spirit beast with its physique. That was fine. Ning only needed to break the skin.

Jin Shu thrust toward his chest with wind qi gathering around the point of her sword. Ning knocked it aside, but Hei Ya was already airborne behind him, jaws opening toward his neck.

Without turning, Ning snapped two fingers backward.

[Golden Finger]

Compressed qi struck the wolf squarely between the eyes. Hei Ya's head jerked backward, its leap collapsing as the impact disrupted its momentum, and Ning used the opening to retreat deeper into the mist.

"You still want to hide?" Jin Shu called as she followed. "Hei Ya already has your scent, and you're bleeding. You're making this too easy."

She wasn't wrong. Blood dripped steadily from Ning's sleeve and ribs, leaving a trail across the frost-covered stone, but hiding was no longer the point. Ning only needed enough time to gather qi for something stronger.

Most of his current arsenal still consisted of techniques originally intended for spiritual farming. Although he had found plenty of ways to turn them toward combat, there was a limit to how much destructive power he could squeeze out of something designed to manage crops.

That was precisely why he had spent the past few days practicing Fireball.

Ning slowed his breathing and began gathering qi.

"Found you!"

Hei Ya emerged first, following the scent of Ning's blood, with Jin Shu only a few steps behind. Wind qi gathered around her curved blade as both of them accelerated toward him.

This time, Ning didn't retreat.

A dense sphere of crimson flame formed between his hands. The moment Jin Shu saw it, she changed direction, but Hei Ya had already sensed the danger and moved in front of her.

[Fireball]

Ning released the spell.

BOOM!

Flames swept across the platform, tearing apart the surrounding mist and turning the frost covering the stone into a wave of steam. Hei Ya took the brunt of the explosion, its enormous body shielding Jin Shu as both of them were thrown backward.

Ning raised an arm against the heat and waited for the smoke to clear.

Somehow, Hei Ya was still standing.

Its left flank had been scorched black, exposing burned skin beneath, and its chest heaved with every breath. One of its front legs trembled under its weight, yet the wolf stubbornly remained between Ning and Jin Shu.

What are they feeding this thing?

A faint metallic sheen spread across Hei Ya's damaged hide.

Body of Steel.

Ning recognized the bloodline ability immediately. It dramatically strengthened Hei Ya's flesh and skin, allowing the wolf to endure punishment that would cripple most spirit beasts at the same cultivation level. Combined with Jin Shu's offensive ability and their coordination, it was easy to understand how they had reached second place in the predictions.

Jin Shu stepped out from behind Hei Ya with only a few burns along her sleeve. She took one look at the scorched platform before turning back to Ning.

"Now that was more like it."

Ning glanced at Hei Ya. "Your wolf is ridiculous."

"Of course he is." Jin Shu raised her blade again, though the grin on her face had faded slightly. "But I think we've played around enough. Let's finish this."

Hei Ya lowered its body as wind gathered around Jin Shu's blade. The metallic sheen across the wolf's hide deepened, and their breathing gradually settled into the same rhythm.

Ning recognized the technique.

[Tempest and Wild.]

It was the Jin family's Earth-grade combination technique. Hei Ya would attack first, using its reinforced body to smash through the opponent's defense, while Jin Shu followed directly behind with a wind-amplified finishing strike.

With Ning already injured, taking the charge head-on would almost certainly end the match.

Hei Ya launched itself forward.

Halfway through the charge, its front leg suddenly locked.

The wolf stumbled violently and tried to recover, only for another leg to give out beneath it. Its muscles spasmed as its entire body lost coordination, sending the enormous beast crashing shoulder-first into the bluestone several meters short of Ning.

"Hei Ya?"

Jin Shu stopped mid-stride.

The wolf tried to rise, but its legs trembled before collapsing beneath it again. It was still conscious, and the furious growl rumbling from its throat made it clear that its mind was perfectly intact.

Its body simply wasn't listening anymore.

Jin Shu's attention remained on Hei Ya for only a moment, but Ning immediately took advantage of it.

Shadow Steps carried him across the gap before she could recover. Jin Shu reacted on instinct and brought up her curved blade, catching his first thrust with a burst of wind qi. Ning immediately changed angles, only for her to deflect the second attack as well.

Even shaken, she wasn't going down that easily.

The third thrust came lower. Jin Shu twisted her blade down and caught it, but Ning stepped forward with the deflection rather than fighting against it. His spear circled around her sword, forcing her to retreat as he attacked again.

She held him off for several exchanges before her attention flickered toward Hei Ya.

Ning naturally capitalized on that as his spear slipped past her guard and clipped her wrist. Her grip loosened, and before she could recover, Ning stepped inside the reach of her curved blade and drove the shaft of his spear into her ribs.

The impact knocked the air from her lungs and sent her stumbling backward. Ning followed without giving her time to recover, stopping the cold iron tip of his spear just short of her throat.

For several seconds, Jin Shu said nothing. Her gaze moved from the spear to Hei Ya, then across the numerous shallow wounds covering the wolf's body.

"...Poison?"

Ning lowered his spear. "The arrows and spear were coated. I was trying to get some into you too, but Hei Ya kept taking the attacks that got past your guard."

Jin Shu looked back at her companion as the pieces fell into place. She had deflected or avoided most of Ning's attacks herself, but whenever he managed to create a genuine opening, Hei Ya had stepped in to protect her.

Every arrow he intercepted and every spear thrust he took in her place had introduced a little more poison into his body.

In the end, most of what Ning had prepared for two opponents had accumulated in one.

Hei Ya's Body of Steel gave him a strong resistance to ordinary toxins, so Ning had prepared a concentrated paralytic and relied on repeated doses. A single wound would have accomplished very little, which was why he had spent most of the fight gradually building the amount in Hei Ya's body while waiting for it to take effect.

The presiding elder's voice carried across the arena.

"Winner: Ji Ning."

Hearing the official announcement, Jin Shu let out a long breath and lowered her sword. "Seriously? I thought we were having a proper fight."

Ning wiped a thin line of blood from his cheek. "You brought a wolf and double-teamed me."

"I'm a beast tamer. Hei Ya is part of my strength."

"And I'm a spiritual farmer. Poison is part of mine."

Jin Shu opened her mouth, paused, then frowned. "...That's different."

"How?"

She stared at him for a moment before looking away. "It just is."

Ning decided to let her have that one.

From the beginning, he had known that fighting the two of them head-on would be stupid. Jin Shu was already a stage above him, while Hei Ya was essentially another mid-stage Qi Condensation combatant with an even stronger body. If Ning had simply traded attacks with them until someone fell over, that someone would almost certainly have been him.

Poison was far more useful against that kind of disadvantage. Ning already used it when hunting stronger spirit beasts in the mountains, and Jin Shu's desire to enjoy the fight had given him enough time for it to accumulate.

If she had used Tempest and Wild within the opening moments instead of gradually increasing the pressure, things could have gone very differently.

Jin Shu crouched beside Hei Ya and examined one of the shallow cuts on his foreleg. "Still, Body of Steel gives him a strong resistance to poison. What did you even use?"

"A concentrated paralytic. I cultivate the plants myself. I am a certified spiritual farmer."

Jin Shu looked up at him. "You already got the spiritual certificate?"

Ning blinked. "You didn't know?"

"...No."

His spiritual farmer certification had been written directly beneath his name on the prediction list.

Apparently, Jin Shu's preparation for this match had consisted of learning that Ning was reasonably strong and deciding that sounded fun.

Somehow, that answer fit her perfectly.

Well, he did account for her nature when planning this fight.

Ning reached into his storage pouch and pulled out a small dried purple bud. "Here. Antidote."

Jin Shu caught it immediately.

"The poison only causes paralysis, so there shouldn't be any permanent damage. Still, don't leave him like that for too long."

Whatever annoyance remained on Jin Shu's face disappeared. She hurried over to Hei Ya, crushed the medicine, and carefully fed it into his mouth before crouching beside him with one hand resting against his head.

It took a little while for the antidote to work. Eventually, the tension in Hei Ya's muscles began to ease, and one of his paws shifted against the stone.

Only then did Jin Shu relax.

She remained beside him for another moment before standing and turning back to Ning. "Fine. You win."

"The elder already said that."

"I know." Jin Shu clicked her tongue. "I'm saying I accept it. But next time, let's have a more fun battle."

...

By the time Ning returned to his private courtyard, the noise from the competition grounds had faded into the distance. His injuries had already been treated, so after closing the door behind him, he went straight to his meditation chamber and sat down.

As soon as he began circulating the Pure Qi Sutra, he noticed the difference. The bottleneck that had held him at the peak of the Fifth Stage for the past few weeks had loosened considerably.

The Dragon Qi Grass had pushed his cultivation forward quickly, perhaps a little too quickly. His dantian could contain the additional spiritual energy, but his body hadn't completely adapted to the sudden increase, leaving a slight unevenness whenever he tried to circulate all of his qi at once.

It wasn't dangerous, but it had been enough to prevent him from advancing to the Sixth Stage.

After the fight with Jin Shu, however, that feeling was almost completely gone.

And it wasn't difficult to understand why.

Throughout the match, Ning had been forced to maintain Hidden Ice Mist and Clear Sight while constantly switching between Shadow Steps, his bow, spear, and spells. With Jin Shu and Hei Ya keeping him under pressure, even the spiritual energy he hadn't fully integrated had been repeatedly drawn into circulation.

By the end of the fight, the lingering instability from the Dragon Qi Grass had finally settled.

Ning checked himself with Know Thyself, confirming what he had already suspected.

He could break through.

In fact, he'd noticed the change near the end of the match. Naturally, he hadn't tried it there. Attempting a breakthrough while Jin Shu was swinging a sword at him and Hei Ya was trying to bite pieces off his body sounded less like an opportunity and more like a very creative method of suicide.

Ning closed his eyes and began circulating the Pure Qi Sutra in earnest.

The qi flowed smoothly through his meridians, without the unevenness that had troubled him for weeks. After several circulations, he gradually increased the flow, pushing toward the familiar limit of the Fifth Stage.

This time, the resistance was much weaker.

Ning maintained his circulation, and before long, the remaining bottleneck gave way.

His dantian expanded, his meridians widened, and the qi within his body surged before gradually settling into its new circulation.

Ning continued cultivating for a while longer, allowing his body to adjust to the changes before finally opening his eyes.

At last, the Sixth Stage of Qi Condensation.
