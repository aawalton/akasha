“Are you sure Morgan le Fay and Machina are going to be here, Captain?” Danny asks the leader of his team.

“That’s what I heard,” she replies. “My friends at the Guild are pretty reliable.”

“I don’t know, Tiffany,” the third member of their 2-Star team says. “Just rumors, right? I’m sure they’re very busy.”

“Like I said, John, my friends are pretty reliable.”

They ascend the concrete steps to the front entrance. Through the glass wall they can see a T-Rex’s skeleton. It silently roars at everyone who enters.

“Look, both of you need to be on your best behavior. There are some powerful people here—whether or not Machina and Morgan le Fay show up—that would make very, very good friends.”

“Who?” John asks.

“I’m always on my best behavior,” Danny says defensively.

Tiffany glares at Danny before answering John.

“The CEO of Titan Defense, for one. They’ve got deep, deep, pockets. Let me say it once again, deeeeeeeeep pockets.”

“No kidding,” Danny says with a whistle. “Aren’t they the ones throwing insane money at a few teams?”

John snorts. “Yeah. Their 2-Star teams make seven to eight figures a dive, easy.”

“How come we’re not making that much?” Danny whines.

“Quiet you two,” Tiffany snaps. She presents their invitation to the host at the door.

“Ah, the Cerulean Guard, we’re happy you decided to join us this evening. I hope you enjoy the gala.”

“Thank you,” Tiffany replies, and all three of them enter under the watchful eye of a few Empowered bouncers. John whistles.

“Wow, they brought out the big guns. Hiring Empowered isn’t easy for anyone.”

“Come on boys, it’s time to look our best. Danny, you wanna make the big bucks? Here’s how.”

Both Danny and John straighten their matching blue suits with an air of nervousness. Tiffany struts in her blue dress that sparkles like the open ocean. Thin fish-like scales shimmer in the light. With every step, waves of light travel from her low-cut collar to the knee-length bottom trim. They bounce off the Empowered styled split waist—to allow for running, if the need arises. She looks like a windy day on the coast personified, and her teammates look the same.

They drift through the party, Typicals often making way for them. Most of the time they don’t even realize it. Their Auras are felt subconsciously by most Typicals, and they move out of the way without thinking about it. A short man, with short-cut black hair and thin-rimmed glasses appears out of the crowd to greet them.

“Ah, the Cerulean Guard, it’s good to see you. I’m Executive Director Benjamin Lam, of the California Academy of Sciences. How are you enjoying the party so far?” He shakes their hands.

“We just arrived,” Tiffany says eloquently. “But so far, quite a lot.”

“I’m glad! I’m happy you decided to attend, I’ve heard of your good work just off the coast. If only the Bay had more teams like yours, hm?”

Tiffany smiles. “Someone has to take care of those low Category Fractures. Some would call it below our paygrade, but I think we’re doing some of the most important work.”

“I agree, I agree! Everyone focuses on the big things from The Deep, while forgetting just how much damage the weaker, shallower Fractures can cause.”

“We’re just trying to do our part, sir,” Danny chimes in.

“And let me thank you on behalf of the Academy, truly,” Director Lam says. “Both for your valiant defense, as well as what you bring out. We’ve made some great strides here in Arcano-biology.”

“Arcano-biology?” John asks.

“Ah, my apologies, I’m so used to being around scientists I forgot that the new lingo has yet to spread. The study of monsters. We’ve received small portions of what you’ve brought out as a team. All of it has been very fascinating.”

“I admit,” Tiffany says, “I didn’t know the Academy did research.”

Director Lam chuckles. “Yes, we’re a world class laboratory. It’s one reason why we’ve been unfortunately forced to suspend our more public facing operations. Not enough resources to go around, I’m afraid. In times like these, we have to take decisive measures. We do the best we can with what we’ve got. Which is very good, mind you.”

“I figured a place like this would have money galore,” John says.

“Oh, yes, we’ve got the money for staff and to run this place, of course. For for the procurement of samples? Well, few pockets run deep enough to compete with some of the larger corporations out there. We get by with what the government distributes to labs across the country.”

“I don’t suppose the Academy accepts other kinds of donations than just money?” Tiffany says idly. Director Lam beams.

“We do! In fact, Machina and Morgan le Fay provided us with something just a few days ago. It was a flower recovered from a Fracture. They entrusted it to us for study.”

“Well, do you think we could come to some kind of agreement? I can’t say we could give everything away for free, but I think we could negotiate a deal that would appeal to everyone.”

Director Lam hands her a card.

“I’d love to discuss this with you in more detail. Please, give my office a call tomorrow, and we’ll see what we can do. Now, while I’d love to stay and chat some more, I’m afraid I have some very pressing duties I must attend to. But please, stay awhile. I’ve been told both Morgan and Machina have some words to say later tonight. If you’d like to see what they brought, it’s in the bio-dome, which is open to all guests. I’m aware that Empowered like yourselves can sense Potentia quite easily, so I’m sure you won’t miss it.”

He then shakes all three of their hands before disappearing back into the crowd.

“Tiffany, did you just promise to negotiate for lower prices?” Danny moans. “I thought you said the big bucks were to be had here.”

“Danny, you’re a 2-Star aquatic warrior based in the Bay Area. If you want to make money, there’s a dozen companies who would hire you in a heartbeat. All of them would pay serious cash, and that doesn’t even count the equipment or other perks they’d find for you.”

“Yeah, yeah. Save the Bay, I got it, I got it,” Danny grumbles. “It’d be nice to be rewarded, at least once, right?”

“Tell you what, Danny, I’ll negotiate a lifetime membership to the Academy, just for you. How’s that sound?”

“Yay.”

A Typical waiter weaves their way through the thin crowd, and offers them a tray of drinks. All of them take a flute of sparkling golden champagne.

“Typical champagne,” John notes.

“The magical stuff is really expensive, John. And near-lethal to Typicals. Us Empowered are the exception here, don’t forget that.” Tiffany points out.

Another man weaves through the crowd, gunning straight for them. This one has the slickness of a business reception veteran. On his suit is a Palisade Security pin.

“Ah, the Cerulean Guard! Just who I was looking for!”

He sticks out his hand with a smile plastered on his face.

“I’m Jeff Robins, of Palisade Security, pleasure to meet you! I’ve heard all about you and the fine work you’ve done for the Bay Area.”

“How can we help you, Mr. Robins?” Tiffany asks, shaking his hand. His smile grows strained under Tiffany’s near-painful grip.

“Ah, it’s less of how you can help us and how we can help you, Captain Barrett. You see, I was taking a look at the dive reports the Guild has been putting out earlier this week when something caught my eye. Namely, you, and I’m convinced you’re a powerhouse team in the making.”

“Oh?”

“Absolutely! With the right gear, maybe pad the team out with a few more members, and I’m sure you could punch above your weight, no problem. You see, Palisade can make that happen for you. We’ve got the best equipment, the best people, and the best prices.”

He hands them a card.

“Please, give my office a call. If we work together, not only can we have the impact I’m sure you want, but we can make sure you’re appropriately compensated for your talents and efforts. I don’t want to keep you, and I’ll let you get back to the party.”

He disappears into the crowd as quickly as he arrived, and Tiffany looks over the card.

“Executive Empowered Recruiter,” she reads aloud.

“Sounds important, and he is right. We’re pretty damn good. We could make some serious bank. Also, what party? I swear, we’ve been here for all like five goddamn minutes.”

“Language, Danny, we’re in polite company. And what exactly are you going to do with all that money if everyone dies? Besides, everyone knows Palisade is just a mercenary company. How likely do you think they’d actually have us working Fractures?”

“They need them cleared just like everyone else.”

“Didn’t you just hear what he said? ‘Pad the team out’, meaning we’d no longer be us. We’d be whatever they want us to be. How much are you willing to sell for a paycheck? Because I’m sure with Palisade it'll be more than just your time.”

“Why don’t we go take a look at whatever Machina and Morgan le Fay brought back,” John says, changing the subject. “We can talk about this another time. The sharks are circling, and I’m thinking we should beat it for a bit.”

Other recruiters or people of note are already angling to get in on talking to them. 2-Stars might not be the most powerful of individuals, but they’re still Empowered. Even a 1-Star has a weight few others can match. Not to mention they’re one of only three 2-Star teams in the entire Bay Area. They make up three of the eleven total 2-Stars.

“John, this is what we’re here for in the first place.”

“Yeah, but I want to see what they brought back. It’s gotta be insane, right? Something totally out of this world.”

Tiffany sighs. “Fine, let’s go take a look. I’d be lying if I said I wasn’t interested either.”

“That’s it?” John says incredulously. “Really, just a friggin’ flower? I know the Director did say it was a flower, but I didn’t expect it to only be a flower.”

“Whelp, back to it boys. You said you wanted to see a magical plant, and now you’ve seen it. I can’t say I expected it to be so boring, but I guess magic doesn’t mean exciting.”

John scowls. “Come on. It’s gotta do something, right?”

He points at a scientist nearby fiddling with a do-hickey. “Hey, Doc, what’s the flower do?”

She looks up at him before looking at the water lily. “Nothing. It sits there. It’s pretty though, right?”

“Surely it can do something.”

“If does do something, it sure hasn’t done it yet. At least, not that we can tell, but it’s only been here for three days. We’ve refrained from taking samples. We don’t know when, or if, we’ll be able to get another one.”

“Come on, John, let’s go.” Tiffany tugs on his shoulder.

“Fine.”

They spiral up the ramp in the biodome. Their Empowered eyes let them peer through the night-time gloom. The lighting on the ramp itself is barely enough to keep it lit. They take the elevator down through the pond at the bottom. All three of them wave at Claude the albino alligator as they leave the aquariums behind to rejoin the party, or at least the outskirts of it.

“I’m starving, boss.”

“You’ll live, Danny. Dinner isn’t too far away. Oh, look.”

She flags down a waiter carrying a tray of appetizers.

“Oh, deviled eggs, my favorite.” Danny takes a plate and scarfs them down. He drops the plate back on the waiter’s tray before they can even sneak away.

“Danny, what did I say about best behavior?”

“What? I’m hungry!”
