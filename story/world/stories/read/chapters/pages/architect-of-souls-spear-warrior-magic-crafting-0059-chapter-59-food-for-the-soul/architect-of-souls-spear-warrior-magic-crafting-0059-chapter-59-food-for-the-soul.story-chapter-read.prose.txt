Ferris turned away from the melted pedestal. He needed to prepare for a fight before going over to the slimespace.

Before getting too distracted, he checked the corpses of the scarab beetles. He’d collected over a dozen of them the day before while refining a sandy yellow spirit gem. Had one of them faked being dead? He wondered. Did it wake up and infect Jade after I brought it here?

They were all there. Unless he’d miscounted, all were accounted for. Ferris did stick his dagger through each one, just to confirm that it was dead.

Next, he took the seed for the Assimilate Object ability and the legacy chest’s key over to his Qi Pool and decided to create a separate storage container for them.

Taking inspiration from the legacy chest, he wanted a strong box to keep valuables. Ferris took equal parts metal chains and qi and melted them in his pot. After the mixture had combined and cooled, he placed a hand inside and imagined what he wanted. The box would be completely metal, a safe in which to store his valuables securely. He wasn’t sure if the intent would help at all. The chain material didn’t add anything other than being made of metal, but it was the best idea he had. The box would also have to be small enough to pull out of his pot, so only about the size of a loaf of bread. Ability seeds were much larger than normal seeds, but they should still fit fine.

Looking around, Ferris considered where to place the box. His eyes fell on the frame that held his cooking pot over the Heartfire. He had formed the frame out of qi, but then it had attached itself to the stone hearth surrounding his Heartfire.

Ferris took one of the metal chains he had lying around, harvested from a chain demon’s corpse. He set the chain on the side of the Heartfire’s hearth, closed his eyes, and imagined what he wanted: a chain connected to the stone.

When he opened his eyes… nothing had happened. There was something missing from the process that had worked for the frame that held his cooking pot, attaching it to the hearth.

Ferris cooked up another mixture of chain and qi, and then formed it into a… chain. This was an idea that he got from the instructions for building the structure for the Assimilate Object ability. That ability required ropes that had been formed with qi to properly form the connection to the object in the middle. Ferris didn’t know if reforming these chains with qi made them better, but he figured it could help form the connection to the Heartfire. It was also the biggest difference he could think of from the frame: that one was made with qi and another wasn’t.

Taking the qi chain, Ferris once again closed his eyes and imagined what he wanted. He immediately felt a difference take hold.

Opening his eyes once more, this time he was not surprised at all to see the chain had sunken into the side of the stone hearth, making a solid anchor. Ferris took the precious ability seed and key, closed them into the box, and then tied the box with the chain. He attempted to mentally fuse the chain to his box, but it didn’t work. Wrapping it would have to do. Taking a moment to look at it, there wasn’t anything else he could immediately think of to help protect it.

Ferris looked at the core. Should I rebuild the pedestal now?

He didn’t think the pedestal was strictly required, but so far he’d always had it.

His uncle had told him to build it after bonding, before visiting his bond’s soul. On the other hand, the man had also told Ferris about the qi rope transfer technique. That involved carrying the core around—although just on one side. Hollowfield’s First Steps of the Soul hadn’t even mentioned a pedestal, instead instructing to build a chest for the core.

Ferris was worried about the core in the slimespace being inside the ants’ tunnel. If he built a new pedestal, where would it appear on that side? Last time, did it appear next to the Heartfire? Or did it form around the core’s location, which just happened to be next to the Heartfire?

For now, Ferris wanted to try transitioning over without the pedestal. If it didn’t work, he could still create one and try again. Jade did appear to be stable, after all.

Ferris had one last item to craft: a new weapon. He’d lost his second imprint spear to the massive scarab. He still had his backup weapon, the dagger made from red lizards, but didn’t end up using it.

He’d made a bone spear imprint weapon twice before, and this third time went at least as well as the second. He didn’t feel he had improved the process at all, but it wasn’t any worse.

After hefting his new weapon, Ferris went to strap the crossbow on his back. Taking it along would risk losing it, but he had much more qi now. These scarabs flew; a ranged weapon could be useful.

Better armed, Ferris considered his next step: investigating the slimespace. If he was right about how damaging the dome worked, then he had flooded the area inside with a wave of acid. Furthermore, if he was also right about how repairing the dome worked, then the acid would have already been drained away.

The core should be in one of the ants’ tunnels, which may be filled with ants, acid, or scarabs. If he was wrong about his guesses, then death waited for him on the other side.

With a heavy sigh, Ferris called his familiar over.

“Jade,” he said, “will you check the slimespace first for me? If there is a fight, I will join you as soon as I can.”

Ferris hated to ask, but it felt… logical. If the slimespace was flooded with acid, Jade would simply have a pleasant swim. If there was a swarm of scarabs, then Jade could hopefully survive long enough for Ferris to come help. After all, Jade had fought the things yesterday in the sandy spirit gem refinement just fine. At least, the smaller ones.

“Yes!” Jade, as usual, had no sense of self-preservation.

He set Jade next to the core, just touching it. Ferris relayed what he wanted them to do over the bond. Jade was confused at first, but was willing to give it a try.

After a few moments, Jade disappeared from sight. It was strange, as there was no sound or sign of them leaving. One second, the slime was there. Then the next, they were gone.

Closing his eyes, Ferris focused on the bond. Jade couldn’t send him a visual of the area—as the slime had no eyes—but they could tell they weren’t swimming in a liquid. That was good enough for Ferris, so he touched the core and moved over himself.

After appearing in the slimespace, Ferris braced for a fight. He wasn’t immediately attacked, so he was able to take a moment to look around.

He was lying down in the tunnel, with several ants frantically poking at him with their hard, stony feelers. At least they weren’t biting him with their mandibles.

The dark green core was beneath him, still wedged where he’d left it. Ferris grabbed it, then started crawling out of the tunnel. He could sense Jade rolling along behind him.

After poking his head out of the tunnel, the slimespace looked almost back to normal, with some key differences. Ants scurried everywhere; out in numbers Ferris hadn’t seen before.

Thankfully, he saw no sign of the scarab beetles.

The giant dome surrounding him was intact, although there were significant scars in the walls. It was odd; some areas of the wall had clear signs where the scarabs had dug in, but others were as good as new.

No acid was leaking in, at least. The ants were climbing all over the dome’s walls. Hopefully, they had found and killed any remaining scarab beetles.

Ferris and Jade were on one of the yellow spires on top of the Heartfire tower. The ants came and went, still ignoring them. Ferris quickly climbed down and stepped back from the spire, not wanting to risk antagonizing the creatures. He was encouraged that they hadn’t been hostile yet, but now was not the time to push his luck.

Ferris decided to first go check on Jade’s Heartfire, carefully stepping through a sea of ants as Jade rolled along behind. It was the most important thing in the slimespace, as it was tied to Jade’s life. As long as it burned Jade would live, and Jade would live as long as it burned. One of the few warnings Uncle Winton had given him about his own Heartfire was: never try to put it out.

It was hard to get over his worries, but he kept repeating to himself: the slimes at the ranch survived being cut every day. Hopefully, the tower had been safely above the wave of acid, but he had no proof. Most likely, a slime’s Heartfire could naturally burn under acid. However it worked, Jade was obviously still alive.

The Heartfire appeared normal, burning happily in its hearth. Despite all his logic, Ferris couldn’t help releasing a sigh of relief at the sight.

He decided to take a moment to secure the core in a new pedestal. Next to the Heartfire was the safest place he knew for it, so Ferris quickly transitioned back over to his soulspace. He decided to leave Jade to protect the core, although he was worried that the blind slime wouldn’t be very effective.

Briefly, Ferris considered using Hollowfield’s recommended way instead. First Steps of the Soul had instructed to make a box to house the core. The box would appear in both his soulspace and the slimespace, as the pedestal had. He decided against it, as now was the time to experiment. He didn’t know what differences it would cause, and was more concerned with making sure Jade was safe.

The new pedestal was made just like the first; Ferris even used two of the red imp demon corpses to stay consistent. More importantly, he put the same intent of holding the core securely into it. After clearing away the melted remains of the old one, he set it up in the same location and transitioned over.

Ferris found himself standing in the slimespace, next to the new core pedestal. As expected, creating one in his soulspace had made one here as well. Jade bounced up to him, happy that he had returned. The core on this side was already firmly placed in its new home. Ferris wished Jade could have watched it appear. Sadly, the slime hadn’t even noticed anything.

Next, Ferris and Jade went to check on the Core Tree. Neither of them was happy with what they found.

Where before there was a young tree with several healthy branches, now there was a stump. The poor thing had bite marks all over it, and Ferris suspected the scarabs had eaten it. There were no obvious signs of melting, so he didn’t know if it was immune or if the acid hadn’t gotten this high.

Jade, in particular, was unhappy at the loss. Ferris couldn’t understand everything coming over the bond, but they were clearly distraught over the tree.

For Ferris, it was a setback in time and qi, but better than losing Jade’s life. Other cultivators that did not bond familiars had to grow a tree from nothing; Ferris would just need to do it the hard way. The stump should sprout anew with enough time and qi. If it did not, he could start from scratch using the techniques described in First Steps of the Soul.

Looking out from the vantage of the top of the Core Tree’s tower, the ants were still out in force. They covered every surface, including much of the walls of the dome. It was a bit shocking to see so many of them all at once. How deep were the holes in the ground, and how many lived down there? Were there other creatures, or just the ants?

Moving on, Ferris checked on the Consume Pool and Digest River. Both seemed fine, although it looked like the winding path of the Digest River was a bit different. In fact, a lot of the ground appeared off, hills or dips in the terrain where there weren’t before. The yellow spires were in the same locations, but some had more or less of the green dirt covering them.

Ferris was glad that he had dug up both the Jump and Grow seeds. Whatever had happened here, being a few feet underground wouldn’t have protected the Jump seed, and the scarabs would have likely dug into the wall to eat the Grow seed.

About half the clams had qi chunks ready, which was normal by this point in the day. At the very least, the battle and flood hadn’t affected qi production yet. He still wondered if the ants somehow took qi from the river. Even now, he didn’t see anything like that. The ants had to come from somewhere, and seeing them cover the entire area really drove that point home. Nyoka’s comment about letting ‘spirit animals’ use the qi a familiar produced made more sense than this endless supply of reinforcements with no apparent cost.

Approaching the wall of the dome, Ferris looked up at the ants all climbing up its side. There was still no sign of any scarab beetles, but he could clearly see where they had dug in. The wall was pristine in some places. In others there were tunnels everywhere. The damage didn’t extend very far upwards, but it did circle the circumference of the dome.

Ferris guessed the damaged areas hadn’t been fully destroyed when he cut Jade, but they would need to test more to see. While it was now a higher priority, it didn’t need to be tested immediately. Jade had been through enough for now.

Returning to the Consume Pool and the acid waterfall that fed it, Ferris sat down to think. Jade rolled up into his lap as he watched the acid fall.

How had the scarab beetles gotten in? Ferris definitely remembered them from the day before, ambushing him and Jade while they were refining a sandy yellow spirit gem in the spiritual device. Like the fight against the small red lizards, Jade had been a good match against the scarabs. They weren’t large enough to really get to Jade’s core without letting the slime eat them.

Ferris looked up at the waterfall. Everything Jade ate appeared here and fell into the pool, only to be ripped apart by ants and dissolved in acid. But… the scarabs could fly. Could a living creature be teleported to the slimespace without killing it? Had a small scarab from the spirit gem refinement been consumed whole, appeared in the air, and then just flown away?

He’d been thinking of the Consume Pool as making a spiritual ‘copy’ of things Jade ate, but it also clearly made some things permanent in the soul, like the nutrients feeding the Digest River.

Did the yellow scarab do something to move fully into the slimespace? Or had the copy become the real version? Or even, did the copy lay an egg?

There were too many possibilities, and no great way to test them.

The spiritual device Ferris used when refining spirit gems was built like a fortress, with massive walls and towers with heavy armaments… but it was all facing inward. Presumably, the defenses were to protect the spiritual device from anything that appeared along with the crystals from the spirit gems.

Uncle Winton had mentioned that slimes could be hurt with spiritual attacks… is that what this was? A creature that could enter a spiritual space? From a certain point of view, that is what Ferris had done. He’d created a mental body, then sent it into gate devices.

It was the best answer Ferris could think of. He would need to be more careful about what he let Jade eat in the future. Long-term, they would need more defenses around this area.

That might explain how the scarabs got in, but not their numbers. There had been thousands of them, so they must have had a way to multiply. Ferris couldn’t know for sure without a living test subject, but he suspected it was related to the way the scarabs ate the dome’s wall. The translucent material had to be magical in some way—it glowed after all. Perhaps eating it gave the scarabs something they needed to make more of themselves?

If the scarabs could multiply once, they could do it again. Ferris wished he had a way to check for any hiding away. At least the ants appeared to be going over the wall with a fine-tooth comb.

With a sigh, Ferris and Jade returned to the Heartfire tower. There wasn’t much more he could think to do, other than keep a close watch and give Jade plenty of food and time to recover.

Still, while he was thankful it hadn’t ended worse, Ferris couldn’t help regret losing the day to this emergency. Even worse, the Core Tree was damaged, putting him further behind.

Despite this, Ferris couldn’t help smiling. Everyone had always said ‘cultivation is hard,’ but no one talked about the details. Just vague references to Adepts that had a setback or were suffering from backlash.

Yes, it is hard, Ferris thought. This isn’t the first unexpected challenge. It won’t be the last.

He sat down at the edge of the tower, Jade patrolling nearby. Just looking out at the slimespace for a few minutes.

But I overcame it. Jade is going to be alright. We’ll do even better next time.

Ideas ran through his head. Ways to better protect both the slimespace and his own soulspace. Tests to better understand the questions spawned by everything he’d just learned. Things he could make to help. The lists were long, but so was the journey. No one said cultivation would be short.

I’d always dreamed of being a delver. This is so much different than what I expected back home.

Ferris called Jade over into his lap. At least in this moment, he was having a hard time imagining doing it any other way.

We’re in this together. I wonder, how far can we go?
