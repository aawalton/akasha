Luke watched in silence as the weary soldiers gathered from their posts. The fragrant scent of cookpots filled the air, breathing life into men who had endured too much hardship.

They had wrested the fortress from Qin, but the victory came at a devastating cost. Many had fallen, and those who survived had barely been granted a moment’s rest before another army appeared at their gates.

It was a wonder they could even muster the strength to assemble. Then again, a hot meal was a powerful motivator.

Some of the older, more perceptive men shifted uneasily. They had fought enough campaigns to recognize that something was wrong. No commander would order so many rations consumed in a single sitting—especially under siege.

The only conclusion was grim: a final stand was coming.

Luke’s eyes glimmered faintly as he picked out the few veterans sharp enough to understand what was happening. Those men would be invaluable if his plan succeeded—and if they were permitted to leave Xiandong alive.

A few soldiers cast uncertain glances his way, but exhaustion dulled their curiosity. Most were too busy focusing on the meal being prepared to question their circumstances.

He didn’t blame them. In fact, he doubted anyone would believe that he and his men had scaled the southern wall in less than a day. Best not to draw attention until their escape was secured.

“Are you nervous?” Luke asked, glancing at the older man beside him.

“I’m hungry,” came the biting reply, followed by a wry grin.

Luke let out a hollow chuckle, shaking his head. That was all the reassurance he needed. Though they hadn’t known each other long, Tristan was now his subordinate. If Luke couldn’t trust someone the system itself recognized as loyal, then he couldn’t trust anyone.

“Get your meal first,” he said, waving him off. “I want to start negotiations before the next probing attack.”

The older man didn’t hesitate. With surprising agility for his age, Tristan appeared before the cookpots, filling one bowl with rice and another with stew.

No one scolded him. Perhaps they were too stunned by his energy—most of them could barely stand upright.

Watching his friend eat, Luke joined the line behind the soldiers, intent on filling his own stomach. They might be without rations for a while after this; this meal could be their last.

As he turned, he caught Commander Bridges staring at him with a strange expression. Luke didn’t need to be a mind reader to know what the man was thinking.

Since when did a general of the king line up with his soldiers for food?

But Luke didn’t care—not right now. The soldiers needed the food more than he did. After all, they were the ones who would be risking their lives during the retreat back to the capital.

Of the ten thousand men under his command, six thousand had gathered for the meal, while the rest remained on lookout duty. Though they intended to surrender, pulling every soldier from the walls would only invite disaster. Selis might see it as a sign of weakness—and strike before negotiations even began.

Once these men were fed, they would relieve their comrades and return to their posts.

But before that, Commander Bridges had an announcement to make.

Luke caught his eye and gave a subtle nod—a silent signal that the time had come.

“Men,” Bridges began, rising from his spot and addressing the crowd in a deep, commanding voice. The soldiers turned toward him, their weary faces softening as they listened. The hot meal in their stomachs had already done much to lift their spirits.

Morale, at least for now, was returning.

“We have received a royal decree from King Julius,” Bridges announced, his tone carrying authority and restraint. “All forces currently engaged against the Qin Empire are to retreat immediately.”

A murmur rippled through the ranks. Though the words should have brought relief, confusion spread instead. Many soldiers exchanged disbelieving glances. They were in the middle of a siege against Selis, not Qin—how could a retreat even be possible?

Sensing the doubt, Bridges cleared his throat before continuing. “It has been decided that we will attempt to negotiate a surrender with the Selis general to ensure our safety when vacating the fortress.”

That got their attention.

Luke watched as disbelief gave way to a flicker of something long forgotten. Beneath the grime and exhaustion, their hardened expressions softened—the faint glow of emotion returning to their eyes.

It was hope.

For the first time in months, the men allowed themselves to imagine survival. To imagine home. The thought kindled a small but unmistakable fire in their hearts—one that had nearly gone cold amid endless death and despair.

They would live. They could see their families again.

But the moment didn’t last.

“Commander,” a voice called out, rough yet steady. “Why give us false hope? You know as well as I do that the enemy general will see this as weakness. We won’t last another week at this rate—and he knows it.”

Luke’s lips curved faintly as he recognized the speaker—one of the sharp-eyed veterans he’d noticed earlier. His words weren’t insolence; they were the voice of reason, the kind every army needed to stay grounded.

Bridges nodded, unoffended. “Christian, there’s more to it than that,” he replied, his tone calm but firm. “In all likelihood, Qin has dispatched reinforcements to reclaim the fortress. If the Selis general has any sense, he won’t want to face them in open battle. Holding the fortress gives him a far greater chance of survival.”

The soldiers fell quiet, listening closely as Bridges continued.

“If he accepts our terms,” he said, “Selis gains the fortress without losing a single man—and they’ll be in a far stronger position to fend off Qin’s counterattack.”

His reasoning was sound—so much so that even the most skeptical soldiers began to look convinced.

Chatter spread through the ranks as hope flickered anew, fragile yet persistent.

The commander glanced toward Luke, seeking silent approval like a loyal hound awaiting its master’s nod. The sight almost made Luke laugh, but now was not the time for amusement.

Suppressing the urge, he rose to his feet and approached Bridges, drawing a few curious stares as he did. Only now did the soldiers realize they had never seen him before today.

Luke ignored their suspicion and leaned in close to the commander, quietly pointing out four men among the crowd—including Christian, the outspoken veteran from earlier.

The soldiers he indicated exchanged puzzled looks, unsure why they’d been singled out. The answer came swiftly.

“Christian, Blake, Zion, and Jason,” Bridges announced, his voice cutting cleanly through the din. “You four will accompany our envoy into the enemy camp.”

Christian’s brow furrowed, and he opened his mouth to object—but the commander’s tone hardened before he could.

“That’s an order, soldiers.”

“Yes, Commander!” the four replied in unison, their discipline asserting itself over doubt.

“Good. Everyone else, eat your fill and then relieve your brothers on the walls. They deserve a hot meal as much as you do,” Bridges continued. He turned back to the selected men. “The envoy departs in two hours. Prepare yourselves.”

A wave of acknowledgment rippled through the assembly. Morale had lifted once again, bolstered by both food and the faint promise of freedom.

Luke found himself quietly impressed. Bridges had managed to organize and motivate his men far better than he’d anticipated.

He had expected resistance—especially considering that he had replaced General Wall out of necessity and held no legitimate claim to their loyalty. Luke had even been prepared to reveal his true identity as a general if things turned sour.

But it seemed the man before him had already earned their trust through hardship and command.

He must’ve gained their respect over the long campaign, Luke thought. Not through title—but through grit.

His reflection was cut short as four figures approached, their expressions sharp and wary. They were veterans—every scar, every weathered feature testified to it. Even their presence carried weight.

Had he still been the man from Earth, Luke might have flinched beneath that pressure.

“Who are you?” demanded the one in front—a bald man with sun-baked skin and eyes like polished emeralds. Scars mapped his face, some old, others raw from recent battles.

It was Christian, the same soldier who had spoken out moments ago. He towered over Luke, his stance exuding both suspicion and dominance.

Before Luke could respond, Tristan and Commander Bridges stepped forward.

The contrast between them was striking—Bridges wore the strained patience of command, while Tristan exuded quiet menace. Though calm in appearance, a dangerous aura radiated from him, sharp and suffocating.

One wrong move, and someone would die.

Bridges hurriedly grabbed the tall soldier by the shoulders and pulled him back, putting some distance between him and Luke while trying not to attract unwanted attention.

Luke, however, stood his ground, back straight and eyes calm as he studied the man before him. A faint glimmer shone in his gaze.

“You idiot—how can you speak like that to a general!?” Bridges hissed under his breath, his tone sharp enough to cut through steel.

Christian froze, as did the three men behind him. Their expressions shifted from suspicion to shock, disbelief plain on their faces. A general? In the middle of a siege?

The air grew heavy. Conversations faltered, spoons hovered midair. Even those not directly involved could feel the sudden tension gripping the camp.

But Luke ignored it.

He stepped forward casually, closing the distance between himself and the man who had looked so intimidating just moments ago.

“I like you,” Luke said with an easy grin, his hazel eyes catching the sunlight.

“Huh?” Christian blinked, caught completely off guard.

“A good soldier follows orders,” Luke continued, his tone light yet deliberate. “But a great soldier also thinks for himself. He knows when to obey—and when to adapt.”

He paused, tilting his head slightly as curiosity flickered in his eyes. “Tell me, though—how is a soldier like you still stuck at such a low rank? You would have had to be in the army, what? Ten years perhaps?”

Christian shifted uneasily, glancing between Bridges and Luke. “General, I’m only twenty-two,” he said after a moment. “This is my fourth year in the king’s army.”

“Eh?” Luke blinked, momentarily stunned. His gaze drifted up to the bald head and scarred, weathered features of the man before him—features that belonged to a veteran, not a youth barely out of training.

The quiet intelligence and composure Christian displayed only deepened Luke’s surprise.

“I… I see,” Luke said, recovering with a faint cough.

Disregard his skincare routine, Luke thought dryly. The man has the makings of a true commander.

Indeed, Christian had the traits Luke valued most—discipline, initiative, and quiet integrity. Once they escaped Xiandong, he would see to it that this man became one of his lieutenant commanders, alongside the others he had chosen earlier.

“Keep what you’ve heard to yourself,” Luke said at last, his tone steady once more. “During negotiations, stay alert. If anything goes wrong, it won’t just be a failed surrender—it’ll be a massacre.”

Christian studied him silently, then cupped his fist in acknowledgment—a silent oath of understanding.

Luke hummed in satisfaction. The man hadn’t uttered a word of his rank since being told to keep it secret.

Good, Luke thought, a faint smile tugging at his lips. It seems my eye for people hasn’t failed me yet.
