Luke, exhausted from a whole day of meditation, groaned and fell back in the bed of his Shelter yet again. Seeing the weave wasn't the problem, not really. It was all an issue of reach. Ever since choosing the Lifeweaver class, reach had been the main problem, and it remained so even now. His threads of mana now reached farther than ever, but it wasn't enough, and they weren't even the problem this time. It was the weave. Even with all his progress, Luke still wasn't free to traverse the weave with his mind, not in the way he needed. The weave was everything, everyone, and all. This meant the portals should be there as well, but so far, they had been out of reach.

The plan was simple in theory, but far from it in practice. Luke's ace in the hole, the radioactive death energy created by a Singer named Leminal, was spent in the process of saving Earth from being subjugated by demonic overlords. Since he couldn't unleash a radioactive plague on the ones still in the process of taking over the planet, they needed an alternate solution. The portals. They were the key. With Luke's recent practice in handling and using them, it should be a breeze, right? Just cut the thread connecting the portal to the Singer homeworld, right? Easy peasy, right?!

As it turned out, Luke's plan had some holes in it. First of all, the idea might work, or it might not. Since the Singers guarded the portal in Central Park, New York, as if it were their most prized possession, getting close enough was almost impossible. At least without bringing some serious force, and most Integrated had fled or been either killed or captured since the invaders arrived a week earlier. The second issue was an extension of the first one: the number of portals. Yes, they might disable one of their paths to Earth, but there were many more in the country and even beyond. These people meant business, and they'd arrived in force. Perhaps they didn't have the numbers compared to Earth's Integrated, but they were a cohesive unit and worked a whole lot like an army, whereas Earth was a scattered mess of guerrilla fighters. Through Milla's connections, they'd learned that many countries had fallen altogether, and most others were in the process of losing. New Belgium, where Integrated had taken over a whole damn country, had done well, but now faced pressure on every side. All in all, it was a shitshow.

The enemy had even found the guild headquarters after less than forty-eight hours. Fighting them off had been easy enough, but it meant having to move the guild entrance, which was a hassle to say the least. With the system event done, Luke, having killed the demon leader Ezezal and destroyed the link between Earth and Infernal Flux, new portals were popping up. Not at the same rate as before, but it would have been enough to keep them busy even without the Singers butting in everywhere. Now, they needed to get in and get out fast to reap whatever experience they could, or the enemy would even take that from them.

Luke didn't join the excursions through the portals. No, he had his main objective right in front of him, and he was close to solving it, so close. The problem was one of concentration, focus, and willpower. It was possible to reach out and grab the damn thing. He knew that, but knowing it and doing it were two completely different things. Whenever he submerged himself in the deep pool that was meditation, annoyances crept up to poke at him, and there were many. Relian lived. The Fallen Shepherd should be dead, but Luke had failed when killing him. How, he wasn't sure, but there you had it. He'd failed. Once he emerged from Infernal Flux, he'd attempted to contact Relian through the communication gem, but found his old enemy no longer carried one, or the person he had taken possession of didn't. Since he never healed Relian, there wasn't an echo to grab hold of either, so learning the location of that madman wasn't possible at the moment.

Another annoyance was Integrated Solutions Group. Alan's old guild, the businessman Luke brought back from the brink of death, was now in the hands of a bunch of businessmen. They had seen the potential gain of joining forces with invaders, and whatever Integrated blood was spilled was on their hands. They needed to be dealt with, and soon. On a happier note, Luke was sure his strange void-pet Smudge was out there somewhere beyond the weave. He'd felt the presence several times now and wanted nothing more than to bring the little voidling back, but didn't know how.

Then there was the guild. The Band had suffered a lot during the double-invasion from Singers and demons. A few were dead. Others had fled. Luke had spent a lot of time and mana to bring a few members back from where they were trapped in dungeons, Priyah of his old group among them. Opening portals was now within Luke's power, but it was a massive undertaking that left him spent to the point of exhaustion, along with everyone else he needed help from. His own store of mana was far from enough to manage it. Still. Wielding that sort of power over the weave was something that would be crucial to pushing the Singers back, Luke just knew it. So, he meditated. At Level 46, he was far above his friends, so it was better that he stayed out of their way and let them gather whatever experience they could. His job was the Singers, but he couldn't help but feel alone.

Hannah was still gone. She'd gone through a pink portal at the same time as Luke, and so far, she hadn't found her way back. Luke glimpsed her once, in his own dungeon, but she'd been little more than a ghost and disappeared before she even realized he was standing right next to her. Even her echo was gone. But she was out there somewhere, just like Smudge. He refused to even entertain the idea that she wasn't.

With a groan, Luke gave up. It wasn't happening this time either.

Luke: "Milla! It's time we did something about ISG."

She'd urged caution, wanting time to coordinate with a bunch of other guilds, and even whatever remained of the US government, but that was days ago at that point. Days!

Milla: "What exactly is it that you propose, little brother?"

Milla: "Do you want to teleport in and start killing everyone you see?"

Luke: "...Maybe?"

That wasn't it, not really. The Singers' secret weapon, the ticking time bomb that had been the unknown object Curtis discovered when following their movements, had been a stroke of genius on their part. At first, everyone thought it just drained humans of their mana. That was bad enough on its own, but it had been nothing more than a byproduct of a more serious effect. When the system arrived on Earth, and people touched the gray orbs that turned them into Integrated, Guide, their mysterious liaison, put something in everyone's heads. It was a sort of emotional blocker that made you able to... deal with your new circumstances a little better by dialing down your emotional response. The Singer attack short-circuited that in everyone affected. Some, like Luke, had somewhat overcome losing the effect, while others had succumbed. There had been a lot of people who couldn't handle it. Some took the easy way out, while others just ran off. Others still didn't show any effect at all. Perhaps they hadn't done anything they regretted, or maybe they were just more strong-willed than most.

Milla was one of the latter ones, but she'd always been the cautious, calculating type. Curtis showed no response at all to losing the mental block. Lemira, the half-demon whose father Luke had killed to end the demon invasion, didn't have one to begin with, and Luke's friends from Chicago, Penny and Mateo, were dealing with it well. Mateo was more emotional than before, but fought on, and Penny took the whole thing in stride. Ray had done a lot of bad things, according to himself, and wasn't dealing well, but overcompensated by throwing himself at every open portal, and even Singers when there were no dungeons to be found. New York was far from the only city affected, and from what Milla told them, things were dire out there in the wide world. They would need to put it right, and they needed to do it soon, starting with New York. The clock was ticking. Luke could feel it in his bones. Time was running out. He wasn't sure in what way, but it was clear to his gut feeling.

Luke: "...Not kill everyone."

Luke: "Just the board of directors, the guildmaster, and maybe the lieutenants."

Milla: "We are dealing with them."

Luke: "What do you mean?"

Milla: "I mean what I wrote. I have people on it. They came into our guild headquarters and attempted to take over. That's unforgivable."

Luke: "You have a plan? When do we strike?"

Milla: "I have a plan, but you're not doing anything."

Milla: "You have your orders, little brothers."

Luke: "You're sending people in without me?"

Luke: "I should be there!"

Milla: "Closing the portal is your job, Luke. Do that, and we'll talk."

Luke gritted his teeth but didn't reply. There was nothing more to say. It was true what she said. He was the only one who had a chance at closing their portal, and that was the most important thing at the moment. If he went along to ISG, no one could stop him, but what would be the point of the guild then? The Band was there so they could accomplish something greater than the sum of its parts, and Milla, his big sister, was the one directing it.

Ray: "Hey, punk! Guess who's going to assassinate the guildmaster of ISG early tomorrow morning?! This guy!"

Ray: "I was pointing at myself when I wrote that last part. In case you couldn't tell from context."

Ray: "Milla said you can't come. Hah!"

Luke: "She said I can't come unless I close the gate first. Don't write me off just yet."

Ray: "Got another nuke stuck up your butt?"

Luke: "Maybe."

Shaking his head, Luke got up and exited his Shelter. He had hidden it inside an apartment building a few blocks from guild headquarters, so he could reach them on a moment's notice if anything happened. Not that he needed close physical proximity for that, but it didn't hurt either. Milla said he needed to close the gate. His attempts at reaching it through the weave these last couple of days had been a huge nothing-burger. That didn't mean Luke couldn't do it, just that he couldn't do it from a distance.

Luke raised the hood of his reaper-like robe. The runes stitched into the fabric glowed, indicating others would have trouble spotting him. The effect was even better in the dark, and night had just fallen.

"Guess I'll just have to get closer," he said as he opened a window and threw himself out into the gloom of late afternoon.
