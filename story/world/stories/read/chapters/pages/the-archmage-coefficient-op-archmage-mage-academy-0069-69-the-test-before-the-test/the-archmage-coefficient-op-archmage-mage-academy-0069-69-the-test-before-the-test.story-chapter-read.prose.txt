The portal interrupted her midspeech, rushing towards her and swallowing her up faster than she could stop it.

No, that wasn’t right.

It was more of a tower that had been made from twisted bamboo and wood sprouting from the sandy desert, a botanical enclave formed without rhyme or reason.

"Welcome to my home," the woman said, stepping into view with a flourish. "What do you think?"

"Did you just threaten me?"

Arielle hadn't meant it as a threat, but she supposed she could see how it could be interpreted as one. "Yes."

The mild breeze whistled in the silence, the hissing of the heated sand accompanying it. Sweat trailed down Arielle's neck, and it disgusted her instantly. The Commander's expression shifted rapidly until she finally settled on what Arielle read as an annoyed amusement.

"This Lyra…she is that important to you?”

"Obviously." She looked disgruntled. “I do not say things that I don't plan on doing. Mostly. Now don your disguise."

Arielle remained hesitant, eyeing Vittoria with suspicion.

Commander Vittoria raised an eyebrow. "I’m not used to giving orders more than once. And I usually don’t accept this much lip from a soldier.”

“I’m not a soldier. I’m a student."

"What you are is a pain in my–"

“Why are you doing this?” Arielle asked. “Did Elric tell you to kidnap me from school and test me?”

Vittoria opened her mouth for two seconds before she gave a clipped nod. “You caught me. Yes, he did."

Arielle narrowed her eyes to be sure. “Are you being sarcastic?"

“Absolutely not.” Vittoria shook her head in a way that seemed genuine to Arielle. Should she check with [Empath Sense]?

No. Vittoria would likely notice and might be offended by it.

She did not want to offend Elric's friend and potentially damage the woman’s relationship with Elric.

“Okay,” Ari said. It was strange that Elric hadn’t mentioned such a test to her first, though perhaps he had. Perhaps she had not seen the message in time. After all, she had not been as diligent in checking her messenger scrolls as she should have been, mostly because every time she opened up the book, there were seemingly hundreds of messages from her family.

"I suppose if Elric says so, then we can do it."

“Yes," Arielle answered honestly.

“You’re a commander.”

Commander Vittoria Di Santinael, the Golden Beast, the Savior of the Nineteen Isles, the one who awakens victory, along with several other titles I will not bore you with. I outrank Elric by leagues, and my knowledge of magic laps his by several miles." She shrugged before she added. "I suppose I could be a legendary mage if I put my mind to it, but I refused to participate in that inane system any longer."

Vittoria paused with that, seemingly waiting for a response from Arielle.

“What difference is any of that supposed to make?” Arielle asked. The commander might be all of those things, but, for Arielle, Elric still ranked higher in importance. All this woman was was Elric's friend.

And a partial Elf, which would be interesting on its own if Arielle were allowed to explore it.

"How much of you is Elven?" Arielle asked. "Can you use Elven essences? Do you know any full elves living in this realm?"

Vittoria’s jaw slackened, and clear surprise flashed in her eyes. Different emotions shifted over her face before it settled in a wry smile.

"It's safe to say, so far, you're not as I expected, Arielle."

"Likewise. Also, you did not answer any of my pertinent Elven questions."

“I'll answer them later if I'm in a good mood. Right now, we must return to the matter at hand. You’re going to be interrupting a pirate crew and finding out what their powers are.  The main goal is essentially to stop their progress and ensure they cannot continue with their journey. Also, maybe steal whatever gold they brought along with them..." She brushed her chin. "Yes. That sounds right. We confiscate the gold."

"Why?"

"Because I'm sure they're ill-gotten. The pirates are rotten thieves after all."

"If we steal it, don't we also become rotten thieves with ill-gotten gold?"

"No. We're the heroes here."

Ari pursed her lips. That didn't align with all the storybooks she'd ever read on the topic. "I don't think heroes kidnap students from school and order them to steal from pirates."

Vittoria's lip twitched. "Fine. You're the hero. I'm the...hero maker."

"I see." Arielle mentally ran through her moral dilemma. Stealing from thieves...did that make her as much of a thief as they were?

Logic told her no, but her storybooks often had lessons that suggested doing bad things to bad people could sometimes make you just as bad.

However, if the thieves had truly stolen coin and Ari took it away from them, it was a form of justice, right? As long as she was not claiming the coin for her own selfish reasons.

For it to be heroic, they would either need to return it to its rightful owners or use it to do communal good.

"We would need to try to find who they stole the gold from," Ari said. "Alternatively, we must donate it to a charitable organization."

"All of it?" Vittoria asked.

"Yes. That's the only way to remain a hero."

Vittoria smirked. "Fine. We'll be the heroes then. At least, you'll be. I'm not doing anything but observing you."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't feel like it. Now, by my count, there are about fifteen to twenty people there. Most are simply barbarians and rogues, but there are a few mages, a Phasewalker, and one creature whom I cannot get a good read of. That might be a bit of a surprise factor, but I’m certain if you’re half as clever as Elric thinks you are, you can manage it."

"Yes," she said. She didn't typically like surprises, but in this case, she was looking forward to something that would actually challenge her. Most of her fights were dull and predictable. This would be different at least.

"One more thing," Vittoria said. "As you fight, I'll be studying your battle styles and your mannerisms to better apply them. I want you to also mimic some of my mannerisms, particularly with the inclusion of Vitae in some of the spells you cast. Like this one." She sent a sprinkling of Vitae, Luxa, and Vacu orbs to the sand, and it awoke.

Just that patch of land pulsed with life, exuded energy, and the grains rose to form the shape of a frog.

The frog hopped.

"Wow," Arielle said.

"Yes, it’s lovely. I don't have time to go in depth and teach you anything like that, and though Elric tells me you're a fast learner, I don't expect you to incorporate this yet. Though seemingly simple, it's quite complex to master, so we’ll learn it later. But just try to use as many Vitae-containing spells as possible."

"Okay," Arielle said. She would simply have to ensure that she didn't use too powerful a spell. Vittora's sand-frog spell could potentially be such if she expanded it, but it would be fine as long as Ari only formed minor sand creatures with it.

Could she make something like that for Lyra, but without Vacu and Luxa?

Arielle couldn't wait to try.

"Take this," Vittoria tossed her a black battery. "A reservoir with five thousand orbs. That should be enough."

"Yes," Arielle said. "Thank you."

"So polite," Vittoria winked. "Also, since you're representing me, I'm going to give you sort of a grand entrance. You have to remember to stand tall and ooze authority as I do.  I'll be in your head, coaching you on what to say while you're there. Understood?"

"Okay."

“Say, ‘yes, commander’.”

“Yes, commander.”

“Good. Let’s go.”

Neat.
