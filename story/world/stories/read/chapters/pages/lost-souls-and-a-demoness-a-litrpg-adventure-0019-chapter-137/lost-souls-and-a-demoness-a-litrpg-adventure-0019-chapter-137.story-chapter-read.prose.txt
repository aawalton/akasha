"Alright." Aylin said, packing up her ritual supplies and returning to where the group waited near the portal. "I think I have a better idea about what's going on here."

It was a quarter of an hour after the system had issued all of them, even those without Wayfarer marks, the quest to stabilize and destroy the Core. They'd remained near the portal in case it proved necessary to make a hasty retreat, but nothing else had happened since their arrival. Jade couldn't even see any monsters in the relatively open area surrounding the large cathedral, and she wondered where they were.

"What did you find out?" Parked asked, gesturing for Ellie to remain on watch while the rest of them gathered around to hear the update.

"This dimensional space is absorbing unusually high levels of magic from the astral, which is what is causing the Labyrinth Core's instability. While powerful, the Cores are magically delicate. Their purpose is to channel a very specific type of magic that is used to carefully extract materials from other dimensions with a high degree of precision. A large part of my duties in the Mystic Order involves studying this process, and it's a very robust system. As long as there aren't any unexpected changes to the levels or type of magic they draw on."

"But since there's more magic than there should be here, it's going haywire?" Jade guessed.

"As far as I can determine, yes." Aylin nodded. "I'm surprised the Core is still intact, it's drawing on magic that is far more energized than it should be. The Voice of the Labyrinth said that the extra energy is being shunted into the monsters of this space, though, which I can only guess is the reason we're having this conversation."

"The quest said that the energy came from the demon ritual we stopped at the other Core site." Naomi said. "That was a couple of weeks ago now. Does that mean the effect is fading?"

"No." Tara replied before Aylin could, stepping into the middle of their impromptu huddle. "I've been running some scans of my own. In the twenty minutes we've been here, my suit has detected a small but measurable increase in the local magical saturation. The effect is getting worse, not better.""Why?" Jade asked. "Like Naomi said, that happened weeks ago."

Tara, whom she had directed the question to, shrugged.

"That, I don't know."

"It's possible that the Core already absorbed the excess magic and is slowly bleeding it off." Aylin replied. "But I'd have to study it physically to say that with any certainty."

"Can't we just wait for it to finish, then?" Luis asked. "Once it's done discharging all that extra magic, everything will go back to normal, right?"

"Not anytime soon." Tara said, crossing her arms. "All that extra magic isn't going anywhere. It only bled across the astral in the first place because the demons were performing a dimensional ritual. It won't just leave on its own now that it's here. And that's assuming there isn't still more power being absorbed from the astral.""The heightened magic is resulting in higher level monsters." Aylin added. "The longer we wait, the more powerful the monsters will be. They're already nearing the limit of what a raid group like ours might be able to handle."

"So, we do this now or we don't do it at all." Jade summarized. "And since not doing it isn't an option, we may as well get started."

The rest of the group voiced their agreement, some with more enthusiasm than others. Naomi and Siora both looked worried, but neither voiced any concerns as the group got moving. Parker suggested investigating the central cathedral first, as that seemed like the most likely place the Core would be. No one had a better idea, so they began crossing the sprawling gardens that lay between the portal and the large front doors of the structure.

"Siora." Jade murmured, falling in at the back of the group alongside the elf. "You should sit this one out."

"Why's that?" She asked, turning an uncharacteristically defiant look on Jade. "Because of my level?"

"Yes." Jade said, surprised by the elf's sudden intensity. "We expected to go up against monsters in the low twenties, not low thirties. I'm worried about you.""And I'm worried about all of you. You're my friends too." Siora set her shoulders. "Naomi is the only other healer in the group, and two is already too few for a dungeon like this. My healing spells won't be any less effective for the monsters' increased strength."

"They will be if one of them gets to you before we can help."

"I appreciate the concern, Jade." Siora said, fire easing in her eyes. "But I'm here to stay. A couple of months ago, a handful of strangers found me lost in the jungle and were kind enough to help me find my way home. Then, once we got here, they saved me again by stopping an army of undead from destroying it. Now, they need my help, and I'm going to give it."

There was nothing Jade could say in the face of the genuine passion and gratefulness she could feel emanating from Siora. For once, Jade's demonic instincts and her human ones were aligned. Both wanted Siora to stay. The latter respected her commitment to their friendship, and the former urged her to make use of any asset she could leverage against her enemies.

"Siora, I…""Besides." Siora cut her off, a soft smile playing across her lips. "This might be the only time I ever get a quest. I can't miss all this bonus experience, you know?"

Jade groaned, putting a hand over her face."I've been a terrible influence on you, haven't I."

Jade stood back as Parker, Luis, and Urgraan threw their weight against the massive double doors of the cathedral. They were two stories tall and made of what looked like solid metal. Even in the dim light Jade could see the tarnish on its surface, as if the structure were long abandoned. The rest of the cathedral bore similar signs of neglect, with chipped stone walls and cracked stained glass windows. The doors creaked as the three men pushed with all their strength, shifting inward under the pressure. Inch-by-inch they swung inward, each movement accompanied by the unpleasant sound of metal grinding against stone.

Radiant light broke through the slowly widening gap, spilling onto the dark cathedral grounds like a shaft of sunlight. Jade squinted, peering through the now-open doorway, her eyes slowly adjusting to the bright light even as she kept her weapons ready for any monsters that lurked within. But there was nothing. No knights charged out to attack them, no monsters rose from the gardens to strike. Everything remained eerily silent.

"Well, we're in the right place." Fenrin said, holding up a hand to shield his eyes from the intense glow.

Jade blinked a few times to clear her vision, her injured eye adjusting more slowly than the other. Finally, she was able to get a good look at the inside of the cathedral. The doors opened into a cavernous space with a high vaulted ceiling. The lower portion of the stone walls were adorned with magnificent tapestries and paintings, while the stained-glass windows were set higher towards the ceiling, where elaborate chandeliers hung from rafters. Any candlelight shining from those fixtures was wholly unnecessary, however, due to the sun-like radiance emanating from the Labyrinth Core. The twenty-foot-tall yellow gemstone hovered near the center of the large chamber, slowly rotating. It was instantly recognizable, although Jade noted a few differences from the other she'd seen.

"What's that surrounding it?" Eita asked. "Some kind of barrier?"

Indeed, an orb of translucent golden light shimmered around the Core, completely encapsulating it. Four beams of golden energy streamed through the windows, tinted with rainbow hues from the colored glass panes. They met at the orb, melding into its shimmering surface.

"Yeah. It's coming from the towers." Jade said. She turned from where she stood in the threshold and pointed back towards the tall towers that rose from the corners of the exterior wall. The beams of light pouring into the barrier came from each one, combining in the cathedral.

"So, we can't get to the Core until we turn those off." Luis guessed.

"That gives us an objective." Parker agreed. "We should get started before we run into monsters."

"I don't think we'll find any here." Aylin said. "Not until we disable the barrier. Look." Jade followed Aylin's gaze. She was staring intently into the barrier, and Jade finally noticed another shape below where the Core hovered. She'd missed it before, but now that her eyes were adjusted to the glare she could see more clearly. There was a humanoid form crouched beneath the Core, head bowed, arms wrapped around curled legs. Two large wings rose from the figure's shoulders, furled behind their back. They weren't the dark, leathery wings that Jade possessed but were white and feathered.

"I'm guessing that's the boss." Jade murmured.

"They'll probably wake up when we finish with the towers." Aylin agreed. "Which makes this the boss arena."

Jade gave the room another look, paying closer attention to the environment. The far end of the room, the part behind the Core, was elevated on a dais. A lectern and some seats looked out over the rest of the vast chamber, where rows of pews extended to the rear of the room. Many of the benches were splintered and shattered, little more than piles of broken wood, while others remained more intact. That, combined with rows of columns that lined the room, would make the large space difficult to manuever through in battle.

"We'll need to watch out footing in here." Luis said, joining Jade in the threshold. "That's a lot of junk."

"Do you think it will let us clear some of it away?" Jade asked Aylin. "If we're going to have to fight here, it'd be easier without all of this in the way."

"We can try." Aylin said, still inspecting the chamber. "Just be careful."

Jade, Tara, Luis, Parker, and Ellie moved into the cathedral with care while the others remained outside to keep watch. There was no reaction from the boss nor the Core, and no monsters manifested to stop them as they began clearing space. Jade didn't drop her guard, staying alert for danger. The brilliant light shining from the Core was enough to illuminate the entirety of the vast chamber, which Jade realized took up the entirety of the cathedral. The entire building was this one room. It left her feeling odd. The aesthetic of the cathedral perfectly matched similar buildings on Earth, but no real church would have this layout. It reminded Jade of the factory on the first floor. At first glance it looked like a real, lived in place. But it was all a façade. A facsimile created by the Labyrinth. It drew upon the worlds it connected to, mimicking them, but with no real understanding of them. The result was places like that looked right but felt wrong.

"The other Core wasn't this bright." Jade said to Tara as they shoved one of the more intact benches out of the way. "Is it because of the extra magic?"

"Yes." The tek'kalla grunted, not even glancing at the Core. "And we can't do anything about it while that shield is around it. My suit got a good read on it."

"So, first the towers, then the boss.""And then we'll take care of the Core. But the longer it takes, the harder it'll be to stabilize it."

"Then let's get moving."

It only took a few minutes to clear a large, open space in the middle of the chamber. Satisfied that they'd have some room to work with when they needed it, they regrouped in front of the cathedral. Jade felt a lingering warmth as she left the interior of the building. The sensation was familiar, but she couldn't place it.

"What is it?" Siora asked, noticing her expression.

"I just… felt something. I don't know." She shook her head. "I feel like I should recognize it."

"This place has a touch of Lady Elaria. You visited her shrine, no? I felt it too."

"Growth…" Jade murmured, remembering the vision she'd experienced in the Lysara grove. "Why, though? This isn't a temple to her, is it?"

"I don't think so. I don't recognize any of the markings."

Jade shook her head again. This wasn't the time to dwell on minor mysteries. The power of Growth had helped her in the past, all she could do was hope it would do so again.

The group made their way to the closest of the four towers, which wasn't far from the corespace portal. Again, they encountered no monsters in the gardens as they crossed the grounds, stopping at the base of the wall. Unease grew in Jade's stomach. The longer they went without opposition, the more concerned she got. They entered the wall through a small door and made their way up a long spiral stairway that took them all the way to the top of the tower. There, they found a large glass sphere the size of a beach ball set in an intricate metal sconce and filled with radiant energy. The beam of energy lanced out of the sphere, entering the distant cathedral through a high window.

"So, breaking this is going to summon a bunch of monsters to attack us, right?" Naomi said, once they were all gathered at the top of the tower. It was larger than it looked from the ground, with more than enough space for their group.

"Absolutely." Luis said with confidence.

"That's how these things usually go." Aylin agreed.

"Or, maybe the extra magic has messed with the dungeon, and the Labyrinth can't spawn more monsters." Eita suggested.

"We're never that lucky." Jade clapped him on the shoulder. "Get ready everyone."Ellie, Tara, and Fenrin moved to the battlements that overlooked the cathedral grounds, ready to respond to any distant threats while the rest of them drew their weapons and guarded the stairway that led to the roof. It was an easily defendable position, so the plan was simple. Whatever monsters the Labyrinth threw at them, they'd fight them here.

"Ready?" Luis asked. He had taken a large hammer from their dimensional bag and held it up, poised to strike. Jade looked around, getting a chorus of nods and thumbs up.

"Ready."

The glass sphere shattered as Luis drove the hammer through it, sending a cascade of glass shards spraying across the stone roof of the tower. The golden light held within the orb erupted out in a burst of radiance that momentarily blinded Jade, bathing her in warmth before quickly fading as darkness reclaimed the rooftop. The beam of energy leading to the cathedral flickered and vanished, dissipating into the gloom. For a long moment, nothing else happened.

"Maybe, we were wrong." Luis offered, slinging the hammer over his shoulder. "Maybe it was that eas-"

A piercing shriek cut through the night, quickly joined by several others. They came from below, somewhere in the direction of the cathedral.

"In the gardens." Ellie said, her calm voice cutting through the cacophony.

"There's a lot of them." Fenrin added.

Jade rushed over to join them at the battlements, arriving just in time to see figures clawing their way up out of the dirt, rising from the grounds they'd just passed through. Some were clad in tattered robes, others in darkened armor.

"It is." Tara said with a grin. She held up her wand, fire coalescing at its tip. "And they're in a nice, big group."

Before she even finished speaking there was a loud SNAP from beneath their feet. Jade looked down in time to see cracks spreading from the center of the tower's roof, spreading from the device that had held the orb. A stone shifted under her foot, sagging.

"Everybody off!" Jade shouted, pushing Ellie and Fenrin towards the stairs. "Back to the ground!"

No one had to be told twice. They sprinted for the stairs, dashing down as quickly as they dared while the tower started shaking itself apart.
