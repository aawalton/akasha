I walk at the head of a party from the ships, each of us carrying a storage container on our backs that holds far more fish than it looks capable of from the outside. The number of eyes that fix themselves hopefully upon us is almost daunting, and when people see the containers and the difficulty with which some of the party are walking – there are enchantments lightening them, but not making them completely weightless – many in the crowd sigh in relief, several almost collapsing against the person beside them.

Lines of stress ease in the faces around us, and the sight makes my heart clench in my chest. We shouldn't have got so close to the edge that the simple sight of storage containers being carried from the ships should elicit such relief.

We are almost provided a civilian honour escort to the kitchens –  clearly these people haven't yet been given the all-clear to return to their daily work, or why would they be standing around? I feel my back itch under the container at how many hungry eyes are fixed on my burden. One of these specialised containers could feed a family for several weeks if rationed carefully. They might not know exactly how many fish are inside, but they can tell from context that it would be a worthy prize to steal. I can only hope that hunger hasn't made people quite that desperate.

To try and ward off any such thoughts, I do my best to smile at those around me and call out in a carrying voice, "We're taking these to the kitchens. Everyone will be given their fair share. And the fishing fleet has already gone back out for more."

Which is exactly what they have done. Through the Alliance bond, Josiah coordinated with the lords still in the settlement, and more crews were sent to their vessels. Upon returning to harbour, we paused only to offload the first catch before they turned around and left once more, three other ships accompanying them, the whole fleet encircled by the honour guard of Nessie and her two hatchlings. Fortunately, the second is not currently showing such obvious signs of mana-warping as the first.

Nessie – I couldn’t resist – has agreed to aid the fishing fleet, both by protecting it from potential attackers and by locating shoals of fish and driving them into the nets. In exchange, she'll take a portion for herself and her hatchlings, and if a Tier three tries to challenge her, she can call upon the fleet to help see it off if she wishes. She's still not entirely pleased about the Bond, and at present refuses to let me Bind and examine either hatchling – I sense that she hopes it will all come right in the end. If Noir and his siblings are anything to go by, it probably won’t, but that’s Nessie’s choice.

My words seem to have a good effect on the crowd. Some of those lingering a little too close to the party draw back, evidently deciding it isn't worth the risk if they're going to receive a portion anyway. We thus make our way to the kitchens unmolested.

The kitchens welcome us with literal open arms, excited expressions turning to relief-filled awe as we open the lids and reveal just how many fish we've managed to pack into the chests.

"An excellent catch," the head cook, Kera Isolde, congratulates us, relief easing the lines in her face. "We'll eat well tonight."

"And there should be more to come," I assure her. "I’ve also got some packed into my Inventory."

She raises an eyebrow.

"You were intentionally stripping the Energy from them?"

I nod.

"The manastorm seems to have saturated the beasts with unnatural amounts of Energy. We decided it would be safer to put the ones with unstable levels into my Inventory to wipe them clean, rather than risk them having a poor effect on whoever eats them."

She nods knowingly and beckons me to one side, directing me to offload the fish from three Inventory slots into what looks like a chest freezer. As I go through my Inventory, I remember the beasts we killed earlier.

"I have some meat for you as well," I say, looking up at Kera Isolde.

"Please finish offloading the fish first, my lord, and then I'll direct you to where to put it,” she replies briskly. “Is it butchered?"

"Not processed at all," I admit, with a slightly rueful grin. "That's not one of my Skills." Not if she wants clean cuts, anyway.

The head cook shrugs and calls over another kitchen member, clearly a butcher – or Butcher – if I judge correctly by the tools he brings with him. I pull the carcasses out and place them where she instructs, and the butcher quickly gets to work.

"Thank you for both the meat and the fish, my lord," Kera Isolde tells me earnestly. "If not for the breakthrough the alchemists made, I'd have barely anything to cook tonight for anyone."

Caught by that one word, I dig a little deeper.

"The alchemists made a breakthrough – is this something new?" She could just be referring to the original processing of Energy-saturated vegetation, but I suspect not.

My suspicions are confirmed a moment later when she brightens and gestures toward several large bowls sitting on the counter with towels over them.

"That River of yours is far smarter than she looks," the cook tells me proudly. It sounds like a backhanded compliment but seems genuinely meant.

"Apparently she's the one who worked out how to do the process without requiring a whole load of acid to be added," she relates, gesturing at the bowls. "The food still has to sit at room temperature for a while, which isn't ideal based on what you were saying about bactas, but without the acid, it should be far more appetising than what we've managed so far. And we've been working out which dishes survive best sitting at room temperature for a few marks. Once we get fresh stock coming in from outside, we'll find it far easier to nourish the whole settlement."

I feel relief loosen the bands around my heart. Hopefully we've managed to turn a corner when it comes to feeding everyone. Now, we have both fish again and a significantly easier process for making local vegetation edible, I’m less worried. Hopefully the forest will calm down soon and we’ll have access to that once more too.

"I'll have to go and congratulate her," I say to the cook, with enthusiasm.

She nods firmly.

"You absolutely should. That Bonded of yours is a lifesaver."

With a few more words, I leave the kitchen staff to get on with it and head straight for the healer's compound nearby. Just through the doors, I almost walk into Healer Thessalanor who’s hurrying down the corridor.

"Ah, Lord Marcus – just the person I wanted to see." His expression is tight, though his tone doesn't suggest an emergency.

Resigning myself to my congratulations for River being delayed, I pause and give him my full attention.

"I'm listening. What is it?"

I'm hoping for good news after the steps forward with the fishing and the food processing, but I brace for bad nonetheless.

"It's the mystery illness," he replies, his expression tense. "The number of cases reported to me in the last day has dropped significantly compared to previous days."

I frown slightly.

"Isn't that a good thing?"

His expression doesn't suggest it is. Healer Thessalanor hesitates.

"Arguably, yes. However, it's unusual for an illness to drop away so suddenly. I think it's more likely that–"

Whatever he thinks it's more likely is lost when he cuts himself off as River rounds the corner at almost a run, pelting towards me.

"We did it!" she cries aloud, rubbing her face against mine so enthusiastically that her scales leave abrasions on my skin, which I heal absentmindedly.

"From what the head cook says, it was you who did it," I point out.

She looks suddenly embarrassed, pink flickering through her scales as she draws back.

"Well, I suppose I was the one to identify the ingredient, but I wasn't the only one to test it."

"Praise where praise is due. Well done. That's a significant advancement that will have a real impact on our ability to feed everyone."

"Now we just need to work out how to avoid the end product having to sit around for so long," she says with determination.

I sigh fondly and wag a finger at her. "Take a moment to appreciate what you've done, at least."

Seeing that Healer Thessalanor has quietly disappeared, I decide the conversation with him can wait a little longer, and spend some time with River instead. I can tell from the Bond that despite her very human attempt to downplay it, she is greatly pleased with herself – and anxious that I be pleased with her too. After giving one of my oldest friends the praise and attention she deserves, I go looking for Healer Thessalanor.

When I find him, though, he's sitting beside the bed of a patient I don't recognise, eyes closed and eyebrows drawn tight in concentration. I draw back, deciding not to interrupt. I know how frustrating that can be.

For now, I resolve to check on what’s happening with our highest priority – growing food. On our way out, Elarion went to investigate what the manastorm has done to the soil, and I told him I'd come by and check on his progress. At least Catch and Pride haven't indicated that he's got into any trouble in the meantime.

Heading back out of the settlement, I check through the Bond to see if anyone would like to join me as I walk towards where I can feel Catch and Pride are. Bastet is currently with the fleet, helping to communicate between the ships and the newest addition to our Bond network. Lathani and Ninja returned to land with me but want to join some of our other companions in exploring the woods. In the end, only Sirocco comes with me, swooping down to land on my shoulder.

In the time it takes me to reach Elarion, she updates me on changes to the landscape after the manastorm. There's evidence of several fires, suggesting true storm elements were at work, alongside some stranger and less welcome changes – trees that have suddenly tripled in height, grown peculiar additions, or are even beginning to move in deeply unnatural ways. I pass word along the Alliance Bond to Layton and Valance to warn their people to keep an eye out. I strongly suspect those trees will not be peaceful.

Reaching Elarion, I find him literally getting his hands dirty – holding a handful of soil and doing something to it with his eyes closed. My best guess is some sort of magical inspection. He doesn't open his eyes even as I draw close enough that he should have heard me coming, so I take a seat on a nearby fallen log, sending silent greetings to Catch and Pride across the link and getting their updates.

From the sound of it, things have been fairly peaceful, though that may be partly because they haven't ventured into the forest at all, focusing instead on the cleared farmland – which is significantly overgrown from just a few days of a manastorm.

They can't tell me whether the news from Elarion is good or not, as he has apparently mostly ignored their presence. Pride takes more umbrage at that than Catch, who accepted it stoically, reasoning that he can focus better on his surroundings without the distraction of conversation.

When Elarion's eyes finally blink open, I end my silent exchange and refocus my attention on him.

"So," I ask with underlying tension in my voice. "Have you made any discoveries?"

"Plenty," he agrees. "And not much of it good."

Not a surprise, but more than a little disappointing.

"What did you find?"

"The soil is even more Energy-saturated than before. The lightning strikes of the manastorm appear to have grounded themselves in the earth, and residue remains in some areas more than others, though it's equalising across the whole area fairly quickly. It will take some time before the Energy saturation levels return even to what they were before the storm."

"And by some time, you mean…?"

Elarion shrugs elegantly.

"It's hard to say. There's been little change since I've been here, which suggests at least days, if not tens of days, or even months."

I nod slowly. "And what does this mean for our farming attempts?"

"Again, it's hard to say," Elarion replies briskly. "However, there are some things I can immediately conclude. First, it will make our experiments more unpredictable – the Energy itself is far more unstable than what I'm accustomed to working with. Second, it's likely to exacerbate the situation we've already experienced, as any plants we try to grow or harvest will probably be even more Energy-saturated for an extended period. Even once the current Energy levels in the soil return to normal, the plants themselves will continue to store it."

I listen with growing dismay. Our success with the fishing and the alchemy had lifted my mood considerably, but this seems determined to drag it back down.

"Is there any good news?" I ask despairingly.

He hesitates.

"It's possible that the unstable nature of the Energy might make adapting our imported crops somewhat easier," he offers, with a hint of reluctance. "Unstable Energy like this is not exactly harnessable, but it tends to produce more extreme outcomes. From my family's records, an ancestor of mine managed to use the aftermath of something similar to a manastorm to make sudden leaps forward with some experimental plants, because one of the mutations happened to be exactly what he was looking for. I will emphasise, however, that this is very much a question of luck and very little of design."

I nod slowly.

"It's like spinning a roulette wheel and hoping it lands on the number you picked."

Elarion stares at me with a faint frown, understanding the words well enough but clearly not the concept. I suppose they don't have roulette here.

"Well, we should do what we can to turn the negative into a positive. If you think now is the best time to plant what we have of our Moriaxan crops, in order to push the odds towards them adapting to the local environment, then you have my blessing to go ahead."

He nods.

"I believe that may be the wisest option. Making a sacrifice to Luck wouldn't be a bad idea either. We need as much of it as we can get."

I feel a sudden sense of foreboding at that, as if we’ve accidentally caught the attention of something far beyond us. When Layton reaches out to me through the Bond, his immediate mental tone grim, I have a distinct feeling it’s the local version of Murphy.

Markus, you need to come back, immediately, he urges me. One of your slaves has just gone mad.
