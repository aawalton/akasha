They led me into one of those interrogation rooms that were stereotypical by virtue of being ubiquitous. It was a simple design that worked. A small room, a table, a few chairs, nothing to distract you from the interviewer. All of those items—except for the cop—were bolted to the floor, so you couldn’t apply your chair as a blunt instrument of distraction, either.

The table had a hook welded onto the surface to chain a pair of handcuffs to, but they didn’t handcuff me. Either because they wanted to make me feel like this really was just a friendly talk, or because they thought it wouldn’t stop me. I didn’t know which option was true, nor which one I would have preferred.

Baker came in and brought with him an ashtray, setting it on the table between us. He pulled an etui from his breast pocket and shook it, catching the appearing cigarette with his pinky. He stuck it in the corner of his mouth and offered me one.

“No, thanks,” I said. “That shit will kill you.”

He shrugged and flicked open a lighter. A moment later, he was puffing the first cloud of smoke into the air.

I leaned back and crossed my arms. The working of Arx’ leather echoed strangely in the small room. “So if I’m not under arrest, then why am I sitting here?”

“I just think you should stay until we have settled this matter,” Baker said, exhaling another cloud of smoke, this one in my general direction.

I coughed and tried waving it away. “Hey, could you fucking not?”

“It’s in your best interest,” he said.

The smoke smelled even worse than his breath. Rancid and heavy, and laden with alluring omens.

“Fine,” I coughed. “Let’s just get it over with before I fucking suffocate. Jesus Christ!”

He grinned and blew more smoke in my face.

For a second, I had a strong urge to light his next and final cigarette using Pharus, but I pushed it down. The decision had been made. It wouldn’t solve anything, just make matters worse.

So I gritted my teeth and answered a barrage of typical questions. Where was I from (“Home”) what was I planning on doing here (Kill Ferals) and did I have any connections to organized crime, either here or anywhere else? (Of course not.) No, they couldn’t be allowed to reach the wall. So my path of action was clear, even if it was painful. But for the city, the children, and myself to survive, it had to be done.
