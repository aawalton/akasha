The smaller bronze golem, Sir Kay, grinned and shrugged. “I’d say he knows who he is, at that. What other would drop such a line, here and now?”

“Certainly, there is only one,” Sir Bedwyr of the Wood muttered darkly.

Sir Lucan shook his head and maintained a firm expression as he gazed upon the new, silvered arrival. “You have to say it. Don’t break our protocols. We have them for a reason.”

The silver golem rolled his eyes dramatically and hopped up spryly off the table. He turned his gaze here and there and smiled grandly, throwing out his arms. “What, no music for me?! No pomp and ceremony? I’ll fix that!” In an instant, a part of himself formed into a silver harp, which he plucked expertly, making a pleasant sound fill the otherwise foreboding chambers.

He seemed to take a deep breath and began singing softly and sweetly, “This is a tale of Tristan, bold; lost his love, his soul was sold-”

A loud crack filled the air that ceased the festivity resoundingly, and even caused a gust of wind and a few alarmed yelps from unprepared magi, who no doubt thought there’d been an explosion.

All eyes were on the causer, who’d simply clapped his huge, powerful hands of granite together. Over the top of this, the dour, severe visage of Bors stared unflinchingly at the silver golem, Tristan, and spoke with a sound akin to grinding boulders, “That will suffice, Sir Tristan.”

Tristan’s eyes were wide as he flashed them side to side… and then he shrugged, banishing the harp in a puff of silvery dust and dusting his hands. “Very well then! What is the current mischief concerning us, then? I can see by your long, stony faces that time and effort are of the essence — as usual.”

By now, the nearly fainted Blind Daughter had recovered, and here she stepped forward, hands together in front of her, bowing her head serenely to the new arrival, the very picture of delicate grace and majesty. “Welcome, Sir Tristan,” she said respectfully. “Though I’m certain your brothers will have the specificity of your cooperation, allow me to say that the principal issue is atypical: rather than varied quests strung out over time, we are trying to rush ourselves in the completion of your table of knights.”

Tristan, like the rest of them, watched her a bit awestruck, his heart clearly touched by the ideal of innocent beauty she radiated. He fell to a knee and reached out to take her hand, which she promptly offered. “Fairest maiden,” Tristan declared, “I will succeed forthwith in whatsoever you require, for your beauty alone; for the virtues I have yet to know, but sense and seek with all my heart, I will heap upon you glories far in excess.”

To this, the maiden blushed profusely and covered her mouth with her free hand, letting out a soft giggle. “Oh, dear, I… didn’t expect-” She cleared her throat and gently pulled her hand away from Tristan’s, nodding her head again. “I appreciate your noble heart, Sir, but do understand you are a golem, and I am bound solemnly to my sorcery, for which I must remain bound only and completely, and to no one and nothing else, lest my focus fail in tasks so daunting, minutes of dedication may make the difference.”

Tristan rose and bowed deeply to this, his expression sober. “So you prove my detections within moments, Lifemaker.” He drew himself up tall, chin up. “Do not take to heart what corrupted tales you may’ve heard of us, by men seeking to satisfy their own weaknesses. We’ve long ago transcended our failures of character, of which even these are overblown. Myself and my own rival — whom I now count my grudging brother in loss — we never broke convention or honor in our competition.”

“What he’s trying to say long-windedly,” the green-stoned golem, Gawain, offered wryly, “is that we have been chaste for untold aeons, dedicated to our path absolutely, the same as you.”

“So we’ve tolerated such forms as these,” Lamorak of the Clay added. “They are all too appropriate.”

“We could ask for no better swan song,” Galahad offered, “than such forms as these. Truly, my dear brothers, they are what we are.”

Tristan turned to Galahad with curiosity. “You claim the end is here, yet again? And with confidence?”

Galahad simply nodded with the grinding of stone.

Tristan considered it thoughtfully before turning back to the Blind Daughter. “To conclude my previous point, milady, it would be fair to say chivalry never dies nor sleeps, regardless of whether the urges of the body, or honors of the mind, draw it forth. My appreciation, you see, resides in higher realms.”

The one known as Ananaeth on Calrenazzod — to Sir Lucan’s eyes wearing a thousand ghostly faces — smiled indulgently and offered her hands up briefly. “As you say, Sir Tristan. I know that you likely desire to meet the Goddess as well, and deepen this… appreciation, if you will. I promise the day will come, though I do not know when. Beyond this, I leave you to better minds than mine to assign your next task.”

Sir Lucan saw something of a strategic opportunity right then, stirred in part by Tristan mentioning his ‘rivalry.’ Oh yes. “Tristan, we seek the rarest of materials first and foremost, but it is unlikely we’ll obtain them before the next to awaken.”

Tristan turned to him with suspicious eyes. “Why would that be?”

“Come now, surely you know the wise counsel of the hunter who will come after your person. Counsel we surely need.”

The silver knight’s face seemed to harden. “If it’s all the same, brothers, I’d like to hear our current leads so we can get started. Time is of the essence, and I’m certain I will be of aid to the predicament.”

Sir Lucan felt the knowing gazes of the others upon him, and he smiled internally.

Getting his best isn’t so hard, now is it?

In Itha Ranon, rituals never ceased. A great one was being conducted at the very top, under the tapered roof, utilizing the power of the tower itself, along with its link to the subtower, housing the incredible energy already poured into it, converting mana to divine light energy, with efficiency unheard of and unfathomable. Samantha had been merely pleasantly surprised by the subtower’s efficiency, but other magi had gone weak in the knees — those that believed it. Others had demanded it be proven. One wizard had fainted.

In Orswyth’s case, who was now utilizing that power for the first tangible results of his new project, he’d been gobsmacked but elated.

I took it well enough, I think. The potential… It’s so far beyond what Last Light accomplished in that vision. The only question is my capability to make use of it…

At the center of the great, carved stone circle, above the control crystal diamond and its pedestal, directly above its top point, a tiny crystal orb of brightness equal to the sun radiated with beams that could blind anyone not protected — Orswyth himself needed special enchanted goggles to conduct his work.

Hands lifted high, making symbols, chanting in the tongue that was not a tongue, the winds of magic swirled and buffeted Orswyth as he channeled unthinkable amounts of light energy into that tiny sphere, which had first started as a point naked to the eye. Again and again, he was binding or ‘folding’ the energy into the tiniest of layered, hardened substances, wrapping and wrapping the new and unnamed material creation millions, perhaps billions of times.

Portability is the problem. To make use of all of this abundance, I have to be more precise in the use of space than mere mortals can fathom.

For hours of sweat and toil, he’d worked at it, this prototype, born of hundreds of virtual refinements in his head, as well as not-entirely-safe experiments. He’d blown his hand clean off, once… but thankfully, the regeneration technology of the strongest troll-blood potions had come a long way. It wasn’t something anyone wanted to make a habit out of, cost- and rarity-wise, but it did make one a bit bolder for situations like this.

I have no choice. Time is limited, and this is necessary. More than necessary! It’s magnificent! What if I bind enough energy to make multiple supreme Entropy attacks irrelevant?! What if I make the Sage’s ‘ultimate’ power completely irrelevant?!

That miraculous potential fueled his soul, drove him to succeed and to push a little bit harder, every hour on the hour, in endless obsession; he’d done everything in his power to sequester it for himself absolutely. He’d closeted himself away in supreme isolation, seeing no one, refusing any counsel save from Tashome or the needs of the Goddess, herself. He’d even told Jeeves off, to make the point stick.

It’s just too important! The Hermit, in truth and full, needs his moment. Alone. Otherwise, we’ll fail!

And more than anything, that vision drove him: seeing those girls, like daughters to him, suffer. It echoed and echoed deep into the past. Lives after lives, those he sought to protect and cherish, those he made the sacrifices for, were made to suffer this continual degradation.

No more, no more, no more!

Despite his inspiration pushing him to the brink of all that he was capable of, the long hours of the ritual took their toll on his weak body. He began to feel it near to faltering.

Knowing what catastrophe would result if he allowed that to happen, Orswyth tapered it off, ‘pausing’ the project at great effort and expenditure of resources — this was another prepared-for complexity, an unheard-of capability, within the world of rituals, for what were the true limits of magic, if you could just keep coming back to something?

I bet I could make a spell to split the world in two, given enough time…

But as was always the case, doing something more high-minded than generic destruction took orders of magnitude more effort, for far less result.

He ‘tied off’ his current efforts just before collapsing on the floor. He blacked out briefly, his head still chock-full of theorem and spellcraft peculiarities, drowning his meager mind in things only his higher self could properly fathom.

When he came to, drenched in sweat, bone weary, the tiny crystal was still there, floating above, shining dangerously intense, like a star caught down from heaven.

Orswyth sat back and laughed for a long while. The prototype was indeed a success.

No more, my siblings — this is the true last of her light to shine in this hideous creation.
