It was damp and cool at the mouth of the cave. Sakura stood beside Gilbert already prepared, her belt tightened and her hair braided. Pampli was ready too. After yesterday’s fight, he seemed more energized. It almost looked as if he liked being bigger.

Gilbert kept studying him, still unable to get used to the beast’s new size.

“You definitely shouldn’t eat anytime soon,” he said in the most serious tone he could manage. “Otherwise, we won’t be able to hide you anywhere.”

Pampli turned his head toward him and let out a quiet, offended growl.

“No, I’m not joking,” Gilbert continued with the same calm expression. “This is a strategic observation. You should listen.”

Pampli growled louder and turned away from him with the air of a creature deeply disappointed in the level of human wit.

Sakura might have smiled, but she did not get the chance, because Saorex stepped out of the dimness of the cave. He approached them with his usual silence, and only the faint clicking of his spider legs against the stone gave him away. Several spiderlings moved around behind him, still uneasy after what had happened by the river. Saorex stopped beside Pampli, let his gaze slide over the beast’s body, and, without wasting any words, did something.

In the next moment, a sturdy leather saddle was already resting on Pampli’s back. It settled onto the beast surprisingly well. Soft but strong leather, tightened straps, reliable buckles, and two large bags at the sides. But not just bags. More like additional storage compartments woven with the same storage magic as an Inventory.

Sakura blinked in surprise.

“This is...”

“A final gift,” Saorex cut in evenly. “Nothing more.”

Sakura had already opened her mouth to thank him, but he immediately waved one of his legs with irritation.

“No long word-s-s. And no goodbyes-s. Jus-st get out before the fores-st thinks of a new misfortune for you.”

Gilbert let out a breath of almost tired understanding.

“Saorex was never good at saying goodbye.”

Saorex said nothing to that. He only looked away, as though he already regretted lingering by the entrance at all.

Sakura still said quietly,

“Thank you.”

He gave no answer again, already turning back toward the cave. Sakura only nodded after him, then noticed two spiderlings peeking from behind a rock, watching them with wary and slightly sad little looks. She raised her hand and waved at them.

“Bye,” she said very softly.

One of the spiderlings twitched, as if it wanted to respond somehow, then quickly darted behind the stone again.

Gilbert stepped over to Pampli and adjusted the saddle, checking the straps. Pampli was still grumbling at him, shifting his paws in annoyance.

“You should really get used to me by now, my friend,” Gilbert remarked dryly, tightening one strap more firmly. “You and I will have to tolerate each other for quite a while.”

Pampli looked back at him with an expression that held far too much human sarcasm for a beast.

“All right,” Gilbert said, stepping back. “Time for Ogrom.”

He grabbed the saddle and tried to climb onto Pampli’s back first. Pampli narrowed his eyes — no, not even narrowed them; he smirked. In the next second, the beast gave his body a sudden, slight, but perfectly malicious jerk to the side, and Gilbert, who had not yet managed to sit properly, slid right back to the ground.

Sakura laughed quietly, not loudly, but completely sincerely. Gilbert muttered a curse under his breath and brushed off his cloak.

“Oh sure, laugh. Very funny.”

Sakura covered her mouth with her hand, still smiling.

“I think he’s hinting that I’m supposed to get on first.”

Pampli growled at once in agreement. Gilbert rolled his eyes with deliberate exaggeration, as though he considered it beneath his dignity to argue with both of them at once.

“Ah, of course. We’ve already developed a nice little hierarchy.”

This time, Sakura climbed onto Pampli first. It felt strange and much more comfortable. The saddle softened the ride perfectly. The height felt different now, more dangerous than before. Gilbert climbed on behind her without trying to argue about the order again. Pampli reacted to that with clear satisfaction.

They set out.

They rode carefully and stayed alert, moving through trees twisted around sharp stones and strange flowers shaped like crescents. After yesterday, none of them were under the illusion that obsidian creatures could appear only in especially “bad” places, and danger was expected at every step. Sakura kept looking between the trunks.

“How are we even going to hide ourselves in Ogrom?” she asked after a while. “I mean... only fools put on black cloaks and think that won’t draw attention. That kind of disguise actually attracts even more attention, especially in a city. People in hoods should be the first ones suspected.”

“It’s good that you understand how bad an idea that is,” Gilbert replied. “Black cloaks and hoods exist only to attract the wrong kind of attention. That’s true.”

Sakura listened, frowning slightly.

“So how do we hide ourselves?”

“You need to disappear convincingly,” he corrected. “A good disguise shouldn’t just hide your face. A good disguise should make you boring. Someone no one wants to look at twice. Someone easy to forget the moment they vanish around a corner. We don’t need to look mysterious. We need to look ordinary within the chaos we’re heading into.”

“Well, that sounds logical, Gilbert. But do you have any actual ideas about how to do that?”

“I do. Don’t worry, I’ve thought it through. Or I’ll improvise easily.”

“You don’t want to tell me?”

“No,” Gilbert answered honestly. “I want to surprise you. Because you definitely will be surprised. And you’ll learn even more about this world.”

Sakura nodded, interested. There was nothing ominous in his words, no dark hint, so she simply accepted that answer.

The forest around them was gradually changing. The trunks grew paler, and low bluish bushes appeared more often between them, bearing strange half-transparent berries as if filled with moon-milk. Somewhere to the right, a tiny swarm of creatures flashed past, like a mix of dragonflies and little birds. Their thin wings glowed bluish whenever they turned, and their long tails left a faint, translucent trail in the air, like pollen.

Sakura lifted her head.

“What was that?”

Gilbert glanced that way.

“A summer swarm. Don’t touch them.”

“Trust me, I wasn’t going to.”

“That’s good. First rule of survival: don’t trust everything that looks beautiful.”

Sakura watched the glowing swarm disappear between the pale trunks and, almost without thinking, focused on it the way she had already learned to focus on items and monsters.

A small line of System text flickered before her eyes.

SUMMER SWARMType: Small Aerial FaunaThreat Level: LowKnown Trait: Irritating dust. Avoid direct contact.

Sakura blinked.

“Irritating dust,” she read quietly. “Lovely.”

Gilbert glanced at her.

“You checked them?”

“I’m trying to make it a habit.”

“That’s a useful habit. Just don’t rely on it too much. The System doesn’t always tell you what matters most.”

Sakura said nothing, but she understood exactly what he meant. Yes, she had already noticed that the System often juggled information in whatever way suited it, not in whatever way helped anyone else.

The thought lingered for a few more seconds, unpleasant and useful at the same time. Then another memory pushed its way to the surface, one she should have mentioned earlier.

“Gilbert.”

“Mm?”

She was silent for a while, mentally returning to another place.

“As soon as we get those bone bracelets off you, we need to find Crowlis’s students. Nobus and Riana.”

Gilbert turned his head to her at once.

“How do you know about them?”

“I didn’t get the chance to tell you,” Sakura admitted. “There were records in the ruins. Crowlis’s personal records. I read them while I was in the castle. They mentioned them. Nobus and Riana.”

Gilbert visibly darkened.

“It would be better not to keep things like that to yourself again.”

“I wasn’t hiding it on purpose,” Sakura answered quickly. “Everything was just happening too fast.”

Gilbert was silent for a second, then gave a short nod.

“All right. That’s very important, Sakura. It’s good that you told me now.”

He thought for a moment.

“I don’t know anything about where Riana is or whether she’s even alive. But I have heard of Nobus. There was a long hunt for him. He slipped out of the authorities’ hands for years. He spread Crowlis’s teachings farther, like an infection. There were rumors that he started taking students of his own. And who knows, maybe he did.”

Sakura listened closely.

“So he could still be alive.”

“He could. I never heard a single rumor about Nobus dying.”

Gilbert was staring somewhere through the trees, looking very deep in thought.

“I know where we can start looking for Nobus,” he said at last. “And I’ll try to find out something about Riana. But first, the bracelets.”

Sakura had already been about to answer when she suddenly noticed a familiar shape ahead. Between the trees, not far away, she could make out the fisherman’s house. The same one. Brein and Lir’s house.

At first, she even felt a foolish rush of relief, as if the sight of a familiar place could mean something good. But that feeling died almost at once. The house looked wrong. It was no longer whole. Crooked, with boards torn loose and the roof sagging out of place.

And then Sakura saw the burn marks.

“No...” she said very softly.

The house was wrecked. One charred side sagged inward. A wall had blackened and partly collapsed. There were signs of fire, destruction, broken doors. Even from here it looked very bad.

Pampli slowed on his own, sensing her tension. Gilbert was already staring forward too, and his face hardened immediately.

“Don’t go any closer,” he said. “It could be a trap.”

But Sakura was already jumping off Pampli.

“Sakura!”

She did not listen. She ran toward the house, barely feeling the ground under her feet, with only the growing horror inside her. The smell of smoke was still fresh. The yard had been churned up and trampled.

“Lir!” she shouted. “Brein!”

There was no answer.

She rushed around the house, breathless with dread, and then she saw him. Brein was lying in the shadow of a tree. Sakura stopped sharply, then ran to him.

“Brein!”

The man was still alive, but barely. Blood had soaked through the clothes beneath him and into the ground. There were too many stab wounds on his body. He was breathing with difficulty, heavily, as if each breath was a struggle by itself. Sakura grabbed his hand.

“No, no, no...”

She was already reaching into her Inventory for her last healing potion, but Brein weakly squeezed her fingers.

“Don’t...” he breathed out.

“You need it. Brein, let me help you...”

He shook his head. Just barely.

“It won’t help.”

His voice was hoarse and broken. Blood showed on his lips. Sakura froze, the vial clenched in her hand.

“Who did this?”

Brein closed his eyes for a second. Even such a simple question took the last of his strength.

“They came... for you...”

Those words hit her harder than physical pain.

Sakura went pale. Of course... of course this was all because of her.

“They were looking...” he rasped. “For you. Didn’t find you... They took Lir...”

For a moment, Sakura stopped breathing.

“What?”

Brein’s hand trembled in hers.

“They took... my boy...”

Something inside Sakura tore.

“No.”

She squeezed his hand harder, as if that could keep him here, conscious, in this world for just one minute more.

“I’ll find him,” she said at once, too quickly and too desperately. “Do you hear me? I’ll help. I’ll find Lir. I’ll get him out. I swear. I won’t leave him. I...”

Brein coughed, and blood rose to his lips again. He wanted to say something more. Sakura saw it in his eyes, in the way his lips moved. She leaned closer.

“What? Brein, tell me. Where did they take him?”

But the words never came. Only a short, hoarse breath. And that was all.

The fingers in her hand went limp.

Sakura froze.

“Brein?”

He did not answer again.

Sakura stared at him for several long seconds, as if refusing to understand what she already understood. Then she felt tears running down her face.

Gilbert came up behind her quickly, still calm and hard.

“Sakura, we need to leave.”

She did not turn around.

“No.”

“This may not be over. The people who did this could still be nearby.”

“I don’t care!”

“Well, I do.”

His voice hardened. He grabbed her by the shoulder.

“Get up.”

Sakura tore herself away.

“These people helped me! They didn’t throw me out when I was exhausted. And now... because of me they took Lir! And they stabbed Brein like livestock!” Her voice cracked. “They were kind to me. They owed me nothing, and they still helped me! And now we have to help Lir! We have to!”

She could not finish, choking on air and pain.

Gilbert looked at her heavily and directly.

“I understand. But going back for him right now is not reasonable.”

“Not reasonable?” she nearly choked on the word. “They could be hurting him right now! Torturing him!”

“And if we rush after them now, they’ll either intercept us or lead us into a trap and finish us off! Then we won’t help anyone at all.”

He grabbed her hand more firmly now and literally pulled her back. At first Sakura resisted. It hurt too much. The guilt was eating at her, hurting somewhere deep in her chest and making it impossible to think clearly.

“We need to go back! We need to...”

“First we take off the bracelets,” Gilbert cut in. “First I get my magic back. Then we’ll have a chance. Only then.”

He had already pulled her farther from the house, closer to the trees, to the safer strip of forest where a tense Pampli was waiting.

“Otherwise we drive ourselves into a trap,” Gilbert said more quietly now, but no less harshly. “And we’ll die for nothing. That’s not help, Sakura. That’s panic.”

The words hurt more because they were true. Sakura stood there, trembling all over, tears on her face, with a feeling of guilt so deep it was as if she herself had brought death to that house. Pampli growled quietly and pushed his muzzle against her side. Sakura squeezed her eyes shut.

“We’re going back for him,” she said hoarsely. “Promise me.”

Gilbert held the pause.

“We’ll go back for him,” he said at last. “They’ll keep him alive, hoping for exactly one thing: that you’ll come to them on your own.”

That was not the promise she had wanted to hear.

“Then that’s what we’ll do. We’ll go right into their hands willingly,” Sakura said with grim resolve.
