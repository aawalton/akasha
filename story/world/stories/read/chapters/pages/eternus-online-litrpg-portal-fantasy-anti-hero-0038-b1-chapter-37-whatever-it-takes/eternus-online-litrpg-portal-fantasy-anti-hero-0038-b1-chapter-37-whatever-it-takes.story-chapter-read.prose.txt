The two arrows left Kate’s bow in the same heartbeat, her use of [Double Shot] dividing her aim perfectly between Tomas and Elin at the cost of 15% of her Stamina total as she rose from her combat roll and immediately dove back into the cover of the pews.

Tomas twisted aside when the first of the two arrows whistled toward his chest, though not quickly enough to escape it entirely. The uninfused barbed arrowhead pierced the outer edge of his left pauldron before carving a bloody line through his left bicep.

Elin reacted with greater precision—pivoting off her right foot and spinning the haft of her enameled spear violently across her body to knock the second projectile from the air, shattering it with a burst of splintered wood.

Well, fuck me, Kate thought sourly, glancing over the pews as Tomas staggered and Elin continued toward her.

Neither Squire had died, but the attack had given her something more useful than immediate victory: a more complete tactical assessment, just like when she’d been player-hunting in Avalon Online.

Mara, meanwhile—the main pain in Kate’s ass—remained near the side aisle with her quarterstaff raised, sustaining the golden-white illumination that had stripped away Kate’s [Night Shroud], while Tomas recovered near the opposite bank of pews and visibly struggled through the pain in his wounded arm to draw another arrow.

Elin was already advancing through the center of the chapel with her spear held low and ready, while Gareth remained beside Sara, his shield protecting both the Priestess and the golden tether feeding power through the open chapel doors.

The formation was not complicated, but it was effective.

Mara ensured Kate could be seen, Tomas punished every moment she spent outside of cover, and Elin forced her to keep moving until one of the others found an angle to strike. Gareth, meanwhile, had not so much as glanced toward Kate after returning to Sara’s side, and even the deaths of Jorry and Lacy—or the presumed possibility thereof—had not distracted him from his assigned duty.

Kate would have admired their professionalism more if it wasn’t so massively inconvenient.

Before she could formulate an immediate answer to her problems, Tomas loosed again, and Kate threw herself sideways with an abbreviated curse as the arrow blew through the pew’s backrest where her head had been. The radiant infusion within it erupted in a flash of heat, sparks, and splintered wood; forcing Kate further down the narrow alley between the benches.

At the exact same moment, Elin thrust her spear toward the same area of cover, and Kate reflexively ducked beneath the weapon—staggering mid-movement as the spearhead scraped sparks from the upper edge of her half-plate’s right pauldron.

These Squires are goddamn lethal!

Kate rolled around the end of the pew before Tomas could nock another arrow—slowed by his injury—and turned when Elin pursued immediately, spear torn back and coming around the opposite end while Kate rapidly reversed direction.

Tomas moved parallel at the same time, trying to reclaim a clear firing line, and Kate found herself trapped between the archer’s implied angle and the spearwoman’s immediate reach.

Another spear-thrust forced the Nightstalker backward, and Elin followed it with a spiteful sweeping strike aimed at the knees—but Kate leaped onto the pew and allowed the spear to pass beneath her boots, receiving an angry look from Elin before jumping down on the opposite side.

Tomas raised his bow immediately and Kate moved left in response, gaze flicking at Elin.

The spearwoman followed her movement instinctively, and Elin’s armored body accidentally interceded with Tomas’s firing line, drawing a curse from the archer.

Much better, Kate thought with satisfaction.

The Squires were dangerous because they were well-trained and well-coordinated, but that cohesion came with limitations of its own. Namely, they couldn’t afford to be reckless: Tomas couldn’t fire through Elin without risking her life, and Mara’s revealing spell channel required enough concentration that the inconvenient brunette had barely moved since activating it.

Gareth could have collapsed the entire advantage by joining the pursuit, but doing so would leave their precious Priestess exposed—which meant that as long as Kate played the angles with the pews and evaded a clear line-of-sight with Tomas, she was dealing with immediate opponents rather than all five together.

Elin, sadly, appeared to reach the exact same conclusion about Kate’s use of her as cover, and fury twisted the woman’s features when she drove her spear through the pew shielding Kate with enough force to shatter the heavy wood.

In response, Kate herself twisted away from the point as it stabbed past her ribs, then stepped onto the spear-haft to trap it before Elin could pull it back. The spearwoman growled a curse upon finding the weapon trapped, released one hand from the shaft, and tried to reach for Kate across the pew in some instinctive wish to close the distance.

Kate had no intention of letting that happen.

Instead, she drew her left-hand knife and launched herself at Elin, slamming into her and driving her blade into her ribs with a flash of [Void Strike], drawing a scream from the spearwoman. The two of them slammed into the ground amid the pews, and Kate heard voices shouting urgently while she and Elin rolled along the ground. There was only a few seconds, if that, before Elin’s friends arrived—Kate knew that.

Thankfully, their arrival was irrelevant.

Kate slammed her knee into Elin’s groin amid the struggle once, twice, and a third time until Elin shrieked upon the blunt force impacting a more delicate part of her body, and Kate responded by rolling to the right and slicing the dagger across Elin’s stomach, disemboweling the spearwoman and rolling under the nearest pew at the same moment—temporarily grateful for being far shorter than Darian.

Elin convulsed where Kate left her and the Nightstalker wiped her knife on her thigh, sheathing it and pulling her bow from its harness as she checked herself for injuries, and found a small cut where the spear had managed to slice her thigh.

Well, I guess she gave something back. Fair play.

Kate’s head rose to peer over the pew thereafter, and she saw Tomas spin toward her with an arrow before she instinctively rolled forward, cursing as the arrow punched through the top of the pew and exploded once more, showering her in burning, radiant sparks.

“Get a new fucking trick!” she shouted, while pulling an arrow from her own quiver and mentally remapping the positions of each of the Squires.

Mara remained near the altar, Gareth remained near Sara, Tomas was hunting for her, and Elin was bleeding out. Four for four, that meant she needed to handle either Tomas or Mara next.

Preferably Mara. That revealing spell is a pain in my ass.

Kate took a breath and went over her options mentally while planning her next move, fully aware of the limits on her time. Outside, Darian was still fighting Roderick if the sounds of smashing stone and angry roars were any indication, which meant that Sara was going to become progressively more of an impediment to her best friend’s efforts if left alone for much longer. Kate needed to end things fast.

Her eyes narrowed and, a second later, she rose from cover while already moving—spinning right and then forcefully pivoting back to her left as Tomas loosed the arrow she’d expected. The missile punched through the air she’d have occupied if she hadn’t forcefully reversed her movement, and Kate hissed as it sliced across her outer bicep, but drew her own arrow in response. She loosed it at Tomas, who dived immediately toward the ground, and then launched herself forward—leaping across the tops of the pews toward Mara.

Gareth, naturally, saw her coming—turning as she did and preparing to intercept her, at which point Kate smiled mirthlessly and fired an arrow at Sara, followed by a second two heartbeats later. Gareth cursed and shifted to intercept, catching the first on his shield and skidding back two paces, and catching the second immediately thereafter. He lowered the shield instantly to intercept her, but it was already too late.

Kate fired a [Double Shot] at both Mara and Tomas as the redhead archer started to rise, forcing him to fling himself away again and forcing Mara to step aside mid-channel, interrupting her spell for two precious seconds. Before she could recover, Kate sprinted forward and shifted her bow in her grip, sliding along the marble floor of the chapel, swapping her bow to her left hand, slamming it into Mara’s shins, and then drawing her right knife and slamming it upward into the woman’s chin when she stumbled forward, tearing it out and spinning to her feet while slashing open her throat in a spray of blood.

“NO!”

Tomas’ voice echoed within the chapel, but Kate was already moving.

[Night Shroud] activated with an expenditure of her remaining mana, dropping her blow 30% of her total mana pool but immediately obscuring her from sight. Tomas nocked another arrow and fired, careful not to let the potential miss threaten Sara, but Kate was already gone; diving to her right and swapping her bow to her right hand.

Before Tomas could fire again, she nocked, drew, anchored, and loosed without infusion—dropping her [Night Shroud] as the ability demanded and punching an arrow into Tomas’ left ribs. The archer staggered and Kate sprinted forward while drawing her left dagger, slamming into him before he could recover, straddling him to the chapel floor, and stabbing him repeatedly in the throat, drawing a gurgle and spasm from the redhead before he fell still.

Panting, covered in blood, and damn-near exhausted, Kate staggered up to her feet, turning toward where Gareth and Sara were staring at her with a mix of shock, rage, and horror, and blowing some blood-crusted red strands of hair from her eyes.

“What, no righteous speeches?” Kate asked while absently watching her Stamina and Mana tick back up in the corner of her vision. “No holier-than-thou castigations about my cruel villainy?”

“Words would be wasted on you, creature,” Sara replied, though her voice sounded strained, and her eyes flickered once toward the doors, beyond which Roderick and Darian were likely still fighting.

A [Party Chat] notification lit her vision a second later before she could respond.

Romulus: The Priestess is sustaining the Paladin. Null her.

Kate stared at the message for a second, and then cursed mentally at her best friend’s timing, sending back here own message.

Kate: Working on it!

“Honestly, as if I can’t see the obvious…” Kate muttered while taking a steadying breath and sheathing her knife.

Gareth stepped in front of Sara as she spoke, and the brown-haired Squire set himself in a defensive posture.

“Why are you doing this?” he asked while Kate straightened, his gaze searching her. “You may use foul magicks, but you’re not a beast, not like the one fighting Sir Roderick. Why are you helping that monster?”

Kate raised her eyebrows and chuckled mirthlessly, fetching an arrow from her quiver and nocking it without drawing. “I guess you could say it’s co-dependence,” she answered honestly, taking a breath before lifting the bow and drawing it smoothly. “We’re Travelers, after all. We stick together.”

Gareth’s eyes widened at her words, and Kate smiled at him as he raised his shield—then lifted her bow and fired, not at Gareth and Sara, but at the rope holding a large censer above their heads near the altar. The arrow punched through the rope like a hot knife through butter, and Gareth cursed, lifting his shield to protect Sara as the censer crashed down toward them.

In response, Kate activated [Double Shot] in the same moment as she poured the remainder of her mana into two more [Null Arrows] and fired.

Gareth reacted with admirable speed at the sound of the bow’s twang, and shifted his body without hesitation to shield Sara. The first arrow punched into his body through his armor, burying itself in his right shoulder—while the second, fired at the same time, passed over his left shoulder.

“Jackpot,” Kate breathed.

The second [Null Arrow] threaded the proverbial needle and slammed into Sara’s raised arm, forcing it sideways and pinning it into the material of her staff in the same instant. The Priestess let out a scream of pain, and Kate watched as black tendrils spread rapidly out from the impact point; coating both Sara’s staff and her body, threading across her arm, through her enchanted robes, and across the visible veins of her face and neck.

Gareth spun back toward Kate with a snarl, and then abruptly, all three of them froze as the staff in Sara’s grip began to vibrate dangerously, and a System warning populated across Kate’s vision—presumably theirs as well.

WARNING!

The damaged [Sanctuary Ward] is experiencing catastrophic Mana inversion!

Structural failure is imminent!

Time Remaining: 00:17

Sara immediately started frantically ripping at the arrow impaling her arm to the staff, but to no avail. Her hand holding the staff shook, and she evidently tried to summon her magic in order to impede the impending disaster, only for another cry of pain to leave her. Beneath her feet, the radiant mana snaking through the floor began to radiate with unstable pressure, and Kate immediately turned for the door.

Gareth ignored her, spun toward Sara, and seized the shaft of the [Null Arrow] while Sara shouted at him to do it. The Squire tried to snap the end of the projectile, but Kate’s Dark Mana still crawled across it in tendrils, burning his gauntlets and forcing him to release it with a pained cry as it ate at the Light Mana in his veins.

Kate sprinted for the exit in the meanwhile, intending on getting far from the explosion, only for a sudden impact to smash her into the pews mid-stride. Dazed, she looked up and saw Elin swaying in the middle of the path, her right arm over her torn stomach, and a health potion discarded in the pool of blood where she’d originally fallen. The woman was pale despite her dark skin, unsteady, and her face was locked in a rictus of rage.

“If we die,” she said around blood-coated lips, “you die too, bitch.”

If not for the situation, Kate damn-near would have applauded her moxy.

Instead, she drew her knife and hurled it at Elin’s chest before the other woman could do more than lurch; burying it to the hilt between the spearwoman’s breasts and staggering to her feet while the chapel began to groan.

Behind her, the ten-second [Nulled] effect seemed to expire as she risked a glance, because radiance abruptly returned to Sara’s palms and the Priestess slammed both hands against the blazing altar, including the arm now-freed from the arrow that had impaled it.

For a single precious moment, the Light Mana seemed to stall in its criticality, and Sara forced it away from the destabilized center of the chapel as it did, redirecting it with veins of brilliant gold across the chapel’s outer wards while Gareth settled himself between the Priestess and the collapsing structure, shield at the ready. Sara’s voice rose in desperate beseeching prayer as she worked, every syllable precise despite the blood coating her arm and the ambient energy filling the chapel.

Kate resumed running after realizing she’d hesitated, sprinting for the exit with a curse.

Then something immense struck the courtyard outside, Sara cried out, and the altar split down the middle.

Kate turned back only enough to see Gareth seize Sara by the waist, drag her backward, and shield her with his body while she fought him—protecting her with both steel and flesh as the altar turned incandescent under the pressure of the Light Mana backlash. The Priestess shouted at him to flee, but the Squire held steady, and Kate felt a pang of regret for his heroism as he raised his shield in sacrifice.

Kate reached the foremost pews as the timer reached 00:03 and threw herself behind them.

Around her, the chapel erupted into light.
