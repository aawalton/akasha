Liao Weiji trudged home beneath the starless sky, grateful that the pervasive smoke in the distance could not reach them through the formations protecting his clan. He had not seen the stars in days, never having considered how much he would miss them. It felt treasonous to think that as the stars belonged to the Yu Clan, but he had always enjoyed summer nights playing the flute for those cousins he could wrangle.

Cousin Jiang would trade pointers with me if he heard that, Weiji thought, struck by a painful longing for his cousin who spent a decade bullying him. It felt easier to move past a decade of slights, bullying, and insults now that distance and disaster lay between them. Easier did not mean simple. Weijian still carried bruises and scars from his cousin’s anger.

He took a detour to a flowering meadow with a great tree in the center. If one climbed high enough, they would find a rusted sword said to have belonged to the first true Liao Cultivator. Weiji suspected that to be a myth people perpetuated because no sword should have survived so long. Certainly not one from after the Age of Heroes.

He sniffed and wasn’t surprised to feel lightning Qi here, it was always a popular spot, but he was bewildered to feel Hua’s Qi still lingering. Why had she been here? Hua loved their clan and would defend them to death, that much he knew, but she didn’t have much reverence for it. Beyond matters of lightning and thunder, Hua’s contempt flowed freely.

Nervous energy crept up his spine. Maybe he was spending too long in one spot. That always summoned Hua’s attention and he wasn’t willing to deal with her intensity right now. He plucked a few flowers, brushing away soot that had snuck past the formations, and placed the fresh flowers in one of his robe pockets. The petals were a shade of sunburnt orange that made his chest squeeze uncomfortably.

With more speed than was polite, he ran the rest of his way home. If he could just get away, then things would be fine. The house Weiji called home was a fair distance away from the clan’s main hall, nestled in a cove of trees, hidden by tall bushes and bamboo thickets. One could walk past it easily if they did not know which stone path to follow. The sight of it filled him with a fool’s relief.

He cast his senses out and groaned in despair because no, things would not be fine. Well, that explained where the Elders were. Weijij considered turning around and never returning. It would be much, much easier. Then he felt an answering shift in Qi and knew he had been noticed.

Damn everyone in this clan. Just one night of sanity. Just one.

With a sigh, Weiji made his way to the kitchens. The dead-eyed servants were busy preparing food and drinks this late at night. The staff bowed to him when they noticed him, ‘Welcome home, Young Master,’ murmured from a dozen nervous lips. He greeted them with a nod and thanked them for their service.

Cultivating loyalty was easier than Cultivating immortality. Unlike Hua, he knew servants could poison his food. It was a good way to kill a Cultivator more powerful than you. One of the best. Especially a poison that actively targeted the spiritual veins and meridians, becoming more lethal as one used Qi. Ever since he had seen someone die from that cause, he had always been polite to the people who handled his food.

Weiji took the tray when the servants were done preparing it. This would not be the first time he had done this. He made his way to the receiving room and knocked twice before sliding open the wooden partition.

Inside was a gathering of Elders who had paused at his interruption. He bowed low to them, calmly knowing they would not kill him as they had occasionally done to servants they thought spying. There were occasional benefits to being in their employ.

“This humble grandson greets the esteemed Elders and comes bearing refreshments. Would this grandson be permitted to serve his Elders?” he asked, setting up the tray without waiting for an answer.

There was a cabinet containing cups and plates. He set them down with the grace of a boy whose backside had been beaten enough times for clinking the cutlery too loudly. Once he was done, he made to rise so he could flee and enjoy a quiet evening.

“Stay, boy. It isn’t often I enjoy the presence of a good, diligent son from your generation,” Elder Shenhou said, dashing what few hopes Weiji retained. “You’ve raised him well, Little Yu. If I could get Weilan to show a fraction of Weiji’s respect, I would be able to rest easy. Until then, these old bones will rattle every night worrying for the next generation.”

“I have tried my best with this one,” Weiji’s grandfather in his pleasantly lyrical voice. Weiji had inherited his musical talents. “After the travesty that was his father, I couldn’t allow him to be rude. Weiji will at least make it past fifty. He’s got a good head on his shoulders. A respectable young man.”

Elder Shenyi snorted, raising his newly poured wine and taking a deep sip. He sighed in satisfaction. Good, the servants had picked the better rice wine.

“As if that’s enough,” Elder Shenyi said. “Our niece Qiang is perfectly respectable, knows the Six Arts better than any in her generation, and she’s as treacherous as a viper. Not an ounce of filial piety.”

Weiji made himself scarce, kneeling a few paces away from the low table, behind his grandfather who barely spared him a glance. His grandfather was a soft-faced man who was young enough to be mistaken for the younger generation. Anyone who made that mistake very quickly learnt to regret it.

“Filial piety from a girl is asking to be disappointed,” said his grandfather.

“Uncle Xiaoyaun would be disappointed by this travesty. A child should always avenge their forefathers. He raised her after her father died in battle and what does she do? She simply watched Xiao Jiu poison her grandfather’s mind and instead of doing anything about it, she bowed her head and capitulated. She is a creature made in Liao Furen’s design.”

Hua’s father, the Liao Patriarch, had killed his elder brother supposedly after the elder first attempted fratricide. Elder Qiang would have been perhaps in her late teens when it occurred. Perhaps even watched it occur. Weiji wondered just who she was meant to avenge herself on: Lady Liao, the first to reach the Foundation, or the Patriarch who reached the peak of the Foundation. Either option was death.

Why didn’t any of you take up the sword and avenge your uncle? He thought spitefully. Liao Tong tried when she learned this and she was never heard from again. Is that not what you feared happening to you, dearest Elders?

“Yes, well, that’s the problem with women. They’re inherently treacherous.”

Elder Shenhou made a sharp gesture. “That two sheep turned out to be wolves does not mean the entire herd must be culled. Let us not fall so far as to generalise, Little Yu.”

“When the two are about to make a third, it becomes evident a pattern has emerged,” his grandfather bit back, furious at the nickname. “The girl Hua is cut from the same cloth as our grandmother and our niece. That thing they call training is a statement. She’s stealing power from us already.”

Grandfather’s words invited mocking laughter from his brother, the Elder Shenyu. Weiji silently kept the Elder’s glasses filled as they discussed treason. This was a more common occurrence than he wished to admit.

He made certain to fill up his Grandfather’s cup less often, occasionally frowning when the man gestured for more. Weiji gave him a half serving despite the glare that promised a beating in the morning.

“Being mothered by your own boy,” Shenhou said mockingly. “If you won’t give him a drink, give us the gift of song.”

Weiji refilled their glasses before sitting down and retrieving his bamboo flute. He’s had it for a year, but it was already his favourite. Thoughtful gifts were rare.

The melodic notes of his dizi served as backdrop to the Elders drinking and plotting their eventual power grab. Weiji played a rendition of The Pines of Xia, a popular comedy that belied a tragic end for the three lovers.

The one foretold to be a great hero was imprisoned and missed the war entirely. The one who was given no fate or destiny led that war and died in a final battle, killed by the escaped prisoner, and remembered eternally for his great acts.

And the last was only found in the spaces between, in the things untold: the howling winds when the trees were cut down to power the war machine of the great hero’s army; the curses that plagued the hero’s army as Xia was razed; and who spoke those last words, ‘who fears death but the tyrant of Xia?’ at the grave of the great hero who led the war.

It was a painfully unsubtle epic. Weiji loved it and so he played his heart out, reciting the verses of the epic in his head. Who amongst these Elders would be the prisoner, the tyrant, and the forgotten? Did they know the roles they meant to play or were they ignorant?

Elders Shenhou clapped when Weiji concluded his performance. Weiji bowed in thanks and accepted the compliments of his elders.

“This is as good a place as any to end. Come to my pavilion tomorrow, grandnephew, and I will have some pills for you. A performance so great should be rewarded.”

As the foremost Elder, it fell to Shenhou to dismiss the gathering. It would have been polite to give the courtesy to the host, but courtesy was the game of the weak, and Elder Shenhou was the most powerful. Weiji stayed behind as they rose and left the receiving room.

His throat was terribly dry despite all the drinks flowing. He poured himself a cup of wine and drank as he waited. Had enough time to pour another cup before hi his grandfather Shenyu returned without his smile.

“Self-righteous, pompous son of three fathers,” he muttered, losing any affectation of drunkenness. “The only pleasant thing about tonight was your dizi and that fool couldn’t even appreciate it.”

“Your grandson is honoured by your praise. Might this humble one inquire as to your anger with honoured Shenhou?”

“You can stop pretending to be polite. If there’s a single member of this clan who doesn’t curse worse than a sailor, then I’ll be surprised. We’re not those pompous Yu bastards who think getting fucked hard by a dragon will make them eligible for the Dragon Throne.”

“That is how succession tends to work, Grandfather,” Weiji said cheekily, throwing a bright smile at his grandfather’s withering gaze. “Why are you irritated with Elder Shenhou? I thought you agreed with him that Hua could not remain as our leader.”

“We act with similar ends for different reasons. He wants personal power. He doesn’t give a shit about the Clan beyond how much wealth it can generate for him. Forever grasping at the next tael instead of securing our supremacy for generations to come.”

And who pays for your bastards, Grandfather, if not him? Weiji thought but would never say about his young uncles toddling about in some brothel.

“Would Liao Hua not be the right choice, then? She has the power to lead us.”

“Her potential is not in question. I’m opposed to Liao Furen’s infection sinking deeper into the Clan. Weijiang is the true heir, not this farce called Weilong. If not him, then any number of grown adults could have been the next Lord Liao. Even you would be more appropriate. This power grab is shameless, even for her. I don’t hate the girl, but I don’t trust any creature raised by Liao Furen. And I don’t believe she’ll be any less of a monster in the future.”

Weiji nodded. “Then what shall be done? The story of Liao Weilong, the heir who purified the dead and restored order, already spreads across the clan. The Magistrate accepted her title and unless the Governor countermands it, she will officially be the acting lord as far as the Imperials are concerned. Which means the Yu Clan will know and accept that the Radiant Lightning Body has a new heir.”

“Unfortunate, yes. Liao Furen acted faster than anyone could anticipate. This was out of character for her. The last time she kicked up a fuss was choosing Hua as a student and now she does this. Her plots are always longer than you imagine. That woman is a monster.” Lightning sparked down his grandfather’s hand as he grew angrier. Weiji stayed very still. Waited for his grandfather to calm. “She trapped us in a corner before we could react. No, we can’t allow anything but unity when viewed by outsiders. That girl Hua will select you as her second if only to appease my generation. Liao Furen can only force us to eat shit so long before we spit it back at her. She’s done nothing but hog resources for a century and she still has the temerity to order us about like we are toddlers.”

Weiji waited a few moments. There were no sparks. It was safe to speak.

“As you say, Grandfather,” he said, just as his grandfather would have begun his next rant.

“Don’t interrupt me again.”

“This servant could not imagine interrupting an elder.”

“You have your mother’s cheek. I’d have you caned if that bastard Shenhou didn’t spend the evening complimenting you. If I did, he’d be on my case come morning.”

“The Elder is a spring of endless wisdom.”

His grandfather reached across the table and cuffed Weiji. It was a gentle hit that still made Weiji flinch. They had big hands in their family. His father, his grandfather. Maybe his child would learn it firsthand as well if history repeated.

“You did well by stalling her at the magistrate,” Grandfather said, indifferent to Weiji’s racing heart. “She thinks she achieved something of import there. It was useful to show the other elders the tyrant she’d become if given a hint of power. Even though I don’t remember telling you we’d be having it.”

I didn’t even know about the meeting. I was just doing a good job because if I didn’t, everyone would blame me.

“Perhaps I have had an excellent teacher or perhaps it was a lucky coincidence.”

“Flattery will get you far, boy. Use it on her. Hua, that is. You’ve done well to enter her circle of trust. I’d hoped it would help you when you were married to her, but now it will serve us in a different way. Find out everything Hua means to do in her grandmother’s name. I will not have that girl be the ruin of our clan.”

Weiji bowed low to hide his frown at the thought of that marriage. “Yes, honoured grandfather. It will be done.”

“Hua thinks she can banish me without consequence. Madness. Everything she does is madness. This plan of hers will get us all killed when the mortals revolt.”

“Is it so foolish a plan?” Weiji asked, confused.

Seizing the supply of food to distribute it properly seemed a reasonable thing to do after a disaster, especially with winter a few weeks away. The mortals might grumble, and they might put a few down, but most would see reason. Weiji had learned they were kind and generous when given the chance.

“Her plan to steal all the gold and silver in our territory. If she wanted to start a rebellion, she could have just declared herself Empress and been done with it.”

Weiji masked his complete shock by drinking from his cup. The burn matched his startlement. Stealing what? How much gold? How? When?

That had nothing to do with the agreement they had reached with the Magistrate. Which meant either Hua was playing a different game entirely or she had been cornered and came up with a more audacious plan.

It would explain the meeting of elders that Hua interrupted and the rumours that had abounded after it.

The next few days were going to be miserable for Weiji. He just knew everything would fall on his shoulders to deal with as they always did.

“That new servant of hers, what can you tell me?” his grandfather asked.

“Very little. He is a foreigner descended from one of the Jurchen tribes to the far north. He’s risen above his blood to train as a scribe and historian. Most likely, he made a living selling information to concerned parties.”

“Can he be bought?”

“Anyone who clings so closely to power has a price. I do not know his, but I can find it.”

“Good, then do so. I’ll reward your loyalty well with pills when you do. It should unlock your fourth Meridian.”

Weiji affected a smile. “My loyalty will be swifter and fiercer than the flows of the Yellow River.”

It was well past midnight by the time Weiji could retire to his room. In the corner, he had built a small shrine. He knelt before it and retrieved the flowers he had plucked. He placed them in a bronze bowl filled with fresh water this morning. The orange blossoms would be beautiful when dawn’s light struck it.

She had liked these orange flowers the most. His love, his heart, the kindest person Weiji knew.

At least, she had claimed to love them. Maybe she was just entertaining Weiji’s poor gift, but he liked to believe it was appreciated.

She had given back in her own way and gifted him the dizi he kept so close. Her favourite song had been The Pines of Xia and so Weiji had loved it as well.

Do you remember the promise we made

In the quiet meadow and gentle bower

to watch the pines grow.

There is no peace without you

only the pines that grow and grow

Oh how they grow, my dear, I promise they grow

They are saplings now, growing from the ashes of their forebearers,

When they are old, fallen leaves will tell the story of tyrants and heroes

and remember, who you were

Before you burnt down the Pines of Xia.

A final verse for a poem that should have been concluded. Weiji had always found the original ending too bleak. Had hoped to finish it. Weiji had journeyed to the city to get her opinion on the day the world. Had he gone earlier, even an hour earlier, he might have seen her again. Saved her. Avoided this gaping emptiness that he could hardly fill.

Weiji lit his incense and prayed to her memory. He hoped she would hear each word for love he hoped to say. He hoped that there were endless pines and sundrenched meadows where she was. Away from this world full of politics and betrayal. Would he be able to stand before her with the blood already on his hands and the blood he would spill in the future?

Could she forgive a kinslayer?

That was the problem with his loyalty; it was without limit. And Weiji was loyal to the Liao Clan. One day, Hua would face the Shen Generation, and on that day, Weiji would have to make a choice. Whatever choice he made would lead to a new shrine being built.

Weiji hoped against hope and he wept for the kin he would betray.
