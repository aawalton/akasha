One might have thought that in their current situation, things like dreams and visions would have been taken more seriously. And perhaps they were. However, the fact that it was coming from Brennon seemed to discredit the whole thing somehow, and with a muttered curse, Magda scowled.

"I can't believe you interrupted our meditation for a nightmare. Are you a child?"

"No." Brennon shook his head. "This was no ordinary nightmare. It was like a vision."

"So, you're not sure?" Baxter asked, his tone thick with skepticism.

Brennon glared at him and gritted out, "No. I'm not sure. But it was very scary."

"And you're a grown-ass man," Destra scowled from her corner, the remains of her divination ice mirror scattered all around her. Obviously, she hadn't had much luck with her own attempts at foresight.

From his meditation spot, Aodhán sighed. "Why don't you tell us about this dream of yours, and we'll tell you there are no monsters beneath the earth."

"But that's just the thing." Brennon's expression grew dead serious. "There are monsters beneath the earth. Four of them, to be exact. I saw them in the vision."

Magda rolled her eyes. "Okay, Brennon, this is not a game. We do not have the time—"

"Do I look like I'm joking?" Brennon cut her off, his expression grave. "I'm serious here. I need you all to take this seriously. There are monsters beneath us." He turned to look Aodhán in the eye. "Believe me, man, I'm not playing. A man called Arkhan showed me the dream."

"What!?" Aodhán's entire demeanor shifted from casual concern to sharp alertness in an instant. He exchanged a glance with Baxter, whose eyebrows had climbed up to his hairline, then immediately turned back to Brennon. "Tell us everything you saw in the vision."

"What?" Destra's confusion was evident as she looked between them. "Surely, you both don't believe this nonsense, right?"

"It's not nonsense," Aodhán replied, his expression grim. "We know who Arkhan is. He's a friend and a dream awakened. If Brennon received a dream from him, then it's important."

He studied Brennon, who seemed even more terrified now that he realized the vision hadn't been just a dream, and pressed him. "Tell me everything that happened in the dream, Brennon. Every single detail. Right from the beginning."

Brennon swallowed hard, his earlier playfulness completely gone. What he described next was the stuff of nightmares. Four powerful creatures, further enhanced by dark energy, were currently imprisoned deep within the earth, surrounded by their spawns—each one at the advanced class. The creatures themselves were supposedly at the 75th tier, strong enough to level a small city on their own.

And they were all breaking free.

"No." Destra breathed the moment Brennon finished speaking, her face as white as a sheet. "What do you mean they're breaking free?"

"The abyssal pins," Aodhán responded, having deduced as much.

"Yes." Brennon nodded, his voice growing hoarse. "Every pin we pull out of the ground weakens their prison. Already, they're all awake. Their spawns agitated. It's only a matter of time before the earth splits open and they break free."

"Fuck!" Baxter swore. "This is very bad. How are we supposed to defeat tier 75 creatures and their fucking spawns? The highest-tiered person in this cursed realm is what? Tier 60?"

"Tier 50, actually." Aodhán corrected in a whisper, and they turned to him in shock. Then anger. Then resignation.

"Oh, we are so fucking fucked!" Destra whispered, clutching her chest as if she wanted to stop her heart from racing.

Dreadful silence reigned for a moment as the weight of the looming calamity pressed down on each one of them like a physical thing.

Aodhán forced himself to breathe and think. "Is that all that was in the dream?"

Brennon nodded but then paused. "No, the boy… Arkhan, he asked me to meet him at the twin-peaked mountain. He said they were gathering people together to fight as one, and he asked me to spread the news."

Aodhán and Baxter exchanged another glance. They had no idea where the twin-peaked mountain was, but it was obviously some sort of landmark. One where people were already gathering.

"Arkhan said they were gathering people," Baxter reasoned quietly, his gaze intense on the crackling fire. "Who's 'they'?"

Aodhán shrugged. "Maybe Sebastian. Maybe even Daruk. I don't know."

Another beat of silence. So much silence.

Destra broke it a moment later, her voice shaky. "Gathering together is dangerous, especially for you, Aodhán."

She didn't need to explain. Aodhán understood her perfectly.

There were still so many people who had taken the oath out there. And even more resurrecteds. Gathering together was a recipe for disaster, especially if the cultists decided it would be fun to activate their sleeper agents right within the battle.

How could he focus on fighting off creatures and their spawns if he was constantly worrying about getting stabbed in the back by a supposed ally?

"Fuck." Aodhán blew out a breath, his heart already sinking into despair. But then he realized one crucial detail they were all ignoring. They were all so caught up with being captives and harvests to be killed that they had forgotten this was some sort of twisted entertainment for the cultists.

Frowning, he raised his wristband to his eye, and the likability ratings revealed themselves.

89% Likability

0% Neutral

11% Detest

With a small burst of excitement, Aodhán realized it had gone up. The last time he'd checked, it was at 78%. But Aodhán wasn't really concerned with the percentage itself. His gaze turned to the number of ratings.

Total ratings: 56,258 votes.

His eyebrows climbed up to his hairline in shock. Fifty-six thousand people were watching this abominable show.

Aodhán couldn’t believe there were so many cultists. But then he realized that if this many people had rated him on this show, then more than likely, there were many more cultists who hadn’t rated yet. Many who might not even be watching.

That realization put into perspective the number of people in the order, and Aodhán’s grimace deepened. The cultists weren’t a group of small-time rebels who had gotten lucky.

They were a formidable army, one with over fifty thousand members. Maybe double.

But if that was the case, then this show was important to the cultists, and the last thing Jethro wanted was to piss off all these people by killing off their favorite prisoners.

Aodhán wasn't sure how he felt about being so liked by the cultists, but if it worked in his favor, then he would gladly embrace it.

From what he had seen so far, the cultists couldn't just kill people. They had rules they were following and variables they couldn't manipulate. But more importantly, they had to make things interesting for their viewers.

After all, putting a bunch of weaklings against four tier seventy-five creatures without any hope for survival was just not the way to go. There had to be a way out somehow. A way for them to survive this. They simply had to find it.

He looked back at his comrades, all of whom were staring at him as if he were their salvation, and when he stared at them blankly, Destra said. “Your face lit up right now. Please, tell us you’ve somehow found a way to spirit us all away from this cursed place.”

"I haven't." Aodhán chuckled. "But I've come to the conclusion that it would be foolish for the cultists to kill us all."

"Maybe they don't want to harvest us anymore." Magda whisper-cried as if being harvested was the better option here. "Maybe the Fated has had his fill."

Aodhán began shaking his head, but as if a lightbulb came on in his mind, he paused. His eyes widened, and he literally burst out in laughter. "By Raol, how did I forget about that?"

"Forget about what?" Brennon asked the question the others were dying to ask, but Aodhán only laughed harder.

How had he not realized it? He had wondered why the bodies weren't disappearing for harvest, but Magda's words had just opened his mind to a truth he suspected Jethro would kill to keep hidden. And that was the fact that his spirit must have reached its limit.

Even before the harvest tournament began, Aodhán had sensed just how strained Jethro's spirit was, and after harvesting the seals and innate skills from over thirty people, he must have reached a limit—one he could only overcome by bonding another transmigrant or finding another way to increase the power of his spirit.

No wonder the cultists had brought the dying back to life. Their spirits would have gone to waste without Jethro to harvest them.

His smile widened, and he looked at his comrades. "Cheer up, bums. We are not going to be killed off."

"And you know this how?" Magda asked in confusion.

"It doesn't matter," Aodhán replied. "What matters now is that we begin preparing for how to survive the coming calamity. When day breaks, I think it's best we begin searching for the twin-peaked mountain. Danger or not, we cannot afford to fight off the creatures as a small group. I'll figure out my safety later."

"In the meantime," he summoned a cloud into existence and began to lay down on it, "I need to have a discussion with Arkhan."

***

Aodhán sighed as his body hit the soft cloud, his spine cracking as he settled deeply. A soft groan escaped his lips as the stress of the day began draining away, sloughing off him like dead weight.

After nearly twenty hours of constant movement and stress, his body was ready to hit the rocks.

His shoulders slumped, and his breath settled into a soft, familiar rhythm as his eyes drifted closed. But Aodhán didn't plan to sleep, despite how much his body craved it. No, he needed to have a conversation with Arkhan, and for that, he needed to be awake even in his dream.

The lying-down gatha Master Gyatso had taught him came to mind, and Aodhán began reciting it, smiling faintly as the words bubbled to the surface of his mind as if from a faraway dream. It was a dream technique he had learned back in the monastery for maintaining mindfulness in a dream.

He hadn't used it in a long time, but the words came to him as if he'd just recited them the day before.

Lying down, I know this is lying down. May my mind remain clear and aware—

"What are you doing?" Destra's voice cut into his thoughts as she moved closer, shock apparent in her tone. "Are you chanting an incantation?"

"It's not an incantation," Aodhán replied with his eyes still closed. "It's a mindfulness technique for dreams."

Destra's frown deepened, but she didn't probe any further, granting Aodhán the space to continue.

Aodhán let his mind begin to drift once again before continuing the recitation.

Lying down, I know this is lying down.

May my mind remain clear and aware.

As my body sleeps, may consciousness stay awake.

Breathing in, breathing out, mindfully present.

He repeated the Gatha slowly, each word deliberate and focused—as soft as a whisper on his lips. The words anchored him as his consciousness slipped deeper into sleep. The comfortable softness of the cloud beneath him faded. The crackling of the fire grew distant. His breathing deepened and then steadied, but his mind remained awake. Alert. Poised on the threshold between waking and dreaming.

He lingered in that state for a moment. And then he was elsewhere. In a dream. His dream.

Only this time, it had taken on a form he was intimately familiar with—one he hadn't seen in nearly a year.

The courtyard of Vat Pha Chaomka—the monastery where he'd spent the largest portion of his childhood. Ancient stone buildings surrounded him, their weathered walls covered in intricate lotus carvings.

The familiar scent of incense filled the air, and somewhere in the distance, temple bells chimed with the soft resonance he remembered from morning prayers.

Nostalgia bubbled within him, but the emotion was almost immediately snuffed out by the veil of shrouded mind.

Nevertheless, Aodhán still took in the sight around him with quiet admiration. The courtyard was exactly as he remembered it, down to the worn stone path beneath his feet and the gentle murmur of water from a leaking tap.

The whole place radiated a profound sense of peace—cut off from the harsh reality of the world beyond its walls.

"Impressive."

Aodhán turned to see Arkhan leaning against a worn pillar at the edge of the courtyard, a strained frown on his forehead and dark circles shadowing his eyes.

He smiled at Aodhán when their gazes locked. "I was expecting another jumble of mismatched dreams, but I'm surprised to see you have some preliminary control over yours."

"Thank you," Aodhán replied politely, noting how tired Arkhan looked. "You look terrible."

"I feel worse." Arkhan smirked and let his body slide down the pillar dramatically until he came to rest on the cobblestoned floor. "Who knew dream-watching could be so stressful?"

"You're trying to inform everyoneof the danger as soon as they fall asleep."

Arkhan sighed. "I see you already know."

"I'm in Brennon's company. He told me of the four creatures."

Arkhan nodded. "We haven't been able to get to the telepath yet, so I'm having to do the work. We need to gather as many people as we can, one way or another."

Aodhán suppressed a scowl at the mention of the telepath, realizing that if Sebastian's plan worked, he would have to be in the same vicinity as a telepath with all his dirty secrets on display.

Oh, what a mess.

Exhaling deeply, he asked, "What about Daruk? Have you managed to get across to him?"

"Not yet." Arkhan shook his head. "Your brother doesn't seem to need sleep. I've contacted Yuna Sōsei, though. They're apparently in the same company, so I'm sure he's been informed. I don't know if he's coming, though. Some people have been uncooperative."

"As is usually the case." Aodhán frowned, thinking. After a moment, he said, "I need an image of the twin-peaked mountain so I can better identify them from a—"

The image appeared before he could even finish speaking, and Aodhán thanked Arkhan. "Is there any other important information you think I should know?"

Arkhan grimaced. "Sebastian has allied himself with a serenity witch, and she thinks it's possible to convince one of the creatures to fight on our side."

Aodhán frowned at the thought, his mind contemplating the idea. "Do you think it's possible?"

"I doubt it." Arkhan shook his head. "I saw the dreams of those creatures. I felt their hatred. They want us all dead. But even if it were possible, who would we even send? Anyone who goes down there will die before they can even get a word in."

"Right." Aodhán replied, but he was no longer listening. Arkhan’s words had sparked an idea in his mind—a daring, reckless idea that just might work. Mind racing, he asked slowly. “There’s a dragon among them, right? What’s its affinity?”

"No idea." Arkhan shook his head. "I didn't bother waiting to find out. I know it spewed out black flames that burned space itself. So, maybe void affinity?"

Aodhán smiled. “I thought you didn’t fear death.”

“I don’t,” Arkhan glared at him. “But I’d rather not be torn from limb to limb by a creature the size of a house.”

“That seems appropriate.” Aodhán chuckled, but then he frowned. “What are our chances of survival according to Sebastian if we gather together?”

“Thirty percent,” Arkhan grimaced. “It’s abysmally low, but it’s the best probability Sebastian could find. Any other tactic, and our chances drop by at least half.”

Aodhán grimaced, not liking those numbers at all. Thirty percent was too low for the risks of gathering. Not only would he be giving a telepath access to his secrets, but he would also be opening himself up to betrayal from the agents of the cultists and the resurrected.

“We’ll be safer together,” Arkhan muttered the words as if he had repeated them a thousand times. “What chances would a small group have against tier 75 creatures?”

Aodhán’s grimace deepened as he was forced to agree. Arkhan was right. Their chances of survival were truly low. But if they could convince one of the creatures…

The thought simmered in his mind for a moment before Aodhán spoke again. "Thank you, Arkhan. You're right. We are safer together."

"That we are." Arkhan nodded. "We'll be expecting you."

Arkhan left his dream a moment later, and Aodhán immediately summoned Varéc's consciousness into his dream space.

Varéc roared loudly as he appeared, and Aodhán smiled. "What say we embark on a dangerous mission, Varéc?"

Varéc growled in excitement, and Aodhán's smile widened. "I hope you have great diplomacy skills, Varéc, because I need you to help me convince a dragon to fight for us."
