Despite his desire to get home already, Ezra knew he needed to at least do something to cut through the worst of the smell, and he wasn’t going to waste a whole three silver on it. Instead, with as brave of a face as he could muster, he found a barrel of water that wasn’t entirely frozen over, got permission to use it, and proceeded to suffer through a very quick rag bath. The water was frigid, and he felt the gooseflesh crawl up and down both arms as his hands touched the water. That was only the beginning, and with brisk, shivering movements, Ezra washed the worst of the filth of travel and battle from his body as he fought to keep from shivering.

Without going naked, only stripped down to his breeches, Ezra quickly found himself miserably cold, even as he drew on the aura from a nearby fire to fortify and warm himself. That only made it surviveable, not at all comfortable. Fortunately, it was only the work of a few minutes to do the preemptive cleaning, knowing that he could get a reasonably priced bath, if not a free one, once he got back to The Dragon’s Toenail.

It wasn’t long before he was back in his uniform coat, which had had the most easily cleaned stains and debris removed from it already. He wasn’t thrilled with how he smelled, but again, he knew he could trust the Madam to have a better priced and possibly more efficient option available than to take it to a fancy inner-city washer or to wash it himself in this frigid bucket.

“I thought you said you didn’t have a girl?” Boyd laughed. “But you’re still going through all this trouble, so there’s gotta be a reason. Do you have a man? Or maybe trying to make some money on the side? I’m sure you could with that face.”

“I’ll never fucking prostitute myself,” Ezra snapped, the response more vehement than he intended.

“Whoa there,” Boyd raised his hands in apology. “I was making a joke. I guess it’s not the kind that you appreciate. My bad.”

Ezra took a calming breath and shook his head. Then, he explained, “Mum was a prostitute, threw me away sometime around being eight years old. I decided back then I’d never take a prostitute or be one.”

“Mommy issues, huh?” Boyd grinned, though the expression was more of understanding than of insult or even teasing. He continued, “Me ’n Cord have daddy issues instead. The kind most people have.”

“Drink of choice?” Leah asked.

“Worse,” Boyd shrugged. “He didn’t care for drink. Bloodweed, though, he was a fiend for that.”

Ezra couldn’t even try to hide his reaction to that. Bloodweed made people violent, thirsty for blood. It was the reason it got its name, and there was no such thing as a casual user.

“The days when he got it were just as bad as the days when he couldn’t,” Boyd said, his eyes obviously on a scene from the past. “Anyways. My bad on the joke.”

“You didn’t mean anything by it,” Ezra deflected. “Thanks for the apology.”

“But are you going to see a girl?” Boyd insisted. “If you’re just going to see a friend, you wouldn’t care as much about it.”

“I’m going to the woman who raised me,” Ezra shrugged. “Wanna show her that I’m able to take care of myself.”

“Well, I guess that’s fine. If you come back early, I’m sure Cord’ll be around.”

“Just ’cause you’re gonna go off to pine after—”

“I’m not pining!” Boyd cut him off.

“Just ’cause you’re gonna go off to beg for Cassandra’s attention and affection,” Cord repeated, making it worse, “doesn’t mean that I’m not gonna see Ma and the kids. Though I’ll probably come back today to make sure that she doesn’t wanna buy food for me from what I give her.”

“I’ll be back today as well,” Leah said. She offered nothing more about what she would be going to do.

“Well, then I’ll be seeing you all,” Ezra nodded as he decided against taking his spear and shield. Instead, he put his knife on his waist and collected his cane from their bunkroom. Quick goodbyes were all they gave each other before Ezra left first, the others soon to follow, he was sure.

Even dirty and patched, his uniform was easily recognized on the streets, and nobody crossed his path as he made his way to the checkpoint outside of the inner city. Those people who did look at him nodded and went on their way or made quiet thanks for his service. It was weird, uncomfortable. He wasn’t sure what to say to those people, and he much preferred those who either ignored him or even those who seemed to hate him. He was used to that.

When he finally arrived at the checkpoint to enter the slums, the guards looked down their noses at him.

“Not even a single wound on him, huh?” one said to the other.

“Little pretty-faced princess signed up just to take the Count’s money,” the other agreed. “Bet your battlefield’s the bedroom, huh?”

“Shut the fuck up and let me pass,” Ezra said, staring at them.

“Or what?” the first shook his head.

“If you start shit here, you’re not gonna kill me in broad daylight,” Ezra sighed. “And since you won’t kill me, if you pick a fight, I’ll either win or lose, but either way, I’m gonna go tell my squad, and the group of ten who killed a force of five hundred goblinoids two weeks ago won’t mind adding two more little wannabe goblins to our record. Who knows, maybe the captain’ll give us honors for cleaning the streets.”

“The little princess is gonna go get his harem to fight because he can’t, huh?” the second said. “Go on, find your next lay, make sure to bring the pay back for us.”

Despite how angry it made him, Ezra said nothing more and shouldered past them, making sure to infuse some aura in his step as he did so. He didn’t break his stride at all, though both men were sent sprawling with his easy steps. A part of him mentally cursed his anger, his inability to control himself. He knew that picking fights with guards was never going to end well, and this was just another instance of him making trouble for himself. On the other hand, he congratulated himself for not actually swinging on them.

Behind him, both guards stuttered and stammered as he continued on his path, but they didn’t try anything else. With a heart that grew lighter with every step, Ezra made his way toward his home. He’d been successful on the campaign, and he had news that Adam would be returning safe as well. It was as good of news as he could be bringing.

As he made his way through the streets, it was strange how quickly he found himself adopting old habits, avoiding blind corners and noting every movement that approached him. He didn’t lean on his cane as he would have if he wore better camouflage, but as it was, he couldn’t hide the fact that he was wearing a soldier’s uniform. Instead, he walked with purpose, his every step quick and firm as the muddy ground underfoot sucked at his boots.

A part of him wished he’d crossed paths with the asshole mugger who’d sold him out, but he didn’t have any such luck. The two people who started to draw closer to him were met with withering glares, and they quickly abandoned any thoughts of attempting to pickpocket, mug, or whatever else they planned to do to him. It wasn’t long before Ezra was back home, his heart in his throat. It’d been two months without actually talking with anyone from there, and the thought that he was going to be actually back in front of the Madam, Tilly, and everyone else was a strange one.

Instead of continuing to drown in his nervousness, Ezra stepped forward and pushed the door open.

“Hello?” he called out. “It’s me. The kid who was once a wannabe bouncer?”

It was right around lunchtime, and Ezra was surprised to see that the doors weren’t already open, but were unlocked. Looking in, the common room didn’t look like it had been set up for lunch, which was doubly strange. Sure, lunch was never as profitable as dinnertime, but plenty of people came, more than enough to provide enough value to open.

“Who…? Oh shit, Madam! It’s Ezra!”

“Language, Liam,” came the Madam’s chiding voice.

Ezra couldn’t help but laugh at the reaction from Liam as he shouted, but the unnatural air in the bar was more than enough to quickly destroy that laughter. The soldier pushed his way all the way into the room before closing the door behind himself and walking forward. By the time the door had closed behind him, several heads had popped out of the kitchen, the Madam’s the first and Matilda’s immediately after.

“Welcome home,” came the expected, deliberately calm words from the Madam. Tilly, however, had a much more volatile reaction.

“You didn’t say a damn thing before you disappeared, and all we know is that you’re going to fight in a war! Are you okay? Where’s Adam? When did you get back?”

As she said it, the freckle-faced girl rushed forward and took Ezra into a hug. She held him for less than a second before she pulled back and looked up into his face, her expression screwing up in disgust.

“You reek.”

“I got home today,” he smiled. “Some of the other soldiers thought I should pay three silver for a bathhouse before I came here. As for Adam, he’s alright. The levies are taking longer to get home, but I saw him a week ago, and he was just marching back.”

“That’s good he’s—wait. How much will inner city idiots pay for a warm bath?” Tilly demanded, forgetting about the news and looking at the Madam. “You need to start a bath service.”

“If this was a brothel, I would,” came her answer, her tone even. “However, The Dragon’s Toenail is not that yet, and men who request a bath also desire a woman to go with it.”

“But you’re still disgusting,” Tilly said, something in her eyes looking uncomfortable as she shifted the attention back to Ezra.

“I was hoping I’d be able to get a bath here,” he admitted. “I wiped the worst of the filth from traveling off, but didn’t want to have to wait around and hope to get a bath in the headquarters when I could come here.”

“You’re always welcome here,” the Madam said. “While you are here, however, I will not have a job for you.”

“I’m still technically a soldier,” he said with a laugh. “I can’t imagine that the Count will take kindly to me defecting to go back to my old ways.”

“I imagine that you’d have signed a contract,” she agreed. “Now, let’s get you bathed. Would you like us to wash the coat as well?”

“If you’re willing to tell me what that price would be,” Ezra laughed, “I might’ve made a little bit of money, but I’m no inner city idiot and I’ll wash it myself if I need to.”

“Five copper,” came the answer, the Madam’s tone approving. “The family discount.”

Ezra nodded agreement as he unbuttoned the coat. Even though it had been big on him when he’d first enlisted, the two months of solid eating and continuous exercise had served to fill him out from a scrawny but somewhat muscular kid into a more firmly muscled adult. Now, the uniform coat fit him quite well.

“Pilar blessed you,” Tilly said, breathing out as she seemingly unthinkingly reached out to touch Ezra’s shoulder. He was wearing a tight, very short-sleeved shirt that exposed all of his muscles with how they pulled against the fabric. Despite the strange air in the inn that he wanted to talk about, he couldn’t help but grin and flex a little, the muscles he’d worked so hard for over the past months since meeting Jormun standing out.

Before he could say anything, Matilda flushed and took his coat, hanging her head as she speed-walked out of the common room and into the kitchen.

The Madam allowed the barest hint of a smile to color her face as she watched Matilda’s escape.

“We have missed you. Now, take your bath, and then we’ll get you some real food, shall we?”

Ezra was about to respond when the door slammed open behind him and a voice he didn’t quite recognize spoke and sent his already frayed patience into an incandescent rage.

“Knifefingers’s done asking. You bend the knee or you get lost. Just comply, okay? I don’t want to hurt anyone, and you’ve got one more day to start working as a whorehouse before things get messy.”
