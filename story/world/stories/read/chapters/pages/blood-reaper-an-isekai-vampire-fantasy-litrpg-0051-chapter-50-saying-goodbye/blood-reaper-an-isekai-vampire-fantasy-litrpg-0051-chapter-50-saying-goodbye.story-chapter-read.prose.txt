As the wind howled through the Highlands, Ace stood at the edge of the crater where Rachel had died.

He had been standing here for what felt like hours. They needed to leave. They needed to find cover. They needed to get away from this place before the carnage drew more monsters to the area.

But he couldn’t move.

He could only stare at Rachel’s remains.

Pieces of the former CEO lay scattered across the crater. Her expensive suit—once a pristine charcoal gray—was now nothing more than blood-soaked scraps of fabric clinging to unidentifiable lumps of goop. Clumps of her hair dotted the depression, caught in the soil and fluttering in the gusts tearing through the hillside. Bone fragments gleamed against the dirt, impossibly white against the reddish-brown stain spreading through the soil.

As before, the only recognizable part of Rachel was her left hand, severed at the wrist and hanging limply over the crater's edge. Her chipped, manicured nails caught the fading light.

Ace forced himself to look at every piece. He cataloged each fragment, each splash of gore, each scrap of clothing. The metallic tang of fresh blood mingled with the rich musty aroma of disturbed earth. His stomach churned, but his expression never shifted. Eight years of combat had taught him how to witness death without breaking. He had seen men torn apart by IEDs, bodies riddled with bullet holes—all friends and brothers-in-arms reduced to unidentifiable pieces.

But this was different.

This was failure.

Behind him, Marcus heaved again into the grass just beyond the crater. There was nothing left in his stomach, but he didn’t seem capable of holding back the bile. Again, his body convulsed with dry heaves and broken sobs.

“She’s gone,” he choked out between retches. “I fucking told her—I warned her about—God, this can’t be real.”

Tara stood a few paces behind Ace. She hadn’t said anything in ages, and he resisted the impulse to glance back at her over his shoulder. For all her experience as a paramedic, nothing could’ve prepared her for this level of destruction. She had probably seen death in ambulances, on gurneys, in emergency rooms—places where tragedy was contained and where protocol mattered.

Not scattered across dirt like this. Not a person she had known.

In Ace’s periphery, he spotted Olivia at the edge of the crater a short ways off. She maintained her distance from the rest of them, and her eyes never strayed from the hole in the dirt. Her face remained impassive, but her skin had gone impossibly pale, even for a vampire. Her dark eyes moved methodically across the scene, drinking in every detail with a disturbing intensity.

Silence stretched between them all, broken only by Marcus's diminishing sobs and the churning rush of wind through the trees. The leaves clapped together in a disturbing symphony, like applause after a show, and an occasional distant howl pierced the otherwise deathly silence.

Ace’s jaw twitched as he gritted his teeth. Rachel deserved better than this. She deserved a burial. A memorial. Something to prove that she had existed at all.

Without a word, he turned around.

Everyone flinched. Their gazes darted to him, and everyone took a step back. Even Marcus, still on his hands and knees, fell backward onto his ass as he stared up at Ace with wide eyes.

They were scared of him.

Trust shattered. Nerves raw.

With a heavy sigh, Ace headed down the hillside.

Silently, lost in his thoughts, he gathered stones from the surrounding hillside. Mid-sized rocks, manageable but substantial, piled in the crook of his left arm until he couldn’t carry any more.

When he returned to the crater's edge with his armload of rocks, he knelt and placed the first stone deliberately beside Rachel's severed hand. Then another. And another. One by one, rock by rock, he built a human-sized circle around what remained of her.

No one spoke.

No one bent to help him.

The others watched in tense silence. The unity they'd built over their time fighting together had fractured, and each of them became isolated in their own grief and fear.

Ace felt Tara's eyes on him as he worked. He had grown accustomed to that particular weight over their time together in this place—a paramedic's gaze, assessing, cataloging, aware.

But this was different.

In his peripheral vision, he caught her expression shifting between grief and something else that he couldn't name. Something that made his hands falter for a split second before he forced them to be steady again. Something… different.

Whatever she was thinking, her view of him had changed.

Doing his best to ignore the possible implications of what that could mean, he arranged each stone with the same care he had once used when handing his squadmate’s dog tag to his widow—deliberate, respectful, final.

The rocks felt heavier than they should have, each one a small monument to his failure as their leader. Rachel had been annoying, sometimes, but she'd been his responsibility. His to protect.

The gentleness in his movements wasn't just for Rachel—it was for all of them.

Stones scraped against each other as Ace continued building Rachel's makeshift memorial. Each rock added to the growing pile formed a barrier between the horror of her remains and the unforgiving world that had claimed her. The rhythm of the work—gather, place, adjust, repeat—gave him purpose while his mind spun through an endless loop of what-ifs and should-haves.

Somewhere along the line, in that haze of placing rocks over her corpse, Marcus had stopped dry-heaving. He sat back on his heels several yards away, his face a hollow mask as he stared at the half-formed cairn. Tear tracks cut clean lines through the dirt and blood on his cheeks. His eyes shifted out of focus, vacant and empty, as though his mind had retreated somewhere safer than this hillside.

“She didn't deserve this,” he whispered, voice raw and cracking. “None of us do.”

No one replied.

A moment later, movement caught Ace's eye. He tilted his head as Tara approached, each step careful and slow, as though worried she might spook him. Her shoulders were tense, her hands slightly raised, as if trying to soothe a spooked horse, and her gaze shifted briefly toward him.

Interesting.

Ace remained focused on the stones. When she finally knelt beside him, she was just inches away—far closer than he had expected her to come, given how she had flinched with the others.

No one had voluntarily entered his space since he'd cut Victor’s head off.

She reached for a stone at the same moment he did. Their fingers brushed, and a current of energy shot through him—a momentary connection amid the horror.

To his surprise, Tara didn't pull away. Instead, their eyes met, and something passed between them. A shared understanding of loss that went beyond words, perhaps, or maybe just a moment of silence for the fallen.

From the edge of the clearing, Olivia's eyes darted between the memorial and the tree line. Her body coiled tight, her weight shifting toward her toes as though she were getting ready to run. She scanned the surrounding forest continuously.

“Thinking about running?” Ace asked.

His voice pierced the air, sudden and startling.

Again, everyone flinched.

Olivia’s gaze shifted back toward him, and she didn’t answer. Her chin raised in a subtle defiant movement, and her eyes darted once more toward the forest line.

“I won’t stop you,” he promised. “Though you’re welcome to stay if you want.”

Instead of answering, Olivia’s eyes narrowed slightly in confusion.

“We should move soon,” she murmured. “Blood attracts predators.”

Marcus glared up at her. “Then go. In fact, that's what we all should do. Go our separate ways. This—” he gestured to Rachel's makeshift grave. “—this is what happens when we pretend we're still human. When we pretend we can be a team.”

His words hung in the air, giving voice to a thought that Ace figured had crept into all their minds.

Perhaps they were better off alone in this world.

Though he faltered at the thought, Ace resumed placing stones on Rachel’s grave.

“Is that really what you think?” he asked Marcus. “You think that we failed because we stayed together?”

“I think we failed because we're playing a game we can't win,” Marcus snapped. “The System wants us dead. These monsters want us dead. Other vampires want us dead.” He dragged his filthy sleeve across his face to wipe away fresh tears as they welled in his eyes. “I mean, fuck, look what happened to Rachel. That’s a horrible way to go.”

Tara placed another stone alongside the others. “I was alone before I met you all,” she said. “Being alone was worse.”

“At least alone you only have to worry about yourself,” Marcus countered.

“That’s not enough for me,” Tara said, her voice steadying as she worked. Though she grabbed another stone from Ace’s arms, her focus remained on the cairn.

But she never once looked at Ace. She never looked at anyone.

Olivia took a step closer, though she didn’t say anything. She watched Ace with a curious expression he couldn’t read—something between horror and lust.

He didn’t understand this woman, and he was too tired to try and figure her out right now.

Dirt accumulated under Ace’s fingernails as he placed his last stone and dug another out of the nearby ground. Sweat beaded on his forehead, mixing with specks of blood that had splattered across his skin during the battle.

Building Rachel’s memorial became both tribute and penance, and he wouldn’t rest until it was done.

“I failed her,” he said quietly. “I failed all of you. I should have recognized the Behemoth's attack pattern sooner. I should have kept her out of the fight. She wasn’t ready.”

Tara stiffened beside him, but after a moment, her shoulders relaxed once more. “You couldn’t have known.”

“I did,” he confessed.

Everyone looked at him, watching him with a blend of hatred and horror as he placed another rock.

“What do you mean?” Olivia asked.

“I should have never let Victor back into the group,” he answered. “I didn’t have any proof that he would go that far, but I had an inkling. I should’ve listened to my intuition. I should’ve told him to stay the fuck away.”

At the mention of Victor's name, a ripple of tension passed through them all. The former teammate whose betrayal had nearly cost them everything. The vampire Ace had been forced to kill to save them.

“You did what you had to do with Victor,” Tara said softly. “We all know that.”

“Do you?” Ace asked.

He shifted his attention to Marcus, and the former streamer's eyes widened. Muscles tensed in the man’s neck as he swallowed hard. For a moment, their gazes locked, and Marcus froze with fear. He looked away first, unable to hold Ace’s gaze. He stared down at the rocks accumulating over Rachel’s remains with sudden, desperate interest.

Ace shifted his attention to Olivia. She stood with perfect stillness, her dancer's posture rigid. Unlike Marcus, nothing in her expression suggested fear. Instead, a strange fascination gleamed in her eyes. Her lips parted slightly as their eyes met, but she caught herself and looked back toward the treeline. Her cheeks flushed with embarrassment, as if he had caught her admiring something shameful.

Finally, he turned to Tara. The paramedic held his gaze longer than the others. She had seen plenty of death, yet even she couldn't maintain eye contact. Her focus returned to the memorial they were building, and she dug another stone out of the ground nearby.

They didn’t trust him anymore.

“Victor would have killed us all,” Ace said. “I'd do it again in a heartbeat. Make of that what you will.”

The words hung between them, honest and terrible. None of them looked up.

With that, Ace turned his back on them and left to gather more stones. His new boots silenced every footstep and gave him insight to the surrounding area—Olivia’s shifting weight, Marcus’s fist as it hit the earth, Tara’s absent fiddling with the stones they had already placed, as if she needed something to keep her hands busy.

This was their chance to run, if they wanted to.

He wouldn’t stop them.

Not this time.
