The first sign of danger was the silence.

One moment, the wind howled across the cracked earth, stirring up the ever-present red dust. The next, the world held its breath. No drones skittered, no whisper of shifting metal or pulsing corruption.

Then the growl came—low, mechanical, a chorus of synchronized vibrations rolling in from the jagged horizon.

Cale's grip on his hoverboard tightened. His muscles tensed as he scanned the uneven terrain ahead. There—he saw them before the others did, a dozen sleek black-and-chrome shapes cresting a hill, moving in eerie unison. The Praetorians. Murderbots. Quadrupedal and lean, their singular red lenses glowed like hunting wolves locking onto prey.

"Contact!" Cale shouted, his voice cutting through the tense silence.

Fiara snapped to attention, eyes narrowing as she followed his gaze. "How many?"

"Twelve," Cale answered, already stepping onto his hoverboard. "Coming fast."

Galvon scoffed, pushing past Cale's shoulder. "We can handle a dozen tier-two Praetorians."

"And yet you're standing behind me," Cale muttered.

The group formed a loose defensive line around the payload. Risto checked his twin pistols, thumbing the power sigils along their barrels. A faint golden glow emanated from the chambers as they charged. Artan drew his sword in a single, fluid motion, the blade catching the dim light. Madia extended her quarterstaff, the weapon sliding from compact to full length with a satisfying click.

The Praetorians attacked as one, a perfect machine-organism, spreading out in a wide arc before charging. They were fast—unnervingly so. Each step precise, each movement synchronized. They didn't rush chaotically. They moved like a single mind controlling a dozen bodies.

"I hate these things," Madia said through gritted teeth.

Ragnak cracked his knuckles, a wild grin spreading across his face. "I don't."

Before anyone could respond, Ragnak was already moving. A burst of fiery light flared around his form, and he vanished—reappearing mid-air, his body streaking like a meteor as he collided with the nearest Praetorian. The impact cracked the machine's frame, sending it skidding before it twisted and righted itself, claws digging into the corrupted ground.

"Show-off," Galvon muttered, extending his hand. Three small, silver spheres floated above his palm, each one beginning to glow with an icy blue light, charging with energy. Cale didn’t know what it was, but it sure looked expensive.

Artan stepped forward, calm and precise. His face betrayed nothing—no fear, no excitement, just absolute focus. His sword sang as it left its sheath. A Praetorian lunged at him—Artan sidestepped without an inch to spare, his blade flickering in an arc. The machine fell into two clean halves before it even registered it had been struck.

Cassidy darted between the chaos, her claw-ring flashing, snapping out in razor-sharp, pinpoint strikes that tore through the Praetorians' joints, hamstringing them with cruel efficiency. She moved like water, flowing between opponents, never still enough to be caught. Every time she sliced with her ring, a streak of pink energy flashed

"You could learn something from her," Aura commented in Cale's mind.

"Not now," Cale whispered, scanning the battlefield.

“Hmph!” Aura said. “I am taking her moves and adding them to both my databank and predictive model. She’s a clean fighter.”

Risto hung back, twin pistols already roaring to life. Sharp golden shots of condensed mana energy punched through black steel, blinding red lenses with precision shots before the machines could reorient. His lazy expression never changed, even as he systematically dismantled two Praetorians with surgical precision.

Sintra and Madia held the line closer to the payload. Sintra raised a shimmering shield, a transparent dome of energy that deflected two Praetorians mid-charge, sending them tumbling sideways. The effort clearly taxed her—sweat beaded on her forehead, her arms trembling slightly.

"Keep them off the payload!" Fiara shouted, directing from behind.

Madia spun her quarterstaff, extending it into a brutal arc, cracking a machine's head back with enough force to warp its plating. The Praetorian stumbled, lens flickering, but didn't fall. It regained its footing, hydraulics hissing as it prepared another lunge.

Cale had no intention of standing still.

His hoverboard hummed under him, and he launched himself into motion, weaving between the attacks, baiting the machines to follow. He dipped and swerved, the pink blur of his board barely touching the uneven terrain before he kicked off again, forcing the Praetorians to chase.

They took the bait.

Two of them peeled off, bounding after Cale with unnatural agility, their claws raking the air where he had been seconds before. Cale twisted sharply to the side, pushing off a jagged rock with his board, letting the machine crash into the spot he had just vacated. Momentum was his weapon. He didn't fight like the others. He couldn't. So instead, he disrupted.

"Come on, you shiny bastards," Cale grinned, kicking the nose of his hoverboard, flipping over a leaping Praetorian as its claws swiped inches from his chest. With Aura’s predictive model starting to form up nicely, dodging was becoming easier and easier.

"You're enjoying this too much," Aura chided. “Have you considered that you might be crazy?”

"Probably," Cale replied, ducking low in line of Aura’s predictive model as another Praetorian tried to tackle him mid-air.

Aura laughed in his mind. “Hahahaa! Good one! I must be crazy too— CRAZY GOOD!”

Across the battlefield, Ragnak was laughing maniacally as he pummeled a Praetorian into the ground. His knuckles were wrapped in a thin layer of fiery energy that left dents wherever he struck. The machine jerked and twitched under the assault, red lens cracking from the repeated impacts.

Galvon flung his silver spheres in a wide arc. They slammed into the Praetorians when Galvon swung them in wide arcs, and returned to his hand after a strike, like a wireless yo-yo. A deadly yo-yo.

"I thought you said these things were tough!" Galvon called to Fiara, a cocky smirk on his face.

The words had barely left his mouth when one of the Praetorians running behind Cale separated from the pack and lunged at Galvon, catching him off-guard. He barely managed to produce a hasty barrier from a ring, stumbling back as the Praetorian slammed into it.

Cale was almost disappointed Galvon got off scot-free.

Rich kids…

"You just had to say something," Madia growled, spinning to intercept. She slammed her staff at the Praetorian, keeping it down while Galvan called his orbs and they smashed through its head, disabling it.

“Shut up,” Galvon said.

Then, Sintra screamed.

The sound cut through the fight like a blade.

Cale's head snapped to the side. A Praetorian had breached the line, had slipped past the chaos and sunk its claws into Sintra's side. Blood bloomed against her shirt as she staggered backwards, dodging another strike, but falling on her side.

"Sintra!" Fiara shouted, but she was too far—trapped behind the payload, protected by DR-12 who only watched and waited.

Cale moved without thinking.

He angled his board hard and shot across the battlefield, ignoring the formation, ignoring the battle plan, ignoring everything except getting to Sintra.

"What are you doing?" Aura demanded. "The others can handle it!"

Cale ignored her. He slammed into the machine at full force, tackling it away from Sintra with sheer reckless speed. They hit the dirt in a rolling heap, the machine twisting violently as Cale fought to keep its snapping metal jaws from his throat.

The Praetorian was strong—much stronger than him—but Cale was desperate. He jammed his forearm under its "chin," straining to keep those razor teeth from his face. Its limbs thrashed, claws raking at his sides. One caught his ribs, tearing through his shirt, drawing blood.

"Cale!" Fiara's voice cut through the chaos.

A whining gunshot of golden energy rang out. It smashed into the red lens of the murderbot. It scrambled backwards and another shot lanced in the same spot. The Praetorian's lens burst, its body falling limp beneath Cale. Cale looked to his side. Risto gave him a small nod.

Cale returned it and then turned his attention to Sintra.

She was not doing well. Blood pooled under her, her breath ragged. She clutched at her side, fingers slick with red.

Cale scrambled to his feet, stumbling toward her as the battle raged around them. The others were finishing off the remaining Praetorians, but he could only focus on Sintra's pale face.

"You okay?" he asked, immediately regretting the stupid question.

Sintra managed a pained smile, blood staining her teeth. "Just... peachy."

"I got you," Cale said, already reaching for her. "Come on, let's get you to the payload."

"Cale, leave her!" Fiara's voice snapped.

Cale gave her a look of disbelief. “Huh? No way.”

“I’m in charge of this mission,” Fiara said.

“Don’t care,” Cale said. “I’ll stabilize her and catch up.”

“You—” Fiara said but bit her lip. “Idiot… Do as you goddamn please then.

She turned to the rest of the group waving a hand in the air. “The rest of us. Move. Back into formation.”

“You going to catch up?” Ragnak asked as he passed Cale, clapping a hand on his shoulder.

“You know it.”

“Good,” the white haired boy said and grinned. “After seeing your moves on the hoverboard, I can’t wait to have a go at you.”

Cale laughed. Suddenly he found Ragnak a lot less annoying after having fought with him. “I’ll see that we make it happen.”

“Don’t take too long, darling,” Cassidy said and blew him a kiss.

The recruits startd falling into formation around the payload and getting ready to continue, leaving Cale and Sintra behind.

“Are you sure about this?” Aura asked.

Cale shrugged and knelt down over Sintra.

Her body trembled slightly. Blood soaked through her shirt. Cale pressed on the wound. The rest of the recruits passed him with varying expressions.

"We need to keep moving," Madia shouted, already pushing the payload forward. "She's done. We can't afford this! We have to leave her."

"Leave her?" Cale echoed, incredulous. "She's still alive!"

"And she'll slow us down," Madia shot back, eyes narrowed. "The mission comes first. You know that."

Cale's fingers dug into Sintra's shirt as he pressed against the wound. He looked up at the rest of them. They were all waiting for him to abandon her.

Artan's face was impassive, but his eyes held a glimmer of regret. "She would understand, Cale. That is how this works."

"No," Cale said, the word coming out harder than he intended. "I'm not leaving her to die."

"You want us all to fail because you're soft?" Galvon sneered. "This isn't a charity mission."

"Then go," Cale exhaled sharply. "I'll catch up."

Madia's eyes blazed. "You selfish, reckless idiot! If we fail this mission because of your—"

"Enough," Fiara cut in. Her gaze lingered on Cale for a moment, something unreadable flashing across her face. Then she turned to the others. "We keep moving. Cale can do what he wants."

The group hesitated only a moment more before Fiara waved them forward. "MOVE!"

They moved.

Cale barely heard them leave, the sound of their footsteps fading into the constant howl of the red wind. The new Praetorians were still approaching, moving more cautiously now, as if assessing the situation.

Sintra's fingers weakly curled into his sleeve. She was still awake. Barely.

"Why...?" she managed, her voice barely audible.

"Because I’d prefer you did the same to me." Cale said, already moving toward a jagged outcropping of rock. They needed cover.

"Stupid..." Sintra whispered, but there was a smile in her voice. “I probably wouldn’t…”

Cale sat her down against the jagged rock and pulled a water bottle from his pack.

"You're not dying here. You hear me?" he said, unscrewing the cap.

She gave a weak, bloodied laugh. "You're a terrible tactician."

Cale popped the cork with his teeth. "You're a terrible patient. Drink."

She did, wincing as the liquid went down. The wound was bad— deadly as far as mortals were concerned. But Cale was sure Sintra would pull through if she was given a chance. But he needed to stop the bleeding.

Cale grimaced. He didn't have many options. He removed his shirt.

"We need to move soon," he said to Sintra, already tearing strips from his shirt to bind the wound. "Those machines won't stay put forever."

"Just leave me," Sintra murmured, her eyes drifting closed. "S'what anyone else would do."

Cale ripped off part of Sintra’s bloody shirt, washed the wound and started binding it.

"Yeah, well," Cale tied off the makeshift bandage, satisfaction blooming in his chest as the bleeding slowed. "I'm not anyone else."

The rest of the recruits were gone now, their footsteps lost in the red wind. The payload—their mission—was moving on without them. Cale knew he'd made a choice that might cost him dearly in the cultivator world's cruel mathematics.

But looking at Sintra's face, she was getting slightly better.

Cale exhaled, wiping his brow with the back of his hand, smearing dust and sweat across his forehead. The fight was over.

But something told him the real battle was just beginning.
