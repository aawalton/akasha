[Lamb POV]

A statue on the battlefield—that’s what I was. I hung frozen in space as men and women screamed and shouted at my back, a cruel magician standing before me with her white-gloved hands raised high above her head like a deranged puppeteer.

Is this how things were destined to end?

I thought frantically, mind racing down new avenues of possibility with every moment. My mana was under my control, but the woman with the mage had ensnared my body—and Jacyntha’s, too—with nary a thought. She’d spared a single contemptuous flick of her dextrous little fingers our way, and we’d been bound tight by strings of invisible force. She had turned back to Vera and rebound my berserking friend, too, though that feat was no only much more impressive but also more tenuous.

Vera had her teeth bared in a snarl, blood red flames bursting from her body in twisting streams. They contained faces that gnashed bloody teeth and screamed bloody murder at the white-robed mage, before vanishing to be replaced by others soon after. I suspected it was some sort of personal empowerment skill, because Vera managed to make a few strides, closing in closer to the shockingly powerful mage.

Despite the pain in my chest, mounting every second, from the frozen air inside my lungs, I felt a thrill of hope. Jacyntha and I might be immobilised, but I’d yet to meet anyone that could stand against Vera.

Then I saw the mage smile. Her white cowl was deep and shadowed, giving me only a glimpse of her thin nose and even thinner lips. They were startingly red, coloured by some cosmetic powder or paste, and they quirked up in amusement at Vera’s plight. I didn’t need to see the woman’s eyes to know it was not a friendly expression.

Her wiggling fingers danced once more towards Jacyntha and I, and while the momentary distraction allowed Vera to advance another few strides, it cost us far more heavily. It was a lazy motion; a swirl of her wrist and a waggle of thin fingers, but it was the herald of pain.

The strange effects that bound me—the links of invisible mana that gripped every inch of my body, piercing through my flesh to hold the very air inside of me in stillness—began to twist. My limbs went taught, and I would have screamed if I had control of my own lungs. Pain rippled through my body at seemingly every juncture, and I panicked.

I activated Break-Step, searching for some way out, and time slowed instantly. It was uncanny, the world creeping by far slower than I’d ever experienced before. I watched the mage’s bone-white cowl flutter in the breeze, and it looked like nothing so much as a geriatric snake rippling its way across a rock in the hot sun—slow and rhythmic.

My momentum was almost nil, considering the supernatural bounds holding me tight, and so time slowed to a crawl. The skill had said long ago that momentum was a twin god; ‘time and movement are two sides of the same coin’, and when I had a bare sliver of momentum, time was mine in abundance.

It was tempting to lose what little composure I had left at the thought. The terror of a near-eternity of torment as my body was torn apart by magical tethers seized me, and I nearly let my reaction skill drop right then and there. But I had time, and I would spend it wisely.

I knew I had a bull-headed tendency to attack problems head on. How many times had I put off examining my own status until the last possible moment? Too many, obviously. I might be a fool, but let it never be said that Lamb can’t learn! I just take longer than most.

Even I had limits to my stupidity. I had learnt from previous experiences, and this time I didn’t hesitate to acknowledge the system prompts that had filled my head with a constant ringing ever since the seed had hit me.

Title gained: Herald of Change.

That was an interesting development and obviously came from whatever interaction I’d had with the seed of the World Tree. It was nice to know that giving the seed away to the strange skill-conjured tree that clutched Castle Ryonic in its gnarled grasp hadn’t resulted in a loss of the title, though I didn’t yet know what effect it had.

Skill ‘A Frozen Pyrre’ has gained in level. A Frozen Pyrre – level 10. Passive.

Skill ‘Axis Shift’ has gained in level. Axis-Shift: Level 10. Active.

Skill ‘Stride The Edge’ has gained in level. Stride The Edge: Level 10. Passive.

Skill ‘Break-Step’ has gained in level. Break-Step: Level 10. Active.

Skill ‘End of the Hunt’ has gained in level. End of the Hunt: Level 10. Active.

Skill ‘Vanguard of the Lost’ has gained in level. Vanguard of the Lost: Level 10. Passive.

Skills ‘Shatter Point’ has gained in level. Shatter Point: Level 10. Active.

Skill ‘The Mountain’s Gate’ has gained in level. The Mountain’s Gate: Level 10. Active.

It seemed that the seed had refined my skills such that the system acknowledged their refinement as well, all my skills reaching level ten simultaneously. I would need to speak to Jorge about it later to understand what it meant, but I brushed aside the errant thought, knowing that I would be lucky to get out of this alive, and equally knowing that even if I did survive, this would be so far down the list of important questions to ask that it would take me weeks to remember anyway.

I skipped past the death notifications for those of the Crimson Company I had slain, as well as the white-armoured soldiers that had tried to block my path to Vera—unsuccessfully, it went without saying. I’d received two levels for their deaths, giving me a nice even thirty attribute points to spend. I didn’t know how the white-robed woman worked her magic, but it seemed to come from, or be directed by, her fingers—why else would anyone stand there like a Blood Bear with their arms waving so stupidly above their head? Given that I wasn’t remotely strong enough to break those phantom threads she wove, ad even Vera seemed to be struggling mightily to do so, I figured agility was a more useful attribute to invest in.

As I spent those precious points, I realised the true bounty bestowed by my new title, and if I hadn’t been immobilised and on the verge of death, I would have grinned. So much for two levels—Herald Of Change had granted me an additional thirty points in each attribute. No wonder my body felt stronger and lighter—everything had been improved in a major way.

With all the changes, my status was looking a far-cry from what it so recently was.

That done, I spent a few moments taking stock of the situation in the world outside.

Vera stood alone against the mage, her muscles straining and her teeth bared in a snarl as she cut through and otherwise overpowered the magic she was bound by. She was in the midst of taking a slow step, but I had little hope in her success. The mage was done with both Jacyntha and I, her fingers already pointing accusingly at the main threat once more.

By the time Vera finished her step, I would be little more than a headless, limbless torso leaking blood into the churned mud below. Jacyntha was in a similar bind, though I saw a faint green glow entering my vision from the left, the magic creeping past my periphery with aching slowness. I doubted she could overpower the white mage, but perhaps she had a plan.

I could try to use The Mountain’s Gate once more. Perhaps initiate it beneath her feet in an attempt to disrupt her and foul her attention? But I suspected she would have no trouble supressing the skill with her aura alone. Even if she couldn’t, that wouldn’t necessarily save me—while it was an almost instantaneous activation, in the time it took for the earth to rupture and rise, my limbs would be long gone and my innards noodled. I’d not call that much of a victory, personally.

Axis-Shift was the only other ranged skill I could activate instantly, and I doubted it would have the power to affect her much given her capacity to stand toe to toe with Vera. It was all I had though, so I marshalled my will and prepared to activate a pointless skill in an attempt to avoid an even more pointless death.

Before I completed the cast, I was distracted by movement in my periphery. Something was causing the air itself to writhe and shimmer, and I watched in awe as an arrow—black as the night had once been and jagged like the edge of a wound—shot through the air in something akin to normal speed.

It was slowed significantly, of course, but compared to the rest of the world it was impossibly fast, moving dozens of feet in an instant as the mage’s fingers slowly pulled apart. The pain was slowly ramping up as the strange magical restraints pulled me apart inch by inch, but Fandar must have released the arrow in virtually the same moment that I’d been snared and had somehow managed to impart the speed of a diving hawk into the attack.

The air screamed as the arrow plunged towards the mage’s head, the sound warped into a horrific groan, rendered deep and reverberating by time’s strange distortion. The demonic sound brought with it a flare of hope. it would hit its target, and soon. Soon enough to matter, that is.

I desperately re-considered my plan, visualizing my core and the skill constellation for The Mountain’s Gate. I would only have a moment’s distraction, but all I needed to do was break the mage’s line of sight, assuming Fandar could break her concentration.

I watched with stinging eyes as the arrow found its mark, slicing into and through the slender neck of the mage. Her hands dropped. Instantly, I felt the magic holding me vanish, and my skill was active before I’d even fallen to one knee. A thirteen-peaked mountain range sprung from the earth before the mage, obscuring Jacyntha and I from view as it reached forty odd feet into to the golden sky above.

I heard cries of pain and the crackling discharge of many magical attacks from behind, but there was no time to take the ley of the land. I darted to my left, grabbing Jacyntha as I did.

“We need to flank her!” I yelled at her as we ran. “Get her hands spread in different directions and then come at her from the front or behind. I’ll go right, you go left!”

I didn’t wait for her acknowledgement though and sprinted off towards the other end of the earthen defence. A quick look back showed Jacyntha waiting at her side, great axe held in her hands and eyes fixed on me, waiting for the signal.

I gave it.

We burst around the edge of the conjured earthen barrier at the same time, Jacyntha’s heavy footfalls distinct from my own lighter steps. I ran out to the right, arms and legs pumping as I tried to gain as much distance from Jacyntha as possible, while she strafed left so that we would encircle the mage and come at her from two opposite sides.

The scene we emerged to was confused.

Vera had once more gained some ground, now straining not fifty feet from her foe, limbs wreathed in an outline of fire. Leering and screaming faces writhed and bit at the air, formed by the ethereal flames that constantly popped and fizzed into and out of existence around her. She seemed to be making progress once more, wading with gritted teeth through whatever magical restriction the mage created with her waving and dancing hands.

The arrow that had nearly decapitated the mage was sliding out of her neck at a crawl, picking up speed with each moment until it left her neck and began to fly through the air back the way it had come. By the time I had made it parallel to the mage, the arrow was emitting a shrill whistle at it flew at a speed I couldn’t track, and the woman’s wound had sealed itself shut, not even a scar to mar the skin where it had once been.

I grinned to have made it this far though, and pulled back my arm to throw the hatchet I’d drawn as I began to close in. My elation was short-lived, for in the next instant she turned on me. Her head whipped around with such sickening speed, her white cowl pointed towards me such that I knew her eyes met my own, even if they were shrouded by white cloth. The painted lips once more twitched into that predator’s smile.

I didn’t see her move her fingers, too focused on my throw, but as my hatchet left my grip, that same grip became frozen. I was caught fast once more—a fly in a web. The proverbial spider swivelled her head to face Jacyntha on the opposite side, and soon enough the barbarian was also halted in her tracks, though she wasn’t quite as helpless as I was.

Where Vera was wreathed in flames and making steady progress, Jacyntha was lit from within by a viridescent glow. I watched as the colour deepened, her scars seeming to shiver and wriggle in place a moment, and then two mana-forged arms burst from her back, ghostly green with bands of grey wrapping the biceps and forearms like the clan signifiers I’d seen back in the Titan’s Crown.

The arms encircled Jacyntha, rippling through the air in front of her and seeming to grip something invisible. Great muscles bunched, and within my mana-senses, I could see those ghostly arms ripping apart the magical restraints the white-robed mage wielded.

Seeing he world through my mana-senses was not necessarily a reassuring thing right now, with my breath frozen in my aching chest. I could perceive hundreds of strings of steel-hard mana the mage had spread throughout the world like a master weaver at their loom, and she plucked them expertly to snare and bind. It reminded me of the way particularly malicious children pluck the wings from flies or the legs from spiders. The vision of her thin lips quirked in the ghost of a smile flashed through my mind again.

Following her development by the seed of the World Tree, Jacyntha’s new power was awe-inspiring… but it wasn’t enough. Each thread she ripped away was replaced with two more, and it took time to destroy them. I had no skills myself to sever the conjurations, and again wished I had spent more time studying Sadrianna’s skill. Razor Beak would come in handy right now.

Thinking about my companions was both a blessing and a curse. I was gladdened to know that my actions, bolstered by Jorge—in fact, he probably deserved the majority of the credit, honestly—had saved both Nathlan and Sadrianna from certain death at the hands of the Crimson Company. But I was also confronted with the reality of my inability to save Vera. And worse—the fact that I had given my own life, and Jacyntha’s, for seemingly no benefit.

The barbarian had made her own choice so I felt no guilt there, but I did rue the fact that we would die here uselessly. Vera would still be surrounded and killed, Jacyntha would never become the hero I knew she could be, and I would never fulfil my promise to Nathlan.

I worried about him the most; to be left alone in a world as chaotic as this one, a new class likely built around his worst impulses. I understood his paranoia considering his past, but I also knew that a lack of trusted companions would lead him down a dark path. I worried for what he might become, without the grounding of friendship I had hoped to provide.

Still though, despite knowing the futility of it all, I had no doubt that I would make the same decision again if given the chance. Vera had been surrounded and bereft of friends. Never would I abandon a companion in need, and while I would curse my weakness until my dying breath, I would never regret my instinct to help. Said dying breath was likely to be pretty soon at this rate, so I wouldn’t need to curse for long.

The brief moment of levity soon fled as I pictured the faces of my friends; Jacyntha, Nathlan, Sadrianna, Vera, even Jorge. I imprinted their essence on the forefront of my mind, determined to take them with me to the end, and closed my eyes. I wouldn’t draw out this moment of agony a thousandfold with the aid of Break-Step.

Let my last breath be one of peace, at least.

While my chest couldn’t move, I felt something build within me at the thought. My soul quivered and End of the Hunt unfurled its metaphorical wings to shadow my soul beneath its protection. It roared to life, my pathbound skill siphoning mana at an ever-increasing rate to fuel one last of burst of presence.

It was unthinkable that I might give up on life at a moment like this, that I wouldn’t go out fighting for every breath. To do otherwise was an abdication of my path, and my skill reminded me of that. I would have smiled if I could.

As I greeted death with a snarl in my heart, I saw a spear appear in my vision. Short-hafted, a white ribbon tied below its slim blade fluttering in the breeze, and a shimmering head emanating a power above any of the skills I could currently muster, feeling to my mana-sense like a bird of prey swooping with sharp beak bared wide.
