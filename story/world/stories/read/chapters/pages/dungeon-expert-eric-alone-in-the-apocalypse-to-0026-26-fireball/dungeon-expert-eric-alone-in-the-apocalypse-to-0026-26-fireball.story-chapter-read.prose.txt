Spell learned: Fireball

The bright orange-white bolt burst from the tip of his sword. The Trinotaur kept showboating for a second, but at the expressions of the crowd, whirled—and faced its death head-on. The ball of fire and energy slammed into its chest and exploded with a might greater than Firebolt or Explosion alone or together. Brilliant light flashed, too bright for Eric to watch, and he covered his eyes, looking away.

It faded. He lowered his hand, holding his breath. Was it enough? Had the second one done it, as Cy predicted?

The Trinotaur stood. It still stood, though its upper half was blackened and smoking. Black-gray smoke billowed from its body, but it stayed on its feet. Eric caught his breath. He scrambled backward, though he knew he couldn’t escape.

Something wet pattered onto his hand. Was it raining? He looked up, then down. Red. Blood was raining from the sky.

It stumbled forward half a step, then fell, first to its knees, then flat on its face. Blood continued to splatter down on them, along with chunks of flesh, smacking down hard on the floor all around Eric. He stared, then retched. I did that? Me? I did?

The goblins stared as well, just as unbelieving as Eric. Then, slowly, the sound rising, they began to cheer. Not just cheer, but stomp, rattle, clang, make as much noise as they could. They’d gone wild for the Trinotaur, but they went mad for Eric. Some even began to chant his name, or something like it: Ai Rick Ai Rick.

A few threw trash at the Trinotaur’s body, smacking it with cans and other detritus. The ones who’d cheered the hardest for it moments ago now threw their trash at it the hardest.

Eric looked up at them, then back at the bloodsoaked ground and the blasted corpse. Is this what I wanted? Is this the following, the fans, the fame I was after? It looks like this? It feels this hollow? This empty? If it was him on the ground, dead, the goblins would be chanting for the Trinotaur. If he’d missed, if his plan hadn’t worked, all that would’ve awaited him were jeers, rotten vegetables, and the grave. Instead… instead…

It’s bloodsports, kid. The Game is bloodsports. You knew that. This is your life, now. You fight on, or you give up; there isn’t another option.

Eric took a deep breath. He pushed to his feet, standing shakily. “That’s right. I win, or Earth loses. I can’t lose. I can’t hesitate. No matter what it takes, no matter what it means… I have to win.”

On his shoulder, Cy shifted uncomfortably, but then patted Eric. He said nothing, just offered silent comfort.

The circle at the center of the arena shifted, then dropped into the earth. There was nothing but a hole for a beat, and then a new circle rose, bearing a chest at its center. Eric staggered forward. He reached the chest and kicked it open, too tired and in too much pain to bend over.

A veritable pile of red tokens and a larger green token awaited him, along with a few shapes he couldn’t identify at first glance, though some looked suspiciously like scorpion tails and carapaces. He sent it all to inventory for later. He was too tired to figure out loot right now.

Turning stiffly, he pointed at the Trinotaur. “I want to loot that for materials. Can I?”

At his request, the Trinotaur’s body dissolved into dark smoke. Bones, hide, and meat all materialized in his inventory.

A moment later, a belated notification popped up.

Defeated the Trinotaur! Rare reward granted.

First to defeat the Trinotaur! Ultrarare reward granted.

Linked Dungeon Reward granted.

Level Up!

Level Up!

Level Up!

He turned to the crowd at last and lifted his hand, hefting the sword high. “Remember me. Remember Eric James!”

The goblins cheered one last time, then they, too, faded out, going pale, then translucent. The noise faded with them, until nothing remained but empty stadium seating and some trash floating around the arena. The wind whooshed, suddenly the only sound in the empty arena. Eric stood there, feeling as hollow as the arena was.

The usual teleport out? notification appeared before him, and this time, he chose yes. He didn’t feel like stomping his way back through a thousand tiny scorpions (and a few large ones). He didn’t feel like anything, honestly, except for trooping back to the safe zone, taking a bath, and having a nice long nap while he waited for Regeneration to fix him up. So much for trying two dungeons in one day. Still, he’d accomplished his goal: he’d finished the dungeon before noon, which meant he’d know whether the fog appeared at noon or if it appeared after he finished a dungeon, regardless of when he finished it.

There was the lifting sensation, the same weightlessness as before, and then he dropped, landing on his feet at the front of the dungeon. Thin fog drifted around, collecting in the trees and gathering around his ankles, but it was low, thin. There was still time before it thickened.

So it is a timer. A timer for noon. Why? Why does it chase me back at noon? He lifted his gaze, searching the forest. Was there something out here in the fog that monstrous thing was protecting? Some kind of cache or reward that opened every day at noon?

He looked at the other dungeon, then lowered his head. Just looking at it confirmed what he’d already decided: Not today. Not like this. I’m in bad shape. Before he took on any other monsters, he needed to go back and heal up. He turned his feet toward the safe zone and limped along the path, glad that he didn’t have anything chasing him right now.

Three rewards. Rare, Ultrarare, and Linked, whatever that meant. At least one of those would be good. Ultrarares tended to be valuable things like spellbooks or shop tokens, so he was looking forward to that. It also looked like there was a shop token in the chest at the end of the dungeon; he’d have to examine it later to see what shop it summoned.

“For those of you still watching, I want you to know that I originally planned to do two dungeons today. That’s why I got up so early; that was my whole idea from last night until now. But I’m all beaten up. I’m exhausted and my mana is drained. I’m all out of mana potions, and I only have one health potion left; I could down the health potion and maybe, maybe press on, but more likely, it still wouldn’t be enough to fix everything that’s wrong with me.”

He paused a moment, adjusting his Regeneration to focus on his ribs. Walking jostled them more than he thought it did. The fog was starting to thicken rapidly, so he didn’t wait long, but pressed on again.

“Rather than blow my last health potion, I’d rather save it for the worst case, especially since I have very little mana anyways. The point is: know your limits. Stop when you’re exhausted. Push as far as you can, but don’t push so far that you hurt yourself. If I kept on and got injured worse, I might be down for days, weeks… and that’s wasted time. Wasted time where I can’t level or obtain loot. Wasted time where I get behind the level curve. Better to do one dungeon, say “that sucks” about my plans, and bounce back to take on the next one tomorrow.”

“It’s quite wise of you. Many Players lost their lives pushing too far rather than taking reasonable breaks in my run,” Cy commented.

Eric nodded. He wasn’t talking to Cy, though. He was talking to the other Players—his other Players. To humanity, writ large. All of them. Everyone. “The point is, your grand plans, whatever… they can stick around until tomorrow. They’ll still be here. Keep yourself alive. You—every one of you—are Earth’s hope. Humanity’s hope. Before anything, we’ve all got to survive. So… survive. All of you. Survive.”

He walked on, trying to think of something else to say, but he was exhausted, and nothing else came to him. He shrugged with the shoulder further from his injured ribs, then grimaced as they panged anyways. “Guess that’s all.”

The fog was swirling around his knees as he stumbled up the stairs to the safe zone. He headed straight upstairs, eyes locked on the bath through the walls. He couldn’t see it, but he knew where it was, and it was all he could think about.

“Hey, kid—” a high pitched voice called.

“Later, Joe,” Eric said, then did a double take. A chubby toddler strutted up to him, walking with that limbs-wide tummy-out waddle that only early walkers could master. “What the…”

“Damn, you’re beat up,” the toddler—Joe—commented, looking him up and down. “Take a load off.”

“That’s where I was headed. The bath. To take a load off.”

Cy hopped off his shoulder as he passed the kitchen and hopped toward the sink, off to take his own version of a hot bath, Eric presumed.

“Hey, you do that. I’ll sit outside the door. We got some viewers yesterday—real, actual viewership—and they had some comments. Stuff for you to work on.”

Exhausted as he was, Eric just stared at Joe. At the same time, the little voice at the back of his head whispered: this is good stuff. Highly valuable. You need to know this!

He waved his hand. “Sure. As long as it doesn’t get in the way of my bath.”

“Excellent. So here’s the rub: good stuff. Writ large, good stuff.”

At the top of the stairs, Eric diverted into the bathroom. As promised, Joe sat down outside. Eric started filling the tub and undressing, and Joe spoke, projecting his voice so Eric could hear him even over the rush of water. “Had a lot of comments asking what your class is, why you aren’t using it. You’re a mage, kid. People want to see the magic. Big, flashy spells.”

Eric chuckled under his breath. “Already on top of that one.”

“Love to hear it. Had some people that loved the commentary, some that wanted you to shut up. Like I said—pick one and stick to it. Quip or don’t, but don’t ride the middle line on it.”

“Yeah… I still need to work on that.” The tub filled quickly with steaming water, and Eric shut off the tap. He sank into the water, letting out a sigh as the water cradled his aching body and the heat washed away his pain. The water instantly clouded with red dust and drying blood, but he was too lazy to draw a clean tub. The dirty water would have to be good enough.

“You know, you can give me material before or after the actual run. I can dub it in over you fighting, if that works better.”

“Eh… I’ll think about it.”

“That’s fair. It won’t be as good as you reacting in the moment. Just remember: you either talk or don’t, but you don’t hit the sour spot in the middle.”

“Got it.”

“Hmm, what else. There was a lot of bullshit. Nonsense, you know. Stuff that’s just abuse for abuse’s sake. We can ignore that—no, let me ignore it for you. Better if you don’t drink any poison.”

“You’d rather drink it yourself?” Eric joked.

“I do love me some poisons. Love them even better when I experience them through other people’s bodies.” There was a pause, then Joe continued. “Some love too, of course. Standard stuff, nothing worth getting excited over.”

“Which one’s more, the hate or the love?”

Joe scoffed. “You had your Internet, no? You know the answer.”

Eric sighed. “I hoped it wasn’t true.”

“You’ll grow into it.” There was the sound of turning pages, which Eric couldn’t explain, since he’d seen the toddler; Joe wasn’t carrying any paper. “Hmm… what else.”

There was silence for a while. Eric just relaxed into it, then sat forward. “Joe.”

“Present.”

“Today, there was… it was like an audience that just materialized around me. They were real. They cheered, they… could throw things. I…”

“Ah. They’re doing that already?”

“Doing it already?”

Joe sighed. It sounded inappropriately adorable in his toddler voice. “No one win… no one has ever won the Game. But there are off-ramps. Options. Things you can choose that allow you to ensure your species’ survival… or at least the survival of the Audience. That is part of what makes up the “grand Audience,” the Audience writ large—the surviving Audiences of previous Games where the Game’s champion… the last man standing—made a choice to ensure their survival. What you saw, I assume, was one of those Audiences. Unless it was your Audience; that’s also an option.”

“No, it was goblins. You know, green skinned, big bat ears, small, wearing rags…”

“Ah. Yes. They are a… notorious Audience. Low-hanging fruit, as it were.”

Eric turned his head, looking at Joe’s voice through the door. “So that’s just a thing that can happen?”

“Among many other things, yes.”

“And, uh… that choice. The thing the champion did to ensure the Audience’s survival that wasn’t winning the Game. What was it?”

There was silence. Silence for so long that Eric thought Joe had just left, for so long that he started to drift off, between the pain, the hot water, and the general quietude. And then, quietly, so soft he almost thought he imagined it: “You’ll know.”

“Huh?”

“I said, you’ll figure it out sooner or later, kid. Don’t worry about it. That’s a long, long way off, assuming you even are the champion.”

“I won’t be. I refuse to be the last man standing. I’m going to help as many people as I can. Save as many Players as I can. We—humanity, that is—we’ll get through this. Together.”

“It’s good to be young! So full of life and vigor and hope!” Joe clapped. “Well, that’s all for today. If you had an audience battle, that’ll hit big tonight; I gotta get to work editing. I’ll see you on the other side, kid.”

Eric leaned back, closing his eyes. Guess that’s one of those things Joe can’t talk about. “See you around, Joe.”
