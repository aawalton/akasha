When it happened, the change was immediately obvious. From one moment to the next, our journey as we barreled down the Long Road toward the Holds was outright perilous. Rattling, and shaking, and impacts as the Final Line was towed by a extremely nimble lizard happened constantly.

And then…it stopped.

Suddenly, the journey turned downright pleasant. The friction and rattling seemed to cease, and I didn’t feel a single change in elevation as the Chamel leapt over rubble. It had been nearly two days now, since we had departed from the caldera, and we’d already rested the Chamels and gotten back underway. In that time, I’d found myself feeling nearly superfluous. There really wasn’t much for me to do, even though I was, nominally, the Captain of this ship. I was in my cabin at the time, working on…something new, in an attempt to keep myself busy.

After all, it would have felt like a waste to just discard the crystal left behind from my experiments. Best to put it to use, somehow.

Still, the change from fraught journey to pleasant jaunt was so immediate that it broke me out of my crafting fugue. I looked up from my bench, tired eyes blinking, and cocked my head. It…felt like we were still moving, but the difference from before startling. The only thing I could reasonably equate it to would be transitioning from a dirt road back on Terra to a smooth, newly paved highway.

I didn’t get the chance to consider the matter more before the door to my room opened, and Syliva poked her head in. My lover had been just as restless as I was, something I’d noticed was common on this ship for this particular trip, but had chosen other means to keep herself busy than I had. I hadn’t seen much of either her or anyone else, for that matter, for the last few days.

She spoke up before I could say anything. “We need you up on deck, Nathan.”

I blinked once more, and she was gone. At my feet, Fade stirred from his dozing state, raising his head and blinking bleary eyes. His new antlers were finally starting to come in, but they were just small nubs poking out of his forehead. Still, that meant he was free of the hated bandages, something he was grateful for.

“What’s this about?” He asked groggily.

I rolled my eyes at him and stood up from my chair, cracking my back and groaning as I did. I threw on the blue and red coat of my station, and smiled wryly at him. “Beyond the obvious, you mean? Let’s go see what they want.”

Fade mentally sighed and stood up to join me. “Alright, alright…”

We stepped out into the hallway, only to have to lean to the side to dodge an excited crewman as he sprinted down the hallway in the direction of the deck. Exchanging a look with Fade, we followed after him.

As soon as we set foot onto the deck, I understood.

The Long Road had changed.

Before, it had essentially been a dilapidated ruin, full of rubble, boulders, and collapsed sections with only a narrow path through the center of it cleared. I was still astonished that Azarus had done that much, but it had looked like it had very much been abandoned for centuries.

Not so anymore.

We were still on the same section of the road, but now it looked maintained. The Final Line had broken through into a completely cleared section of the ancient Dwarven roadway, and for the first time, I could really appreciate the enormity of it.

Because it was lit. We didn’t even need the lanterns on the Final Line, arranged all around the perimeter so both our drivers and the Chamels could see where they were going. I could see people going around to all of the light sources and snuffing them out. Embedded in the ceiling of the Long Road, far above us, were the largest Mana lights I had ever seen. Huge chunks of rough-hewn crystal glowing a soft, warm light, bright enough to illuminate the entire area in a light evocative of a merrily dancing fireplace.

The thing is, though, that they were partially obscured by what almost looked like clouds. White, wispy ones. Intellectually, I knew it had to just be a light fog that had collected near the ceiling, but still.

I couldn’t keep my eyes off of them, entranced by the sight, and I wasn’t the only one. The entire scene was so evocative of an evening sky, even with the jagged tips of glowing crystal poking through it, that I was moved by it.

It had been…I don’t even know how many months, now, since I had seen a sky that wasn’t dark, and grey, and evil. I knew it was artificial. I knew that we were who knows how many miles beneath the surface right now.

But goddamnit, it almost moved me to tears just to see even an artificial sky that wasn’t tainted by the Skyfall.

Reluctantly tearing my eyes away from the artificial skyline, I maneuvered my way around the crowds of gawking crewmen on the deck, making my way to the wheelhouse on the upper level. There, I could see that what amounted to the new circle of Captains for the expedition had gathered. Sylvia was already here and had been acting as the representative of the remaining Martial Orders aside from the Polaris Reach from Herztal, as the de facto highest ranking officer still present for the Eclipsed Dawn. To Renauld’s discomfort, even though he was nearly an exile, he became the voice for the Gnolls who had survived the massacre at the Ashen Bride, almost entirely because he was the son of Marcel. Bleddyn, of course, was here as the leader of the Unchained, as was Ragnvald as the war Chief of the Elves. As was Olag, looking…much recovered, from his bender following the loss of his city and the majority of his people. Thankfully, he was a bit, and I emphasize only a bit, more humble than he used to be. The Dwarf, at least, no longer attributed vaguely disturbing motivations to my actions in an admiring manner. He was more…quiet these days, more thoughtful. It was a welcome difference, frankly. Finally, I exchanged an easy nod with Bella, as well, since we had finally buried the hatchet. And even though she wasn’t an official Captain of the expedition, Maria, as my second, tended to loiter during leadership meetings as well.

And then, of course…there was Gustave.

It had been so long since I’d seen the young Herztalian officer that I had nearly, but not quite, forgotten about him. From what I’d been told, Gustave had been one of the Captains who had supported Marcel when it came to his little insurrection while I’d been captured by Anguis. In the entire time since the disaster at Rhoscara, he hadn’t shown his face in my presence, and according to Maria, he hadn’t shown his face in general. He hadn’t shown up for any of the strategy or planning sessions either before or after I had woken from my coma. According to her, he had squirreled himself away among some of the very, very few remaining Herztalian military personnel that had survived Rhoscara, and basically…hidden with them.

At this point, he really wasn’t much of a Captain. He had no forces to contribute; he had hidden from any kind of responsibility for trying to usurp me, and he had yet to even apologize for his actions.

I wasn’t terribly impressed with him.

In the old expedition, he had been a competent enough administrator. But in the new one? I barely considered him any different from any of the other soldiers.

Still, he had presented himself, haggard-looking, unshaven, when it came time for us to leave the caldera. He hadn’t been able to meet my eyes at the time, or speak above a whisper, but he was still apparently willing to risk his life for the future of Vereden.

That earned him the barest amount of acknowledgement.

But not a seat on the leaders council. And yet, he still showed up, as if he had difficulty letting go of the position he had once held. He never barged in to take a chair or join us in a circle, oftentimes loitering either outside a door or around a corner. Still within earshot, but without a voice. I…allowed it. We had no rule about secrecy when it came to our decisions.

What we did was too important for such pettiness.

This time, he was leaning against the side of the stairs as I approached them. I deliberately met his eyes as I passed him, and like always, he immediately looked away. I shook my head and just climbed past him. Sylvia was there to meet me at the wheel, as was everyone else.

“Beautiful, isn’t it?” She said with a quiet smile, sapphire eyes gazing off into the artificial sky within the titanic scope of a fully maintained section of the Long Road.

“I didn’t know they still maintained this,” Olag wandered up to us, a pensive look on his tattooed face. “I had heard the legends, of course, of how our cousins in the Holds preserve their sanity, without ever truly seeing the sky. Of the myriad heated sunstones embedded in their ceilings, vaporizing the condensation that accumulates upon the stone. Artificial clouds, with artificial suns. Apparently, they can even seed these clouds, further in on their farms, to cultivate their crops. But I’m surprised to see the system still maintained this far out.”

“And how far out are we?” I asked, joining the others in staring off into the distance.

“Hours,” Olag managed to drag his eyes away to look at me. “I believe we made better time than we expected. We are, perhaps, only about three or so hours out from the gates of our destination. Karag-”

“Varla,” I finished for him.

Coincidentally, or perhaps not so, the exact same Hold that Azarus had once resided in as a youth. To my understanding, there had been a distant relation in Karag Varla that had sponsored his stay, only for him to be essentially kicked out some years later, after he had learned to smith. Varla was the southernmost of the Mountain Holds that held the Dwarves who had lost the civil war in the wake of Yorgun’s death in the War in Heaven.

And also, coincidentally, right next to the Hold that Nehushtan was apparently hidden under. That…was going to be a bit of a problem, actually. Not for the distance, though. What I had discovered is that when I was told that particular Hold was right next to Varla, they literally meant it was inside the sister mountain that Varla resided in. They were basically twin peaks.

The problem came from the fact that the other hold had fallen centuries ago. Not to any kind of outside invading force, oh no, that would be a simple matter.

Instead, the Hold had fallen to madness. As in, every last member of Clan Korvok had apparently gone stark raving mad and started practicing cannibalism, frequently hunting down members of the other Holds, torturing them to death, skinning them whole, and fucking eating them.

Karag Korvok was essentially a charnel house on a level that the most savage Elves couldn’t even imagine.

At least the Elves would just kill you. The Korvokian Dwarves would make sure you experienced Hell first, and then disappear your corpse down into their monstrous gullets.

That, in the end, was why we had taken so many soldiers with us on the journey north. If it had just been a matter of delving down into wherever Nehushtan had hidden himself below Karag Korvok, I could have taken the journey myself. Maybe taken a few others with me.

But it was entirely possible we were going to need to fight our way through the hold so we could reach the Spirit. Which meant…we needed to be prepared.

I shook myself out of the wonder the artificial sky had inspired in me, and approached the railing that overlooked the main deck of the Final Line. I cast my sound amplification Spell, and clapped my hands. The noise rocketed out over the deck, catching the attention of the near hundred people who had gathered there to stare up at the ceiling.

“Alright, enough gawking!” I said loudly. “We’re three hours out from, so everyone needs to suit up! Arm yourselves, and armor up at that! I want us ready to march as soon as we set down! Spread the word, and get a move on, people!”

As my soldiers scrambled to obey orders, I gazed back out into the fully illuminated distance ahead of us in anticipation. Both because we might finally find a way through Smaragd’s war wards…

And because it was entirely possible I might be only hours away from reuniting with Azarus.
