Chapter V.XLVI (5.46) - Final’s Week

Kizu’s first final project was for Politic’s F. It was a mirror of what he’d done for History F in the first semester. He had to write up a report about a specific law and how it changed the political landscape of the nation as well as its influence on other nations. Thankfully, this one was a solo assignment so Kizu was able to do the report on his own. He went with an easy out for it, writing about the Elite Act of the Hon Empire. The founding of the Elite guards was something that came into effect shortly after Sasaki went into stasis.

The decision to choose that as his project focus was simple. He had access to Taroe, who knew everything about the subject. It also helped him get the grumpy ex-Elite talking during their nightly dinners down at his house. It added a bit of conversation to the otherwise quiet meals.

Combat F’s final was just the tournament itself, which would take place after all the other finals were complete. They spent each of the class periods leading up to the tournament practicing combat maneuvers. Evie was turning into a really competent sparring partner. Each fight with her felt entirely unique as she tapped into the memories and skills of different ancestors. The variety was exactly what Kizu needed as he went into the tournament blind to his future opponents.

Rejuvenation and Restoration F’s final was a paper anatomy test. Between both his own knowledge and Zayne’s memories, Kizu completed the test feeling confident about the results. The subject material was mostly just simple bone and muscle structure. The very basics of anatomy meant for them to build off of in future classes.

Divination B required him to showcase a combination of divination spells and another branch of magic. While the classes had been focused on illusions as their primary examples, Professor Grove had expanded the subject over the last few months. They were allowed to showcase any combination as their final examination. That, of course, led Aoi to bring in an undead head and show off a spell that allowed her to see through its eyes. That hadn’t gone over well with the rest of the class and led to new restrictions on future divination exams. According to Aoi though, she still received an excellent grade on the exam.

Kizu went with a far simpler option. He showed that his illusions were untraceable by standard spellsense. He’d mastered the technique months ago and was actually working on expanding it to mimic his necklace that completely blocked out a person’s detection through magical means. Hiding the innate magic of an individual was proving more challenging than he’d first assumed. Still, Professor Grove approved of his examination and passed him with very little critique.

Like with Divination B, Illusions A required them to showcase what they’d been studying over the semester. That consisted of three scents they’d mastered. Kizu had two subtler scents, the sea and the scent of the jungle after rain. The third scent had been of smoke, like he’d produced in their first lesson on the topic. He’d mastered it enough to control how much he created at a time as well as expanded the aura of its effects. During their test, Professor Ignis asked them about tactile illusions as well. The two students who’d proved they’d gone above the standard curriculum were awarded some sort of extra credit on the exam. That basically equated to extra notes of praise that future employers would receive when examining their records. Not something Kizu particularly cared about, and yet… he couldn’t help but feel annoyed he missed out on those studies. Tactile illusions were an interesting branch and he itched to study that niche of illusion magic. However, the simple fact was that he couldn’t master everything all at once.

That fact was hammered into him as he utterly whiffed the Conjuring F test. Both he and Sene created flawless summoning circles, but it equated to nothing. Kizu pressed his hand against the outer rim of the circle and fruitlessly channeled into it. Sene didn’t even bother attempting to summon anything.

Finally, Numerology F final exam was exactly what he expected. Questions about numbers and formulas. Where it deviated from the standard tests Kizu took weekly was that it instead asked about the theory involving the formulas and what situations would best call for the individual equations. It was an interesting way to approach the subject. Still, it was an F class final and Kizu had recently been studying B class material. He blitzed through it with ease.

He assumed that meant the end of his studies for the year. That assumption was proven wrong as he took his gate to Owl’s Respite with the intention of working on some brews in preparation for the upcoming tournament, only to find a solitary hooded figure standing on the deck.

“Wakino?” Kizu called out, nervously keeping his distance from the intruder in case it turned out to be someone else.

“Yes, Kaga Kizu,” Wakino said, not looking at him. “I arrived at this location with the intention of assisting your dimensionism. However, I sense an interesting portal on this ship. Care to explain?”

“You know about the gate to Taroe’s house,” Kizu replied, confused. “You studied it on his side of the portal weeks ago. There’s nothing really all that remarkable about it.”

“That is not the gate in which I speak of,” Wakino replied, her voice monotone.

Kizu blinked and thought for a moment. He had a few gates on the ship. One that let in sunlight for his plants and, of course, there was his bath. As his mind passed over the thought, he resisted the urge to slap his forehead.

“Oh. It’s a gate that leads to a hot spring. I designed it so I can fill my bath. It’s tiny, barely larger than a yennie coin.”

“What hot spring?” Wakino pressed. Her cloak rippled, despite the lack of a breeze.

“One… in the mountains north of Kyonaka.”

Silence. It stretched for a long minute.

“You have an open portal into Hon and you did not think to inform me?” Wakino’s voice was cold and flat.

“It’s the size of a thumbnail,” Kizu insisted. “Really, that’s the only reason it remains active. I just forgot to turn it off because it required so little blood for me to create.”

“Occasionally, I forgot you are a child, barely older than the one you mind. Occurrences such as this remind me of this fact. You are extremely naive if you truly believe this portal to be inconsequential.”

“What—”

“Take me to it,” Wakino demanded. “Do this, and after I will instruct you once more in spatial magic.”

Kizu guided her down into the ship’s cargo hold. Every step increased his heartbeat. He genuinely couldn’t see any way the tiny portal would be of any use, but he should have remembered that he wasn’t one of the most powerful spatial mages in the world. Wakino would obviously have a far more in-depth concept of what a portal like this meant and how to best utilize it.

She paused on the threshold of his laboratory. “Interesting interior decorations. You use this as a brewing station? And I see you have another gate designed to let in sunlight over those flowerbeds. The dimensionalism you’ve used to stretch the ship’s hull is admirable for an amateur. Not especially efficient, though decent for your skill level."

“Thank you?” Kizu was silently panicking as he looked over his workspace. It was an absolute mess. A half-chopped root was on the counter of his brewing station next to a spilled over bottle of kappa milk. The cauldron itself was crusted over with his last concoction. Normally, he cleaned all his potions immediately after use, but he’d been in a hurry to get to his final Politics F class that morning and decided to forgo cleaning, since he hadn’t brewed a concoction where it mattered.

The rest of the space wasn’t any better. His desk was piled high with open tomes and scattered quills. Next to his garden was a pile of weeds he’d been pulling last night and there were monkey footprints on the wall from Mort trotting through the soil.

His bath tub was the least disastrous area. It had been cleaned last night. And yet, as his eyes scanned over it, the sight caused his heart to miss a beat. Half-concealed by the curtain, a knife with the name ‘Anata’ etched into it rested on the short table beside the tub. It rested on a black towel and the metal glinted in the overhead lantern’s light. Worst of all, Kizu spotted the familiar specks of blood on the blade. He hadn’t absorbed all of it when Anata offered her blood to him yesterday. He’d been a fool and forgotten to wash the blade. Both an unsanitary and potentially dangerous mistake.

Wakino strolled in the opposite direction, over to the wall where he’d etched in one of the spatial glyphs. She tapped one of the characters with a finger, then dragged the carving through the wood. The other characters squeezed to the side, to make room for the implanted one.

While she was distracted by his glyphs, Kizu, as tactfully as possible, maneuvered across the room and over to the tub. He carefully slipped the knife into his spatial ring.

When Wakino stepped back, the room had expanded at least ten percent. “A newbie’s mistake, etching that glyph there. That placement only makes sense when you lack the full fundamental knowledge of glyphs. It’s obvious for the more educated. I will assign you a tome to study on the subject.”

“Thank you,” Kizu repeated. His legs were weak. He didn’t think she’d noticed. And, even if she had, she wasn’t likely to understand exactly what that knife meant.

Wakino glided forward, directly over to the wooden tub. The curtains fully snapped aside, completely uncovering it from view. Never even glancing at the short table with the black towel, she waved a hand and the spigot turned. Hot water rushed through the pipe, pouring into the bottom of the small basin.

It gurgled, then stopped.

“Unexpected,” Wakino said after a pause. “I understand now why nobody else has learned of the exploit to the Hon border system. It needed to be activated before Emperor Sasaki pulled the card and it sadly only functions in one direction. It is the same concept as the stories I’ve heard of people caught dead center on the border. They found their bodies only able to move in one direction. Away from Hon.”

“So my portal isn’t useful?” Kizu asked hopefully. He rather liked his bath system and wasn’t keen on it getting commandeered by the Hon nobility.

Wakino released whatever spatial hold she’d placed over the spigot and water once again poured free. Steam rose up from the bottom of the tub but remained trapped by invisible walls around the bathing area. Kizu had etched in additional enchantments in this curtained off section of his lab to retain all steam to keep it from messing with his horticulture and supplies.

“Not as useful as it potentially could have been,” Wakino replied. “But far from useless. I will report back to Wan and Emperor Sasaki on this development.” She hesitated. “Upon further consideration, perhaps, I will only report this to Wan.”

Kizu chuckled nervously. His heartbeat was finally returning to a natural pace. “What do you think Sasaki would do? There’s not any space for anything more than water.”

Wakino turned away from the tub. “Next, I will teach you the art of telekinesis. It is an ancient spell and iconic for spatial mages. You have displayed enough skill with barriers that I deem you sufficiently skilled to learn the spell.”

If Wakino’s strategy was to distract Kizu from his question, it worked.
