“Sir Frederick, I believe this pair of golems would make a fitting gift for you.” Victor waved his hand, and a pair of mechanical golems emerged from his interspatial ring.

Unlike the mass-produced models that patrolled the academy grounds, these were of an entirely different class. Each stood over two meters tall, their arms fashioned into versatile weapons — some ending in long, gleaming blades, others in mana-powered cannons. These units, boasting combat capabilities on par with an Elemental Adept Magus, were known as Specialist Aegis Mek, and they came in numerous specialized variations.

As for the two that Victor presented, one was a melee unit equipped with bladed arms, while the other specialized in long-range bombardment with limbs reshaped into mana cannons.

Frederick’s brows lifted slightly as he examined them. “Impressive craftsmanship…” he trailed off, extending a hand toward one of the constructs with clear interest.

“They’re capable of withstanding the attacks of an Elemental Adept Magus and can move on land at speeds comparable to a Ki Master,” Victor explained with a composed smile. “In essence, they can protect their master from nearly any conventional threat below the Nexus Temporal rank. Consider them a token of goodwill and of the Sanctum’s sincerity.”

“Then I shall accept them.” Frederick stored the two golems inside his interspatial ring. “A generous gift. I will remember this gesture.”

“I’m glad to hear it.”

Victor felt no concern about the golems being dismantled or reverse-engineered because each of them was fitted with a concealed self-destruct sequence, ensuring that any tampering would result in instant obliteration. Moreover, they remained secretly bound to his command, incapable of being turned against him or the Sanctum. Even more advantageous, the constructs were designed to transmit information back to him at regular intervals, effectively turning them into spying devices.

In a way, this gift benefited Victor far more than the recipient. And if it also earned him Frederick’s gratitude in the process, then all the better.

“Ah, right. One more thing, Headmaster Victor,” Frederick said, pausing mid-turn.

“Actually, Sir Frederick,” Victor interjected with a calm smile, “you may address me by my title — Astralium.” He wanted that title “Astralium” to spread far and wide across the kingdom. A name could be forgotten, but a title could last for generations. If the world remembered Astralium, they would take him far more seriously than any mere Magus named Victor.

“Very well, Sir Astralium. By the decree of Headmaster Ragnar of the Arcane Radiance College, you are invited to visit Mount Caelum in two weeks’ time, after your terms have been reviewed and accepted. Naturally, in the unlikely scenario that they are declined, another negotiation will be arranged.”

Mount Caelum? Victor wasn’t unfamiliar with this place, as it was where the Arcane Radiance College was located, the place where he had begun his path as a Magus.

Was this what the main mission referred to as buying time about?

In response to this invitation, Victor shook his head and politely refused, “I’m afraid I must decline for now. Circumstances prevent me from leaving the Sanctum within such a short span. The earliest I can make the journey would be... perhaps a year from now.”

Frederick nodded, not pushing further. “Understood. I won’t press the matter. I’ll inform Headmaster Ragnar of your situation.”

He then rose to his feet, saying, “Now, I must take my leave and report our agreement to the Arcane Radiance College,” before stepping toward the door.

Victor stood as well, extending his hand once more. “Safe travels, Envoy Frederick. Please convey my regards to your headmaster.”

Frederick clasped his hand firmly. “Until next time, Sir Astralium.”

As the envoy turned and strode out of the room, accompanied by Roland, Victor watched in silence. Through the window, he caught a glimpse of Frederick’s figure ascending into the sky moments after leaving the building — a streak of light cutting swiftly through the clouds until it vanished from sight.

Victor waited for several minutes, expecting to hear the familiar melodious chime that signaled a mission’s completion. Yet, no matter how long he waited, there was only silence.

“Hmm? The mission hasn’t been completed? Is Frederick not truly the envoy of the Arcane Radiance College? Was I deceived?” No, that’s impossible. He showed me an envoy’s insignia, and I’m sure it’s not fake… So, what seems to be a problem?

Don’t tell me… Victor watched at the horizon where Frederick had vanished moments earlier, a faint unease stirring in his chest. Something didn’t feel right.

Oh well, he thought, exhaling softly. Even if something is amiss, there’s little I can do about it now. Resolving the curse takes priority. Whatever happens… happens.

Besides, the mission might not be completed until Frederick reached the Arcane Radiance College and reported back. There was no need to assume the worst just yet — perhaps it was all just his imagination.

Having said that, Victor turned away from the window and made his way back toward the Headmaster’s Building to continue his experiments to find a way to dispel the curse.

The sky over the western plains was clear, painted with streaks of fading gold as dusk settled. A single figure cut across the horizon — Frederick Lightstone, his Magus robe fluttering in the wind as he sped through the clouds like a comet toward the east.

His mind was still occupied with thoughts of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi, the Magus force that he had just been sent on a diplomatic mission. A self-sustaining academy, an ambitious headmaster, and mechanical golems of Elemental Adept rank power… Astralium, you are far from ordinary, just like the rumors said.

He smiled faintly to himself. If the Arcane Radiance College handled this properly, it could become either a highly profitable alliance — or a ticking time bomb waiting to explode if not.

He soon began descending, intending to rest and recalibrate his mana reserves before resuming the long flight southeast. The elemental density here was thin — a barren stretch of wilderness hundreds of kilometers from the nearest settlement, unclaimed and untouched. Or so he thought.

All of a sudden, a faint ripple disturbed the air.

Mana fluctuations — subtle, but organized — appeared from somewhere beyond the hills. Frederick immediately sharpened his mana sense and turned toward the source. Several figures emerged from the shadows, cloaked in dark robes enchanted with concealment runes that masked their identities. Their movements were disciplined and precise, and Frederick recognized them as a strike unit from the Arcane Radiance College.

The envoy slowed, landing lightly on the ground. “Hm? Agents of the College?” he asked, his tone curious rather than wary. “You’re far from headquarters. What brings a strike team all the way out here?”

He recalled the instructions he’d received before departure: if the worst should occur, he was to activate a magic transmission device given by Headmaster Ragnar to summon the nearest strike unit. Yet, he hadn’t triggered it, so it was a wonder to him that they had come for him.

The leader stepped forward — a tall man clad in a black mantle embroidered with the insignia of the Arcane Radiance College’s Shadow Division. His presence radiated immense mana pressure, unmistakably that of a powerful Nexus Temporal Magus, at least at the Attuned Stage.

“Envoy Frederick Lightstone,” the man greeted in a neutral tone. “We’ve been sent to ensure your safe return.”

Frederick relaxed slightly, lowering his guard. “Ah, I see. I wasn’t aware additional escorts had been dispatched. You have my gratitude.”

He took a few steps closer, and that was when he saw him. Behind the strike formation, half-concealed beneath a distortion spell, stood a figure Frederick recognized instantly: Dean Rovar, head of the External Affairs Division.

Frederick creased his eyebrows. Rovar? What are you—

The thought never finished.

A flicker of light appeared, then pain. A Lux-elemental spell, silent and precise, lanced through the air like a beam of divine judgment and pierced his abdomen cleanly. Frederick gasped, looking down in disbelief as radiant magic seals ignited across his body, forming a lattice of light that sealed his mana before he could react.

“W-Wait… Why…?”

The leader of the strike team lowered his arm, the residual spell fading from his palm. His expression was calm, almost indifferent. “Forgive us, Envoy. The College no longer requires your report.”

The other members were holding magic tools and artifacts that neutralized his defenses, making him extremely weak in the process.

Frederick’s vision blurred. His body trembled violently, blood spilling from the wound as he dropped to one knee. “You… fools… do you have any idea what you’re doing? Do you truly think I’ll fall this easily?!”

He tried to forcefully activate his sigils and cast spells in retaliation, but before he could complete even a single incantation, another spell struck. A blade of condensed violet energy carved cleanly through his chest, silencing him completely. The envoy’s body collapsed, seemingly lifeless… Or at least on the verge of dying. Even the formidable life force of a Nexus Temporal Magus could not withstand multiple Gimmel-tier spells while caught unprepared and defenseless.

“What are you saying?” came a new voice, calm and cutting. “The envoy sent by the Arcane Radiance College was killed by the headmaster of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi after being lured into a trap. That is the story the world will hear.”

Dean Rovar stepped forward from the shadows, his expression cold and calculated.

“Oh, and that Sanctum…” he continued with a thin smile, “it holds far too many secrets. It would be best if they handed them over willingly. So”—his gaze turned toward the strike leader—“we’ll need someone to take Frederick’s place.”

“Let’s leave this place quickly,” Rovar added coldly. “Frederick’s life force should hold long enough for us to get some distance. We don’t want the Arcane Radiance College’s investigation unit to grow suspicious if he dies right before our eyes. And make sure Headmaster Ragnar is informed that the envoy perished based on my story. Truly tragic.”

The strike leader bowed slightly. “As you command.”

Without another word, they turned and vanished into the distance, melting into the shadows as the night wind swept across the barren plain.

Silence returned, broken only by the faint hiss of unstable mana leaking from the envoy’s body and the shallow, uneven breaths he let out. Frederick’s body twitched weakly. Though his vision flickered in and out and his consciousness hung by a thread, he was still alive.

In truth, when he had activated his sigils earlier, he hadn’t attempted an offensive spell at all but focused on casting a defensive spell. That single, desperate choice had preserved a sliver of his life force, leaving him barely clinging to existence. Unfortunately, his injuries were far too severe. He couldn’t even lift an arm, let alone cast another spell or reach for a potion. All he could do was lie there and wait for death’s inevitable call. So, this… is how it ends…?

Then, a soft ripple of light emerged from his interspatial ring, and two silhouettes materialized beside him — the pair of mechanical golems the headmaster of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi had gifted him earlier: the Specialist Aegis Mek. Their eyes flickered to life with a dim blue light as they began assessing the scene.

“Master’s vitals… critical,” one of them intoned in a flat, mechanical voice. “Emergency directive initiated. Administering healing procedures.”

The second golem retrieved a high-grade healing potion, smearing part of it over Frederick’s wounds before carefully pouring the rest between his lips.

Once the potion began to take effect, one golem lifted Frederick in its arms with surprising gentleness while the other activated a cloaking field. Together, they turned and started moving across the wilderness, their heavy steps leaving no trace behind.

Within moments, the three figures vanished into the night toward the unknown.
