It had been centuries since the Spirits met. Their Voices and Vowed often gathered in groups to seek out the strongest and nourish them, expanding the Spirits’ forces for the Final Era. But even though they gathered forces, the Spirits had grown weaker with the passage of time. Creating a Voice cost them dearly, and while bestowing Blessed with fractions of their power did not drain them nearly as much as it affected the Blessed, it required Spirit Essence to complete the Ritus.

And Spirit Essence could not be recovered easily. The old Spirits lost the Realm they were bound to when they retreated to Razarn, leaving them only with two options for recovering Spirit Essence: consuming the Core Essence of sentient life or hibernating in aspect domains.

After the disaster with the human livestock, most Spirits focused on recovering their power. They retreated and left civilization to fend for itself. They graced a few beasts and mighty warriors with enough spirit power to slay Spawns and went into hibernation. But even those that hibernated for centuries stirred when Razarn rumbled.

The spirituality of the entire plane trembled, prophesying the return of one of them. However, following the Primal Spirits’ last words, the Spirits realized something else as well.

The age of the Spirits was ending. After standing at the top of the food chain alongside the Gods for thousands upon thousands of years, their time was coming to an end.

But they were not like the Gods. While the Gods died out to never rise again, the Spirits would never cease to exist.

As long as the Devourer was stopped and the seals on his prison remained reinforced, that is.

“What in the Allfather’s name is that thing?” Death asked into the silence of the Spirits’ war room.

Her siblings were not yet back in Dracos, but it was only a matter of time before they would. Even the lost lamb would return at some point, if she surrendered the path of madness and slaughter. For now, the projections of her brothers and sisters materializing from their Spirit Fragments were all Death needed to decide what they would do next.

Time was changing. Fast – too fast for her liking – but there was nothing she could do about it. Nothing other than doing what she was best at. Death.

Her senses zeroed in on the black marble that radiated the power of the Devourer, making her frown deeply.

It was unmoving and not dangerous, or it would have attacked the Primal Heir and the Proto Knight who had returned with it from their hunt, but that only made it more dangerous in the Death Spirit’s eyes.

The other Spirits thought the same, worry marring the age-old creatures’ faces. Except for Thyria, of course. The Spirit Fragment of their mad sibling had been used for the first time since they sealed her and the fae away, but she did not care about their problem. The World Tree, the very Spirit who had been the most obsessed with the Devourer, could not care less about the threat looming over all of Razarn right now.

Tears streaked down her unblemished face, and a brilliant and completely misplaced smile crossed her features.

“He’s back…” she muttered.

“The Earth has returned, yes.” Death nodded slowly, though even as she spoke, irritation seeped into her voice. “But that is not Garus, nor will it help us against the Devourer.”

“Not yet,” Volca’s voice echoed in her ears, correcting Death. “The Earth Spirit may be young, but he wields the power of a full-fledged Spirit.”

“And he is connected to the Primal Heir.” Glacia’s sonorous voice rang out from her projection. “I did not approve of their bond, but Volca was right. The human is the Primal Heir, and we will be better off supporting him.”

Death agreed. Even if it was only for the sake of the Primal Spirit, Allfather of Spirits, Death would support Adam. And once the vessel was ready, she would use the Primal Fragment to replace the young human with the only being strong enough to fight the Devourer to a standstill.

Aureus felt it first when the Insignia appeared on the World’s core. It did not belong to him but to Adam instead, and it was so much more than the cracked lines etched on his own heart.

The lines were always there, ever since he evolved from a Mutant Soilback, but it took him a while to understand what they meant. They were a silent reminder of who he was bound to. Nurturing the lines and molding them into the right shape had not been easy. However, with the Primal’s blessing, it was possible. Golden-brown light spread through the lines carved into his flesh now that he finally succeeded, and he couldn’t be happier about the result.

Ether surged toward him in tides. As for the surrounding earth, it answered him through the borders of the World and Adam’s body. Stone’s movement, dust and upturned soil rising into the air, even the faintest of grains were drawn toward Aureus as though Razarn recognized something that had been long overdue.

But Aureus’ attention was not on Razarn and its call. It reached inward toward his heart where ancient letters inscribed in Runic Tincture rested. The symbols made little sense to the Earthheart, but they had been there ever since he evolved. It was just that his understanding had been too limited. Even now he was not fully ready, but Adam needed him. And he needed Adam.

As the connection to the young Primal grew stronger and stronger, meeting the third threshold, the blessing of the Primal grew stronger. Understanding dawned upon Aureus where confusion had been before. The cracked lines realigned themselves, creating marks Aureus would have never dreamed of were etched on his heart. Each heartbeat seemed to engrave another mark, strengthening his connection to Razarn’s being.

Every pulse filled him with more power, deepening the connection that had already been strong before. At last, as if it had always been waiting for this very moment, something clicked.

It was not physical, nor in his mind. The change was in his soul. A lock Aureus had only faintly sensed before came apart as the last missing piece settled into place. Then, once more, the connection between the Earthheart and the young Primal surged.

The surge was intense, yet it was unlike the soulshare of an ordinary Blessed and an ordinary Soulkin. It was the resurrection of the Earthen Spirit, heralding the return of the Primal and the Final Era.

Daniel Zerog was frustrated. He trained tirelessly and pushed further every day, yet it felt like he was falling behind. Lea had already given up and focused on her career as a Huntress, but Daniel didn’t accept defeat. He would not go under this easily.

How else was he supposed to rise to a Ruler with Adam?

He will become a Ruler with or without me. Daniel reminded himself once again. It was painful, knowing that his talent was nowhere near Adam’s level, but he had to acknowledge that talent was not everything.

Luck played an important role as well, sure, but everyone knew that Adam was crazy. He was a damn workaholic, or else they would have had some fun together in Dracos. But those days were over. They were no longer children and had other responsibilities to take care of.

That included finding the Elemental Phoenix’s location. Adam was adamant about finding his dead Soulkin, which was easier said than done. After all, one of the Spirits was in possession of the phoenix’s...egg?

He is the friend of Spirits. Coco mooed.

Daniel narrowed his eyes but didn’t deny his Soulkin’s comment. Coco was likely correct. He did not know how it happened or why, but Adam was somehow connected to most beasts and Spirits. If not for that, Aureus would have never gotten this powerful, and the exact same could be said about Volix, Sapsie, and that murderous Ferronox Mantis.

Being close to him was hard. It made people jealous and doubt their own talent. Or so Lea said.

Daniel never believed that. If anything, being close to Adam helped him grow much stronger than he would have otherwise. He helped him procure the World Aspect, and it was only thanks to him that they made it to Dracos. And that was worth a lot.

I would have evolved even if not for him. Coco commented, to which Daniel agreed loud enough to silence the denial floating through his mind.

In all honesty, he doubted Coco would have evolved if they stayed back in the Bastion. Their connection would have never grown enough to trigger a phenomenon.

The lightning-coated letters etched on Daniel’s core still hurt from when Coco evolved, and he knew that his Soulkin was still hurting from becoming something the Caldera, working with Alchemist Zuan, had called a ‘sacred beast’.

He heard the term from Adam once, and it filled him with pride.

Don’t idle around too much, or I will catch up to you, Adam! Daniel promised in his heart before returning to the alchemy cauldron, concocting yet another Elixir to stabilize Coco’s evolution.

Thaira Heilos could not believe it. He glared at the hundreds of failed scrolls sprawled all over the table. His feet didn’t reach the ground, or he would have crushed the floor, which would have undoubtedly been punished dearly.

“Whyy?!? Why is this not working!!!” he screamed, ripping apart yet another parchment.

“Thaira… What did I tell you about etiquette and respectful behavior?” His mother appeared by his side, though Thaira could have sworn that she hadn’t been in the room a moment earlier.

He flinched and tried his utmost to put the torn parchment back together. When that failed, he flailed his arms and cursed that ocean-cursed human.

Thaira never expected that human to trouble him so much. His mother forced him to teach that ignorant man so that he could learn from teaching, but also to understand more about himself and how much his teachers struggled to teach him.

He didn’t really understand his mother’s reasoning, it still didn’t make any sense to him, but he had taken a liking to the human. At first, at least. Experimenting on that lunatic was still fun and all, but with him growing stronger every time they met nowadays, everything was changing. The weave of humans was not static. It was ever-moving, unlike the Mikoniv’s nodes, or was that, too, just an issue of that particular human? The Gate Outflow Syndrome or whatever.

“Why is he growing so fast? He should slow down…or is he making fun of me?!” Thaira squealed and was about to continue to rant when his mother stared daggers at him.

“He is working very hard. Much harder than you are, you know?” Mother said, using the very tone Thaira hated the most – matter-of-factly. Even worse was that his mother was likely right. Human Adam, or whatever he was called, had improved greatly over the last few months. So much so that he could create the first few vortexes of the Vortex Gate technique by himself already.

It was just that they had no idea how to perfect them. And that was his mistake. Human Adam’s, not Thaira’s. He was growing stronger and his weave was moving. Why would it be Thaira’s fault that they couldn’t find out how to create a technique that was usually used by the Mikoniv on that stupid human?

“I know what you’ve been thinking.” His mother flicked the young Mikoniv’s forehead. “And we both know that you can do it. All you need is to work a little harder. Don’t you want to show off to Adam when you meet him again? Demonstrate your superiority to him?”

He was better than Human Adam. Of course he was, but it did look like not everyone believed as much.

Maybe I do have to show everyone what I’m capable of. Thaira mused and reasserted himself. It was time to show that stupid human just how great he was!

His mother smiled in victory as her son retrieved a feather and a scroll. Water-attuned ether swirled around him, creating a perfect copy of the ill human’s weave. The weave’s movement and autonomous circulation system, as well as the ether outflow rate and the location of every linked Gate, were incorporated into the replica, showing just how hard Thaira had worked. And how much of a genius the young Mikoniv was.

Thaira Heilos was going to become the greatest Razarn had ever seen. His mother was certain of that. And that he needed a little motivator.
