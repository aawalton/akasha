Mapel’s gods were the ones who had invited outside help, so Percy didn’t think they’d harm him even if he were discovered. Besides, a weak lesser spring like this was unlikely to have any karmic deities capable of tracking his main body or planet, nor anyone strong enough to threaten Remior.

He viewed this mission as an excellent opportunity to develop a non-intrusive method of cleansing sapients of corruption and operate on another world for a prolonged period of time without anyone noticing. His trip to Tenefyre had also been a little like that, but Percy intended to get more involved with the natives this time.

If he didn’t care about the suffering of the infected, he knew that he could cleanse them with formations like the ones he’d used in the tournament, but his heavy-handed approach wasn’t suitable for weaker beasts and sapients – least of all children and low-borns.

‘I suppose we should start by figuring out what we’re dealing with,’ he told his fellow aspect who nodded in agreement.

Percy had previously estimated that the corruption had affected about a quarter of the population, though the ratio varied slightly depending on the natives’ grades. Unsurprisingly, Greens appeared more resistant than Reds, yet the difference was rather small.

Based on what he had learned from Nephthys, attaining divinity helped a lot more than regular promotions, as the goddess had resisted the Slumber for significantly longer than the Amenthei mortals. A creature’s intelligence also seemed to matter much more than their grade, since the first insects and animals on the destroyed planet had supposedly been affected years before the people had started falling asleep.

To test whether that applied here, Percy initially ignored the town and had his sentries search the wilderness for infected animals.

Sure enough, over seventy percent of the creatures he examined had a blob of corruption swirling within their bodies. The putrid mass was hidden quite well, preventing Percy from detecting it with a cursory scan. He had to pay closer attention to the wildlife to identify the corruption, which was probably why he hadn’t noticed it earlier.

‘At least we’ll have plenty of test subjects to try our formations on before touching the sapients,’ Micky said.

‘I’m more relieved that our host happened to be clean,’ Percy replied.

Even if he had possessed an infected creature, leaving the planet should have cured him of any negative influence, though he was glad to have dodged that hassle entirely. What he didn’t know, was whether he had avoided the corruption out of pure luck, or whether it was the squirrel’s death that had cleansed it.

‘That shouldn’t be the case,’ he corrected himself. ‘The curse probably persists as long as one’s soul does. I don’t see why a few spectral injuries would purge it.’

It was possible that Percy had just gotten lucky, yet it was safer to assume that he’d picked a corrupted creature and inadvertently eliminated the infection while possessing it.

Ningirima’s Mercy was supposed to grant him resistance to infestations, and its interactions with the other facets of his Decree had probably strengthened it and expanded its features beyond its creator’s original intentions. The purification runes that Percy had woven into his Cradles might have also played a role after years of constantly altering the very nature of his mana – and by extension his body, willpower, and soul – though he had no way of estimating the extent of their contribution.

‘Let’s figure out which dominion the infestation falls under, and how exactly it affects its victims,’ he decided.

Percy had interacted with infestations from four different dominions so far, and each had functioned very differently.

The black vines that had devoured Takeo’s village had probably belonged to the dominion of Gluttony, while the dark ocean that had swallowed Amenthes had likely belonged to Sloth. The Cardinal Devil that had driven the people of Robari to kill one another was almost certainly Avarice itself, while the berserk creatures Percy had cleansed or exterminated on Spiralis B-3 had been infected by Wrath.

He hadn’t personally experienced infestations of the remaining three dominions, but he’d at least heard mentions of them in his travels – they were Pride, Lust, and Dread – so he already had some idea of what to expect from them.

Figuring out which one the creatures of Mapel were infected with proved slightly harder than he would have liked.

Beasts were inherently aggressive, lustful and gluttonous, and Percy wasn’t sure how to even recognize signs of an inflated ego or greed in their behaviour. Ironically, while the wildlife was corrupted in a much higher ratio than the sapients, Percy was forced to send a couple of sentries back to the village, since the natives’ behaviour was varied and nuanced enough to help him pinpoint the affected traits.

He wore his translation device on his main body to understand what the apes were saying. His newfound ability to control his soul clones directly allowed him to spy on people of multiple planets simultaneously without having to possess them or purchase a second device from Metatron. Another fortunate consequence of his third affinity that he hadn’t considered before.

After observing the tailed apes for a couple of hours, he ruled out Sloth and Dread. The corruption hadn’t made them any lazier, sleepier, nor particularly meek.

Much to Percy’s dismay, the natives did have a tendency of courting and mating with one another quite often, causing numerous awkward scenes that he had no interest in watching and had to actively avert his magical senses from. However, they got into plenty of heated arguments as well, so Percy had trouble separating the demonic influence from their natural behaviour.

‘Those without the sludge can’t keep it in their non-existent pants either, so it’s probably not Lust,’ Micky pointed out. ‘Arguments, on the other hand, seem to be more common when the infected are involved.’

Percy agreed. He also spotted no pattern that he would attribute to Gluttony, yet he still wasn’t sure whether to blame the locals’ increased aggression on Pride, Avarice, or Wrath.

Despite the fact that he had yet to identify the exact dominion, he had learned a bunch of other things about the natives.

Firstly, even these mostly-low-born commoners living in a small village in the middle of nowhere seemed to be aware of the sinister infestation plaguing their lives, and were taking certain precautions to protect their tiny community from the worst of it.

The majority of the infected had the tails on their right forearms coiled tightly in a crisscrossing pattern around the limb, regardless of whether this pattern fit the rest of their “clothing” style. Those tails were adorned with lots of transparent rings – which was one of very few types of external accessories that the villagers wore.

Percy guessed that this was done to identify the corrupted and let others know how to interact with them.

Most of the infected tended to stay at home. From what little Percy had seen, going out in public wasn’t exactly forbidden, but the corrupted actively avoided approaching others unless necessary, likely to lower the odds of spreading the infection. Even when they had to interact with the rest, everyone seemed to walk on eggshells. The apes kept more physical distance between them and were mindful of their words, so that they didn’t say anything too provocative.

Whenever a conversation involving an infected person got out of hand, the strange rings would begin flashing with a deep red light, at which point everyone just stopped talking and took steps to isolate themselves. It seemed to be against the law – or at least heavily frowned upon – for both the infected and the healthy to risk everyone’s safety by continuing to fan the flames.

There were a few corrupted people who didn’t wear any rings and took no precautions around others, though Percy wasn’t sure whether they were doing it out of selfishness or simply because their condition had yet to be diagnosed.

He was a little surprised to see these people treating the infestation so nonchalantly, though perhaps he shouldn’t be. Unlike the Amenthei or Takeo’s kin, the natives of this planet obviously knew a thing or two about the wider cosmos, and had clearly been dealing with their infestation for a very long time.

Their gods couldn’t be everywhere at once, so it made sense that they’d introduced certain rules to help the mortals manage the disease for prolonged periods of time by themselves, and that everyone had just accepted the corruption as an inevitable – if inconvenient – part of their lives.

Percy had also seen how the infection tended to spread.

Whenever a corrupted person was agitated, the dark mass inside their body would swell to multiple times its original size, causing them to discharge lots of tiny chunks of sludge that tried to latch onto other living beings. Most of the flung particles missed entirely, failed to stick, or were neutralized by the recipient’s body, but one would occasionally manage to take root in a new host.

This was probably one of the reasons the natives were obligated to stop interacting whenever their negative emotions flared up. Leaving aside the physical damage they could cause if their arguments devolved to violence, the infection clearly spread faster whenever its effects triggered.

Percy didn’t know whether that was just a feature of this particular dominion.

The Slumber on Amenthes had seemingly affected people randomly, and the beasts on Spiralis B-3 had only tried to infect him upon dying. The Cardinal Devil on Robari appeared to have been more in control of who it corrupted, and the infestation on Takeo’s planet had twisted the environment much more than the people.

‘Zoris? What do you think?’ he asked the sentient ring wrapped around his human finger while explaining his findings and conjecture.

‘Unfortunately, every demonic infestation is its own unique puzzle,’ the former god replied. ‘Some rules – like the inability of regular infestations to leave their planet – seem to extend to all of them, but the exact method by which they spread or affect their victims varies not only from one dominion to another, but also between infestations of the same dominion.’

Percy sighed, not liking what he was hearing. Then again, it only made sense that purging these cosmic cancers was a complex matter, otherwise the peak factions would have found a way to eliminate them all a long time ago.

Leaving only a couple of sentries behind to keep collecting information on the sapients, he decided to start experimenting with the forest animals. He captured two birds, another squirrel-like beast of the same species as his host, and a pair of lizard-like creatures.

Each of his test subjects was corrupted, and none had a higher grade than Orange, since Percy wanted to try his formations out on more fragile animals than the giant monsters from Spiralis B-3.

‘Let’s see how many of you are going to survive,’ he thought while scanning his unwilling assistants up and down, his predatory gaze causing the poor things to shudder.
