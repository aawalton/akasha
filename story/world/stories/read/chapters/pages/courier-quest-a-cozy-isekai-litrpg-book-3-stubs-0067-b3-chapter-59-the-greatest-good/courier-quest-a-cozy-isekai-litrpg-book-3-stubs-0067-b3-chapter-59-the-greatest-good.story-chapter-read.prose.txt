“I don’t understand,” Mayor Nester said as he looked at the spoils the group had brought back. A crowd of people had stopped to watch.

More than fifty pristine hearts of ice laid in the snow just outside of Froslan Heights. Now that they weren’t in the dungeon, they were little more than inert reagents. They wouldn’t be able to reconstitute their bodies even in the cold.

In contrast, there were only twenty heartless ice golems. That included the giant, twelve-foot-tall one that had stepped outside. As similar as they were to each other, they were big enough that Trevor had filled his inventory even with Stacks active.

“My husband is uniquely suited for fighting this kind of monster, and we worked as a team in order to get the best possible outcome,” Adabelle informed the mayor with a smile.

“Yes, I understand that part,” the elf said, placing a hand on his cheek before gesturing to the real surprise. “It’s more that I’ve never seen anything like this before.”

Clay slithered out from underneath the massive, slightly charred heart of ice. They had been too tired to bring it down the mountain themselves, but they had enough energy to turn into a sled that Hans pulled behind him. Sometimes it wasn’t needed, as they were going downhill most of the way, but he still had the strength and size to keep it from going out of control so it wouldn’t break the cargo.

“My wife is uniquely suited for fighting this kind of monster,” Trevor replied as he picked up his familiar and put them over his shoulders. The earth elemental oozed slightly, becoming a stoat-shaped scarf as they rested. He made a note to retrieve the rest of the husk later before he forgot. “All by her lonesome, too, in order to get the best possible outcome.”

“Hans just smash. Adbelle not do that. She scary,” the yeti grunted.

“There’s also that,” the courier agreed with a smile.

“Well, this is certainly something else,” Nester said, still staring in disbelief. “I’ll be honest, Asalorana told me to expect the moon but she didn’t tell me I’d be getting the stars as well.”

“I’m sure this is a bigger haul than you’ve gotten even before Hans showed up,” Adabelle stated, her tail wrapping around Trevor’s wrist before giving it a gentle squeeze. “And it would have been bigger if I hadn’t melted the rest of it. My apologies.”

“You have nothing to apologize for because you’re right, this is far bigger,” he confirmed before shaking his head. “I feel like you must take at least half of these hearts. The amount of wealth you’ve brought back today could very well represent at least a year’s worth of income for the entire village. We cannot afford to pay you what it is worth.”

Hans shuffled slightly. “That lot?”

“Yes, that’s a lot,” Nester answered patiently. “You do good work, Hans, and you protect the village. That’s all we ask of you. But, this? This is at a level beyond what I’ve seen before.”

“Oh,” the yeti grunted. He looked at the hearts and shells. It was clear that he was thinking of something, and everyone was quiet as they waited for him to reach whatever conclusion he could. He turned to Adabelle. “How Hans do better?”

She arched an eyebrow at him. “In the dungeon?”

“Yes,” he answered, leaning down so that he was eye level with her.

“Hmm.” Adabelle tilted her head. “You could only smash the arms, legs, and heads. I have to imagine that bringing back the torsos and hearts would be a lot better than pieces.”

“Oh. Okay,” Hans said, immediately accepting her advice. “Go practice now. Get better. Nest praise Hans. Next time, Adbelle, Hans be scary one. You see. Will get strong, cause can read now!”

Before anyone could say anything, the yeti took off into the air with another physics-defying leap. There was a large crash out in the distance as he landed, and then there was silence.

“I’ve been trying to instill that in him for years,” Nester said with a weary smile. “I guess it’s good that he learns it eventually. Maybe a fellow warrior was just what he needed to take it seriously, huh?”

“That could be it,” Adabelle replied with a nod.

“And what was that about being able to read?”

“That one’s my fault,” Trevor answered, raising his hand. “I’m actually glad he brought that up because I’d forgotten, but here.”

He retrieved the three magical language marbles from his inventory and passed them off to the mayor.

“What are these?” the elf asked curiously.

“You can’t really see it because of Hans’ fur, but he’s wearing my Language Fish trinket. Well, his now. It lets him read Mercantile, Dwarven, and Elven, but those are for Lucern, Gnomish, and Minotauran,” he explained. “It’ll also help him remember how to read the more he does it. I figured the guy could use some help, you know?”

Nester stared at the marbles before laughing. “I find myself farther and farther in your debt, Lady Adabelle and Trevor. You must allow me to do something for you two. I know we didn’t set quest rewards, but if you name what you’d like then I’ll do my best to make it happen.”

The demonkin looked at her husband, who shrugged, before smiling at the mayor. “While there’s nothing we really need, I do propose a trade facilitated through Klarkartar. Your hearts of ice are indeed powerful, and I think we can put them to great use in Tosa, especially during the summer season. In return, we can offer you a local product. Lumber from Firesap trees.”

“I am intrigued. Let’s discuss it over a meal, then,” Nester said with a smile. “As per Asalorana’s instruction, we have made many of Froslan Height’s delicacies for you, both to eat and take with you, as I understand it.”

“That will do quite nicely, Mayor Nester, thank you,” Adabelle replied. “Raiding dungeons does work up an appetite, and we’ll be happy to dine with you.”

That night, Trevor and Adabelle had returned to the entrance of the Froslan Peak dungeon for some privacy. One of his recliners had perished here today, but he was ready for many situations and had set out a loveseat this time so he could share it with his wife.

The ice was already starting to repair itself, which was a feature of the magic that spawned the monsters, but there was no danger. Clay had decided to stay behind and help fix the gouges that Hans left behind every time he landed. It wasn’t all that taxing, and it was something they enjoyed doing.

Trevor’s thumb rubbed against the back of Adabelle’s hand, and he kissed her shoulder. “How are you feeling?”

“I’m feeling good,” the demonkin said. She didn’t turn to face him, but leaned a little more heavily into his embrace. “But it sounds like you have a topic in mind.”

“The dungeons we faced,” he replied. “I was hoping I was able to live up to your expectations.”

Adabelle arched an eyebrow. “My expectations?”

“Well, yeah. You were very excited about being able to go adventuring with me,” Trevor answered with a shrug. “I really didn’t feel like I was doing more than flailing around for most of it, and you were put on babysitting duty both times with Clay.”

“I wasn’t babysitting you, son of Ander,” she chastised, giving him a look. “I was watching your back, and you were doing the same whether you realized it or not. That’s what it means to be a party.”

“So it wasn’t disappointing?”

She shifted, lifting her hand to cup her cheek. “I don’t mean to make light of your feelings, Trevor, but do you really want to be insecure about this after showing me such a cool side of yourself?”

Unconsciously, he sat up a little straighter. “Cool?”

“Yes, cool,” she laughed. “I’ll admit that you started off a little rocky against Tanazi’s men, but no one can blame you for that. You took it in stride, even going so far as to become relaxed enough to speak with the ghosts while I was dueling their captain.”

“Well, they were kind of goofy,” Trevor said as he scratched the back of his head.

“That’s true,” she allowed before resting her hand on his chest. “You really impressed me today, though.”

“Oh... I don’t know what to say to that. All I did was my best.”

“You didn’t panic when you were facing a monster all by yourself,” Adabelle pointed out, and he nodded. “Not only that, but there was no hesitation about going into the dungeon properly. You trusted your party to defend you, just as we trusted you to be our offense. I wouldn’t call you a proper adventurer just yet, but I could see you reaching that point in the future.”

Trevor chuckled. “I’m fine with just being a courier. Adventuring is okay, but that’s just because I have an overpowered party.”

“While that is true, you’ve still become very brave. I’m proud of you, honey.”

“Well, you know, it’s easy to be brave with you around,” he deflected, unable to help the smile on his face.

“I’ll gladly be your muse, Trevor Anderson,” Adabelle said before leaning up to kiss his cheek. She sighed contentedly before looking back over the landscape. The other peaks were dim thanks to the lights of the heavens, but she could see them clearly. “We have to go back tomorrow.”

“It’s been a great ride, hasn’t it?”

“Yes, it has,” she agreed with a nod. “A part of me is ready to be home. Sleep in my own bed, shower in my own bathroom, see Wayne, Rashie, and everyone else again.”

Trevor reached up and started stroking her hair. “Just a part, though?”

“Just a part,” she confirmed as she looked up at the magnificent night sky. “There’s a not insignificant voice in my head telling me to abdicate my responsibilities to Bocoro and continue traveling like this. Something more structured, though. It feels like we visited a lot of places, but we didn’t really pass through anywhere except for when we boarded the Red Dream. Do you know what I mean?”

“The pros and cons of teleporting,” the courier said. “We visit places without any of the in between. That means all the Haffle Inns and other tucked away places go undiscovered.”

Adabelle nodded. “Yes, precisely.”

“There’s an easy fix for that, you know,” Trevor stated, and she looked back down at him. “We just have to take another trip.”

“You know I’m far too busy for that,” she replied quietly, fixing a strand of hair so it was behind her ear.

“There’s an easy fix for that, you know,” he repeated, and she pouted. “You just need to get off your lazy bum and finally, finally find an apprentice who can learn the ropes.”

“Excuse me?” Adabelle laughed incredulously, her voice raising an octave at his statement. “My bum is anything but lazy. Take it back.”

“No,” he said, sticking his nose in the air. “You’ve been putting it off and off and off. You were supposed to find one before we got married, remember?”

“Of course I remember,” she replied, her pout becoming even more indignant and adorable. “But I’m very busy, you know that.”

“There’s an easy fix—”

“Now you’re just teasing me,” Adabelle accused. Her tail removed itself from her husband’s waist and gently tapped him against the forehead. “Stop it, or I’ll cry.”

“As you wish, my lady,” Trevor said, suddenly very serious. “I won’t have you shedding any tears on my account.”

The two looked at each other, one pouting and the other solemn. He broke first as he began to laugh, and she followed not long after. She leaned up and kissed him gently, her fingers lightly scratching his chest.

When she finished, she sighed. “I’ll find an apprentice after the summoning, promise.”

“Okay, I’ll hold you to that,” Trevor said before pulling her onto his lap. She squeaked at the sudden movement, but allowed it, and he spread out on the loveseat. “You’ll get it this time, I know it.”

“Thank you,” Adabelle said with a smile. She wrapped her arms around his neck and her tail around his waist. “I do think getting away was good for me. Reset to zero, like you had said before. My head is clearer than it had been, and I only worried about what was going on back in Tosa around twenty percent of the time.”

“Only twenty?”

“Maybe twenty-five.”

“That’s still so low! This honeymoon really has worked wonders,” he teased.

“Ha, ha,” she returned, rolling her eyes.

“For real, though, I’m glad,” Trevor said, dropping the joke. “I was worried about how hard you were pushing yourself. I mean, you’ve been like that since I met you, but it seemed like it got worse every time you dropped a summoning ritual. There was a point where I wondered if you would snub the wedding if it meant you could prepare another.”

“I didn’t really get that bad, did I?” she asked, wincing slightly.

“A little, but you were usually susceptible to back rubs, foot massages, holding hands while reading, and other tactics when you became a bit too high strung.”

Adabelle gasped, covering her mouth as though scandalized. “Trevor, are you telling me you weaponized our hand holding sessions?”

“I did, and I would do it again if it means upholding the greater good,” he said without shame. “Because you’re the greatest good I’m ever going to have.”

“Then it seems I owe you an apology. I didn’t realize you thought our wedding was in danger,” Adabelle replied with a sad smile.

“That might have been hyperbole,” Trevor admitted sheepishly, lacing his fingers between hers. “Regardless, I trusted you to be there. I know you wanted this as much as I did, and now the only thing we owe each other are the rest of our lives. When you get home, you’re going to do great. I’ll even be there with you, if you want. For moral support, obviously, because we both know I’d mess up the ritual otherwise.”

“I would like that,” Adabelle replied, leaning her head against his shoulder. “Before, I was so worried about doing it wrong that I didn’t want anyone to see, but I’m okay now. You and this trip have inspired me, and I’m ready to stop being the insecure apprentice that I’ve been hiding deep down. I am Adabelle Ramansa Anderson-Katine gen Tosa, and I have nothing to fear nor worry.”

“Preach it, lady,” he said with a laugh. “You’re going to do great.”

“That’s true for the next five to eight rituals, if I’m remembering correctly,” she replied. “I’ll have to keep a careful eye out for Mr. Tiggles for number nine.”

“Oh yeah, I did prophesize that, didn’t I?” the courier chuckled.

“You did,” Adabelle confirmed. “And thank you for believing in me, Trevor, even when I didn’t.”

“That’s just something you’re going to have to get used to, love,” he said softly. “You’re going to have to deal with me believing in you for the rest of our days.”

“I think I’m okay with that. How about I show you how much I appreciate you,” the demonkin offered as her tail swished behind his back. She shifted on his lap, pulling his lips to hers to share a kiss on the crown of the world.
