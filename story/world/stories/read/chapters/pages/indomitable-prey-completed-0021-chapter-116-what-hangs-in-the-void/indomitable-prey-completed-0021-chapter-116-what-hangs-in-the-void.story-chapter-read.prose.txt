They exchanged glances but soon turned and descended further. I followed along dutifully, heart still pounding, though I noted with relief that the duke’s aura retreated slightly to give me a little more breathing room. The threat was still very much present, but it was more distant at least. I’d take what I could get.

The ruin was shaped like an inverted pyramid, I soon realised. The top had been massive—hundreds of blocks wide and thick, the square top a flat plane that extended off into the darkness with an eery delineation between the dark stone and even darker void beyond. As we descended, that square narrowed into a triangular point, with each layer smaller than the last.

We had descended nearly a thousand feet and still weren’t more than halfway down the structure, but it was a harrowing journey, and I was thankful it was coming to an end. The scaffolding that wrapped one side of the pyramid was sturdy, built of strong wood and thick planks that would let out the occasional creak or groan, but otherwise didn’t protest our passage too heavily. But the cavern was monstrous in size, and because the inverted pyramid was wider at the top, every set of stairs we descended was hanging out over empty space.

The duke’s aura was still close at hand, wrapping me in a threatening embrace, and I was acutely aware that a single shove would see me falling out into empty air. The darkness of the cavern would also prevent me from timing my Break-Step correctly, and I’d more than likely splatter onto whatever ground lay far below.

It took only a few minutes to get down to our destination, and nobody spoke. The silence was ominous, and while the vault’s aura had been deactivated, the perpetual gloom and the alien design of the massive structure more than made up for the lack.

“Here we are, boy,” the duke said as we arrived at the vault’s main entrance. He laid a gentle hand on my shoulder. “Do not let me down.”

The reminder was enough, and I shivered out from his hand and stepped forwards. His announcement was unwarranted—not only was I fully aware I didn’t want to upset the man, but I’d already decided to open the vault myself.

It was also abundantly clear that this was the entrance. An archway carved from the same overlapping blocks of obsidian as the rest of the ruin hung large before us, its open mouth still resembling an alien maw no matter that the ominous aura had long since retreated. The fear of the unknown was strong, but the fear of the known danger hovering just behind me pushed me forwards.

I activated the door. No chanting, no strange rituals or flashy magic. Simple mana manipulation had circular a door appearing before us. It was large enough for a man three times my height to walk through with ease, and as it slid backwards, seams in the obsidian were unveiled, that I’d not noticed despite my enhanced eyesight. I wondered for a moment if the duke had seen them, but I suspected not. No matter how advanced one’s senses became, there was a perfection to the work of this ruin; a timeless intentionality that gave me the impression each piece was placed perfectly according to an inscrutable plan.

Before I could so much as take a step forward, a hand clamped down on my shoulder. The duke’s hand. It was scarred and solid, unwavering in its strength and unshakable in its grip.

“Good work, God-Touched. Perhaps you’ll live after all.”

A promising sentiment, though I somewhat doubted its truth. I had just opened the way for him; given him the metaphorical keys to a very literal kingdom’s worth of resources, if the rumours about Ashkanian civilisation vaults were true of course. Still, it would take time to explore it all, and I was in no rush. Just occupy the duke’s time­—that was my role for now. Keep him busy.

“And this section here,” the duke said. “What’s this?”

I held in another sigh as I looked at the upsettingly detailed and well-labelled map we were standing around. The ghostly raven was perched once more on Varice’s shoulder, so I couldn’t even risk lying. “That appears to be the armoury.”

“And next to it?”

I did sigh this time. “The library.”

Varice whistled, and even Estan chuckled at that. “This vault was built to pass on knowledge lost during a cultural apocalypse—I can only imagine the secrets and power they’ve left just waiting for us to read,” the arrogant young man said with glee. I doubted he’d ever read anything without a goblet of wine in hand, but it didn’t matter anyway.

It was his father’s interest that mattered, and the duke looked very much in agreement with his slimeball of a son. “And why have someone who can translate such wisdom to us. Good. God-Touched, lead us to the library.”

And with the order given, I had no choice but to comply. It didn’t feel right though. I was supposed to be delaying the duke for a few days, and down here, there was the potential for him to find empowerment in hours. I didn’t know what secrets the Ashkanian’s had, but I could make some guesses. They were a continent-spanning civilisation—such a feat would be impossible without 4th tiers of their own to combat and repel the wandering calamities and creatures of Tsanderos. This vault was setup to help a small group establish such pre-eminence in the vent of a civilisational collapse, and therefore, such power would be needed.

I didn’t know if there was a way to short-cut to the 4th tier, but I knew the duke had been a 3rd tier warrior for at least a decade. It wasn’t inconceivable that he was right on the cusp, and if ever there was knowledge that could push him over that lip, it would be found here.

Stalling him would do no good if he emerged from this vault after two days but had somehow broken through into the rarified ranks of the true powers of Tsanderos. I had faith in Jorge and Vera, I did, but to defeat a fourth tier? And even if that was still out of reach for the duke, the armoury no doubt boasted powerful magical artifacts. My own experience with Resolution and my shield proved just how effective powerful gear could be. It wasn’t transformational, but even if it brought the duke only an inch closer to victory, it could spell disaster. A fight as a game of inches, after all.

So, I couldn’t let the duke discover the secrets of this vault. A bit late now that we were already in here. The duke led the way, Varice just behind providing that eery green light. Estan hovered over my shoulder, keeping a hand resting there, ostensibly to guide me. I suspected he just enjoyed the feeling of power it gave him.

I couldn’t simply run. Even if I could slip Estan’s hold—which privately I suspected I could do with ease—I’d be caught swiftly by the duke. I’d seen Jorge and Vera’s prowess, and if it was any indication of a 3rd tier’s might, then I’d get no further than a dozen feet before being killed or captured once more.

No, I needed a barrier. Ideally a maze, but I’d take a simple door if I had to. The walls were impossibly durable in this place, and my mana still couldn’t slip through the blocks of obsidian. The duke, or even Varice, might be able to destroy them, but it was a chance I’d have to take. Decision made then: the next door we came to, I’d slip through and hope I could close it in time. Then I’d run further into the vault, I guess. What a plan that was—well done, Lamb, good work as ever.

Corridors of darkness slipped by on either side of us as we descended, the caustic light of Varice’s magic protruding only a little into each entrance before skittering back as she moved on. Despite the ominous aura no longer in evidence, I still found my gaze jerking away reflexively from those passages, as if scared what I would see.

We descended thick carved steps, spiralling around and around, and periodically I would be called forward to read patterns in the obsidian. Varice could illuminate them, causing a glow from within the rock itself to shine forth in flowing script, but it was my feat that allowed us to navigate the gloomy ruin. Not exactly a ruin, I supposed, despite the time since its creation. More a tomb. A crypt. A repository of ancient knowledge that I led my very enemy towards with each step.

Fuck, I hoped the doors opened and closed swiftly.

Before I could dwell too much on the stupidity of the plan, we arrived. Nothing monumental; just a simple carved entrance in the unrelenting black blocks that lined every wall, floor and ceiling in this massive structure. But a gesture and brief expenditure of mana from the spymaster had emerald light bubbling up from within the magmatic glass to spell out the words for ‘library’—or the closest approximation, anyway.

You could learn a lot about a culture from the way their words were constructed. The Ashkanians—or at least, whatever subset of their many cultures had created and maintained the language of High Ashkanian that this ruin was built on—held an almost religious reverence for knowledge. Contrary to my expectations, they were not rigidly structured in how they sought it. I’d thought a massive and enduring empire would have been built on strict hierarchies that would doubtless be reflected in their very opinion of truth; their own cultural practices dominant and ‘closer’ to any real understanding of the world.

But the word I’d been translating as ‘library’ seemed more to be a mashup of ‘museum’, ‘display’, ‘container’ and ‘knowledge’ all rolled into one. Connotations spooled within my mind as I read the word, and so when I unlocked the door with the relatively simple mana-sequence that was written into the wall nearby, I wasn’t surprised by the room in the same way that my companions were.

They’d probably been expecting a massive room, strictly organised and containing only one single type of knowledge. Books, or perhaps scrolls, or even rows upon rows of the same obsidian pillars that could store knowledge and regurgitate it when activated. But no, the library that unveiled itself to us was more of a hodgepodge of different rooms all smushed together.

Unlike the dark hallways, this space was well-lit. I suspected that the act of opening the door also powered on whatever lighting they had—similar in function to the ever-burning mage lights found in the depths of Colchet’s cavern-system, if of a different colour—but either way, we were all treated to a fine view of the Vault’s library.

The room was cavernous; a ceiling some hundred feet or so above, and the edge seemed to stretch out seemingly forever. I couldn’t see the walls on either side, and whether that was due to a lack of lighting around those walls, or simply the sheer scale of it, I couldn’t be sure. I had to consciously remind myself that we were currently inside of a structure hanging in the void inside of an even more massive cavern underground, because it simply didn’t feel real.

Small dividing walls, no more than a dozen feet high, rose before us to separate the cavernous room into smaller cubicles. Each, I suspected, would contain their own displays. But I had no time to guess at what each might contain before the duke was stepping through, a small satisfied smile on his face. Shit! I’d missed my chance.

As Varice followed, I briefly considered locking the door and running. It would trap the two in the Vault, and we could then siege the castle above in peace. Unless the duke escaped quickly. Or he simply stayed down here powering himself up with ancient alchemy and magical mind-control powers or whatever else the Scholar-Kings had dreamed up in their no doubt impressive laboratories.

This was the problem with personal growth. Had I still been the savage, scared, endlessly reactive man that the endless valleys of the Unclaimed Peaks had made me, I probably wouldn’t have missed this chance. I might be lying in a pool of my own blood right now, but at least I wouldn’t have been umming and aahing about the right approach.

And now I was wasting more time overthinking. Gods-dammit. Focusing, I took a breath and stepped into the library. The duke was already striding over to the nearest ‘room’, Varice trailing on his heels but looking around in every other direction with wide eyes. For a spymaster that presumably prized knowledge over all else, it must be maddening to have to restrict her curiosity. Estan still watched me, and there was a suspicious set to his face that I didn’t like. Maybe it was just his face in general that I detested, on second thought. He was an odious man—oozing smugness and wafting a superiority complex with every movement and expression.

As I entered the small room, I realised that this cavern was more maze-like than I’d thought. The room resembled a diamond with gaps at either end, so that the entrance and exit were clear and empty, but the room bulged in the middle to accommodate its valuables. In this case, it was a trio of large cases; glass fronted and containing rolls of fabric. Curtains, perhaps? Or sashes. As I drew closer, I realised that they were, in fact, dresses. Slit all the way down the back to allow them to be rolled out and displayed in their entirety but clearly items of clothing that were worn from the shoulder to the ankle.

I wasn’t quite sure how these counted as knowledge—other than in the anthropological sense—but there was a raised pillar of obsidian off to one side, tucked into the corner of the diamond-shaped walls, that I had no doubt would illuminate the purpose of these items. The duke turned my way, likely about to ask me to investigate and translate, when he cocked his head.

A few moments later I heard it too. Footsteps. The slapping of solid boots on stone. Moving quickly, too. I grimaced and turned, already frantically trying to work out how to turn a new arrival to my advantage. Alas, my mind was slow even when racing, and one of the soldiers from the castle above skidded around the corner, catching sight of the duke and hustling over like his life depended on it.

“My lord!” he called, panting from exertion. I could see a sheen of sweat on his forehead below the leather binding cap. “There has been… There are…”

“Breathe, soldier,” the duke commanded, striding over and producing a waterskin from his storage device. “Take a moment, drink. Deliver your message once you have composed yourself.”

I swallowed a laugh. It was almost comical how he managed to turn what should have been compassionate words and actions into a cold and vaguely threatening statement.

“The sultan is at the gates, my lord. He has demanded ours surrender and given until dusk for a reply, or else he will lay siege to the castle.”

The duke’s aura billowed forth for just a moment, sending the guardsman right back to gasping. I managed to resist flexing my own aura in response once more and simply bore the weight of it with a quiet stoicism. It hurt, obviously, but I wasn’t doing anything demanding and even a 1st tier body could last long minutes without air.

“The Sultan?” the duke asked, expression nearly serene once more. As close to serene as I suspected the man ever truly got, anyway—more of a permanent grimace, really. “But he has not yet attacked? How long until dusk?”

“Just under an hour I would guess, my lord.”

“Give me a moment,” the duke said, turning to Varice. They wandered to the hallway beyond the library to stand in the hallway out of earshot and discussed the news in low tones.

I gave the guard an awkward little wave. Estan slapped my hand down and I found myself nearly rounding on him before I remembered the role I was playing. He caught the slight movement though, and when he caught my eye, I could see that he had seen through my ruse. I guess he was used to people lying to him and had learned to spot it.

I looked away, aiming for meek but really just collecting more data. The duke and Varice were still beyond the edge of the library. Separated by an open doorway…

I sidled closer, doing my best attempt at nonchalant.

“Where do you think you’re going?” Estan demanded archly. Gods, he’s a prick. As if I owed him anything other than a quick death.

I kept walking. Unhurried, slow, aiming to skate beneath notice as the duke and his spymaster talked of far more important matters than the actions of a meek little God-Touched like myself.

“Hey, I’m talking to you, boy.”

I couldn’t help a smile curl my lips as Estan used his father’s words. I was older than he was, for crying out loud. Still, he was getting louder. I broke into a run.

“Hey!” Estan shouted. “Get back here.”

The duke looked up with a frown, mouth open as if to reprimand his son for the noise. Then his eyes landed on me, slapping a hand to the obsidian doorway between us. His eyes widened as he felt me flare my mana, and then he was lunging forwards.

I stumbled back, barely avoiding the suddenly corporeal blade of transparent force that materialised where I’d just been. The blade then shattered, and his angry growl was cut off entirely.

The door to the library slid seamlessly closed, sealing myself, Estan, and the guard inside.
