“Well, if I’m going to be stuck with you lot again, we might as well get down to it,” Balthazan muttered.  The rooman smithing instructor looked around the smithy with a practiced eye, the wheels of his mind almost visibly turning as he sized up what the group was working with.

“Compared to the last smithy you brought me to, this is pretty sparse,” he complained as he rubbed just under his beak.  “But I’m sure that we can bang out something that will suit you.  What are we looking at, here?”

Phatagin quickly explained the room clearing condition and showed Balthazan the box.

“My plan was to construct a simple spear,” the Squire concluded, “when Ser Charlemagne summoned you.”

“Well, he’s a reckless chap, but at least he’s well-heeled.  I was able to pay down a few years on my System contract with the points I earned from him.”

“Ooooh, that reminds me of something,” Josephine broke in.

“What’s that?” Balthazan asked.

“If Ser Charlemagne accidentally kills me during the Delve, I am totally claiming emotional and spiritual damages.  I bet I’ll make a killing.”

“Bawk!” the rooster objected.

“It’s just a joke, Ser Charlemagne,” the Attendant complained.  “You don’t have to threaten me like that…”

“Ahem…can we get back to the business at hand?  My time, while not as valuable as that of some others present, is still precious to me.  I’d like to get started,” the smithing instructor begged.

“Yes, let us all get back on task,” Phatagin added, hoping that Charlemagne would refrain from further vulgarity.  “Are you still amenable to us working together on forging a spear?  There is some decent quality wood that we can use as the staff, and the head should be doable with the tools that we have.”

“Ho ho, and which of you four is going to use this spear?” Balthazan asked, looking at each of the four in turn.  “You need no weapon beyond your claws and beak.  Your range of motion is insufficient to properly wield such a weapon two-handed.  You are strictly a non-combatant.  And you…great System’s jowls…are you a Vessarian?”

“I am,” the Empress of the Forest confirmed.  “My name is Phasima, pleased to make your acquaintance.  I’ve met a few roomans before, but I didn’t realize that your kind had an aptitude for smithing.”

“Oh, yes, it’s one of the things we take pride in.  ‘Strong as rooman steel’ is a common saying across the multiverse.”

“I’ve definitely heard it,” Josephine confirmed.

“Well, as fascinating as this is, you are correct that the spear is not for any of us,” Phatagin admitted.  “I chose it because it seemed something that was both challenging and within my abilities.”

“Now that I’m here, we can probably pick something just a bit harder,” Balthazan replied with a wink.  “Hmm, let’s see.  The forge is hot enough, and there are a few ingots over there that are clearly higher quality than the others…maybe even good enough to take an infusion of mana.  Now, the real question is, should I make the Knight an item, or the Squire?”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne answered without hesitation.

“That’s very kind of you, Ser Charlemagne,” Phatagin said.

“Bawk,” the rooster clarified.

“Ah, well, that’s about what I expected,” the pangolin responded with a sigh.

“Josephine, why don’t we sit in this corner and have some tea while the others do….whatever it is they are going to go,” Phasima suggested.  Josephine’s eyes lit up.

“That sounds amazing, but where are we going to get a tea kettle?”

Phasima reached her hand out.  It disappeared into a hole that suddenly appeared in reality and emerged shortly after holding a metal pot.

“I’ll set it on the hearth to warm,” she said.

Phatagin and Balthazan ignored the two women and continued their own conversation.  Charlemagne’s attention was torn between the two groups, but, since the women had no actual food yet, the majority of his interest went toward seeing what the smithing instructor was doing.

“Well, since I’ll be making you an item, it would be a great help if you were willing to share your Status with me,” the rooman claimed.  “All you need to do is to think about sharing your Attributes, Skills, and Special Abilities with me, and the System will make it happen.”

The pangolin did as Balthazan suggested.  The latter spent a good bit of time staring into space with his eyes glazed over, no doubt examining the pangolin’s details.

“There are a few options that we can go with this,” he finally suggested.  “First off, let’s talk about defense.  Your Skills are pretty good there.  In fact, they are great for a level 49.  Show me what it looks like when you curl up into your ball form?”

Phatagin began to float above the ground and curled up into a scaly sphere.

“Yeah, I see the problem.  Your head is pretty well protected from the front, but you’re vulnerable around the eyes on each side.  What I think we should do is to make you a spiked helmet.  That way, you’ll have both offense and defense in one!”

Phatagin spent a few moments considering the idea.

“But I fear that a spike would get in the way of my tail,” the pangolin pointed out as he waggled the appendage in question.  “It covers my head quite snugly here.”

“I was thinking that we could make it with two spikes set fairly far apart,” explained Balthazan.

“Ah, that might just indeed work,” Phatagin agreed, and the pair got to work making the item.

Charlemagne, not having much to do, watched the pair work until something much better caught his attention.

“Yes, you may have a scone,” Phasima said as he ran over with an excited look on his face.  The rooster gobbled the treat up with two pecks and waited for more.

“If you’re still hungry, I suggest either foraging during the next event or purchasing something directly from the System’s store,” the Vessarian urged as she calmly stared down the murderous rooster.

Charlemagne, realizing that threats weren’t likely to get him what he wanted, moved along.

***

It took Balthazan all of thirty minutes to make Phatagin’s helmet.  It would have taken him less time than that, but the pangolin had insisted on helping with almost every step, from heating the metal, to hammering it and folding it, to forming the spikes, and then finally quenching the piece.

“There, try that on for size,” the rooman said with pride as he handed the nearly completed helmet to the pangolin.  Phatagin placed the cap on his small, tapered skull and waggled his head back and forth.

“There is a little movement, but it mostly feels natural.  I assume that, in time, I will get used to wearing it,” he noted.

“If it wiggles, then the job’s not done.  Let’s have it back for a quick adjustment.  Does it pinch you anywhere?”

“No, aside from the added weight it felt comfortable.”

“Let me take a quick look see here,” the smithing instructor said as he placed his strange, veiny hands on each side of the helmet and manipulated it himself.  “Ah, I see where the issue is.”

It took only ten hammer strikes for the expert smith to make the adjustments that were needed.  In between strikes, he explained to Phatagin what he was doing.  Charlemagne listened as well, but the instructions made little sense to him, even with the Squiggles supplying information directly into his mind through his Magic Smithing Skill.  He regretted tuning out so much of what the rooman had said earlier, but, in his defense, there were scones.  Still, he felt that something was missing as Balthazan took the helmet and once again placed it on his Squire’s head.

“There we go…that’s much better,” the smithing instructor said.  “Fits you like a glove.”

“Bawk!” the rooster interjected as he approached and examined the helmet more closely.

“Of course it’s not finished?” Balthazan said indignantly.  “I know that!  We’re not quite done yet…I need to give it a final heat treatment before we can call it fully completed.”

“Bawwk!” Charlemagne asserted.

“I can’t possibly infuse it with mana.  Do you think that I have access to all my Skills?  You think this represents the best I can do?  I’m very, very limited here.  I swear, having to babysit a bunch of cocky, cosseted scions who couldn’t even be bothered to learn the basics before asking for a private lesson!”

“Balthazan,” Josephine called from the corner.  “These are First Gens.”

The look of pure, absolute mortification on the rooman’s face was almost worth putting up with his unprovoked insult.  Almost.

“Bawwk!” the rooster repeated.

“I said that I can’t!  It’s not that I just don’t want to…the System literally has handicapped me because you didn’t pay for more expensive lessons.”

Understanding dawned in Charlemagne’s eyes.  He paced over to Phatagin and nodded toward the helmet.  The pangolin, understanding his Knight’s intent, picked up the helmet and placed it back on the anvil.

“If it’s all the same to you…” Balthazan began as he scurried to put the rooster’s body between him and the helmet.

“Phasima, we may want to shield ourselves,” Josephine suggested, causing the other woman to conjure a thick wall of translucent ice.  Through it, they could see Charlemagne’s beak begin to glow with a beautiful pale blue color.  Then his beak streaked downward.

The rooman smithing instructor had dropped to the floor and put his head between his hands, clearly suffering some sort of PTSD from the last time the rooster had attempted to smith in his presence.  Charlemagne’s beak struck the top of the helmet, right between the horns.

Ting!

The pale blue glow leapt from the rooster’s beak and into the smooth metal of the helmet.  After a while, the light faded.

“Well, that was certainly, less eventful than I had feared,” Phatagin joked.  “And my helmet does still seem to be in one piece, so I think that we can call this venture a success.”

Charlemagne said nothing as he stared down at the item that he had just transferred a good portion of his mana into.  His stomach rumbled once again, reminding him that he was expending calories that he had yet to recover.

Balthazan took a long time to come out of his duck and cover posture.  When he did, he approached the helmet with trepidation, as if expecting the metallic item to explode at any moment.  His strong hands trembled as he picked up their combined handiwork and examined it.

“Ser Charlemagne, what have you done?” he gasped.

As if on cue, the Squiggles arrived to reward Charlemagne for helping to forge his first ever magical item.
