After a day of training as Sir Ricdine, I returned to my tent. Knowing I was under surveillance, I had to make it convincing that I was done for the night, and that I wasn’t going to leave my tent. I took off my boots and left them just outside the door, then sealed the tent flap and blew out the candles.

I took off my armour and set it down, then examined it, hunting for any sign of tampering. If someone had moved it…

There was no sign of tampering, but the illusion pendant did leave a tiny scorch-mark on the back inside of the chainmail, an eye-shaped scorch mark. If someone knew what they were looking for, it could be a sign of me using illusions.

But there were no regulations about having to let anyone inspect my armour. If someone asked to check my armour, I could always tell them they weren’t allowed to.

Besides, the best use of my time would be to keep improving, ensuring that I was ready to fight when the time came.

Once I’d put away my armour, I laid down on the stack of furs that was supposed to be my bed, and Burr settled down in the middle of the tent. I wasn’t going to stay here all night, but I could wait here while I merged my Skills, in case anyone got curious and checked to see if I was sleeping here.

First, I checked my list of Skills. The slate listed them as:

Skills: Spearmanship (Apprentice), Quick-Witted Crossbow Use (Journeyman), Art: Mass Hammer (Advanced), Memory Modification (Apprentice), External Squad Synergy (Apprentice), Jousting (Novice), Lancework (Apprentice), Sun Splinter (Apprentice), The Cooper’s Column (Apprentice), Shifting Grip (Novice)

When I drew myself into my nodespace, I gathered up everything relevant to spear-fighting. [Spearmanship], [Sun Splinter], [The Cooper’s Column], [Jousting], and [Shifting Grip].

I left [Lancework] behind for the moment, because I only needed five, and I would rather keep it unmerged for the time being. It would be far more useful that way, and I could probably work it into a better Skill later, which I could then combine with Spearmanship later down the line. Plus, it was Apprentice-tier, and I didn’t need to put four Apprentice-tier Skills into a single Journeyman merge. That would be a massive waste, when a Novice-tier Skill would work just fine as fodder.

First, I used [Spearmanship] as the base, then added in [Sun Splinter] as a secondary Skill. Next, I added the [Cooper’s Column]. The closer my preferred fighting styles were to the core, the better. Lastly, I added [Jousting] and [Shifting Grip] to finish off the merge.

The Skill orbs pressed together inside my mind, condensing into one single dense orb. When I checked it, my mind told me what it was: [Evolved Spearmanship]. It was a Journeyman-tier Skill.

At the moment, it didn’t have a fancy name, but I’d have to integrate more of my own style into it for that to work. It was getting stronger, and it had a more precise style, but it would still take some practice to see what it could do.

But I had more pressing concerns.

After sneaking out the back of my tent, I checked to make sure my escape route was still safe. There were no tracks around the back, but if I could hide my tracks, then so could someone else. It wasn’t a proper comparison. I listened, using my enhanced Perception to try to pick out footsteps or even just someone breathing.

Nothing.

I was safe for now. I snuck off back to the castle, returned Burr to the stable, then rushed back out into the tournament camp. The Byrnwold Company was performing again, and I searched the crowd for any sign of Alis. She should’ve been here somewhere, right?

When I’d circled around the edge of the crowd at least once, I veered off to the edge, finding some place out of the way, where I knew she’d probably try to turn up and catch me by surprise. It was her style.

I waited for about thirty seconds, but there was nothing, until finally, a swell of pressure rose behind me. I turned around—just in time to see Alis emerge from behind a stack of barrels, magenta sparks swirling around her. She adjusted her shawl and her eye-covering bandage.

“I was hoping you’d show up,” I said.

“Is something wrong?” she asked. “I was hoping you were just here because you wanted to meet me.”

“Well, that is true. I wanted to ask about the pendants,” I said softly. “How common of a shape are the illusion pendants you gave me?”

“Common enough,” she whispered. “Most of them look like an eye of some kind. Why?”

“If someone saw a pattern left by one, would they know exactly what it was?”

“A lord probably would, yes.”

I nodded hesitantly. That wasn’t a good sign.

“But an illusion scorch isn’t going to give you away,” Alis said. “So long as you have hidden the pendants well, it should not be an issue. No one will see the inside of your armour, and even if they are suspicious, they cannot prove that you are using an illusion just by looking. Even if they were to touch your face, it would not break the amulet’s effect.”

I glanced at her bandana. It was uncomfortable to look at, but I supposed that wasn’t enough to be obvious proof. Just a gut feeling that something wasn’t how it looked.

It certainly wouldn’t be enough to justify disqualifying me from the tournament. And once again, they couldn’t ask me to inspect my armour if I chose not to let them.

Alis said, “Look, touch my bandana.”

I did. I reached over and patted her on the head. My fingers passed through the fake fabric and compressed her hair, but had I not known it was an illusion already, I wouldn’t have noticed. There wasn’t that much of a difference. It was a perfect charm, after all. Maybe I would’ve noticed that there was no fabric beneath my fingers, only hair, but that wasn’t the point.

The point was that just touching the illusion wouldn’t dispel it. It was still working overtime to trick my eyes.

I nodded. “So, even if someone hits me in the face, it’ll be fine?”

“Yes,” Alis said. “It will be fine. As long as you win. I promise everything shall work out alright.”

“You would make a promise like that?”

“Y—yes.”

“You don’t sound too sure about that.”

“As long as you do your job, I’ll do mine,” she said. “I promise, the illusions shall hold for you.”

I nodded, then leaned back against the barrels. “Alis, what’s up with the covered eye?”

“Just an old injury,” she said. “I keep it covered because…it’s a little gross.”

“A little gross?”

“A little.” She crossed her arms. “No, you cannot see.”

“Alright, alright,” I said. “Well, I’ll see you around. I have some preparations to make, and I do have a new Skill that I need to get more practice with.”

“I will see you around, Levi.” She gave me one last nod, as if trying to promise that everything would be alright, and I set off back toward the castle.
