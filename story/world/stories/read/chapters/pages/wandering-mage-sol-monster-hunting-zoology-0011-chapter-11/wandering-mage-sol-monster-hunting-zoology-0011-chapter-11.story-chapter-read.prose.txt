Tressa fell.

For one frozen heartbeat, there was only wind in her ears and empty air beneath her feet. Her spell had helped Briadon but now she had no way to save herself.

Then, she saw the broken bridge rope whipped past her. Without thinking, Tressa reached out with her free hand and snatched it.

The rope snapped tight, stopping her descent but yanking her into a sharp swing of her own. She tried to force whatever last bit of mana she might have into even a weak wind spell, but it wouldn’t respond.

She swung hard into the cliff, striking her shoulder first, bouncing along its face, and then her head clipped a jut of stone hard enough to send pain flashing through her skull.

Her grip slipped.

Tressa dropped with a gasp, once again plunging towards certain doom.

“AAAAAAH!”

But Briadon threw his free arm out just in time, snatching the back of her clothing. For just one more terrifying second, she dangled over the endless drop before he yanked her into the crook of his arm.

The minotaur stuck his hooves out and landed against the wall. Tressa was draped over his arm like a blanket set out on a clothes line, just staring at the raging waters below.

“Gyaaah!” she gasped in surprise as he turned her around in his arm, holding her a little more comfortably with her legs dangling alongside his chest. She really was like a child cradled in one arm, and he could move her around with such ease she might as well have been weightless.

Briadon looked down at her. “Are you insane?”

Tressa blinked up at him, dazed. “Insane?”

Then he noticed the blood running down the side of her face, but she barely seemed to react to it. “Briadon, okay?” she asked.

He huffed. “I’m fine. What about you?”

“Okay!” she said, making the okay gesture with her hand once more.

“You don’t look okay. That’s a deep gash in your head.”

“Briadon? Tressa?” Lisha called, looking down from the cliff above. “Oh thank the gods!”

“We’re not safe yet,” Briadon called, looking up at the climb. He held onto the rope with just one hand, and had has hooves planted firmly on the cliff face. Tressa’s ears twitched as she heard a slight creak from the rope. It seemed firm, but they couldn’t just take that for granted and hang around all day.

“Elf, I can’t climb with one hand. Get on my back.”

She blinked at him and wiped the blood away from her eye with the back of her wrist.

“Back. Now,” he said, gesturing with his head, and finally she got it.

With his help, she carefully repositioned herself, grabbing a horn to keep herself stable, which made him grunt in irritation. She slid her legs behind him and wrapped them around his back as best she could. She kept one arm over a shoulder and the other holding onto one of his horns.

“Alright, I’m climbing now. Hang on, elf,” he said, and began ascending the rope.

Tressa realized the rope actually was quite stable, and he practically walked up the cliff face, one hoof after another as he pulled himself up.

After a minute, his hand reached the top of the cliff, and as soon as they were on solid ground, Tressa hopped down from his back.

The massive cow man fell to his knees and practically kissed the ground. “Damn it! I thought I’d never see solid ground again!” he bellowed. Then he looked over his shoulder at Tressa. “That was a close call. Thanks, I really thought I was a gonner,” he said as Tressa wiped blood off her face. The wound really was bleeding badly.

“Tressa your hurt!” Lisha said, coming to her side.

“I okay,” she breathed.

“No you're not!” Lisha said as she reached into her own bag and pulled out a rolled strip of linen. She cut it and then wetted it with some medicine to clean Tressa's head wound. The small elf winced, but when it was done, Lisha finished by wrapping a bandage around her head to help stop the bleeding.

“What's that?” Tressa asked, pointing at the bottle of liquid as Lisha returned it to her medical kit.

“Medicine,” Lisha answered.

“How to…” Tressa made kneading gestures with her hands, not sure how to say it.

Lisha tilted her head.

“Make it?” Tressa tried.

Lisha’s tongue flicked and she shook her head. “I’m not sure. I only buy it from an apothecary.”

Tressa sighed in disappointment.

“Seriously, elf?” Briadon scoffed. “You nearly fall to your death and this is what you’re worried about?”

“Research.” She grinned.

Her own people had plenty of healing salves, but not quite like the one Lisha had just used. Most of what the elves would use was more like ointments typically created from thistle berry and bitterleaf.

“Thank you.” Tressa bowed deeply. She flinched as she realized the condition she was in. She could only imagine if she’d ever go home like this. She was covered in dirt, cuts, bruises, and probably smelled. Though she had bathed in a river the day before, so maybe her smell wasn’t too bad.

Knowing her father, he would give her an earful about how she was clumsy and unfit to be out in the world, and it would probably give him another excuse to try to send her to the temple. If not just lock her up.

Well, those were old concerns, and nothing she had to think about now. For now, they had a Farron Moth to hunt!

“Are you sure you're okay?” Lisha asked.

Tressa nodded as she got up. “That way.” She pointed and started leading the way again, limping as she went.

The two hunters looked at each other and sighed. For as many injuries as Tressa had, she didn’t seem to care.

Lisha couldn’t help a slight chuckle as Tressa continued on ahead of them.

“What?” Briadon asked.

“Just thinking. She doesn’t seem at all bothered. If anything, she seems eager to keep pressing the adventure forward.”

Briadon let out a huff. “That elf is actually insane.”

“Perhaps.” Lisha’s tongue flicked from between her lips. “But I was just thinking, she would certainly make a good monster hunter.”

“A tiny elf? Ha! Now that’s the funniest thing I heard all day, Lisha. And it’s been quite a day already.”

“But you can’t deny, she’d fit right in at the guild.”

Briadon huffed as he hefted his club up over his shoulder. “But she’s still an elf. A High Elf.”

Lisha’s tongue flicked. “I was merely saying.”

With that, Lisha grabbed her halberd, and they ran after Tressa to catch up with her.

***

Hours later, Tressa stopped and pointed up at a tree and made a shooshing gesture. The two looked up following her finger and saw massive moths and other types of monsters they had no clue about, even the names.

“Are those…?” Lisha began.

“Farron Moth… not,” Tressa explained. “Follow.”

She waved for them to walk quietly as she crouched and led the way even deeper into the thick trees and foliage. The canopy was thick above them, blocking out what little remained of the sun for the day, leaving them in near darkness.

Briadon grunted as he tripped over a root, falling with a massive thud.

Tressa winced in worry as she scanned the trees, and thankfully, nothing seemed disturbed. It was too dark to know what monsters might be up there and if there were some hidden dangers. She let out a sigh of relief and then turned and glared at Briadon.

“Briadon shhh,” Lisha whispered.

“Sorry, I can't see very well. I don't have the best eyesight in the first place, remember?" he spat in a whisper.

“I know, but be careful,” she whispered and turned to Tressa. “Um Tressa?”

Tressa turned around, pulling her eyes away from the tops of the trees. “Yes?”

“Can you make light?” Lisha made an exploding gesture with her hands. “Light?”

Tressa shook her head and pointed up. She actually had no clue what she was asking, but she didn't think making an explosion would be a good idea right now. She only regained a little of her mana anyway and wanted to save it for an emergency.

“Briadon can’t see,” Lisha explained, covering her eyes then pointing at Briadon. It took Tressa a moment to understand, and she nodded. She guessed that it wouldn't be too much of a problem to give them some light, as it was growing difficult for her to see as well.

She cupped her hands together, channeling just enough mana to cast a weakened version of the spell, and she muttered, “Lumas.”

A small ball of light illuminated the area around them, but Tressa kept it dim, trying to avoid scaring off the moths and sturring the other creatures that might be in the trees. She wondered if the right amount of light could actually attract them? As the saying goes, like moths to a flame. It was true she had seen moths and other insects go to light, but she wasn't sure if that would work in this case with the Farron Moths, as they seemed to prefer darker environments. Although, their own wings had a soft glow. If she could replicate that glow, could she actually use it to attract them? She’d definitely have to experiment with this more some other time, as she believed they used that glow to attract mates.

Moving in deeper and looking up, through the dense trees and foliage, she finally spotted the semi-glowing wings. Most sat in major groups. She knew it would be a bit difficult to find one on its own. She beckoned the other two to follow and remain as quiet as possible as they headed farther in.

“Tressa, do you think you can make it brighter?” Lisha asked after several minutes of silence and fighting with the thick underbrush.

Shaking her head, she motioned around them and pointed up once more. “Monsters. Danger.”

Briadon paused. “Monsters?”

Tressa nodded. “Light. Monsters. Ummmm grrrrr,” she said, not knowing the words, but bared her teeth and made a clawed hand gesture.

“Um, what kinds of monsters?” Lisha asked, smiling at the face Tressa had made. She preemptively hit Briadon’s stomach before he started laughing.

Tressa pulled out her sketchbook and flipped through, pointing out a Scrallow Bird, a Blue Mimic, a Tagle Taer, and a Baring Berg.

“Those are kinda dangerous monsters. Didn't think elves had those kinds of monsters in these forests,” Lisha said, staring at the pages. “Wait, are these ones around here?” She tapped the page with the drawing of a massive bird with a long beak and a hook under its chin and backward talons. A bit of fear gripped her chest as she stared at what she knew well as the Garrongon Talon.

Tressa looked at the page and nodded, and then pointed at the moths and spoke. “Eat. Needs eat.”

“They eat the moths?” Briadon asked, looking at the page in the dim lighting and seeing the same monster as Lisha. “Damn, if I knew Garrogon Talons were in these forests, I'd have said give up on this hunt and take the loss.”

“Well, we’ve come too far now,” Lisha said. “And the penalty on our other bounties will be steep if we don’t complete the list. And she led us all the way here. It would all be for nothing if we just turned tail and ran.”

Bridon pulled at his beard and groaned. “Looks like we are doing this then.”

“But don’t worry,” Lisha said. “I think Tressa knows these woods well and wouldn’t have taken us this far if she thought it too dangerous.”

Unknown to her, she was very wrong about that. Tressa had only studied the maps and geography books from the library and was doing her best to go based on the knowledge she had. Normally, even she would know better than to travel so close to the Thimberman Mountains.

However, she wasn’t worried about it now, as she had two skilled monster hunters with her! She was sure they were ready to deal with anything these mountains could throw at them.

“Elf, why are we still walking? Those are Farron Moths aren’t they?” Briadon asked.

Tressa looked up, then back down. “Um… little? Small? No… one, then one,” she said.

Lisha nodded and explained for her. “They are dangerous if there are that many together. One or a few isolated by themselves wouldn’t have enough toxins to do serious harm, but we’d be stupid to risk getting swarmed,” she explained, pointing up.

After walking a little longer, Tressa finally spotted a lone Farron Moth up in the trees. Their bodies were velvety black, allowing them to blend with the dark if you didn’t look carefully, while the wings were beautiful and softly luminescent, with markings that looked like eyes, making them seem almost like faces floating in the dark to scare off predators.

She made a circle motion with her hand, signaling everyone to carefully look around a little and make sure it truly was alone.

Sure enough, there was only one other nearby, perhaps its mate. But this seemed like the best opportunity they were going to get. It was actually the perfect target for their hunt.

Tressa paused, staring up at it, wishing she had brought her bow. Shooting it out of the tree would have been easy.

“Looks like we found our bounty. But how do we get to it?” Lisha asked. There were currently two moths sitting in the tree above them. One lay on a branch and the other on the trunk as they feasted on the sap.

“Damn, if only we had a bow,” Briadon said, repeating Tressa's earlier thoughts. “Hey, don’t elves always carry bows?”

Lisha hit his stomach. “That’s just a stereotype, fool. Do all drunk minotaurs try flirting with cattle?”

“Ah—that was only one time! And it was dark out!” Briadon defended himself in a loud whisper. Then he muttered, “And… I drank too much.”

“Shhhhooosh,” Tressa snapped at them, putting a finger to her lips.

They both quieted down, each freezing as they heard a rustling sound and looked up, expecting the moth to have taken flight. But the moth remained where it was, and they looked through the canopy for what had made the sounds. But all seemed still now, so they pushed the rustling out of their minds, assuming it was only the wind.

After a moment of thought, Briadon asked, “Magic?” making a gesture with his hand.

Once again, Tressa shook her head.

Then an idea struck her. Tressa turned and poked Lisha.

“Eeep!” She slammed her hand over her reptilian mouth and looked up. Thankfully, the moths didn't stir; all they did was move their wings.

“What?” Lisha whispered.

Tressa pointed at herself, then the moth. “Um… sharp?” she asked, making a stabbing gesture.

Lisha pulled out a small hunting knife. “Something like this?”

Tressa nodded and held out her hand. Once Lisha handed it over, Tressa fastened it to her belt, banished her Lumas spell, and began climbing the tree.
