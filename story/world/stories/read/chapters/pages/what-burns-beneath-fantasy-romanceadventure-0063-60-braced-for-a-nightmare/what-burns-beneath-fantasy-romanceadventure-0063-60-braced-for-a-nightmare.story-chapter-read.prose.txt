Royce slid into the deserted inn common room behind me. “Do you want to… should I… I…” His voice grew very small and he finished, quite helplessly, “Brin, I don’t know what to do.”

He’d been deathly quiet throughout our gloomy walk back, and the quaver in his voice now somehow made me feel a bit calmer. As if, because my friend was frightened, it was now my job to offer reassurance.

“Well… it’s late, and I’ve had maybe the longest day of my life. Probably the worst one, too. And I’m sure you’re tired from traveling, so I… I suppose…” I lifted my chin, mouth twisted to one side. “I suppose you should tell me goodnight.”

Shadows flickered across his handsome features. Royce’s face usually held a very carefree cast, but now it was all twitching muscles and tight lines. “And after that?”

My attention trailed to the stairway. The steps rose and curved away, leading into darkness. I swallowed and said, with a lightness I did not feel, “Well, then tomorrow you’ll tell me good morning.”

He was looking everywhere but at me. One hand was clenching and unclenching.

Keep going. Breaking down will just make him panic. I need to hold it together. Just for a little bit longer. “He hasn’t hurt me, Royce. He hasn’t. It’s going to be fine.”

He still couldn’t seem to look at me, but he gave a stiff, jerky nod. “Right,” he rasped, then cleared his throat. “Right. It’s gonna be fine.”

“It is.”

I focused on keeping my breathing even as we climbed the stairs. Our feet made hollow thumps against the wood. Once in the hallway I crept to the green door, which had been left open. Renner was nowhere in sight, and it looked like the washbasin had been refilled. Two low-burning candles sent shadows flitting across the walls. My belongings lay on the bed beside a thick linen towel.

I turned to Royce, forced a smile, and gave him a quick hug.

He drew me close. “Are you sure you don’t… I could stay up for a while.” His voice was a low, hoarse rumble that vibrated through his chest. The arms around me tightened. “Sleep on the floor. I don’t mind.”

Fresh tears pricked at my eyes. Keep it together. Breathe. Just a few more moments. I didn’t trust my voice not to betray the rising tide of emotion, so I just shook my head.

“Brin…”

I pulled away, offering up a watery smile that surely wasn’t convincing. “I’ll see you in the morning. Goodnight,” I murmured, and slid the door shut before my resolve broke. As soon as the wood was between us I sagged against it, stifling a sob.

Several moments passed in silence. Then the muffled thump of my friend’s boots echoed down the hallway. I heard a distant creak as he, presumably, entered his own bedroom.

“Right,” I breathed, pressing my palms against rough wood. “It’s going to be fine.”

I tugged my clothing off and crept to the tub. Someone had left a bar of pale, fresh-smelling soap beside the washbasin alongside a sponge. I grabbed both and eased myself into the water.

A gasp came out unbidden as heavy, comforting warmth closed over my aching feet and legs. I sank in with a little moan. The soap and sponge slipped away as practical needs were abandoned for the more immediate luxury of an actual, much-longed-for bath.

It felt wonderful. I tilted my head back with a sigh, letting my tangled hair spill down into the water as everything below was enveloped in heat.

Salt, I needed this. It’s a good thing I let Renner go first, otherwise I might never have given him a turn! I could stay in here forever.

It wasn’t forever, but I did spend quite a while just… sitting. I let my hands float atop the surface, fingers gently detangling the knots of my hair. Flickering candlelight cast everything in a cozy glow.

Eventually I tilted my cheek against the curve of hard stone and glimpsed a pale moon rising from beyond the window. It was a faint, dull thing in comparison to what I’d see later.

At least it’s beautiful there. The moon, the sky, the garden… there’s worse places to be trapped, right?

Something cold brushed the inside of my belly. I lowered my lashes and shifted forwards, grabbing for the soap. One hand smoothed suds across my neck and hair as the other tightened against the tub’s edge. The stone was no longer gray, but white. Smooth ivory, not rough rock. The warmth pressing at my back wasn’t water, it was him, and-

“Stop,” I gasped, clenching the soap until it slipped free. Water pit-patted from my hair, dripping and rippling around me.

My movements turned methodical as I kept my mind firmly fixed on the task at hand. Fingernails scraped away loose dirt. Soap and suds slithered along my skin. The sponge swirled along, pleasantly rough, until I felt smooth and polished to cleanliness.

I was wringing my hair out when a muffled creak made me jump. My gaze flicked over. The door remained closed, just a looming rectangle in the flickering glow, and the sound came again. Floorboards groaning ever so slightly and a faint, muffled thud from outside my room.

The sounds fell silent and I rose, water dripping around me, shivering against the sudden flush of cool air against bare skin.

More creaking came as I wrapped myself in the towel. Another quiet thud, then another, and another… from just outside my door, and a bit to either side. I trembled, clutching the soft cloth to my body, and stared. Agnes’ words came unbidden, echoing through the back of my mind.

‘Baltha’s alive. She’ll come back. She’ll free us.’

I shook my head. Wet hair clung to my throat and drops of water spattered against the floorboards. “No. No chance of that,” I whispered to the quiet room.

Silence fell again as I sank onto the mattress. My fingers twisted into the towel, holding it like a shield against the pressing dark. A moment later I jumped up, snatched the shard from my pack, and held it at eye-level.

“Everything can die,” I hissed, clutching the golden steel like it was a dying breath. “Right?”

The sound of groaning wood came again. Before my nerve could fail, I tip-toed to the door. My knuckles were white around the towel. I froze at the threshold, pulse thrumming. It came again; faint, creaking floorboards. The sounds trailed to my left. Slightly down the hallway. Then they came back, very slowly, and wandered to the right. Then back, to the left, and…

My heart sank. Someone was pacing outside my door.

Was it Royce? I wasn’t sure. His steps were heavier, though perhaps he was trying to stay quiet so as not to disturb me. I very much hoped it wasn’t Teela. I didn’t want to imagine the young woman wandering the hall, alone and frightened in the darkness.

It can’t be Renner. He’s probably fast asleep. I hope he’s fast asleep. He surely needs it.

I pressed my forehead to the rough wood, fighting the urge to swing the door wide.

I shouldn’t. I can’t. I’m not decent! And… and I’ll stand there stammering and I’ll probably burst into tears and it’ll just make everything worse.

Slow, shuffling thuds. Creaking wood. Another pause as whoever it was stopped, probably just on the other side of the door. My chest grew tight and, before my resolve could break, I lifted one hand to slide the bolt into place. The lock shut with a sharp click. I held my breath in the ensuing silence.

There was a quiet sigh from the hallway. Then… I sank against the frame, blinking back tears, as whoever it was trailed away. A few moments later I heard the sound of another door opening and closing.

Sleep did not come easily. I stared at the ceiling for a long while, tracing barely-visible lines of woodgrain. Tried in vain to chase away terrible thoughts of what my dreams would bring.

He didn’t hurt me. He threatened to, but he didn’t. Not even after I tried to kill him. After I swore to kill him. He hasn’t hurt me.

The moon rose higher. Pale stars blinked into view.

He hasn’t hurt me. He hasn’t hurt me.

The silent mantra wasn’t enough. The darkness was closing in again, coating me in memories that left my skin clammy and my muscles tight; firm hands at my waist, fingers tangling into my hair, warm lips at my ear. Memories that, under very different circumstances, could have been lovely.

And then he’d smiled while Renner lay dying. Told me that I could watch my friends bleed until I was ready to cooperate. Trapped me against stone, promising in no uncertain terms what the cost of defiance would be.

Anything lovely had been swallowed by terror.

‘You weren’t the only one enjoying yourself.’

My eyelids slammed against the silky murmur. I pulled the quilt tight as warmth spilled down my cheeks.

He didn’t mean it. He couldn’t have. He was only trying to frighten me. Please, please… let him not have meant it.

I turned onto my side, fists clenched and pulse pounding. Watched the looming shadows as surely as they watched me. Dug my fingers into the blanket, nails scraping at loose threads, thoughts winding their way through dreadful darkness until they finally found a tiny shred of light.

My mom saw this.

I blinked, growing very still. The thought came again and again, flickering like a candle in the back of my mind.

She saw this. She knew this would happen. Or… or at least some of it. Surely she wouldn’t have set me on this path if it only led to misery.

My heartbeat slowed. My fingers unclenched.

I’ve been so afraid, so lost and helpless, but… I’m alive. Royce and Teela and Renner are all alive. Things have been horrible, but… but when you really stop and look at it all, we’re alright. We are. We’re all alive, and we’re okay.

My friends were safe. Baltha was dead. Velidan waited a realm away, but… so far, my dreams had been bloodless. He’d been cruel, he’d been terrifying, but he hadn’t hurt me.

She called me beautiful. Clever. Brave. Beloved.

Heat spilled from my eyes. But it wasn’t wracking, heavy sobs that broke me in the darkness; these were the gentle, steady tears from when I’d first read that word on faded parchment. A declaration made before I was even born. Her vow to me.

Beloved. She loved me. She knew. She saw it all. And still she… she let it happen. She wanted it to happen.

Beloved. Me. Beloved.

I let it whisper through my thoughts. Wrap itself around my heart.

And when the moon was high and the world outside was silent, the words sang me to sleep.
