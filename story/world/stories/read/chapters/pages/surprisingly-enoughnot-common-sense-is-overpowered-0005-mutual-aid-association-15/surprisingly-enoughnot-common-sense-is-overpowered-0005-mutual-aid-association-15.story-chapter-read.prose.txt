"I'm planning to form a group and gather some like-minded people," Wei Zhusang said as the three of them gathered near the entrance after receiving their respective assignments.

Lang Rulang arched a brow. "Was that your idea, or Aunt's?"

The three of them had grown up together, their families even tied by marriage. They knew each other too well to bother with pretenses.

Wei Zhusang gave a small smile. "My mother suggested it. She said that while our talents are among the best in our families, mid-grade spiritual roots won't allow us to skip the usual path. So she suggested forming our own group within the sect. If we can establish a faction here, our families will benefit from the connections and resources we gain."

Zhang Feng threw up his hands. "The Xiao family is so lucky! Xiao Hong already has a high-grade spiritual root, and now they've got that monster as well. Where's the fairness in that?"

"Spiritual roots aren't everything. Given time, we can catch up," Lang Rulang replied, and she meant it.

Wei Zhusang inclined his head. "Exactly. That's why this group matters. And I don't plan to limit it to just the three of us."

Zhang Feng blinked. "You mean Ning?"

"Mm." Wei Zhusang nodded. "His aptitude is at least as good as ours, perhaps even better given his attributes. His temperament seems steady, and most importantly, he doesn't have any backing either. If we bring him into the group now, he may prove to be a reliable ally."

"He has a sense of humor. I approve." Zhang Feng's criteria were very simple.

Both boys turned toward Lang Rulang.

She huffed. "Recruit whomever you like. I don't care. At least he seems... good-natured enough."

Zhang Feng chuckled. "If he weren't, he'd have taken offense at your sharp tongue already."

Wei Zhusang laughed softly, while Lang Rulang shot Zhang Feng a glare that promised pain.

Zhang Feng quickly raised his hands and mimed zipping his mouth shut.

At that moment, Ning emerged from the hall, a jade slip in hand. His steps were unhurried.

"It seems I'm the last one. Did something happen?" Ning blinked.

"Hmph." Lang Rulang turned away, while the two boys only laughed.

"It's nothing," Wei Zhusang eventually said. "Brother Ning, what task did they assign you?"

"Spiritual farming," Ning answered. "What about you three?"

"Weapons refining," Wei said.

"Weapons refining as well," Zhang added.

After a pause, Lang Rulang spoke coolly. "Talismans."

Ning's brow rose slightly. Just from the names, their assignments already sounded better than his.

"So it's not random, then."

Wei and Zhang glanced aside, while Lang Rulang answered bluntly, "Some disciples give offerings to the elders. That way, they avoid the less desirable jobs."

Ning fell silent. He had nothing to say. After all, his purse was like an onion; opening it only brought tears.

Sensing his mood, Zhang spoke up cheerfully. "Don't worry, Brother Ning. Farming's not the worst. It's far better than woodcutting... or taking honey from silverback bees. Their stings can cripple a man for months!"

Wei Zhusang nodded in agreement. "Yes. Farming can even bring certain advantages if done well."

Lang Rulang let out a quiet huff but didn't contradict them.

Ning smiled faintly. "I'm not worried. Things like this happen wherever there are people. The better positions will naturally have more competition. No need to fuss over it."

He shifted the topic with ease. "So, what were you three discussing just now?"

Wei Zhusang explained, "Cultivation rests on Dharma, wealth, companions, and land. We all come from Blue Flame City, and now that we've entered the sect, we should support one another. I was thinking we could meet once a month, exchange knowledge, and lend aid where needed. I'd like you to join us."

Ning narrowed his eyes slightly.

To be honest, he'd never intended to go full lone wolf. In his situation, that simply didn't make sense. As an ordinary disciple with little knowledge of the cultivation world, he needed to build connections. That was just common sense.

From what he'd seen, the trio seemed like decent people, and he liked their vibe. Especially when compared to Xiao Hong and his lackeys.

Besides, since he wanted to join a group anyway, it made sense to start with people who were also new to the sect. From what Wei Zhusang had said, this would be a newly formed group, so there wouldn't be any senior disciples trying to coerce them into doing things. And since the three seemed to value his spiritual root and attributes, he would likely have a decent standing within the group.

He could also gain information and connections through them.

With that in mind, Ning nodded.

"Makes sense. As the saying goes, a single thread cannot make a cord, nor can a lone tree make a forest. Cultivation isn't something achieved in isolation." He smiled. "I'll join."

Wei Zhusang's eyes brightened. "Good. And in time, I'd like to expand this into a proper association, not just the four of us."

"That sounds wise," Ning agreed with a smile.

Wei Zhusang looked around, his eyes glinting. "Then let's call it the Mutual Aid Association."

...

The jade slip sat cool in Ning's palm. A faint thread of silver light sparked to life the moment he fed a wisp of qi into it, unraveling into a glowing line that stretched into the distance. His own personal guidepost, pointing the way toward his assigned spiritual farming grounds.

It was convenient. At least he wouldn't have to wander around the sect looking like a lost chick. He'd had enough sightseeing for now.

Ning followed the silver thread away from the General Affairs Hall, passing through the steady flow of disciples moving between the surrounding halls.

There was still a lot he didn't know about this place.

Wei Zhusang's earlier words came back to him.

Dharma, wealth, companions, land.

The four prerequisites for cultivation.

Ning quite liked that. It took this enormous, mysterious path toward immortality and reduced it to four things he could actually think about.

"Dharma..."

The path and the methods used to walk it. Cultivation techniques, spells, martial arts, and the knowledge passed down from one cultivator to another.

Ning already had the Pure Qi Sutra, so the most basic part was covered, but beyond that, his hands were quite empty. He knew only the mortal martial arts he'd inherited from the original Ji Ning and barely knew what else was out there.

Still, he was now a disciple of the Pure Qi Sect. If the sect had produced a Golden Core Patriarch, there should be no shortage of things for him to learn.

For now, Dharma wasn't much of a concern.

Then there were companions.

Fellow cultivators who could exchange knowledge, help each other when needed, and watch each other's backs. His decision to join the Mutual Aid Association had already given him a good start on that front.

Next was land.

From their earlier conversation, Ning had learned about spiritual veins and the blessed grounds formed around them. Qi gathered more densely in such places, making cultivation easier and providing better conditions for growing spiritual herbs and other valuable plants.

And, naturally, the best places already had owners.

Ning looked down at the silver line steadily leading him toward the farming grounds.

"Well, I'm about to have a field."

That had to count for something.

Besides, he had barely begun cultivating. Worrying about securing some blessed land while he was still at the First Stage of Qi Condensation was getting ahead of himself. Whatever the sect had assigned him would be enough for now. He could worry about finding somewhere better once he actually needed it.

That left the final one.

Wealth.

Ning's expression fell slightly.

Spirit stones, pills, artifacts, medicinal herbs. Cultivation required resources, and from everything he'd heard so far, none of them came cheap. Unfortunately, Ning currently had none of those things.

"This one's going to be a problem."

The earlier conversation about disciples giving offerings to the elders came back to him. He certainly couldn't afford anything like that.

His gaze fell toward the silver thread again. He had no idea how much spiritual farming actually paid, or whether it paid anything at all, but there had to be some way of earning spirit stones from it.

"I can only hope farming pays better than it sounds," Ning muttered as he slipped through the crowd, keeping enough distance to avoid bumping into anyone without straying too far from the path.

Perfectly balanced, as all things should be.

Then he chuckled under his breath. "Dharma, wealth, companions, land. Really, it's just the four food groups of cultivation. Keep a balanced diet, or starve."
