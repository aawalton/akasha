Much like the last time Viktor had been here, the grand arena was comically empty. Two contestants stood on the sand below, and two spectators occupied the stands above. The only difference was that he and Galatea had switched places. Now she sat beside Khenemhotep up there, while he was down here, preparing to engage in a duel with the mighty Crocodilian.

Viktor gave his rapier several testing slashes, the slender and delicate blade cutting the air before him with sharp whistles. After drawing it from its scabbard, he had waited until the radiance flowing along the metal had completely fizzled away before settling into his stance. The blade was not going to return to its sheath, not even once, throughout the fight. Which meant, for all practical purposes, it would be no different from a normal sword, albeit extremely durable thanks to its status as a Reliquary, and that would allow it to clash against Sebekton’s monstrous axe without fear that it might snap under the sheer force of his crushing blows.

Across the sand, the Crocodilian stood motionless, his enormous weapon resting casually upon one armored shoulder, as though such an impossible weight meant absolutely nothing to him.

“Are you ready, Master?” he asked.

“Yes.”

Without giving any warning beyond that single word, Viktor slammed his boot into the ground and, at the same time, fired up his wind gems. A blast of compressed air erupted under him, launching him skyward with explosive speed.

Well, yes. Unlike the spar with Haku, this fight was never meant to remain purely physical.

The arena shrank away as he ascended. Down there, Sebekton planted his feet onto the ground with a heavy crunch that sent grains of sand scattering outward in rippling waves. His scales glimmered between the gaps in his armor, dark green beneath layered steel plates. Calm and composed, he shifted one massive shoulder, lifting the gigantic battle axe into a guarded position.

Viktor dove.

The wind curved his fall into a sharp sweep around Sebekton’s flank, rapier darting forward like a silver needle toward the narrow seams between the overlapping layers. But the Crocodilian reacted with terrifying speed. His tail whipped around, swinging in a brutal arc at Viktor like a living battering ram.

He twisted just in time to avoid the strike. The spiked appendage whipped under him, so close he felt the air crack against his ribs. The shockwave alone sent him reeling backward. He triggered another pulse from the wind gems to stabilize himself. He hovered for a fraction of a second, then tried to launch himself upward once more.

But before he could get back to the safety of the sky, Sebekton was already moving. The Crocodilian lunged forward, his axe coming around in a savage arc that could cleave straight through solid iron.

Viktor barely managed to raise his weapon at the last possible instant to block the attack. Mithril met steel with a deafening ring, and a violent jolt exploded from his wrists all the way to his shoulders the moment the strike connected. Before he knew it, he found himself being flung across the arena.

The rapier was undamaged despite the impact. Thin as it looked, it was still a blade forged from mithril and later converted into a Reliquary. It would not easily break, no matter how monstrous a collision it had to endure.

The same thing could not be said of the hand wielding it, though.

A direct hit from Sebekton had more than enough force to shatter bones and tear muscles apart. Fortunately, most of it had gone into sending him flying instead of driving straight through him. Had he remained stationary and taken the strike head-on, the consequences could have been utterly catastrophic.

[Master, isn’t this spar a bit too dangerous?]

Celeste’s voice echoed in his mind the moment he managed to steady himself using the power of his gems.

“What are you talking about? There’s no risk whatsoever. I’m still under the protection of my Supreme Thauma, so even if the worst happens, it will automatically trigger to prevent my death and heal all of my injuries in an instant.”

[But... what if you receive a serious but non-fatal wound?]

“In that case, I’ll have Galatae patch me up.”

[I don’t know, Master. I feel like... you’re more reckless than usual.]

“I’m fine.”

And he launched himself at Sebekton again.

Wind screamed around him as he zigzagged through the yard in quick bursts. Once he closed the distance, his rapier—now in his left hand—flashed in and out, a silvery sting aimed at the seams of the Crocodilian’s armor. Throat, armpit, elbow, anywhere the steel plates left even the smallest invitation.

Sebekton grunted.

Then the axe came down.

Viktor shot a wind blast that hurled his body backward a heartbeat before the impact. A thunderous explosion, and the ground where he had been ceased to be a surface and became a crater. Earth detonated in a choking bloom of dust and fractured stone, swallowing the center of the arena in a thick, disorienting haze that reduced everything to shifting silhouettes.

As he hovered at the edge of the cloud, he heard the footsteps.

Fast, heavy, closing in.

A towering shape emerged through the haze, and before it was even fully visible, the massive slab of metal in its hands was already in motion again.

The wind gems screamed into activation, and Viktor shot upward, barely evading the devastating horizontal sweep.

Well, he had to admit—only to himself and never to Celeste—that the situation could spiral into something akin to actual danger. Sebekton didn’t hold back at all. Because Viktor had told him not to. He knew the Crocodilian wasn’t built for subtlety or careful moderation like that Tengu, and asking him to restrain his strength would only complicate things. So he had ordered his Guardian to treat this like a real fight. Which meant it was now his responsibility to avoid those lethal attacks. And, thanks to the gems, it was not entirely unmanageable.

So the spar continued.

Rounds came one after another, punctuated by brief intervals where Viktor withdrew from the arena’s center to rest and get healed by Galatea. Between those breaks, Celeste’s voice returned again and again, each time more concerned than the last. There was even an instant he thought her voice had begun to lose its usual monotony.

But of course, he ignored her every single time.

[Master, I think that is enough for today.]

“You think so?” Viktor chuckled. “Actually, there’s one more thing I want to test.” He drifted back to the ring. “Sebekton,” he called out. “Use that power of your axe. The invisible slash. Aim it at me.”

[Master!?]

Even the Crocodilian tilted his head, as though checking he had heard correctly.

“Are you sure about that, Master?”

“I am,” Viktor said, raising the rapier in front of his chest into a guard position.

After a brief pause, Sebekton gave a small shrug. He lifted his axe high above his head, then brought it crashing down. An invisible crescent of compressed force tore forward, cutting straight toward Viktor with lethal purpose.

He couldn’t see it, of course. He could only feel it when it made contact with his weapon. And, at that very instant, he activated the Thauma he had prepared beforehand. Then he swept his blade aside, as though swatting away a physical object. The invisible arc of destruction bent away from its original path, redirected toward the seating on his right.

For a fraction of a second, nothing happened.

Then, stone exploded in a long, tearing line across the stands. A thunderous detonation rolled through the arena as dust and debris flew in every direction. The sound lingered long after the impact had passed.

Sebekton stared, jaw hanging open. “That’s... impressive,” he finally said.

“It’s but a simple trick,” Viktor said, lowering his blade as the Thauma’s effect faded from his grip.

Well, it really was, once he had figured out the true nature of that Reliquary. As it turned out, when Sebekton swung his axe and activated its power, he was effectively sending out a blade of compressed air, fast and unseen and sharp.

But if it was something made of air, then it was something Viktor could manipulate. There were a lot of ways to deal with the projectile. Phase through it, for example. Or what he had done just now, using a Thauma that allowed him to parry the air slash at the moment of contact. A fun technique to try, though he wasn’t entirely sure how useful it would be in a real fight. Perhaps one day it would shine when he met an enemy who used air itself as a weapon.

And that marked the end of the spar.

Viktor was just about to ask Celeste to teleport him back to the Core Room when a thought suddenly came to him, pulling his attention away from the lingering ache in his arms and toward a detail from the conversation he had with Galatea yesterday.

When they discussed the masked men’s hideout inside a Folded Chamber, he recalled a spell the Riftwalkers used to create containers capable of holding far more than their appearance suggested.

Yes, spells like that existed.

Powerful mages did have the ability to bind fragments of their power into physical objects, creating artifacts that could reproduce lesser versions of their spells. The enchantments gradually decayed over time as stored mana deteriorated and the embedded structures destabilized. So unless the mage continuously maintained and replenished the artifact, its functionality would eventually fade away. Yet, despite all the drawbacks, a magical artifact could still be very useful in certain situations.

Such spells were rare, but he did have a mage of unparalleled power right here, one who had lived for millennia. So he fired up his wind gems once more, flying toward the stands above.

“Sovereign of the Dungeon,” Khenemhotep said as Viktor reached his side. “And it came to pass that you have yet again made manifest your great strength, and have proven yourself exceedingly formidable among men.”

“I’m still not good enough,” Viktor said. “Which is why I need help from all of you. Anyway, that’s exactly what I came to ask. Can you make some magical artifacts I can use when I’m outside the dungeon?”

“What manner of power must such an artifact be endowed with, that it might suffice for your purpose?”

“The power to reanimate the dead.” Viktor grinned. “I want soldiers. They don’t have to possess strong offensive capability, but I want them to be durable.”

Offensive capability he already had in abundance; what he lacked was meatshields to stand between him and his enemies.

“I shall see what can be done, if it be within my power to accomplish.”

“Thank you, High Priest,” Viktor said with a nod. “Celeste, bring me back to the Core Room.”

[Actually, Master, Galatea has just requested an audience with you. In the inner sanctum of her cathedral.]

Huh?

Viktor shifted his gaze to the towering suit of armor seated beside Khenemhotep, still and silent. If she wanted to speak to him, why didn’t she just say so directly? Did she want a private conversation without the other Guardians knowing about it?

Oh, well. It was not like he had any other urgent business he needed to take care of immediately.

“Fine,” he said. “Teleport me there.”
