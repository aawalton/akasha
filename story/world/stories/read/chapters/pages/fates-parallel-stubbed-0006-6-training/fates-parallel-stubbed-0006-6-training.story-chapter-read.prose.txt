Lee Jia sat sprawled out on the couch, upside down, staring at the fire. An Eui had once again locked herself in the bedroom—

Isolated. Alone. Where she belongs.

—and Lee Jia was distracting herself by exploring her brand new sense. Apparently their meditation session had somehow managed to last all day. The sun had already set by the time they had finished, and the fire had gone out entirely. It was roaring now, though, without any fuel—or at least, no physical fuel. Lee Jia stared in fascination, not that she needed to look with her eyes. It just felt appropriate.

Dae’s notes were right, mana really was everywhere. It was so ubiquitous that Lee Jia could scarcely believe she’d never noticed it before. Except she had, hadn’t she? That was what her trusty intuition was—some kind of latent mana sense. It seemed so obvious, now. There were no words to describe what Lee Jia sensed, at least not that she knew. Instead she tried to visualize mana as a sort of fluid that flowed into and around everything.

Immediately surrounding her, there was a dense cloud of mana that seemed to cling to her. According to Dae’s notes, this was her aura. Some mana flowed in or out of her aura, but it was always there, wrapping her up like a blanket—it felt comfortable and familiar. If she concentrated, she could control the mana in her aura—well, sort of.

Mostly all that she could do is concentrate a bit of it into a sort of projection, like a second body that was palm-sized and only had mana sense. That was the awareness she had felt while meditating. Without her real body to distract her, it had felt like that mana projection was her entire being.

Lee Jia stared at the fire with both her eyes and her mana sense. She watched as the formation drew mana in from the air and then converted it into a different kind of energy. Lee Jia had no idea how it actually accomplished that conversion, but just starting or stopping the mana flow was—well, not easy, but simple.

Lee Jia concentrated on her aura and willed her awareness to move in such a way that it blocked the flow of mana. After a moment, the flames sputtered out and the room was plunged into darkness. It didn’t matter, the formation remained brilliantly visible to her mana sense—she wondered what it was made of.

Earlier, Lee Jia had used the stone to activate the stove. The stone had a huge concentration of mana trapped inside of it, and the formation had drawn some of that mana out. It had only needed a little bit to get started. After that it was self-powering. This time, Lee Jia tried using her awareness to direct some of the mana in her aura into the formation. After a few failed attempts, Lee Jia managed to get the fire started again.

Lee Jia smiled, then giggled quietly to herself. She’d just done magic—sure, it was super basic magic that had been pre-prepared by another, more experienced mage. That was just it, though—another mage. A mage that wasn’t her. That was a thing she had to clarify because she was a mage! A terrible mage with no spells, but still the thought made her giddy. Almost enough to forget about—

She wasn’t worth the air she breathed.

—the incident with—

She should have starved to death out in the wilderness.

—An Eui.

Murderer.

Lee Jia clutched her head and curled up into a ball. The thoughts weren’t hers. Like the vision—

Her disgraceful past.

—they felt disconnected. Like a memory without context. The intrusive thoughts came whenever she thought about—

The murderous exile.

—and they wouldn’t go away!

Lee Jia felt like crying again. Were those An Eui’s memories? Were these her thoughts? Why was this happening? Was it like this all the time for An Eui? How did she live like this? Lee Jia was hungry, but she couldn’t bring herself to eat. Exhausted—because apparently meditating for twelve hours straight was draining—but she couldn’t sleep.

Instead, Lee Jia reexamined the jade slip she had received in Elder Qin’s ‘lesson’. It lit up in her mana sense in much the same way that the elemental core had. The engraving reminded Lee Jia of the formation in the stove. Curiously, she tried controlling her aura, pushing her awareness into the jade slip like she had done to start the fire.

Instantly, the information flooded her mind. It was overwhelming, and she wasn’t able to retain most of it. After reeling from the unexpected impact of the jade slip, Lee Jia focused on what she could remember from the deluge of information. Mostly it seemed to be another meditation technique, though there was a lot of information that Lee Jia didn’t have the context to understand. For now, she decided to just focus on learning the breathing techniques.

Settling down to meditate, Lee Jia focused on what she could remember from the jade slip. It was jumbled with a lot of that extra information that she didn’t understand yet, and she could now understand why Dae had been so keen to transcribe it into writing. Still, after a while she felt that she was getting the hang of it. As she felt the physical sensations of her body growing distant, instead of her awareness expanding outward into the cloud of mana that made up her aura, it drew itself inward, focusing on the mana—or qi she supposed—inside her body.

Unlike the free flowing cloud of her aura, her inner qi was a labyrinth of interconnected passageways. The word ‘meridians’ came unbidden to her mind—something the jade slip mentioned that she could no longer recall. When Lee Jia tried to use her awareness to control her qi, she found that it seemed to resist her. Intuitively, she felt that this qi was not hers the way her aura was. It felt more like the mana that flowed outside of her aura—beyond her reach.

Before Lee Jia could consider her next step, she was snapped out of her meditative state by a familiar feeling poke on her cheek.

Lee Jia opened her eyes to see An Eui’s rat-like tail in front of her face. Following it upwards, she saw her roommate, arms crossed, staring down at her with a complicated expression. Lee Jia braced herself for the intrusive thoughts, but none came. Either the meditation had somewhat soothed her mental state, or whatever was causing them had simply worn off.

“It’s almost dawn. Physical training starts today.”

An Eui’s voice was stiff and monotone, as if she was forcing herself to keep her emotions out of her voice. Lee Jia decided not to push things since if An Eui’s experience was anything like Lee Jia’s, she’d probably need some space to think about it—Lee Jia certainly did. Lee Jia acknowledged An Eui with a nod, and the two of them left wordlessly, heading towards their first training session.

The training field turned out to simply be a flat, grassy field, surrounded by a dirt track. In the center of the field was a large stone platform, flanked by weapon racks full of wooden training weapons of varying size and description. Ienaga Yumi stood before the platform, wearing the same red armor Lee Jia has seen before. Her hands rested on a wooden training sword stuck in the ground in front of her as she regarded the bleary-eyed disciples.

The crowd of disciples was quiet, likely due to the early hour. As Lee Jia scanned the crowd for familiar faces she saw a pair she hadn’t expected approaching. To her surprise, An Eui did not perform her usual disappearing act, though she still held herself at a remove.

“We meet again, Miss Lee. I see you’ve managed to avoid getting trampled underfoot by the cultivators so far.”

“Good morning, Miss Lee.”

The receptionist pair that she had met on the first day greeted her. The lazy one was as rude as ever, while the other was as stone faced as she remembered. Looking at him properly, Stoneface was also an absolute brick wall of a man.

“Good morning, Stoneface.”

Lee Jia covered her mouth. Ancestors damn her big mouth. Her lack of sleep was catching up to her, and while she had forgotten the pair’s names, she had not meant to vocalize her internal nicknames. Before she could apologize, the lazy one burst out laughing.

“Hahaha! St-stoneface! Haha, Guan, I underestimated her talent. She’s seen through your soul and found your true name.”

Guan’s expression didn’t change, but Lee Jia couldn’t help but feel as if it had suddenly grown significantly more steely.

“I’m so sorry!”

She bowed at a ninety degree angle, blushing furiously as she apologized.

“I’ve had a long couple of days and I forgot your names. Please forgive my rudeness!”

“I take no offense, Miss Lee. I appreciate the sincerity of your apology. It’s not an inappropriate nickname, though perhaps iron or steel would be more appropriate than stone. Allow me to reintroduce myself—I am Guan Yi, and my giggling companion is Xin Wei.”

Lee Jia stared blankly. Was that a joke? His face and tone had not changed in the slightest. Xin Wei seemed to find it hilarious, though—he was doubled over in laughter and slapping his thighs in a way that was drawing unwanted attention on their conversation. Lee Jia decided to test the waters.

“If it’s any consolation, I was thinking of him as ‘the rude and lazy one’.”

Xin Wei’s face fell as Lee Jia gestured towards him. Though Xin stopped laughing, Lee Jia thought she saw just the barest twitch of a cheek muscle from Guan Yi. It might have been a smile, or she might have been imagining it.

“Aw, I suppose I was asking for that. So how have you enjoyed your accommodation so far? No questions? Complaints? Puncture wounds?”Lee Jia cringed as Xin whispered, casting a surreptitious glance at her roommate standing at a distance. Sure enough, An Eui was glaring figurative daggers that carried the threat of becoming very literal. Xin Wei had underestimated half-spirit senses again. An Eui’s weak bloodline meant that the only expression of her spirit ancestry was her tail, but she was still a half-spirit, and a mage at that—her hearing was much better than a regular human’s.

“I have no complaints. It’s as nice a living space as I’ve ever had, and the company is lovely. Though I do look forward to finding out how comfortable my bed is.”

Lee Jia glanced back at An Eui again, who at least had the good grace to look slightly embarrassed.

“Also, I would have thought that after our last encounter you would have learned to watch your mouth around half-spirits, Xin Wei.”

Lee Jia waggled her oversized cat ears in order to punctuate her statement and Xin Wei put his face in his hands as he protested.

“But she doesn’t have—ah never mind. Another lesson learned, I suppose.”

Guan Yi shook his head.

“It will take more than a small dose of shame to teach Xin anything.”

“Guan, what have I told you about sarcasm?”

“I assure you I was being completely genuine.”

Xin turned to Lee Jia and gestured exasperatedly at Guan Yi, who’s expression remained completely unchanged.

“You see what I have to put up with? How does one banter with that? It’s completely unfair.”

Lee Jia giggled. Despite the bad first impression, she couldn’t help but find herself liking the unusual pair of foreigners. Before she had a chance to retort, their conversation was cut short by a deafeningly loud shout.

“The sun has risen! Your training begins now!”

Ienaga’s voice resonated throughout the field, completely crushing the noise of the crowd in an instant. Lee Jia thought she could feel the sound of Ienaga’s shout vibrating in her bones.

“Advanced students, step forward! You know who you are.”

A small group stepped away from the crowd, and Lee Jia recognized Takeda Rika among the group of entirely Yamato students. She didn’t know the rest, but one girl stood out. Her long hair was a brilliant red, tied into a ponytail similar to the one Ienaga wore. She was beautiful in a way that reminded Lee Jia uncomfortably of Seong Eunae. The others in the group seemed to keep their distance from her out of deference. Ienaga regarded the group critically for a moment before speaking. In a normal voice this time, though Lee Jia could still hear it.

“Your lessons begin tomorrow. You are all dismissed until then.”

That was it? Lee Jia thought that they would be upset about being called out so early only to be dismissed, but the group of martial artists simply bowed and left without another word. Before they had even gotten clear of the field, Ienaga turned her bellow back on the remaining students.

“Martial Arts are a way to forge the body! As with a blade, the body must be beaten into the appropriate shape, and honed to perfection through constant maintenance. As with a blade, the first step must be to refine the metal from which the blade is to be cast.”

Lee Jia saw Guan Yi nodding along enthusiastically. The most emotive she had ever seen him, for all his face still didn’t change.

“That metal is ki! In your current state, your bodies are nothing more than vessels for ki. Only when that vessel is full can the ki be refined, only when that ki is refined can you begin to forge yourselves anew.”

Lee Jia found the analogy surprisingly easy to follow, compared to the deluge of information that came with the jade slip from Elder Qin. She wondered if the same analogy applied to spiritual cultivation.

“All living things have ki. However, the ki in your body now is corrupt! It is stagnant and impure. Before you can begin to fill your vessels with ki, you must first empty them of these impurities. After all, a blade with impurities in the metal will shatter under stress—and make no mistake, should you neglect this first crucial step on the path to mastery, that will not be a metaphor.”

Lee Jia shuddered at the mental image of a person literally breaking apart. She was going to pay very close attention to Ienaga’s lessons.

“That’s enough explanation. Let’s begin the actual training. All of you start running laps. Do not stop under any circumstance.”

There was a pregnant pause as the crowd of disciples took in Ienaga’s words. Guan Yi and a few of the remaining Yamato disciples had already begun running.

“NOW!”

Ienaga’s commanding shout shook Lee Jia to the core. She started running laps.

Lee Jia’s heart was pounding and her legs burned. How long had she been running? Her vision blurred and her eyes stung from sweat, but she lacked the strength to wipe it away. Ienaga was ruthless—she had said not to stop running and she meant it. The first group of disciples to stop to catch their breath had discovered exactly what that wooden sword was for. Ienaga had hit Xin Wei so hard his head was bleeding, and still she pulled him back to his feet and made him keep running. Lee Jia was not about to let that happen to her.

Lee Jia felt like she was going to vomit. Several disciples had, and she was pretty sure she would have too if she hadn’t skipped breakfast this morning. Eventually, people started falling—not simply out of breath, just completely spent. The first ones to go had been Qin disciples—and Dae. They had lacked either the physical training of the Yamato students or the enhanced physique of half-spirits—except for Dae. Just how out of shape was that boy if Lee Jia had more stamina than him? She had been literally starving to death a week ago.

As Ienaga approached the fallen students, Lee Jia braced herself for the sound of cracking skulls, but instead Ienaga simply dragged each one out of the way of the runners. Lee Jia saw her sitting each one upright and whispering something to them. She couldn’t overhear the words, and besides, she couldn’t afford to spare any of her attention away from putting one foot in front of the other. Lee Jia wasn’t sure if the awkward shamble she was performing counted as a run at this point, but Ienaga didn’t seem to mind as long as she kept moving.

Over time, the number of runners slowly dwindled. Not all of them had the willpower to run until they broke. Faced with the choice between running themselves to true exhaustion and the kiss of Ienaga’s wooden sword, several took a third option and simply left. The first to do so set off a chain reaction as dozens of students gave up on the training. Ienaga made no attempt to stop them. To Lee Jia’s surprise, even some of the Yamato students gave up. She had thought they seemed accustomed to it—and weren’t they already martial artists?

Finally, only a handful remained on the track, the rest either left or were now quietly meditating on the grass. Lee Jia and An Eui still stubbornly forced themselves to push forward. The only other mages that had lasted were Seong Eunae and a boy whose half-spirit bloodline was so strong he looked like a white tiger had simply learned to walk upright. Only one Qin student persisted—Guan Yi’s expression still refused to change even as he gasped for air. The rest were all Yamato students and Lee Jia didn’t know any of them.

Lee Jia had only managed to persist for this long because of a trick. While running, there was a point that felt like she had hit a wall. When Lee Jia broke through that wall, she had felt a brief rush of energy—a cool, soothing feeling that permeated her entire body. Through her mana sense, she had sensed her body absorbing the mana in her aura. Since then, Lee Jia had been intentionally burning her aura as fuel for her exhausted body. She suspected the other mages were doing the same.

When at last Lee Jia’s perilously thin aura had nothing left to give, she fell. It happened very suddenly—Lee Jia tried to move her foot forward and found that it simply didn’t move. Thrown off balance by the unexpected bodily failure, Lee Jia fell forward. She tried to throw her arms forward to break her fall, but they didn’t cooperate so she instead collapsed face first in the dirt. With rapidly mounting alarm, Lee Jia discovered that her lungs weren’t working either. She tried to gasp for air, but her chest just heaved uselessly.

Suddenly she felt a rough hand grasp her by the collar, drag her away, and gently lift her into a sitting position. Lee Jia’s panicked gaze met Ienaga’s as her chest heaved uselessly for air like a fish taken out of the water.

“You’ll be alright. Calm down. Breathe.”

Ienaga’s voice was different from the commanding shout Lee Jia was used to. She spoke in a quiet whisper that was soothing, almost matronly. Lee Jia tried to communicate with her desperate gaze that she was trying to breathe. She felt like she would be crying if there was any moisture left in her body.

“Okay, stay calm. You’ll be fine. You’ve just pushed yourself too far.”

Ienaga produced a small, white, crystalline object and popped it in her mouth. After biting down with a crunch, Ienaga placed her hands on either side of Lee Jia’s face, leaned forward, and—Lee Jia’s eyes widened in surprise and confusion as she felt her instructor’s lips press firmly against her own. Then, she felt the breath of air and mana filling her lungs. Her body consumed the mana as hungrily as her lungs did the air. Lee Jia coughed and gasped as Ienaga separated from her. Finally she was able to breath on her own again. She felt Ienaga’s hand on her back, keeping her steady.

“Good work. You did well, now don’t waste it. Breathe. Focus on your breathing, your heartbeat, the flow of blood through your veins, the ache of your muscles.”

Lee Jia followed Ienaga’s instructions and sat up under her own power, closing her eyes to meditate.

“Good. Don’t worry about specific breathing techniques for now. An empty vessel can’t help but be filled, just focus on the feeling of ki moving through you. Remember the way it moves through your body. That was a clever trick, but it’s missing something. I’ll explain later but I need to stop the others from repeating it.”

Distantly, Lee Jia heard Ienaga start shouting something, but she was already entirely focused on her own meditative state. She felt the energy seeping into her body. Like a cool bath on a hot summer day, it was at once relaxing and invigorating. With each beat of her heart, and with each breath she took, the ki washed through her. It was a level of relaxation Lee Jia had never felt before, as though all the pain and hard work of the last few hours were being repaid with interest.

All too soon, that feeling began to fade, and Lee Jia could no longer feel the ki moving through her body so acutely. She thought she was beginning to understand how it moved. From her aura to her lungs, from her lungs to her heart, from her heart to her veins, from her veins to her muscles. When she woke from her meditation, she found that the sun was already beginning to set.

“You’ve all done well to make it this far.”

Lee Jia heard Ienaga addressing the disciples as she rose shakily to her feet. Everything hurt.

“Today, you learned how to empty your vessels. Next lesson, you will learn to fill them. After that, you’ll repeat this exercise until either your body fills entirely with pure uncorrupted ki and you break through as martial artists, or you die.”

Lee Jia groaned in anguish, and she heard that she wasn’t the only one to do so. She was going to have to do this again?

“For now, go home and rest. You’ve all earned it. Make sure you eat and drink—slowly, or you’ll just throw it back up.”

Lee Jia’s mind was a bit of a haze as she trudged her way back to the dorms. She vaguely recalled meeting up with An Eui. Lee Jia didn’t remember when they made it back home, only that they’d been too exhausted to talk along the way, too tired to follow their teacher’s instruction to eat and drink—too tired to think, really. Without a second thought, Lee Jia climbed into bed. When she slept, she slept soundly—and when she dreamed, she dreamed of memories that weren’t hers.
