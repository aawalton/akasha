Chapter 97

The office had not changed since the last time Velen had been called into it. Dark hardwood on the walls, polished to the kind of shine that caught the crystal lamps overhead and threw the light back in pieces. The ledgers along the long shelves were arranged the way they had been arranged for years, gold leaf on the spines lined up to the eye like a row of small flames. The blackened oak desk in the centre of the room had its silver inkwell exactly where it always sat. The tapestries between the windows showed the same ships and the same caravans, the threads had washed out in places where the sun had reached them over time.

Averic Corvessa stood behind the desk and did not sit. That was the first thing Velen registered when he came through the door, and it told him most of what he needed to know about the conversation ahead. He stopped two paces in front of the desk and waited.

Averic did not look up, instead he continued to read without acknowledging Velen’s presence. Velen used the time to centre himself for the conversation ahead, this wasn’t his first meeting like this. He had been pulled off a different assignment to be here. He did not know which assignment he was being given so he just waited. When Velen was called, it was because of his experience and his efficiency.

Averic finally lifted his head.

"Word from the compound is days late," Averic said. "Ride out and investigate and bring your findings directly to me."

Velen nodded once. Averic held his eyes a moment longer, which was not his habit.

"There have been rumours of a force rising just past the south-north divide, this needs to be confirmed with evidence."

Velen nodded again, without asking for an explanation of the type of force or the nature of the rumours. He had learned long ago to not ask questions, following direction was more important with matters like these.

"Yes, sir."

"Today."

"At once."

Averic looked back down at the page on his desk sending the signal that this conversation was over. Velen turned on his heel and walked out of the office; the servant closed the door behind him without sound.

He was on the road moments later. He rode north out of Grey Ridge at a steady pace, neither fast enough to draw attention nor slow enough to lose the day. The cover he was using was the one he used most often, a regional factor for a mid-tier merchant family with business in the towns north of the river. The papers in his saddlebag would hold up to any inspection. The clothes he wore had been chosen the way he always chose them, well-made enough to suggest a man who travelled for trade, plain enough that no one would remember the cut of them an hour after he had passed.

The road thinned as it left the town. The morning traffic was farmers and the occasional rider going about the routine business of the region, and by midday even that had fallen off. He ate from his saddlebag at a stand of trees a league further out and was back on the horse inside ten minutes.

He thought about the briefing while he rode, in the way he thought about all briefings, which was not to dwell on them but to let them sit at the back of his mind and turn slowly while the rest of him paid attention to the road. Word from the compound is days late. That was a sentence Averic had not had to say. The compound's reporting schedule was known to the office, the lateness was already a fact in someone's ledger, and a routine field agent could have been sent on a routine check. Averic had brought him into the office personally, this in itself was a message.

Velen thought about the second piece of information: A force rising just past the south-north divide. He had worked the south of the realm for years and he knew the divide. He knew past it there was very little compared to the south. A few cleared holdings, the old tower that had stood empty for as long as anyone living remembered, forest, and then more forest. A force rising was not a phrase that fit the geography he carried in his head. Either the geography in his head was out of date, which was possible and which Averic was clearly entertaining, or someone had brought a force into that country recently enough that the office had only just begun to hear about it. He did not speculate further, because speculation cost him accuracy and evidence was concrete.

The light had gone the colour of late afternoon by the time he reached the bend in the road where he had to turn into the forest. He sat his horse for a moment. The rough path was overgrown more than it should have been. Not by much. By the amount it would be overgrown if no one had ridden it for several days. He had ridden this track six months ago and the grass had been kept down by the regular movement, this path of late had been less traveled. He turned his horse towards the path and continued his journey.

The first thing wrong was the smell. He registered it before he had a name for it, a thin sour edge in the air that had nothing to do with the forest around him. He slowed the horse, the wind was coming down off the higher ground ahead, and the smell was coming down with it. Wet ash, that was cold for a time. The smell of a fire that had burned out and then been rained on and then dried again, the kind of smell that lingered in stone and earth for weeks after the flame itself was gone. All of this rang true, but there was something else, this ash had a slightly different smell that he couldn’t put his finger on.

Velen drew the horse up at the last bend before the compound and dismounted. He tied the reins to a low branch and went the rest of the way on foot.

He stopped at the edge of the clearing and stood looking at what had been the compound for a long count of breaths, and he did not move while he looked.

The wall was down, along the whole of the front section he could see, the timbers pulled apart and scorched black along their lengths and lying where they had fallen. The gate was gone. Not broken open. Gone, the frame and the leaves and the hinges, all of it burned through and collapsed inward. Past the line where the gate had stood, the ground was dark and pale, in patches. The dark where things had burned hot and long; the pale where the ash had been scuffed away by weather or by the boots of whoever had walked out of the place when they were done.

He stood at the treeline and made himself count what he was seeing before he went in. The longhouse was gone. The outbuildings along the eastern wall were gone. The stable was a low black square against the earth with one corner post still standing. The watchtower at the far corner had come down across the inner ground and lay in a long-charred line.

There were no bodies. A compound this size, lost this completely, should have had bodies. Even allowing for the time that had passed, even allowing for scavengers, there should have been remnants and there were none. Whoever had finished the work here had cleared the dead afterwards. That was not the action of raiders; they left bodies. They were just after the valuables. The people who had done this had stayed long enough to walk the ground when it was cool and cleared what they had killed.

Velen took a slow breath in through his nose and let it out, as he walked the clearing. He went through it carefully. He stayed off the patches of ground that were still soft with old ash. He kept to the harder packed earth along what had been the main path between the gate and the longhouse. He stopped at the centre of the compound where the longhouse had stood and looked down at the ground for a long time. The stone of the hearth was still there, broken in three places and blackened, but still recognizable. He squatted beside it and put his palm flat on the largest piece. The stone was cold. He had not expected it to be warm, but he wanted to feel it anyway.

He continued to the inner perimeter. He had reached the eastern wall, where the outbuildings had been, when he saw it. It was half under a fallen beam, at the place where the wall met the ground, in the narrow space between the end of the burned timber and the base of what had been an outbuilding wall. A piece of wood. Curved. The edge of it showing through the ash where the beam had fallen across it and shielded it from the worst of the fire. Velen crouched and brushed the ash away with the side of his hand.

It was a shield.

He worked it free with both hands. The face of it was scored along one side where something had cut into it and dragged. The edge was bound in leather that had charred along the back but held. He turned it over in his hands. He looked at it for a long time.

He had handled a great many shields in his years. He knew the round shields the southern levies carried, and he knew the long shields the Clawborn auxiliaries used along the river country, and he knew the cheap planked things the raiding gangs put together when they needed to look organized. This was none of those.

The shield was metal, not wood based with metal fittings. Metal throughout, edge to edge, hammered to a slight curve that pulled the centre forward from the rim. Heavier than wood by a noticeable margin. He had not seen a shield made that way before.

This shield had not been made in a village forge. This shield had not been made in twos and threes by a smith working between other jobs. This shield had been made in a place that made shields, by people whose work was making shields, to a pattern that someone had set and that everyone making them followed.

Velen stayed crouched with the shield across his knees and did not move.

A force rising just past the south-north divide.

He had been sent to confirm a rumour with evidence. He was holding the evidence in his hands.

He pondered this for a long while, taking the time to consider all aspects of how this information would play out. The compound was gone, and it was meant to be gone. This wasn’t random, it was organized and wasn’t for profit.

He did not know how many other shields like this were currently on arms in this region. He did not know whose forge had made them. He did not know who had given the order to burn this compound, or how many of them there had been, or where they had gone after. He knew one thing, the house in Grey Ridge did not know any of it either.

He stood and prepared the shield for transport then he mounted.

He turned the horse and looked back across the clearing once, at the line of the fallen palisade and the dark square of the stable and the long-charred remnant of the watchtower lying across the ground. The light had gone amber along the western edge of the trees. There was no sound coming out of the clearing. There had been no sound coming out of it the whole time he had been there. Even the crows were not there.

He rode south and the shield rode with him.
