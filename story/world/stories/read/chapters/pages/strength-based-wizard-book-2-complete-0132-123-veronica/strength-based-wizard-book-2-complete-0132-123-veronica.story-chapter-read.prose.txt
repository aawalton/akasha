Chapter 123

Veronica

Two hospital visits in one day. Even if this one feels totally different. This one feels like somebody took a boutique hotel, removed all the fun, and doused every surface in medical-grade hand sanitizer. It’s a small suite, really. There’s a couch, art on the walls, and a little sitting area near a wide window. The bed is tucked against one wall, neatly made. There are monitors, sure, but they’re small and quiet and not hooked up to anyone.

Veronica is sitting in an armchair near the window. She’s wearing a hospital gown, which looks weird on her. Probably because she looks completely healthy and there’s not a single scratch on her. Her curly dark hair is tied up with a scrunchie. She’s reading a book with one leg tucked beneath her, completely absorbed, like she’s waiting at a coffee shop instead of recovering from being used as a chew toy by a priest of Chernobog.

At first, I think she looks tired. Then the light shifts through the window. Or maybe my [Perception] catches more than my eyes initially do—the looseness in her shoulders, the softness around her mouth, the way she turns the page without tension in her fingers.

She doesn’t look tired. No, she looks peaceful.

After what she’s been through, I’m glad. Really glad.

“Hey Joe,” she says without looking up. She turns the page. “Or should I say, Muscle Mage?”

I laugh awkwardly.

“Please don’t,” I say. “At least it’s better than Jorts man, or something.”

“Daisy Dukem Nukem?”

“That’s terrible.”

She smiles at the book.

I step farther into the room.

“No Jelly Boy?” she asks.

“He wanted to stay home and relax.” I pause when I’m a few feet away from her armchair. “How are you feeling?”

“Honestly fine,” Veronica says. She places a bookmark between the pages with careful precision, then looks up. A stray curl has slipped loose near her cheek. She tucks it behind her ear. When she does, I can’t help but notice the muscled curve of her arm. I remember when I first met her and Clyde. She’d been softer back then. How quickly her body shifted under the influence of the System and her Class. Did people think the same thing when they see me?

“The System regen did most of the work,” she says. “I’m just here for one last mental wellness check before they let me leave.”

“Oh,” I say. “Er, good.”

My eyes drift because eye contact feels too confrontational in this moment. Like I’d be staring down so much more than my friend. There’s a stand near the wall, covered in vases of flowers. Bright arrangements with little cards tucked between them. A small figurine catches my eye. It’s made of metal, cast in the shape of a tree. Not huge. Maybe the size of my hand. The trunk twists upward in clean, elegant lines, branches spreading into delicate leaves.

“It’s from Clyde,” Veronica says, noticing what’s caught my attention.

I blink at it.

“Clyde?”

“A get-well-soon gift. He dropped by to see us both,” she says. “But said you were already gone when he came by your room.”

“Oh.” My mouth goes dry. “Um, yeah. I had something I had to deal with. It was pretty urgent.” The words come out flat. Too small for what they’re carrying. I cringe at how lame I sound. My dad’s smile flashes in my mind. Jelly Boy on his lap. The pudding cup dissolving. Comfortable.

I keep looking at the metal tree because it’s easier than looking at Veronica.

I hear the book set down with a soft “thump.”

Then movement.

Veronica gets up from the chair, bare feet touching the floor. She crosses the room without saying anything. I feel her before she reaches me, that presence of hers—steady, warm, stubborn as a shield raised between me and whatever is coming next.

Then her arms wrap around me.

It’s a strong hug. She hugs me like she means it.

My breath catches.

“Thanks, Joe,” she says against me. “For everything.”

For a second, I don’t know what to do with my arms.

I finally just hug her back. Carefully at first. Then not. I let my body drink in the warmth and wash away the sadness that cakes my soul like a layer of winter salt-grime on a car.

The room is quiet around us. The flowers smell faintly sweet. Sunlight sits across the floor in pale gold rectangles.

I close my eyes, and hold on.

Until the moment melts away. “Veronica,” I breathe.

“Yeah?”

“What’s your Strength score again?” I wheeze into her shoulder. “Because you’re squeezing the air out of my lungs with this hug.”

Veronica laughs, tightening her hug.

“Not as high as it was before I opted out of the God Game,” she says.

I chuckle automatically. Then the words finish processing. My laugh dies in my throat like it stepped on a land mine.

“That cursed Trait of mine meant I lost all of the enhanced growth I’ve been experiencing,” she adds, like she’s explaining a weird billing issue and not casually tossing a grenade into the room.

I pull back.

Not fast, but enough to break the hug. Enough that I can look at her face.

Her eyes meet mine, dark and steady. A little confused by the sudden shift in me.

“You opted out?” I ask.

Veronica blinks. Then her eyebrows knit together.

“Joe, what do you mean?” she asks. “Of course I did.” She studies my face for half a second, and the confusion turns sharp. “Did you not?”

The room gets quieter. Or maybe my ears just decide they don’t want to deal with anything except the sound of my own heartbeat.

I shift my weight. “No,” I say. “Uh… Not yet.”

Veronica stares at me.

For a second, she looks taken aback in a way I almost never see. Veronica can get angry. Veronica can get amused. Veronica can look at a monster with the expression of someone deciding where to put the hammer.

But this is different. This is fear wearing disbelief as a jacket.

“Are you seriously considering opting in?” she asks.

I don’t answer immediately, which is its own answer, I suppose.

She exhales hard and steps back, running one hand over the top of her head and catching the scrunchie with her fingers. A curl slips free again. She doesn’t fix it this time.

“What more do you need?” she asks. “Look at what you’ve achieved already.”

I glance away.

The flowers sit on the stand. The metal tree catches the sunlight again, all silver branches and careful little leaves.

“I don’t know,” I say. It’s a lame response.  It comes out like a kid caught with his hand in a cookie jar, except the cookie jar is a divine death tournament and the cookie is maybe a miracle that could save my father.

Veronica’s expression softens, but only a little.

“What are you going to do,” I ask, because deflection is cheaper than therapy, “now that you’re System locked?”

She sighs.

“I’m tired, Joe,” she says.

The words hit harder than I expect.

Because she is. I can see it now. There’s no weakness or fear in her face when she admits it. Just exhaustion. The kind that sinks down past the body and starts taking up space in the soul. In that moment, I know she’s given everything she’s got to the System.

“For starters,” she says, “I’m taking a break from dating.”

A laugh escapes me before I can choke it down. It’s just such an unexpected turn.

Veronica points at me.

“Seriously!”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You absolutely said something with your stupid face.”

“My face is recovering from trauma, by the way. Maybe my facial nerves are still all messed up?”

“Your face has always been like that.”

“Like what? Messed up? Rude.”

“Telling.”

That gets another chuckle out of me. Hers too, a quick flash of it, and for a moment the room breathes, and whatever tension had been building up evaporates.

She folds her arms, leaning against the edge of the bed that’s at our side.

“I think it’s time I focused on myself,” she says. “Like really focused on myself.”

I wait. She looks toward the window, at the city beyond the glass.

“I’m actually thinking about going back to law school.”

I blink. “Really?”

“Yeah.”

The word comes out cautious, like she’s still testing how it feels.

“I could join a Guild, I guess,” she says. “There are offers. There will be more offers. I know that.” Her jaw tightens, and she lets out another breath. “But…”

She looks back at me. “This is all just so exhausting. I’m over it.”

I nod slowly.

I want to say something supportive. Something clever. Something that sounds like I understand without making it about me.

What comes out is, “I guess I can’t blame you.”

She watches me.

Then her face shifts.

“I’m sorry,” she says.

That knocks me back a little.

“Don’t be sorry,” I say quickly. “There’s nothing to apologize for.”

And that’s true. Veronica has been through hell. Actual hell-adjacent ritual sacrifice hell. She has every right to stop and call it quits. Every right to walk away. Every right to pick law school and quiet mornings and terrible coffee and maybe someday dates with people who are not secretly chosen priests of cosmic evil.

She deserves peace.

But some ugly little part of me still feels abandoned. Like the floor is dropping one ally at a time. Reshma gone. Dad sick. Veronica stepping off the board. I don’t say any of that. I’m too emotional right now. I just swallow it down instead.

“I’m glad you’re okay,” I say.

Her eyes search my face.

I force a smile.

“And after all this publicity, I may need a lawyer anyway. One who’s not from the Harvest Guild.”

The corner of her mouth lifts.

“If you can afford my rates,” she says, “sure.”

I snort.

“Wow. Not even a barred lawyer yet, and you’re already predatory.”

“I prefer aspirational.”

“Right, right…!”

We both chuckle at that.

Later that same day, I’m sitting cross-legged on my rooftop on a yoga mat. My Full Metal Staff rests beside the mat.

Jelly Boy is inside, allegedly napping. He had made a soft buzzing noise when I said I was going up to stretch, then rolled into my laundry basket and absorbed one of my socks in what I hope was an accident.

I’ve just finished stretching.

Now I’m…

I don’t know what to call it. Meditating?

I’m desperately trying to unfurl the tangled mess in my mind. Trying to take all the grief, fear, guilt, rage, and cosmic dread and lay it out where I can see it. It does not want to be laid out. It wants to form a knot the size of a bowling ball behind my sternum.

My thoughts keep coming back to Dad.

I close my eyes.

The wind moves across the rooftop, tugging at my shirt, cooling the sweat still drying on my skin. My breathing is slow, controlled. In through the nose. Out through the mouth.

None of it helps.

I feel defeated.

That’s the word.

Defeated.

Which is stupid, because I have spent the last day doing things that should be impossible. I killed a demonic Avatar that threatened to open a portal to some kind of hellscape smack dab in the middle of Public Square. I fought off a lake invasion before breakfast. My Level is higher than I ever thought it would be. I have Skills and Spells and a flying staff and a magical slime roommate who watches reality television with alarming emotional intelligence.

And none of it matters.

I can punch a demon. I can punch a shark king. But I can’t punch my way out of this dilemma.

My hands curl into fists on my knees.

That thought sits there.

Then another thought follows it.

My eyes open.

“God dammit,” I sigh. What if I can do something that could help here? It’s worth a try.

My pulse picks up.

With a mental command, I summon my System menu.

The interface blooms in front of me, blue-white and clinical, floating over my view of the city’s rooftops. I find the minimized System message I’m looking for and select it. There’s a small tingling sensation, before a new window appears in my vision.

TRAIT: [Flexible Casting].

Description: The User has the ability to modify the nature of their spellcraft.

You have 1 Modified Spellcasting available. Use Modified Spellcasting?

I issue the mental command, affirming my desire and am greeted by the familiar haptic buzz as the System accepts my command.

Ping!

Please select one Spell to see available options for Modified Spellcasting. Please note that Spell selection is final. Modified Spellcasting will grant the User with enhanced or unique modes of crafting the selected Spell. However, the current base form of the Spell will remain available as the default option when casting the selected Spell.

Choose Spell:

A menu of all my known Spells springs to life before me. I scan the list, considering each of my available options.

The air tastes like evening and ozone and distant lake water.

My thoughts go to Dad.

To his smile. To his hand patting my back while I cried into him like a kid. To the way he said I didn’t have to fix this.

Maybe not today.

But I can try.

I select the Spell I want to modify.
