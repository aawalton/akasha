Sys looked on the sea of supplicant Argwylians, seeing everyone he’d wished to step on ever since he’d woken up bound to Ethan:

Mara, the prodigal little mage.

Borlor, the festering badger-blacksmith

Malak – the traitor druid.

Langley, the peasant priest.

Each one of them bowed at his feet, prostrate, ready to meet their end. He let his gaze fall on each of them in turn, forcing them to bend lower, and lower, into the dead sea of their home.

But above all, his eyes lingered on the torn threads of Ethan’s form on the glassy floor under him.

The wind picked up again to carry the fragmented pieces of the demon hat away to some far-flung corner of the land, while the rest of the skies clouded over again, blotting out all light so that nothing but the luminescent green sheen of his eyes shone in the infinite, expanding dark.

For the first time he could remember in all his campaigns, he smiled.

“Keadmon!” he shouted at the heavens. “Jun’Ei! Are both of you watching? Are you seeing all of this through my eyes? Now, you witness the end you dragged out for so long. Now you witness me!”

He spread his arms wide and cackled madly. He couldn’t help it. He needed to let the pent-up pressure within his multitudinous spirit release – at least in this moment. It was the moment he’d stopped even dreaming that he would have ever had.

“What do you think of the end of this performance?” he snarled, staring at his reflection in the ground, hoping Kaedmon, tucked away in a corner of his being, would see. “And Jun’Ei – here is what your resistance got you. Here’s what it got all of you – including your chosen champion!”

At that thought he searched the ground behind him, leaving the mute crowd to stare on in silence. Eventually, he found what he was looking for: what remained of Ethan’s thready crimson eyeball.

“Look at him – all of you!” he roared – and this time it was unclear who exactly he was addressing. “Your savior. Your hero.”

He spat that last word more than spoke it, and as soon as he’d uttered both syllables he crunched the eye in his hand in pure disgust.

And that’s it, he told himself. That’s…that’s the last of him.

His body suddenly felt heavy, as though the burden of victory was somehow greater than even his insurmountable will. As the sun of Argwyll disappeared behind the dark clouds he’d summoned, he raised a single blade and let loose a roar of triumph that shattered the ground beneath him and his captive audience both.

It was a roar of pure, primal satisfaction.

And it felt so good that, for a few moments, he didn’t even care that it was a lie.

[Dreamstride: DEACTIVATED]

Something happened.

The world shook.

The crowd’s forms started to bend, coagulating like the lava lamps in Ethan’s dimly lit bedroom…

Their faces twisted, contorted, and then melted away into the ether – into the dark canvas he’d painted that was starting to drain away. All color was leaving the earth. Including his.

He watched the spectacle unfold for a few moments. Moments that preceded a full-on panic.

His body now felt weightless. And yet his limbs wouldn’t obey any of his commands to move, to run, to leave, to survive…whatever was happening here.

And before his lips could utter a single syllable of shock, the vision – for that was what it was – melted away.

The glassed sea blurred into the vortex of color.

The shoreline met the sky in a blinding kaleidoscope.

His own body dripped away from him into the ether that had opened up above, and it swallowed everything that Argwyll had ever been until, finally, the blanket of color faded and revealed…

Ethan Hawke. The Demon Hat.

Alive. Together. Patched up and smiling that same snarky smile he’d worn on his hem on the day they’d first met.

Enjoying your power trip?

That voice – that agony inducing, painful, mind-numbing voice…it was his. It was unmistakable.

Beside him were…the hybrids. The dog and cat were holding him down…like they had been doing before…before…

…no.

He didn’t even have to say the word. And, checking his status in a sudden onset of sheer frenzy, he saw what couldn’t have been and yet simply…was.

None of it…he couldn’t help but murmur. None of it was…was…

Ethan…he whispered. Ethan –

ETHAN!

Ethan opened his eyes and saw clouds.

It was the best way he could describe the environment that stretched out before him. A series of puffy white clouds stretched as far as his eye could see around him.

He blinked, adjusting his vision to this new reality – whatever it might be. In any case, he knew that it couldn’t be another dream of his or of Sys’s making.

He recalled – dimly – the events that led up to him coming her…the fight in the middle of the boiling seas…Sys almost ending him more times than he cared to admit…possessing all his friends at their own behest and finally beating the old parasite at his own game and activating [Soul Wipe].

Soul wipe…that must be why I’m here, he thought, regarding the endless void around him with renewed interest. This could be some ancillary effect of the Skill. And if that’s true, then that means…

Just as the thought formed, several spheres sprouted up from the cloudcover like balls of fluff being plucked by an infant and blown dreamily into the air. They drifted above him slowly, lazily, and he found that he had to exercise no small degree of focus to actually resolve their contents in his mind’s eye.

Once he did, it all became clear.

Each one of them held memories. Thoughts, no matter how fleeting, elements of his own consciousness – and the consciousnesses of others. He stared at them as they drifted into the ethereal whites of the sky – seeing images play out like recorded video clips across the sky of this plane.

Images of dark, narrow tunnels, and the skittering of tiny legs, twitching of noses, and the promise of light that came when a hat was finally summoned.

He watched that bulb with a mixture of curiosity and laughter – seeing Theo the rat, the Slayer of all undead, Bane of Bones, traverse the depths of Argwyll’s darkest dungeons before finally joining him again as Sanctum’s savior.

Next to the rat-knight’s memories he saw those of the troll he’d taken command of at the end of Theo’s mine. He saw its desire for conquest being satiated when he plopped himself upon its bulky head, and its satisfaction as it fought the Lightborn himself, through him, loving every moment right up until its demise…

The next bulb bounced around like a jovial pup – zipping around the others with unbound glee. He looked into it and saw the forests of Argwyll disappear beneath the creature to whom this memory belonged – the Dark Raven that had served him and, as it turns out, had felt nothing but pride to be paraded before Sanctum as a hero-figure. All birds were born free, that was true. But this one, it seemed, had longed for something different. It had longed to be recognized. And even its eventual death did not phase it for that reason.

Another bulb near him showed a spider crawl out of its egg-sac and know that it was more than its brethren. He felt it fight through its years of captivity – using all the venom in its disposal – before it too felt the power of the demon hat and saw the surface world for the first time…and the pride and joy it experienced in those moments of light swelled in his being. Ethan couldn’t have imagined that, all the time they’d been together, Rachneros the Spider Lord had longed for freedom just as much as he did…

Yet still more bulbs sprouted out of the void and sailed towards the empty sky. He saw Valgraiva’s past as a failed general, reborn as an undead lord and then finally having the chance to strike back against the surface world who had imprisoned him. He saw the Leifblade next, the Salamandrike who had lived nohing but a simple life as a short-lived forest-being who had managed to avoid Triant huntsmen more than others of his kind. The Demon Hat had served him, too, just as much as he had been its servant. It had shown him that accepting one’s fate meekly wasn’t the way its life had to be.

The memories of Irlanda, the Druid-turned-Drytchling Prime, came next. Their colors were dark, brooding, but ultimately uplifting. Ethan had never known how she’d felt about his mercy killing at the end of his Griffon’s Watch raid. But he saw now. She’d embraced her end after destroying the being who had so corrupted her husband with a lust for power that he’d corrected – in her own body, no less. In so doing, he’d granted her dying wish. He saw that now.

Even Revok’s memories – the darkest of all his Hosts’ by far – were tinged with a sense of hope. After enduring nothing but pain and torment at Dr Haylock’s claws, it had finally known a world that was more than just agony. A world that welcomed him, mind skewed as it was, as a guardian, and as a savior.

All these memories floated away from him as he looked into their contents, and he knew that this must be exactly how the [Soul Wipe] Skill functioned. For him, this felt like an eternity of prospecting every life he’d altered across his journey in Argwyll. But for the Klax, Tara, and Fauna, all of this probably would encompass about 2-3 seconds of real-world time.

His mind lingered on the last image he’d seen on Argwyll’s surface – that of Fauna sadly bidding him farewell. Smiling – yes – but probably only so he remembered her like that…if he’d even be able to remember anything at all soon…

As this thought took him, one memory ball ballooned into existence at the far end of the cloud-sanctum. Instantly he was drawn to it – or, more accurately, it was drawn to him.

Unlike the others it sped towards him with actual intent, as though it was looking into his soul and not the other way round.

He fought against the need to speed away from the translucent bulb as it made a beeline right for him. For some reason, fear didn’t rule him right now. After all, these were nothing but his own memories. And he’d already accepted that he was good and done as soon as he activated his final trump Skill.

The bulb finally closed in on him and circled his hatty form, edging closer to him but giving nothing away as to its contents. Finally, he rolled his eye and simply took the plunge himself, thrusting into the surface of the orb and feeling himself being pulled through into whatever aspect of his mindscape lay beyond.

And when he made it through the memory’s thin veil, he couldn’t believe who he saw there waiting for him.
