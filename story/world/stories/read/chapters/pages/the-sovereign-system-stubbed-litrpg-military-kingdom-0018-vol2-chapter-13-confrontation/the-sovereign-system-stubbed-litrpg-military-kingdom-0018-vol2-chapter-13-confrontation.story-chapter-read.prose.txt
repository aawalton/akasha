It took Luke over an hour to recover from the pounding headache brought on by overexertion. Rather than return to the dormitory, he remained on the fifth floor of the library, settling onto one of the many benches that lined the walls.

Victoria sat nearby, tending to him with the quiet efficiency of a seasoned servant. Luke noted how flawlessly she performed her duties in public—likely the reason she had evaded capture in both Clayton and Valand City.

“Classes will be starting soon, young master,” Victoria reminded him, her voice soft but insistent.

“Mmm. Let’s head down to the first floor, then,” Luke replied. He took a steadying breath, pushing down the lingering nausea as he rose to his feet.

Without another word, Victoria moved to his side and linked her arm with his. Her support was subtle but firm, steadying his steps as they made their way toward the stairs. Luke glanced at her and murmured his thanks.

Though the stairs were a little steep, he managed the descent without issue, largely thanks to Victoria’s quiet assistance. Together, they reached the lecture hall and found their seats, waiting in silence for the class to begin.

As his dizziness eased, Luke’s thoughts drifted back to what had happened earlier on the fifth floor. He remembered leaning casually against a bookshelf when a flood of information had suddenly poured into his mind.

‘It was almost like I was downloading the contents of the bookshelf…’ he thought, struggling to put the experience into terms that made sense to him.

‘If the Scholarly Pursuit skill can do this…’

His expression brightened. He had absorbed the knowledge of nearly two thousand books in less than ten minutes—a feat beyond comprehension. And he hadn’t needed to flip a single page or draw any attention to himself.

A surge of excitement coursed through him. If that was the case, he could absorb the entire collection on the fifth floor in just five days. How many books were housed on the other floors? At this rate, he could master the entire Royal Academy Library within a matter of months.

‘This is exactly what I needed,’ he thought, his hand clenching into a fist.

He didn’t know how long he could remain here, but one thing was certain—absorbing the library’s knowledge had to be his priority. As for the lectures, he suspected only Master Hail’s classes would hold any real value for him.

The Scholarly Pursuit skill was so intuitive that it not only absorbed knowledge but also filtered it, offering him the best course of action at any given moment. No theoretical lesson from any of the current Masters at the Royal Academy could compare to that.

‘I still lack physical strength, even if my tactics are polished enough,’ Luke mused. ‘It must have been fate that I chose the Scholarly Pursuit skill.’

He silently thanked his lucky stars for that decision. If he could stay long enough to finish the books in the library and take advantage of Master Hail’s one-on-one instruction, he would be ready to leave the capital—if it came to that.

Soon, the other students and their servants began filtering into the lecture hall. Without prompting, Victoria rose from her seat beside him and crossed the room, joining the other servants waiting at the back.

Kayson entered the lecture hall alongside Sebastian. The former’s face was pale and drawn, as if he’d just clawed his way out of hell. He shot Luke a tired glare before slumping into the seat beside him.

“Sebastian is a demon, I swear…” Kayson muttered, casting a wary glance over his shoulder at the butler, who stood calmly nearby.

Luke chuckled. “I’d be careful using that word around him,” he warned, wearing a grin. A flicker of smug satisfaction passed through him. It was good to know he wasn’t the only one subjected to Sebastian’s merciless training.

Kayson shuddered. “Thanks for the heads up.”

After a brief scan of the room, he leaned in close. “You think the instructor’s going to make things difficult for you?”

Luke gave a careless shrug. “If he does, it won’t end well for him.”

Almost as if summoned by the conversation, the thin frame of Instructor Quentin glided through the doorway and into the hall. His movements were precise, almost rehearsed, as if he fancied himself an actor about to take the stage.

At once, the students rose in unison to greet him.

All but one.

Luke remained seated, legs crossed, expression unreadable.

The instructor’s pale face flushed with color the moment he noticed the lone figure still perched in his chair. By the time Quentin reached the front of the room, his features were twisted in fury.

He spun on his heel and leveled a sharp glare at Luke.

“Mr. Drakon,” he said, his thin voice trembling with suppressed rage, “it seems you lack the common courtesy to greet your instructors. Stand and show respect, or I shall have you removed from this class at once.”

Luke’s grin widened.

“Instructor,” he said smoothly, “I may lack courtesy, but you seem to have forgotten your own teachings. How do you expect your students to respect you when you don’t practice what you preach?”

A dangerous silence followed.

Then Quentin shrieked, his face contorting as spittle sprayed from his lips. “Excuse me? You insolent little fool! How dare you speak to me like this!”

Luke’s grin vanished. He rose slowly to his feet, his gaze turning cold as steel.

“You’ve made two mistakes,” he said, his tone hardening. “First, addressing me by name. I am a Viscount, appointed by His Majesty himself. You will refer to me by my proper title.”

He took a step closer, his presence suddenly filling the space between them.

“Second,” he continued, his voice low, “you expect me, a Viscount, to show courtesy to you? How laughable.”

His scorn was palpable, each word cutting deeper than the last.

Quentin’s furious expression faltered. His eyes widened, and his mouth worked soundlessly for a moment, as if he’d only just realized the extent of his blunder. Perhaps he hadn’t realized that Luke would be well versed in noble etiquette.

It made sense, he was technically a commoner before being appointed as Viscount just a day prior.

Luke stepped forward and jabbed a finger into the frail man’s chest, pushing him back a pace.

“It is my choice whether I offer you courtesy,” he said. “But you have no choice when addressing me.”

Quentin stumbled but managed to stay upright. His face, once flushed with rage, had drained of all color. His lips quivered, teeth chattering faintly in the dead silence that followed.

Luke took a measured breath, then raised his arms in a gesture that was almost magnanimous.

“I’ll give you one chance to make amends,” he said. “But I suggest you choose your next words wisely. The consequences may be more than you can bear.”

For a heartbeat, no one moved.

The room was still—too still. Even the other students held their breath, watching the exchange with wide, uncertain eyes.

Instructor Quentin’s lips trembled. His face was ghostly white, his body rigid as if frozen in place. Luke wouldn’t have been surprised if the man soiled himself on the spot.

But what happened next was something he hadn’t expected.

Instructor Quentin threw himself to the floor, prostrating at Luke’s feet.

“Please, Viscount Drakon, have mercy on me! I apologize for my transgressions—it will never happen again, I swear it!” he cried, banging his forehead against the polished stone with audible thuds.

For a moment, Luke stood there, surprised.

But that surprise quickly gave way to disgust.

The sniveling wreck before him had no backbone whatsoever. Moments ago, Quentin had lorded his status over the students like a tyrant. And now? He groveled on the ground, begging for mercy at the first sign of consequence.

Luke’s gaze shifted around the room. He could see it wasn’t just him who found the sight distasteful. The noble scions seated nearby looked upon Instructor Quentin with thinly veiled derision.

Many wore sneers; others shook their heads in silent judgment.

‘There’s no way he’ll keep his position after this,’ Luke thought. Quentin had humiliated himself beyond repair.

Luke turned toward Sebastian and Victoria and gave them a subtle gesture toward the door. Then his focus returned to the pathetic figure groveling at his feet.

“This will be the last time I forgive such transgressions,” Luke said coolly. “Don’t let it happen again.”

Without another word, he nudged Kayson with his elbow and made his way out of the lecture hall.

He doubted class would continue after a scene like that—and even if it did, he had no desire to stick around and watch Quentin scrape together whatever scraps of dignity he had left.

The group exited the library and stepped into the surrounding garden. The air outside was crisp, and the faint scent of blooming flowers drifted on the breeze.

It was a welcome change.

“Wow… I never expected things to turn out like that,” Kayson said, dragging a hand through his blond hair in disbelief.

“I enjoyed the show, young master. You did well to put him in his place,” Victoria added, her tone as even as ever.

Luke exhaled, shaking his head. “He had his chance. Of all people, he should’ve known the proper etiquette when addressing a Viscount. He can only blame himself for underestimating my knowledge.”

As they continued walking, the image of Quentin groveling was still fresh in Luke’s mind. But something else soon caught his attention.

At the opposite end of the garden, a figure was approaching with a steady, deliberate gait—hands clasped behind his back.

A single glance at the man’s drooping mustache was enough for Luke to recognize him.

“Master Hail,” Luke greeted, cupping his fist with respectful formality. “It’s good to see you.”

The others followed suit, offering more formal bows and greetings than his own.

“Viscount Drakon,” Master Hail said, returning the gesture with a short bow. “Just the man I was looking for.”

His expression was serene.

“There’s a representative of His Majesty here to meet with you. Would you mind accompanying me to the gate?”

‘It seems the King is rather impatient to get the gunpowder recipe,’ Luke thought, his hazel eyes glinting for a moment.

“I shall accompany you,” he replied.

Then he turned to Kayson and the others. “I’ll be heading to the palace to meet with the Alchemist. I should be back by evening.”

With that, he fell into step beside Master Hail as they walked leisurely through the Academy’s sprawling estate.

Luke held a good impression of the man—at least on the surface. But that was as far as it went. Without a proper background check, he wouldn’t lower his guard completely. He didn’t yet know where Master Hail’s loyalties truly lay.

As they walked, Master Hail spoke up, his tone casual.

“I apologize for Instructor Quentin’s behavior last night, Viscount Drakon. The man is obsessed with propriety. One day, it’ll be his downfall.”

Luke gave a faint, amused smile. “There’s no reason for you to apologize, Master Hail. You could say… we sorted out our differences this morning.”

“Oh?” Master Hail’s brow rose slightly. “That is for the best, then.”

The rest of the walk felt awkward for Luke.

He had no doubt that word of his confrontation with Instructor Quentin would soon reach Master Hail’s ears—if it hadn’t already. Luke only hoped it wouldn’t sour their relationship. The Master Swordsman’s favor was valuable, and it wasn’t something he intended to throw away carelessly.

Thankfully, their journey was short, and they arrived at the Academy’s main gate within minutes.

“This is the man sent by the King,” Master Hail said, gesturing toward a short figure waiting nearby.

“Viscount Drakon,” the man greeted, bowing with surprising grace. “I am Blake Thompson, Royal Alchemist to His Majesty.”

Blake’s brown beard covered most of his face, save for a pair of keen eyes that watched Luke with quiet focus. His robes were scorched and stained with what smelled like burnt herbs—strong enough that Luke caught the scent even from ten feet away.

Luke returned the gesture, cupping his fist. He studied the alchemist in silence for a moment, then spoke.

“It’s good to finally meet you, Alchemist. I assume His Majesty wishes for me to accompany you?”

Blake exhaled with relief, as if a burden had been lifted.

“That would be much appreciated, Viscount.”

Luke gave Master Hail a final nod in farewell and stepped through the Royal Academy gates alongside the Royal Alchemist.

They hadn’t made it far when Luke felt a faint prickle at the back of his neck—unease.

Something wasn’t right.

He turned to the shorter man. “Is it just you? Where are your escorts?”

Blake blinked, clearly confused. “Escorts? Why would I need an escort in the capital?” he asked, his brows knitting.

Luke’s frown deepened.

Without another word, he strode toward one of the nearby guards stationed at the gate.

“You there,” he said curtly. “Lend me your sword for a moment.”

The guard hesitated, clearly puzzled by the request.

But after a brief moment, the soldier quickly unfastened his sheathed weapon and handed it over without protest.

Luke gave a brief nod of thanks and secured the sword at his belt. He then motioned for Blake to continue walking.

It wasn’t that Blake was acting suspicious—at least not overtly—but Luke couldn’t shake the feeling that something was off.

If the King valued the gunpowder recipe so highly, why send his Royal Alchemist alone? Even within the capital, such a man should have had an escort, or at least some form of protection.

‘Better to be cautious,’ Luke thought.

The route to the palace was straightforward, but the streets were crowded. Midmorning traffic had filled the roads with merchants hawking their wares and citizens going about their business. It made for a perfect cover—both for them, and for anyone who might be watching.

Luke’s gaze moved constantly, cataloging faces, noting movements, watching for signs of danger.

And then he saw them.

A group of men in black robes had emerged from a side street, moving with purpose toward his right flank.

There were at least five of them—maybe more.

‘Shit,’ Luke thought grimly.

For a heartbeat, he weighed his options. Then he placed a firm hand on Blake’s shoulder and leaned in, his voice low and controlled.

“Don’t look back. We’re being followed,” he said.

“As soon as I take my hand off your shoulder, run. Do you understand?”

He felt the alchemist stiffen under his touch. Blake’s face had turned pale, but after a tense second, he gave a short, jerky nod.

Luke’s heart thundered in his chest, but his mind remained clear.

He scanned the road ahead, searching for escape routes—alleys, side streets, anything that might give them an advantage. The palace gates were still too far. They’d have to make do.

His eyes narrowed in determination.

The moment he released Blake’s shoulder, he broke into a sprint.

“Fuck! They’re running!”

The shout came from behind them, raw and angry.

Luke didn’t waste time looking back.
