On the twenty-first day since my clash against the Maharani of Aryavarta, I dressed like a king.

I wore black and gold formal robes, armored beneath the cloth. The Heavenly Demon Divine Cult's lone-star sigil sat over my chest. My hair was tied back. The Elven Spirit Pendant rested at my neck, cool against my skin.

Amelia stood beside me in a fitted dark-gold gown that looked diplomatic until you noticed the runes woven into the seams.

Yes, I wasn’t brave enough to meet the Maharani alone today. Initially I’d considered taking Vargathrian with me, but she refused to take a human form, and in the end, I decided Amelia would be the better option.

She was coming because she was the daughter of an Arcane King, disowned or not. Blood mattered in rooms like this. Denying that would be childish.

The Bifrost light cleared around us.

The receiving courtyard of Shunya Mahal appeared under our feet.

Amravati's floating palace probably looked different without the Stillness pressing through every surface. I couldn’t tell since I hadn’t been here when the Stillness was present. A star-silver lotus hung above the Lake of Unbroken Vows, and the city below operated clean enough to be therapeutic for the mind.

The air here was not polished flat, for the Stillness was truly gone. Somewhere below, people were arguing. Life. This place felt alive, unlike the stories I’d heard.

Vyrn slipped out of the pendant without me calling him. The ghostly white owl circled once above my head, then flew toward the open garden arches with a pleased trill.

I watched him go.

He had hated coming out in Aryavarta before. Or maybe he had hated what the Lucent Air did to spirits. Either way, he didn't seem to mind now.

"Hey, don't steal any treasures or something."

Vyrn ignored me with a hoot.

Prince Avyaan waited at the far end of the courtyard.

He looked better than he had in Vanga, which was not saying much. His jaw had been restored, his robes were immaculate, and he carried himself like a prince again. His eyes were different, though. Less still than before.

He bowed. "Lord Romani. Princess Amelia."

I was a little surprised.

"Lord?"

"You are sovereign of Nevaramis, after all. Unless you’d prefer Heavenly Demon…?"

"Depends who is saying it."

His mouth did not twitch. "Lord Romani, then."

Amelia inclined her head. "Prince Avyaan."

He looked at her more carefully. "You've grown. Last time I saw you, you were a child. It is a shock to see you beside this man, however."

The two exchanged some stories that I was too young to have been born to see. Avyaan allowed himself a small smile. The man had survived having his emotional spine pulled into a cosmic blender. A small smile was something.

He turned and gestured toward the inner gardens. "My mother is waiting. She returned from Nagadipa this morning."

"How did that go?" I asked as we walked. "The neutral table."

"Nagraj Vasuki complained to me about security, tribute protocol, seating arrangements, the number of armed guards allowed in the coral court, and Bagha Hamid's habit of calling him 'snake king' with a bad tone. However, he was very accommodating."

"Sounds productive."

"It was, somehow."

"Ah, right. How’s Sathari doing these days?"

"She is well. She asked that I tell you she expects a proper apology for being used as a hostage."

"I already apologized."

"She said the next apology should include gifts."

"I wasn’t aware reptiles were this greedy- ouch,” I held back a hiss feeling Amelia, all clawed up, pinching my side. Then I remembered she was a dragon herself, and her father wore the Arcane Crown representing greed.

Funny how that worked.

Avyaan ignored the embarrassing sound and glanced to the side. "She also said Princess Amelia should visit her properly at least once, rather than abducting her through you."

Amelia's lips pressed together.

"I will."

As we walked, I let my Demonic Sphere open.

Before the Third Flower, the Sphere had mapped space. Bodies, mana, movement, threats. Which had been very useful always, but somewhat flat.

Everything there had the same depth.

Now the map had depth.

Within my range, I could feel people in layers. I could see, I could hear, I could smell, I could touch… not really, but it felt like touching. It was such a strange feeling. That’s why I’d called it almost Omnipresence. Of course, I could also feel people’s minds.

Not mind-reading, exactly… I couldn't hear thoughts like words.

But intent brushed against the edge of my perception. Like a servant pressing panic down behind a trained face. A guard who had shaken during breakfast and hadn't fully steadied. Two court officials smiling at each other while both held political knives they hadn't drawn yet.

The capital wasn't settled yet.

Then again, normal was a stupid word here. Most of these people had lived their entire lives with their emotions dampened. Now their feelings worked properly, and they were discovering that being a person was inconvenient. Some were brighter for it. Some looked close to breaking. A few seemed almost drunk on anger or joy.

I had never seen Amravati under full Stillness in person, I’d merely heard stories and reports, seen it in Valeria’s memories, so I couldn't compare with perfect fairness.

But compared to the dead calm I had felt from afar, this city finally had blood moving through it. Being the reason behind this change, I couldn’t help but smile.

We found the Maharani in a garden suspended along the eastern side of the floating palace.

There were flowering trees in pale stone planters, still water channels, and little bridges that likely had seven layers of symbolism I did not care about. Shayura was walking beside her mother, wearing a simple blue dress instead of royal white.

Her body was sculpted in the same way ancient statues were. Hah, perhaps she was the ideal model for those ancient Goddess statues for she was proportioned for divinity rather than practicality. A goddess who had decided, on a quiet afternoon, to be a woman instead.

Huh, what’s up with her? Cute.

"You will die if you try anything with her." I froze. Amelia froze too. The Maharani turned to her. "Dragon Princess, make sure your man remembers my words."

Amelia's shoe touched mine under the table. She was saying Behave, apparently.

The Maharani took a sip. "The Arcane Crowns overlap more than most people realize. Dominion can imitate Stillness through command. Avarice, representing greed, can steal fragments of another Crown's behavior. Euphoria can produce peace through pleasure. At least she didn't pretend otherwise. But while I appreciated the lecture about Arcane Crowns, I wasn’t here for that. "So what did you decide?"

She did not answer immediately. Instead, she raised one hand, and a mirror surface formed in the air.

It showed Bagha Hamid.

He was in a noble hall somewhere not in Vanga, wearing a formal robe that looked like someone had lost a war against his shoulders. His missing arm had been wrapped in black cloth. That was a little scary, for it meant Maharani’s blows could hinder a demigod from regenerating even after weeks. Perhaps forever, too.

The same entity that returned Valeria’s soul?

Wrath. Speed. Relentless advance. Destruction by motion.

It was simply far too risky for him to try that?

She looked at me. "I see that you’re understanding my words quite accurately. What I’m trying to say is… Why would Valtherians, the Royals of the World, need to wear an Arcane crown? The world recognized them before artifacts learned how."

Royals without crowns.

I kept turning the phrase over.

Unless I was stupid, she was outright saying that the Valtherians had the potential to match an Arcane King without bothering to wear a Crown themselves? What the fuck?

The Arcane Crowns were shortcuts made visible. The Valtherians were something older, recognition without an artifact, authority without anyone's permission to grant it. Weirdly enough, that sounded uncomfortably close to something I myself had been building toward without knowing it had a name.

I thought of Moui laughing. Yavanna's speed. The four of them standing before dragons with confidence they had no business having at their levels.

"Holy fuck," I said.

The world grew larger in my head, the way it did when something I'd thought I understood turned out to have another floor underneath.

Arcane Crowns, Origin Evils, the Godmother, the Valtherians, mantles older than the concept of a kingdom. It was a graveyard of ages stacked on each other, and I'd been walking the top layer thinking I understood the ground.

The Maharani watched my expression with a very amused smile.

"Go west, Iskandaar Romani. If you want to stand against the Arcane King of Pride, learn what kings were before crowns started doing the work for them."

It was Tuesday, and Tuesday was a bad day. At least, to Ha-Yun’s experience.

So much often happened on Tuesdays that people had a saying for it, ‘Typical Tuesday.’ The most eventful days ever were often Tuesdays. It was a Tuesday that day too, when Zhen Wei, Baolian's Thunder Fist, had beaten her to near death. Until Ao'kai intervened and saved the day.

Today, too, was one such day. Princess Jin Ha-Yun of Goryeo left the royal council with a headache and the strong urge to throw a minister into a pond.

Her younger brother walked beside her through the corridor leading toward Baekryeong Palace, hands clasped behind his back and expression far too composed for someone who had spent the last hour watching grown men argue over border grain audits.

"Don’t be so bitter, sister. You handled Minister Gwak well," Ha-Young said.

"I wanted to stab him."

"That was less visible. Which is commendable.”

The latest argument had been about Xianli reparations, the problematic border families, and whether accepting too much money from the new puppet king would make Goryeo look weak. Her father had taken the cautious position. Ha-Yun had argued that dead soldiers' families did not eat caution.

They had not resolved it.

Royal governance, she was learning, was mostly people saying obvious things slowly until everyone was too tired to keep arguing.

She walked with her little brother, who was far too composed for his age as usual, but the walk helped her mood slightly. Just a little, as they walked toward her abode. She stepped into her private yard and stopped.

The cherry trees moved in the evening wind.

Someone was in her garden.

Lady Yueling's mark stirred behind her eyes. The presence did not hide. That was what stopped her cold. It sat in her garden and made no attempt at concealment, which was worse than hiding would have been.

Her senses had sharpened since accepting Lady Yueling's deeper blessing, and the world now carried hints it never had before. Small lies buried in posture, emotional residue in the air, spiritual pressure pressing against the edge of her awareness.

This presence was far beyond anything she was built to handle cleanly.

Ninth Ascension at minimum.

Her hand found her sword.

"Ha-Young."

Her brother had already noticed her posture.

"Sister?"

"Run to Father. Tell him we have a guest."

His face tightened.

"How bad?"

"I don’t know. Run."

He did.

Good boy.

Ha-Yun drew her sword and walked toward the gazebo.

She kept her steps light, breathing steady as fox-fire gathered quietly beneath her skin. Whoever waited there had allowed her to sense them, which meant this was either courtesy or arrogance. At that level, the distinction was mostly academic.

Is it an Arcane King?

Yes. The presence was so suffocating that she was certain it wasn’t any of the local Mountain Gods. There was a chance it was Emperor Shengzong himself.

She made the last turn across a wall. Then she saw him, as her heart skipped a beat.

Iskandaar Romani, dressed like a Western king, sat in her gazebo with one ankle crossed over his knee, laughing softly while a ghostly white owl perched on his shoulder and pecked at his hair.

The owl looked offended by something he’d said.

A rolled sea map lay on the table beside him, weighed down by a black volcanic stone.

Iskandaar looked entirely too comfortable.

Deja vu struck her so hard she almost lowered her sword by reflex. His golden eyes met hers. He smiled and lifted one hand. "Ah, hey, Princess. I’ve been waiting for an hour."

Ha-Yun stared at him. Then her scowl arrived all at once.

"You piece of shit little bastard, I almost had a heart attack!"
