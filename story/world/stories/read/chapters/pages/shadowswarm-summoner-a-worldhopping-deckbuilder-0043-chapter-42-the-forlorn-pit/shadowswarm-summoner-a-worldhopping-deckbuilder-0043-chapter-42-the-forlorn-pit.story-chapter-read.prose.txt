Chapter 42: The Forlorn Pit

SNAC gave them all the relevant details, but to be honest, Raeleq wasn’t really listening. It was hard, he found, to pick up everything when they spoke at length. Especially with all of the random noise around them that no one else seemed to hear. He caught about half of it, remembered enough that his memories would stir later, and dedicated himself to looking out for their group.

There they were, on their first new world, and he had his ears open, listening to shifts in the wind, and leathery wings, flapping just above them in the endless night. Every rustle of feet on dirt crunched and shifted, leaving little avalanches in miniature. Both Raeva and Luthor had sources of light out, but Raeleq found himself wishing they would dismiss them, or maybe dim them a little, something to let his eyes adjust to the environment better.

It wasn’t as bad as he expected, once they got moving up the great slope. He and Raeva had climbed steeper, rougher mountains in the past, but the rest likely hadn’t. Luthor was fine, Jessica struggled until she realized she could use her [Leap] card to go from one rocky ledge to another, waiting for everyone to catch up after.

Ambrose, however, wasn’t in the same great shape as everyone else, and had an additional handicap.

“Was I seriously the only one who wore dress shoes to the party?” he asked, huffing and puffing his way up the narrow, winding path upward. “Or did you all actually go home and change before interrupting my heist?”

Raeva laughed and poked his pack from behind. “I never wear dress shoes. If I am going to have anything on my feet, it will be something for a fight.”

“There’s something to be said about being practical at all times,” said Luthor. “And not fussing over what you wear every day.”

“There’s also something to be said for not looking like a homeless person or common soldier on leave,” Ambrose grumbled.

Raeleq chuckled, and then looked as a sharp, barely audible screech echoed in the cavern. Raeva did as well, but no one else noticed, and they kept on.

But now he was even more on edge, and doing his best to block out everyone else as he paid increasingly close attention to their environment.

Looming in the dark, stalagmites and boulders appeared on their way, some forcing them to sidle around it for the best path forward. Then came some of those glowing mushrooms, as big as an ox and scattered around in patches, teasing more of the open cavern. Occasional flurries of movement from creatures just barely passing within sight kept him from relaxing.

That was one thing the light was good for, at least, but it wouldn’t last forever. Some creatures, especially in darkness, would avoid any light they could. And others, the dangerous ones, would see it as an invitation before too long. Raeleq would worry about that, so the others didn’t have to.

“How far do you think we’ve gone?” Jessica asked, jumping ten feet up and a few feet forward to land lightly on a ledge next to a brilliant blue mushroom the petite wizard could’ve used as a bed. It looked awfully soft.

“Not far enough,” Ambrose grumbled, a second before stumbling. He slid, displacing dust and dirt before Raeleq caught him by his pack and pulled him to his feet. He might have been tall, but he wasn’t heavy.

“Not far enough indeed,” said Raeleq with a pat on his shoulder. “We’ve made it half a mile, and that’s not bad for going uphill. Especially with your current endurance. But don’t worry, we’ll toughen you up before too long.”

“Half a mile? What makes you say that?” Ambrose made a face.

“Because it’s been half a mile.” Raeleq smiled, enjoying the frustration on his face.

“Don’t question it,” Luthor said, taking a moment to pause and catch his breath as well. Beads of sweat lined his brow, but he never liked showing weakness. “If Raeleq says it’s been half a mile, it’s been half a mile.”

“He’s always had a knack for that,” said Raeva. She took a waterskin from her pack and took a swig. “Just like I don’t miss when I throw, and our dad always knows where to find fresh water. It’s a remnant of the old magics. You weak northerners don’t have as much of it.”

Ambrose and Raeva began to bicker, or flirt, or whatever it was they did. Raeleq didn’t really get it, but he never understood or wanted romance in his life. Instead, he ignored them and inhaled deeply.

The entire time, there’d been a constant dank, damp smell, dirt and dust, as well as the sickeningly sweet aroma of mushrooms that definitely weren’t safe to eat. A second sniff told him there was water nearby, and the third brought on the metallic tang of blood and something else. Something sour, and dirty.

“...and yes, you do look like a goat, Amby-lamb,” she finished, before noticing the expression on Raeleq’s face. “What is it?”

He exhaled sharply and looked into the darkness, straining to see anything between the increasingly thick mushroom patches.. “There’s a predator nearby. Maybe more than one.”

Jessica stood straight up. “There are? Where?”

Ambrose looked like he wanted to argue, but instead summoned his Signature Creature. Or creatures. It didn’t feel fair that the shadow wizard got two, but they were still only imps, and two of them still didn’t equal one [Reggie].

“What are you doing?” Raeva asked. “Do you really want to risk losing them in a fight if they get taken out?”

“Oh no,” said Ambrose, “where ever will I get more imps?”

They finished summoning, and immediately took to the air. At first they struggled, and then had to pump their wings twice as hard as they should have, but the creatures flew up the chasm, separating soon after.

“Can they see in the dark?” Luthor asked.

“Mostly. So far, there’s nothing that way.” Ambrose looked over his shoulder. “Might be following us. Maybe we should get moving again.”

No one had any objections to that.

Despite their fatigue, there was an unspoken agreement to pick up the pace. Ambrose stayed in the back near Raeleq, who was prepared to catch him again, if need be. Jessica no longer jumped so far ahead, and Raeva turned to face each new sound with increased paranoia. Luthor, of course, kept whatever worries he had to himself and kept pushing them forward.

The incline steepened, and the path they took veered off to the side, still going up. They followed it, and Raeleq at least grew wary at the increasing number of mushrooms. They lit up a trail winding around the slop, glowing ever brighter at the top.

“Cut out the lights,” Raeleq said suddenly, in a harsh whisper that came out with a slight echo.

They turned to look at him, but Luthor nodded and his golden light disappeared. Raeva dismissed her flame a second later, stranding them there in a dark patch between fungal growths.

“Why are we…” Ambrose started to say, but paused. “Good call. My imps are close by, and they see a fire a bit further up. How the hell did you know?”

Raeleq shrugged. “The wind’s changing, getting faster and cooler, so I could smell it. And there’s more mushrooms. I think we’re close to the top of the chasm. And our prey is up there as well.”

The reason for his whisper sank in. Raeva nodded and rolled her shoulders, readying for a fight immediately. Jessica looked sharp, and Luthor nodded.

“Okay,” he said. “So we kill the chatter, and split up into two groups to flank them if we can. Jessica and Raeva with me going this way, Ambrose and Raeleq over there.”

Ambrose nodded. “You’re the anvil, we’re the hammer. And one imp is in position, nearby. There’s only two of them. This might even be overkill.”

“You complaining?” Raeva said.

“No, just happy to have an easy task for a change.”

Luthor sighed. “Alright, let’s move. Be careful in the dark.”

At this point, Raeleq liked to think even their eyes adjusted. They still couldn’t see particularly far, but now the fungal glow showed stone spires and boulders, and a few steep but clear paths leading up to the top, where there was indeed a flickering light and the distant smell of smoke.

Luthor’s group went first, stepping lightly to the right while Ambrose awkwardly tried to find a good way up and Raeleq patiently let him set the pace. At a few points, he was tempted to laugh, but didn’t have it in him to be cruel when things were serious, and the man was in uncomfortable dancing shoes.

It took longer than it should have, and at several points Raeleq worried about the sounds of rocks and dirt shifting from clumsy footsteps alerting the people above. But between the light moan of the wind and a rumbling in the distance, they were probably fine.

“They’re there,” Ambrose whispered, right before they reached the top. “Two of them. About a hundred yards off. Their ship crashed and is in two big pieces, and they’re arguing with one another. I don’t think we need to worry about a fight.”

That was just fine to Raeleq. If they could solve this without further bloodshed, great. He nodded, and they went up over the lip, where the ground leveled out considerably. The top of a chasm ended in a smaller cavern, with the stone tips of stalactites looming overhead. There was  a clear break where the Wanderer had broken through on their way down.

Ambrose breathed a sigh of relief and took a moment to stretch and loosen up his ankles. Raeleq wiped some sweat from his brow, grateful for the constant flow of air coming from the far end of the cavern. That previous smell of blood and sweat was nowhere to be found.

After a few seconds, they got moving again, creeping closer and closer until the two came into clearer view, bathed in the light of a small campfire. Their ship had broken almost in half, save for a strip of metal turning it into a sideways L, cradling and sheltering the two survivors.

One was human, and the other an odd, petite thing. They looked like a cross between a human, a dragon, and a goat. Green, speckled scales, sharp slitted eyes that darted around constantly, a slightly elongated mouth, not quite enough to be a snout. Twin horns sprouted from their forehead, curling back at the top. Raeleq was immediately fascinated.

“They’ve basically trapped themselves,” Ambrose whispered. “We could just rush them and they’d have nowhere to go. How’s [Reggie] feeling? Hungry at all? Ready to stomp and smash?”

Raeleq shook his head with a chuckle. “No need. Let’s just approach them. If they start a fight, we put them down, but we give them a chance.”

Ambrose made a face. “You’re no fun. Well, Luthor and the others are in place. So, on three. One, two…three!”

In just three strides Raeleq took the lead. He didn’t roar, yell, or do anything threatening other than show up a few seconds ahead of Luthor. No one had their weapons out yet, but on their approach, the human survivor stood up and produced a knife and held it out.

“Stay back!” he said, voice cracking. “We’ll fight to the end!”

The lizard-person spoke, in an odd, nasally purr, “No we won’t. I surrender, I surrender!” She, at least Raeleq assumed it was based on the voice, threw herself to her knees, hands on the ground.

The young man with the knife, he realized, was just a teenager, younger than all of them. And he was scared shitless.

Luthor stepped forward, unafraid of the knife shaking violently in the air. “Put it down, kid. Are you two hurt at all? Do you need water?”

“What?” the boy asked.

“What?” Ambrose echoed, staring incredulously.
