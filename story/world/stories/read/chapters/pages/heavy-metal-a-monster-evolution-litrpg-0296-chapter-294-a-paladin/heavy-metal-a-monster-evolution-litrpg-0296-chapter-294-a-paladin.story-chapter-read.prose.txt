“I knew this was a mistake.”

Jakub shouted as he and the other mercenaries were attacked once more by the reanimated corpses of the orcs that had once lived in these mine tunnels. Even though their bodies had been hacked to pieces, the dark necrotic energy binding them refused to dissipate. Twisted masses of rotting limbs, mismatched heads, and severed torsos knitted themselves back together with a sickening squelch.

The mismatched monsters attacked again, swinging their limbs wildly and making their attack patterns difficult to predict. Even when one of the creatures was brought down, the mana permeating the area quickly filled its remains with energy, knitting the pieces back together.

The source of that power was at the end of the cavern. A hooded figure stood there, its face obscured by shadow. There was, however, one feature that made its nature obvious. A bony hand.

“It’s either a Lich or a Necromancer, Rusty.”

Alexander commented as they looked toward the mysterious figure. Rusty activated his mana sense and immediately felt a resemblance to the necromancer he had encountered during one of his summoning missions. However, the energy surrounding this figure was somewhat stronger, meaning that whatever this monster was, it was likely more powerful than his temporary summoner.

“If there weren’t so many people here, I could probably dispel these necrotic energies using my light.”

Rusty commented as he sliced apart an undead orc with his flame sword. If he wanted to, he could use Elemental Discharge and release his light in all directions. It would be highly effective against the undead monsters.

However, doing so would force him to explain himself to the humanoids around him, and he was not sure that would be a good idea.

His boss, Galiena, was one thing. She would probably be astonished and assume he had used some kind of one-time holy item. The real problem was people like Jakub and his men. Holy items of that magnitude were not easy to obtain, and they were certainly not something a D-rank mercenary or adventurer should have access to.

Both of his guides had warned him that displaying that kind of power in the open could have serious consequences. The people of the church in this kingdom were apparently very zealous when it came to non-ordained practitioners of holy light, and the last thing Rusty needed was a Grand Inquisitor prying into his origins.

Instead of relying on a sweeping holy blast, Rusty opted for a far more direct solution. He turned his full attention toward the hooded figure standing on the elevated ledge at the back of the cavern. As long as that spellcaster remained untouched, the horde of flesh and bone would simply keep rising, no matter how many times Galiena severed their rotten limbs.

“Hold the line!”

Galiena shouted, her twin axes swinging in wide arcs as she cleaved through a pair of stitched-together monstrosities.

“They just keep coming. If this continues…”

She panted as her stamina reserves began to run low.

For Rusty, stamina was not an issue. He was a magical suit of armor, after all. The humanoids, however, would eventually grow exhausted, and once that happened, they would likely succumb to the undead monsters.

To make matters worse, the undead energy was beginning to pour into the corridor they had come through. That meant retreating would not necessarily save them. If they tried to fall back, they would likely run straight into the undead they had already encountered.

“Boss, leave that one to me. Just clear the path, and I’ll take care of it.”

Without another choice, he pointed to the robed figure in the back. If the mage responsible for the necromancy was taken out, the magic animating these monstrosities would break.

“Ha, let’s do it, Second! Leave these guys to me!”

Galiena was, as always, very accommodating when it came to his plans. If it had been another mercenary leader, they might have taken offense at one of their subordinates taking center stage. Some might have been angry at him throwing around orders or trying to outshine them, but she didn’t seem to have that kind of ego. Her trust in him was immense, and he wasn’t sure where it all came from, especially since he was still deceiving her.

“I’ll leave it to you. Once that thing is dead, the other undead should collapse, so just hold them off for now.”

He shouted and charged forward right after Galiena shoved multiple undead orcs out of the way. Gleam finally utilized her magic as well, sending blasts of magical arrows in various directions. While she could also utilize healing and light magic, it was best to keep her full capabilities hidden. Monsters capable of using holy magic were rare, after all.

Rusty lunged past the wall of rotting flesh that Galiena had breached, his metal boots thundering against the damp stone floor as he charged forward. Several monsters rushed to attack him, but before they could reach him, chains erupted from the ground, tripping some and holding others in place. They were restrained so quickly that the mercenaries barely noticed what had happened.

‘Good thing I used that training chamber.’

He was using his Penumbra Chains skill, which had become easier to control after his training and his recent evolution. With their help, combined with the undead creatures’ low intelligence, he was finally able to approach the corridor where the leader of this small undead horde was hiding.

Instantly, a green bolt of energy shot toward him. Rusty was a being susceptible to magic. His body was highly resistant to physical damage, but when it came to mana, the situation was reversed. Yet instead of dodging, he simply raised his shield and allowed the green bolt of energy to collide with it.

“...That tickles.”

He mumbled the words as he continued sprinting forward. His enemy looked confused as greenish energy spread across Rusty’s body from head to toe, yet nothing happened. The answer was simple. Rusty was living armor, a creature born from magic. His body could not be corroded by undead energy, and his soul was protected by the Soul Forge in some strange way. Together, these traits rendered him immune to this kind of energy.

“Hey, wait up! Where are you going?”

His enemy raised one hand, clutching a strange bony staff with a skull mounted at the top. Green gems glowed within its eye sockets. After waving the staff around, a wall of bones erupted from the ground, blocking the path into the tunnel he had quickly retreated into.

Rusty immediately began hacking through it while realizing something important. This being wasn’t acting like a mindless monster.

Liches were said to be intelligent, and even lesser liches were C-Rank monsters. Rusty had been hoping that his resistance to necromantic energy would give him an easy victory. But if this being was capable of retreating and reacting to the situation, then it might not be just another monster.

The wall of calcified matter and interlocked spines wasn’t particularly sturdy. With the help of his flame sword and a few repeated slices, it came crashing down. To crack it open further, he delivered a ferocious kick, sending bones spraying in all directions.

Soon, the chase continued as he surged into the tunnel after the hooded figure. Inside, he encountered a few more undead orcs, but before they could attack him, bolts of concentrated mana flew toward them.

Rusty did the same as his whole body pulsed with radiant energy. Even though the output wasn’t focused, it was enough to disrupt the regenerative process of the undead orcs blocking his path.

The sickening greenish glow permeating the area faded almost instantly as the tunnel was bathed in light. With every step Rusty took, soft holy light washed over the narrow corridor, cleansing the ambient corruption and causing the nearby corpses to crumble into various body parts.

Gleam remained behind to keep any potential undead from sneaking in from the rear. Soon, his enemy came into view.

“Not a great runner…”

Mage types usually had limited physical capabilities, and it was no different here. Liches and necromancers relied on their minions as their primary source of defense, offense, and survivability. Once stripped of their minions, they became weaker versions of regular mages with considerably fewer offensive capabilities.

At the end of the winding passage, the tunnel expanded into a hidden alcove lined with ancient ritual altars and glowing dark runes. The hooded figure spun around, revealing a humanoid face with sunken eyes.

“Impossible!”

This was not a lich, nor was this person an orc. Instead, he looked like an average pale and sleep-deprived human.

“They never told me that they would have a Paladin among them! They tricked me!”

The necromancer immediately began shouting, but he was struggling to properly summon his powers. The light radiating from Rusty’s body was interrupting the spell gathering process, rendering the man’s magic almost useless.

“What is he babbling about? Who tricked him?”

Something was off about the situation, and Rusty quickly realized that this could potentially be some kind of trap.

“You’re right, Rusty. Why would a necromancer be here? And he seemed to have been expecting our group.”

Alexander commented, and Rusty nodded. He pointed his flaming sword toward the human, who had moved behind one of the glowing altars.

“State your name. Who sent you?”

“Hah, a dead man doesn’t need to know! Now die!”

The necromancer took out a strange effigy from his pocket and broke it in front of the altar. The moment he did, the ground began to rumble. The tunnel right behind him slammed shut, while at the same time, a trapdoor opened in the middle of the large chamber.

“It doesn’t matter. Once I kill this Paladin, the rest will be easy pickings. I’ll just get them to pay me more.”

The stone floor split open with a loud screech, revealing a deep pit beneath the altar. Yellowish smoke surged upward, carrying the foul stench of decay. From within the fog, a massive clawed hand erupted, and soon after, the monster lurking below revealed itself.

It was a hulking undead construct cobbled together from the bodies of various monsters, not just orcs. Multiple heads were attached to its grotesque frame. Some were orcish, while others belonged to humans and even goblins. Its belly was tremendous, swollen with poisonous pus.

“Destroy him, my undead creation, and then take care of those mercenaries!”

The necromancer shouted his order, already certain of his victory. His words quickly turned into laughter, causing him to miss the moment when Rusty’s flaming sword vanished and was replaced by a brand new halberd made from twilight metal.

“It’s just a bigger undead monster. Not a problem!”

Rusty shouted as he gathered his energy within the halberd’s blade. It began to shine with a radiant light, far purer and more potent than anything wielded by a priest or cleric. The yellowish smoke that had permeated the chamber was instantly purified before him, and the massive creature began to sizzle.

“GRAGH!”

A creature like this was not supposed to feel fear. Yet, the moment it saw the light gathering within Rusty’s weapon, something deep inside it knew that it was already doomed.

Before the fight could even begin, Rusty swung his blade downward in a powerful cleaving motion. A burst of concentrated light magic erupted from the halberd and flew toward the massive monster, which was completely unprepared and incapable of dodging.

“W-what?”

The necromancer shouted as the radiance spread throughout the entire chamber. The energy washed over everything in its path. The pulsating magical symbols carved into the walls instantly cracked, and the evil energies sustaining them were completely eradicated.

Then there was the undead beast, which had taken the full brunt of the attack. Its screams quickly faded as the magic holding its various body parts together was severed within seconds. The massive construct disintegrated, collapsing into harmless piles of ash and dried bone.

The blinding light finally subsided, leaving behind only softly glowing traces of residual mana and the pale, trembling figure of the necromancer. He fell back against the stone altar, staring at Rusty with wide, horrified eyes.

“What are you? Did they send a High Paladin after me? How did the Church know?!”

Rusty had been mistaken for a powerful paladin, and it was clear that he could use that misunderstanding to his advantage.

“...Who hired you, vile necromancer? Tell me, and I’ll make your death swift!”

He wasn’t sure how a true paladin would act, but he still wanted to extract some information from the man. His creature had been slain, and the undead magic powering the mine was quickly dissipating.

“No, you don’t!”
