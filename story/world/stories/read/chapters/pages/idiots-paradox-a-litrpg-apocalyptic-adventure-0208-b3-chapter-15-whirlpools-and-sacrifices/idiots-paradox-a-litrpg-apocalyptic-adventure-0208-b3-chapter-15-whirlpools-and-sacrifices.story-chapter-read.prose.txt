“Is everyone in formation?” Logan said to Ernie as he surveyed his minion army. Ernie had them lined up in formations underwater so it would be easier for Logan to transport them using [Threshold Shift].

Funky looking octopi the size of teacups were bobbing together in a line, looking like something out of one of Sawyer’s cartoon shows. Next to them, Giant Pacific octopi rested tentacles against the one in front of them like they were in a warped line dance. Over to the side, at least a hundred undead sturgeons were using their ratty bone fins to swim in place, emitting gunk from their mouths like spoiled milk.

And in a mass of white, a school of undead fish, what had to be two hundred strong, swarmed together in one continuous mass. Although he’d teleported Ernie’s surviving octopi, including Vicious, back to the ocean, it hadn’t been anywhere close to 500 beings. This would be the first test to see if [Threshold Shift] would let him transport a massive amount of life at once.

Underwater, even though Logan had reformed his helmet and facemask, visibility was difficult since Ernie’s minions were so excited that they kept moving and kicking up sand on the bottom of the lake.

The Rattuspede was a particular problem; it kept twitching because it didn’t like that Tasha and Jack had hitched a ride. They were on top of it, riding it like a massive horse, digging their feet into the scars that had formed and healed when Ernie transformed it into the undead. Logan had fried the hell out of the thing back in New York, which meant a ton of scars, and a ton of handholds.

Logan eyed them and held back a worried frown. Logan had no idea what they were facing, which meant that Tasha and Jack could be fighting other undead humans who were out of their league. After all, it had been over two weeks since he’d last encountered his first human flower minion at the resort. Whereas the queen serpent had only recently turned Tasha and Jack into the undead, which had stalled their growth. And once Logan came along and reverted the infection, it took them time to grow accustomed to their human bodies once again. On top of the transformation into a Liche? There was a possibility they’d be outclassed.

But then again, Logan doubted the queen serpent had created many Liches, at least purposely. Ernie had only escaped with his own independence because of his bonded companion animal status. Logan was betting that the queen serpent wouldn’t want to share the lamplight with anyone else.

That’s why Logan wanted to conduct recon first.

“Can you ask Tasha and Jack if they want to do this?” Logan said to Ernie with his mental voice. “Tell them they could protect the community while we’re gone instead?” Although Ernie could communicate with his minions without speaking, Logan didn’t have the same ability underwater.

“Tsk tsk! They already said they wish to go! They want to get revenge and stop anyone else from becoming queen serpent victims!” Ernie popped out of his backpack and then flicked Logan’s head with a tentacle. “You are being a… what do they call it? A mother! An overprotective mother!” Ernie cackled. “No wonder Shoot calls you mother!”

Up on the Rattuspede, Tasha scrunched her eyebrows and opened her mouth, bubbles blowing to the surface. Then she tapped her wrist, as if tapping a non-existent watch. She was telling Logan to move his ass. Okay, okay. Logan sighed. In a way, Ernie had a point. Still, Logan would never forgive himself if Tasha and Jack had struggled, overcame a zombie infection, turned into Liches, only to end up dead because of Logan.

“Tell your minions to get ready. To stay as still as possible.”

Concentrating on the mass of life swirling around him, Logan used [Life Fabricator] to latch onto their auras. First Ernie, then the octopi, fish, sturgeons, Rattuspede, and lastly, Tasha and Jack.

Unlike normal beings, every lifeforce had a tinge of cold, like frost on a bright day, which helped him distinguish them from the normal fish that was darting around the minion swarm and gobbling up the sturgeon’s spoilt milk. Brave little buggers.

With the rest of his concentration, Logan fixed a point in his mind. Rattlesnake Island. With its jagged rock protruding out of the lake, to the pine trees that struggled to survive, to the shallow bottom. A shallow bottom wouldn’t work for their purposes though, so Logan altered his destination, aiming for the middle of the lake between Rattlesnake Island and the shore of Okanagan Lake.

Latching onto Ernie’s minions and keeping that picture in his mind, Logan deployed [Threshold Shift]. Using the skill always brought a cold sensation, as if he were walking into a walk-in blast chiller, but this time was different. The cold went down to his bones. His vision warped, and then, in one blink, they were gone.

Rather than a massive splash, they materialized underwater, so it was more of a displacement, a woosh of air. One after another, each minion materialized, their eyes wide, their mouths gaping.

Logan surged for the surface and then popped out his head, peering around. Far off in the distance, was the ragged edge of Rattlesnake Island, and to the other end was the shore. Perfect.

“Ernie, are we close?” he said out loud.

Ernie crawled out of the backpack and scooted on top of Logan’s helmet, balancing with his tentacles like a lumpy hat. Peering around, he adjusted his helm before staring at Rattlesnake Island. “This is the stinky snail place! We are close, but still a distance to swim.”

“And you can call your minions if we need them?”

Ernie leaned over Logan’s head, one of his tentacles covering Logan’s faceplate as he peered into the water and waved. “They will come!”

Logan brushed away his tentacle. “That’ll work. Leave them here. Tell them to be quiet, don’t move so they don’t attract attention. Tell Jack and Tasha to be safe. We’ll survey the queen serpent’s lair and then come up with a plan of attack.” After all, there was an enormous difference between an army that was a thousand strong versus five thousand. Ernie had done his best to estimate, but Logan knew he hadn’t been down below since he’d stolen the queen serpent’s last minions. A lot could happen in a few days.

“Are you ready?” said Logan. “Are your minions good?” Ernie made an affirmative noise. “Best get back into the pouch, all the way, so your helm doesn’t give us away.” As long as Ernie stayed inside the backpack and Logan had his camouflage effect active, they should be invisible… except for the hilt of the dagger, but it was dirty as hell, and at the mirky bottom, Logan was betting that it would go unnoticed.

Once Ernie was inside the backpack, Logan dropped underneath the surface, making sure that [Deepwater Explorer] was active. Although the smog was worse here without Logan’s carbon sucking trees to funnel away the smoke, there was enough sun to light up the water below. “Which way?”

“That way!” Ernie poked a tentacle out of the pouch and pointed in a straight direction heading south. “Proceed, noble steed.”

“Okay, hang on. You’ll be in for a ride.”

Logan had mastered using one of his socks to hurtle himself through the water like a submarine, but what would happen with both socks? He wasn’t sure how his flying ability would translate underwater.

Here goes nothing. Using the same method, Logan envisioned rockets on the ends of his feet, rockets packed with nothing but force. A force that wouldn’t just send him through the water like a submarine; it would turn him supersonic.

There was a built up of pressure in the water, and underneath his feet, a whirlpool formed.

And just like that, they were off.

***

“Wheeee! More zooming, Logan! More zooming!” Despite Ernie’s cry for more, Logan envisioned his feet stalling out and running out of fuel, and just like that, their rocket pace slowed until they came to a stop. Making sure [Deepwater Explorer] was still active, he let himself drop. Humans were naturally buoyant, but his metal suit weighted what had to be a hundred pounds, at least. All Logan needed to do was stop swimming and the weight of his armour would do the rest.

As they dropped what had to be at least three hundred feet, the light from the sun disappeared. Logan had no problem making out his surroundings though—that was the beauty of [Deepwater Explorer]. After a free fall of nothing but blue water, they were closing in on a sheer cliff covered in algae and milfoil—that damn underwater plant that looked like seaweed and spread like dandelions.

“Is this the place?” asked Logan.

Ernie popped out of the backpack and zoomed around the water, staring at the cliff face and then peering below, so deep that even with [Deepwater Explorer], Logan couldn’t make it out. There were no fish here, the water was still and calm. Almost too calm.

Ernie twirled in the water, tilting upside down as if he were doing a somersault before zooming back over to Logan. “It’s possible.” His voice sounded dubious.

“You aren’t sure?”

“I hung around the lair during my stealthy mission! But once I lured away enough of her minions…” Ernie’s black eyes were wide and bottomless. “It was a very upsetting time! A time of pain and meanness! Once I stole enough minions, I decided to… er… well, go on an adventure! Yes, an adventure.”

He'd decided to flee. Logan was starting to wonder if they should have brought one of the undead sturgeons.

As they continued to drop, Ernie zoomed over to another rock formation and ran a tentacle across its surface, paying attention to a deep score mark etched into the stone. It looked fresh, the algae torn from the surface, the marks showing raw rock that hadn’t been exposed to the elements for long.

With a twitch, Ernie surged back over to Logan. “This is it,” he whispered, his eyes cautious. “The lair is down below, deep in the down below.”

Logan’s mouth grew dry with anticipation, and his scalp prickled as he stilled, glancing around. But as far as he could see, it was nothing but water. And more water. “Good job, Ernie. Best get inside the backpack to hide your helm. We’ll keep the camouflage effect active and stay under the radar while we check it out.”

Ernie scooted into the backpack. There was a tenseness to him, a held back anticipation. For once, he wasn’t cracking jokes or chanting about slaughter. He knew this was serious. Just like Logan, Ernie remembered how they’d both barely escaped with their lives during their last encounter with the queen serpent.

Logan made his arms grow limp, his legs boneless, letting the weight of his armour drag him down. It was dark as hell down here, dark to the point that they lost all light. “How did you see anything down here? Were they all stumbling around in the dark?” Even though they were communicating mentally, Logan still lowered his voice into a whisper. If there was no light, he could see a problem, logistically. How could you fight what you couldn’t see?

“She has an underwater foggy canopy of light! Magic, Logan, magic.”

Huh. And yet as they continued to drop, there was no light. The water around them decreased in temperature, making his metal armour feel cold against the skin not covered by his clothes. The hair on the back of his neck rose and he broke out into a cold sweat. It was dark. Quiet. Devoid of life. Unnatural.

As Logan’s boots touched the bottom of the lake floor, he tried to be as soundless as possible. But still, he had yet to hear or see anything. It didn’t make sense. Deploying [Life Fabricator], Logan scanned the area, searching for signs of life.

Deadness.

Holy crap, the ricocheting effect of scanning for life and getting nothing back was beyond disconcerting. Even when there weren’t any monsters or people around, there was always something. Plants, bacteria, hell, even mold. And yet this whole area was a dead zone.

With a blink, Logan took out a flashlight from his spatial storage collar. He doubted that it was waterproof, but even if it wasn’t, it should give off just enough light to illuminate the area before the thing fried itself and ruined the wiring.

Switching it on, he shone the light on the ground. He was standing on flat ground in an area the size of a baseball field. It was bare stone, devoid of sand. There were scratches and gouges, as if something immense had clawed the stone. But other than that, there was nothing. No fish, no monsters.

The flashlight flickered, and Logan panned it wide over the area, illuminating yet more nothingness. With a last flicker, the flashlight died, and Logan willed it back inside of his spatial storage collar.

“Are you sure this is the right place?” Logan asked, his voice dubious.

“I’m certain. I recognize this rock formation from my nightmares, Logan.” Ernie sounded glum.

“Well, the queen serpent isn’t here now.”

It made no sense. What would cause the queen serpent to relocate? And why?

And the most worrying possibility of all, was it because she’d learned she was about to be under attack?

***

The queen serpent had struck him as someone who held a grudge, and she had a particular grudge against Logan, who had crushed her young under a rock. If anything, if she knew that he’d returned, wouldn’t she be eager to meet him in battle? It would give her a chance to get her revenge. Moving her army just because of Logan didn’t strike him as a possibility, which meant that something else must be at work.

According to the legends, this monster had been living in the Okanagan Lake for centuries, which meant she must know all the areas. She was smart, spreading her undead network to humans and insects. And unfortunately, because of her familiarity with the area, she had an advantage over both Ernie and Logan.

In a fjord lake that was almost a hundred miles long that reached a depth of 700 feet, she could be in a hundred different places. Logan didn’t have time to go up and down the lake bottom searching for her. That would be a waste of time, time that he could be using to grind monsters. He hadn’t forgotten that the Integration Tournament was ever looming.

And yet, he refused to give up. He’d promised Ernie slaughter, and he was going to get it.

So. What did he know? He knew that the lake monster was centuries old. That the locals touted myths about it creating whirlpools that sucked you down to the bottom of the lake. That centuries ago, you needed to sacrifice an animal to receive safe passage across the lake.

And that the monster lived underneath Squally Point.
