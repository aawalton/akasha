Klaid woke up to orange light on his ceiling and the immediate, bone-deep conviction that he had ruined something.

The apartment was too warm. His blanket had twisted around one leg during sleep, trapping him in a fabric-based crowd control effect. His mouth tasted like cheap instant coffee. Somewhere outside, a truck backed up with a steady beep that drilled through the wall and directly into his skull.

He reached for his phone.

Wednesday.

Wednesday.

He had lost a day, not the day.

Very normal reaction to a calendar.

His hands still had that post-dive tremor, the subtle buzzing in the tendons after too many hours in full immersion. His real body always acted offended when he spent an entire night letting a game puppet his nervous system.

The thought finished, and the second problem arrived.

Lyra.

He had known he had something today. There had been a slot in his head labeled important Korros errand, do not ignore, and then yesterday had taken that label, thrown it into a dungeon, stabbed it with a paintbrush, and made it fight a level forty page demon for legal custody of his class.

He checked his internal notes.

House Vane Commission. Lyra final check. Greywater Ward. Today.

Yesterday morning, he had woken up before Lady Vane’s commission. That felt like three weeks ago and also like fifteen minutes ago, which was one of the more annoying things about high-intensity gaming. The brain filed major events in emotional weight, and apparently “noble matriarch hires you to weaponize poetry for an art debut” and “accidentally becomes landlord of a raid” occupied the same shelf.

After Lady Vane, he had handled Korros business. Then the Songstone Ruins had turned into the Songstone Amphitheater because the System had looked at his day planner and chosen violence. From there, he had gone directly into the Shifting Galleries raid, which had ended sometime past the hour where human beings with any respect for their organs went to sleep.

He had logged out, collapsed, and slept until dinner.

Now he had an NPC artist to visit, a commission deadline, an entire city of art to navigate, and tomorrow he had to visit his mother.

Elara would kill him if he missed it. That part was simple. His sister had the kind of exhausted patience that could survive hospital bureaucracy, late rent, insurance forms, doctors who spoke in paragraphs, and Klaid’s emotional constipation. Missing the hospital visit would push her past human limits and into a rare-class execution state.

But it wasn’t only Elara.

He stared at his phone, thumb resting over her last message.

Elara: Tomorrow. 11:30. Please don’t forget.

Below it, from two hours ago:

Elara: Are you asleep?

He added one word.

Klaid: Just woke up. I’ll be there tomorrow. Promise.

That made his stomach twist for a reason he refused to inspect. He sent it before he could delete it.

The read receipt did not appear immediately. Good. Maybe Elara was eating, or at the hospital, or living one of the thousand tiny hours he had outsourced to her while he fought monsters for money.

Tomorrow he would go for Mom.

For Elara.

For himself, too.

Klaid tossed the phone onto the bed and stood.

Now that he had money, he could buy actual food. Fresh vegetables. Meat that came from a source more trustworthy than “discount sticker at 11 PM.” Maybe fruit. Rich people fruit, like berries that cost as much as an uncommon potion.

Instead, he microwaved the rice, fried two eggs, and ate standing over the counter because sudden financial stability had not installed a dining table.

The rice clumped. The eggs stuck to the pan and tore into yellow-white scraps. He added pickled radish and miso straight from the jar with a spoon, creating a meal that would make a nutritionist quietly close the tab.

Fuel.

He had a problem now. If he woke up at six in the evening, his sleep schedule had become a feral raccoon. Nothing new, really, but tomorrow’s hospital visit was late morning. That gave him two routes.

Route one: force himself to sleep early tonight despite having just slept for most of the day. Unlikely. He could not reliably fall asleep when told, unless absolutely tired. His brain treated bedtime like a negotiation with terrorists.

Route two: power through another long session, handle Lyra, handle the Shifting Galleries management, spend time investigating his rewards, basically - stay awake until the hospital visit.

He already knew which route he would pick.

He cleaned the pan because his mother’s voice lived somewhere in his spine and still punished him for leaving dishes in the sink. Then he sat at the desk, woke the monitors, and opened the Crown of Destiny forums.
