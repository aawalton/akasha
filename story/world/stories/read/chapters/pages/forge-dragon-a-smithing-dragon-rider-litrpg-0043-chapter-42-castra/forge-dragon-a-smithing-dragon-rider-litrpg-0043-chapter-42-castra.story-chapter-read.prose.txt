They departure from town was not as drawn out and heartfelt as Caleb had feared. Their village was on the only path deeper to the mountains, and—should they survive their mission—the whole army would be passing back through again shortly. Caleb’s father told his sons that he loved them and for them not to do anything stupid—looking directly into Grisim’s eyes as he said the latter part. He then gave his new daughter a hug and asked that she keep both of his boys safe.

Kelia’s father was still missing, though Kelia told Caleb not to worry. She suspected he was lying low due to the current level of anti-Frigidi sentiment. It was a wonder to all of them that Kelia hadn’t received any more disdain or disrespect than normal since the attack. When it had been revealed that she too had a rare class, it had been an uproar. Everyone was suddenly falling over themselves to congratulate her, though none dared apologize for any cruel childhood teasing they may have partaken in.

“Things will be a lot better here when we return,” Caleb said idly as they rode horses at the front of the procession.

they’d been given the mounts as part of their position. Caleb was a passable horseman but if battle broke out he’d quickly abandon the mount to fight on his own two feet. bog followed along, able to match the marching pace without issue.

“Do you want to come back?” Kelia asked. “To stay I mean. We can go anywhere when our contracts are up. Why come back here?”

Caleb hadn’t ever really thought leaving would be an option growing up. As a smith, he’d always have a trade, but not one that moved easily. But now, he’d see the whole empire and have his pick of locations to retire too—and the wealth to move there. He could send for his father, and even…

Search for mom.

He and Grisim had talked about it, but they’d both never really thought they’d have the chance. But now, suddenly, that seemed like a very real possibility.

“No,” Caleb said. “I’d miss Master Dimel, but I’m sure I could convince my dad to move elsewhere once we’ve retired. “And nothing we could do would convince Grisim to leave us alone.”

“True,” Kelia said.

At first, Kelia had been the third wheel to the brothers, but over time as Kelia and Caleb had grown closer, suddenly Grisim had become the third wheel. Despite that, the brother had always chosen to spend time with the pair even though he was rather popular with their age group and never at a loss for other options. They all teased each other, but they’d always been together and they didn’t foresee that changing—so long as Grisim survived meeting the Legate. As Equites—as Kelia had told Caleb—they had some say over where they were deployed, and it wouldn’t be hard to stay in the same legion as Grisim who—if he survived—would be permanently attached to the Thousand Blade Legion.

Grisim was off ahead of the procession, his training as a scout having begun. Kelia had offered to assist but she’d been ordered by Canus to stay.

“You are an Equites. You do not look for trouble. You wait for it to find you.”

They were told their training would begin once they reached the castra—the encampment the legion made each night no matter where they were unless it was in a permanent fortification. The legion had sent a forwarded guard ahead to set up this castra the day before when Kelia had filled the recruitment quota. They were in the supply train following behind the next day. This was outside of normal legion protocols but they were behind schedule due to the raids and Canus feared disappointing the Lagate.

The castra came into view just as the sun was setting over the mountains to the west, long after it had dipped behind the ring, turning the long twilight into night. If someone had told Caleb the fortification had been a permanent one, he’d have believed them.

The walls were 5 meters tall, made of large timbers each carved to a point. Behind the wall men stood watch on a platform constructed behind. A tower stood at each corner of the fort, 15 meters tall. The legion had dug a moat around the walls and a drawbridge lowered to greet them. Canus was the first to cross it with Kelia and Caleb right behind him.

Within neat row after row of tent was set up, all uniform save for larger ones evenly distributed among them.”

They followed silently taking it all in as people came and took their horses. Bog was encouraged to follow them but was hesitant to leave Caleb.

“It’s fine, I bet there will be some horseshoes you can steal.” Caleb advised.

“The legion knows how to accommodate a dragon,” Canus assured them. “Even a strange one like yours.”

Reassured that there would at least be treats if he was separated from Caleb, he agreed to follow the grooms with the horses.

“Fetch Mettius,” Canus commanded a messenger.

Canus led them to the largest tent filled with tables covered with papers and maps.

“Mettius will be your commander,” Canus explained. “He is a royal heir, so watch your tongue around him—and maybe make sure your brother never interacts with him at all.”

Kelia and Caleb both nodded at the comment, wincing at the thought of Grisim interacting with the son of the emperor.

“I should explain to you yokels since no one else will,” Canus said after a moment of consideration. “That’s not as impressive as it sounds. The emperor has something like 100 acknowledged heirs. Certainly more. It’s long been tradition of the empire for the heirs of the emperor to vie for the throne. Somehow the emperor is chosen from among them at the death of the current emperor and the rest fall in line.”

“Why is Mettius here?” Caleb asked.

“Here in the legion? Or hear in the furthest corner of the empire?” Canus asked but then continued on explaining. “He’s not one of the favored heirs.”

A knock came from the pole outside.

“I heard that,” a voice said.

A young golden-haired man of above average height. He wore loose fitting robes with the legion standard pauldrons and gauntlets on, but no other armor. The gauntlet flared to Caleb’s metal sense as the most magical object he’d ever seen. The pauldrons too were magical but they paled in comparison.

Ever since entering the camp Caleb had to suppress his metal sense lest all the metal around him overwhelm, but these gauntlets burst through even his lowered sensitivity.

“I’m and experiment,” Mettius said, fixing his robes after walking through the tent flap. “My father—may be reign forever—in his infinite wisdom thought it would be valuable if my royal person were given a non-combat class and deployed to the front lines.”
