"Good afternoon," Mister Blackthorn greeted the girls curtly. "Autumn has come, which means that all of you will have a chance to go through the summer survival test. Should you complete this successfully, I shall officially endorse you to travel outside the village during summer."

Callie could feel her palms getting sweaty. This was it! She had spent the whole summer diligently honing her survival skills, all so she could travel to town and learn more about magic in the library. She even practiced in her spare time together with Callen.

"To pass the test, you're going to have to navigate to a location marked on the map. You will have to find a safe area to build shelter, find a source of water, and avoid the danger that you might encounter on the way. Everything that we have been learning this summer," Mister Blackthorn explained.

The girls all nodded. Callie recalled toiling over the old map together with Callen, trying to understand what the weird lines meant and how to use them to navigate the forest.

She recalled how Mom helped her to learn how to find fresh water and the tracks of an animal or an arcane beast. Tracking lessons were by far her favorite. To think that there was a chance to find an arcane beast by following its tracks. It was like the whole new world had opened up for Callie.

Had there been time, Callie would have searched the whole forest for new arcane beast tracks. Unfortunately, there was simply no time. Learning survival skills took nearly all of her free time, and whatever she had left, she used to play with Wiggles and Snowglow.

"Each one of you will be tested at a different location during dusk. You must be able to apply everything you've learned, even in the dark," Mister Blackthorn leaned on his cane as he adjusted his bad leg. Callie could feel his gaze linger on her. "Remember that your main goal is to survive. If at any point you do anything that could endanger your life, you will fail."

There it was again. That harsh gaze. After the glauxis incident, he seemed even more irritable than before, often losing his temper when someone did something wrong. Especially if Callie did something wrong. It made Callie wonder if Mister Blackthorn had been the thief, and that was why he was so angry with her. Because she had ruined his plans.

But if that was really true, then there was no real evidence. It wasn't like Mister Blackthorn ever harmed Callie. Then again, she was never alone, so maybe he just hadn't had the chance.

"Thank you for providing us with the opportunity!" Aurelia said, looking unusually enthusiastic.

Each one of them wanted to pass. For older girls, it was like a rite of passage. It would prove that they were knowledgeable enough to travel by themselves. In a way, it was like becoming an adult for them.

"Thank you!" Callie also added, bowing alongside other girls.

For Callie, passing this test was a necessity. She had to travel to the town and learn something, anything about magic.

"Okay, we'll start with the youngest and work our way to the oldest. Callie, you're first!" Mister Blackthorn announced and looked at one of his older disciplines.

Callen was also among them, so Callie guessed that the older boys were here to watch over the test and make sure that nothing bad happened to the girls. Obviously, Callen wouldn't be the one to watch over Callie's test.

She didn't feel ready. She didn't want to go first, but there was no choice. With a reluctant nod, she stepped toward Mister Blackthorn.

The grizzled man motioned towards the supply tent. "Head inside and pick out everything you believe is necessary to pack with you."

This was easy. Packing the bag was the first lesson she had received, and since then, she had already learned exactly what was needed. The knife would be her weapon of choice since it could be useful for other things as well, and it took up little space. She also took flint and steel, a small bundle of dry bark, a waterskin, a wool cloak, twine, clean linen strips, millefollia leaves, and a torch.

With her bag packed, she emerged from the tent, after which Mister Blackthorn gave her a map. A small pin was attached to the location she needed to get to. It took a moment for her to understand where it was. It appeared to be somewhere beyond the flower meadow.

"The test begins now," the man said firmly.

Callie nodded. This meant that she couldn't just walk casually through the village. Instead, she would have to watch her every step for possible traps and "monster marks" that Mister Blackthorn and his apprentices must have prepared in advance.

At first, everything seemed easy. She avoided the obvious places she had to avoid, like paths with puddles. It indicated the closeness of the swamp and thus should be avoided. She found a small spring along the way and filled her waterskin with clean water. She avoided the large "animal" trails which could have been left by wolves, and instead traveled the narrow ones which were made by deer.

When she got into the forest, she got a small fire going to light her torch. It took a few tries, but since Callie had been smart enough to pack the flint and steel, not the bowdrill, it was something she could do.

The problem arose when Callie noticed unusual marks along the path. Not those of a wolf, but clearly larger and more cat-like. To amplify the effects, scratch marks had been carved into several trees. Not in an obvious location, but visible enough if one looked.

Callie was willing to bet that those were meant to be signs of a glauxis presence. She wasn't quite sure what to do. In the case of wolves, they had to turn back and return to the village. In the case of small arcane beasts, they had to choose a different path forward. But what about a large arcane beast?

Imagining the glauxis Callie had encountered before, she thought of what she would do should she encounter it again in the forest during her travels.

Choosing a different path should be enough. I doubt the glauxis would be interested in pursuing me. If I don't approach its nest, it will leave me alone.

With that, Callie took a different path and arrived at her destination, where she promptly made a shelter that would protect her from the rain and cold. Mister Blackthorn's apprentice had observed her every move silently, not commenting on anything, merely following after her.

Once the shelter was complete, they silently headed back to the village, where the apprentice talked with Mister Blackthorn for a good while. Callie was nervous the whole time. Did she make a mistake? Was the shelter good enough? She built it to the best of her abilities, but there was a limit to what her small hands could do.

After talking with the apprentice, Mister Blackthorn turned to Callie and announced. "You fail."

"What? Why?" Callie exclaimed in surprise. Was the shelter not good enough after all?

"You saw the marks of a monster, didn't you?" Mister Blackthorn said.

"Yes."

"Why didn't you turn back?"

"Because there was no need to. Those are meant to be glauxis marks, are they not? If I stay away from its nest, the glauxis will not harm me,"  Callie said without a moment of hesitation.

Mister Blackthorn's eyebrows furrowed. "Has the one positive encounter with a monster gone to your head? Just because you were lucky enough to find the monster's egg doesn't mean it is friendly. Had you not had what it wanted, you would be dead. When you see predator tracks, you always turn back!"

Callie shrank back. With how vicious Mister Blackthorn's eyes looked, she didn't dare to argue back.

"C-Can I take the test again? I need to pass it this autumn," she pleaded.

"No. You had your chance. You'll have to wait until spring!" The man said coldly.

"Wh-What? But that's not—"

Callie didn't manage to finish the sentence. If looks could kill, Callie would be dead on the ground. She had no choice but to accept his words, despite the frustration she felt bubbling inside her.

The walk back home was difficult. She was struggling to hold back both her tears and the magic that seemed to be roiling within her. Why was it so difficult to control? Why was Mister Blackthorn so unfair? She had to go to the town this autumn, otherwise… her magic would go completely out of control.

The only thing that kept Callie in control was the leather vambraces her Mom and Dad had made for her. The thick leather not only protected her hands but also hid the glow of her magic mark. With how upset Callie felt, she had no doubt that her mark was currently glowing.

What am I supposed to tell Mom now?

When Mom inevitably asked whether Callie had passed, she could barely force her voice out. "I-I didn't…"

"Did Mister Blackthorn explain what you did wrong?" Dad asked.

"It was about the glauxis tracks. I saw them, but I thought that if I chose a different path, I could safely continue. But apparently that was wrong!" Callie said, feeling her magic welling up again within her. Like a fire burning within her—it was almost painful. She wanted to release the energy… somewhere.

"Callie, Mister Blackthorn was right. If you see tracks of a large mons–, I mean arcane beast, you must turn back," Mom said.

"But I…" Callie wanted to protest, but Mom shook her head.

"I understand why you did it. But in the wild, you must always remain cautious. You can't expect every arcane beast to be understanding, even if you can talk to them," Mom insisted. "Perhaps, you could take the test again?"

"He said no…"

Dad frowned, looking at Mom. "Holding her at home until spring, when she has learned everything else, seems… counterproductive. We need to understand what is happening to Callie."

Mom nodded. "I agree. I'll talk to Chief Aemon. Perhaps we can acquire his permission for one time, and then you can retake the test in the spring to acquire full rights to travel to town."

"Would that be possible?" Callie looked at Mom, feeling her magic calming down.

"I believe that the chief wants you to be able to control your magic more than anyone else. Show me your hand," Mom urged Callie closer.

She obeyed, and Mom gently untied her right vambrace. Removing it, they all saw that the mark was glowing gently.

"It's glowing again…" Dad noted bitterly.

"I… I'm sorry. I was just feeling so… so angry!" Callie said. Vambraces had made it easier to hide her magic, but since her third petal had appeared, it just seemed all over the place. She hadn't been able to make heads or tails of it, her magic often acting exactly like she didn't want it to.

"I'll go with you to talk to the chief, Maeve. We must get this under control," Dad said firmly.

If both her parents talked to the chief, he would surely approve this one trip to the town. But Mister Blackthorn's words did ring in her ears.

"Had you not had what it wanted, you would be dead."

Was that really the case? Callie wanted to reject those words. The glauxis had left peacefully in the end. But if she didn't have the egg? Would she have been able to calm down the glauxis?
