The gentle breath of wind faded as the view resolved into focus. Risens tensed, the hairs on the back of his neck standing erect at the unexpected scene. The portal had deposited him into an area unlike any he’d experienced thus far. Most of the trial chambers had been hewn of the same dark stone, matching that of the Roost’s main hall. His cautious gaze now scrawled over a vast cityscape—one that was eerily similar to Windwake.

A weathered, cobbled street stretched into the distance before terminating at a wide, nondescript structure. The facades of buildings, shops, and residences were mainly adorned with a layer of decorative stone, while others sported wood distressed by the elements.

Along the length of the avenue, several alleys exited off either side, hiding their details under the cover of shadows. The familiarity of the scene toyed with his mind. He racked his brain to reconcile where he’d seen this location before, but failed with each attempt. The air was ripe with the expected aromas of a city, though the lack of the ever-present tang from the brine of the Sea Solace was telling.

A solitary figure stood ahead. Clothed in utterly unremarkable utilitarian clothing, it made no sound. Its back turned to him, hiding its face.

All seemed normal with the exception of the sound and light. Even in the depths of the night, Windwake was abuzz with noise. Whether it be the activity of animals or man, or merely the whisper of the wind through the streets, not an hour passed that the city seemed truly asleep. Here, the silence was pronounced. The area was illuminated as if it were midday,even though Risens was sure it all came from the lanterns that burned on posts. The sky above was unnaturally dark and entirely featureless. It was as if someone had painted it with monochromatic, matte charcoal, a color he was entirely unaccustomed to seeing in the firmament above.

The shrill cry sounded and sent a shiver down his spine. The bird’s call was accompanied by rapid wing flapping. Over the last days, he’d come to find an odd comfort in that sound. Amongst the sounds in Windwake, the raven’s echoing caw was likely the most common, but it was only now that he’d found himself so utterly tied to the bird that he truly noticed it. What started as nothing more than the shadow against the eerie, hueless sky grew as it descended. It ignored his proffered arm, instead alighting on the corner of the tallest building that stretched several stories above the cobblestones.

Instructions were never provided regarding the required tasks in the trials. In this situation, however, the bird’s intent was clear. The raven’s focus, watching from high above, was singular. Its target stood in the center of the avenue.

Even still, the figure remained motionless, seemingly oblivious to the attention.

Risens’s hand remained close to his blades as he stalked down the empty avenue. The muffled crunch of the grains of dirt below his feet did nothing to alert the being. It remained frozen like a statue, seemingly waiting for him.

Risens closed the gap. A matter of feet stood between them. His heart rate increased in preparation.

Without warning, his target twisted itself around, wheeling about to face him. For a moment, it greeted him with an expressionless face before vanishing into nothingness.

Risens reached for the Raven Talons, his frustration mounting as the blades refused to pull from their sheaths. Only mocking laughter greeted his repeated attempts. Shifting to his steel blades, he fell into a defensive stance. Motion and sound swelled from all sides. The formerly desolate avenue now teemed with life as dozens of nearly people scuttled along as if going about their daily business. But they too appeared to have no faces or discernible details. They materialized from the buildings, the shadows of the alleys and the streets, blossoming to life with the natural sounds entirely lacking in the area. A pair of laughing children weaved around him, disappearing around the edge of the building, though the fading sounds of their passing remained. Bird calls filled the air, competing with the drone of conversation and the steady percussive beat of a hammer against steel somewhere in the distance. The audible stimulation matching his lived expectations of daily life and grind wound through the city.

Risens slipped out of the middle of the street, hovering close to the building, letting the figures move about, oblivious to his existence. Each was clothed in the expected gear of the city’s civilians. None bore the trappings of soldiers, though he could see a few blades hanging from hips. This alone was neither unexpected nor unfamiliar. Posture was key to his observations. He was sure that none carried the weapons as more than decorations to the costumes they currently wore.

Frustration mounted at the uncertainty of his purpose here. With blades in hand, he weaved his way through the passing crowd. His eyes scanned every passing citizen for any sign of warning, concern, or nervousness—for anything that might separate them from the rest.

As he progressed, the charcoal-colored sky brightened. At first, it was only slight, nearly imperceptible changes. As the firmament above lightened, the faceless townspeople increased the pace of their movements. They rushed through their tasks, not as if a mere deadline loomed, but as if their failure to complete said duties was akin to life or death.

Then, one by one, each citizen disappeared, exiting through one of the alleys or avenues or by slipping into the doorways. They moved like specters as they hit the facades of the structures, sliding through the wooden panels rather than opening them as one would expect.

With the disappearance of the final person, the raven that had watched silently from its perch gave off a final call. There was a noticeable agitation to its tone as it flapped skyward. Every beat of its wings sounded like a thunderclap. The sound hammered through him, each percussive thud registering in the ground underfoot, and the ground heaved and buckled.

Forcing himself to a lower stance to maintain his balance, Risens stumbled to the center of the cobbled avenue. Small chunks of mortar were the first signs of the crumbling facades. Like the first few drops of a coming rainstorm, they clattered to the stone, giving a preview of the deluge that was to follow. The buildings themselves followed close at the heels of their parts as they crashed around him, spitting out showers of sharp stone fragments. Moving as rapidly as the undulating ground would allow, he hastened toward the black void of the portal as shards of stone peppered his body from all sides, sending lances of pain where they struck. Dodging a veritable boulder, he lunged forward, diving for the safety of the portal. A cloud of gray dust and stone accompanied him as he returned to the main hall of the Roost.

His exit from the trial was as hasty as it was frustrating. The painful sting of skidding across the dark stones of the hallowed space was becoming a familiarity he desired to be rid of. He cursed himself for the continued failure, though he understood that much of his continued annoyance was a direct result of the complete lack of instruction for the ever-changing tasks and rules of the trials.

The pointed stares of the ravens did nothing to lighten his foul mood. He had never completed a task on his first attempt. Why then did the ravens act as if he should have?

Why, however, had he failed this time?

The mysterious figure disappeared before the rest had arrived was the key. Of that there could be no doubt. The purpose of the ever-brightening sky was clear. It was a warning that he, unlike the others hastening through the scene, had failed to understand. He had been right in his jest: those who toiled with haste did so at the very risk of their lives. Had he seen that the sky was the timer, signaling impending destruction, he too would have quickened his step.

But it mattered not, as Risen had still yet to discover what task needed to be completed. At least having an understanding of the time limit would allow him to position himself closer to the exit if failure greeted his next attempt.

“Wish me luck,” he grumbled to his stone audience before turning his attention back to the waiting doorway.

Doubt crossed his mind, finding its home in the remnants of the taunting cackles of the Ravens Talons. They would be affluent in their beliefs that he would fail yet again, that he would feel the sting of defeat and the mounting mental toll of his continued inadequacies. Brushing off the dust that had settled over his clothing, his hands accidentally brushed the hilts of the Talons.

Their laughter affirmed his assumptions, doing nothing to lessen the quietly roiling internal anguish.

There was a connection inside the puzzle that he was missing, one that clearly stemmed from the mysterious solitary figure that had waited in the center of the avenue. The raven had a purpose, too. It had watched the man, not with mere curiosity, but with purpose and intent.

In that moment, the answer seemed clear. It was the raven that would guide him to the solution. It was the raven that would show him the way. With the answer—or at least the beginnings of a plan—solidified in his mind, Risens strode back through the inky void into the chamber beyond as if the destruction that had torn it to pieces was merely a bad memory.
