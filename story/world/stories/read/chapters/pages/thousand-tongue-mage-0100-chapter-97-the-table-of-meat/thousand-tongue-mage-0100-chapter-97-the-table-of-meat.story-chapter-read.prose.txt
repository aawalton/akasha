Four weeks passed—three days before the full-frontal assault—and a hundred thousand soldiers from the east marched to the Salaqa Region right above the Divine Capital, where the Reunification Army was based, to join up with the west and central’s fifty thousand soldiers.

It’d been a while since Zora was in the Salaqa Manor, but in all honesty, it wasn’t so much a manor as it was a war headquarters now. Pretty banners had been stripped for signal clothes, the doors had been widened to admit cartloads of earthworks, the gardens had been ploughed into drill squares, and the fountains had been shut to conserve water for the field kitchens. Where servants once whispered about dinner courses, orderlies now whispered about casualty estimates, and where guards once patrolled lightly around the gardens outside, soldiers in full armor patrolled instead.

Tonight, Zora stood once against the end of the long rectangular table in the dining hall, which had been turned into the military adjournment hall.

Beside him were Enki, Kita, Machi, Ifas, and Sora. Across the hall, along both walls and flanking the great table, stood the fifty-one Outer Regional Lords—or their chosen heirs and generals—arrayed by household and ant lineage. The Carpenter Ant Class Households were clustered together, and so were the fire ants, the mortar ants, the silver ants, so on and so forth. Even still, the Salaqa Lord alone represented the Army Ant Class Household at the far opposite end of the table, because the Salaqas were the only Army Ant Class Household in the entire empire.

He briefly wondered why that was, but he supposed that didn’t matter now.

“... It has been a long time since all fifty-one Outer Regional Lords have been gathered in the same hall,” Lord Baya said, pressing both hands against the table. “These lands were once chaotic and fractured, embroiled in eternal war, but when the Swarm arrived sixty-three years ago, the fifty-six Regional Lords of the southern end of the continent bound themselves together and founded the Attini Empire. We kept the Swarm from our fields and our children. Regions Fifty-Two through Fifty-Six became the Divine Capital, while the sole Regional Lord of the Fifty-Sixth Region became the Divine Empress, and in her manor—made Divine Temple—our laws found height and refuge. Since then, we have never gathered again.”

He paused. Reverence made a fine dust on breath.

“It has been sixty-three years since we worked together to drive out a bug from our lands. I thank you all for coming.”

The murmurs that rose were low and weathered: blessings in fifty-one dialects, a fire ant oath bright as tinder, a mortar ant pledge that fell like gravel into place. Somewhere near Zora’s right, a young lord’s sword tip touched the floor in a controlled rasp—a vow of loyalty set point-first in the day’s plank.

Zora only sighed.

“Spare everyone the history,” he said mildly, and every voice folded back into silence as the Outer Regional Lords looked at him facing Lord Baya. “There is a table for ancestral tales and a table for meat. This one is set for meat. Let us eat strategy.”

Lord Baya smiled faintly. “I see you are as impatient for tact and etiquette as ever.”

He simply shrugged and thumped his staff once. “Assemble the battlefield.”

Wood answered. From boxes and trays and a dozen careful sacks across the long table, carved pieces rose: miniature walls, infantry blocks, siege coils, engineer regiments, and banners. Fifty-one banners, to be exact, all carried by the wind to their positions that’d be in three days. At Baya’s end of the table, the miniature walls of the Divine Capital rose with northern, northwestern, and northeastern bulwarks rendered in heavy timber. At Zora’s end of the table massed a field of one hundred and fifty infantry blocks in scattered formation, each the measure of a thousand men.

“Play out the battle,” he said.

The wooden army immediately slid forward, unfurled into its opening shapes, and the Outer Regional Lords watched them with bated breaths. The eastern and western armies moved. Tunnellers began their shallow cuts across the fields, and the Divine Capital’s artillery blocks took out chunk after chunk of the engaging armies. The central armies carried with them little ladders and mushrooms, aiming to either break through or climb the central walls—but however one looked at the battle being played out, one thing was certain.

“The longer the battle goes on, the more people die,” he said plainly. “This may be a civil war, but it must not end in the total annihilation of one side. After all, the Divine Empress and Her Four Households are being manipulated by Decima, which means the Spore Knights are being manipulated in turn, which means of the hundred and fifty thousand soldiers in the Divine Capital, at least a hundred and twenty thousand of them could be unwilling soldiers forced to fight.

He angled his head toward the eastern lords. “The proverb of the Chulach Granite Coast says ‘when the tide is foul, do not blame the fish’. Remember the fist,” he said sternly. “Humanity’s enemy is the Swarm, not your countrymen. Fight with pride. Fight with love for your empire. Do not water hatred like a potted herb.”

A rustle of assent moved the line of the Mortar Ant Class Households. The fire ants held a sharper stillness. Kita, on Zora’s left, had her hands laced before her, knuckles pressed white.

“With that said,” Zora continued, and his staff tapped once to urge the pieces to move again, “this is how the battle will most likely go.”

The wooden infantry blocks surged again. The Reunification Army pressed at the northern gates and their northwestern and northeastern sisters, and the Carpenter Ant Class Households began propagating mushrooms along the wall face, growing and pushing soldiers straight up the walls.

“This full-frontal approach will not fare well at all,” he said. “It will be a wall teaching lesson. Casualties will mount the closer the Reunification Army gets to the wall, so the small elite team must act quickly.”

With his staff, he manually pushed a small wooden piece off from the side of the main force, and he flicked it over the wall before pushing it forward again and again, straight to the very end of the table where the Divine Temple was.

“In the shadow of the main assault,” he said, “a small team of no more than fifty men will infiltrate the Divine Temple, slay Decima, and lift her deceit from the Divine Capital. You of the Outer Regions know her true identity, but I wager information has been tightly controlled within the Divine Capital, and most of the people behind the walls have no idea she is the Divine Attendant. Our goal is not to slay the Divine Empress. We will simply slay Decima, remove her as the head of strategy, and force the Divine Empress to give the order to stand down.”

He paused and listened to the room carry that bell. It moved through plate and strap and teeth, out into the hall, into banner, and it came back subdued. The Outer Regional Lords may not be happy with the current Divine Empress—not after a whole decade of humiliation, whether it be by Decima’s influence or not—but they respected tradition, and tradition said there must always be a Divine Empress on the throne.

In these tumultuous times, they couldn’t afford even one week without someone carrying empress blood on the throne. The Divine Empress was the leader of the Attini Empire, and without her authority to move the hearts and blades of men, the entire front would collapse in the face of the ever-growing Swarm.

“Once she has given that order, the rest of the Reunification Army will march in to seize control and stabilize the Divine Capital,” he finished. “It will be messy, and it will be very chaotic afterwards. Civil wars are like broken cups. Even when the tea is saved, someone must still sweep the floor… but that is neither my concern nor Enki’s. We are merely bug-slayers. You are the lords of this empire.”

Silence settled over the hall as the wooden pieces finally stilled. The carved armies had halted in their miniature shoves, and the waxed maps lay dry and barren—until Lord Baya inclined his weight forward, nodding at the battle table.

“It is a sound plan,” he said. There was no flattery in it. “But Decima is not a simple bug. She has wormed herself into the Divine Temple and stayed there for an entire decade, so I must ask once again, Zora Fabre—can she truly be defeated? Can you defeat her?”

Zora was quiet for a second.

“... All Magicicada Mages and Witches are capable of casting what I call ‘physical spells’,” he said. “We can refill the room’s thirst, curl a draft to cool a fevered blow, and tidy a table of wooden soldiers back to order. These are physical tasks that anyone can imagine themselves doing.”

As he spoke, water jugs lifted themselves from their stands and tilted over every lord’s empty glasses. A wind, gentle as a lullaby, flitted across the table and cooled the stuffy room, while the carved wooden pieces slid back to their original trays and boxes. More than a few mouths made small, surprised shapes, but most of the eastern lords had already seen his spellcasting first hand, so not all of what he was saying was news to them.

“But every Magicicada Mage and Witch are capable of casting their unique ‘impossible spells’, which I call as such because they are the only people capable of imagining themselves performing the impossible. In my cast, my impossible spells are fire-related spells,” he continued, and a ball of flame sparked above his staff, suspending there. “The late Nona, the Witch of Children, was capable of frost-related spells. Decima is known as the Witch of Adults, and if her decade-long infiltration into the Divine Temple is anything to go off on, I presume her impossible spells revolve around mental manipulation, trickery, and deception. Not anything physically powerful like mine and Nona’s.”

But the Regional Lords were starting to look a bit confused now—unsure of the point he was trying to make—so he looked all of them over slowly, smiling frankly.

“In short, if I am allowed to fight Decima face-to-face, then I will annihilate her without fail,” he finished. “Her spells will not work on me. I will not let them work on me. There is no need for any of you to make a theatre of worry, so focus on protecting yourselves.”

A shiver moved through the lords as if a cold current had passed the table—admiration and fear braided in the same stitch—while Sora’s grin was a quill poised over a fresh page in her journal. Zora heard her scribbling down every last word spoken, and while he didn’t quite like the idea that someone was jotting down private military affairs to be published in an open journal afterwards, he doubted he could stop her.

So he picked up a single wooden piece, tapping it on the table. “Now, about the small elite team. I will be going, and so will Enki. His role is more key than mine: he will open a sequence of consecutive wormholes to warp all of us past the walls, past the city, and straight into the Divine Capital. Alongside the two of us will come Lord Baya, Kita Salaqa, Machi, Ifas, and thirty Salaqa Household retainers. Furthermore, the west, east, and central regions will each offer three Regional Lords to follow us so that when the Divine Empress and Her Four Households come to their senses, there will be more familiar faces in the throne room.”

Murmurs rose in the room. Some Regional Lords were inevitably unhappy at the idea of exclusion—everybody wanted to be in the throne room at the moment of Decima’s death—but Baya cut in before Zora could.

“The Reunification Army must have its generals still,” he said. “Even if the full-frontal assault is but a distraction, the armies must still be commanded at their hearts, and we cannot hollow that out. I alongside nine other Outer Regional Lords will explain matters to the Empress and Her Four Households. The rest of you must hold strong and minimize casualties on both sides of the wall. Understood?”

Baya’s word was final. Reluctantly, the lords nodded one by one, the stubborn angles of dissent folding as they thought about the lives of their men. Duty won out; the battlefield needed generals more than it needed extra blades in the dark.

So once nobody had anything else they wanted to say, Zora scanned the room one more time and threw on a casual—hopefully comforting—smile.

“Well then,” he said. “By noon, three days later, the Attini Empire will either be liberated or we will all be dead. Get some rest, bless your men, and reconvene here at the third crack of dawn.”
