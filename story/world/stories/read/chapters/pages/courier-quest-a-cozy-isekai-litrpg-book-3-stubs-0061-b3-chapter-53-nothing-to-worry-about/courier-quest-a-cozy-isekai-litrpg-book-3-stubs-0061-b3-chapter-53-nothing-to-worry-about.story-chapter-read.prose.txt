As promised, the view from the dome was absolutely worth traveling from one tower to another, and the newlyweds enjoyed their meal as they slowly spun around it. They noticed that more boats were starting to arrive. Most of them were clearly passenger vessels, and it was in line with Klar’s words that people were returning to the Floating Isles today.

Their walk through the park, the same one Trevor had gone through before, was peaceful with only a few others on the trails. They went through it rather quickly, though. There was only so much time in the day, and they wanted to make sure they returned in time for whatever event was taking place at the observatory. It seemed like a strange option considering the sky was still overcast.

They returned to the hotel at Adabelle’s request. The humidity had caused her hair to become frizzy in an unacceptably unruly way. They still weren’t sure if this was going to be a social gathering or a private one so she didn’t want to risk it. Both took the time to dress up, and that was when Clay finally unveiled their masterwork.

“Oh my, what is all this?” Adabelle asked as she and Trevor returned to their bedroom.

The earth elemental was sitting on top of the dresser with the fruits of their labor. They stood up on their hind legs and struck a pose that would have made Rashie proud. Sitting before them were four pieces of diamond jewelry.

Two of them were earrings with thin, hexagonal columns hanging from the shimmering silver made of Sammie Steel. Adabelle didn’t have pierced ears, so they could be clipped on. The diamond was flawlessly cut with an almost imperceptible etching on them.

A necklace with a thin chain followed. This one was also a hexagonal column, though it was much larger and heavier. They leaned closer to get a better look, and it appeared as though Clay had traced their master’s Summoned tattoo around the diamond. Trevor took out a magnifying glass from his inventory to check out the earrings, noting a similar pattern.

The final piece was a bracelet. Small diamonds were inserted into every link. They were circular, but twisted upwards slightly to make a three-dimensional pattern not unlike the other pieces in the set. None of them were raised enough to catch on things any more than a normal bracelet would.

Each piece was beautiful. The light from the glowstones in the room caused both the diamonds and metal to shimmer brightly. Adabelle’s expression lit up as well when Clay pointed from them to her.

“For me,” she said, not needing to ask.

The earth elemental nodded, then pointed to their master.

“From Trevor,” Adabelle giggled, remembering this act from earlier in their honeymoon.

Clay nodded again, more vehemently this time, before picking up and offering her the necklace. She took it, all smiles, and offered it to her husband.

Trevor didn’t have to be asked. She moved her hair out of her way and he slipped it around her neck. He brought the clasp together and let it rest before wrapping his arms around her waist to look down upon it. The diamond rested on her chest in a most pleasing way, and he reached out and offered his familiar a fist to bump. They did so happily.

“Thank you, Trevor,” Adabelle said, kissing her husband’s cheek before picking up the earth elemental. She kissed the top of their stony head twice. “And thank you, Clay. This is a beautiful gift. I daresay these might be the most well-crafted jewelry in my collection, and likely will be for a very long time. The etching is exquisite.”

The earth elemental shimmied in her arms, very happy with the praise, before urging her to put on the rest. Trevor could count the times he had seen Adabelle wear earrings on two hands, but she wore the set without complaint.

In fact, she was quite clearly pleased when she went to the mirror to check herself out. Adabelle had worn a black dress tonight to match the fine clothes Trevor had donned, and the silver looked good with it.

“We’re going to have to get Clay something very nice,” the noblewoman decided aloud as she fixed the earrings to a better position on her long, sheep-like ears. “Such a gift cannot go without being reciprocated.”

“I’ll get to work on finding something suitable, lady,” Trevor agreed, smiling.

Adabelle admired herself for a little longer before turning and taking her husband by the hand. She was about to scoop Clay back up when they evaded her grasp with a roll, then bounded off of the dresser. They jumped onto the bed before flattening out, losing some of their shape as a stoat.

“They’re all tuckered out,” Trevor chuckled. “I can’t blame them. This was a lot of work, and they’ve been going at it for a good while.”

Adabelle nodded, but walked over to pet the earth elemental anyway. “Thanks, Clay. I appreciate it.”

The courier could feel his familiar’s sense of accomplishment through their bond as they left. There was still an event to visit, and it wasn’t in their nature to be late without good reason.

The overcast sky was already dark as Trevor and Adabelle started their ascent to the observatory. While it was the tallest of the towers, it was still an easy climb for the two Summoned. The demonkin occasionally glanced down towards the town square.

“Is something wrong?” Trevor asked.

Adabelle didn’t answer right away, though she did stop and step towards the railing. She squinted as she looked down. “May I see your binoculars?”

“Sure.”

Summoning them from his inventory, Trevor passed them off. Adabelle brought them to her eyes and took a look. She panned across the town square, where even he could see unaided that there was something going on.

“Yes, that would explain it,” she mumbled to herself.

“But would you explain it to me?” the courier asked.

“No,” Adabelle teased with a smile as she resumed walking up the stairs. Her tail caught his arm and pulled him along. “I’m fairly certain I know what they’re doing, and there’s nothing to worry about.”

“But what if I want to worry?” Trevor pouted, playing up his disappointment. “What if I want to get anxious that something is happening, even if there was no cause for it?”

“That sounds like the Trevor that first came to this world. You’ve grown a lot since then, haven’t you?” she asked, looking back at him.

“That’s true. I guess there’s no use worrying about it,” he replied with a nod. “Can I have my binoculars back?”

“You can. And go ahead and look if you want to, but you’re not missing much,” Adabelle said as she handed the pair to her tail, which passed them along. “There seems to be a few ritualists in the square. I sensed their magic. Almost everyone else seems unperturbed by their work, even going so far as to set up small parties, so it’s either a cult or something completely innocuous.”

“We forgot to ask,” Trevor stated.

The demonkin smiled. “I wouldn’t say forgot, we graciously allowed the surprise to take place.”

That got a small laugh out of the courier, and he did take a short look down at the town square. It was exactly as she described. Some had even set up grills, getting ready for dinner. After a quick pass, Trevor threw the binoculars back into his inventory and continued following after his wife.

The observatory was a metal dome made out of dull gray metal. There was a circular piece of glass sticking out at an angle, though it wasn’t long enough for Trevor to call it a telescope. Then again, magic could have made it more powerful than he’d expect at first glance.

A minotaur was waiting in front of the entrance, eyes on the sky. They wore fine greens and yellows, and had gold coins hanging from their wide horns, which were wrapped in bright red scarves. The newlywed’s presence was quickly noticed.

“I am Lord Gertabare Altabrae,” he introduced in a smooth baritone. His eyes scanned Trevor first, and his lips seemed to want to curl into a frown. That changed when he looked at the demonkin, and his gaze became more appraising. “Lady Adabelle, I presume?”

“Yes, that is correct,” she replied with a slight bow of greeting customary in Tosa. Her ears twitched, and her posture changed ever so slightly.

“Excellent, the event will be proceeding soon, so I am glad you are on time,” Gertabare said before she could introduce Trevor. “Merchant prince Klarkartar had stated that you would be coming with your husband. I hope he is well, though this weather isn’t good for those who don’t have a hearty constitution. Has your butler come in his place due to some illness?”

Adabelle paused just before she was fully upright, and Trevor tried not to laugh. The minotaur had clearly recognized her regal bearing and new jewelry standing next to his rather plain style and made all sorts of assumptions. He could see where the guy was coming from, especially if he himself was close to the merchant prince family.

She wasn’t going to let this stand, however.

Putting on a diplomatic smile, Adabelle clasped her hands in front of her. Her ear twitched again, a little more violently this time. “Is it safe of me to assume that you are not the merchant prince himself given your title?”

“That is correct, Lady Adabelle. I am his first nephew,” Gertabare proudly answered. “Should you prefer it, I would be willing to accompany you tonight in absence of—”

“My husband?” she asked, a cool edge to her voice. Adabelle’s tail reached out and grabbed her courier by the belt, pulling him. He stumbled slightly at the force of it, but she wrapped an arm around his. “This is Trevor Anderson, the man whom I pledged myself to, and most certainly not my butler. In fact, I cannot for the life of me understand why you would not only dismiss him, but then turn around and try to step into my honeymoon like that.”

The longer she talked, the wider Gertabare’s eyes got. He appeared shocked. “Excuse me?”

“Under normal circumstances, I would, but I am appalled by this lack of decorum coming from someone belonging to a family of such a high station,” Adabelle replied before arching an eyebrow. “Tell me this, Lord Gertabare. I am not familiar with minotauran culture, so in what manner should I respond to this slight? Shall I bring it up with the Altabraes? Merchant prince Klarkartar? Or shall we settle this ourself with a duel? Trevor, I may need my sword.”

“A duel?” the minotaur sputtered, taking a reactionary step back. “Lady Adabelle, there is no reason to take things so far. I merely misspoke.”

“If I hadn’t been wearing this jewelry, would you have looked at me with the same dismissive disgust of someone you deem lower than yourself?” she asked, unwrapping her arms from Trevor’s so she could approach. He was quick to follow although he did nothing to impede her path.

“I have simply become accustomed to those in positions of power flaunting their wealth, my lady. I intended no disrespect,” Gertabare said nervously. It had the cadence of an apology, but wasn’t one.

“It doesn’t change the fact that you have met us with disrespect no matter your intentions, and I won’t stand for it,” Adabelle returned, placing her hands on her hips. “Where we come from, we do not wreathe ourselves in gaudy displays of wealth. My husband prefers a more relaxed mode of dress, as do I. The only reason why I am wearing these diamonds is because they were a gift from a dear friend. Now, is there something you’d like to say?”

For the first time, the lord set his jaw. It appeared as though he was going to argue back, but any chance of that diminished as he wilted under the Summoned’s gaze. With no small amount of hesitation even now, Gertabare turned to Trevor.

“I am… sorry,” he said, though there was some bile in his voice. “I misspoke. You are, of course, welcome into the Altabrae Observatory with your bride due to the courtesy of merchant prince Klarkartar.”

“Hey, no worries, mate,” Trevor replied with a smile. “Honestly, I thought it was pretty funny. I’m always saying she’s too good for me.”

“Darling, you’re undermining my fury,” Adabelle said, her voice only slightly warm as she spoke to him.

His smile was completely unrepentant. “Sorry, love.”

She huffed, then turned back to the minotaur. “It seems as though my husband has forgiven your careless words, so there’s no reason for our families to feud,” the noblewoman stated as if it were fact. “We would like to be escorted inside now.”

“R-right away,” Gertabare said quietly, opening the door behind him. There were two other minotaurs inside, one dressed just as gaudily as the lord, and another in comfortable clothing. “Lady Ralsten, there has been a change of plan. We are leaving.”

“Leaving? To go where?” the well-dressed woman asked.

“Anywhere but here,” he answered before turning back to the newlyweds. “Verdar will be in charge of your evening. I hope it is a pleasant one.”

Trevor and Adabelle watched as Lady Ralsten complained about the trip down after climbing so high. Gertabare said nothing, even when she thumped him on the shoulder.

“A good show, Lady Adabelle,” Verdar said. There was gray amongst his fur, especially around his muzzle, and his bow of greeting didn’t dip very far. “It is not often I get to see the young nephew get put in his place, and I wish I could say it happened more often.”

“She just can’t help but get heated sometimes,” Trevor teased, wrapping an arm around her waist. “Especially when it comes to me. I find it rather endearing.”

Adabelle rolled her eyes and patted her husband on the chest gently. “Yes, I do hate it when people look down on you, especially when your first reaction is to be amused by it. However, Lord Gertabare’s date tonight was bragging to Verdar here that they were crashing our honeymoon. She even went so far as to talk about how much better it would be if we didn’t show up at all, leaving them to their show alone.”

The minotaur bowed again. “Your hearing is praiseworthy, Lady Adabelle.”

“Dang, I actually felt a little bad for him there, but now I’m glad he’s gone. Lady Ralsten can do a lot better.”

That made Verdar smile, though he quickly hid it as he gestured for them to enter the building. “Come, let me take you to the observation room. Merchant prince Klarkartar has prepared a private viewing for you two.”

“A viewing of what, exactly?” Adabelle asked.

“If you have not been told, then I shan’t spoil it,” the minotaur replied with a laugh. “Right this way.”

They went up the stairs not far inside of the observatory and emerged under the dome. There was no telescope like Trevor had expected, but the glass they had seen from outside was clearly magical. It magnified the night sky a great deal, though it only showed clouds.

A minotaur-sized couch built to seat people at a 45 degree angle dominated the middle of the room, and Verdar gestured towards it. While they moved to sit, he walked to a series of levers on the wall. “I will be right downstairs. If you need anything such as food or drink, just shout. This could last upwards of two or three hours.”

“Could?” Trevor asked as he and Adabelle got comfortable.

“You shall see, my lord,” the minotaur said as he worked the levers.

One half of the dome started descending, showing off the clouds above. There was no wind coming in, leaving the room comfortably warm. A gigantic ritual circle appeared in the sky. There was a flash of light that caused them both to shield their eyes.

When they looked again, the night sky was open to them. It was as though a giant circle of clouds had gone missing all at once, letting them see the moon and stars.

Not just those, though. Streaks of light started crossing the sky, burning brightly before fizzling out completely. They came slowly at first, but they picked up rather quickly. It became apparent that this wasn’t just a meteor shower, but an entire storm.

“This is awesome!” Trevor exclaimed with a laugh. He pulled Adabelle closer, and she snuggled against him. “No wonder they wanted to be up so high. There’s no way this would hit the same if we were at ground level.”

“You’re right,” she replied with a smile, resting her head on her husband’s shoulder. They fell into comfortable silence as they watched the lively night sky together.
