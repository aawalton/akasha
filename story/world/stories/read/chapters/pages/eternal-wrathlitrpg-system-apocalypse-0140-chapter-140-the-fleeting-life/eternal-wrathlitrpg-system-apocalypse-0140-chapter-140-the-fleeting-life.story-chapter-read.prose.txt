The red moon ceased. The tide had ended, and the barrier dissipated, leaving the monsters ripe for the picking. James hopped out from the wall and went for the closest monster—a two-legged mantis that preyed on a boar. James drew his bow, and with his bow skill, he popped the mantis’s head.

“Three days’ worth of meals for each monster. It’s a good haul,” James nodded. He retrieved his arrow and went on hunting more. He glanced at the hunters and farmers in the distance. They plowed the land, scavenging a tuber-like plant. Another bulldozed an entire apple tree, picking all its fruit and turning the wood into firewood or materials.

James was flabbergasted by the locust-like behavior. They didn’t care about preservation at all. Any environmentalist group would roll in their grave looking at such behavior.

This world had no noon and night. The sky was always dark, with the crimson moon constantly letting out an eerie red glow. James scavenged another 1,500 biomass before he decided to call it a day. When he returned, Kouki and several soldiers were dismantling a monster corpse—a huge bull-like animal, except it had wriggling tentacles with sharp tips as its horns. They most likely didn’t kill it cleanly since Kaina could be seen buying health potions and sharing them with others.

“James! How are you?”

“Fine. I am thinking of going to Goliath Town. Are you coming?”

“Can’t we do it tomorrow? We just had a haul today. It was tough.”

“Well, it’s not like you have to come,” James muttered. Now that he had recovered his biomass, he planned to explore the world after sleeping.

“Incoming!” a soldier yelled. A massive three-headed bird swooped down from the sky, picking a helpless soldier off the ground. The other soldiers came to help, but it happened so fast the pitiful soldier was already high in the air. James shot several ice lances at the bird to drop the soldier.

Despite hitting the flying monster, the bird didn’t drop the soldier. Only when James shot it with his bow skill did it finally let go. The pitiful soldier was only level one; the drop had unfortunately fractured his legs, but he would live.

“Team One, rescue him,” Kouki said. He turned to James, nodding.

“Andrew told me to keep an eye on you guys. No promises, though,” James said as he shouldered his rifle and returned to the city.

Inside, James slept on the street alongside his raptor. The residents ignored him. Since he was not registered, he couldn’t even ask for an inn. All commerce denied him service.

James thought it was weird; there were supposed to be traders here. How did they conduct business if all shops denied him?

“Sorry, but we do not offer service to the unregistered,” the shopkeeper said when James wanted to buy rope.

“Aren’t there traders from other cities? How are you supposed to deal with that?”

“Such service can only be bought via barter in the trading district beside the census bureau.”

“I see.” James nodded. It was strange, but he understood that the management of this city wanted a monopoly somehow. When he went to the trading district, the said trading was very limited. They used points, and each point could only be used in that shop alone. It was absurd.

“How much for a rope? I have arrows for trade.”

“An arrow is one point. If you want a rope, you need twenty points.”

“I have a rope; does that mean you are willing to buy it for twenty points?” James brought out his steel wire, he didn’t need any of these junks, he just wanted to know how much things are worth. Of course, the greedy merchant haggled. “Do you have food?”

“This is a steel cord. I will buy it for five points,” the merchant said. “And no. Food and water are not sold anywhere in this trading district. They are banned. Only the central bureau could sell food and water.”

“What the fuck? you buy them for 5 points and sold them for 20 points? I am not selling or buying anything here. Goodbye.” James left. He then mounted his raptor and rode out of the city to the next.

Cities welcomed anyone to enter, even if they were not registered. However, services for the unregistered were restricted. It held true even in the neighboring town of Goliath. James wandered the city and found out it had a similar layout to Maruk’s town. It didn’t take long for James to find Andrew outside. When James came upon them, he found Andrew hunting in the wilderness with Sanders and Anderson.

“Yo! Boss James, you sure worked fast. I thought you would do several quests inside the town for a few days before coming here.” Sanders waved at him.

“I am just visiting. How is it going here?” James asked. “Did you guys register?”

James expected a yes—especially from Andrew. He thought Andrew would be chummy with the city leader or something by now. Andrew was always a sociable man, a natural born leader. He would never have thought Andrew was going to say no.

“Boss Andrew insisted the three of us not register. He sensed something fishy. But if nothing comes along, we will register. Ah, Anderson registered, by the way. Otherwise, there is no way we could get food.”

“The system forbids us from giving you food from the district. There is a warning, for God’s sake, so I can only give you shop food. I had to register to get quests so i could get coins,” Anderson said.

“I see. The rule about not trading with the non-registered was enforced by the system, huh? That was weird and suspicious,” James said. He had also had the same experience. When Kouki wanted to give James a meat skewer, a system message popped up before him, talking about how the system would penalize Kouki if he gave James food that originated from the city. Anderson’s food came through the gate, so he was exempt from the rule.

“Thanks, Anderson,” Andrew nodded.

“Well, in return, we have to help him earn more than others, though. Hahaha,” Sanders said.

“Coins don’t drop from the sky. You don’t even get coins from monsters here!”

“Yeah, that sucks. You can only get coins from the city’s quests.”

“That’s our situation, James. So, how about you? Did you register with Maruk’s city?”

“Nah. I didn’t register either,” James said.

“Really? How did you survive then? Even if you have water magic, you still have to eat. How did you survive eight days without eating?” Sanders asked.

“My skill, Chimera, can heal my body or keep me satisfied if I provide it with biomass. That means killing monsters and storing their meat with my skill.”

“Damn. If only we had that. Doughlas has an item box skill, but it was emptied during the transfer. Moreover, food is still spoiling inside,” Sanders said.

“I don’t have an item box,” James clarified.

“That’s it. James, since you are outside the system, please venture out as far as you can.”

“Why don’t you do it too, Andrew?”

“You see, we have a quota to reach. Anderson said we need to capture at least twenty beasts every day,” Andrew said.

“Huh? Why?” James tilted his head. No matter how you put it, that was a ridiculous amount of meat. If you butchered them properly, each monster was enough for five days’ worth of food. Why did they need to hunt so many?

“You see, Goliath said that all these monsters and greenery here will die within a week, starting from the vegetation. You see, even the grass here has begun to wither. Soon enough, monsters and animals will all die one by one because of hunger. So we need to gather as much meat as possible. The underground chamber below the government office will preserve the meat until the next tide,” Andrew said.

It had been just a day, but some of the grass they stepped on had turned light brown and dried out. James observed his surroundings. And just like what Andrew said, even some of the trees had begun to shed their leaves.

“When will the next tide happen?”

“In about a month. That’s why we need to gather as much food as possible,” Andrew said.

“I see. But you guys will be fine, right? You guys didn’t buy food from the town.”

“Yes. But five hundred people inside the city still need to eat. They are working hard to harvest anything of value told by the system, and we need to help them. Besides, Anderson needs much more coins if we are to survive until next month.”

“I see. Understood. Good luck, Andrew,” James muttered. He looked over at his status. He now had 2,200 biomass. Enough to last him twenty-two days. If he killed five more monsters, he would be good until next month. Should he hunt more, or should he explore?

“How stupid. I will just hunt while exploring. Alright, I will be a scout. See you later, Andrew,” James muttered.

“Please scout for us. I am relying on you, boss! If you need any help, just ask,” Sanders said.

As he was about to leave, he suddenly remembered Silas. “Andrew, what about that government guy? Did he behave himself?”

Andrew smiled wryly. “Ah, he didn’t do that well. He tried to pick up weapons this morning, but he was injured after his first hunt.”

“Tch. What a useless guy. Make sure he doesn’t cause any trouble, alright.” James then left through the withering plain. After the plain came the forest. It had just been a day, but monsters had prowled, looking for nourishment. He noticed that Goliath had hunted every single animal on the plain beside their town. They would come here tomorrow at the latest.

James galloped his raptor past the monsters, ignoring any that came for him. He galloped into the great beyond, looking for answers.
