I ran as fast as my stick legs would carry me, clutching a rattling crate that slammed against my chest, its contents screeching and hissing.

Three guards were on my trail, one of them screaming for me to stop. My laboured footfalls clattered against the ground, birds fled in my wake, and I slipped around a corner, adrenaline burning in my chest.

I ran down the dilapidated street, over loose planks and through muddy divots, squeezing through the wreckage of an upturned cart and ducking into an alley, the air acrid, the world dull, my mind racing.

Halfway down that alley, there was a door. I banged on it repeatedly, panting, not daring to scream Fazo’s name. I stared at the road behind me as I waited, squeezing myself inside the doorway.

Thankfully, he unbolted the door quickly. The tall, grey-haired man gave me a judgmental look, then ushered me inside.

“What I told you about stealing from the market?” he asked me as I stepped inside, placing the caged animal onto a table, knocking aside papers as I did.

I panted. My whole body felt like it was on fire.

“Don’t… don’t take things that you can’t run with.”

I dropped to the floor; my lungs burned.

“What else?” Fazo asked, bolting the door as he spoke.

“Don’t take things that we can’t move easily,” I recited.

He turned to me, raising a dark eyebrow. “What else?”

I bit my lip.

“Don’t bring the city watch to my damn door, stick.” Fazo grabbed me by the ear, pulling hard enough that it hurt. “How many times I gotta tell you before it sinks in?”

I risked a smile, knowing I was risking a clout either way. “Last time?”

He considered me for a moment, then let go of me. “Last time…” He walked back to the door, then slid open an eye-hole. “You sure you lost them?”

He looked at me and I nodded. I’d gotten a big headstart, and I knew the back alleys better than anyone.

A mewling sound broke his gaze. He closed the eye-hole and walked across the room, prodding at the box. “What’s this thing, anyway?”

I gave him a funny look. “It’s a cat.”

Fazo looked at me as if I were an idiot. “I can see it’s a damn cat. This is what you risk your life for?”

“I wasn’t in danger,” I defended.

“And this isn’t worth anything,” Fazo concluded.

“I-I mean… it’s obviously valuable!” I said, pointing at the creature, which was tucked in on itself at the far side of the box, looking warily at the two of us. “Look at it. Have you ever seen a cat with golden fur like that?”

Fazo stared at the cat I’d stolen from one of the creature stalls for maybe two seconds. “It looks like a cat to me.”

He narrowed his eyes at me. “You know how many cats there are in this city? You can walk down any street and find twenty. But this cat is special, because it’s in a cage.”

“Well… yeah. I saw a price tag on it. It said eight silvers.”

“So, what? It shoots fire from its paws? Shits gold?”

I threw my hands up, still panting, realising my whole body ached.

Fazo stared at the cat once more. I did the same. Why was it apparently worth so much?

“Maybe it’s a kitten?” I offered. “Maybe it’ll turn into a lion one day, or something.”

Fazo scoffed at me. “You ever seen a lion before?”

“Have you?” I countered.

That earned me a smack round the head. A light one, I barely felt it, but it basically confirmed to me that Fazo hadn’t seen a lion either.

And, yeah, this thing looked like a domestic cat, just with plush golden fur. I was definitely reaching.

Fazo walked across the room and picked up a clay jug, pouring some of its contents into a cup and passing it to me. I drank greedily.

“I’ll give you one silver for it,” Fazo finally announced.

“One?” I all but spat at him.

“You’re lucky I give you one,” Fazo snapped. “How the fuck am I supposed to sell this thing?”

“Three,” I argued.

“Too high.”

“I almost died getting this thing!”

“Well, then it’d be a stupid fucking death!” Fazo shouted right back. “One, and I give you another slap for wasting my time.”

“Two,” I countered.

Fazo raised his hand threateningly. “Two slaps? You are testing my patience.”

“Two silvers,” I bartered, looking for any give.

Fazo smirked at me. “Look at the little businessman.” He prodded my chest. “You ain’t getting two for this little rat thing. One silver for the cat, and I’ll give you twenty coppers to buy new boots. I can’t watch you running around in those anymore.”

I glanced down at my utterly worn out shoes, which were beginning to accumulate tears.

I opened my mouth to negotiate more, but the look that Fazo gave me told me I’d already pushed my luck.

“Alright…” I nodded.

Fazo fished into his pockets and fished out some coins. “Hey, don’t be moping. It’s me that’s gotta find a buyer for the damn thing.” He counted out the coppers, murmuring as he did, and deposited the cold coins into my hands. “There. Now, are you staying for beskam?”

It’d been a few days since I’d eaten with Fazo, and beskam wasn’t a meal I knew how to say no to. I nodded and Fazo walked with me into the front room, where a large pot was simmering.

It was a leek, cabbage, and mutton stew that Fazo usually threw salt and spices into. Beskam traditionally contained mushrooms, but I always picked them out. It didn’t have the strongest smell, but the moment I entered the living quarters, I picked up the scent of—

Thud, thud, thud.

“Open up!” came an aggressive shout from outside.

“Who is it?” Fazo shouted, putting on a thicker accent than usual.

“Open your door, or we kick it down!”

Chills raced through my body. Fazo gave me a glare, and I knew what it meant. I grabbed the crate from the nearby table and ducked under it, tugging on the tablecloth until it almost completely concealed me.

“I’m coming, I’m coming…”

I heard the unfamiliar clack of a cane. I’d heard him mention this trick before, but never witnessed it. The moment he opened the door, two guards pushed their way past him, one telling the other to ‘search the place’. I watched as Fazo fell to the floor, losing his grip on his cane.

“Ahh!” he shouted for effect.

The guards—who I could only see the boots of, one pair muddy—both rounded on the fallen man.

“What exactly are you playing at?” the guard with muddy boots asked as Fazo continued to flail.

“What am I doing?” Fazo seethed. “What are you doing?! Assaulting an old blind man in his own home, the shame of it!”

He shook his fist for effect. I only knew as much because of the angle he laid at.

“W-well I—” muddy boots blustered.

“Shit. Uh, let me help you…” clean boots added.

“I’m fine! I do not need your help!”

Fazo made a show of clutching for his cane for the next twenty seconds, then spent another agonizing thirty climbing to his feet. He wobbled after, leaning forward like he was clutching the cane with both hands.

“Are… are you alright, sir?” clean boots asked.

“Oh, I’m sir, now, am I?” Fazo asked, some bite in his tone between the pants. “You threatened to kick my door down not two minutes ago.”

“Well, a wanted criminal was seen on this street, and—”

“In my house?” Fazo asked, sounding completely outraged. “You think he’s here?” He asked, turning on the spot. “Has anything been stolen?!”

Fazo began clacking his stick against the floor, aggressively walking across the hall. “You see anything missing? Is my coinpurse still on the table? You better not have taken my coins!”

“Sir…”

“I’ll find you, theiving little mestro, come out!”

“Sir!” Muddy boots shouted, grinding Fazo to a halt. “Stop making a scene and listen. Did you see—”

“Daron!” the other guard spat.

Muddy boots groaned. “Apologies. Did you hear anything out of the ordinary in the last ten minutes?”

More stick clacking as Fazo turned. “You mean besides two brutes banging down my door and trying to cripple me?”

“You’re already crippled,” muddy boots commented.

“Oh, that’s how you talk to me?” Fazo grumbled.

“We’re just trying to figure out if—” clean boots started.

Fazo paused. “Oh. So you don’t know that he came in here.”

“We were trying to tell you tha—”

“Let me tell you something,” Fazo started, his voice raising as he spoke. “I’m a political refugee. I got fuckin’ rights. You two bang down my door with no fuckin’ warning and then you start knocking me around.” He prodded the cane forwards.

“What you gonna do next? Beat me up? Kill me?”

Neither of them responded immediately.

“I didn’t hear nothing,” Fazo stated. “That’s it. Now get the fuck out of my house.”

“You hear the mouth on this guy?” Muddy boots said to clean boots.

“Daron, for fuck’s sake. Look at him. You think this poor bastard’s hiding a criminal?”

“I don’t care. I ought to punch him in the mouth for talking to us like that,” muddy boots replied.

“We’re wasting time,” clean boots concluded. “Let’s check the next street.”

Clean boots walked to the door. Muddy boots didn’t budge.

“Daron, I will write you up this fucking instant. Get moving.”

“Grr… fucking Assack bastard.” Muddy boots sighed. “Fine. Coming.”

The two of them filed out. Fazo paused for a few moments until he heard the voices fade.

Then he whapped my ankles with the cane.

“You.”

I came out feeling shame-faced and awful.

He threw the cane away, reaching out and snatching the crate from my grasp, causing the cat to mewl.

Something it thankfully hadn’t done the entire time I’d been down there, barely allowing myself to breathe.

“No silver for you,” Fazo decreed, a damning condemnation. “That is the last time I am making a damn fool of myself for you, boy. Next time I give you up.”

He grabbed me by the arm, dragging me into the living quarters. He all but pushed me into a chair and began to stuff a bowl with beskam. He shoved it into my hands, then gave me a spoon.

“Next time, that performance won’t work,” Fazo said, musing over his own bowl. “Then what?”

“Then you give me up?” I repeated.

“No. I get six months hard labour for harbouring a thief,” Fazo corrected me. “Next time, you go hide somewhere else.”

I nodded quickly.

“And eat your food.”

I did as I was told. Hot stew calmed my rapid heartbeat, the taste familiar and delightful.

“It’s good,” I said to Fazo, hoping he wasn’t too mad at me.

“I know. Fuckin’ pain in my ass…”

I ate a few more spoonfuls. I didn’t really know what to say.

Eventually, a few words came.

“You should’ve told them where I was.”

Fazo didn’t like that. He waved his spoon at me so aggressively it almost flew into my face.

“You should value your own life. You know what happens if you get caught stealing like that?”

“They throw me in a cell for a few days,” I said. “I’m a kid. I don’t have my class yet. I might get beaten up if I get unlucky. What else?”

“They make a record of you,” Fazo explained, tutting between bites. “You want to be known as a criminal? You know how many doors that closes for you?”

“So I should stop stealing?” I asked Fazo, an eyebrow raised.

“You should stop getting caught,” Fazo corrected.

“But—”

“No ‘buts’,” Fazo waved a finger. “Think before you speak.”

I started to understand his meaning eventually. I chewed a tough piece of mutton as I considered just how close I’d just come to being caught there. I hadn’t made a smart decision.

“But what if I just stop stealing?” I asked again.

Fazo pointed a finger.

“Look around you.”

I did as I was told.

“What?”

“I fought in the streets of Assan,” Fazo stated. “I did my part to topple the regime. This is what I get for it.”

For all that Fazo had told me, I still didn’t understand everything about the Assack civil war nor Melusia’s role in it. I knew that Melusia’s king had offered refuge to soldiers who had fought on the side they funded.

And Fazo, who lived in a slum only a little less destitute than Flea’s End, wasn’t happy with his prize.

“They are the oppressors,” Fazo told me. “They find a four year old boy on the road, his parents dead, they stick him in the slums. They take a man who led warriors, who watched his friends die, who they’d made a promise to, and they stick him in the slums. We are one and the same.”

“I don’t know where I’m from,” I defended. “I could be from Melusia.”

“It doesn’t matter, you’re not one of them. You don’t matter to them.”

“Well, if I keep training, and I get a good class, then—”

“Then you’ll be a more effective slave,” Fazo finished.

My face burned from hearing that. I didn’t like being told what I could or couldn’t do.

“How do you know?”

“Because your worth isn’t determined by your class,” Fazo explained. “Not really. It’s determined by your birth. By who your parents were.”

I’d heard this before. It always bothered me. Mainly because I knew nothing at all about my own.

“They won’t give anything to you that you don’t take from them,” Fazo said. “They won’t hesitate to throw you away when you stop being useful.” He shook his head. “But you’re nothing. You’re weak, and you’re poor, and you’re worthless, and you’re disgusting. They don’t want to look at you. They don’t want to think about you.

“And that makes you invisible.”

I opened my mouth to respond. I didn’t know what to say.

“Now eat your beskam. You need some meat on you.”

And so I did.

It was only when I was leaving Fazo’s house that a couple of strange things began to occur to me.

One, I wasn’t ten anymore. I was thirteen or fourteen now.

Two, I hadn’t seen Fazo since I was ten. He’d gotten himself arrested or killed. I had no idea which. He’d vanished after more than two years of me knowing him, knocking on his door just about every week, bringing him things to sell and eating my fill.

It’d been a pleasant ritual until one day I’d stopped short in the streets and seen a bunch of guards around his home, the front door having been caved in. I wasn’t in time to see what had happened to Fazo, but whatever it was, it made me angry.

Not just with the city. Angry at him. He’d told me over and over for years to just be smarter, to keep myself out of danger, to not get caught. And what did he end up doing? Hypocrite.

I wasn’t invisible, and neither was he. It was stupid fucking advice. I wasn’t sure how he’d screwed things up for himself, but I was certain it was loud and rebellious. Fazo hated Melusia and everything it stood for, and everyone had to know about it.

Still, Fazo had taught me a few things. The little bit I knew about cooking came from him, plus a lot of my haggling skill. He’d told me that drunks were easy to con and that even a drunk orc was shrewd. That looking too clean could get you robbed and being too dirty could make you sick. He’d shown me how to stitch my clothes, how to start a fire, and how to tie a good knot.

He told me that lies are cheap and the truth is expensive. I still believed that one.

The sun ran between two tall buildings as it continued its path down to the western hills. The city bustled. I felt at home for a moment, whatever home really meant to me.

I had a somber thought. I’d probably never see Fazo again after this.

I stopped atop a bridge, memories still rushing back to me. I didn’t freak out as I thought I would. I realised where I was and why I was here. I knew that Maya was trying to get me out of this.

But I didn’t know how far along she was. And I didn’t know how my dream would reset.

Would it be another wolf? Or something else?

“Hey!”

My question was answered. Two guards appeared on the right side of the bridge. I was to be chased.

I turned to run. I felt my legs ready to spur into motion. I’d been here a thousand times. This was nothing new to me.

As soon as I turned around, I saw four more guards on the other side. All of them standing in wait.

I turned again. Running into two sounded easier than four.

There weren't two anymore. Instead it was six.

I didn’t fancy my odds. But my brain was screaming at me to run. I needed to do something.

I clambered onto the banister of the bridge, ignoring the shouts of protest all around me. I jumped, hitting icy, dirty water as my vision began to blur.

For a moment I wondered if I’d drown. I’d had at least two experiences where I’d felt I might before. Even now, at my current age, I wasn’t the strongest swimmer.

Water didn’t terrify me, but right now, water was filling my mouth and nose, and I was thrashing to keep above the river’s current. I pushed, I paddled, I attempted to surface myself and swim, but…

Something grabbed ahold of me.

A hand. Then two. Then four. Then eight.

Before I knew what was happening, I was grabbed and hoisted onto the shore. I panted. I wheezed. I attempted to thank my saviour.

A guard cocked back his fist and punched me in the face.

I felt something burst in my mouth as the adult crashed his fist into my face a second time. Teeth fell from my jaw as he continued to punch me; all the while another began to kick in my ribs, and a third stamped on my legs.

“S-sto—”

I couldn’t speak. The pain was indescribable. I was pretty sure I ended up paralysed after the first several hits. Maybe even brain damaged. I stared upwards, disoriented, unable to move, half-blind as the men continued to beat me, every muscle and nerve I had either burning with agony or chillingly numb.

I wanted to close my eyes but I couldn’t. I wanted to wail, but I couldn’t move my mouth. After thirty more seconds, I was dead. The world went blank. My mind went blank. And reality repeated again.

Only, this time…

“Adam? Adam, is that you?”

“Mom?”

Toar entered Mansol’s office to a variety of reactions.

One of them was surprise. All three of them shared that emotion. They paused in whatever conversation they were having and all turned to look at him.

Then, the reactions split into three distinct camps. Finn looked concerned. Jackal more confused. Mansol… looked amused. He smiled a wide smile.

“Toar?” Jackal asked, blinking a couple of times as if he were an apparition. “What the hell are you—”

“Yes, little cousin. What are you doing out of bed?” Mansol asked.

Toar froze at Mansol’s words. Blood dripped from his calves onto the stained carpet.

“Cousin?” Jackal repeated.

“Guys,” Toar started, his heart pounding, adrenaline coursing through his system. “You need to leave. You need to—”

“What?” Mansol laughed, drowning out Toar with a deep chuckle. “My… you are a flower boy, aren’t you?”

He cleared his throat. “Stay still,” he said, pointing a scalpel at the pair of them. “I need to educate my cousin.”

Finn put a hand on his belt. Jackal growled as the knife was held only inches from his face.

“Tell me,” Mansol asked Toar, circling his hostages as he spoke. “If you were in my position, what would you do?”

“They won’t say anything,” Toar told him.

“Won’t they?” Mansol inquired.

“They don’t know anything,” Toar insisted. “Nothing that matters, anyway. Even if they did, who the hell would listen? You’re a respected staff member. They’re workers. They’re no threat to you.”

“Thank you for telling me!” Mansol’s voice swung upbeat. “They did look very threatening until you said that. I feel a lot less worried now.”

He pressed the blade closer to Jackal’s face. So close it was nearly touching.

“How about it?” Mansol asked. “Do you two promise not to tell anyone anything you saw or heard today?”

Jackal and Finn said nothing. Toar felt a growl rising in his voice.

“Just promise!”

“We promise,” both stuttered out at varying speeds. “We promise.”

“Ah… you hear that? They promised,” Mansol smiled. “Well, all of my worries are assuaged now. I’m sure that neither of you will say anything.”

He patted Jackal on the shoulder.

“Well, off you go then.”

The two of them stared at him. It was clear that neither of them felt remotely safe leaving. Toar didn’t feel safe either. He was subconsciously moving closer to Mansol as his cousin spoke… but what could he do to stop him, so weak and injured?

Toar had been sure, sure that Mansol wouldn’t go this far with him there. He’d thought that if he just made it, then…

“Well, off you go then,” Mansol repeated in a more emphatic voice.

Still, neither of them moved. Finn continued to clutch at his belt. Toar inched closer.

Mansol huffed. “You don’t want to die with dignity? Fine.”

He reached forward with his scalpel, aiming it at Jackal’s neck.

Jackal had been prepared for the lunge, but not fast enough to dodge it. He brought his arm up just in time to be impaled there instead.

The beastkin grunted as Mansol withdrew his blade, all the while laughing.

“Stand and watch, cousin. See how a man deals with threats.”

He reached back to finish Jackal. All the while Toar ran at him.

Perhaps it was Mansol’s belief that Toar was a loyal underling, or his faith that Toar feared him too much to rebel. It might’ve been Mansol’s lack of empathy and care for others that made him fail to see it in Toar, or it might’ve been some honour code about attacking one’s own family.

Or maybe he’d simply believed that Toar was too injured to move so fast.

Whatever the reason, Mansol seemed to be taken off-guard by his cousin’s assault. Toar landed a punch on the back of his head, driving his arm forward after he connected and wrapping his arms around the tiger’s neck.

Mansol flailed and pushed his elbows back in an attempt to get Toar off of him, who hugged onto his neck as if he were dangling from the edge of a cliff.

Finn pulled his dagger, lifted the weapon, and stabbed into Mansol’s chest.

Toar heard a squelching sound, then a clink as the metal refused to move any further, the knife soon falling to the ground. All the while, Jackal lunged forward, slashing at Mansol’s face with his claws.

Mansol growled, headbutting backwards and dislodging Toar before throwing a punch directly at Finn.

Finn attempted to block it, but Mansol’s punch went wide enough that it barely grazed him. One of Jackal’s slashes had caught his left eye.

Jackal attempted to claw out the other one, but his arm was grabbed in midair. Mansol squeezed hard, squeezed until bones crunched. Then he pulled forwards, wrapping the other hand around Jackal’s neck.

Toar fought through dizziness, fishing the knife from the ground, crawling on all fours, reaching up and stabbing into Mansol’s ankle.

The titan roared, dropping Jackal, as all the while Toar pulled back the blade and stabbed a second time.

He caught a boot to the face, sending him skidding across the floor and into a pool of blood. He watched as Finn was smashed from his feet with a backhand and kicked so hard that something snapped.

Toar slipped and slid his way to crouching, running across the room on all fours, jumping at Mansol and clamping his jaws to the back of his neck.

His cousin attempted a headbutt again, but this time the angle wasn’t quite right and he was unable to dislodge him so easily. Toar fastened his arms around Mansol’s neck once more, clinching himself in place, desperately squeezing at tendons and sinew as solid and unwavering as oak.

“Argh!”

Jackal followed Toar’s lead, biting at Mansol’s forearm, clamping on tight and refusing to let go. Combined with Toar’s assault, this was enough to elicit more than a roar. Genuine pain. Mansol was panting. He couldn’t dislodge Jackal no matter how much he shook him, and with the bulk of his frame and the way his right arm was being dragged down, he couldn’t reach Jackal to punch him with his left.

Still it felt that they were doomed. Finn was barely conscious and was gasping shallow breaths, Mansol was now attempting to drive his elbow into Toar’s chest over and over, and Jackal, despite the level of pain he was putting Mansol through, couldn’t drag the beast to his knees, even while him and Toar attacked at the same time.

Eventually, something gave. And it wasn’t Mansol. The doctor didn’t fall to his knees. He stood above all of them. He finally smashed his way into Toar’s ribs, shattering multiple, causing the boy to dislodge his arms. A grasping hand then ripped Toar from his neck like a troublesome mosquito, throwing him down to the floor below.

Next, he focused on Jackal. Toar watched, barely able to breathe, as Jackal flailed, grasping at Mansol’s neck as the larger, taller, superior beastkin drove him into the ground.

He leaned down on him, pressing his weight into Jackal as still the canid defiantly squeezed and scratched at him, eliciting a grim laugh. With a flick of motion, Jackal’s hand was ripped away.

“Well. At least you tried. Now, let’s see what sound you make as I—”

Mansol’s words were cut off with a glug.

Blood streamed from his neck.

He fell atop Jackal’s legs, looking as if he were attempting to make out words.

Toar stared as he watched his cousin die. He didn’t look away. He didn’t attempt to speak.

A part of him felt as if he should say something. He didn’t know what. He didn’t know how he felt.

This had all happened so quickly.

Finn suddenly gasped. It seemed he’d become lucid of the scene again. He raised a shaky hand, pointing at the dying doctor.

“Dude… you…”

“Yeah,” Jackal grunted, trying to slide out his legs. “I think I got his carosick artery.”

Those were the final words Mansol heard before he died.

“Guys,” Finn began with a cough.

“We just murdered a staff member.

“I think we’re fucked.”
