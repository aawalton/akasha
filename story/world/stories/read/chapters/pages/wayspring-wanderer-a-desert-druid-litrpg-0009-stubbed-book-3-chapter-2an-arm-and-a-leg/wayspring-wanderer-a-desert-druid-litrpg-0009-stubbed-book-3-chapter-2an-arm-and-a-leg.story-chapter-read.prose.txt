Chapter 2—An Arm and a Leg

The leg worked great, of course. After some awkward mental gymnastics, Oskar wrapped his mind around the odd feeling of almost sentience he felt from the wood infusing the foot. He’d never had that issue with the spear, maybe because the tree the spear had come from was now gone, but it hadn’t taken long for Oskar to realize the new and improved foot felt more like his real foot than the old prosthetic ever could. It had a sense of “presence,” instead of weight, which was something Oskar didn’t know he missed until it finally sank in why it initially felt so odd to wear.

Hopefully, it’s as strong as the spear, though, Oskar thought as he wiped sweat from his forehead with his now raggedy scarf as he rose into the air.

Even in darkness, the temperature rose steadily as Oskar left the area of the Great Tree. It wasn’t until he rose so high in the sky that even the tree looked less imposing that it cooled down again. He circled around far overhead and out of sight and then, when he was in position, came back down to scan the Collectives that had been gathering in the area the past few days.

It was a nightly chore and the safest way to keep an eye out for the movement of the wandering groups. After a loop, it looked like it was still just Crocs, Hyenamen, and Kobolds so far.

But Drakon aren’t far behind, and they’re truly dangerous. Still, if they all group together and make a push for the tree, it could get messy.

After a few seconds, he admitted to himself he wasn’t too worried about that.

Not yet at least. Even with a mass attack, I could still most likely keep everyone safe.

PUBs agreed, flashing green in his vision before replying.

// Probably, if you stay out of melee range, at least. You’ve got tools to avoid that now, though, so you’re probably right. //

It was a weird thing to admit to himself that he could probably fight off a few dozen of the creatures that had tormented them for so long, but he’d had a huge jump in power after Bastet’s gifts and again as the Tree grew, adding magic back into a world starved for so long. That, added to his growing mastery over his druidic power, made him a force of nature.

Sora hitting B Rank was massive. He could do almost anything he could imagine with Sora now, but one thing he was struggling with was how to progress from here. Sora was now progression locked, and he knew he was still missing whatever it was that could push him past it.

C Rank Sora had felt more like a boost in power and some control, but B Rank? Control for days, which led him to believe that progressing from here was going to need it. Somehow.

Stronger in every way, and both Rakiyu and Talau aren’t too far from ranking as well. I’m surely not the only one benefiting from this, though. Then again, it is my type of magic… a perfect balance of living Sora, Talau, and Rakiyu, empowered by the Wayspring magic that makes up the world.

Wayspring magic wasn’t creation magic, but it was a magic of growth, a magic of life, and sometimes of death. Even in death, though, it was still part of the cycle of rebirth.

Desiccation was a magic of entropy. He’d seen in work its magic in the rot and shadow of this world. Closer to a void than its own branch of magic, and Oskar had seen what happened to those who tried to wield it.

As far as Oskar could tell, Desiccation seemed to “eat” the wild Wayspring magic whenever it could. Unfortunately, it also took the mind and reason of anyone infected by it.

Oskar didn’t know the source of the Desiccation magic, but the small blue sun somewhere on the other side of this giant world seemed to be where it now lived—fighting to dominate and destroy the giant red sun that kept this world alive. The first moment he’d seen it was forever seared into his memory, sitting atop the mesa the morning after he met Bluebottle and unlocked the use of his Seed Forge.

If he activated his True Sight, he could likely see the flaring blue and red ropes of fire and magic battling between the suns, but now, staring through his Goggles, there was only a cloudless sky, subtly dotted by stars that were only now becoming familiar to him. No Big Dipper here, that’s for sure.

Stargazing wasn’t what he was here for, though. He felt a larger group further away, moving closer steadily, and turned back around to see who it was, the weight of worry growing in his gut. Drawing closer replaced the weight with curiosity.

Clinkers?

// From the looks of it, yes. Close to twenty of them. //

Stop acting like you don’t have an exact count. How close to twenty?

// Really, really close. Like, really close. //

A smile crossed his face, but the generally more resourceful than violent Clinkers were still added numbers. Added unknown factors to an already stressful situation. They were made up of outcasts from all races, and there were even a few cubs in the mix.

Fox is gonna love this.

Even though Clinkers had tried to rob them outside Gramm’s Collective, they seemed more like pirates, well… sand pirates, than highwaymen. And Oskar had always loved pirates. He couldn’t help it. From books to movies, he had a soft spot in his heart for swashbucklers.

They slowed when they drew close enough to see the Tree in better detail, but thankfully, after staring for a few silent minutes, they set up camp while the sounds of chatter started back up.

A quick scan showed nothing alarming, but he didn’t want to risk getting close enough or staying long enough to scan everyone, especially when a few lookouts separated from the rest and started climbing the dunes for guard duty.

With a burst of Sora, he was out of sight before anyone got into position who might see him, and a minute later he landed near Fox and Touwon to relay what he’d seen.

“We have new arrivals,” Oskar said, walking up to Fox, who stared appreciatively at the prosthetic before replying.

“Anything interesting, or more of the same?”

Her coloring was the same as the darkening sky, a smooth dark purple that gave off a dangerous feeling aura. It was a fair assessment; she was dangerous. In the night, he could barely make out the small streaks of dark orange that wrapped around her neck and shoulders. Sharp green eyes watched him expectantly through Goggles of her own. Like Touwon, she also wore a Bracer, but it wasn’t matched to the Goggles, only paired. Well-made, but still…

// She doesn’t have a brilliant best friend inside her head like you do! //

Almost exactly what I was thinking.

// Uh-huh. //

He hadn’t known anything at the time, but Bastet had gone through a great deal of trouble to secure a matched Gear set for Oskar, along with the weapon he now carried. Leaning a little on the Branch of the Omni-Tree, now in its spear form, Oskar told Fox about the group of Clinkers that set up camp a few miles out from the tree.

“They were singing a ditty about stealing water from Crocs. Even the Croc Clinkers were singing it and laughing. They’re so cool,” Oskar laughed.

Smirking, Fox replied, “They are a complication, though, ‘cool’ or not.”

“I know, but… how come we don’t have any songs?”

“Do you want a song, Oskar?”

“Bad idea, Fox. You haven’t heard some of the marching cadences he came up with,” Erik said, walking up behind him and throwing his skeletal arm over Oskar’s shoulders teasingly, but Oskar was more warmed by the gesture than Erik could have known. Casual brotherly affection spoke more about how Erik was adjusting than anything else.

“They were catchy, though.”

“Yes, Oss, they were catchy. I’ll give you that.”

“Noted: do not encourage Oskar to make music. I will keep that in mind, Erik. Thank you,” Fox said, smirking. “Do you think we should reach out to them?”

“Well, I doubt anyone came out just to stare at the tree. Sooner or later, we’re gonna be forced to deal with everyone. Especially now that there’s more out there than just a spattering of small Collectives.”

There was no reply, but Fox’s long exhale said enough: the days of relative relaxation in the shade of the Great Tree were drawing to a bittersweet close.

“The usual? Tomorrow morning, you and Touwon take a few others to talk to them, and I’ll provide over-watch from the air?”

After a second, he added, “This time, though, it’s best to have Erik with a second quick reaction group, out of sight but close by.”

Nods all around, and Fox walked over to fill Touwon in on their plan. The quiet Kobold was working on some contraption under an open lean-to he had set up for just the occasion.

Turning around, Oskar watched his brother for a few seconds. The white hair and beard looked odd on Erik’s scarred, dark face. Erik caught him looking and glanced up, his piercing mismatched eyes seeming to look into Oskar’s soul… but then his brother winked his blue eye and looked back down, again focusing on his skeletal hand. Fluctuations of power drifted through Oskar’s senses as he watched.

He’ll be at that for a while, and I can’t blame him. The faster he figures out what he’s now capable of, the better it’ll be for all of us.

*Erik*

It hurt to use his power. It wasn’t at all like before, when using it had taken directly from his life force, but it was pulling from somewhere other than just Capacity.

Even more unusual, the arm, full of Light magic, dimmed when he healed, which would make sense if healing used Light magic as fuel, but that wasn’t what was happening. If anything, the Light magic felt suppressed by the healing somehow.

This is frustrating. I’m on the edge of understanding it, but I’ve felt like this for a week.

[[ A quagmire, to be sure. ]]

Yeah, okay. A real quagmire, man.

Erik shook head, bemused at the PUB, but quickly returned his attention to the hand. The day before, a Kobold cub twisted an ankle jumping down the dunes trying to fly like Oskar.

Of course, the cubs knew they couldn’t fly, but it had become a game to jump down the dunes and try to be as still in the air as possible, moving only at the last second to slide, or in this case, roll down the dune. They also yelled out random words from Earth they’d overheard from conversations Oskar and Erik had about earth.

Lately, they’d settled on “Froyo!” for some reason, which was better than last week’s “McChicken!”

The cubs jumped in with a million questions every time and started throwing around earth terminology fully out of context.

Come to think of it, Erik realized all the words they used were earth foods the brothers talked about craving that didn’t have a direct translation. Either way, the cub, known for shenanigans, had both won and lost the game, and ended up needing Erik’s magic to keep from “fadin’ fast!” as the cub had called out desperately.

Without thinking, Erik healed the boy, expecting the magic to pull from his life force—to react the way it had after his imprisonment—but the healing had gone off without a hitch. There was some pain, but the cost in pain and flesh he was expecting never came.

Instead, the bone darkened slightly, and the Light in the arm suddenly felt like it was heavier and harder to access.

He and the PUB couldn’t nail down what was happening exactly, but accompanying the magical suppression was a vague feeling deep inside that he should not try healing again until he fully recovered—which he had—or figured out what was going on with his magic… which he had not.

The notification he received should have clarified things but only made things more confusing.

[[ Sion Debt Incurred ]]

Sion debt? What’s Sion?

It gave an overall feeling of a pendulum swinging in one direction and then stopping before swinging back in the other direction. It was… uncomfortable to say the least.

The PUB wanted to pretend he could help, but he knew from Oskar’s own experimentation that his evolving Unique class was not something the PUB could teach him. The class was perhaps, still building itself around Erik’s own needs and growth. It didn’t even have a name yet, according to the PUB. It gave him the impression that he was being weighed. Tested before the class decided what it really was.

Erik wished that the world network thing that they’d unlocked had some information, but no luck there either. As soon as he and Oskar connected, there was a database access error, whatever that meant. Oskar was trying to reach out to Lavern Prime, which is the name Oskar had come up with for the brain in the satellite, but had been thus far unable to reach her.

That last time had been a bit of an “out of body” experience, and Erik wondered if Oskar was a little nervous to fully commit to reaching her.

Ahh, probably not. He’s got more guts than I thought possible. The very epitome of rising to the occasion.

[[ Don’t you dare tell him that. ]]

I’m gonna. And then I’m gonna tell him I love him just for you. It’s only gonna get sappier until you chill out with the cripple hating.

[[ How dare you! I have an entire module saved on sensitivity training. ]]

Oh yeah? Show me.

A second passed before the PUB answered.

[[ It seems it was accidentally deleted during my last memory defrag. ]]

A real quagmire, huh?

[[ … It would seem so. ]]

A soft pitter-patter behind him announced the arrival of Sara. The large calico rubbed her head up under the skeletal hand, uncaring where the attention she received came from, as long as it was given. She settled down in his lap, and Erik finally relaxed, looking out at the growing Collective.

A wave from a pack of Kobold cubs running by made him smile. Oskar was glad that the cat and the kids, at least, thought the skeletal arm was neat—and not at all a super creepy artifact stolen from Valla.

[[ Well, no one said it couldn’t be both. ]]Erik could feel Oskar watching him when he dropped his gaze back down to the arm, his brother’s curiosity almost tangible. He just wished he knew the answers himself.
