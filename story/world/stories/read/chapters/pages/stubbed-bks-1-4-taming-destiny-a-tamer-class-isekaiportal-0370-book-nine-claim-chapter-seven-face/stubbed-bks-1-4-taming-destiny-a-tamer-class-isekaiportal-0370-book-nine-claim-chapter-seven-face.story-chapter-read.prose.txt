A rain of arrows arches towards Kyrian’s ship, the closest to us and our target. I see them splash against the shield of the ship – not one makes it through. But at the same time, hope rises inside me – the shield is flickering, weak. It won’t take long to break it completely.

Maxen is clearly thinking along the same lines as he orders our archers to fire at will.

Ice is replaced by arrows as a new hailstorm takes place. And it’s not long before Kyrian responds, his fighters returning fire. Interestingly, the Air Mage doesn’t try to blow the arrows aside, even when some start piercing the shield as it begins to falter. Either they’re building up to something, or blowing the cloud away took all of their power, I guess.

I itch to do something, but, though I could use Air-Shaping to blow away the arrows, or even Fire-Shaping to burn up their shafts, that’s not my job. Even though my mana has all but regenerated itself, rather than using it for defence, I leave that to the shields and instead feed the extra to Aingeal. Even if the ships have been enchanted against easily catching light, we might need his help before this whole thing is over.

He whirls around our masts, the extra mana I’m feeding him making him even more restless than I am. But our enemy has many Water Mages – his main weakness. Like me, we need to save his strength for a moment where it will actually count.

Our ship sails towards Kyrian’s, both fast and slow at the same time. Though I could do with having eyes in the air, I don’t want Noir, Ivor, or Sirocco getting shot down. Again, I curse Kyrian for attacking us so soon after setting sail before we’ve managed to organise equipment for everyone.

From my position on the quarterdeck near the captain, I can see almost all the other ships – the only one I can’t spot is Bladestrike’s, hidden on the other side of Kyrian’s as it is. Long may that last – if Kyrian’s ship is between us and them, we won’t need to fear Bladestrike’s most famous Skill.

Sor’az’s ship has altered its trajectory to strike at House Siren’s with Valence. Layton’s ship, carrying the two cousins, is heading for Bladestrike’s – as lords of a Lesser House, they are wary about targeting a Great House heir, even if that heir attacked us first. Josiah’s ship, without its lord, is trailing in their wake to back them up.

Which leaves Elarion’s ship and my own to face off against Kyrian’s. And, as I note the way Elarion’s ship seems to be lagging behind a bit, I have to hope that that doesn’t mean we’ll be fighting this battle alone.

It seems to take forever to come level with Kyrian’s ship. Our forward-facing ballistas begin blasting as soon as we’re in range, each strike bringing an increasingly dim flare from the shield.

I have an uneasy feeling in my stomach. It’s been too easy. Where is the sea serpent Kyrian tried to bite me with? Or the myriad water tendrils that attempted to pull me into the sea? I know that our mages are controlling the water just around our ship, but still, even I can think of ways in which Kyrian could get around that – why hasn’t he tried? Is he not creative enough to have thought of it or is it something more?

Everyone who isn’t currently fighting, return to me, I command my non-human Companions. The humans have their duties – the fighters are in or preparing for combat; the non-combat crafters are tucked away deeper in the ship. But beasts like Bastet or Artemis are unable to fight unless we board a ship – or they board us. Yet they’re still out. If our shields fall, they will be vulnerable, and for no good reason.

The youngsters aren’t keen, Trouble and his little pack of raptorcats in particular, though Ivor objects strenuously too. But it’s too dangerous for them to be in the air right now, so I just send him a sense of pleading. He caves.

Bastet doesn’t like the idea of leaving me without anyone protecting me, but I remind her that she can come out whenever she wants – and I will call for them if there’s a situation where they can help.

Several of the people around me look rather wide-eyed as they see beast after beast run towards me, and disappear just as they brush against my skin. Finally, the samurans and Aingeal are my only non-human packmates not held in my spatial tattoo, and I sigh in relief. The uneasy knot in my stomach isn’t gone, but it’s loosened slightly knowing that they’re safe as long as I am.

Because Kyrian still hasn’t made a move. All he’s done is return fire – and he hasn’t even opened up with his own ballistas. Perhaps he doesn’t have forward-facing ones, but I doubt it.

Even as I watch, Kyrian’s ship’s shield fails and the ballista bolts stab into the ship’s planks, blackness spreading out from their tips which are covered in a potion designed to rot wood. A cheer goes up from the fighters below, but I can’t help feeling that something’s wrong.

I glance over at Captain Tolly. His expression is creased and worried.

“It’s too easy, isn’t it?” I ask tightly. The captain gives a short, sharp nod.

“Aye, my lord.” Then he glances quickly over at me. “It’s not too late to abort.”

I consider that for a moment, pressing my Intelligence stat to compress my thoughts into as short a time as possible.

We could abort, that’s true. Turning the ship at this point might make us vulnerable, but only for a short time until we’ve put enough sea between us and Kyrian.

But for what purpose? It feels like we’re heading into a trap, yes, but we can’t know what without springing it. Neither normal nor magical sight reveals anything. And what do we do next if we retreat now? Kyrian’s ship’s shields are down – if we give them too much time, they’ll start recovering.

And perhaps that’s his plan – to bluff us into retreating for no real reason, giving up our advantage. It would be smarter than Kyrian has presented himself to be so far, but perhaps he’s not the one making the calls.

“No,” I decide, gazing at Captain Tolly firmly. “This is our best chance. We’ll just have to prepare for it to potentially be a trap.”

“Aye, my lord,” the captain agrees, not letting on with his tone whether he thinks it a good idea or not.

Maxen’s voice calls up from below.

“Ballista crews! To your stations!”

I watch the mass of people scurry to their positions. Due to not having massive crews, the melee fighters do double duty, helping the actual sailors to man the ballistas since, if we’re boarded, the ballistas will be of little use. Meanwhile, the ranged fighters continue shooting arrows.

The combat mages, most of them young journeymen from the Guild of Mages, line up on the other side of the ship, already starting to pepper our enemy’s anti-magic shield with their fire bolts, air blades, force arrows, rock grenades, and other similar basic attacks. Thanks to the potion I dropped earlier, the magical shield is already weakened and it doesn’t take too long for those projectiles to start landing. I note that the ships obviously have fire treating since even the fireballs do little apart from creating an explosion of light and heat.

And then we’re broadside. We’re faced with more ballistas and cannons than we sport, and yet, still, Kyrian doesn’t attack.

Sir?

I hesitate for a fraction of a second.

Do it.

“Fire!” Maxen cries as soon as I give permission and the ballistas open up almost simultaneously.

Eighteen bolts strike Kyrian’s ship simultaneously….and rebound, striking our own ship, deflected by our own shield and weakening it at the same time.

“What?!” I exclaim. Captain Tolly swears.

“Bastard must have an emergency shield!” he grinds out angrily.

I curse too. The only good thing about this is that an emergency shield is, by nature of how quickly it can be erected, never very long-lasting.

He’s got an emergency shield! I tell Maxen, though the man has probably arrived at the same conclusion. We need to break it – fire at will.

Yes, sir, Maxen agrees grimly and gives the orders. In the background, I’m aware of Loran communicating what’s happening with us to the other ships. Just as well – I don’t have the focus to do that as Kyrian has just made his next move.

Those ballistas and cannons, previously so quiescent, are now striking with abandon. Kyrian doesn’t seem to care that more than half the projectiles are rebounding off our own shields and onto his, doing some of our job for us. And that worries me because a strategist who lures us into a trap like this is not someone who’s likely to overlook such important details. Which indicates that either Kyrian’s emergency shield is stronger than we’ve giving it credit for – or Kyrian has something else up his sleeve.

My eyes scan the deck of his ship until they snag on a well-dressed figure standing on the opposite quarterdeck. There!

Kyrian seems to have been looking for me too because we lock eyes almost immediately. A malicious grin is on his face, and it expands as he sees me watching. Exaggerating his movement, he mouths words at me and a chill goes down my spine. Now, you die.

Suddenly, the ship rocks violently beneath my feet. Cries ring out from all around, and curses from the captain who keeps his feet only by gripping the wheel. Loran slams into me and we both go down.

What’s happening with the mages? I demand in the Bond network. They’re supposed to stop this from happening.

They’re trying! Alyna’s scared voice replies. But they’re being overwhelmed! And something worse is coming!

I don’t need her to tell me that. Open-mouthed, I stare at the shape that’s taken form on the port side of the ship.

“Damn, that’s a big snake!” I exclaim.

Rising out of the water is a shape that’s almost the size of the steam dragon Torrent senior used in the fight near the rift. Its tooth-laden head batters against our anti-magic shields near where the mages are lined up. Though it’s kept at bay for now, I don’t blame them for stumbling away, their faces pale.

And then another barrage rocks the ship towards the serpent and my blood goes cold as the shields collapse – both of them.

That shouldn’t happen! I exclaim to myself – or perhaps in the Bond network as the cacophony of concerned voices explodes.

“Lord Torrent supplied his son with a shield-breaker,” Sarran concludes grimly. I wonder briefly where the hell he just came from, but don’t spend any time asking stupid questions.

For now, the problem is that we are now open to attack on both sides – Kyrian to starboard, and this massive sea serpent to port.

Rage wells up inside me. If Kyrian thinks it’s over, he ain’t seen nothing yet.
