I jab a thumb toward Cyros. “Talk to him. He’s the one who needs your help.”

Álvaro glances at Cyros with mild curiosity before looking back to me. “Why are you here, then?”

“Because he needs to find an animal and I think you can help him find it.” I throw my hands up in the air. “Why am I even talking to you? Cyros, this is your job.”

Álvaro rests his cheek on a fist. “If you want me to track something down, I don’t know if it’s something I can help you with.”

“What?” I squint at his stats. “But your Class is literally Tracker! And your Role is Monster Hunter!”

Álvaro shrugs. “Tracker just gives me some good camouflage spells and hunting skills. And my Role means I randomly have to go on animal-type quests. Retrieve eggs, or collect shed skin, or whatever. I can’t magically manifest whatever animal it is you’re after.” He tips his head at me, amused. “What kind of janky Class is Culinary Rogue, anyway? Do you assassinate vegetables?”

I glare at him. “Keep trying my patience and you’ll get to find out.”

Álvaro grins, turning to Cyros. “Is she always this angry?”

“Pretty much,” he admits.

“Hey!”

“So what animal are you after, anyway?” Álvaro asks, ignoring my objection. “If it’s something I’ve come across recently I could point you in the right direction.”

“We need to find a lindwyrm,” Cyros says.

Álvaro laughs.

Cyros and I don’t.

Surprise slowly dawns over Álvaro’s face. “Wait, you for real?”

“We are, yes,” Cyros says. “And it needs to be an adult. At least ten meters.”

Álvaro snorts. “That’s suicidal. I’ve seen one of those snakes from a hundred meters away and it felt too close. You know they’re basically dragons, right?”

“Technically, they are dragons,” Cyros says, unable to resist an opportunity to be technically correct. “Just wingless and with only two front legs.”

“Yeah, and equipped with dozens of razor-sharp teeth in a head twice the size of an alligator,” Álvaro says. “Oh, and they spit poison.”

“Do they?” I ask, perking up. “That could be pretty useful. I wonder if I could collect any to experiment with.”

Álvaro looks at me like I’m crazy. What? It’s not like I said I was going to drink any of the poison.

Although, I probably will. Gotta keep increasing my poison resistance.

“So you have seen one?” Cyros asks, trying to steer the conversation back on topic. “Even if you could just tell us where, that would be incredibly helpful.”

Álvaro sucks on a lip. “I mean, if you guys really wanna go throw yourselves into a serpent’s nest, I’m not going to stop you. But…” He abruptly trails off, his gaze going unfocused.

Cyros and I exchange a look.

“Uh, Álvaro?” I prompt after he’s quiet for a few seconds.

The boy abruptly shakes his head, dragging his hand down his face with a groan. “Are you kidding me?”

“Is something wrong?” Cyros asks.

“Yeah,” Álvaro grumbles, refocusing on us with a glare—there’s no real heat behind the look, though. “Just got a new Quest for my Role Requirement. Looks like this conversation triggered it. So thanks for that.”

“Role Requirement?” Cyros repeats, glancing between us.”’

That’s going to take way too long to explain. “What’s the Quest?” I ask.

Álvaro gives me a flat look. “Take a wild guess.”

I grin. “So you’re going to help, then?”

The boy sighs. “Doesn’t look like I’ve got much of a choice in the matter.”

Cyros tips his head at the two of us, clearly confused.

“Explain later,” I tell him before quickly turning back to Álvaro. “So how does your Role work?”

“In this case, I’m supposed to ‘Lead the assassins to the lindwyrm’s den.’” He blinks. “Are you guys really assassins?”

“And where’s the den?” I ask, brushing past that last question. “How will you lead us there?”

“I’ve got a dot on a map that tells me where to go,” Álvaro admits. “Though it doesn’t give me any info about the terrain between here and there, so finding it might be annoying. Okay but seriously: You’re an assassin? Have you killed anyone?”

“How is he doing that?” Cyros asks me.

“Both of you are getting off track,” I huff.

The boys stare at me suspiciously, narrowed eyes.

“Fine!” I fold my arms. “Yes I’ve killed some people—”

“More than one?” Álvaro cries.

“—and he can Check people and learn information about them through this weird magic System we both have access to,” I tell Cyros.

After a moment of silence, he says, “I have more questions.”

Álvaro raises a hand. “Same here.”

I let out a long sigh. “Do we have to have this conversation while standing around in an alley?”

“Good point.” Álvaro hops off the crate he’d been seated on. “My house is much more comfortable. And we’re gunna have to talk about this lindwyrm Quest I have to do now—thanks again for that. Come on; my place isn’t far.”

I hesitate. “You’re inviting us to your house? Just like that?”

Álvaro appears surprised by the question. “I mean, yeah? I’m stuck with you guys at least for this Quest. And we’re both Travelers, so we’re cool, right? I mean,” he adds, glancing between me and Cyros with a hint of impishness. “Unless you’re planning to assassinate me. In that case, you’re uninvited.”

“I’d appreciate it if you wouldn’t keep bringing that up in public,” Cyros says, glancing around the alley, as if his master might be hiding somewhere nearby. I mean, given they have a camouflage spell, too, that’s entirely possible. Suddenly I’m also keen on moving this conversation behind a locked door.

Álvaro snorts. “An abandoned alley isn’t exactly public.” But he waves for us to follow. “Come on. This way!”

Somehow, this all went better and worse than I was expecting. With little else to do, Cyros and I follow.

Álvaro’s place is about as different as you could possibly get from mine. He’s got a large flat built halfway up the mesa, the rooms filled with glass windows that look down on a breathtaking view of the city. His house has several rooms, and each one is decorated with fancy yet mismatching furniture. The room we’re currently seated in features a low table with a golden wyvern embossed in the surface. The floor pillows are fluffy and bright red. A painting featuring two moons over a dusk desert landscape hangs on one wall, while an array of spears and weapons are mounted to another. It all looks like the thrown-together decorations of a teenager who has more money than sense.

Honestly, I’m kind of jealous.

“First thing’s first,” Álvaro says, gesturing for us to help ourselves to the plate of snacks that he’d tossed together. They’re all things I haven’t seen before, mostly because I cook for myself, given my Role. “We’re going to need a lot of money for this trip.”

I raise an eyebrow, plucking something that looks like a dried flower off of the plate. “Why’s that?” After Checking it to make sure it’s nothing suspicious, I pop the blossom into my mouth. The petals melt like cotton candy, lightly sweet and tangy. Holy crap. This is delicious.

“Because it’s far away,” Álvaro says, grabbing a handful of what look like actual rocks out of a bowl and tossing them into his mouth, one by one. Echo assures me these are actually rock-candied dates, but I remain unconvinced.

“We’re going to need supplies,” Álvaro says, his mouth still partly full. Cyros winces. “Given it’ll be about a week to get there, we’re going to need to rent a star drake to carry everything. Water will be the biggest problem.” He swallows. “The other option is to rent three footbirds and cut the travel time down to a couple days, which will use less supplies, but that’ll be way more expensive.”

Cyros glances around the gaudy apartment. “Money doesn’t seem to be a problem for you.”

Álvaro nearly chokes on his rock candy. “You can’t just say that. But yeah.” He grins. “Hunting down rare creatures makes a lot of money. Buuuuut,” he adds, his gaze drifting away from ours, “I sort of spend it as I make it. So I don’t have a lot on me right now—and unless you guys are offering to pay, this trip is going to put me even deeper in the hole.”

I can kind of relate. Kind of. Even though I’m not spending all my money on dumb (but admittedly kind of cool) stuff like crystal salt shakers in the shape of dragons, I basically spend everything I make from the Guild on more potion and poison supplies. My house is bare as a result, but my Apothecary is stuffed full.

“Why not just stick the supplies in your Inventory?” I ask him. “That would save needing to rent a pack animal.”

“Because we only have one Inventory slot,” Álvaro says, rolling his eyes. “Duh. Putting one water skin in there isn’t going to make much of a difference.”

I stare at him. I look at Cyros. Then I reach a hand out to the side, away from the table, and take my Apothecary out of my Inventory.

Álvaro yelps as the giant spread of potion equipment covers almost his entire living room floor—and it’s a big living room.

“I should be able to make some adjustments to add in some more straps for whatever food we might need,” I say, scratching my chin as I look over all my belongings. “And I guess I could leave some of the alchemy equipment behind for the trip, as long as I make enough potions in advance. Water might be a bit trickier to store. How much do you think we’ll need?”

Álvaro is leaning over the Apothecary, eyes wide. “What is this? How did you do this?”

“What,” I ask, unable to keep the smugness out of my tone. “You didn’t know you could put multiple things in one Inventory slot? I thought the solution was a bit obvious.”

To my slight disappointment, Álvaro is too excited to be annoyed by my sarcasm. “This is amazing! Can I do this, too? It would be a total game changer.”

“I don’t see why not,” Cyros says. “If you two share the same spatial power, I would assume it would operate the same way for both of you.” He glances at me sidelong. “But I don’t know much about this magic System of yours.”

“Yeah?” Álvaro finally looks up from my Apothecary. “Well, I can tell you whatever you want.”

Cyros is silent for a moment. “Really?”

“I mean, sure,” Álvaro says. “Why not?”

Cyros looks back at me.

I shift uncomfortably beneath the stare. “It was because of the god thing,” I object. “Widengra was looking for people like me. I couldn’t tell anyone—I couldn’t risk it getting out what I am. It wasn’t safe.” For me or for those I told.

“God thing?” Álvaro asks. “Who’s Widengra?”

Oh boy. Where do I even start with that? “You do know the gods are looking for us, right?”

Álvaro frowns, scratching his ear. “Nye said something about that, I think. They saw a champion take another Traveler. But I haven’t seen anything myself. And hey, aren’t you a champion?”

I hunch my shoulders, as if that could cover up my stats. “That’s—that’s totally different.”

“How’d you get to be a champion, anyway?” he asks.

“It wasn’t my choice,” I snap. “And I don’t want to talk about it. It’s irrelevant, because I’m not going to be his champion, anyway. But what is important is that Widengra’s sent two of his champions to this city, and if they find out you’re a Traveler, too, they’ll try to kill you as well.”

Álvaro raises a skeptical eyebrow. “Nye said the one they saw was just taken. Why do you think these ones want to kill you?”

“Because his last champion tried to,” I snarl, squeezing my fingers into the pillow I’m seated on. “So I killed her right back.”

Álvaro glances between me and Cyros, as if to get confirmation I’m serious about this. Then he sits back, blowing out a breath. “You really weren’t kidding about having killed someone.”

I deflate, gaze falling back to the table of snacks. I halfheartedly grab another blossom. “No. I don’t want to talk about it.”

The room is silent for a long moment.

“Well,” Álvaro says, “I guess that’s all the more reason to get outside the city for a couple days, right? Get away from these champions you mentioned.”

“Yeah,” I say, perking up. Actually, not having to worry about them for a week or two sounds like a nice break. And if these lindwyrms really do have poison abilities, maybe I’ll be able to grab something on this journey that will help me fight them when I get back. “So, you were saying we need a lot of water. How much do you think?”

After that the mood picks back up. Cyros calculates how much we’ll need to travel in the desert; Álvaro estimates the round trip will be ten days, but Cyros suggests we should bring extra to be safe. We can save some water just by the fact that all our supplies will be stored in an Inventory, so the trek will be far less laborious for us, but again, Cyros prefers to play it safe.

In the end he estimates we’ll need around three hundred liters of water, just for drinking, which is about enough to fill a large bathtub. That’s a whole logistical issue all by itself.

Food is made much simpler with my Apothecary; we can keep it from spoiling, and store raw ingredients, instead of pre-dried rations, since I’ll be cooking every day, anyway. And of course, adding in tent supplies won’t be a problem.

Álvaro has a ten liter water skin that he’s used for previous day trips, so I fill it up and try storing it in my Apothecary. It works!

But will it work for twenty-nine more? At what point does an Apothecary stop being an Apothecary and start being a giant pile of water bags all tied together?

That’s something we can’t test until we have twenty-nine more bags. For now, however, we have a plan. Tomorrow we’ll go shopping.
