Noir is flapping hard, but we seem to only be drawn further into the behemoth’s mouth with every wingbeat. I feel his panic as if it’s physical heat, radiating from his side of the Bond.

I can’t– I can’t get us out! he exclaims, and his words create a rebounding wave of anxiety across the Bond network as those who are listening in closely hear him. It’s holding on too tightly!

Try using Quick Grab on the air ahead of you, I tell him urgently, then feel the ripple go through him as he follows my instructions.

It’s not working! he reports in a fearful tone. There’s nothing to grab onto!

And you’re already using Flight?

It’s pushed to its limits, he confirms.

I clench my jaw, my mind racing through the options. I don’t have much mana left, and I don’t dare take another mana potion, not yet. My companions are all out of mana too – either they’ve been using it actively, or have been feeding it to those of us who were. But what I do have, I pour into Noir’s wings as air-magic, trying to offer him something to push against.

It helps – briefly. But we barely make any headway in the seconds before I run dry. Then we begin losing ground once more.

Frustration and fear rush through me, my hands shaking from a combination of emotions and potion overuse. I pull out a mana-recovery potion and hesitate. If I suck this back, I could damage my Core – again. Potion overuse isn’t something to play around with, and the more powerful the potion, the more detrimental the effects of using too much of it. The potions I’ve been using have definitely been on the more powerful side.

My determination firms. I’ve healed my Core before. I have a lot less hope about being able to survive a Tier five’s mouth.

But maybe there’s something I can try before taking the drastic step of risking my Core shattering while on a journey that has already proven to be even more dangerous than I was expecting.

Growing my air-wings with transformation thankfully takes very little mana – no more than I have to give. Wrapping one arm around Noir’s neck. I fly forwards with him. The relief of my weight definitely makes an impact to his ability to fly. My hope flickers to life.

A moment later, that flame dies again. Though we’re making some progress, we’ll tire long before we escape the trap. I swallow, then clutch onto Noir’s neck like a lifeline, closing my eyes.

Meditation is hard to grasp in such a threatening situation. Only months of practice enable me to succeed. I cling onto the peace and calm it gives me like it’s a stick and I’m a drowning man. My head clears only briefly, but it’s enough. I’ve remembered something that might save our lives.

Like I once did when facing an angry Kalanthia, I reach for the edges of my soul and press at them with my Willpower. It’s been a while since I did this, and I’m taken aback by how much easier it is to extend my soul beyond my body than the last time I tried this. If not for the intense pressure of the Tier five’s domain, so much heavier here in its maw than on the ship, I might even be able to extend it out a metre or two. As it is, I just about manage to make a bubble around myself.

If the Tier five were focusing its attention on me, the protective balloon I’m blowing would be popped within an instant, I don’t kid myself that it wouldn’t. But the Tier five’s attention is fixed elsewhere. We are gnats to it – and long may that last.

Already, I feel the effects of my bubble. The air is less cloying, and is easier to fly through. I’m still flying against the wind, but now I’ve got a windshield that deflects its power around me rather than directly at me.

The problem is that Noir is still affected, and dragging him through the air with all his wind-resistance is robbing me of most of the benefits of my bubble.

I’m slowing you down. You should leave me, he tells me with fear and worry intertwining in our Bond.

I’m not leaving you! I promise fiercely, determined to get us both out.

Then I almost lose control of my soul-shield and curse myself as an idiot when I remember something important.

You don’t need to fly.

Realisation and then incredulous exasperation go through Noir’s side of the Bond. He vanishes from the sky – our strategy against the roc is still useful. I feel him settle inside my tattoo – he’s weightless, but the responsibility for getting us both out of this weighs heavily on my shoulders.

Free of encumbrance, I press my soul-shield just a little larger so it fully encompasses my wings, and then fly through the air almost as easily as I would without the presence of the Tier five.

Relief shoots through me as I pass first over the leading wing-tip that is just about entering the beast’s maw, then over the wing, and then over the ridge of ice itself. I’m relieved to note that we’ve calculated the rock and ice spur lengths correctly – unless it twists at the last moment, it should catch at the corners of the Tier five’s mouth.

I arrow towards my ship, feeling the reciprocal relief of my Bonded at our narrow escape. Glancing back at the behemoth’s mouth, I shudder – we’re all far, far too close to that world-ending maw. And we’ll all have to get closer yet.

Landing on the wooden deck, relief ripples through me and I have to grab on to the railing before my legs collapse from under me. It’s not only relief that weakens me, and when I release my soul-shield, I feel an ache deep within that I’d been refusing to acknowledge. Even my mana channels ache – I’ve pushed myself too far.

I had no choice I tell myself firmly, though resolve to take some time to recover after this. Assuming there is an after.

Loran rushes up to me and I don’t waste any more time on my own condition. There are more important things right now.

“You made it,” he breathes, almost collapsing in relief himself.

“How are the preparations?” I demand and he straightens up, his face falling into an expression of professionalism he must have learned from Sarran.

“Captain Garrous’ ship is linked in at the head, Lord Elarion’s behind to provide power, and ours behind theirs. On the other side, Lord Josiah is leading Lord Layton’s ship, and Captain Sor’az is towing Lord Valence’s. Everyone is in place and anchor chains have been fixed.”

I nod in relief.

“And the Core?”

“Ivor returned it to the carcass after he had returned Lord Elarion to his ship.”

“Good. Are all the flyers and Air Mages redeployed?”

“They have shifted positions and as many have taken potions who can. We need Noir to join Ivor, though. That might make the difference for us,” He’s anxious, as well he might be. Our ship is the most at risk in this matter, largely because we still can’t steer for ourselves, and we have little power to propel ourselves.

“Of course,” I agree. I call out the black alcaoris and he quickly rematerialises on deck. “Do you have enough energy to help pull us?” I ask him, eyeing him as if to remind him to be honest.

I am fatigued, but we are no longer in the Sovereign’s immediate area. I can fly, Noir replies with determination, and then we feel the backdraft of his wings as he leaps up to join his brother in the air above us.

“Take a rope with you!” Loran yells at him, waving the end of one in the air. Noir quickly swoops back down to grab it.

I can’t help eyeing the little space that remains between the body of the roc’s carcass and that disintegrating darkness.

“Is there anything else left to do?” I ask when Loran is focusing back on me.

“Do you have more magic to give?” he asks me unusually critically. Then, at my startled look. “I felt how little you had left when you were trapped in the beast’s mouth.” He swallows, fear flashing through his eyes. “I wasn’t the only one who feared you would be lost.”

I follow his gaze to where all my female feline Bonded are currently standing on the quarterdeck. As they sense my gaze, Bastet pauses her pacing and the three others look up to join her in glaring at me. I swallow as I realise that I’ve managed to anger all of them at the same time.

Then I pull my eyes away from them and focus back on my manservant.

“Not right now,” I admit. “And I don’t dare take a potion. But if I Meditate for a bit, I should recover at least something.”

“Then go rest,” he suggests in as close to an order as I’ve ever heard from him. He casts an apprehensive glance over to starboard. “We don’t have much time. The Mages indicate we’ll make impact within the next ten flames.”

I nod at him and then watch as he hurries off. Not only is he in charge of coordinating my companions, but with Sarran needing to step up to take over the organisation duties of the captain, Loran is also coordinating all the mages and fighters. Not that the latter have much of a role right now unless they’re Classers who are capable of somehow helping us move forward.

I hesitate for a moment over where to go to Meditate. The quarterdeck will keep me close to the action without being in the way, but if I go there, I’ll have to face the raptorcats and nundas. Maybe I should go to my cabin and avoid this. At least if we’re eaten, I’ll escape the lecture, I think to myself with gallows humour.

Then again, if I do that and we survive, the lecture will be so much worse.

Glancing over to the quarterdeck, I see Bastet pacing, her feathers bristling with agitation. My concern for her makes up my mind.

Climbing up the stairs, I frown as I see Bastet’s condition up close. Something doesn’t feel right.

“Bastet?” I ask. She glances at me, then snarls and leaps at me. Surprised, I don’t dodge the attack and shortly find the wind knocked out of me as I land back-first on the newly-repaired planks of the deck.

You were reckless! she hisses at me. You almost died!

“I came back as soon as I realised we had got that close to the mouth,” I defend myself, though I know I shouldn’t – her anger comes out of her own helplessness to help me.

You weren’t listening! We were shouting at you and you weren’t listening!

My frown deepens. Anger at me putting myself in danger is one thing, but the intensity of her emotions are beyond unusual for Bastet. She’s actually trembling, her eyes wide and staring. When I reach out to stroke her head, she flinches for a moment before pressing back into my hand.

“Hey, what’s wrong?” I ask gently, shifting up to a sitting position. She allows me to do so, then springs away from my lap and resumes her pacing after I’ve stroked her a few more times. Reaching through the Bond, I sense that she’s a tumult of emotions. Fear, anger, worry, love, and affection form a maelstrom that allows no single emotion much room to be expressed. All she seems able to do is to pace, nervous energy bleeding out with every footfall, only to be replaced a moment later.

She doesn’t respond to my question, so I look up at the others. Ninja and Lathani keep alternating glances between Bastet and me, their body language clearly expressing their concern and lingering anger at what they saw as my recklessness. Kalanthia meets my gaze with her usual calmness, though I can see by the tense lines in her body that she’s not as comfortable with the situation as she appears at first glance.

“Kalanthia?” I prompt. “Do you know?”

The great cat hesitates, and then dips her head fractionally.

The Core was too strong for her and she took too much. I was not here to counsel her, and by the time I realised what she had done, it was too late.

I curse as her explanation sinks in. Closing my eyes, I sink my mind into Bastet’s Core space – by this point, I know it well enough that I don’t even have to touch her.

Inside, it’s like she’s been electrified. I see exactly what Kalanthia means – Bastet has far too much foreign Energy in her system, and I can sense her battle to assimilate it. The movement of her frenetic pacing is her only way of maintaining control, and even then it’s only just. I don’t know what will happen if she allows the Energy to run riot, but I can guess – Bastet’s internal matrix being corrupted beyond redemption might be the best potential outcome in that case.

I curse again, but try to keep it from the raptorcat – I can understand her urge to take as much of the Core as she could before it had to be returned, but the timing leaves a good deal to be desired. And she wanted to lecture me about being reckless!

Guilt rapidly replaces the anger – I very much doubt she intended on this happening. Just like I didn’t intend on ending up in the beast’s maw.

Sunk deeply within Bastet’s system, I do my best to help smooth the jagged Energy that rips at her channels, help Bastet redirect it into growing her internal matrix in ways that would have otherwise taken weeks to achieve. Risk and reward, as ever, go hand in hand. If she succeeds in riding the wave of this Energy, she’ll be so much further ahead.

Calming a little thanks to my intervention, Bastet copies what I’m doing and slowly, we start to see the tidal wave rushing through her internal matrix diminish. But the world around us hasn’t stopped, and Kalanthia and Ninja both pull at me, urgency in their Bonds.

Surfacing, I see that the behemoth is once more looming over me, the sky blotted out by the blackness of its mouth’s interior. Still just settling back into my body, I’m sent stumbling as the ship’s progress is abruptly brought to a halt.

My heart leaps into my mouth.

There’s no more time.
