Jonathan Ames awoke to the sight of a sterile white ceiling above him, and the distinct awareness of restraints on his arms and legs. His eyes squinted for a moment against the light, and his head shifted thereafter, immediately assessing his environment as his tactical training overrode his momentary alarm.

Square room. Single door. Five other beds; three left, two right. No visual on faces. Team status unknown. No guards.

His eyes shifted quickly to the other beds once more and he strained against his bindings, testing their fidelity and finding them offensively strong. Not even the subtle enhancements that the US Government had given him for physiological superiority in battle managed to affect them, and he quickly ceased wasting the energy.

Instead, he paid more attention to the beds, focusing on those upon them. All he could see were limbs and bindings where they were necessary. The occupants themselves were blocked by machines on either side of the beds, restricting visual confirmation as to whether or not it was his team or unknowns.

Five beds? Can’t be a coincidence. Hail Mary time.

“Sound off,” Jon said on instinct, glancing between the beds.

“Trident Two, Yamato,” Zack said immediately, sending a ripple of relief through Jon.

“Trident Three, Williams,” DeShaun answered next, his voice hoarse but otherwise hale.

“Trident Four, Sampson,” Gabriel responded next, wheezing slightly as he spoke.

“Trident Five, Ivanov,” Aleksei chimed in next, coughing as he did.

“Trident Six, Rosen,” Michael sounded off last with a hollow voice.

“Good to hear you, brothers,” Jon said without preamble, testing his restraints again and grimacing. “Anyone been awake long enough for a SitRep?”

“We’ve been here for hours, boss,” Zack said immediately, his voice a mix of frustration and resignation both. “Maxwell patched up what his responders could and staunched the wounds. My—my hands are gone, skip. They did something to Aleksei and DeShaun to save them from suffocating on their own blood, and Rosen’s lost all four limbs below the elbows and knees. Gabe’s got no legs, and you might be the only one that isn’t completely fucked.”

“A-Firm,” Michael said in a resigned voice. “I’m a fuckin’ potato, boss. I can slug crawl and that’s about it. I’m dead weight.”

“No man left behind, Mikey,” DeShaun growled, and then wheezed around the words. “Navy can get you bionics or cloned parts. You’re not out of it forever.”

“That’s assuming we can even find a way to exfil, DeShaun,” Aleksei croaked.

“Where there’s a will, there’s a way, Aleksei.”

“You and that positivity bullshit, el-tee…” Michael said around what sounded like a bitter smile.

“We’re out of the fight when we’re dead, boys,” Zack put in grimly. “I don’t feel dead yet.”

“Hooyah, Two,” Gabriel said with quiet determination, despite his wounds.

“First step is to figure out where the hell we are, boys,” Jon said. “After that, we can—”

His words cut off when the single door to the room opened, and Adrian Maxwell IV walked through it, accompanied by Fiona Hayes, E2’s CFO. The golden-haired CEO and his financial lieutenant glanced across the awakened operators and then exchanged a look before Adrian stepped forward, casually rolling the same platinum coin across his knuckles which Jon had seen him turn into an impossibly sharp bastard sword.

“Gentlemen,” Adrian greeted them, his eyes shifting from one SEAL to the next. “I am glad to see you all survived. Even my medics were uncertain you would manage it.”

“Fuck you, psycho,” Michael said immediately, rage replacing his despondence. “You put us in these fucking beds!”

“As a result of your uninvited incursion into my facility, yes,” Adrian said in an unruffled voice, glancing at Michael without offense or anger. If anything, there was an almost sympathetic look on the trillionaire’s face—one that unnerved Jon more than gloating or smug superiority would have. It was easy to understand that sort of reaction. The one Adrian was giving them was anything but normal.

“So what now, Maxwell?” Jon asked before his brothers could hasten their own demise. “What’s your endgame? You can’t keep us here forever.”

“Well, given that you are all written off as KIA, Commander, I daresay I can,” Adrian replied calmly, vanishing the coin into his grip and settling his hands into his pockets. “You will find no aid from Admiral Halsey or the Neptune command bunker, either, I am afraid. Sterling was quite displeased by the good Admiral’s choice of intrusion. I fear he was quite precise in eliminating any chance of a repeat offense.”

“I don’t know what you’re—”

Adrian cut Jon off before he could finish.

“No need for that, Commander Ames. I am fully and exhaustingly aware of your SERE training and denial protocols. I can only tell you that Neptune and everyone of influence associated to it is quite dead, and allow you to come to your own conclusions as to what that means for your circumstances. I do have an offer for you, though I doubt you are all in the right frame of mind to hear it.”

“They will need to be, Your Grace,” Fiona said before anyone else could speak, lifting her tablet and idly tapping the holopad. “The Revenant-King only has a few days before the crusade. We cannot afford to wait for the Commander and his men to adjust to the inevitable.”

Adrian sighed at Fiona’s words, but didn’t seem bothered at all by the disagreement. If anything, he looked resigned and accepting all at once. Jon filed that away—and especially filed away the strange way she’d addressed him.

She talks to him like a ruler, not a boss. European nobility, perhaps? That wasn’t in the files.

“What the hell are you talking about, lady?” Gabriel demanded. “What crusade? This is the twenty-first century!”

“And what about our families?” Zack asked immediately. “If we’re deemed KIA, are you—”

“I have no designs on your families, Lieutenant Commander. I disabled your team because you forced my hand through your actions. You are not my enemies, you are simply foes of circumstance. Your families are safe, informed of your apparent death, and will be left untouched to grieve.”

“How can we trust that?” DeShaun asked.

“You cannot,” Adrian said without evident concern. “But your lack of trust does not make it less true. To you, I may be a monster, Lieutenant—to me, conversely, you are useful but ultimately irrelevant unless you choose to accept certain facts, which Fiona will now illustrate.”

“Primitives…” Fiona muttered in derision before swiping her hand on her tablet obediently and throwing a three-dimensional holographic projection into the center of the room. It showed an immense city, one created from a gothic template and black marble, though the design of it was strangely alien in a way Jon couldn’t properly identify. More than that, it looked… wrong… for any city on Earth.

“Kakogo cherta…?” Aleksei muttered, shifting against the restraints in his bed.

“This, gentlemen, is Blackstone,” Adrian said without preamble, stepping forward with his hands still in his pockets. “It is a city on the south-western peninsula of the main landmass of Eternus.”

“That’s the name of your game,” Jon said immediately, remembering it from both his briefing and the relentless news coverage preceding its launch. “Why are you showing us a game location, Maxwell?”

“Ignorant…” Fiona muttered again, though Adrian only glanced at Jon with quiet pity.

“I suppose this would make more sense if you were fully informed, Commander, though I confess that I doubt the truth alone will satisfy you. I will attempt elucidation regardless, and perhaps you will show greater flexibility than Fiona believes possible.”

“I’m all ears,” Jon said warily, looking from the beautiful-but-hostile CFO to Adrian.

“Eternus is a game in the eyes of Earth, yes,” Adrian continued without preamble, smiling faintly as he spoke. “To you, and your families, and to ninety-nine percent of the population, it is little more than the most realistic full-dive immersion game ever created. That, of course, is a lie. Eternus, gentlemen, is not a game—Eternus is a world. The true world. It is the anchor-point of this cosmos, the central planet upon which the universal constants that govern this entire reality are housed.”

“You expect us to believe that your video game is real? Are you nuts?” DeShaun growled.

“No more deranged than a man who saw his comrades disassembled by an unassuming CEO in a business suit, I wager, Lieutenant Williams,” Adrian said calmly, looking toward DeShaun before turning his gaze across all six of them. “These are the facts, Trident: Eternus is real. Eternus Online is nothing more than a cleverly worded cover to obscure that truth from the everyday public. Every single person on Earth entering an immersion pod is, in fact, entering Eternus itself through an Eidos the System which governs reality creates for them.”

“You’re talking about Transmigration,” Gabriel said abruptly, speaking again for the first time in a while and shifting on his bed. “Isekai. Portal fantasy. You’re talking about fiction, Maxwell.”

Jon glanced toward his injured subordinate’s bed and then back to Adrian when Gabriel finished, searching the CEO’s face carefully. To his annoyance, surprise, and mild uncertainty, he saw nothing. No twitch, no grimace, not even a flicker. Nothing Gabriel had said seemed to have left an impact on the tycoon.

“To you, that is a plausible way to define it, I suppose,” Adrian answered instead. “I assure you, however, that such concepts are not mere fiction, Lieutenant Sampson. They are built on true experiences. Did you believe that I subordinated the United States Government, the Congress, the Supreme Court, the Governors, and the policy-makers and secret societies of Earth because I wanted a video game to succeed? I am not the Mars-obsessed simpleton of the twenties and thirties, Lieutenant—I am an Eternian myself.”

“We both are,” Fiona put in, her voice scathing. “And we are also wasting time. His Grace is offering you patience against my advice, Trident. I was in favor of skipping this entire exercise and forcing you to face reality in less pleasant ways. You should appreciate His Grace deciding to take a more gentle approach.”

“What the f—”

“Enough!” Jon cut in before Aleksei could finish. “Let him speak, Trident. We’re in no position to piss them off.”

“Boss is right,” Zack followed up immediately. “Listen for now, brothers. We can spit at them later.”

A grunt followed from Aleksei, then DeShaun, and finally from Gabriel and Michael as well—not pleased, not happy, but falling into the old rhythm of trusting their leadership.

“Thank you, Commander, Lieutenant Commander,” Adrian said mildly, and turned back to the hologram. “As I was saying, Eternus is not a video game. The abilities I demonstrated when I defanged your assault on me were not gimmicks using technology, either. I was wielding Dark Mana. My coin turned into a Revenant Runeblade. I was born on Earth, yes—I am not from Earth. I am an Eternian, as are Fiona and Sterling, as are thousands of my employees. I am an alien, gentlemen, and I want to go home.”

Silence greeted his words, and Jon felt his mind spinning. He didn’t believe Adrian, not really; it would be complete insanity to believe a word of it. He’d need far more drugs, far less logic, and a hell of a lot more exposure to unhinged internet message boards to believe a word of it—but there was one detail that had nagged at him, and that was how Adrian had so easily dispatched his team. The way he’d moved, the way his coin had shifted not just shape but mass and even what appeared to be material composition, and the way he’d hit with such force that men twice his mass had been thrown like ragdolls.

There were a lot of things science and technology could explain, but the force of Adrian’s hits and the mass disconnect between the coin and sword were not among them. Nanotechnology was extremely advanced compared to his father’s time growing up in the early 2020s, but nothing was like what Adrian had demonstrated.

When all rational explanations become inadequate, all that remains are the irrational alternatives.

“Okay, Maxwell,” Jon said into the skeptical silence. “For argument’s sake, let’s say we believe you. You’re from a world called Eternus, you wield magic, and we walked into your facility uninvited and you took umbrage with that. Tell me this, then: why the game? Why have people log in to go full murderhobo on your homeworld instead of going there yourself and leaving Earth behind?”

“That, Commander, is the correct question,” Adrian said with a faint smile, and nodded to Fiona, who swiped her hand and changed the hologram to feature the immense chamber Trident had originally encountered Adrian within. The tycoon smiled at the image. “This, gentlemen, is D.E.U.S.”

“God?” Gabriel asked skeptically.

“Not the way you think of it,” Adrian answered calmly without looking away from the hologram. “D.E.U.S. stands for Direct Eternian Uplink System. It is an extension of the omniscient System that governs and controls Eternus’s reality, and acts as a local node for that System here on Earth.”

The CEO looked back toward them as he continued.

“The point of the game, Trident, is to bridge the two worlds—not simply to allow visitation, but to create a conjunction. When enough Earth-born souls reach the requisite levels in Eternus, when enough of those souls are ‘playing the game’, and when enough synchronization occurs between D.E.U.S. and the main System, Earth will undergo a kind of bridging event. We call it the Revelation.”

“Sounds biblical,” Zack said carefully. “We read reports about something called ‘Genesis’, too.”

“That is Eternus Online,” Adrian said while turning toward Jon’s second. “The activation of D.E.U.S. was Genesis. The conjunction is Revelation. I named it as such for dramatic purpose as much as literal adherence to Earthborn biblical belief.” The CEO turned away and looked across the entire team thereafter, his eyes calm and bewilderingly absent any kind of manic gleam or stereotypical madness. The sanity Jon saw in them only served to make his blood run cold.

He really believes this. Maxwell genuinely thinks all of this is true. What the fuck?

“There are over three hundred and fifty million people playing Eternus, Trident,” Adrian continued with calm self-assurance. “Within a year, that number is expected to increase over six times. In two years, there will be an anticipated four billion people playing Eternus Online—each and every one of them working in complete ignorance toward the conjunction by the mere act of playing the game.”

DeShaun cursed quietly in his bed, and Jon glanced at him by habit.

“That’s why you subsidize the technology to enter the game,” the heavy weapons specialist said with a hoarse growl. “You need more players, but giving it away for free arouses too much suspicion. Debt, even ridiculously forgiving debt, is much easier to believe than a trillionaire’s charity.”

“That is well-deduced, Lieutenant,” Adrian said with faint approval. “Yes. We subsidize the technology required for the immersion pods in order to evade suspicion, and we do so only to a point, because a complete lack of predation is more alarming to humans than mild predation. The terms are generous, but they remain indebting. It is, unfortunately, the way the world has learned to function.”

“What about the kids?” Zack asked, his voice echoing with a father’s concern. “What would playing this game do to children?”

“We aren’t monsters, Lieutenant Commander,” Fiona said before Adrian could, shifting her severe gaze to Jon’s second. “The technology has hard biometric locks. Anyone who plays has to have fully passed biological puberty and be at minimum sixteen years of age. Each one alone is not enough—both conditions must be met before D.E.U.S. will accept them into Eternus.”

“Oh, so you’re ethical aliens,” Aleksei muttered. “Good to know.”

“Call it as you wish,” Adrian said without offense while looking at Aleksei, and then turning back to the rest of Jon’s team. “But many of us are parents. Children, no matter the world, remain innocent. I have no desire to kill or endanger anyone, gentlemen—what I am pursuing, what my people are pursuing, is an enlightenment.”

“By your standards, maybe,” Jon said again at last. “That doesn’t mean it adheres to the standards of everyone else. You’re robbing choice, Maxwell, if what you say is true. You’re deceiving the entire planet.”

“I am,” Adrian agreed, though there was no shame in his voice. “And I hold no regret for that fact, Commander. This planet is dying; its people are riddled by soul-crushing mundanity and endless irrelevance before a conglomeration of puppet governments who could not care less of a whit about them. Eternus is not perfect—no world, no society, and no structure is—but it is, in my eyes, a far better alternative than the soulless corporate hegemony throttling your species into spiritual death.”

“And what, you’re going to save it from that fate?” DeShaun asked skeptically.

“Frankly, Lieutenant, I have no intention of being anyone’s messiah,” Adrian answered candidly. “But I have a duty to my people as their King, and if that means putting my interests ahead of the masses and delivering humanity from a slow erosion on this miserable, mana-void grey-scape of a planet in the process, I view that as a mutually-beneficial victory.”

“And besides,” Fiona added flatly. “It’s not as if humanity hasn’t been screaming for change for the past century or more.”

On that narrow point, at least, Jon found nothing to argue.
