"Wait," Ater said. "Crowlis spoke to you?"

"That’s exactly what I said."

"During the memory?"

"Not exactly. He took me out of it. Into some empty space. He called it the space between a memory and the person observing it."

The old man slowly lowered himself into a chair.

"And what did he tell you?"

"That he was only a fragment of his consciousness, preserved in a magical memory. And he was able to notice me because of the water. Apparently, it somehow connected us." Sakura rubbed her tired eyes. "If you want details, I’m afraid Crowlis wasn’t much better at explaining things than you are."

Ater said nothing to that.

"And he mentioned Nobus himself?"

"Yes. He mentioned him and told me to beware of him. He said Nobus’s ambition was always stronger than his fear..."

The old man remained silent for a while.

"Did he say anything else about him?"

"No. There wasn’t enough time. I think that whole space was too fragile and couldn’t last long."

"I need to think about this..."

"Of course." Sakura got up wearily. "And I need to get some sleep before another inexplicable disaster happens."

Gilbert snorted, but Ater was no longer speaking to anyone. He sat motionless, his head turned toward the stove.

The next few days passed relatively peacefully. Ater tended to the wounded, Lir helped with chores, and Gilbert gradually regained movement in his shoulder, although the pain still made it difficult for him to use his arm normally. Sakura was allowed to take short walks, but the wound on her chest and her injured thigh still kept her from moving freely, though she felt like she had almost gotten used to being injured by now, considering how often it had happened since she arrived in this new world.

They no longer kept Faerd tied up. He made no attempt to escape and gradually began helping with chores. He brought firewood, repaired the door, and once took over for Lir at the stove without being asked. Gilbert, however, still kept a weapon close whenever Faerd was around, and Pampli, if he was nearby, always got to his feet whenever the man approached Sakura.

On the fourth day, Faerd approached her himself. Sakura was sitting against the guardhouse wall, trying to comb through her tangled hair. Nearby, Gilbert was checking the contents of his [Inventory], occasionally pulling something out of thin air and examining it. He even threw away a few things he considered insufficiently useful.

"I’m starting to remember the journey after Ogrom," Faerd said. "Not all of it, but certain places."

Sakura lowered her comb.

"And what do you remember?"

"People I questioned about you. There was a wagon in the forest. Wanderers, lots of colorful clothes. One man wore a ridiculous, brightly colored scarf."

She immediately remembered the noisy group, the donkey, the frightened horse, and all those smiling faces. Then she tensed at once, because Faerd was talking about them. That could not mean anything good.

"What did you do to them?"

"One refused to answer, so I put an obsidian spike through his shoulder."

"What?!"

"He was still alive. I didn’t touch the others."

"Are you sure he survived?"

"He was alive when I left."

Sakura frowned. Those people had sold them the supplies they needed, talked with them, joked with them, and had been so nice. And Faerd had wounded one of them simply because he had not answered a question right away...

"He didn’t attack you, did he? None of them were hostile."

"I needed information."

"And that made it okay to cripple someone?"

Faerd looked away.

"Back then, to me, yes."

Sakura wanted to answer, but he spoke again.

"There was also a camp in a canyon. Mages."

The comb slipped from her hands.

Gilbert raised his head.

"A canyon?"

"Yes, with stone ledges. The camp was at the bottom. I followed your tracks there."

Sakura immediately knew which camp he meant. After all, they had only visited one camp of mages located at the bottom of an enormous, deep canyon...

Meanwhile, Faerd continued in the same dry tone.

"They tried to stop me, and I killed several of them. Wounded others. Destroyed their shelters."

"There was a man named Marven," she said. "He led the camp. Short hair, stern face, solid build. Do you remember him?"

"The man by the fire? He was the first to put up a barrier."

"Yes. What happened to him?"

"I broke through it. He fell and didn’t get up again."

Sakura fell silent, trying to process what she had heard.

"You killed Marven?"

"Yes."

Gilbert slowly stood up.

"He let us stay," she said. "Even though we brought him a whole lot of trouble. And then you went there and..."

"Sakura," Gilbert said quietly.

"There were children there. Did you...?"

Faerd looked away.

"I was looking for you. Once I learned which direction you went, I left immediately."

Sakura turned away. She remembered Marven by the fire, how unhappy he had been about their arrival, and how he had allowed them to stay until dawn anyway. He could have simply thrown them out of the camp, but he had not.

"How many people died?" Gilbert asked.

"I don’t know. I remember the ones I killed myself. But some of the tents collapsed, and I didn’t see everyone who was inside."

Gilbert swore quietly.

"And you’re only telling us this now?"

"My mind is still foggy after everything. But I decided you needed to know."

"Marven was an acquaintance of mine," Gilbert said. "A good man."

"I didn’t know."

"I believe you," Gilbert replied shortly.

He was silent for a moment, then lowered his gaze to the open case, but did not touch the scrolls again. Clearly, Marven’s death had upset him more than he wanted to show.

Sakura remained silent too. She remembered what she had seen in Faerd’s memories: the white room, the straps around his wrists, the torture, and the orders forced into his mind. She also remembered how he had tried to turn a spear against his own chest just to stop obeying them. Faerd had not chosen that life. But Marven had still died by his hand...

"I understand that you weren’t in control," she said. "I really do. But that doesn’t make it any easier."

"I understand."

"No. I’m not sure you do." Her voice trembled. "I remember those people when they were alive. Marven helped us, and now he’s gone, and I can’t even be as angry with you as I want to be because I know what they did to you..."

Faerd was silent for a long time.

"You can be angry," he finally said. "You have every right to be."

Sakura gave a bitter little laugh.

"Thanks for the permission."

She got up and walked toward the forest without waiting for an answer. That was the end of their conversation.

The next morning, Gilbert found her by the stream. He sat beside her, and for a while they remained silent. Sakura was still thinking about yesterday’s conversation, although she had absolutely no desire to return to it. Gilbert looked grim too, but he brought up something entirely different.

"You rely too much on someone being there in time to protect you," he said. "And I might not make it in time. You need to keep training."

Sakura looked at him.

"Do you want us to go looking for more obsidian monsters to practice on?"

"No. You’ve already learned quite a bit about obsidian monsters. You need a more intelligent opponent."

Gilbert fell silent, but he looked as though he had already made his decision. Sakura waited, and the mage continued.

"Faerd. He knows how to hunt mages. And I’ll be watching his every move."

Sakura stared at him.

"After everything? And after yesterday?"

"Sakura, I still don’t trust him, but we need his experience. It’s your decision. This could be more effective training than fighting another obsidian centipede, don’t you think?"

She did not agree right away. She had absolutely no desire to face Faerd again, even if this time he had no intention of killing her. But Gilbert was right. If they encountered another powerful mage, simply hoping someone else would help her would not be enough. And obsidian creatures could not compare to an opponent like that.

They chose a clearing a few minutes’ walk from the guardhouse for the training session. Lir was asked to stay with Pampli so the beast would not interfere if he saw Sakura being attacked. Gilbert positioned himself a short distance from the two sparring partners, where he could watch them both.

"No spears, blades, or destructive spheres," he warned Faerd. "Only weak constructs. The moment I tell you to stop, you stop."

"Understood."

"And you," Gilbert said, turning to Sakura, "do not use [DEVOURER] on a person. At all."

"I’m not planning to devour anyone. This is training."

"Good."

Faerd raised his palm. A small obsidian plate appeared in front of him and flew toward Sakura. She jumped aside, barely keeping her balance as her injured thigh immediately flared with pain. The movement was so clumsy that she felt embarrassed at once.

"One step would have been enough," Faerd observed.

"Thanks. Next time I’ll just let it fly right through me."

"I limited its power, but you can’t waste that much energy every time you dodge."

The second plate came in lower. Sakura stepped aside, but it changed direction, forcing her to duck.

"It can turn in midair?!"

"As long as I’m maintaining the spell, yes. Watch me as well as the projectile."

Faerd raised low walls from the ground, blocked passages, and sent blunt shards toward her feet. Sakura managed to dodge, but she constantly found herself in awkward positions. Gilbert occasionally interrupted with advice, while Faerd barely moved from his spot, only wincing once as he held his injured side.

"You keep running wherever I leave an opening," Faerd said.

"Because there aren’t any other openings!"

"Then you need to make me create one."

Sakura wiped the sweat from her forehead. It irritated her how easily the training came to him. She remembered his confession about Marven, who had also tried to defend himself against obsidian magic. Except no one had agreed to use weak attacks back then...

Faerd raised another wall in front of her. Sakura went around it and rushed straight toward him.

"Control yourself!" Gilbert called out.

She barely paid attention to his words. Faerd stepped back and raised another barrier, and Sakura barely managed to stop in time.

"You lost your balance," he said. "A real opponent would take advantage of that."

"You certainly know how to take advantage of someone who can’t defend themselves."

Faerd lowered his hand.

"Sakura, we should stop."

"Why?"

"You’re tired. And angry."

"No kidding!"

"Break," Gilbert said firmly.

But Sakura was in no hurry to leave the clearing. Faerd had already retreated several steps, leaving an obsidian wall between them, and apparently had no intention of attacking again. Throughout the entire training session, she had not managed to put him in a difficult position even once. He had countered every move she made without much effort, and now he had decided she had had enough.

Sakura looked at the wall. Faerd’s words about the canyon camp, about Marven and the people he had killed, were still running through her mind. She knew perfectly well that the Faerd standing before her now was no longer the same man who had hunted them and left a trail of dead and wounded behind him. But she could not get rid of her anger.

Then she had an idea.

She had a skill she had barely used against people. Choking Petals. She usually used it against monsters, but she had already learned to feel how the swarm tightened around its target. She could try catching Faerd’s arms and force him to defend himself without any devouring or deadly attacks.

"Sakura, I said stop," Gilbert repeated.

"One more try!"

She raised both hands.

[CHOKING PETALS]

A swarm of dark scarlet, translucent petals burst from her palms. Sakura directed it over the obsidian wall, toward where Faerd stood. The petals swept around the barrier from both sides and reached for his arms, but Faerd noticed them immediately.

"What are you doing?"

"Why don’t you try getting out of this?"

He managed to raise one hand, preparing to create another defense, but the petals had already wrapped around his wrist and were climbing up his forearm. The other part of the swarm caught his other arm.

Sakura concentrated on maintaining the spell. She had no intention of squeezing him the way she squeezed obsidian creatures. She only needed to restrict his movement and force him to lose his advantage for at least a few seconds... but it worked even better than she had expected.

Faerd jerked his arms sharply, trying to free himself, and the petals stretched tight but held.

"Take them off," he demanded.

Sakura noticed the change in his face. Faerd had stopped watching her. He was staring at his own wrists and trying to shake off the petals with increasingly violent movements.

"Faerd?"

"Take them off!"

The second demand was much louder and sharper. The man stepped back and jerked one arm so hard that pain showed on his face. The petals continued holding his wrist.

Sakura immediately loosened her grip.

"Wait, I’m..."

"No!"

Faerd pulled his arms apart with all his strength. A painful spasm crossed his face, and his breathing became fast and uneven. Sakura had already seen him in a similar state near the ruined station, when memories of his past had begun breaking through the compulsion. Except there was no compulsion now, which meant something was wrong...

"Faerd, it’s me. We’re training."

He was staring somewhere past her.

"Let me go..."

Sakura froze.

Let me go?

She could see him losing himself. Some old trigger had awakened inside him. Could the way the petals were holding him now have reminded him of the straps in the white room?

"Faerd, I’m letting go!"

She opened her fingers, releasing the pressure, but Faerd had already thrust his hand forward, and an obsidian spear burst from the ground between them.

Sakura saw it too late. She tried to jump aside, but her injured thigh could not take the sudden strain, and the point drove into her side beneath her ribs. The pain took her breath away.

The spear crumbled almost immediately, but Sakura had already lost her balance. She fell to her knees and instinctively pressed a hand against the wound. Blood quickly soaked through the fabric and seeped between her fingers.

"Sakura!"

Gilbert struck the ground in front of Faerd with lightning, cutting off his path to her. The man recoiled and finally stopped. The last scarlet petals were still circling his arms. They quickly faded and disappeared.

Sakura struggled to raise her head. Faerd stood a few steps away, breathing heavily. He looked first at his hands, then at her. His expression gradually changed, and Sakura saw him lower his gaze to the blood spreading across her clothes. His eyes cleared.

"I..." he began.

Gilbert was already kneeling beside Sakura. He moved her hand away from the wound, examined it quickly, and pressed a folded cloth against her side.

"Don’t move."

Sakura tried to take a deeper breath but immediately winced. It hurt badly.

"Gilbert..."

"Stay quiet for now."

Faerd took a step toward them.

"I didn’t mean to..."

"Stay back," Gilbert said sharply, making no attempt to hide the threat in his voice.

Faerd stopped.

"She’s hurt. I can..."

"Yes, she’s hurt. Because you hurt her."

Gilbert did not even turn his head toward him. He kept the cloth pressed against Sakura’s side, watching how quickly it soaked up the blood.

Faerd did not come any closer.

"Gilbert..."

"One more step and I’ll kill you."

A thin white-blue spark appeared above the fingers of his free hand.

Sakura wanted to say that she was partly to blame too, that she had been the first to break the rules of the training session. But as soon as she tried to speak, the pain in her side intensified, leaving her gasping for breath.

Faerd looked down at his own hands.

"I lost control."

Gilbert finally looked up at him.

"I noticed."

He spoke calmly, but Sakura knew him too well not to hear the anger in that short reply.

"I trusted you with her safety," Gilbert continued. "And look what happened."

Faerd did not answer.

"This is partly my fault too..." the mage muttered.
