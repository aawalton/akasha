Stella’s shock was subtle. No popping eyeballs, dramatic gasp, or even lengthy silence. It was very subdued, the words landing on an anticlimactic note.

Or maybe the shock wasn’t there at all. Because Stella didn’t take Lexie seriously.

She sighed with adult exasperation. “Lexie, what is this about?”

“I just told you,” Lexie said. “I’m half-Eldritch.”

“Yeah, and I'm half garden elf. Is this a new teenager thing? I figured you were too young to get sucked into NET subcultures and cultism, but maybe I was wrong."

"I'm not joking, Stella. I really am half-Eldritch."

"Look, I understand why you're confused. Torin told me about the rumors going on about you, and I know the Fae are very cautious about your newfound powers–”

“They aren’t rumors, and the Fae are only cautious of me because I’m not in their jurisdiction. They can’t control me, and I think they know, secretly, that I have the power to bring them down.”

Stella’s eyebrows ruffled. Her mouth was still formed in the shape of her last words, and her eyeballs moved like she didn’t know where to start looking for the answers.

“Let me start from the beginning," Lexie said. "You know when I was born, and I kept getting hurt?”’

“Uh-huh. You were the most accident-prone child I’d ever met.”

“Yes, well…somewhere in the midst of that, I got an Eldritch essence in my soul. I’m not going to tell you how–”

“Was it your father?” Stella demanded. “Or his rumored Eldritch friend?”

Lexie bit her lip. She wanted to lie, but she’d kind of painted herself into a corner. There was really no way to tell the story without bringing Aiden into it. Stella was too smart to buy the tale she’d fed Xena and Dewie, and she knew Aiden too well.

Unless Lexie tried to pretend like this was a recent development with the dungeon excursion, there was no way out.

Lexie didn't want to pretend it was the dungeon that gave her these powers because she didn’t want Stella to think that she'd been possessed by an Eldritch in the dungeon. Then the other woman would think Lexie's 'ailment' was fixable, something they could exorcise, rather than it being just a part of Lexie at this point.

“It doesn’t matter how the essence got entangled in my soul,” Lexie said finally. “All that matters is what happened next. At first, the Eldritch was so tiny that it was negligible, but then it grew and grew, and in the dungeon, it grew even more. It's at least fifty percent of me now. That’s why I have the powers that I have.”

Lexie paused after the reveal, waiting for Stella to digest it. It took a while. She didn’t blink once as she stared at Lexie, the astonishment seemingly rendering her completely still. She tried to control her expressions, but they were all over the place, transitioning between disbelief, sadness, outrage, and finally landing on disbelief once more. She got to her feet and started pacing.

“I…” she stopped turning around to face her, “I really can’t understand what you’re telling me. You’re saying that you, my completely human-looking goddaughter, is somehow a half-eldritch monstrosity.”

“Yeah,” she said. “I only look human because that’s how Ryn stitched me back together. She found my Eldritch form much less pleasant.”

“Ryn-Byul-Fo? The Fae that was with you in the dungeon?”

“Yes. She met me in my true Eldritch form. I was kind of ugly and terrifying.”

Stella laughed. She ran her hand over her face and released a loud belly laugh that echoed around the room and had just a tinge of hysteria hidden within.

“Oh Lexie…” She shook her head as she cupped her hand over her mouth to trap the peals of laughter. “I can’t..I just can’t right now.”

“I get it. It’s a lot to understand.”

“No, it’s not just that. This entire week has made no sense whatsoever. I mean, I must be finally losing my mind, aren’t I? This is all an illusion brought on by my mental breakdown. You’re not really standing there telling me these things.”

Lexie blinked. “I am.”

“No, you’re not.” Stella shook her head. “I know what happened. I'm dreaming. We’re still on the night Luther told me that our grandmother was dead, and everything else that followed has just been a bad dream after I fell into a shock-induced coma. I mean, how can you tell me that a few days later, my sweet, innocent goddaughter snuck into a pocket dimension and slaughtered a man in cold blood? And then on the same day, a world war is on the horizon…” She got cut off by more laughing. “I just can’t.”

“Your grandmother is dead?” Lexie asked softly.

Stella nodded, still rubbing her hands over her face repeatedly like she was trying to rub herself raw. "Last Tuesday."

That was the night Lexie had killed Vulcan.

That couldn’t be a coincidence, could it, or was Lexie simply now seeing patterns everywhere, whether they fit or not?

"I’m sorry,” Lexie said, making Stella finally look at her as the laughter died.

“It’s fine,” she said. "She was really old, and it was probably..." She didn't finish the sentence. She also didn't sound fine at all. Now that they weren’t fighting, Lexie got a chance to look at her and noticed how terrible she looked. She somehow looked worse than when she was sleep-deprived and fighting on the front lines of the Shatters. Now she didn’t just look tired. She looked emotionally drained as well.

Lexie went over and took her hand. She didn’t know what else to do at this point, didn’t know if she should finish the story or wait for Stella to come back to her senses.

“Breathe,” Lexie said. “It’s okay. Everything’s okay.”

To her surprise, Stella did just that. She closed her eyes and started doing breathing exercises.

“I’m in Dr. Shaw’s office right now, aren’t I?” she asked. “Dr. Shaw? Is that you speaking to me through my goddaughter?”

Lexie had no idea who Dr. Shaw was, but since Stella seemed to be losing it, she figured she might as well go along with it. “Sure.”

“Okay.” Stella looked relieved to hear that. “I’m just going to keep breathing then until I come back to reality.”

Has something like this happened before? Lexie wondered.

Stella was acting like it had and she seemed to know exactly what to do in this situation.

She walked over and lay prone on the bed, breathing deeply, with her eyes shut.

Lexie watched her, feeling sad and helpless, and also very sorry about how her actions had affected Stella. Truthfully, she hadn't been thinking of anyone else when she'd done the things she did, or at least she hadn't been thinking as widely and deeply as she could.

Eldritch brain tended to be more impulsive and more concerned with the immediate problem rather than those in her periphery.

It was up to Lexie to consciously temper that.

Lexie went over and patted Stella's hair like Aiden used to pat hers whenever she was going through something. Eventually, Stella’s breathing slowed, and she somehow slipped into sleep.

Well.

That was…kind of anticlimactic and climactic all at the same time. Definitely unexpected.

Not knowing what else to do, Lexie left the room and ventured downstairs.

She could hear Tate and Uncle Max in the living room before she got there, Tate pretending to watch TV and Uncle Max pretending to cook.

They both gave Lexie their undivided attention when she came into view.

“So,” Max said, wiping his hands on a napkin as water boiled in the background. “How did she take it?”

Lexie pressed her lips together before admitting, “I think I broke her.”

“Huh?”

“Well, I think she had a mental breakdown. She’s napping on my bed right now.”

Both males shared a look and appeared just as befuddled as Lexie.

“Should we do something?” Tate asked. “Call someone?"

“I would say we should call Dr. Shaw, but I’m not even sure who that is.”

“What exactly did you tell her?” Max inquired, and Lexie shrugged weakly.

“I didn’t even get a chance to tell her everything. Only that I was partially Eldritch and that seemed like enough to set her off.”

“I’m calling Emma,” Max said. Lexie agreed. She debated whether or not to call Torin, but one, she didn’t want to bother him, and two, she didn’t have his number anymore.

So she simply went to sit by Tate to watch the news, while Max called Emma to stop by.

It wasn't long until Lexie's picture came up on the TV. Lexie gaped as footage of her appearing at her doorstep was played by the anchor, announcing that Lexie was home safe and sound.

Great.

The asshole who'd put that PHORB there had gotten the footage before they destroyed the device, and now the entire world knew she was back and knew where she lived.

Fantastic.

"That's messed up," Tate murmured.

"They can just do that?" Lexie asked, gesturing to the TV. "Just show where we live?"

"They've been doing it for the past two days," Max said. "Now that Vacek is gone, it seems to have given them free rein. You have no idea how many people I've threatened to shoot off the property. Thankfully, though, Aiden's wards keep them away for the most part."

"Still. That's a disgusting invasion of privacy." Lexie glared at the screen as the news anchor continued talking about her life, down to her father, her friends, and her hobbies.

Almost none of it was entirely correct.

They mentioned that she'd attended a semester at the Victoire before she'd gotten trapped in the dungeon, but it had also said she was a very popular first year and was friends with the Elementals. As Lexie remembered it, she wasn't popular, and she was only really friends with Torin, Xena, and Flora and maybe Kai.

The anchor also said that Lexie's AFC career had been short-lived because she'd felt a calling to become a hero, not because she'd traumatized a girl so bad she'd needed a year of therapy.

The core facts were correct, but it was all so sanitized, designed to present Lexie in the best light possible. Was that Stanley's doing, or were people just believing whatever they wanted to believe?

Emma showed up a few minutes later, and after giving Lexie many hugs and relieved kisses, she certified that Stella was okay.

"She must have gotten very little sleep in the past few days,” Emma explained. “So I guess when she lay down her body kind of just shut down. You should be alarmed if she doesn’t wake up in the next few hours, and then you should call a healer."

“Okay,” Lexie said. “Thanks.”

Emma nodded and gave Lexie a gentle look, “Are you okay, darling?”

Lexie thought Emma was talking about the battle at Indigo, and she assured her, “Yeah, I feel fine. I didn't get hurt."

“No, not that.” Emma shook her hand. “I mean, yes, that too, but I’m more so asking emotionally. I get the sense that you and Xena had a fight.”

“Oh. Yeah, we did. But it’s okay. I’ll fix it and make it up to her somehow.” She wasn’t entirely sure how, but she would think about it when she had time.

“Anything I can help with?” Emma asked.

Lexie shook her head. “No. I made a mistake, and I just need to show her I’m sorry.”

“Okay. Hopefully, the two of you will make up sooner rather than later.”

“Hopefully.”

After Emma left, it was a few more hours before Stella woke up again. Lexie spent some of that time downstairs, partially watching the news about Indigo and partially catching up on text messages. She sent both Xena and Dewie short messages telling them that she was okay and that she would come over to talk to them soon. Xena didn’t respond. Dewie responded with a relieved emoji and more questions about how she was feeling and if she wanted him to come over.

He was one of the people who had sent dozens of messages while she’d been passed out. Xena had only sent one.

Where are you?

Lexie knew that the lack of messages didn’t mean that Xena wasn’t worried, but that she was more angry than worried at that time.

Lexie also replied to her dojo friends, and then she realized that a lot of the people texting her were from numbers she didn’t recognize. Most of them were exclamations of excitement from people who claimed to be her biggest fans. Others were reporters, and a bunch were offers for legal and PR representation.

Lexie responded to the important people, and when she heard a sound upstairs, she finally jerked.

“She’s awake,” Uncle Max said with a mouth full of whatever mush he’d made. It looked like it was supposed to be rice, but he’d messed up, and now he was the only one willing to eat it.

Lexie got up and headed back upstairs.

When she opened the door, Stella was sitting up at the side of the bed, her hands clenched and staring at the floor.

Lexie closed the door slowly behind her, so as not to startle her. Stella looked to her anyway, not startled, but carefully still.

“It’s real,” Stella murmured.

“Yeah,” Lexie murmured. “It’s real.”

Stella took a deep breath.

“Okay. So you’re half Eldritch.” She nodded a bunch of times before she continued, “Tell me what that means exactly.”

“It means I’m not entirely human,” she said. “I don’t always think or feel things exactly like a human would. Especially with emotions like shame or guilt, or conscientiousness and empathy...they're more muted. I have human feelings that are more intense. Like love. And pride. And wrath. I also have a hard time lying."

"I see."

When Stella didn't freak out at that, Lexie continued talking, "I don’t need food to live, and I don’t need sleep either. I probably secrete some pheremones that make people uncomfortable, and I do scare a bunch of Eldritch by being around. But most importantly, I do not respond well when my loved ones are threatened. By that I mean my brain jumps straight into eliminating the threat without considering anything else, like the long-term effects of my actions.” She sighed. “That man has been trying to kill me since I was young. He’s probably the reason I got hurt so many times as a kid, and he killed my mom, too. He tried to hurt my dad, threatened everyone…he would have figured his way out of the dungeon, I’m pretty sure. That was all I could think about when I went there, how many people would get hurt because of him. I don't have the natural human aversion to killing. I lost that in the dungeon. But I don't kill for no reason. I just wasn't thinking clearly because I was afraid. I was afraid that he would escape and hurt the people I love, and I could only think of one way to get rid of that fear." Lexie bit her lip and laced her fingers in front of her. "But you're right. I shouldn’t have done what I did. It wasn't smart. Being Eldritch makes me stupid.”

“You’re not stupid,” Stella reached out, and to Lexe's surprise, she leaned forward and cupped her cheek. Lexie thought Stella would take a second to figure out if Lexie was a monster or not, but she responded instantly. "Look, I know you don’t think so, but I understand why you did what you did with Vulcan. It doesn't make you a bad person necessarily. I've wanted to do the same thing, too. So many times." She tucked Lexie's hair behind her ears. "My reaction toward your actions wasn't just anger. It was guilt. Because, as young as you are, you shouldn’t have had to make that choice. That should have been our job, and because we couldn't do it–"

“It’s fine,” Lexie said. “It doesn't really matter anymore. Just know that while I don't feel guilty for what I did, I am sorry that I didn't discuss it further with you. And I'm really sorry I called you a coward.”

“You weren’t wrong.”

"I was. Sometimes it’s brave to do the harder thing. Keeping him alive was the hardest thing.”

Stella didn't look like she agreed or disagreed. Instead, she chose to change the subject. “So, have you always been like this?”

“Not necessarily. I was far more human at first, and the dungeon was what really ramped my Eldritch side up, because I needed it to survive."

"And you're sure you didn't just get possessed in the dungeon?"

"Yes. I'm sure."

Stella bit her lip. "Can I have St. John take a look at you then? Please. I won't share anything with the association, I just need to know for myself what's going on."

Lexie shrugged. "If it will make you feel better."

"It will."

"Okay then. Now about my punishment...there's something I need to tell you about the Fae–"

Lexie was interrupted by the sound of her phone ringing.

Lexie was going to ignore it, but then she saw who was calling. It was her father.
