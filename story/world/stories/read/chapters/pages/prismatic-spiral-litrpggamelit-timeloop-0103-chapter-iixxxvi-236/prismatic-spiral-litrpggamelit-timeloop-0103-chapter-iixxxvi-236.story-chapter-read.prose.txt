Part V - Amber

Chapter II.XXXVI (2.36)

While Mitsuko was relieved to find her shirt, indeed, back on her. She was less pleased when she once again failed to best the kraken at the start of the loop. The Selcouth Sable survived the attack with no casualties and no permanent damage, thanks to her. However, that wasn’t really her goal anymore. It was the minimum required of her. If someone died in the kraken attack, she’d see that as a personal failure at this point.

Mitsuko slumped on the gunwale next to the ship’s bow and sulked, chin resting on folded arms. The pink water churned under her as they approached Mauve Island. As the wooden bow cut the surf, Mitsuko turned over an object in her hands. Something she’d recovered from the ship’s cargo hold.

A button-eyed doll with twine for hair. Its stitched mouth seemed to grin at her as the black buttons gleamed in the sunlight.

This loop she’d decided to go out of her way to question The Selcouth Sable’s captain about the object. As a moderately competent mage, he’d confirmed through his spellsense that it wasn’t cursed or magical in any meaningful way but couldn’t tell her anything else about it. With no recollection of ever seeing the doll before today, he had no idea how it had ended up in his cargo.

Mitsuko reflected on what the hag had said when she’d cast Retrospection on it before. It had asked her to help an acquaintance of hers and offer this doll to someone. Neither of which were clear instructions.

“She said that I needed to pay a… toll,” Mitsuko muttered to herself. That specific word felt important. It didn’t take a genius to make the connection to the screaming troll she fought in nearly every loop. However, that thing was mad. It was a witch talking to her though, so maybe she actually intended for Mitsuko to use his body parts in some sort of brew or dark ritual. Trolls were known for their regeneration capabilities and long lives. Both of which Mitsuko had already secured for herself through the Prismatic Spiral. What could a troll do for her that she couldn’t already accomplish? The troll was absolutely mad. He had been probably for centuries at this point. Maybe she could use his body parts for a brew that could induce madness. To what end though? It all seemed useless to her. And, besides, Retrospection allowed her to negate that.

Then her newest level up in Retrospection crashed into her thoughts. Revert Memories, Others.

Mitsuko’s posture jolted upright so quickly she lost balance and had to Mend herself back into position to keep from falling in the sea.

“If I could turn back the clock on his mind… then he might recover. I could hypothetically bring back his sanity.”

Maybe that was a stretch, but it was definitely at least a lead.

The first thing Mitsuko did when arriving on the island was make a beeline for the menagerie. She wanted to get there before Sam the clown could make a ruckus.

The deer Kemon woman was beckoning pedestrians over to take a look. Unsuccessfully, as most people had their eyes locked skyward on the newly formed dome above their heads. Her face lit up as she spotted Mitsuko headed straight for their display of animals.

“How much for your turtle?” Mitsuko asked.

Whatever greeting the deer woman had prepared, it died on her lips in confusion. She blinked her big, brown eyes at Mitsuko. “Excuse me?”

“I want your ice turtle. How much?”

“Our animals aren’t for sale.” The Kemon woman adjusted her uniform as if it held authority. It was embroidered with all sorts of mystical creatures. Griffins, basilisks, even a Dragon. With that outfit, Mitsuko couldn’t imagine their little collection of animals had ever made it over to Edgeland. Any Dragons who saw that would doubtlessly assume they were being compared to lesser magical monsters and were liable to erupt in rage. Literally.

“Everything is for sale. Even if money isn’t the cost.” Mitsuko stepped past the woman and into the shadowed tent.

Just as she’d remembered, the ice turtle sat in a small tank. Its shell glistened as a beam of light shined down on it. She scowled at the sight.

“You haven’t paid! You’re not allowed to view the beasts without payment. I need to insist—”

“Ah, stuff it.” Without even a glance in the woman’s direction, Mitsuko cast Mend and dragged her back out of the tent by her underwear. There was a yelp before she disappeared. Mitsuko could have targeted other clothes or even the woman herself, but the turtle left a foul taste in her mouth. Last time, she’d assumed it had been the result of Sam’s kerfuffle that led the turtle to end up exposed to the sunbeams. Now she saw it for what it was. The poor thing had been left in the sun as a prop, prioritizing its shiny appearance to customers over the animal’s welfare.

“Verna?” Mitsuko said as she removed the turtle from its tank. “You feeling okay?”

The turtle turned its head to meet her gaze. Its eyes literally glowed a pale shade of blue, a clear signifier that it was a possessed creature.

“Swell,” Verna replied dryly. “You failed to mention that I would be left sunbathing in this body for the first few hours of the loop.”

Mitsuko winced. “Sorry, I didn’t realize.”

Verna sighed. “I know. The first bit of every loop follows the exact same pattern. I shouldn’t expect you to know exactly how events on other islands are playing out simultaneously before your arrival. However, suffice it to say I am not pleased with the owners of this menagerie. Being subjugated to their conversations was nearly as bad as suffering the sun itself. They’re extremely selfish and obsessed with money. A monkey from the Hon Basin died two days ago and they were bemoaning the loss in revenue.”

“I will make your dissatisfaction well known in the final loop,” Mitsuko promised. She wrapped Verna in a discarded rag in hopes of keeping the turtle’s shell cool and out of the sunlight.

“Do so. Now, let’s get moving. Your time is limited. What’s your goal for this loop?”

“One minute. Let’s get somewhere more secure first.” Mitsuko stepped out of the tent and the Kemon woman again tried to approach her. The Kemon man who seemed to co-run the menagerie with her likewise moved to block her path.

A simple Mend put them back in their place. Mitsuko left behind a roar of outrage and loud accusations of thievery.

“Are you concerned?” Verna asked from within the rag bundle. “They’re besmirching you. Not very nice words are being used to describe you.”

“I doubt anyone on this island could lay a finger on me,” Mitsuko replied. She grinned and felt a deep sense of satisfaction at the truth in her statement. “Even if this was the final loop, I wouldn’t be concerned about any fallout. I won’t sugarcoat it. I’m quite powerful.”

“True. Only the guardians pose any true threat to you now.”

“Yes. Them.” Mitsuko mulled over that. “I have two more to kill. There’s Amber Island and Azure Island. I don’t even know how to get to either island. My one visit to Amber was due to dumb luck that I doubt I can replicate.”

“As you know, Amber Island moves on its own schedule. One that I’m not privy to. Azure, however, I do know of an entrance to. Though that would require a question.”

Mitsuko considered as she stepped into an alleyway. It could be an easy solution to get to the island. She was sitting on a small wealth of questions again after that recent loop reset. Four questions currently at her disposal.

Sitting on an alley step that led up to a bricked up doorway, Mitsuko decided to take a minute to think over her options. She set Verna down on the cobblestone street.

The ice turtle skirted around on the purple stone walkway. Its magic smoothed out Verna’s journey and allowed it to slide across the alley’s ground. Like Sterling had claimed, the little reptile was surprisingly speedy.

“I think, for now, I’ll leave Azure be,” Mitsuko decided. “Once I access Amber again, there will be information about Azure.” “Seems likely,” Verna replied evasively.

Mitsuko watched a man in a balcony overhead hang up his wet clothes on a clothesline that stretched across the alleyway. Unlike the others in the city, he seemed to have already adapted to the dome stretched out over their heads and moved on to take care of his daily chores. Mitsuko supposed that life went on, regardless of chaos and the collapse of norms.

“And your plan for accessing Amber Island?” Verna pressed.

Mitsuko pulled up the cloth doll and offered it to the turtle. The sage twisted her head around. A mistake, as she only just barely managed to divert her path to avoid crashing into a sack of garbage. A startled rat darted out of the sack and slipped down a storm drain.

“What is that?” Verna dragged herself back over to Mitsuko, smoothly gliding across the ground.

Mitsuko stopped the turtle’s slide with a foot and lowered the doll for Verna to get a better look.

“Does it look familiar to you?”

“No. Should it?”

Mitsuko’s shoulders slumped. “I suppose not. It belonged to a witch. It received a strange message when I cast Retrospection on it in a previous loop. The hag told me to find the daughter of an old friend of hers.”

“Isn’t there a witch on this island? An acquaintance of yours?”

Instead of answering, Mitsuko glared at the doll. Its button eyes mocked her while its stitched mouth continued to smile. She desperately did not want to get involved with the schemes of witches. Alas, fate seemed to have other plans for her.

“Yes. There is one of those here,” Mitsuko said, defeated. “A witch from the Hon Basin, where I grew up.”

“That seems like a good place to start. Or do you wish to visit with Sage Escara beforehand to question him about the odd message? Perhaps he might have some insight into how you used his spell to see the witch’s message for you. I don’t think that’s something normal. Plus, he might also know something of the witch’s daily agenda. After all, he does sit up there in the clocktower, staring down at the city folk day after day.”

“No, it’s fine. Better to get this all out of the way quickly. You know, rip off the bloody bandage, and all. I know which inn Fuku is staying at and can find her there easily enough.” In truth, Mitsuko knew a fair bit about Fuku’s schedule on the first day of the loop. She simply wanted to put off meeting with the mental mage witch until she felt more confident in herself.

And nothing boosted her confidence quite like emerging victorious from a life or death battle.
