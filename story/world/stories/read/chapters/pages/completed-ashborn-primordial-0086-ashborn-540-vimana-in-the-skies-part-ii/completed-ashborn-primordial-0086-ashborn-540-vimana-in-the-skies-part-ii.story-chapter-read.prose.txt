Hours had passed, and not one of the scouts had spotted a thing. Nor had there really even been sightings of any kind. With the lands that used to be the Ash now cleared of Ash Beasts of all kinds, the skies were frustratingly empty.

Normally, Vir would have called that a good thing, but in this case, every minute wasted was another that this rogue Vimana drew closer to civilization.

It was once again Siya who made the monotony pass swiftly, launching into yet another barrage of her endless questioning. Just when Vir thought she had asked every question there possibly was, she came up with something new.

It seemed, however, that even Imperium children had bounded energy reserves, and Siya reached hers some time ago, currently napping away beside him.

It gave Vir a good opportunity to survey the land for himself, with its rolling hills and mountains and rivers. There would be cities down there someday. Possibly someday soon, with highways and airways connecting them in a crisscrossing web.

What new adventures would await generations to come? What stories would be spun and new lives lived?

There was enough prana in the land now such that no human or demon would ever have to worry of any deficiency hampering their growth. Furthermore, for the very first time in an age, there was more land than anyone could hope to claim.

Another, more somber voice in his head said that demons had also lost the one advantage their bravest members had in testing their mettle against the Ash, and that both humans and demons had lost a threat that had, if nothing else, unified their cultures against a common foe.

Vir suspected there would be more wars and more fracturing in this new realm. He at least hoped that, under his and Maiya’s guidance, such things were a long way away.

At least, he had hoped for that until this Vimana came along. What happened now was anyone’s guess.

It was Vir who ultimately identified the large craft, its golden hull glistening against the bright sun as it trundled across the sky, some few thousand paces above the ground. Having long ago finished searching his own grid, it wasn’t long until he’d encroached on the others, and his efforts had borne fruit.

He sent a signal to the Sarvaak through the communications orb, circling the great structure at a sizable distance. Even this far, he knew he was not safe, but if the crew had the intention or the means to attack him, they would have long before he spotted them.

It was a good sign, however small.

Hearing him speak woke Siya up, and she peered at the Vimana intently.

“You wouldn’t have any way to remotely power down a Vimana such as that?” Vir asked, though there was little hope in his voice.

As expected, she shook her head. Things were never so easy.

The fleet converged on his position over the following hour, assuming formation at Vir’s position, and in all that time, it felt as though the Vimana had barely moved.

While nothing but an illusion, Vimana were certainly not the fastest vehicles to occupy the skies. That, at least, bought them time. Time enough to gather around the structure and observe it, charting its course.

Maiya arrived then as well, her dozen Altani fast skyships coming in from the northwest to assume formation next to the demonic fleet.

Vir landed on the deck of her skyship and Maiya leapt up into the craft the moment he did, wrapping him and Siya in hugs.

“Honestly, it’s not been long at all, but it feels like forever,” she sighed, taking a seat behind Siya.

“We felt the same,” Vir said. “Having you on the communications orb was nice, but I would have liked to take a walk with both you and Siya in Samar Patag.”

“After this,” Maiya said. “Hopefully.”

Neither commented on how this situation would likely not be resolved so easily.

Lifting off again, Vir brought them all to the command vessel’s deck, landing as he had hours prior.

They disembarked and headed straight to the command room.

“Cirayus, Balagra,” she greeted, nodding at them both. Cirayus stood as usual with his four arms crossed, while Balagra was in his half-naga form, which Vir noted he’d been using more often these days.

“You’re looking well, lass,” Cirayus replied, offering a nod back and a bow to Siya, which Balagra and every other demon in the room echoed.

Siya stiffened, nodding awkwardly in response. As much as Vir wished she didn’t have to, she would be well served by growing used to such reception from now on. This was likely the most muted response she would ever receive.

“Wish I could say the same for the rest of us,” Balagra muttered after the greetings were over.

“Have we plotted its course yet?” Vir asked. “Where is it heading?”

“We have,” Balagra replied with a scowl. “And that’s the thing. Nowhere, it seems. They are headed for Iksana territory.”

Vir turned to Siya. “Do Vimana have anything capable of destroying cities buried a thousand paces beneath rock?”

Siya thought for a moment before shaking her head. “I’m not sure. But I think it unlikely. The Vimana were not built as weapons. Their armament is solely for the purpose of defense.”

“Of course, Vimana are quite capable of making course corrections,” Balagra said. “It is possible they are attempting to confuse us, to thwart our attempts to counter them.”

“Or… they might not know where they’re going?” Maiya said, earning the attention of every eye in the room.

She cleared her throat. “I have some new information. For one, no others have activated,” she said. “Just this one. Every other Vimana is exactly where it has floated for an age.”

“Just one?” Vir asked, frowning. “Could it be the one our enemy corrupted? The one with the Gate that took us to the doomed universe?”

“It certainly looked the same as Vimana Raaka to me, now that I think about it,” Maiya said. “How the Vimana in that other universe has anything to do with ours is beyond me.”

“And me,” Vir admitted, though there was still so much about their enemy he didn’t know. That he’d likely never know.

“Whether it’s them or not, they’re not directly controlling it. That much is certain. If they are involved at all, then all they did was maneuver the right people to be in the right place at the right time.”

“Which brings us to who those people are.” Vir looked at her. “What did you find?”

Maiya’s eyes narrowed. “The commander is almost certainly a Matali general. Devan Gurveer is the name. My handmaidens dug up what they could on short notice.” She cleared her throat. “He’s, uh, had a rough past. Lost his wife and son in a botched Pagan Order raid years ago. Killed rather horribly, by all accounts. And it seems he knows it was demons, from his repeated calls to eradicate the Order after. I suppose a general would be in the know about the Order’s little secret.”

That much seemed almost certain to Vir. The Order masquerading as humans might have fooled the masses, but every leader in power knew their true nature. That their fanatical raids were nothing but a disguise to rescue demons, offering them a sanctuary in a nation that despised humankind.

The room went quiet as the implications sank in.

“So it is as we feared, then,” Vir said. “A Matali general who harbors a deep hatred for demons is now in command of a supercreation from the Age of Gods. And the first thing he does after gaining control is point it at the demon lands.” He looked around the room. “Is there any way this isn’t a hostile act?”

“Hard to see it any other way,” Cirayus replied. “This Gurveer approaches demon lands blind, unable to rely on old maps. Then the moment the Vimana nears close enough to show him the nearest population center, he brings its weaponry to bear…”

“It’s worse than that,” Vir said. “The world only knows of the Vimana’s defensive armament. What those flying fortresses are actually capable of is in another realm entirely. They might not have the capacity to strike deep underground, but I assure you, the weakest of what they have easily outstrips the humans’ most fearsome S rank magic. They could level an entire city in an instant.” He glanced at Siya, who had been standing quietly, half-hiding behind Vir and Maiya since the start of the meeting. “Isn’t that right?”

Siya nodded somberly. “The offensive systems would be more than capable of causing severe damage to a city made of non-Imperium metals and lacking a prana barrier.”

“Which simply means we cannot let it,” Cirayus said, looking at Vir. “Isn’t that right, lad?”

Vir groaned, fully aware of what Cirayus was hinting.

Because if there was anyone alive who might survive a direct hit from the full output of a Vimana’s arsenal, it was the Akh Nara.

That, at least, answered the question of who was going to attempt to board the thing.

“Look on the bright side,” Maiya said, smiling sheepishly. “Maybe they haven’t taken control of the weapons yet?”

Vir sighed loudly.
