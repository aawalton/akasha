Hayden flicked his hands, and a hundred lines shot around the trees like a spider web. The bird flew into the wires, getting caught for less than a second before its brain, core, and organs exploded.

“I’ll be honest with you,” Kalia said. “I watched Photel melt the skin off a group of A-ranks, and he doesn’t scare me half as much as you do.”

“That means you’re wise.” He shot more lines into the creatures, and the beasts separated into meat and bones and liquid organs.

“W-What the hell was that?” Lina cried.

“A good usage of a third evolution spirit,” Hayden said as he cleaned the meat with more lines. “Now make a fire. We should move as quickly as possible.”

The others were so shocked that they silently complied, staring into dead air before Photel muttered, “When’d it evolve?”

“Forget when… how?” Yaka asked. “I thought Clear Lines didn’t have a third evolution?”

“It has five,” Hayden said. “Just like every other spirit.” He butchered the meat with a flick of his wrist, creating even cuts before putting them on a rock he polished with another set of lines.

“Is there anything that thing can’t do?” Kayline asked.

“Spirits are an extension of one’s body. So the question isn’t if there’s something a body can do that a spirit can’t, there question is whether there’s things a spirit can do that a body can’t—and the answer is yes. An exceptional amount.”

“He’s so casual about this,” Photel grumbled.

“Right?” Lina grumbled back. “It’s like the ultimate humblebrag.”

“Who cares,” Kalia said. “I’m so hungry right now.”

Twenty minutes later, the group that was too hungry to move was now too satisfied to move. So when Hayden stood and said, “Alright, let’s go,” they all groaned and stood like zombies.

Hayden didn’t like to abuse them, but he was on a ticking clock. There was no way that the rift’s treasure would be easy to obtain. If he had to fight causality while vying for it, he would fail. He was certain of it. So he trudged on, determined to claim it freely.

It was two hours later when the team fought their way to the most distinctive area in the rift. It was inside a bowl of mountains, and it had rocks that individuals had painstakingly placed in the shape of a flower. It had big rocks on the outside for petals they spun, getting progressively smaller as they moved deeper into the bowl, and there was a black area on the inside.

The RSA had officially named the area Rose Rock Cavern in his last life—and the name was fitting.

“It’s beautiful…” Kayline whispered.

“It’s dangerous,” Kalia said.

“Yeah… anything that requires this much work to build is a trap,” Fringe said. He then looked at Hayden. “Probably.”

“I’m not sure,” Hayden said. “Humans create elaborate structures to protect the things they find precious. That said, I can’t imagine that they made its treasure easy to obtain.”

“Did bosso just tell us something?” Lent asked.

“Yeah, it’s weird,” Tibbi said.

Kayline and Lina exchanged amused glances, but lost their luster when Kalia said, “Are you sure you want to bring the girls?”

The sisters looked at Hayden nervously.

“Of course not. But I think it’s more dangerous to leave them behind.”

“Not to question you, but…” Photel looked at them and back. “Why’d you even bring them? It’s increased our difficulty substantially.”

The two looked at the ground.

“Trust me,” Hayden said. “When this is all over, you’re going to worship the ground at their feet.” He walked forward, signaling the end of the discussion.

Kayline and Lina blushed, but hurried along as they descended the spiral flower and slowly entered the black cavern at the center.

They fought that thing? Kab thought. I’m not even sure I could touch it….

“Keep focused,” a stern man on Stella’s team said.

Kab spread his angelic wings. “If you want to join me—keep up.” He shot into the skies.

“Wait! Get back here!”

Kab wasn’t even going to pretend to care about the law. He had a job to do—and a life to live. So he flew above the forest at top speed, thinking, I gotta hurry. If he opens his gate before I get there…

It would be game over. Kab could feel that in the marrow of his bones.

Hayden didn’t know about the crisis happening outside the cavern, and he was too focused to care. The cavern had a beautiful staircase that had been meticulously carved over decades, or even a century, spiraling into a pitch-black abyss.

“Do you know if this is where the rift key is?” Kalia asked. “You don’t need to tell us, but… it’d be helpful to know.” The rift key was the crystal that kept the rift open. If it was taken out of the rift, the rift collapsed, and the microcosm returned to its original universe.

“It’s not,” Hayden said. “The team found the key in a cave in the far northeast corner of the rift, and they only found it after the rift break. There were hundreds of A-rank walkers, as you call them. The founders went through painstaking effort to make this rift unconquerable, and that makes sense. No one would spend decades making such an elaborate staircase if they planned to let its secrets go unseen.”

The group stared at the stairs in a new light.

“So this is where the treasure is,” Kayline said with the inflection of a fact.

“I believe so.”

They were about twenty minutes into their descent when a distant glow inked up from the abyss. It was faint at first, but it gradually grew into a dazzling orangish white light.

Crystals jutted out of the wall, small slivers at first, but piking out a foot in areas. They were on the staircase’s wall, in the spiral, and below.

“Are these seriously cora crystals?” Tibbi said.

“Sure are,” Yaka said. “That’s crazy.”

“Hey leads, what’s the chance you’ll let us snatch a chest of these?” Lent asked.

“If you want to repeat history and break the rift early, sure,” Hayden said. His words were just as dry and factual as possible, but the sarcasm was blunt as an ancient axe.

“Sorry but… what are you talking about?” Lina asked.

“Crystals power the rift and keep it open,” Kalia said. “If you mine too many, the rift collapses. If the rift loses too many, the gravitational repulsion magic that prevents rifts from becoming asteroids also can’t activate. That’s why it’s illegal to mine crystals even though they’re all drained by the time the rift reaches the ground. It can spark an extinction.”

“Yeah, you kiddos are too young to know it, but back in the day, militaries used to break mines on purpose,” Lent said. “World ending nuker. Grim times.”

“You’re so old it’s crazy,” Photel said. “I finally get what triggers age dysphoria.”

“Right?” Tibbi said. “It creeps me out. But seriously, guys, this does too. If this isn’t powering the rift, what is it doing here?”

“It is powering the rift,” Hayden said. “Just not the gravity repulsion. That’s what history suggests, anyway. As for what it is, it’s a resource well. There’s a good chance that they cultivated something down here. Something they wanted us to have.”

Fringe examined the glowing crystals as he walked. “Like, no trap type wantin' it? ‘Cause I could really use some non-trap livin’ in my life right about now.”

“Preach,” Photel said, glancing at Hayden.

“I doubt it’ll be safe,” Hayden said. “Rifts and tombs are often designed to pass legacies, but those who make them only pass their secrets to those they find worthy. If there’s something special down there, it will come with a trial.”
