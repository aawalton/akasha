The remaining targets are beginning to act very differently. Explosive, exponential growth. Unsustainable perhaps, but it’s possible at least one of them could cause a collapse in local space. If we wish to continue to allow this “Jianghu” to exist, I recommend a higher degree of force. We have several target sites in containment or in transit- eliminating the rest might be preferable to allowing them to cascade.

Additionally, the primary asset is being… difficult. Requesting further clarification of its original orders. I suspect it may have eliminated at least one house asset, potentially more, and continues to operate independently of the rest of our deployment.

Awaiting orders.

For the Glory of the Family.

-Operational report from forward assets of the Clan’s military operation in the Republic of Morae to higher command.

They don’t bother announcing themselves, which she respects. If not for her actively searching for an attack, she might not have even noticed them. Their Qi is almost skin-tight, radiating barely anything, and the way the air warps around them from some sort of artifact makes it hard to tell they’re even present. It’s only by sheer quantity of eyes that she detects them in the first place.

She started extending her senses the moment she started taking command of the manse, expanding her perspective in waves. By the time she started making the fortifications around the edges of the mansion’s own arrays (which are well-designed, tasty as hell) she’d set up a ring of thousands of eyes, coming in and out of existence rather than expending the time needed to make them able to blink.

Thousands of lines of sight, cascading over her surroundings in three-hundred and sixty degrees, noticed a slight discrepancy in the sky nearby. It didn’t appear to heat-vision or to any of the other, weirder wavelengths she can shape her eyes towards, which she’s sure probably have names, but the ripple remained.

A second later, they were overhead.

Less than a second after, the invisibility vanished, revealing three black dots, so far up in the sky that there are multiple layers of cloud-cover between them and the mountainside compound.

They’re sneaky. Keeping quiet, moving stealthily. As she’s learning, every cultivator is different, moreso as they develop, but she’s pretty sure that most of them wouldn’t be able to detect anything at all until the enemy were right upon them.

Raika starts outputting more Qi, the grass beginning to droop as sap and chlorophyll become supplemented by blood, the trees starting to grow stranger branches, the people beginning to shift. She still can’t bring everything to bear without losing her biggest and best snack, but if she can accelerate the equalizing pressure, she’ll be better off in the end.

Ah. One more thing, too.

“The Feng are here,” she tells Aria, the mouth appearing and disassembling itself as fast as is needed to say the words.

A glint of light falls from the sky above, faster than rain or tumbling stone or thundering bullet.

The borders around which Raika has started building fortifications lights up in an instant, briefly stunning her Qi senses from the sheer complexity and density of defences that manifest all at once. On instinct, she throws some of herself into it, building channels of flesh directly against runic scripts and circulating hyper-real blood through them.

The microsecond of contact is still enough to blind the few mortals or mortal-adjacent people in the manse who happened to be looking up.

And that’s with a layer of the shield stopping most of the light from coming in.

She can’t read the runes, but Raika can feel the layers of it, interlaced like puzzle-pieces. To her senses it’s something like an full-course meal, crafted by an expert in the culinary arts, each component made to augment the next. She might not recognize the ingredients or know how to cook, she still can admire the expertise on display.

Layers of symbols, organized into formula and sentences and poetry, weave their magics from all across the acres of the mansion. Built into the walls, added to the plants, molded from the everyday movements of the people living in it, growing from where it’s been planted.

It visibly distorts under the force of the pearl-orb, the energy it’s trying to push back that much vaster and more complex than what it was designed for.

But it holds.

The light fades. The sky above starts to come back into view, new eyes replacing the ones that Raika’s lost from the flare and the resulting backwash of Qi. The defensive array, for all that it bent and shook beneath the pressure, survives.

Looking past the dissipating energies of the first pearl, Raika sees tens, then dozens, then hundreds more droplets.

She hates this.

She hates this.

Slitting herself open, demi-divine blood floods the array from everywhere there is Flesh.

The shield lights up, warping and wobbling from its original shape as she shoves more Qi than it was originally designed for into it, throwing her saturated flesh at the problem until it drowns it. The dome surrounding the mansion thickens, doubling and tripling in uneven patterns as the droplets fall.

She shoots spikes of bone out from her anchors and the construction she’s a part of, harpooning towards the top of the dome and around its perimeters as she accelerates her growth along the boundaries. Flesh shoots up fast enough to steam and start to oxidize, embers of herself shedding as she forces growth that pushes at the laws of Heaven’s physiks, linking pieces and lining the dome as best she can. Membranes shoot out, hearts form and add explosive pressure to limb-sized veins, stars of bone bursting in the air to link disparate pieces as she adds a second dome beneath the first.

The pearls begin to land.

It hurts.

That’s almost a surprise, actually. Weird to think that there’s still some Pain that leaves an impression on her.

The dome bends and she presses it back, the energies sizzling and burning as it deforms, bones breaking and skin charring under each impact as she bleeds into the shield to sustain it. It’s not designed for this, not fully, though there are parts of it that seem almost eager to accept her, but a vibrating wall of energy is almost as useful as a solid dome, so she pushes it further even as it continues distorting.

It’s like pressing her bare flesh against an open flame as it punches hard enough to shake apart organs and shatter bone.

Heavens beyond heavens, as they say.

There are many screaming voices, but one of them keeps saying her name, over and over. There are two of her here, the other one can handle it.

She does just so, growing a body out of the walls of Aria’s office again. A bit more piecemeal than the last one- it’s mostly just an eye, a pair of lips, and a spinal column.

“Hi Aria! I’m kind of dealing with a big problem right now. Is there a reason you keep yelling my name?”

Three pearls hit the shield at once, and the light and heat are powerful enough to turn the dome’s interior a glowing pink through a few dozen feet of armored flesh.

“What’s the situation? How many of them? What can we do?”

Those are all pretty good questions. Aria’s got a nice brain in her cranium- controlled fear reaction, synapses firing ever so efficiently, minimal waste. It’s enough to make Raika feel a bit jealous.

“Well, there’s three of them, they’re bombarding for now but probably going to switch things up soon, and nothing much, in that order. Unless some of the spouses start trying to kill each other or you or me, I guess, but it’s sort of weird, because they’re under some tough circumstances.”

“I- wait, why? What do the spouses have to do with anything?”

“Oh, it’s just that I can’t exactly use them for stuff if they haven’t done something bad enough, you know? Feels a bit funky. It’s what we were talking about before?”

Part of the shield distorts inwards so violently that the flesh-dome she’s made tents inward, raining crimson onto the buildings below as a massive chunk of her surface area is ripped apart.

The other Raika fixes it before this Raika has to do anything, but it’s enough to convince them both that they’ll have to start cooperating sooner than later to try and figure out a solution.

“How do you use them?”

Oh. Right. Aria’s still talking. Even with other Raikas to help, it’s kind of stressful being in agony while under attack. Brains are tricky like that.

“Well, I’m experimenting with integrating different types of Qi and such, so if someone does something particularly bad, I make them into a sort of obelisk flesh-rock and then build a bunch of channels. All pretty complicated, kind of busy to explain it.”

“Does it hurt them?” Aria asks hurriedly, seeming to push for something.

“No? I put them to sleep first. What would be the point?”

“Is there a way to make it so they don’t have to be some kind of flesh rock or whatever you called it?”

“Well, sure, it just wouldn’t be as strong. The flesh rock is just the first shape I found and it works really well. It felt sort of instinctive, actually, like I-”

“Have you ever done it to someone who wanted it to happen?”

Raika pauses at that, briefly surprised.

She… hadn’t considered that, actually. Like, at all. Because why would someone want that? She wouldn’t, probably. But she can taste Aria’s synapses, firing in little staccato bursts as she thinks ever so quickly.

She’s onto something.

Raika decides to see where she’s going.

“No, I haven’t.”

“Can you?”

“Yes.”

“Good. Come with me.”

Less than a minute has passed since the bombardment started, but already, the shield is fizzling out, sparking and twitching almost like it’s alive. The flesh behind it, very much alive, grows to fill in the gaps, but that only increases the pain, the sensation of burning getting the addition of razors cutting into her tissues. A fresh wave of bombs falls and crushes the shield between the explosion and her dome behind it, and the entire thing spasms against her.

The agony is exquisite, even if she has to shape parts of her brain to compensate through the suffering.

People are milling about, many of them in agony, but Aria grabs a talisman from her storage ring and sends a burst of Qi through it, activating some effect. An alarm starts to ring through the compound, and a mixture of arrows and highlighted streets begin to appear from illusory arrays, guiding the people of the manse towards shelter.

Aria, moving at the height of a mid-realm Nascent Soul cultivator’s speed, launches herself towards the highest concentration of Qi in the compound (besides Raika herself). She heads to a section of the manor that’s got a more expansive wing and nearly connects to another building, large and beautiful enough that it would be a mansion in and of itself in any less expansive show of wealth and territory. Raika, already a part of the entire compound, is already there, flexing muscles of Flesh and Space and simply going up-down-sideways-inwards from Aria’s office to the other location.

There are so many people there. Like, at least two hundred of them.

And they’re all cultivators.

Some of them are only in the Qi-Gathering realm, but some of them go all the way up to the Nascent Soul realm, approximating Aria’s own level of power. Men, women, a few that don’t cleanly fit either, all of them distinctive, bedecked in unique outfits that speak of disparate cultures and dissonant aesthetics, all of them attractive. They range from so stunningly gorgeous that it’s obviously part of a technique or Truth they carry, all the way down to “just” hot enough to have poems written about them and lives taken for their sakes. One or two are almost normal, the genuine uniqueness of individuality making them stand out, but they’re the minority in a sea of jade and ivory and silk.

Raika mostly dislikes the lot of them on sight. All that bone-white skin and night-black hair… Each is clearly and distinctly an individual, from their flavor down to their biology up to their outfits and looks, but so many are so similar that it’s almost like staring at a room full of arrogant, sharp-edged dolls.

The dislike is mollified by the fear.

Even the strongest among them are standing still, senses attuned to try and understand what’s happening. The forces raining down and the defences raised against them are far above what a Nascent Soul realm cultivator could survive, and there are very few in the crowd that are even that strong. They’re afraid, confused, and, at least for the first minute and a half of the cataclysm, they haven’t tried to kill each other yet.

A second later, Aria has crossed nearly a mile and navigated through the mansion with incredible expertise, appearing on a balcony overlooking most of the spouses. Some are on higher floors alongside her, but even they immediately turn to her, her aura washing over the room in a burst of porcelain-ivy, delicate and sharp, growing from breakage.

“Enemies are assaulting the manse,” she says, discarding any preamble. “They are in the Warrior realm, and there are multiple of them. The defenses won’t hold for more than a few minutes- our defenses could hold against one elder or a minor sect war for days, but what’s here is worse.”

A wave of voices rises, panic and anger intermingling like a wall of sound. It lasts barely a second before a dozen different Qi signatures flare, silencing those foolish enough to interrupt.

“Why are you here?” asks a voice.

Bright blue hair intermingles with skin like ivory, black, blue and pale aqua decorating a body that rings with the power of the peak of the Core Formation realm. She stands on one of the higher platforms, servants surrounding her, her aura actively shielding some of them from the pressures crashing down on the manse.

“What can we do?” asks another.

Golden skin and eyes of burnished copper, bedecked in rings, bracelets, and fanciful armor. The speaker stands alone, off to one side of the crowd, his gaze certain even as his heart beats wildly.

“We have little time. I will be brief. An aspect of the master of the Crimson Manse has returned in our time of need, but requires our aid to properly defend us. I am asking for volunteers to undergo a ritual that will bind them closer to the Crimson Divine, for the sake of all of our survival. Those who agree will be empowered by her, but even those who do not will still be protected. I swear this on my cultivation.”

The oath stirs some, but not many. In a crowd of vipers, shiny things often mean more scales rather than gold.

“Who’s attacking us?”

“What proof do you have of this? Are we to be some alchemical experiment?”

“We won’t be used for your battles!”

“Shut up! Can’t you hear what’s happening outside? You think you are worthy to stand against our survival?”

“You dare? I-”

This is taking too long.

Raika emerges into the chamber, growing down from the ceiling above.

She makes sure to do so dramatically.

A hundred arms flower open, each one distinctive, each one somehow subtle malformed and suited for predatory purposes. Layers of undulating chitin and sinuous sinew ripple across the fabric of her, adding multiple tiers to the train-sized column of flesh from which she blooms, making a song of sibilant, crackling tissue. Skulls and horns and antlers and effigies emerge from her in sequential patterns, and fractals of reactive quartz serpentine through her construction.

At its center, lowering towards the crowd, a face as large as a body opens all five of its eyes and smiles, teeth made of pearl shining from earth-dark skin and pale, faded scars. Crowned by a halo of horns that bleed and ooze out Qi, each eye aglow with something too pale to be iridescent, Raika greets her spouses.

“Hello! I’m Raika. Who among you would like to live?”
