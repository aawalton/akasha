I was being cradled against something firm and warm. My body rocked from side to side in steady motion. Cold air bit at my face and throat. Afraid of what I might see, but with no option but to look, I slid my eyes open.

I was in Renner’s arms. I could make out the hard set of his jaw, shadowed with brown stubble, and the planes of one side of his face. He was looking ahead, lips pressed into a bloodless line and the muscles of his neck twitching. My pack was slung over his shoulder.

The sky behind his head was a dim swirl of overcast clouds and hazy golden light. Most of it was obscured by the dark outline of wooden cottages and slatted roofs. It appeared that he was carrying me through an alleyway.

A gust of wintry air swept over through the street, making shutters creak and dry plants rustle. I shuddered, pulling my arms tight across my chest. As soon as I moved, Renner stopped walking.

“You awake?” He stared straight ahead, face unreadable.

“Y-yes.”

The arm beneath my legs slid away. My boots tapped against the ground, icy water sliding to and fro within them. His other arm slowly withdrew, and I was left shivering on the dark cobblestones of one of Snowmelt’s alleyways.

“Good.” He stalked forward, backing me against the rough wooden siding of a house. “Let’s get one thing straight, ladyship. You ever pull a stunt like that again, and I won’t hesitate. No more letting the Order have a look at you, no more benefit of the doubt. That’s it.”

The blood drained from my face. “I wasn’t trying to hurt you.”

“Yeah? Well, you did.”

The statement made me wince. I glanced down, dreading what I might see. His boots were blackened and singed, and some of the fabric of his lower trousers had been burned away. I bit my lip as more nausea welled up.

“Don’t look so worried, I’ll live,” he snapped. “Maybe next time think before you-”

“I’m sorry!” I cried. “I’m sorry, alright? I didn’t mean to hurt you, I just… I couldn’t just stand by and let you… let you…” my breath curled out between us as silvery fog. “Gil?” I hardly dared murmur the poor man’s name, terrified of what the answer would be.

His face darkened. “What do you think?”

The world shone too brightly as fresh tears filled my eyes. “No. No.”

“He’s not dead yet, if that makes you feel any better. I’d give it a few hours.”

“Wh-what?” My heart skipped a beat. He left the stablemaster there- what, bleeding to death on his kitchen floor? That can’t be what he means. The image of a different kitchen, and a different pool of blood, flashed behind my eyes.

“Order says the monsters might be able to tell when someone they’re bound to dies. We need to be out of this place before that happens. Get a head start.”

“Get a… what?” He wasn’t making sense. “Renner, we need to go back. We can take him to the Watch and ask for aid.” I took a step forward, the plan taking shape in my head.

He moved, blocking my path. “No. He’s gonna bleed out, and we’re not gonna to do a damn thing about it.”

I gaped at him. “Are you mad? How can you… look, even if you don’t care about Gil, he might be able to give us more information! Some kind of clue about where to start looking for Teela.”

“No.”

“Yes!” I tried to shove past. He didn’t budge. “Saints, Renner, what is wrong with you?!”

He glowered down. “With me? You’re the one trying to save a murderer.”

“He isn’t-”

“He may as well be! You heard his story, same as me. People are dead because of him.” He slammed one hand against the wood, punctuating the accusation.

I sucked in a breath and looked around, abruptly aware that we were getting quite loud in the middle of this sleepy, silent backstreet.

Renner drew back a bit, features cast in shadow, and his eyes darted to the shuttered windows of the nearby houses.

We both stood still for a moment, waiting. There was no movement, no reaction. Snowmelt was silent.

“He asked if I was alright.” I pressed cold fingers to my eyelids. “He was hurt, and I nearly killed him, but he asked if I was alright.”

There was an audible click as Renner’s jaw snapped shut.

“He’s a person! We can’t just leave him! He made mistakes, and he deserves to pay for them, but that’s not your decision to make!”

“No? You heard him. ‘She has others.’ That’s what he said. That means there are more people in this salted town who are gonna get violent the moment you start asking questions. Your friend back there said he’d already tried going to the Watch. Nothing got done. The authorities you’re so keen on running to can’t be trusted.”

My mind raced. His observations changed nothing about Gil or what his fate should be, but they did cast our situation into a more sinister light. “Well, then… the town cleric. We can ask them for help. Surely a cleric-”

“Ask for help? What exactly are you hoping we’ll get help with?”

My jaw dropped. “Finding Teela, of course! And I still think we should go to the Watch. Maybe not to explain everything, but someone there could still shed some light. If this has been going on for years, if Teela wasn’t the first… then maybe there have been investigations done in the past.”

Renner was shaking his head.

My voice turned shrill. “Or… or trails that went cold, or, I don’t know, something!”

“Ladyship. Brin.” He closed his eyes briefly, nostrils flaring. When he opened them again, they held a flicker of that same softness from this morning. “Just… think for a moment. Think. When we woke up, it was past dawn. The room was cold. That means the window had been open for a while. It’s been hours since she was taken.”

“That doesn’t mean-”

“We are alone. Do you understand? You and me, alone, in a place where anyone could be waiting for an opportunity to drag us off to whatever this thing is. It’s just us. I’ve got a crossbow and a sword, and you’ve got magic that knocks you on your tail half the time you use it.”

My teeth began to chatter from more than just the cold. What he was saying was correct, logical, but was he really thinking about just… giving up? He couldn’t be.

Renner pressed on, low and harsh. “There’s a Fae nearby that’s messing with people’s heads, has eyes and ears in the town, and managed to kidnap someone from a room with other people in it. I don’t like our odds.”

“I don’t either, but we’re not giving up. We can’t. No one… Renner, no one even knows she’s missing! If we don’t find Teela, no one will!”

“I don’t think-” His voice was too gentle again. Too soft.

I clapped my hands over my ears, swallowing a scream.

Gloved fingers wrapped around my wrists and pulled, dragging me closer. Forcing me to listen. “Ladyship. I don’t think there’s still a Teela to find.”

“H-how can you say that?” Heat pricked behind my eyes. “How can you-”

“Because you need to hear it!” Every word rang off the cobblestones. His face twisted into a look of regret almost immediately, and his grip loosened.

I slumped against the wall, knees shaking and blood roaring in my ears.

When he spoke again, every word held that brutal, knifelike edge. “I don’t say it lightly. But it’s the truth. It’s been hours.” He leaned against the house beside us, tilting his head back with a mirthless laugh. “And even if we find her, then… what? We kill a Fae? One that’s been preying on people for years, from the sound of things? Without any time to plan or set traps or scout things out?” He closed his eyes. “No. No, we need to run.”

That’s it, then. He’s giving up. Salt, how will I find her alone? I pictured myself running from house to house as a hazy sun sank slowly into the horizon and a new night of terrors crept ever closer. It was too much. I sank to the ground, arms wrapped around my middle, and wept. Heavy, hitching gasps wracked my body, tore the breath from my throat.

Renner was silent. Some time later, when I had managed to calm myself, I peered up. He had paced some distance away. His gloved hands were clenching and unclenching and his face was flushed with cold.

He’s wrong. Or he’s… he’s not, but he is. Salt, I can’t think. But we can’t run away. We can’t just leave her. It’s not right. She needs us.

I just have to say the right thing. Convince him, somehow. We are in danger, and we are outmatched, but… abandoning her is not an option. I just have to make him see.

I hiccoughed, lifting numb fingers to wipe my face. That accomplished very little. A fresh, frosty breeze bit my skin and I lifted the hem of my tunic to help get clean.

Renner approached me cautiously, like one would a wounded animal. “Need a hand up?”

I sniffled, nodding.

He swung my pack to the ground and reached down, helped pull me to my feet… and frowned. “Where’s your cloak?”

My blood turned to ice. I dragged a hand up to feel for the clasp at my throat… gone.

An entire world away.

He scanned the flat gray stones around our feet, brows knotting together, then looked back up the alleyway.

I followed his gaze. “It m-must have… fallen.” It’s not as though that could mean anything to him. Things go missing. It happens all the time.

Copper glinted in the dawn, narrowing at me.

I swept damp hair back from my throat, suddenly unable to meet his eyes. “It’s f-fine. I don’t need it. We don’t need to w-worry about it.”

Stillness crawled like frost across the cobblestones. My breath puffed out, misty and gray and too fast.

“You had it when I picked you up.” He stepped closer, voice dropping to a murmur. “Where’s your cloak, ladyship?”

I swallowed, unable to find my voice. Silence loomed. Snaked its way around my throat. Just like during our walk beneath the silvery dawn, Snowmelt was too quiet; there were no birds, no insects, no chattering townsfolk. Just the rustle of wind through brittle plants.

“Do you know,” he crowded me against the wall, gaze holding mine, “That you’re a really bad liar?”

My pulse fluttered.

“Every thought you have is just written on your face. And there’s this look you get, sometimes.”

I started to turn away, and gloved fingers caught my chin. Tilted my face up.

“When I ask what’s on your mind. When we talk about making deals with Fae.” His breath was warm against my skin. “It’s the same look you’ve got right now.”

Musky leather and oak filled my nostrils. The intensity of his gaze kept me trapped just as surely as his grip, and I shivered against the wood. “R-Renner, we’re… wasting time. We need… n-need to go.”

He didn’t answer.

The very air around us seemed to freeze, and I hardly dared to breathe. He’s suspicious. That’s all. Nothing’s changed; he’s always been suspicious of me. He doesn’t know anything for sure, no matter what he says about me being a poor liar.

The next thought hit like a blow. He was only suspicious of Gil at first, too.

Uncertainty hadn’t stopped him. He’d hit Gil. He’d smashed Gil’s head into a countertop.

My palms grew clammy as Renner continued to study my face. Would he draw another blade? Throw me against the rough wood and demand the truth? I wasn’t sure I had the stomach to fight him, not after earlier. Not after I’d nearly killed us both.

“We need to go,” I pleaded, slowly wrapping my fingers over his. “Please. It’s… it’s just a cloak.”

Warm breath curled out in a small cloud of gray. “Just a cloak. Yeah. Sure.” He stepped back. “Come on, then. We’re almost to the gate.”

I sagged against the fence, dizzy with relief.

Renner made it a few steps before pausing. He turned back to face me, one eyebrow cocked.

“I’m not going to the gate.” Before he could argue I straightened, squaring my shoulders. “I’m not leaving Teela. I’m going to find her. And it’s okay if you’re scared. I am, too, but she needs us.”

He shook his head, mouth twisting into a scowl. “No. We’re leaving.”

“I’m not.”

“We’re. Leaving.”

“No.”

Renner stalked towards me, cloak lashing at his singed boots. “Ladyship-”

“I’m not changing my mind! If you want to run, fine, run. But I’m not going to just abandon her!”

He stopped a pace away, glaring down. When he spoke again, his tone was low and lethal. “We had a deal. I kept up my end. You owe me.”

I folded both arms across my chest. “Well… I guess our deal’s off, then. I’m sorry.”

Something flickered across his face; something vicious and dangerous and directed solely at me. My blood went cold with the thought that, just minutes ago, he had tried to kill someone. And now I was alone with him. In a dark, empty alleyway.

“I’m not leaving, Renner,” I whispered again. My lips trembled, but I kept my voice even and ground out, “I’m not. It’s… it’s as simple as that. You can’t make me.”

His jaw tightened. He glanced around. Slowly. Deliberately. As if confirming that no one was nearby.

Surely he wouldn’t… surely he won’t try to…

Leather creaked as one of his hands flexed, and the expression he turned towards me was…

My heart stuttered.

I could see it written across his face.

He would.

The wood dug into my spine. “I… don’t want to use a rune. N-not on you. But I’ll… I’ll scream every step of the way. I’ll wake up everyone in this whole town. I’ll t-tell them what you… what you did to Gil.”

He went perfectly still at the threat. “I don’t think this is a road you want to go down.”

I blinked, not quite understanding his meaning.

It became abundantly clear a moment later when he leaned in, breath hot on my face, and promised, “It ends with you tied up and gagged. Not fighting me. Not calling for help.” Frozen silence curled around us until he added, almost gently, “But safe.”

My eyes went wide, heart hammering and nervous heat flooding my stomach. My breath came in ragged, hitching gasps as frustration and anger and stark fear swelled, too heavy for me to breathe beneath.

Teela was gone. Taken by a monster who was going to devour her. And her only hope was me, standing here half-frozen and spent after nearly setting myself on fire.

And Renner. Renner, who wasn’t going to save her. Renner, who was threatening to drag me out of this place.

He meant it. His face held that ruthless expression again; the look when he’d tried to stop me from running out of that cave, the look when he’d stabbed Gil. Like the choice in front of him was ugly and brutal, but he wasn’t going to hesitate.

He would drag me out of this awful town, kicking and screaming, and Teela…

Teela would die. Alone and afraid and without anyone fighting for her.

Something snapped.

“You’re a coward.”

His eyes widened.

I leaned in, toe to toe with him, and hissed it again. “You’re a coward, Renner. You were going to just sit and watch when those men tried to hurt her back at the inn. And that night in the cave, you hid. And you only helped me in the first place because you thought you could get something out of it, and now… now…”

His face had gone white. I couldn’t tell if it was from anger or something else.

I dropped to my knees, yanking open my pack. Frozen fingers clawed past my runebook and spare clothes and packs of food until…

“Here.” I stood. The shard was hot and bright in my hand. “Here. This was all because you wanted a favor from the Order, right? Well, take it. Get out of town and go north and get your reward. Give this to them and tell them about Fellbrook, and this place, and, salt, tell them everything!”

He didn’t move.

I swiped fat, warm tears from my cheeks, shoving the shard out. “Take it! Maybe they won’t be as grateful as if you’d delivered a runekeeper, but it’s still got to count for something. And you’ll probably keep it safe better than me, anyhow. It’s a… a good trade, right? For me?”

He flinched back as though I’d hit him, but still didn’t reach out.

I snarled, pressing the steel into his hand. “And you can drag me out of this awful town, but you know what? I’ll fight you every step of the way. Every step. I swear it. And when we get to the Order, I’ll tell them you’re a coward and a murderer and I’ll tell them you left Teela to die and… and…” my voice broke. More tears spilled out, blurring my vision. I pressed a hand to my face.

His fingers wrapped around the metal. For a moment they began to close around mine, too, and I wasn’t sure if the touch would be gentle… or one intending to carry out his threat.

My pulse stuttered and I flinched back against the wood.

“You’re going to get yourself killed,” he murmured.

I choked out a bitter, exhausted laugh. “So? It’s not as though you care.”

Renner swallowed. Opened his mouth.

My chest tightened. For a moment, I thought he might argue.

But he didn’t. His jaw snapped shut and his eyes grew hard, and his gloved hand clenched around the shard.

And without another word, he walked away.
