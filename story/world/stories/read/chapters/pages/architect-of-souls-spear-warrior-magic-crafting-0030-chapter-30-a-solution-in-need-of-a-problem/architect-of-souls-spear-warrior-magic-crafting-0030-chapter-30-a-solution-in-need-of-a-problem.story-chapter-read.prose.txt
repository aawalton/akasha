Ferris stared back at Serena, considering her words. What was a problem he wanted Jade’s Adaptation ability to solve?

Despite her earlier disappointment, Serena had latched onto the question. Ferris knew it was frustrating to sit around and wait on others, she might just be happy to have something she could fix.

“Hemon informed me that, due to my leg, the only job assignment I qualify for is spirit gem refinement. Pardon the assumption, but if you have an acid core, what typical jobs within the clan would an acid core excels at?”

Serena pulled the short bow off her back and set it on the table. She had to use her left hand, as her right was obviously missing.

“Yes,” she confirmed, looking down at her bow, hesitation now showing on her face. The taboos against discussing personal cultivation details were strong, but the research project practically demanded sharing. Ferris felt it was clear at this point that she had an acid core, but it still took her a moment to say it aloud.

“I know of a few acid core abilities that would qualify for a job assignment, although I would suggest you consider options beyond monetizing a valuable ability, or what is convenient to the aspect of your core.” She assumed a sarcastically lofty tone, as if quoting advice that had been told to her. “A profession should be a passion, not simple ease based on a random lucky ability. The difference: the passion pushes you to higher tiers, while the convenient ability leaves you comfortably stagnate.”

Ferris felt a bit lost; growing up he hadn’t thought about cultivators having professions beyond delving. The advice mirrored Hemon’s, although the man had mentioned finding advantages rather than advancement. He must have looked as lost as he felt, as Serena chuckled at him.

“To find what your passionate about… I guess try different things? The library has information on almost every topic. The basic technical manuals won’t teach you enough to land a job assignment, at least without a lot of time and work, but they give you a taste.”

“That sounds interesting, but probably doesn’t help me immediately,” Ferris said, “those cost merit, after all.”

Serena frowned. Ferris wondered what her access to the library was like, or how she earned merit points. The clan would make her work for it, just like everyone else, but there were surely perks to being part of the family. It was the same way back home, at Clan Bluereef, although perhaps less distinct.

“Well, as far as valuable acid abilities…” she thought for a moment, before continuing.

“There are various methods to strengthen metals, one of them is an ability called Acid Tempering. It uses a chemical reaction to change the structure of the surface of a weapon, usually to make it harder or resistant. The forges in the second district pay well for such an ability.”

“Next, if you bonded a slime, you know about the use of slime fertilizer for farming? There is a manipulation ability that can easily move basic acids around. The farmers have a decent irrigation system, but there is still value in abilities that can move the liquid around easily. Water core abilities work for this as well, but the clan doesn’t have many of those.”

It was interesting to hear about the crossover between acid and water, as he hoped some of his clan’s water magic could be used with acid.

“The alchemists process a large bulk of monster corpses daily, brought back by delvers. There is an ability, Acid Dismantle, that can selectively dissolve around and between components, making it ideal for cleaning and separating corpses into separate parts.”

Ferris immediately perked up.

“That one,” he said, “Acid Dismantle.”

Ferris had seen it in the book of tier one acid core abilities at the library, listed among others that created selectively corrosive acid. It hadn’t stood out to him at the time compared to any other.

He also remembered Hemon’s job listings wanted a ‘corpse dismemberment ability.’ He assumed they wanted it magically cut up, not connecting it to Acid Dismantle.

The piece that pulled it all together was Serena clarifying that the corpse would be separated by dissolving away undesirable parts, or along the edges of connected tissue. If this was happening inside Jade’s body, then the slime would eat the remains.

Ferris could get paid… to feed Jade monster parts.

Serena had a couple other suggestions, but none that had the upside of Acid Dismantle.

“Okay,” she said after he had confirmed his choice, “you could simply purchase the method for it from the clan, but that wouldn’t help you investigate Adaptation. Additionally… Acid Dismantle is not cheap. It requires that manipulation ability I mentioned as a prerequisite, plus an open meridian and some expensive resources in its own right. Plus,” her eyes shifted over to Jade, “it is an acid aspected ability, which means you need to touch the core to use it.”

Ferris frowned. Of course, things wouldn’t be easy.

“But that is where you have an opportunity,” she continued, “The Acid Dismantle available at the library is generic.” Her voice got lower, almost whispering, “Often, there are versions of the same ability, but specific to certain bonded weapon types… or familiars. Perhaps there is an Acid Dismantle that only works for slimes. A restriction like that would usually imply that it is cheaper than an ability usable by any soul.”

She leaned back, a conspiratorial smile on her face. Ferris nodded excitedly, that was exactly what he needed. It even made sense why this version of Acid Dismantle would be cheaper: it could depend on the slime’s biology in ways it couldn’t with a human. Finding a random ability that perfectly solved his current problem would normally be a hopeless dream, but not with Adaptability.

It was what had convinced him to choose a slime familiar in the first place. Karl’s—and everyone else’s—opinion of slimes be damned.

“So,” he said, “I need to consider the ability, then create problems that the ability would overcome.”

They spent some time brainstorming ideas to prompt Jade to adapt Acid Dismantle. Serena had several interesting proposals, but they usually used specially enchanted equipment. One would be to special order the creation of a magical maze puzzle cube. It would have food inside, but if any of the maze’s walls were melted it would jolt Jade with a shock. Serena was confident such a device could be created, although she admitted it wouldn’t be cheap.

Most of the ideas had the downside of multiple directions Adaptation could solve the problem. For example, it could just make Jade resistant to shocks. Ferris wondered if there were ways to target a desired ability. He wouldn’t be able to look for them until he understood more how it worked. At least it was something he could overcome with persistence.

Ferris liked the puzzle cube idea but thought he could replace the expensive magical part with… himself. He could watch Jade consuming something and react when the slime ate out of line. He didn’t like the idea of hurting Jade; in fact, he hated it so much he was sure it was the bond affecting his feelings. At the same time, he didn’t want to hurt his familiar just to prove he could. He could try the plan with a reward instead of punishment.

The other key would be to watch what happened in the slimespace. First Steps of the Soul had advised him to study his abilities, attempting to understand what concepts were being represented in his soulspace. This was the first step in understanding how Jade’s healing ability worked, so it made sense to do the same for Adaptation.

All their plans were based on a few intentionally vague remarks by Janice recorded in the slime journal. As Ferris learned more about the how the ability worked, and what restrictions it had, he would have to… adapt.

Eventually, it approached the end of their scheduled meeting. They hadn’t directly worked on the research project, but Ferris believed that learning more about Adaptation could speed it along. Still, he was grateful Serene hadn’t immediately left after learning he couldn’t participate in the planned experiments.

“Thank you again, Adept Serena,” he thanked her as they left the training hall, falling back on the more formal title to express his sincerity.

She shoved him slightly with her hand. “Yeah, well, Elder Harper wasn’t wrong when she said time is needed to develop. Just try to be ready next time.”

After parting ways, Ferris decided to spend the rest of the afternoon at the library. He couldn’t use any jade slips, but there were plenty of books to read.

Gilbert Booker once again stepped forward to help when he reached the front of the line. Ferris was glad to see the man, as it would make his request easier to explain. Also, the man was growing on him. Perhaps it was due to receiving help and advice during the crisis of having his oath statue stolen.

“Adept Ferris, welcome back to the library,” Gilbert greeted him, “How may we help you today?”

“Thank you, Gilbert. It is good to see you again. Previously, you helped me with a study on the nature of acid. To be honest, I felt unfamiliar with many of the concepts discussed. I am hoping for mundane books that could help my foundations. Also, I am interested in learning about any non-aspected first tier abilities the library has available for purchase.”

The man nodded, as professional as ever, and took him to a private room.

“First, for the general abilities,” the man began, “the library has a limited list available to new delvers. Please understand, this is different than the catalog of first tier acid aspected abilities you read before. That larger volume was provided to you with the intention of letting you plan your path through the first tier. To achieve this, it details many abilities considered more advanced. If you had a traditional weapon, like a sword, there would be a similar book with compatible abilities.”

Ferris nodded. Frankly, he didn’t think any of the offensive abilities from the acid aspected book were achievable before he started delving.

“For more general abilities, the approach is different. The library provides a curated list of recommendations, but more advanced techniques and manuals are only available with recommendation from a tier two within the clan. This restriction is lifted after an Adept reaches the middle stage of tier one.”

Ferris sighed. That was probably meant to let his mentor guide him, but Uncle Winton wasn’t part of Hollowfield. Ferris had heard the term ‘stages’ before; apparently Adepts had to ‘break’ into the early, middle, and late stage of each tier. Ferris hadn’t yet even ‘broken’ into the early stage, and he had no idea how it was done. Something Uncle Winton had told him not to worry about yet.

When Gilbert handed over the list, Ferris sighed again. There were only seven options on it.

The first was Basic Body Refinement Techniques. The description claimed it gave general improvements to the entire body, and it was recommended for all tier one Adepts to purchase. However, it warned additional resources were required to see results. He noticed it didn’t even have ‘ability’ in its name, just ‘techniques.’ That meant it would contain things he could do in his soulspace to make his body stronger.

The next two were defensive abilities: Harden Skin Ability Construction, followed by Spiritual Armor Ability Construction. Harden Skin did what it said, but Spiritual Armor was a bit more complex. It even required an open meridian, but was able to manifest plates of armor made of qi.

Then, there were two perception abilities: Enhanced Sight Ability Construction and Enhanced Hearing Ability Construction. He wondered if Cyrus had Enhanced Sight, or something more unique. Given the eyeball molded into the cover of his bonded book, Ferris guessed it was the latter. While he could see the value in these, they wouldn’t help him anytime soon.

The last three options were related to mobility: Jump Ability Construction, Burst of Speed Ability Construction, and Powerful Run Ability Construction.

It was interesting to see an ability called ‘Jump.’ Ferris knew it would be different than the seed he had growing in Jade’s slimespace, as that one was meant for a slime instead of a human. Both abilities came from Core Trees, given the same name by the strange information received by eating its fruit.

Perhaps it was like Serena had said, where an ability could have different versions with different requirements. Or, it might be two completely different concepts achieving the same goal. If merit points weren’t so dear, he would have loved to compare the two.

While the abilities were all interesting, only Basic Body Refinement Techniques felt like a guaranteed purchase. Ferris wasn’t sure when, but the recommendation for all Adepts to acquire it made sense. He would need to ask Uncle Winton for advice. It also had the benefit of only costing six merit points, while all the others were twelve.

As Ferris was reviewing the list, Gilbert was helpfully tracking down his other request. ‘Foundational information so I can understand a book about how acid works’ was a broad topic, so it wasn’t surprising it took some work.

Thankfully, the man appeared both familiar with and happy to accommodate such requests. It wasn’t like Ferris could look through the shelves himself, after all.

After about thirty minutes, Gilbert brought Ferris several books, adding more over the course of the afternoon. They ranged in complexity from schoolbooks to more advanced levels. None of them were precisely what Ferris needed to know to better understand chemical reactions of acids, but they all contained pieces of the puzzle.

While most of the books were dry and hard to read, one was fascinating. In fact, Ferris got distracted from all the others and spent the rest of the day reading through it.

Titled On the Preparation of Food, it was a book on cooking. Or rather, it was an analysis of what happened while cooking. It didn’t contain any recipes, just studying the process.

Ferris knew that beating egg whites made them big and fluffy, but that was due to air being combined? How do you mix air? He wondered. Why is it different with the yolks?

The book had the answer, and more.

Ferris had grown up with pickles in his basement his entire life. They were served at his family’s restaurant, so they made them in bulk. He often helped prepare and check on them as part of his chores. He knew every step of the process by heart… but he had never questioned why.

Why did they bubble? Why did they smell? To Ferris, that had just been what pickles did.

But now, reading through the book’s details on the process of fermentation, Ferris felt a bit… conflicted. He was enthralled, but at the same time unsettled. This was a precious memory of home, why was the book trying to change it?

Ferris eventually got to the reason Gilbert had recommended the book, which detailed the use of acids in food. There were charts where citric juices were used to soften tough meats, recording different soaking times before cooking and testing. Apparently, acids could also be added to baked goods, specifically combined with other ingredients to let a reaction happen while mid-bake. This reaction released gases that made the final food more ‘fluffy.’

Ferris wasn’t as familiar with baking, but he’d cooked using sour fruit juices extensively. More for their flavor than to soften tough meat, but the information was strangely fascinating. What could he do with it? It sparked several ideas to try changing some of his family’s recipes.

But… did he want that? To change them? Would it even matter, he wondered, if I never even get to cook?

He sighed, and tried to lose himself in the book.

Eventually, it was time for dinner. Reading about food had worked up his appetite, not to mention poor Jade. His familiar had listened to him talk about each chapter of the book in growing frustration, wondering when they would get to the eating part.

He hadn’t read more than the preface of the other books Gilbert had brought, but Ferris knew this would be a long-term project. After returning the books, he carried Jade back to Lily House, arriving just in time for dinner.

He had tested Jade’s memory several times at the training hall and library, and the slime still remembered everything. Serena had gotten a curious look when he pulled out the journal and crossed numbers off, but all his communication with Jade was non-verbal over the bond.

Dinner was not as exciting as the previous day, Cyrus and Brix being better behaved. Perhaps it was due to Karl’s presence, but maybe not.

In a bit of a coincidence, Mother Lily once more served bird meat with a sauce heavy on the citric. Had the woman once read the same book he had spent the day reading, or was it just more common knowledge here? Perhaps she had gotten the recipe from a friend of a friend, who knew the person that wrote the On the Preparation of Food. The meat was tender, all things considered.

Still, it could use a bit of heat. Why couldn’t it have been peppers that softened meats, instead? He lamented.

Ferris even dutifully recorded Jade’s rating of the meal in his new notebook, taking some time to record any previous ratings he could remember. It appeared most foods ranged from three to six.

Everything had been rated a ten to Jade, until compared to the magical berry. If Ferris found something better, would Jade shift the entire scale? The demons were rated low—with ones, twos, and threes—although the lanky demon got a five.

Just below my shoe, Ferris thought glumly, at a six.
