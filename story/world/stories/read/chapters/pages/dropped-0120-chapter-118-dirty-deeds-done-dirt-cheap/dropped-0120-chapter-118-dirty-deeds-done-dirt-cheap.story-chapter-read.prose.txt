Chapter 118: Dirty Deeds Done Dirt Cheap

“I did say you could visit any time, but I must admit, I didn’t expect you to come again so soon,” Joe Blow remarked, scratching his head as he watched me walk through the door to his shop.

”Well, you know how it is,” I shrugged. “I went to take a look at the main gates, but the queue stretched all the way to the horizon, and there’s no way I’m putting myself through that. It’s one thing to travel by cart, but I’m not sitting for days just to pass through customs.”

That much was even true, as I’d taken a detour on the way to the market district, making sure to be highly visible as I gawked at the blockade that had effectively cut the city off from the rest of the world. I didn’t think I’d done anything to warrant surveillance just yet, but it was always good to act like I had, and establish a proper alibi, in this case a reason why my plans had abruptly changed.

”They acted faster than I expected,” Joe grunted. “I did a bit of reading over the past day, of the last time emergency measure like these were brought in. Back then, the lockdown came in stages over the course of twenty four hours, which let the rich and connected avoid it, because they had the resources to move at a moment’s notice and beat the queue, while leaving the regular folk behind to suffer.”

“Of course,” I snorted, well familiar with the idea of express boarding for higher class tickets.

It had been my favourite perk bar none, back when forced to fly abroad on business, getting to walk the ramp ahead of everybody else, to the envious stares of the adults and the impatient wailing of their children. What can I say? It was important to take pleasure in the little things in life, even on something otherwise as dull as an extended commute. The lounges were a close second, of course, but everyone who made it into once of those was well-heeled, so it was far harder to stand out and show off.

“This time, the door slammed shut instantly,” Joe continued. “Even the local nobility were caught unprepared, and I hear more than a few were forced to reschedule or cancel hunting trips on short notice. One baron was dumb enough to complain about it in earshot of the Governor, and was sent to hunt in the western mountains instead, the poor sod.”

”What’s so bad about that?” I wondered. “Didn’t he get what he wanted, in that case?”

”You’d think so,” Joe laughed. “But nobody goes into those peaks unless they’re close to ascension. Some real old monsters live amidst the stones, their levels in the high nineties, and disturbing them is little more than an elaborate form of suicide.”

”Yikes,” Pumpkin muttered. “The higher ups aren’t keen on dissent, are they?”

”At times like this, showing a united front is important,” I lectured for his benefit, since Joe was unlikely to understand Cat. “Even a single voice raised against the lockdown risks causing a cascade of lawbreaking, so the only real option is to crack down hard. It might work, as well, so long as no scandals emerge, like nobles sneaking off to party through some back passage; it’s all about keeping disciplined, which is far easier said than done, especially if this drags on through weeks or even months.”

“There’s too many people in Heaven’s Reach,’ Joe added, unknowingly echoing the early discussion in Harvey’s safe house. “It’s fine for a few weeks, but if we start crossing seasons, then we’re in big trouble. The food will run out, and once that happens, the entire city will burn, no monsters needed.”

”Right, which is why we need to get a stockpile of the essentials together: food, weapons and medicine, the core requirements of civilisation. If we can get a foothold and keep it, then there’s a chance to come out of this, not only surviving, but far wealthier than how we started.”

”Ah, that’s your play then?” Joe raised an eyebrow. “This is where you make the sales pitch to get me to cut you in?”

”Not at all,” I rebutted, keen to avoid a misunderstanding. “This is more of a courtesy call, to let you know that we’re setting up shop nearby. Preferential rates for all our products, if you’re looking to buy, assuming you extend us the same privilege.”

”Pretty bold of you,” Joe observed, neither accepting nor rejecting my proposal right off the bat. “I don’t mind, necessarily, things have been boring until lately, so a bit more competition might be fun, but I know people who would take that as a declaration of war.”

Pumpkin picked that time to teleport, blinking from my shoulder to Joe’s, a claw placed softly against his jugular in warning. The old warrior’s reaction was fast, a hand moving reflexively to swat Pumpkin away, but the cat was already back where he started, half a second later.

”He’s got levels,” Joe blinked, looking properly at Pumpkin for the first time. “Not just a bet, but connected to the community? Alright, fine, I’ll bite. Give us ten percent below market rate, and I’ll do the same. Deal?”

”Deal,” I replied with a grin, reaching out for a handshake to seal the deal.
