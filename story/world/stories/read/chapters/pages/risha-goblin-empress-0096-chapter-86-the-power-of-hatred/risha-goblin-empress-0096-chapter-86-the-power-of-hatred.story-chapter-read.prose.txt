There was silence among the gathered packs, until a single wolf broke the silence.

“I will challenge you, goblin.”

A Gray Lupus Pack Elder spoke in a low tone.

Risha turned to the Kobold. He stood to the far left of the Elders, and he looked at her with dark eyes. The look he’d first shown her was one of hatred, and she had not forgotten him during her meeting with the Keeper.

As she watched, the kobold reached out, and several of the watching kobolds ran out to hand him long sword and a small shield.  She reached down to the two swords at her waist, and gently unwrapped their chains from their handles.

“Are you scared? Show me your bloodlust, goblin. Prove your power! Make us show our throats before you, killer.”

Risha paused at the last word, and stared at the kobold. “I do not want to draw your blood, and I do not need you to show me your throat. I only want your loyalty.”

The gray wolf narrowed his eyes, “You expect me to believe that? We all know the stories. We all have heard how you killed Earth-Shaker. Was there no pleasure in that? Did you not drink his warm blood in your frenzied murder?”

Risha glanced at the Keeper, who returned her look with a calm gaze. He was observing, they all were, wondering how she’d respond to the challenge, to the hatred.

Sun-Chaser stepped forward, ready to take the challenge, but Risha held her hand up. The first would be Risha’s. Her people could take the rest.

Risha finished unwrapping the chains, and held them tight in her fists. “He was a tyrant. And Risha does enjoy the blood of tyrants.” She gave the Grey Lupus a smile, her teeth razor sharp. “Are you a tyrant, Elder?”

“What a goblin thinks does not matter to me. I see where your path leads by looking at your past. It is filled with bloody corpses, and it will see all of our children dead.”

“No.” Risha drew Crimson and Scarlet. “The Orc chief would’ve seen all your children dead. Risha gave them a future beyond that. Do you look at my people- the evolved kobolds, and not see what they are now? Risha knows what it’s like to be unable to speak. I know what its like to be unable to say ‘I’.” She blew out a sharp breath, her eyes narrowing at the kobold as he stood ready, “I give my children more than death, even if it waits for us all.”

“Your words are hollow, killer. I will end you.”

No more words were said, as the Kobold stepped towards her, his speed making him appear before her in a second.

Risha stepped back, matching his speed with [Pack Leader: Gray].

Their exchange was brief and brutal. Risha deflected his blade, her swords singing through the air as she danced around him. He was good, and he was fast, but Risha’s technique was beyond him.

Switching to [Pack Leader: Black], her sword impacted the shield, knocking it from his grip. His eyes widened in surprise at her sudden force, but she kept moving.

She knocked his legs out from under him, then pierced his sword arm into the ground with Scarlet.

The claws of his freehand flashed forward, drawing blood from her side, before Crimson pierced that arm into the earth too.

Risha looked down at the pack elder, her blades calling for more blood.

He glared up at her now, his lips pulled back to reveal bloody teeth.

She matched the glare, her own teeth bared as she pushed her swords into the creature’s skewered arms, the earth giving before her blades. “Submit.”

The Kobold did not answer, his spirit unrelenting.

“You have lost,” Risha said, her words tinged with anger.

“Bloodied Murderer.” The words were said with a growl, the words that followed were fueled with the kobold’s hatred. “Butcher. Child Killer. Monster.”

Some part of her was cut by those words, and for the briefest moment, she considered ending him. She knew it was her right to do so, and she would have one less creature that wanted her dead. Her anger at this stupid kobold’s resistance filled her.

She wanted to bring them to freedom. And yet, they hated her. Cutting out these enemies would be so easy, and it would be quick.

Her eyes narrowed as she wrapped the chains of her swordes around her hand. Then she stepped away, and pulled her swords to her hands.

They dripped with the kobold’s blood, and she did not sheathe them as she walked back to her waiting packs.

The kobold pushed himself from the ground. “What, childkiller? Monster! Do you fear to end me? Everyone here knows the monster inside you.”

Risha spun around, and stepped through the shadows, appearing before the bleeding kobold.

She kicked him back down, and stabbed her swords into the dirt next to his head. “Risha is a monster, you brainless, ugly puppy! Risha takes that title with pride. She earned it through every death, every life she’s taken. Every friend that’s died for her. You. You are some spineless creature that hid away here, while better wolves died to protect you from the orcs. Earth-Shaker was a tyrant, but he fought them on the front lines. He saw the field of corpses.” Risha kneeled on the creatures chest, then leaned closer to the wolf, a hair away from the creatures bared teeth as she spoke. “You are no better than a rabbit, ripe for the hunt. Except a rabbit does not disgust me so.” Risha punched the wolf with her fist wrapped in chain.

His head flew to the side, blood dripping down through the fur of his cheek from the metal of her chains.

She stood up from the dazed wolf, and pulled her swords from the dirt. She looked up to the other elders.

They looked away, unable to meet her eyes.

She spat on the ground, then walked back to her people. She had no more patience for these elders. It was time to meet the shaman for more lessons.

***

Risha pushed the strange arcane symbols away and laid her forehead against the table of the small cabin.

“Are you alright, Risha?”

Risha looked up at the ancient shaman.

He sat calmly on the bed, reading a large book.

Risha opened and closed her mouth twice before speaking. “How are they so blind? I make their people better, and they still hate me.”

“Risha.” He snapped the book closed. “You do not want them to fall in line like blind sheep.”

“What’s a sheep?”

“Human cattle. That’s besides the point. You have a vision for the world, and it’s a good one. But it is not everyone’s vision, and that’s okay. You need people who see things differently.”

“Of course.” Risha frowned, “That’s why I have Elder Oltak and Elder Kulos. I know that they bring different things to my people. These wolves. They’re… blind! They hate me for no reason-”

“No reason?”

Risha paused, and stared at the shaman.

The shaman sighed, “Hatred does not come from nothing. It can be misguided, yes, but it always has a deep and personal cause. Why do they hate you?”

“For things I can’t control. They hate me for the kobolds that have died.”

“Yes. They do. Now how can you use that? How do you tell them that you see their hatred? There is a path to bring them to you, what is that path? How can you use their hatred to better your people?”

Risha didn’t answer the questions right away, instead contemplating her older brother’s words. It seemed insane to her, that someone who hated her so much could be used to better her people, but the shaman had wisdom she didn’t, and she wasn’t a foolish goblin that would ignore a wise shaman.

***

The next morning, the Elders waited for her outside their sanctuary. The Elder she’d bloodied the night before stood there as well, his hatred diminished, but not gone.

She walked forward slowly, looking between the many gathered wolves. Her own packs stopped several paces behind her as she walked towards the keeper.

She stopped in front of them, then slowly, she bowed to them. “I have caused your people pain. I have watched my children, your children, die. And no matter how much I wish that fact could be changed, it is the past. Risha, Mother of Monsters, bows to the elders of the pack.” She tilted her head, showing her throat, “And she shows her throat to those who hate her.”

Silence was the response. Not a soul had expected the actions of the Mother, her people least of all.

Risha stood up, and looked into the eyes of the Gray Lupus she’d fought the day before. “Join me, Elder, so that you can use your hatred to protect your people. Protect them from those who would cause them death. Protect them from me, if I find myself so far gone that I cannot see the lives I have taken. They need you, and as much as it angers me, I need you, most of all.”

The Elder met her eyes for a long moment, the silence heavy on the field in front of the kobold sanctuary. Slowly, inch by inch, the Elder bowed his head to her, “I do not forgive you, goblin. But you were right in one thing, I stood by when our people died to the orcs. I won’t sit still this time. Elder Fire-Tender recognizes the next Alpha, and will protect his people from her.”

His words shocked those who had sided with him, but after his acknowledgement, the other followed.

The Pack Elders bowed to Risha, Mother Of Monsters, one by one. Finally, only the keeper kept his head high. He smiled the smallest of smiles, then let out a long howl.

The Elders took up the howl, then her pack took it. Beyond them, the wolves watching from the hills howled. Every wolf in their homeland howled, a sound that rolled over the hills, spreading through the forest, and to the mountains.

Risha was theirs, and they were hers.
