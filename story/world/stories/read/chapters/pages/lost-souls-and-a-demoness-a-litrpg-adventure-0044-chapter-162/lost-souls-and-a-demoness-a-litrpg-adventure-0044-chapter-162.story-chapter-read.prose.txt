The Sanctuary was calm and tranquil, removed from the ongoing chaos of the Labyrinth proper. None of the tremors followed Jade and her companions into the moonlit forest glade, and even the remaining notifications faded away as Naomi closed the door behind them. It was as if they'd stepped into another world, leaving behind all the troubles of the old.

If only it were that easy…

Jade took in their surroundings, unsure what to expect. She felt slow, weighed down by a growing pit in her stomach. Luis's encouragement was all well and good, but their group's morale had taken a major blow. Jade didn't need supernatural empathic senses to tell that much, she could see it in their faces. None of them knew what to do, or even if there was anything they could do. This was so much bigger than them. Too much so.

"Skraag said one of the attendants would appear…" Jade said, forcibly focusing on the present. One step at a time. The Labyrinth, or maybe the goddess, had clearly been trying to get her attention for some time now. If she could find out why, maybe that would show them the way forward. "But I don't see anyone."The forest clearing was calm and silent. A row of tents was set in a loose circle in the middle, illuminated by soft rays of silver moonlight. They were pitched alongside familiar boxes of the basic supplies the Labyrinth always provided in its Sanctuaries. This one was no different than any other they'd visited.

"Do we have to do something to get their attention?" Luis asked.

"This new crisis might be keeping them busy." Tara suggested, crossing her arms. "I've never seen the Labyrinth system in such an uproar before, there's probably a lot that needs doing."

"Should we wait, then?" Naomi asked.

"I don't think we have much of a choice." Fenrin said, moving up to join Jade at the front of the group. "Tara is the only one of us that can even get to the fourth floor right now. Even if we wanted to help, we can't.""Your people live up there, right?" Naomi asked the tek'kalla. "Will they be okay?"

Jade hadn't even thought of that. She'd been so busy worrying about what these events meant for Earth that she hadn't considered Tara's people lived right on top of where the demons were apparently emerging.

"Our cities can move, remember? If things get too dangerous, they can evacuate the area. They're faster than most people think. Besides, they're full of experienced Wayfarers. They're probably already working to put a stop to this."Jade couldn't tell if Tara's confidence was genuine or forced, but she hoped her friend was right. They weren't alone in this fight. She had to remember that.

"If the conflict spreads to the second floor, I'm not sure what my people will do." Siora said anxiously. The elf paced back and forth nervously, unable to stay still. "We hardly have any warriors, and if they had to leave… I don't know where we'd go…"

"It won't get to that." Luis said firmly, clapping his hands once. "We don't know how bad this is yet. Instead of assuming the worst, let's do our best to make sure the worst doesn't happen."

"Luis is right." Jade rolled the crescent token around in her palm. "Step one is getting more information."

She extended her magical senses, using them to probe the coin in her hand. The system wouldn't identify it, but she could detect a faint trace of magic infused into the dark metal. It wasn't much, hardly more than the background magic that flowed through the air around them, but there was something there. Acting on a hunch, Jade held the coin up to her mouth and murmured into it.

"Damos."

Everyone fell silent, waiting to see if anything would happen. Seconds ticked by, the only sound the soft rustling of distant treetops. The chill night air crept in on them, and Jade pulled her cloak tight around her shoulders. Just as she became certain that there would be no response, the wind shifted. Currents of air swept around them, sending rippling waves through the tall grass. The shaft of moonlight brightened, standing out in stark contrast to the backdrop of dark woodland. And then, finally, a figure took shape in its depths. Tall, with greying hair and dark, formal attire, Damos emerged from the shimmering pillar of lunar light. He was exactly as Jade remembered. Calm, immaculate, and possessed of a reassuring aura that made her think, if only for a moment, that all would work out in the end.

"It's good to see you again, Jade. And all of you."

"It's good to see you, too." Naomi greeted him. She swallowed. "Are things… is everything…"

"I hope you have better news for us now than you did last time." Jade said, remembering their last conversation with the Labyrinth attendant. They'd last met all the way back on the first floor, when Damos had explained what the Labyrinth was doing to Earth, and how the current iteration of the Wayfarer trial had gone horribly wrong.

"Jade…" Naomi shot her a look. She hadn't intended the remark as a reprimand, but perhaps it had come off more strongly than she'd meant."Sorry." Jade said, grimacing. "But we're worried."

"You're not the only one." Damos said. He waved his hand, conjuring a table and chairs between them. Once, that display of magic would have shocked Jade. Now, she hardly even noticed it. "Please, sit." The attendant's gaze flitted to the token held in Jade's hand for a moment. "I'm curious to hear what your experiences have been of late. After that, I may be able to shed some light on your current troubles."

A short while later, after a quick round of introductions and an abridged retelling of their recent adventures, they all sat in silence around the large table. Damos bore a contemplative expression, his gaze lost somewhere in the forest depths. Jade itched to say something, to press him for answers and solutions, but held her tongue. When she'd first entered the Labyrinth, Damos had saved her life. He'd also been the first to explain the true nature of the Labyrinth to them and had been genuinely sympathetic to their plight. He'd earned a little patience.

"The goddess is trying to speak with you."

Jade blinked in surprise. She exchanged a glance with Luis, who shrugged.

"The goddess… you mean, Growth?" Jade asked hesitantly.

"Lady Elaria?" Siora supplied. "Is this about what happened when we were at her shrine?"

"It is." Damos hesitated for a moment. "This isn't something we, the Labyrinth spirits, that is, are supposed to share with anyone. But recent events have made clear that the old way of doing things isn't working anymore.""The old way?" Fenrin asked.

"The way the Labyrinth has operated. For thousands of years, it has stood strong and unyielding, always maintaining the strength of the seal on the world of demons, as you have surmised. It grows, changes, and connects to new worlds across the cosmos, doing everything it must to continue to function. But now, everything is starting to fail. The damage the demons have done is spreading. There won't be much time to fix it."

"So, it is possible to stop them?" Jade asked, a spark of hope flaring in her soul.

"I don't know." Damos admitted. "But I have a feeling that the goddess has been trying to speak with you for a good reason."

"How do we find her? Why can't she talk to us?"

"The Lady has been… indisposed for quite some time. The strain of…" Damos flinched, his words cutting off abruptly. "I should not say more on the matter, you would be better served hearing it from her. As for finding the goddess, that is something I can aid you in."

That was a start, but Jade wasn't satisfied. She needed to know more.

"Why didn't you tell us about the demons when we were learning about the Labyrinth?" Jade asked, crossing her arms. "Why keep it a secret? If we'd known earlier… if everyone had known earlier…"

"That was not my decision, but the collective decision of those who gave birth to this world." Damos replied calmly, unperturbed by her obvious frustration. For some reason Jade couldn't quite grasp, his implacable calmness was getting on her nerves. "It is also one I happen to agree with."

"But, why?" Jade pressed, leaning forward. She felt Naomi's hand on her shoulder, pulling her back. She slumped back in her chair with a soft sigh, relinquishing her anger.

"That is another question best suited to Lady Elaria, but I can give you at least part of the answer. Think of it like this. The more people who knew about the seal, the more people there are that could try to help undo it."

"Who would want to release an army of demons into the Universe?" Naomi asked skeptically.

"Anyone who wants power." Tara said quietly. "I can think of many ways this kind of disruption could be used to advantage on group or another, if they handle it right.""Not including anything the demons themselves might have offered for their aid." Fenrin agreed.

"Quite so." Damos nodded. "There was very little anyone could do to increase the security of the Labyrinth as a whole, its vital mechanisms are well hidden. So, additional knowledge of the nature of this world was only a liability."

"Except now they escaped anyway, and no one was ready." Jade pointed out.

"True, things haven't quite worked out as planned. Which brings us back to the present." Damos stood up from his seat at the table and slowly walked to the middle of the forest clearing. "It's time to answer Lady Elaria's summons. Perhaps, this time, she is ready to hear our call…"

"This time?" Jade asked. Everyone said the gods were gone. What had happened to them? And why was this one supposedly back, now? Surely it had something to do with the demons, but Jade knew too little to put all the pieces together.

Damos didn't reply. He stopped in the center of the ray of moonlight spilling through the leafy canopy, a pale slash of silver-white against a backdrop of inky blackness. Anticipation hung thick in the air as they watched the Labyrinth spirit raise his hands, slowly drawing them apart.

"Seed to sapling, Winter to Spring. Lady of Blossoms, we call upon your grace. Send to us your winds of change and grant us the guiding rays of morning's light."

"That's one of the ancient prayers." Siora whispered, watching with fascination. "From our oldest texts. The ones our ancestors brought from our lost home."

"Is she really listening?" Tara asked. "Prayers have gone unanswered for centuries. Longer, even."

Her answer came in the form of a ray of sunlight. It was faint at first, a weak shaft of sparkling color that struggled to outshine the moon. But then it was joined by another, and another. Before long, the entire clearing became alight with the brilliant rays of a breaking dawn. Somewhere in that glimmering curtain, a shape began to form. It began as a graceful arch, a rainbow of colors born from sparkling dewdrops. And from that haze of warmth and light, a doorway took shape.

Roots and vines rose from the ground, twisting a gnarled and curving path through the air. The two sides met in the middle, forming a tall, empty arch of wood. Buds grew along its length at an impossible rate, bursting into blooming flowers of white and gold. Jade had seen enough portals to recognize one forming before her, but this was nothing like any other she'd encountered. A sheet of falling petals blew across the opening, somehow thick enough to obscure their view through it. Despite that, a pleasant warmth radiated through the opening. It carried the warm scent of earth and life, carrying with it a promise of hope. Jade felt a sharp pain in her core as her twin aspects warred with each other, both yearning for and rejecting the presence of a divine influence. She masked her discomfort with a grimace, bearing with the pain.

"Thank you, Damos." A gentle breeze swept around them, carrying with it a melodious, gentle voice. Jade recognized it immediately; it was the woman from her dreams. Did that really mean… "My awareness of this realm is still limited, and your assistance is most welcome."

"Of course, my lady. And may I say, it is good to hear your voice once more." Damos gave a slight bow in the direction of the portal. Then, looking back at them, he gestured towards it.

"Wait." Naomi said apprehensively. "We're really going to meet a goddess?"

Fenrin muttered something under his breath that Jade didn't catch, but it sounded reverent. Siora also looked nervous, but there was a glimmer of eagerness and excitement in her face as she stood. Luis leapt to his feet without hesitation, apparently sharing none of his companions' nerves.

"Sure sounds like it." Luis said with a grin, already striding towards the portal.

"Wait, Luis!" Tara tugged at his arm. "We can't just barge into a divine audience. We don't know the proper protocol. Siora, your people follow her, right? What are we supposed to do?"

"I… um…" Siora floundered as she was put on the spot. She was saved from having to form a reply by a peal of gentle laughter.

"Do not be worried, child." The goddess's voice seemed to rise from the earth itself, her words tinged with amusement. "This is no time to stand on ceremony. Please, enter my domain. There is much I wish to share with your group, and little time with which to do it."

Jade followed the others towards the portal, lingering towards the back of the group. Her demonic essence rebelled against the divine essence emanating from the portal, shying away from it like a skittish animal. With effort, she was able to suppress the instinct to flee, aided in part by the celestial infusion in her spirit. It welcomed this, and Jade had the strange feeling of being nostalgic for something she'd never experienced.

One by one, they passed through the curtain of falling petals. It was time to meet the goddess, and to hear what she had to say.
