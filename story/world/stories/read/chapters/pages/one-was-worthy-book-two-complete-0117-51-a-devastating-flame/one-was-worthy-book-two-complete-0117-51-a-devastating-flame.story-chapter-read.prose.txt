***

There was a quiet lull in the combat as the sound of shifting earth finally faded. Men and women from both sides, paused to stare in shock. The twang of crossbows ground to a halt as hardly anyone had spent enough points in footwork to remain on their feet when the walls rose.

Below, the sound of magic also faded. Only now that I was gone did I notice the low hum that all of that wind magic had been making. The sun was almost completely down, and it was dark enough that some soldiers might not even be able to comprehend what had happened.

The moon was bright though, and it shone down on the walls making them shine like beacons.

In the distance, boulders still flew from trebuchets, though. I watched a few of them crest the massive wall only to fall beneath my line of view. They hit the wall with a resounding gong that shook the ground.

The wall didn’t budge.

It was a castle, made from unnaturally straight blocks of steel. Each one was easily the size of Stoutgruff, and from where I stood, they looked as smooth as marble.

I gaped in awe. I’d known my talent was legendary. I’d felt it in my bones when I was gaining free points at rates that others only dreamed of, but this… this?

I turned, looking back to the castle. I couldn’t see it. Of course I couldn’t. It was hundreds of yards away, but somehow I could feel the calming pulse of the lacquered wooden crystal that hovered on the dias. It suffused the entire area with a gentle aura that enveloped me, and made me feel like I was safe in the bar. I could almost picture it. Reid’s arms wrapped around me, Mom and Dad goofing off at the next table, and Hadra poking fun at us, Edra and Lupkep serving anyone, and Korlotom singing some awful hunters tune. Probably at his son’s expense.

I needed to be up. I needed to move, but I couldn’t. Instead I was struck dumb by the feeling, the awe of this place my talent had created, and the…

The…

The fourteen hundred and seventy fucking points I had earned from this!

“Rally! All magic free, all crossbows, fire!” came the general’s booming voice as the battle resumed.

There was a treeline on the north side of the river. Before I hadn’t been able to see much of anything over it, but now the walls stood above them, having cut through the woods in some places. Many Eschal soldiers had made it into the trench, but now that that trench had become a thirty or even forty foot battlement, those ambitious soldiers were left alone on top of a fortress with an army of angry Tacurians. Sharp screams echoed from a short battle on that battlement before they ended with a roar of cheers. I heard a few splashes of water as men were thrown off the wall into the moat.

Healing lights were on top of the walls, signalling that our magic was still working, and fire magic began pouring down off the battlements, but none returned toward us.

Arrows did still crest the wall, occasionally flying over it. One landed dangerously close to me and it was enough to snap me out of my stunned stupor.

I ran toward the North Wall, where I was sure the general would have orders.

“Eschal… Retreat…” came a furious voice that echoed throughout the battlefield, heedless of who heard it.

Freyna. I stopped, shocked. Sudden, rapturous, joy overtook me. They were retreating? They were fucking running away! We’d won!

My joy was short lived as a sudden scream pierced the air. I tripped mid stride and landed hard, but I barely noticed as that fucking woman nearly blew out my eardrums for a second time.

The Crystal stopped hostile talents, and magic, but not skills, it seemed. Luckily, I was far enough away, and the sound was not as strong behind and beneath the wall, but the moans of agony from atop the walls had me worried.

I didn’t have a long way to go, but I didn’t dare try to make the leap to the top of this wall. Instead, I leaped halfway up one of the many staircases. Men and women were shuffling down them but most were up on the walls so I made it to the top in two leaps.

Almost everyone was holding their ears, and I couldn’t blame them. I couldn’t think of any defense against such an attack. Fortunately, the attack seemed to have affected a large number of Eschal troops as well. Men who had been nearly at the walls were now screaming at the banks of the newly formed moat, while crossbows continued in a relentless barrage.

Wind magic didn’t protect those near the walls. The crystal didn’t allow hostile magic, and it seemed capable of differentiating. Wind barriers protecting those with hostile intentions evaporated, but the effect only extended a few yards beyond the moat. After that, the bloody and battered Eschal troops were making their way back to the distant treeline, protected by wind mages once more.

A cheer rose up, mixed with the wail of the injured, but with adrenaline pumping through each of us, the sonic attack hadn’t been nearly as effective as Freyna had probably hoped.

No more orders were issued audibly but I saw the General looking up at his banner, changing the symbols. He’d adapted immediately, and was already giving new orders. The attacks on the retreating soldiers resumed almost immediately.

“General!” I called.

He scowled, and pointed at his ears. He looked slightly dazed, but was focused enough that he could still issue commands via the banner.

Public Speaking allowed me to get my words to people far distant from me, but that wouldn’t help at all if they couldn’t hear me. I would likely be more useful on one of the other walls, issuing orders from one of the General’s subcommanders.

“We must have words after this is done!” he called, voice a little too loud.

I nodded. There was… there was no hiding anymore.

Suffer no legends. The town would do with me what they would. I’d saved all of their lives. If that wasn’t enough for them to let me keep mine, I didn’t know what was.

The general began giving me orders, but I frowned as I noticed a body on the battlement. An arrow was sticking out from her chest, and a pool of blood was still leaking out on the shining steel floor.

My heart seized.

The general’s eyes widened. He reached out and grabbed me, covering my eyes. I struggled, pushed him away. He was stronger than a bear, but he toppled easily, still dazed by the sonic attack and I rushed to the body, hoping it wasn’t who I thought it was.

“No…” I breathed. “No, no, no! I made them what they needed! I made them the shoes, and the crossbow, and the fucking jacket! I… I made her a helmet!”

To my horror, I saw that helmet a few paces away. A jagged hole was carved into the side. It had saved her from an arrow. Just… not the next one.

Sightless eyes stared up at the bright moon. A burgeoning hunter, just now finding her passion. She’d managed to shoot a deer before the first attack almost a week ago. I’d spoken to her just a few hours ago.

Elsee.

A knot lodged in my throat. I saw red.

I turned, and saw the woman, still flying, retreating with her troops. I was panting. Furious. I didn’t think I’d ever been so enraged in my entire life. Murderous rage. The type of rage that had caused someone to place their hands around April’s throat in my dreams.

The type of rage that ended nations.

Four hundred points in fire magic. Another hundred in Mana Pool. Two hundred more in Public Speaking. Every remaining point went to Mana Regeneration. I needed more mana and I needed it now.

My mana pool was a staggering thirteen thousand nine hundred and sixty five, and with so many points in mana regeneration, I could restore nearly the entire thing in an hour. I was gaining mana back at three per second.

My fist was clenched and my nails were drawing blood from my palms. I was panting like a dog, unable to contain my wrath.

Freyna would fucking die today.

“Soldiers, flee the gate!” Neogon shouted, as he backed away. He wasn’t afraid of the enemy soldiers anymore. He was afraid of me.

He needn’t have worried. I was furious, but I wasn’t so angry that I would ever hurt my own allies.

A wisp of flame burst from my palm before floating lazily around me, the same as my mother had done in another battle.

Another. Another. The wooden crystal allowed this because I meant no harm to anyone in Pemolar’s Hill.

“Freyna!” I screamed, before launching myself into the air. As I did, the night sky burst into flames.

Hundreds of wisps floated around me as I burned my mana. Maybe thousands. I didn’t care. I bounded all the way across the moat, landing amid the bodies of fallen Eschal soldiers before I dashed toward the enemy army.

Arrows began to fire at me, and I leapt high, moving so fast that even the idea of them hitting me felt impossible. I used flame to propel myself further, dashing after the woman with more fury and rage than I could ever remember.

My lungs were burning, but I didn’t care. Elsee’s dead eyes were all the fuel I needed and as I approached the closest retreating troops, I engaged Public Speaking and howled.

Some of them had been affected by their own queen’s attack moments before, but all of them thrashed, screaming as they fell to their knees.

“How the fuck do you like it, huh?” I asked.

Freyna herself seemed unaffected, though I had no idea how. Some sort of earplugs?

“P-protect the queen!” I heard the shout echo across the battlefield, as soldiers turned to face me. Good. I wanted them all to die.

My mana was rapidly replenishing, so I sent my army of wisps towards the enemy soldiers still attempting to cut me down. Flames pierced into dozens of retreating mages and fighters, but still more were coherent enough to use wind magic to disperse my tiny wisps.

So I made more.

I lined their hands with the oil as mom had taught me to do on the river and ignited the the spark, burning them well behind their ability to defend. They screamed as my wisps multiplied.

I heard it before it came. A sudden loss of sound, before a gale enveloped me. I was mid leap, and blasted backward. Tiny cuts formed all over my skin, ripping my armor. I could hardly feel them through my rage.

I held my hand to the right and a gout of flame burst out of my hand. Classic fire magic, but I used it as an engine, propelling myself away from the whirling storm of wind magic.

I landed hard on my shoulder, skidding along the dirt until I smashed into one of the tree stumps, but I rolled back to my feet before she could attack me again.

I surrounded the queen with mana in a ring, and lit it, only for her to fly upward. Wisps chased her as I was forced to grow creative. An entire sphere of mana.

I didn’t remain idle, dodging another blast of wind magic that came from a soldier to my right. Irritated, I pointed my hand toward him and a gout of flame engulfed him. His screams filled the night.

Elsee. There’d never been a sweeter girl in this world, and now she was dead. I was going to take as many of these bastards down as I could. Maybe that would bring her back. Maybe that would make it better. Maybe that would…

I yelled and more wisps formed. Freyna hadn’t been affected by my orb of flame. A whirlwind of magic had allowed her to burst free unharmed, but she was frowning down at me now. Her clothes were singed.

“You…” she murmured, her voice reaching me despite the vast distance between us. “I remember you. So you are the one making all these toys. I never imagined you could–!”

“Shut the fuck up!” I screamed. “Just fucking die!”

I used hundreds of mana and made four rings of fire mana before lighting them one by one. Then I sent them hurtling in towards her, flame lighting the night like a third sun. Each one separate and hopefully more difficult for her to disperse with wind.

I didn’t stop though. Nearby on the ground, I found a spear, dropped by an Eschal soldier. Scooping it up, I leapt into the sky, high above her, intent on skewering the murderous bitch.

Even through all the flames, and all the roiling wind, I still heard her.

One word.

“Weak,” she said.

All of my flames, including ones that weren’t anywhere near her, suddenly extinguished, night returning in full force. Wind magic used in the same way as my fire mana. I watched her gasp, at least glad I’d made her catch her breath, but she grinned suddenly as I fell.

Horrified, I couldn’t cease my momentum as she raised a spear of her own. Unlike mine though, hers appeared to be made of pure wind, glowing green lights that danced into the shape of a weapon…

…and I was falling straight toward it.
