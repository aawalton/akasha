Using all the attacks at my disposal, I pushed on. Left without an obvious weapon, the footman resorted to casting spells in his attempt to slow me down. It probably seemed a one-sided fight, but the truth was that I had a very limited window of opportunity. The boost effects of the monster spheres had already begun to wane. Every second the brightness of the room decreased. The magic circles had become little more than outlines.

Tactically taking a step back, I thrust my right blade into the monster’s shoulder. Blood and magic burst out in a final explosion of light.

The boost effects had finally worn off. Now I had only my mortal senses to rely on.

“Which side are you on?” the footman asked, grabbing his shoulder.

There was a chance that he was casting a healing spell of sorts, but I could no longer see it.

“Are you making an offer?”

It was always a bad approach to divide one’s attention during a fight. Without the right cards, a person’s efficiency in such circumstances would decrease by roughly a third. Veterans used to warn hotshot newbies about this all the time, and just to spite them, the newbies did the opposite, getting themselves killed. I had never been considered good enough to be told this directly, but I listened. Also, I was now experienced enough to know that the same could be said for the footman as well.

“Do you even have the authority?” I continued with my arc attack, targeting areas I remembered spell circles forming while they were visible.

“Scum like you would never be invited!” the footman hissed, losing any trace of etiquette he had. “My masters will crush your house for the arrogance you’ve shown!”

A boss monster shouting idle threats at me? That was a first. It suggested that in his mind he believed me to have the upper hand. With a grin, I performed another thrust, aiming at his chest.

The tip of my blade bounced off, as if I had hit a solid wall of glass.

Damn it!

This marked the turning point. One unsuccessful hit could be viewed as a misstep, but after the next, he would see that I had lost the initiative. I had to finish this now.

“Cathy!” I shouted. “Aim for the head!”

Using every ounce of strength I had, I performed a series of arc attacks. All of them missed, passing inches away from his body. Yet my goal was never to harm him, but to get rid of any magic circles he had created.

I crouched down, providing my teammate a clear line of sight to the footman’s head.

A flaming arrow split the air. A split second before it could hit, the monster raised its left hand. The arrow froze mid-flight. The footman had managed to cheat his destruction by casting a spell at the very last instant. Unfortunately for him, that provided me with just the distraction I needed.

Focusing both my attacks on a single point, I thrust my flaming blades into his stomach from below. Feeling the soft resistance of flesh, I let out an internal sigh of relief. My last attack had disrupted any protective spell circles, leaving him completely open.

“You…” blood spluttered out of the footman’s mouth.

Likely he was about to add something more, but whatever it was, he never got to finish. In a single instant, the seemingly invulnerable entity crumbled to a pile of dust on the ground, a single golden card on top of it.

My former self would have dropped both weapons at the mere chance of grabbing such a prize. Looking back at it almost made me disgusted.

The flames surrounding my blades turned orange, then disappeared altogether. Returning both weapons to my inventory, I took hold of the card.

THE MAGICIAN (VI, MAJOR, CHALICE)

(Rarity – Rare)

SKILLS (1/4):

1 MAGIC SIGHT: See magic

2 ???

3 ???

4 ???

Just one active skill. Harlequin hadn’t exaggerated when he had said that there would be prizes scattered about. The footman had a lot more skills than magic sight, and still they were locked. Not that I was complaining. Already I had gained far more than I would have if I had rushed the challenge. Now, it was a matter of not getting killed until the end. This was the real part of the trial: the more one got, the more inclined they were to hit and run. Getting a valuable Major Arcana card was already a big deal. It also proved that facing the actual boss would be tremendously more difficult.

“You can take their swords,” I said, hearing movement behind me. “Just be sure you can handle them.”

To little surprise, Carl rushed at the opportunity, a cheap lighter in hand.

While he rummaged through the remains, I went to Cathy and handed her two cards.

“Thanks,” I uttered.

“You keep them,” she said. The fear in her eyes couldn’t be hidden. Had I improved so much that she’d view me as a monster? Last run it had been the complete opposite.

“You need cards to get stronger,” I pressed on. “Trade them for something else if you want, but don’t leave food on the table.”

Once back in the corridor, I leaned against the wall. The fight had utterly exhausted me, but I couldn’t afford to show weakness. This was a performance not only for the deities watching, but for my companions as well. Right now, they saw me as superhuman, which meant they would follow my instructions without hesitation. More importantly, this way I was sure that none of them would try to backstab me.

Carl spent over ten minutes exploring the room. That was just as well, since it gave me a chance to catch my breath. The first thing I would do in the morning was to head to the promenade and take a nice long nap in the sun.

“Do you think any of the others got anything so far?” Cathy asked.

I cracked an eye open.

“Probably,” I lied.

With the exception of Marcus and the goth girl, I didn’t see anyone doing so well. In truth, I was still surprised that we were given a trial as advanced as this. Harlequin wasn’t trying to kill participants, that was obvious. What did he gain in the process, though? Trial three participants didn’t have enough skills to make it interesting, nor did they have the knowledge to come to any conclusion.

“A third might come out on top,” I added. “Unless there are further changes.”

“You think there will be?”

I turned her way.

“There always are changes.” At least during the first five trials. Afterwards, when the deities had full reign to play around with us, the changes would become less visible. As a rule, only the strong players got the attention, leaving the rest of us to watch from the sidelines. Occasionally, some minor deity would throw us a bone, but even that was rare. No one liked mediocrity.

I looked at my hand. The three rings were a bit too conspicuous. I would have to find some gloves before the next day.

Moving away from the wall, I took out my blades and extended them forward. Nothing seemed to be pulling them in any direction. Uncertain what to think of this, I turned around.

“What is it?” Cathy asked, seeing my change of behavior.

“Nothing.” I lowered my swords. “There are no more enemies aboard.”

“That can’t be. Harlequin said that—”

“There are no more significant monsters for the night,” I interrupted. “Or at least for the hour.” I looked both ways down the corridor. “We better find as many of the others as we can.”

“Alright.” She didn’t argue. “Are you done?” she shouted into the dark room.

“Give me a minute!” Carl snapped from inside. “It’s not easy to check everything without light.”

“Just hurry up, or we’ll leave you behind.”

I cracked a smirk. Three trials and Cathy had already formed the right opinion about the cockroach. Several minutes later, we were on our way. With my swords no longer serving as a compass, we had to figure out the layout of the yacht the hard way. A ship this size was hardly impressive compared to the other challenges I’ve been through. The walking was still annoying.

The first place we went to was the atrium. On the way, I came across the occasional duo of undead only to get them killed off by Cathy before I could make a move.

“Holy fuck!” Carl said. “What happened here?”

For the first time since I’d known him, the cockroach had actually made a valid point. The floors were clean, and all signs that there had been a fight in the area were completely gone.

“Careful,” I whispered. “Stay close and look out for anything.”

“Do you think there’ll be another wave?” Cathy whispered back her bow at the ready.

“I have no idea.”

Normally, the answer would be no, but that was for the standard early trials. Here, everything was possible.

“Carl, careful with that blade. Don’t swung unless something is out there.”

Having him get a bone sword was no doubt a bad idea, though not worse than having him hide behind us at every opportunity.

“Carl,” I whispered. “Was there anything interesting where you started?”

“My quarters?” His expression twisted in confusion. “The minibar was full.”

“Anything else?”

“Nope, not a thing. I checked the drawers and the closet. Nothing.”

“What are you thinking?” Cathy joined in.

“Maps,” I replied. “We’ll need one to figure out where is where. There must be a gift shop somewhere around here.”

Of the many shops visible, most offered clothes, sports goods and alcohol. I took advantage of the fact to take a pair of nice leather gloves. Carl, on his part, was quick to pocket a few bottles of vodka. Even without asking, I could see he was thinking of doing something stupid, like trying to make Molotov cocktails.

The only tourist shop was a small place next to the main lobby. It was full of postcards, keychains, and fridge magnets, but also had ship brochures. I grabbed a handful and started going through them.

“What are we looking for?” Carl asked.

“Information,” I replied. “The last challenge had info on the monsters disguised as newspaper articles. If they follow the same principle, there’s bound to be some info here as well, along with a map of the ship.

“Err, I don’t think you’ll need to go through those,” he said.

Nine times out of ten I would have ignored him, yet something in his tone made me look over my shoulder.

“There,” Carl pointed at the wall behind me.

A giant poster of the ship covered the wall. It was so large that I initially hadn’t recognized it. The typical junk that tourists would buy, it combined scant information with needless details in an attempt to appear serious. The majority of the decks were only passenger cabins, crew quarters, engine compartment, and such. At the same time, the atrium, the promenade, and the captain’s area were given a uselessly long description.

Even with that, it didn’t take me long to see the pattern. The escape area, as well as all starting areas, were indicated by name. Curiously, the casino wasn’t present.

“Found anything else?” I asked Cathy.

“The movie posters,” she said.

Both Carl and I rushed to join her.

“That’s the thing we fought just now, right?”

“Footman of Death,” I read the poster’s title. It was like one of those movie posters of the seventies with monsters, generic names, and screaming damsels in distress. There was no mistaking the footman, though. And just to reinforce the image, there were two flaming skeletons visible behind him.

“The Titanic?” Carl asked.

“Not everything is a hint,” I said as I skimmed through the others.

Of the dozens of monsters, only about four seemed that they might be actual opponents: the Dead Mariner, the Skeleton King, the Siren of the Dead Sea, and the Plankton Blob. Of them, only the Skeleton King seemed familiar. In one of the trials of my previous run, he was an area monster. A few deities had whispered that killing him would grant me something very nice, yet I still hadn’t dared. Attacking anything with a royal title was suicide. Unsurprisingly, the deities in question never spoke to me again.

“Think we’ll face a kraken?” Carl asked, still rumbling through the posters.

“I doubt it.” I took the five posters from their exhibition frames and rolled them up. “Let’s go to the pool.”

“What about the map?”

“Take it if you want. We already know what’s there.”

The pool was on the same deck as the bottom of the atrium. That didn’t mean that reaching it was straightforward. As we soon found out, there was no direct connection between the atrium and the pool section. We had to go several decks up, then continued on before heading a few decks down again.

As we approached, the double doors marked as the pool’s entrance, I gave Cathy and Carl a sign to remain quiet. There was no telling what we might face once inside. From what I was able to assume, the indoor pool area had to be as large as the atrium. That meant that a large group of undead could be expecting us there, along with a few skeletons to boot.

I pointed at myself then at the door. Getting the message, Cathy nodded.

Gripping my swords tightly, I mentally counted to three, then rushed through. Both doors swing to the side, revealing a well lit area. The smell of water and chlorine filled the air, creating the familiar swimming pool experience. Instead of undead, however, what I saw were several inflatable boats in the middle of the Olympic-sized pool. They had people on them.

“Glad you made it, Greg,” a man in a green jogging outfit said. “What took you so long?”
