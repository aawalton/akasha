They were already committed.  If I ordered what were nominally “my” forces back, they’d get cut down by crossbow bolts and magefire.  Somehow, Harald had turned a bunch of the once-unhappy citizens of Longbottom into a small but relatively powerful force.

Magical barriers flickered into existence to counter the barrage of blasts of ice and fire that came sporadically from the walls.  Harald’s militia fielded an unusually large number of mages for a provincial force, but so did the defenders it seemed.  The large wicker shields were used to soak the crossbow bolts and arrows.

I’d only just gotten over my full belly and couldn’t afford to just wantonly slaughter the defenders crowded atop the walls and gatehouse.  But the sight of a dragon was often enough to send guardsmen scurrying for cover.

I blasted fire into the air and roared at maximum volume as I tucked my wings and stooped at the gates.  As anticipated, a good third of the defenders simply bolted.  At the same time, Harald’s soldiers let out a ragged cheer and charged forward to cover the last fifty metres to the gates.

“Missa Somnambulis!” I yelled, and the guards who hadn’t been bright enough to run fell down snoring.  My claws bit into the stone, and I caught my balance perched atop the wall like I was using a French toilet, my misbehaving minion and his troopers were figuratively—and possibly literally—in the shit.

Not planning on actually taking a dump on them—they were still my people, after all, and I felt compelled to protect them—my tails coiled together.  All eight of them formed a thick club of scales and muscle that slammed down and forwards below me.

The gates boomed.  Again.  On the third strike, they shattered, shards of wood shooting across the stone-paved courtyard on the other side of the barrier.  My snout was already hovering ten feet off the ground on the inside, scanning left and right, looking for enemies rushing to try and hold against the breach.

None presented themselves.  I would have just put them to sleep anyway.

My tails unravelled and rose up behind me like a multiheaded serpent was growing from my ass,  and the handful of guards who did arrive immediately turned tail and fled.

“Bob!  Good timing!”  Harald grinned up at me from the courtyard.  His hair had grown out, and his beard was thicker.  He wore simple plate armour and carried what I could only describe as a massive bloody claymore.

“What the hell are you doing, William Wallace?  Bringing FREEEDDDOOOOMMMM to your neighbours at the point of a sword?” I snapped.

“You got bigger!”  he said happily.

“Answer my damn questions.”  This time I physically snapped, my fangs clopping together a foot from his face.  Master of Puppets kicked in, and he started to spill the beans.

“Dimpleworn raiders were attacking the miners on their way to work.  When we started sending guards out with our people, the bastards switched to the farmers and traders.  You couldn’t get a wagon more than ten miles down the roads!  We caught a few of them, and they were all coming from Dampcut.”

“So you decided to take what, ninety dudes off to war?  Why didn’t you tell Kat?”  I opened a portal, and a ready team of bunnyborgs spilled out.  They spread across the yard to encircle Harald's fighters.  For the time being, they faced outward, looking for external threats.  That could change, and that knowledge probably wasn’t lost on my wayward minion.

“You wanted us to just take it and wait while Kat organised some relief?  We’re men!  And women—” he added hastily when a woman wearing red robes scowled at him, “—We aren’t weak!  With a Hardprick in Longbottom, I had to show my strength.”  His voice dropped so only I could hear.  “You have no idea how weird it is to be the focus of a bloody cult.”

“At least it’s not a weird prophecy… oh.  Sorry.”

“Yeah, I’ve got that one too.  One thing leads to another,” he said glumly.

Should I just kill Harald?  It would solve the whole issue of the prophecy associated with his incarnation by the system.  If IMPS wanted a Hardprick in play, it was probably for a reason, but did I need to care?

“Go and take the town,” I growled.  Now I had my infinite respawn, my no permadeath loophole, karma wasn’t too much of a concern, but… I didn’t want to be that dragon.  The second Golden Rule was undoubtedly the best Golden Rule: don’t be a dick.  Killing Harald would definitely fall under the category of “dick”.

“Hardpricks!  Let’s go take the mayor’s residence and claim this shithole for the Barony of Bob!”  A cheer went up from Harald’s soldiers, who broke up into squads of ten and began charging off down the streets and alleys to finish off the guards and secure the prize.

I sighed and crouched lower on the gatehouse before jumping upward and flapping my wings.  I circled the town and contemplated how burning it down might well be a mercy killing.

The thatch blew off the first few houses I passed, it was old and rotten.  Normally, I expected to see chipped paint on the rundown parts of towns on Helstat.  This place just ignored the whole concept of paint and skipped straight to mouldy-looking, rotten wood and moss-covered stone.

It seemed that Dimpleworn did not care for his villagers.  Having met a few of my own, I really couldn’t hold it against him, but I’d found that improving their lives improved my reputation, which I was planning on converting into higher tax rates when I got the chance.  Fat, happy, and rich peasants were in the interest of the hoard, and if there was one thing I would happily admit to, it was loving my pile of shinies.

The surrounding farms were equally dilapidated. Fences were badly repaired, no paint to be seen, and all the animals were thin and half-starved.  The handful of people, all of whom fled in terror as my shadow passed over them, I saw were all as skinny as their animals.

I did like to see mammals flee beneath me.  Resisting my urge to stoop and terrify them further—I definitely wouldn’t try and eat them if I indulged the urge—I appreciated this affirmation of the natural order.  Scaly overlords on top, mammals knowing their place.  Vanity preened in my mind and sidled towards Lust, so I blocked out my degenerate menagerie.

The woods surrounding the farms were thick and dark.  I circled down and walked along the edge of the one closest to the town.  There were scattered patches of trees out for another five miles, but even this isolated little woodland felt old and… slightly evil?

It was hard to define what it was that set my scales on edge, but there was some kind of threat amongst the trees.  How the humans managed to live so close to this sensation, I had no idea.  It would be like sleeping next to a tiger cage, when the cage bars were made of cardboard.

The branches rustled and waved, seeming to reach for me as I got closer.  Stepping into the shade of the first tree, I felt the temperature drop.  Whoever had cleared most of these woodlands and beaten back the primary forest had been tough.  One of the branches was definitely growing in my direction.  Slowly, but unmistakably.

Backing away, I crouched and launched myself skyward again.  Someone else could deal with the creepy trees.  I headed back towards the town and flew down to settle next to the largest, fanciest-looking house in the centre of town.

It still didn’t have any paint on the walls, like all the rest, but it was in much better condition.  The windows were all shuttered, and the door was firmly closed.  Harald and his people were fighting their way through the town; street fighting seemed to come naturally to them.

They had been advancing carefully, scouting ahead with magic to trigger traps or ripping their way through walls to avoid bottlenecks.  It had been impressive to see, hence why I’d just skipped helping them and went straight for the belly of the beast.  A lip curled as the thought passed through my mind, because that was probably the final destination of at least one mouthy guard and the town mayor.

But first, diplomacy was probably worth a shot.

“Open up and come out with your hands up!”  I yelled at the mansion.  Nothing moved.  “I’m not kidding, if I have to come in there, you’ll regret it.”

“Piss off, dragon!”

“Are you a guard or the mayor?”

“Does it matter?  I said get lost!  Lord Dimpleworn will be here soon, and then you’ll regret it!”

“Not soon enough for you.”  The voice was coming from the window next to the door.  The ground floor windows were more arrow slits than anything else; only the upper windows looked like they would be useful for letting in light when the storm shutters were open.  I saw a glint of a shiny steel helmet in the gloom behind the enchanted wooden shutter.

“Captain, maybe if we surrender he’ll—”

“Hello, sensible guard who has a slightly longer life expectancy than your boss!  Yeah, if you surrender, we can stop the fighting.  My troops will be here soon enough, but it’s not like I need their help.  I mean, look at me.”

I stuck a pose, head curled up to one side, armoured bulk flexing for maximum effect as golden sparks rained down around me.  A crossbolt bounced off my throat.  My head turned slowly back towards the window like a tank turret tracking a target.

“Back off monster!”  The captain didn’t sound so confident anymore.

“Was that some kind of magic bolt?  Allow me to introduce myself.  I am the Dragon called Bob.  Baron of Fidlers Mill, Longbottom, and soon, Dampcut.  I run a series of fine dining establishments as well as the only current portal network in the Empire.  I am the master of the Unnamed Dungeon, as well as Bob’s Bargain Bonanza, soon to be the greatest trade hub in the world.  Let me make some assumptions about you…”

I paused for breath and savoured the terrified whispering behind the arrow-slits.

“You are probably not locals.  No decent person would look around this town and tolerate it in this condition if the people were their loved ones.  So you’re outsiders that Dimpleworn has brought in to oppress these poor bastards for some unknown reason, but most likely because he is a dick.  This place means nothing to you, so it hardly seems right for you to be eaten by a dragon for your dick of a boss.  Am I about right?”

“You have no idea who you’re messing with, Baron.  I suggest you pack up your little mob and piss off fast.”

“You genuinely believe he’s going to rescue you.  You’ve got an orb to speak to him.”

“He knows!” hissed the guard who might yet live.

“Shut it!”

“I can still hear you.  So… this relief force must be close, right?  Really close if you think keeping me talking here will buy you enough time to get rescued.”

My mind whirled through the possibilities, but I kept coming back to the simplest explanation.  All the shit that had been going on with Longbottom had been this nineteenth division, and it had been operating out of Dampcut as a base of operations.    That explained why the asshat hiding behind the wall had a direct line of communication to his relief force.  It would also explain why the local guards had so many mages and had been fighting so well.

Not well enough that Harald and his berserkers hadn’t cut through them, but much better than low-paid, badly trained local guards should have been able to manage.

I opened a portal and called through a dozen bunnyborgs.  “Go make them into organic origami.”  I waved my nose at the mansion and braced my wings as the door exploded under mana-cannon fire, and one of the robobunnies started singing Auld Lang Syne.

Climbing for altitude, I flew east.  Harald wouldn’t have managed to slip past a small army's worth of scouts, so the main force must have been pulled together and sent back to trap him against the walls as he laid siege to the town.

Nothing out of the ordinary was moving.  Deer, birds, the occasional stoat, they all scattered as I passed ahead, but there was no sign of thousands of soldiers tramping closer to what I was preemptively thinking of as my latest real estate acquisition.  I started cutting back and forth to the north and south and gained some altitude.
