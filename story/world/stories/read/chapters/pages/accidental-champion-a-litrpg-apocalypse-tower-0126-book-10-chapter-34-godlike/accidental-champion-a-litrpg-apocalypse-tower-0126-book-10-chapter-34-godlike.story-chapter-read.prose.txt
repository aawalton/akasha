Xavier stood in the Dark Tunnel, twenty feet from a Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast. His hearting was switched off. His spiritual sense was switched off.

His rage was switched on—to one of the highest settings it had ever been on.

The Ragatni struggled, trying to prise itself from the grasp of the undead hands wrapped around each of its legs, but it wasn’t strong enough to wrench itself from their grip. The thing was still on fire, and those flames were burning through the flesh of those hands, revealing the bones beneath, but even that wasn’t enough for them to weaken their grip.

The fear heightened. The entire Dark Tunnel absolutely reeked of it.

It cast another spell—Roots of the Grave again. The Void Dungeon created beings didn’t seem to be affected by cooldowns, even though they were all inside an incredibly powerful time dilation field. Xavier didn’t care to wonder why that might be right now, though he assumed it must have something to do with how the void functioned.

Xavier sensed the spell easily. Even without his spiritual sense he could tell exactly where the hands would come from. He felt the vibration in the ground, saw the outlines of energy about to burst free.

He didn’t make the mistake he’d made last time. This time, he simply teleported away from the hands. The rage may be fierce within him, but it was still a cold, calculating rage that allowed him to use all the faculties of his mind. He wasn’t about to let his arrogance get the better of him again. Not against this damned thing.

As the grasping hands shot out of the stone, Xavier sent out a wave of energy. Speed Energy—or, more accurately, for it had a mixture of other things within it, temporal energy. The spell pattern had formed instantly, on instinct, and he knew exactly what it did and how it did it.

The wave flowed toward the hands, caging them, freezing them in time before they could reach far out of the ground, stopping them in their tracks well before they’d become a threat.

He hadn’t even needed to touch them with that spell. A Time Prison wave. Until that moment, he hadn’t imagined such a thing would be possible. He made it so it only affected the undead hands, so when it brushed over the Ragatni it did nothing at all. He didn’t want to trap the Ragatni. He wanted it to remain exactly as it was, watching everything that was happening, amping and amping up that fear.

Xavier grasped the haft of the war hammer and twirled it in his grip. It was heavy, especially in this gravity, but he found he could still do this with ease. He started whistling. He couldn’t hear the whistle, not while his hearing was switched off, but he could see the sound waves emanate from him and flow down the Dark Tunnel.

The sound made the Ragatni twitch.

He walked toward the Ragatni, starting at a sedate pace just as the beast had done when it had strolled toward him not that long ago. Then he began to jog. Soon, he was sprinting, headlong toward the beast. Eventually his wings flapped, taking him to the air. Other things were at work too—spell patterns that were helping to enhance his speed, spell patterns that were pushing gusts of wind down from behind him to make him move even faster than before.

He even burned a small portion of his Reality Energy, making his entire body work even more powerfully.

When he deemed he was moving fast enough, he teleported, using a specific spell pattern that allowed him to keep all of his momentum. His hammer was held in both hands now. He couldn’t do an overheard strike, there simply wasn’t enough room in this place, so he swung in from the left.

As he swung, another spell pattern was cast. Something that made the bone war hammer weigh a thousand times as much as it had just a mere moment ago. The weight was so much it slipped from his grip, but that didn’t matter.

The hammer slammed straight into the target—the butt of the bone spear that was still sticking out of his enemy.

Xavier kicked off the beast’s head and watched as the spear was plunged deeper. It pierced through the toughness of its skull and slid straight into the thing’s brain. There was a snap. Not one he could hear, but one he could see. Both the soundwaves and what had caused the snap. The bone spear had broken in half, but it had done its job.

The eyes that had been gazing so intently at Xavier with unbridled fear now deadened as a flood of energy flowed into his body. He spun around in the air, made his wings catch him, then landed smoothly on his feet.

The Ragatni collapsed. The undead hands Xavier had summoned through sheer instinct were still grasping its legs. There wasn’t really any room for the beast to collapse, so it sort of slumped into the wall as dead weight.

Xavier released a long breath, shutting his eyes. The cold rage he’d felt dissipated about as quickly as it had come now the threat had been defeated. Though when Xavier had switched off his hearing and his spiritual sense, he was sure the beast had no longer been a threat to him at all.

Now that the cold rage was gone, he felt… different. Sober, he supposed. He’d been in full use of his mental capabilities. Not a single sense had been deadened. And he’d certainly been in full control of himself.

But the thoughts that had entered his mind didn’t feel quite as though they were his own. The way he’d wanted that beast to suffer, the way he’d wanted it to feel fear… it wasn’t usually something he craved. Though he knew there were times he’d been enamoured by the powers he wielded—many times, in fact—it made him wonder what had come over him in that moment.

He shook those thoughts away. They were concerns for another time.

Xavier opened his eyes and walked over to the Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast he’d just slain. As he tilted his head to examine it, he banished the hands he’d summoned. He climbed over several of its legs so he could get where he needed to go, putting himself in the right position so he could cut through the beast and retrieve the Void Dungeon Mini Core from inside it. The longer they remained outside his Storage Ring, the more their energy seeped out from them—not that he knew how to access that energy. Not yet.

And speaking of energy, the energy he’d gained from the kill had been far more than he’d expected. It was more than he’d needed to refill all his cores from what he’d used up to kill the beast. He was still cycling all that energy now, funnelling it to the different cores, and he realised that while he may not know how to advance in his Denizen path here without the ability to gain levels, he could certainly advance in his Cultivation path.

He pumped all the extra energy he’d gained from killing the beast into his Reality Core. As he cycled it through his body and in and out of that core, he changed and shifted the cycling technique, wildly improving its efficiency. All the while he was cutting into the Ragatni with a long bone scalpel, thinking about what he’d just done.

Xavier had known his ability to create spell patterns on the fly had been drastically improved since he’d expanded his senses—and that was most definitely an understatement—but he couldn’t help but be a little shocked by everything he’d done.

The ice flowing had been easy enough, of course. So had the temporal cage; the first one, at least. But what he’d done after he’d pushed free of the Ragatni’s hold? The wave of temporal energy, as his mind seemed to think of it, that had pushed out and frozen all those undead hands—hands that had turned to ash when the Ragatni had died, he’d noticed, cancelling out the hold time had over them…

How in the hell had he managed something like that?

And that wasn’t all—he’d recreated the Roots of the Grave spell. There were subtle differences between his spell and the Ragatni’s spell, but it was essentially the same thing. Except, Xavier had never performed that kind of spell before. He’d never practiced any of the necromancer’s art, though he had a few similar spells from his Reaper line they weren’t things he found himself using very often anymore.

Yes, he knew how to create spell patterns, he knew how to speak the language of the runes—at least in a rudimentary way—and he could make spells do almost anything he wished… but he’d never done it like that before, never on this level. Usually, he relied on spells he’d already tested when in battle, or only adjusted them a little.

And this had been different. Incredibly different. He hadn’t even needed to think about what he’d wanted to do, not really. It wasn’t like solving a puzzle. He just willed and it happened—the right things came about. He’d increased the speed and power of his body. He’d increased the weight of that bone war hammer by a thousand times—and it was still that heavy, lying on the ground where it had fallen, having caused a crack in the stone floor of the Void Dungeon.

Once he’d retrieved the Void Dungeon Mini Core and deposited it along with the corpse of the Ragatni into his Storage Ring, Xavier brought himself back to his full height. He stood there a moment, wondering what this could all mean.

It feels like I have the kinds of powers a god would wield…

He was no god, and he didn’t want to fashion himself as one, but there was no other way for him to think of it. What he’d just done… they were indeed godlike powers. It wasn’t the first time he’d felt like he’d possessed such powers. When he was combined with the other Xaviers, their minds as one and their powers as one, it felt as though anything was possible, and perhaps it was.

But this? This was all him, alone. One being.

It made him wonder what would happen when he doubled this power with an unfolding.

Or when he timed it by a billion or a trillion, then pulled power from all the inhabitants in the Xaviers’ shared empire… He would be so much more.

There was a selfish part of him that coveted the power, that only wanted it for himself, but that was a very small part. He knew such a thought was a foolish one. He needed that unity between him and the other Xaviers, and the other universes, if he were to ever defeat the Interdimensional Dragon.

Were these the powers you intended for me to learn to wield when I came here, Derrida? I wonder…

Xavier moved to grab the bone war hammer from the floor, but he found he wasn’t strong enough to lift it. He grinned and shook his head. He could reverse the spell, even bring the item into his Storage Ring, but he rather liked the idea of another being stumbling upon the hammer and wondering where it had come from.

He turned from where he’d slain the Ragatni and walked down the tunnel, stretching out his arms and rolling his head around his neck. He’d already switched his hearing and spiritual sense back on and could feel another being watching him farther down the Dark Tunnel.

Xavier smiled. It was time to move forward in the Void Dungeon—time to move farther than Derrida even imagined he could.
