The holodisplay took up the whole east wall of the WARCOM, and Veric had put every name on it himself.

Twenty-two columns… with a hundred to a column and right somewhere in the middle of the seventh row he had stopped thinking of it as just a roster and started thinking of all the brave men that were willing to fight for the city which even the gods had abandoned.

His hand ached from typing out all the names. He was glad of the ache. He could have had the computer or the analyst just come up with a list, but typing in each name had made Veric aware that… some of these men would not be returning.

This was the most honest thing he had done in a while, and it helped. He knew perhaps sixty of those names by the face and all of them were leads since he was in charge of commanding them over the past few days.

The room had slowly filled while he wasn't looking, and the cold blue lights along with the holodisplay only made the room look and feel dead. Veric had asked Illiana twice to warm the color.

All twenty-two company leads were in one room along with the other high commanding personal for the first time and the last time.

The tension was at an all-time high, but Veric didn’t care for that. Knowing his boss, he left all those worries to him.

His eyes swept the room.

Bram sat with his forearms flat on the desk, like a man bracing for a wave, and as the 1st company lead, he was effectively also the commanding officer for all the companies. For a newbie in this line of work and to put this heavy responsibility onto his shoulders was a big ask. But the man was holding just like the others.

Fear can only grow so much, he thought.

Veric found his two men who he was pretty sure were uninvited for this meeting after showing little interest in bearing the burden to lead. Jack turned a pen over between two fingers, a small dry click each time it came round. Gleeward looked bored but sat at attention anyway.

"You're not on the list," one of Veric's analysts said, low, not quite meeting the scarred slab of Gleeward's face.

"I'm aware."

Nothing else came. The analyst caught Veric's eye, and Veric moved his chin a half inch toward the door. The man nodded, letting the two stay.

Across the table, the twins were also present. They had taken more of a police role after the cleanup, and the civilians held them in very high regard.

"Cheer up, brother," Brocki said.

"We are planning a battle," Flocki said, "that we might not come back from."

"And you'll do it with a smile, or Mum hears about the sulking."

Flocki did not do it with a smile.

Mathew stood at the head of the table, and Joe held up the far wall with one shoulder. Mr. Silvers stood on the commander's other side, so still that a junior lead had already absent-mindedly tried to hang a coat on him.

"Everyone. I insist, please find some chairs." Veric had counted eleven people standing, and he wanted them all sitting before the boss opened his mouth, lest they find the rug swept out from under thier feet.

"I'm fine standing," Salvatore said.

"It's not for your legs, sir. You're frightening the new ones." Veric said. "Sit down and be a person for an hour. Besides—” He gestured towards Boss. “—Once the boss opens his eyes and starts laying out whatever humanly impossible plan, he's thought this time, we'll all want to be sitting."

The laugh went round, and it was a real one, and Veric felt the room let out about a quarter of the breath it had been holding.

"Fair enough." Salvatore took the nearest chair.

« I think that covers everything, Jin. »

Jin opened his eyes. The room was packed, everyone was settled in with a briefing document and various reports running on the holodisplay.

He looked around, returning nods, and judging by their expressions, it seemed everyone was waiting for him to start.

He took a deep breath.

"Alright, people.” He said. “I hope you all are settled?"

A chorus of yes sirs.

“Give me a moment before we begin,” He said.

This is the best that I'm getting for the war, is it?

« Yes. I’ve run close to four hundred and eleven thousand simulations. The plan is the one with the highest survival probability I could find. »

Jin sighed and slid into the grey world of [Sovereign's Indifference]. For the next hour, he needed to be a calm and cold leader.

"I know how everyone is feeling here," Jin said. "Uneasiness, uncertainty, despair and doubts… whether we can even do this? Can we face 100 to 1 odds?”

“But we have to do this, people,” he panned his gaze across the table. “This will be the last meeting. Tomorrow we either win or we die. Its as simple as that."

He gestured at the display. "I know 2200 may not seem enough and might not be enough to match the cult in raw strength, and I'm not going to stand here and pretend otherwise." He tapped the top of the board with two fingers and worked down. "Time was short and we couldn’t practice or drill you guys more, but what you all have learned over till now has been for very this moment. The cult isn't a hydra where you take one head and get two. It's a nest of nasty insects, and you don't clear a nest by counting. You take the queen they've all coiled around and erase them."

He let a wisp of aura off his shoulders, and every eye in the place came up off the briefing sheets and onto him.

"The queen is the ritual. With the trojan in place I can wrest its control for a very brief moment from the cultist and use that to wipe them out. Reaching the nexus of the ritual… that's the target. Everything else in this room exists for me to get in to break the damn veil."

His hand moved down the columns.

"Companies one through six are the head of the spear. They will be on offensive protocols. Seven through sixteen are the body… sustainer protocols. They keep the head from burning itself out, and they carry the wounded back down the throat of the formation while we are and we would be moving."

His hand dropped to the bottom.

"Seventeen through twenty-two are the tail. The Anvil holds the causeway behind us… nothing gets in, and nobody runs out, and I do mean nobody.”

"Every company has a lead. Every lead has a second." Jin said. "If a lead falls, the second steps up inside five seconds, and the company keeps moving. A company that stops moving on that ground is a company that dies on that ground."

"Aid stations at the barbican and here." Two taps. "Body recovery is not a mission. If your man goes down and he's confirmed, you mark him, you leave him, and you hold the line. We come back for the dead when the cult is wiped out." He waited. "Anyone who breaks formation to carry a corpse gets two people killed instead of one.”

Nobody argued.

Jin wished somebody had. An argument would have meant one of them still believed there was a version of tomorrow where the order didn't matter.

He then brought up the map of Cathedral Ward.

“One road in.” He traced it with his finger. A narrow causeway stretched along a spur of land, black water on either side, leading to a single gate set into a single wall.

“That’s it. The Ward is a fist of stone thrust out into the sea, and that causeway is the only wrist connecting it to the mainland. Once you’re through the gate, there’s only one way forward: up the main axis, into the precinct, through the cathedral, and then down again. That’s the terrain.”

Elenor leaned in, frowning. "How do you know the interior? We've never had eyes past the outer wards."

"Not important at this moment," Jin didn't look up from the map.

"From—"

"Not important." He let his aura mix into his voice, and that shut her up. She had a valid question, but all this info was based on one very weird experience that he wasn’t sure he could explain to them. He had no choice but to silence her.

"Then we shouldn't take the causeway at all," she said instead. "Sir Joe puts us past the wall and we skip the gauntlet entirely."

"No," Joe said.

Every head turned.

"Better you all understand it now." He said. "Cult's got the Ward area wrapped in the most complex, layered spatial seals that I have ever seen. No doubt this is one of the countermeasures they have taken to combat me. Which means our plan worked. Bad news: me, Sal and Mathew would have to face more difficulties. My doors will still work, but they won’t work for anyone beyond myself."

"So hear it plainly." Jin's voice came up over the muttering and cut it in half. "There's no extraction. No reinforcement from outside. Once we cross that threshold, the only door left in the world is the far end of the fight." He looked round the table, and there was nothing funny anywhere in him. "Forward is the way out. There isn't a second one."

"So we're walking into a cage," Lennon said, slowly, "that locks behind us."

"Pretty much."

"Right," Lennon sat back. "Just wanted it said aloud."

"That's fair." Jin's mouth did something that was nearly a smile. "Alright. Simple version, for those of you who've been awake for two days. Think of the army as a rocket."

The display flared, and the rocket that came up on it had been drawn by hand, badly, with what appeared to be a face on the nose cone. Half the room laughed. Somebody at the back said oh, that's going in the histories, and Jin cleared his throat until they stopped.

He snapped his fingers. Labels bloomed along the hull.

"Give or take, that's our formation. First objective is to breach the abominations and drive all the way through the Ward." He raised a finger. "Companies two through six carry the chain offensive… AOE sorcery in sequence, on Veric's count, and nobody fires early."

He looked at Bram, and at Bram's second, a young woman called Clara Mashburn.

"First Company and X are the core. The destroyers. They stay dormant until the breach opens, and until then the glory belongs to everybody else in this room." He paused. "X is Veric—"

Veric sighed before his own name finished arriving.

"—Joe, Salvatore, Reyana, Rudy, Elenor, Vera, Gleeward, Jack. And me."

Elenor's head came up and so did Vera's. Neither had expected it.

"Nobody's ordering you to fight," Jin said, straight at the pair of them. "Elenor, Vera, Jack… you're with Veric's contingent and First Company. Your job is that we don't get swarmed from behind once we're in the depths. That's it. That's the whole job, and if you do it properly nobody down there ever knows your names."

"Comforting," Vera said.

"It's meant to be."

The display updated. Three colored rings laid themselves over the Ward.

"These, I’m sure you can tell, are the effective ranges of the groups. Two through six, offence only. Seven through sixteen, sustainer protocols." Jin found the leads by their company badges and went along them one at a time. "Your task is to stabilize the essence inside the green. Healing, buffs, debuffs, everything you've drilled. Seventeen through twenty-two run heal and essence-regen, cycling with seven through sixteen as they burn out." He put two fingers on the green ring. "For this battle Veric is your commanding officer. Most of you have been shouted at by him already, so nothing changes."

The meeting ran for another hour. Succession lists and munitions counts, then the fallback order for the aid line. Who inherited the army if Mathew fell. Who inherited it if that one fell too. It was logistics with a body count stitched into the margins, and the veterans discussed it in level voices, and every one of them disliked how good they had become at it.

By the end the leads had gone quiet. It was the sound of people who had finished being frightened and started being resolved. He thought it was the saddest noise a room could make.

Quieter than grief… grief at least still argued.

While the others bent over their briefing sheets, Jin came round the table.

A few eyes tracked him and went back down. Jin reached into his jacket and set a sealed envelope in front of Veric.

"What's this, boss?"

"For if it goes wrong down there." Jin's face had arrived at the expression it wore when he had decided a thing and had no intention of being argued out of it. "If I don't walk back… You'll know what to do."

Veric looked at the envelope. Then he looked at the boss.

Two thousand two hundred names heavier than he'd been a fortnight ago. Grey under the eyes from a week of not sleeping. He had just finished describing how to open a hole in the world with his own people standing inside it, and he hadn't blinked once doing it.

There was still a kid in there somewhere, under the command and the ledger and whatever the harvest had made of him. Veric knew it for a fact, and the knowing sat in his chest like a swallowed coal.

He picked the envelope up. He turned it once in his fingers and slid it unopened into his breast pocket, next to the cigarettes he wasn't allowed to light in here.

"Old bones like mine shouldn't be trusted with a will."

"You're the only one I trust with it."

"I know." Veric leaned forward and put both hands flat on the cold edge of the table, and looked down the length of the map, and did not look at the boy at all. "Go and finish your briefing, sir."

Jin went back to his seat.

"One more thing." The flatness came back into his voice and took the last of the banter out of the room with it. "It's about your uniforms, and why they're built the way they're built. You're going to hate it. I'd rather you hated it in here than out there."

Nobody spoke. Jin nodded at Illiana, and the display changed.

"I know every one of you has complained that the kit feels wrong."

Twenty-two heads nodded at once.

"It feels wrong because of what it's made from." Jin jerked his chin at Joe. "All thanks to Joe who provided the materials. What we built you offers nothing in the way of protection… I'm sorry about that, and there wasn't another way. What it does is muddle your essence signature."

Every face in the room had gone flat and attentive.

"Two reasons for that, first, the cult doesn't get to count our actual strength before we arrive. Second… well, there's a runic threading through the gear, and that threading makes your essence and your life signature read as the creature the hide came off once activated.”

"And in the chaos of arrival," Jin said, "I want us holding that advantage, and not them."

The room came apart.

"What?"

"Boss, what aren't you telling us—"

"You said no reinforcement, you said—"

"So," Veric raised one hand, and the noise dropped out from under the room like a floor.

He kept his eyes on their boss. He'd felt this answer coming since the word chaos, and he already knew he was going to hate it.

"Something we should know, boss? What's the chaos of arrivals you mention, what’s this creature called."

Jin smiled.

"Demons."
