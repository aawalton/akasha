There was no doubt in his mind that he'd grown stronger in every way, with his newfound control, or at least understanding, of the weave being the most impressive of his abilities, if he could say so himself. He'd done a lot. Saved Integrated, defeated many a foe, perhaps you could even go so far as to say he had saved Earth itself once or twice, but grabbing hold of the weave and weavestepping while falling to his death was something he'd always remember as a moment that would be difficult to trump no matter what the future held.

Screaming, he grabbed hold of the first echo thread he found, then jumped through the weave just before his body slammed into the hard ground. It had been a long fall, with Penny unable to reach him in time, but it wouldn't have been enough to kill him. He knew that much, but still. It would have hurt like a bitch, nerve-numbing or not. Instead, he emerged next to Hannah, who was in the process of ripping a Singer apart with tendrils of shadows while simultaneously shielding some of the others with a writhing wall that somehow looked more solid than the ground it stood on. There were no hints of tattoos on her, which meant she hadn't reached for her void mana. Good.

"Can you keep one alive for me?" Luke asked as he reached over and healed Nick, who'd taken a blow on the side of his face with enough power behind it to crush bone.

She nodded at Curtis, who was deep inside the small army of Singers fighting back. "I can, sure, but I can't promise Curtis won't eat whoever we catch."

"Noted," Luke said, craning his neck in an attempt to get a better view.

Curtis wasn't alone out there. A streak of light flashed again and again. At first, Luke thought it was a new lightning spell from Penny, but he soon realized that wasn't it.

"Is that Elijah?" he asked.

"Yup," Hannah said with a grunt as she heaved another Singer from their front line. "He's a lot better than when I saw him before." She slammed the Singer into the ground, then dropped him at Luke's feet. "This one is out cold. Hope you don't mind."

"Perfect," Luke said.

He looked away from the battle to focus on his new test subject. Going down on one knee, he grabbed hold of the unconscious Singer's meta-heart with a thread of mana, then closed his eyes and did his best to ignore the chaotic nature of the battle roiling only a few steps ahead. Luke had considered just taking a Singer from the prison break, but decided against it. What he wanted to attempt was to do with the Singer what he'd done with Raphael, and learn to distinguish a Singer thread from the rest of the weave. Doing that on Earth would be more difficult, he figured, since there, at this point, weren't all that many of them, at least not as many as in their own system. Here, Luke hoped to get the same ripple effect as the one on Earth, except with Singers.

Gathering his focus took longer than last time, but Luke got there in the end. Like before, he examined the Singer's thread and was now even able to compare it to the few Earth Integrated threads available in this system. There were differences between the two. That much was obvious, but the similarities far outweighed those differences to the point it became difficult to distinguish between the two at first. Going deeper, though, Luke saw those differences. After meditation on them for a few minutes, he knew he'd never mistake one for the other.

The weave lit up with a pale white light, showing the Singer Integrated. Luke swallowed hard at the sheer number of them. Even if every human on Earth touched the orb, they still wouldn't amount to even half of what the Singers were putting up.

"So many," Luke whispered.

Hannah looked over at him. "What?"

Luke (Guild): "There are more Singer Integrated than we can ever hope to win against. Their planet must be huge for there to be so many of them."

Luke (Guild): "This plan needs to work."

Milla (Guild): "They have no more anchors as far as we know. Close the gates, and we'll deal with the ones who are left behind on Earth."

Curtis (Guild): "The Intruders will still be able to come to Earth. I'll deal with them."

Milla (Guild): "Stick to the plan, Curtis."

A glowing light in the sky made Luke look up, and he saw Penny descending. The words from her chant cut through the chaos of sound with enough force that he felt them in his bones.

Two! Four! Six! Eight!

Storm clouds gather, don't be late!

Thunder, hit and devastate!

Clouds had formed in the sky from all her spells, and they now gathered in a much smaller area as bolts of lightning slammed into the Singer ranks, killing them in droves. Each bolt also sent an outward-growing nova, hurting everyone around the impact site.

"What's the plan here?" She called down as she descended enough that she hovered over their heads. A spell of green flashes struck against Penny, but a sphere of lightning flashed around her, destroying the attack. Arrows followed, but they were even less effective.

"I'm going to close the gates!" Luke shouted back.

"Wasn't that always the plan?" Nick asked.

Luke nodded. "Well, yeah, but I'm going to do it even harder now."

"More difficult?"

"What?" Luke asked. "No! Just keep doing what you're doing. Fall back or don't. I don't know."

"Good thing you're not the commander on this mission," Nick grunted. "I'll just talk to your sister."

"Fine," Luke said. "Just be ready for a portal?"

"Portal to where?" Nick asked, but Luke was already searching through the weave for a thread to use.

Weavestep brought him away from the combat zone and into a small, dark room without windows. His eyes adjusted, and he saw toys on the ground. Gentle breathing turned his attention to a small bed with a child in it. The Singer, a boy maybe five years old, slept like he didn't have a care in the world. A child Integrated? Their version of the Tutorial Dungeon must have been a horrific experience for the little guy. Luke reached out to the weave again. There was no way he was killing children.

Next up was a female Singer in her thirties who was reading a book. Luke appearing out of nowhere made her lose her shit, and she threw the book at him, screaming as she climbed over the back of her sofa and fell down on the other side with a thud. Luke left. While this one was an adult, she couldn't have leveled a single time, judging by her weak meta-heart. With mana running low, he weavestepped again, hoping for better luck. Was every single Singer Integrated? That would explain their overwhelming numbers.

New Quest: Kill The Savage Oola.

Reward: 5000 credits.

He'd entered a dungeon and appeared right next to a mage who was in the middle of casting a complicated spell. Not far off, other Singers fought the dungeon boss, Oola, trying to take what looked like a moving tree down by hacking at its trunk. Luke and the mage turned to look at each other.

"Hello," Luke said as he reached out with threads of mana. "Goodbye."

The mage, to Luke's surprise, put up decent resistance against his threads and even changed the spellweave, reforming it into a quicker blast of fire. Not that it helped her. Slicing through the spellweave made her waver, and that was enough. Luke didn't even need to press the issue. The Singer's eyes widened when his thread reached her meta-heart, drained it, and pulled it out of her chest. An arrow slammed into the side of Luke's throat and went right through, an impressive shot from that distance, and blood poured out of him until a small application of healing mana stemmed the flow and fixed the internal damage. The next arrow missed, and the third was way off. One of the Singers was caught in Oola's long branches, and another stayed behind to get him out, but the rest were charging toward Luke. Even from a distance, their screams reached him.

He considered staying and dealing with them, but a glance at his experience bar told him there wasn't much to be had from fighting them, and these Singers weren't attacking Earth, just minding their business. Maybe killing the mage had been a step too far as well, but at least he didn't need to make the matter worse. Instead, he turned and ran. Using Weavestep to put a fair distance between himself and those chasing him, Luke spotted what he'd been searching for: the dungeon portal.

The light blue glow drew his attention to a crevice halfway up a tall mountain. Despite the distance and height, he climbed. From all the way up there, having pushed himself again and again with Weavestep, Luke saw that the Singers hadn't opted to follow. Small blurs down on the plains fought a larger blur. He had plenty of time, and the portal wasn't guarded. Luke walked right up to it and closed his eyes. The weave of a dungeon wasn't the same as, say, Earth's. It was more limited in scope with fewer objects, both living and not. That made it far easier to find the portal's metaphysical representation, and he used that to find the thread.

Like before, he studied it, again finding differences. While threads representing Earth's Integrated glowed a light blue and the Singer's had a paler sheen, the portal's thread showed up as red in Luke's mind. Ominous, but at least easy to distinguish.

"Interesting," he muttered, "maybe this will work a little better."
