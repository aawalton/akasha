Gilbert managed to catch Elvin by the collar before the old man fully collapsed onto the floor. Gilbert did not feel sorry for the runic warden. It was just that if the warden fell loudly, it could draw unwanted attention. And unwanted attention right now was worse than a knife in the side.

He carefully lowered the old man onto the stone, then sharply turned to Lir. The boy was already standing pale, wide-eyed, with his back pressed so tightly to the wall that it looked as if he hoped to grow into it. He clearly understood far too little of what was happening here.

"Is he... is he dead?" Lir asked.

"No."

Gilbert said it more sharply than he wanted, but right now he had neither time nor strength for softness. Under his collarbone, it burned as if someone had driven a red-hot hook in there and was now slowly turning it inside him. Every movement answered in his chest with nasty pain. He lowered his gaze and saw the core by the wall. A small dark shard with an almost dead light inside. After hitting the stone, a thin crack had run across it, and the glow jerked unevenly, like a flame right before it finally died.

That was it. The end. It had already been running on its last hours. The time had almost run out, the shell had weakened, and the impact had simply finished it. Stitching that filth back in made no sense. Even if Gilbert had time, medicine, and a special needle, he did not have the slightest desire to drive someone else’s magical rot under his skin again.

But he could not leave the core on the floor either. Gilbert bent down, clenching his teeth, and picked it up with two fingers. The light inside trembled one last time and went out. The core smelled of blood and corrupted magic.

"What is that?" Lir whispered.

"Trash."

"Then why are you..."

Gilbert did not explain anything to the overly curious boy. For one thing, they simply did not have time for that. The mage shoved the core into the inner pocket of the uniform. The fabric immediately grew unpleasantly wet with blood, but that no longer mattered. Then Gilbert tore a strip from the lining at his collar and quickly pressed it to the wound. Blood still seeped between his fingers, but at least it was not dripping onto the floor so visibly.

Lir was still staring at him. At first, Gilbert thought the fisherman’s son was looking at the wound, but he was wrong. Lir’s eyes were moving over all of Gilbert. And very soon, the mage understood why. The core was no longer working. That meant the System was reading him correctly again.

"Your name is different," Lir said, confused.

Gilbert slowly raised his gaze to him.

"What?"

"It was... When you came in, I saw it. It was Gilven. And now it’s Gilbert. You’re Gilbert?"

That was not very good, but it was not critically bad either. After all, he and Lir were in the same boat, and time was running out fast.

Lir clearly did not know who this “Gilbert” in front of him was. He had been taken too early, locked up, and kept in fear for too long to have learned who exactly was helping Sakura outside.

"Yes," he said. "And if you say my name that loudly again, I’ll regret pulling you out of that cell."

Lir quickly shut his mouth. Gilbert had no intention of babysitting him. First, the boy had to be taken out of here alive, and all friendly conversations could wait until later, when they were beyond the walls and outside the Castle’s mist zones.

Gilbert turned to Elvin. The old man lay on his side, breathing heavily. He was unconscious. At his belt still hung the key ring, the white bone key, ordinary iron keys, a small service plate, and a couple of useless little things whose purpose Gilbert did not understand, even though he had managed to study a great deal in this prison. Gilbert removed and took everything. You never knew what exactly might save your life during an escape from this place.

"Help," he threw at Lir.

"What?"

"Hide him."

Lir swallowed, but came closer. His hands were visibly trembling. He clearly did not want to touch the old man who had just been hit with a knife hilt, but he did not argue. Together, they dragged Elvin into a narrow side niche behind an old cabinet for healer’s supply boxes. The cabinet had long stood empty and crooked. Gilbert pushed it with his shoulder, barely stopping himself from hissing in pain, and moved the cabinet so it covered the warden’s body from a first glance. Now the body did not stand out at all, and it would be found only in two cases: if someone searched deliberately, or if Elvin came to on his own.

"He’ll wake up, right?" Lir asked.

"Yes. But not immediately. We have time."

Gilbert did not know exactly how much time they had. But the blow had landed cleanly, and Elvin was no longer at the age where he could jump up a minute later and run for help.

Gilbert picked up the iron rod and threw it under the cabinet toward Elvin. Then he quickly swept his gaze over the floor. The blood trail from the core still stretched toward the wall in a thin dark line. He wiped it with the edge of the torn lining as best he could and hid the dirty cloth in his pocket. Pain shot through his chest again, and Gilbert squeezed his eyes shut for a moment. He counted silently, then straightened again, pulling himself together.

"Let’s go," he said to Lir.

"Where exactly?"

"To the emergency section. There’s a door without a runic lock there. Without a runic mage, it’s our only chance now."

"And Sakura..."

"You shouldn’t know the whole plan. Safer that way," Gilbert said shortly.

He knew that if Sakura had done her part correctly, she would be waiting for them outside. If she had not, they would have to improvise, and that would not be easy. It would be even more dangerous than everything they had already managed to do. Still, risk was a necessary part of this insane plan. They had to put all their cards on the table or die. There was no other path left.

They moved on, now without the old runic mage as a hostage. Ahead of them, there was only a narrow technical corridor, old stone, and a low ceiling. Pain stayed with Gilbert too, making it hard to breathe. Getting rid of the core should have freed his body from the disruptions, but that required time. Too much inside had broken for it to fix itself in an hour. Recovery would take a day, maybe more.

Voices still sounded beyond the wall, reminding them of the chaos in the Castle. Gilbert led Lir the way local guards led prisoners under escort, and from the outside, they did not draw suspicion. Gilbert had managed to put himself in enough order and not limp. And all Lir had to do was keep looking like Lir.

Gilbert reached the turn and stopped. In front of them was an ordinary door. It was old and heavy, made of darkened iron, pressed so deep into the stone wall that it looked as if the Castle itself had tried to forget it existed. A large rusted wheel sat on it, and below it jutted a crossbar bolt. Elvin had not lied about the lock being purely mechanical, without runes.

"Is this it? The emergency exit?" Lir asked.

"Yes."

"And now what?"

"Now we turn it."

Gilbert grabbed the wheel with his right hand. With his left, he pressed the wound. He tried to turn it, but the wheel did not move.

"They probably haven’t opened it in a long time," Lir muttered.

"Thank you. I would never have guessed."

Gilbert gripped the wheel harder and leaned his shoulder into it. Pain struck so sharply that dark spots swam before his eyes. The wheel creaked, but did not turn. In the distance, he heard the noise of approaching footsteps and voices. That was bad.

"Are they coming?"

Gilbert listened. The voices had just turned in another direction.

"Not here. For now. Hurry."

The boy swallowed and grabbed the wheel beside him.

"What do I do?"

Gilbert looked at his thin, shaking hands. And still, they were not useless. In front of him now was a fisherman’s son. Someone who had often dealt with nets, ropes, and crates. The body could remember work even if the head was now drowning in fear.

"On three," Gilbert said. "Turn it down. With your whole weight."

"And if..."

"Lir."

The boy fell silent.

"One."

They both braced their feet against the stone.

"Two."

Gilbert felt the wound under his collarbone open wider again.

"Three."

They leaned into it together. The wheel ground, but did not turn. Lir cursed quietly, clumsily, almost childishly, and despite everything, Gilbert gave a short smirk.

"Again."

"It won’t move."

"Then it doesn’t want to. We’re not asking."

The second attempt gave more: the wheel jerked, turned by the width of a palm, and froze again. Somewhere inside the door, the old mechanism groaned. The bolt trembled, but remained in place. Behind them came a distant metallic knock.

Lir sharply turned his head.

"What is that?"

"Problems," the mage answered dryly and shortly.

Gilbert grabbed the wheel again. His fingers slipped over rust. Blood flowed lower under the uniform, hot and sticky. Soon, stains on the uniform would start giving him away. And he could also be exposed by the first check, now that the System no longer marked him as Gilven. Now he was a dangerous criminal, an accomplice of Sakura Arai, and that was not the best name for a stroll through the Castle of Justice.

"Come on," the mage said.

Lir leaned in with him, and this time, the wheel moved. Slowly, with a vile, drawn-out scrape, as if the door were waking after a long sleep. The bolt inside jerked, then shifted aside with a dull hit.

Lir nearly fell from surprise.

"Did it work?"

"Don’t celebrate too early."

Gilbert pulled the door toward himself. It did not open. Gilbert felt himself starting to get nervous, so for a short moment, he simply closed his eyes and counted his breaths. Then he pulled more calmly, and the door finally opened. A narrow strip of murky green light appeared between the iron and the frame.

Gilbert smelled mist and a damp yard. He pushed the door wider. From outside came the crash of voices, swearing, and a distant metallic ringing.

Behind the door was not a yard, but a broad firebreak between the prison building and the inner service partition. The place was perfectly designed for a situation where a fire started in the Castle and people needed to be rescued and the flames put out quickly. The walls were wet with green mist, old stone darkened underfoot, and a low drainage gutter ran along the right side. The main exit from the prison was still not close. But getting out of the lower building into open air was already a huge achievement.

At the far end, wide iron barred gates were visible. During a fire, people were probably led through them into the outer assembly yard. But right now, it was too open there, and a meeting with the guards was almost guaranteed. So they could not go that way.

On the other hand, behind the service partition came a more interesting noise, as if someone was arguing. In that direction was the inner yard. Gilbert made out someone cursing about a wheel, and someone else demanding levers. He listened to the voices and recognized one. It was Sakura. Almost certainly her.

Gilbert lifted his gaze. The partition was high, but it was not the Castle’s outer wall, only an old inner boundary between the firebreak and the service area. Protrusions stuck out from the stone, a drainage gutter ran higher up, and by the wall stood two empty barrels and a broken ladder frame someone had abandoned here long ago.

"We climb," Gilbert said.

Lir looked at the partition with obvious doubt.

"I can barely stand after the cell," he said.

"You’ll have to move if you want to live. Otherwise, you stay here and die."

Lir opened his mouth, but to his credit, he did not argue. Gilbert dragged one of the empty barrels to the wall, then the second, set the broken ladder frame on top, and tested it with his foot. The wood cracked softly, but did not fall apart yet.

Before climbing, Gilbert quickly looked around. He checked the passages between the storerooms, the dark corners, and especially the roofs above them. After everything that had happened, he was not going to assume their escape had gone unnoticed. For several seconds, he studied the rooflines, trying to catch movement, a silhouette, or a watcher’s glint, but he noticed nothing suspicious.

"You first," Gilbert said.

"Why me?"

"Because I can push you up or catch you in the worst case. You can’t do that for me."

Lir swallowed, but climbed. Awkwardly, scraping his fingers on the wet stone, clinging to protrusions and hissing quietly. Halfway up, he froze, pressing his whole body against the wall, and for several seconds simply breathed, as if hoping the partition would change its mind and carry him to the other side by itself.

"Lir," Gilbert said quietly. "Faster."

The boy cursed under his breath and climbed farther. Gilbert had to push him from below with his good hand, and the wound under his collarbone immediately flared with pain. For a moment, his vision darkened, but Lir still managed to roll over the edge and disappear on the other side with a dull thud.

"Alive?" Gilbert asked.

"I think so," came from below. "There are crates here."

"Wonderful. Try not to die among the crates."

Now it was his turn. And that turned out worse than Gilbert had expected. His fingers slipped on the wet stone, blood under the uniform stuck to his skin, his left hand obeyed badly, and voices by the barred gates were already audible below.

Someone was approaching there.

Gilbert climbed faster than he should have. At the top edge, he swayed, and for a second, he almost fell backward into the firebreak. Lir, on the other side, grabbed his sleeve with both hands while standing on some crate, on his toes.

"I’ve got you!" he said fearfully.

Gilbert did not bother clarifying that Lir was holding him about as reliably as a wet thread holding a bucket of stones. Still, the help was useful. The mage jerked himself over the partition and collapsed down on the side of the service yard. The landing was tolerable. The crates softened the fall.

They ended up behind a stack of empty crates and wet tarps at the edge of the service area. From this side, the noise was louder. People were running, carrying levers, arguing about some shipment, and swearing like it was a royal debate.

Gilbert carefully looked out from behind the crates and saw the wagon.

It stood crookedly under the wet green tarp, with the load shifted and such a concentration of irritation around it that even without explanations, it was clear: Sakura had done her best. Two workers were currently trying to put the wheel back on the axle. A third held a lever. The guard on duty stood nearby, angry, wet, and miserable.

And on the driver’s bench sat a figure in a protective suit and mask.

Lir looked out beside him and froze.

"Is that her?"

Gilbert looked at the fallen wheel and at Sakura, who even from a distance looked as if the entire Castle personally owed her a new wagon.

"Yes," he said. "Of course it’s her."

The wheel was already almost back in place.
