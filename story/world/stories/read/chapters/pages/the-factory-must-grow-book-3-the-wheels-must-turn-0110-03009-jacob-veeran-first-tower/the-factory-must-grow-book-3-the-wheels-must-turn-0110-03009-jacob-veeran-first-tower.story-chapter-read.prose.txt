A dull thrum, echoing with the distant sound of clanging metal, filled the air. Veeran paid it no mind, accustomed as he was to Smith's tests, instead keeping his attention fixed upon the bronze and iron ballista he was currently constructing.

Though they still lacked proper steels, that was liable to be altered soon enough, once Commander Inq was capable of creating the appropriate industrial alchemy equipment improving their Blast furnace. Most of what they lacked, from his understanding, were certain parts which they were expecting the Metalpress to be capable of making. Veeran certainly awaited that day, when they had access to mankind's greatest tool.

Spells were a grand invention, fire instrumental to create so much, yet it was steel that stood as the bastion of humanity's triumph over the Tyrants, their dominion of the world, and the distribution of grand machines.

Alas, nearly to proper steel manufacturing was nonetheless in a situation where all of their mass iron manufacturing resulted in something either too soft or too brittle. The art of proper steelmaking was not one which Veeran knew, that grandest of alchemy. And thus, Veeran was working with bronze.

Even as Smith worked upon his developments with the Metalpress, the development of his more general hologram-blueprints had effectively eliminated days of work for each of these ballistae. Where before, Veeran would have spent days slowly drawing and forging multiple ingots of bronze into a proper recurve shape, and had the resultant product still be somewhat inexact and imbalanced, the metalwork for a piece as tall as he was took hours at most, most of which was spent getting the raw materials into a very approximate shape. Then Smith and Haleford would work their joint magics, and a nigh-finished piece would be produced.

Even if Smith would become the new bottleneck for production, at least until such a time that they developed a means for all of them to create the holograms which Metalpress would rely upon, the ludicrous amount of time saved was more than a worthy tradeoff.

Assembly was yet another matter, but Veeran was confident their brilliant young Artificer would solve that issue in due time. In the meantime, Veeran hammered a bronze spike into a wood-stock, then hammered it over to hold up the ballista's arms while he worked on the screws which would more permanently hold the weapon together.

Veeran was absorbed in his task when the music changed.

It had taken Veeran some time to properly hear the local Song, not least due to the inappropriate Elements which his Class was built upon. He had merely been capable of hearing some of the instruments composing it, yet not the melody itself. Yet he had adjusted and grown more attuned, and though being less than a quarter of his former level carried with it its own set of hindrances, he had reclaimed a fraction of his prior mastery.

He supposed it could have been Force which granted him that particular awareness, some overlap between the movement of physical objects and the momentum of the story of the world. It was certainly more of an understandable connection than Ice or Metal, yet it did not fully align with his understanding of any of his elements.

Perhaps, then, it was connected in some way to the local magics of this world. The capabilities granted to them by the Jump were surely present, even if undiscovered. They lacked a corpus of human ingenuity and invention to help them master their abilities, yet Veeran had utter confidence that they would discover them in time. His fellow members were simply too intelligent, too perceptive, for it to forever escape their notice.

Yet for now, Veeran merely listened, and drew his [Frostblade] in preparation.

There was eternally an underscore of violence, of Bloodshed, within the Song of First Tower. Understandably so, given the veritable war between dragons and inklings constantly taking place at its gates. It ebbed and flowed somewhat, yet never fully abated. And this change...

Veeran stalked towards the front gates, momentarily closing his eyes to better hear what was developing.

Notes of anticipation. Of rising tension. Something ominous. Something had changed, and not to their benefit. There was a grand force mustering, some great and terrible power which was amassing its strength, and preparing to strike. A three-part harmony between Dragon, Ash, and... yes, that was Bloodshed. The trio fed off one another, creating a motif that pulled itself and its accompaniment downwards. They dropped octaves with each cycle, a continuously descending set of multi-instrumental chords that sought to distort the rest of the Song in accordance with it.

Veeran passed through the gates, and the music enveloped him. Arcanoception was the tool that the soul utilized to read the story, hear the Song of the spheres, and its interpretation could be... loosened. Though it often suited him to keep a distinguishing ear out, hearing the individual elements and inferring knowledge from their usage, right now he needed to listen to the full orchestra, hear the story the world was attempting to scream at him.

So, he let go.

His eyes closed, his sword rose, and the dragons threw themselves at him.

Its attack was the thrum of a guitar just above electric. A singular chord with little supporting it. No build-up, simply a blast of violent musicality. Veeran's response, a mighty clash of cymbals, drowned it out and once the louder sound ebbed, the guitar was no more. A pair of violins took a bit more time, slowly building off one another. A similar sound to the guitar, a never-ending and intense scale. The drums started, first following the lead of the violins as the strings grew more and more intense, more tortured, faster and faster and then...

Merely a background instrument to the steady beat of the drum. The drum crowded them out, becoming an implacable rhythm which changed tempo too quickly, to suddenly for the violins to keep up. One broke, and then the drum crushed the other with a clash of metal.

A gentle flute began, underlying the score with moments of brightness. It was thin and weak here, and its melody vanished every time the drum sounded, every time a new screech of strings began.

The steady beat of the drum continued, undaunted and fully in control of the melody's heart. Tap-tap-clash, it went. Tap-tap-clash. A punishing tempo, firmly overpowering the lesser instruments that sought to accompany it. Yet the flute persisted throughout it all, filling in the moments of silence with its calming tune.

And then, the change Veeran had heard returned. The notes the flute played were overtaken by a violin, a long and gentle note that soon softened. It began accompanying the steady beat of Veeran's drum, each tap accompanied by a chord and each clash accentuated by a powerful note of its own. Then the violin gave way to a harp, each note plucked in perfect time to accentuate Veeran's own melody. A subtle foe, it seemed. A clever one.

Then, another shift. It still accentuated Veeran's notes, but it had risen several octaves, making Veeran's melody unnaturally low by comparison. No longer an accompaniment, but a mirrored melody of its own. Yet Veeran remained undaunted, his own tune holding fast and refusing to play by his opponent's rules. Then, a sudden drop. The flute had returned at some point, yet it had been drowned out by the harp, playing just slightly out of time and much lower, a deep sound which made the higher notes almost painful in comparison, and which contextually dragged Veeran's ever-constant beat into a far lower register than he truly played within. Six notes, played in sequence, two between each of Veeran's tempo. Tap-low-low, tap-low-low, clash-low-low. The harp had been replaced with... perhaps that was a cello of some kind.

It was establishing new music, utilizing Veeran's own constancy as its tempo. An enemy which could adapt, and use an opponent's strength against them, then. The Tyrants had been masters of that. Master composers, and with an incredibly broad slate of tools at their disposal. Fanatic cultists, demis loyal to their ancestors and parents, zealots convinced of their own righteousness solely due to the powers which they had aligned themselves with.

A Tyrant would insert themselves into a story, into a Song, whenever they pleased, and would distort the grand orchestra of reality around them. No matter what the melody had been prior to their involvement, once they appeared, they were the main tune. Their shadow was cast over nigh every performance, whether or not the performers could see it. They were loud and commanding, and where they directed, all others tended to follow.

And such was the basis for The Binding Wars. The Emperor had come into the orchestra, a powerful and resonant instrument, one which refused to give way to the Tyrants, and they had sought to bend his melody towards their desires. Yet he remained steadfast, and raised a chorus around him. No instrument, no matter how mighty, could overpower a full orchestra. No conductor could fight all of their players simultaneously, and no Song could constrain the indomitable spirit of a mere human. The greatest violinist in reality could not play with a pile of splinters.

And these strings, this... Calamity. It was a clever and subtle one. It subverted or overpowered the flutes of the world, stealing their melody and drowning them out until the main player was barely audible, the weakest of accompaniment, and then when the strings changed, it sought to command the tune of the world, overpowering it until there was nothing but it remaining. Devastation and desolation, Death and Ash. A Plague of Dragon, that which could not be constrained, wild and uncontrollable power sufficient to drown out anything.

It commanded the beat, was louder than even the drums, and was clever. A truly worthy opponent.

Yet, it faced Jacob Veeran, and it was no Cerulean One. To be but a minor instrument in the defeat of false divinity left scars, but so too did it leave experience. It left potential.

For what are you mere humans unto a god, it had boasted, Your machines mean nothing to me. Feast upon your birthright, as I claim mine.

Death. So, so much death.

Yet, at the end of it all, mere humans stood over the broken idol. The birthright of a Tyrant was not life, was not power. It was usurpation. That which had stolen human potential to feed itself, the vilest monsters of all, held no rightful place. And now, Veeran once again felt the melody he had learned so long ago.

Veeran could hear the means by which the tune continued. It was a spreading sickness, an all-consuming hunger without deeper purpose than to feed. Devastation incarnate.

There was more to the tune, which Veeran could hear. A great clash between orchestras, echoing forth. Not long ago, yet well before their latest clash. The prior battle had been far more even, grand pipe organs dueling with ferocious electric guitars. They built up, and up, and up, and then everything crashed. Things fragmented, and the Song's cycle was forced to begin anew.

He was certain that Inq and Smith would be capable of learning far more from Veeran's latest insights than he could ever manage to conclude on his own, but he had learned enough for now.

The Dragon was preparing for a strike. It had some great clash with the opposing force, whatever that may have been, in the not so distant past. The storm of dragons from some weeks back had been a new fight, an establishing skirmish taking place as the Dragons regained their strength. And the Dragons certainly needed to regain their strength. They consumed voraciously, taking whatever they could get and harnessing it for their own ends. Given enough time, they would overpower any melody, until all that was... was simply their own chorus, blaring their Song of destruction to anything that might hear it.

There were many weaknesses to that approach, and Veeran could exploit any number of them. But for right now, he was focused on a singular one, the one which suited his goals just as well. It stole Songs, but it needed time to do so. The start of a new Song, a new factor which it had not adjusted to, that contained the potential to devastate it.

Veeran opened his eyes, and pulled himself from the full depths of the Song he had been within. The corpses of dragons filled the Ironroad, their bodies strewn about haphazardly, even blocking some of the transport carts as they sought to bring their bounties of iron to within First Tower.

Few dragons remained, yet enough remained that Veeran could conclude the Song he had been playing with a [Sweeping Cut] for a finale.

The lingering hum of his magic settled down, and Veeran cast aside his blade. Doing so firmly ended the Song he had been a part of, and in the space between, Veeran could feel the one that had been there before, the one he had interrupted, start again. He recognized a few of the notes, how the Dragon sought to establish itself, to usurp the music already there. Currently, it had latched upon the hum and rhythm of First Tower. It was new, it was different, and it was... weak? Its melody was ill-defined and new, compared to that which was around it. It was easy prey, therefore, and so the Dragon circled, preparing to lunge.

"Soldier Veeran?" Haleford hesitantly made his way through the slaughtered bodies of dragons, "The Commander asked that I check on you. Dinner was close to an hour ago."

"I am well." Veeran replied, his eyes looking forth into The Jungle, and his ears ready to hear. "I would advise you await within the wards."

"What is happening?" Haleford asked, but still started moving towards the protected area of First Tower.

"I am anticipating an attack," Veeran replied. Notes he had been waiting for began to stir up, finding themselves uninterrupted by Veeran's own actions, and began to call themselves into a fervent pitch. "I intend to protect us."

Tsunami Stance, then.

Veeran's hands were empty and behind his back, his left hand clasped around his right wrist. His left foot led, pointed towards The Jungle. His right foot was perpendicular to it, pointing down the Ironroad. His knees were slightly bent. His breathing was slow and steady.

His spirit flowed through his body, broken though it may be. His life-essence leaked from every part of him, and Veeran encouraged it, giving himself a faint echo, one following a sonorous clap.

His breathing changed. Two quick breaths, one slow breath. On the first quick breath, he tapped his foot. On the second, he clenched his hand, and on the slow, he allowed it all to pass by him.

The fragmentary power of a false divinity, forever lodged within his spirit, resonated with a deeper, inner reflection of true divinity, the vital and most wonderful image that drove humanity forwards. That was the certainty which powered their First Empire. The First Empire of actual Humanity, mere humans in control of their own destiny. Untainted by the eternal temptation of idols and driven by the community, the inviolability of the human spirit. That tiny fragment of something wonderful. Their desire to explore, the compulsion to know, the dedication to create.

And the need to protect. The self-sacrifice which allowed others to flourish. The mark of a true leader. Did they feast upon the rewards of their underlings, or did they work to ensure those beneath them were all the more capable? Were they willing to suffer, to risk themselves, for the good of others?

Here, Veeran stood before the bastion of humanity. The place created in the wake of exploration, built without knowledge yet with a grand desire to learn. It was a Place Of Greatness. Veeran was its defender. Something sought to undo that which humanity had built, it desired to consume all that was good for its own ends, and that Would Not Be Allowed.

The tempo had picked up yet again. It was ferocious, uncaring of anything save itself. It was greedy, malicious, and self-serving. No good came of it, it served no higher purpose. It expanded because it could, and would simply consume anything it came across given the scarcest opportunity. It was a flood of destruction.

The Song reached its crescendo, music swelling into a grand symphony. The Dragon, sensing weakness and opportunity, pounced. The Song was one of an overwhelming chord of violins and electric guitar, ferocious and loud. It threw itself at the grand artifice of humanity, prepared to devour and destroy.

And a mere human barred its way.
