The last time I’d been here, it had been winter, and even then, the Sylvan Silverbark had been weirdly vibrant.  Now the forest was bursting with life.  Green shoots tangled everything; the dead branches swarmed with vines, using the relics of last year as the skeletons for the new one.

I’d wanted to come alone, but naturally, my bodyguards had objected.  Bottleburner and his gang of misfits had tagged along and were glaring at the foliage like I would if it owed me money.  The four invisible soldiers were making the hairs on the back of my neck itch.

“I don’t like it, sah.”

“I know.  Why do there always have to be bloody ninjas?”

“Not that, sah.  The trees are watching us.”

“I look around, and I see a vibrant forest in spring.  Blossoms are out but not yet falling, turning the damn trees into giant bouquets.  You see… evil tree eyes?  Trust me, I’ve seen faces in trees, and this isn’t it.”  I shuddered at the memory of Viktarlpth emerging from his flesh-wood.

“You can hear it, though, can’t you, sir?” Corporal Slownick, the squad mage, muttered.  “There is power here.”

“Of course there’s power here.  The bloody unicorns and the elves are all over this place.  The elves are kind of pussies, but the Friendship is Magic crowd are terrifying.  Do not ‘accidentally’ try to kill any of them.  It won’t work, and I hate apologising.”

“No.  You’d be dead.  Keep your issues with furry things to yourself.”

“Are you sure she was a lass?  I mean, I legit can’t tell the difference between the men and the women when it comes to the dwarves.”  I shelved my bizarre fascination with the reproductive processes of other species.  It was starting to feel like a problem even to me.

“You-you scaly bastard!” Slownick stammered, but the rest of the squad laughed, so I figured it wasn’t too much of a faux pas.

“I don’t judge, man.  Love is love.”

Slownick started sputtering angrily at me, unable to form a coherent word in his rage and embarrassment.

“Love is love.  It is surprising to hear such an enlightened perspective from humans.”

“Cornie.  I’ve come back to fulfil my promise,” I lied.

“You have been busy; the skeins of life have been wracked by your rise to a lowly level of power.”  Twilight Sparkle materialised a dozen metres ahead of us, causing the squad to start to draw their weapons.

“Hold.  Cornie isn’t a threat,” I ordered.

“By Bulb’s Balls, that’s fugly,” muttered Slownick, but he lowered his spell-casting hand.

How are the stallions?”  I moved forward to put myself between the unicorn and the visible members of my guards.  If one of the ninjas did something stupid and got themselves killed… well, one less ninja in the world wasn’t a bad thing.

“Of course.  If you fulfil your oath to us, we will facilitate the next part of your quest.”  She pivoted her head to stare at an empty space between the bushes.  “I would advise against trying to approach too closely in this fashion.”

“Just checking,” came One-Hand's whisper.

“You know about my quest?”  I wasn’t thinking about it like that.  I was many things, but a hero was not on the list, even in the privacy of my own head.

“Your enemy.  Our enemy.  Why do you think you survived the experiment?  We would never endanger this verse by treating you as you deserve.”

Cornie was moving ahead of me, and if the guards decided to take her down, or try to, off the back of that casual admission of choosing not to murder me, I wouldn’t intervene.  I might even help.  But then I had a thought.

“Why should I spill all the tea about my life if you have to help me anyway?”

“It would make your acquisition of the horns we gave you theft.”

Bastard.  I did hate thieves.  But the entire concept didn’t fit for me now.  I was the chosen one, destined to save the world, and while the full impact of that hadn’t really sunk in yet, it gave me the impression that the whole damn world was mine.  My natural draconic territoriality kicked in, and everything changed.  Vanity swelled in my mental menagerie and began bullying the other aspects of my psyche.

“I can’t steal what is already mine.  If I’ve got to do this whole saving the world shit, that makes the world mine.”

“How would you like to be remembered when you’re gone?” Cornie asked calmly, looking back over her flanks and winking at me.  “Tyrant and monster or noble beast?”

“He’s ugly, but that seems a bit harsh,” whispered One-Hand.  Oh great.  A ninja who thought he was funny.

We had reached the edge of Cornucopia, the home of this herd of My Little Pony wannabes.  All signs of my battle with Umbraxis had been cleared away.  Broken houses had been fixed, and the mighty tree that dominated the centre of the town still towered overhead, wooden walkways spiralling up around the broad trunk.

“I’m perfectly comfortable with my masculinity.”
