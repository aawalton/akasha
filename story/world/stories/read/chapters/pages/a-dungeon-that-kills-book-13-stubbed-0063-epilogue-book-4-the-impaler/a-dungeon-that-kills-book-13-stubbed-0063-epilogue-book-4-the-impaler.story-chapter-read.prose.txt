Xander couldn’t understand what had happened.

One moment there had been something hurtling toward him through the air. A piece of meat, tied to what appeared to be a brat. He had taken a couple of swings at it, but in the end, the thing had managed to slip past his guard and fly straight into his mouth.

Then, without warning, massive tentacles had burst out from deep within his body, writhing and thrashing as they attempted to tear him apart. He had fought back with everything he had, but with each passing heartbeat, the fire in him had guttered lower, the stones of him had ground looser, and his mind had slowed into a thick, sluggish fog.

And now, here he was.

Lying on the soft sand, eyes staring up at the clear sky, as though he had just woken from a dream.

The sound of water reached his ears as awareness slowly crept back. Pushing himself up with arms that felt strangely weak, he looked around. Blackened stone lay all about him in great broken slabs, stones that looked uncomfortably similar to the very ones that had once made up his own majestic body.

He looked down at himself, and he saw ordinary skin and flesh. He had returned to his human form, it seemed. His clothes had been burned away, his Reliquaries gone. He didn’t appear to be suffering from any injury, but he felt completely and utterly exhausted.

“Ah, good. You’re still breathing. And in one piece,” came the familiar voice in his head. “The other function has also worked as intended. Excellent. I’d call this experiment a resounding success.”

“What?” Xander found he could speak with his own mouth again. “What function? What experiment?”

“Simple. Once it became clear that titanic body of yours was no longer sustainable, the last bit of power has been used to eject you safely. It would be a shame to let the host die, don’t you think? Much better if I could extract them and have their body thoroughly studied afterward. Though I have to admit, in this particular case, getting you out of the dungeon seems impossible.”

The mockery in the man’s tone crawled right under Xander’s skin, but he swallowed down the anger that rose in his throat.

“What happened? What the hell were those tentacles? How could I have lost to them? I am a fucking god.”

“Do I really have to say this again? You were not a god. You were never one. On the other hand, your opponent, most unfortunately, was.”

“What?”

“I can’t tell you exactly what happened. I no longer have eyes inside the dungeon, you know. Only this mental link. Still, I can make an educated guess. For a brief moment, I could sense His presence in this world. Which means He had come here, defeated you, and then left. That’s the most logical explanation I can come up with.”

“Who?”

“The Destroyer of Worlds. A dark and terrible god. Be proud, Xander. Not many mortals get to boast that a god has come and beat the shit out of them personally.” The Apostle chuckled. “Still, I don’t understand why He, of all people, has chosen to help the dungeon. Did they already have some arrangement with each other beforehand, or...? I’ll need to look deeper into it.”

Xander didn’t know how to feel about any of this. Not long ago, he had held a power that could reshape the world, a power that made lesser men tremble and grovel before him. And then, just like that, it had been taken away, as easily as his clothes had burned off his body, leaving him bare and exposed.

“What now?” Xander asked with a low growl.

“Walk out of the dungeon on your own feet, I’d love to tell you. But as I said, your odds of escaping are practically zero. Thus, what becomes of you now depends on how merciful the lord of the dungeon is. Anyway, this link seems to be reaching its limits. We may speak again, though I wouldn’t wager on it. Farewell, Xander. I wish you the best of luck.”

“Hey, wait—”

The voice was gone. As suddenly as it had come.

For the next several minutes, Xander sat at the shore, doing nothing. Many, many thoughts swirled inside his head, but he couldn’t decide, not for the life of him, what his next course of action should be.

And then, he heard footsteps. Heavy, metallic.

He turned to see a towering suit of armor bearing down on him. Plates of gold, a cape of purple, and a grinning skull suspended in a crystal jar embedded in its chest. He recognized it at once. That was the same enemy he had fought before. How the hell was it still alive even after he destroyed the whole building?

He scrambled to get to his feet and prepare himself for the fight, but his body refused to cooperate, his legs trembling uselessly beneath him. The construct, meanwhile, didn’t seem to be in any hurry. It casually walked up to him, its massive hammer still resting upon its pauldron, showing no sign of being needed anytime soon.

“What do you want?”

The construct gave no answer. Whether it was unable to speak or simply didn’t bother to, he had no idea. It reached down, closed one gauntleted hand around his shoulder, and turned to drag him back the way it had come.

“The fuck are you doing?”

He yelled at it. He punched it. He tried to yank himself free. The construct didn’t seem to notice. Its grip was unyielding as it marched on, hauling him without ceremony through the scorched, half-destroyed entrance, the very same one he had tried to widen when he was still an unstoppable titan of rock and flame.

It took him down a staircase through the dark until it emerged into a vast chamber drowning in light, so bright and so sudden he had to shield his eyes.

Is this the fourth floor?

The construct brought him to a floating platform. Releasing him, it closed its hand around a lever. After it pulled it, the platform slowly rotated as it began to descend, carrying them both down into whatever waited below.

Once it came to a stop, the construct seized him again, dragging him out into view of a massive building in the distance. It matched the one on the second floor in scale but surpassed it in grandeur, a complex of soaring spires and sweeping arches and countless windows.

The grim staircase leading up to it was a sharp contrast to such splendor, however. Blood everywhere. Corpses littered the place. Smoke still curled off broken stone. Living monsters ran back and forth, working to clean up the wreckage. There had been a battle here, and not a small one. Ah, yes. His own fire golems. The dungeon’s minions must have made their stand right on these steps, throwing everything they had at the burning constructs to hold this ground.

When he had created those golems, they had looked like nothing more than ants scurrying about. But now, as he passed through their blackened remains, he realized they could easily double his height.

The towering suit of armor hauled him up the staircase, to a rock before the gates of the building, upon which someone sat. A brat? Was this the same one who had flown toward him earlier, tied to that strange piece of meat? Why was there a human here? And why did every monster nearby treat his presence as though it were the most natural thing in the world?

To the brat’s right stood a muscular Crocodilian in battered armor, body marked with several visible wounds. To his left was an Ettin, its three heads turned toward Xander, each wearing a vicious grin. Before the rock, eight gnolls were arrayed in two lines, and between them, an iron rod stood upright. One of its ends had been buried deep, the other a wickedly sharp point that gleamed ominously in the light.

Xander gazed at the brat. And the brat gazed back.

What?

There was something in those eyes that unsettled him. Cold. As cold as a blade that sliced through his heart. They made him flinch, instinctively looking away. The hell? This... this didn’t make any sense. The one sitting before him was a mere child. Small. Scrawny. Someone he could knock over if he breathed a little too hard. So why couldn’t he look straight into those eyes without recoiling?

“So he’s indeed alive,” the brat said with a chuckle, glancing almost lazily at the armored construct. “How curious.”

“I believe the last bit of power from the divine residuals had been spent restoring him to his original form, while shielding him from all harm during the destruction of his colossal body,” came a feminine voice. What? That skull can talk? “Still, it seems he’s completely exhausted. And, without any weapon and without his Reliquaries, he’s hardly a threat anymore.”

“Is that so?” The brat’s voice carried no particular reaction. “Leave us, then.”

The construct threw Xander forward. He grunted as his knee struck the rough stone floor. Behind him, the metallic footsteps faded into the distance. Ahead, the Crocodilian gave the brat a respectful bow before turning to leave as well. The Ettin and the gnolls, on the other hand, remained exactly where they stood.

“Who the hell are you?” Xander yelled. “Why do they follow your order?”

The brat looked at him again, and this time something had shifted in that gaze. It was no longer cold, no longer sharp with the intensity that had made him flinch and avert his eyes moments before. Instead, it was one of indifference, almost bordering on boredom. The kind of look one might give to those who were far beneath them, those whom they didn’t care enough to properly acknowledge. And this kind of look, this casual dismissal, pissed him off even more than the coldness had. Hey! How dare you look at me like that?

“I was thinking about what I’d say to you,” the brat said. “I found none. Oh, well. Whatever. Let’s get this over with.”

What?

The Ettin moved. It circled around behind Xander, grabbing his shoulders with its two massive hands and hauling him up before he could even think to resist.

“You filthy dimwit! Get your hands off me!”

Xander screamed, thrashing and twisting and wrenching, but the Ettin held him firmly as it dragged him forward. He kicked and kicked, so the gnolls moved in as well, four hands closing around each limb, pinning them and spreading them apart as they positioned him directly over the waiting stake.

“You can’t be serious?”

His heart hammered with rage. How dare these curs think they could do this to him? To him of all people?

“Do you know who I am? You think you can get away with this?”

Xander felt something below, cold and sharp, touching his skin. He desperately tried to twist away, but the Ettin and the gnolls’ unyielding grip ensured that no such escape would ever come.

“You’ll regret this!”

His muscles clenched hard, every fiber of his being straining to resist what was coming. But it was in vain. Pain flared white-hot as the moment of entry came. He gritted his teeth so hard he thought they might crack, tears now pouring freely down his face.

It hurt.

It hurt.

It hurt.

The monsters didn’t push him down. Instead, they held him steady and let him sink, lower and lower, by the weight of his own body.

“You... bastards... I’ll... kill you... all...”

He felt a cramp in his guts, a sickening fullness spreading through his abdomen. The stake was no longer cold. It had been warmed by his own body heat. And it kept advancing. Deeper and deeper.

It went relentlessly upward, sliding through his torso with wet, squelching sounds. Pressure was building in his chest; his lungs struggled to draw breath around the invading metal.

And that was when he saw a pair of eyes through his blurry vision.

Indifferent, almost bored.

The fucking brat’s, of course.

He was the one who had sentenced him to such a humiliating execution, yet there he was, watching it unfold with nothing more than mild interest, like someone who was enduring a dull performance he had seen countless times before.

“Who... are you? Why... do you... do this... to me...?”

There was no answer. Only a spear that kept pushing up. His body spasmed. The agony was constant now—a deep, throbbing ache that radiated through his pelvis and up his spine.

Why?

Why did this happen?

Just not long ago, the power of a god had coursed through his body. The world had bowed to him, as it should have. How could it come to this?

His breathing grew shallow, each inhale pressing his heart and his lungs and his liver and his stomach and his intestines against the metal. He fought back the involuntary gag, using the last bit of his strength to force the words out.

“Please... stop... I beg you... I’ll give you... all that I have... and more... Anything you want... Please...”

“Anything I want?” The brat chuckled.

He jumped off his seat. Slowly, he walked up to Xander.

“...Yes,” Xander replied as a flicker of hope stirred in his heart.

And the brat leaned closer, whispering into his ear.

“I want my sister to wake up, you son of a bitch.”

...Huh?

What?

What on earth was he even talking about?

But before Xander could say anything, he felt a sudden pressure settle onto both of his shoulders. The brat had placed his hands there. And then, he pushed.

Xander wanted to scream, but the only thing that emerged was a wet, strangled gurgle.

Metal filled his mouth from below.

And, in the next instant, he saw the tip of the spear rise before his very eyes, glistening with saliva and blood and torn tissue.
