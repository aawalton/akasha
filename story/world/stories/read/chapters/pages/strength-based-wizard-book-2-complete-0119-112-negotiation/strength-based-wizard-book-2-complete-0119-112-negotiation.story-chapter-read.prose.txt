Chapter 112

Negotiation

July 1st.

Behind the Wheel of a Muscle Mage’s Beat-Up-Piece-of-Shit Car, Almost to Detroit.

32 days, 13 hours, and 6 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

“Oh FUCK!” I scream as I steal the steering wheel back from the spectral hands, hitting the brakes with a screech, and yank my car back into the far righthand lane.

Note to self: [Wizard’s Fist] does not have the finesse to operate a vehicle. But it was worth a try to see how much control I could exert over my spectral hands. Unfortunately, attempting to strangle and throttle the steering wheel almost resulted in us taking a hard right hand turn straight off the highway. Thankfully, I only thought to try it because no one else was on the road this morning.

Well, it was worth a try, I grumble to myself. Lefty and Righty are now hovering in the passenger seat like two school kids who just got picked up from the principal’s office by their dad. Lefty stops the sulking to flip off a deer grazing on the side of the highway.

I’m driving into Detroit with the kind of nervous energy you usually only get before a first date, a job interview, or a near-death experience. So… basically my new normal now.

The highway hums under my tires. My coffee, whatever gas station dark roast I could get, tastes like burnt hope. The radio is off, because I forgot to turn it back on. I keep drifting in and out of thought over everything that transpired over the past couple of weeks. Because, apparently, I’m officially a member of the Harvest Guild now.

That sentence still doesn’t feel real. I’m the member of a Guild!

Chase Guild’s offer was… stupid good. Like ridiculously stupid good, based on my research on what market starting positions within Guilds were. They offered me a Band 3, Tier 1 position.

At first, I didn’t even know what that meant. I thought it sounded like a music festival ranking. Then, like the adult I am (I swear), I looked up what any of that actually meant. Apparently, most Guilds in the U.S. structure themselves into three Bands. Band 3 is the lowest: newer associates, the fresh meat, the “please don’t die on your first Gate” tier. Typically, of course it all depends on the Guild. A Band 3 member of a top Guild may still wipe the floor with most Band 2 members of a weaker Guild. Each Band has five Tiers, Tier 1 being the highest.

So, Chase was offering me the top rung relative to all other new hires. Which, for a Guild as big as Chase, is basically them saying, We don’t know what the hell you are, but we want it.

The money was real good too. $200,000 base salary and a $20,000 signing bonus to boot. There were also performance bonus opportunities that could net me an additional $100,000. What was more interesting was the Loot Rights that came with that position. I would be entitled to keep 2% of all non-Boss loot that my party and I claim from Gates on Guild Quests or from dispatching monster outbreaks here on Earth. This part I wasn’t so happy with. It seemed to be pretty on par with market compensation for the larger Guilds, but the idea of taking down a Boss and not sniffing any of its loot was frustrating. Of course, assisting in Boss fights factored into the performance bonus.

There was also access to high-end facilities throughout Ohio, and relocation assistance, if I chose to move to Columbus.

And, of course, the offer letter stressed that Chase Guild’s offer was not up to negotiation. It was take-it-or-leave-it, and I had a single week to respond, or else the offer was automatically revoked. Which was doubly frustrating, because Clyde and Veronica both felt good about their chances, but general offers hadn’t been sent yet following the assessment. And there was no way I’d know their outcomes before Chase’s offer expired. According to Veronica, they were told offers would be made approximately two weeks following the assessment.

So, I did the only thing that made sense. I called Labonte. It was time to get more color on Harvest Guild’s offer.

I can still hear his voice like it’s in my ear right now. The raspy, high tones of the entrepreneur-turned-Guild Master’s voice was smug and amused, like I was interrupting his day just to entertain him.

“Sully Boy! I expected you to be calling me back, but didn’t think you’d take this long!”

I asked him if there was a formal offer to join Harvest Guild, though didn’t mention my offer from Chase Guild. Yet.

He didn’t answer like a normal person. Instead, he asked: “What do you want?” And implied Jerome would draw up the contract.

So, I told him exactly what I wanted: $300,000 base salary. $30,000 signing bonus. Similar performance bonuses to what was set out in my other offer letter.

“Easy enough,” he had said in response.

“I’m not done,” I said.

Then, I went for the part that made me feel like I’d either become very smart or very delusional:  a luxury apartment downtown. Paid for by the Guild as long as I’m an active member. And the Loot Rights: I wanted to keep 4% of all non-Boss loot. And I wanted the ability to select one item from any Boss I fight.

Labonte accepted almost everything immediately, only pushing back on that last point. Boss Loot. It was a big deal, and it was how most Guilds made their profit. It was apparently sacred territory—even for a man who turns sugar into mana and has about the most atypical recruiting strategy I’ve ever seen.

But after some back-and-forth, we landed on terms that made both of us happy enough not to stab each other: I get one item from any Boss or the equivalent percentage (in value) of non-Boss loot, at the Guild’s discretion. But—and this part mattered—I must be given the Boss loot option at least once every three Gates. So, approximately a third of the time, I would be selecting my loot from Boss fights. It wasn’t ideal, and the control ultimately was more in Harvest’s hands, but I also understood that what I had been requesting was overly aggressive.

Not perfect, but fair. And honestly? Better than fair.

“What Band and Tier will I be in?” I had asked as we were wrapping up the call.

Labonte laughed. “Think we have enough members yet for that to matter?... Listen kid, I think you’re gonna do great things. Terrible things. A monster like you can’t be worrying about climbing the corporate ladder. You’ll be Band 1, Tier 1 – a member of our Premier Team!”

That news made me think of my ex, Sarah, for some reason. She was on the Premier Team for Pegasus Guild, the most prestigious Guild in the entire country. I pushed the thought away.

“Sounds good, Mister Labonte.”

“Please, Joey, it’s Amos. We’re co-workers now.”

With that, the deal was done. The only matter was to formalize things.

I rolled up to Harvest Guild headquarters like a guest of honor. Which is hilarious, because I still feel like the guy who used to break down cardboard at Save-Some-Bucks and pretend his life wasn’t collapsing.

Labonte had the contract ready. I reviewed it, and for once in my life, something important actually looked… solid. The commercial terms we had discussed on the phone were all there in plain English. The term (three years), termination provisions (I could quit at any time), and exist restrictions (unless I quit for good reason, I wouldn’t be permitted to be a member of a Guild actively participating in Ohio for two years after termination). Not the worst non-compete, all things considered.

Jerome was there, an avatar of coolness in a crisp, tan suit. He formed spell shapes in the air with his long, pianist fingers—precise, geometric sigils that spun and twisted in the space above the conference room table. They drifted down and pressed into the parchment like hot wax seals.

Labonte and I shook hands over the contract. And the spellwork burned into the contract, then vanished—leaving the document feeling heavier than paper has any right to feel, the tang of metal on the tip of my tongue, and the small of ozone.

The contract had been magically bound.

After that, as I was talking out, I mentioned the Great Lakes Spellcaster Society meeting on July 1st. Labonte immediately decided he and Jerome should go too, to scope out potential talent. Jerome did not look enthused by the idea. I could see the hours of work he was calculating needed to be done at some other, inconvenient time.

Labonte offered to pay for my admission, meals, and even a hotel if I wanted to stay the night in Detroit. I accepted gratefully.

A day later, the apartment was secured. Move-in date set and movers scheduled for July 3rd.

It all happened so fast it felt like someone hit the fast-forward button on my life. My parents insisted I didn’t need to move out, if I wanted to save up some more before getting my own place. When I told them it was being paid for by the Guild, they were shocked. My dad was proud, and happy with my accomplishment. My mom… Didn’t take the news so well.

“The house is going to be so quiet again,” she said, frowning.

I told her Tom, Jax, and Other Tom could stay with them. Knowing she would at least have her viewing buddies around made the news of my departure a little easier.

“Good,” Liv had said when hearing the news. “You need to get out. Touch grass. And I could use your room as my personal alchemy lab the rest of the summer.”

“Thanks, Liv…”

Now, here I am, driving into Detroit, officially a premier member of the Harvest Guild, headed to a meeting of the Great Lakes Spellcaster Society, with a shopping list, which include the service of someone who can help me use the Augmentation Array.

And the wildest part of this all?

It wasn’t that long ago I was sitting in my parents’ driveway with a busted window, trying to figure out how to patch it so rain wouldn’t soak my car interior. Living in the basement. Counting dollars because my bank account had been cleaned out in a messy escape from New York City. Taking freelance jobs like scraps because it was all I had.

My hands tighten on the steering wheel.

“How the hell,” I mutter to myself, watching Detroit grow closer through the windshield, “did my life change this much this fast?”

No answer comes. Though Lefty and Right open their palms upwards in their equivalent of a shrug.

I dispel [Wizard’s Fist] as I approach the Cadillac Hotel, right in the heart of downtown Detroit. I pull up to the valet lane and the guy in the red vest jogs over like my junker is a Lamborghini.

“Valet?” he asks.

“Yep,” I say, punching my car into Park and leaving my keys in the ignition.

I step out, taking a ticket from the valet, which I pocket. I’m in ordinary street clothes: jeans, sneakers, an oversized t-shirt, and a baseball cap pulled low.

The valet gives me a ticket. I tip him ten bucks, because Harvest Guild money has already started rewiring my brain in dangerous ways. If this conference had been a couple of weeks earlier, I would have parked several blocks away if it meant free street parking.

I walk into the lobby and I follow the signs.

GREAT LAKES SPELLCASTERS’ SOCIETY à

BALLROOM LEVEL / 3RD FLOOR à

I take the elevator up with a cluster of people who are largely also in street clothes, except one guy who is wearing a metal mask over his face and is draped in a dark cloak. One of the women in the elevator quietly take a step away from that guy. I don’t blame her.

Ding!

Third floor.

The doors open and the hallway is already buzzing. We all flow out of the elevator and I move along with the group, until we reach eight doors held open to a large ballroom. There’s a table checking people in.

I give my name and receive a badge on a lanyard. Some peoples’ badges are adorned with various colored stickers, which I learn are marked for entrance to some of the events, panels, and workshops being held in the various conference rooms on this floor. I really hope one of those workshops isn’t ‘How to Use Your Augmentation Array.’ Because if it is, I may very well need to crash it. My badge has zero stickers. I was too late in signing up and all the side events were already fully booked.

The ballroom itself is huge, with high ceilings. People bustle around in clusters, talking, gesturing, laughing, arguing, showing off gear, demonstrating spells in little controlled display spaces with runed barriers set up between various tables manned by all sorts of sorcery-focused businesses.

This looks like an anime convention. But instead of cosplay, people walk around wearing their System-empowered gear. Enchanted cloaks, spell rings, boots with subtle glow patterns. A guy in the corner is showing off a cape that ripples like smoke and keeps rearranging itself into different silhouettes like it’s alive. There are plenty of people in street clothes, like myself. Almost everyone is carrying a spellbook. Some are large, leather-bound tomes, others palm-sized planners. There are spellbooks floating alongside or behind their masters like loyal service dogs.

The center and outer wall of the ballroom are filled with booths. Vendors are selling a variety of wares: components, spell scrolls, trinkets, vials, System-produced books that can be ‘read’ in an instant and impart knowledge on their user.

“Guaranteed mana conductivity!” One booth’s signs read.

And the spell displays…

Some guy is projecting shimmering geometric formations into the air like a neon origami show. Another booth has a ring of floating marbles orbiting a staff tip, spinning faster and faster until the air whistles. There’s a sign that reads ‘Staves of Elemental Attunement.’

No capes, though. I really want to replace my old cape with something indestructible. And maybe new items for my ring slots too. Maybe something that provides me with no-cost usages of Spells, like my ring that can store a single spell for future use.

I ignore all of the looks and gawking I get as I politely move my way through the crowd. Much like the Chase Guild assessment, I stick out like a sore thumb. Not to say I’m the only obvious gymgoer at this convention. There are all sorts of shapes and sizes, but I definitely look like a Barbarian who took a wrong turn and ended up at a gathering of the local Wizards’ Circle.

I weave through the ballroom trying not to step on the hem of anyone’s cloak, or bump into a slow-moving spellbook. A guy with a cape made of shimmering scales brushes past me. Across the aisle, someone is arguing about mana purity. “Actually!...” A booth labeled “SIGIL SAFETY: NO REFUNDS” has a little scorch mark on the carpet in front of it.

Then, through the crowd, I spot a couple of familiar faces.

Labonte stands out like a neon sign in a library, or better yet a piece of gum stuck in someone’s hair. He’s short, and would probably be easy to lose in a crowd, if you were deaf. He’s loud, and dressed like he lost a fight with a golem made exclusively from bright-colored pinstriped fabric. Jerome is beside him, tall and stoic, in a navy suit, looking like the only adult in a room full of excited teenagers with access to magic.

Jerome’s eyes meet mine first. He nods politely.

“Mr. Sullivan,” he says, voice smooth. “Good to see you, again.”

Labonte turns at the same time, already scowling.

“A bunch of losers,” he declares loudly, gesturing at the ballroom as if he’s blessing the peasants with his disappointment. “And many of them could learn to use deodorant, or at least find a simple hygiene passive! My goodness!”

I awkwardly freeze. A couple nearby attendees glance over. One guy in a rune-stitched trench coat looks personally attacked. He tries to stealthily sniff himself, only to look absolutely mortified by the results. He scurries towards the doors of the ballroom.

“Uh,” I mutter, cheeks warming. “They… seem nice. And welcoming.”

Labonte snorts. “Nice isn’t useful. Seems like we’ve wasted our time coming here, Jerome.”

Jerome’s eyes flick shut for half a second like he’s mentally counting to ten. “Mr. Labonte,” he murmurs, “perhaps lower your volume.”

Labonte ignores him and points vaguely toward the back where a schedule board lists panels and side events. “I’ll check out some panels later. But first, I’ve got a virtual meeting I need to take from my room.” He produces a small bag of sour gummy worms and begins tossing them into his mouth three at a time.

“A meeting?” I ask. “Guild business?”

Labonte’s eyes gleam. “Something big is in the works. Potential joint-Guild exercise.” He leans forward like he’s sharing insider gossip. “In Cleveland.”

I blink. “Cleveland?”

“IN Cleveland,” he repeats, savoring the words as though they’re another handful of gummy worms. “It’s too good an opportunity. And if a local Guild is going to get the nod—not some big-city Guild swooping in like vultures—it should be Harvest!”

Jerome exhales through his nose, a barely contained sigh. “He means he wants to look important in front of the AEA.”

Labonte shoots him a glare. “I am important. We are important, kid.”

Jerome’s face stays pleasant. “Of course… And please don’t call me kid, sir.”

“When you get to be my age, everyone is still a kid. You two are still spring chickens, in my eyes.” Labonte points a stubby finger at me. “Don’t waste your time on the weirdos selling enchanted socks. You might find some bargains here, but you’d be better off using Guild channels to acquire specific equipment. Or just waiting til we get a few more Quests under our belt!”

Then he spins and vanishes into the crowd like a furious little sugar-fueled gremlin, leaving Jerome and me standing there amid the hum of spell commerce and chatter of enthusiastic hobbyists.

Jerome watches him go, then rolls his eyes so smoothly it feels practiced.

I clear my throat. “So… quick question.”

“Yes?”

“Do you know how to use an Augmentation Array?”

Jerome’s pleasant expression doesn’t change, but his eyes sharpen with interest. “I do not.”

“Damn.”

“I can research it,” he adds, like he’s offering to read a thousand pages of arcane forum posts without blinking. “But I cannot advise you offhand. Not part of my Class’s repertoire.”

I nod, storing that disappointment away with all the other ones.

Jerome adjusts his tie. “In the meantime, I’m going to see if I can acquire equipment for the Guild’s lower-leveled members. Practical purchases. Things that keep people alive. Despite what Amos may think, there are some very talented vendors here.”

“Good plan,” I say.

We start walking, drifting booth to booth, and Jerome immediately turns into a one-man economic event. He buys potions. Scrolls. A set of runed gloves and enchanted shirts. A “starter ward kit.” A “miasma neutralizer charm.” He doesn’t haggle. He doesn’t hesitate. He just points, pays, and the items vanish into his Inventory like he’s feeding an invisible whale. Watching him is making my brain itch.

“How big is your Inventory?” I ask before I can stop myself. I know Jelly Boy has an unnaturally large Inventory because of his Class… Or because he’s a slime. I’m not entirely sure, actually.

Jerome smiles, just a little. “Some Classes provide for more Inventory space. But most people require an item to expand their capacity.”

He reaches into the inside breast pocket of his suit jacket and produces something that looks like… a thin wallet. It is a thin, black leather wallet.

He holds it up. “Bag of Holding.”

My eyebrows shoot up. “That’s a wallet.”

“It increases my Inventory by approximately six hundred slots,” Jerome says calmly, like he’s describing a minor convenience. “Everything I’ve bought today is inside here.” He places the wallet back into his jacket, patting his chest.

My brain does a small cartwheel. “Need to get me one of those.”

“Did you say you need a Bag of Holding?”

The voice comes from the next booth over.

A woman leans forward from behind a table covered in leather pouches. She’s got tool calluses on her fingers and the focused, slightly manic gaze of someone who has absolutely blown something up in her workshop and enjoyed the experience.

“Artificer,” she says by way of introduction, tapping the badge on her chest. “Pretty good one, if I can toot my own horn. Bags of Holding are one of my best sellers since I hit Level 25.”

She spreads her arms toward one of her tables, where pouches of different sizes and stitching patterns are laid out like candy.

“Different holding capacities,” she says, pointing. “Fifty additional slots… one hundred… two-fifty… five hundred.”

My mouth waters.

I point at the biggest one. “Price on the five hundred-slot upgrade?”

“Ten thousand,” she says, matter-of-fact.

I almost faint. My knees actually wobble.

“Ten thousand… dollars?” I croak.

She shrugs. “Quality materials. Stable enchantment. A one-year warranty on all my work.”

Jerome watches with mild amusement, like he’s observing a nature documentary where a young mage encounters capitalism.

I swallow hard. “Okay. What if I…” I glance at her wares, then at Jerome’s calm face, then back. “I can… Uh… Trade?”

Her eyebrows lift, interested. “What’ve you got?”

After a quick sift through my Inventory, I notice a couple of slots full of Storm Essence Shards we collected from the dragon’s nest back in the Bronze Gate.

“I’ve got Storm Essence Shards,” I say. “How much would you be willing to knock off for those?”

She considers. “Could definitely use those in my crafting work. How many are we talkin’ about here?”

“A hundred work?”

Her eyes widen a fraction—professional interest spiking. “A hundred is… significant.”

She considers, eyes flicking between me and my bib and the faint glow in my eyes that never fully goes away anymore. “I’d take of $6,000 for one hundred of those shards.”

“Make it $8,000. As you said, 100 shards is significant.”

“$6,500.”

“$7,000.”

Finally she nods. “Done.”

My stomach clenches as I imagine my signing bonus bleeding out in real time, but I nod too. “Done. So, $3,000 cash, then?”

“Yessir!”

As she starts preparing the exchange, she looks up again. “You want the leather pouch, or the customization enchantment?”

I hesitate.

“It’s no extra cost,” she says. “Just some people don’t like the aesthetic of the leather pouch. Or, would prefer to enchant the item themselves.”

I think of Jerome’s Bag of Holding. His wallet is sleek and professional. But then my brain flashes to my cursed jorts. Which sort of ‘customization’ would I receive?

I take a breath. “Custom. Please.”

The Artificer grins. “Good choice. It’ll be based on your Class, mostly. But some other factors are also considered. I would be lying if I said I completely understood the System’s manifestation of the enchantment.”

I sigh. “Just give it to me.”

She hands me a plain pouch. I hold it, feel the faint hum of contained space inside it, and begin attuning. It takes half a minute of focusing. Warmth spreads up my fingers, the sensation of the item connecting with me.

The pouch flickers, a brief flash of light. Then, the entire item is consumed in pixelated light. It begins to transform within my grasp. The leather stretches, colors shifting into loud neon stripes. The shape warps.

When the light fades, I’m holding…

“You’ve got to be kidding me,” I breathe.

An 80s colored fanny pack. Hot pink and teal, with yellow accents. The kind of thing you’d see on a dad rollerblading in 1987. I stare at it.

Jerome’s lips twitch. I swear I hear him snort.

The Artificer laughs.

“Now I’m curious what your Class might be,” she says.

“Great!” I say, forcing enthusiasm through gritted teeth. “Love it, actually. Thank you!”

I clip it around my waist.

It sits there proudly, like it’s been waiting its whole life to embarrass me. I can only imagine what it will look like with my jorts on.

The fanny pack sits on my waist like a middle finger from the universe. I tug it once, hoping it might morph into something less humiliating if I glare at it hard enough. It doesn’t.

Jerome’s eyes flick to it and he gives the smallest, most professional smile I’ve ever seen. We keep moving.

And eventually I spot a booth that looks like it was designed by someone who doesn’t want friends. Black banners with harsh red lettering. A logo that looks like a skull wearing a cowboy hat with a lightning bolt through it.

DESPERADO DAN’S — CHICAGO’S FINEST

COMBAT-FOCUSED ENCHANTED EQUIPMENT

IF YOU’RE BUYING THIS, YOU PLAN TO LIVE!

Sounds right up my alley, I think. I do in fact like living. A lot!

The strange thing is, they’re not getting much business.

The aisles around them are busy—people crowding potion booths, arguing about spell formation, buying cute enchanted trinkets that float or sparkle or make your outfit look different in some way. But Desperado Dan’s stands there like the edgy kid at a prom, ignored and slightly offended.

“Why is no one here?” I ask, slowing.

Jerome glances around, hands clasped behind his back like he’s touring an art museum. “Most attendees are focused on the… broader applications of magic.”

“Broader?”

“Quality of life. Crafting. Commerce. Social enhancements. Domestic spellwork.” He lifts a brow. “Outside of Gates or monster outbreaks, combat doesn’t have high usage for most of the population that gained access to the System.”

I stare at the booth. “That’s insane.”

Jerome shrugs. “It’s also true. Does entering portals to other, monster-filled worlds sound any less insane?”

“Touche.”

I step up to Desperado Dan’s anyway.

A man stands behind the table. He’s got the vibe of someone who definitely has a conspiracy board in his basement. Stringy hair, eyes too wide and rimmed with deep bags. A grin that never quite matches the rest of his face. Face dark with five-o-clock shadow, except for his upper lip, which bears a thick, bushy mustache. The man’s name tag reads ‘Doug.’ And here I was thinking this was Dan himself!

Doug leans forward the moment I approach.

“Heyyyy,” he says, dragging the word out like taffy. “You look like a guy who gets into all sorts of trouble.”

“Frequently,” I admit.

Doug’s grin widens. “Love that! Good for business.”

I scan the table. It’s a full spread of gear to fill any of the many equipment slots: Enchanted gloves, reinforced wraps, utility belts, capes and cowls, and at least one leather cuirass.

Doug produces something from under the table like he’s unveiling a priceless artifact. It’s a pair of loafers. They’re made from a shiny brown leather, with tassels on the front.

“These,” Dan whispers reverently, “are murder-proof loafers.”

I blink. “Those are loafers. Murder loafers?”

Dan nods aggressively. “Murder-proof. Or, should I say, murder-resistance granting loafers. They are deceptive. But don’t let their appearance fool you.”

I examine them more closely.

Item Identified: Murder Loafers.

Description: When equipped, these loafers provide the User protection against murder. If targeted by a creature that has murderous intent, the User will turn invisible and be under the effects of a silence charm. While invisible, the User takes 10% less damage from stabbing, bludgeoning, and slashing damage. The User’s invisibility ends once the User takes damage. The loafers’ ability can only be used once per day.

“Hm,” I say. “These are pretty good, actually.” I actually have decent Resistances as it is thanks to the training Walter and Preston put me through, but an additional ten percent wouldn’t hurt. The stealth bonus was nice too. But would it end if I used [No Pain, No Gain] while invisible? And it could only activate once per day. So, it was clearly most effective to flee a dangerous situation.

“I’m good,” I say, shaking my head.

Doug looks devastated for half a second, then recovers instantly. “Okay okay okay. I noticed you eyeing those capes. You’re a cape guy. I can smell it.”

He slides a cape toward me.

It’s… familiar. A thick, dark blue material. A simple brass button where the cape clasps closed. It looks a lot like my old cape, though this one has a collar to it, high to cover the neck.

“Is it indestructible?”

“Indestructible?”

“Indestructible.”

“Nothing here is indestructible, truly. But if you mean will it survive through anything that would otherwise obliterate the heartiest of stuff here on Earth? Indeed!”

“Okay.” I tap my chin. “Can I examine it?”

“Be my guest.”

I do so, summoning a System-generated message.

Item Identified: Cape of Unnatural Balance

Description: This cape grants its User an uncanny sense of balance. The User will be incapable of losing their balance when performing a feat of acrobatics. (+20 DEX)

My eyes narrow. “Okay, what’s the catch?”

Doug looks offended. “The catch is you pay me.”

Jerome watches quietly as I examine it. I run my fingers along the hem.

Doug continues, voice low like he’s selling me a secret. “Balance is only half the battle. It doesn’t mean you suddenly become some sort of ninja.”

That… actually matters. But with my Strength and Dexterity scores, having perfect balance could be huge.

I look down at my humiliating fanny pack, then back at the cape. I’m already resigned to looking stupid as hell.

“How much?”

Doug doesn’t hesitate. “Seven grand.”

My soul leaves my body for a brief second, then crawls back in because it remembers I just joined a Guild and this is all just an investment in my own career.

I exhale. “Done.”

Doug grins like he just won a duel. “Good choice, sir! I don’t think this would serve many of the other attendees, but something tells me this cape with find a good home with you.”

Jerome’s gaze flicks to me. “You are spending quite a bit today.”

“I’m investing,” I say. “In not dying.”

We complete the purchase. The cape vanishes into my Inventory with a comforting ping accompanied by a mental weight.

After that, because I’m already hemorrhaging money like it’s a skill in its own right, I stop at another booth and pick up a Ring of Fire Mana.

It’s cheap compared to everything else. Five hundred bucks, to be specific. It’s a common-grade enchanted ring, and doesn’t do anything more than generate fire mana. About the amount used to create a single bon fire, per day. It’s a training tool for new spellcasters specializing in fire elemental magic, because fire mana makes casting those spells easier than general mana. But I want it for a different purpose: to feed by Behemoth Cap. It’s not much in the moment, but it’ll accumulate over time. And eventually, the synergy will allow me to re-access the hat’s higher-grade spells again.

It’s a simple silver ring with a pinprick sized ruby. I slide it onto my finger and feel a faint warmth bloom under my skin, like holding your hands near a candle.

I take in my purchases, pleased with the haul. But in the back of my mind, that Augmentation Array still sits there like a cigarette-shaped puzzle.

I glance over the bustling ballroom again. Somewhere in this chaos is someone who knows how to use that thing. And I intend to find them.

I’m about to lose my mind. Three more hours of searching, asking questions at booth after booth, and no luck on finding someone who knows how to use the Augmentation Array. At this point, Labonte has already returned to reclaim Jerome.

“See you back in Cleveland, Joey!” exclaims the old man.

Jerome shakes my hand before the pair make their way out of the bathroom.

I search for another thirty minutes to no avail.

Resigned, I too decide to leave the convention. But, first thing’s first, I need to hit up the restroom. Business complete and exiting back into the hallway outside the ballroom, I almost run into a wall of people. They’re clustered tight in a semicircle, bodies angled inward.

I stop, curiosity flaring.

In the middle of the group sits a man in a maroon cloak stitched with tiny stars. It’s the kind of cloak that screams wizard in the most classical, storybook way. He’s also got a conical hat. A thin, long beard hangs from his round, broad face. He’s short, kind of chubby, kind of (really) hairy. He looks like someone’s friendly uncle got isekai’d into a fantasy novel and decided to go all in on the motif, but hadn’t yet gained the gray age of wisdom most stereotypical wizards have achieved.

It’s his eyes that make me stop and watch. They’re burning. Burning with passion. With the kind of intensity you see in someone explaining their favorite niche hobby to a captive audience. He’s mid-lecture, hands moving as he speaks.

“—you can alter the array’s curvature here,” he says, stabbing a finger at a large piece of paper on the carpeted floor covered with sharpie-drawn lines, “and that changes not just the casting efficiency, but the nature of the spell itself. You can modify the output, modify the effect, even modify the category of spell, in some cases. It’s how you make a spell into a new spell entirely.”

The group leans in. A few near the back of the semi-circle are checked out, clearly just in the hall to wait for whatever panel or workshop is next on the itinerary. A couple are taking pictures with their phones, listening intently.

The man’s spellbook floats open in front of him, pages flipping of their own accord like they’re being turned by an invisible breeze. Each page is packed with lettering and diagrams, layered sigils, branching spellforms that look like circuit boards. Page after page after page.

“And it’s a fantastic way to add most of a spell to your spellbook,” the wizard continues, voice bright. “Not the full thing every time—sometimes you only add parts of a spell—but you can absolutely build your library.”

I feel something inside me go click.

This is it. This is exactly the kind of obsessive nerd I need.

The man wraps up his explanation with a flourish and the spellbook snaps shut, hovering for a moment before settling against his side like a loyal pet. The little cluster of listeners murmurs appreciatively, then slowly breaks apart, people drifting away like they’ve just attended church.

I step forward before I can overthink it.

“Hey,” I say. “Sorry—uh. That was… really impressive, dude.”

The wizard looks up at me, blinking at me, as if shocked I’m even speaking to him. He quickly remembers himself. “Er—thanks! Glad you caught it. I’m a bit of a spell nerd.”

I stick out my hand. “I’m Joseph. I’m looking for a spell nerd, actually.”

He reaches up and takes it. His handshake is soft, not as confident as I’d expect from someone who was just lecturing to a small crowd of people.

“Scott,” he says. “Nice to meet you, Joseph. Uh… You said you were looking for a nerd?”

“Okay, Scott,” I say, trying not to sound like I’m about to propose marriage. I squat down so we’re eye level. He scootches back, just a smidge. “How are you so good at arrays?”

He laughs, genuinely delighted. “Oh. I’m not sure I’d say good. I just… obsess. Ya know? I think they’re really fun.”

“Not part of your Class?” I ask. “You’re not like an Array Artillerist, or something?”

Scott shakes his head, chuckling. “Nope. I’m actually a Level 15 Wizard. Plain ole’ Wizard.”

I blink. “Level 15?... And you have that many spells?”

“Yup.” He shrugs like it’s nothing. “I need to study and practice a ton before my spellbook will add a new spell. Or even parts of an array. Any Wizard could do it, but I find filling my spellbook very satisfying.” He pats the floating book fondly. “It’s chock full of them now. Took me forever.”

That’s… honestly inspiring in a way that makes me feel guilty for relying on brute force and trauma-induced upgrades.

I lean in slightly. “Scott. I… need advice. Assistance, really.” I feel like the jock genuflecting before the top student in the class in a last ditch attempt to copy their homework and pass the class.

His eyebrows lift. “Uh. Sure? About what? I can do what I can…”

I swallow and pull the Augmentation Array from my Inventory. It appears in my hand—a tiny scroll the size of a cigarette, dense with folded spell geometry.

Scott’s eyes widen immediately.

“Oh,” he breathes.

He takes it carefully, like he’s holding a rare insect. He studies it for a full two minutes in silence, rotating it, squinting, tracing invisible lines in the air with his finger as if he’s reading the spellwork through the paper. Then, he looks up at me with something bordering on reverence.

“Wow,” he says. “This is rare. And valuable. Like… extremely.”

My stomach tightens. “Good rare or ‘I shouldn’t be holding this in a public hallway’ rare?”

Scott laughs. “Both.”

He hands it back reluctantly. “It’s powerful. If used properly, it can do incredible things. And… yeah. I’d be happy to help you. Actually, I really want to help you. Please let me help you use this Array!”

I hesitate, because nothing in my life is free anymore unless it comes with a curse.

“How much?” I ask. “For you to do this?”

Scott bursts out laughing.

“For this?” He shakes his head. “Free.”

I blink. “Free?”

He nods, eyes gleaming again. “It’s as much a favor to me as it is to you. If we do it successfully, it’ll add part of the Augmentation Array to my spellbook.”

He says it like a kid talking about adding a legendary card to their deck.

My chest loosens. Relief hits so hard it feels like I could cry.

“Okay,” I say, voice a little too eager. “Then… where do we start?”

Scott leads me to a quieter stretch of hallway with a couple of those awkward hotel lounge chairs that look expensive but feel like they were designed by someone who hates spines. We sit anyway. The convention noise fades to a muffled roar through the ballroom doors behind us.

Scott rubs his hands together eagerly. “Okay,” he says, businesslike now. “Here’s what I need. First: the spell array of the spell you want to apply to an item. Second: the item itself.”

I blink. “Right.”

Then I blink again. “Problem,” I say.

Scott tilts his head. “Problem?”

“I don’t… know the arrays of my spells. No idea where to start, actually.” I gesture vaguely at my head like the information might spill out if I shake it hard enough.

“Don’t you visualize the arrays when you cast your spells?” he asks.

“My method of spellcasting is… unconventional.”

Scott’s eyebrows rise. “Unconventional how?”

I exhale. “I flex.”

He looks confused.

“Like my muscles…”

“Ohhh!... Cool, cool.” Then Scott stares for half a second—then his face lights up like I’ve just told him I have a pet dragon. “That’s incredible,” he whispers. “It’s like your body is the array.”

“Thanks,” I deadpan. “It’s also deeply unhelpful in academic settings like this. So, am I fucked? Or is there still a way to use this Array?”

He considers the question. I see his mind working behind those burning eyes. “No worries,” Scott says finally, waving a hand. “We don’t need your spell arrays specifically. There’s no requirement the spell has to be yours. I’m happy to offer up my spellbook.”

He slides his floating spellbook between us.The cover is worn in a loved way. The edges are frayed. It looks like it’s been dropped, soaked, scorched, and cherished.

“You sure?” I ask.

He nods. “Go for it.”

The moment it touches my hands, a System prompt pops into my vision.

Access Request: Scott’s Spellbook

Would you like to access this Spellbook?

[YES] / [NO]

I mentally tap YES.

The book warms slightly under my fingers, and a translucent menu appears, hovering a couple feet over the book.

KNOWN SPELLS

(Scroll for full list)

A list unfurls. Spell names march down the screen in neat script. There are… a lot.

I scroll, occasionally stopping to consider a spell in more detail. Then, I see it.

Fly.

My throat tightens like I’ve just spotted a life raft in open water.

I look up at Scott. “Can we do it with the Fly spell?”

Scott grins. “Oh, absolutely. Fly’s a classic.”

I try not to sound like a desperate man in a desert. “Great. Then let’s do that one!”

Scott nods once, then leans forward. “Which item are you thinking?”

I withdraw my boots first—my enchanted boots appear with a soft ping, leather and the faint smell of swamp water. Then I withdraw my new cape from Desperado Dan’s.

“One of these probably makes the most sense,” I say.

Scott examines both carefully, fingers hovering just above the surfaces. He hums.

“Neither of these is ideal.”

My heart sinks. “Why not?”

Scott taps the cover of his spellbook lightly. “The cape could work, but do you really want a flying cape?”

“Uhhh… I don’t know. Yes?”

“Huge strangulation hazard.”

I consider it, and then nod, understanding his point.

“As for the boots,” he continues. “They would work, practically speaking. But aren’t combatable with the Augmentation Array. Applying an array like this one is a negotiation. The item taking on the two arrays has to be convinced: first, to change itself and accept what the Augmentation Array is trying to convince it to become. Then, it needs to accept the spell array you’re trying to apply.”

“A negotiation,” I repeat.

He nods. “You’re asking an item to accept a new nature. Some items resist. Some don’t have the right structure. Some already have too many enchantments or an identity that clashes. You can brute force it sometimes, but success rates go down, and the array can destabilize.”

I stare at my boots and cape like they’ve betrayed me. With a silent command, I pull them back into my Inventory.

Then my brain supplies the obvious solution. I reach into my Inventory and pull out the Full Metal Staff. It appears with a satisfying weight in my hands—black iron barbell-staff, plates glowing faintly, the whole thing radiating a powerful energy.

Scott’s eyes widen.

“Oh,” he says, reverent. “That is… beautiful.”

“It’s a staff,” I say, though even I don’t believe how casual that sounds.

Scott leans closer, studying it like a scientist examining a rare meteorite. “This item is… like a sponge. It’s built to store things. Built to take in energy. Built to hold onto things. Probably even a couple of new spell arrays.” He looks up at me, grin spreading. “It will definitely work.”

My chest loosens. “Yes.”

Scott cracks his knuckles like he’s about to do a crossword puzzle with lethal consequences. He flips his spellbook open to a blank page. Then—this is the part that makes me love him—he pulls out a cheap ballpoint pen. A ballpoint pen. In a world of magic and runes and floating spellbooks, this man whips out Office Depot’s finest and starts drawing like a mad genius.

He sketches lines—curves, angles, repeating symbols that spiral into a cohesive lattice. This, he explains to me, is the Fly array. He draws it fast, confident, like he’s written it a hundred times. Then he adds modifications—little tweaks branching off the main structure like veins. Scott tells me that this is an alteration that allows him to apply, or link the first array to both the Augmentation Array and the staff.

“Okay,” he murmurs. “Okay. Now we introduce the augmentation.”

He takes the cigarette-sized scroll—my Augmentation Array—and carefully unrolls it.

The moment it opens, the air between us hums. The array inside isn’t ink. It lifts off the scroll like smoke rising, forming a glowing geometric sheet in the air.

Scott holds the scroll above his spellbook page. The Fly array rises off the paper too, peeling up like the page is shedding its skin.

The two arrays overlap in midair. They merge. Then they turn and drift toward my staff like they’ve found their target. The lines of light burn onto the metallic bar in a spiraling pattern of glowing symbols, etching themselves into the iron with a sound like faint sizzling rain.

For a split second, my staff looks like it’s been tattooed by lightning.

Scott’s spellbook flashes. A portion of the array appears on a page in his book, as if it’s been copied there by an invisible hand.

Scott gasps softly, delighted. “Yes—yes! It took!”

And then my HUD pings.

SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:

Augmentation Successful!

The properties of [Fly] have been applied to Full Metal Staff.

I stare at the staff. Then at Scott.

Then back at the staff.

My grin spreads so wide it almost hurts.

“Oh my God,” I whisper. “I’ve never been happier, dude!”
