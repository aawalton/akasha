Kyle watched the departing form of Benjamin, before deciding to finish his dessert.  He was curious, certainly, but he also wanted to make sure that he didn’t come across as rude when he was a specially noted guest.  As the crowds began to disperse, he and C.H.A.D.D. finally made their way past the war room and into the library, which was packed floor to ceiling with shelves upon shelves of books.  Gentle warm light glowed from stones set into the walls, with orc-sized plush chairs interspersed throughout.  If he didn’t have serious business to attend to, Kyle would have been tempted to sit in one of them, if for no other reason than to see how quickly he’d be sucked in by the oversized furniture.

Rounding a corner, he saw Benjamin poring over a tome, appearing to take snapshots of the pages with his nav bracelet as he slowly flipped through the document.

“Looks like some serious reading,” Kyle said as he walked up to the other man, looking down at the book.  At a glance, it looked to be a text all about farming mushrooms, leading Kyle to frown.

Benjamin gave him a serious look.  “It is important reading.  And documenting.  Skippy and I hope to be off planet before the Tournament of Ascension begins, and part of my role here is to help archive as much as I can from Duroc’s village.”

“Be honest with me, how bad are Duroc’s chances?  Back in D Grade, he was monstrously powerful, and I can’t imagine that’s changed with his evolution.  Yet everybody is acting like the village is doomed.”

“It has less to do with Duroc, and more to do with Mangalica,” Benjamin said with a sigh.  “Shortly after rising to C Grade, he challenged and killed the War Chieftain of his village, a mid-tier C Grade.  That’s put everybody on edge, particularly as Duroc will still likely be the weakest of the War Chieftains at the tournament.  If Mangalica uses it as an opportunity to declare war, targeting this village makes the most sense.”

“Mangalica is really that strong?”  Kyle asked.

“I admit that I don’t know the specifics, but from what I’ve been able to gather, he sounds like an absolute force of nature.  And coupling that with a might-makes-right philosophy, he could herald an era of change for Guancious; not one I believe would be for the better.”

Kyle nodded thoughtfully.  A powerhouse like that would certainly make waves, and if his path centered around combat…  “I’ll keep an ear out during my travels to see if I can learn anything about him.  Fighting styles, weaknesses, all of it.  If any of it can prove a benefit to Duroc, I’ll see what I can do.”

“I’m sure he’ll appreciate it.  He puts on a strong face, but matters here have been weighing on him far more than he lets on.  But enough about this world’s problems.  You have a job to do, and we have to trust Duroc to handle his part.  I asked to meet to discuss matters more relevant to you.”  Benjamin gestured to a table nearby, and Kyle walked over to it, sitting in an oversized wooden chair.  He noticed idly that Benjamin had a human-sized chair on his side of the table, and wondered if Skippy had to bring it along inside his storage item.

Benjamin drew a deep breath, then began.  “I mentioned the future of Corthian Mining.  It won’t be formally announced for a few months yet, but Angela is looking to liquidate the business.  Too much went sour after Kertan Six.  We’ve been bleeding market share, revenues are down, and we won’t be able to maintain our grasp on the industry.  We’ll be maintaining a smaller estate, a couple of ships, and a small crew of elites.  My great grandmother will be taking to field work again, personally.”

Kyle was silent for a moment, taking it all in.  Businesses rose and fell all the time – that was just part of the natural order of things – but to see Corthian Mining fold like this was unexpected.  “What does that mean for you?” Kyle asked.

“I don’t know,” Benjamin replied with a sigh, leaning back in his chair.  “I’m one of the only unawakened in the family, so it’s not as though I’ll be terribly useful out in the field.  Skippy’s contract is up for renewal in a year, so we’ll see what’s in the budget for security.  But I’m not telling you this to give you my sob story; I’m telling you because the refugees from your planet will be the ones with the largest fallout.  We also bear some responsibility for their relocation, at least for those under our employ.”

“I didn’t know things had gotten that bad,” Kyle began, taking a moment to gather his thoughts.  If Corthian Mining could help cover some of the infrastructure costs… “There may be an opportunity for us to help each other with a project I’ve been working on.  Let me consult with some of my people and follow up.  Should I connect with you directly?”

Benjamin nodded.  “That would be best, at least until the liquidation is made public.  Then there’s the other matter that was brought to our attention, shortly before the lockdown was placed across the entire Kertan system.”

Kyle clasped his hands, and Benjamin continued.

“When my uncle was killed, all of our attention was focused on his fight.  However, when looking at some of the other readings that came out during that time, we noticed some anomalous energy signatures resonating from a moon in a different part of the system.  The energy fluctuations were minor, but we believe them to be connected to the strange behavior exhibited by some of the fungal creatures.”

Kyle felt his blood run cold as a focused fury built in his chest.  “Are you telling me they were all being controlled from somewhere else?” Kyle asked, ice in his voice.

Benjamin shrugged.  “We can’t be completely certain, one way or another.  What I can tell you is that if there wasn’t so much scrutiny on her and the company, my great grandmother would have already made the trip to investigate personally.  And if it’s a journey you want to make, we would financially support your expedition.”

Kyle felt a wave of caution from C.H.A.D.D., which he promptly ignored.  “Then tell your people to make preparations on that front, too.  If it’s who I think it is, I’m not going to leave that business unfinished.”

“We also suspected it might have been the bureaucrat from your planet.  Looking back over some of the shipping manifests, it’s likely that he found methods to smuggle some of the fungal material onto the shuttle we sent to evacuate your people.  Not that we bear you or the other refugees any ill-will, mind you.  The fault lies solely with him.”

“If DeRosa is behind it, and he’s still alive, I’ll make sure that gets remedied,” Kyle said cooly.  He still had unfinished business with Valentine, but for all his crimes, DeRosa was far, far worse.  The man had sacrificed an untold number of lives in his manic pursuits.  Looking back, leaving DeRosa alive was one of Kyle’s great failings when they’d first gone back to Nierburg.  He was too naïve, too idealistic.  DeRosa had banked on that, and taken full advantage.  Not a mistake I’ll make again, Kyle thought bitterly.

Benjamin regarded Kyle for a moment, seeming to choose his words carefully.  “I see that you’re serious about this.  I’d warn you that it’s not a fight to pick lightly, but you already know that.  I’ll ensure the logistics get arranged after we return.  Skippy and I will be leaving once I’m done archiving the most important documents, certainly before the Tournament of Ascension.  When you get back and settled, we’ll have the beginnings of a plan together.”

“I look forward to it,” Kyle said, forcing himself to calm down as C.H.A.D.D. sent one admonishment after another.

“I know several others in your circle were impacted by him, should they be included?”  Benjamin asked.

Kyle considered for a moment, forcing himself to think through it rationally.  He shouldn’t go alone; he knew that.  While he’d formed some theories about DeRosa’s path, given the man’s ability to bring out a literal army, there were still a lot of unknowns.  Plus, he knew that Tillienne would relish an opportunity to get some closure.  Arhades, though he wasn’t directly impacted, may also find it interesting enough to tag along.  Not to mention, it would give them a chance to talk about the issues with the hodags here on Guancious.

“I’ll reach out to them,” Kyle finally decided.  “I don’t plan to go alone, but I will keep you informed.”

“Then I’ll pass along word when I get back,” Benjamin agreed with a nod.  “That was all I have for you, but you’re welcome to stay here for a while and help me archive, if you’d like.  The work is dreadfully boring alone.”

That statement actually made Kyle do a double take, snapping him out of his angrier thoughts.  “If it’s so boring, why are you doing it?” Kyle asked.

“I owe Duroc that much,” Benjamin replied simply.

Kyle let those words sit.  Benjamin, still the rich scion of a massive industrial operation, felt that he owed his former bodyguard enough to come and do these menial tasks to hedge against the risk of something going wrong.  He’d liked the man before, but this made Benjamin rise a few notches in Kyle’s esteem.  He’s a good man.

“Of course I’ll lend a hand,” Kyle finally said.

Benjamin had a list of topics for archival, sorted by importance.  Getting a couple to Kyle, they worked with amicable chatter, Benjamin sharing stories of some of his travels before coming to Earth, while Kyle talked about his dreams for reconstruction.  The hours passed by, until it was time for Kyle to get some rest and prepare for his deliveries.

“Good luck out there,” Benjamin said as they parted ways.  “I’ll let you know when we leave, and when we’ve got our details sorted for your trip back to the Kertan system.”

“Thank you, Benjamin,” Kyle replied, lightly rapping his knuckles against the other man’s.

“Don’t thank me,” Benjamin said with a twinkle in his eye.  “We’re clearly using you as an instrument of our revenge, given our inability to do it ourselves.”

“And in this case, that’s just fine,” Kyle said with a chuckle, turning to go back to his quarters.  DeRosa would need to wait until he finished dealing with his next deliveries, and learning more about the monster named Mangalica.

Victor DeRosa was having a bad day.  Just another tacked on to a span of over a thousand.  His losses on Kertan Six were massive, but he’d truly thought himself able to rebuild, able to remake his empire into something even greater.  And yet, despite all of his efforts, despite occupying nearly the entire subsurface of this moon, he’d failed.  Completely and utterly, he’d failed.

As another E Grade fungal soldier emerged from the spore Victor had been cultivating, he had to resist the temptation to smash it like a bug for the failure it was.  He’d learned so much from the infestation and ascension of the other humans that he thought it would be easy to replicate the process, using nothing more than the mycelium as a medium.  And yet, despite years of effort, not a single creature born from the fungus alone had been more than E Grade.

Even if his army numbered larger than ever before, it meant nothing if they couldn’t cross into D Grade and beyond.  Of course, the worst part of his failure ran deeper, more personal.

In all this time, he hadn’t gained a single level.  His ascent through early C Grade made him confident in his path and in his power.  He truly believed he was an unparalleled talent; a glorious leader whose empire would span the stars.  Now, he was relegated to simply trying to expand his colony, and keep his presence hidden from the occasional probes from the one creation he regretted making.  With a sigh, Victor shuffled to the next spore.  Maybe this will be the one.
