After Oswald returned from his Animus realm, he felt warmth pressed against his chest. It was something familiar and soft. The kind that soaked into the bones without asking.

Oswald’s eyelids twitched open, and he saw a thin line of silver light carved across the bridge of his nose. Moonlight filtered through the room in narrow slivers, stretching across the wooden floor.

When his eyes opened fully, Vivi was already there. She’d curled herself into a little black loaf on his chest, one tiny paw draped over his collarbone like she owned the place. Good to be back.

Oswald let out a chuckle as his fingers slipped through her fur in slow, quiet strokes. Vivi let out a soft murmur, but didn’t lift her head. Her golden eyes cracked open for the briefest moment, offering him a single, sleepy glance. Then she flicked her tail across his stomach and tucked her chin against his chest again.

“C'mon, we're leaving soon,” he whispered.

A grunt was her only answer. The little weight of her made the room feel less empty.

Oswald stared up at the ceiling, watching shadows shift lazily across the beams. Zvarah’s chains… that was real too.

The image forced its way to the front of his mind: the screech of metal rending open the air, and the smell of burning skin. Still don’t know how she kept her cool through it. Must’ve felt like her bones were cookin’ inside her skin.

Oswald turned his head toward the window. Beyond the parted curtains, the moon hung fat and pale in the sky, its light stretching across rooftops and into the quiet streets. I must've been out for a while.

He sat up slowly, careful not to jostle Vivi too much. She grumbled in protest anyway, paws stretching forward before she climbed up his chest, and then up his shoulder a heartbeat later, her claws careful but firm through the fabric of his shirt.

Oswald swung his legs off the bed and stood. He grabbed his cloak from the chair in the corner, slung it over his shoulders, and gave Vivi a moment to shift her balance before heading toward the door.

He made his way down to the lobby. Behind the front desk, Gilbert hunched over a thick, scuffed ledger. His sleeves were rolled up to his elbows, arms dusted with ink smudges and age spots. The tip of his quill scratched across the yellowing pages in sharp, methodical strokes. His brow furrowed, tongue tucked slightly into the corner of his mouth in quiet concentration.

Across the room, Selene sat in a wide-backed chair upholstered in faded velvet, legs crossed, arms draped loosely over the armrests like she belonged there. Her white cloak spilled over the seat, catching the light in soft folds. She looked perfectly at ease—too still, almost—but her eyes tracked him before her head even moved.

Gilbert was the first to notice him. The old man’s head jerked up, startled just a hair before recognition smoothed his features. “Well now! Evenin’, lad! You’re finally up, eh?”

Oswald nodded, hesitating just past the last step. His gaze bounced from Gilbert to Selene and back again. “Yeah, feelin’ fine now, thanks.”

“Good, good,” Gilbert said, setting the quill gently aside. “You gave us quite the scare, y’know.”

Oswald opened his mouth but came up short. What am I even supposed to say to that? He shuffled his feet, eyes dropping for a beat before Vivi gave a tiny mewl near his ear, the tip of her tail flicking against his cheek like a nudge.

“Didn’t mean to, I… uh. Didn’t think it’d hit me that hard.”

“Reckon it weren’t just a fever dream, was it? Wasn’t sure if you were sleepin’ or driftin’ somewhere else entirely.”

Yeah. Somethin’ like that... Oswald didn’t answer, just gave a half-shrug and looked over at Selene again.

Her gaze met his then, slow and steady. She hadn’t moved, hadn’t shifted an inch, but something about her posture had changed, barely noticeable. Like a hawk adjusting its wings mid-perch.

“You walk quietly, unusually so. Especially for someone your age.” Selene said.

Oswald shrugged. “I didn’t wanna wake anyone, didn’t think you’d still be here, to be honest.”

Selene tilted her head, just a fraction, at his response. “I returned from my investigation some time ago. But I chose to wait until you were awake to show you this.”

From the folds of her cloak, she drew out a small slip of paper, folded with care. Her fingers held it delicately, suspended between her thumb and forefinger as if the note carried weight beyond its size. “It was delivered earlier this evening, addressed to you. Gilbert was uncertain how to proceed and entrusted it to me instead.”

Oswald reached out, his hand brushing against hers as he took it. The paper itself felt cold, he corners had been bent, no doubt from whoever had carried it, and a faint scent of ink and something sterile clung to it, something like alcohol or old parchment. Weird, wonder who sent this?

He unfolded the note slowly, eyes narrowing as he took in the writing. The penmanship was sharp, but the pressure was uneven, some letters darker than others. It had been written in haste, no matter how careful the hand behind it.

Oswald,

There is a matter of great urgency I must discuss with you. Please come to my estate at your earliest convenience. The situation is pressing, and I would prefer to speak in person.

—Doctor Cecilia

He stared at the last line. Then, he folded the note again, before slipping it into the inside pocket of his cloak. "Did you see the doctor while I was out?"

Selene gave a single nod. “I did… which is precisely why I must ask you, Oswald, do you trust her?”

The question caught him off-guard. He blinked, then looked past her for a moment.

“I guess? She’s helped a lot of people around here, always workin’, always tired... like she’s carryin’ too much and won’t say a word about it.”

“In that case, there’s something you need to see urgently."

Selene turned without waiting for a response, her cloak sweeping behind her as she walked toward the front door. Oswald hesitated, throwing a quick glance toward Vivi.

The cat let out a low rumble, still perched on his shoulder. Her tail tapped against his back, reluctant.

Selene paused at the door and looked over her shoulder. “Leave her with Gilbert for now. Where we’re going, it’s safer that way.”

“She’s not exactly fond of bein’ left behind.”

“This isn’t a matter of fondness."

Vivi mewled again, but didn’t claw or bite. She just gave him a look. If cats could pout, that’s what she was doing.

Oswald let out a quiet sigh, the sound escaping more in breath than voice. His fingers moved to the scruff of Vivi’s neck. “Sorry, Vivi, you’ll keep the desk warm for me, yeah?”

Gilbert stepped forward without a word, arms already outstretched. “Hand ’er here, she’s got more claim to that counter than I do.”

Oswald chuckled and carefully passed her over, their hands brushing briefly in the exchange. Vivi’s golden eyes stared up at him with quiet betrayal.

“She’s gonna hold this against me, isn’t she?”

Gilbert let out a low laugh as he nestled her onto the ledger. “Aye, lad. Cats don’t forget indignities like bein’ moved.”

Vivi didn’t resist. With a pointed huff, she turned a full circle atop the old book, then flopped down in a dramatic curl. Her tail swished once more before she tucked her nose beneath it, but her gaze followed Oswald the entire time.

“Thanks,” Oswald muttered, pulling his cloak tighter around his shoulders. The fabric tugged at his collar as he fastened it beneath his chin, the lining still faintly warm from sleep.

Gilbert gave him a nod. “Watch yourself out there, lad. Air’s gone sharp.”

Oswald offered a faint smile in return. He turned back toward the doorway. Selene had already stepped outside.

The door hung half-open, letting in a draft that crawled along the floorboards like a creeping tide. Moonlight spilled across the area, catching on the edge of her cloak as it fluttered in the wind.

He followed, the night air biting at his skin. The moon loomed above them. Looks like its a full moon tonight.

Selene walked ahead without a word. The air grew colder as they left the open street behind. She turned into a narrow passage between two old storehouses, the kind long forgotten by everyone.

Oswald followed close behind, brushing against a slick stone wall. The space narrowed quickly, just wide enough for two people, if they didn’t mind being uncomfortable. Crates lined the sides, some cracked with rot, others stacked high and leaning under their own weight. Moss clung to their corners, and the faint drip of water echoed from somewhere deeper in the alley.

Selene knelt beside a squat crate wedged into the corner. Her fingers slipped underneath the wood with a practiced ease, and after a moment, she pulled something out: a small bundle wrapped in cloth, no larger than a loaf of bread.

She stood again, unwrapping it slowly. Oswald stepped closer to get a better look.

Selena held out a glass bottle, the kind you'd find on a kitchen shelf. But as he squinted his eyes, a thin red film clung to the inside of the glass, subtle until the light hit it just right. At the bottom, a darker ring had settled: thick, clotted like syrup left out too long.

The smell hit him before his eyes fully registered it. His nose wrinkled. “That smells weird, what is it?”

“It’s blood, Oswald. There was certainly an effort to disguise the scent, but I would recognise it anywhere.” Selene said.

Blood? He stared at the bottle, something cold settling beneath his ribs. “Where’d you find this?”

“I didn’t, one of the nurses from Doctor Cecilia’s household discarded it. Tossed it into a waste cart. I caught the scent while passing through an alley nearby.”

“But it just looks just like any regular bottle.”

“That was probably the idea.”

Oswald leaned in, sniffing again. The smell wasn’t fresh, it didn’t hit like a nosebleed or fresh meat. It was older, settled. But it clung to the glass like smoke in a closed room. “Wouldn’t people smell that?”

“Not unless they had our senses, to a normal person, it would resemble a strong wine, perhaps something a little past its prime.” Selene said.

She set the bottle back on the crate with care. Her eyes shifted to him, weighing his reaction. “You understand what this implies, don’t you?”

Oswald exhaled heavily. “You think the doctor’s feedin’ on people?”

“I am saying it is possible,” Selene replied..

“But I’ve seen her help a lot of people here in Volstarn."

“That doesn’t mean she isn’t hiding something."

Oswald stared at the bottle for a moment. “But… she didn’t look like a vampire."

“Purebloods rarely do, not on the surface. Their bodies are built to pass. No fangs unless they will it. No pale skin unless starved. Even their heartbeat can mimic ours when necessary.”

Oswald looked down, his thoughts churning. I never even questioned it. She’s always exhausted, always surrounded by the sick… but maybe that’s exactly what she needs.

“She saved people,” he said quietly, more to himself than to Selene.

“She may have, but at what cost?” Selene asked.

“You really think she's a vampire? Should I even bother seeing her then?"

Selene didn’t answer right away. Her eyes remained on the alley’s far wall, the moonlight casting pale silver across her cheek. Then, slowly, she turned back to him.

“Has she ever invited you to her house like this before?”

“Nope, this is the first time. Last time I saw her was to finish up on that quest."

“Then it’s worth considering that she may have discovered what you did.”

“What I did?”

“She may be aware that you’ve slain one of her kind, like that vampire you encountered in the cave."

The words hit like a stone in Oswald's gut. “You think she’s tryin’ to… what, lure me in and finish me off?”

“There’s a chance.".

“Still, that’s a big leap.”

She gave a faint nod. “I know that I might be wrong. But the possibility is there. And if she is what I think she is… she wouldn’t risk drawing attention unless she felt threatened.”

And if she sees me as a threat… Oswald swallowed hard.

He looked up again, meeting Selene's gaze. “You’ll come with me, then?”

“Yes. I’ll come with you, just in case.”

Oswald nodded once, tension easing just a bit. But before he could speak, something shifted in the air.

It struck like a ripple through stone, an invisible force pressing against his chest, cold and heavy, as if the night itself had inhaled. Then came another pulse. Then three more. Each one hit harder than the last, like icy hands crawling down his spine.

Oswald flinched. What the—?

Selene’s cloak stirred behind her, though no wind touched them. Her expression hardened.

The ground felt wrong beneath Oswald’s boots. The ground had a sickly give to it, like it had been fed something it wasn’t meant to hold.

“You feel that?” he muttered.

“Yes,” Selene said sharply. “Draw your sword. Now.”

He didn’t need telling twice. Oswald took a quick step back and reached for Zareth. The moment his fingers closed around the hilt, the sword pulled free with a low hum.

Cold night air rippled around the blade as flames surged across its length. Heat flared against his face, then, the shadows moved.

Shapes detached from the darkness ahead. One by one, cloaked figures stepped into the open, boots whispering against the earth. Think that's ten of 'em.

Black cloaks hung from their shoulders, hoods pulled low over their faces. They advanced like wolves that already knew the distance to their prey.

Oswald’s heart kicked harder. His eye darted across their forms, hand tightening on the grip of Zareth. I know those faces…

The firelight flickered across them, and his stomach twisted. Beneath those hoods were nurses from Cecilia’s clinic. He remembered their faces vaguely, but it was clear that they weren’t the same anymore.

Their eyes glowed bright. Some shimmered red. Others burned gold or violet, lit from within like lanterns in the dark. Their skin had drained of color, turned pale as pressed bone, smooth and lifeless.

One woman raised her arm, sleeve falling back to reveal a dagger. Another cocked a crossbow with a soft click, the bolt already drawn tight. Others flexed their hands, fingers curling like claws.

They held weapons. But they didn’t need them.

“Careful, the full moon strengthens them." Selene warned.

Oswald gave a tight nod. “Got it.”

But the truth gnawed at his spine. Ten of them. And every one of ’em looks like they could gut me before I could swing twice.

One stepped forward. She reached up, lowering her hood. Long brown hair spilled over her shoulder in a neat braid.

Her face looked untouched by the night, almost gentle. But her eyes blazed gold. “I am Marla, and I'm here to inform you that the Doctor wishes to speak with the boy. I believe he should have already received her note.”

Selene’s hand shifted toward her sword, but she didn’t draw. Not yet. “Why?”

Marla smiled. No warmth touched it. “I’m not here to answer your questions. The boy may go to the Doctor's estate willingly, or else we will have to take him there."

Oswald exhaled through his nose. “You’ll let me go if I go see Doctor Cecilia?”

“Yes, no harm will come to you, not unless you make it necessary.” Marla said.

Oswald paused and thought deeply. He knew that the Doctor was probably up to no good. And all of the nurses here didn't look friendly. If he were to leave and see Doctor Cecilia, he'd be leaving Selene alone to deal with all of these vampires.

“Hey, you gonna be alright if I go?” Oswald asked, looking to Selene.

Selene didn’t look at him right away. Her violet eyes stayed locked on Marla, her expression unreadable. “You needn’t concern yourself with me. It’s your life that’s in danger tonight, not mine.”

“But Selene, do you trust me?”

That question made her pause. The legendary adventurer's eyes flicked toward him now, really looking. Something passed behind them, not doubt exactly, but weight. She didn’t answer right away. “I want to trust you, Oswald, but I’m not yet certain if that would be wise.”

He met her gaze for a moment longer, then turned to face Marla.“Doctor really only wanna see me?”

Marla nodded once. “She has no business with the swordswoman.”

Oswald looked back at Selene and gave her a half-shrug. “You heard her.”

A cold breeze slipped through the narrow alley, curling around Oswald’s ankles and tugging at his cloak. His fingers still hovered near Zareth’s hilt, the heat from the blade fading fast, leaving only the ghost of warmth against his palm.

Selene’s voice broke through the quiet, soft but edged with something that clung to the air.

“Oswald… why are you so certain about this?”

Her words didn’t challenge him outright. They weighed more than that, like she already knew the answer and just wanted to hear him say it aloud.

Oswald let out a slow breath. “’Cause I know I gotta fight my own battles sooner or later, and if you really thought I didn’t stand a chance, then you would have put a lot more effort into stoppin’ me.”

For a moment, neither of them moved. Then, Selene's features softened, and the faintest smile touched her lips. “I’ll come find you as soon as I’m able.”

Oswald gave her a short nod, holding her gaze a second longer before turning toward the vampires. Just stay cool.

“I’ll go, take me to the doctor.”
