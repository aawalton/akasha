Chapter 139: Graduation

Jack took a final look in the mirror in his dress uniform, deciding he was sharper than ever despite the lack of an appropriate haircut. But he’d already decided he didn’t want one, as one little remnant of his old, ‘rebellious’ life before.

His eyes drifted to the medalwork at his left breast, and he sobered several degrees. It dredged up some of the worst of his memories, after all — especially the ‘premier’ item, the golden star with three swords inset on the silver wings of Babylon. ‘The Trinity Medal,’ as it was called, was the highest medal achievable. The swords stood for glory, honor, and sacrifice. The largest sword was representative of the latter.

You damn sure got that right, Memoria.

He grimaced a bit, remembering that detail, and how Mini had lectured him about having to wear the medal in the first place, even if it was, indeed, a sacrifice for him, in the petty mentality he had been in at the time.

Mini was kind enough not to rub it in a little more, right then, for which Jack was grateful.

He hurried out the door, but hesitated to take one last look at his quarters behind him. It really was the last, too — hyper-efficient movers would soon come to transplant it all to a new place, symbolic of a sudden transformation, perhaps, because boy, did the Agentus love their symbolic gestures. He was no longer a Junior Agent going forward, no longer a cadet, and couldn’t stay there even one minute after obtaining the jacket. Stepping foot in the hall as a whole again, it would be as a full Memorial Agent Nonpareil, a person of authority.

Jack took a breath and nodded to the place, as if in thanks. Maybe it was silly, but it had seen him through an incredible transition, and he’d truly loved his short time among the students, full of mostly fond memories. He couldn’t think of a place that compared to that. Most gave him more mixed feelings.

He closed the door and strolled away, telling himself he’d still be among them. After all, the mess hall was for anybody who had obtained access and wanted to eat there.

But he knew deep down, it wouldn’t be the same.

A moment later, he smiled, because he was imagining how his friends would soon look in their best uniforms, proud and relieved in measures he couldn’t even begin to fathom, compared to him.

More than anything, he was proud he’d helped them get there.

Being processed into Command HQ in Central Processing was certainly a process. He was rather embarrassingly greeted with “Ah, the final straggler” by a typical secretary in a standard-looking waiting room, before being hustled and shuffled off to another inner elevator that required a supervised scan before entry. It was at least less of a hassle than getting into Containment.

‘Down’ an elevator he could barely register as going anywhere at all, he was finally dumped off in a space that looked like an oversized hallway, with a secure airlock door at the end. All the other graduates were here, mingling idly in their full military dress uniforms, most of which didn’t have much on them from what he could tell.

Great. I’m the sore thumb.

Most, though not all, looked up at his entrance, and many waved at him — including Délight — so we waved back amiably. Naturally, Bo and Highfive saw him and immediately rushed over.

“Finally!” Bo called, shaking her head at him.

“Bro!” Highfive called excitedly as he nearly ran over, looking like he was going to scoop Jack up into one of his ridiculous hugs again. “We’re all here! It’s happening!”

Jack forestalled Highfive with thrust-out open hands. “Yeah, yeah, it’s awesome, don’t wrinkle my uniform, dude!” This elicited bursts of laughter from many in attendance.

Highfive shrugged as he stopped short, grinning widely. “No problem, bro! I wouldn’t!”

Meanwhile, Bo had stopped and was staring wide-eyed, slack-jawed at the medal on Jack’s breast, amidst the other varied marks. “Th-that’s…” Her hand went up to her mouth.

Others had taken notice, too, and were whispering loudly about it. He heard ‘Trinity Medal’ muttered multiple times within a few seconds, and more and more of the graduates were turning and looking, in some cases craning their necks around to see it.

Highfive was perhaps the last to see, despite being one of the closest. Hands on his hips, he looked over at Bo and frowned, then looked back to where her eyes were. Highfive’s expression became a giantized twin to hers as his jaw dropped. “Holy apeshit.” He pointed and called, “That’s the Trinity Medal of Supreme Glory, Honor, and Sacrifice!”

Amused or sarcastic responses followed from the others, including at least one ‘duh.’

Jack winced, feeling his neck burning from the attention. Nonetheless, he shrugged and nodded, as he was helplessly caught. “Yeeep.”

Suddenly, other graduates hurried over and crowded around, staring, gasping, and so on, and multiple queries about how he got it were thrown out.

Jack just smiled and gave the typical, canned response he was used to. “If I told you how I got it, I’d have to kill you.”

Amidst the titters, a smiling Délight replied, “I’m pretty sure we have the clearance, now, Long and Gorgeous.” Snickers answered this nickname, which probably sounded like a pet name… and probably was a pet name in her imagination, even though they were not an item.

Jack suppressed a groan. Did she really have to drop that in public?!

Highfive thankfully broke that tension a bit by looking down at her sternly with his brows knitted. “Hey! You can’t just migrate a name like that from your ex to another guy!”

Délight looked up at him and rolled her eyes. “Don’t be a dummy — I just called you ‘tall drink,’ and still do.”

“Tallest Drink, you mean.”

“Mmn. I did climb you like a tree, didn’t I?” She looked him up and down, which made Highfive do a slow blink.

“Look, it’s not a big deal,” Jack said as he presented his hands in a calming gesture and swept his eyes around. “If you all had extra years in the field, there’d be more of these in this room, I promise you.”

“I don’t know about that, bro,” Highfive said, shaking his head and smiling at Jack with what could only be brotherly pride. “That’s a pretty rare one. Not everyone can be a true hero with proven sacrifice on their breast. That’s the key, they say. Accomplishment, right conduct in crisis, and suffering to do it.”

Jack winced yet again and followed it with a sigh. “It’s what we all train for. Each and every one of us would’ve done the same, if not more.”

“I seriously doubt it,” Bo said.

Highfive opened his mouth, but just then, the airlock made a hiss, and most of those crowded around turned to look.

A man with the fancy-brimmed hat of a Central Processing official appeared behind the airlock, with the exit behind him open. He gestured with a hand. “Let’s go, Agentus.”

Response from the former cadets was immediate. Once everyone started filing through the airlock, he turned around and led them through, for the most part, single-file.

The wide, crowded area they entered could only be Central Command. It was stacked with button-covered consoles, screens, holographic displays, and interactive maps. It reminded him very much of a sci-fi starship bridge — a very militaristic one. Central Processing personnel were everywhere, most of them quiet and watching, though a few whispers propagated here and there.

They walked on the edge of the ground floor level, in a space convenient for everyone to see. There was another level, reached by twin curling steps, with a balcony overlook that curled itself around to give many vantage points. It had fewer people, and they were obviously high command. The most central of these spaces seemed to have three high seats, though only two were there at the moment, both standing at the balcony: Boss Lady Memoria and the one known as General Mask, in the same silver mask as before, holding his cane in one hand.

He’s the other high official? Shit.

Jack was a bit late to notice something Highfive pointed out with a finger, and was drawing awed whispers — above the heads of high command, blue longcoats floated as if in midair, each of different sizes.

Highfive could be heard to whisper with shining eyes and a big grin, “That one’s mine,” eyeing what was indeed the largest of the jackets on display.

“Someone has a sense of humor,” Bo muttered, eyeing the ones next to it on either side, which were so much smaller it was like the big one was flanked by its children.

“Clearly,” Délight said wryly.

Jack suppressed a laugh. They were both the rather obvious owners.

Though they weren’t marched to their spots, the graduates were more or less naturally led to face the great, imposing mass of people, evidently stopped in the middle of the progress of their daily jobs. Naturally, they were probably still doing things in their head or through interfaces.

The show must go on, after all.

Their guide faced them from the side and called, “Atten-SHUN!” which caused everyone to straighten out and adopt a precise posture. Because they were before high officers when the call went out, they all saluted, too, per convention.

Interestingly, only General Mask returned it, in what seemed a rather automatic response. He then called, “At ease,” in an old, gravely voice suited for battlefields and grinding granite.

As everyone shifted and put their hands behind their backs, there was a pervasive silence for a spell. It felt like everyone was waiting on the old man.

General Mask didn’t even seem to be looking at them as he finally began, “The speech I give here and now has never changed, focused on one, singular subject: the jacket. I was the first to wear it and the one to define it. To earn it, to wear it, is not to serve any creed, organization, or government. It doesn’t exist to glorify any nation or people, nor to prove individual superiority. It does not exist to serve an alien adoptee.” He held up his hand slowly, sticking out his pointer finger. “It’s dedication to one, singular thing: survival.”

He let that settle in the silence to follow as his hand dropped. “We’re all a sacrifice on the altar of that principle. We are at war in a foreign wilderness made out of our home, and until that war is ended, no other principle can be served. Don’t let the shine of our pretty technologies and cultured sophistications convince you we don’t remain in the Stone Ages, that we’re not primates with our fangs bared with blood beneath our claws. If you want a future out of this hole we dug, then mind your purpose foremost in the path ahead of you. Be the Age of Mankind that you’re in.”

Finally, he seemed to look over them, from one side to the other. “You must be asking yourselves, ‘Who the hell is this?’ Who isn’t relevant, but what is. We’ll deal with that after the ceremony, because this is about you and the jackets. Suffice to say, Memoria and I are the highest authorities. Period.”

That bomb dropped, the man looked over at an official to the side and nodded. The official did a salute, then went down the steps, pointing at the graduate closest, at the end, and insistently waving them up, as he called, “A graduating junior agent cadet ascends!”

Applause rang out, and the former cadet was brought up to receive quick, quiet words from Memoria and General Mask, as one of the jackets floated down. A smiling Memoria took the jacket and placed it around the young man’s shoulders before helping his arm through one sleeve, and General Mask completed the jacketing with the other arm coming through, after which he grabbed the graduate by the lapels, straightened him up, and patted his shoulder roughly. Finally, he shook hands with both of them.

With that, the grinning graduate was beckoned over to the stairs on the other side, where another official called out, “An Agent Nonpareil descends!”

More applause rang out, now a bit more cheerful.

The process was repeated down the line. Bo was before Jack, and Highfive after. Highfive looked ecstatic and pumped as it got closer to their moment. He leaned over to Jack and whispered, “I can’t believe this is happening, bro! Can you believe this is happening, bro?! It’s happening!”

Jack nodded and leaned over to whisper, “I’m also looking forward to whatever our masked supreme leader here has to say.”

Meanwhile, when Jack glanced over at Bo, she looked like a nervous wreck, eyes already red with barely restrained emotion as she watched proceedings. He suddenly wished that Lighthouse was here for her again.

Nonetheless, Jack put a hand on her shoulder and squeezed supportively, as he leaned in and whispered, “Hey, you got this! It’s a happy moment! Smile through it.”

To his surprise, Bo didn’t give an acrid quip. She glanced at him, nodded, swallowed, and tried to smile. “I know… it is. It is.” She took a deep, steadying breath.

When it was time for Bo to go up, though, she immediately started sobbing as the applause rang out. Jack and Highfive let out supportive whoops and cheers that got them a stern look from their guide. Meanwhile, Memoria glanced at them and beamed with warm amusement.

Her face a wreck, Bo got through the process, barely keeping standing as she unsuccessfully tried to hold in her sobs. Memoria was thankfully very sympathetic, holding her and whispering encouragement in her ear. General Mask just seemed awkward, and handled his part with particular gentleness compared to the others. He did lean down and say something to her that had her nodding emphatically.

A red-faced Bo rushed down the stairs in her new jacket to riotous applause and cheers from the whole command center.

Jack couldn’t wait for her, as he had to go up, but he saw Bo rush into Highfive’s arms, who leaned down to embrace and hold her consolingly.

Only a glance, and he had to make his own ascent up the curving stairs. The official above called, “A graduating Junior Agent cadet ascends!” It was music to his ears, as was the applause and scattered cheers he heard, with Highfive and Délight being the loudest.

Each step had him feeling more elated, until he was there on the overlook, approaching a beaming, clapping Memoria and the foreboding presence of the masked honcho, the blue jacket that would be his floating down above them like a gift from heaven.

Even today, I really can’t believe this thing is mine.

Without really thinking about it, Jack was feeling so good that he went right for a hug with Memoria, who received it with a pleased, nonverbal noise and hugged him back tightly. It was quick, though, because the jacket was coming down. But the Archon of Humanity did whisper to him, “You earned this, Jack, and don’t ever believe otherwise — that’s an order!”

He chuckled and nodded to this as Memoria, with glee plain on her face to do so, put the blue jacket around his shoulders and helped him get the first sleeve on. As his hand came through, she said, “I’m proud of you, Jack! Well done.”

General Mask helped Jack move his other arm through, and then the jacket was on. His lapels were grabbed, and he was nearly lifted as the jacket was straightened up, in a display of easy strength. As the masked man patted his shoulder, Jack found himself forgetting the jacket and once again getting struck by that sense of being caught in a terribly powerful gravity well. Now, it was somehow both better and worse. It felt more… controlled, restrained, held back.

Nonetheless, when their eyes met, he felt tremendous awe, albeit just a smidge reduced from last time. Within was a mass of unyielding, cold steel; a sense of tremendous, withheld violence bound in a construct.

Q-Loth could be ‘felt’ paying attention again, but this time, it was more like curiosity. It didn’t react like before, which might’ve been whatever the equivalent of shock was to that entity. It was controlling itself, perhaps for Jack’s sake.

Time seemed to skip a beat as he found himself shaking the man’s hand, and the affinity, the wavelength synchronization of that gravity intensified. He felt practically absorbed by it. And finally, his innate fear, his instinctive biological terror at the feeling abated, because he suddenly comprehended that he was safely lodged there. He was not entirely absorbed, just… magnetized and attached. Utterly protected directly under the wing of a titan.

Jack was less a random rock around a planet, but more like a jet on a carrier amidst a fleet of carriers. He was right where he belonged. For now, anyway.

This was, truly, the champion of humanity before him.

His shake might’ve gone on a little too long, and he was suddenly aware he was clenching the man’s hand hard, to little avail. If the man even noticed this, he didn’t let on.

Unable to help himself, Jack blurted, “What are your powers? Sir. If I may ask…”

Memoria threw her head back and laughed uproariously at this.

General Mask might’ve huffed in response, as finally his hand pulled away. Exasperated, he replied, “No, you may not ask, Agent. Now shake your Archon’s hand and get moving so the next in line can graduate. Unless you think we don’t all have jobs to get back to?”

“No, sir,” Jack said automatically, clearing his throat. “Right away, sir.”

Damn. I guess I wasted my scarce chance of words with him. Would’ve been pure gold if he’d answered, though!

Jack shook a still-amused Memoria’s hand and hurried around to the other side of the stairs. The official there declared loudly, “An Agent Nonpareil descends!” Applause erupted again.

Jack, smiling, moved through a row of other jacketed and non-jacketed graduates, shaking hands as he moved toward Bo. Délight was, of course, not satisfied with a mere shake and went for a hug as she murmured her congratulations.

Highfive had to move up, but he met Jack’s eyes, pointed, and made a thumbs-up as he scooted over to the stairs. Jack returned the gesture.

Bo looked like a bit of a mess but was calmer as he moved to hug her, both of them smiling. Jack muttered, “Congratulations, Dark Nugget.”

Bo huffed and said, “Right back at you, Cowboy.”

They mutually turned to see Highfive climbing the stairs, pumping his fists, very much making it into a smug victory lap. He never lost his big, ecstatic grin through the whole process. His words were brief and just as happy, whatever they were.

When he was descending, sporting his new jacket, he grabbed it from either side and made a show of sashaying ridiculously down the steps, calling, “Ohhh yeah, baby, we graduated! Wooo!”

Some tried to shush him, but peals of laughter drowned it out.

Jack had to endure being lifted off his feet by the giant yet again, as did Bo, who was protesting even before he did it, shaking her head in warning, emphatically declaring, “No, Fiver, no Fiver, not me, don’t pick me up, no, Fiver, don’t you dare, no- Fiveeeer!”

With their rowdiness, they weren’t exactly popular with the officials in charge, but all that resulted was baleful frowns and calls for quiet. Meanwhile, the rest of the place was entertained, many of the crew smiling over at them indulgently.

Délight was next up, and she seemed fairly solemn through most of it. Memoria whispered things in her ear more than anyone prior, to which Délight mostly nodded along. Her lip quivered a little at one point, and her eyes watered, but she didn’t get to the point of sobs. Finally, Memoria hugged her, and the process went on. As Délight came down, though, she smiled big and even mimicked what Highfive did in his descent, which got quite an uptick in applause and cheers.

So it went. Former cadet after former cadet got their jacket, until the last long coat was on the newest Memorial Agent, and the ceremony was complete.

General Mask looked down at them and declared, “There’s just one last matter to address, so listen up.”
