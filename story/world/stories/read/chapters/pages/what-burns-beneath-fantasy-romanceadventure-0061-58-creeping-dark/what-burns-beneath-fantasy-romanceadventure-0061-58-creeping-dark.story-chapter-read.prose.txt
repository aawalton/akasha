I spent a long moment just staring at the wall.

Renner’s still planning to come north with me. Even after I nearly got him killed. Even though I’ve been lying to him this entire time.

I suppose he still wants to tell the Order everything and get his favor. Which is fine, of course; if anyone can help me get out of my pact, it’ll be the paladins. And maybe he can help me explain everything; that I never wanted to make a deal, that I want to be free and I’ve never hurt anyone.

I wonder if they’ll still lock me up. Or, saints, if they’ll do something worse… but I don’t need to think of that right now. Right now I just need to get through the night. Then tomorrow we’ll all set out and perhaps things will be better, now.

Teela would be her exuberant, cheerful self. Royce would be an unshakable source of humor and warmth. And Renner…

He’s hated me this whole time. Not that I blame him, really. But, now…

Now, I found myself wondering if things were different.

He’d held me. Out in the woods, after apologizing for his callousness, while I’d cried and laughed and babbled nonsense. Salt, I could still feel the way one arm had curled around my waist. Had pulled me close, firm and strong and gentle all at once. The sensation lingered even still.

I practically threw myself at him. No, not practically; I literally threw myself at him! He was probably caught off-guard, and of course he was exhausted and feeling sorry for me and he’d hit his head, he might not have been thinking clearly and… and of course it didn’t mean anything.

Even so, the memory did not fade. Rustling leaves and the creak of swaying pine boughs, the feel of hard muscle at my waist and rough stubble against my cheek. He’d smelled like sweat, smoke, and iron, a testament to the nightmare we’d gone through together. One he’d walked into willingly, rather than watch Agnes Green slit my throat. And underneath that… warm, woodsy scents. Familiar things. Pine, earth, and leather. He’d felt safe and solid and… My heart gave a little flutter.

“What was that about?” Royce’s mutter interrupted my much-too-vivid thoughts. He thumped into the hallway behind me as the bedroom door creaked shut.

I dropped to my knees, looking studiously downwards. My face burned as I tugged my boots back on. “What do you mean?”

“Just… who exactly is that guy?”

“Renner?” I rose slowly, drawing a deep breath and chasing away the remnants of my blush. “I met him a few days south. He promised to get me to Pinnacle.” Perhaps it wasn’t the full story, but it would have to suffice as an explanation. Especially since, at the moment, I wasn’t quite sure of my prickly savior’s motivations.

“So he’s a guard for hire? A mercenary?”

“Something like that.” I suppose it’s close enough to the truth- not that I’m even fully sure what the truth is. I hardly know a thing about him.

Royce cast a frown towards the closed door. “How are you paying him? He doesn’t seem like a, uh, ‘out of the goodness of his heart’ type of person. And last I checked, you don’t exactly have coins spilling out of your pockets.”

I fidgeted. The fortune currently nestled in my pack was an odd thing to feel embarrassed about, but embarrassment came nonetheless.

“Brin?”

“I’m not paying him. Not… exactly. Although, on the subject of coins, there’s… look, it’s not a big deal, it’s really not, but…” I tugged open the drawstring of my bag, rummaged until I felt familiar calfskin, and tilted the opening towards my friend. Gold glinted from within the fabric.

Royce’s eyes grew to the size of saucers.

“It’s not a big deal,” I mumbled again, heat creeping up my throat.

He sifted one hand through the pile of coins and let out a long, low whistle. “Where in the realms…”

“My mom.” I offered a shy grin. “She told Durst to keep it tucked away until the other night. And now I’m kind of-”

“Rich?” he choked out, giving a breathy little chuckle.

The blush deepened. “Kind of.”

“Wow. Guess I didn’t need to try and pay for dinner, huh?”

“I’ve, uh, got our expenses covered. Indefinitely.”

We pattered down the stairs and into an empty common room. There was no noise save for the occasional crackle from the fireplace.

My attention drifted towards the front window. It looked out onto flat gray stones and brittle grass beneath a darkening sky.

Nearly nighttime. And then… and then.

One hand balled into a fist. “Royce, I… I actually have an errand I need to run.”

“An errand? It’s nearly sunset.”

“I know.” I swallowed and, before I could lose my nerve, hurried towards the door. Trembling fingers lifted, affixing the clasp of my borrowed cloak. “I need to do it before I… go to bed.”

“Oh.” He seemed uncertain for a moment. Then he strode forward, aiming a smile downwards. It didn’t quite reach his eyes. “I’ll go with you.”

The sky was a swirl of murky purple and hazy gray as we bustled along. The evening air was crisp, promising a night’s frost, and every breath carried a chill down my throat.

We saw very few other people until we reached the town square. And then we saw what appeared to be most of the town, gathered with cloaks drawn and hoods lowered and flickering candles held in every hand. They stood around a yellow-shuttered cottage with a chipped porch and wilting flowers potted on each sill.

A familiar, stooped figure stood on the porch. Brother Pel’s face was drawn and his soft, lisping voice was unintelligible from our distance.

I could guess well enough what he was saying.

“Looks like a funeral,” Royce murmured, pausing to observe. He turned to me, an unspoken question on his lips.

I hadn’t seen what happened to Terrence Black. Renner had made sure of that, and I’d probably never be able to thank him enough for that mercy. But I’d heard it. Every bit. Every scream and sob, the smells of iron and rot, the wet rip of flesh and the hag’s wicked laugh and the dull thump when his body fell and all of it swallowed me now, filling my head and drowning out-

“Brin?” A heavy hand closed around mine.

I jerked back, biting down a scream. It was her hand, huge and rough, wrapping around me. Grinding me into earth as smoke filled my lungs. My ribs groaned beneath her weight. I wasn’t strong enough to push her off and her other hand covered my face, blocking every scrap of light, and her head drew down to-

“You’re shaking.”

Blue eyes. Clear. Worried. The hand was gentle as it smoothed my hair back. Warm fingers traced the curve of my brow.

I closed my eyes. Cold air filled my nose, my throat, my lungs.

It was warm. The cave was warm. Not cold. Not like this. Hot and thick and smoky and it smelled like… like… it wasn’t like this. I’m not there. I made it out. We all made it out.

The creeping dread had been easier to chase away in the light of day. Fresh from my dream of sunlight and sanctuary, I’d felt strong. Brave. And inside the inn, surrounded by flickering firelight and the faces of friends, things had been alright. The dark hadn’t swallowed me. The memories had gripped, but not gouged.

Now, though…

Every shadow was too deep. Night was falling too fast. The town was too quiet, too still, too little to shield against the monsters.

I needed more than jests and laughing faces and the warm hands of friends. I needed hope.

I blinked away stinging, too-hot tears and forced my feet forwards. My heartbeat drummed, breath coming too-fast, every pant sending out little puffs of gray fog. The shadows along the street seemed to grow with every step. Their edges sharpened, stretching out along the stones, like jagged black claws ready to grasp my feet and drag me screaming into the night.
