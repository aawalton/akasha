Epilogue 1: Prince Aric

Prince Aric patrolled the city with Sir Deren as he did most mornings. The pair moved through the slums and the poorer districts, accompanied by a small honor guard, their armor and noble insignia enough to clear entire streets before them.

People noticed them coming long before they arrived. Merchants bowed their heads behind their stalls while shoppers disappeared into side alleys, mothers called children indoors, and even beggars shuffled away whenever possible.

Aric watched it happen for the hundredth time and still didn't understand it. Even the criminals weren't feared nearly as much.

A few weeks ago, one of his honor guards suggested that perhaps the common people were afraid because they all had something to hide.

Deren had laughed at the notion.

"If every single commoner is a criminal," he had said, "then perhaps we're the real criminals."

The comment had been intended as a joke. Unfortunately, Aric had never been able to stop thinking about it.

Today was no different. As they crossed a narrow street between two decaying buildings, Aric spotted a young boy digging through a pile of refuse. The child couldn't have been older than twelve. For a moment, he reminded Aric of Lyth, his younger brother.

The prince slowed his pace and approached.

"Excuse me," he began.

The boy looked up, and the moment he saw their fancy armor, the child abandoned the trash pile and fled as though a monster had appeared behind him.

Aric watched him vanish into the darkness.

He hadn't even managed to ask a question.

"What do you think he was looking for?" Aric asked quietly.

"Food, probably," Deren answered.

Aric looked back at the pile of rubbish.

Food. That's what the world had come to: children looking for food in the trash...

He couldn't help comparing the world around him to the world described in the ancient texts he had been reading in the royal library.

After the first travel log, the new library caretaker kept finding books about Almara and the Lost Age of Magic.

Back then, nobody should have needed to search through garbage for food. Entire fields could supposedly be cultivated by a single mage; Tier Eight spells were said to make crops sprout and mature in moments.

Not only that, but cities floated through the sky, massive magical teleportation gates powered transportation networks spanning continents, magical communication enchantments connected vast distances, and healing miracles could cure diseases that modern healers could not.

Most scholars dismissed half those stories as exaggeration.

Aric wasn't so certain. The library caretaker had found twelve different records, half of them translated from across the world.

An incredibly advanced civilization must have existed. Then the city of Almara fell. With it gone, the greatest magical civilization in history collapsed, and somehow magic itself became weaker afterward.

Modern archmages struggled to cast Tier Five spells without generating enough mana disturbance to make further spellcasting impossible for weeks. Anything stronger would attract immediate attention from the Mage Guild.

He remembered reading a report about a rogue mage casting a weather spell to stop the snow during a particularly terrible winter in one of the northern kingdoms. The snow stopped, but every runic enchantment in the region stopped functioning as well, including the runic heating plates, causing thousands of deaths from freezing.

Magic was restricted for a reason. Cantrips and Tier One spells were one thing, but even as a royal, he needed permission from the Mage Guild to learn anything higher than Tier Three.

The entire situation felt absurd. Humanity had once touched the heavens. Now, comparatively, all of them were just like that child searching through trash.

"You're thinking again," Deren observed.

"When am I not?" Aric smiled slightly.

Then he activated one of his spells.

"Pika. Shontan. Syneth."

Mind. Death. Interpretation.

The familiar sensation settled behind his eyes.

Death Detection.

The spell highlighted dead organic matter within his perception. As a cantrip, it wasn't especially powerful, but it was useful during patrols. Hidden corpses usually meant murders.

He swept his gaze across the area as a whole.

Dead rats beneath floorboards, a couple of birds on roofs, insects inside walls, and an unfortunate alley cat that had lost a fight a couple of days ago.

Nothing out of the ordinary. Still, Aric kept the spell active as they continued their patrol.

The mana cost was negligible. Cantrip spells existed in a category all their own. The disturbance they generated was so minor that most mages could maintain several simultaneously without issue.

The rest of the patrol passed uneventfully, and eventually the group returned toward the palace.

At the gates, Aric dismissed most of his escort. The guards saluted and departed, leaving only Deren walking beside him.

The knight waited until they were alone before speaking.

"So."

Aric sighed. He rarely liked it whenever Deren used that particular tone.

"So?" he replied cautiously.

"We finished early."

"Yeah."

"We could go to the library again."

"We probably will."

Deren's grin widened.

"So. Lira."

Aric immediately groaned.

"I don't like where this conversation is going."

"I'm just saying..."

"I know exactly what you're saying."

"The girl is interesting," Deren laughed.

"The girl is just a library caretaker."

"The girl rebuilt half the library in a few months."

"That is admittedly impressive."

"The girl also somehow knows more about history than half the royal scholars."

"That is less impressive considering some of our royal scholars."

Deren barked a laugh, and Aric couldn't entirely suppress a smile.

The truth was that Lira fascinated him. Not romantically; at least, he was fairly certain it wasn't romantic.

The girl simply didn't make sense.

Most people became predictable and easy to understand with enough observation.

In her case, the more you observed, the more facets you uncovered.

Every week, she would accidentally reveal another skill she had no business possessing.

Calligraphy. Historical research. Logistics. Even accounting, according to Cedric.

She was a maid who frequently sounded like a professor when she talked with Lyth.

"You're thinking about her," Deren nudged him. "So you are interested in her after all."

"The girl is so far beneath my station that the suggestion is ridiculous."

"If you don't make a move," Deren shrugged, "I might."

"Seriously?" Aric looked at him. "You?"

"Why not?" he protested. "Unlike you, I am only a baron's son."

"You spent your first conversation with her convinced she was attempting to proposition you."

"Argh..." Deren covered his face. "Don't remind me of that slip-up."

"Who does that?"

"In my defense, maids ask me out all the time," he frowned, "and she was extremely confident when she approached me."

"You thought confidence was flirting."

"It could have been."

"It wasn't."

"I know," Deren groaned.

Aric finally laughed.

"I shall never recover from this."

"You probably won't."

"Do you think I lost my chance because of this?" asked the knight.

"You really are into her." The prince smiled. "I didn't know you were into freckles."

"Her looks are the last thing on my mind," said Deren. "You know my father's lands barely break even. A wife as capable as her could help me turn them into something worth inheriting."

"Don't let her hear you say that or you will really lose all your chances." The prince shook his head.

"What do you mean?" asked the duelist.

"Women love having their appearance dismissed." Aric chuckled. "If you want her for her skills, try recruiting her, not marrying her."

"Oh."

"You're lucky you're handsome because you are really terrible at this," the prince laughed.

They continued walking toward the military keep.

For a brief moment, everything felt normal.

Then Aric stopped. His body froze so abruptly that Deren took several steps before realizing his friend was no longer beside him.

The knight turned.

"What is it?"

Aric didn't answer immediately. His eyes remained fixed on the keep. The Death Detection spell was still active, and something had just been highlighted inside the building.

"Deren."

The prince's expression hardened.

The knight immediately recognized the tone and moved his hand to his sword.

"What happened?"

"Someone is dead."

Aric slowly pointed toward the keep.

The smile vanished from Deren's face.

"Inside?"

"Inside."

The two young men exchanged a look, then both started running.

The prince watched as Thadwick hung from the ceiling, a rope tight around his neck.

"I don't believe it," Deren said. "Why would Thadwick do this?"

"Did you know him well?" asked the prince. Unlike Deren, Aric didn't get to interact with the regular guards that much. Even one as highly ranked as Thadwick was beneath his notice. Only knights like Deren dared approach him casually.

"He was an ass," Deren grimaced. "Unless I'm missing something, he was too self-obsessed to kill himself."

"Pika. Maja. Syneth," whispered Aric.

Mind. Magic. Interpretation.

With his magical sight activated, Aric searched for signs of recent spellcasting. He noticed no significant disturbances. Still, he knew it wouldn't be that easy.

So he cast another spell.

"Pika. Cipos. Syneth. Cognar. Velar."

Mind. Amplification. Interpretation. Knowledge. Clarity.

As his mind expanded and his perception sharpened, the prince carefully examined the room. Information collided with old memories and accumulated knowledge as his enhanced senses processed every detail.

"This does seem like suicide," he said slowly. "There is no blood on the bed. The man might have struggled a bit, judging from the claw marks on his neck, but that could simply be the result of last-second regret. The bed seems slightly disturbed, but that is probably from tossing and turning before going through with it."

Still, something bothered him.

Why would Thadwick do this?

His enhanced mind led his eyes to the letters lying neatly upon the desk.

He let the spell fade. Maintaining it for long periods was mentally exhausting.

Slowly, he approached the desk and picked up the first letter.

His expression darkened with every line he read.

He knew his brother could be arrogant, selfish, and cruel at time, but he had never imagined this.

"What is it?" Deren asked, noticing his friend's shaking hands.

Aric folded the letter.

"I'm sorry. I don't think I should let you read it."

"That bad, huh?"

"It's worse than you could imagine."

He picked up the second letter, then the third, and with each one, his face became colder.

The room seemed quieter than before; the corpse hanging from the ceiling was no longer the center of his attention. When he finally finished reading the fourth and final letter, he stood silently for several moments.

Deren didn't interrupt.

Eventually, the prince folded the papers together and tucked them beneath his arm.

"My friend, I need you to guard this room."

The sudden formality in his voice immediately caught Deren's attention.

"Of course."

"No one enters."

Deren nodded.

"Understood."

"You are authorized to kill anyone who attempts to bypass you by force."

The knight blinked. It was not something he expected Aric to say.

"Anyone?" he asked carefully.

"Anyone."

Deren's eyes narrowed.

"Even Sergeant Salvek?"

The prince's expression hardened.

"Especially Sergeant Salvek."

For the first time since entering the room, Deren looked genuinely alarmed.

"What exactly was in those letters?"

Aric looked toward Thadwick's corpse.

Then back toward the letters in his hand.

Finally, he met his friend's gaze.

"The kind of thing that could shake the kingdom."

"I may tell you later, once everything is handled. I trust you that much," the prince added, a grimace settling on his face. "But for now, I think it's better if you don't know."

Deren nodded. Then he slowly rested his hand on the pommel of his sword.

"I'll hold the room."

"I know you will."

"Where are you going?" the knight asked, frowning.

Aric turned toward the door.

"To my father. He needs to see this immediately."
