The next morning, the soft glow of dawn filtered through the treetops as Lila, Ronorrh, and a few of Lamcada’s instructors stood at the edge of a large open courtyard, ready for their first training session. The air was warm but still comfortable, the volcanic heat from Lamcada’s core barely noticeable in the early hours.

“Alright,” Lila began, stretching her arms and rolling her shoulders to loosen up, “I’ll start by showing you the basic routines we’ve been following for physical training. Aira and Alliot will join us, too. The main thing is that consistency and focus are key.”

Ronorrh gave a respectful nod, her fiery eyes curious. “It’s been many centuries since I’ve seen anyone practise like this,” she mused, her gaze sweeping over the quiet courtyard. “But I must admit, the idea of building strength without magic has its appeal.”

Sweat beaded on Lila’s brow as she demonstrated the first drill—a rapid succession of lunges that left even her enlightened muscles burning. The stone beneath her feet radiated residual heat, while around them, the distant hiss of underground magma provided an oddly rhythmic backdrop to their labored breathing.

“This isn’t just about moving faster,” she explained, catching Ronorrh’s skeptical glance. “It’s about teaching your body to remember without thinking. Roots before branches.”

She guided Ronorrh and her instructors through each movement. Though enlightened, Ronorrh and the others moved with fluid grace, their years of fire affinity training evident in the way they balanced their physical prowess with the energy coursing through their bodies.

The progression moved quickly: push-ups, sprints across the courtyard, balance exercises atop stone platforms, and dodging drills designed to increase reaction time. Aira and Alliot joined in, their bodies already accustomed to the routine, but Alliot still pushed hard to keep up, driven by his ongoing quest to boost his agility.

Ronorrh, for all her years, seemed surprisingly agile, though she laughed between breaths as sweat beaded on her brow. “I’d forgotten the physical exhaustion of the old ways,” she said with a grin. “This takes me back to the days before the Nexus, when mortals trained their bodies as much as their minds.”

“Don’t expect to be walkin’ high on the cliffs by tomorrow,” Lila said. “Progress is slower than moss growth, but when it comes—by the fox’s tail—it feels like catching a howl in the wind. Sharp as thorns, though, we haven’t found the edge of what’s possible yet.”

Ronorrh wiped the sweat from her forehead, not even hiding her amusement. “So, you may not know when you’ll hit a ceiling, but I admire your perseverance. I wonder if any of us have forgotten what that felt like... achieving something purely through hard work and persistence, rather than through energy or magic.”

As the group paused for a break, Alliot leaned against a stone pillar, still catching his breath. “I’ve only been improving my agility,” he said, glancing at Lila. “But there’s got to be a cap. If there wasn’t, we’d all be unstoppable by now.”

Ronorrh smiled knowingly. “A cap, indeed. Everything has its limits—even we do, though we rarely acknowledge it. Still, this training... it’s a way to rediscover what’s within ourselves, rather than what magic offers. I wonder how far it can truly take us.”

Lila stretched her arms again, muscles feeling the strain of the workout. “We’ve only scratched the surface, to be honest. But it’s worked for Aira and me so far, and Alliot’s seeing some improvement, too. The more we explore, the more we learn about our own potential—and our limits.”

Aira nodded as she sipped from her flask, her eyes scanning the courtyard where they had spent the morning training. “Limits are necessary,” she said, her voice soft but certain. “They remind us we’re still connected to something larger. But that doesn’t mean we won’t push beyond them.”

Ronorrh stood up, straightening her posture. “Indeed. Perhaps your journey will teach us all something valuable about how far the mind and body can truly go, even when bound by limits.”

The group prepared for one final set of exercises, determination etched on their faces as the sun climbed higher in the sky, casting long shadows across the courtyard. The day had only begun, but their strength—and their understanding of it—was growing with each moment.

As they completed the final set of exercises, Ronorrh’s expression shifted from playful exertion to something more solemn. “You’ve fulfilled your part of our exchange admirably,” she said, her voice carrying that distinctive fire-crackle. “Now I shall honor mine. The path to the Emberwood Grove awaits, and with it, secrets few outsiders have ever witnessed. But be cautious. It may be more chaotic than any powers you have experienced before.”

To complete her side of the deal, Ronorrh led Aira, Lila, and Alliot through the winding streets of Lamcada, guiding them toward the heart of the city where the Emberwood Grove awaited. As they approached, the air became noticeably warmer, and the scent of charred wood and molten stone filled their senses. The flame-touched trees that surrounded them burned with a soft, eternal fire. Flames flickered with an otherworldly glow, casting eerie shadows on the ground but never consuming the wood. The bark of the trees was a deep, charcoal black, while the flames that enveloped them ranged from bright orange to deep crimson.

Each Emberwood tree pulsed with its own heartbeat of flame—inhaling darkness, exhaling light. Aira could feel it in her chest as they drew closer: the rhythmic surge of power flowing upward from deep below, where molten stone breathed like a sleeping dragon. The flames danced along bark that should have been ash hours, days, centuries ago, yet remained solid as iron beneath Aira’s tentative touch. These weren’t trees adorned with fire; they were a fire that had dreamed itself into trees. It was as if the very essence of the lava coursed through their roots, creating a delicate equilibrium.

Small streams of magma occasionally bubbled to the surface, flowing harmlessly along carefully crafted channels that directed the molten stone away from the city. The trees absorbed the heat and energy, keeping the surrounding environment safe. The entire grove thrummed with latent energy, the firelight illuminating the deep connection between nature and the volatile forces beneath the earth.

“The Emberwood Grove,” Ronorrh began, her voice reverent as they ventured further. “Our source of power, a delicate balance of fire and life. Without the trees, the lava would overwhelm us. But through the roots of the Emberwoods, we harness the heat and convert it into magical fire that feeds us, empowers us.”

The grove pulsed with an almost sentient energy, the roots glowing faintly with the power they channeled from the magma lake below. The heat was intense, but it wasn’t overwhelming, as if the flames themselves were aware of their guests and were holding back.

“This is incredible,” Aira whispered, her eyes wide as she took in the sight of the eternal flames. “I’ve never seen anything like this.”

As she stood there, surrounded by fire that nurtured rather than consumed, Aira couldn’t help but compare this moment to her previous encounters with elemental Sources. The Heart of the Forest had welcomed her under Ainorrh’s watchful gaze—a calculated introduction where every revelation felt like part of some greater scheme. Then came Ziemrot’s Roots of the Mountain—hostile and guarded, with Khanorrh’s suspicion creating barriers at every turn. That Source had responded to her only after she’d forced her way into its depths, an act of defiance that had changed everything.

The Rune on her wrist pulsed faintly, as if recognizing this difference, too.

“The fire isn’t just destructive,” Ronorrh explained. “It’s a source of life here. The heat and energy from the lava are channeled into the trees, which allows us to tap into the power of the land without being consumed by it.”

Lila, walking beside Aira, nodded thoughtfully. “So, the trees protect the city, while the city feeds off the energy of the lava?”

“Exactly,” Ronorrh said. “It’s a symbiotic relationship. The Emberwood Grove sustains us and prevents the lava from overwhelming our home. And in return, we draw on its power to protect our city and craft our magic.”

They continued walking until they reached the heart of the Emberwood Grove, a clearing where the trees stood taller and the flames burned even brighter. Here, the magic of the grove was at its strongest, the air thick with energy. The ground beneath their feet radiated heat, but it wasn’t unbearable—it was controlled, balanced, just like everything else in Lamcada.

Ronorrh gestured to a small circle of stones set in the center of the clearing. “This is the place where we practice our meditation techniques to enhance our intelligence. The reason it only works here, so close to the source of power, is that the energy from the Emberwood Grove amplifies the effects. The proximity to the trees and the lava lake strengthens the connection between your mind and the energy of the land, allowing you to push your mental limits further than you could anywhere else.”

Aira furrowed her brow, intrigued. “So, the energy here essentially enhances your ability to focus and connect with the Nexus?”

That’s why this meditation technique only bears results when performed near the source of power. Lila nodded slowly. “So, the source of power isn’t just a physical place. It’s a catalyst for transformation.”

“Exactly,” Ronorrh said, smiling. “The energy of the Emberwood Grove helps us channel the mental strength needed to improve our intelligence. It opens up pathways in the mind that would otherwise remain dormant.”

As they stood at the center of the grove, surrounded by the towering, flame-touched trees, Aira could feel the weight of the magic in the air, the power thrumming just beneath the surface. Ronorrh looked around, finding the perfect spot for their meditation.
