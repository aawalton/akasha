I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 27 – The Shape of What Was Done

ARC III: THE WORLD REMEMBERS

SYNOPSIS: The morning after the session. Livia finds Morty in the kitchen. They have a proper conversation for the first time. The crystal is off. Mostly.

The morning after Alondra’s session, Livia found the intake form still on the floor outside the planning room door.

She picked it up. Looked at it. Set it on the table inside.

Alaric’s form has seven pages.

The first page asks for: name, class, level, guild affiliation, presenting issue, and preferred session time.

The second page asks for: prior therapy history, significant life events in the last twelve months, and what Alaric has labeled "Narrative Arc Classification (see attached rubric)."

The attached rubric has seventeen categories.

One of them is "Protagonist (Confirmed)."

One of them is "Protagonist (Emerging)."

One of them is "Anomalous (Other)."

I am in that last one.

T.S. is also in that last one.

Alaric made a category for people who don’t fit any other category and put me in it.

I am going to think about that later.

Later has a very long queue.

She set the form on the table with the offline crystal Morty had left. Both were problems for a different hour. She made tea, which was a thing she had started doing in this world and which did not exist in the original systems documentation for her class. The system had never commented on the tea. She had decided this was implicit approval.

He was in the guild kitchen.

Livia stopped in the doorway.

She had known, intellectually, that Morty lived in the guild. She had known this since Chapter 18 when she found the subreddit video. She had known his follower count, his content format, the fact that he filmed Miles leaving the planning room and set it to ominous music. She had processed all of this as information.

Processing something as information and walking into a kitchen and finding a Pastel Lich making breakfast were different experiences.

He was at the counter. His layered coat was absent—he was wearing something simpler, a pale blue shirt with small skull buttons, which somehow made the fact of his being a skeleton more rather than less apparent. The star-tipped crown was still on, sitting slightly more upright than yesterday, which gave him the air of someone who had woken up and adjusted it deliberately. His heart-shaped magenta eyes were focused on the task in front of him, which was scrambled eggs.

The recording crystal was on the counter beside him. It was off.

He noticed her in the doorway and made a sound—not words, just the specific note of someone registering a presence and deciding how to handle it. He turned back to the eggs.

“I owe you an apology,” he said. “A proper one. Not the version from yesterday where Glimmer had just knocked over a pot and I was trying to manage four things.”

Livia came in and sat at the kitchen table, because there was nowhere else useful to be and the tea was already made.

“Go ahead,” she said.

Morty turned slightly from the eggs. Not fully—maintaining the task gave him something to do with his hands, which was not a thing skeletons were supposed to need and which he clearly did. “I’ve been in this guild for three years. I document the guild. I do it because the guild matters to me and documentation is the way I know how to show that. I have never intentionally recorded anything I shouldn’t. Yesterday I was careless.”

“Glimmer opened the door,” Livia said..

“Glimmer opened the door,” Morty confirmed. “I still could have turned off the crystal the moment I realized where I was. I didn’t. That part is mine.”

He’s not distributing blame evenly.

He’s taking the part that’s his.

That is different from most apologies.

“How many people were watching?” Livia asked.

A pause. He plated the eggs. Set the plate down across from her and sat. The skull ornaments on his belt clicked softly as he settled.

“When I turned it off: 4,300.”

Four thousand three hundred people.

Watching my therapy session.

Live.

“None of them saw Alondra clearly,” Morty said, quickly. “She was off-angle. I was in the doorway. The framing was corridor. You would not be identifiable from the footage either, unless you knew to look. I have not uploaded it. I deleted the live segment.”

“What did chat think was happening?”

“A broken pot and a slime,” Morty said. “Nobody knew there was a session. They thought I had walked into a storage incident.”

A storage incident.

4,300 people watched me stand there processing the existence of a Pastel Lich for the first time and they thought it was a storage incident.

That is either very good or deeply insulting.

I have not decided which.

They ate breakfast. The eggs were good, which Livia noted because she had not expected to have opinions about food made by a skeleton, and yet here she was having opinions.

“We haven’t been introduced,” she said, at some point. “Properly.”

“I know who you are,” Morty said.

“I know your follower count,” Livia said. “That’s not the same thing.”

Something in his expression shifted. The magenta hearts brightened slightly—not the reflexive warmth he had produced in the planning room, but something more specific. He was pleased she had said that. She could see it.

“Morty Calvarium,” he said. “Level 92. Pastel Lich. I joined this guild three years ago because Miles posted a recruitment notice that said, and I am quoting directly, "no drama, serious raiders only, emotional stability required." I thought that sounded like exactly the kind of environment I needed.”

“Was it?”

“The first week, Bron cried for six hours about the concept of continuity,” Morty said. “The second week, Alaric bet his raid slot on a coin flip. The third week, Nyx refused to leave a crate for forty-eight hours and nobody was sure whether to be concerned or give him space. I decided both.”

“And you stayed.”

“They were interesting,” Morty said. Simply. “I had never been somewhere interesting before.”

I had never been somewhere interesting before.

Three years in this guild because it was interesting.

I understand this more than I expected to.

“The camera,” Livia said. “Three years of documentation. What do you do with it?”

Morty considered this with the seriousness of someone who had been asked the question before and still found it worth taking time over.

“Some I post. The raids. The lighter moments. The occasional corridor that captures something real.” He glanced at the counter where the offline crystal sat. “Some I keep. Private archive. Things that aren’t mine to share but are mine to remember.”

“How do you decide?”

“Whether someone would want it seen,” he said. “Not whether it’s interesting. Whether the person in it would choose to be seen that way.”

Whether the person in it would choose to be seen that way.

That is a more careful standard than most people apply.

That is a more careful standard than I expected from someone who filmed Miles through a corridor gap.

“Miles,” Livia said.

Morty’s expression made a small adjustment. “The corridor video.”

“You posted it.”

“I asked him first,” Morty said.

Livia stopped.

“You asked Miles before posting a video of him.”

“I showed him the footage and asked if he wanted me to delete it,” Morty said. “He watched it. He said the music was accurate. I interpreted that as permission.”

Miles watched the video.

Miles said the music was accurate.

Miles gave permission.

Miles has seen himself from the outside and decided it was fine.

I need to sit with that.

I am sitting with it.

Filed in a section I am going to revisit.

After breakfast, Morty picked up the offline crystal from the counter and held it out to her.

“The corridor footage,” he said. “From yesterday. Before the session. This is what I was actually here for.”

She took it.

“What’s in it?”

“Miles and Alondra,” he said. “In the corridor. I was already positioned when she arrived. I didn’t know there would be a session. I didn’t know she would be here.”

He filmed the corridor moment.

He has it.

He is giving it to me.

“Why are you giving me this?”

Morty was quiet for a moment. He looked at the crystal in her hand.

“Because you were watching from the doorway,” he said. “I saw you. You were trying to read something you couldn’t see clearly from that angle. This has a better angle.”

He saw me watching.

He knows I was trying to read them.

He filmed it.

He is giving it to me.

He is the most useful person in this building and he came with a crown and an accidental live stream.

“You’ve been documenting this guild for three years,” Livia said. “You have footage of things I wasn’t here for.”

“Yes.”

“Would you show me some of it?”

A longer pause. The skull ornaments were still. He was thinking about the question with the seriousness he gave things that mattered to him.

“Some,” he said. “Not all. I would need to review what’s mine to share and what isn’t. But some—” he looked at the crystal in her hand, —“yes.”

He said yes.

He has three years of footage.

Three years of a guild I have been in for nine months.

I am going to watch what he gives me very carefully.

He was at the door when she asked it.

“Four months before I arrived,” she said. “You noticed something changing. In Miles. In the guild.”

Morty turned.

“You talked to Bron,” he said. Not a question..

“T.S.,” she said.

His expression did a small, careful thing. “T.S. is here?”

“Session one was Thursday,” she said. “You know about T.S.”

“I know about most things in this guild,” Morty said. “T.S. I have only seen twice. They move in ways that don’t show up in corridor footage. I know they’re there. I have never managed to document them.”

T.S. doesn’t appear in his footage.

Three years of documentation.

T.S. has been on this server seven years.

Morty has never caught them on camera.

That is either impressive evasion or something more structural.

“The four months,” Livia said. “What exactly changed?”

Morty leaned against the doorframe. Thinking about how to say something accurately.

“Miles started preparing the planning room,” he said. “Not for strategy. The way you prepare a space for a different kind of use. He moved the chairs. He tested the lamp positions. He sat in the room by himself twice that I saw, doing nothing. Just—” a pause, —“checking what it felt like.”

Miles moved the chairs in the planning room.

Before I arrived.

He was preparing the space.

He knew.

He knew something was coming and he prepared a space for it.

He prepared a space for me.

She did not say anything for a moment..

“That’s in the archive?” she said..

“Private archive,” Morty said. “Not posted. I didn’t know what it meant then.”

“Do you know what it means now?”

He considered this with the specific pause of someone who had been thinking about a question for a long time and was deciding whether the answer was theirs to give.

“I think,” he said carefully, “that Miles knew something was coming that the guild needed and that he couldn’t provide himself. And I think he was—” another pause, longer this time, —“trying to make sure there was room for it when it arrived.”

He left after that.

Livia sat at the kitchen table for a while.

He prepared the space.

He moved the chairs.

He sat in the room alone and checked what it felt like.

Four months before I arrived.

He was making room for something he didn’t know the name of yet.

She added to her notepad, at the bottom of the page marked Things I don’t understand yet:

Miles prepared the planning room four months before I arrived. Morty has footage. Private archive. Miles moved the chairs and tested the lamp and sat alone in the room checking what it felt like. He knew something was coming. He made space for it.

Morty noticed. T.S. noticed the system changing. Miles felt it before either of them.

She looked at the corridor crystal in her hand.

Then at the tea, which had gone cold.

Then at the lamp, which she had bought because it didn’t intrude.

He tested the lamp positions.

Four months before I got here.

He was already thinking about the light.

Miles found her in the kitchen at some point mid-morning, still at the table, the tea cold and the notepad open and the corridor crystal in her hand.

He looked at the crystal.

“Morty,” he said.

“He was apologizing,” Livia said. “For yesterday.”

“The live stream.”

“Yes. He also made eggs.”

Miles processed this. “He’s a good cook,” he said, which was not the response she had expected and was somehow the most revealing thing he could have said about his relationship with Morty.

He sat across from her. He looked at the notepad, which she did not close. Then at the crystal.

“That’s corridor footage,” he said.

“Yes.”

“From yesterday.”

“Yes.”

A pause.

“Have you watched it?”

“Not yet,” she said. “I was going to ask you first.”

He looked at her.

“Ask me what?”

“Whether you mind.”

Miles was quiet. He turned his coffee mug once—the habit she had catalogued, the thing he did when he was deciding something.

“Watch it,” he said.

“Are you sure?”

“It’s a corridor,” he said. “It’s not—” he stopped. Started differently. “You were trying to read something. I know you were. You didn’t ask me directly. You let it go. I appreciate that. But if Morty’s giving you a better angle, use it.”

You let it go and I appreciate that.

He noticed I was watching.

He noticed I didn’t push.

He is now actively giving me permission.

Progress: 19%.

That number still feels wrong.

By afternoon:

Thorne had received Alaric’s intake form follow-up—pages three through seven, with a note that said "the Narrative Arc Classification rubric requires cross-reference with your field notebook data, please see Tab C." Thorne had, apparently, sent back a completed rubric with annotations. Alaric had forwarded Livia a copy of the annotations with the subject line: "Thorne Pinewhistle is the first external researcher to engage seriously with the rubric. Attaching for the archive." Livia had read exactly one annotation before putting it down. The annotation was about the epistemological implications of categorizing entities by narrative function. She was going to read the rest later. Later’s queue was very long.

The crow, whose name she had learned from Alaric’s form (field: "Any accompanying entities?", answer: "Rigor. He is a crow. He does not need a level."), had established a semi-permanent position on the guild hall’s main table. Bron had given it something to eat. It had accepted. Bron was now telling people he had a crow. This was technically inaccurate. The crow was Thorne’s. The crow did not appear to consider this relevant.

T.S.’s second appointment was on the calendar for the following Thursday. Livia had written, in the margin next to it: Ask about the system change. What specifically changed. Who could have changed it. Then, underneath, smaller: Not all boot prints are theirs. Someone else was here.

Alaric’s intake form for Alondra was completed. Alondra had returned it through the portal with the Narrative Arc Classification field filled in as: "Undetermined." Alaric had written back: "Undetermined is valid. Welcome to the system." Livia was choosing to consider this wholesome.

That evening, Livia watched the corridor crystal.

It was forty-three seconds of footage.

Morty had been right about the angle. From the planning room doorway she had seen peripheral shapes. From the crystal she could see faces.

Miles, when he said Alondra’s name: careful. Contained. The expression of someone who had rehearsed this moment in some internal way and was executing the rehearsal. His hands were at his sides. His eyes met hers directly and did not move. He was being fair. Consciously, deliberately fair.

Alondra, when she said Miles’ name: something else. Not hurt. Not anger. The expression of someone standing in front of a consequence they made and have been carrying the weight of and are now, for the first time, close enough to see the shape of what they actually did.

Her chin dipped. One second. Maybe two.

Then she walked away.

Miles watched her go for exactly three seconds.

Then he walked the other direction.

He did not look back either.

Livia sat with the crystal in her hand after the footage ended.

She was standing in front of a consequence.

That was the expression.

Not guilt about what she felt.

Guilt about what she did.

Something specific.

Something that involved him.

And she has been close enough to see the shape of it for seven months

and has not said it to anyone.

She put the crystal down. Opened her notepad to the Alondra page. Added one line:

Session 3. She said she would say it. I believe her. I also believe Miles does not know what’s coming.

She closed the notepad.

From the main hall, she could hear Bron telling the crow about the existential implications of immortality. The crow was responding. Thorne was not intervening. This had apparently become a thing that happened now.

The crow is going to have opinions about immortality.

Bron is going to feel heard by a crow.

Thorne is going to act like this is not happening.

This is fine.

Everything is fine.

I have leveled up.

Session 3 is next week.

The robe is doing something different in the light.

I am going to go to bed.
