The entire passage shuddered violently, threatening Ace and his team with a watery grave.

The black lake stretched out around them like polished obsidian, broken only by the surging walls of water that flanked their path along the lakebed. A deep, ominous groan reverberated through the water walls like the death rattle of some colossal beast. The liquid barriers trembled, and ripples pulsed through their surfaces in hypnotic, terrifying waves.

Tara froze mid-step, her eyes wide. “It’s collapsing.”

Another violent shudder ran through the corridor. The walls contracted again, squeezing to just four feet apart. Everyone held their breath, waiting for the inevitable collapse.

Instead, the passage went completely still.

The groaning fell silent. The surging barrier protecting them from the rest of the lake settled again, rushing upward in steady waves—though they remained uncomfortably close.

Seconds passed.

Then, a full minute.

Nothing happened.

Ace didn’t trust the sudden calm. He’d seen enough of the System’s twisted games to know better.

“Did it stop?” Marcus asked, still watching the watery walls with a cautious glare.

“For now,” Ace replied. He pointed toward the massive doors that waited at the far end of the passage. “Let’s not stick around to see if it decides to finish the job. Move.”

The others hesitated, still wary of the narrowed space.

“Now,” Ace added, his voice carrying the weight of command that had once directed Marines through active warzones. “Single file. Keep to the center. Don’t touch the walls.”

They resumed their journey with renewed caution, each step measured and deliberate. The path remained stable, but the earlier display left nobody feeling secure.

“You think it was just testing us?” Tara whispered as they crept forward.

Ace glanced back at the walls. “I think this whole place is one giant trap, frankly. We’ve only seen the beginning.”

“The System wouldn’t have sent us here if there wasn’t a way through,” Olivia reasoned, though her hand remained near her weapon.

“Maybe,” Ace said, his eyes fixed on the doors ahead. “But let’s not test that. Pick up the pace. This whole setup feels like it’s waiting for round two.”

They quickened their gait, the massive doors growing larger with each passing moment. Through it all, Ace kept his arms firmly at his sides, his eyes fixed on the distant doors.

“Do you see that?” Tara asked.

Her voice sent a startled chill through the group, and everyone followed her outstretched hand as she pointed at something in the water.

This far in, the ink-black color of the water had thinned enough to show silhouettes floating in the darkness. It took a moment to make sense of the figures, and Ace’s grip on his sword tightened as he squinted at whatever was in there.

If something attacked them here, they were screwed.

His jaw twitched with the pressing weight of the growing tension in the air, but it took only a minute more to figure out what they were seeing.

Corpses.

Hundreds of them.

Most of the bodies hung suspended and motionless in the void, their expressions frozen in horror. Some of the victims’ faces contorted, as though they were forever screaming, their jaws stretched impossibly wide. Others stared off into nothing, lost in their resigned despair and their eyes half-lidded, as though accepting their eternal prison.

Every single one of them had been destroyed in horrible ways. For some, their armor had melted into their skin. For others, their limbs twisted at impossible angles or were missing entirely. So many of them had skin completely bleached of color except for vivid wounds that pulsed with unnatural light. Most were clearly vampires like themselves, with half-extended fangs and rusted weapons floating around their heads. Several corpses had been fused together in grotesque mutations that had perhaps once been human, with legs and heads missing as they blurred together in an impossible glob of skin and bone.

Several—the most disturbing of all—pressed their hands against the invisible barrier separating the path from the water, their palms flattened and fingers splayed. Their nails had worn down to nothing, leaving raw, bleeding crescents that leaked cloudy fluid into the water. Their faces pressed close to the watery walls, their noses and cheeks distorted by pressure as they silently pleaded for help. Their eyes followed the team’s movement with unmistakable awareness, widening occasionally with recognition or narrowing with calculated interest.

One woman, her lower body entirely dissolved into tendrils of darkness, mouthed words against the barrier that fogged the surface with each silent syllable. A child—or what had once been a child—scratched endlessly at the same spot, leaving faint marks that healed in the water almost immediately. An elderly man in tattered formal wear tapped a complex rhythm against the barrier, his movements too precise and measured to be random.

Behind these foreground horrors, deeper in the black water, larger shapes moved with purpose. Massive silhouettes that reminded Ace of a shark’s silhouette swam at the edge of what he could see through the cloudy lake.

“Fuuuuuuuuuck this,” Marcus whispered under his breath. “Do not like. Do. Not. Like.”

Ace didn’t say anything—mainly because he agreed.

To his surprise, Olivia stepped toward the edge. Her hand reached for one of the women trapped behind the invisible barrier protecting them from the floating entities, evidently fascinated with what lay beyond. Her fingernails inched ever closer to a rotting palm on the opposite side of the surging water.

“The fuck—” Ace caught her wrist before her fingers could breach the surface. “Don’t.”

Her eyes shifted toward him, and something unsettling lurked in her gaze—not fear, but hunger. “They’re beautiful, though. The way they’re suspended. Neither dead nor truly alive.”

“Focus,” Ace chided.

Her lips twisted into a pouting frown, but with one last glance toward the corpse beyond the surging wall, she nodded.

Ace sighed with relief.

“This isn’t our fight,” he told the others. “Whatever they are, they’re trouble. Keep your distance.”

“No shit,” Marcus said with a confused glance at Olivia.

A muscle in the man’s eye twitched, and his brows furrowed. His attention returned to the gaunt and rotting faces trapped in the inky lake. As he studied them, an expression of dawning horror slowly crossed over his face.

“They’re still conscious,” he whispered, his face paling. “Some of them have been here for decades.”

“Eyes forward,” Ace ordered, forcing his own gaze away from the haunting spectacle. “We can’t help them. We can only make sure we don’t join them.”

The path widened as they approached the far end of the lake, opening into a small platform before the entrance doors. The floating bodies thinned out here, as if they’d been pushed back by some unseen force—or perhaps they’d learned to keep their distance.

“Well, Fang Gang,” Ace said under his breath as he looked up at the towering entryway. “Looks like we’re here.”

Before them stood a pair of doors that dwarfed anything Ace had ever seen. At well over fifty feet tall, they were carved from some material that resembled obsidian but lacked its natural, polished gleam. Nothing reflected across the surface—not even the fading sunlight. Elaborate runes covered every inch of the massive portal, arranged in spiraling patterns that hummed and faded like a pulse.

Tara pointed to a series of indentations that ran in a straight line across the doors at chest height. “Look at these.”

Fifty palm prints had been carved into the surface, each identical and each bearing a small, needle-like protrusion in its center.

Because, of course, there had to be a blood sacrifice to get in.

Ace scoffed in disgust. “Everything in this fucking place wants a taste.”

“Of course it does,” Olivia said, her voice unnervingly eager. “Blood is power. Power is the only currency that matters here.”

“Aside from iron chits and steel cubes, apparently,” Marcus muttered.

Ace took a cautious step forward and carefully examined the central palm print. The spike in its center gleamed with a razor-sharp edge.

“Who wants to bet this is a test?” he concluded. “The System said we have to be a certain level to even get the map to this place, so that probably means we have to be a certain level to enter.” He glanced back at his team. “If I had to guess, it’s checking our level, class, maybe our evolution stage.”

“And if we fail?” Tara asked.

Ace nodded toward the floating bodies behind them. “We probably join the welcoming committee.”

“Here goes nothing,” Marcus said with a bracing sigh.

Ace sheathed his sword. He positioned his hand over the central handprint, hesitated for only a second, and then pressed his palm against the cold surface. The spike drove into his palm with surprising force, penetrating much deeper than seemed possible for its size.

Pain instantly lanced through his hand and up his forearm, spreading clear to his shoulder like liquid fire.

The rune above his palm shifted from a murky, dormant gray to vibrant crimson. The light blinded him, and he shielded his face with his free arm as he waited for whatever happened next.

Something drank him in—his blood, his stats, his level, his class, his skills, his abilities, his EXP Solutes. It laid him bare in an instant, dissecting his strengths and weaknesses as though it could see into his very soul.

And he fucking hated it.

After what seemed an eternity, the light above his palm stabilized to a steady crimson glow. A soft chime echoed through the air, but the doors remained sealed.

Marcus scratched his head. “Uh…”

“Nothing’s happening,” Ace said.

Damn it all.

He tried to pull away from the door, but the spike’s grip on him never wavered. He tugged gently, and when that didn’t work, he yanked with all his strength. Gritting his teeth, he grabbed his wrist and set one foot against the door for leverage.

But the spike wouldn’t let go.

“Fuck,” he whispered under his breath.

“Wait, Ace. Stop.” Tara set a gentle hand on his shoulder, and he instantly calmed.

With a sidelong glance toward her, he raised an inquisitive eyebrow.

“I think we all need to do it,” she said. “There’s a party system, after all. We’re linked. I mean, we couldn’t even get the map until the last person leveled up. Maybe it needs to check all of us.”

Before he could protest, she placed her hand on the indentation beside his. The same process repeated—the spike, the pain, the analysis. She stifled a pained whimper as her whole body tensed with pain, and she swallowed hard. Her teeth ground together, loud enough for Ace to hear, and her breath quickened.

Just as before, however, the rune above her hand glowed crimson, and her shoulders finally relaxed as the rush of pain apparently faded.

Still, nothing happened.

“C’mon, you two,” Ace said with a look over his shoulder at the others. “Let’s get this over with.”

They obeyed, and both tensed in pain as the door assessed them as well. After a few moments, they went still, and their breathing returned to normal as the runes above their hands glowed red.

And yet, nothing happened.

Ace huffed in annoyance. “What the—”

Before he could finish his thought, a low hum thrummed through the air. The active runes brightened until they were painful to look at, their light spilling across the platform in dazzling patterns that blinded them all.

A deep, resonant chime gonged from somewhere beyond the doors, followed by another, and another, and one more—counting them. Four chimes. Four vampires. Four blood sacrifices accepted.

A fierce, grinding scream broke through the air, vibrating through Ace’s teeth and stirring a rush of primal fear in the back of Ace’s mind. He tensed on impulse, and though he still couldn’t break free, every impulse in him warned him to run. To get away. To do anything other than stand there, framed by corpse-riddled water and a bloodthirsty door.

Through the dread, he held his ground.

After everything they had endured thus far, he wasn’t about to let this place break him.

A crack formed in the center of the doors, and the spikes thankfully relinquished their hold on Ace’s palm. He took a wary step backward, his thumb rubbing the palm of his hand as he glanced upward at the opening entryway, and the others followed suit.

Chunks of dark crystal fell from the seams as the doors parted, disintegrating into motes of light that danced through the air before vanishing entirely. The stone platform beneath them trembled and shifted, subtle at first, but with growing intensity that sent hairline fractures racing underneath the team’s feet. The cracks wept a silvery fluid that evaporated on contact with the air, leaving behind the scent of burned hair.

“Why does everything in this place have to be so goddamn creepy?” Marcus said under his breath.

“Because the System is a psychopath,” Tara answered.

Even as his attention remained on the opening doors, Ace nodded in agreement.

Darkness spilled from the widening gap—not merely an absence of light but something viscous and alive that poured across the threshold like a fog. The shadowy smoke flowed around their ankles, cold enough to burn any exposed skin. It carried whispers just below the threshold of what Ace could hear—wordless communications that bypassed the ears entirely and resonated directly within his mind, promising secrets and power while simultaneously hinting at costs too terrible to imagine. The essence clung to them like something starved, wrapping up their legs like living tendrils.

“Well,” Marcus said, his voice tight. “Ladies and gentlemen, I believe our table is ready.”
