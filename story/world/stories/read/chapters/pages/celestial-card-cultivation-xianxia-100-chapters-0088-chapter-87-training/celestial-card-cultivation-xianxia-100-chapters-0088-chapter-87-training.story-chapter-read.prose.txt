As funny as it was seeing Miyao so excited about the training and his progress with the help of body cultivation, he had to admit he was quite eager, too.

He had always told himself that he didn’t mind being first in group Lapis, then in Jade. He also never begrudged Miyao or Shuhua the training they had received from early childhood and their superior abilities with the spear.

However, he had always wanted to close the gap. Primarily, of course, to be able to defeat the demons, but…

Anyway, they had arrived at the training ground, and he moved to get a blunted weapon from the rack on the side.

Miyao did not seem open to discussing how they would train or who would spar with whom. The moment he had a spear, she nodded towards the closest ring and took the position across from him.

Without much further ado, she attacked.

The first thing he noticed, he could see the attack coming.

Not that he had ever struggled with other disciples in the same way that he struggled with cultivators of higher realms, but now he could see every move of the Piercing Fang coming towards him.

Seeing wasn’t the same as reacting, though.

But he managed to get his spear between himself and the attack and managed to redirect it to the side.

That was the second thing he noticed, his strength had truly grown, and moving the other spear from its trajectory felt easier now. Not easy, but easier.

The third thing he noticed, strength did not replace technique.

With a flick of her wrist, Miyao managed to twirl her spear around his and got the spear back on its original trajectory.

Though it had lost a lot of its speed, it still connected with his hip. It would not have been a debilitating wound in a real fight, but it bothered him.

He didn’t let that mess with his focus, though. Instead, he launched a counterattack with a Blazing Arc that Miyao could only dodge by retreating quickly and losing the momentum of her initial attack.

She grinned at him—whether because of her hit or the sheer pleasure of the fight, he didn’t know. Maybe both.

This time, he initiated an exchange, choosing a Flowing Current horizontal sweep. Instead of the typical evasion or redirection, she chose to block with an Unyielding Wall. With a loud smashing sound, the spears met each other, and she just held the block, daring him to try to push through.

He obliged.

With all his newfound strength, he tried to wrestle her spear out of the way. He could see her grit her teeth; slowly her arms started to shake, and the spear gave way.

He was stronger.

The next moment, he felt her spear poking him in the chest.

He had let himself be distracted and missed her dodge and counterattack.

Still, this was promising.

Physically, he had at least closed the gap, likely overtaken her, and the gap in technique had shrunk by a lot.

Given that she was one of the absolute strongest in the group of disciples, having started with them, this was encouraging.

***

As the afternoon progressed, it became clear that the others had not been lazing about during the time that he had been gone.

Naturally, the biggest improvement could be observed with Hong. Despite the time he had spent with his family after Jun’s death, he had improved by leaps and bounds. In particular, he had improved his agility. In combination with his natural strength, he had become a very competent contender.

Would it be enough to challenge members from group Jade? No, but Jianyi felt that he should be able to make it up to Lapis if things worked out on the day of the assessment.

Shuhua had advanced to the third stage of Qi Gathering and had spent quite some time adjusting her technique to deal with the increased strength. She was very good.

Jianyi thought that Miyao was likely still a bit ahead of her, but he would be surprised if there were many disciples who could beat her.

He was aware that he currently lacked insight into how all the other disciples had developed; still, he thought that his group of friends should be positioned to do well.

And there was still time.

After they had sparred against each other, Jianyi suggested that they train forms for a while. Typically, it was done the other way around, but it didn’t matter.

As they wound down the training, it became clear that he was less exhausted than the others—the energy of Wood coursing through his heart and lungs coming into play.

He wasn’t the only one who noticed.

After the spar, Miyao had reverted to her calmer, quieter personality, but she looked at him with a fierce and determined expression when she asked for his help. “Now that you have gone through this, can you review my choice for a body cultivation art again?”

“Of course,” he nodded. “I am happy to help, but I would caution you against using whatever you choose prior to the test. It takes some time to get used to the changes in your body.”

Jianyi was asked to describe his time in the wilderness, which he did, only leaving out the battles against the spirit beasts, but he was also curious about what had happened in the sect during his absence.

Shuhua grimaced. “Things have not been pretty. It is all rumors at this point, but supposedly, still a large part of the core and inner disciples are out on missions fighting cultists.”

Hong nodded. “Yeah, there was even somebody who said that a demon had been seen… Here, in our region. People are going crazy right now.”

It was Jianyi’s turn to grimace. Of course, he hadn’t told anybody about his own mission with Elder Sun that had led to the confrontation with a demon. If more had been found since then, things were worse than he had thought…

***

Jianyi had trained very hard over the last few days. On his own, with his friends, and with Minghao.

Now, the day of the mid-year assessment had come.

To Jianyi’s chagrin, Elder Sun had not yet returned. He wasn’t sure why it was so important to him, but he felt disappointed nonetheless.

As he walked down the path through the inner sect, he wondered what it would feel like to see Elder Fang again.

The last time he had personally interacted with the man had been on the day that he had destroyed his father’s spear.

He had no desire to see him ever again, but he would handle himself with dignity; that was more crucial than ever.

According to Shuhua, Fang had taken a break from overseeing the training of group Jade for a few days after the duel with Elder Sun, then he had reappeared as if nothing had happened.

In truth, losing a duel itself was not a reason to disappear, but the way he had lost, and the accusations from Elder Sun, basically calling him a coward for not being on the frontlines, had apparently caused quite the stir.

To Jianyi, none of that mattered. He had the parts of his father’s spear in his storage ring and would eventually find the time to rebuild it—not to use it; that was not possible after such a break, but to put it back on the wall of whatever home he would eventually make for himself.

He didn’t need it to honor his father, but it was important, anyway.

“Junior brother!”

He turned to the voice, pulled out of his thoughts. It was one of the inner disciples working as guards of the inner sect.

Jianyi nodded to the young woman respectfully. “Senior sister, how may I help you?”

“Easy, do well today. I have bet 100 contribution points on you winning all your spars today!” she said with a slightly predatory smile.

Jianyi looked at her, eyes wide. He struggled to process what he had heard. Betting? On him? Why?

To gain time, he bowed. “Thank you for your trust, sister. May I ask, why am I worthy of your attention?”

She chuckled. “Well, after Elder Sun’s bea… win over Elder Fang, we were reminded where the real power in the sect lies; and if you are his student, it seems obvious that you will follow in his footsteps.”

His gut clenched.

He had been relatively calm about the assessment, felt well prepared. But this… This meant that his performance would not just be about himself alone; no, it would reflect on Elder Sun as well.

When he had left the compound just minutes ago, Minghao had wished him well but done so quite calmly, not indicating that the assessment was important.

Maybe he had just tried to shield Jianyi. Maybe sect politics…

Stop!

He calmed his thoughts and took a deep breath.

Never had Elder Sun or Minghao ever indicated that they cared about what others thought, nor that they expected him to do anything but his best.

He would give it his all anyway. There was no reason to panic now.

Smiling, he bowed again to the guard, who looked at him curiously. “Thank you again for your trust. I will do my best to be worthy of it.”

With that, he continued his journey down the mountain to the outer sect.

He had confidence in his abilities and the progress he had made; there was no reason—and certainly no benefit—in getting nervous now.

***

As Jianyi neared the training ground, he felt as if he were being pulled back in time. Almost exactly six months ago, he had stood on a field with lots of other hopeful youths, looking at a dais with Elder Gao Yingxue standing on it, ready to address them all.

To the side, half a dozen inner disciples stood in a row, trying to look impassive, but one or two of them couldn’t quite stop themselves from grinning. Jianyi wasn’t sure what to make of it.

One of the ones looking relaxed and unperturbed was Liu Guangming, the disciple who had been part of the duo that had led Jianyi and the other hopefuls through the day of the acceptance test.

Jianyi found his friends sitting on the stands surrounding the training grounds and joined them. All of them looked stressed—more so than he would have expected. Especially Hong was pale and clammy.

He had barely sat down with them when Shuhua grabbed him by the arm. “Jianyi, we just heard: Yan Ping is dead, died on the frontlines.”

“What?” Jianyi was prepared for many things in that moment, but not for this kind of news. Yan Ping had, of course, been the inner disciple who had been with them on the trip where Jun had died.

“Yes, apparently, he volunteered right after… You know. And now he is dead.”

He nodded slowly, more to acknowledge that he had heard than anything else.

It all came down to demons, in the end. Always…

He had been far from the last to enter the field, and it took a few minutes until the ancient-looking Elder on the dais spoke up, her slightly raspy voice easily carrying across the grounds.

“Good morning, I hope you have been using the last six months well. If not, we will find out today. Every year, we change what we do specifically during the mid-year assessment. What doesn’t change is the purpose: making sure we sift out those who do not have what it takes to become defenders of the Empire, be it through lack of talent or lack of will.

“We cannot waste energy on cultivators who won’t be able to rise to the occasion…” The look with which she observed the crowd did not leave any doubt about the disdain she had for those people.

“However, those that perform well—and that means not just in absolute terms, but also relative to your starting position—will be rewarded handsomely.” There was the faintest start of some excited murmuring among the disciples until her raised eyebrow led to utter silence.

“You should use the day, not just to show us how you have progressed, but also to observe your brother and sister disciples. For one, you might learn something, and two, we will soon start forming circles of disciples—understanding those around you can help you form a view of with whom you would like to be grouped.”

This time, the buzzing could not be so easily suppressed, and the elder seemed to accept it as she observed the youths before her.

“Today’s test will comprise two blocks. First, we will test your fitness, for which you will have the pleasure of returning to two of the challenges from your intake day. The second part will be focused on your abilities with the spear.”

There was some groaning at the reveal of a physical fitness test, but nobody dared to do so openly.

Jianyi was more than fine with this. His physical fitness was incomparable to the ravaged body he had in the aftermath of his gate-opening accident.

He nodded to himself. This would go very differently from how it did six months ago.
