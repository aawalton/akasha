Figuring Milla should know what happened, Luke filled her in through a brief series of messages, and she said she would look into the guild Challa mentioned. Luminous Lark was a stupid name. Savannah was right in that regard, but a stupid name was not a reason to take them for weak fools. Perhaps it was even named like that just to throw people off! Devious indeed!

Not caring all that much whether Ana joined The Band or not, Luke left them to their own devices and headed off in a different direction, the one Lee fled toward. Catching up to the little bastard was unlikely, but it didn't hurt to try. Sending a message that you want someone to die when they're in the middle of almost being killed by another Integrated and a dragon is just poor form, and some consequences were in order.

Even so, speed was not something he focused on as he sauntered down the street, looking for more items to enchant. The reason for the walk was to check out the new possibilities opened up by reaching level three in his profession. That was more important than a vermin like Lee.

Soon, Luke stopped in his tracks.

"Bingo."

A closed clothing store. Peering in through the window, it became apparent that it hadn't been open in some time. Another victim of the new credits-based economy, perhaps. Or monsters, judging by the broken-down windows. No, not monsters. A shoe, along with other trash, showed it was just another boring looting scenario. The empty racks inside were another clue, but not all the clothes were gone.

Faux Leather Jacket of Flow: Provides a slight increase in muscle activation.

Luke put it on. A difficult task, since it was several sizes too small. After jumping around a little and shadow-boxing, then slamming his staff into the floor when failing to execute a bojutsu stance, he came to the conclusion that he didn't feel any difference whatsoever. Perhaps a more fighting-oriented Integrated could feel the difference, but he couldn't.

Flower-Patterned Pink Ankle Socks of Soothed Nerves: Provides a slight increase in steadfastness.

Nothing. Nothing at all.

"Bummer," Luke told the deserted store as he blew a thin layer of dust off a rack of jewelry. Since they hadn't been taken in the looting when so much other stuff had, he figured it was nothing more than useless junk, but it gave him an idea.

What if jewelry slots didn't take away from clothing or armor slots? If he were able to enchant more pieces of equipment, that would make a lot of difference. At the moment, a lot of the enchantments were somewhat useless, but that would change. Luke just knew it. The attribute enchantments were good even now, especially since he could stack them together. Come to think of it, there was no reason for him not to enchant every piece of gear for all members of the guild. Except for the time it would take to do so. A lot of time. Maybe a few pieces would suffice.

Plastic Silver-Colored Bracelet of Health: A slight increase in maximum health.

Nope, it didn't work. Stupid enchantments wouldn't go over the limit he'd found, not even when he wore things that made him look stupid. Such a shame. Luke didn't mind looking a little foolish if it meant being stronger.

"Well, well," he told the store as he continued rummaging and filling his inventory with every little piece of clothing, jewelry, and miscellaneous crap he could find. Before leaving, he'd tried the other new enchantments but found no effect whatsoever. Perhaps it would be best to just push toward level four right away. With everything he'd just found, he didn't doubt he could get there if he got a few days of peace and quiet.

Laughing and shaking his head at the absurd idea of taking it easy and not having to rush from place to place for several days, Luke turned to regard the store. Nothing remained. For a brief moment, he considered the ethical implications of stealing all that stuff, but then he shrugged and headed back out onto the street. No one would be coming for any of the things he'd taken.

By now, it even felt like he might be able to get some shuteye. Judging by the graying skies, dawn wasn't far off. This meant that if he wanted sleep, he would have to go get it soon. Still, even as he walked back and kept a lookout for Lee returning, Luke pushed a thread of mana to wrap around his body, keeping it hidden underneath the robe. If he kept it up at all times, the reach would improve. That was the single most important truth in growing stronger. For Luke, reach was everything. Despite his intent honed in on Threads of Mana, that didn't mean he slacked with enchantments. Walking, pushing a thread, looking for Lee, and enchanting at the same time put his mind under a lot of stress, especially considering how tired he was, but in the end, it paid off.

Boon of Potential Grants You an Attribute Point (+1 Focus).

Boon of Potential Grants You an Attribute Point (+1 Willpower).

Little by little, Luke was growing stronger. In eight days, when that countdown reached zero, no matter what sort of ominous event happened, being stronger would always be better.

The next morning, after having slept for two whole hours, Luke found himself feeling rested. It was strange needing so little sleep, but he wouldn't complain. He sat on a new couch in the main chamber of headquarters, watching the rest of the guild members wake up. Some came stumbling out of their rooms, appearing by the plaques along the wall, red-eyed and groaning, while others woke from having slept right there on the floor. Judging by the look of them, not everyone enjoyed the same diminished need for sleep as Luke. Or perhaps they'd just partied a little too hard.

"You look chipper this morning," Milla said. She sat next to him on the sofa, taking in the scene before her with a stern expression on her face that seemed to put fear into her guild members. Luke knew it was just a front.

"Slept a little," Luke answered, assuming his own scowl. No one would beat him in a look-off, least of all his sister. "What about you?"

"I think we're going to do great things here. No matter who is trying to stand in our way."

"Confident," Luke noted. "Have you decided what you want to do about the countdown?"

"We have to share it."

"What do you mean?" Luke asked, his scowl breaking into a wide grin when he saw Ray emerge from his room looking like a withered ghost. His new and improved physique did not mean handling alcohol any better. That much was obvious.

Milla followed Luke's gaze and couldn't help but laugh when she saw Ray. Once they'd settled down, she resumed their conversation. "I've told everyone I can think of. DIA, other guilds, even the cops here in NY, the few who are still here. This is not something we should keep close to the chest."

"Does anyone know what it is?"

"No," Milla said, shaking her head. "Not yet. ISG is doing some preliminary testing, whatever that means, and my old employer has people out looking for Intruders. They have a Seeker, apparently."

"That's good," Luke said. "It shouldn't just be on Curtis."

"What about you?"

"I'm an Enchanter now. My Seeker profession sort of got combined with one of my skills. It was weird. Still works as before, but I never sensed the Intruders."

"That's strange."

"It is," Luke agreed.

"Guess we'll find out one way or another," Milla said, gesturing to the wall where they'd placed a large digital clock that counted down to match the one Curtis found. "At least we have a week to prepare."

Priyah entered the room with Ana in tow.

"You let her join?" Luke asked, nodding toward the pair.

"Sure, why not? Her class will be useful, and she seems bright enough. I'm not about to accept anyone stumbling into us, but some people deserve another chance."

"What's her class?"

Dot: "Hey, Luke. I'm back in Chicago. Just thought you should know. How is Elijah doing?"

Elijah was out cold on the floor.

"You can just look in the guild interface yourself, little brother," Milla said.

Luke: "Elijah is doing well. Be careful."

"Luke?"

Luke looked back up at his sister. "Sorry, what?"

"Distracted much?" she asked.

He stuck out his tongue, which made it a little difficult to speak. "Bischy musch?"

Milla laughed. "You're such a child."

"Part of my job as the youngest sibling," Luke said, going over the interface. "Void Mage? Sounds ominous. Aren't we supposed to be fighting the void? She's obviously a villain!"

"Keep your enemies closer," Milla said, matching Luke's grin to show she understood that he wasn't serious.

"Speaking of enemies," Luke said, stretching his arms over his head in a yawn. "I need to heal you again?"

"Because I'm your enemy?"

He shook his head and assumed a look of utter seriousness, trying hard not to break into laughter as he spoke, "Illnesses are the enemies of all healers."

Luke failed in keeping it together, but once Milla had punched his arm a few times, she allowed him to take a look.

"It has deteriorated again," Luke said, keeping his eyes closed, a hand on his sister's forearm. "I don't get it."

People were waking up, and some loud discussions were taking place, with some arguing about which place to order food from, while others tried to make them understand that there were zero restaurants operating in the city as of that moment. Well, perhaps a few were keeping up the good fight, but it was doubtful they did deliveries. One nice piece of news Luke caught from that conversation, as he tried to make sense of his sister's illness, was that the Internet was back. That was something, at least.

Irritation grew in him, and he was about to erupt and shout at everyone to keep it down, but Milla caught on before he got the chance. "Just do what you did last time. You don't need to fix anything more than that today. We have plenty of time later."

"Fine," Luke grumbled, repeating the procedure he'd used to restore her atrophied mana channels. It worked this time, too, which was something at least. "All done."

"Thank you," a new voice said. It was Lana, Milla's sweetheart, who plopped down on the couch next to them. "Could you maybe help me with something as well?"
