The main deck was everything Trevor and Adabelle had hoped it would be. It was open air with the thick white envelope above them. While it was safely ensconced by the hull, it could be clearly seen from below. There were several ropes and struts underneath it, which looked like a safety precaution for whenever it needed to be deflated.

There were more people here than there had been in the dining room. Most of them were lounging in groups, situated by the railing so they could look down at the sea. The area was set up with plenty of chairs to lounge on with tables nearby, and a few sets were taken up by guests who were reading or had relaxed so much that they had fallen asleep.

Trevor paused to watch some of the games going on in the middle of the main deck. The Red Dream had a long stretch of sand with poles for throwing wracor shoes around, though there were dents in the wood around the areas from people missing, and a few boards and bean bags for cornhole.

“Man, I haven’t seen anyone play that in a long while,” Trevor said as he watched a little demon girl throw one of the bean bags.

It only made it halfway to the board, and she turned around and looked up at who the courier assumed was her father. He nodded and made a gesture to go get it, and the little girl complied. She ran up to grab the bean bag and, from there, tossed it again. This time, it made it onto the edge of the wood, just barely, and the onlookers clapped and cheered appropriately.

“Ah, backyard Texas toss,” Adabelle replied with a nod. “Wayne has a couple of those boards, but I haven’t seen them in ages. I had forgotten they existed.”

“Backyard Texas toss,” Trevor repeated with a chuckle.

“That’s… not what it’s called, is it?” the demonkin pouted as she crossed her arms. “That Wayne. He’s always giving us the wrong name for Earth things when he thinks he can get away with it. What’s it actually called?”

“Cornhole.”

Adabelle hummed, thinking it over. “I think, in this instance, I like backyard Texas toss more.”

“To be fair, that might be something people in Texas call it,” Trevor offered with a shrug. “Cornhole is just its official name, but I’ve heard it called bean bag toss more often than not. Not saying that Wayne isn’t full of it, because we both know he is, I’m just saying that it’s a plausible name. I think it’s one of those things that was different from state to state.”

“Sometimes it's hard to imagine a country that is basically cobbled together from a bunch of smaller countries,” the noblewoman mused. “But it is what it is. There are stranger things out there. Would you like to play later if it’s available?”

“Even though I’m sure I’ll get my butt handed to me, sure, I’ll play with you,” he answered with a smile before leaning in to whisper conspiratorially. “Maybe I’ll hide my Summoned tattoo and we can trick some hapless rubes into playing doubles with us. We’ll be unstoppable!”

Adabelle giggled at the idea before looping her arm around his. “Don’t be bad,” she jokingly demanded.

“Nah, this is Vacation Trevor,” he replied with a shake of his head. “I'm allowed to take a break from being good. It’s taken a while for my evil coils to warm up since they’ve been dormant for too long, but they’re getting there. I’m going to take over the world any day now.”

“Being married to the man who has to rule the world doesn’t sound fun. You wouldn’t have a lot of time for me.”

“Oh, you’re right. Never mind,” he replied, holding up his arms in the shape of an X.

“You know, Vacation Trevor sounds like the same one that tried to keep me away from my sword sister. Remember? Back during our first trip to Eskretet?”

“You're right, he does.”

“Yes, he does,” Adabelle agreed, tapping her chin in thought. “Now, to be fair, I don’t think you were on any quests then. Other than the king’s quest, I mean. You did extol the virtues of being selfish… Did you show me your true colors all the way back then and I simply missed it?”

“Missed it? Please,” the courier scoffed before gently bumping his hip against hers. “You don’t miss anything, lady. If I remember correctly, you even went so far as to call them my evil clutches. You were hoping for more, weren’t you?”

“More? Of you refusing to relinquish your fair captive unless your demands were met? As though I enjoyed being a mere damsel in distress? Perish the thought,” she returned, bumping his hip in return. “I certainly didn’t think it was really hot of you to be assertive like that. No, sir, I did not. Also, I did say that, but I was playing off of Lailah. She called them your evil clutches first.”

“Oh, yeah, she did say that,” he said as he thought back. Then, he chuckled.

“What’s so funny?” Adabelle asked, arching an eyebrow at him.

“You found it sexy when I stood up for myself, did you?” Trevor challenged teasingly.

“I’m not sure how to tell you this, honey, but yes, I am attracted to you when you’re being confident,” the noblewoman told him as she let him go and started walking towards the railing of the ship. Adabelle’s tail brushed against his side down to his leg before she looked back over her shoulder with a knowing smile. “And that road goes both ways. It’s why you can’t get me out of your head, son of Ander. Because I’m always confident, and you can’t help yourself.”

“Well, you see, I…” the courier trailed off. He watched the practiced way she moved, ever graceful, and her tail beckoned him to follow. There was no refuting her words, so he didn’t. “Yeah, you got me. I really can’t help myself.”

Adabelle laughed as he caught up with her, wrapping her tail around his wrist, and guided him to the railing. The gold-painted barrier came up to their waist, and Trevor looked downwards towards the water. They weren’t nearly as high up as he thought they would be, but he wasn’t sure what their altitude was. Luckily, he could find out.

“Quest Master, how high up are we right now?” he asked aloud.

[[You are currently flying at an altitude of 850 feet above sea level.

“Eight-fifty,” Trevor said with a whistle. Clay jumped down and stuck their head through the railing to get a better view from solid ground. “Yeah, I thought we would be around a thousandish feet up. Thanks, Quest Master. And for all the additional information, including about Belgium.”

“That’s one of the countries, isn’t it?” Adabelle asked.

“Yeah. It should be coming up in a few minutes,” he answered.

While Trevor watched the waves, she was looking at the sky. He shifted his gaze towards the clouds above. They weren’t very dense, and there was more than enough light filtering through to illuminate the world below. It also didn’t seem like there was anything interesting to see. Not to his sight, anyway.

“Ohhh,” he said as the realization struck. “You’re looking at the magic, aren’t you? Like back with Croal.”

“I am,” Adabelle confirmed, smiling as she patted her husband’s arm. “Have you noticed that the wind isn’t anything more than a gentle breeze despite our speed?”

Trevor blinked as he looked at the demonkin’s hair. It was being gently tousled when it should have been whipping all over the place. Having experienced riding quickly on wracors and Clay, he certainly expected more wind than this. He just hadn’t noticed it until she brought it up.

The look on his face was all the confirmation Adabelle needed. “I can see Braidley’s magic at work. It’s a lot more intricate than I thought it was going to be,” she explained as she leaned against him. Reaching out, she started pointing along the Red Dream’s hull. “The wind that we’re flying through is matching our speed and following the lines of the ship perfectly. It’s why things are so gentle here, like a regular breeze.”

“Huh, that’s pretty neat. Who would you say is better at controlling the air, Braidley or Croal?”

“Croal, definitely, but being a dragon is cheating,” she answered without hesitation. “They’re born with an innate understanding of the world and the magic in which to change it. It takes years and years of practice for a Summoned to match them, though it’s certainly easier than wizardry. There are elves who have studied the arcane arts for literal centuries without matching the ease at which a dragon can cast.”

“That sucks,” Trevor replied. “But, on the other hand, magic is cool.”

“It certainly is,” Adabelle agreed. “What Stammy said about the Red Dream also comes to mind. About how it was custom built in accordance to Braidley’s needs, I mean. It certainly looks like he knows the airship inside and out based on how tightly his Power clings to it despite being, I assume, on the bridge. And if he does this for eighteen, maybe twenty hours a day, well, he’s certainly got a high Magic and Mana stat.”

“He’s probably been at it for a while now,” the courier said. “Having an airship opens up all kinds of avenues for quests. Not just courier quests, either; there are tons of applications out there. I imagine there’s no shortage of them in Aletander.”

“You’re right. Tosa might not have a lot of them for you, but they’re all over the place in Eskretet or any other capital that embraces Summoned.”

“Yeah, I’ve seen that first hand.” Pausing for a moment, Trevor looked away from the clouds to regard his wife. “Would you prefer to be introduced as simply Adabelle while we’re outside of Lucent?”

She looked at him curiously. “What brought that about?”

“You introduced yourself as Lady Adabelle, well, your full name, to both Tanazi and Mital, but you only called yourself Adabelle to Stammy,” Trevor explained. “For Mital it made sense because he’s, you know, the mayor of Paladona and you’re in the same position, but I was wondering about situations where the other side isn’t nobility.”

“Well, first off, I need to inform you of something,” she said as her tail gave his wrist a small squeeze.

“Go for it.”

Adabelle turned slightly to check the main deck for eavesdroppers before speaking. “Stammy is a nobleman. Or at least someone very influential. I’m not quite sure how he fits into Aletanderian politics or if he is a merchant, wizard, or someone else of high status, but he most certainly is.”

“I did think he was wearing rather fine clothing,” Trevor mused as he looked back down at the ocean below.

“Not only him, but everyone in his family, too. Including the children, even though they’re still growing,” the noblewoman continued. “That, plus the way he spoke and carried himself. He’s clearly a man of means and power.”

“But when he introduced himself, it was just Stammy. Then his wife, Dee. So you were basically just matching his energy? He’s not outing himself as a noble, so neither are you?”

“Basically,” she answered, smiling as he understood. “He approached us and set the terms, which was one of friendly equality. I could have rebuked him and asked that he call me Lady Adabelle, but I saw no reason for it. We are strangers in a strange land, after all, and the only titles that would really matter this far from home are Master Summoned. Though, now that I’m thinking about it, I’m not sure how they would address us in Aletander.”

“We can always ask. I’m sure Braidley would be happy to tell us all about it. You know, if he comes out of the bridge. If he doesn’t, then Stammy seems approachable.”

Adabelle nodded. “I’m sure he would have a lot to offer on the matter, as would most people here on the Red Dream.”

Trevor turned and looked at the mostly demon crowd before chuckling. “Yeah. I think it’s safe to say there’ll be at least one or two experts we can consult.”

“Indeed. But, to answer your actual question, as long as you introduce me as your wife then I don’t particularly care,” Adabelle said, looking at her courier with faintly glowing eyes. “Adabelle, Lady Adabelle, the most wonderful woman in the world; I’m fine with all of them. You already know I tend to step in quickly if I realize we are talking to other nobility, so if I don’t do that then it’s up to you.”

“Up to me? And you put ‘the most wonderful woman in the world’ directly into my mouth?” he asked with a laugh.

“Do you think differently, Trevor Anderson?” she challenged, raising an eyebrow.

“Not in the slightest.”

“Good,” she replied, patting his chest. “But also, please don’t, I was only making a point.”

“Nah, it’s too late,” Trevor said, shaking his head. “You handed it to me, and that’s the end of everything. I’m Trevor, and this is my wife, the most wonderful woman in the world. But does that mean people have to shorten that to Wonderful? I imagine it’s a bit daft and tacky to ask them to call you Woman.”

“While it is an adept description, it is rather lacking as far as names go,” Adabelle agreed. “Like lady, for example, and yet someone continues to use it to tease me.”

“And it’s never going to change, because it’s the first time you smiled at me,” Trevor said haughtily. She gave him a look. “Okay, it was a smirk, but it was a bold play that worked out.”

“I suppose it did, Earth Man,” she replied.

“Huh?”

“That’s how you introduced yourself to me, remember?” Adabelle asked. “Trevor Anderson, Earth Man. Maybe I should have gone with that instead of the son of Ander.”

“I appreciate you leaving that in the past where it belongs,” he said with a laugh. Something new appeared in the horizon, catching his eye, and he turned to look at it. “Oh, we’re coming up on Belgium. Which, if you can believe it, is different from the one on my old world.”

“What a coincidence. Not just that two places are named the same thing, but also that you find a distraction from the moment I brought up Earth Man,” she teased.

“Man, that’s two whole coincidences. Can we make it three, little dragon slayer?”

Adabelle laughed before leaning in to kiss his cheek. “I’ll drop it for now, but no promises.”

“Appreciate ya, love,” Trevor said before wrapping his arm around her shoulders. They turned their attention back to the island, ignoring the others who started to come and see it, too.
