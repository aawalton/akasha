Sandra went straight to her room when she got back to Holding Lys. She didn't detour to see her mother, to tell her anything about meeting Anya's cousin. She didn't stop by the Seneschal's office, or report her safe return to anyone. She was supposed to, but she didn't want to, not yet.

They'd treated her like a child. They all had. It was bad enough to have Anya worrying about her well-being, her sister had gone from sad and bothered by what their mother's court thought about her to someone who worried about everyone else being safe and happy.

Finding out that Seb had been letting her take bacon specifically left for her, not bacon she managed to steal, had both hurt and made her happy to know they'd known and not tried to stop her. Finding out they were all in on it was even more surprising.

Even Gwen and Cara had treated her like she was still a kid. She wasn't clueless, she could tell her questions had annoyed the woman, that she'd made an effort to keep answering calmly rather than tell her that was enough.

Yes she was younger than all of them, yes she wasn't past the Wall and they all were. But that spindly-looking instructor had said everything was safe. She could have explored on her own and had nothing bad happen. She could have slept on her own, she didn't need to share a suite with Fay and Briana. Not that she was the actual reason for that, there was no space in the dorm for them as it had turned out.

And how dare Anya use Sam as her example of someone watching her train or spar and thinking she was an example of talent. He loved proving her wrong, showing her that he knew better. He'd grown up around Engineers, she'd only had access to the few bits of information she could find on the EDN without being caught.

He wouldn't care about her sparring, he wouldn't compliment her. He wouldn't stand around and watch her for the sake of watching her. He was Sam.

Sandra realized she was glaring at herself in the mirror and took a deep breath. She'd gotten distracted thinking about the visit to Scattered Creek. Looking at her bed she saw she'd dumped her changes of clothes on it, she'd have to put those up later, she could tidy up after herself after all.

Turning back to the mirror, Sandra went through the motions of tidying herself up and ensuring her makeup was appropriate for Court. She didn't mind putting in the effort, even if she rarely bothered anymore. It didn't last long when she was working with Yshe. Maybe she should reconsider though, it might- No, she wasn't letting her thoughts wander, not there.

She had to go let Sovereign Lys know that her elder daughter had met her cousins and had learned about her heritage. And then inform her that despite knowing the answers herself, she was sworn to not provide any details but that Lady Lysanya Syl would happily tell her if she came to visit in six months. Or that Holding Syl's Seer was available to do so.

Sandra knew her mother was going take the message poorly. She knew that she'd be spared her wrath because it wasn't her fault and she was her daughter. She knew she could have explained about the Fae well enough to answer the first set of questions, but everyone else had agreed she shouldn't talk about it with anyone.

She wasn't a child. Now she just had to figure out how to get others to see that, to see her as a responsible young woman. To actually look at- No.

Morgan hesitated as he stared at the closed door. He knew he wasn't supposed to be here, wasn't supposed to even know where this door was. Students and instructors were housed separately, noticeably so. Access to the instructor side was supposed to be handled by the Doorkeeper, not by finding the right path to follow through the grounds.

The path was there for emergencies, for the unforeseen situations where a portal simply wouldn't do. It was meant as a way for the staff to reach the main grounds directly, not for a student to use.

It didn't matter. He was here and he had to make a decision. Knock on the door or turn around and return to the dorm. He hadn't intruded yet, he could still change his mind.

"Why are you here?"

Morgan didn't move, didn't breathe, didn't react at all to the voice coming from behind him. The fact that he managed to remain still was testament to the training he'd received from the speaker among others. The fact that he'd been discovered before he made himself known was simply proof of how much he still had to learn.

"I suspect you know why I am here, Lord Percivus."

"I can think of more than one possible reason you might wish to speak to me, none of which would require you knocking on my chamber door in the evening."

"The twins."

"That narrows things slightly."

"Might we speak inside? These are not matters that should be discussed where others might hear."

Morgan tried not to flinch as Lord Percivus stepped past him and opened the door. He knew he was safe, that there was no risk to his being here. He also knew how dangerous his teacher was, both in combat and in influence within the Royal family. The twins, and their parents, merely knew he was someone of significance, that he held no official rank beyond leader of those trained in Bladesong.

Morgan knew, was expected to know, more. Lord Percivus was an old friend of His Eminence, had direct access and familiarity with the actual head of the Family. He had no title because he claimed none, he had no rank because he needed none.

"I will not ask how you found your way here. You will not use the route in the future. If you wish to speak we will arrange proper meetings."

"Yes Lord Percivus," Morgan said and bowed his head. "You are aware of the discord between the twins, and with their parents?"

"I am aware that they have very different views. I am aware that Lady Phila harbours beliefs that remain toxic even after decades."

"Is there truth to her opinion of those with Emerald eyes?"

"Would there be truth to accuse those with Sapphire eyes of hostility and dreams of grandeur?" Percivus answered. "Truth of Amethyst eyes being stubborn, of being more openly emotional, or of trying to appease others? Is there truth to call the Distaff branch one that observes and meddles while claiming neutrality?"

Morgan frowned. Lord Percivus was describing the twins and their parents, then describing his heritage as well. All were true, yet all lacked nuance or clarification that explained the situation.

"She holds a true opinion of individuals, yet applies it as a generality." Morgan ventured. "She views Anya as part of something she might not actually be."

When he got no reply he continued, trying to reason his way through what had not been said.

"There was an Emerald line. There no longer is, at least not one that is recognized. Anya is likely of that line and represents something Lady Phila wishes to forget or eliminate. Lord Bern recognizes the same history, but cares more about his daughter than past grievances."

"Go on."

"Bern sees Anya as a threat to his potential status. Gwen's Path and Gifts are not those that he would see as a challenge. Cara will follow Gwen where she leads, will support and protect her even if it means confronting Bern and fighting her geas. Keldren will do Bern's will without question."

"And?"

"Bern wishes to defeat Anya, to prove himself superior in every way. Discrediting her and ensuring she is not seen as family means even should she win in combat it matters not. She cannot be named above him within the current generations if she is not family."

"That explains his stance. It does not explain why you are here."

Morgan nodded and stood straighter. He hadn't taken more than a few steps into the chamber, had not looked up from the ground. He did not wish to see, did not wish to know. Some knowledge was beyond his current status, knowing how his teacher lived would serve no purpose.

"Lady Phila would have Named Gwen to have her way had I not interrupted."

"You involved yourself?"

"Not by intent. I was not aware of their meeting in our dorm. I was fetching a change of clothing and walked in to an argument. Gwen's Name was spoken as I opened the door. The words that followed were platitudes, a desire that she do her best at school and not make any foolish mistakes. I am certain they are not the words that would have been spoken."

"How can you be certain?"

"Gwen ordered Cara to leave, phrased in such a way I was knocked aside by her haste. Gwen bid Lady Phila, not her mother, enjoy dinner and her trip home before stepping past me. She mouthed thanks as our eyes met."

"Likely a valid assumption then."

Morgan fought not to smile at the faint praise. He knew he had failed at the sound of Lord Percivus chuckling. He had one last thing to say, to ask. Hopefully the answer would be one he could act on.

"You know quite well where my family stands among the Royals. You know why I am part of this team, why I have not distanced myself from Bern despite the implication of choice."

"Yes? Out with it."

"Am I making a mistake to choose one over the other? To hope for reconciliation between their views? Am I positioning myself on the wrong side?"

"You know I cannot answer that for you. Even if I could foretell fates I would not give you the knowledge. I will only suggest this. Do not make an enemy of any of the lines you have connection to. You need not be their close friend, but do not find yourself unable to speak with them should there be need."

Morgan had hoped for more yet had expected less. The answer pointed to the hardest path, especially with history already against him. Bern viewed him as a tool, as part of his arsenal, already aligned and guaranteed.

He knew how Cara felt about him, she might think she hid it well, but he knew what to look for. He knew Gwen was indifferent to him at best. As for Anya? There was no connection, nothing in common. Her husband might be more amenable, he was observant enough to find common ground in that domain.

"Thank you My Lord. I will see myself out."

"The Doorkeeper will return you to the training hall, no need to spend an hour when seconds will do."
