“You know me?” Lucian asked from beneath his hood.

The entire auction hall seemed to pause. Even Varetus fell silent, watching the exchange.

“Not personally,” Veronica replied. “But I know enough.”

Another ripple of murmurs spread through the upper balconies. The sight of those three mages appearing to validate the spell-compression notes was still fresh in everyone’s minds. Now, a young woman from a mid-level balcony had called one of them by name.

Gwen gripped the edge of the couch. “Veronica, what are you doing?”

“Calling in a favor,” Veronica said easily. Her voice was still amplified; everyone in the hall could hear her.

Nasha whistled beside her. “Well, this definitely woke me up.”

“What is it you want?” Lucian asked.

Veronica smiled. “I trust Ballamus is doing well?”

Lucian seemed to note that she had dropped the title Archmage. “…He is in good health. He has been in high spirits as of late.”

“That’s wonderful,” Veronica said. “I don’t want to take up too much of your time, but in return for the matter you and I both know about, I would like his sponsorship for this lot.”

This time, the murmurs grew too loud to ignore.

Varetus’s face stiffened. Harris, standing behind him, stared at Veronica with sudden recognition, his eyes widening as if a piece had snapped into place. The three commerce groups immediately turned to their aides, presumably asking them to dig up information about her. Even the auctioneer looked momentarily lost, unsure whether to close the current bid.

Lucian didn’t answer immediately. The two mages beside him shifted slightly, looking toward him for guidance. For several seconds, the hall fell quiet beneath the illusion of the floating airship and false morning sky.

Finally, Lucian asked, “Are you certain this is what you wish to request?”

“Yes.”

“This request may limit any future favors.”

“That’s for Ballamus to decide, is it not?”

Lucian studied her for another moment, then lowered his head. “Very well.”

Varetus immediately stepped forward in his balcony. “What is the meaning of this? The current bid belongs to the Ivy Mage Tower.”

“The item has not been sold,” Lucian replied, not looking at him. Instead, he turned toward the auctioneer. “The Grand Auction has not declared the final bid.”

Varetus turned toward the stage with fury. Nearly a minute had passed since his bid, but the auctioneer had been so stunned by the arrival of the three mages that he had never finalized the sale.

Varetus’s expression darkened. “You intend to bid under Archmage Ballamus’s name?”

“I do,” Lucian said. “We bid twenty-three million.”

The auctioneer jolted, then quickly recovered. “Tw-twenty-three million from the representatives of Archmage Ballamus!”

The hall erupted into gasps, complaints, whispers, and hurried conversations.

Varetus gripped the balcony railing, his jaw tightening. For a moment, he looked prepared to bid again. Then his gaze shifted toward the three mages and settled on the eight-star badge gleaming on Lucian’s chest.

No one else raised a paddle. Besides him, only a few others wanted the airship. None of the larger merchant groups wanted it since it didn’t benefit them. No, the only ones remaining were those bidding in self-interest. But now, their interests had changed; no one wanted to compete against an archmage and draw their ire.

The auctioneer waited cautiously before speaking. “Twenty-three million… going once!”

No answer.

“Going twice!”

Still nothing.

“And sold! The second personal airship goes to the three beautiful women in balcony sixty-nine!”

The illusion above the auction house shimmered brightly one final time before dissolving into pale motes of light. The false sky dimmed; the morning clouds faded until the auction house’s original ceiling slowly returned.

Lucian glanced down at Veronica once. Then the three mages vanished.

The auctioneer cleared his throat several times before regaining control of the room. “Honored guests, with that, tonight’s Pelotema Grand Auction has officially concluded. Purchased lots will be prepared for their buyers in our secured collection halls. We thank you all for attending.”

The lights slowly brightened.

Conversations erupted throughout the hall.

Maftem’s various factions were all looking toward Veronica now. Many turned to their assigned auction servants, already asking questions about her identity.

Gwen stared blankly at the empty air where Lucian had been, then toward the stage as the auctioneer departed.

Finally, she turned to Veronica. “Did you really just make an archmage buy you an airship?”

Veronica shrugged. “I didn’t make him do anything. It was a favor, and I called it in.”

“But… doesn’t that defeat the purpose of hiding your identity? Now everyone knows you have some kind of connection to Archmage Ballamus. Not to mention those three mages. Lucian, you said his name was?”

“You’re betting that no one will dare mess with you now that you have an archmage backing you, aren’t you?” Nasha asked, tilting her head as though she had already read Veronica’s mind.

Veronica slowly stood. “I wasn’t expecting the Mage Association to make contact like this, but it works in my favor. With Lucian here, no one will dare touch me for the time being. Everyone will probably connect me to the notes now, but that doesn’t matter anymore.”

She glanced over at each balcony, her eyes meeting several of the others. In particular, Varetus glared at her, but she spared him a glance no longer than the rest.

Finally, she turned around, ignoring them all. “Let’s go collect our winnings, girls.”

The Ivy Mage Tower was quiet by the time Varetus returned to his office. The room was large and circular, lined with dark bookshelves that nearly reached the ceiling. A wide desk sat before a tall window, overlooking Maftem’s streets.

Most of the tower residents had already retired to their rooms, asleep, save for a few particularly studious mages who lingered in the lower libraries and research halls. But for now, inside this room, it was only him and Harris.

Varetus stood at the window, staring out into the night. “You said you recognized that woman?”

Harris nodded solemnly, standing straight by the door. “Yes, sir. She is the one I told you about before. The woman who humiliated me in the Starfault Archives.”

Varetus’s brow lowered. “The one chasing that fairy tale. Avalon, was it?”

“Yes.”

“And you are certain?”

“I didn’t realize it was her at first,” Harris admitted. “She was too far away during most of the auction, and I wasn’t focused on her balcony. But when she called out to Lucian Dane, I saw her clearly.”

Varetus hummed. His expression was more relaxed than it had been during the auction, but there was still a hint of tension beneath his eyes. “What is your evaluation of her?”

“I first assumed she was someone who got lucky. Perhaps she learned a spell or two from a renowned mage. Her stunt inside the archives was unlike anything I had ever seen, but I didn’t believe it made her important.” Harris paused, choosing his words carefully. “Once I realized it was her in that balcony, I looked through the catalogue again.”

“The catalogue?”

Harris nodded, though Varetus still didn’t look back. “She bought nearly every matching material described in Morgan’s book. Not perfectly, but the items were close enough, with a few substitutes. The main ones were the voidglass and lunar salt, though she failed to win the salt. She also acquired drakebone ash, which I assume is a substitute for a dragon ingredient.”

“Sounds like you don’t believe it is a coincidence.”

“I do not,” Harris said.

Varetus turned from the window and walked toward his seat behind the desk. He sat down, leaned forward, and steepled his fingers before him, his arms resting against the polished surface.

“When you told me what happened to you in the archives, I called you incompetent.”

Harris stood still, lips pressed tight, not daring to make a comment.

“That you, of all people, would be humiliated by some lesser mage. That your story was simply exaggerated.” Varetus stared straight ahead at the wall, not even regarding Harris directly. “But now… it seems I was mistaken.”

Harris remained silent.

“That same woman who humiliated you with some unique spell resembling balefire appeared as a VIP inside the auction. A commoner with unknown origins, but carrying a viscount’s signet, according to one of the auction servants. Not only did she steal the airship away from me, but she even called upon Association mages to assist her. Mages who directly represent Archmage Ballamus.”

His voice deepened slightly. “Those spell theory notes… those were probably hers, too.”

“The… notes?” Harris asked slowly.

“It is too convenient for those three mages to suddenly appear and act as guarantors for those auction items. The fact that she knew them leads me to believe they were doing her a favor. She is most likely the author, or someone selling the notes on the author’s behalf. If it is the former, then she is a genius. Someone who created a breakthrough in a dead field.” Varetus’s eyes narrowed. “That would explain why Archmage Ballamus has taken an interest in her.”

“That… seems reasonable.”

For the first time, Varetus looked directly at him. “You said you’ve read that book? The one about Avalon?”

Harris nodded. “Yes. I copied all of its contents after scrying that woman reading it.”

“Good,” Varetus said. “Your task for the next few days is to use the twenty-two million vix I had reserved for that airship and buy every material needed for Avalon.”

Harris’s eyes widened. “Sir… you believe Avalon is real?”

Varetus stood. “Who cares if it’s real? The fact that woman is interested in it means there is something significant there. This could be an opportunity of a lifetime. If this Avalon place is some disconnected dimension that only she is trying to reach, then there will be no issue if she happens to disappear during her trip.”

Harris’s eyes flickered with realization. Then his expression became more serious. “What if the Association mages accompany her?”

Varetus smiled. “Most communication artifacts don’t function properly inside disconnected dimensions. If they do accompany her, then we can take care of them quietly.”

“How?”

“Use your brain, Harris,” Varetus snapped.

Harris stiffened, unsure of his mistake.

“You are the lead appraiser of the Starfault Archives. With your authority and mine, we can close the library for a few days.”

“The… library?” Harris’s thoughts spun until he landed on the answer. His expression slowly changed. “Master, you don’t mean—”

“Precisely,” Varetus said, cutting him off. “We take the elementals with us. With the two of us and those guards, even Ballamus’s representatives will struggle to interfere. I don’t know what tier those cloaked figures truly were, but I doubt all three were eighth-tier. Perhaps the one in the middle was, but that doesn’t matter. A single eighth-tier mage won’t be enough to defeat the elementals.”

Harris stared at him.

The elemental guardians were not ordinary constructs. They were relics of the Starfault Archives, bound to protect its restricted floors. Even Harris, as lead appraiser, had never treated them as tools.

But if it were them, then few could match their strength. Especially not some weak, low, third-tier mage with a few fancy tricks.

“In the morning, contact the Assassin’s Guild,” Varetus said. “Have them track down and spy on this Veronica woman. Make it clear that she may be under the protection of Association mages, so they are not to move carelessly.”

“That will… probably be very costly, master,” Harris said.

“Just do what I said,” Varetus replied, his voice nearly a growl. “I already prepared to spend over twenty million vix tonight. If what you said about Avalon is true, then we will be taking home far more than that.”

Harris swallowed, then quickly composed himself with a slight bow.

“Yes, master.”
