First things first, Samantha needed to get rid of the force spell pressing and holding everyone in the room to the walls. She wasn’t going to play math with whether she could overcome it, and minimal success was perfectly fine for dispels, so she’d utilize [Alter Fate] for success.

She’d spent almost two seconds on very needed and productive information-gathering, so it was pretty much the strategy of: do a double-spell for the moment and hopefully stall out the lich while she set him up for the Big Plan. She was not going to be able to wedge it in before giving the lich a chance for another spell or attack. She decided the best bet was pocketing the casting to use for a [Deific Counterspell] on whatever the lich did. That would give her time and focus to react on a dime to any casting.

She cast her double-spell, first using Justified Dispel, as she had only one target, and it banned the spell successfully dispelled for ten seconds. She spoke a few words of incantation to materialize the spell and used [Alter Fate], wiping out the force field utterly.

Samantha flew forward across the room immediately, seeing the lich already casting. Meanwhile, all the combatants crammed up against the walls fell forward, a brief pause before bursting into action. She wasn’t focused on them, though.

With the incantations for fluid spellcraft still on her lips, reading the lich progressively, she had one other thing she could do, basically a free action. At best, a distraction, though. Shrugging internally, Samantha called upon Ring of the Cheater: Janky Physics and turned the stony dais the lich was standing on into a pool of acid.

The shadowy being dropped into it immediately with a splash that sent fizzing liquid up over the top. Despite this, as he was dropping, he was still casting his spell through the staff.

Samantha detected first that it was targeting her, and it was something destructive called Annihilate Flesh. Well, that was all fairly logical after pinning everyone. He likely thought it unlikely she could dispel the forcefield effect, after all.

Samantha utilized [Deific Counterspell] on it… and failed.

Oh shit! Oh fuck! Shit, shit, fucking shit-fuck!

Cursing continuously within herself, Samantha had the splittiest of split moments to decide whether to force it with yet another [Alter Fate] use. No. If she feasibly could, she needed to save those. Besides, if she really, really had to, she could resist the wounding itself with an automatic success. And she could gamble on other shit this way, so…

The spell materialized, single-target, as baleful necrotic energy attempting to disintegrate her body utterly. But as soon as it deigned to touch her, the little mirror floating around her flashed and then disintegrated into dust. Spell Reflector triggered, partially reflecting the maximum spell levels of 20. She took some sort of weak-ass necromantic destruction effect of spell level 3, instead. She resisted it utterly and took no wounds from it.

The spell reflected onto the lich with the remaining power, who had his own spell reflection… which thus reflected the spell yet again back to her, now at a slightly reduced level than it had hit the lich with — likely due to whatever rules governed his protection. Definitely not the same as her spell. At least she saw his reflection effect totally disappear, which was an indication that it only worked for one spell.

She sadly couldn’t use [Reflect Force] on it due to it being a direct target effect. No emanation point, no trajectory.

However, she could try Foolmaker’s absorption effect as a basic defensive action. Its item level was 12, which was less than the spell levels, but it might be enough to absorb partially. It depended on the caster levels…

She went for it, holding up her staff with one hand and channeling the destructive magic into it in lieu of her…

The crystalline orb at the top of the staff pulsed and fizzled with necrotic, black energy that smelled of foul rot and embalming fluid. It intensified, and the staff itself shook and grew strangely warm, as the total capacity was exceeded and the spell attempted to overpower the magic of the staff.

Before it did, it ran out of steam. It fizzled into nothing, just barely more than half of the total spell energy sapped into the staff than what was attempting to materialize on the outside.

It worked! The staff had absorbed its maximum of 12 effective spell levels as broken-down, raw magic energy, ripping out the guts of the spell and making it unable to still organize enough to affect her.

Well, that was an interesting sequence of events! I’m definitely playing in the big leagues today. The absorption would’ve been doomed if our little ‘reflection game’ hadn’t divided and mitigated that nasty business up a little bit.

The shadowy lich, his hand of void energy grasping the edge of the acid pool, bobbed his head up and his red eyes seemed to smolder at what he saw. Muted noises — surely of frustration — filtered out of that weird substance around him.

Meanwhile, the others around had jumped back into the fight. They had some difficulty getting their footing due to the mass of undead gore coating the floor. It had bunched up against the walls as the undead mooks were smashed, and they flowed like chunky ooze once the barrier went away.

Gabriel was backing away from one giant with precise strategic motions, while Deathsting occupied the other in an endless, furious, hyper-speed assault of claws, teeth, and tail. They were both getting strikes in, though the wyvern had diced up the undead ape-thing significantly more, and remained virtually untouched himself. He’d been smashed by the forcefield, but it mostly just seemed to have pissed him off. Meanwhile, Gabriel was taking quite a few dings despite the defensive counterplay. Well. She’d be alright.

Azure was otherwise free, stomping through the gore with supreme agitation, her body and attire splattered with it, and she was none too happy about it. She was glaring murder at the lich, ignoring the fortunately well-occupied guards.

Samantha sent a mental warning, bundling thoughts that would unravel as memory all at once in her lover’s head. “Hey! He can’t be burst-killed with that shadow over him. His mana will absorb wounds now. I’ve got a two-stage solution. I can weaken him a lot, hopefully stop him from recharging, and then we can turbo whittle him down with rapid-fire. Can you debuff will contests? Do that. Otherwise, start rapid type attacks, to drain his mana quicker.”

Azure absorbed all this with barely any hesitation. She nodded once and then flung out a spell as the lich was trying to crawl out of the pit.

Samantha’s remaining wire mass attacked just then, a bundle of tendrils entangling the lich’s arm, while another slashed the shadowy being’s back. He got jerked back into the acid, and the striking mass also brushed over his staff, trying to disrupt it, but it was not enough. The staff was nothing like the amulet. It was too high-level, too linked with the lich.

Still, it was worth a shot. Good killer wire thing! Good boy!

The lich’s shadow aura suddenly seemed to flare, melting the wire entangling him completely so he could get free.

Azure’s spell took hold as an aura of discord surrounding and suffusing the lich. The air resonated, and spite became a tangible thing directed at the undead, something that would put even Deathsting’s animosity to shame, as if a dreadful alien god were suddenly present and very pissed at their enemy. Reality seemed to be falling into the lich ‘harder,’ begging for something else to be directed at him and magnified.

Yes! Samantha kinda recognized this as something her witches did — a curse against an ensuing contest. That would be a huge boon to what she had planned next. Azure must’ve gone out of her way to have something like this, which she was suddenly quite thankful for, lacking Resemblant and Cat Sith support types as she was in that place.

She checked her personal mana and quickly winced. Well, okay. It was decent enough. She needed to use some level of the My Future Now effect for a boost, but it was questionable how much, and she’d need mana afterward, so not all of it, mana items or no.

I can add [Rogue’s Luck] for another little boost. We’ll make it work. Okay. Seven. We’ll do plus seven. Alright! Let’s do this!

Samantha gestured out with a flat palm at the lich, and her aura of deity flared as she called upon [Goddess], called upon her supremacy as, rightfully, the Goddess of Magic. “Whatever you think you touch upon for power,” Samantha declared, heavy with derision, “your magic still goes through me…”

The air trembled. The Domains of Magic, Pneuma, Light, and Fate filled the material and spiritual world with power, flaring out from the goddess and swallowing the chamber. Those red eyes behind the shadow seemed suddenly panicked as reality buckled under an incredible gravity.

[The Unseen Animus: Rescind the Hand of Magic], [Magineer] and [Goddess] levels, the [Ascendant] boost and other will contest boosts, [Alter Fate: Hoping for Daylight], [Rogue’s Luck], My Future Now, Azure’s dreadful debuff, it all came together and orchestrated into something nearly undeniably potent and extreme, which even most max-level individuals would probably have trouble reaching.

She terrorized the ancient lich with it; unleashed her ire, her indignation, her ice-cold fury.

“You are hereby banned.”
