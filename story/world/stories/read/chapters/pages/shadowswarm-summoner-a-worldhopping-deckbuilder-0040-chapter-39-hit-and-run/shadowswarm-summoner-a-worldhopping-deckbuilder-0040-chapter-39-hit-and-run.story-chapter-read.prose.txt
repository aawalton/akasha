Chapter 39: Hit and Run

They didn’t have much time to plan, and it wasn’t an especially good one, but they were ready when the scouts sent people over.

It started with a rope ladder, lowered down once they removed the barrier protecting them from falling out of the ship. Ambrose made a point of climbing down from the captain’s area and waited for the boarding party to meet them. Raeleq and Luthor flanked him, looking like the muscle to his mastermind, while Raeva and Jessica stayed behind. The latter to fly the ship instead of relying on SNAC, and the former to throw some ranged firepower when the fight started.

There was always the chance they could talk their way out of it and walk away peacefully but Ambrose wasn’t holding his breath.

Three of them were human, two men and a middle aged woman, and the last was one of the leonine men. He looked so different from the feline people of their world, so much bulkier and more muscular rather than lean, and he skipped the ladder and dropped with a heavy thud beside the other three.

The leader was a short, slim young man, just a few years older than they were. His shaggy dark hair fell into dark eyes, and he brushed it out of the way to give Ambrose a once-over. The lights remained out, hiding their bruises, cuts, and damaged clothing, but the light of Stonskarr was enough to give them an impression.

“Alright then,” the leader said, “let’s see some identification and hear about your mission. Because from where I’m standing, this ship is a bit big for just you lot, and you’re skulking about.”

His tone made it clear he expected either compliance or a fight. Soon, but not yet.

Ambrose laughed derisively. “That’s not happening. It’s above your paygrade. You don’t need our names, just the knowledge that we’ve got precious cargo that Vanderborn doesn’t want exposed to nobodies with inflated egos. I called you down here to give you a chance to act like you did something, and then leave us alone. Are you going to take it, or are we going to have a problem?”

Ambrose didn’t know much about being a good friend, or how to get along with people he didn’t care for. But he did know how to project strength and importance, and how to push people around. He might have been prickly, but he understood aggression.

When the leonine warrior snarled, Ambrose snarled right back. Behind him, Raeleq and Luthor tensed, but combat didn’t start. Not yet.

The leader held out a hand to silence his man. “Vanderborn, you say?”

“I do,” said Ambrose. “We’re his agents. That’s all you need to know. If you keep us, you’re going to piss him off and end up as a card he gives to his grandchildren.”

They tensed up, but the leader smiled, scratching his neck. “Funny you should say that. You wandered into the wrong slice of infinity, friend. Whatever you’ve got, Raynor will be happy to take from that bastard. Bad luck, but that cargo’s ours now.”

He slashed with his hand, and his ship’s spotlight went right on Ambrose. And then, naturally, everything Paused.

Round One

Cantrip: [Vampiric Kiss], Spell: [Assassin’s Strike], Creature: [Festering Wretch], Enchantment: [Mark of Service], Spell: [Thought Tap]

Who the hell was Raynor? It seemed like Vanderborn had his rivals, who were not above sabotaging him. So much for being a big deal in the empire, he thought. But that was a problem for later, after they won.

There was no telling what their opponents were capable of, but Ambrose was more than confident. It was, at least at first, going to be a four on four match. He couldn’t imagine anyone patrolling the Aetheric Expanse being without combat cards, so it wouldn’t do to underestimate them.

He was tempted to put all of his mana into [Assassin’s Strike] for a supersized imp. If he did, then his cantrip would probably be enough to buy time for his friends to do their part. [Thought Tap] was useless for now, but the other two spells were worth considering.

Ambrose immediately cast his cantrip when the fight started for real, taking a step back while Luthor and Raeleq advanced, the latter with his [Ironwood Club], and the former calling on his signature creature.

Their opponents were ready for them. Before his cantrip could finish casting, their shaggy-haired leader cast a counterspell.

[Denial: Cantrip. Assert your will over others, and take away their tools.

Spell Effect: Disrupt and prematurely end a Cantrip or Signature Creature. That spell can’t be cast again for 2 rounds. 0 Mana]

Ambrose’s blood ran cold. The short, unassuming scout was a wizard-hunter, like him, with a mind affinity and an upgraded Cantrip. His deck would be based on control while his buddies pounded their faces in. Or maybe just his.

The rest of the scouting party leaped into action. The one woman cast a [Wall of Frost] just in time to cut Luthor and his [Spirit Warrior] off at the last second. They collided with the ice with a hollow thump. It wouldn’t hurt them, but it cost them precious momentum and time.

The lion-man roared and flashed a sanguine red before he grew half again in size. The same light shone in his eyes, standing out in the dark and making the already intimidating man downright nightmarish.

[Avatar of Aggression: Rare. Summon a spirit of anger into your body and transform.

Special: Avatar. The caster transforms into a powerful war form, but cannot summon any additional creatures.

Special: Rage. Each creature defeated by the Avatar’s hand increases their strength for the duration.

Special: Fleeting. This spell lasts for 2 rounds.

Strength: A. Defense: D. Speed: A. Magic: D.]

For a moment, Ambrose wondered if he’d made a terrible mistake, and one that might cost them all their lives. But then, greed took over. With no one else to stop them, these cards would be theirs if they won. When in doubt, double down.

“The last one’s a healer,” Jessica yelled from behind them. “Take him out first!”

The final person was an old man wearing round spectacles. He looked out of place in the group of younger people, but it made sense each ship had their own balanced squad. The healer looked happy to hide behind his friends, far away from the action. The woman with the wall and their leader hung back, while the lion-man pounced at Ambrose.

Raeleq intercepted with a swing of his club. It hit the Avatar, but even his strength paled compared to the enhanced warrior. A massive paw backhanded him, sending him stumbling back a couple of steps before he caught himself.

The healer may have been a better target, but the Lion-man was the obvious threat. It gave Ambrose an idea. He continued to back up, and cast [Mark of Service] on him, unsure if it would even work on someone transformed.

To their great luck, it did, and not as an imp. A collar formed from darkness encircled their foe’s throat. Spells and creatures changed into imps, but apparently Enchantments did not.

It had the dual effect of painting a target on the lion-man, but also on Ambrose. With another roar, he pushed past Raeleq and a recovered Luthor and slammed into the shadow wizard.

The funny thing was it didn’t hurt at first. It was like all of his senses cut out for a second or two as he went sprawling. Ambrose blinked stupidly and tried to move, but his body wouldn’t listen. A red flash showed the lion-man getting stronger. And then a fireball exploded in his face, stopping him in his tracks.

Raeva hadn’t sat by so much as layered her spells before entering the fray. She had her [Obsidian Spear] out, her [Fiery Hatchling] at her side, and waited until the right moment to save Ambrose from the follow-up attack. Grinning fiercely, she darted forward, quick as lightning.

Only to suddenly slow down to a crawl before she could attack. Ambrose blinked, but the rest of the world kept moving. Then he saw the grin on their leader’s face, only visible from the fight lighting the dark deck up. He was trouble, already, and it would only get worse, but then the Timer ticked.

Round 2

Cantrip: LOCKED, Signature Creature: [Hateful Imps], Creature: [Night Terror], Creature: [Black Hound], Spell: [Shred Mind]

He really needed to change out his deck when they had a chance. [Shred Mind] and [Night Terror] were now almost identical, but the former didn’t cost him ongoing mana to keep around. He’d need to re-focus on spells over creatures in general, if he wanted to make better use of [Oops All Imps].

It didn’t help him now, with the lion-man ready to crush either him or the currently vulnerable Raeva. He could cast two spells though, due to his enchantment’s cost. One was obvious, as it was free, and the other…Well, he had a nuanced but important choice to make.

When the Pause ended, chaos broke out. Luthor and his [Spirit Warrior] went after the healer like Jessica had called out, but the ice-user and he were a defensive pair, and it was like a match between two pairs of turtles. Flashes of gold and blue came from their enchantments and shields.

The lion-man’s huge fist swung at Raeva, but before it could connect, he shot up straight into the air. At the apex of his height, he was nearly level with the flying ship he’d come from. Ambrose, still dazed, tilted his head back to see an upside down Jessica at the railing, bracing herself against the wood, strain warping her face.

When he looked down again, the shaggy-haired leader lost his smug look as legs shot forward out from under his body, and he slid a couple of feet on his back. It was just in time for the descending lion-man to land on top of his stomach.

Physics worked, and the results were not pretty, but they could wash the blood out later. Worst of all, the lion-man glowed red from his leader’s death, growing stronger.

In the meantime, Raeleq finished casting his spell, and [Reggie] burst out onto the scene. He was about as big as the lion-man, and the two clashed while a [Kindling Phoenix] soared overhead and went after the two defensive mages. The healer raised a golden dome that shattered under the suicide dive, allowing Luthor a clean strike at the ice-mage, who backed off with a hiss.

Several crucial seconds passed before Ambrose shook himself out of it and staggered to his feet. He cast [Hateful Imps] as he ran parallel to the clash of giants. Once formed, he directed them around the lion-man and toward the healer and friend. They got a running start before they took to the air, and crashed on their targets.

[Reggie] got his jaws around his foe’s arm and twisted until there was a crack. The lion-man roared in pain, cut off when Raeleq fought dirty and brought that heavy club between his legs.

An alarm came from the ship above as the fight turned in their favor. It pulled away from the Wanderer, and Ambrose nearly laughed as he realized that whoever was left on the scouting vessel planned on abandoning their allies.

Things ended quickly. His imps were good at being unignorable little monsters, and Luthor seized the opportunity to get a clean strike on the healer. Once he was done, he and the imp finished the women with the annoying ice magic. At the same time, [Reggie] and the Ransa twins took out the lion-man.

That pressure in his head during a fight lifted with the death of the final combatant.

Ambrose held his breath, waiting to see what would happen. The lion-man wasn’t normally a summoned creature. He had no clue what this would mean

The collar around his throat glowed malevolently, and the body disappeared. On the ground, next to the mess he’d left of his ally, was a shimmering card in its place.

The magelights around the Wanderer came on all at once, lighting up the battlefield. There were a number of cards from the slain patrol, but Ambrose approached the one the lion-man had left behind. He felt the ship shift beneath him as Jessica raised the barrier once more and got moving, but he ignored it and picked the card up.

[Tirgahl, Raging Imp: Unique. The spirit of Tirgahl, who raged so hard he lost his very soul.

Special: Flying.

Special: Rage. Each creature defeated by this creature makes it grow stronger for the duration.

Special: Fleeting. This creature lasts for 2 rounds.

Strength: A. Defense: B. Speed: A. Magic: D. 5 Mana, 3 Ongoing]

It had taken the lion-man, Tirgahl, he presumed, and bound the creature he’d become…but as an imp.

Without meaning to, Ambrose had bound a wizard to a card.
