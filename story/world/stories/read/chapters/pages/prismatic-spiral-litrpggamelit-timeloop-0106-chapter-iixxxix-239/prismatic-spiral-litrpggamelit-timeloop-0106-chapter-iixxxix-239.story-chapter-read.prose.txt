Chapter II.XXXIX (2.39)

The Lucky Apple was a dreary inn. In contrast to its name, it seemed as if the building had been hit by every misfortune known to man. Mitsuko spotted the fire burns along one wall, water damage on the floors from flooding, boarded up windows with shards of glass strewn around them from either the influence of wind or child, and even the scars of lightning along the building’s few remaining shingles.

Mitsuko entered the inn, ice turtle carefully hidden away in her shoulder bag. In the past she’d been told the inn had a strict ‘no pets’ policy and she had enough troubles coming up without that argument.

“I’d like a room here,” Mitsuko said to the girl at the front desk.

“A… room?” The girl’s attention flicked to the side, as if baffled by the request. Or perhaps, looking for someone. “Here?”

“This is an inn, right?”

“Y-yes! It’s just that, I mean, you came to stay here? Just… of your own free will?”

Mitsuko grit her teeth. “Did my father and ex give you my description?”

The color drained from the girl’s face while she stuttered incoherently. Again, she looked over her shoulder at the people in the dining area.

“Never mind that. Just get me a room, I’ll deal with them later.” Mitsuko wondered if she would rob Akuto’s rings in this loop. She probably didn’t need the cash… but who knew when a few extra hundred doubloons would come in handy?

“How—how…”

“How much is it?” Mitsuko asked. “That’s a question for you, not me. And just charge it to my father’s tab.”

“No.” The girl licked her lips. “I—I mean, that’s fine.” She took a deep breath, then spoke with clear, enunciated words. “How long do you wish to stay?”

“I don’t know. A night?” Mitsuko frowned. This girl was acting extremely strange.

“Only one?” a soft voice said behind her.

Mitsuko’s heart skipped a beat but she did her best not to show her nerves as she turned to face the witch standing over her shoulder.

“Fuku,” Mitsuko greeted, her voice steady. She’d activated Retrospection previous to entering the inn. There should be no issues with permanent damage to her mind.

Fuku tilted her head. Her white hair flopped to the side and her large, owl-like eyes examined Mitsuko. “You believe I wish to damage your mind?”

Mitsuko reminded herself to breathe. Fuku was reading her surface level thoughts. Nothing more.

“You’re a mind mage. It’s what you do.”

“Sometimes. But not all times.”

“H—here’s your key. Sec—second door on left.” The girl held out a key in her shaking, open palm. Only as Mitsuko moved to take it did she realize that the girl was looking beyond her. Her eyes were fixed on Fuku, not Mitsuko. She supposed she couldn’t begrudge the girl fearing that witch.

“Fuku. I want to discuss something with you.”

“Me?” The witch raised a white eyebrow. “Curious. I believed you would wish to limit your time in my presence as much as possible.”

“That’s a correct assumption,” Mitsuko replied dryly. Rather than wait for a response, Mitsuko walked towards the stairs. She did her best to keep her mind clear of important surface level thoughts. Hyper-fixating on each step she took and thoroughly examining each plank of wood the inn was made of. Unlike the other two inns she’d stayed in while in Crocus City, The Lucky Apple’s purple wooden hue was stained with grit, transforming its coloring into a grayish shade. She ran a finger along the wall as she ascended the steps and grimaced as it came away with a layer of filth. She had to resist the urge to Mend the gunk off of her finger.

Her room was as poorly maintained as the rest of the inn. She could hear the ruckus of the common room under her feet. She smiled as her mind went back to the memory of her stabbing Akuto through the skull on that early loop when he killed Sterling. The satisfaction as his body fell limp to the floor.

“Interesting memory,” Fuku murmured. “Care to explain it?”

The smile dropped from Mitsuko’s lips. She reached into her bag and withdrew the cloth doll. She tossed it at Fuku. “I was instructed to hand this over to you.”

Fuku’s already big eyes bulged as she gingerly caught the doll. “This is—” Then she cut herself off and her attention flitted over to the window. As if on wings, Fuku glided over and shuttered the window.

“It’s a doll. I found it in the cargo hold of the ship that brought me here. A divination spell revealed an old witch.” On the walk here, Mitsuko and Verna had discussed exactly how much information to share with the witch. They decided a divination spell was close enough to the truth that it would take a powerful spell to detect any nuances of falsehood. “She instructed me to seek you out.”

“She knows I am here?” Fuku chewed her lip. Her fingers jittered, as if pulling an invisible puppet’s strings. “What do you want from me? What exact words did she use? What is the spell's name?”

Rather than answer directly, Mitsuko cast Retrospection on the witch and doll, sending her into a trance for a few seconds. When Fuku’s mind returned to the present, she collapsed on the floor, her legs bent at an awkward angle.

“Are you… alive?” Mitsuko asked after a minute. Fuku seemed to still be breathing but she hadn’t moved, her unblinking eyes gazing at the ceiling.

In response to Mitsuko’s question, those eyes darted over to examine Mitsuko. They went from her boots and slowly made their way up her body until the gaze halted on her face.

The white haired witch turned her attention to the doll in her hands and sat back on the lip of her bed. She muttered and growled a one-way conversation at the fabric doll. Not in a language that Mitsuko recognized.

As the incoherent argument continued, Mitsuko crossed her arms and leaned against the closed door, frowning at the woman. Different methods of escape flashed through her mind as she considered Fuku’s current mental instability.

The doll fell to the bedsheets as Fuku stood up abruptly, her eyes flashing as they met Mitsuko’s. “I have been ordered to assist you. And I am supposed to reassure you that I will not tamper or peer into your mind unless you specifically make a request of my talents.”

“Under whose authority?” Mitsuko asked. “Why should I trust you?”

“Her’s,” Fuku hissed. The doll remained unmoved by Fuku’s hostile glare at it. “She will melt my brain and reforge it into an addled one with no knowledge of spellcraft. I will be locked within a prison of torment for eternity, never properly permitted the release of death and escape to the underworld.”

Mitsuko felt her back unconsciously press up harder against the door in response to the vitriol in Fuku’s words. She’d never heard the witch as distressed as she was in this moment. It made Mitsuko want to run. To flee from her fury. A moment later, Mitsuko realized that the mind mage was releasing an aura of fear through her metal magic, perhaps not even intentionally as it seemed to lash out directionless as it touched Mitsuko. That knowledge helped her steady her resolve and reclaim her voice.

“So, you’ll assist me?”

Fuku ripped her attention away from the doll and her enraged snarl smoothed into her usual blank calm. “Of course,” she replied dispassionately. “All you needed to do was to ask.”

Mitsuko doubted the legitimacy of that claim but set it aside. “I need to get over to Amber Island. I don’t suppose you have any means in which to reach the island?”

“I do. However, I planned to wait until after the barrier dome dissipated.”

“When is the earliest you can depart? I’m in a hurry.”

“Now.”

A moment of silence. “When you say ‘now’ do you mean, like, right this moment?”

“The only limitation to our departure rests in how swiftly your legs move your body to the docks.”

Mitsuko’s satchel stirred. Verna popped her turtle head out of the top.

“This went way smoother than your scuffle with the troll.”

Mitsuko opened her mouth to respond but cut herself off. Her words would sound nonsensical to Fuku. The witch likely had enough experience dealing with madness to recognize something was off.

“Interesting. Is that one of the polar tortoises?” Fuku asked, stepping in for a closer look. “I’ve never encountered one prior to this moment. I sense nothing from its mind. Fascinating. Is it the species’ unique mind blocking me or a magical protection? I’ve long since mastered control over reptiles.”

“No idea.” Mitsuko shoved Verna back down into the bag. “Let’s get moving.”
