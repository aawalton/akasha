Control, it turned out, wasn't an illusion at all.

It was real—tangible in a way Aodhán could almost touch as he stood before a block of silver-blue orichalcum ore.

"Are you ready?" Elder Sedgwick asked.

Aodhán nodded. Elder Sedgwick had explained the process. Unlike the last time he'd imbued a metal for Professor Caldwell, he wouldn't be imbuing this ore with storm essence but with the truth of his path.

Ruinous Authority.

It wasn't an essential step, not with a Mythic imbuing the ore, but Elder Gumi believed that imbuing his path into the ore would grant it some authority against chaos for when they eventually fused it.

And Aodhán couldn't wait to test it out.

Inhaling deeply, he placed his palms flat against the cool metal and closed his eyes. The orichalcum ore felt empty beneath his palms, bearing no magical properties of which he could sense. It was mundane.

But that was about to change.

With a deep exhale, Aodhán let his mind sink into his spirit, feeling a slight heaviness come over him—a displacement of mind and body. His job wasn't to fill the ore with storm essence at all. But to prepare it. To lay a foundation of capacity within its lattice.

And to do that, he needed to imbue it with the power of his spirit. He needed to channel—not chaos or energy—but the essence of his aura into the block of metal.

The problem was that he had never done this before. The closest he had gotten to this was when he imbued the miracle rain he'd given Aldric with his bloodline. But where the raindrop had been open to such an infusion, the metal ore beneath his palms was closed—a perfect lattice of molecules resistant to change.

"Don't force it," Elder Sedgwick muttered quietly. "Just release it."

The words rang true in his mind, but Aodhán felt his spirit rebel against it. Authority wasn't release. It was presence. It was control. It was command.

And no amount of releasing would open this metal ore to him.

He had to exercise his authority over it.

The thought brought a smile to his face, and in that moment, Aodhán knew just how to do it. He unleashed his aura immediately, feeling the sudden shift in the atmosphere—so subtle he wouldn't have noticed it if not for Crixis. Unlike his new friend, however, none of the Mythics present seemed affected by his aura.

He had no authority over them. Yet.

But this block of metal? That he could do.

With a burst of willpower, he compressed his aura, leashing it till it coated his body like a second skin and his hands like gloves.

And then he commanded it to enter the metal ore.

His aura flowed forward, almost like water, seeking a path, seeking entrance. A memory of his almost-inspiration on the trial mountain flashed in his mind, and Aodhán felt a shudder run through him.

Water always found a path in. It always found an entrance. And his aura would too.

That inspiration drove his actions, and a few minutes later, he found it. His entrance.

His lips tugged into a wide smile as he pushed his aura through the imperfection—not begging, not asking—but commanding to be let in. The metal stood unresponsive at first, but as his aura pressed deeper, Aodhán began sensing a shift, not physically, but conceptually.

Slowly, the metal's rigid structure developed something it hadn't possessed before. Capacity. A framework of Authority that threaded through the spaces between its molecular lattices.

The next few minutes passed in a blur as Aodhán poured his aura into the metal, filling it up with his authority until it practically hummed to his senses. He opened his eyes then and pulled his hands away, noting how unchanged the orichalcum looked, but there was no denying how different it felt. How much more present.

He glanced up at Elder Sedgwick and smiled. "Your turn, sir."

The elder nodded and placed a hand above the ore. Chaos surged. And an instant later, Aodhán felt his spirit shudder as Elder Sedgwick's INTENT fell upon the room like a mountain.

No, that was wrong. It slammed down on him like a mountain.

Aodhán had known that the gap between advanced-class awakeneds and Mythics was not one bridgeable by mere skills or even seals. But it was one thing to know, and another entirely to feel.

The INTENT of a spirit was the next step of origin plane relationship after the FORCE. Yet the difference between them was like night and day.

Chaos-storm essence streamed down from Elder Sedgwick's palms as easily as a beam of lightning. There was no sign of strain on his features. Not even a slight grimace at the amount of chaos he was redirecting straight from the origin plane through his spirit.

It was amazing.

Aodhán had thought pulling chaos into reality and manipulating it was impressive. And it truly was, at least for his level. But witnessing Elder Sedgwick's seamless control made his achievements seem pale in comparison. A fluke compared to the potency of Elder Sedgwick's INTENT.

And under its potent ministrations, the orichalcum's appearance changed.

Bathed so densely in chaos-storm essence, the metal's silvery appearance began turning black. Blue veins shifted to gold, and by the time Elder Sedgwick cut off the infusion, the orichalcum had become a magical treasure in its own right.

It gleamed brightly, an almost exact replica of Aodhán's bloodline manifestation. Midnight black with gold Lichtenberg scars.

"Beautiful," Elder Sedgwick breathed in awe, shaking his head. "It drank up the chaos-storm essence like a sponge."

"That's good?" Aodhán asked in a whisper, mesmerized by the beauty of the ore.

"That's more than good, Aodhán. It's amazing." Elder Sedgwick smiled at him. "Imbuing your path into the ore really did a number on it." He glanced back at the ore and nodded slowly. "The path of Ruinous Authority. That's a merged path, isn't it?"

Aodhán nodded.

"I see," Elder Sedgwick smirked. "Perhaps we can discuss that later. For now—let's go see what the others have done."

Aodhán smiled, and together they walked over to the Tri-forge where they met Elder Kobi and Elder Summer, both of whom had imbued their block of orichalcum with space essence. A pile of core dust lay on the ground beside them, and Aodhán raised an eyebrow at the number of cores they must have used.

Still, the space-imbued orichalcum was nothing compared to theirs. It was paler, less tangible, and though its appearance had turned a beautiful violet hue, its blue veins were still visible.

Elder Summer noticed his attention on the ore and smiled. "It's the best we could do with over a dozen mythic cores." She glanced at their own ore and raised an eyebrow. "You and Sedgwick seemed to have made a treasure."

"For a tier fifteen cultivator perhaps," Elder Sedgwick stated, and they all chuckled.

The next few minutes passed in a blur of questions and blistering heat as Elder Sedgwick and Elder Kobi fired up the Tri-forge and began the forging process. Metal melted, filling the air with the acrid scent of charred essence and ozone.

Aodhán stood a distance away from the forge, unable to handle the heat the Mythics so casually shrugged off, but he was near enough to watch the process closely. And closely he did, jotting and drawing out details he thought would be very useful later.

The entire forging, softening, shaping, and annealing process took almost two hours, and by the time they were done, Aodhán couldn't help his admiration for the beauty Elder Sedgwick and Kobi had created.

They had created one side of the pendant each. The first, a gleaming black-gold structure that seemed to press against the world around it with a tangible weight. The second, a striking violet structure cut through by the purest of blue.

It was beautiful, but Aodhán couldn't wait to see how the colors and affinities would fuse when chaos was added to it.

Done with the forging, Elder Kobi and Sedgwick began carving runes onto the orichalcum structure, their hands deft, their movements perfect, their skill undeniable. Aodhán drew closer for this part, unable to tear his gaze away as both men worked.

One day, this would be him. One day he too would draw out elder runes this beautifully.

Elder Kobi carved more runes into the space-orichalcum structure to try and match it with that of the storm-orichalcum, and when he was done, they forged both structures together, creating the pendant.

Now came the last part, the fusing of chaos.

While they had been imbuing and forging the pendants, Elder Gumi and the last apprentice had been working on the chaos strip. So when they presented her with the finished pendants, she simply accepted them, placed the rune-covered chaos strip within, and began weaving blood and spirit runes across the surface of the pendant.

This part only took a few minutes, and by the time she finished, it was time to put the pendant into the fusion chamber.

Everyone stepped back once more as Elder Kobi brought the obsidian chamber forward, its weight making the table groan.

"Please work," Aodhán whispered to himself as Elder Gumi opened the chamber and placed the pendant inside.

The memory of the first explosion was still fresh in all their minds, but this time felt different.

Elder Gumi looked at him and smiled. "You might want to take a few more steps, Aodhán. If this pendant explodes, I guarantee it would be significantly more dangerous than the last."

Aodhán obeyed immediately, and an instant later, the fusion chamber whirred to life, light blooming around the pendant in a meter-wide circle. A low hum echoed next as metal and essence began to fuse, runes blending, ridges and joints vanishing…

Aodhán's heart thudded in his chest as he watched the fusion intently, scared that it could suddenly explode once again. But that didn't happen. With each second that passed, the pendant glowed brighter, runes spiraling around each other like dancers learning the same rhythm.

They were not clashing. Not fighting.

They were harmonizing.

And then there was a flash of searing light. The fusion chamber darkened. And then it stilled.

Silence.

Aodhán glanced at Elder Gumi, worried they had failed again, but then the chamber brightened, and Aodhán's breath caught.

The pendant lay inside, intact—a stunning, seamless fusion of midnight-black and violet, gold Lichtenberg scars threading through azure veins. It was much more stunning than his original pendant, and dare he say, far more tangible, bearing a profound weight that pressed against his senses even from a distance.

Elder Gumi carefully opened the lid and lifted the pendant into the air, turning it in the light, her gaze analytical rather than awed. "It's stable. Completely balanced." She smiled then and glanced at them. "We did it."

Aodhán shuddered as a wave of relief rushed through him, his smile wide as Elder Kobi gave him a high-five. "That beginner's luck of yours came in handy, Brystion."

Aodhán laughed. "Hopefully, I haven't used up my luck for the rest of the year."

"That's impossible," Elder Gumi smiled and then turned to Elder Luseph. "I believe we already have a test subject?"

Elder Luseph nodded. "Yes, Elder Vasmos. She's been waiting for this since the project began."

"Then we'll attach a chain, and you can give it to her. All she needs to do is bond it with her blood."

Elder Luseph nodded. "I'll come back for it then." He glanced at Aodhán. "I have to report this to the high elders."

His gaze lingered on Aodhán for a moment longer, and then he left, leaving Aodhán alone with Elder Gumi and her apprentices.

But Aodhán didn't have the time to analyze Elder Luseph's gaze because Elder Gumi spoke, a wide smile on her face.

"How would you like to join us to create a dozen more of these pendants?"

Aodhán smiled widely, finally feeling like he'd become a member of their group. "I would be honored, Elder Gumi."

"Good," she smiled and gestured toward the treasure tray. "Because we'll need more chaos strips."

Elder Luseph arrived at the public hall a few minutes later, a contemplative frown on his face.

Just the day before, Aodhán had been battling with four paths, and now, only in a few hours, he had merged them into one. Luseph had been unable to believe his ears when Elder Gumi had stated it.

Four paths into one unheard-of path called Ruinous Authority.

He had to report this to Zarxan. However, the moment he stepped into the hall, his steps were halted by the sight before him.

Dozens of cultivators filled the hall, only half of them storm cultivators. And before the high elders stood a throng of different affinities, a few to the right, and a larger number to the left.

A thick aura of anger hung heavily in the air, and with a sinking sensation, Luseph realized what was going on.

The agents of the other sects had come to discuss the restoration of balance.

And it most certainly wasn't going well.
