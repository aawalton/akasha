Riding Natura was a bumpy, incredibly uncomfortable experience. It didn't help that there was not much to hold on to. It wasn't like riding a horse—not that Alice ever had the opportunity to do that. And it didn't help that Natura did not have a mane to hold on to. Her saving grace was that her scales were bark-like and rough, so Alice could at least use the tips of her fingers to grip and keep her balance.

However, they made tremendous speed and arrived at the forest much more quickly than Alice ever would have on her own.

Natura plunged in, not slowing. She seemed to instinctively know the best path through the forest. It helped that many of the plants bowed out of her way.

"Shall we keep going?" Natura called back to her.

Alice grit her teeth and glanced back over her shoulder at the ashy remains of the forest. It was all too easy to imagine the gray searchers soon flying overhead. "Are you okay to go on?" She worried that she was too heavy for her dragon.

"I am fine. I can keep running if you can keep riding."

It was a bit of comfort that her dragon knew this was supremely uncomfortable for her, but Alice could continue. "Keep going,” she called.

Iggy flew down from above. He had been keeping a watch for any oncoming searchers. "Another dragon has fallen."

Alice's expression turned grim. She could imagine the gray searchers were exhausted and were quite likely on their last drags of energy. "Has there been any change other than that?"

"No. None have broken off from their flying pattern."

Alice nodded, but she dared not hope that the message had not gotten out, at least in part. They needed to put as much distance between themselves and that spot as possible for Bella's sake.

She glanced over to Bella, who had been alternating between sitting crouched on Alice's shoulder and bumping along with her wild ride, and taking off to fly on her own. But she was not as experienced in the air as the others, so she was forced to rest often. Alice wondered how she was feeling. Bella was her newest dragon, and Alice didn't know her as well as the others, but at a glance, Bella did not seem upset that anyone might be coming after her. Instead, she seemed pleased, as if this was verification that she was important.

Alice had worried that she lacked self-confidence, but this might be a sign otherwise.

They rode into the night until finally Alice was forced to ask Natura to stop. Her muscles from her calves all the way up to her ribs were sore from using them in such an odd way, and she suspected that if she had not been wearing the thick canvas trousers of a worker—a holdover from her General Laborer days—her thighs and rear would be raw from Natura's scales.

Iggy reported that another two gray searchers had fallen from the sky.

Natura slowed to a stop at the edge of a stream. All were silent as they went to the bank and drank deeply. The water was cool and crisp and did not taste at all of ash.

"It will be dawn soon," Iggy said. "Perhaps that would be the best time to catch some fish?" Though he had suggested it, he did not seem enthusiastic. He looked exhausted—they all did. It had been a long night.

But Alice did not know if they were safe yet.

She was just considering if it would be better to continue on foot, when suddenly magical light fell upon them. Alice whirled to see a jagged glowing tear forming not twenty feet away. It was like a bolt of lightning, frozen in place, but still crackling with fierce energy.

Everybody jumped back, and Alice's boots landed in the stream.

Iggy, however, flew forward to let out a fearsome roar that Alice never knew he was capable of making. He sounded terrifying. So terrifying that as the lightning widened into a circle to reveal Oliver, the man took a step back. Recovering his composure, he held up his hands and stepped forward through the portal. It snapped shut behind him.

"It's only me. I'm sorry if I woke you..." Oliver trailed off, taking them all in and looking vaguely confused.

"Iggy, it is alright," Alice said hurriedly, though Iggy had met Oliver before. The red dragon was still glowering at him for the surprise.

"Who is that?" Bella hissed, her head low as if she was going to launch herself at him.

"A friend," Alice said quickly, though "friend" might be putting it a bit too strongly.

Meanwhile, Oliver was gazing around. "I admit, I expected to find myself somewhere within a quaint village. It doesn't seem like I woke you up after all." He looked at Alice for another long moment. "Is everything… okay?"

That was when Alice realized her hair must be a mess from the wind, and she was standing a little oddly from riding. She knew that she could be truthful with Oliver, at least partially.

"We may be in a dangerous situation," she said. "There are dragons above, and they seem to be searching for something. They’re flying in a pattern and seem very fierce about it."

She had no intention of admitting they were looking for her, one of her dragons.

"Oh, they're here too?" Oliver said.

"Here too?"

"That is part of the reason why I've come." He hesitated and looked around. "Uh, where are we, if I might ask?"

Alice opened her mouth to say that she wasn't exactly sure. They had been wandering in the wilderness for weeks. To her surprise, however, Natura stepped forward.

"We are currently fifty miles to the southeast of the village we left from."

Alice had not realized how accurate Natura's sense of direction could be.

"How do you know that?" Numi asked bluntly.

Natura blinked at the other dragon. "I can feel the magnetic pull of the earth. Can't you?"

One by one the others shook their heads.

"You should always mention whenever you have a new skill," Prim said reproachfully.

"It is not a skill! It is an ability."

Alice made a motion to shush them before they could get into a sniping match. She glanced at Oliver in apology, but he was smiling at them. "I see that you have a couple of new dragons. That's a good thing, I think." He cast a glance up at the brightening night sky. "As I was saying, several packs of gray dragons have been spotted in different locations throughout the kingdom. Most are over farming and rural areas, though a few packs have appeared over cities. They won’t be driven away, and it's actually taken several high-level guild members to kill them. We were hoping, as our dragon expert, you could lend some assistance."

"I don't know why they're here," Alice lied. “Or why they’re searching.”

He looked at her. "But you're aware of them through your class, yes? And they haven't attacked you."

"Should they have?"

"Well, to be fair, we've attacked them first, and they turn out to be quite fierce.”

“How do they fight?" Iggy asked.

"The best we can tell, it's a version of an extremely sharp cutting magic, possibly with a sword affinity. When they release their power, it cuts through anything." He snorted and gestured as he spoke. "It even took out Sir Warner right at the waist. One half landed here, the other half landed there."

Alice was horrified, but Oliver went on. "It took two healers to put him back together. You should have heard him cursing." He grinned, not seeming upset at all at the thought.

That calmed Alice's nerves, though it was replaced by a bit of irritation. Of course the higher rankers had access to real healers. Getting severely injured was more of an annoyance than anything else.

"I believe I am too low of a level to reach them," she said instead of the real truth which was that they likely belonged to someone else in her class. “But I don't think that the gray dragons will be a danger for very much longer. They're falling from the sky with exhaustion here."

"They've begun to do so in other places as well. And some have landed rather inconveniently." Now Oliver did look serious. "Some of them on homes. That's where the actual casualties have been. As you can imagine, the Protectorate is interested in whatever you can glean from them, if anything."

It was a request that may as well have been an order. She knew this had been coming when she'd thrown in her lot with them to get help. Her part of the bargain was that she would be called to do so when they needed her.

But that may not be a bad thing. She had done enough in this forest. They had cleared out the tree wraiths and the terrible blood eels. She and her dragons had leveled up some and even found Bella.

Yes, that was quite enough, and it was time to leave.

Alice was about to say that she was ready to go now when Numi scuttled up to her shoulder. That was an unusual enough move that Prim immediately made room for her. Sticking her muzzle close to Alice's ear, the little green dragon whispered, "Don't forget to ask for them to pay you in treasures for your help."

Smiling, Alice patted her. "I think I have a better idea."

Perhaps it was her exhaustion, or weeks out in the wilderness had made her a bit daring, but she felt bold as she met Oliver's eyes. "Of course I will do my best to help, but as I said, I'm so low of a level in my class that I can't reach the gray searchers. Do you think that the Protectorate can help out with that?"

Far from being offended, he beamed at her, showing very white, even teeth. "Alice, we have a saying in the Guild Protectorate: Leveling is the answer. The difficult part is finding the question.”
