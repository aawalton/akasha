Another dream.

A cauldron on the hearth is simmering with a brew, emitting a subtle aura of mana with each bubble that bursts on the potion’s surface. The room is filled with jars of strange ingredients—preserved eyes, dried herbs, bone dust, and a hundred more things I don’t recognize. Potion bottles are strewn haphazardly over the countertops, just as many empty as full. The room smells sharply of burned herbs, and the air clings damp and hot against my skin.

I activate a spell circle inscribed in the table to cause the liquid inside a glass container to start bubbling. Throwing a handful of ingredients into the potion, I swirl my finger in the air above it, and the mixture stirs itself.

“Two grams of wingsmoke this time,” I say. “Seven ounces of Lifespring water as base.”

A feathered pen lifts from the counter and goes hunting for the inkwell I left… somewhere. It angrily nudges a couple bottles out of the way, dips itself into the ink, then returns to a small corner of the table not overrun with alchemy equipment to begin scribbling down the notes I recite.

“Once again, three grams of brownskull, one half gram of—” I’m cut off as a coughing fit wracks through my chest, scraping through my lungs like hundreds of tiny claws.

I put a hand over my mouth, turning away from the potions for fear of spoiling the batch and needing to start over. I attempt to stifle the coughs, focusing all my attention on suppressing the instinct, but it’s a futile effort.

My chest stabs with abrupt pain, accompanied by a flash of heat and an impulse of fury. I stagger to the side, throwing a hand out over the counter to catch myself. Vials scatter across the table, several shattering as they hit the floor and send up hisses of noxious smoke. I dig my fingers into the counter, clawing grooves through the wood as I fight through the pulsing pain that feels like it’s trying to shred its way out of me.

Pushing off the table, I stumble over to the fireplace, grabbing a random bottle on the way. My legs buckle in front of the cauldron. Gritting my teeth, I dunk the bottle into the brew. The glass cracks from heat shock, and my skin sears as the boiling potion engulfs my hand. Ignoring the pain, I pull the bottle out of the cauldron and dump the brew down my throat. It burns me from the inside out, but it’s nothing compared to the pain in my soul.

The heat quickly dims, and something in my chest spasms once—twice—then goes still. I lean against the fireplace, breathing hard as the pain slowly leaches away. Absently, I rub a hand against my chest, and it sticks to my robes. I brush my thumb over my fingers, and the sensation is tacky: blood.

“Shit.”

I gesture toward the cauldron, and the spell circle beneath it extinguishes. The brew is useless now that it’s been contaminated. A few drops of blood could cause entirely unpredictable effects; even if the result would be in my favor, the imprecision makes it worthless for reproducing a recipe.

The quill scribbles something in my notes.

“That wasn’t part of the recipe,” I tell it.

The quill writes this down, too.

Grumbling, I push myself to my feet and survey the mess I caused. Spilled potions hiss against the stone floor, and the ground is peppered with glass shards. The main brew needs to be restarted from scratch. Probably need to redo the catalyst I’d started on the countertop, too. I sigh, my chest still throbbing with a ghostly pain.

“Well, then.” I gesture to a corner of the room, and a well-worn broom rattles to life, rushing over to begin sweeping up the broken bottles. “Let’s try this again.”

I summon a fire to my hands, burning away the dried blood and potion residue, then pluck the feather from where it’s hovering over the page. The ink is still wet in my notes. I strike out the lines “Shit” and “That wasn’t part of the recipe,” and draw a line beneath it, releasing the feather to once more float ready and waiting over the page.

“Iteration four hundred and seventy two,” I say, and the feather starts writing.

I turn back to my equipment and start the process from the top, idly wondering if this is the time I finally get it right.

I awake with a start, the details of the dream still fresh in my mind. It was so clear that I can recall exactly which ingredients were used. I’m not given much of an opportunity to dwell on this, however; I never wake up suddenly for no reason.

My pace quickens as I realize I’m not alone. My room is dark, yet I can sense another presence in the far corner, black against the black. The front door is still closed. How did they get in?

Memories of Captain Enrold surface unbidden. Was this what he felt like when he had woken in the middle of the night to find someone sneaking around in his house?

Ice prickles over my skin, and I shove the thought away. I silently edge a hand into the corner of my pillow, where a small vial is tucked into a pocket I’ve sewn there. I blink against the darkness, focusing on a shadow that all my instincts scream is the form of a person. My hand closes around the potion.

With one sharp movement, I pull it from my pillow and hurl it across the room. The glass clinks as it shatters against the wall over the intruder’s head.

[Major Poisoning effect inflicted,] Echo says, and my stomach lurches at the confirmation that there really is someone there. I leap to my feet, drawing my knife.

[Major Poisoning effect negated.]

“Well that was rude.”

I snap my hand forward, throwing my knife at the shadow, and activate the Noxious Gauntlet at the same moment. Green light floods the room, exposing the intruder.

An irritatingly familiar intruder.

Shirasil is lounging on his side, head supported by his fist, dressed in the typical smokey black robes I’ve seen him in before. Literally smokey, just like his eyes and hair.

The god casually tosses and catches my butcher knife with his free hand. He waggles the handle at me. “Throwing weapons at your company is extra rude.”

“What the hell?” I hiss, alarm quickly turning to irritation. “Were you watching me sleep?”

“Of course not!” the god objects. He gestures toward his eyes with the knife. “I’m quite incapable of watching anything. Say, what’s that word you just used?”

I narrow my eyes at the god. “Sleep?”

He laughs. “No. Believe it or not I am familiar with the concept. The word before that. Heal? I don’t understand the exclamation. Unless it’s a strange sort of greeting.” He grins. “Don’t tell me you’re happy to see me!”

“What?” I say, baffled. “You mean Hell?” Then I shake my head. “It’s a term from Earth. You know what? No—it doesn’t matter. Why are you in my house?”

“House is a bit of a stretch, don’t you think?” He twirls the blade around in a gesture to my room. “Bit sparse. You haven’t even decorated. No furniture to speak of—and that bed mat hardly counts.”

“Shirasil,” I growl.

He sits up, switching to a cross-legged position as he tosses my knife back toward me. I snatch it out of the air on reflex, my Knife Handling skills kicking in to keep me from stabbing myself.

“That poison was a nice composition,” he remarks, picking up a sliver of glass from the broken vial. To my disgust, he gives it a considering lick. “Much more concentrated than the last batch you made. Still far from enough to hurt a god, though.”

I dispel the Noxious Gauntlet, activating one of the spell circles built into the room to provide a different source of light. “Get out of my house,” I say. “It’s the middle of the night.”

“Middle of the day, technically,” Shirasil says. “Just because the sleep schedule is flipped in this city doesn’t mean words like night and day are also flipped.”

It hasn’t escaped me that he’s managed to talk around every single thing I’ve said. I resist the urge to massage my temples. “What. Do. You. Want?”

“Glad you asked!” the god cries. He jabs a finger in my direction. “That relic in your Inventory. Where did you find it?”

I stare at him for a moment, the subject completely throwing me off my stride. Or maybe it’s because I was just woken up with a jolt of adrenaline and now have to deal with a chaos god who takes that title to heart.

“This isn’t about Piria?” I ask.

Shirasil brightens. “Oh good! You met her, did you? I’d love to know what you think of the dear.”

I heave a tired sigh, falling back onto my bed mat as I rub sleep from my eyes. “Can we not do this now?”

“No can do, I’m afraid,” Shirasil says. “I’ve got a limited window to chat and thought I’d take advantage of it.”

“By jumping between a dozen topics in as many seconds?” I grumble.

He laughs. “Guilty as charged. There’s just so much to discuss! I suppose you’re right that I should create some semblance of priorities, though. That relic is why I actually dropped by.”

I eye him suspiciously. “Why?” Then I have a sickening realization. “You planted it for me to find, didn’t you?”

The god snorts. “No, actually, not everything you encounter in life is due to my machinations. In fact, it was you interacting with it that drew my attention.”

He could be lying, of course. Maybe this is another way for him to nudge me toward doing what he wants—though if that’s the case, he’s not being particularly subtle about it.

“What is it?” I ask.

Shirasil frowns. “What indeed? A remnant, I suspect. But I’d have to examine it to be sure.”

“And what’s a remnant?” I ask, not really expecting to get an answer. In fact, I ask Echo the same question on the assumption that Shirasil won’t be even notionally helpful.

[<ACCESS DENIED>]

I raise my eyebrows. That’s never happened before. “And how come Echo won’t tell me?”

“Ah, right.” Shirasil waves a hand in my direction. “Certain permissions need to be unlocked manually.”

[Permissions Updated.]

“Oh.” I didn’t think he’d actually help. “Thanks.” I ask Echo again.

[Remnants: powerful relics that <REDACTED>]

I take back my thanks.

“What do you want with it, anyway?” I ask.

“Nothing, to be honest,” Shirasil says. “I was merely curious. These fellows have been popping up more frequently since you Travelers arrived, though none of us are really sure why.”

I give him a skeptical look. “You showed up here in the middle of the night—”

“Day.”

“—because you don’t want anything?”

“I don’t want anything with the remnant,” Shirasil clarifies. “Assuming that’s what it is. They’re not particularly good for my health.”

That piques my interest. “Remnants can hurt gods?”

“In a manner of speaking,” Shirasil says. “Which brings me back to the original purpose for my visit: these relics can be dangerous to mortals, too. Which includes you, I feel I should add.”

I snort. “Since when have you cared about my well being?”

“Why, since you became my champion, of course!”

I roll my eyes. Figures. “So, what. You’re here to take it from me?”

Shirasil throws a hand over his chest. “I’d never!”

I wait a moment.

“But if you wanted to hand it over, I’d be more than happy to dispose of it for you,” he adds.

“I need it for a job,” I object. “I have to turn it in tomorrow to get paid.” And to not be put on a list once the job poster realized I’d stolen their artifact.

Shirasil laughs. “Come, now, you had no intention of letting go of that thing once you learned it was a threat to the gods.”

I glare at him. “Maybe.”

Shirasil sighs dramatically. “Me and my big mouth.” He plants both of his hands on his knees and leans forward intently. “Then allow me to be utterly transparent. If you use that relic, there is a high likelihood of it endangering you. Which, in turn, may endanger me. That object has just as much potential to destroy your enemies as it has to destroy yourself.”

I watch the god, unsure what to think about all this. This is the most useful information he’s shared with me since he was still pretending to be a scholar working for Talia. “Are you going to stop me, then?”

He frowns, remaining silent for a long moment. Then he throws his head back in a laugh. “Absolutely not! Far be it from me to stop someone from experimenting—from digging into the unknown.”

Shirasil leans to one side as he fishes around in a pocket of his robes. After a moment, he withdraws what appears to be a small black marble. “Here.” He flips it to me, and I snatch it from the air before I can think better of it. I Check it.

[Refiner,] Echo says. [Empty.]

“What’s this?” I ask, squinting up at him in suspicion.

“A decoy,” he says. “If you’re really set on meddling with that remnant, you can swap it out with this. Your employer won’t be able to tell the difference—and that’s a Shirasil guarantee!”

I’m immediately suspicious, of course, but my curiosity is stronger still. “Thank you,” I say dubiously. “I think.”

The god laughs. “If you’re not careful with that thing, you’ll be cursing me rather than thank me.”

As if I don’t already curse him.

“All I can offer are my warnings,” Shirasil continues. “Perhaps with them you will be cautious enough to prevent a volatile reaction.”

“Volatile?” I grin. “I have a poison affinity. Volatile is my middle name.”
