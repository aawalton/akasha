I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 12 – Glimmer’s Wedding Planning

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS:

The announcement was made at breakfast.

Phina stood up, which was unusual. The guild quieted, which was also unusual. Glimmer sat on her shoulder, jiggling with what Livia had learned to recognize as a specific frequency of calm anticipation.

“I have decided,” Phina said, with the serenity of someone who had been thinking about this for a long time, “that Glimmer and I will be married. Officially. Under guild law and server registry.”

The guild processed this.

Bron went through three expressions before settling on supportive. Nyx looked at his flashlight. Alaric produced a notebook. Miles set down his coffee with the care of someone who was going to need it later.

Livia wrote: Phina escalation confirmed. Should have seen this coming.

[Achievement Unlocked: Should Have Seen This Coming. +0 EXP. +10 Hindsight Points.]

“Congratulations,” Livia said. “Have you looked into whether that’s legally possible?”

Phina smiled the smile of someone who had prepared for this question. “That is why I need your help.”

Livia spent the morning researching interspecies marriage law.

It was not a simple task. The server’s legal architecture had been built in layers, each expansion adding rules without reconciling the previous ones. Slimes fell into three separate categories depending on which subsection you consulted: neutral creatures, tameable companions, or—in one archived patch note from four years ago—entities of unclear ontological status whose classification is pending further review.

Pending further review. Four years.

[System Note: Glimmer’s classification remains pending. The relevant committee has not met since Patch 2.4. The committee may no longer exist. The system is looking into it.]

The clerk at the Guild Registry Office was professionally blank in the way that suggested he had seen everything and judged none of it.

“Interspecies union?”

“Yes.”

“Species two: slime?”

“Yes.”

“Classification: pending four years?”

“Yes.”

He produced three scrolls. Form 7-C, Form 7-C Unusual, and a Request for Ontological Clarification requiring co-signature from a Philosophical Adjudicator who was, currently, on an unscheduled journey of self-discovery since Patch 3.1.

“How long has he been on this journey?”

“Two years.”

“Is there another option?”

The clerk considered this. “Elder Tobren. He holds veto power over unusual unions within fifty kilometers. Veto operates in both directions. He can approve as well as block.”

“Where does Elder Tobren live?”

The clerk gave her an address.

She did not go alone.

Miles came, because Miles came to everything he deemed potentially complicated. Bron came because Bron considered himself Livia’s structural support in all situations. Nyx came because, in his words, “Elder Tobren’s village is near the Silvermarsh and the path goes through the salt flats and I know a better route.” Nobody questioned this. Alaric came because he had already opened a betting pool on the outcome and felt his presence would provide useful data.

Phina stayed behind with Glimmer to continue wedding planning.

“If he says no,” she said, before they left, “tell him I have seventeen months of observational data and a color-coded chart. Tell him Glimmer glowed.”

Noted.

Elder Tobren’s village was two kilometers from the main hub, and the path went through a stretch of road that had clearly been designed during an earlier, more optimistic expansion and then quietly forgotten. The signposts still pointed to destinations that no longer existed. An NPC merchant was set up by the road selling goods from Patch 2.2, his prices frozen at values that had not been relevant for two years, his dialogue loop referring to a dungeon that had since been removed.

Livia stopped to talk to him.

“She is going to do this,” Miles said, watching her..

“She does this,” Nyx confirmed..

“I have a bet on how long it takes,” Alaric said..

It took eleven minutes. The merchant, it turned out, was not simply looping—he had developed a specific, melancholic awareness that his inventory was outdated and his dungeon no longer existed. He did not know what to do with this information. Livia gave him the words for it: your context changed without your consent, and your purpose does not fit the current world, and that is genuinely difficult, and he stood slightly straighter after that, even though nothing about his circumstances had changed.

[You conducted an impromptu roadside session for a Patch 2.2 merchant. +150 EXP. The system notes this is not efficient use of your time. The system also notes it was the right thing to do. These are not always the same thing.]

Bron wiped his eye. “That was—” he started..

“Don’t,” Livia said..

“I’m just saying—”

“Bron.”

He clutched his plushie. “Okay.”

Elder Tobren was pruning a rosebush when they arrived.

He was very small. He had the particular quality of NPCs who had been in the game since launch: too detailed, too specific, built by someone who cared more than the surrounding world eventually warranted. His house was old. His garden was meticulous. He did not look up when they approached.

“The therapist NPC,” he said..

“I prefer Livia.”

“The one from the subreddit.”

Livia paused. “You know about the subreddit?”

Elder Tobren finally looked up. His eyes were the particular sharp of someone who had been paying attention for a very long time.

“I have been on this server since launch,” he said. “I have watched seventeen major patches, four world events, and the complete collapse of the Eastern Alliance guild federation. I have nothing to do in this village except tend my roses and maintain internet connectivity. I have 847 posts on the server’s main forum. My username is—” he paused, with faint dignity, —“RosemasterTobren_OG.”

RosemasterTobren_OG, Livia thought. I have seen that username in the comment sections. He has opinions about loot balance.

“I need your approval,” Livia said. “Interspecies union. Elf archmage and a slime named Glimmer. Eighteen months of documented relationship, color-coded observational data, and the slime glowed during the confession.”

Elder Tobren pruned a branch. He was quiet for a moment.

“I have approved fourteen unusual unions in my tenure,” he said. “I do not approve them for free. Each one requires a quest. Proof of commitment. Evidence that the petitioner takes this seriously enough to do something uncomfortable.”

“What kind of quest?”

He looked at Livia for a long, considering moment.

Then he smiled. It was a very small smile. It was the smile of a man who had been deeply online for a very long time and had developed extremely specific tastes.

“I,” Elder Tobren said, “am the owner of the largest private collection of vintage ASMR recordings on this server. I have been collecting since before it was called ASMR. I have 2,340 entries. I am missing one.”

Livia had a premonition.

“The recording I need,” Elder Tobren continued, with complete composure, “is a live, unedited session of the Empath Eternal saying the standard grounding sequence. Breathe in for four, breathe out for four. The phrases she uses in her sessions. I want the original version. Forty minutes. Uninterrupted. Recorded in the guild’s formal sound chamber, which has better acoustic isolation than anything else on the server.”

Silence.

“You want me,” Livia said, very carefully, “to record a forty-minute ASMR session for your personal collection.”

“For my archive,” Elder Tobren corrected. “I am a collector, not a fan. There is a distinction.”

Behind Livia, Bron made a sound that was clearly a suppressed laugh failing to be suppressed. She did not turn around.

[Achievement Unlocked: The Quest is Exactly What You Feared It Would Be. +999 Embarrassment Points. Achievement Unlocked: I’ve Seen Too Much (Again).]

I am not going to do that, Livia thought.

I am absolutely going to have to do that.

Forty minutes.

For a slime wedding.

“Fine,” she said, through her teeth..

Elder Tobren returned to his rosebush. “The recording does not need to happen today. But it must happen before I sign. I will have the forms ready.”

He paused.

“There is a secondary condition.”

Of course there is.

“My favorite rosebush,” he said, gesturing to a magnificent specimen near his back fence, “is being terrorized by a group of players who have made it their hobby to trample my garden for sport. High-level individuals who believe that because I am an NPC, I have no recourse. They come through every three days. Today is the third day.”

He looked at Livia’s party with the measured assessment of someone who had been calculating odds for a long time.

“I cannot fight them myself. I am a quest NPC. My combat module is deprecated.”

A long pause..

“Handle them,” he said, “and I will have the paperwork ready before the session ends.”

They did not have to wait long.

Three players came down the village road twenty minutes later, moving with the casual arrogance of high-level characters in a low-level zone. Their name tags identified them as Level 85, Level 87, and Level 84—well above the area’s recommended range, which meant they were here specifically because nothing here could touch them. The Level 87 was in front. His gear was expensive. He had the walk of someone who had not been genuinely challenged in a very long time.

“There’s the garden,” he said, to his companions. “Old NPC. Zero combat response. Easy grieving, clean exit.”

He had not yet noticed the guild..

Bron stepped into view.

The player looked at Bron. Then at Phina’s absent slime-print in the garden. Then at Nyx, whose flashlight was casting a precise pink beam across the road. Then at Alaric, whose holy aura was running at full brightness for reasons that seemed to involve positioning himself advantageously relative to a betting outcome. Then at Miles, who was standing in the way of the garden entrance with both hands in his pockets and no expression at all.

“This area is occupied,” Miles said..

The Level 87 player assessed the situation. He had, Livia estimated, about three seconds before he correctly calculated that Bloodbath & Beyond’s combined level average in this grouping was somewhere around 95, and that he had made an error.

He used those three seconds poorly.

“I don’t care who’s here,” he said. “This is a public zone. Move.”

[Combat: Initiated. Party vs. Level 85-87 players. Threat assessment: moderate. Note: Livia has zero combat stats. Note: The system is watching what she does instead.]

What happened next took about four minutes.

Bron absorbed the first hit from the Level 87 with the expression of a man catching a tennis ball. The impact, which had clearly been calibrated to be devastating to zone-appropriate enemies, rang off his armor with a sound like someone dropping a coin.

Bron looked down at where he had been hit.

He looked at the player.

“Did you just—” he said..

“Yes,” Miles said, before Bron could complete the sentence and escalate it. “Handle it.”

Bron handled it.

The combat was not, by any objective measure, interesting. The Level 85 and 84 retreated immediately when they saw the stat differential. The Level 87 did not retreat, which said something about his personality that Livia was already filing away involuntarily. He was fast—genuinely fast, his build clearly optimized for burst damage—and he landed three solid hits on Miles before Miles stopped treating it as a mild inconvenience and started treating it as something requiring actual attention.

Nyx moved through shadows. Alaric smote with purpose. Phina was not here but Glimmer was, and Glimmer, when properly motivated, could produce a localized arcane field that made movement in a three-meter radius somewhat gelatinous.

The player was good. He was simply not good enough, and he knew it now, and he was not stopping.

He’s not going to stop, Livia thought, watching. He’s going to take a full wipe rather than retreat because retreating means admitting he miscalculated. This is a pride fight now.

I know this pattern.

She had not planned to intervene. She had no combat skills. Her role in field situations was to stay in the center and document.

But she was watching this player—Level 87, expensive gear, probably hours of grinding, currently burning through cooldowns against opponents he could not beat because stopping now would feel like losing—and her Empathic Insight was running, and what it found underneath the bravado was not aggression.

[Empathic Insight: Player. Surface: aggressive. Beneath: deeply, specifically humiliated. Secondary: exhausted. He has been having a very bad week. This is the third thing today that hasn’t gone the way he planned. He is not here for the garden. He is here because this zone was supposed to be easy and he needed something to be easy.]

Oh, Livia thought. Oh, that’s familiar.

She stepped forward.

Miles said, sharply: “Livia.”

“I know,” she said. “Give me a moment.”

She activated Soothing Aura. And Empath Surge. And then—without meaning to, without knowing this was a thing she could do—she let the Empathic Insight reading of him flow back outward.

It was not a skill she had been taught. It was not in her skill tree. It was what happened when you took in someone’s pain with enough precision and then, instead of holding it, reflected it back to them—not as an attack, but as recognition.

You are exhausted. You are humiliated. You needed something to be easy and nothing has been.

She did not say this out loud..

She just… radiated it. Quietly. The way a room gets warm when someone in it finally stops pretending they are fine.

[Something new happened. Skill not yet named: Empathic Reflection. You took in what someone was feeling and returned it to them without judgment. It did not hurt them. It did not force them. It simply said: I see this. The system is cataloguing this for later.]

The player stopped mid-swing.

He stood there for a moment, sword raised, not completing the motion..

Then his shoulders dropped.

Then, slowly, with the expression of someone experiencing a feeling they had been specifically not having all day, he sat down on the road in front of Elder Tobren’s garden and started crying.

Not quietly. With genuine, full-throated, completely unstrategic abandon. The kind of crying that only happens when something cracks the specific seal a person has been maintaining through willpower alone.

His companions, still at a safe distance, stared..

“Dude,” one of them said..

“I’M JUST REALLY TIRED,” the Level 87 player said, into his hands..

[Achievement Unlocked: Ended a Fight Without Fighting. +1000 EXP. New combat log entry: Livia Quinn — KDA ratio: 0/0/1 (emotional assist). The system is not sure how to categorize this. The system is categorizing it as a win.]

Bron lowered his shield and looked at Livia with the expression of a man who had just seen something he did not fully understand but respected enormously.

Alaric quietly wrote something in his betting notebook..

Nyx’s flashlight beam was very still..

Miles was looking at Livia with an expression she could not read, which was unusual, because she was getting better at reading his expressions.

That was new, she thought. I did not know I could do that.

Livia sat with the player for eleven minutes.

He was called Drayken. He was Level 87. He had been grinding for a rank push that his guild had ultimately abandoned, he had spent three hundred gold on gear upgrades that were now irrelevant, and that morning his main raid team had been poached by a competing guild and he had found out via the server forum.

“I came here because I just wanted to break something that wouldn’t fight back,” he said, between controlled breaths, having progressed from full crying to the quieter aftermath. “And then you—” he gestured vaguely at Livia, —“did whatever that was.”

“I didn’t do anything,” Livia said. “I just stopped holding what I could see.”

“That’s the most unsettling thing anyone has ever said to me,” Drayken said..

“I’m a therapist,” Livia said. “That’s sort of the job.”

He looked at her for a moment. Then: “The garden. I wasn’t going to actually destroy it. I’ve done it before but—” he stopped. “I don’t know. It felt small and pointless and I did it anyway.”

“Do you want to talk about the raid team?” Livia asked..

He laughed, which came out slightly unstable. “I don’t even know you.”

“You just cried in front of five strangers and an elderly NPC. We’re past introductions.”

Another laugh, more stable this time..

They talked. Briefly. Enough. When Drayken stood to leave, his companions had returned and were waiting with the specific expression of friends who had witnessed something they were going to pretend hadn’t happened for exactly as long as it took to get home, and then would not stop talking about it.

[You provided emergency field support to a player experiencing acute burnout. +600 EXP. Patient: Drayken. Prognosis: He needs to find a different guild. He is not ready to hear that yet. Follow-up: not possible. You will not see him again. That is okay. Some sessions are single encounters.]

Elder Tobren, who had been watching from his doorway throughout, returned to his rosebush..

“The forms will be ready within the hour,” he said, without looking up. “Bring the recording when it’s done. I will sign.”

“You’re not going to ask about what just happened?” Livia said..

“I have been on this server for eleven years,” Elder Tobren said. “I have a forum post from 2.1 predicting that the Mental Supporter class would break server balance if anyone ever played it correctly. I am pleased to be right.”

He has a forum post predicting me.

I do not know how to feel about that.

I am filing it.

They walked back through the salt flats in the late afternoon, the sky doing its orange-pink meaningful-moment color again.

Bron was describing the fight to his plushie in real time, specifically the moment Livia had stepped forward and he had wanted to object and then hadn’t. Alaric was reconciling his betting results. Nyx walked in his usual position—just behind Livia, flashlight covering the path.

Miles fell into step beside her.

He was quiet for a while. She did not fill it..

“That thing you did,” he said, eventually. “With the player.”

“I don’t fully know what it was,” Livia said. “It’s not in my skill tree.”

“It stopped a fight.”

“He stopped the fight,” Livia said. “I just— gave him permission to stop.”

Miles was quiet again. Then: “Is that what you do? With all of us?”

The question was asked carefully. Not defensively. Genuinely..

Livia thought about it.

“Sometimes,” she said. “Not always. Sometimes people just need to say the thing out loud. Sometimes they need to hear that the thing they’re feeling is real. Sometimes they just need someone to sit there while they figure it out.”

“And sometimes you… reflect it back.”

“I didn’t mean to, with him.”

“Does it work on everyone?”

He’s asking something specific, Livia thought. He’s asking if it would work on him.

She looked straight ahead..

“Only if they’re ready,” she said. “You can’t reflect something back to someone who’s sealed it completely. It just bounces off.”

A pause.

“Good to know,” Miles said..

Filed, Livia thought. Very carefully filed.

The recording took place the following morning.

The guild’s sound chamber was a small room off the main hall, used primarily for pre-raid concentration sessions and, on one occasion, Phina’s experimental arcane poetry project. It had good acoustics. It had a recording enchantment built into the walls. It had, currently, four guild members standing outside the door listening through the wall because the door was not as soundproof as it looked.

Livia sat in the chair in the center of the room.

She looked at the recording crystal.

She looked at the door, through which she could clearly hear Bron’s armor shifting and Alaric making notes.

“I can hear you,” she said..

Brief silence from outside. Then Alaric: “We’re not here.”

“You are absolutely here.”

“We are deeply committed to not being here,” Nyx said, muffled..

Livia exhaled. Activated the recording crystal. Spent one moment contemplating the trajectory of her life that had led to this specific moment. Found it did not help.

She said, in her cleanest, most professional voice:

“Breathe in for four.”

She breathed..

“Out for four.”

Outside the door, she heard a very faint, quickly suppressed sound that was unmistakably Bron starting to cry..

She kept going..

[Recording in progress. Duration: 40:00. Status: Livia is doing this for a slime wedding. The system has enormous respect for this commitment. The system will note this in the permanent record.]

The recording was delivered to Elder Tobren that afternoon.

He listened to approximately ninety seconds of it, nodded once, and signed all three forms.

“The quality is excellent,” he said. “The acoustic isolation in your sound chamber is superior to anything in the Nexus Bazaar area.”

“I am glad it met your standards,” Livia said, in the tone of someone choosing words very carefully..

“The wedding may proceed,” Elder Tobren said. “I will attend in my official capacity. I will also be bringing my own chair.”

“That’s— fine.”

He returned to his rosebush.

“The recording,” he added, without looking up, “goes in the archive. Not publicly available. Catalogued as entry 2,341 of 2,341. For the record: I have been collecting since before the subreddit existed.”

He was collecting before the subreddit, Livia wrote in her notepad. He is the origin point of the entire parasocial fandom. I am not going to think about that.

[Quest Complete: Secure Elder Tobren’s Approval. Method: paperwork, one (1) forty-minute ASMR recording, and accidentally making a Level 87 player confront his feelings mid-combat. The system considers all three equally important. EXP: +800. Forms: signed. Wedding: approved. Scheduled: pending venue resolution.]

[Level Up: Mental Supporter Lv. 5 → Lv. 6]

[Robe updated. The blue threading has thickened slightly at the collar and spread to the cuffs. The robe no longer catches on things when you run. The system considers this a practical upgrade as much as a visual one. The system has been watching you run from things.]

She looked at her cuffs.

The blue thread was there now, definite and even, running cleanly along the edge. When she moved her hand, it caught the light for a moment.

Level six, she thought.

Achieved partly by doing a forty-minute ASMR recording for an elderly NPC’s personal archive.

The system has a very broad definition of emotional progress.

So, apparently, do I.

She walked back to the guild, forms in hand, with the particular expression of someone who had done something ridiculous for good reasons and had made peace with it.

For a slime wedding, she thought.

I did that for a slime wedding.

I would do it again.

[Note from the system: Yes. You would. That is who you are now. The system has noted this.]

She was almost at the guild gate when Elder Tobren’s voice reached her from behind, quieter than his usual register.

“One more thing,” he said.

She turned. He was still in his garden, shears in hand, not looking at her.

“There was another visitor asking about you,” he said. “Before you came. Some weeks ago. Not a player. Not an NPC I recognized.” He snipped a stem. “They wanted to know if you were real.”

Livia stopped.

“What did you tell them?”

“I told them I had 847 posts on the forum and had not left this village in eleven years,” Tobren said, “and that I had no useful information for anyone.”

He finally looked at her. His eyes were the sharp kind.

“I suggest,” he said, very mildly, “that you ask yourself the same question they were asking.”

He went back to his roses.

Are you real.

Someone was asking if I’m real.

Before I arrived.

Livia stood at the garden gate for a long moment.

Then she walked back to the guild.

She did not write it down yet.

She was not ready to write it down yet.
