Hayden ran his fingers around the wind catalyst’s box. “This is a pure catalyst with remarkable transformative power,” he said. “It would be ideal for a cyclone spirit, or any other siege that doesn’t require precise control. Since it has high purity, it’s technically compatible with any spirit—it’s just harder to perform the end conversion. That said…”

He put his hand over the stone, and the chaotic air tamed, condensing into a beautiful, flower-like wind that spiraled outward.

Jeela was awestruck by the sight, lungs stressed and mind hazy.

“You can contain transformative pressure to turn this catalyst into something far more manageable.” Hayden looked her in the eyes. “I’m sure you know that certain catalysts pair best with certain spirits; that’s not a fluke. I can teach you how catalysts really work behind the scenes. Once people realize that the catalyst-spirit pairs you send have massive success rates, I’m sure your prices will blossom.”

Jeela sucked a deep breath and stood, offering him a handshake. “I will need time to set up corporations to handle your requests; I’ll help you, but I refuse to be sued directly. That said, it won’t take long. I’ll make preparations before then.”

“I understand.” He shook her hand. “Thank you for the wonderful gifts, Ms. Rian. I will pay them back with interest.” He gathered the boxes into a bag she provided and then opened the door. “Contact me through Mayle. She’ll have something set up. Talk soon.”

“Understood.”

Jeela crashed onto her chair after he left, holding her hand over her heart to quell her stress. She didn’t know what it was that made her feel so small and insignificant during that meeting—but it came in a tidal wave. He made her feel like a little girl again—an adult giving their child advice or instructions. No, that was exactly what it was like. Hayden’s technology would move the Big Four—the four largest galactic chain empires in the universe. It could spark wars.

This matter was way over her head—but Hayden gave her confidence—confidence that she could come out on top of it all. This was her chance to become a true titan—the undisputed queen of auction houses.

It made her heart thump with rushing blood, and her mind lightened from the increased oxygen.

Jeela forced down the knot in her throat, took a deep breath, and called her assistant. “See Mr. Reskco out, and make sure he leaves with a favorable impression. And order Clear Line and Horix spirits, but do not corner the market or sell them. Lawsuits are coming—and the last thing I want is to actually lose.”

As Jeela was instructing her assistant, blindfold boxing legend Sairan Ikol was reviewing footage a past student—Tiasha Sault—had sent him. Hayden’s recording device had a cora filter, so it allowed Sairan to review his incredible illusions with eerie clarity.

The matches were disturbing. Hayden had suppressed eighty percent of the cora in his head and arm during the last matches, and his illusions were almost real underneath the coran filter. Yet he could see by the side-by-side video that Hayden didn’t move during his defending round, and he only punched after Aysha dodged during his attack. It was a gruesome match. He doubted he would have won during that round, simply because of how alien Hayden’s skills were.

Tiasha was right—Hayden could change the sport forever. If Hayden taught the whole team, they would sweep the Universal Fold. They could capitalize on his techniques—maybe even patent them. Then, they would turn Hayden's name into a universe-wide brand. The income would be boundless.

Sairan needed Hayden on the Blind Monks team—immediately.

Hayden couldn’t care less about the Blind Monks. He finally had a battle spirit, as well as cleansing and expansion elixirs. It was time to get to work.

Hayden pulled out the elaborate blue and green marbled core expansion elixir and popped the cork. He took a deep breath and drank. His coran channels burned the second he swallowed. His muscles followed, burning hot as the paradoxical feeling of ice water flooded through his veins. Once the sensations overtook his body, he cracked his neck. Alright, let’s work this off…

He tied his shoes and opened the door, only to find Mayle right outside it, speaking to Keaden.

“Can I help you?” Hayden asked.

“Yes.” She pushed her way into the room, only to see the core expansion elixir on his table. She froze. “What’d you just drink?”

“My dual expansion elixir.”

“Your what? Sit down! You shouldn’t be standing right now!”

“That’s correct. I should be running. That’s why we’ll need to delay this meeting until I return.” He strode into the hall and jogged down the stairs, followed by the guards whom she assigned to protect him.

Mayle stared down the hall with a stupefied expression. How’s he still standing? Normal people struggled to handle a normal expansion elixir; it sent their cora channels into a feeding frenzy, sucking in a massive amount of cora that spread through their mind and limbs and core. It was a painful, arduous process—

But he was still standing? And running? How was that even possible?

“We should go,” Keaden said. “It’s getting late.”

“No, I need to talk to him. It’s remarkable how little we’ve spoken.”

“Mayle, it’s 9 pm. I don’t think that’s appropriate.”

“Appropriate?” Mayle laughed. “Keaden—I could take a shit on the grand council’s floor—on video—and be in a better legal position than I am now. I couldn’t care less if people think I’m sleeping with a student.”

Keaden’s face cramped. “But you’re not… right? Actually?”

Mayle furrowed her brow at his awkward expression. So it’s true…

She thought back to Kain’s words. I said I wouldn’t release an intoxicated woman to an unrequited lover, he had said. Now, she could see it clearly. It was surprising she never noticed before—or maybe it wasn’t surprising at all. Keaden had worked for her for half a century; she wouldn’t have known he had feelings for her if he offered her a wedding ring. That said, his constant warnings to cut ties with Hayden had new meaning.

“No,” Mayle said coldly. “I’m strictly here for business. And I don’t care what you or other people think about me meeting him. Now I’d appreciate if you left.”

Keaden’s eyes filled with betrayal, and he tried to protest. She didn’t let up. He left, dejected, eyes focused on the stairwell—as if waiting for Hayden to arrive so he could stop him. Hayden didn’t make it before the elevator dinged, opening up to take him on his path home.

Hayden’s run took over two hours, and Mayle couldn’t wait that long, so she walked home, worried about Keaden. She had lived for over a century, and during that period, she had never found an emotion more destructive or unpredictable than jealousy.

Keaden’s betrayed expression burned itself into her mind.

Why now, of all times? Mayle silently screamed. She had never needed Keaden more—and now this? This was the last thing she needed.

Hayden knew Keaden would be a problem from the moment he demanded to take Mayle away the night she got drunk, so he didn’t even think about the man as he completed his night’s routine.

Mayle was gone by the time he finished expanding his core, so Hayden was free to finish his assimilation with the Clear Line spirit. He unlocked its box, admiring the rock inside for a while before whispering, “Waken.”

Beautiful tendrils shot out of the stone. He put it against his chest, and the tendrils pierced through his pecs, attaching to his core and pulling themselves inside a free spirit lock. He then linked his mind with it and lifted his hand. Clear tendrils came out of it. He tested out the spirit’s malleability by making the lines thick and thin, sharpening them, and then twisting them together to create a blade.

Hayden spread both hands, and wires shot around the room, sticking to the walls. He then crossed his hands to form a net.

Adequate, he thought. It shouldn’t take much to evolve.

Spirits and cores improved from killing humans or beasts with cores. Such entities released a refined form of cora that spirits ate and distributed. That was the true path to power. All Hayden had been doing so far was showing just how much power could be harnessed from skill and efficiency alone—but he had no oomph. That would be required to make true weapons or powerful lines. Until then, he would make do with its special properties.

Hayden released the lines, then used a few to pick up his clothing from a hamper and put it into the washing machine. If he were training his spirit, he might as well do laundry.
