Magic is might, and more magic is more might. But gargantuan amounts of mana cannot always be contained and channelled correctly. It is, at these levels, simply a matter of efficiency. The more magic mages draw unto themselves, the harder it gets to control, direct, and use all that magic for the mages’ purpose.

Consider a lake and a dam. When the dam releases a small flow, the water remains within its channels and flows in a controlled manner. However, when the dam unleashes a tremendous outpour, the water floods out into the surroundings, overflowing its original bounds and spreading everywhere.

Such is what happens with mages and inconceivable amounts of magic. For most mages, when they require a ridiculous amount of mana for a ridiculous work of magic, they tend to pull in more than necessary. As such, there is an overflow of magic to deal with.

In benign cases, the leftover mana used to create a gigantic fireball merely leaves its surroundings at a high temperature for a day or two.

At far larger scales, such as at the creation of a planet, leftover magic can have far more… significant consequences. Such as the creation of a continent-wide ice shelf.

Overly Magical

Atona K.

Ryland might not have fully ended the threat that Harker had presented, but it seemed the Grand Frame was still intent on recognizing his efforts.

[ Achievement Recognized: Boundless Depths

You’ve overcome an Empyrean-ranked Soulstealer despite being merely Titan-ranked.

Rank up from Titan [6] to Titan [13].

Reward: 90 Encapsulation Prints.

Reward: Glyph of Melding [Tier 25]. ]

Hmm. An interesting little Glyph. He’d have to see where he could use that later. For now, he had other worries to attend to.

Despite Ryland’s fondness for deliberation, he was aware of when not to get lulled into its trap. Such as right now, when that hand had just taken the main link they needed to discover where the missing students were.

However, despite Ryland deciding to follow, he couldn’t exactly follow through on his decision.

“Ryland.”

The call made him turn around. High Academ Zevo, having recovered just enough by the end of Ryland’s battle against Harker, was now hurrying over to him. With him came Mage Knight Ophelia. Meanwhile, the Guardian was rushing about to help the other mages who had gotten trapped by Harker’s Inscriptions.

Well, not necessarily his Inscriptions, for Ryland doubted Harker himself had been capable of that level of magic. No, that was the work of someone else.

Someone who was capable of manifesting ethereal arms that could steal people away.

“High Academ,” Ryland said, then turned and nodded to the Mage Knight. “And Mage Knight. I trust that you’re alright.”

Mage Knight Ophelia’s expression twisted just for a moment before her righteous concern reinstated itself. “We are, thanks to you. As ever,” she added with a mutter.

“Thank you again, Marionys Ryland,” he said. “But it seems even you were…”

“I intend to correct that,” Ryland said. “The interference was surprising, but also revealing from a certain perspective.”

“The Hand of Freedom.” Zevo looked equal parts concerned and furious. “If they’re the ones stealing our children, then we will finally end the threat they’ve presented for far too long.”

Ryland nodded. “I intend to confront them as well. You’re free to accompany me.” He started moving away from them before they could continue speaking. “I will be heading out momentarily. The less time we waste, the better.”

“Of course. It’s just that—”

Whatever he had been about to say was interrupted by the arrival of the Guardian. Ryland didn’t know who exactly the man was, though he was quite certain he was one of the strongest mages present in the entire Realm of Rhel, just like Nesmel was.

For someone like him to have been more or less defeated by the Goodman of a forgotten temple, it had to sting. Quite badly at that too. It was all too evident on the stormy expression on his face. What made it worse was the fact that the Goodman had technically been a follower of Ryland himself, the man who had just rescued the Guardian. Salt in an open wound.

“I thank you for the great service you’ve done for Rhel,” the Guardian said with stiff formality. How nice of the man to swallow his displeasure and at least pretend to be graceful. “However, no further participation will be necessary.”

Hmm. Maybe Ryland had been too quick to judge the seeming gracefulness.

“Is that so?” Ryland asked. “Why?”

“Because the threat has been dealt with. As such, we can now proceed.”

Ryland tilted his head to one side a little. “Surely you just witnessed the hand stealing away the culprit behind all this. I don’t think whoever was behind the hand went to such efforts to destroy Goodman Harker, and even if they did, I will not allow that to pass.”

The Guardian just looked even more belligerent. The dishevelled state of his hair just lent him an extra edge to his bullishness. “I’m aware, sub-Academ. However, my point remains. If you insist, you can go see for yourself why you aren’t needed any further. Rhel has all it needs to safeguard itself without the intervention of foreign entities.”

“Does it now?”

Ryland was almost tempted to ask what he had left unsaid, especially regarding the fact that he had been needed here. For the Demiurge had to have witnessed much of what had happened, yet he had chosen against interfering. Something that really went against the Guardian’s supposition that Ryland wasn’t needed.

“As I’ve said, you’re free to travel and witness it yourself,” the Guardian said. “I will be heading there myself.”

“Where is there?” Ophelia asked.

“The location where Goodman Harker has been taken to by his master,” Ryland said. “And where we might discover the missing students. Finally.”

“I’ve come this far, so I intend to see this through. No matter where it takes me.”

“Then give me a moment.”

Ryland turned back to the spot where he had kept Harker trapped before his sudden disappearance. The crack from [Reality Scar] had dissipated to nothing since Ryland didn’t want to leave a permanent wound on Rhel. For now, he focused on figuring out the exact location.

“Can’t we just ask the Guardian?” Ophelia asked.

The man had returned to the rest of the Rhellian mages, who had regrouped and were awaiting their next orders. High Academ Zevo, as their official leader, had gone with them.

“He doesn’t really know,” Ryland said. “I’m quite certain he’s merely waiting for the sign.”

“The sign? Of what?”

“The sign of why he thinks I’m not necessary for this leg of the operation. [Rewind].”

Ryland’s Soul Glyph of Time burned through the air. The earlier moments reappeared before them. Ophelia gasped slightly as a realistic vision of Harker being taken away by that ethereal arm popping out of a rent in space replayed over and over.

“It looks real enough to touch…” Ophelia said, a little mutely. She even reached out a hand but hesitated to make actual contact.

“You may try and touch, but it won’t do anything,” Ryland said. “There’s a Subglyph of Light to ensure that not only does it recreate what exactly we saw, but only recreates the light itself. As in, the stream of rewinding time is affecting only the vector of light, nothing else. So touching it…” Ryland moved his hand through the vision. “…will not harm you.”

“Intriguing.”

Ryland quirked an eyebrow at her before focusing on the magic on display. He investigated it with the aid of [Omniscience]. The methodology revealed itself to him before long. He read the Conceptual Glyphs making up the spell, and soon enough, discovered just what he needed to target.

“Mage Knight Ophelia,” Ryland said. “Could you please inform the others that I’ve discovered where to go and intend to head there immediately?”

“Wait, you found out already?” she asked.

“Indeed.”

She looked surprised for a second or two longer before fixing her expression. Then she nodded and left. Ryland had to wait for less than a minute before Ophelia returned, with High Academ Zevo accompanying her.

“Honoured Guardian Vinum has taken control of the Rhellian search party,” he informed Ryland. “So I’ve taken permission to accompany you, sub-Academ.”

“Did you willingly cede permission?” Ryland asked. “Not that it’s any of my business, of course.”

Zevo merely shrugged. “The last hour indicated that I might not be the best suited to lead the party, not against the level of opposition we’re currently facing. What few regrets I harbour pale in the face of necessity.” His grey eyes gleamed. “Besides, I’d like to see what exactly you intend to do going forward, sub-Academ, if you don’t mind.”

Ryland actually blinked at that. It was quite the transformation for the High Academ. From standing against Ryland at the Crypts to now willingly accompanying just to see more of his magic and capabilities.

There might also be some level of humility at play here. He had just been utterly humbled by a mage who had gone on to be effortlessly destroyed by Ryland himself.

The difference in the sheer magnitude of their prowess had been reinforced twice over. Brutally.

“Alright, then,” Ryland said. “I trust other issues have been taken care of?”

High Academ Zevo nodded. “We’re sending messages and personnel to apprehend everyone involved with the Recreator Cult, and we’re updating all relevant authorities regarding what has happened here. Rest assured that this incident will not go unpunished.”

That wasn’t quite the reply Ryland was looking for, but the relevant authorities bit reassured him. They might not have been as overt about their goal to rescue all the missing students like Ryland was, but he remained reaffirmed—well, hopeful—that finding the missing Rhellian children remained their true motivation.

Ryland raised his hand as his Soul Glyph of Rift emerged between them all. “I assume we’ll be seeing the Guardian and the rest of the party there when we get going. [Dimensional Lock]. [Translocate].”

The first spell would hopefully steady the Mage Knight and the High Academ against the displacing effects of the translocation. Of course, his second spell created the rift that swallowed them all.

Depositing them on top of a glacier.

They were all struck by the sight at first. Even Ryland and High Academ Zevo, who had both been here. Ryland had suspected an influential and powerful Rhellian such as Zevo would have come to the Continental Icedrift at some point, for one reason or another. Some just came here to sightsee, though of course, they did so from a safe distance.

“C-cold!” Ophelia exclaimed. She was clearly trying not to shiver and immediately start casting spells to keep herself warm.

“Ah, my apologies,” Ryland said. “[Oneself World].”

The cold disappeared, leaving them all in a bubble of warmth. They were no longer being assaulted by a wind chilly enough to leave their fingers frostbitten.

As the other two marvelled at the spell keeping them warm and safe, Ryland tried to focus and see if he could detect anything. It wasn’t working. The atmosphere was expressly suppressing his ability to sense magic, even with his high-Tier Glyph of Detection at play.

“Where are we?” Ophelia asked.

“The Continental Icedrift,” Zevo said. “A glacier has grown to cover almost a quarter of Rhel, thus we have named it the Continental Icedrift.”

“And that bastard came here?”

Right. Ophelia was likely missing a lot of contextual information about everything going on. Not that Ryland was in the know about everything either. However, what he did know was that this enormous glacier wasn’t an entirely natural formation.

He should perhaps not have used as much mana when building Rhel, or this wouldn’t have happened.

Unfortunately, the past couldn’t be changed. This led to their current location being a little too suitable for certain entities who might have wished to remain undetected.

“We must go inside,” Zevo said.

“Inside?” Ophelia looked down at her feet, trying to fathom how that was even possible. “If this thing is the size of a continent, then how can you be sure we’re at the right spot?”

“Not needed,” Ryland said. “I think we’ve already found our quarry.”

Both Zevo and Ophelia turned to look in the direction Ryland was already seeing, eyes fixed on the distant figure drawing closer to them. Both their magic came to life, though they didn’t cast any spells just yet. Not when the man was still distant.

But despite the distance, they were all quite certain they were looking at Goodman Harker.

“What is the meaning of this?” Zevo asked.

Ophelia pulled out her sword. Ryland could feel the tension trying to leap off her skin. Clearly, she had no intention of getting caught by the same kind of trap again. “Why? What was the point of stealing him away if he’s just going to come over here by himself again?”

Ryland just frowned. That was certainly a good point.

Harker appeared much the worse for wear after being trapped on the edge of unreality for a minute or so. His form was still that of a Manifestation, but at several spots, the glowing mana had been removed entirely, revealing torn and twisted flesh that was permanently, bloodily deformed.

“You came,” he said. His laughter was broken too. “I shouldn’t have doubted you.”

Ryland wasn’t quite sure what was going on. But he also knew that allowing someone like Harker any time at all would just be to his own detriment. As such, he decided to end things before they could begin again.

Harker had already thrown himself backwards with a cry. “No, no. Not again. Not this time.”

The hand appeared again. That amethyst arm bursting out of a rent tearing through space. For a heartbeat, Ryland wondered if they were about to face the same frustration again. Though, he was already readying his counterspell to take care of the interfering issue.

But that wasn’t it. Instead of taking Harker away, the hand deposited someone else.

A student.

Ryland overcame his surprise within a second or two. A student. Here. It was now truer than ever that the mastermind behind all this was well aware of where the missing children were present.

The worst part, however, was that the student was on the opposing side.

“That’s enough,” he said.

Ryland frowned at the youth. He was the one who had challenged Ryland a month or two ago. The one who had led his companions against Ryland himself.

“Step aside, Suthor,” Zevo said as he hurried over. “I don’t know what you’re thinking, but whatever it is, it’s wrong.”

Suthor didn’t move. “I’m not the one in the wrong here. You are.”

The conviction in his voice made even the High Academ come to a sudden stop, though he still looked like he was moments away from forcefully pulling the Rhellian student away. Something that would no doubt cause further chaos that they all wanted to avoid.

Zevo tried arguing with the student, trying to convince him to get away. Suthor remained unmoving. He was convinced he was in the right here.

“You’re too afraid,” Suthor said. “I’m not, High Academ. I know what we all need to free ourselves from the yoke we’ve been trapped under for our entire lives. My eyes are finally open. I’m not going back. Never!”

Ryland shook his head. He decided to take control of the conversation before things spiralled out. Plus, he also had to keep an eye on Harker before the Goodman did something truly regrettable.

“I understand how you feel, Suthor,” Ryland said. “But you haven’t achieved real freedom. All you’ve done is exchange one yoke for another, subtler one.”

Suthor scowled at Ryland. “You have no idea what any of us are going through. You have no clue about any of this!”

“I’m afraid I know quite a lot. For instance, I know your soul hasn’t been your own for a long while now. And I also know that it still isn’t.”

“That’s a lie!”

“Is it? Is it truly? Take a moment and think. I’m not saying you’re lying, Suthor. I’m merely stating that you’ve been misguided. Manipulated. And that can’t happen without a kernel of truth to change your worldview.”

Suthor’s eyes started shifting madly. He was looking from Ryland to Zevo to Harker, jumping from one to the other without settling. His whole body was as tense as a drawstring.

Harker noticed it. He had begun to move. Ryland had already raised his hand, ready to cast the required spell, while Zevo had the poor, confused student in his sights. Behind them, Ophelia approached stealthily but with growing power.

They were all ready to act.

Only for it all to be made unnecessary. The heavens split open with a surge of frightful power and light. A sudden pressure descended on them all, magic starting to spark with rising potential. It felt like the sun itself was descending upon them, turning space itself into radiant, burning energy.

Every single one of them, even Ryland, stared upwards.

“Bless the Great Gods,” Zevo whispered. “The Demiurge is coming.”
