I ran. Not because I was trying to cram two days’ worth of work into eight hours even if I was. Not because I didn't want to be in here when night came, the lights shut off, and the zombies got extra nasty.

No, while those were all rational reasons to be running, I was currently sprinting for my life, having already attracted a small horde of zombies.

I’d killed them religiously at the start, but that had quickly proven futile as I ran into more and more on my journey north. No matter how many I killed, the horde always rebuilt itself as I passed zombie after zombie. Eventually, I realized letting them build up behind me actually slowed them down, as they tripped over one another, each fighting to feast on my sweet, living flesh.

But that was alright, you might say. I had a whole crew of Champions, after all. Plenty of firepower to keep them off my back, right?

Except not one of them could keep up with me, and every one of them knew it. If we had any hope in hell of pulling off all three train runs by the time night fell, well, me in my Grace suit was the only option.

I had never cursed choosing the most versatile Blessing like I did now. If I’d realized at the time it would make me the best choice for most missions, I’d have chosen being a Baker or something equally useless.

As it was, the only assets I had were my powers and my whirling, Soulwoven Mythril blades, which did a rather fine job of keeping things off my back anytime one got close.

Oh, and Aerion and Galia, of course, both tucked away in my inventory. Galia was nearly useless in this confined space. As for Aerion? Despite her being, by far and away, the best person to remain behind at the control center, she’d insisted on joining me.

It was both a little frustrating and infinitely touching that she refused to let me go in alone. It might not have been the rational choice, but I appreciated it. Besides, I couldn’t protest too much, considering she’d trained the others on how to operate the system—even going so far as to test their ability to control the trains.

Yep, teaching the others wasn’t all my genius girlfriend did. She’d figured out how to override the lockdown safety protocols, unlocking the ability to start the trains up remotely. As for stopping them… well, there was a reason I was out here.

Another ten minutes of running had me arriving at Kyobashi Station—a smaller stop on the Ginza Line that ran all the way to Asakusa, where we needed to go. Though “small” was, of course, relative, with it being bigger than most metro stations in the US. Never mind that those were so old they still ran on vacuum tubes, were filled with more trash than a dumpster, and had late trains as the norm rather than the almost unheard-of exception they were in Tokyo.

“Okay, you’re going to make a right, a right, then a left before you get to the next station. Remember, two rights and a left!”

“Easy enough,” I muttered, though I knew they couldn’t hear me. Each train had a comm system back to HQ, but until then, I was on my own. Worse still, if I fucked up, took a wrong turn, and ended up at the wrong station, I’d have to wait until they searched all the cameras to find me again. They only had camera visibility at the junctions and stations, and the PA system was only at the stations.

Still, it was more advanced than most of the rest of the world. I couldn’t exactly complain.

Sadly, there was no way for them to reduce the volume, so by making that announcement, whatever zombies had been following redoubled their fervor, forcing me to move at my max speed.

Given the zombies were still in daytime mode, my top speed was easily enough to outmatch them. If I fell behind schedule and we ended up in night mode? That was another story. To say nothing of the pitch-black darkness, with their enhanced speed and senses. Yeah, I was going to do everything humanly possible to avoid that terrifying possibility.

Of course, upon breaking through the glass of the operator’s car at the rear of the train in question, I ran into my next predicament.

That zombie horde? It hadn’t gone away. In fact, it had grown to a size that I could no longer handle on my own.

Luckily, I wasn’t on my own.

I brought Aerion out of my inventory. Thanks to the brief period inside, her daze lasted only a few minutes.

“Aer? I need you to pilot the train while I keep these motherfuckers off us. Think you can do that?”

Aerion’s eyes widened, but she nodded anyway. “I’ll need to head to the car at the other end,” she blurted. “Stay safe.”

“Hey, it’s me!” I said, planting a kiss on her lips the moment before she turned to run off, her cheeks undoubtedly flushed red. I didn’t know why, but messing with her just felt so damn good.

Cracking my neck, I turned my attention to the swiftly approaching horde. “Alright, you sacks of rotting shit. Time to test out my upgrades.”

“Anytime now, Aer,” I muttered as bullets whizzed through the air, bullseyeing zombie brains before returning to my inventory, only to repeat the process.

Mythril blades spun like circular saws, slicing apart anything—but mostly necks—as they flew through the air. It was difficult to say which was more effective.

Despite the glorious carnage they were creating, however, there were simply too many enemies out there, and I couldn’t exactly deploy my whip unless I jumped out of the train.

So that was exactly what I did, unleashing the full might of my new weapon the moment I was on the ground. Or rather, it was more accurate to say it unleashed itself. Like it couldn’t resist being restrained.

A scary thought. But then again, I had poured dozens of souls into the thing. It was only when it uncoiled and whipped through the air like a possessed snake—gouging hearts, eating heads, and generally eviscerating anything its long body touched—that I wondered if the chain had gone too far.

This thing was beyond my control now. It had a life of its own, in a way none of my other swords I’d Initialized had shown. Sure, they were capable of autonomy, but they were more like… loyal dogs. Intelligent to a degree, but always under my control.

But this? This was different. I almost felt a malevolence burning off it. An insatiable hunger that only grew deeper as it fed. Because it was feeding. The thing didn’t just rip necks and torsos like they were paper—its end opened into a jaw that chomped on every zombie it caught.

[Soulwoven Mythril-Voidsteel Chain Whip]’s ability [Buzzsaw] has leveled up! [Buzzsaw] is now Foundation-1.

[Buzzsaw] is now Foundation-2.[Buzzsaw] is now Foundation-3.[Buzzsaw] is now Foundation-4.

And on it went, but the horde never got any thinner. The zombies never stopped coming.

Which was why the screeching of the train’s wheels—and Aerion’s announcement to “Get in now or be left behind!” over the train’s PA system—came as a welcome relief.

I recalled my swords… and then, with hesitation, I recalled the rampaging chain whip. It did not want to stop, and the moment I told it to come back, its head whipped around, lashing out in protest.

“You will return,” I ordered, facing it not as a weapon, but as a living thing that needed to be shown who the alpha was.

The whip eventually complied… but not before biting the head off another zombie.

I quickly shoved it into my inventory, feeling a great relief wash over me the moment I did.

That thing was fucking terrifying.

Then again, I supposed the best weapons always were.

All the best weapons also had names but that felt like taking things one step too far. Call it superstition, but I had the feeling that naming this thing now would be the worst thing I could do. Maybe once I'd fully tamed it. Maybe then.

I activated [Spacefold], jumping into the train just as it lurched forward, leaving the zombies scrambling and failing to catch up—but not so far behind that we lost them altogether.

Exactly as we wanted.

Not even the full might of my weaponry had managed to eliminate that horde, and we were still miles from the city center. It just went to show what we were up against—and how much greater the danger ahead of us would be.

I’d intended to hang back to confirm that the zombies couldn’t catch up, but Aerion’s next PA announcement changed all that.

“Greg, I need you up here now! We’ve got zombies. Lots of them, and unless you can thin them out, we’re going to have problems.”

“On my way,” I muttered. Out of the frying pan and into the fire.

The SEALs on Earth had been right.

There really was no such thing as an easy day.And then I woke up and realized I was still on Earth and all of it was just a dream.“Welp,” I said, yawning. “Time to go to work, I guess!”
