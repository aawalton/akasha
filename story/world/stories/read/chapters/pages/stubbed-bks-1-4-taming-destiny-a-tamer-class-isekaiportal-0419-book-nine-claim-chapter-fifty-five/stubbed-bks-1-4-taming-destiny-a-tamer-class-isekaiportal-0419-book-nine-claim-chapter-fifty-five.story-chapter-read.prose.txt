I’m unsurprised when the messenger leads me over to the area set aside for our farming developments. Seeing as I can spot Elarion from the top of the slight hill that overlooks the area, I dismiss the messenger and take a moment to survey things myself. The settlement is certainly growing in size and development, but our food situation is no less precarious than it was when we arrived.

If anything, it’s more so, as we’re reaching the end of the produce we brought from Moriax. Though we can eat the beasts hunted in the forest or the fish from the sea, we haven’t had much success with farming, nor with identifying plants that are safe for everyone to eat. As I cast my eyes over the cleared fields of barren earth that should contain furrows of quickly growing crops from Moriax, my lips press themselves together in a line.

Taking a deep breath and consciously relaxing my facial expression and body language – if I’m tense and worried, everyone else will be too – I stride forward to join the group of people huddled near the field.

“Another failure?” I ask Elarion, doing my best to keep any hint of accusation out of my voice.

He turns to greet me with a nod, but the grim set of his expression answers my question before he even opens his mouth.

“Another batch has failed to germinate,” he tells me evenly with a clear edge of frustration to his voice. Reaching down into a bag at his feet, he pulls out a handful of soil-blackened seeds sprouting wizened almost-roots. “This lot we actually allowed to begin sprouting before sowing them, yet they have developed no further since they were put in the soil.

I nod slowly, my heart sinking at the evidence of failure.

“Are you any closer to understanding why this is happening?”

This time, his nod is more confident.

“We’ve reached some conclusions that we’re fairly sure are correct,” he replies. “The Energy levels in the soil are simply too high. The concentration overwhelms the plant during its early stages, and it fails to germinate as a result. Or, having germinated elsewhere, then fails to develop.”

I frown.

“Shouldn’t a higher energy concentration help plants grow better?” That was certainly my understanding and what I observed in the samurans’ world.

Elarion purses his lips.

“Generally,” he admits. “But we learned early on that it’s dangerous to pour Energy into a plant without direction. That leads to uncontrolled growth, mutations, and unpredictable results. Plant magic is often subtle and must be precisely measured.” He pauses for a moment, then continues, gesturing to the field beside which we’re standing. “That’s exactly the problem here. Instead of receiving Energy gradually – through potions or controlled magical input – the plants are absorbing it unchecked from the environment.” He shakes his head. “I’ve never seen an environment with this much ambient Energy. It’s far beyond what we can draw from the air alone.”

“How much more?” I ask, my frown deepening.

“At least three times as much,” Elarion replies grimly.

My eyebrows shoot up.

“Three times as much as we can absorb naturally?”

He nods.

“That’s…incredible.”

And concerning. My own Energy absorption rate has roughly doubled since arriving on the Lost Continent compared to when we were in Moriax, which means that the soil holds six times as much. No wonder the imported plants are struggling.

I think back to the vine stranglers I encountered on the samuran world, imagining what they might become with that much additional Energy. I can’t help but shiver.

Then I blink that thought away and focus on the present.

“I thought you had successfully germinated some wheat,” I say, recalling our most recent council meeting – that report is one reason I’m disappointed to see so many barren fields right now. Not that it’s actually wheat like on Earth, but it resembles the plant, fulfils most of the same functions, and translates as such to me from Moriaxan.

Elarion grimaces.

“It did germinate,” he admits. “The only plant species that has. In fact, we managed to bring it to full growth within five days. But unfortunately, while the plant coped with the Energy in its environment, it did so by transferring most of that Energy into the kernels during development.” He pauses, his expression tightening. “That made the wheat dangerous to consume. Two Classers suffered minor Energy poisoning from eating only a small portion of bread made from the flour.”

I grimace in sympathy.

“Are they alright?” I ask with concern. I’m glad when Elarion nods reassuringly.

“The healers treated them. They did not enjoy the experience, I am certain, but the majority of the foreign Energy was flushed from their systems soon after they presented the initial symptoms. We were looking for them, of course,” he hurries to add.

I nod, but then pause as a thought occurs to me: if even Classers are affected, non-Classers would fare far worse.

“Did any non-Classers try it?” I ask, unsure whether I want to hear the answer.

To my relief, Elarion shakes his head.

“After seeing the effects on the Classers, we deemed it too dangerous.”

I give him an appreciative nod. I may have had plenty of problems with Elarion – and continue to do so – but at least he is a lord who genuinely cares about those under his immediate command.

I redirect my thoughts.

“And you haven’t found any way of removing the Energy? Either during growth or after harvest?”

My hopes aren’t high; they sink even further when Elarion shakes his head again.

“We’ve tried,” he replies with a note of tired frustration in his voice, “but plants don’t willingly give up what sustains their growth. Even when that Energy becomes harmful, they continue to hold onto it. And once harvested, the Energy is so deeply interwoven with the seed’s structure that we haven’t found any way to extract it.”

“I take it that putting it in your Inventory does nothing,” I comment.

It’s not really a question but Elarion answers it anyway.

“It seems to lessen the concentration a little – those who tested were able to eat more of it than the substances that had not been through an Inventory – but not enough to make it safe.”

I sigh.

“And no progress on identifying local plants that everyone can eat?”

Elarion shakes his head immediately.

“No. For the same reason. The local flora contains too much Energy. It renders everything toxic in sufficient quantities. Leaves hold less than fruit or tubers, however, they are also less nutritious. As matters stand, an appropriately nutritional amount of even the leaves of plants that are otherwise edible requires most Classers to visit the healers. Again, we have not tested with non-Classers.”

The others around us have remained silent, a mixture of Druids, Plant Mages, Cultivators, and farmers, but now one of them steps forward hesitantly.

“My lord, there is one plant we’ve had some success with,” she offers.

I turn to her, a flicker of hope rising.

Elarion makes a dismissive gesture.

“Mayanis, you can hardly call it a success,” he says, before turning back to me. “There is a local plant that stores most of its energy in its bulb, leaving the leaves comparatively low in energy.”

He spreads his hands.

“However, the leaves are nutritionally poor, since most of the value is in the bulb – and the bulb is entirely inedible.”

“I see…” I murmur, my thoughts churning.

“We would need to consume large quantities of the leaves to gain any meaningful nutrition,” he continues. “And even then, the plant responds poorly to growth potions or magic, redirecting most of that energy into the bulb.”

I nod slowly.

“Have you been working with the cooks? To see if there’s a way to reduce the Energy during preparation?”

“Of course,” Elarion replies, sounding faintly offended. “But our cooks are trained on Moriax. Their techniques focus on enhancing and amplifying the Energy in food – not reducing it.”

“That makes sense,” I concede. “But perhaps they can adapt. Use those same principles in reverse.”

Elarion leans back slightly, clearly taken aback.

“Create an entirely new technique?” he says incredulously. “Just like that? Markus, do you realise how much effort goes into developing something like that? It would be the masterwork of most cooks – like creating their own Skill.”

I shrug.

“In my experience, necessity is the mother of invention,” I point out. “How do you think I got most of my Skills?”

Elarion’s face smooths out, becoming unreadable.

“I suppose I thought you had received them as I did – from your…from Lord Titanbend.”

“You know I’m from another world. Nicholas gave me my Class and a single other Skill. I arrived in Moriax with over fifteen non-Class Skills, and due to testing the boundaries, the one Skill he had given me had turned into something very different.”

The silence stretches and I have to fight not to shift uncomfortably under their eyes. Those standing around us aren’t even trying to pretend they’re not listening in now.

“How…how did you do that?” Elarion asks, clearly fighting to keep his tone nonchalant.

I shrug.

“I pushed the limits. I suspect that I had an advantage too.”

“What was that?” Elarion prompts when I don’t continue, sounding almost hungry for the knowledge.

I gaze at him seriously.

“I knew so little that everything seemed equally impossible – and conversely, equally possible. My survival and that of my dearest companions depended on me figuring some of the Skills out – and then when I knew I could earn Skills just by demonstrating enough understanding, or transform Skills by pushing the boundaries just enough, there was no reason for me not to just keep going.”

Elarion doesn’t seem to have anything to say to that, though the quiet murmuring that springs up between others in our audience indicates that he might be the only one.

“Anyway, that’s something of a tangent,” I say after a brief pause to help them think about my words. If they can help anyone here to somehow start growing food that my people can eat, the revelations will be well-worth handing out. “To summarise the food situation, we have plants that fail to germinate, plants that germinate but are inedible, a plant that is edible but nutritionally poor and difficult to accelerate due to the way it stores energy, and cooks who are unable to reduce Energy in food – only enhance it. Have I got that right?”

Elarion nods, his gaze sharp once more, and the people around him shuffle a little and avoid my eyes.

“That’s about right,” he admits, though I can tell he’s uncomfortable about having it stated so baldly.

I nod slowly, my mind already working through possible solutions. After a moment, I come to a decision.

“I’ll speak with the healers and the cooks,” I decide. “Perhaps the healers can develop a better way of dealing with excess Energy in cases of poisoning. And perhaps the cooks can begin experimenting with ways to reduce the Energy content of what they prepare.”

“Do the healers have enough time for that? It was my understanding that they’re already busy,” Elarion points out.

I grimace.

“They are busy, but I suspect that they’ll do their best to make space for this. Well-nutritioned people tend to be less likely to visit a healer, after all. Extra work in the short term should help in the long run.”

Elarion nods in acceptance.

I pause for thought, then continue.

“While they’re doing that, I’d like you to investigate that plant further. I understand it’s not currently useful to us, but perhaps we can crossbreed it with our own crops. If we can replicate its method of handling excess Energy, we might be able to apply that to our food plants.”

Elarion frowns.

“In what way?”

“Well,” I say, thinking it through as I speak, “you mentioned that the wheat currently stores the excess Energy in its seeds. Perhaps we could develop a version that grows a bulb as well as seeds – and redirects the excess Energy into the bulb instead.”

Elarion seems hesitant.

“That kind of cross-breeding can take many generations to produce – is it really the best use of our time? Especially as it’s likely to make the seeds less nutritionally beneficial – and perhaps make the plant more difficult to accelerate with magic, as the local species is.”

“Do you have any better ideas?” I challenge. “Because right now, it sounds like we have nothing to reliably supplement our meat and fish, and that’s not sustainable long-term.”

Elarion holds my eyes, his expression unreadable for a long moment. Then, with a slow, shallow dip of his head, he accepts my reasoning. In his gaze, I see an understanding of just how precarious our nutritional situation really is, and how close we might be to serious problems if this continues.

For now, we’re managing and such ideas are best not discussed outside the council rooms or the Alliance Bond: there’s no need to spread fear through the settlement.

But the longer we go without a balanced diet, the more strain we place on the healers to keep people healthy. And that, in turn, means fewer resources available if we face disease – or attack.

We have limited resources available to us right now, and a stable, predictable food supply is absolutely essential.

“It bears exploration,” Elarion admits, then nods decisively. “We shall see to it. Gunther, Rafaelis, Merlis, and Chris – you’re with me. We’ll test what we can do magically with this bulb. The rest of you continue what you were doing before – whether that’s searching for plants that may feed us or testing what might work with the crops we brought.”

“Yes, my lord,” comes the response from around him, and the group quickly disperses.

Elarion nods to me – far more genuine respect in the gesture than when we first met – and then strides off himself.

Seeing that I have been effectively dismissed, I head back towards the settlement.

The path up the hill towards the gates gives me a view of the harbour, where two ships sit at anchor – a reminder of both how far we've come and the problems still unresolved. The crews have started to complain. They’ve remained aboard the ships to keep them operational at all times, and they’re beginning to demand time off.

Captain Sor’az has returned a few times from scouting missions, adding to our maps in both directions. At present, he’s on a longer expedition, heading further along the coast now that the immediate surroundings have been charted.

I’ve considered sending some of the other ships out to support him. It would give the crews something to do – something different. But the ships are vital for fishing, which is currently a key part of our food supply. And they’re also a critical part of our defence, one I’m loathe to remove just yet.

The main issue is that there isn’t enough activity to keep the crews fully occupied, yet if we give them other tasks, they may be unable to react as quickly in the event of an emergency as we need. The ships are our fall-back positions – losing even one of them would be a catastrophe. The more craft-inclined have been helping to bring the ships back to perfect condition, and the fighters have been training, but they’ve already spent six months at sea – they tire of being confined.

I had considered offering them some form of entertainment, but that quickly became complicated. They’re used to certain amenities when making landfall – amenities we simply don’t have here. Layton suggested establishing a brothel using some of the slaves we took from Kyrian and his followers, but I shut that down as soon as I was able to control my reaction enough not to start shouting at him.

I’m aware that morale and entertainment will need attention eventually, but right now most people in the city are too exhausted after a day’s work to care much about such things. Frankly, it’s not a priority for me either. Once we have a secure food supply, perhaps then I’ll turn my attention to it.

For now, the captains are managing the situation with the crews by rotating them between fishing crews, skeleton crews guarding the ships at anchor, and shore parties coming to aid in the city. But that’s only a stop-gap solution – it’s just another concern I don’t need.

Walking up through the city proper, it's hard not to feel a flicker of pride. Four weeks ago this was an empty, echoing cavern, carved out with magic. Now almost two hundred buildings line streets connected by a functioning sanitation system. Everyone was relieved to leave the ships for proper housing, and morale is good despite the cramped conditions.

Most people are still sharing and sleeping on rushes while the carpenters work through their backlog and the mages and builders continue their tasks, but at least it’s not a dormitory anymore.

There are even proper roads these days, though we haven’t started surfacing them in any way. Fortunately, the city design makes it quick and easy to access the healer’s building.

Inside, I find River with the six healers who accompanied us from Moriax. Each of them has taken at least one apprentice – healers are definitely a Class we need far more of. Thessalanor, the healer from my ship, has become the leader – it turns out he’s the healer with the most experience out of all of them.

I wait until he has finished with his current patient, chatting with River and catching up.

Then, when he comes out, wiping his hands on a white piece of cloth, I step in his path.

“Do you have a moment? We need to talk.”
