The attack on the Noala Kingdom began without warning. At least, according to Donvath. Asher, on the other hand, received plenty of warning. Mainly in the form of a screaming High Prince kicking down his bedroom door and sprinting inside.

“Wake up!” Donvath shouted at the top of his lungs, causing Asher and Samantha to jolt back to consciousness and gasp as they realized their private room had been invaded.

“Donvath!?” Asher shouted. Whatever sleep was still left in his eyes vanished in an instant when he saw the panicked look on the High Prince’s face. “What in the—”

A sphere of green energy exploded out of Donvath, sweeping over the two of them before coming to a stop at the edge of the room. Asher had no idea what was going on, but he didn’t have to wonder for long.

“It’s a skill, Temporary Time, the rest of the world is on pause so long as we remain in here,” Donvath said, his eyes wild as the words tumbled from his mouth one after another. “The Council is here, or they were. We lost three cities almost instantly, all within minutes of one another.”

“What?!” Asher gasped, quickly pulling on his clothes as he leapt out of bed. It seemed the complete annihilation of multiple cities-worth of people was far more important than modesty, as Samantha did the same without complaint. Neither of them slept in the nude, at the very least, and Donvath didn’t look like he cared all that much about their undergarments at the moment. “What happened?”

“The Council hit five cities all at the same time. Three of them with enough force to wipe them out within minutes,” Donvath explained, his face pale enough that Asher had a painful flashback to how Lanra looked after waking up from her tritessence debt. “Dormaul, Poltar, and Juhstain, all of which were destroyed with almost the opening salvo. Furoroa and Whikoga were attacked as well, but not to the same extent. I undid time as soon as I heard.”

“How long do we have?” Asher asked, dreading the answer. Donvath had always kept the limits of his abilities close to his chest, but he had to have gone back as far into the past as possible this time. “How long before the liches strike?”

“Roughly seven minutes once we leave Temporary Time,” Donvath admitted, not even seeming to notice the national secret he’d just revealed. “But even so… I don’t know what to do, Asher, I didn’t expect this level of destruction! The number of guards doesn’t matter when a mountain is thrown at your head!”

“Take a breath,” Samantha ordered while she tied on the last piece of her armor. “First, how long will Temporary Time last?”

“As long as I want it to,” Donvath said, doing as instructed and taking one deep breath after another. “This is one of my blended skills. Time and Mind. I didn’t actually freeze time, none of this is really happening. We’re in a temporary recreation of the room within my own head happening so fast the rest of the world might as well have stopped.”

“Wait, what?” Asher asked, his brow furrowing as he looked at his own hands. “This isn’t real?”

“The memory will be,” Donvath tried to explain, a bit of color returning to his face as he calmed slightly. “That’s not important right now. Also, I’m going to have the mother of all headaches once this is over, so let’s try not to spend too long in here.”

“What happened to the cities?” Samantha asked. “Start with the three worst ones.”

“Dormaul fell the fastest,” Donvath started, his fists clenched as he shook his head. “I didn’t even think such a thing was possible, but the Council proved me wrong. One of the liches collapsed the mountain.”

“The entire mountain?” Asher asked, struggling to comprehend how such a thing could even be possible. Dormaul was a city carved into the side of a gigantic mountain that the people of the Noala Kingdom had slowly been mining out for centuries. The portion containing the city was extremely stable and didn’t even take up a third of the full mountainside. “How?!”

“Your guess is as good as mine,” Donvath admitted. “We know very little about the last five liches. Though this feat seems far too powerful for any simple skill. I’ve seen some crazy things in my time, but collapsing an entire mountain? I’d guess some sort of grand-scale ritual was required.”

“One of the liches specializes in rituals,” Samantha perked up. “He’s the one who created the ritual of extraction. Maybe that’s him.”

“That means he’s the easiest one to stop. All we need to do is disrupt the ritual,” Asher said, letting out a sigh of relief that at least one of their three main worries wouldn’t be too bad. “What about the other two? What happened to Poltar?”

Asher wasn’t sure how one would go about attacking a floating city, but Donvath’s next words answered the question for him easily enough.

“It was torn to shreds by dozens of enraged sea serpents, based on what I was told. But sea serpents shouldn’t have that kind of strength, not even in that number. And they don’t normally come up to the surface, it’s outside their zone. One of the liches must have empowered them somehow and commanded them to attack.”

“Jormon had that giant lizard monster guarding his base in the forest,” Asher pointed out. “If he could influence monsters, then it doesn’t surprise me there’s another lich that can as well.”

“And Juhstain? We’ve never been to that city,” Samantha admitted. “I don’t know anything about it.”

“It’s in the northern part of the kingdom. One of the smaller cities that basically functions as a giant quarry,” Donvath explained. “Almost our entire supply of stone comes from them. Dormaul’s stuff just can’t compare, which is why it’s mostly discarded and they focus on metals and gems instead. Anyway, a giant stone golem was constructed out of the scattered stone, and it rampaged through the city, destroying everything.”

“That one’s going to be difficult,” Asher muttered. Most likely that was Krotao, a lich they’d read about in one of Zygrul’s journals who seemed to be focused on creating the world’s deadliest golem. “What about Whikoga and Furora? You said they were hit as well, but not destroyed?”

“Whikoga was beset by an army of thousands of undead corpses with plants growing out of them that came out of nowhere. However, unlike the others, the defense that the nobles mustered was still ongoing by the time I got the report. That’s not too surprising, Whikoga is the home of Winged Death. It sounds like the liches didn’t do their homework, because swarms of weaker enemies is where she excels the most. Her Dragon element lets her burn entire armies to a crisp.”

“Go Theorn’s mom,” Asher said, wishing he could see the greater power in action. “And Furora?”

“Similarly, the nobles in Furora were managing to hold off total destruction before I snapped time back. Whatever lich attacked them managed to change the weather, making it rain down thousands upon thousands of chunks of hail ranging in size from cannonballs to entire wagons. Wagons that shattered the moment they hit the ground, sending out a spray of deadly shrapnel in all directions.”

“How in the world are they holding that off?” Asher asked.

“Again, pure luck,” Donvath admitted. “The noble family in charge of Furora is the Ignital family. Their specialty is literal firepower; each member of their family from the head to even their servants has the Fire element. They managed to combine their efforts and melt most of the ice before it landed, but a good half of the city was still destroyed from what I heard. Not to mention I’m willing to bet the lich will change up its tactic soon enough once they realize their strategy isn’t working. Remember, the attack had only been going on for two or three minutes before I used Timeline.”

“Got it,” Asher said, going over everything Donvath had told them as he made a snap decision. He’d prefer to take a bit more time, but Donvath had said Temporary Time was a blended skill, and he didn’t want to make the accompanying migraine any worse when it hit him. They’d need the High Prince for the battles to come.

“Okay. Here’s the plan. Groln and his team take on the lich with the stone golem over in Juhstain. They’re much better equipped to handle something like that, and I don’t have a mark over there, so Richard will have to send them anyway. Amanda was practically built for the lich attacking with ice, she can handle Furora on her own. The members of her Order should go provide backup to Whikoga. Even if Winged Death is there, I doubt she can hold the lich off forever, and Amanda’s Order is all about fighting undead. Mako, Cora, and Jameston should be more than enough to figure out where the lich is near Dormaul and stop him from completing his ritual. It’s not ideal, but we’ll do what we can to save Poltar. At the very least I have experience fighting the sea serpents, and Samantha can fight in the water thanks to her Water element.”

“We should make a substitution with Groln,” Samantha said, surprising him with the certainty in her voice. “If we’re going to Poltar, Rulfar’s not going to be able to do anything, and Groln’s team has that woman with the blue skin and gills. Rulfar should be able to help with the golem, and I bet that woman will be much more comfortable fighting to save a floating city.”

“It’s risky adding someone we don’t know to our team at the last second… but you’ve got a point,” Asher admitted, trying and failing to imagine Rulfar flailing around in the water in his dense battle form. “Alright, let’s do that. Moxy can remain on the city and fight from there. Even if she can create her own air now, her Air Blasts will be much weaker underwater. Kroult should stick here with you, Donvath, maybe he’ll be able to provide some sort of insight into the liches’ movements.”

“Right... that’s a good plan,” Donvath said, looking almost fully recovered as he took a few more shaky breaths. Asher couldn’t blame the High Prince for almost losing it. Living day after day, just waiting for the moment he received word that his people were dying… He’d gotten even less sleep than usual these past few weeks trying to prepare for the Council’s inevitable attack, and yet despite everything he’d done, he’d still received news that nearly half of his entire kingdom had been destroyed instantly. It was no wonder Donvath was struggling. Asher was impressed the poor guy wasn’t having a full blown panic attack and rocking back and forth in a corner somewhere.

“We’ll do what we can, Donvath, but we’re still going to be relying on you,” Asher said. “Is there a limit to how many times you can use Timeline in a row? If we mess up, can you give us another shot?”

“There are limits,” Donvath confirmed, grimacing as if deciding he’d officially said too much. “Just… try not to fail, and let’s leave it at that. Are you both ready?”

“Ready as we’ll ever be,” Asher said, getting a nod from Samantha as well.

“Alright. As soon as I drop the skill, get dressed and meet me in the conference room. I’ll get the others there, and we can distribute everyone before the attacks. That should give us a few minutes to work with.”

“Get dressed?” Asher asked, glancing down at himself. “We already are—”

A flash of green light radiated off of Donvath, and Asher blinked when he found himself lying in bed again, covers in hand as he prepared to get up. He and Samantha were right back where they’d been when Donvath had first burst into the room, and the High Prince staggered as if hit in the head with a sledgehammer.

“Ugh… cutting that one close,” he muttered, scrunching his eyes shut while he glared at them. “Conference room, hurry!” With that, Donvath ran out of the room, cursing up a storm about how bright they kept the lights in the palace hallways. The moment he was gone, Asher jumped out of bed a second time, having the strangest sense of Déjà vu as he and Samantha got dressed again.

“We got this,” Samantha said. Slinging her quiver over her back and grabbing her enchanted bow, she gave him a reassuring smile. “Let’s go kill a lich.”

“Forget that,” Asher said, matching her smile while he secured his bandoleer of daggers to his chest and they took off down the hall together. “Let’s go destroy the Council of Death.”
