Of course, it wasn’t the crowd who had so furiously shouted that word. Many of the people who had gathered to hear me speak looked downright feverish with how anticipatory they were. So, so many people in this place had once been slaves, or had seen the suffering of the chain of slavery.

And Anguis was the greatest slaver of all. There was no question that they might want to strike back at him and possibly end the Skyfall all at once.

So, where had that enraged rejection of my plea come from, loud enough that it had drawn every eye in the plaza to the source?

Why, Olag, of course.

The Captain-General of the Raven’s Beak, one of the last surviving nobles of House Florens, and, I suspect…the de facto current Lord of it had not sat idly by as I had weaved my speech. I had noticed his stirring some time into it, right around the time I had started speaking about Anguis directly. His head had darted up from within the section of the crowd where his formerly despondent men lingered, more and more agitated by my words as I spoke them.

There had been a distinct snarl on his lips, and only moments later, he had shot to his feet and begun forcing his way through the crowd, trying to reach the steps of the central pillar.

And when he had, he had shouted at the top of his lungs…

“NO!” Olag veritably screamed into the suddenly shocked silent crowd. “Are you mad?!”

I didn’t allow the smile I felt in my heart to show up on my face.

Finally.

I was wondering when this would happen. I had considered the matter before, of just what to do with you, Olag. When I was injured and nothing but a clone, I couldn’t do anything reasonable about your little slump.

But that was over. You didn’t get to wallow in your own personal sense of misery like this anymore. Not while the world was burning down around us.

It was time to be noble in action and not just in fact.

Really, I wanted to thank him. The entire drama he was helping act out had only enraptured the crowd even more. I swear I could see watchers starting to appear on the roofs of the depot buildings that surrounded the plaza. There certainly wasn’t enough room within it for anyone else.

I kept a calm face as the Dwarf started to stalk up the steps, ranting and raving all the while. “It’s pure foolishness! The Mountain Holds couldn’t care a whit about what happens down in the lowlands! We’re all just bare-faced barbarians to them! I have absolutely zero doubt they’ve weathered the Tribulation with no change in their daily routines, and that’s if they’ve even noticed it happening in the first place! They’re probably better insulated from the effects than we are! The Jarls will simply not care to let you in through the front door! And even if they did, they wouldn’t allow any kind of powerful weapon you may seek in their Holds to leave at all! Who put this fool idea in your head, Hart?! Was it Azarus?! Did that ignoramus wax poetically to you about the virtues of the holds?! About where he fled when life became too strenuous among a true civili-”

And on and on and on he went, as he climbed the steps with a despair-filled fury on his dirty face. I wasn’t paying much attention to it as he started in on the absent Azarus. Instead…

I started walking down to meet him, slowly descending the steps he was climbing. I’m not sure he even noticed. Not till I had, to his utter confusion, passed him by. It wasn’t until I was a step below the Dwarf that I turned to face Olag. And we truly were face-to-face, for perhaps the first time since we had met.

I spared no mind for his confusion. He would understand shortly. Instead…

I just smiled at him. “Olag,” I said kindly. “You should grit your teeth.”

And before the booze befuddled, enraged Dwarf could react, I lashed out with a punch. Beautiful form, if I do say so myself. A right hook aimed right at his chin that Hook himself would have been proud of. I put nearly all of the strength I could into it, too, without enhancing myself with Skills.

Mercenary Captain that he was, I was sure that he could take it.

Still, I was a bit gratified to see Azarus’s cousin drop like a stone from the punch. It was always nice to get confirmation that you weren’t sandbagging.

I neatly caught Olag’s collar before he could fall very far, only helped by the fact that I was below him. I think that the force of the punch was enough to briefly, very briefly, knock him out. Still, the Florens was made of sterner stuff, and so he recovered admirably quickly, struggling weakly against the grip I had on his collar.

I didn’t let him. I drew Olag close enough to my face that I could smell the alcohol on his fetid breath, and shook him. My voice was calm as I spoke in the aftermath of it. This wasn’t the time for raised voices.

I wanted Olag to know I meant business.

“Olag of House Florens. It’s time to grow up.”

That certainly woke the Dwarf up. His eyes flew open in furious affront at my words, and he opened his mouth to snarl something at me.

I stopped that before he could by shaking again. When I was done, I very firmly turned his head to the side, so the resulting cascade of vomit wouldn’t hit me. Once that unpleasant necessity was finished, I turned his dazed eyes back towards me.

“I’ve known you for some time now, and I think I’ve begun to understand you, Olag. You are, fundamentally, not a terrible person, I believe. Don’t get me wrong,” I huffed a dry laugh. “You’re not a good person. Not at all. I’m no stranger to the inner wheelings and dealings of the nobility, at this point. I’ve known nobles that I wouldn’t even piss on if they were on fire, much less try to stage an intervention. You are, at your very worst, maybe a four on the irredeemable piece of shit scale. Which to me means you are redeemable, if someone would just sit you down and finally tell you what your problem is.”

A familiar snarl crept back onto Olag’s face, and he set his hands on mine. I think, if he had the strength, he would have tried to overpower me and remove them from his collar.

But he didn’t, because he had spent the last several weeks in a booze fueled bender feeling sorry for himself.

“And what’s that, if you know me so well?” Olag asked bitterly.

I leaned in until he could feel my breath. “You…” I said seriously. “Are spoiled absolutely fucking rotten.”

I don’t think Olag had expected that at all, judging by how he had started gaping at me.

“It’s to be expected, I suppose. It’s an extremely common problem in the nobility. For most of them, it destroys even the foundation that a good personality can be built from. Luckily enough, you Florens are built from sturdier moral fiber than that. You’re not a lost cause, Olag. I think, for all of your faults, you can still become a person who’s more than just another squabbling fop with an overinflated sense of self-importance, desperately clutching at whatever scraps of power you can.”

Olag…looked away, a soft snort escaping his nose. “What does it matter to you, Hart? What’s your point?”

I almost wanted to shake him again, but through a downright Herculean level of self-control, I restrained myself. “The first reason should be obvious, you thick-headed fool. In case you haven’t noticed, Olag, the entire fate of not only your people, but the planet itself is in the balance. You, for all your faults, are an experienced military commander used to operating at a level above small squad tactics. You’re not a real general, despite your pompous title, but you’re valuable. I’m going to need all of the people I can find with experience leading soldiers when it comes time to storm the gates of Smaragd. From a purely pragmatic point of view, you’re useful. At least, you would be, if you can pull your tattooed, defeatist skull from your ass. But, I’ll admit…that’s not the whole reason I want you functional. I owe it to someone to try and save you from yourself. There’s someone in your family I owe a lot to, and I don’t want to insult their memory by not trying.”

The Dwarf in my grip suddenly snarled, far angrier than I had expected. He reared back his head, in a split second, I could tell he was going to use it to ram into my own.

Alright then.

I copied the movement, and at the same time Olag tried to headbutt me, I met it with one of my own. I’d never actually countered a headbutt with one of my own, and it was…a bit jarring, but nothing I couldn’t take. I saw stars for a moment, but I maintained both my stance and my grip on Olag. I barely managed to without a curse at the dizziness, but Olag didn’t even try.

“Damn you, I’d rather drown to death on whiskey and rum than owe anything to that fool Azarus!” Olag hissed at me, tensing up like he was going to fight me even harder.

My eyes narrowed. “I wasn’t talking about Azarus, you dumb bastard.” I hissed right back. “I was talking about your grandfather.”

Olag…froze. I’d never seen so much confusion on his face before. The Dwarf was outright gaping now. “What?”

I heaved a sigh, momentarily looking away from him before I forced my gaze back. “Your grandfather,” I confirmed. “Baldric, of House Florens. Once of House Florens, that is. He’s…been dead for some time.”

My eyes drifted past Olag, up to both Maria and Sylvia on the steps above me. They’d been watching patiently while I had my little confrontation with the grandson of our former leader in the Nocturne Division. Olag twisted in my grip to see where I had been looking, only to be even more confused at the knowing looks he was receiving from the two women. “What the devil are you talking about? Of course he is, the Dwarf died when I was barely three years old. I remember nearly nothing about him.”

I slowly shook my head. “That’s not…quite true. You have to know that Baldric used to be in the running to be the Prince, correct? I’m betting that’s the entire reason you even challenged Elysael’s claim to the throne. After all, even if Morok as the previous Prince was her Father, you’re still technically from the elder line that descended from Baldric, who should have been Prince…”

“If he hadn’t died in a private expedition to the Deadlands, yes,” Olag replied, audibly confused. “You…know more about our genealogy than I expected, Hart. Where did you-”

“He faked his death,” I said bluntly, cutting him off.

Olag gaped at me, stunned.

“Morok asked Baldric to step aside, for the good of Rhoscara, when the faction of nobles who no doubt backed you tried to back him for the throne. Neither one of them were power hungry enough to want such instability in House Florens…so Baldric faked his death, and from there, found himself in Herztal. He had been approached by someone who was looking for a new commander of the spy division of his Order, you see. The previous one had just died from Core Collapse, and Baldric was the perfect candidate. And so, Baldric of House Florens came to be the head of a division of spies known as the Nocturne Division, of the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn, recruited by Grand Marshal Greycton himself.”

Slowly, Olag shook his head, looking like a stiff wind would blow him over. “You…how do you know this…”

Seeing how the fight had vanished from the Dwarf, I slowly set him down on his feet. I’m not sure the Dwarf even noticed from how he was looking up at me. “I heard it from his own lips,” I said heavily, feeling the weight of an old regret. “Not days before he died saving me. Me, and…one other. Just like Maria, and just like Sylvia, I was a member of the Nocturne Division, and Hook-”

(I was gratified to see Olag’s eyes widen even more, as he made the connection. I had been told, by the Dwarf himself, that he had been famous in Velancia for the hooked daggers he fought his duels with. I guess Olag had been raised on stories of those weapons, which now lay with Liora.)

“-was our commander. He died on the day Rhazal, Calamity and Father of Monstrosity, lay siege to the city of Elderwyck. You should know…if you want to learn more about his later years, you’ll not only have to pull yourself together, you’ll have to make sure we all survive this. There’s someone who knew him better than anyone that he de facto raised, in the absence of his family.”

“I…you…you could be making him all up,” Olag said, visibly overwhelmed. I couldn’t help but notice his hands were shaking, just a bit.

“I could, I suppose,” I inclined my head slightly. “But I’m not. There’s even proof. And you can find it, if you do your duty to your people and pull yourself together to follow us north. Baldric asked me to give a certain locket to Azarus, and I did. I happen to know he never takes it off, and Azarus…happens to be making his way north as well.”

For a long, silent moment, Olag was still, staring off into the distance. It was strange. Up until this moment, Olag had looked lost, yes, but it had been a much more despair-filled state of being. He still looked lost…

Now, there was a glimmer of life in how lost he looked. As if yes, he didn’t know for sure what he was doing with himself…

But he was interested in finding out.

“Alright,” Olag eventually whispered, only barely loud to be heard. “I’ll…try and muster my forces. For all of how foolish it is…you’ve pulled off foolish stunts before, Hart. As of now, the Raven’s Beak…is yours once more. Excuse me. I…need to gather the men.”

Wordlessly, I nodded and stepped out of the way of the Dwarf, as he descended down the very steps he had charged up only moments ago. I very carefully repressed a smirk as his steps stuttered for a moment when he noticed something.

It’s not like the enormous crowd surrounding the lift steps had ever dispersed…

And I had never dispelled the voice amplification Spell. The entire enraptured crowd had heard every last word of our little confrontation and seemed to have enjoyed the drama.

Very much so.

A rousing speech, a little bit of dramatic flavor after…it was a winning combination to sway hearts and minds. Especially with the Rhoscaran Dwarves, those refugees who had just lost their very home.

Dwarves loved the little dramas their nobility involved themselves in. A rousing tale of heroism about one who had faked their own death, and the confirmation that at least one of House Florens was still willing to fight for them, even with the loss of Rhoscara…

It would do wonders for their morale.

Which is what I had wanted, of course. Sentiment like that had a habit of spreading like wildfire, in a crowd this size.

I spread my arms wide, there upon the steps of the lift. “What say you?!” I cried out into the space above the plaza, my amplified voice carrying far and wide. “Will you fight with me once more, soldiers of the expedition?! Will you war to break the greatest chain of all, warriors of the Unchained?! Has Anguis of Savoy broken your SPIRIT?!”

The answering roar, echoing from thousands of voices, certainly lifted my own.

“NO!”

Never had a ‘no’ sounded so sweet.
