“That’s not a Spirit Beast,” Fade said in a low mental tone. At the same time, I couldn’t help but notice that he had crouched lower, almost as if he were anticipating an attack. “I can feel it. Something about it is different.”

My eyes narrowed, and I slowly took a deep breath. I…hadn’t expected that. For a moment, I had been so sure that whatever and whoever that enormous mole had been was one of the first Spirit Beasts. The sheer size and scale of the long-dead creature were such that I had suspected it might have been one of the first. A contemporary of Shurenga and Taran. I had never forgotten the mysterious words of the enormous, flaming tiger, daughter of Tarus, about how there were other firstborn out there. The original spawn of the Great Spirits, who had been crafted more than born, as a weapon meant to aim at the heart of the Netherim usurpers who had tormented them.

Shurenga was Tarus’s. Taran was Elys’s.

That still left Orus, Neris, and presumably, Anima. I’m…not sure the Great Spirit of Life even had Spirit Beasts, in the same way she didn’t have Envoys. And it’s not like I could ask her.

Something about this cave was still blocking my connection to her.

The point being, I had thought that this creature might have been Orus’s. But I was willing to trust Fade’s word that this wasn’t a Spirit Beast…

And if that was true, then what the Hell was it?

“Let’s just…leave it be,” I said lowly, my voice nonetheless still echoing out over the cavern. “We should look in the other areas down here for a way out. And when we do, we leave.”

“And seal this place behind us,” Sylvia whispered tensely. I heard the creak of her gloves as she tightened her Mithril fists on her still drawn swords.

Wordlessly, I nodded. Almost reluctantly, oddly afraid to look away from the deathly spectacle, I flickered my eyes around the rest of the cavern. Now that we had tentatively identified this creature as some kind of mole, the rest of it made sense.

This place was festooned with tunnels. It’s why I had initially pegged it as a home of some sort. They were all over the place. On the ground floor, on the walls, and I think…

I squinted through the dim light of our Skills.

Yeah, there were a few tunnels on the ceiling of the cave as well.

Had that creature done all of this? Carve out the cave and the tunnels? Was it all…just for itself?

Or had it been for the Humans that now surrounded it in death, like loyal followers?

I couldn’t see very far down any of the tunnels, but that didn’t matter to me. I doubted they were short, because they sure as Hell weren’t narrow. The reason I had considered whether the mole had carved them was that all of the tunnels looked big enough for the long-dead creature to have comfortably traversed them.

There was no visible way that whoever had lived down here had reached those higher tunnels. If there had been ladders, or ropes, or something, they had long since rotted away into dust. Still, if we needed to, it wouldn’t be hard for any of us to reach them, considering our physical abilities. With Thorn Grapple alone, I’d be able to ferry us to them.

I met Sylvia’s eyes and cautiously nodded towards the closest tunnel from where we stood at the spiked entrance. She returned it slowly and deliberately turned her back on the dead, trusting me to watch it. This time, I nudged Fade lightly with one foot, from where he had been absorbed in watching the dead with narrowed eyes. He startled, and when he snapped his head up to me, I nodded toward the tunnel where Sylvia was waiting. He understood and trotted past me.

I was the last one to enter into it, and when I did…I couldn’t help but look over my shoulder one last time.

Maybe it was paranoia, or maybe it was just instinct.

But when I did, I felt a chill run down my spine.

It had to just be the different angle, I told myself. The way light was now reaching across the cavern was the only logical explanation.

The mole’s skull only looked to have moved slightly from its original position.

I shuddered lightly and followed after Fade and Sylvia.

…………………………

I was right. This was a home, once upon a time, or at least something close to that.

Maybe shelter was a better term. Or even, perhaps…monastery.

Set into the walls of the tunnel we had searched through were what looked to be cells. Not in the sense of a prison, though. Instead, they were carved out spaces in the rock designed to house a person. They were barely big enough to have a raised surface to either sit or sleep upon, and then another carved surface slightly higher to act as a…maybe a table or something? I had no way of knowing. There were, to a one, bare of any decorating or furnishing. They didn’t even have doors.

Maybe, like ladders or ropes, they had succumbed to the passage of time. Or maybe they had just never had any in the first place.

For all I knew, these people had liked to sleep on solid beds of granite.

Of the technological wonders I typically associated the Netherim with, I saw nothing. If those bodies out there had been Netherim, they had lived an incredibly spartan existence in comparison to anything I had seen in the bunkers.

An odd thought. I had never once wondered if the Netherim had a religion of their own. Was this…actually a monastery as I had wondered?

Had they worshipped the mole? I would be astonished if that were the case. The Netherim had always struck me as very scientifically minded.

I…what did I really know about them? Not much, in the end. The repository back in Blutstein was more of a collection of technical documents, not cultural. Had those long-dead people worshipped anything?

What had the Netherim believed in? I didn’t know, and Aveline couldn’t tell me either. She had been too young to really absorb such things.

Staring out into yet another deserted stone cell, I wondered. This couldn’t be something the Netherim had practiced…could it?

I shook my head at Sylvia and Fade’s looks of curiosity…

And moved on.

………………………………….

The three of us found nothing in that tunnel. Just a long stretch of empty, deserted, nigh identical cells on either side of us, until we hit an abrupt end.

Unwilling and unable to give up, we traced our path backward to the main chamber and tried again in another tunnel.

Nothing there, either. Just more of the same.

So we tried again.

And again.

And again.

By the fifth tunnel, I’ll admit that I was starting to lose hope when it came to finding a way out. I wasn’t the only one who was discouraged, either. Fade’s endurance was, well, fading. All the tension of navigating these empty halls, with nothing but the sensation of Death Aether in them to greet us, was fraying at his senses. Death Aether by itself wasn’t oppressive. To me, it was an almost sense of ever-present unease at the back of my neck. An impression that something here was antithetical to life. That while we weren’t unwelcome, the rushing of warm lifeblood through our veins was only barely tolerated. For Spirit Wolf, whose senses were so much keener than our own, it must have been much more grating.

Fade never once uttered a word of complaint, however. He didn’t need to.

I could see the strain it was putting him under, just being in here. Sylvia noticed, too.

With how long used to non-verbal instructions, the two of us were with each other, both from our work and our relationship, Sylvia nodded to the exhausted wolf and flashed me a few symbols. Cocking my head in consideration, I nodded back to her. Without a word, Sylvia gently guided Fade over to one of the walls of the central chamber and sat down with him, resting against it. Almost immediately, the erstwhile son of the moon slipped into an uneasy, exhausted state, laying his head on Sylvia’s lap.

Quirking a smile at the sight, I turned my back on them to look upwards.

At the near exact center of the cavern was a tunnel that led upwards. It had to have been nearly thirty feet in the air from where I stood. In consideration of just how hard it would have been to ferry Fade up to that hole, we hadn’t explored that tunnel yet. But with my Familiar resting in the lap of my lover, I had more freedom to act.

I wasn’t scared of going off on my own at this point. At least, not for myself. I was merely a clone, and if something bad happened to me to pop this artifice, I’d just create another one to send out. The only danger came from how I would be leaving Sylvia and Fade to fend for themselves. I had no idea if I could trace my path back to this place through the darkness of the caldera outside. Maybe I could do so by following my bond to Fade, but that was a hard maybe. And there was still no guarantee that I could open the door once more from the outside.

No, our best chance was unraveling this ‘trap’ from the inside.

I…was having a hard time considering this a real trap, actually. I had sensed no danger at all in the nearly two hours we had spent sealed within. Nothing in the tunnels, and not even the remains in the central chamber, had triggered my instincts at all.

Even though I suspected something was going on with the bones of the mole…I didn’t think it had any ill will toward us.

Quid pro quo.

If it didn’t want to attack me and mine, I was fine leaving it alone.

I cast out a hand and triggered Thorn Grapple, aiming it up toward the opening in the ceiling. Unfortunately, the hardened tip of my wasn’t strong enough to pierce the stone. I had to cast out several different lines trying to get it to catch onto something, standing, aware that I likely looked like a fool.

Some people would ask why I didn’t just transform and fly up to the tunnel.

I would answer by saying…shut up, man.

Feeling a flush roll over my cheeks, aware of the knowing, amused look on Sylvia’s face, I triggered Vis Maledicta Exactoris, transformed to my full height, and leapt.

In moments, I had cleared the rim of it, and shot a clawed hand upward to grab the lip of a rim that had revealed itself. I spared a single glance to the side, noticing what looked to be a very weathered-looking stone loop of some kind set into the wall next. Sourly, I realized I…probably could have caught that with my grapple.

Oh, whatever. I cancelled my transformation as I came to stand on the floor of what seemed to be a hidden second floor to the cavern.

Turns out, it wasn’t just another tunnel.

I stood in the middle of a platform nearly ten feet in diameter, and branching out from it in a spoke arrayed around it were more tunnels. Five, it looked like, each of them was just as large and just as identical to those found on the first floor.

I stifled a sigh, only to choke on it as something startled me badly enough that I started coughing. I must have been loud enough for the sound to carry all the way back down where I came.

“Nathan, are you alright?!” I heard the faint voice of Sylvia call up to me in concern.

I pounded my chest, clearing my false ‘lungs’, and answered. “Y-Yes! I’m fine, it’s…probably nothing!”

“Alright…” Was the skeptical reply.

But I paid it no mind. I was busy goggling at something else instead.

What had surprised me wasn’t anything that was actually up here. Instead, it was something that I had brought with me.

The staff of twisted ebony wood, with its craggy amber capstone nestled in the boughs, had suddenly started to glow.

That…wasn’t where I had been projecting my light Skill from. The staff had just suddenly started doing that by itself.

I stared at the strange sight uncomprehendingly for a moment. I had never once seen this thing do tha-

Wait. Waitwaitwaitwaitwait.

That wasn’t true, was it? I had seen the capstone light up before, hadn’t I?

Back during my battle with Rhazal, I had been transported physically into the Concord, where it had been shaped by the Calamity’s will into a reflection of my home on Terra. At the time, I had taken up this staff after the previous owner had been scattered by Rhazal. It had been the only weapon I had at the time, and I had been very startled when the stone had lit up and a voice had started to speak to me in my mind. The voice of the original owner.

The Lich I had found living underneath the streets of Elderwyck. The Lich who had apparently been one of Grey's old adventuring buddies. The Lich who had once upon a time been Honoka’s husband…when he had just been a man named Rafael.

Tlazo.
