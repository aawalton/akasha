“[Are you sure there are people around? It… doesn’t look like it.]”

Perched above Henry, Maurice—in his crab form—took in the coast. The crab was awed. And so was Henry. A high, wall-like cliff faced the two of them. From one side of the horizon to the other, jagged rocks blocked their view of the land, and unlike the Emerald Isles, the Reach was no archipelago. It was a continent, stretching endlessly toward the horizon. Even though they were still a decent distance away, that fact was undeniable.

“There’s supposed to be a couple of cities somewhere that sees most of the traffic, but I think we got blown off-course a little,” Henry projected with an illusionary voice, being also in his natural form. Looking around for a moment and through his clones, he made sure nothing was sneaking up on them before focusing back on the pillars of golden light he could see far beyond the cliff. He didn’t miss the swarm of birds that made the steep rocks their homes, and he didn’t miss that some of them were approaching, flying lower and lower in a circle above them.

“[Lots of birds,]” Maurice commented, looking up, and Henry followed his gaze. He wasn’t sure whether the birds were curious or planning something, but the two of them were ready. They hadn’t spent the last couple of weeks idling. “[Sooo…]” Maurice trailed off for a little, and Henry felt his stomach tighten a little. “[Today, then?]”

“[Yup,]” Henry answered. “[Today. Before we start dealing with whatever’s waiting for us up there.]”

“[Are you ready?]”

Henry had spent the last three weeks preparing and stockpiling cores. And though Zerathstra and Arisia had warned him it might be risky, he’d had a lot of time to sleep on it and consider it, and in between them leaving First Landing and now, it had been nearly three weeks of hunting, training, and sparring, to the point that finally, all of Henry’s Aspects had hit the top of B-rank. So if he was to attempt a second Aspect fusion, it was now or never.

“[I can consistently compel their essences into fusing. It’s a lot harder than it had been with the basic version, but I think I can make it work and if I do… I’ll think of something for the required rest and stabilization period. That might be the hardest part. Hopefully, the fact that I haven’t started pushing any of them for A-rank helps, even if that makes me fall a little behind. It would probably impossible to fuse them, otherwise.]”

“[Yeah. I guess… Then again, it doesn’t feel easy to get them anywhere near that point. I’ve been giving mine everything I’ve been getting, and it doesn’t feel like any of my Aspect’s ready to jump to A-rank. Probably need to kill more A-ranks,]” the crab muttered.

That, Henry had to admit, did lift a little weight off of his shoulders. He had been a little worried about falling behind. But he had a good reason. Back when Henry had been fusing his Aspects for the first time, he had needed a couple days of rest then a rank-up to finally feel like he’d stabilized the fusion. Now though? It felt like the Aspects were almost calcified, and they weren’t as willing to fuse or work together as they had been. So likely, the healing might take a while, and to help on that front, Henry had done his utmost to not let the energies from the core they’d been harvesting encroach on the Aspects. Just so he wouldn’t create any imbalance that might trip him up later.

I have to get started on the fusion now, then start pushing everything to A-rank. But the fusion first. If I don’t do it now, I’ll never be able to, Henry reminded himself, eyeing the couple of B-rank, seagull-looking birds as they flew closer to them. Zerathstra had been categorical about it. Once Aspects hit A-rank, they wouldn’t fuse. The ape himself had tried. B-rank was the last chance. And it was unfortunate that Henry’s Aspect had all hit level 10, but it wasn’t too late, yet. Still, the fact that Henry’s Aspect had all been fused once already was a challenge. He had no intent on fusing everything, but one pairing in particular could open a lot of doors for him.

“[You think it’ll work?]” Maurice asked, raising a claw and snapping it toward the closest bird, which made the air right next to the seagull’s head detonate with a pop that sent the creature scrambling.

“[Probably. Let’s find a better spot, though,]” Henry answered, diving back below the wave as they continued their approach. He should be able to find a decent, large enough cave under the cliff, which he’d seal up and start to work on creating his second—and likely only—twice fused Aspect.

The combination of Thaumaturgy of the Trickster and Dancing Shadows.

That was the fusion Henry had been working on these last few weeks. Fusing the arcane-based Aspect responsible for his Octominds to the stealth and mobility-focused Aspect that gave him his Trickster’s Shadows. The resulting combination might help save Velistraine, if she was even still alive. It’d empower his Shadows further, potentially making them all Octominds and it might even end up with him becoming some sort of collective, like that Fractalkin kraken evolution he’d been offered before. Unsurprisingly, his Octominds made it trivial to remember that specific evolution’s entry, and Henry had to admit, this evolution had inspired him a bit when he’d been busy working on corralling the essences.

Fractalkin Kraken

A kaleidoscope of flesh, shadows, and illusions. The Fractalkin Kraken splits into mirrored selves, each one real enough to wound and vanish, and as long as any one of them lives, the kraken cannot know death. A perfect predator for sowing confusion and surviving all of its enemies.

Of course, Henry didn’t want to stop there. Fusing Thaumaturgy and Dancing Shadows wasn’t just about creating redundancies and relying on illusions. No, he wanted the network of Shadows, but he wanted them each to have a portion of himself. He wanted them to be elusive, but just as real as his Octominds and himself. He wanted his ability to move between them to become easier. He wanted more reach.

Letting out a slow breath, Henry dragged his expectations back to reality as he began scanning the rocks for a good hideout. The potential was there. For sure. He just had to focus and get a hold of it. Henry wasn’t sure if his vision would work, but the core idea had been accepted by the essence, and the proof was the many little vibrant, purple orbs of crystallized essence sitting in his storage.

Just to keep himself sharp, Henry drew some of the essences in his core and started fusion them again. More practice wouldn’t hurt.

“[Oh Henry, check that one!]”

Henry quietly changed direction and headed toward the widening crack Maurice had pointed out while in front of his two frontal arms, a little nimbus of intense blues and elusive purple began to mix.

Once Henry wrestled the Aspect together, he could work on fusing the cornerstones. And then… And then they’d see if the upgrade managed to propagate to the clones he’d left with Velistraine. It was unlikely, but it wouldn’t hurt to try.

“[Oooh. This cave’s nice! There’s even little crabs here!]”

It was a cove, Henry realized as he broke above the water. And it was gorgeous. Somehow, the light was spilling into the cave from the water, so there was a little bit of vegetation growing, but deeper into the cove, it was mostly moss. Some small, weak, pale crabs seemed to have made this spot their residence, and now that Henry and Maurice showed up, they clacked their pincers at them. From afar. They might be territorial, but they still did their posturing from the lip of a smaller tunnel on the right side of the cove. Safety first, and all that.

“[We’re gonna intrude for a bit. But be nice and we’ll give you food,]” Henry broadcasted to the pale crabs, which made them all scurry back into the tiny tunnels. Lifting the finished essence combination, Henry stared at it, thinking for a moment.

“[I feel like it’s staring at me,]” commented Maurice, peering over Henry’s head. “[It almost looks like it’s made of roe, inside. But the roe are eyes.]”

Henry let out a snort. It did somewhat look like that. The essence tended to crystallize in some sort bead that was full of other tiny beads, which glowed and almost seemed to swirl, but Henry was almost sure they weren’t moving. It was like an optical illusion of some sorts, which… gave him confidence that his idea would work.

“[It’s time,]” Henry muttered. Both to himself and to Maurice.

It was time.
