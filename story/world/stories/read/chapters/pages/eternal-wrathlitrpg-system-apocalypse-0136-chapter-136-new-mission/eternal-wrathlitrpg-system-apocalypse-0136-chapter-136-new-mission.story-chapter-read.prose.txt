“That boss must have been worth a lot,” James muttered under his breath. The helicopter that saw him earlier landed a few meters from him, saluted him for a job well done, and offered a ride back. The military helicopter was large enough to fit his raptor, so James took the offer.

“Greetings, Mr. James. I see you worked fast as always. Incredible. Our liquid nitrogen bombs worked wonders. Not as good as your axe, though. I suppose you are not willing to part with it? It would help our effort against the remaining monster.” Silas, the shameless bureaucrat he was, welcomed James with open arms. As usual, James ignored him and called Ryan to order a plane ticket home.

“Hey, Ryan. I killed the boss here. The army will be cleaning up the rest of the monsters. The town was already decimated by the time I got here. Sorry about that.”

“No. I am sorry to impose on you, Mr. James. The fact that my hometown wasn’t glassed with radioactive material is good enough. The town could be rebuilt anytime. You do not need to concern yourself with such trivialities. May I know which dungeon you plan on conquering next?” Ryan thanked James on the phone. James pondered Ryan’s nonchalant attitude. Ryan was already old, so he probably didn’t have much to lose except a place for his funeral.

“Dunno. I didn’t plan that far ahead. Any interesting dungeons? Maybe the one that only accepts one person or five at maximum for my volunteers? I am tired of working in groups. We also took quite a lot of losses in the previous medium-dungeon raid.”

“Well, about that. Silas has been offering me and your company a generous benefit to accept a mission just now. Was he there?” James snapped his head to Silas, who fiddled with his phone. When he sensed James turn to him, he gave him a broad smile.

“Alright. Thanks for the heads-up. Silas was indeed here. Let me ask what he wanted.” James strolled over to him. Like a thug, he asked, “So, what do you want? Stop beating around the bush. I don’t hate you as much as Andrew did, but I don’t appreciate people sneaking up behind my back.”

“Well, I was informed that a dungeon just turned red a few minutes ago, Mr. James. Seeing that you have successfully beaten the boss of this dungeon, I was wondering whether you could help us defend against it. They may require some specialized weapons to defeat, you see.”

“Just tell me so. Why bother getting to Ryan?” James sighed.

“I merely thought helping your company would put me in your good books, Mr. James. Money clearly is no longer an issue for you.” Silas gave a disgusting smile.

“So, when do we leave?”

“At your earliest convenience, Mr. James.”

“Will there be anyone else?”

“Everyone you know from the guild was invited. Andrew, Kouki, Kaina, Anderson—all of them,” Silas answered. James blinked in response. Suddenly, he got a premonition that this was not a normal red dungeon.

“What dungeon turned red? Was it a medium dungeon?”

“It is an advanced dungeon, Mr. James. The one at the nuclear complex. It is either a blessing or a curse to have it pop up right now. Now we don’t have to gather medium-dungeon cores to free it,” Silas said.

“But you risk destroying it during the monster assault, huh?” James said. He didn’t miss Silas saying that it was both a curse and a blessing.

“Indeed. That would be catastrophic. That’s why we are doing our very best to gather all survivors we can and several nuclear engineers to make sure such things do not happen.”

“I see.” James nodded. “Then, let’s get on with it.”

“Of course. Right this way, Mr. James. Ah—Mr. Andrew, so what about the offer?”

“We will accept,” Andrew said. His face was bitter.

Andrew hated Silas for taking over his guild and stopping all activity related to dungeon exploration. But Andrew did all of this to prevent the nuclear meltdown in the first place. Knowing that the dungeon had turned red meant Silas was right. It was a waste to explore dungeons; they could just camp outside, waiting until the dungeon attacked them. Here, humanity had the high ground. They had tanks, fighter jets, missiles, and even nukes.

“Thank you for your understanding. Let us depart immediately. I have arranged your accommodation. It won’t be a five-star hotel, but it should suffice. There is a village near the nuclear complex, and we have rented a motel there.”

“A motel? I suppose you not only robbed us of our weapons but also our money, huh?” Sanders said.

“Ah, this is not something mere money could solve. We could not possibly build a hotel in a day. It is better than the alternative. Most soldiers camped outside the village, you know. If you would prefer a hotel, there is one over fifty kilometers out. But that would mean over an hour drive to the nuclear complex.”

“I’ll take the village. How far is it?” James said.

“Practically next to the complex. Less than five kilos,” Silas said as he welcomed the group into his helicopter and called another one to bring James’ raptor to the airport.

Two days later, when James arrived at the nuclear complex, soldiers and nuclear physicists were already on the scene. Tents were erected, and people in lab coats were hanging around with their eyes glued to their tablets and monitors. James had no idea what they were trying to do with how impenetrable the dungeon was. Were they gathering data on dungeon monsters?

“They were fast. We didn’t take any breaks and still needed two days to arrive. Look, they already have tents right next to the dungeon,” James’ volunteer said.

“...Silas, why are they putting their tents so close to the dungeon? What if monsters attack them? We don’t know whether their guns and tanks would affect the monsters,” James said.

“Please don’t worry, Mr. James. They are willing to risk their lives for this mission,” Silas said. “The nuclear shutdown is critical, and we must do everything we can to achieve it.”

“I see.” James understood what Silas meant. He meant the team would not wait for the survivors or the military to beat the boss, but would attempt to shut it down in the middle of the fight.

“Ah, to increase our chances further, we will also teach you how to press the emergency shutdown button, or as we know it, the SCRAM button,” Silas said as he took out his tablet and showed him a picture of the big red button and a map of the nuclear complex with several red dots on it. James tilted his head; he assumed that was where the SCRAM buttons were, but why were there so many? There must have been at least ten buttons there.

“Here is the map of the nuclear complex. The SCRAM buttons are located here.”

“Why so many? Do we have to press all of them?” James frowned.

“Pfft. Ah, my apologies, Mr. James. No, you need to press only one button. Any button will do. So if you find any button like this, you press it, and the nuclear reactor will be turned off. Then you can duke it out with the invading monsters at your own leisure,” Silas said.

“...That’s it?” James said.

“That’s it. No weird ‘but’ or ‘if’ or ‘only.’ Press the button under any circumstances and the mission is complete,” Silas said. It was the first time James had ever heard a short and concise answer from government bureaucrats.

“That sounds easy enough. Why have those smart people in lab coats then?” James turned toward the researchers. If all he needed to do was press a single red button to stop the reactor, why did Silas hire a bunch of these guys?

“Ah, they are there to ascertain the damage afterwards. You see, our government is a bit greedy. They thought maybe we could reuse the reactor if the damage was minimal. This nuclear reactor could provide energy to the entire country. I hope you understand. They are also here to ensure the SCRAM button works properly,” Silas answered.

“Alright. Is there anything else? Call me when monsters begin spawning from the dungeon. I’m going to grab something to eat,” James said, returning the tablet to Silas.

“Of course,” Silas said as he received the tablet while bowing. James glanced at the nuclear complex through the translucent red barrier. It pulsed with an ominous light, as if it were ready to paint the world with blood.

The following days were peaceful. James hung around the small village, occasionally riding his raptor. There was a time when he went near the dungeon to read its information.

There were no survivor restrictions for this dungeon. That made James wonder whether he should assault this dungeon instead of waiting for the red dungeon to pop. But Silas and Andrew disagreed. If they assaulted the dungeon and it turned out they needed medium-dungeon cores, all their hard work would be in vain. They would have to redo everything all over again.

Moreover, the water reservoir in the nuclear reactor was draining out. They didn’t have time for trial and error. It was safer to just wait until the dungeon popped. Red dungeons usually popped within a week or two. That was why Silas and Andrew agreed to just wait it out.

The wait certainly frustrated him. If this was to be the case, he would rather go back home and store his new weapons.

As the fourth day passed, an idea struck James.

“System. I remember you talking about a merit shop, where you can exchange merit for skills,” James asked. Now that he didn’t need to explore dungeons, there was no need to save merit. He could spend it now to obtain new skills to further his survival.

[Yes. After you have leveled up to level 50, you may exchange merit for a new skill. However, the skills you obtain will be random. Would you like to roll for it?]

“Yes, please.”

“...So this is how you obtained the infamous shield skill. But I don’t have a system-registered shield!” James cursed at his luck. He should have known better than to rely on luck in dungeons.
