"Where are my numbers?"

Ning froze.

The best part of Know Thyself was, well... the numbers. Watching them go brrr was half his motivation. If Elder Shen had something like this, he probably wouldn't have abandoned his cultivation path and evolved into a full-time couch potato.

So losing the numbers?

A devastating emotional blow.

But Ning quickly calmed himself and noticed something in the corner of the interface.

He focused on it mentally.

Lo and behold, a three-dimensional model of a human body materialized before him.

Ning's attention immediately sharpened.

Meridians ran throughout the model, following routes he was intimately familiar with after years of cultivation. Most were green, while several sections were blue. A few points along the route still glowed red.

After studying it for a while, Ning began to understand what he was looking at.

The model wasn't merely showing his meridians. It was showing their current condition. Green represented areas that were functioning normally, while the blue sections had endured greater strain during his breakthrough. The few red points marked where the pressure had been greatest.

Ning stared at the model.

His panel had become more comprehensive.

But why now?

He had broken through before without anything like this happening. Fifth Stage wasn't even a major transformation, so the advancement itself probably wasn't enough to explain it.

Perhaps the difference was how much more clearly he understood what had happened inside his body.

For the past two years, Ning had treated Know Thyself mostly as a convenient status screen. Numbers went up, Ning became happy.

But that wasn't what the ability actually was.

Know Thyself.

The name had been telling him from the beginning.

Ning's expression became strange.

Ironically, to better understand Know Thyself, he first had to understand himself.

If his guess was correct, then the ability wasn't simply improving because his cultivation had advanced. The more Ning understood his own body, qi, and cultivation, the more information Know Thyself could present to him.

And if that was true, this little human model might only be the beginning.

If he could eventually see exactly how pills, poisons, or spiritual foods affected his body, that alone would be incredibly useful.

After all, cultivation was basically:

Eat mysterious things → hope you don't explode → see what happens.

Having a panel that could warn him before he detonated?

A phenomenal quality-of-life upgrade.

There was just one problem.

Ning looked back at the panel.

His numbers were still gone.

"Show my numbers."

Nothing happened.

Ning frowned and tried again. Since the panel responded to his intent, the old numerical interface should still be there somewhere. He hadn't necessarily lost the information itself; Know Thyself might simply be presenting it differently now.

At least, he hoped so.

For the next few hours, Ning replenished his qi while continuously trying to switch the interface back. Nearly six hours later, the panel finally flickered, and the three-dimensional body model disappeared as the familiar number-filled display returned in its place.

Ning nearly shed a tear.

The numbers were back.

"So I can only use one interface at a time for now."

That was his best guess, anyway. Switching between them had taken six whole hours of concentration, which made changing interfaces in the middle of a fight about as practical as asking the enemy to wait while he finished reading the manual.

Still, this was the first significant change Know Thyself had undergone in two years. If it really was a growth-type ability, Ning certainly wasn't going to complain about gaining another function simply because it was troublesome to use at the beginning.

Satisfied, he dismissed the panel and collapsed onto his bed. The exhaustion from breaking through, followed by six hours of repeatedly bullying his own ability into returning his precious numbers, had finally caught up with him.

Just like that, Ning entered his favorite kind of relationship.

Death without commitment.

He slept.

...

The martial courtyard buzzed with heat and vigor.

Numerous outer-sect martial artists swung swords, sabers, and staffs. Every movement seemed to stir the already-warm air, making the courtyard several degrees hotter than the outside world.

Fortunately, Ning had already reached the Skin Refinement stage of body training. At the very least, his body temperature wouldn't rise too quickly. You could even say he was cold as ice.

...Well. Not really.

Having reached the Fifth Stage of Qi Condensation, Ning was determined to improve his close-combat ability. Hence, the martial courtyard.

Training spear in hand, Ning made his way toward one of the arenas.

Yes. A spear.

After pondering the matter for some time, Ning had chosen the spear as his secondary weapon. The reason was simple: he had discovered a fondness for weapons with long reach.

Simply put, he liked hitting enemies with a pointy stick.

Among close-combat weapons, the spear stood near the top in that regard. It also suited his strengths surprisingly well. Proper spearwork relied heavily on judging distance, angles, and movement, all things Ning's spatial perception happened to excel at.

"Ning, you're late."

The one calling out to him was none other than Zhang Feng.

Zhang Feng was usually a carefree fellow, but when it came to martial arts, he became a fanatic. He trained body refinement even more diligently than qi cultivation and spent much of his free time honing his physical combat skills.

"It took me a while to tend to the fields," Ning replied.

"Ah, that's different." Zhang Feng waved him over. "Come on, then. Let's start."

Ning didn't need to be told twice.

After deciding to practice the spear, he had searched for a suitable manual, which was where Zhang Feng came in.

Falling Leaf Spear Technique.

It was the technique Zhang Feng had recommended, and after reading through the manual, Ning immediately understood why.

The Falling Leaf Spear Technique didn't begin with flashy moves or mystical attacks. It started with the fundamentals of spear training. Several pages were devoted entirely to posture, balance, exertion, and how force should travel through the body.

Spear training wasn't just about learning techniques. It also tempered the body, stirred the blood and qi, and trained explosive force until a simple thrust could carry the strength of the entire body behind it.

At the heart of the manual were two principles: Four-Sided Stability and Double Dragons Overturning the Sea.

Four-Sided Stability focused on maintaining balance throughout the body and weapon. The head, shoulders, feet, and spear had to remain properly aligned, while the so-called eight stabilities referred to the shoulders, hips, arms, and legs. Without that foundation, force would disperse before ever reaching the spearhead.

Then came Double Dragons Overturning the Sea.

The first dragon was the spear itself, trembling and churning in the wielder's hands. The second was the cultivator's body. Power began from the feet, passed through the legs and hips, then traveled along the spine before finally entering the arms and spear.

The manual naturally described it somewhat more impressively.

Man like a dragon. Spear like a dragon.

With each thrust, muscles and bones surged together like a dragon overturning the sea, propelling the spear with the swiftness of wind and the speed of thunder.

...Okay, that last part was clearly exaggerated.

But the principle behind it was solid.

"As I told you before, weapon mastery has its own progression," Zhang Feng said, spinning his spear once before resting it against his shoulder. "There are all sorts of names for it depending on the sect, inheritance, or weapon. Some even divide the stages differently. But once you look past the names, they usually come down to roughly the same thing."

He counted them off with his fingers. "Scattered Hand, Penetration, Unity, Transformation, and finally Ultimate Intent. Those are the names I'm used to, so I'll teach you using those."

Ning nodded.

Zhang Feng pointed his spear toward him. "Show me how far you've gotten."

Ning adjusted his grip.

Scattered Hand was the most basic stage. A practitioner could already strike, dodge, and parry, but those movements remained separate from one another. Once a real fight became chaotic, whatever form they had practiced tended to collapse along with it.

Ning inhaled and recalled the instructions from the manual.

Relax the tendons and bones... Engage the skin and hair...

He stabilized his stance, loosened his waist, and drove force upward from his feet. The spear trembled in his hands. It wasn't particularly impressive, but at least the movement remained under control.

"Better," Zhang Feng said after watching for a moment. "You've actually been practicing."

Ning had spent the past three months working on the basics whenever he had time. Progress hadn't been particularly fast, which had also taught him something about himself.

His talent with the spear was decent, but nowhere near his talent with the bow. Archery had come to him almost naturally, while even the basic spear stance had required months of repetition before it stopped feeling awkward.

Zhang Feng stepped closer and nudged Ning's elbow upward with the shaft of his spear. "You're still using your arms too much. The force starts from below. Root your feet, loosen your waist, and let everything travel upward together."

Ning tried again.

He imagined riding a powerful horse, his body rising and falling with each stride while transmitting that rhythm into the weapon. The image helped... somewhat.

For a brief moment, the spear finally trembled properly.

"Hoo..." Ning lowered it slightly. "Spear shaking is brutal. My arms feel like noodles."

Zhang Feng grinned and told him that was a good sign. Apparently, suffering meant his muscles were finally doing their jobs.

Ning wasn't particularly comforted by that.

After a short rest, he began again. Zhang Feng circled him like an overly enthusiastic instructor, occasionally tapping his shoulder, elbow, or leg with the shaft of his spear whenever something slipped out of place.

"Relax your shoulders. Sink your waist a little more... and root your feet properly. You're swaying like a drunk duck."

Ning nearly lost the rhythm. "Was the duck necessary?"

"It got the point across."

Unfortunately, it had.

Half an hour later, Ning's spear was trembling with noticeably greater force. His movements were still flawed, but the weapon no longer shook randomly in his hands.

Zhang Feng watched another few repetitions before giving a satisfied nod. "Much better. Your stance is still a little stiff, but that'll improve with practice."

Ning relaxed his arms and lowered the spear, expecting another correction. Instead, Zhang Feng stepped back and readied his own weapon.

"Let's spar for a while," he said. "It's easier to find your problems once someone starts hitting back."

Ning glanced at the spear in Zhang Feng's hands before raising his own. He had expected they would eventually spar anyway, though his aching arms would have preferred eventually to mean another day.

Once Ning settled into his stance, Zhang Feng gave him a moment to prepare before stepping forward.

His spear shot toward Ning's chest.

Ning reacted immediately, raising his own weapon to intercept. The shafts collided with a sharp clang, and the impact sent a vibration through both of his arms.

He had barely recovered when Zhang Feng attacked again.

"You're still using your arms too much," Zhang Feng said as Ning parried the second thrust. He continued pressing forward, forcing Ning to adjust his footing while correcting him whenever an opening appeared. "Move your feet with the spear. Turn your waist... good, like that."

Ning finally managed to redirect one thrust instead of meeting it head-on, only for Zhang Feng to sweep toward his ribs.

He retreated two steps and knocked the spear aside, but Zhang Feng followed immediately and forced him back again.

"Don't retreat so much," Zhang Feng said. "You're making space for me every time I attack. Sometimes you have to hold your ground and redirect the spear instead."

Ning understood what he meant. He had been so focused on avoiding the spearhead that retreating had become his first response whenever Zhang Feng pressed him.

During the next exchange, he forced himself to remain closer.

Their spears collided repeatedly as Zhang Feng drove him around the arena. He never struck hard enough to injure Ning, but he gave him little time to think. Every exchange exposed something else: his stance rose whenever he was pressured, his grip tightened too much, and his feet had an unfortunate habit of forgetting everything he'd practiced whenever a spearhead came toward his face.

The manual had explained all of those things.

Unfortunately, reading about them and having a spear aimed at his ribs were very different learning experiences.

Zhang Feng suddenly swept low.

Ning instinctively stepped backward while lowering his spear to intercept, but Zhang Feng changed direction before his footing had even settled. The follow-up thrust forced Ning back another step.

"You did it again," Zhang Feng said as he pulled his spear back. "Try guiding the next one past you. Don't meet it directly."

Ning adjusted his stance.

When the next thrust came, he stepped forward instead of retreating. Twisting his waist, he guided the spearhead past his body rather than trying to stop it outright. The force traveled from his planted feet through his hips and spine before entering his arms.

His spear moved with it.

For a brief moment, the two shafts locked together.

Zhang Feng's eyebrows rose. "That's it. Remember that feeling."

Ning held his ground as Zhang Feng explained that his body had finally remained connected under pressure, which was exactly what Four-Sided Stability was supposed to accomplish.

Then Zhang Feng twisted his wrists.

Their spears separated, and his weapon immediately came back toward Ning's chest.

Ning barely blocked it.

Another attack followed from a different angle, then another. There was no overwhelming strength behind any of them. Zhang Feng's spear felt almost light in comparison to Ning's, yet every movement seemed to place the weapon exactly where it needed to be for the next.

Ning gradually realized what was different.

A blocked thrust naturally became a sweep. The sweep brought Zhang Feng's spear into position for his next attack, and even when he defended, the movement flowed smoothly into whatever followed.

This was Penetration.

Zhang Feng seemed to notice when Ning began paying attention to the flow between his movements. Rather than stopping to explain, he simply motioned for Ning to attack.

Ning thrust toward his chest.

Zhang Feng guided the spear aside with a small turn of his wrist and told him he had hesitated. Ning recovered and tried again, then again. For the next several exchanges, Zhang Feng continued correcting him whenever his movements broke apart, while Ning concentrated on carrying the momentum of one attack into the next.

It was considerably harder than it looked.

Against someone with Zhang Feng's experience, blindly defending would only leave Ning reacting forever. So after another exchange, Ning drew upon Shadow Steps and closed the distance himself.

His spear shot forward in the smoothest thrust he had managed all afternoon.

Zhang Feng blocked it with practiced ease, but his eyebrows lifted slightly.

This time Ning didn't stop after the failed thrust. He allowed the momentum to carry into a sideways sweep, forcing Zhang Feng to take half a step backward for the first time since the spar began.

Ning's eyes lit up.

He had actually forced him back.

"Good!" Zhang Feng laughed. "Keep going!"

Ning happily obliged.

For the next several exchanges, his movements became noticeably smoother. He was still firmly within Scattered Hand, but every so often one attack naturally connected to another without him having to consciously stop and decide what came next.

Then Zhang Feng's stance changed.

It wasn't particularly dramatic, but Ning immediately sensed something different. The openings Zhang Feng had deliberately been leaving him disappeared, and his movements became much harder to predict.

A thrust came straight toward Ning. It wasn't aimed exactly where he stood, but slightly to the left, along the direction his body was already beginning to move.

Ning's spatial perception caught the trajectory.

There!

He shifted his spear to intercept, only for Zhang Feng's wrists to turn at the last instant. The thrust slipped away from Ning's defense and flowed into a sweeping strike.

Pa!

The wooden shaft tapped his shoulder.

There was almost no force behind the blow, but Ning knew perfectly well what would have happened if Zhang Feng had been attacking seriously.

He stepped back, staring at him. "...I thought I had that."

"You almost did." Zhang Feng rested the butt of his spear against the ground. "Your senses are too good, so you've gotten used to trusting them. That's a strength, but they can still be shaken. If someone changes their intent midway through an attack, what you sensed a moment ago might already be useless."

Ning fell silent.

That was something he hadn't considered much before. His spatial perception had always been extremely reliable, especially with arrows and other straightforward attacks. Against someone capable of changing their movement halfway through, however, sensing the initial trajectory wasn't necessarily enough.

Zhang Feng rolled the spear between his palms. "What I showed you just now is related to Transformation. Penetration comes first, when your movements begin connecting properly. Unity goes further, bringing your body, weapon, footwork, and intent together. Transformation comes after that."

He paused for a moment before adding, "I've only glimpsed it myself. Sometimes during a fight, I catch that feeling where the technique can change however I need it to, but I can't do it consistently yet."

Ning was somewhat surprised. Zhang Feng had made the exchange look effortless.

"So you're not at Transformation yet?"

"Not properly." Zhang Feng shook his head. "I'm still trying to get there."

He looked at Ning for a moment before smiling.

"That's also why having you as a sparring partner is good for me. Your senses are troublesome enough that I actually have to think about how I'm attacking you. Teaching you helps me sort through some things as well."

Ning hadn't expected that.

He had assumed Zhang Feng was simply doing him a favor by teaching him the spear. Knowing that Zhang Feng was also gaining something from their sessions made the arrangement considerably easier to accept.

Zhang Feng raised his spear again. “Anyway, don’t worry about Transformation yet. You’ve got enough to work on already.”

He settled back into his stance.

“For now, just focus on getting out of Scattered Hand.”

Ning looked at the spear in his hands and raised it again.

The next few rounds were less combat and more being beaten into understanding. According to Zhang Feng, this was YOUTH. Only by pouring one hundred and twenty percent of yourself into training could you experience the burning passion of martial arts.

Ning suspected Zhang Feng had been struck in the head one too many times.

Still, the beating worked.

By the time they finally stopped, nearly two hours had passed. Ning was drenched in sweat, his arms were trembling, and several muscles he hadn't known existed had begun filing formal complaints.

Zhang Feng, naturally, looked perfectly fine.

"The spear's strength comes from the whole body," he said as Ning tried to remember how arms were supposed to function. "Keep practicing the stance and spear shaking. Once using your whole body becomes natural, everything after that will be easier."

Ning looked down at his trembling hands. "I think my body has understood enough for today."

Zhang Feng chuckled. "From the looks of it, you're finally using the right muscles. If you weren't, you'd probably have snapped something by now."

Ning slowly looked up.

"Brother Feng, please don't say terrifying things after the training is already over."

Laughing, Zhang Feng clapped him on the shoulder, painfully enough that Ning nearly reconsidered their friendship. He reminded Ning to practice spear shaking every day and get the foundation right before worrying about anything more advanced.

Ning rubbed his aching shoulder and nodded.

"I understand."

Zhang Feng was in high spirits, eating as though the food might escape if he slowed down.

“Teaching you was absolutely worth it,” he declared between mouthfuls. “Not only do I finally have someone willing to practice martial arts with me, since some people”—he shot a pointed look across the table—“refuse to sweat, but I also get fed like a king.”

“Hmph. I don’t want to practice with you,” Lang Rulang snorted without hesitation.

“You people simply don’t understand the passion of youth!” Zhang Feng lightly slapped the table, nearly knocking over a bowl. “The burning sweat! The pounding heart! The heat of battle!”

He leaned over and slung an arm around Ning’s shoulder. “Right, Ning?”

Across the table, Lang Rulang silently stared at him.

Ning felt a chill that had nothing to do with cultivation.

“I believe… each approach has its own merits,” he said smoothly.

Zhang Feng clicked his tongue in disappointment, while Lang Rulang looked away, satisfied.

Since Ning was already treating Zhang Feng to a meal, he had simply invited the others as well—Lang Rulang, Si Sihua, and Wei Zhusang included. Unfortunately, Wei Zhusang had accepted a mission earlier and couldn’t make it.

Zhang Feng glanced at the empty seat and sighed. “Old Wei is going to regret missing this.”

Ning’s cooking was already excellent, but what really drew them in was that half the things he made were foods they had never seen before.

Case in point: the small bowl of ice cream sitting at the center of the table.

Even Lang Rulang, usually proud to the point of arrogance, hadn’t hesitated to show up once she heard what was being served.

Ning glanced toward Si Sihua.

He had been slightly worried that the senior sister might feel awkward eating with people she barely knew.

He was clearly overthinking it.

“Damn!” Si Sihua exclaimed, shoveling another spoonful into her mouth. “This Frozen Breath Rice ice cream is perfect for summer!”

She ate without the slightest hint of restraint, completely absorbed in demolishing the food. She didn’t spare a single glance for anyone else at the table.

Truly, this senior sister was immune to social pressure.

Then, without warning, she spoke.

“Oh, right. Speaking of missions,” Si Sihua said casually, still holding her spoon. “I found one for you, junior brother. The red-paper mission you were looking for.”

“Finally.” Ning smiled.
