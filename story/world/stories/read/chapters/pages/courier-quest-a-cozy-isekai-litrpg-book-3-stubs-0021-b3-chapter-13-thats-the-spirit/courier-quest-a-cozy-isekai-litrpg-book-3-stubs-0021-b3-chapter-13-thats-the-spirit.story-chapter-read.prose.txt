Once the voice, which likely came from the Dread Pirate Tanazi, finished speaking, Adabelle scanned their environment. Whatever she saw, it appeared as though she didn’t like it.

“Clay, lead us forward to a place with enough space to fight,” she ordered.

The earth elemental jolted at her tone, hesitating for only a second before running forward. They still flattened the ground in front of them, though it wasn’t as smooth as it had been when they walked. It was packed down enough to be navigable and that was it.

Trevor fell in behind Adabelle after she began chasing Clay, careful not to slip on the ground, and kept his eyes peeled. The mist had cleared up enough that they could see a good distance away, but it still spun and twisted in the corner of their vision. It was enough to make him jumpy.

The tunnel opened into a large room, and the light illuminated it well. There were two islands up ahead with gaps big enough they would have to jump across, and there were a few more paths to take on the other side. Clay lunged onto the first island, tucking and rolling into a ball like an armadillo. They began flattening out the area just as the others jumped.

Adabelle landed gracefully and turned around to make sure Trevor was going to make it. Her eyes narrowed as she reached out and grabbed his hand before forcefully pulling him towards her. She didn’t move when the armored courier slammed against her body, instead standing firm as she swung her sword.

Surprised, the courier tried to move out of the way to see what was happening, but he couldn’t escape her grip. Trevor went completely still since that seemed to be what Adabelle wanted, only moving his head to see what she was swinging her blade at.

Floating behind him was a set of armor that looked like boiled leather and cloth. It was obviously well worn, waterlogged, and had dripping seaweed hanging off of it. Wearing the armor was an indistinct, humanoid mass. No face, no features, just a big, human-shaped light. The only thing that was identifiable was the ghostly saber in one of its hands.

Adabelle was defending him with rapid parries while holding him still. For a moment, he was far too impressed to be scared of the fact that a ghost had just snuck up on them.

“Think you can protect him from me, girly?” the ghost asked, clearly meaning it as an insult.

“Just wanted to beat you myself,” Adabelle replied easily. “I can't let him do all the work.”

A tail wrapped around his wrist and tugged Trevor towards her side, and he allowed it. Adabelle spun around him, giving him passage to safety, while thrusting her slender blade. The ghost was pushed back as she pierced its armor. So long as enough damage was dealt to the spirit’s gear, it would eventually disperse. That’s what they had been told.

Once Trevor was safe, her tail let go so that she could remain mobile. That was her fighting style, after all. Clay’s work on the ground made sure that there were no gaps or stones to trip over, which allowed Adabelle to focus on the enemy in front of her.

A loud crash rang out from just behind Trevor, causing him to jump and turn. Clay was standing there, no longer in their ball form, with both arms raised. A pair of ghosts had attacked while he was distracted, one with a boarding pike and the other with a sword, but his earth elemental was there to protect him.

“Oi, what's this?” the pike pirate asked.

“Miniature lighthouse golem?” the other guessed.

“That's a dumb pet.”

Trevor frowned. “Don't knock lighthouses. You might not be in this situation if there had been one here.”

The pirates paused in their attack to consider what he said.

“Don't like that.”

“Nope. Get him!”

Clay pushed them both back when they mentioned going after Trevor. The familiar grew in size to block their path, and a sense of accomplishment at doing their job came through their bond.

The ghosts went around each other to perform a pincer maneuver when Clay didn't make things easy for them. The one with the boarding pike had much longer reach, striking out before the other one, and the courier thought he saw the weapon pass through the stone husk.

It didn’t go far, though. The parts of Clay that were covered in salt pushed it away when they shifted. It seemed as though the mineral really was coming in handy, and Trevor realized that it also meant that his armor was useless.

Turning around, he watched as Adabelle sliced the leather core of her ghost into ribbons. In the moment, he had forgotten that her sword was magical and could strike such opponents. She probably wouldn’t have been able to block the ghost’s attacks if it weren’t for that. It became obvious that this was a dangerous place where most adventurers would have to put their dodging skills to the test.

The ghost in front of Adabelle dispersed as its armor took enough damage to fall apart. It was going to fall into the water, but she lowered herself and thrust her sword, catching it through the shoulder and flipping it backwards onto the island. Trevor caught it, grimacing at the slimy feel, and sent it into his inventory.

Adabelle flashed her husband a brilliant smile before focusing on the two ghosts that were trying, and failing, to assault Clay. Salt was falling from their shield arms as they were repeatedly struck only to land on enlarged feet and travel right back up the familiar’s body. The demonkin was a blur of motion as she surged around Trevor to go after the ghost with the boarding pike.

While the two versus one had been a stalemate, it quickly fell into the adventurer’s favor. Adabelle put the ghost on their back foot, as much of one as it had, while Clay was able to start swinging. It wasn’t as effective as the noblewoman’s sword, but they had something she didn’t: the ability to grab.

The earth elemental’s shield arms turned into large hands with unnaturally long fingers. It almost seemed like the ghost recoiled from the grasping appendages, something Trevor agreed with, before actually doing so and trying to fly away.

Clay stretched out, giving their arms more reach as their body became skinnier to compensate. They managed to grab the ghost, locking its sword arm against its armor as their hands clamped around their prey. The earth elemental’s body snapped back the way it had been before, still holding the spirit in salt-encrusted shackles, and turned to Trevor.

Pure joy and accomplishment radiated from the familiar, causing their master to give them a smile. At this point, he wasn’t sure what he should be feeling. A lantern-headed earth elemental offering him a ghost pirate was not on the list of things he expected today. Regardless, Clay presented him with their caught quarry.

It struggled, but couldn’t break free. Trevor came to the conclusion that the ghosts didn’t have a lot of physical strength. That made sense to him as they were incorporeal. He wasn’t even sure if they could actually cut something. Their attacks might be spiritual in nature. There was no way to tell because Adabelle hadn’t been hit yet, thankfully, and Clay was a surprisingly small target despite their bulky husk.

Thinking things through was helping him calm down. The initial spike of adrenaline was still there, but his heart was no longer pumping so hard that he could barely hear anything. Looking down at Clay as they offered their catch to him like a cat with a mouse had him force out a smile.

“Thank you, Clay,” he said as he reached out. The familiar performed a small shimmy out of joy.

Trevor shivered as he touched the armor. Not only was it just as slimy as the first one, but it was also cold. That might have been because the ghost was still using it. Not wanting to think about that any longer, he pulled what he could into his inventory.

The armor disappeared, leaving the ghost hovering there in Clay’s grasp. All three of them were silent and unmoving. He had thought that they would disappear when the armor was gone, but was that not the case? Trevor was just about to back up when the situation changed.

“Oh, how embarrassing,” the ghost wailed as it slowly collapsed in on itself. The light comprising its body disappeared in a blink.

“How embarrassing?” Trevor repeated incredulously, looking down at Clay, who shrugged.

“Did… did you just destroy my crewman in one attack?” the voice of Dread Pirate Tanazi asked.

“That’s right,” Adabelle said before Trevor could. She approached the courier, another piece of armor hanging off of her sword, and stood proudly next to him. “Do you believe we were going to get scared and run just because we’re new? If so, you're naive. We're more than a match for you and your crew. My husband here will be doing the same to you soon enough.”

“I mean, that’s…” Trevor started to say.

“Ha! I saw him quaking in his boots earlier. He’s terrified!” Tanazi roared.

“All a cunning ruse to get you to let your guard down,” Adabelle bluffed with confidence. “Watch as he utterly destroys another set of your minion’s armor.”

Trevor looked down at the padded jacket that was hanging off of his wife’s sword. It had several cuts in it due to the fight, and it was still dripping saltwater onto the floor. She waggled it in front of him expectantly.

“Behold my power, I guess,” he said before reaching out and touching it with one finger. It disappeared into his inventory.

There was a moment of silence before Tanazi grunted. “Come and get me, if you dare. We’ll crush you and your weird, creepy golem!”

“You’ll fail,” Adabelle returned confidently, and the oppressive aura in the room receded. She turned to Trevor and smiled once it did. “Alright, he’s no longer watching. Are you okay? How are you feeling?”

The courier took a deep breath. Now that the will of the dread pirate wasn’t pushing down on the room, the atmosphere was brighter. The mist no longer swirled ominously, and it overall felt far more natural.

“I think I’m okay,” he remarked, searching his feelings. It took a few seconds, which Adabelle gave him, before he nodded. “Yeah. I think so. Kind of feel like I’ve been thrown under the bus, though?”

She tilted her head in confusion as she took his hand. “What do you mean?”

“You just told them that I could destroy them in one hit,” he said, chuckling weakly. “Pretty sure I’m becoming target numero uno over here.”

Clay puffed up, shaking their lantern head as they slammed their shield arms together.

“Thanks for protecting me back there, little buddy. You did a great job,” Trevor praised as he reached down and patted his familiar. “You, too, lady. I wasn’t sure about why you grabbed me, but that ghost was way too close.”

“We will always protect you, darling, and don’t you worry about them targeting you,” Adabelle said.

“Because you two have my back?”

“Yes, but also because they’re probably not going to be brave enough to face you after the next wave,” she answered. “We’ll take the brunt of any assault they have cooked up, and all you have to do is touch them and do your thing.”

“You seem awfully sure of yourself,” Trevor said, squeezing her hand.

“I’ve been through a lot of dungeons before, son of Ander, and you are not the first mage I’ve had to keep an eye on,” Adabelle replied with a smile. “This was the initial test to see where we were, and we aced it. No one got hurt, we managed to secure two harmed armor sets and one pristine one, the boss has been sufficiently intimidated by what he thinks is a touch of complete destruction. Which will make my specific quest easier.”

The courier blinked before he remembered the task the Quest Master had given her. “Oh, right, you need to duel Tanazi and he’ll be more likely to agree if he thinks I can one shot him.”

“Exactly right,” she said. “And the thing is? You absolutely can one shot everything in this dungeon. All you have to do is dodge and tap. That’s what you did against the earth elementals, right? It’s the same thing here, just a little wetter.”

“Yeah,” he agreed, thinking of the thin layer of slime on the armor he had grabbed so far. “Definitely grosser.”

“And that’s it. The same as then, but grosser,” Adabelle stated before kissing him. Her forehead pressed against his instead of drawing away, and she looked into his eyes. “For the record, brave Trevor? I like him a lot.”

“I’m not going to fall for that, you know,” he said with a laugh. “My feelings aren’t driven by bravado and machismo, so appealing to my inner toxic masculinity won’t get you anywhere.”

“Bravery isn’t the absence of fear, but how you act when you are afraid, Trevor,” the noblewoman replied, kissing him again. “You don’t put up a facade. That’s what I like. So, keep being brave, okay?”

Trevor looked down at Clay, who nodded and raised their arms.

“Okay, okay,” he agreed with a smile. “You’ve got me feeling brave, so let’s go be brave. We have some pirates to one shot.”

“That’s the spirit,” Adabelle giggled.

“No, them’s the spirits, this is just me doing my best.”

The demonkin’s tail playfully smacked him on the side for his joke before she pulled away. With nothing more to say, they continued onward.
