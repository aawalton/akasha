Welcome to the multiverse.

Xavier blinked after having received the Personal System notification. It made it sound as though he hadn’t been in the multiverse all along.

Everyone’s in the multiverse, unless they’re in the void—which, technically, I’m in right now…

He shook his head, getting rid of those tangential thoughts, and focused on the System.

Uh, hello? he thought, communicating with it exactly as he might with Roln—Derrida, the ancient Wanderer who’d given him this thing, had told him it was a mental construct, after all.

Hello, Xavier Collins.

Xavier swallowed. So, what exactly can you do?

He’d been expecting… more after the installation had finally completed. Some sort of introduction. A tutorial. A status screen. Something. But none of that had happened.

I can interpret the world around you. I can translate the meaning of things in ways you can understand. I can funnel knowledge to you, knowledge given to me by the Great Derrida, when necessary.

Xavier tapped his foot on the stone, bit his lip. This was what he’d been waiting for? When she’d called it a rudimentary System, she’d been right. Though he knew he was making a rather snap judgement.

He looked around. He should test this thing before coming to any conclusions.

Xavier pulled out one of the corpses of the lion-octopus-nightmare beasts from his inventory. It appeared on the ground in front of him. He was about to ask the System what this was, when a notification sprang up. He supposed the Personal System, unlike the actual System back in his home universe, knew exactly what he was thinking.

Xavier had, of course, interacted with his own System with his thoughts before. But that had only been when he’d willed something. Willed a yes or a no in response to a notification. It hadn’t simply read his mind. He’d long come to the conclusion that the System couldn’t read his mind, because if it could… well, it would know that Xavier wanted to destroy it one day. And, considering what it had done when one of the others had mentioned something like that back in the Roving Seed Base, writing The System Is Watching in the sky above them, it would have reacted to that particular goal of Xavier’s by now.

This Personal System was clearly rather different.

He read the notification, listening to the Personal System’s voice as it read it out at the same time.

Name: Gorgotlin

Type: Void Dungeon Beast

Multiversal Standard – Class C

Gorgotlin are a type of amalgamated, created beast that appear all over the multiverse, coming in all shapes, sizes, and varying levels of abilities. As created beasts, these beasts show up most prominently in natural and artificial dungeons, but they are also known to be created by other beings for protection or as pets. Gorgotlin have a limited sentience.

This particular Gorgotlin has taken traits from six different naturally occurring beasts in the multiverse. The Lionade, the Secinsa, and the Printan being the most prominent.

Its main attack is Draining Spike.

Do you wish to drill deeper into any of the stated information?

Xavier tilted his head to the side. He’d never heard of a Gorgotlin, and he’d never heard of any of the beasts it had come from either. Curious, he used his Identify spell on the beast. He’d levelled the spell as high as it could go for his grade.

The spell failed. Because of course it failed. This was a spell that had been created by his System, back in his own universe, and it relied on using the System’s knowledge of something. His home universe’s System wasn’t here. It couldn’t give him the information he needed.

Besides, he hadn’t even been able to sense this thing while it had been alive…

There were other ways Xavier could gain information about this Gorgotlin without having to resort to any System, but mostly that would be through dissection—it certainly wouldn’t have told him anything about what the spell it had used had been called, what the beast was called, and anything else that had been mentioned.

Xavier looked at the third line of the notification. Which was, to him, the most interesting part of the description.

Multiversal Standard – Class C

Class C? What’s Class C? And what is this Multiversal Standard, anyway?

He’d never heard of it before—Roln certainly hadn’t mentioned anything like that to him. That must have meant Roln never had a reason to tell him, or that Derrida had kept the information from the mental construct for some unknown reason.

Explain what the Multiversal Standard is.

The Multiversal Standard is a way that Wanderers classify power levels. The description of power varies widely in the multiverse. While paths of advancement have many similarities, no two paths are exactly alike. A Denizen at S Grade and a Cultivator at Tier 7 are not necessarily at the same level of power. Even two S Grades may vary wildly in their level of power. Class C is a wide designation. There are smaller designations for each class.

Class F10 is the weakest designation.

Class S1 is the highest designation.

The only being in the multiverse above Class S1 is the Interdimensional Dragon.

The Interdimensional Dragon defies Multiversal Standard classification. It breaks the scale.

This Gorgotlin is more specifically Multiversal Standard – Class C10, the weakest designation for Class C.

Class C10 has several different levels of strength in itself. Do you wish for me to drill deeper?

Yes.

This Gorgotlin is Multiversal Standard – Class C10-1 of 1,000.

Xavier tapped his boot on the stone again. Multiversal Standard. It sounded as though this particular system of classification completely ignored the grade someone was at. Well, that would certainly come in handy. Especially when there were beings out there who not only had wildly different types of advancement, but several different types.

Xavier himself being a good example of that.

Xavier was Tier 3, but he was also B Grade, then there was all the refined Celestial Essence he’d been using to alter his body. On top of that, there was the Immortal Soul lens he had access to, giving him the ability to scatter energy on the subatomic level—which allowed him to sever the line of energy from one of those Draining Spikes the Gorgotlin has used on him.

Then, of course, there was his ability to manifest spell patterns inside himself, meaning he wasn’t limited by his class or the spells he’d learnt, nor the types of paths of advancement he held.

His abilities had been breaking every scale he’d dealt with, but this scale was different.

Tell me more.

The Multiversal Standard can be used not only on individual enemies but grouped enemies. For instance, if one were to face a single Class C10-1 of 1,000, the designation would remain Class C10-1 of 1,000. If one were to face ten Class C10-1 of 1,000s, then the grouped designation would be Class C10-2 or 3, depending upon their abilities. If one were to face an army of thousands of Class C10-1 of 1,000s, the army might be classified as Class C2 or Class C1.

Xavier blinked. Well, that would come in handy.

Could you classify the strength of an entire planet’s population?

Yes. I can classify the strength of a planet, galaxy, universe, or inter-universal empire.

Which meant the Personal System would be able to classify how strong the Xaviers were all together, and then he could classify the remaining top ten beings in his universe… assuming he was able to get close enough.

My powers of classification are limited to your powers of perception, just as the Identify spell was limited. There are certain enemies that I will not be able to classify.

Xavier blinked. Once again, the Personal System had responded directly to one of his thoughts, rather than an actual prompt. He supposed it was more like Roln in that sense, though it so far appeared to lack much of a personality.

He frowned at its response, looking down at the beast before him.

You were able to classify this beast, even though I couldn’t sense it when it was alive.

You could sense more of it than you realised. There are still parts of your senses you have yet to unlock. Parts of yourself you have yet to unlock. Your potential is higher than you realise.

Besides, it is easier to perceive something when it is no longer blocking your perception. In death, there are more secrets that can be revealed.

Huh. Well, that was good to know.

He strained his ears. This conversation had taken roughly a second or two. He was still wary of the fact that more of the lion-octopus-nightmare beasts—or, rather, Gorgotlin—might be in the area, ready to come for him.

You have defeated the beasts in this part of the Void Dungeon. You should not encounter any more until you move forward down the Dark Tunnel.

Well, that was a relief.

What do you know about the Void Dungeon?

A great deal.

Xavier sighed. He supposed his question hadn’t been very specific.

Does this Void Dungeon have floors?

This Void Dungeon has ten floors. Each floor of the Void Dungeon has a different Challenge a Delver must complete before descending to the next floor.

Only ten floors? Well, that didn’t seem like a lot. Not compared to the Tower of Champions, certainly.

Is this a private instance?

No. There are no private instances in the Void Dungeon. You may encounter any other Delver from the multiverse at any time.

What is the likelihood of that?

Hard to predict.

Xavier sighed. He found himself doing that a lot, speaking to the Personal System.

He wondered whether he wanted to encounter any other Delvers and came to the conclusion that he very much did, and he very much didn’t. Derrida had told him that she wouldn’t be able to protect him from everything while he was out here in the multiverse. He’d taken that warning seriously.

Anyone he was likely to come across would be a Wanderer. And if they might not be a Wanderer, they were at least a Universe Hopper with enough confidence to come to a place like this. That would mean the chances of them being more powerful than him were high to really high, especially without access to the power from his empire, or the other Xaviers…

It felt a bit strange being alone again in this way, but it also felt oddly liberating. He was still sure that unity was the right way to go to gain the power needed to defeat the Interdimensional Dragon, but venturing out completely on his own like this again…

Well, it was nice having something that was his, something that he hadn’t shared with the other Xaviers.

At the same time, he couldn’t wait to share this with the others. He couldn’t wait to go out into the void and create more unfoldings of himself. Hell, if he needed to, couldn’t he create more unfoldings of himself right now? And every one of those unfoldings would possess the new Personal System? He was in the void, after all.

What exactly would be stopping him from doing that?

Other than Derrida. Something tells me that wasn’t what she had in mind when she sent me here.

He also had no idea if he would be able to summon the Spirit of Time from way out here. There could be ways for the Void Dungeon to block it from entering this place. And while he theoretically could come up with a spell pattern that would do something very similar to what the Spirit of Time could do, perhaps even earning him the ability to create an unfolding himself, he didn’t think he wanted to mess with time in such a way. Not without the time and space to research.

Something could go very wrong if he messed it up…

Xavier looked down the Dark Tunnel, then he looked down at the Gorgotlin corpse. Now that he knew no more beasts were about to come running at him, he relaxed slightly. He knelt down and prodded the beast with a finger, running his Immortal Soul lens through it.

The Personal System had been right. Now that the beast was dead, every one of its energy channels had been revealed to him. Xavier didn’t need to follow those energy channels to know where they led to.

The Gorgotlin had a core, and even though the beast was dead, that core was still very much full of energy.

The energy he’d gained when he’d killed the monster apparently hadn’t been everything it possessed.
