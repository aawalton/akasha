“Are you fucking st—incapable of doing this very simple glaze correctly?”

Gorboon was a very unhappy chef.  I could see in his eyes that his loss of absolute power within his own domain was driving him slowly crazy.

“Soften the onions, add the brandy, a fucking pinch of garglewort, not a ham-sized fistful of the stuff, then stir to drive off the alcohol.  If the onion sticks, you screwed up and neglected to heat the oil properly.”

“Heat is at two, like you told me.  I’ve been stirring this shit so long my arm is likely to detach at the elbow.  Exactly how am I not following your weirdly explicit instructions?  It’s a goddamn sauce to get drizzled over a Snufalow joint!  It’s not—”  I struggled to find a metaphor that wouldn’t baffle the native to Helstat.  “It’s not complicated,”  I finished lamely.

“If it wasn’t complicated, why can’t you do it?” he asked acidly.

“Well, maybe it’s because my teacher is a monomaniacal asshole who gets his rocks off by being a dick to people?”

The entire kitchen fell silent.  If any of the sous chefs, kitchen porters or serving staff had said something like that, it would have led to an outburst.  But I wasn’t staff.  I was the big boss, and I could see the frustration and rage eating up his tiny, wrinkled soul.

“You need to soften the onions first—” he began to patiently explain the basic recipe to me once again.

“I know that!  You’ve told me four times.”

“Yet you still can’t manage it,” Jenny chimed in sweetly.

“Shouldn’t you have a moustache net as well as one for your hair?” I shot back.

“Hmmph.  I can cook that sauce, no problem.”

“Don’t start on her,” Gorboon growled, earning a smidgeon of respect from me.  If he would stand up for his hirsute lover, he was probably deep down a decent bloke, despite all the evidence to the contrary.

The kitchen quickly buzzed back to life.  Steam spurted as lids were lifted and pots shuffled around on hobs, rich smells filled the air, mingling with the hot, humid atmosphere.  My tongue flicked in and out regularly as my stomach rumbled.  Having to eat my prey whole, and either alive or very recently dead, in order to gain any biomass from them had not completely robbed me of my human palette.

The place smelled divine.  Meats were roasting, chops were…chopping. Veggies were being pre-cooked, and sauces like my own were being batch-produced.  There was a frantic but channelled energy to the space.  People moved with a purpose in a way that I rarely saw other than on a battlefield, when the motivation was literally “do or die”.  In this case, the same motivation was being applied to someone who was more used to being the motivator than the motivatee, and it was amusing me more than was probably healthy.

“Jenny has been on Team Bob for longer than you have, dude.  She knows I like her.”

“Aw,” she said.

“And I’m a lot less likely to eat her if this doesn’t work out.”

“Aw.”  Jenny sounded a lot less happy despite making functionally the same noise.  Tone can communicate a lot.

“I cannot work under these conditions!  A mor-an idi-a dragon had no place in a civilised kitchen.  What do you even eat?”  Gorboon snapped, his voice bordering on shrill.

“Recently, I’ve mostly been eating unspeakable horrors from the depths of the underworld and people.”  It wasn’t entirely true; I hadn’t actually eaten any of the people I’d been forced by circumstance to kill.  IMPS had teleported their remains into my stomach and added a bit extra just to mess with me.  The end result was pretty much the same, though.

“How do you feel about vegetables?” Gorboon asked thoughtfully.

“I dunno, man.  I mean, eating a baby bunny sounds pretty horrific, but eating a potato not so much.  A spud is basically a baby potato plant, in potentia.  Either way, it kind of feels like I’m robbing something of a future and eating its parents young?”

“A potato doesn’t have feelings?” offered someone with a tall white hat.  The same kind of hat that Gorboon had refused to let me wear.

“Prove it, asshole.  And I’ve got an entire dungeon floor of cute fluffy bunnies that would happily murder you.”

“But they don’t have nerves,” Jenny said.  “There’s no whatsis, feedback mechanism for pain.  They literally can’t feel, Bob.”  She had leaned up against the side of the counter next to where I was ostensibly working.

“So you got to spend a life as a potato before reincarnating here?”

Again, everything went quiet in the kitchen.  Someone dropped a spoon, and the clang echoed out into the silence.

“No, I wasn’t treated to a life as a vegetable before I came to Helstsat,” Jenny said coldly.  “It’s not polite to bring that kind of thing up.  YOU!  Stir that creme!  And if you stop kneading that dough again, I’ll get the orlics to replace your hands with rolling pins!”

The panicked kitchen staff resumed the hubbub of their normal routine.

“How would that even work?  You need to be able to hold both ends and push to use a rolling pin?”

“I would find a way, Bob.  Why do you even care if a potato has feelings, which it obviously doesn’t?”

“When you eat your food raw and whole, fingernails, teeth and all, it gives you a different perspective.”  I shoved away the thought of what had passed through my cloaca and avoided wondering why it hadn’t suffered as the result of passing little things like breastplates and the daggers my poor meals had still been clutching in their hands.

“A unique diet?  This could be the key,” Gorboon said.

“It’s hardly unique.  Any predator in the wild would eat the same way.  Lions don’t fricassee antelopes.”

“What’s a lion?”

“For fu—it doesn’t matter.  A big predator.”

“And an antelope?” he asked.

“Like a gimblebonk,” Jenny said.

“Oh.  Ok.  We can work with this, I think.”

“I’m not going to win a cooking competition by offering them uncooked mammals.”

“You might be surprised.  I made my name on gantlmok smash, which is pounded gantlmok liver served with a nargleberry vinaigrette.”

“You made that up,” I snapped, lifting my steaming pan off the hob to poke at the onions that were thoroughly welded to the bottom with a fireproof finger-claw.

“I did not.”  He sounded more offended by that than on any of the half a dozen times I’d threatened to eat him in front of his staff.  “It put my restaurant, The Filling Station, on the map.”

“Filling station?”

“You have a problem with that, dragon?” he sneered.

“Nope.  Real classy.  Next time I’m in the city, I’ll book a table for Esme and me.”

“You can’t go back to the city without getting stabbed, arrested and killed, possibly in that order,” Jenny pointed out, rubbing at a thick eyebrow with one finger.

“It’s unlikely they’d bother to arrest me after they killed me?  And I’d like to see them bloody try.”  It might be worth burning an Immortality Injection just to see what would happen.  Greed threw a fit in my mind, and I hastily shelved the thought of wasting a billion golds worth of product just to fuck around and find out.

The Longhorn in the middle of Ankmapak had not gotten quieter since the presumably planet-wide announcement of my claim to the Empire went out.  If anything, business was booming.  Lots of people were looking at the odds and voting for Bob, either moving out of the city entirely or panic-spending in the Bonanza before its demise in the event I lost.

Business, as they say, was booming.

“The point I was trying to make is that non-traditional cuisine always has a space among culinary innovators.  People crave something new, something exotic.  Perhaps with a hint of danger.”  He looked me up and down, sniffed at the onion-charcoal in the bottom of my pan.  “Cooking isn’t your strong suit, dragon.  But that doesn’t mean we can’t make a chef of you yet.  The first bite is with the eye.”

“Speaking from experience, the first bite is with the fangs.”

“No!  Only a savage could think such a thought!”  I fixed him with a level three glare.  “I didn’t question your intelligence!”

“You’re playing with fire, Gorby.  So you think I could lean into draconic dining habits to win over a bunch of hoity-toity elven gourmands?”

“Chef!  I’m finished!”  Katprick had materialised next to me, holding out a skillet to Gorboon, upon which something that was clearly aquatic in origin, but possessed far too many eyes for me to want to eat, lay marinated in a bright orange coloured sauce.

A teaspoon appeared as if by magic in the chef's right hand, dipped, and raised a sample of the sauce to his mouth.  He made a series of facial expressions that I would have associated with extreme bowel discomfort, then nodded.

“Evergreen… this is moderately acceptable.  You are learning.”

“Yes chef.  Thank you chef.”  He spun away and rushed back to his station.

“How did you break him of the alliteration thing?  That was really starting to piss me off,” I asked.

“I threatened to name a dish after him, and not in a nice way.  He buckled like a slice of soggy vernfruit.  Leverage is what matters, dragon.  Not the flavour or the ingredients.  Presentation and having the other guy by the balls.”

“I love you,” Jenny blurted, then her face blushed bright pink.

“It’s only natural.  I’m very fond of you as well,” Gorboon replied.

“I hear wedding bells,” I deadpanned.  “What kind of leverage can I get on the Fallimpiad judges?  I don’t know who any of them are.”

“I can put out some feelers.  Find out who’s up for judging this year.  There will be a price, though.”

“Beyond not being eaten and the frankly lavish salary you earn here?”  He’d used the P word, and immediately put my back up.

“Uh, well, yes?”

“Bob, don’t eat my fiancée!” Jenny yelled at me, grabbing a frying pan, dousing a kitchen porter in hot fat and making me flinch involuntarily.  While she had a baking class, I’d seen her brain more than a few people, Tom and Jerry style, with kitchen implements and had no wish to test the strength of my skull again.

“I wasn’t going to eat him.  Congratulations on your coming nuptials, I guess.  What’s the deal?  Another week for me in Hell’s kitchen getting yelled at?”

Gorboon stared at me defiantly.  “Take me with you.”

“This isn’t the last lifeboat, dude.  And it is, if I recall correctly, a death game.  Battle royale, ten chefs enter, one chef leaves kind of shit.”

“You get to take a porter with you.  Between us, we can create an entirely new branch of cuisine!  You can do the killing, I’ll quietly supervise the cooking… I always wanted to compete, but I’m not an elf.”

“Me neither.  Why the hell would you want to put your life in the hands of a bunch of food snobs?  Scratch that, why would you want to risk your life?”

“He’s a genius.  If anyone can help you—” Jenny poked at the burnt contents of my pan, “—it’s my Gorboon.”

“I’ve got some knife skills that should be handy if we run into the other competitors early on.”  He snatched a blade from the counter and set it spinning between his hands for a few seconds before casually flicking it across the kitchen to bisect the tall white hat of a sous chef who promptly soiled himself and ran screaming through the nearest portal.  This appeared to be so commonplace an event that no one else reacted.

I gave him my toothiest smile.  “Oh, I think I’ve got anything like that covered.  But I could use an assistant.”

His face turned purple at my words, and my smile became a smirk.
