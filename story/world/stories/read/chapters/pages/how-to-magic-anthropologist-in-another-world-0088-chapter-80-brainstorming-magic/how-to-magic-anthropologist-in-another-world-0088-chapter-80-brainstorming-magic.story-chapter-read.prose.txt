Vena and I returned to the Sunless Reach just as Kan was ladling rabbit stew into wooden bowls. The smell of herbs and slow-cooked meat greeted us the moment we appeared beside the campfire.

"Welcome back," Ja'a said, resting on her chaise lounge.

"Food's ready," added Kan.

Before joining the others, I walked over to one of the wash basins and scrubbed my hands with soap. Vena followed my example, carefully rubbing away the salt and grime from treating the cursed girl.

Only once we were both clean did we settle around the fire. I let out a tired sigh as I sat down.

"So?" Raik was the first to speak.

I accepted a bowl of stew and blew on it.

"So what?"

"What happened?"

Vena took over the recounting, talking about the girl, the curse, and the stolen necklace from the shrine to Rokh.

The entire camp listened quietly. Even Shingo paused his eating long enough to pay attention.

When Vena finished, Katar let out a low grumble.

"This is why I hate the gods."

"To be fair," said Calr, "stealing from them is kind of stupid."

"In her defense, she thought it was a grave marker, not a shrine."

"Still..." he insisted. "She should have looked into why a man was leaving silver in the same place three years in a row."

"Fair point."

Raik looked thoughtful for several moments before turning toward me.

"Do you do this often?"

"What?"

"Teleport randomly to help people," he clarified.

"No, not really." I lowered my eyes, ashamed. The answer came out quieter than I intended.

"I only started recently."

I shifted uncomfortably as everyone looked at me.

"It started after the battle with the mercenaries."

The memory of Edmund and his team moving through the carnage flashed through my mind for a second, but I pushed it away.

"I was in a bad headspace that night," I admitted. "So I made a wish."

"What wish?" asked Kan.

"I wished I would safely go somewhere I could actually be useful."

Vena smiled immediately. Somehow, it made me feel even worse.

"The first time, I ended up beside a river, where I saved the couple we saw at the inn a couple of nights ago."

"The second time, I saved a merchant who was choking on his food," I added.

"The third time?" asked Vena.

"A woman with a fever. I bought a potion to help her."

"And after that?"

I shrugged.

"I think I ran out of people to save because I was sent to Yon, where we discussed his training."

Calr blinked while Raik frowned.

"You never mentioned that," said our team leader.

"It never seemed important."

"How is helping people not important?" Vena asked, looking perplexed.

"I don't know."

"It is wonderful that you've been using your power like this." The cleric nodded excitedly.

"It really isn't." I grimaced.

"What?" She blinked.

"I've had this power for half a year." I stared into the fire. "I could be doing so much more. I just chose not to."

Vena opened her mouth, but I continued before she could speak.

"If I really wanted to help people, I could spend every day and night doing it."

Nobody interrupted me as I word-vomited.

"I could teleport from place to place." I gestured toward the darkness beyond the camp. "There are probably hundreds of people suffering right now."

My voice softened.

"People starving."

"People lost."

"People dying."

"People trapped."

I stared at my bowl.

"And most of the time, I simply choose not to help."

For a moment, nobody said anything. Then Raik shook his head.

"I don't think you should be doing it either."

"Why not?" I looked up at him.

"Yeah," Vena frowned. "Why not?"

"Don't you get it?" he asked with a frown. "Each time she appears in a random spot, she has to assess the situation and find a way to help. That is extremely dangerous. What if she makes a mistake and puts herself in harm's way?"

"Yon said a similar thing." I sighed. "He said that just because your power takes you somewhere safe doesn't mean you'll still be safe five minutes later."

"You should listen to him," nodded Raik. "He has a lot of experience helping people avoid getting themselves killed."

"He is probably right," said Calr. "We need to fully understand how your powers work before we decide whether it's safe or not."

"Yeah." Raik nodded. "We need to discuss how your power actually works."

"In detail," he added.

"Fine."

I trusted them with my origin, which was a far more consequential secret. I could trust them with my powers, especially since those two were best suited to help me. Raik had a lot of experience dealing with unusual powers as a divergent bloodline noble, and Calr had Analyse.

"Start from the beginning." Raik pointed his spoon at me.

So I did.

I explained my first wish, the one that brought me to this world. I described my earlier failed tests where I tried to wish for things other than teleportation. I explained the need for star mana, the use of my spear for star mana storage, and my conclusion about the correlation between certainty and mana cost.

I also explained how my power selected destinations based on the wish itself rather than requiring a place I had already visited, like normal teleporters. Then I went through all the major uses of the ability: finding treasure, finding a book, finding missing people, banking my wish by adding a timing aspect, and finally helping strangers.

The discussion continued for nearly half an hour as I detailed everything.

Once I was done, Calr's eyes fogged over, the telltale sign of his Analyse ability. The telepath descendant sat silently while processing everything.

"The destinations are changing," he finally said.

Everyone looked at him.

"What do you mean?" I asked.

"The wish scope is broadening."

I frowned.

"When helping people, the first several jumps focused on immediate danger," Calr continued.

"Monster attack and choking." He began counting on his fingers. "The medical emergency was less urgent, but still."

I slowly nodded.

"Then eventually it took you to Yon."

"Right."

"Nobody was in danger."

"Yeah."

"The wish exhausted the obvious targets," Calr nodded.

"I figured as much."

"But why didn't it take you to Yon first?" he asked.

"Different priorities," said Vena. "Like in triage. You go where you're needed most first."

"Priority according to whom?" he asked.

"Oh," I said dumbly.

"I suspect the same thing happened with the treasure wish."

Ja'a immediately caught on. She leaned forward.

"At first you found coin caches."

I nodded.

"Then ancient coins. And then jewelry."

The merchant's eyes narrowed.

"The wish kept broadening its interpretation of treasure."

"Your power doesn't find the perfect answer," Calr pointed at me. "It finds the closest answer."

"And when the obvious answers run out..." Raik said with a nod, "It expands the search."

Silence settled around the camp. I suddenly didn't like where this conversation was going.

Raik didn't either.

"If that's true," he said slowly, "then the safety restrictions might also become broader."

"You mean," Kan said carefully, "the power might eventually decide that being almost safe is good enough?"

"Maybe," he shrugged.

"Or loosen the restriction on what counts as treasure and leave her with useless junk," added Ja'a.

I stared into the flames, considering.

"That's only speculation," Vena folded her arms.

"It is," admitted Raik.

"Then I think the risk is worth it." The cleric nodded.

Vena met everyone's gaze without hesitation.

"If even one person is saved because of it," she said calmly, "if one child gets to grow up, or if one family doesn't lose a loved one."

The cleric smiled softly.

"Then I think it is worth taking the chance."

The camp fell silent for a few seconds.

"I don't think I can do it," I finally admitted.

Everyone turned toward me.

"I am sorry, but I can't live like that."

"What do you mean?" asked Kan.

I stared into the fire.

"I am not a hero," I said. "I can't spend every day of my life witnessing suffering."

The flames crackled between us.

"I can't live in a world where all I see are starving people, sick people, dying people, and children who need saving."

"I know that sounds selfish," I said, shaking my head, "but I just can't."

"It’s not selfish," Vena said immediately.

I looked up, and the cleric simply shrugged.

"If you can't do it, then don't."

I blinked.

"But you just said..."

"I said it was worth doing," Vena interrupted. "I didn't say it had to be done by you."

She folded her hands beneath her chest.

"There is a difference."

The cleric's expression remained gentle despite her firm posture.

"Alice, it is not your responsibility to carry all the burdens in the world."

"But I could be doing so much more."

"The Lady often says to leave the world better than how you found it. That's what matters."

I opened my mouth to argue, but Vena continued.

"You helped advance this world's medical knowledge."

She raised one finger.

"You saved Luna and the other cult victims."

A second finger.

"You saved us in Pikar."

A third.

"You are very generous with your money."

A fourth.

"You've risked your life for people who could never repay you."

A fifth.

Vena smiled.

"You are doing enough."

Some part of me wanted to believe her. Another part kept thinking of every person I hadn't helped.

From great power comes great responsibility and all that...

Calr broke the silence.

"If the criteria broaden when you repeat a wish, then you probably don't want to keep making the same vague wishes over and over."

Everyone looked toward him. The blood-haired boy tapped his fingers against his bowl while thinking.

"Not unless you can scope the danger beforehand."

"How?" I asked.

"You could maybe use the power for information gathering."

"What do you mean?" I frowned.

"Let's say you wanted to find Todor."

That immediately got everyone's attention.

"Or Donas."

Raik's expression darkened substantially when hearing that name.

"Or anyone, for that matter."

"Yeah, I considered that before," I nodded, "but saying, 'I wish I was safely next to Todor,' sounds like a death sentence."

"Exactly," Calr agreed.

"I can't think of wording that would be perfect, and like I already told you, if my level of certainty is challenged, then my spell costs more."

"Right, there is that as well," he nodded.

"I'm not an idiot. I would never try that."

"But could you locate them without actually going to them?"

I blinked.

"What do you mean?"

Calr leaned forward.

"Using the divination aspect of the power."

"Oh."

Slowly, understanding dawned on me.

"Oh!" I sat upright. "You mean I could try teleporting one meter toward something instead of teleporting all the way there?"

"Exactly," he grinned.

The entire camp suddenly looked interested.

I rubbed my chin. That was actually clever.

"I don't know if it works."

"Only one way to find out," said Ja'a.

"Yeah, let's not try untested magic on a Commander-level threat," Raik immediately warned.

"What if they're protected against divination?" he continued. "Or worse."

"Good point." I thought for a moment. "I'll use it to find Nakera."

"Why her?" asked Vena.

"Because I have absolutely no idea where she is right now."

"Smart," said Calr, nodding.

I stood and stretched before stepping away from the campfire.

Above us, another shooting star crossed the sky, and star mana flowed into my soul. I focused.

"I wish I would be teleported one meter in the direction of Nakera."

Nothing happened.

I frowned.

The wish didn't take at all. No mana movement, no teleportation, nothing.

I tried again.

"I wish I would be teleported one meter in the direction of Kuru."

Again, nothing.

My star mana remained completely still, aside from the regular conversion into normal mana that happened gradually.

"It didn't work."

"Interesting," Calr muttered.

I scratched my head.

"Either the concept is faulty..." A sudden unpleasant thought entered my mind, making my heart beat faster. "...or something happened to them."

"They're probably fine," Kan immediately said with a frown.

"Probably..."

But probably wasn't very reassuring at all.

As another shooting star crossed the heavens, I decided to check on them.

"I wish I would safely appear next to Nakera or Kuru."

The mana reacted instantly.

"Oh."

Mana bled through me at an alarming rate, consuming all of my star mana and half of my regular mana. Then the world vanished.

A heartbeat later, I found myself standing in the middle of a small camp.

Both Nakera and Kuru nearly jumped out of their skins when I appeared.

Nakera's stance immediately shifted into a fighting posture, her weight settling onto her back leg, ready to spring into a kick. Kuru had similarly reached for her weapon.

Their tension immediately lessened once they recognized me.

"Alice?" said Nakera.

Relief washed over me.

They were alive.

I stepped forward and wrapped both girls in a hug.

"You had me worried for a second."

"What?" Kuru asked, sounding dumbfounded.

"Worried about what?" Nakera tilted her head.

I released them.

"I was testing my teleportation," I explained, "trying to use it as a divination tool."

"You can do that?" Nakera asked with a frown.

"I guess not, since it didn't work," I grumbled.

"So you thought we died because you failed to find us?" Nakera smiled.

"Maybe."

Both girls immediately burst out laughing, the sound echoing across the camp.

"We're fine," Nakera assured me.

"Where are you anyway?" I asked.

"We're near the northern border of the Colossus Dead Zone."

"We're finally heading back home," Kuru added.

That was good news.

"Need anything delivered to you?" I asked. "Or maybe sent to Hano?"

Both shook their heads.

"Nah."

"We'll manage."

Nakera hesitated.

"Unless you can teleport us home," she said.

I frowned.

"Not from this distance, I'm afraid, and not both of you."

"Figured as much," said Nakera.

Kuru just nodded.

"I'm sorry I can't be more helpful."

"Don't worry about it."

"And be careful on the way home," I added.

"We will."

"It was nice seeing you again." I finally smiled.

Nakera smiled.

"You too, Alice."

For a moment, I considered staying longer. Then I remembered I had disappeared on my team without any explanation.

So I waved goodbye.

I lifted my spear to access my reserve star mana and vanished once more.

I reappeared beside the campfire and nearly stumbled.

The back-to-back teleports had drained far more mana than I had expected. My legs felt heavy, and there was a dull ache behind my eyes that always appeared when I pushed my reserves too hard.

Without bothering to hide my exhaustion, I walked over to my chaise lounger and practically threw myself onto it, the comfortable chair swallowing me whole.

Above me, the endless night sky of the Sunless Reach stretched across the heavens. Countless stars glittered overhead. Every few minutes, another shooting star crossed the darkness, slowly replenishing the mana I had spent.

I let out a long breath.

"Good news," I announced. "They're both fine."

"Bad news," I continued, "that means our little test didn't work."

"You seem out of breath," Vena said, looking concerned. "Do you need healing?"

"No, I'm fine." I waved the suggestion away, feeling slightly better as I watched another shooting star cross the sky.

"They were just much farther away than I expected," I explained. "Near the northern edge of the Colossus Dead Zone."

"The northern edge?" Kan let out a whistle.

"That's where they said they were," I nodded.

"That's over a week of travel from Hano," she said.

"And at least nine days from here," Raik frowned.

"Really?" I blinked.

"Huh." I smiled. "I guess my range has improved quite a bit."

"Quite a bit is an understatement," Ja'a laughed. "Judging by your Soul growth rate, I wouldn't be surprised if your strength has doubled since your last round of tests."

"Probably," I said as another shooting star drifted overhead. This time, I used it to refill my spear reserves.

"So..." Calr said, bringing the conversation back to the original topic. "The test failed."

"Maybe," I said. "Let's try something else first."

Raik raised an eyebrow.

"Such as?"

I thought about it for a moment. If the power couldn't be used to locate people through directional teleportation, perhaps there was another way.

"I could try using it like a yes-or-no divination tool."

"What do you mean?" asked Kan.

I sat up slightly, no longer sprawling across the chair.

"Ask me a question I don't know the answer to," I said.

"Then?" Raik asked.

"I will wish to teleport one meter to the right if the answer is yes." I pointed right with my thumb.

"And one meter to the left if the answer is no." I pointed in the other direction.

"Huh," said Ja'a. "That's actually clever."

"It should be something Alice knows absolutely nothing about," Calr suggested.

"Then let's keep it simple," Raik nodded after thinking for a moment. "Is the Sky Island going to cross over Hano this year?"

Kan immediately turned toward him.

"Wait!" Her eyes widened. "Do you have news?"

"No." Raik shook his head.

"Then why ask that?"

"Because if it works, we'll find out."

I blinked.

"What’s the deal with the Sky Island?"

Everyone stared at me in surprise.

I pointed at myself.

"Otherworld, remember?"

"Oh. Right." Kan nodded.

"The less you know, the better," Calr said. "Try the divination first."

"Fair enough."

I stood and waited for another shooting star to pass overhead. As fresh mana entered my soul, I focused.

"I wish to teleport one meter to the right if the Sky Island is going to cross over Hano this year, and one meter to the left if it isn't."

Nothing happened.

"No luck." I groaned.

"Interesting," Calr muttered.

I did a few more tests for good measure. Normal yes-or-no questions, questions about whether it was going to rain in Hano, or whether Ja'a's old family dog was still doing fine.

Nothing.

Not a single jump.

"I don't get it." I sat back down. "I remember doing similar experiments while testing delayed wishes."

"The banking trick?" asked Raik. "Where you add a timer to your teleports."

"Yeah," I nodded. "When I did that, the teleport worked just fine. So why not this?"

Calr immediately shook his head.

"Because they're not actually the same thing."

"They seem pretty similar." I frowned.

"No," he insisted. "When you use a delayed wish, the timing condition exists to clarify the teleportation."

"Oh." I blinked.

I immediately understood what he was getting at.

"The objective is still teleportation," Calr continued. "The timing simply helps define when it happens."

"You think intent matters?" Raik had also caught on.

"Intent always matters." Calr nodded. "If she made a delayed teleportation wish while thinking about using it as an hourglass instead of a teleport, I bet it wouldn't work either."

"You think so?" I frowned in consideration.

"Try it."

Since I was in the Sunless Reach, a place divorced from sunlight, I thought of a wish that would make timing the primary focus. I waited for a shooting star, then concentrated.

"I wish I would teleport one meter forward at the dawn of Waterday."

Nothing happened.

Again.

I sighed.

Calr was correct.

"Okay, you were right. It didn't work."

"Told you," Calr smirked.

"That confirms it." I leaned back into my chair.

"The condition has to be a tool that clarifies the teleportation." I rubbed my forehead. "It can't be the actual purpose of the wish."

"When I tested delayed teleportation before," I continued, "my focus was on creating delayed teleportation, not a stopwatch that happened to use teleportation as its alarm."

"Exactly." Calr nodded.

"The wording is the same, though..." I sighed. "I can’t believe such a limitation exists in my powers."

"It’s better that you were able to identify it now," said Raik.

"Yeah, you are right," I agreed. "This was useful."

For a few moments, everyone sat quietly, thinking.

Then Raik spoke again.

"That means you probably can't use your power as a divination tool."

"Yeah." My shoulders slumped.

"Which means you can't divine the safety of a location before jumping to it."

"Also, yes," I grimaced.

"And that means," Raik continued, "you should probably stop making random jumps."

I opened my mouth, then closed it again, because he wasn't entirely wrong.

“I wouldn’t go that far,” said Calr. “More like avoid repeating the same wish over and over again, especially if the risk of danger is high to begin with.”

He rested his hand on his shin.

"Take the treasure wish, for example," Calr added. "Unless you remove the hundred-year restriction, what are the odds a new location would be added for you to find?"

“Her range could expand, reaching a location she couldn’t find before,” said Kan.

“Or a ninety-nine-year-old treasure could turn a hundred,” added Ja’a.

“Yeah, but both of those are not high probability,” Calr said.

Ja'a immediately nodded.

"So, more likely you will eventually start finding increasingly weird interpretations of treasure. Old pottery, rare insects, or someone's favorite spoon."

The camp laughed at that last suggestion.

"Hey now, maybe I want to find someone's favorite spoon."

“Yeah, I find it more likely that she would find something useless than that the safety condition would lower,” Katar spoke for the first time in a while.

“If intent is what matters, I doubt Alice would value money over safety.”

“Maybe when it comes to the treasure wish,” said Kan. “But she did stand between me and a spider matriarch.”

“She shouldn't risk it when it comes to helping people,” she added. “She could accidentally risk her life in that case.”

"That makes sense," Vena said. "But I also don't think she should stop the helping wishes completely."

"Of course you don't," Raik sighed.

"I think she should limit them." The cleric ignored him and kept explaining herself. "Maybe once a day."

"Why once a day?" Ja'a asked.

"Treasure takes years to accumulate," she gestured with her hand toward the darkness beyond the camp, "but people needing help happen every day."

Nobody immediately disagreed with her.

"If Alice chooses to do it," Vena continued, "then once a day seems reasonable."

"I'm not saying you should do it every day." She looked directly into my eyes when she said that, her expression softening. "I mean that as the ceiling, not the floor."

I nodded, understanding what she meant.

"As much as I hate admitting it," Calr finally said, "her logic is sound."

"I know." Vena immediately grinned in victory.

I laughed despite myself, then collapsed fully back into my chaise lounger, the exhaustion catching up with me. Between the hike, the jump to help the cursed girl, the experiments, the teleportation to Nakera and Kuru, and the discussion afterward, it had been a long day.

I looked lazily toward the distant tower rising on the mountaintop.

Even from here, it looked unsettling. The mismatched windows covering its surface gave the structure the appearance of something that had grown rather than been built. Looking at it for too long made my eyes itch. The thing would give people with trypophobia nightmares.

"We should get some sleep," I said, pointing at the tower.

Everyone followed my finger as the tower loomed against the stars.

"Tomorrow," I said with a yawn, "we have an elemental to kill."
