Headed back to his Heartfire to recover, he thought about the fight with the frogs. What could have done better? The classic counter to multiple small enemies was armor. Ferris remembered the demon chewing on his arm. If he had some kind of forearm guard, he would have been fine, instead of a mess of cuts and bruises. Unfortunately, he just didn’t have enough qi remaining to experiment.

He didn’t see any short-term change that would improve his spear fighting style. He’d practiced it for years, so he doubted there was much he could do differently. As he developed his cultivation, he expected to get stronger and faster, which would help a lot—but not today.

The crossbow was his lifeline against the chain demons, but useless against the frogs. If he fired it at them, he wouldn’t have time to swap to his spear before being attacked—at least without dropping the valuable weapon, potentially losing it. The frogs moved quickly enough he doubted he could hit reliably anyway.

Back at his Heartfire, Ferris once again checked that nothing was missing. He might become paranoid if this kept up, but the cloaked demon thieves appearing after losing the oath statue had him cautious. Perhaps it wasn’t paranoia, and instead just a good habit? It’s fine… as long as the fear doesn’t spawn any new heart demons, he thought with a grimace.

Checking on the slimespace, the green acid at the start of the river was now a slightly darker color. Jade had probably consumed at least fifty demons, and this time the water level was slightly higher. While it had gone up, it wasn’t as much as a plate of food from dinner… once again suggesting anything from the real world was enlarged by the Consume Pool.

Happily, the fruit on the tree was starting to gain some color. It wasn’t as vibrant as the other two had been, but it might be soon.

After being assured that no new problems had appeared, Ferris left his soulspace. He opened his eyes back in his room at Lily house, laying on the floor with Jade chained up next to him.

Stiffly, Ferris got up and stretched. That had been the longest single session he’d ever cultivated for, and the phantom pain of his mental body’s injuries ached. He’d started meditating before lunch, and it was now dark out.

Checking outside his door, Ferris found four plates of food in the box, meaning he’d missed both lunch and dinner. Seeing the food awakened his hunger, and after placing half in front of Jade he ravenously dug in. Mother Lily really gave generous portions, and soon he was full. He couldn’t eat it all, but Jade was happy to finish the leftovers.

Ferris checked Jade’s memory of the names of fish he had given that morning, but as expected the slime had forgotten them all. It had been at least eleven hours since he gave the names, and the previous day Jade hadn’t been able to recall after just nine. He really needed a day where he could stay near the clock. Ferris repeated his safety lessons, although it was hard to tell what Jade remembered.

Finished with the food, Ferris wanted to give his mental body some time to recover. It would be best if he took a nap, but he didn’t trust himself to wake up anytime soon.

He did some light exercises to stretch his body. It was strange, his real body had been inactive all afternoon, but his mind had been run ragged. Jade watched for a moment, and then started to imitate his movements… poorly. The slime would shift and roll their body around, barely recognizable as the motions Ferris made.

He waited about an hour before meditating again, one hand laying on Jade. The day had been exhilarating. Ferris had trained most of his life to fight, but today was the first day he had really pushed himself against real enemies—if you could consider the demons real. Years of drills, exercise, sparring, and training… and yet he still felt unprepared. His skill with the spear helped, but oddly his true successes came from improvising solutions to counter the enemy’s weaknesses…

When Ferris opened his eyes in the soulspace, he carefully checked his wounds. They weren’t fully healed, but they had improved a lot. They ached as he moved around, but he no longer felt like it would reopen the wound.

After retrieving Jade from the slimespace, Ferris checked over his equipment. He only had nine demon reinforced bolts remaining, and five regulars. The qi chunks he’d collected earlier had fully melted into the Qi Pool. There still wasn’t much there—maybe half as much as he could collect on a normal day.

Regretfully, Ferris filled as much as he could into the cooking pot, leaving only a tiny bit left. The pool looked more like a symbolic cupful than anything else. Ferris had no idea what would happen if he removed it all, but it wasn’t something he wanted to risk. After adding an equal amount of demon corpse, cooking, and cooling, Ferris formed five additional bolts. Hopefully they would be enough.

After adding the new bolts to his quiver, Ferris looked at the leftover demon corpse. It was a gruesome resource that he now had in abundance, and he wondered if there was some other way to use it.

What if I melt it without adding any qi? He wondered. Then I could make all the bolts I’d ever need.

Ferris tried cooking the corpse by itself, without any liquid qi in the pot. It melted down to a liquid, but when it cooled it hardened into a grey stone-like material. When he tried to remove it from his pot, it stuck fast inside. Frustratingly, not even heating it up again would melt it, making his pot unusable until he figured out a way to clean the stuff out. At least it’d happened after he used almost all his liquid qi.

Giving up on experimenting with the corpse, Ferris and Jade set out towards the fort. He’d made the trip enough times to believe he could find it even without the trail. It would be hard for Ferris to ever truly get lost in his soulspace, as he felt like he could always point at where his Heartfire was…

Ferris and Jade did run into a group of demons, although it wasn’t the blue frog or chain demons. This group had three of the red imps. These had been the first type of demons Ferris had ever run into, and the ones that had torn him apart when he first explored the soulspace. They were blood red, with feral snouts that seemed out of place for a creature that walked on two legs. The imps were not large, but they had vicious claws and teeth.

Although Ferris and Jade were outnumbered, this fight went much better than against the frogs. The imps were small and quick, but they didn’t have the explosive charge of the blue frog demons. Ferris was able to use his spear to keep them at a distance, even three against one. Jade stayed back, waiting for an opportunity, but the slime ended up not needing to do anything. Ferris was able to dart forward with quick jabs, and then pull back fast enough to wear the imps down.

The fight was a good example of what the spear’s longer reach should be able to do, keeping enemies at a distance and striking from beyond their range. It also made Ferris feel better, as he’d been on the back foot against the frogs a lot that day, unable to keep them at bay. Jade was also happy, as the slime received a snack—two out of ten. Delicious.

Arriving at the fort, they found the surrounding area still deserted. Several demons walked along the top of the walls, looking out upon the hill. A solitary blue frog demon perched atop one of the towers.

There were no patrolling groups outside the fort. At least Ferris and Jade’s previous efforts had paid off.

They watched for a while, then moved closer. As soon as they were spotted, the demons on the walls wailed and screeched at them, but this time stayed inside the protection of the fort.

This behavior felt odd to Ferris, as he was used to the demon’s hyper aggression. They had always shown an animalistic cunning, but this restraint felt more tactical than before.

The fort was small for a fortified structure, but up close it was intimidating. The walls were twice his height, and the towers higher than that. Chains hung everywhere, draping the walls and connecting the towers. He could climb up them pretty easily, although that would put him too close to the defenders.

A pair of chain demons guarded the walkway atop each of the four walls. If he tried to climb, the demons would be in range to grab him with their chains.

The front entrance was open, and had no gate or way to close. Ferris guessed he could just barely touch both sides at once, if he were closer. He couldn’t see very deep into the dark entryway.

Ferris was not looking forward to fighting the chain demons in tight spaces. They wouldn’t be able to fully swing the chains around, but they could still constrict and pull him in. The close walls would take away his primary advantage of mobility. Potentially, the environment would be ideal for Jade, but he still didn’t know if a chain could pull out the slime’s core before being melted.

Despite having the high ground advantage, the chain demons on the wall weren’t able to capitalize on it. They outclassed him at medium range, but beyond that they were useless. If Ferris had enough bolts, he could just sit back and pick them off the wall one by one.

The problem was… he didn’t have enough bolts. In fact, far from it. Even worse, it would be impossible for him to retrieve his ammo if any fell inside the fort.

Still, Ferris felt that shooting the chain demons off the wall was a better option. Lining up a shot on his first target, he found himself in the unfamiliar position of having unlimited time to aim. He carefully did everything he could to make the bolt count.

Of course, the shot flew over the demon’s head, missing completely. The demons guarding the wall, already unhappy with him, let out another scream of range. The pudgy things walked up to the edge, and awkwardly started crawling down the side of the wall.

Perfect, Ferris thought, I can just bait them down here.

Backing up, Ferris took a moment to reload and look around. All eight of the chain demons on the walls were now moving towards him. Smiling, Ferris waited until one of them reached the ground and lined up his aim. This was exactly what he wanted.

After a few minutes of retreating and firing, he finally killed all eight chain demons. When the last one died, he had two of the weaker qi bolts remaining, and none of the demon reinforced.

Moving to recover his bolts, Ferris tried to understand the demons’ behavior. His uncle had warned him of some things, but the man was frustratingly vague about details.

Heart demons were supposed to be reflections of Ferris’ feelings. Like everything else in the soulspace, they were a spiritual representative. They weren’t truly alive, or capable of human intelligence, but more a part of him.

The walls covered in chains didn’t make sense from a tactical standpoint. Why build a wall and then make it easy to climb? But that wasn’t the point. If Ferris was right, it was formed from his frustration and fears of being bound. The chains were a conceptual part of it.

This was the first time his heart demons had shown any restraint at all. While they’d come down off the wall after he’d attacked them, Ferris was more surprised they hadn’t charged immediately on sight…

He recovered all of his bolts except his first shot, leaving him with thirteen reinforced and five normal. Jade quickly disposed of the bodies, and they approached the base of the wall.

It was time to see what was inside.
