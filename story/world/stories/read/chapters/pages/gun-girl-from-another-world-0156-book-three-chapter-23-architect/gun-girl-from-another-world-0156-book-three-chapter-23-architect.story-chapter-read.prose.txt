Chapter 23

Architect

Of course, my friends took down the boss on their own. Even without me, they’re all still at the brink of Gold-rank, and this is still only a Bronze-rank dungeon, even with my help. Well, Kyuuga’s behind that curve, but even he could have soloed this dungeon in its original format.

Yorin was reading my papers and I was monitoring the dungeon energy levels when a portal like the one that normally sends us back to the entrance from the dungeon core’s chamber spiraled open before us. All three of them stepped out of it, and Ayre was complaining about me taking the infinite storage with me, and praising the fortuitousness of wearing backpacks for appearances. Probably because both packs were stuffed to overflowing in order to get everything.

Some of it, coming exclusively from the boss cache, is even halfway decent for stats, like the amulet that none of us want to wear because it’s so ugly. We cataloged it all for the guild, of course, then converted the valuables to tins and offloaded the rest onto the guild for bits. All told, it wasn’t a terrible haul for a Bronze dungeon, but it wasn’t outstanding either. Far less than a Silver one either way, of course, but that’s to be expected. Honestly, that’s probably right about the sweet spot it should be at.

I took the opportunity while we were there to pick Zeiya’s brain about brewing different alcohols, too. Apparently, she hadn’t done it herself, but she’d picked up enough general knowledge to tell me the gist of what I would need and how to do it. At the very least, being the one running the guild’s bar and diner, she knew how it needed to be stored, but the knowledge of an accelerated aging spell I was hoping for wasn’t something she could provide. I’d have to ask a professional shiner or dig through the list myself.

Dina, the receptionist, also let it slip that Guildmaster Kobi is very eager to schedule our Gold-rank exam, but we begged for prep time and got it by promising we would schedule the test before we left town again. She complained that we’re the only team that begs for training time before every exam. I pointed out that it’s because we’re the only team forced to take the exam, and every couple of months at that. If they keep at us like this, we’ll be Platinum before the leaves turn.

She was, of course, entirely unsympathetic and blamed it on us being overachievers. I said that, if she were feeling so left out, she could join us for some gold dungeons once we rank up, and she scoffed and told me not on my life.

Well, that's fine. We've already got the training planned anyway, and it gives me plenty of time to work on restocking and upgrades. First, though, I'm going to need a workshop. But then, that's what Daelon is here for.

Yorin wasn't kidding when she said she'd made preparations. Just as she'd predicted, the tall, dark-haired elf and his team roll into Dabun Village before the week is out, looking like they are temporarily moving in. They pay their respects to the mayor, find out where they can find me and Yorin, then show up in my front yard with three wagons, half a dozen burly men, and two women I'm not surprised to find out are wives of two of the workers.

Apparently, this is completely normal. If you aren't hiring locally, and Dabun doesn't have a local architect, they basically pack their entire shop onto wagons and go where the work is. Which makes sense. Technically, work crews do the same thing back on Earth, it's just that transit means they can go home after the shift ends.

Yorin makes certain we’re all outside waiting for Daelon to arrive, as he’s apparently quite highly regarded. Even Kyuuga is here, though probably just to scope out the new intruders coming into his territory. She must have received a letter by post ahead of his team’s arrival notifying her of when they expected to be showing up. At least, that’s the only way I can figure she could have known.

No, that’s not true, it’s not the only way I can figure it. It’s just the least concerning way I can figure it. Sure, it matches her knowing when we’d teleport in, and we certainly didn’t send any missive, but that still has saner possible explanations. I’d much rather assume there’s a benevolent spy charged with trailing us and reporting back to her than that she’s the benevolent spy without ever leaving Dabun and there’s nowhere in this whole world that I could go to escape her gaze. One just makes good logistical sense; the other changes the entire genre of my life in terrifying and depressing ways.

“Oh my, so many lovely faces lined up to greet me,” he says in a gentle cadence by way of hello. “I can guess which of you is Sacred Yorin, but shall I have to find Hero Remmi by deduction, as well?”

… Oh no, he’s charming …

Before I can step forward, he moves to Korrigan with a warm smile on his face. “Is it you, Little Miss? I had heard she was the youngest of the summoned Heroes.”

She giggles, but shakes her head quickly. “Not me!”

“No?” he asks, then moves to Ayre. “Perhaps one of my own kin, then?”

“Sorry,” the archer entertains the game while trying not to smirk.

“Hmm …” His gaze goes to Kyuuga. “Certainly not the rabbit, is it?”

Kyuuga doesn’t look as entertained by the game. In fact, he heckles at the suggestion he might be me.

… Nevermind, Daelon is a giant dork, all is fine.

I take a step forward and give a slight bow as Yorin has taught me. “Master Daelon, I am Hero Remmi Lee. It is a pleasure to make your acquaintance.” I pause for a moment. “Also, please don’t antagonize the rabbit. He will kick you, and he is the only known wild monster to have cleared a dungeon. It will hurt.”

That drops the amusement off of his face and replaces it with concern, though the smile stubbornly remains as if it’s unsure where else it’s supposed to go. “Ah, noted,” he intones, then clears his throat as he moves to face me. “Forgive me my games! Hero Remmi, I understand you have, not just a house, but an entire estate for us to labor upon, and all of it as strange and alluring as the moon!”

I nod once, then, in a flash of light as I pull from my storage, papers appear in my hand. “That’s right! In fact, I’ve already taken the liberty of drawing up some of my thoughts to go over with you! I hope you don’t mind!”

“Ah, of course,” he replies, recovering his good mirth. “Sacred Yorin warned me you liked to be involved!”

Warned? My smile remains bright on my face. You warned him about me, did you, Yorin? Not informed, you didn’t let him know. You warned him. Is that his words or yours, I wonder?

“Oh dear, you’re both making the same face,” Daelon observes of me and Yorin with concern. “I find myself torn between asking if I’ve caused offense and risking causing further harm by inquiring whether the two of you are related!”

“A wise man follows what he knows,” Yorin calmly replies, “rather than meandering after that which he does not.”

Dang. In other words, you’re on thin ice, mind your own business.

He apparently gets the message too, as, though he chuckles, it’s with a clear loss in gravitas this time. Daelon takes a moment to look over the small crowd of us. “Ah, yes, well, will all of you be consulting, or just the yellow lady?”

That makes me grin and, papers still in hand, I plant my fists on my hips. “What’s wrong, Master Daelon? Feeling outnumbered?”

He looks us all over one more time before clearing his throat with a quiet admission of, “... Quite.”

Yorin turns her head toward Ayre and Korrigan. “Thank you for coming to greet Master Daelon. You may both return to your training. The Gold-Rank exam will not await forever.”

“Gold-Rank?” Daelon waits until after they head off, tailed by Kyuuga, before repeating that. “So young? A Hero, I could understand, but all of them?”

“They have all been very busy for the sake of the land,” Yorin replies, then turns back to him. “I shall remain, but primarily as Hero Remmi’s financial consultant, as I know her affairs in that regard better than she. My concern beyond that encompasses only adherence to legal and spiritual requirements, of which I am certain you are more than familiar and require no correction.”

“Of course,” he nods, and finally turns once more to me. “Well, then, Hero Remmi, shall we step inside your house to go over these ideas of yours at a proper table? I suspect it might take some time.”

“Sure, right this way,” I agree as Yorin and I turn to lead him up the steps. The rest of his troupe are setting about unloading their things and arranging their own base camp.

He pauses just before stepping onto the deck to run his hand over the trim of the balcony. “... Hardwood? Hero Remmi, the design of this home is quite unique. Who did this work?”

“I did,” I reply without hesitation. “Built the whole thing myself, and the furniture inside. Don’t worry, though, the whole thing’s designed to be easily dismantled. I’ve been planning for a more total project since I came here.”

All he does is give a short, thoughtful hum as he looks back to the work before following us inside.

Thanks to Yorin’s totally legitimate and not at all Chekovian forewarning, we’ve already got places set with tea and snacks ready to go. The cakes are Elara’s recipe, though I had her show me how to make them. In exchange, I taught her how to make the sugar cookies stacked next to them!

Personally, if we had to have a hot drink with these things, I’d do it with warm milk or malt, or at least add some milk to the tea, but when I suggested that last idea, it was the closest I ever got to feeling like Yorin might actually attack me without it being a joke, so I don’t think I’ll bring it up again.

She moves to pour the tea, so I sit across from Daelon once I’ve gotten him seated. “I’ve got a lot of ideas, like I mentioned, and, to be honest, I don’t really know what of them you might already be familiar with or no, so you just speak up and tell me if you recognize something, and it can save us some time.”

I put the first sheet of paper down, illustrating a central unit with toggled element stone chambers connected to big, metal, boxy pipes. “For instance, my people use a thing called Central Air. I haven’t exactly seen a lot of noble homes, but it’s certainly not something in use around here. Instead of every room having to have a drafty fireplace, the house is sealed, and all air is circulated through a central hub, where it is either heated or cooled, and then is distributed to vents throughout the building. No fire unless you want one - and I do, in the tea room-slash-family room, I think - and nice, consistent temperatures all year round. Especially when we add in the radiant heat, which you guys do have …”
