They lingered at the table a while longer. With most of the night’s patrons gone, they were among the few still seated in the restaurant.

Nasha rested her chin lightly against her hand, watching her. Their dinner would be over soon, considering that they had both ordered dessert.

“So,” she said, almost casually, “what are your plans from here?”

Veronica blinked. “Plans?”

“Mm. You’ve come back from the future. You have Galland, some big-shot merchant. But what comes next?”

“I’ll leave Ronswick in a few days. I’ll continue traveling to secure more resources and find allies to bring to my side.”

Nasha gave a small nod, listening.

“I’ll keep teaching Gwen magic,” Veronica continued, “and work on raising my standing within the mage association with my notes on compression theory.”

“And after that?”

“I’ll head to Annesheim and become a professor. I’ll teach magic and spread my name wide. My goal is to prepare the world and have plenty more powerful mages to fight in the war.”

“Is that it?”

Veronica paused. “What do you mean?”

“No. I mean—you said you’d gather allies,” Nasha said. “Not friends.”

Veronica frowned slightly. “They’ll become both, naturally. Strong relationships are necessary for—”

“But that’s not how you described it,” Nasha cut in. “You spoke about them like… pieces. Like things you need to collect.”

“That’s… you’re reading too much into it. They would of course become my—”

“How come you didn’t mention the people from your past?”

She stilled.

“You talked about them earlier. Victor, Viya, Maeve, and the others. But just now, you didn’t say anything about finding them.”

Veronica pressed her lips together. “That doesn’t need to be said. I’ll meet them again eventually. There’s no need to seek them out now since—”

“No need?” Nasha repeated. “What about your family? The ones who loved you and tried to build ties with Vitian just to treat your illness?”

“My family? I…”

Nasha’s voice sharpened, her eyes fixed on Veronica. “You’ve been given a second chance. To save people. To save the world. That’s admirable. Heroic. And yet… you haven’t once mentioned trying to reconnect with your family or friends. They aren’t even part of your plan. You’re treating it like, ‘if it happens, it happens.’”

“There are… more important things right now,” Veronica said. “I can find them again after we win the war.”

“You… do realize they won’t be the same people, right?”

Veronica froze; the words she had been about to say caught in her throat.

“In your timeline, they would have known you for years by now. But here?” She tapped a finger lightly against the table. “You didn’t exist. There is no prodigy in Annesheim. Not one anyone remembers, right?”

“Time changes people,” she continued. “That’s what they say, isn’t it? It can heal, but it can also harm. And yet here you are, trying to carry the world on your back while ignoring your feelings.”

Veronica wanted to speak. To argue back. But no words came. Because Nasha… she wasn’t wrong.

In her thoughts, there was no real plan to meet her parents again, nor any intention to seek out Victor.

Viya and Maeve were still useful, but Victor? He was just a friend. Parek had been a capable scout, but there were plenty like him. And Martin would likely create Sage again regardless of her involvement. To see him, simply to talk? Perhaps about the future, maybe. But leisure or to catch up? She hadn’t thought about that. Some of them were useful—but the others weren’t.

Footsteps echoed across the restaurant floor as their waitress returned and took their plates.

On Veronica’s plate, she had finished her sweet gelatin. On Nasha’s—she had ordered and eaten a few sugared strawberries. They remained unfinished.

“It’s midnight, so Allsman’s will be closing soon. I hope the food was enjoyable for you two.”

Veronica gave a weak smile. “It was. Thank you.”

The waitress passed a small receipt over to Nasha, and she took care of the bill.

They both left the restaurant, with Nasha leading the way out. “I’ll escort you back home,” Nasha said.

They walked in silence past the cobblestone sidewalks. The dimly lit street crystals made the city feel… small. Like there wasn’t anybody else but the two of them.

As they walked along in silence through an alleyway, the night rays filtering dimly through the narrow walls, Veronica felt it too awkward to continue the atmosphere like it was.

“Nasha.”

She stopped and turned to face her.

“What… exactly were you trying to say back there?” Veronica asked slowly.

Nasha crossed her arms, sighing. “Before I answer that. Let me ask you something first.”

Veronica nodded.

“Why do you think I invited you for a dinner tonight?”

“Dinner? Well, to listen—”

“Please,” Nasha interjected. “Think about it. Why I paid the bill. Why I told you to dress nicely. And where we were seated.”

Veronica paused. With that many hints, even she could piece it together. Perhaps Gwen had been right after all.

“Was it… to court me?” she asked, her voice quieter than before.

Nasha sighed. “This is what I mean, Veronica.”

“What?”

“You’re too dense,” she said. “Not stupid. You’re obviously the most capable mage in the world. But you are just…” She searched for the word, then shook her head. “Empty, in a way. You know so much about magic. About war, demons, survival.”

She stepped closer. “But you know almost nothing about everything else.”

Veronica’s brows drew together. “That’s not—”

“You couldn’t even tell I was interested in you,” Nasha continued. “Not because I was subtle. But because you don’t look at people that way.”

She closed the distance, stopping inches away. The faint scent of strawberries lingered on her breath.

“I think what you’re doing is admirable. And you should keep doing it. No one wants to die, after all. But it also seems to me that you’re holding yourself back.”

“The demons will invade—”

“Twelve years from now,” Nasha said. “Are you planning to work every single day until then?”

“If I become complacent, innocent people die.”

“And if you burn yourself out?” Nasha leaned closer. “There are so many things you missed because of your curse. When you recounted your story, I could feel the weight of your words. The stress you’ve built. I even felt the anger and sadness in them. Now that you have a second chance... you’re spending it working. Again.”

Veronica swallowed.

Nasha stared directly into her eyes. “What is it that you want, Veronica?”

“What… I want?”

Nasha straightened. “Since you arrived in Ronswick, you haven’t taken a single day to rest, have you?”

Veronica opened her mouth, then closed it. It was true. Every day had been spent working. Even on the slower days, she had kept moving—speaking with people, preparing the next step. Paperwork. Notes. Research. There had been no pause. The only time she could recall was when she gambled on the street corner for about an hour. But even then, she only did it once, promising not to do it again.

Veronica gave a small shake of her head.

“Right now,” Nasha said quietly, “you’re trying so hard to become strong enough to save everyone… that you haven’t even thought about whether there’ll be anything left of you when you get there.”

The words didn’t feel like an attack. They felt… too close to something she didn’t want to face.

Nasha reached out, taking her hand. Her fingers slipped between Veronica’s, lifting it slightly between them.

“To answer your question—yes,” she said. “I was trying to court you. I’m interested in you. You seemed like my type. And I think you’re cute. So I just wanted an excuse to get to know you a little better. That's all. Because that's what normal people do.”

“W-what?” Veronica stammered, face slightly flushing.

She had been complimented before, even by noble men, but none of it had felt like this. Nasha’s words carried a kind of directness she wasn’t used to.

“But that’s something you never would have noticed, right?” Nasha continued, her gaze steady. “Because you’ve been isolated for so long. Because you’re not used to this. I’m guessing you didn’t even think women could be interested in each other. Am I right?”

She was.

“Socializing isn’t as important as saving the world,” Veronica said quickly. “Just—just because I didn’t realize that doesn’t mean—”

Nasha’s voice lowered. “Did you think this was just… a transaction?”

Veronica went still.

Nasha frowned slightly. “I invited you out for dinner. To spend time together. To get to know you. And… I did.” Her grip on Veronica’s hand tightened just a little. “I learned a lot about you. Things I like. Things I don’t.”

She paused.

“And from what I can tell…” her voice softened, “you thought this was just a way to repay me. Didn’t you?”

Veronica said nothing.

“That this was something you had to do,” Nasha continued, quieter now. “Because it ties back to Galland. To your plans. Just another step toward saving the future.”

Her thumb brushed lightly over the back of Veronica’s hand.

“Just another obligation.”

Veronica’s fingers twitched in her grasp.

“…I didn’t—” she began, but the words fell apart before they could form. She didn’t know how to answer. Because she had thought that. At least in part.

No. Maybe entirely.

Of course it had been a transaction. Why else would she spend time or even vix on something like this?

Even now, she searched for a way to justify it. Maybe she had time to spare. Maybe it was a reward for recruiting Galland. For finding an apprentice—it had to be something.

Nasha’s fingers slipped from hers as she turned away, taking a few steps forward. “Sorry,” she said. “I’m not trying to lecture you. I don’t know exactly how you feel. I’m not the one in your place.”

Moonlight streamed into the alley as Nasha tilted her head back, looking up at the open night sky.

Veronica’s gaze followed her without thinking.

The light framed her, casting a soft glow around her figure. Her short red hair caught it, faintly shimmering. For a moment, she looked like a silhouette painted into the center of the alley.

“I should apologize,” Nasha said. “When Gwen asked me to help you, I agreed to do it for three thousand vix. Not ten.”

“What? Only three thousand? Then why—”

“I just came up with an excuse to have a nice dinner with you. It was kind of hard to get your attention otherwise unless you thought it was a transaction. I wouldn’t have actually taken ten thousand vix.”

She lowered her gaze, looking toward the far end of the alley. “I ended up lecturing you after what should’ve been a nice date. That was selfish of me. So I paid the bill.”

Silence settled between them.

After a few moments, Nasha took a step forward. “Let’s go. We’re close to your place, right?”

She took another step—then stopped when she felt a hand catch her wrist. Turning back, she saw Veronica standing behind her, head lowered, face hidden in shadow. Her grip was tight.

“Things like… friends. Lovers. Leisure… I never really had those.”

Her voice remained low. “Festivals and holidays were limited to a small circle. Theater I saw in Annesheim was always sanctioned by the Kingdom. But when I watched your performance at the Crimson Court... it surprised me. It felt new. Something I didn’t dislike.”

She paused. “The times I enjoyed most were when I was sent across the Kingdom. Talking to people. Taking part in local customs. Even if it was only to gather information... it still felt different.”

Her voice lowered. “But those were missions. As for doing something just for myself...” she hesitated. “I’ve never been able to do anything I’ve wanted before. Is it really all right to be selfish? Even a little?”

“Selfish?” Nasha echoed.

“To want things. To experience them for myself. To enjoy them, even knowing what’s coming?”

“…why wouldn’t it be?” Nasha asked.

“Because I was the one sent back,” Veronica said. “I have a responsibility to do everything I can. Every day I waste is another day someone could have been saved.”

Nasha tilted her head slightly. “Then… was tonight a waste?”

Veronica’s head lifted.

“N-no. I…” She paused, searching for the right words. “…I did think of it as a transaction at first,” she admitted. She took a breath before continuing. “But I did enjoy it. I appreciated that you listened. That you heard my story. That you tried to understand… even the parts I’m not proud of.”

Nasha remained quiet for a few moments, her eyes scanning over Veronica. “You mentioned earlier,” she said, “that you used to sneak out of Valeborn. To see Victor. To visit gambling dens.”

She paused. “That sounds like you were trying to find your own happiness.”

Her voice softened. “You are capable of making your own choices, Veronica. But you’re afraid of what others will think if you do. This burden placed on you—because you happened to be the strongest—it’s holding you down,” Nasha said. “And you’re using it as an excuse.”

She turned fully to face her. “You’re afraid to take that step forward. So you keep convincing yourself not to. Because if you have just a single day off to enjoy the new life you were given, free from some stupid curse that made you suffer your entire life—that everyone who died, and those that loved you—that they would reject and hate you.”

Nasha stepped closer, lifting Veronica’s chin gently in the dim light. Their eyes met. They held each other’s gaze, searching for something unspoken.

“If you aren’t happy while living... can you truly call it living?”

She leaned in closer. Veronica’s pulse quickened.

“In this timeline, you don’t have the curse anymore. You’re just acting like it’s still there,” Nasha said softly. “No one will fault you for saving the world while also chasing your own happiness. You’re a real person just like anyone else. In your last life, you had assistants, but they still all relied on you, solely. That's not shared responsibility. They didn't share your burden."

Her voice lowered. “Tell me. What do you want?”

Veronica hesitated.

Then, slowly, she found her words.

“I… still have responsibilities. That won’t change,” she said. “I’ve already committed to saving the world. That’s how I survived for a year on my own while everything fell apart.”

She swallowed.

“But… it’s true that I want to experience life for myself. I want to spend some days without thinking about the future. To see things I haven’t seen. To hear things I’ve never heard. To… understand people… in ways I never could before.”

Nasha smiled, letting her continue.

“When I first came back and realized my curse was gone… I had thoughts. That I was no longer imprisoned. But as the days went on in Greystone… I realized that wasn’t true. That I was still stuck. That I resented being the one burdened with the responsibility on my shoulders. That… just for a second. I could walk away and enjoy my life.”

“Would you like me to help you?”

“Help?”

Nasha’s hand slid from beneath her chin, rising to rest against her cheek. “Seeing you like this... I can’t help myself from meddling. The expressions you showed recounting your story... it feels like something I should help with. Maybe it's selfish of me, but I don't want to see someone destroy themselves for not being able to dream. I've been there. But nothing as serious as your burden.”

She paused, letting the words sink in. “My story isn't important. If you want, I can stay by your side. Help you live your life the way you want. Keep you moving toward your goals—but without chaining yourself down and suffering.”

Silence filled the air, the quiet night ambiance stretching long and thick around them.

“Let me ask you, Veronica. Do you want it? To experience what others have had all their lives—things that were never possible for you. Their share of happiness. Do you want a part of that? Do you want to change?”

All her life…

Veronica had been alone. No one could understand her. No one had to do what she did. No one had to suffer as she had. No one knew the constant pain she suffered from having stone in her skin. No one knew how jealous she became when other students could leave and party. No one considered her an equal. It was all fear—or reverence.

And yet… here stood someone who listened to her story. Truly listened. Someone who didn’t understand—but wanted to.

No—maybe she already did.

She understood exactly what Veronica had wanted all along. And that same woman stood before her now, hand against her cheek, gazing right into her eyes. Offering her a chance to change.

“…yes. I want to—”

Veronica’s eyes widened as something soft and warm pressed against her lips.

The taste of strawberries filled her senses. Her heart pounded, as if it might leap from her chest. Almost instinctively, she remained still, letting herself experience it. The unfamiliar feeling of being pursued without gain. Of her own emotions, answering in kind.

The feeling of wanting someone.

No prestige or status. Just someone who wanted her—for her.

After several long seconds, Nasha was the first to pull back.

Veronica felt a faint sense of loss as the contact broke, as if she wanted the moment to last just a little longer. She could still feel the sensation of her lips.

“There’s nothing wrong with wanting things for yourself. Especially when they were never offered to you before,” Nasha said, smiling. “It’s up to you to grab it.”

Nasha turned, walking a few steps ahead before stopping. “We’re close to your inn,” she added. “So I’ll be heading back now. But if you want to… chase that happiness for yourself a bit more…”

Her voice lingered. “…then you can come with me. I know you’re not that dense to miss what I mean,” Nasha said, glancing back with a small smirk.

“Though you might not make it back to your inn tonight. Your choice.”

With that, she turned and walked out of the alley.

Veronica remained where she stood, frozen, her thoughts in disarray, cheeks flushed red. To be that direct… of course she understood exactly what was being offered to her.

Veronica swallowed.

Then she took a step. Then another. And another. All until she reached the end of the alley.

She turned to look in the direction of her inn. And then glanced to her right, watching Nasha walk the opposite way.

Nasha’s words echoed in her mind.

There’s nothing wrong with wanting things for yourself. Especially when they were never offered to you before. It’s up to you to grab it.
