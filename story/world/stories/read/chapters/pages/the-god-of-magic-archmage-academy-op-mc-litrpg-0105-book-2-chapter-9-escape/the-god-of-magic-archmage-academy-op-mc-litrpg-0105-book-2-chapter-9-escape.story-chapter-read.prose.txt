Dreadbeasts of various shapes and sizes moved in a blur of horns, antlers, tusks, and more. Some gathered the pre-packed supplies they’d prepared for the trip. Others began ensuring everyone was accounted for. The warriors cracked open crates filled with weapons and began distributing them among themselves.

Two hundred years ago Korsca — the dreadbeast homeland — had fallen; since then their people had been scattered, hunted down, and enslaved. But five years ago Garvhadda rose to power and set a plan in motion to free them all in a single night.

Tonight Sen would do all he could to loan his power to the cause and repay a promise he’d failed to uphold when he abandoned the world a thousand years ago.

Tonight was for Garlon.

Tutor’s innate analysis spell spun into action and spread like a spider’s web in every direction. Sen ignored the flow of his own mana under Tutor’s authority. The interface would alert him to whatever was necessary; he needed to dedicate himself to his own role.

The slaughter of slavers and the like.

Any moment now, the first step of their plan would begin.

The instant the signal had come, a single dreadbeast had run out of the room. The door to the underground warehouse burst open as he returned. He was not alone.

Two dozen human men spilled into the room behind him. The flurry of dreadbeast activity came to a halt. All eyes fell on one man. He was short and bald, and his eyes darted around the room. A short sword was secured at his waist on one side, a whip on the other. When he saw Drussan, who was currently in his dreadbeast form, the man hurried over.

“Drussan! That one said the master had fallen ill. Where is he?” the man demanded.

Drussan held large hands up to calm the man down. “He’s stabilized. This way!” He turned and led the humans deeper into the warehouse. Sen glanced over his shoulder. He saw a couple of dreadbeasts exchange a look before shutting the door to the warehouse and moving to stand in front of it.

The fight was over before it started. Most of the slavers fell without so much as a startled cry. The hands of dreadbeast warriors could easily crush heads and snap spines. By the time Sen caught up to the ambush, only the bald man remained. He was quiet as he stared at the crushed bodies of the other slavers. He shook his head. “Always thought it was strange you sort never fought back. So why now?” he asked, glaring at Drussan.

Drussan towered over the man. “You told me once, when the alcohol had overwhelmed your reason, that you thought a dreadbeast would be the death of you. That you would probably deserve it. You were right about that, Aaron,” Drussan said.

“Yeah? Well, bring it on then.” He drew his sword. “I’m not scared of a bunch of mons—”

Drussan’s hand cut through the air like an axe. He cleaved Aaron in half at the waist. As the man gasped in pain on the ground, Drussan stepped over him. “Let’s move,” he called out to the dreadbeast warriors. Most of them were staying behind to protect the others. The rest was coming with Drussan and Sen to secure the exit.

They left the warehouse. It sat at the base of the stairs that led to the top layer of the underground complex, where Sen had passed rooms filled with illegal actively and goods. In either direction, a tunnel stretched to their left and right. The right path led to storage areas deeper unground, but the left led to a building inside the city. Lord Thessus, as corrupt as he was, couldn’t smuggle goods in and out of the front gates of his home. No, he had a tunnel constructed for that. The very tunnel the dreadbeasts would use to escape. But first, they’d have to deal with the guards.

As they left the warehouse, Drussan barked instructions. “Bar the door leading to the main house. Clear the rooms in the underground. Restrain those who work for Thessus, slaughter those who serve Malachi. Hold that door until the path is secured. No one enters, no one leaves.”

Each of the warriors balled a hand into a fist and slammed it into their chest. They hurried up the stairs to the upper part of the complex.

Drussan turned to look at Sen. “Are you ready, honored one?” he asked.

Sen drew his katana. Malleore pulsed with anticipation. “Of course I am.”

Drussan looked back at the only three warriors remaining with them.

The other three Khans. Ebron was a tall and lean dreadbeast. He had the face of a horse, and a long mane trailed down his back. He had a long face with a snout to match and a scruffy beard. His horns were short and curved. He carried no weapon, but mana-light gleamed off long, sharp claws. The final dreadbeast Khan was heavyset and had the face of a cow. Hair poured down his face like a mop. His horns were long and jutted outwards. His weapon of choice was a large battle-axe. It meant he didn’t have to hold back.

His katana slammed into the neck of one of the knight-commander mages. A spectral version of himself erupted from his back simultaneously and struck another knight-commander in the same spot. The result of his Sword Twin spell. As soon as the specter struck, it returned to Sen and dissipated, waiting to mimic his next sword strike. Both of the mages fell unconscious, and Sen’s momentum carried him forward past the others. The guards focused on him, and he held his blade up.

Micah

There was a balance to it. He’d climbed up Orianne’s wall to visit her numerous times, but he’d never done so in the body of a dreadbeast. His mind still knew which hand and footholds to use, but not all of those familiar spaces were adequate for his new size. Still, he’d found an alternative that suited him when nothing else would. He simply pushed his fingers through the wall and gripped the holes he had created. Orianne and her family were rich; they could repair the damage later. Besides, these were extenuating circumstances.

His real anxieties surrounded what came next. If he couldn’t convince Orianne of his identity, the minutes that followed would certainly not play in his favor. For one, she was the daughter of one of the most powerful men in Vitree. If her father thought a dreadbeast had climbed up to her room with bad intentions, then Micah wouldn’t survive the night. It was hard enough getting past the patrols and climbing over the gate. His legs still hurt where he’d scratched them on the fence’s stakes.

“Orianne—” No. Too deep. “Orianne!” He tried again, trying to make his voice sound more familiar and less threatening. “Oh, for System’s sake!” He shook his head. He should’ve practiced more, but time was of the essence.

What if I say something only the two of us would know?

“Remember that time—”

“Who’s there?” A familiar voice called out.

Oh great. She was already sitting on her balcony. It was now or never. “It’s me, Micah!” he said.

“Micah?” He could hear the frown in her voice. “Why do you sound like that? Are you sick?” Her voice grew closer as she spoke.

“Wait! Don’t look at me!” he called out quickly.

Her steps halted mere feet from the balcony’s edge. “Why not? Is something wrong?” She sighed. “Did you get into a fight again?”

“No, no, it’s nothing like that. Just…I’m coming up. Close your eyes and turn around.”

“Uh…okay.” She paused for a moment. “I’m doing it. Did you—did you bring me something?” There was a quiet hint of excitement in her voice.

Micah swallowed and turned to look below. Maybe this was a bad idea. No. No, his father was still in danger. He needed help, and she was his fiancée. He climbed up the last few yards to her balcony and tumbled over the edge. His landing caused several of her belongings to fall off a balcony table.

“Oh my—” Orianne jumped forward, but kept her eyes closed. “What was that? How did you carry something so heavy up the wall?”

“Orianne, I need to tell you something. But you can’t freak out. Okay?” he said. He turned to look over his shoulder at the town behind him. “I…well, look at me.”

Orianne turned slowly and opened her eyes. “What is it? Oh my—”

He placed a hand over her mouth before she could scream. Only his hand was massive, so it was more like covering her entire face. That did not help matters. She only freaked out more.

“I’ll explain, but you have to calm down, Orianne! It’s me. I’m going to take my hand off now, please don’t scream. You know what your father would do if he thought you were in danger.”

Orianne slowly relaxed. He felt her nod in his hand and slowly pulled it away.

When he’d finished explaining, Orianne looked taken aback at it all. “So you…were abducted by your father?”

“By a dreadbeast,” he corrected.

“Right…a dreadbeast that looked like your father? Then some mage turned you into a dreadbeast as well?” She asked, trying to piece it all together.

He smiled. Her eyes widened, and he realized a smile might be less effective in his current state. “Your father has lots of mages that work under him, right? So he can help us turn back, and then we can take care of the traitors. That way none of this nonsense gets in the way of our wedding, Orianne.” He held out his hand to her.

Orianne nodded along. She slowly reached out and took his hand as well. Or she started to, but then she pulled back. He could see the gears of her mind slowly turning. “Wait, who all knows about this?” she asked.

“Just you. I came straight here. Don’t worry, it won’t affect our reputations. I made sure I was careful. We can go to your father and solve this without involving anyone else. He has the manpower to hunt Drussan and this mage down,” he said. Now that she was on his side, victory was guaranteed. They could find who else was involved in this conspiracy and bring it crumbling down. He’d show his father just how capable he was and impress Orianne in one fell swoop.

“I see. Good. That’s good,” she said. She turned and faced away from him for a moment. “Micah…did your father take care of the paperwork already? The documents father asked him to handle before our wedding?”

Micah frowned. “Uh, yeah. It’s in his room. Why?” It was a weird time to ask about that. But she just wanted to make sure everything was in order for their wedding once this was resolved. If anything, it was even more of a reason to love her.

“Good. Good. So, if you weren’t around anymore…my father and I would inherit all of your family’s assets.”

He blinked. “What? What are you talking about?”

She turned around and faced him. The look on her face was one he’d never seen from her. He was the one who had been turned into a dreadbeast, so why did she look like such a monster right now?

Orianne screamed at the top of her lungs. “Help! Help me! A dreadbeast is trying to hurt me!” she screamed.

“Orianne, stop—” His blood ran cold when he realized what she was doing. He took a step back toward the wall. “Why would you do that?”

Orianne shrugged. “Nothing personal, hubby, just business. Don’t worry though, I’ll take good care of your things.”

“Lady Orianne!” A man called out from inside the building. Armor clinked as the family guards hurried to her room.

Micah’s eyes widened, and he vaulted over the balcony’s edge and plummeted toward the ground.

“A dreadbeast! A dreadbeast was just here! He swallowed my fiancé whole and fled! You have to stop him! You have to kill him!”

Micah had to get out of the city as fast as possible. If he could just make it to the closest gate and escape the city, he could buy time and return for his father later.

Micah ran as fast as he could, barreling through the garden of the estate and throwing himself over its fence. If Lord Thessus’ forces caught up to him, that was it. They would kill him.

He just needed to make it to the east gate.
