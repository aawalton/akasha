“So, what do you want to do now?” Seth asked as we reached the top of the stairs. “We didn’t finish that movie the other night, so we could always grab an iPad and watch it in my room? Or we could go up to the roof?”

Both of those suggestions were tempting to me, but still, I found myself hesitating. As much as I did truly want to hang out with Seth, I was exhausted. The adrenaline that had coursed through my body during the training exercise had long since abandoned me, and I had been barely able to even make it up the stairs. I’d almost caved and asked Seth if we could take the lift.

“Ummm…” I started reluctantly.

“Or we could do something else if you want?” Seth continued. “I mean, we don’t need to finish the film if you weren’t feeling it?”

“No, it’s not that,” I told him. “It’s just… I think I might just go to bed. I’m really tired.”

Seth turned towards me, the smile falling from his lips.

“Oh… yeah?”

“Yeah,” I said before adding, “Sorry.”

Awkwardness had begun to settle over us. I could feel it jostling me, and I hated it. I didn’t want things to be awkward or weird between Seth and me. I just wanted them to be normal, but I wasn’t sure how to fix it.

“Don’t worry about it. I mean, you’re tired. That’s fair,” he told me with a smile.

I still felt bad, though. I was worried he would think I was lying because I didn’t want to be around him anymore. He’d flirted with me during dinner, and I’d liked it, but I wasn’t sure if he knew that. He might think I was just making excuses, that I was no longer comfortable with him.

“Yeah… but I’ll meet you out here in the morning?” I asked. “We can go down for breakfast together?”

“Sure. You start at eight thirty, right?”

A smile pulled at my lips. Seth knew my timetable almost as well as I did.

“Yeah. Your first class is at the same time, isn’t it?” I checked.

“It is,” he confirmed, hesitating before adding, “Well… sleep well.”

“Thanks. You too. See you in the morning.”

Seth smiled at me before turning away, and guilt churned in the pit of my stomach as I did the same. I still felt bad, and that felt silly. He was right. I was tired, and I needed to sleep, but that didn’t make me feel any better.

My eyelids grew even heavier as I made my way towards my room. It was getting harder and harder to open them again every time I blinked, and my feet were starting to drag on the carpet. My steps were slow. I must have looked like a zombie, but it was the best I could do.

I have no idea how long it took me to reach my dorm room, but by the time I did, I was yawning, and my eyes were barely open. My hand dropped heavily onto the handle, and it took far too much effort to actually push the door open. The urge to let myself fall into a heap on the floor right there was so strong that I almost gave in, but I managed to stumble into my room and collapse on the bed.

The soft duvet cushioned my fall, and I stayed there, lying face down on my bed, for far too long. The room started to blur around me. I fought to keep my eyes open and remain awake, but it was too hard. My bed was too comfy, and I started to drift off.

With a jolt, I ripped my eyes open. I’d fallen asleep, I realised. I was pretty sure that it hadn’t been long, that I’d only dozed a little, but I truly had no clue. Hours could have passed since I fell onto my bed. It could have been the middle of the night, and I still hadn’t brushed my teeth.

I need to do that, I told myself. I have to brush my teeth, and then I can change into pyjamas and sleep. I couldn’t go to bed without doing it. That would be gross and unhealthy. I knew it took more than one day for cavities to form, but I was still concerned. I didn’t want to risk it, just in case.

Dragging myself out of bed again took an immense amount of effort. My arms dangled heavily by my side as I shuffled towards the bathroom and used the toilet quickly before staring at myself in the mirror.

Another yawn racked me as I tried to remember what I was doing, but it took me far too long to actually reach out and grab my toothbrush again. Both my brain and body had begun moving at a snail’s pace, but I managed to do it.

Screwing the top back on the mouthwash, I stumbled out into the bedroom again. Next, I needed to get changed. As tempting as it was to just curl up in bed fully clothed again, I couldn’t. It felt unhygienic, and I had pyjamas. It wouldn’t take me long to put them on.

Clumsy fingers pulled my school dress over my head, dropping it on the floor next to my wardrobe. I didn’t have far to go to put my dirty clothes in their proper place, but that was still further than I could manage. I’d deal with them in the morning, I decided as I pulled a pair of pyjamas out of the drawer and put them on.

Reaching up, I pulled my hair tie out, freeing my hair from the high ponytail it had been in all day, and let out a sigh. My scalp hurt. It had been pulled far too tight, and the sudden release of tension was painful, but that would dissipate soon. It always did.

Dull excitement gripped my heart as I turned towards my bed again, unable to help the smile on my face as I took a step towards it. I could not wait to flop onto the bed, to worm my way under the covers and stay there until morning, but something made me freeze. Ice slipped down my spine, and my eyebrows drew together as I looked around my room.

Something was wrong.

I couldn’t understand what I was looking at. My brain was struggling to keep up, struggling to process it. All I knew was that something was very, very wrong.

One of the beds was missing.

Ever since I’d left the induction wing, there had been three beds in my dorm room. There were three sets of every piece of furniture. One for me, one for Abbie, and one for Katie. That was what I had always assumed, but one of the beds was gone.

It has been there in the morning, though. I hadn’t been in the world at the time, hadn’t truly seen it, but I was certain it was there. I would have noticed if someone had removed it, wouldn’t I?

There were dents on the carpet, though, so it couldn’t have been gone long. I stumbled towards one of the flattened squares of carpet where the bed had stood so recently, my knees threatening to give way. All I could do was stare at it for a few seconds before glancing towards the wall.

The wardrobe was still there. Three wardrobes still lined that side of the room, but one of the chests of drawers had been taken too. There were only two, and it looked wrong. The room seemed too empty. The space between the wardrobes suddenly felt cavernous.

Panic thrashed within my heart as I moved towards the wardrobe in the centre. I needed to see whether there was anything in there. Maybe it was all just a misunderstanding. Perhaps one of the beds had been broken, so they’d just taken it away to fix it before Katie and Abbie got out. That didn’t explain the drawers, but…

I was shaking, I realised as I reached for the wardrobe door handle. I could see my fingers trembling as they closed around the cold metal, but it wasn’t just my hands. My entire body seemed to be shaking, as if the panic was vibrating within me and fighting to escape.

“Oh.”

The word left my lips involuntarily as I caught sight of the empty rack within the wardrobe. It had been full before. I knew that. A row of Academy uniforms had hung from the bar, but there was nothing. They were all gone, and I didn’t understand.

Someone must have come by and emptied it earlier, I told myself, trying to make sense of it. And removed the bed. I knew that, but still, my brain refused to process it. I didn’t get why it had happened. Why would they do that?

They probably just had a fight, I decided. My mind had immediately leapt to the worst-case scenario, but I forced myself to not even acknowledge it. That couldn’t be true. It must have just been an argument or something. It probably wasn’t even that bad, but maybe Katie or Abbie decided they wanted some space. They could have just requested to have a dorm to themselves when they left the induction wing.

I doubted it would be a permanent thing, though. It was probably just a snap decision, something that happened in the heat of the moment, and I was sure that once they were both out of the induction wing, I could talk some sense into them. They would be able to make up, and then they’d both be happy with sharing a room again.

That wasn’t something I’d ever had to do before, though. I’d never been caught in the middle of two friends. Phoebe and Duncan were my only friends, really, and they’d never fought. I could do it, though. I was pretty sure about that, but I knew that I had to try when I saw them again.

If I saw them again.

I might not. It was possible that either Katie or Abbie had failed the induction period. That happened sometimes. Fairly often, actually. Something might have happened to make the teachers believe they were wrong, that the Academy was not a good fit for them, that they were not right for it.

And if that were the case, I would never see them again. We all knew what happened when a person failed the induction period. They were forced to return to their everyday life, the one they’d lived before being recruited, and I knew that would be hard for them both. They had been shown a glimpse of what they could become before it was ripped away from them, and I had no doubt that whoever it was that had failed would spend the rest of their life trying to find a way back, trying to prove that they were truly worthy.

That’s what I would have done, anyway.

But it might not be that simple. It might not be possible for them to earn the chance to become a field operative again, I realised as nausea started to build within me. They may not have the rest of their lives to earn another chance. They could already be dead.

The room spun around me, and I clung to the wardrobe door, trying desperately to remain on my feet as the realisation gripped me. Bile burned the inside of my throat. It was rising, threatening to escape, but I could not move. I was frozen to the spot, paralysed by fear.

What if they were dead? Something could have happened in the induction wing. They were learning how to handle weapons in there. Maybe the tutor had asked for a volunteer again and shot them. Perhaps she’d missed and caught something important, or there could have been a problem with the bulletproof vest. It might not have stopped the impact at all.

There were other things that could have killed them in there too, though. The tutor might have brought some poisonous plants into the wing. He’d been wanting to for a while, and maybe one of them got stung by one or something. The barbs could have hooked into their skin, releasing their toxins before anyone even realised what had happened.

My room began to spin even faster around me, and I squeezed my eyes shut to block it out, but that just made my vertigo worse. The other worlds in my mind huddled closer, taking advantage of my weakness and trying to convince me to flee into them, but I forced myself to ignore them.

One caught my attention, though. It felt familiar. It hadn’t quite come close enough for me to know for certain, but I recognised it. My head snapped up, my eyes still shut as I stared at the possibilities in my mind.

It felt just like the Academy.

Perhaps it was the same, I told myself. It could be another world, another reality, that was almost identical to the one I was already in. But perhaps in that one, we’d all survived or passed the induction period. We might all be already living together in our dorm room within the main part of the Academy, and I longed to know. I wanted to let myself fall into that world, but hesitation held me back.

I had done that before, though. I had found two worlds that were almost identical, and I’d spent time in them both. It was early on. Back when I first started visiting the other realities, when I thought I was just daydreaming rather than… whatever it was that I was doing, I’d found myself on a speedboat with Mitch. I didn’t spend much time there, but the next world I went to was similar. The only difference seemed to be when we met.

Perhaps in that second world, I’d made a decision at some point. I could have chosen not to go somewhere, and so I didn’t run into him years before, like I had in the first. The outcome was still the same, though. I still ended up working with Mitch. Regardless of when we’d met, he had still adopted me, but I was pretty sure he would have done that in any world. He was a good man.

Is, I corrected myself. He is a good man. I did not know whether or not he was dead, but I clung to the hope that he had not. I could not be responsible for his death.

But I could do that again. It was clear something bad had happened in the world I was in, so I could just go to the other world that felt similar, and hopefully, it would be different there. Then, I could just be happy.

I didn’t know what would be different, though. That was the problem. There was no way to check until I went there, and that concerned me, but Seth and I probably wouldn’t be as close as we were in the world I was already in. If the others had gotten out of the induction wing earlier, we wouldn’t have had so much time to hang out just the two of us. We’d still be friends, obviously. We were before, but we were starting to become more than that, and I wasn’t sure if that would happen if there were others with us.

If I had to trade the potential of a relationship with Seth for Katie and Abbie both being alive, it would be worth it. That would be a price I was willing to pay if it meant I could have all of my friends back.

But what if they weren’t my friends in that world? What if we weren’t the same people? I didn’t know how it worked, what the difference between the two worlds was. They would probably still be the same people, but something would be different, so maybe they wouldn’t have the same personality as they did in my current world. What if they didn’t like me anymore?

I would still be able to remember it, though. My memories of our friendship in the world I was in would remain. I still would have spent evenings sitting out on the roof with Seth, laughed until I cried in our room in the induction wing with Katie and Abbie, and spent hours chatting with Scott, but none of those things might have happened in that world. I would still technically be with my friends. They might all still be alive, but it wouldn’t be the same.

It was different when I’d done that with Mitch. I’d barely spent any time in that first world, and I didn’t truly know him. I’d only said a handful of words to him before I’d had to leave the world, so it didn’t matter as much when I went to the new world where we had never met, but that wasn’t the case with the Academy. I had spent too much time there, and I wasn’t sure that I could just turn my back on it and go to a new version of the Academy.

Would I be able to return if I realised it wasn’t right? If I liked my old reality better, would I be trapped in a new one and surrounded by friends whom I barely knew? What if I could never go back?

My breath was coming in short, shallow gasps. Tears stung my eyes, and I looked around my room desperately, searching for answers. I needed to know, needed to know what had happened. I had to find Rodgers, and he would tell me. It might have just been nothing. Perhaps I was right the first time, and Katie and Abbie had just had a fight.

It could all be fine, and I might have been panicking for nothing, but the only way to find out was to speak to Rodgers. Then, I could figure out what to do. Either I’d remain in that world and live with the knowledge that I will never see one of my best friends again, or I could try the other world. If one of them died, maybe that would be the easiest thing to do.

Forcing myself to take a deep breath, I glanced at my hand. I was still clinging to the wardrobe handle, but I loosened my grip. My fingers hurt. My knuckles screamed with pain as I let go, but I barely noticed it. My mind was too fixated on what I had to do.

Rodgers was probably in his office. It was late, and I assumed he had probably retired there by that time. I just needed to go downstairs and knock on his door. That wouldn’t be too bad. Unless…

Anxiety exploded within me. Did he live at the Academy? I knew he had once upon a time. He was trained there, so he must have done, but he was not a trainee anymore. He was a teacher, so he couldn’t have lived in the dorms with the others, but where else could he be? I’d never seen a dorm for tutors, but surely they couldn’t all just abandon us the moment it got to the evening. We might all be trained in self-defence, but we were still just kids. It didn’t feel safe.

I’d definitely seen teachers at the Academy in the evening, though. I knew I had, but I couldn’t recall any specifics. My mind had turned utterly blank, and hopelessness started to wash over me, but I pushed it aside.

He might be in his office still, I told myself firmly. I’d check there first, and if I didn’t find him, I’d go to the labs. That felt like a good call. Sara would probably be there. She seemed to always be there. No matter the time, whenever I passed the big glass windows, I spotted her inside, and she’d be able to get in contact with Rodgers. They were dating, after all. If I told her what was going on, she’d call him.

And if Sara wasn’t there, I’d go to the GSOD. There was always a tutor supervising the analysts there, no matter the time. I’d just ask them to call Rodgers, and if he’d left the Academy, he’d either come back or tell me what happened over the phone. Or maybe that tutor wouldn’t need to call Rodgers. They might already know what had happened, and they’d be able to tell me. Either way, I’d get my answer.

Having a plan made me feel a little better. I knew it would work. By the end of it, regardless of how long it took me to find a teacher, I’d know what had happened to Katie or Abbie. Panic was still raging in my chest, but at least I was able to cross my room to my door and pull it open.

“Oh! Hey…”
