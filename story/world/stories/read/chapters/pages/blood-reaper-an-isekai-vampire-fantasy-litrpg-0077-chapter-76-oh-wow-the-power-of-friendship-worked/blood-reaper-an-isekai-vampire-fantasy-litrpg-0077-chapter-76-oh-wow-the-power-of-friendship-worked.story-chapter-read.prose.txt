Ace waited with baited breath to see if Raven had survived their last stand against the darkness.

Raven’s shadow form destabilized as chunks of darkness broke away from his central mass, each one keening with a high-pitched wail before dissolving into the void. His human shape became visible inside the diminishing vortex—a thin man with hollow cheeks and eyes that held too much pain. His body appeared translucent, composed more of memory than matter.

He dropped to the ground, his legs folding beneath him with surprising grace while shadow essence continued to leak from his form, returning to the Shadow Realm in wisps and streams. Each departing tendril carried with it a fragment of his presence, leaving him more human with every passing second.

Every breath scraped Ace’s lungs like sandpaper. His muscles had gone beyond pain into some new territory—a fiery numbness where signals misfired and nerves screamed at him to sit the hell down already. His Soul Meter registered as a hollow void, so close to empty that he might well have gone closer to zero than ever before.

But he kept his eyes fixed on the sentinel, waiting to see what the man would do next.

His body wanted to collapse. To shut down. The rational part of his brain—the part that had kept him alive through three combat tours—screamed at him to rest. He’d pushed further than any vampire should. The shadow-blood linking had peeled away protective layers between his consciousness and the void. Something cold lingered in his marrow.

Didn’t matter.

He forced his legs to support his weight. One foot, then the other, then another as he walked ever closer to his target—and, if they were lucky, the end of this fight.

His team had sacrificed so much.

Ace couldn’t let them down now.

His vision blurred. Refocused. Blurred again. He blinked away sweat and blood. The kneeling man before him wavered like a mirage. The edges of reality still felt thin after channeling so much shadow power.

He took another step toward his prey.

Something dark and quiet whispered inside him. A voice not entirely his own. Not entirely foreign either. It offered power. Offered rest. Just a small opening to the Shadow Realm. Just a tiny crack in his defenses.

Ace clenched his fists until his knuckles cracked.

He wouldn’t be fooled by a siren’s whispers. Not today. Not ever.

“Well done,” Raven whispered, his voice suddenly clear—a young man’s voice, untainted by corruption or madness. Each word emerged crisp and loud in the devastated air. “I underestimated you.”

Exhausted, weak, and about ready to keel over, Ace finally reached his opponent. He lowered the Crimson Sword, its blade once again simple red-tinted metal. The weapon felt impossibly heavy in his grasp, its edge still smoking from the clash as he approached Raven cautiously, the others following behind him with equal wariness.

The fallen guardian knelt in the center of a perfect circle of scorched earth where nothing had survived—no grass, no insects, nothing. The soil itself had been stripped away, leaving a muddy puddle in which Raven now knelt. Particles of shadow drifted upward like inverted rain.

“I hope you win,” Raven said weakly.

Ace narrowed his eyes in suspicion.

“I do,” the sentinel insisted, though his head lolled back on his neck as he struggled to stay upright. “You did what we couldn’t.”

“And that is?”

“Trust each other,” Raven answered, his eyes closing with exhaustion. “They’ll test you. The Sisters. They’ll do everything they can to break you.” His gaze lingered on Marcus, whose shoulders stiffened under the scrutiny. “For some, a glimpse of what they might become.” The guardian’s attention shifted to Olivia, whose expression remained impassive despite the intensity of his stare. “For others, a reminder of what they once were.”

Finally, his eyes met Ace’s. Something unspoken passed between them. The weight of the lives they had carried. The burden of decisions that haunted their sleep. The scars from sacrifices others would never understand.

In Raven’s eyes, Ace saw not an enemy but a mirror—another man who’d shouldered responsibility until it carved permanent grooves into his soul.

Ace nodded once in farewell. The silent gesture spoke volumes, brimming with acknowledgment, respect, and the tragedy of paths that should have run parallel instead of colliding. His fingers tightened around the Crimson Sword’s hilt, not as a threat but in solidarity. One warrior honoring another’s final stand.

“Save them,” the fallen sentinel whispered. “Do what I couldn’t.”

His form grew increasingly transparent, the landscape becoming visible through his chest. The last of his shadow essence pulled away from his human shape, returning to the Shadow Realm in a gentle spiral rather than the violent vortex it had formed before. For a brief moment, they saw Raven as he must have been—a young man with keen eyes and a squared jaw, unmarked by corruption. He looked at them not with regret, but with relief and the resignation of someone accepting a long-delayed end.

Then he was gone.

Silence fell across the lakeshore as the unnatural cold dissipated, allowing warmth to creep back into the air. The shadowy connection between Ace and his team faded. The unnatural darkness of the Shadow Realm finally lifted, revealing a sky turning to dusk. Purple-orange light bathed the devastated landscape with long stretches of shadow from the mountains and trees circling the lake.

Ace staggered, suddenly aware of how completely drained he felt. Every muscle in his body screamed with fatigue. His Soul Meter registered nearly empty. A hollow void burned in his core where his power should’ve been.

His knees buckled beneath him.

Before he hit the ground, Marcus caught him. The telepath’s arms trembled with exhaustion, but he managed to support Ace’s weight.

“Easy, boss,” Marcus said, his own face pale with exhaustion. A thin trickle of blood ran from his right ear, evidence of psychic overexertion.

Olivia collapsed gracefully a few yards away, somehow making even her fall look deliberate. Her body folded into a seated position that resembled meditation more than a collapse. A smile played at the corners of her mouth.

“That,” she whispered, her eyes half-closed with the need for sleep, “was beautiful.”

“You’re fucking weird,” Marcus said. “You know that right?”

Olivia just laughed and craned her head toward the sky as she drank in the light from the setting sun.

Tara crawled to Ace’s side, her movements slow and pained. Stone particles embedded in her palms and knees, drawing tiny beads of blood as she placed a hand on Ace’s chest. Weak healing energy pulsed from her fingers—not enough to restore, merely enough to assess.

“Stop,” Ace ordered.

He grabbed her wrist and gently pushed it away, refusing to let her sacrifice any more of her SM and HP than she already had.

“Too late,” she said with a weak chuckle. “Already diagnosed you.”

Ace sighed in defeat. “Will you please just rest?”

“The wound’s closed,” she said, ignoring him. “But you’re running on empty. We all are.”

Ace nodded, too exhausted to speak. Through the hivemind, he sensed their collective relief, triumph, and bone-deep weariness. The connection had weakened, but it still transmitted emotional states. Without words, he conveyed his pride in them—in how they’d fought together, trusted each other, survived.

From Marcus came confusion tinged with awe. To Ace’s surprise, the man blushed and weakly batted away the compliment with a flick of his wrist.

From Olivia flowed satisfaction so intense it bordered on pleasure, alongside something darker that slithered beneath her conscious thoughts—a hunger awakened by their shared power.

Tara’s essence remained steadfast despite her exhaustion, her healer’s instincts automatically monitoring their conditions even now.

For a time, they simply sat there on the lakeshore in silence. Catching their breath. Closing their eyes. Breathing deeply as they savored their survival after such an intense brush with death.

“What was that?” Marcus asked after several minutes. “That shadow-blood thing you did? Is that in the strategy guide, or did you just make it up?”

Ace’s laugh came out as a wheeze. “I improvised.”

“Warn a girl next time,” Tara muttered. She hoisted herself upright beside him, but her shoulders slumped with fatigue. “Felt like my soul was being pulled through a straw.”

“The shadow didn’t just enhance our powers,” Marcus said. “It connected them. I could feel how my fear energy was destabilizing the vortex’s structure. Could sense exactly where Olivia would strike next.” He shook his head in wonder. “Whatever the hell that was, it was awesome.”

Olivia sat up, her graceful posture returning despite her exhaustion. “No, it was intoxicating.”

The hunger in her voice made Ace uneasy. She ran her fingers through her hair, restoring some semblance of its previous elegance. Olivia’s expression revealed nothing, but through the hivemind, Ace sensed her eager ache to do it again.

With effort, they helped each other stand. Tara leaned on Ace, while Marcus found support from Olivia. Together, the four vampires faced the eerily still lake and the distant waterfall they’d come to find.

The battle had transformed the landscape. Cracks radiated from the points of impact, splitting the ground in patterns reminiscent of a shattered mirror. In places, the soil had fused to glass from the heat of released energy. The grass remained charred or completely disintegrated in a hundred-foot radius. Nothing would grow here for years, perhaps decades.

“So,” Marcus said, breaking the silence. “Did we win? Because this doesn’t look like an entrance to anything. Just a whole lot of destruction and an untouched lake.”

“We won,” Ace reassured the telepath. “Wherever the entrance is, we need to wait. To rest. We have to get our energy back up before we push onward.”

“Yeah,” Marcus said with a weak nod. “I vote for the rest bit. I need some sleep.”

“Good. Let’s see how we’re feeling after an hour or two,” Ace said.

He wanted to give them a pep talk. He wanted to celebrate their wins and lift up their spirits. He wanted to wax poetic for ten fucking years because that had been truly epic and he was so goddamn proud of them.

But Ace could barely open his mouth, and he needed some sleep.

In the meantime, Ace used the hivemind to send wordless pulses of gratitude and pride to each of them. They’d earned this brief respite.

As they settled against the boulders that hadn’t been obliterated to dust, Ace kept his gaze fixed on the lake. The water remained eerily still, and the waterfall crashed against the surface as if nothing at all had happened.

As they gathered themselves, a familiar giggle rippled through the air. The sound had no source at first, then coalesced into the System’s childlike form standing atop one of the boulders. Her dress shifted colors with each breath—crimson to midnight to the exact shade of Raven’s shadow essence.

“Oh, my precious darlings!”

At the familiar voice, everyone collectively groaned.

The System pretended she didn’t notice as she clapped her small hands together and hovered nearby, her eyes wide with theatrical delight. “That was absolutely spectacular!”

None of the team responded.

They were too tired for her bullshit.

Apparently unaware of—or actively ignoring—their exhaustion, the System skipped around them. Her dress flowed around her legs, and her pristine feet never quite touched the scorched ground.

“Did you see what you did to poor Raven?” She spun in a delighted circle. “All that lovely shadow magic mixing with blood essence? I’ve never seen anything like it! Not in all my centuries of playing with my little toys.” She stopped spinning and leaned forward, her hands clasped behind her back. “You’re full of surprises, Ace. And I do so love surprises.”

Ace rubbed his eyes and leaned his head against a rock. “Glad we could entertain you.”

“Oh, you most certainly did!” she replied. “The way you linked your abilities through the hivemind? Absolutely innovative! And that final blow?” She mimed an explosion with her hands, complete with sound effects. “The landscape will bear the scars for decades. I couldn’t be more proud.”

Marcus scoffed in annoyance. “We’re not doing this for your amusement.”

“Aren’t you, though?” The System’s smile widened until it seemed to stretch beyond the boundaries of her face. “Everything you do amuses me. That’s rather the point.”

Marcus opened his mouth to argue, but Ace caught his eye before the man could say anything. With a simple shake of his head, Ace warned the telepath not to engage.

With an exasperated little grunt, Marcus simply looked away.

The System’s eyes gleamed. “Now the real fun begins! The Crucible is waiting, and oh, it really does love fresh meat. There’s so many little tricks and traps for you to find in there, not to mention my little errand.” The System’s expression shifted, something calculating replacing the childish glee. “The Evolutionary Keystone—such a trivial thing to die for, isn’t it? But then again, I’m sure you all want to impress me.”

“I wouldn’t go that far,” Olivia said under her breath.

“Tick tock, darlings!” The System’s voice dropped to a stage whisper. “I’m not exactly known for my patience.”

With a sound like a bubble bursting—a soft, wet pop—she vanished, leaving only her final words hanging in the air.

In the silence that followed, Ace let out a long sigh of relief. Once again, the lakeshore was peaceful, and the team that had rallied around each other through hell and high water had survived.

Together.

The System might have designed this world for monsters and solo hunts, but his team had changed the rules of the game. They’d combined powers in ways she had never anticipated, and they had forged bonds she couldn’t break with simple pain or fear.

Whatever waited for them in the Crucible, the team had everything they needed to face it head on—after a well-earned nap, of course.

With that, Ace closed his eyes. Tara leaned her head on his shoulder, her breathing already evening out as she fell asleep, and he smiled in victory.

“Well done, team,” he said. “Well done.”
