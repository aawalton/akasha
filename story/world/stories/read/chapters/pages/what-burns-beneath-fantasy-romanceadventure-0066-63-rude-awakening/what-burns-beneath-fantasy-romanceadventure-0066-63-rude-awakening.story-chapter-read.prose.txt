He didn’t seem angry. Not at me, at least. Nothing… nothing happened. I’m not hurt. I’m alright. I’m actually alright.

The thoughts come slowly. Foggy, through a haze of sleep, and soaked in tentative relief.

Soft, warm cloth cushioned my spine. I could feel a few stray wisps of hair, tugged free from the braid, brushing against my cheek with every breath. Muffled voices echoed from outside the window and somewhere on the streets below.

It’s morning. I’m awake. Nothing happened and I’m… I’m okay.

“I’m telling you, she’s fine,” a furtive whisper came from near the door. “Stop fidgeting. You’re putting me on edge!”

A low, trembling baritone hissed back, “On edge? On edge? Something awful could happen at any moment, and I’m what’s putting you on edge?”

“She’s fine. She was mumbling things just a few minutes ago. Her skin’s all rosy, and she’s breathing just like normal.”

“You keep saying that, but-”

“Look, her chest is rising? See? And falling, and rising, and fall-”

“Can we just sit here quietly?” Royce sounded a bit hoarse.

I let my eyelids slide open. Swirls and streaks of wood grain patterned the ceiling above me. Hazy morning light cast the brown beams in a warm, welcome glow. I turned my head to the side, nostrils flaring with a breath of cool, oak-scented air.

Royce and Teela were crouched near the open doorway. The young woman was drumming spindly fingers on her knees, tail snaking back and forth behind her. Royce was a coil of clenched, twitching muscles. Both hands were fisted in his unkempt hair.

They both bolted upright as soon as I moved. Teela beamed at me and Royce bounded forwards. The mattress jostled and jerked as he practically crashed into a seat beside me.

“You’re awake!” He was shaking. “You look… how do you feel? Are you… is anything… do you-”

“I’m fine.” I shifted back with a yawn, scooting into a sitting position and blinking against the morning light. “I’m alright. Really. But… why are you two in here? Didn’t I lock the door?”

Teela dimpled. “Uh-huh, sure did. You know, it’s kinda funny, I’ve really never met a lock I can’t get the best of. Just gotta go real slow, take some nice quality time with it, whisper a few sweet nothings, wiggle and wobble and eventually, pop!”

I wasn’t sure if I should be touched or concerned. “You broke in? After I asked you all to just let me sleep?”

Her cheeks turned pink. “Oh, no, that’s not what happened! Er, not exactly. I did break in, but not to watch you. And we haven’t even been in here that long! Promise. We were eating breakfast and the captain came by to… well, that’s a whole big problem. We’ll fill you in once you’re up.”

“I’m up.” I yawned again, sliding the quilt down and lifting both hands to rub the sleep from my eyes. “What problem?”

“Brin!” Royce’s blue eyes went wide and his fingers closed around my wrist. A strangled sound choked out from the back of his throat.

I immediately saw why. “It’s not blood!” I stood quickly, pulling free and moving towards the washbasin. It was still half-full from last night, though far from clean. My crimson-streaked hand slid into the cool water. I grabbed the sponge, scrubbing loose every trace of fragrant juice and dark soil. “It’s not. I promise. I’m fine, I’m not hurt.”

His face was white, and he followed so closely that we nearly knocked elbows. “It sure looked like blood.” Every word oozed doubt.

“It wasn’t.” I lifted my hand, flexing out my now-chilly fingers to show that they were unharmed. Water pattered to the floorboards between our feet. “See? I’m not hurt. It was from… er, a plant.”

Thick, blonde brows furrowed as he squinted down. “A plant?”

“Yes. There was this fruit that I…” heat crept up my throat. I was relieved to be awake and unharmed, and I didn’t want to immediately relive my incredibly jarring dream. “It’s… not important. Really. But I’m fine, see? All in one piece.”

“I even see an extra.” Renner’s voice turned my attention towards the doorway. He was leaning against the frame, arms folded, looking distinctly unhappy.

“An extra…?” I looked down at the thick, black fabric draping to my feet. One hand lifted and my fingers curled around the golden clasp. “Oh. Y-yes.”

“You’re alright.” He didn’t phrase it like a question, but I could just feel the curiosity burning beneath his tone. And something else, too… he almost sounded angry.

Odd. Perhaps he didn’t sleep well? I examined Renner’s face. Lips pressed together, brown hair a manic tousle, shadows beneath his eyes. It did indeed look like he hadn’t gotten enough rest. Or perhaps he’s… I mean, I’m not exactly handling all of this very well. Salt, how many times did I nearly break down yesterday? And he went through that nightmare in the cave, just like me. Perhaps some of it was even worse for him.

The thought was sobering. My shoulders sagged and I answered gently, “I am. Are you?”

“No.” His voice was rough. He glanced towards Teela. “You didn’t tell her?”

The Fae-touched woman winced. “I was just about to.”

The air grew thick. I looked between the stiff, unhappy faces around me. “Tell me what? Did something happen?”

Teela’s angular features pinched together. “Look… don’t panic, okay?”

My brows quirked upwards. “I don’t think telling people not to panic ever really works.”

“Okay, try not to panic?”

“Teela, what’s going-”

“Baltha’s head is gone,” Renner growled. “And so are the pacted. All three of them.”

My jaw went slack. “What? What? What do you mean, they’re gone?!”

Teela fidgeted. “Not panicking, remember?”

“I mean,” Renner looked like he was in no mood for the young woman’s meek attempt at humor, “That the stars-damned, worthless guards in this salted town fell asleep on watch and woke up to find all their prisoners free. Cell closed, door locked, bars unbroken. And three murderers on the loose.”

The words made sense, and yet… they just didn’t. “No, but… Gil surrendered. He turned himself in! He wouldn’t have done that and then escaped. And I saw Josiah last night, he was terribly hurt. He wasn’t in any shape to run away!”

Copper eyes sharpened, snapping to my face. “You saw him? What do you mean?”

“At the Watch, I mean. I went last night. I… I wanted to know if any of them had ever had dreams like mine. If they’d figured out how to wake themselves up or… or avoid them altogether.” I sighed, braced both hands on the stone tub, and frowned down at my murky reflection. When I looked back up, Renner’s expression was stormy. “Don’t worry, I was careful with how I worded things! I don’t think the captain was suspicious at all. I just… I had to ask.”

“Fine, but you shouldn’t have gone alone,” he muttered, jaw tight. “That old bird was probably just itching to sell you out. And you’re an awful liar.”

My face grew warm. “Well-”

“She wasn’t alone,” Royce grumbled, folding his arms. He frowned at the smaller man. “I went. You were probably too busy relaxing in the tub to help, anyways.”

Renner looked briefly nonplussed. Then his scowl deepened and he said, with very deliberate slowness, “Okay, fine. You should have gone with someone competent.”

My brows rose at that, and Royce stiffened. He opened his mouth, looking quite angry, but Teela spoke first.

“Did they have anything helpful to say?” she interjected, nudging the door closed and glancing between both men.

I lifted one shoulder in an unhappy shrug. “No. But… salt, that’s not the point right now, is it? Are you all sure… I just… how can they be missing?”

“That old woman,” Royce spoke slowly, eyeing me cautiously, “The one who was… er, angry at you.”

“Agnes,” I supplied with a wince.

“Agnes. Right. She kept saying the hag wasn’t dead. That she’d come back and free them.” He glanced uncertainly between the rest of us. “That’s… I mean, she’s dead, right? You said the other Fae killed her? She can’t… er, come back?”

“He ripped her damn head off,” the smaller man growled. “Granted, some monsters don’t always stay down. There’s some kinds that can… I don’t even know. Piece themselves back together after a while. Regenerate. But… salt, yours didn’t seem like the type for half-measures.” He frowned at me, a silent question simmering in his eyes.

He wasn’t wrong. My pulse raced as I imagined the aftermath of Velidan and Baltha’s bloody battle. She was in pieces! Magic and monsters are one thing, but her head was torn off! There’s no way… “I… I don’t think he would’ve left if there was a chance of her… saints, regenerating. Getting back up.” I shuddered at the thought.

Renner began pacing. Wood creaked with every step, and his cloak snapped unhappily at his heels. “Yeah. I don’t think so, either. Sounds like the captain left the head in his office, and her body was in the cave. Maybe some Fae can get back up from a salted decapitation, but I don’t think that’s what happened. Probably one of the pacted was smart and didn’t show their hand. Had a little magic left over from the hag and used it last night. Agnes, I’d wager.”

I sank onto the bed, brows knitting together and hands wringing at my cloak. “But Gil… you all saw him! He turned himself in! He wanted to pay for his crimes! It just doesn’t make any sense for him to have escaped!”

“Maybe he didn’t have a choice in it.” His voice was dark.

“Should we stay?” Royce sounded uncertain. Bright blue eyes trailed to the window and the hazy sky beyond. “Maybe just for a day or two? Offer to help find these criminals? Make sure everyone here in the town is safe?”

My chest tightened at the suggestion. I don’t want to stay. We can’t stay, can we? We need to get to Pinnacle. But… ashes, he makes a good point.

Clearly, Renner disagreed. “No. We’ve got more important things to do, and these people can fend for themselves. They’re the ones that lost the damn prisoners.”

Royce’s broad, expressive features creased. “I wasn’t asking you.”

My pulse jumped at the harsh tone. I shot my friend a questioning look and he quickly turned away, face flushed with color.

Teela leaned back against the wall, cheeks puffing out, and heaved a long sigh. “Ashes, staying to help sounds nice, but… look, if the hag’s alive, there’s not much we can do about it, yeah?” Her red eyes glinted, flicking towards mine. “I mean, sure, we could go back to the cave, storm in, and you all can go for round two. ‘Cause that went so well last time.”

I flinched. She had a point.

“I don’t think she’s alive.” Renner rubbed one hand along his jaw. Leather and stubble scraped, and then he shook his head. “Like you said, ladyship; your Fae wouldn’t have left if she was getting back up. He needed you to go get the shard and fall asleep, right? He didn’t know how long it’d take. So he wouldn’t have risked her hunting you down.”

“I think you’re right,” I whispered, silently praying that the words were true. That all makes sense. It’s logical, and… and I can’t bear to believe she might still be alive.

It was a grisly image; Baltha roaming through the woods at night, twisted and bent and bloody after being pieced horrifically back together by some dark, death-defying magic.

No. She’s dead. She has to be.

Teela shrugged, as if that settled the matter. “Well, then there’s no danger. Not to the townsfolk.”

I blinked at her.

She gave a little huff. “Well, the whole reason they were killing travelers was so the hag didn’t hurt anyone in town, right? They have families here! Loved ones and friends and all that salt. They escaped because they don’t wanna die, not because they’re planning to come back and start offing their neighbors.”

“You know, that’s not a bad point.” Renner sounded begrudgingly impressed. “You’re probably right.”

The young woman rolled her eyes. “Of course I’m right.” She fixed each of our companions with a flat look and added, quite sternly, “So you two can be done butting horns, yeah?”

“‘Butting horns?’” I repeated, shaking my head.

She dimpled, tapping one finger against a sharp, ember-hued bone. “Heh. I thought it was clever.”

“At least someone did,” Renner muttered. He snickered as she made a rude gesture, sticking her tongue out at him.

“That’s it, then?” Royce sounded a bit deflated. “We’re still leaving for Pinnacle?”

I nodded slowly. “Yes. Yes, I think Teela’s exactly right. The townsfolk probably aren’t in any danger, and the best we can do for them is get to the Order and explain what’s happened. Surely they can send people down to help.”

My eyes were fixed on the floorboards, but I didn’t quite miss the way Royce’s face fell. And I didn’t quite miss the way Renner flinched when I said ‘help.’
