At the words of the giant, mutated Dwarf…the rest of the Korvoks lingering before the building suddenly came to full alertness, and turned their gaze upon us. Unlike all of our past encounters with them…

They looked far more focused.

I didn’t mind saying that it was downright eerie. The transition from their previously mindless state was complete and instant, in a distinctly inhuman manner. However, I didn’t get much more than a split second to wonder at the oddness.

Because as one, the mob of Korvoks, the ‘Skarned’ according to the giant, they threw back their heads, screamed a battle-cry, and then charged for us.

I cursed and drew Terractus with my right hand, hastily slinging my staff into its holster on my back. “Form up! Form up!” I bellowed into the air of the mountain. I needn’t have bothered. Like a well-oiled machine, my forces had already been on edge and had already been in the process of forming our battle lines. Like always, Olag’s armoured contingent was at the fore, while my group of primarily casters and ranged experts arrayed themselves behind them.

Only, this time I didn’t join them. With a quick nod at Meia, which the half-Orc thankfully understood, I stepped forward into the melee. I quickly found myself next to Olag and Walter on the front line. I…couldn’t help but notice that my own short-sword looked a bit small in comparison to the claymore held by my squire. Even the two double-bladed axes that Olag tended to favour looked larger than my own.

Still, it was my best option, for now. The one glaring weakness of the ranged fighting style I had begun to develop around Grasp of Calithorne was that it generally required a number of fires to be present in my surroundings for it to be useful. There were none around us, in this section of Karag Varla. The only things on the street before the Hagia Dynamis were the bodies of slaughtered Varlayans, rubble, and the two forces seconds away from engaging each other.

That meant it was time to get back to my roots.

Almost as a matter of course, I rapidly triggered a number of different Skills.

Thorn Cloak.

Grinding Crimson Sunder.

Might of the Wyrdwood.

Forty percent should be enough, I felt. Vis Maledicta Exactoris could wait as well.

For now.

As I felt the familiar comfort of my cloak settle over my shoulders, and Terractus erupted into a whirling mass of crimson thorned burrs, I settled into a stance.

I had already picked out a target when the mass of ‘Skarned’ hit our front lines. And it wasn’t the screaming berserker howling for my blood charging at me.

He was just the appetiser.

Still, I stepped forward and swung my grinding blade at his neck nonetheless. I was actually a bit surprised when, for the first time since we had set foot in this Hold, the Korvok I had aimed for actually tried to block my blow. That was the first time I had seen any of these madmen ever display even the slightest amount of self-preservation.

It didn’t help him, though, considering the poor quality of his shoddy, jagged blade. Empowered as it was, Terractus just sliced right through both the weapon and his neck, completing the strike I had committed to in the first place.

A thought occurred to me. Well, really more Middle, but we were the same person. I cast an eye about as I ducked over the overhead chop of another Korvok seeking to take my head, and considered the battle as I bisected him instead.

It wasn’t just my opponents. These Korvoks were different. They were actually displaying the bare minimum of combat ability I would have expected from a warrior host. It wasn’t, as I had thought, an attempt at preserving their life. It was instead a shockingly, for them, more considered attempt to take ours. They blocked, they dodged, they attacked in groups instead of mindlessly charging at us one by one. There was an actual flow to the battle that I hadn’t experienced before when fighting the Korvoks.

This, I could more easily believe, would have been a challenge for some professional forces to confront. For all of their newfound discipline, they were still bloodthirsty, insane barbarians. They were more resistant to pain, they fought at a level above your average ground pounder, and they were, one hundred percent, willing to sacrifice themselves for even a minor tactical advantage for the next Korvok to take advantage of.

I watched as, out of the corner of my eye, one Korvok tried to tackle one of Olag’s armoured axe-Dwarves, relentlessly trying to drag him to the ground even as said axe found itself buried in the mad-Dwarf’s skull. All so another Korvok following behind him could swing a maul at his helmed head. The alarmed Dwarven mercenary was lucky that he was fighting side-by-side with his fellows, and another could spear that Korvok before he could ring his comrade’s bell, but the point was the same.

Something about the Korvoks had changed, and I was betting it had something to do with the mutant giant. Who, even now, was watching the battle with all of the polite consideration one might give to an impromptu sports game.

Well, I’d just have to do something about that, wouldn’t I? If he was ‘enhancing’ these madmen in some way.

Maybe they’d revert if he were dead.

This time, it was Olag that I exchanged a quick nod of understanding with. I had full confidence that my very own Dwarven commander could handle this, and in all likelihood, win, without my presence.

I crouched and triggered Vis Maledicta Exactoris…

And both exploded into my full, transformed, monstrous self, and exploded into an upwards jump. With strength that was enhanced both by Might of the Wyrdwood and Rhazal’s once-cursed gift upon me, I easily cleared even the rooftops of Varla with the force of my vault. I didn’t even need my wings to gain enough height to soar above the chaos below and set my eyes on my target.

I did need them to swoop down at him, like the world’s second-largest bat monster. So that’s what I did, Terractus held at the ready, trailing crimson red thorns through the air like a shower of malevolent sparks.

Aimed right at the neck of the giant, mutated Dwarf standing on the doorstep of the Hagia Dynamis. As I closed in on him, I could see that he hadn’t failed to notice either my leap or my dive, and was watching my descent with a keen, interested smile on his grotesque face. In the moment before I crashed into him, the thing unfolded his arms and held open his left palm in the path of my blade, the width of it as large as my torso in my base state.

Somehow, I was unsurprised that even with all of my enhanced strength, multiplied with the force of my dive…Terractus stopped dead upon impact with the gruesome, maroon flesh. I hung there for a moment, suspended at the point of contact, as my Skill-empowered blade ground against that palm, actual sparks somehow flying in the air. A Grinding Crimson Sunder enhanced Terractus tested itself against the bizarre flesh of this monster…

And found itself wanting.

My instincts suddenly screamed a warning at me, and I acted without thought. My wings twitched hard, sending me rocketing backwards.

It almost wasn’t enough. Somehow, the giant mutated Dwarf had either twisted or even dislocated his left leg in its socket enough that he had scythed a kick upwards at me from a standing position. The sight of the contortion was nauseating, because such a movement was so blatantly wrong. His torso remained straight and upright throughout the entire movement, with no twisting in either his upper body or hips to lend power to the unnatural movement, and yet…

It was still strong and fast enough that I only dodged it by the skin of my teeth. The tip of his mutated foot passed within inches of my face as I narrowly avoided it. The air screamed from the speed of the kick, and I think that might have either seriously injured or even possibly killed me if it had landed.

As I touched down onto the street, about a dozen feet away from him, I felt a wet sensation on my cheek. Without taking my eyes away from the broadly smiling mutant across from me, I raised my free hand and wiped two fingers across the area.

When I pulled them away and looked…

Blood.

That kick had been so fast and so powerful that the air-pressure from its passage alone had been enough to breach my scales and draw first blood. It wasn’t a deep cut…but it was enough.

This guy…he was dangerous.

So.

This was how the Korvoks had managed to so successfully conquer Varla. I wonder just how many more warriors like this ‘Karvokar’ the flesh-maddened Dwarves actually had?

I probably wasn’t going to like the answer.

I let a breath escape me in a sigh, and then firmed both my will and my stance. I angled my body to the mutant, drew Terractus into a high stance with the point facing toward him, and set my free hand, palm open, against the pommel of the blade.

This wasn’t the first time I’d needed to fight a physically superior opponent. That just meant I needed to get tricky about matters.

“So,” I said aloud, eyes locked on my opponent. “Do you have a name, or should I just write ‘Karvokar’ on your tombstone?”

The creature actually laughed at me, as he finally settled into an actual stance. He, too, angled his body toward me, left side first. He clenched his broad hands into fists, thrust one arm before him, arranged the other into an arc over his head, bent his legs into a broad horse stance, and then spoke once more. “Karvokar is merely a title, human. My name is Djarn. And I applaud you on your most virtuous pride. It will do you well when you are shown the light of Truth. First, though…you must be humbled. Come. Let us see the limits of your capability.”

‘The limits of my capability’, eh.

It might have been a while since I’d sparred against an unarmed fighter, but I was more than willing to show this bastard what I was capable of.

I ratcheted Might of the Wyrdwood up to my current maximum.

Seventy-five percent.

The crawling, ghostly crimson thorns across my body suddenly hardened into visible plates of armour, all across my body. Within those expanses of eerie Mana, the burrs and whirrs of my Skill expression still writhed, but now they served a greater purpose than just deflection. It wasn’t full coverage. Just greaves, gauntlets, and a breastplate…

But it was damned effective.

I barred the fangs of my transformed state at Djarn the Karvokar, and exploded toward him in a lunging stab, moving far quicker than I had previously. There was a big difference between forty and seventy-five per cent, and he was about to find out why.

This time, it was he who only had a moment for his eyes to widen at my greater speed, and then I was upon him. He tried to deflect the path of my oncoming blade with one open palm again, only this time, it was the skin of his right hand that was found wanting, and not my scales. Thick, nearly black blood oozed from the gash I had opened on his hand. For an instant, Djarn looked almost fascinated to see it, but I wasn’t intending to allow him any kind of near religious fervor.

First blood may have been his, but the second and all subsequent ones were going to be mine.

Then, he started to take me seriously, and I was introduced to the martial arts of the Karvokar. They were, to my complete unsurprise, very brutal. He was skilled, I would admit that, far more than most of my opponents. Far more so than his ‘Skarned’, as he had called them. Hard, fast strikes with his fists were his primary method of engagement, though he wasn’t afraid to utilise kicks when it suited him. For all of my enhanced strength and speed, he was keeping up with me. I think I still had a good edge on him when it came to sheer quickness, but if I had to guess, his raw power still outpaced mine.

That suited me. I just had to not get hit, in the end. The goal wasn’t even to really defeat him like this. No, a straight-up battle only played into his advantages. We may have been darting back and forth in our exchanges for several minutes, now, as the greater battle played out behind me, but that wasn’t the point of this.

I just wanted him to get comfortable. He was enjoying this, I could tell. This Djarn was one of those battle junkies you sometimes ran into on Vereden. People who almost sexually appreciated the flow of combat. All I needed…

Was for him to enjoy this so much that he lowered his guard.

Ah.

There it was. A laugh of joy escaped the Karvokar after our next exchange, and I seized my chance.

I thrust my open palm toward the laughing giant and triggered a rarely used Skill of mine. Something I had found little use for, up until this point.

That was, until I had acquired my capstone.

Stellar Blight.

I had a theory about this one. And most of the time, my most impromptu combat theories ended up being my deadliest.

A small flicker of bright blue flame flew from my palm and latched onto Djarn’s right shoulder. The affliction Skill burned on the surface of his skin, doing no noticeable damage. He stopped laughing long enough to look down at it doubtfully, before turning to me with a raised eyebrow of disappointment.

I don’t think he was expecting the savage smile of victory on my lips as I triggered my capstone Skill.

Grasp of Calithorne.

The small, flickering, fist-sized ball of fire sizzling uselessly on Djarn’s skin turned crimson red, and from it burst forth six lengths of vine, hooked and barbed. Before he could even react, I clenched my free hand, and they curled in on him, quickly binding his arms and legs together. He only barely managed to retain his footing, and I think if he had enough time, he might just manage to rip his way out of those vines.

But those vines weren’t meant to kill him on their own. That wasn’t the point.

I just needed him immobile for a single instant.

I rocketed toward him once more, blade extended and met his eyes. He was tracking the path of my blade as it descended toward his neck, and he didn’t seem all that wary of it. After all, his bizarrely hardened skin had deflected my blade plenty of times.

That was, it had when it was enhanced with Grinding Crimson Sunder.

I abruptly cut the Skill and replaced it with another.

The Scintillant Blade.

As Terractus erupted into brilliant burning, rainbow-hued flames, I bared my teeth at the suddenly alarmed-looking mutated Dwarf.

Check…and mate.

The blade sheared straight through his neck, like a hot knife through butter…

And that was that.
