“Can I keep this?” The Shaman pointed at the body.

Risha shrugged, and a serpent-like spirit leapt from the shaman, swallowing the dead winged god-touched in one bite before disappearing back into the shaman.

“Like I said, very interesting. It seems there are more troubled times coming for you lil sis. Perhaps we should continue your training?”

Risha shook her head, “I need to get back. Can I see you soon?”

“Same time tomorrow. Find me in her cottage.” He gave her a small bow, then stepped through space, disappearing.

Risha took one last glance around the council room, then stepped back to reality.

***

She stirred against soft furs as she sat up.

Klu slept soundly in the bed nearby, while Glo leaned against the wall, his spear held in his arms.

Glo lifted his head, “Did you learn something?”

Risha smiled at the goblin. “Yes and no. We already have some of our people near the oceans, right?”

Glo nodded, “A few. Many were called back for this conflict.”

“Good. Send more to build bigger boats. It’s a priority.”

“Yes, Mother.”

Risha let out a sigh. “I would go there right away, but I still need to meet the wolf packs.”

“The ocean is that important?” Glo asked.

Risha pushed the furs off of her as she stood and stretched. “Our enemies are endless, Glo.”

“Glo knows. Glo chose this life, for you and for all the goblins.” He smiled, “And the wolves, and the orcs, and any other you chose to protect, Risha.”

“Risha’s glad to have you.” She walked over to the exit of the tent and opened the flap, staring out over the moving army. “I have made a deal with one of the dragons. I will meet her tomorrow, then I will leave. There is too much to do and too little time.”

Glo stepped up behind her, “Yes, Mother. I will follow once things have settled here.”

Risha paused, then looked at Klu before turning to Glo. “The vultures will strike again, quietly, and you must be ready.”

His mouth settled into a hard line before he bowed his head. “I will be.”

Risha laid her hand on his lowered head, then she turned and left for her tent.

She walked alone to her tent, the monsters she passed bowing or giving her smiles.

The guard who stood outside her tent stood tall as she approached. “Mother, your guest waits for you inside.”

Risha tilted her head, then stepped past the flap to see a familiar demonic woman kneeling on the ground.

“Mother Risha.” Siyah, inheritor of Freera, lowered herself further to the ground.

Risha blinked in surprise, then made her way to her chainswords. She’d had to take them off for her trip into the Scaled city, and it felt strange to only have her old blade at her waist.

She took Crimson off their stand, the spirit inside of the sword eagerly meeting her.

“Siyah, I’m glad you’re alright. What can Risha do for you?” Risha asked as she gently put the sword back.

“I need your help.”

Risha walked over to the woman, and helped her to her feet. “What do you need Risha’s help with?”

“I need to go to Undercity Freerala.” Siyah slowly raised her chin to meet Risha’s eyes.

Risha hummed, then walked over to the small workbench she kept in her tent, several unfinished arrows were thrown haphazardly across its surface.

“There are… creatures in the deep. I won’t be able to make it there alone.”

Risha grabbed an arrow, studying the symbols carved into it as she thought. Grand Mother Maya’s words from so long ago came back to her, telling Risha to meet her in that city. The conversation had escaped her mind with everything that had happened, but it had been a distant goal of hers.

Risha nodded, decision made.

“We will go. But not right now.”

“We?”

Risha smiled wide, “I’ve been meaning to go. How long do we have before you must go?”

“I… I don’t know. Some time, I think.”

“Good. Everyone wants Risha’s attention, and Risha is exhausted. You will meet me in the Orc Town, and then we will go there.”

Siyah bowed low, then fiddled with her hands, “Thank you.”

“Monsters strong together.” Risha laughed.

Risha rode on the front of a wagon, happy to have finally made it to the road under construction. It had taken far too long to escape the swamps that surrounded the Scaled Stronghold. Kliks sat on Risha’s head, clicking with enjoyment as the breeze went over her.

A High Goblin Spider-Tamer, one of Glo’s lieutenants, sat next to Risha, happily chatting with the spider that drew the cart in a conversation that had been going on for the majority of the ride.

Two packs of kobolds ran beside the three carts. Mist-Prowler’s pack, and Sun-Chaser’s pack to be precise.

Risha had caught sight of the Wind-Whisperer, the Jackal who had fought beside her against the assassin, and it had made her happy to see him healed up. Apparently, he was the public Alpha of the pack, allowing Mist-Prowler to be a lesser known leader.

All of Mist-Prowler’s pack wore those strange capes, and seemed to keep to the trees, while Sun-Chaser’s pack rode close to the wagons, a much more visible guard.

Occasionally, Risha would see a Sapling on the side of the road, working their magic to help in the construction above the swamps, but they’d become fewer as they traveled closer to solid ground.

They’d have to leave the road again soon, as the wolf packs lived in the hills to the west of Orctown, and it would be faster to take the spiders through the forest. Still, the respite of the cart was welcome, and Kliks was certainly enjoying the free ride.

Kliks was the only spiderling still with Risha, the rest choosing to hibernate with Klaz’zks’s spiders for the winter. Risha appreciated the company of the little spiderling, who’d been with her through so much.

Sun-Chaser leaned over the divider of the wagon, her eyes meeting Risha’s. “Do you remember what I told you?”

Risha hummed. “Greet the Keeper first, then the other Elders. Bow my head, but not my body, and don’t show my neck.”

“The Keeper is the real test. If he does not approve of you, then you will have a civil war on your hands.”

“Yes, yes.”

“What do you give?”

“The future of the pack.”

“What do you take?”

“The burden of the past.”

Sun-Chaser tilted her head, “I’m surprised you remember so well.”

“Risha’s a smart goblin.” Risha smiled, “Risha is a good alpha.”

Sun-Chaser snorted. “Remember, they will test you. Physically, and mentally. Accept any challenge, no matter who gives it. I will take one duel for you, as will Wind-Whisperer. After the duels, you will meet the Keeper privately. The time spent with the Keeper changes for each Alpha, and I cannot tell you what happens in this meeting.”

Risha nodded. “Do not worry, Sun-Chaser. Risha has faced scarier Elders.”

Sun-Chaser huffed, “Worrying is my job. I will follow you even if they reject you, as will most of the packs. But it will create another enemy on our doorstep. “

“They will not reject me.” Risha had a rare moment of seriousness as she looked into the distance.

Sun-Chaser smiled wide, her fox face giving her the image of a trickster.

“What’s the best insult for the Keeper? Puppy? Orc-fodder?”

Sun-Chaser froze.

Risha burst out laughing. “Risha’s kidding!”

Sun-Chaser sunk into the back of the cart, her ears lowering in great sadness. “You’ll get us all killed.”

Risha clutched her belly as she continued to laugh. The goblin driving the cart did his best to hide his own snickering. He turned to her, “I’ve heard mud-coated used before.”

“Oh! That’s a good one! Or maybe Risha should stick to the classic ugly stinky.”

Sun-Chaser further collapsed as the two goblins broke into laughter.

Mist-Prowler walked up to the cart in the form of a Gray Wolf, “Stop bullying poor Sun-Chaser. She’s terrified for you.”

Risha smiled at the shapeshifting kobold, happy to see her. “Sorry, sorry. But goblins can’t be serious all the time. It’s exhausting.” She looked over to the other goblin, and he gave a nod of agreement. “Plus! A good insult is as good as a sword. Remember when Risha met Earth-Shaker?”

The spider-tamer nodded, “Watching Risha insult the ugly alpha was like watching a great hunter fire their best arrows.”

“See?” Risha looked at the wolves hopefully.

“She’s hopeless.” Sun-Chaser moaned into the cart.

Mist-Prowler laughed, then quickly turned away from the heartbroken fox. “You’ve got this Sun-Chaser. We’re all rooting for you.”

A sad moan came from the poor kobold.
