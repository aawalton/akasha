The gates creaked open slowly, and Tavian felt a rush of excitement as ghouls howled in the city beyond. He climbed up into the cart at the head of the column, taking a seat and collecting the horses’ reins. Ten men sat nervously in the back of his cart, watching the gate.

The sun was setting. Braun had gathered one hundred and twenty men—not quite a full block— the most capable, fit fighters. They’d leave everyone else as security for the walls. Braun made his final checks. He moved with more confidence. [Regent III] had been expensive to bestow, but his brother had seemed extremely pleased to hold a class of the same tier as Donath.

Tavian knew the plan should work. But his pounding heart made him feel like he was about to launch into a suicide run.

Braun gave the signal, and the gates swung slowly open.

Tavian snapped his reins, and they were off, horses straining as they pulled carts out into the cobbled streets of Tar Corinath, moving too fast. Horns sounded, and ghouls closed in. Tavian had expected to feel something as he left his kingdom, because of the change in Influence. But the miasmic dagger at his side negated all of the effects of Influence, even his own kingdom’s.

Because Tavian was driving the horses and cart, they also seemed immune to the resistant pressure of enemy Influence. He’d theorized that this might be the case, and regretted that he only had four of the daggers. He briefly wondered if Calli would be able to make more.

Glancing over his shoulder, he saw Braun driving the cart behind him.

Braun wore one of the black miasma daggers at his hip, as did two other captains. These three carts kept up with Tavian’s own. The remaining seven carts would have a rougher time of it, working against Anarvia’s Influence.

Ghouls ran into the streets. Tavian urged his horses onward, and they crashed through the rank undead. Tavian wove through the street, avoiding ghouls where possible and running them down when he had to.

Men on both sides of the cart braced their shields and lowered their spears. Cart wheels bounced on cobbled roads and horses galloped forward. Speed was their friend here; they needed to move quickly and not let the ghouls pile up on them.

All twelve carts rolled on, leaving a trail of broken bodies in their wake. If they slowed, Lord Anarvia would be able to figure out what they were doing, and gather a force to oppose them. If Tavian didn’t get there first, they’d have to wait for him to break open an entrance.

The streets sloped downhill toward the harbor. They were racing forward at a breakneck speed, and the jolts and jerks of the cart as it bounced along threatened to throw men out if they didn’t hold on tight enough. All his men were feeling the effect of Anarvia’s Influence, and they grunted and moaned as they struggled to stay in the cart.

Someone shouted behind, and then a tremendous crash split the air. Splintering wood, clattering weapons, and the scream of a horse signaled a disaster. Tavian glanced over his shoulder, and saw that one of the carts, toward the rear, had turned over. Men stumbled to their feet, preparing to fight ghouls.

Tavian fought against his impulse to stop and help them. The operation’s success depended on speed. The soldiers would group together, take shelter in a nearby building where they could fend off ghouls more easily, and wait for an opening to get back to the fortress.

He’d spent the Essence to give every man on this assault [Fortify I], boosting their constitution by one. Tavian knew it was risky. He hoped they’d be safe, even as he wished he’d been able to afford higher Tier Classes for his men.

Screams rang out behind him.

As they navigated steep roads and narrow turns, Tavian struggled to rein in his horses. They were terrified of the ghouls, which seemed to spring out of every dark shadow, and throw themselves at the cart, teeth gnashing. Usually a thrust of the spear was sufficient to send them stumbling back or even collapsing to the ground, but occasionally one got close enough to grab at the horses.

They were getting close to their destination now, and Tavian checked over his shoulder again. The four carts at the front had outdistanced the other carts. Counting quickly, Tavian saw nine carts. Had another two gone missing? It was hard to count and keep his eyes on the road.

Tavian cursed; if he lost a quarter of his force just getting to the warehouse, they were going to have a hard time executing the next portion of the plan.

The cart swerved around another corner, squeezing down a narrow lane. There were fewer ghouls lurking in this quarter of the city, but Tavian knew that Anarvia would be sending thousands of them to head him off.

Then the warehouse was there. A few men in Anarvia purple were driving carts up the hill toward the Anarvia compound. These picked up their pace when they saw the Verosian soldiers flying in around the corner, shouting in panicked voices to each other.

They needn’t have worried. Tavian wasn’t there for them.

Just at the end of the road was the large gate leading to the shipping warehouse. The sky was darkening as Tavian turned the final corner and raced toward their goal.

Guards sprang to their feet and began throwing the gate shut. Tavian ignored them. A gate wasn’t going to stop him.

The Aegis was secured just behind him in the cart, covered in a thick cloth. It wasn’t well concealed there, and anyone who cared to look would easily figure out what he had. But he didn’t want it glittering in the night.

Tavian slowed the cart, and then passed the reins to the man kneeling in the cart just behind him.

Jumping from the cart, Tavian landed in a run and pulled the cover off his shield.

He held the Aegis in front of him. The turmoil around him stilled for a split second as Tavian exhaled.

Shockwave.

Full power.

A blast of dark power surged ahead of him, smashing into the bars of the gate. Mangled bits of cast iron shrapnel exploded outwards, catching a pair of unlucky soldiers and shredding them.

Tavian felt the cold wave of miasmic power surging outwards around him, and he heard the unearthly cries of ghouls in the distance, attracted to what he’d done. The connection to the shield throbbed, and he knew that he was only accessing a fraction of the shield’s power. It wanted him to unlock its new abilities. If he would give himself over to the miasma…

Silent soldiers suddenly burst out in cheers as fragments of the gate rained down. Tavian saw shining eyes, faces flushed with excitement as his men entered the gates.

He hadn’t told them what he was planning. He could see now that they believed in him.

Tavian’s cartloads of soldiers began rolling in. Men jumped out and ran, securing their objectives, as Braun stepped up, coordinating the attack.

Tavian counted nine carts. Soldiers at the rear reported that the other carts had also crashed, and no more would be coming. Those soldiers would be working their way back to the fortress now. He regretted their absence; it would only make the next portion of the plan that much harder.

They tilted the carts up in the opening Tavian had blasted in the gate, setting up a barricade. Braun pushed one cart away from the others, and lit it on fire. The column of smoke rising into the fading light was Donath’s signal that the decoy attack had begun.

With ninety soldiers, Tavian moved in against the warehouse. He had no idea what they would find inside, but it didn’t matter. They just had to make enough noise to bring all the ghouls in the city down on them.

“My lord,” a captain said, before they had a chance to progress. The dead guards were climbing back to their feet, heads twisting strangely, teeth gnashing. Tavian drew his sword, squaring up with the men, when the captain spoke again.

“They’re wearing blue,” he said. He was right. These men wore the livery of House Calenor; blue with a white flower.

Tavian dispatched the ghouls, severing heads and limbs. The bodies leaked miasma from their wounds.

Soldiers moved without orders, following the plan as they surrounded the warehouse.

Shouts sounded from nearby, swords clashing and ringing out as soldiers met resistance.

Tavian entered the building, and grabbed a man.

“Find out who’s fighting us!” he said. “I need to know if it’s Calenor or Anarvia!”

The man ran off and Tavian pushed inside the warehouse.

An eerie cry echoed through the space.

There shouldn’t have been anyone, or anything inside the warehouse. Tavian and his men had secured it too quickly.

The call sounded again.
