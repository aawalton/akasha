Caide’s sword grew blinding gold, and he slashed twelve times in a little more than a second.

A lattice of twelve thin, golden sword-arcs whipped through the air, so quickly that if Alex was merely reactively shielding, even his instantaneous shield would’ve been too late.

Like his force-slashes, but even faster; these slashes were pure Light, and every slash was brilliant; you could hardly see its original shape, only a vertical form of piercing Light.

Alex wasn’t merely reactively shielding, luckily.

He was watching Caide’s body very carefully; because of the sheer visibility of his Light mana, Alex realized it was kind of possible to predict when the guy might strike.

Two Mana Shields burst into existence; at the same instant, six slashes struck it at once, a lattice of golden X’s.

Alex’s shields both shattered. And there wasn’t time to get away—Alex could barely brace against it, trying to take as much of it on his armor, when—

SCHING—SHHH!!

He gasped.

Thin lines of blood exploded across his body. Arms, all over his legs, his core; plenty of the slashes ping’d off his armor, but plenty also slashed straight into him.

Alex didn’t even have time to process his shock, because Caide once more blurred into golden afterimages.

And if Alex stayed in that shock for even a fraction of a second, his throat would be gushing blood.

CLANG!

A second block, once again at his neck; Alex didn’t let him get a second slash off. He forcefully expelled the guy from close range with Force slashes and two Mana Spears.

He barely got half a second’s respite. Caide was unleashing yet more golden arcs. Lines of Force mana ran up Alex’s posterior chain, and his legs especially. Alex used that half-second to create a burst of Force; he stepped, hard.

Golden arcs slashed where he’d been a split-second before. But Caide kept slashing, and arcs kept chasing him, and in two steps Alex was forced to a halt, forced to shield, weathering the last of the arcs, his eyes very wide.

And Caide went for his neck again, this time with a cry.

CLANG! CLANG!

It’s like he’s trying to drown me.

Alex’s teeth gritted.

The margins for error were so thin, but he kept his eyes focused very firmly on Caide’s form—and more, kept his mind, his soul senses, on Caide’s, because really sensing mana flows, and getting that split-second’s warning, and using his brain, and all his tools, to cling on, were the only things keeping him alive—alive, but still taking new bloody slashes every single second.

He regretted a few things, in the next few exchanges; the first was not investing a bit more in Perception, perhaps a Perception-related gear item, which would’ve seriously helped with his soul senses.

The second was not using that freaking Reflect Skill Shard, because fuck would it be useful right now against all this Light mana…

Caide stepped back after a flurry of almost a dozen exchanges, and he was breathing slightly heavy.

Alex’s eyes met his, and he seemed surprised, like he was thinking—why hasn’t he broken yet?

Alex felt a bit amused. Or, more realistically—Why didn’t this random die in, like, the third exchange?

If Alex did have a Health bar, he was sure at least a quarter of it was wiped out already; he’d taken so many of these thin slashes, his body was already a bleeding, slashed-up mess. It felt like half his front side was burning with sharp pains.

All he was doing was trying to survive, and he was still failing at it, exchange by exchange.

But Alex was also seeing quite a few things.

First—

This kind of pace isn’t sustainable, even with all his gear, there’s just no way. He’s used way more mana trying to kill me than I’ve been using defending, anyway.

That instance item on his chest—it’s helping him somehow, with all this insane burst-casting; it’s helping his soul, maybe?

Alex flashed Caide a grin, which didn’t seem to please the guy.

Again, Caide attacked; five exchanges, which Alex weathered painfully, wincing and gasping, before Caide was forced to reset again.

At this rate, I’m going to bleed out… all around him, the battlefield was splattered with lines of blood. All Alex’s blood.

His lips quirked—have I really yet to land a single slash?

Except Caide was the one who wore a look of consternation; there was even a flicker of irritation in his pretty features.

“Caide, quit playing around already!” A joke, from the Aegis guilders, with a few laughs. “Finish him already, what are you waiting for?”

It was clearly intended as a little playful support, but Caide’s jaw clenched a bit.

He’s realizing the same thing I have, mused Alex. It’s getting clearer now.

As their exchanges kept going, they kept getting less and less one-sided; fewer slashes were slipping through Alex’s defenses.

Because Caide was so obvious.

And Alex was also realizing why Caide was so obvious. He could see it in Caide’s face—Clarissa St. Clair’s words, strangely, came to mind.

It’s a matter of optics.

He wants to be seen to be wrecking me, with overwhelming force; if he’s just Awakened, this has to be one of the first times he’s in the public eye.

He can fight much smarter, much more tactically, and be much more patient, but he shouldn’t need to try hard to beat some random nobody, right?

He wants it to look like a supergenius crushing a can, as it should.

Except that’s precisely why I can read him more and more, because he is kind of just, charging at me predictably.

A glimmer in Alex’s eyes. If you switch it up, he acknowledged mentally. I’m sure I’ll have a really hard time. If it’s even kind of hard to read you I’m probably fucked.

But if you can’t swallow your pride… you’re just handing me more and more reads and experience with you.

Already Alex’s sense of Caide’s weapons was sharpening; he was almost halfway to death, he was certain, and Caide was still untouched, and yet—

He licked his lips.

Why am I kind of liking how this is going?

Alex’s Sword began filling with Swordforce; he hadn’t yet gotten a chance to properly counterattack.

He met Caide’s eyes again.

Maybe this is delusional, but—

I feel like, with the way this is going, I’m going to hit parity in our exchanges. And he’ll have wasted so much mana…

And he’s fast, but there’s no way he’s all that durable, his entire build screams speed and offense.

If I can just keep weathering this, and force him to weaken, and get one really, really hard, charged-up Force slash in—

What happens then?

I’m really freaking curious.

Alex noticed, out of the edges of his eyes, the walkways seemed to be filling with a few more people, a few more eyes.

The observation deck of the Rising Stars Cup featured the highest concentration of talent and power Stark City had seen in this year.

There were a dozen of Stark’s finest Guild talents, as well as a few New York City talents, there to oversee their very promising new classes. The Vice Guild Masters of Aegis, and ArchKnights, and O2, were all present.

This deck featured nine D-rankers; several were these Vice Guild Masters, and several were highly ranked Guild duelists, folks most Stark Awakened would recognize; Alex had seen one such person before, a short-haired girl with a very large, gem-studded bow before, in a digital billboard advertisement.

Except, the focus of the room wasn’t on any of them.

It was on two Awakened standing near the glass, drinks in hand.

The first was 6’4, very well-built, with a harsh, angular face, a face pretty much everyone in Stark would recognize, Awakened or not.

It was Adrian St. Clair, Ace duelist for Echelon, and one of the most powerful men in the Awakened world.

Adrian was the only one the folks in that room had expected. But he was standing next to a second well-built man with wild locks of black hair and piercing green eyes—a man who’d simply strolled in, to the visible surprise of almost everyone present.

He’d leveled his gaze at Adrian, and said, “If it isn’t Adrian St. Clair.”

Adrian St. Clair had glanced back, inclining his head slightly. “Marius.”

For a second there’d been an incredible tension as the two locked eyes.

Marius broke into a grin—“You fucking hard-ass, how the hell’ve you been?”

There’d been collective relief as the two embraced. Half the folks had been wary of violence. This was, after all, two men who’d dueled each other dozens of times before, even if their careers since their youth circuit runs had diverged quite drastically.

“Fine,” said Adrian, with a very slight smile. “And why are you in Stark, exactly? It can’t be to see me.”

“It is, stunningly,” said Marius, pouring himself a drink. “Well, you, and this so-called prodigy you’ve been hiding. I was finishing up this rift clear a hundred miles off the coast—Flood Dragon rift, its contracted Guild couldn’t tame it, you know how these rifts are.”

Marius was also known as ‘Legendkiller;’ he was a specialist in clearing certain D-rank rifts, rifts with creatures called legend-class creatures. It wasn’t the only reason for Marius’s nickname; whenever very high-ranking Awakened severely violated international law, such as elite Guild Masters or Vice Masters, the world’s Delver’s Associations would call upon certain task forces, drawn from several world elite Guilds, to sort it out, so to speak; the spoils would then go to the Guilds of these special forces folk. In his time, Marius had slain quite a few such human legends as well.

“Then I hear there’s this little talents pageant going on, as we’re flying back,” said Marius. “And you’re here, of all places. And this kid—what is he, the second coming of Aurélian?”

“He has potential,” was all Adrian said, lips pursed. “We’ll see if he has the mentality.”

“Harsh,” said Marius. “Where is he—ah. Gold trim, matching set. Very cute. You St. Clairs, and your PR—well, we’ll see, won’t we? Who else’s here? Let’s see—first seed’s an Institute kid?”

The pairings had flashed up. “They’ve given him the jade set, the flying swords,” said Marius. “I’ve never liked giving that kind of instance item to a talent. It’s far too easy to get reliant on it. Sure, you’ll be broken at first, but at what cost? And this isn’t that Clearwater prodigy, is it? Wasn’t she some kind of Profession prodigy? I swear, all these so-called prodigies.”

The rest of the room seemed noticeably quieter, like everyone else was surreptitiously listening in.

Finally, the talents began to be paired up. And after a few minutes—“Here we are,” said Marius. “arcstyrion?”

Marius grinned. “I like his eyes.”

The duel commenced, and Caide attacked.

“Well,” said Marius, and then, a few seconds later—“Well.”

A series of exchanges went by, and the folks in the deck were seeing the consternation in the crowd; plenty seemed to be feeling it themselves.

Except the two men at the front weren’t.

“Your little prodigy will have to expose a little more of his arsenal, it seems like,” said Marius, eyes gleaming with interest. “Who do we have here?”
