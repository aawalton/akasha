The final vote tally shimmers above the senate floor for a few moments before fading into nothingness.

“One hundred and twelve votes for the motion; fifty-seven against. Fifty-one absent or abstained. The motion passes,” Ionith reports in a bored tone of voice. Hints of satisfaction flicker through the eyes of those in favour of the new amendment to mining regulations. Most care more that this was the final matter to vote upon – stomachs are no doubt grumbling after the marks-long session.

Perhaps if we met more often, we would not have so many matters to vote upon, Nicholas muses, not for the first time. And, as always, he dismisses it – he has no true desire to visit the palace more often as it is.

“With that, I declare this session of the senate to be closed,” the king calls, rising from his throne. Ceremoniously, he lifts the blade for which his family is known from its tip-down position before him, twirls it in a mesmerising display, and then makes it vanish into his Inventory. Nicholas would be more impressed had he not seen the motion so many times.

Around Nicholas, nobles begin rising from their seats, conversations already breaking out in low murmurs as attendants hurried forwards with cloaks, documents, and refreshments.

Another tedious session concluded.

Nicholas is just reaching for the cane resting against the side of his chair when an unwelcome voice rings out across the chamber.

“Before this assembly disperses, I demand satisfaction from House Titanbend.”

The busy movement across the Senate floor slows, the nobles perking up at the thought of drama after the mind-numbing boredom of the last few candlemarks. Unsurprisingly, several lesser lords abruptly discover pretexts to remain exactly where they are – thin though they might be.

Nicholas looks up calmly to find Roland Torrent standing rigidly on the floor, staring at his seat with undisguised hostility in his bright blue eyes.

Nothing new, but Nicholas is curious as to what has prompted this sudden challenge. It has been months since Roland reluctantly accepted the consequences of his machinations regarding Zlona. The transfer of domains is progressing – slowly, as is expected given Torrent’s reluctance, but within acceptable limits

Upon the elevated dais at the head of the chamber, the king returns to his seat on the throne, one elbow resting against the armrest as he watches the exchange with mild interest. He makes no move to intervene.

Nicholas suppresses the faintest hint of resigned amusement.

Of course he wouldn’t.

“Answers regarding what?” Nicholas asks evenly.

Roland’s jaw tightens, perhaps irritated by the complete lack of concern in Nicholas’s voice.

“Regarding your heir’s conduct during the royal expedition.”

A murmur passes through the chamber.

Nicholas leans back slightly in his seat.

“I was unaware Markus’s conduct had become senate business.”

“It becomes senate business,” Roland snaps, “when your heir assaults mine, steals his vessel, extorts noble heirs, and threatens unlawful Binding.”

That draws sharper reactions. In particular, Nicholas notes several lords stiffen at the final accusation.

Yet Nicholas does not panic.

“And this information comes from Kyrian, I presume?” Nicholas asks mildly.

“From my son and every surviving witness accompanying him,” Roland replies sanctimoniously. “Your heir’s actions disgrace the expedition and overstep the authority granted to him by the Crown. I therefore formally petition that Markus Titanbend be stripped of his place within the competition pending royal investigation.”

A bold demand. Very in character.

And very foolish.

Nicholas allows a few moments of silence to stretch through the chamber before speaking – all the better to build tension.

“You seem remarkably confident for a man presenting only half the story.”

Roland’s expression darkens and he opens his mouth.

Nicholas continues before the other lord can interrupt.

“Fortunately, unlike you, I considered the possibility that a young heir might provide a somewhat self-serving account of events.” He folds his hands neatly atop his cane. “The story in my reports is somewhat different – and is corroborated by a source I trust without question.”

That causes a visible shift amongst the watching lords, agog for the drama – better than any play at the theatre.

Roland’s eyes narrow slightly.

Nicholas almost smiles. You’ve overplayed your hand, Roland. Did you not think that I, too, would have contact with those far from home?

“My understanding,” Nicholas continues calmly, “is that your son was the instigator. That, a mere tenday away from the Moriaxar shore, he pursued Markus’ fleet with malicious intentions.”

“He defended his honour–”

“He refused all offers of peaceful reconciliation,” Nicholas corrects flatly. “And swore that his only satisfaction would be with my heir’s death.”

The senate quietens further, those around almost seeming to hold their breaths. Roland goes red but does not speak. Wise, for once. Almost enough to make up for his earlier foolishness.

“Let us review your complaint, Lord Torrent. First, that my heir assaulted yours. I will not deny a battle, but your assertion fails to acknowledge that your heir attacked first. Markus fought in self-defence alone. Hardly a matter for you to demand satisfaction of me – if anything, it is the reverse.”

“The reverse?” Roland sputters. “Your heir stole my heir’s ship. And took all of his Inventory!”

“Rightful bloodgold for the resources he had to expend to defend himself against your heir’s unprovoked attack.”

“He robbed him blind! And his allies with him – who had done nothing to him!”

Nicholas’s eyebrow lifts slightly.

“Did they not? From the reports I received, they might not have attacked Markus directly, but an attack on one of his allies counts as an attack on him.” His gaze sharpens fractionally. “Demanding reparations in that case is generally considered a restrained outcome.”

Roland opens his mouth, but Nicholas continues smoothly over him.

“By all accounts, Markus was unexpectedly merciful.”

“Merciful! He’s crippled my son’s ability to use magic!”

Nicholas tilts his head to one side allowing the reverberations from Roland’s shout to die down again.

“Merciful indeed, as the crippling is not even permanent.” Nicholas fixes Roland with a flat gaze, allowing his long-time rival to see the sheer lack of patience he has for the man’s ridiculous statements. “Roland. You must see that your complaints are a camp built on a mire. Kyrian forced the fight; Markus finished it. Yet despite having every practical advantage after winning the engagement – and every reason to see Kyrian dead, he did not execute him. He did not even Bind him – he has too much respect for the rule of law to usurp the right that is only the king’s.” Nicholas carefully does not look at Ionith – that he’s stretching the truth of Markus’ respect for authority is something he doesn’t want the canny king to see. “Instead, he demanded entirely reasonable bloodgold, and released your heir to continue with his own expedition – with something to think about in the process.”

“Nothing my heir did to him deserved having his magic so limited! He wasn’t even trying to kill yours – things just got out of hand.”

“Roland,” Nicholas sighs, “your son attempted to sink Markus’s ship with a tidal wave. Does that really seem like he was trying to hold back?”

Several lords begin murmuring, their expressions agog.

Nicholas sees the exact moment Roland starts to question his own information. Perhaps the fault isn’t only on the part of the father. Then again, if the son omitted pertinent details, that is the fault of the father’s raising, so Roland still only has himself to blame.

Unfortunately for him, it is far too late to retreat gracefully now.

“He still interfered greatly with Kyrian’s ability to demonstrate his mettle in the competition,” Roland tries, clutching at straws. “In many ways, he’s more crippled than if your heir had removed a limb. Such interference is against the rules of the competition.”

It is utterly ridiculous that he’s trying to use the same rules against Markus that could so easily be applied to Kyrian. Nicholas wonders briefly if he’s missing something – Torrent is usually better at playing the game than this.

After a moment’s pause, Nicholas decides to reply, allowing the faintest edge of dry amusement into his voice.

“Indeed? I confess, I am surprised to hear Kyrian still struggles with the suppression cuffs Markus placed upon him. I had understood Great House heirs were taught methods of circumventing such restraints relatively early in their magical education.”

Roland goes very still.

“If you are as well-informed as you seem to fancy yourself to be, you would know that your heir wielded a Curse against him, preventing him from evading the binding himself,” Roland continues, his voice low and biting.

Nicholas raises an unimpressed eyebrow.

“A Curse is sufficient to overcome one of the Torrent line? How…interesting.”

Roland flushes even darker red.

Nicholas catches the faint twitch at the corner of Lord Bladestrike’s mouth several rows away and behind Torrent.

Even the king’s expression shifts slightly, amusement flickering briefly through otherwise unreadable eyes.

Roland’s face darkens dangerously.

“You insult my House.”

“No,” Nicholas replies calmly. “Your heir has accomplished that himself.”

For several long moments, neither man speaks.

Then the king finally straightens slightly upon the dais.

“I see no cause for royal intervention at this time,” he declares lazily. “The expedition is dangerous – that was established at the start. It is unsurprising that tensions run high, however, the matter appears resolved.”

Nicholas’s expression doesn’t twitch though he can’t help a flicker of satisfaction as he receives the message: the Crown will not weaken Titanbend merely to soothe Torrent pride.

Roland clearly understands the implications as well as his hands clench, only partially hidden by his robes.

“The matter between our heirs is not finished,” he says coldly, his eyes hostile.

Nicholas inclines his head slightly.

“No,” he agrees. “I rather suspect it is not.”

Roland holds his gaze another moment before turning sharply and striding from the chamber.

Only once the doors slam shut behind the lord does the noise within the Senate begin again in earnest. No doubt the last few flames have provided enough gossip to see them through to the next session.

Nicholas rises slowly to his feet, ignoring the whole-body aches that stem from his most-recent session with his healer. The improvements his condition has shown make the pain more than worth it, even if it does leave him relying on such humiliating aids as a cane.

As he steps down to the senate floor from his seat high above it, Nicholas is almost glad that Roland was so foolish as to bring up the matter – unless he is playing a deeper game. Nicholas will have to be wary.

That he’s only brought it up now makes Nicholas wonder if his methods of communication are significantly worse than Nicholas’ – for Nicholas, it is old news.

Still, Markus continues to prove his worth. He defeated a Great House heir, held the advantage strongly enough to dictate terms, and then brought his House out of it politically intact afterwards. And that is only one moment – if Sarran’s reports are anything to go by, Markus’ accomplishments do not end there.

The boy is becoming dangerous faster than I’d anticipated, he thinks, not without some pride.

Nicholas almost regrets his own sense of restraint. He wishes he could have casually mentioned the encounter with a Beast Emperor – Nicholas is glad he only heard of that long after it was settled – and the destruction of two Tier fours. He doubts Kyrian has anything so impressive in his list of accomplishments. However, it would have been in bad taste to continue bragging of his heir’s successes when Roland’s own son had been so discredited.

Perhaps I can drop a few hints over refreshments, Nicholas muses. His heir should be celebrated for what he has achieved – and Nicholas cannot help but wonder how the other Great House heirs who have departed are in comparison.

As Nicholas makes his way from the chamber beneath the watchful gazes of half the Senate, he finds, despite himself, that he is smiling.

A figure stands on a balcony, dark-skinned hands resting lightly on the white marble balustrade. His gaze is distant, staring towards the high mountain range, yet not truly seeing it.

“High Elder?”

The voice behind him is no surprise – he heard the approach long before.

“Yes?” he asks calmly, allowing no hint of his slight irritation onto his face.

“I have a message from the diviners.”

A faint curl of interest replaces the irritation.

“Go on.”

“They report…disturbances on the eastern coast.”

“Disturbances?” the figure asks. “What kind?”

The messenger hesitates.

“They…cannot say, High Elder. Only that a king has fallen, and another is moving.”

The interest dies, replaced once more by irritation.

“Kings die.” It is not frequent, but it happens.

“Yes, High Elder, but the diviners say something is different about this one,” the messenger reports hesitantly.

The figure pauses, contemplating the mountains. It’s true that the diviners would not normally raise such a banal occurrence to his attention.

Perhaps it is worth investigating.
