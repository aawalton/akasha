The Hyperian chapel had transformed the eastern quarter of Blackstone into a false dawn when it died.

Isolde Argent had watched the golden-white eruption rise above her city’s roofs from the western wall of her family estate, its radiance so immense that every shadow within the courtyard of her father’s villa had vanished beneath its blossoming detonation. The sound had arrived seconds later, while she was still trying to comprehend what she’d seen: a shockwave of audible force that had impacted the inner city in a wave powerful enough to rattle windows and shake even the ancient foundations of Blackstone, while sending the night watch scrambling for their weapons.

By the time the first alarms had tolled across the city, Isolde was already doffing her robe and reaching for her armor.

Thirty minutes later, she reached the compound, twelve Wardens at her back and a unit of the city watch reinforcing them, only to find that the word explosion had failed to fully encompass what had occurred.

The entire front of the Hyperian chapel had been torn open as if from the fist of an angry Power. Its doors lay twisted and burning, sundered into distinct pieces across the courtyard, while the stained-glass windows had been shattered with enough force that the fragments shimmered and sparkled as they reflected the light from the still-burning radiant flames. Large sections of the stone walls were cracked, or had simply ceased to exist, and the roof and ceiling of the chapel were gone—turned to dust by the detonation of power that had bloomed above Blackstone like a golden-white flower.

Worst of all, bodies lay everywhere—killed not by the explosion, but by something far less ordinary. Some were intact enough to resemble people Isolde had known, if only casually.

Others were not so lucky, and to one, every inhabitant of the Hyperian sanctuary was dead.

“Captain.”

Isolde turned as Warden Lamonte approached from the gate and slammed his silver-mailed fist against the rearing white horse adorning his green tabard. The square-built man had served the Wardens for almost three decades, and the gray invading his dark beard had done nothing to diminish the raw power lurking within his precisely built body. He’d been the first to respond to the explosion and was normally one of her most unshakable warriors.

Even so, and in spite of his normal stoicism, there was a pallor beneath his weathered complexion as he looked around the courtyard.

“What do we know?” Isolde asked instead of making small talk, stripping the gauntlet from her right hand as she moved past him toward the bodies.

“The first watchmen arrived five minutes ahead of us,” Lamonte replied while falling into step with her. “They found no living occupants, as you know, and the chapel is very unstable. The rear half is still too hot for us to search safely, and I ordered everyone out until your arrival.”

“You made the right choice,” Isolde agreed while walking. “Have the watch establish a closed perimeter two streets outward around the sanctuary and move every gawker and civilian beyond it. No one is to enter the compound proper unless they are a Warden, a healer, or someone specifically summoned for investigation.”

“What of the King’s garrison, Captain?”

Isolde paused, narrowed her eyes, and then grunted.

“If an officer shows up, that’s one thing, but no self-important royal grunts will bully their way inside.”

“Understood, ma’am,” Lamonte said with a nod, and turned to fulfill her orders.

“Kalaria!” Isolde called, glancing back at the twelve Wardens who’d directly accompanied her.

A black-haired woman detached herself from the other twelve immediately and approached swiftly, her dark eyes already moving over the courtyard stones rather than lingering on Isolde. Kalaria Mengst was among the finest trackers in the Blackstone Wardens, having spent much of her career hunting the sporadic beasts that wandered too close to the city from Darkwood Forest. People were often more difficult to follow than animals, in fairness, but they usually left some evidence behind—and if anyone could find it, Isolde knew Kalaria would.

“Sweep the compound and identify every entry and potential point of exit,” Isolde commanded, “including the unconventional ones. Start with the walls. The gate shows no signs of forced entry, and I want to know whether that means the killers were invited inside, or simply ignored the gate altogether.”

“Yes, Captain,” Kalaria said dutifully. “I’ll also check the rooftops, just in case.”

“As you wish,” Isolde agreed, and then nodded for Kalaria to go before turning to her wardens again. “Hector, start a count. I want the dead separated by location, identity, and cause of death where possible—but avoid moving the corpses unless absolutely necessary to determine signs of life.”

Hector, the oldest and most silvered warden in Isolde’s party, grunted his acknowledgment and scratched idly at the scar cutting across his shaved scalp. “That may be a mite trickier than it sounds, Cap’n,” Hector warned while glancing at the partially collapsed communal hall. “There are parts of these poor sods on both bloody sides of the room in some cases, according to the first responders.”

Isolde was entirely immune to Hector’s particular gruffness at this point—he’d been a Warden for longer than she’d been alive, and combined with how dutifully he undertook his service, a little rough-around-the-edges quality was nothing Isolde was about to reprimand him for.

“Then count the heads, Hector,” she said instead while turning back to the courtyard. “Make your best judgment afterward. I need to know who is missing as much as I need to know who is dead.”

“Ah,” the veteran Warden said gruffly. “You’re thinkin’ about the Priestess.”

“I am,” Isolde confirmed. “She isn’t here, or else we’d already have met with her.”

“Correct,” Lamonte confirmed from Isolde’s side. “Neither the watch nor our initial search found her body.”

Isolde looked by instinct toward the chapel, where flames still smoldered, where the walls had been blown apart, where the ceiling and roof had been vaporized, and where the thousands of prismatic stained-glass fragments had apparently exploded outward to cover the courtyard. The case itself, despite the relative normalcy of the investigation, was far beyond a simple assault. This was the Hyperian Sanctuary.

The ramifications of it being desecrated could be far worse than simply the Duke’s justice.

“Until we find evidence to the contrary, consider Priestess Sara Lightspeaker missing,” Isolde said after a moment of thought. “See to your task, Hector. The rest of you, assist Hector or Kalaria. Tricia, you’re with me.”

The Wardens dispersed at Isolde’s orders while Tricia—a chestnut-haired woman already donning a pair of leather gloves—followed the Captain toward the blood-streaked courtyard and the body of Sir Roderick Vayne. Tricia had been selected by Isolde for a reason; though she lacked the talent to wield magic directly, as did most souls in Blackstone, she had developed a scholar’s understanding of magical injuries, enchanted weapons, and the lingering effects of Spellforms. Her knowledge had been hard-won through two decades of investigating monster attacks, failed rituals, the occasional murdered hedge-mage or adventurer that drifted through Blackstone.

“This looks like someone was sending a message, Captain,” Tricia said as they walked and came to a halt near Roderick. “This wasn’t random. There’s rage in the aftermath here.”

“I see it, too,” Isolde agreed, while looking down at the corpse of the Paladin. “Now tell me what’s behind it, if you can.”

Tricia nodded and knelt beside the Paladin’s corpse.

Sir Roderick Vayne lay face down near the center of the compound, one arm folded beneath his silvered warplate and the other extended with futile determination toward the chapel. His warhammer, a colossal weapon still radiating Light Mana, had fallen several feet away; its immense metal head buried deeply enough into the stones where it had fallen to fracture them around the point of impact.

“The ground here is completely destroyed,” Tricia observed immediately, “but anyone with eyes can see that. What’s interesting is the burn patterns. Sir Roderick wasn’t just swinging like a madman; he was evidently attempting to hit something, and the scorch marks are consistent with Hyperian area-of-effect [Censure] abilities.”

Isolde looked across the courtyard as Tricia spoke but didn’t interrupt.

“Whatever fought the Paladin, Captain, it forces him to expend an extraordinary amount of Mana. These stones could withstand far more than a simple strike, and they’ve been obliterated in multiple locations. I’d wager the odd bloodstains have something to do with it—the pattern is inconsistent with the corpses around the place, and there’s an almost ritual nature to how they fell.”

“You believe someone was casting a ritual and Roderick interrupted them?” Isolde asked.

“No,” Tricia said after a moment of consideration. “I think someone was wielding the blood as a weapon.”

“That seems troubling,” Isolde noted calmly. “And a little insane.”

“Half the things I’ve studied are insane, Captain,” Tricia agreed readily. “But that fits the pattern of the violence. The blood is stretched and condensed in various places, and while some of it may have fallen naturally, there’s an intention to these patterns.”

Isolde followed Tricia’s gaze and saw the same, now that she was looking for it: blood wrapped around a stone outcropping, another stain forming a near-perfect crescent that had seemingly cut into the pavement; bloodstains around Roderick’s untouched ankle as if someone had wrapped the vitae around his sabaton, and more.

“So,” Isolde said at last, “it was shaped, then.”

“I think that’s the only real possibility, Captain,” Tricia confirmed.

“Blood sorcery?” Isolde asked with a frown.

“Could be,” Tricia said cautiously. “I’ll not name it merely because the alternative is admitting we don’t recognize what it is, but that’s as close an approximation as I can make.”

Isolde accepted that with a nod, and Tricia moved on to examining Sir Roderick properly. She ran her gloved fingers along the Paladin’s wounded right knee, where the rear of the armor had been sundered by a powerful cutting strike, and frowned. The injury beneath was far worse, with flesh, tendons, and bone savaged so completely that Sir Roderick should have been unable to stand.

“These wounds bear the signs of repeated healing,” Tricia observed after a moment. “Not total healing, no, but repeated, yes. The tissue has been knitted and torn apart several times—you can see it in the non-uniformity of the wounds compared to the armor’s damage.”

“Priestess Lightspeaker?” Isolde postulated.

“That would explain why the older wounds are so inconsistent in their damage,” Tricia agreed. “It does not, however, explain this.”

Tricia moved Roderick’s hair from his neck and showed them two dark, blood-stained tears in the man’s neck. They weren’t neat punctures; they were savage, violent, almost rageful. The flesh had been torn rather than merely pierced, and the surrounding veins were visibly collapsed. Roderick’s body had contained far less blood than the wounds and surrounding scene could explain. He should have been lying in a pool of his own red life force.

Instead, he had barely expelled any blood at all.

Lamonte chose that moment to return, after seeing to the perimeter security, and cursed when he did.

“Hyperion’s bollocks, we’re dealing with a Vampire?”

“Possibly,” Tricia agreed. “Though not like one I’ve ever investigated.”

“Could it be one of the feral bloodlings from the forest?” Lamonte asked while glancing at Isolde.

The Captain looked toward the chapel at his question, and felt herself hesitate. It was an answer that fit, but it was too simple. There was no madness in the attack. There was no sense of uncontrolled aggression. There was fury, yes, and there was rage—but the fight showed Roderick struggling, and whatever had killed him had done so while using its sorcery intelligently.

That didn’t fit anything they knew about the ferals.

“A feral bloodling could not have broken a Paladin’s warplate,” Isolde said finally. “Nor could one have wielded whatever magic this is, or forced Sir Roderick Vayne into an extended duel. This creature used a piercing weapon—probably a sword—as well as magic and legitimate tactics, based on the patterns.”

“It also understood that Sara was healing him,” Tricia said quietly. “He died out of position. Whatever attacked him, it separated Sir Roderick from Priestess Lightspeaker before doing so—a feral wouldn’t be that strategic.”

“How powerful are we thinking?” Lamonte asked while glancing between both women.

“Powerful enough to survive repeated exposure to Roderick’s Light Mana, and skilled enough to attack him through it,” Tricia said immediately. “That’s about as detailed as I can get without further investigation, but I’ll wager this is no nascent bloodsucker. We’re probably dealing with a mature or Elder Vamp.”

Isolde’s blood ran cold at the words, but she didn’t allow it to show on her face. An Elder Vampire was an entirely different category of enemy to a feral bloodling. Bloodlings were strong, but largely disunited and unfocused savages—an Elder Vampire was a force of utter evil and possessed a wicked level of malevolent intelligence.

“We’re going to need a full strike force,” Lamonte said grimly. “We’re not equipped to deal with an Elder Vampire with anything less than that.”

“Which is a polite way of saying,” Hector added as he approached with a clank of armor, “that we’re in for a proper fight. My own examinations match your assumption.”

Isolde nodded to him when he returned and scanned his face. “Do you have the count, Hector?”

“Eighteen dead so far, Captain,” the old Warden reported, his habitual gruffness muted into formality by what he’d seen. “Eleven attendants, give or take, all six Squires, and Sir Roderick Vayne himself. Some of the servants’re cut apart so badly that you’ll want a proper Medicae to verify the count, but the clothing and remaining heads match the compound census.”

“And Sara Lightspeaker?” Isolde asked, already expecting the answer.

“Definitely not here,” Hector affirmed, and his eyes went to Roderick’s throat. “Which is bloody odd. I’ve killed plenty of leeches in my time, Cap. None of them operated like whatever the hell this is.”

“The ones you fought were starved ferals, Hector. They nested in caves and fed on livestock or isolated farmers. This one came here with a purpose.”

“What purpose are you thinking, Captain?” Lamonte asked as Tricia rose.

Isolde studied Roderick’s outstretched arm before responding, then followed it toward the chapel with her eyes.

“If the creature simply wanted blood, it would have had much safer targets than a fortified Hyperian Sanctuary. It could have preyed on the drunks in the outer city and left no one any the wiser to its presence for weeks, yet. This wasn’t a convenient hunt. This wasn’t about hunger, at least, not in the broader scope.”

“I agree,” Tricia said while looking back down at Roderick’s neck. “This was an execution.”

“So what happens now, then?” Lamonte asked.

“Now we keep investigating,” Isolde said steadily. “And when we have a better idea of what we’re dealing with, we make a plan for what comes next, and then we kill whatever this is before it strikes again.”

“That’s bloody optimistic, Captain,” Hector warned.

“No, Hector,” Isolde said as she turned toward the housing block and the other three fell in with her. “That’s our duty.”
