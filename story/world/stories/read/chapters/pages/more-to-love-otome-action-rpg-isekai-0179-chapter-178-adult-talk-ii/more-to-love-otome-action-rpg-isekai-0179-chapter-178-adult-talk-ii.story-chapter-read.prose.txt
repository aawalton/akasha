Of course this is happening. Once they left school, she had expected some new nefarious development to occur, but even so, it felt much too soon. They hadn’t even had a chance to recover ground on humanity’s side, and they were already preparing for a war.

Or is the lull in attacks a chance for us to prepare? That seemed like an even more worrying thought, that whatever was directing the monsters was viewing this whole ordeal as a game, and was being fair in giving the humans time to recover.

There had better be a god on our side. The sarcastic thought—albeit hopeful—brought to mind her vehement animosity towards the idea of preordained outcomes, along with the pale-haired subject that caused her the greatest aversion towards leaving her fate in others’ hands.

“’Large-scale attack?’” Aida shook her head to dislodge the mental brick that was threatening to occupy her thoughts. “But—monsters are animals, aren’t they? Surely they can’t coordinate—“

“The odd thing we’ve noticed during our patrols is that monsters… I hesitate to say they’ve become more intelligent,” Yoon interjected hesitantly, looking at Tyda to confirm she could say more. At his nod, she looked back to Aida. “But they seem to be more coordinated. As if something is directing them.”

She shook her head, perplexed. “It’s very strange, because… as you said, they’re mere animals. Individual monsters don’t exhibit intelligence beyond that of satisfying their natural urges, but the way they are attacking patrol groups… it’s not normal.”

“We—not just us, other patrols as well—had been suffering more casualties as the attack waves have become more and more concerted. Fortunately, we haven’t lost anybody yet… other teams haven’t been as lucky.” Tyda folded his arms as he looked to Yoon, who returned a melancholy look. “Anyway, that’s what has been happening until this sudden lull. The outlook was looking worse and worse, since we were obviously losing the war of attrition. In fact…”

Tyda paused, his brows furrowing ever so slightly as he chose his next words.

“In any other circumstance, this… calm would have been a welcome reprieve. But the fact that we don’t know what led to it, why it’s happening, is what concerns us. We can’t predict anything. Most other groups we partner with regularly share our concerns, but we haven’t been able to pull together enough information to make sense of these patterns…

“That’s partially why I’m desperate enough to take on your request,” Tyda said wryly. “If we were still battling back mindless monster attacks, I’d regretfully inform you we’re too busy to be able to take on this hunch of yours. If we had another lead, I’d give you the same answer. But since we are in a holding pattern at the moment…”

“I understand,” Aida said quickly, leaping to her feet to bow gratefully to the two of them. “I appreciate anything you can do.”

“Well! I’m glad that’s settled,” Yoon said cheerfully as she clapped her hands together. “Aida, are you free this morning? I’d like to ask you some more details about your visit with the nonpractitioner…”

“Of course.” Aida followed Yoon out of the spare room with a much lighter heart. Tyda and Yoon’s descriptions of what had been happening with the monsters was disheartening, but at least they agreed there was something worth investigating in Agnes’s and Ezra’s predicaments as they waited for new developments; she’d take whatever victory she could get.

“Are we going to the new Gullbeaks office?” Sue asked brightly as they all traipsed down the stairs.

“Yes, it would make most sense to,” Yoon said agreeably. “Have you brought Aida there yet?”

“Not yet!” Behind Aida, she could feel Sue give an energetic wiggle of her body at the prospect of showing Aida somewhere new.

“Ah, Suelina, since you’re here already, can you stay behind at the training yard? Kawa is picking up some new weapons for you to try, and he should be back soon,” Tyda said, stopping Sue from following Yoon and Aida out the front door.

Sue’s eyes widened. “Really? What ones?”

Tyda smirked. “You’ll see. We think you’ll like them.”

Sue looked back and forth between the Gullbeaks leader and her best friend, clearly torn between the decision to follow Aida as emotional support and getting to play with new firepower. Aida nodded encouragingly at Sue.

“You should stay. I think it’s in everyone’s best interests for you to find a better weapon than the greataxe.”

We need our Main Character geared up and ready to fight as soon as possible.

“Oh… okay! But come back after you’re done talking to Yoon, okay? I want your final opinion!” Sue snatched a quick hug from Aida before she skipped out into the training yard.

Aida didn’t think she was qualified to help choose a weapon for Sue when she clearly had more seasoned Adventurers invested in her already, but since Tyda and Yoon looked so amused at their exchanged she couldn’t help but acquiesce.

“Our brainstorming session shouldn’t take too long. I’ll pull some of our logistical staff together as well so that we can all get on the same leaf about things to keep any eye out for,” Yoon told Aida as they exited the old Headquarters. She sighed. “Since we expanded our branches, we might as well make use of them.”

“It’s better that you expanded, isn’t it?” Aida asked tentatively. Yoon’s disappointment when talking about the expansion was a little unexpected; she would have thought the Gullbeaks would all be excited about the growth. She could already see the second Gullbeaks building down the street; they had installed a large sign with the Gullbeaks logo on it. The structure itself looked quite new, as if it had been freshly built or remodeled.

“Oh yes, our new staff have been very helpful. Most of them were already helping us a lot, but now we just hired them in an official capacity; so in a way, this is perhaps the best way for us to give back to the community.” Yoon smiled at Aida, though she thought she could still detect an undercurrent of unease in the older woman.

Perhaps Yoon’s sentiments mirrored Aida’s own apprehension: she understood the Gullbeaks were overdue for a Headquarters that represented their prestige, but she was also apprehensive to see just how fancy they had gotten. Before, in their grungy old tavern that had now been converted to something of a barracks for their Adventurers, they had seemed grounded and approachable. But now, with the new facade and expanded staff members, all wearing blue armbands with the fierce-looking bird embroidered on the cloth, they seemed a bit…corporate.

Several people recognized Yoon and opened the door to allow them to enter first, murmuring greetings and keeping their gazes lowered.

Yoon sighed again as they passed through the doors, though she quickly hitched a smile back onto her face as she warmly greeted the groups of civilians who quickly bowed at the sight of her. Aida stared as she took in the sight of the “new” Gullbeaks Headquarters.

They were greeted by a long counter in the center of the room, divided into five receiving stations where a Gullbeaks employee sat listening attentively and taking notes as the supplicant across from them spoke. Beyond the counter, Aida spotted several more stations with signs designating them as offering different support services: WORK, RATIONS, and HEALING.

“Mimi, can you please join us,” Yoon called to a woman with graying hair who was overseeing the counter with a snaking line of nonpractitioners. “And bring George along as well.”

The older woman nodded tersely before turning to the two other women at the counter with her, giving them some final instructions.

“You have quite a crowd to deal with,” Aida commented as Yoon led her beyond the counter and up a flight of stairs to the second floor, which was much more quiet than the lobby but still hummed with activity.

The layout of the second floor struck Aida as some sort of war room. There were several maps lining the walls, each one color-coded with different metrics the Gullbeaks were tracking. In front of the maps were smaller desks that contained piles of reports that other Gullbeaks employees were sifting through as they updated the flags on their maps. Just like on the ground floor, the employees up here nodded to acknowledge Yoon before returning to their tasks, with only a few curious glances cast towards Aida.

In the middle of it all was a large table covered with another map, along with figurines.

“Yes, we’ve become something of a de facto job and community center for now. So many residents have been affected by the monster attacks that they can’t do their normal work, so we have been trying to help keep them occupied as we all try to ride through this unprecedented time.” Yoon paused briefly to look over the activity in the war room before leading Aida to a smaller room off to the side.

“Here, come get settled as we wait for Mimi and George to join us. Would you like anything to drink?”

“I—tea, I guess,” Aida said, settling into the chair Yoon had pulled out for her. The woman nodded as she stepped back outside.

Right before the door closed, Aida heard Yoon’s voice speak again.

“Ah, Dev. You’re here—perhaps you would be good for this discussion as well. Please join us.”
