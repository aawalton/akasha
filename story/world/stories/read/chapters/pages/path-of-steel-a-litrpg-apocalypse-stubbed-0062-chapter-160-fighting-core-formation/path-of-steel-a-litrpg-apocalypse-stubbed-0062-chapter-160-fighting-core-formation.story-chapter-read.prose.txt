As Gary expected, the merit points had slowed down after they left the vicinity of the labyrinth’s entrance. They’d also streamlined their battle plan, which worked out great.

After some quick calculations, Gary had realized that hunting Core Formation enemies was too risky and not worth it. They gave the same amount of points as five Birth of Auras, who were much easier to kill. Of course, the difficulty there lay in finding the opponents, but Gary’s squads had spread out and vastly increased their effectiveness. Asim’s imp, Edgar, flew overhead and scouted for enemies too, when he wasn’t harassed by the spirit birds of wizards.

Safety-wise, the combination of Gary’s minimap and group chat gave them a decisive advantage over their enemies, who wandered around like headless chickens. Goldie could see the mini-map and chat as well, if he gave her permission, and he had her on monitoring duty while he explored. She was surprisingly adept at it. She fired off instructions, keeping everyone spread out within a certain range around Gary. Gary himself was trying to stay off battles. The squads were earning him enough points already—his main task was to react in a heartbeat if they found something they couldn’t handle.

The only thing that worried him were the ethereal birds which occasionally passed overhead…

Merit Points: 760

Gary kept his eyes peeled as he made a turn. The path opened into a forty-foot-wide circular space containing the most bizarre tree he’d ever witnessed. It was short, less than nine feet tall, so it didn’t stick out of the grass walls. Its leafing was the oddest part—it only existed right on top of the tree and on its two branches, as if the tree wore a hat and gloves.

“Are those branches flexing?” Goldie wondered.

“I think that’s their natural shape. Let’s not get close. Asim and Cog had their asses kicked by one these trees, whatever that means,” Gary said.

“I’m sure we can take it!”

“Probably, but there’s no need.”

Muscle Tree, Level 26

Don’t be fooled by its name. This thing is alive through and through, and it will fight you if you get too close. Its sap tastes like shit.

Gary didn’t know why Quirk was salty with these trees, but he also didn’t care. He’d just wanted to look at one, and possibly take some sap, but its bark was dry and full of holes. This tree had been harvested not long ago.

“The poor thing,” Goldie said. “Can we pet it?”

“I’m pretty sure it will slap us if we get close.”

“No it won’t. Hello, Mr. Tree! We’re coming over to pet you, okay? Please don’t slap us!”

“It doesn’t have ears, Goldie.”

“Come on, Gary. Pretty please?”

The tree hadn’t reacted at all. Gary sighed and took a hesitant step towards it, ready to dodge at any time. He wasn’t ready enough. The leaf end of a branch struck him from the side, sending him flying into the grass wall. Gary gasped as a series of thorns dug shallow into his back. The strike had been near-instant.

“Wow, what a jerk,” Goldie said.

Yeah. These guys are dicks. They don’t feel pain when they’re harvested, and in fact, it’s healthy for them, otherwise the sap builds up until they explode. They still slap anyone who gets close, though. Zero gratitude.

Goldie snorted. “Let’s slap it back, Gary,” she said.

Before Gary could respond, he heard a faint boom, and a red flare exploded in the distant sky.

“Red means Core Formation over level 35,” Gary said. “Tell everyone to stay away from there, Goldie.”

“Doesn’t the flare mean that someone needs help?”

“Yeah, but not by us. We aren’t strong enough.”

“Okay,” Goldie said, jumping into the chat and firing off a series of directions for the groups. Gary had already ordered the summoned bosses to follow Goldie’s commands as if they were his own. Meanwhile, he noticed another flare in the sky. This one was blue.

“Blue means low Core Formation,” he said, licking his lips. “Goldie, direct everyone in that direction. Keep them spread out.”

“Ok,” she said, muttering something more under her breath while rescinding her previous orders and giving new ones. Gary rushed, leaping over grass walls in his way to arrive faster.

I’ve gathered almost eight hundred points already, and I’ve only lost two mercenaries. I know I have advantages, but this is almost too easy… What’s the enemy doing? Didn’t they organize at all?

I haven’t seen many flares either, so everybody must be winning…

“Watch out!” Goldie screamed, and Gary thunderstepped mid-air, barely dodging the mouth he’d almost fallen into. It had opened right in the middle of a grass path, where there was normal ground just a moment ago, and then the mouth snapped shut with green, thorny teeth. A deep, black hole stretched underneath. Gary landed to the side, sweating.

Grass Eater, Level 30

You’d think this guy eats grass, but nope! He eats you. Thankfully, it can only do that if you literally step on its mouth, which you almost did, you blind idiot. Anything chopped by those teeth falls a hundred feet into the Grass Eater’s throat, which then closes with sturdy thorns like those of a labyrinth wall. If you fall in there, good luck getting out.

“What a stupid trap,” Goldie said. “If you weren’t distracted, it would be so easy to dodge.”

“Unless I was fighting something or getting chased,” Gary said, eyeing the dirt around him with newfound wariness. Would their description pop up while camouflaged? he wondered, and the answer was so obvious he didn’t even ask Quirk.

He resumed to the site of the flare, keeping his attention on his surroundings this time, and actually ran into another Grass Eater. He ignored it and kept running.

We’re getting deeper. There weren’t traps before, he thought, messaging everyone to be careful.

He’d expected a second flare to help him pinpoint the position. When none came, he jumped high. He found them. Gary landed near the flare’s origin, observing the bodies underneath. Three witches lay dead, their eyes opened forever in terror. All had their throats slit.

“This was a slaughter,” Goldie said.

“It was a miracle they had time to shoot the flare,” Gary said. Leaning down, he gently closed their eyes, then stood and sighed.

“Whoever did this could still be around,” Goldie warned.

“I know. We’re leaving,” Gary said. He prepared to leap.

Alicia: Gary, help! Core Formation!

His eyes widened. Coming, he messaged the group chat, then launched off the ground. “Thirty degrees to your right!” Goldie said, and Gary thunderstepped mid-air to change directions. He landed on a path and leapt again.

A red flare exploded far ahead.

“It wasn’t them,” Goldie said quickly. “The flare is too far away.”

“Thank God,” Gary replied. He saw them after two more leaps—Alicia and Delilah running desperately away from a wizard. Two imps flew at him but were sliced to tiny cubes by a mesh of energy. The wizard, who seemed in his late twenties, floated after them on a blue flying disk that zoomed around the labyrinth corners like a race car. He was far faster than them.

The wizard entered the same corridor as the girls. A blue beam crackled, searing the wall around them. It made them seem as if moving in slow motion. Alicia was in the beam’s way.

“No!” Gary roared.

A creaking sound reach him. A cheerful figure rounded the corner, whistling a merry tune as it used both hands to push a cart overflowing with human hearts. Its hammer had already been thrown, spinning over itself to crash into the beam, which shattered as if its light was something physical. The hammer then buried itself in the grass wall.

“More points,” the wizard said, pushing his palms forward. A blue shockwave spread across the path, razing the dirt and grass walls. Pebbles flew. The leaves shook violently. The shockwave reached the cart, upturning it and throwing hearts everywhere, including over the walls. The jester crossed his hands. The shockwave crashed and dissipated against him, revealing the jester still standing, a line of blood leaking from his mouth. His arms unfolded. He glanced at his cart, and though his smile didn’t budge, a vibration of pure rage erupted under his heavily made-up face.

“Take my—” the wizard said as Gary fell on him like a meteor. Black lightning sparked. A boom came with the impact, raising dirt everywhere and shaking the walls. When the dust cleared, the wizard floated off to the side, a cracked blue shield emerging from his raised hand. His hair floated to the ground.

“You’ll pay for this,” he said, teeth gnashing.

“That’s my line,” Gary said, aiming his spear at the wizard. “Thunder Step!”

He dove in, spear flashing as it moved at blinding speed. The wizard resisted. Blue shields rose one after another, as quickly as Gary could break them.

“Cubic Annihilation!” the wizard roared, unleashing a blue mesh on Gary, who’d already seen what it could do. He jumped high, over the walls to dodge it, only to find a blue beam already heading his way. He tried to block. It broke through his guard, sending him spinning into the top of a wall. Thorns dug into him. He gasped.

Gary got up just in time to see the jester peeling its hammer free from the wall and rushing the wizard. Three finger-flicks threw three star-like projectiles at the jester, exploding when they got near despite being dodged. The jester was flung outside a field of smoke. Gary fell from above, landing behind the wizard.

“Nail Gun!” he roared, unleashing a hail of thrusts. The shattered, glass-like energy of the wizard’s shield was tinted red by a flare somewhere behind him. Gary pressed on, not giving his opponent time to strike back. The jester had gotten up, hobbling towards the wizard’s back with his hammer raised. The wizard noticed. He extended his hands in both directions, raising waves of shields.

“Impregnable Corridor!” he yelled. Then, in the middle of his open palms, blue light shone. “Lance of Light!”

Gary had no time to dodge. He brought his spear before himself and prepared to take the blow. Suddenly, an imp fell on the wizard from above, scratching furiously at his face while yelling obscenities. The wizard screamed. He abandoned his attack to shield his eyes, but that’s when the jester hammered the shields on his side. They shattered. Gary followed a beat later, thrusting his way through the energy shields. The wizard had nowhere to go. His disk flew up, but blinded as he was, he had no way to see Gary’s spear coming for his back. It emerged from his stomach. The wizard gurgled.

The jester landed on the platform then, grinning wider at the wizard, then jabbing his fingers into the man’s chest and fishing out a beating, bloody heart. The imp flew away, revealing a face etched with disbelief, which morphed into horror when he saw the jester so close to him. He tried to move, but all he achieved was to fall on his back on the disk. The jester leaned after him.

“Your heart…is mine…” he whispered in the wizard’s ear, showing him the heart. The wizard’s blood stopped flowing.

The disk under him lost its floating powers, depositing both jester and wizard on the ground. The wizard lay there, eyes open in death, while the jester whistled in glee. He walked to his cart, flipped it upright, then dropped the heart inside and wiped his hand with a handkerchief from his chest pocket. Then, still whistling, he set to gathering the other hearts strewn all over the corridor.

“Where did you find this guy?” Goldie asked.

“I killed him on the first floor…” Gary replied. The sight of the jester cleaning his hand was horrifically familiar.

“Well, kill him again. He’s creepy!”

“I know, Goldie…”

Realizing that the wizard’s disk hadn’t disappeared, he walked over. It shrunk before his very eyes, becoming the size of a dinner plate. It was shaped just like a flying saucer.

“Huh,” Gary said.

Disk of Hovering, Rare

A hovering treasure which can be powered even by Core Formation climbers. Allows the user to glide on the battlefield rather than walking. Especially helpful while casting spells that require staying still. Speed depends on the density of one’s aura.

“Interesting,” Gary said, picking up the plate. That line about casting spells made him think it was an item meant for wizards—which made sense, given who had been using it.

Chloe might like this.

Gary stashed the disk into his space ring. He couldn’t use it, anyway. He also got the ring on the wizard’s finger, transferring the two thousand merit stones it contained to his own. Besides that and a few changes of blue robes, the ring was empty.

He discovered that the ring couldn’t be placed inside his space ring.

“Gary! Thank you!” Alicia cried out, rushing towards him.

“No problem,” he replied, tossing her the new space ring. She caught it, panicking. “What— Are you giving this to me? Gary! It’s expensive!”

He laughed. “It’s okay. I already got one.”

Delilah, who was also rushing, grabbed his hand.

“I thank you too. Now add me to the group chat,” she said.

Still shaken, Gary opened his chat, finding more messages than he expected. It wasn’t just his group chat, either.

War announcement: Field Bosses, listen up! We have successfully isolated the Kamowaga Gym with radioactive waste. The resistance we faced was minimal. This is a great step for our freedom!

Nobody had mentioned this in his group chat, and he didn’t remember getting something similar during the first mission, so he assumed that war notifications were only sent to field bosses like himself.

Beef Seven: Someone’s here! It’s

Gary raised his brow. The message had been cut off. That could only mean one thing.

Osteo: Boss, we found a Core Formation sector boss. It was busy chasing someone else, but be careful! It looked like a frog!

Asim: I see red flares, Gary. I see them in all directions. Something’s wrong.
