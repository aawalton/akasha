[A village has reported the appearance of a “talking beast” that steals food, insults the villagers, and appears only at night. Although the villagers have managed to fend it off several times, the creature continues to return and cause trouble.

Investigate the matter and, if possible, capture the beast.

Reward: 75 Spirit Stones for completing the investigation; 200 Spirit Stones if the beast is captured and presented.]

The mission itself sounded simple, basically the kind of beginner “investigate the suspicious activity” quest that every RPG threw at you.

Under normal circumstances, Ning would have dismissed most of the villagers' claims as exaggerations, but the part about the beast being able to speak caught his attention. In the cultivation world, speech was not an ability ordinary beasts possessed.

Demonic beasts normally needed to reach the Purple Mansion stage before they gained the ability to speak, and Ning doubted anything at that level would casually raid a village this close to the Pure Qi Sect. The sect had a Golden Core cultivator at its helm, and that reputation alone was enough to keep most powerful demonic beasts away from the surrounding territory.

There was another possibility. A beast with an exceptionally powerful bloodline might awaken certain abilities far earlier than normal, and considering the villagers had managed to fend this one off themselves, that seemed far more likely.

With that possibility in mind, Ning arrived at the village early to investigate.

A large tree with yellow leaves stood near the center of the settlement, which, according to the mission records, was where Yellow Tree Village had gotten its straightforward name. The village itself was equally ordinary, consisting mostly of simple wooden houses and farmland, while the strongest cultivator Ning could sense was an elderly man at the Second Stage of Qi Condensation. His qi was already thin with age, and everyone else appeared to be an ordinary mortal.

The moment Ning entered the village, several people noticed his Pure Qi Sect robes and hurried toward him.

“Immortal, you're finally here! Please save us!”

“That beast has been tormenting us for days!”

Before Ning could get a word in, the elderly cultivator raised his hand. “Enough. You're all speaking at once.”

The villagers reluctantly quieted down, and the old man turned toward Ning. “We are grateful that the sect sent someone. This old man is Gu Ping. I was the one who submitted the mission.”

“Can you tell me exactly what has been happening?” Ning asked.

That was apparently all the permission the villagers needed.

“It keeps stealing food from the granary,” one man said.

“And it only comes at night,” another quickly added. “It shouts at people too!”

“It argues with us!”

“It insults people for no reason!”

One particularly offended villager pushed his way forward. “It called me useless!”

“That's nothing,” another man said. “It called Old Zhang bald!”

Old Zhang, who was indeed bald, looked much more offended by this than the repeated theft of their food.

Ning listened without interrupting. A talking beast that raided granaries, picked arguments with villagers, and had strong opinions about their appearances was not quite what he had expected when he accepted the mission.

“Where do you usually encounter it?”

“The granary, Immortal. It always comes back there.”

“All right. Take me there.”

Gu Ping led him through the village until they reached a large wooden building near the edge of the settlement. Like everything else Ning had seen so far, the granary looked completely ordinary, though that didn't tell him much.

Ning walked inside and activated Pure Eyes before beginning his inspection. He checked the floor, support beams, crates, and the spaces between them, looking for tracks, damaged wood, scraps of fur, traces of qi, or anything else the creature might have left behind during its visits.

After searching for a while, Ning paused beside a stack of wooden boxes, where several faint claw marks had been scratched into the wood. He crouched to examine them more closely and quickly noticed that the marks were shallow and uneven, with very little force behind them.

“Not large,” he muttered, checking the distance between the scratches. “Small claws, and not much weight behind them. Whatever made these shouldn't be much bigger than a small beast.”

Ning stood and looked around the granary again, but even with Pure Eyes active, he couldn't detect the slightest trace of demonic qi.

“Either it can conceal its aura, or we're dealing with something else.”

He continued searching, his metaphorical detective hat firmly in place, though there wasn't much evidence to work with.

The villagers watching from outside gradually relaxed as they saw how seriously Ning was taking the investigation. He looked quite young for an immortal of the Pure Qi Sect, but none of them dared question someone sent by the sect.

“Alas, it's a pity the Yellow Tree Spirit didn't take action,” one of the younger villagers said.

“How can you say that?” The woman beside him, likely his mother, immediately chastised him.

Ning heard them but didn't pay much attention. He had already examined the yellow tree with Pure Eyes when he entered the village and found nothing unusual about it.

Still, the villagers' belief wasn't that strange. Spiritual energy permeated this world, and given enough time and the right circumstances, even ordinary plants and stones could develop extraordinary properties. Villages worshipping ancient trees, unusual stones, or other objects as guardians was an old tradition with roots stretching back thousands of years.

According to one popular tale, an ancient village had once been attacked by a beast over a hundred feet tall. The villagers had been powerless to resist, only for the ten-thousand-year-old tree they worshipped to manifest great power and drive the creature away. The story claimed that generations of faith and worship had granted the tree spirituality, though cultivators generally believed the tree must have already been extraordinary if it had truly survived for ten thousand years.

Whatever the truth of the story, the custom had endured. Trees and stones were the most commonly worshipped objects, partly because of that ancient tale and partly because they could exist for centuries or even millennia, giving them plenty of time to accumulate spirituality.

Such practices weren't limited to mortal villages either. Some cultivation sects maintained similar traditions, and the Pure Qi Sect itself was said to have a guardian beast enshrined somewhere within the sect.

The Yellow Tree, however, was just a tree.

Ning could also rule out ghosts and similar entities since he hadn't detected any yin qi, which left him with very few useful leads.

“Would the immortal like to rest for a while?” Gu Ping asked. It was still morning, and according to the villagers, the creature rarely appeared before nightfall.

“There's no need. I'll keep watch until it appears,” Ning replied. “You can all continue with your work. Since I've taken the mission, I'll see it through.”

“Just as we thought, the immortals of the sect are indeed kind.”

Several villagers bowed gratefully, apparently believing Ning was sacrificing his entire day out of concern for them. In a way, they weren't wrong, though Ning was equally concerned about missing the beast and turning a one-day mission into a two-day mission.

For the rest of the day, Ning used the Turtle Breathing Technique to conceal his presence and found a suitable hiding place near the granary, close enough to intervene immediately while still leaving himself enough room to retreat if the supposedly harmless talking beast turned out to be anything other than harmless.

Night eventually fell, and the village gradually became quiet. Hours passed without anything happening, and by one in the morning, Ning was beginning to wonder whether the creature had chosen this particular night to ruin his plans.

“Hmm... perhaps it isn't coming today.”

He waited a little longer before deciding there was no harm in trying an ancient and reliable method.

“It's awfully quiet tonight.”

There. The flag had been raised.

Whether his luck was actually good or the cliché had somehow worked, Ning soon sensed something approaching the granary. He immediately focused on the presence, only for his caution to turn into confusion when he realized just how weak it was.

The aura was barely distinguishable from that of an ordinary animal, which made no sense for a beast supposedly capable of speech. Even a weak spirit beast should have possessed some degree of spiritual presence.

Ning remained hidden and waited to see what would appear.

A small green bird with a red beak slipped into the granary and landed near one of the grain sacks.

“OLD! OLD!”

Ning blinked when he finally got a clear look at it.

It was a parrot.

For a moment, he wondered if there was something unusual about the bird that he had missed, but Pure Eyes still showed nothing beyond what he had already sensed. No demonic qi, no spiritual bloodline, not even the faint aura of a spirit beast. It really was just an ordinary parrot.

The bird noticed him soon afterward and immediately tried to fly away. Ning used Shadow Steps to catch up and grabbed it before it could make it out of the granary.

He looked at the struggling bird in his hand, still half-expecting there to be more to the situation.

“So this is the talking beast?”

“That's it! That's it!” the parrot immediately replied.

Ning took a breath.

Well, the villagers hadn't lied.

Several villagers who had noticed the commotion cautiously approached the granary and peeked inside. The moment they saw the bird in Ning's hand and heard it speak, their earlier fear immediately returned.

“It talks!”

“It really talks! It must be a demon!”

The parrot turned toward them and began repeating the loudest words it heard.

“Demon! Demon!”

Then it noticed Old Zhang.

“Bald!”

Old Zhang's face darkened immediately.

By this point, Ning had already figured out what had happened. The villagers had encountered an unfamiliar animal capable of imitating human speech and, having no idea such creatures existed, naturally assumed it was some strange demonic beast.

In reality, it was simply an ordinary parrot that had found an easy source of food and picked up some rather unfortunate vocabulary along the way.

The villagers had yet to realize that, however, and seeing the troublesome beast safely restrained in the hands of a sect cultivator was enough to send them into celebration.

“The demon has been captured!”

“As expected of the immortal's might!”

Old Zhang pushed his way forward and pointed furiously at the bird. “Bald? I have hair in my heart, you stupid beast!”

“Bald!”

Old Zhang looked ready to fight the parrot personally.

Once everyone had calmed down somewhat, Gu Ping cautiously approached Ning and examined the bird still struggling in his grasp.

“So... it's not a demon?”

“No.”

“But it can talk?”

“It can imitate human speech,” Ning explained. “Some ordinary animals have that ability, though they don't necessarily understand the words they're repeating.”

The villagers exchanged confused looks. An ordinary animal capable of imitating human speech was something none of them had ever encountered before.

Still, with Ning standing before them and the supposedly terrifying demon struggling helplessly in his hand, nobody seemed particularly eager to argue.

The parrot puffed up its feathers.

“Bald! Bald! Bald!”

Old Zhang's shoulders slumped.

“Then why does it only insult me?” he asked weakly.

Ning looked at him and thought about the question seriously for a moment. Old Zhang's bald head was rather noticeable, especially whenever light struck it, so the answer wasn't difficult to figure out.

“Probably because you're the easiest to remember.”

Old Zhang fell silent.

That somehow made it worse.

...

Explaining the entire situation had been… something, especially the part where Ning had to convince everyone that he hadn't simply grabbed a random parrot and declared it the target of the mission.

Fortunately, Si Sihua was the one who handled his report and verified that the mission had indeed been completed. Watching her expression change as Ning explained how a supposedly mysterious talking beast had turned out to be an ordinary parrot was almost worth the trouble.

The reward certainly was.

Two hundred spirit stones for catching a parrot.

Ning wasn't very concerned about whether the sect had expected something more impressive when they offered two hundred spirit stones for the capture of a “rare beast.” Strange missions appeared often enough that this probably wouldn't even be the most ridiculous report Si Sihua received that month.

Ning had expected the rest of his day to be uneventful, but shortly after leaving, his communication talisman activated with a message from Xiao Fan.

By the time Ning arrived, Xiao Fan was already waiting beneath a tree with his white fox beside him.

“You sure took your time. I've been waiting for a while.”

“Sorry. I was finishing a mission,” Ning replied. “I came over as soon as I got your message.”

Xiao Fan nodded, but Ning's attention had already moved elsewhere.

A white raven was perched on one of the nearby branches. Its feathers were almost completely white, and it occasionally glanced at Ning without showing much interest.

“Is this the spiritual beast you found for me?” Ning asked.

“Yes. I tried to find an egg as you requested, but suitable spiritual beast eggs are rarer than I expected.” Xiao Fan glanced toward the raven. “I found something better instead.”

The raven tilted its head and studied Ning in return.

“So, are you going to introduce us?”

Xiao Fan stood up. “It's a White Flame Raven, a fire-attributed spiritual beast. Ravens are naturally intelligent, and this one can produce the white flame its species is named after.”

That immediately caught Ning's interest.

“A special flame? That's perfect for alchemy.” He looked at Xiao Fan with appreciation. “As expected of you. I knew asking you was the right choice.”

“It was nothing.”

The raven suddenly let out a sharp cry from above.

Xiao Fan coughed. “Well, there is one thing.”

Ning looked back at him.

“It has conditions. The raven wants a strong master, so it won't follow you unless you can defeat it first.”

“That makes sense.” Ning nodded. “You don't get a Pokémon without beating it first. Classic.”

Xiao Fan paused. “…What?”

“Nothing.”

Xiao Fan shrugged and turned toward the raven. “This is the person I told you about. He's agreed to fight.”

The White Flame Raven looked Ning over before giving a slow nod.

...

They found an open field nearby for the fight.

The White Flame Raven remained on the ground at first, watching Ning as white flames gradually spread across its feathers. Once the flames had stabilized, it spread its wings and launched itself forward.

Ning's Pure Eyes activated, turning his eyes bright blue as the raven rapidly closed the distance. He used Shadow Steps just before it reached him, moving aside as the bird swept past with enough heat to make the air around him warmer.

The raven turned sharply and attacked again, approaching lower this time before pulling its wings close and diving straight at him.

Ning watched it approach and once again relied on the fifth move available to every anime character when confronted with a problem.

He dodged.

The raven tried again, only for Ning to evade it just as easily. By the fourth attempt, the White Flame Raven was clearly becoming irritated.

Xiao Fan, meanwhile, was sitting comfortably some distance away and eating the jerky Ning had given him. It was rare for him to be the one relaxing while someone else dealt with a troublesome beast, so he was enjoying the opportunity.

The white fox beside him watched another exchange before nudging his arm and barking.

Xiao Fan had lived with her long enough to understand what she wanted despite the lack of words.

“You're wondering why he keeps dodging?”

The fox nodded.

“Worried about him?”

A burst of cold air struck Xiao Fan in the face, leaving a thin layer of frost across his hair.

He brushed it off without much concern. This was hardly the first time she had done that.

“You really do like him. Was it the food?”

The fox gave him an unimpressed look.

Ning had made a habit of giving her various ice-attributed snacks whenever they met, and that had been enough to earn her favor. Xiao Fan had also fed her plenty over the year, but unfortunately, most of his cooking experience came from roasting the beasts that regularly tried to kill him.

“There's nothing wrong with barbecue,” Xiao Fan muttered.

The fox snorted, earning a slightly offended look from him.

“There isn't,” he insisted, but another snort convinced him to abandon an argument he had already lost several times.

“As for Ning, he's fine. He's watching the raven.”

The fox looked back toward the fight.

“He hasn't fought it before, so he's checking how fast it is, how it turns, and what that white flame can actually do before he makes a move.” Xiao Fan took another bite of jerky. “The raven thinks it's testing whether Ning is strong enough to be its master.”

He watched as the White Flame Raven swept past Ning once again, only for Ning to avoid it by a narrow margin.

“It hasn't realized Ning is testing it too.”

...

On the field, the White Flame Raven grew increasingly aggressive as Ning continued to evade its attacks. It was fast in the air, changing direction with a few powerful beats of its wings before opening its beak and spewing another stream of white flame toward him.

Ning stepped aside with Shadow Steps, and the flames swept across the grass where he had been standing, leaving a scorched trail behind.

The raven switched tactics, spitting several smaller bursts of white flame in quick succession. Ning slipped between them with Shadow Steps, still making no attempt to counterattack.

With Pure Eyes active, Ning studied the raven's movements and the way it controlled its flames. Its acceleration was impressive, especially over short distances, while its white flame was far more concentrated than ordinary fire, allowing it to attack with surprising accuracy.

However, he was beginning to notice a pattern. Whenever Ning moved outside its immediate range, the raven immediately gave chase, and at full speed, it needed wider turns to change direction, leaving brief openings he could exploit. The more attacks he avoided, the less patient the raven became.

The White Flame Raven let out an irritated caw, beat its wings harder, and opened its beak. A much larger mass of white flame gathered within before erupting outward.

Ning's expression grew more serious as the flames spread across a much wider area than the previous attacks. Dodging to either side would still leave him within their reach, so he immediately used Shadow Steps to retreat beyond the edge of the blast.

The raven had seemingly expected that.

It shot upward and flew after him, opening its beak for another attack before Ning had completely regained his footing.

Another stream of white flame descended from above.

Ning twisted aside, feeling the heat pass close enough to scorch the edge of his sleeve.

That was enough.

Ning drew his bow from his storage pouch and fired just as the raven turned for another attack. The first arrow passed directly across its flight path, forcing it to pull back before it could release another flame.

The second arrow followed immediately, making the raven twist aside, only for Ning to fire a third before it could regain its balance.

This time, the arrow came from the direction it had just turned toward, forcing it lower. Before it could recover, another arrow passed beneath its wings and denied that route as well.

The White Flame Raven cried out angrily and spat a burst of flame toward Ning, but the distance gave him plenty of time to move aside. Another arrow was already nocked by the time the flames passed him.

He fired an arrow beneath the raven, forcing it to climb, only for the next shot to cut off its ascent. When it attempted to escape through another opening, Ning's arrow passed directly through the space it was about to enter.

By then, the raven finally seemed to understand what was happening. Ning had barely tried to hit it since drawing his bow. Every arrow had been aimed at restricting its movements.

The White Flame Raven beat its wings furiously and opened its beak, sending several bursts of white flame toward him in rapid succession. Ning moved between them with Shadow Steps while continuing to shoot, each arrow forcing the raven farther toward the part of the field he wanted it to occupy.

Its speed meant little when Ning was already anticipating its movements. Each time the raven tried to climb or turn away, another arrow cut off its escape, forcing it lower until it attempted to rush past him near the ground.

An arrow struck the earth directly ahead of it, making the raven pull back sharply. For the first time since the fight began, it stopped attacking.

Ning drew another arrow and aimed directly at it.

The White Flame Raven immediately searched for somewhere to move, but the previous exchange had pushed it too low and too close to the edge of the field. Rising would expose it before it could accelerate, moving sideways would take it directly into Ning's line of fire, and at this distance, trying to attack first would give Ning more than enough time to release the arrow.

White flame gathered inside its beak, but when Ning pulled the bowstring a little farther, the raven hesitated. The flame gradually faded, and after several seconds of silence, it lowered its head and descended to the ground.

Ning lowered his bow with a satisfied nod. “Good.”

The White Flame Raven had performed better than he'd expected. It was fast, intelligent, and its flame was remarkably powerful for such a young spiritual beast. Most of its weaknesses came from inexperience and its tendency to become impatient once an opponent refused to fight the way it wanted.

Those could be fixed.

The raven looked at Ning for a while before walking closer and nudging his hand with its head.

“All right, all right.”

Ning put away his bow and took out a Beast-Raising Pill he had prepared beforehand.

“Here. This will help your cultivation.” He held the pill out and gently patted the raven's head. “Follow me and you won't lack resources. We'll get stronger together.”

Having already accepted its defeat, the White Flame Raven swallowed the pill without hesitation. Warm medicinal energy spread through its body, and the bird closed its eyes as it absorbed the unfamiliar power before letting out a pleased caw.

Ning smiled. Strength first, benefits afterward. The classic carrot-and-stick approach.

“It seems you two are getting along well,” Xiao Fan said as he approached.

“In this world, there's nothing that brings two beings together faster than beating each other up first.”

Xiao Fan decided not to comment on that.

Ning suddenly remembered something and reached into his storage pouch before tossing a small bundle toward him.

Xiao Fan caught it instinctively and opened it.

Several beast cores rested inside.

“This is your reward,” Ning said. He already had a decent idea of what Xiao Fan would find useful, and these beast cores had come from Ling Xin, so their quality was exceptionally good. “It was nice working with you.”

Looking at the beast cores, Xiao Fan felt that everything he'd gone through might actually have been worth it.

Finding the White Flame Raven hadn't exactly been simple. First, Xiao Fan had stumbled into a bandit ambush, and after dealing with them, he'd discovered clues pointing toward a hidden treasure.

Following those clues had brought him to a confrontation between two powerful beasts, one of which happened to be the parent of the young White Flame Raven. Naturally, Xiao Fan had taken the treasure while they were distracted, only to get dragged into the resulting conflict.

Somehow, he'd ended up fighting alongside the adult White Flame Raven against the other beast, even helping protect its offspring during the battle. Apparently, that had been enough to earn the parent's trust, because afterward, it had entrusted the young raven to him.

Xiao Fan looked down at the beast cores again.

“Then I'll accept them.”

He truly felt that Ning was one of the better quest-givers he had encountered. There was no arguing over the reward and no sudden revelation that he needed to complete three additional tasks before receiving what had been promised.

For once, Xiao Fan actually felt like a normal disciple.
