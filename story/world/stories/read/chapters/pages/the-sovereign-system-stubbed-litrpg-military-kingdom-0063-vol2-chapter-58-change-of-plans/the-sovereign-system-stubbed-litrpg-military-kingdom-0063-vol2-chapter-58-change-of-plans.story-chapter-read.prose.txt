Silence blanketed the Jade Silk Pavilion as Qin Hu stepped onto the stage, her movements graceful and practiced. She settled upon a simple stool at its center, cradling a huqin in her hands with the ease of one who had mastered it over a lifetime.

A hushed anticipation hung in the air. All eyes were on her as the elegant fairy lifted her bowstring in preparation. Even before a single note rang out, she had captivated the entire room—such was the weight of her presence.

Then the music began.

The melody was soft, almost mournful, each note steeped in emotion. It tugged at the heart, stirring something deep within those who listened. In a place like this—meant for pleasure and distraction—such sorrow should have felt out of place.

And yet, no one dared to interrupt.

Qin Hu parted her lips and began to sing. Her voice, clear and haunting, slipped past the ears and nestled into the soul. It was as though her song bypassed language altogether, resonating with the essence of being human.

Even Luke faltered, his thoughts silenced by the melody.

The lyrics told a story—of her mother, of grief and absence, of a longing that never dulled. But her words were more than just personal. They were universal. Replace “mother” with “wife,” “husband,” “sibling,” or “child,” and the ache remained just as sharp.

For a moment, Luke could do nothing but listen, spellbound.

Then something stirred within him—a flicker of understanding.

I miss my own family back on Earth…

The thought came unbidden, catching him off guard. But thanks to his Dragon’s Heart skill, he was able to recognize the emotion for what it was. Still, this wasn’t the time for reflection. He forced himself to push the feeling aside.

His gaze drifted back to Hilda. She remained vigilant, performing her role as bodyguard, eyes sweeping the crowd with practiced efficiency. Beside her stood the man who had introduced the performance—a towering presence with a broad chest and arms folded tightly across his robes. Despite his imposing build, there was a softness in his gaze as he watched the stage.

He looked like a proud father watching his daughter shine.

Luke’s heart sank.

Could that really be Fairy Qin’s father? Then…

A shiver ran down his spine as the thought finished forming.

That man… he’s the owner of the Jade Silk Pavilion…

Or more accurately—the leader of the Black Tiger Gang.

The same man who had tried to have him killed during his first week in the capital. Luke had barely survived that assassination attempt. He would’ve died returning from the palace if Victoria hadn’t intervened.

This complicates things…

Luke clenched his jaw, glancing toward Hilda. He had only a narrow window to approach her—she’d likely vanish alongside Fairy Qin once the performance ended. But with this man nearby, even exchanging words would be a dangerous gamble.

His mind churned, searching for an answer.

Then, as if pulled by an invisible thread, the bald head of Ming Hu turned in his direction. Their eyes locked.

A wave of pressure crashed into Luke.

He felt it instantly—the suffocating air of latent violence, coiled like a beast beneath the man’s skin. The smile that played at Ming Hu’s lips didn’t reach his eyes. It was not a greeting. It was a warning.

The man was like a sheathed sword—silent, but ready to be drawn at a moment’s notice.

Luke cursed under his breath and tried to avert his gaze. But it was no use. The large man’s stare bore into the side of his face, unwavering. He hadn’t done anything wrong, but catching the attention of the big boss—now, of all times—was the last thing he needed.

After a beat of hesitation, he made up his mind.

“Wait here,” Luke said to the prince beside him.

Lyon didn’t even respond. His eyes remained locked on Qin Hu, utterly spellbound. Luke let out a quiet grunt of annoyance but didn’t waste time repeating himself.

He wove his way through the crowd of silent onlookers, careful not to jostle anyone. The music still hung in the air, delicate and full of longing, yet Luke’s focus was elsewhere. Even as his small frame disappeared into the sea of bodies, he could feel it—that unwavering gaze.

Like a spotlight tracking his every move.

Luke, you crazy bastard… you’re going to get yourself killed, he thought bitterly, fully aware of the risk he was taking.

Moments later, he slipped past the last row and emerged into the narrow space between the audience and the side entrance of the stage. Two imposing figures stood guard, both locking their eyes on him the instant he appeared.

One of them stepped forward.

It was Hilda.

She moved with practiced precision, her towering frame positioning itself between Luke and the stage, cutting off any path to Qin Hu. Her hand rested on the hilt of her sword, fingers flexing slightly—ready to draw in an instant.

“This is a restricted area,” she said, voice low and gravelly. “Return to the crowd, or you will be cut down where you stand.”

Luke clicked his tongue. “Tch, we don’t see each other for a few weeks and you’ve already forgotten me, Hilda?”

He deepened his voice, adding a gruff accent to disguise it. The strain tickled his throat, and it took every ounce of willpower not to cough afterward.

Hilda’s grip on her sword only tightened.

“I do not know you, little one,” she said coldly. “This is your final warning. Leave now—or face my blade.”

Luke’s brow twitched at the insult. Little one? He nearly fired back with something vulgar but forced himself to hold it in. He was incognito, after all. Only a few subtle adjustments had been made to his appearance—enough to fool strangers, but not those who knew him well.

He sighed, running a hand through his hair in mock embarrassment.

“Sebby would be quite disappointed if he found out you turned me away…”

That did the trick.

At the mention of Sebastian, Hilda’s body visibly tensed. She leaned in slightly, squinting to get a better look. Even in the dim glow of the lanterns, Luke saw her expression shift—recognition flickering in her eyes, quickly followed by confusion.

“Luke—?”

“Yes, yes,” he interrupted, offering a small bow. “It is I… Lucas Draven.”

His heart thundered in his chest as he held her gaze, subtly gesturing with his fingers to go along with it.

A pause.

Then—thankfully—Hilda caught on.

“Hilda, who is this man?”

A deep voice cut clean through the music, sharp enough to silence thought. Ming Hu stepped forward, his presence like a boulder rolling into the space beside Hilda. Though only slightly taller than her, he radiated a far greater menace.

Before she could answer, Luke stepped in. He cupped his fist in a respectful greeting.“I am Lucas Draven. We haven’t met before, but I believe you’ve profited quite handsomely off my work recently.”

The rasp in his voice projected confidence, but inside, Luke was anything but calm. His muscles were tense, and a quick glance upward revealed Ming Hu studying him intently, one hand stroking his spiked beard in thought.

Then, without warning, a wide grin stretched across the gang leader’s face, flashing two perfect rows of teeth.“So it is you—my golden goose,” he declared, breaking into a guffaw that echoed through the pavilion.

He slung an arm over Hilda’s broad shoulders and leaned on her as if they were old drinking companions.“You should’ve told me your friend was coming, Hilda. I’ve been wanting to meet him for quite some time.”

Hilda raised a brow, shooting Luke a quick glance before brushing the arm off her shoulder.“This was a surprise for me as well. I barely recognized him,” she replied coolly, stepping aside to create some space between them.

“What brings you here, Lucas?” she asked, pointedly using the false name.

“Oh, nothing much,” Luke said with a dismissive wave. “Just wanted to touch base with my business partners. I’m also working on a new product to sell alongside the perfume. Should be just as profitable.”

Ming Hu’s eyes lit up like lanterns in the dark. His grin widened, and he let out another belly laugh—this one loud enough to turn several heads away from the performance.

“Wonderful! You’ve come at an auspicious time, my friend.” He clapped a heavy hand onto Luke’s shoulder. The weight of it nearly made him stumble.

This bastard is strong… Luke gritted his teeth, smiling through the discomfort.

“Now, now—there’s plenty of time for business,” Luke said quickly, forcing a strained grin. “The night is still young. Let me enjoy the atmosphere of your fine establishment before we talk numbers.”

Ming Hu let out a final laugh and gave Luke’s shoulder a few hearty slaps.“Yes! You are right. One must savor life’s pleasures!”

He reached into his pristine white robes and pulled out a bronze token, holding it out between two fingers.“Here—take this. Show it to any of the staff, and they’ll take care of you,” he said with an exaggerated wink.

Luke accepted the token with a nod. It was heavier than expected.

Etched into the bronze was the snarling head of a tiger, its exaggerated fangs jutting out from the surface. The craftsmanship was exquisite—beautiful, but unsettling.

“I’ll be waiting upstairs,” Ming Hu said, his voice turning firm. “You have two hours.”

There was no room for negotiation in that tone.

Without waiting for a reply, Ming Hu cast one final glance toward Fairy Qin on the stage, then turned and ascended the staircase to the second level, leaving Luke and Hilda standing in silence.

The moment the man’s imposing figure disappeared from view, Luke felt the strength drain from his limbs. A wave of exhaustion crashed over him, heavier than anything he'd felt on the campaign trail. Weeks without proper rest or nutrition had already worn him thin—but those few tense minutes in Ming Hu’s presence had sapped what little energy remained.

Before he could collect himself, Hilda stepped in front of him, towering over him like a storm cloud ready to break. Her expression was hard, her voice a sharp whisper edged with fury.

“What the hell are you doing, Luke!?” she hissed. Her body was taut, coiled like a spring. For a moment, it looked like she might strike him on the spot. “I thought we agreed—you weren’t going to get involved with the Black Tiger Gang!”

“Things have changed, Hilda…” he said, voice low but resolute. “I can’t tell you everything right now, but... you’ll probably hear about my “heroic death” in the coming days.”

Her eyes widened.

“I need you to find me a place to stay. Somewhere off the books. And send a message to Sebastian and Kayson for me. We don’t have much time.”

“Huh!?” Hilda blinked in disbelief, her brows scrunching together. For a second, it looked like she couldn’t decide whether he was playing a cruel joke or speaking the truth.

Just then, the huqin’s last note faded, the final stroke reverberating through the air like a whispered goodbye. The moment of silence was shattered by thunderous applause, loud enough to make Luke’s ears ring. The entire Jade Silk Pavilion seemed to come alive with cheers.

Hilda’s gaze darted back to the stage. Concern flickered in her eyes, but she quickly turned back to Luke, leaning in close.

“I don’t know what kind of mess you’ve gotten yourself into,” she muttered, “but I’ll do as you say.”

Then, with a firm grip, she clasped his shoulder, fingers digging into the muscle. Her voice dropped, heavier and more serious.

“But I can’t stress this enough—stay away from Ming Hu. He’s bad news, Luke. Bad news.”

Luke gave a tight nod, though inside he was already cursing himself for getting in this deep.

With a final glance, Hilda turned and marched up the steps toward the stage, the wood groaning under her weight. Onstage, Fairy Qin bowed with grace and poise, her black silk hair cascading like a waterfall as she dipped her head.

She turned to leave the stage, but the crowd, intoxicated by her presence, immediately grew restless.

“Fairy Qin! One more!”

“Please, don’t leave!”

But the moment Hilda’s broad silhouette stepped into view, the clamor died down like a snuffed flame. She swept her gaze across the room with the intensity of a battlefield commander, daring anyone to speak again.

Then someone did.

“Boo! Who invited the heifer? Bring back the Fairy!”

The words cut through the silence like a whipcrack.

Time seemed to stop. Every head in the room turned in unison toward the source. As if rehearsed, the surrounding patrons stepped away, creating a neat little clearing around the offender.

A lone young man, no older than twenty, swayed on the edge of his seat. Clearly drunk, he blinked up at the wall of glares now pointed his way. Realization dawned slowly. His eyes widened, round as saucers, as he finally took in the weight of the silence and the danger of his mistake.

Hilda jumped off the stage without a word, her heavy boots striking the ground with a thud that seemed to shake the floor. Instantly, the crowd parted like the sea, patrons scrambling to get out of her path.

Terror flashed across the young man’s face as he saw her barreling toward him. He let out a shrill, inhuman shriek and stumbled backward in a drunken panic.

Luke watched, wide-eyed, as Hilda charged like a bull unleashed, catching the man in one fluid motion. She hoisted him above her head as if he weighed nothing, then hurled him toward the entrance—like Donkey Kong flinging a barrel in some arcade game.

The poor drunk landed hard, letting out a winded gasp as he hit the ground and rolled to a stop.

Hilda threw one last glare at the groaning man before turning away, her boots stomping a heavy rhythm across the wooden floor. Again, the crowd cleared a path for her without hesitation.

At the other end of the room, Luke stood rooted in place, goosebumps crawling across his skin from the display. As she approached, her tone brooked no argument.

“Qin, head up the stairs. You too, Lucas.”

Only then did Luke realize that Fairy Qin was standing beside him.

She gave him a sharp, evaluating glance, her lips pursed in subtle distaste. Her nose scrunched slightly, as though she’d caught a whiff of something unpleasant.

Luke let out a hollow chuckle. The irony was almost amusing.

I wonder what face she’ll make when she finds out I’m the one who created the Jade Mist perfume she’s wearing…

Shaking his head, he followed her up the stairs. He had only climbed two steps when he froze.

Ah, fuck. The prince…

Turning slightly, he scanned the crowd. It didn’t take long for his Eagle Eye to lock onto the young man’s despondent figure. Prince Lyon stood in the midst of the audience, visibly crestfallen as he watched Luke ascend the stairs alongside his beloved Fairy Qin.

Luke quickly made a series of hand gestures and signals, hoping to tell him to stay put. But the hurt in the prince’s eyes was clear.

Luke sighed, shoulders slumping slightly.

Let’s just hope he doesn’t do anything stupid. As long as he keeps his head down, we can stay under the radar—for a few hours, at least.
