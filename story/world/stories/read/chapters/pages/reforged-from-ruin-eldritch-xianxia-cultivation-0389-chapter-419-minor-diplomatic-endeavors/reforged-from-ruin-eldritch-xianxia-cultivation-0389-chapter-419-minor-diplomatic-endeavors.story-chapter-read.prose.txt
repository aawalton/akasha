In a world of billions, it is rare that any single event can lead to the sort of consequences that affect everyone. Simultaneously, the chances of such events occurring only multiplies by the number of people which might incite them, and the ways in which they might come to pass. In any world, the chances of anything happening that affects everyone all at once is infinitesimal. In a world with billions, the number of chances is also near infinite. Everything is connected, after all, and it’s difficult not to acknowledge that once you learn more about the world.

A single child with a Truth might lead to a revolution in technology and our understanding of esoteric physics. A single person with a blade might cut down existence itself. The chances of these unique individuals appearing grows larger in sync with the number of individuals appearing in general, after all, even as the ever-growing number of people makes it more likely that some will remain forever unaffected, spread out as we inevitably become. And yet, connections remain. The fluttering of a single leaf can change destinies, just as the taking of a life, any life, can alter whole communities forever.

Many of my fellow immortals lose sight of this. It’s easy to, when you can tear up mountains and break the skies open. When you see the world in epochs rather than moments, when you can bake someone a pastry that makes their ancestors come back from the grave and compliment them, when you can weather a million blows, what do the little things matter? What elephant cares about the ants beneath their feet?

We are not elephants, though. And the only thing in the world that’s an ant are ants. Plus, it’s scary how dangerous ants can be, and what effects a well-placed anthill can have on an environment. Everything matters, all the time. In a world of billions, every single person has a chance to make the world wholly different, and even beyond that, they will make it different in some way.

Everything matters, all the time. In a forest, every leaf makes a difference, even if you can’t see it. That’s your lack of vision, not the leaf being unimportant, nor the chain of events it might create not leading anywhere. Immortality is no excuse for forgetting this.

To her joy, most of the cultivators seem pretty excited about the whole thing. Some of them stop what they’re doing so completely that they look like they’ve frozen stiff! Sure, a lot of them also start trying to attack the mouths or keep fighting each other or whatever they were doing beforehand. She’s pretty sure some of them are doing it to survive, since she can’t (and doesn’t really want to) control all the pieces of herself and the other nodes swimming around. In particular, most of the more dangerous “plants” and “animals” that are being hunted don’t have any qualms about continuing to try to tear open the ones attacking them just because she made an announcement. Also, some of the cultivators look really tasty, so she can’t even blame them for wanting a slightly more aggressive taste of them!

Though the funniest “aggressive tasting” happening definitely has to be the one in the caves. They are moving so fast to get their robes back on! Without being able to sense their biology, she wouldn’t even be able to tell how they’d moved.

Huh. She can feel their biology.

They’re not a part of her, not… totally? Maybe? But they are inside her, kind of. This space is her, and there’s… there’s something about that. Some Truth on the tip of her tongue.

More importantly, she’s pretty sure she’s just so in-tune with biology that she can touch it even if it isn’t hers. That’s… how she made Geng He’s arm. Right? Mostly.

Good enough!

“Ten seconds are up! Enjoy the journey!”

The ground comes up and swallows about two-dozen people. Some of them are fast enough to avoid it, but that’s fine- just means less work because less trials. The ones that hold still, or who aren’t quite fast enough to escape, get wrapped in cocoons of vital fluids and a membrane of flesh and mucous, which helps to insulate them from the ways that she warps space around them. They fall inward, which is also down, which is also sideways in multiple directions on the compass that pull them back to her “temple”.

She could build the trials where they’re standing, technically, but that would require her to spend a lot of focus just keeping her awareness that spread out. The temple isn’t even a very central location, but it’s where “she” is, kinda sorta, so why not?

Several dozen cultivators fall unceremoniously out of their slimy little pods before space warps back into place properly. Several of them vomit, and apparently the protections weren’t quite perfect because several of them start bleeding from their orifices and suffering minor backlash.

They land, chaotically, all the floor of the temple. Organically curved, coral-like decorations make the center of the ritual-like place hard to stand on, but not so much that proper cultivators can’t compensate.

“Hello everyone!” Raika says, growing rather explosively out of a far wall. “Now that you’re-”

A blast of frozen lightning shocks, freezes, then shatters her body, turning her into so much shrapnel.

“-all here, we can get started!”

She notices several people holding back all sorts of techniques, which is nice. Eager and raring to go, even if they’re pointing themselves in the wrong way.

“It was recently brought to my attention that people like rewards, and that rewards go hand in hand with trials,” she says out of the body that grew on the opposite side of the room. “Obvious enough. But then I realized that I didn’t really have any trials ready, or any special rewards. Everyone’s seemed fine with wandering around doing whatever they please, but I very recently started experiencing the ability to think in more than three minutes at a time, and frankly, could use the practice.

“So! I’m going to make a big maze with a bunch of super interesting traps and if you die, you die. But if you live or find any cool secret rooms, you get prizes! I’ll try to make them tailored, but I might lose interest if I get everything I want out of this early. Get ready!”

“Wait!”

She turns to a cultivator, his robes the color and style of almost a third of the others in the chamber. Browns and golds and blacks, juxtaposed with the other third of the space, which is mostly in blues.

“You’re the guardian in charge of this hidden realm?”

“Technically!”

“Honored guardian spirit, this lowly one-”

“I’m sort of excited to get to the whole violence and crafting part of things, can you not talk like that?”

“It’s only to defer properly to your power in this place, oh great spirit!”

“Wow, not a good listener,” she says, opening a hole underneath him.

Like any halfway-decent cultivator, he doesn’t just fall into it, reacting almost instantly- which is why a pillar of bone is waiting to slam down on him from the ceiling, the sound of bruising flesh and a yell echoing until he’s shoved into the hole and out of sight.

Several of the disciples are moving, powers cycling, stances shifting-

“Don’t worry! He’s alive! I just got bored. My only real interest in any of you is because I just started being able to think and there’s a lot of stuff I need to figure out, and you all sort of owe me, and someone gave me the idea. So. You can all do the cool challenges I’m gonna make and get cool new body parts and weapons and magics and things, or I can also shove you down the holes.”

“We owe you?”

She turns her head to match the hundreds of hidden eyes turning to look at the cultivator who spoke. Someone in the black and brown robes again, his gaze confused, suddenly tense under her scrutiny.

“I apologize, honored one, but in what way do we-”

An arm emerges out from behind him, grabs him by the back of the head, and slams his face into a wall.

He recovers fast, cycling an art that pulls at her new spatial senses, warping the weight around him-

She slams his head into the wall again.

An artifact activates, empowering him with far more strength than before, making his efforts to escape multiply in power-

And again.

He activates some kind of movement technique, aborting only when her fingers stay just as firm as before, ripping into the flesh of his scalp as he tries to move, and-

And again.

Seven more impacts echo through the very quiet temple. She only stops when she hears him start to wheeze from the blood he’s inhaling through his broken nose.

Her body grows and extends, moving on a serpent-tail of multiple spinal columns, exposed bone and musculature, until she’s moved over half the room to loom over him. He pants, hard-pressed to breathe.

“You are in my body. While I couldn’t think for myself. Taking pieces of me. Killing in me. Using me as a garden to pluck from. I don’t like that. But you probably didn’t know any better so I also don’t like killing you for it. So I’m going to make trials and give rewards, and you’ll accept them or you’ll leave.”

She opens her hand and lets him drop to his knees, coughing up blood. One of his sect-members goes to offer him something from his storage ring, but stops, wisely deciding to wait until Raika moves back.

“I don’t have a ton of myself together at the moment,” she says, still staring intently at the one who decided to interrupt. “I can’t tell you all of why I feel certain things in certain ways. But I do. And I don’t like you. You remind me of something I can’t think about and I don’t like that. I don’t really like most of you. There’s so much to do and so much I need and you are here and you are taking, because that’s all the world is to you, something to take from. I’m going to get something back. And I am being generous enough to decide that all I need from you is inspiration.”

She leans down closer, until her face is right over his, until her body is sprouting new arms to plant themselves around his head, until she eclipses him entirely.

He’s not talking now. He’s listening. They’re all listening.

It feels good. To be listened to. To have these people in the shape of memories she can’t reach, whose existence itches in some way that means something she can’t remember, paralyzed, waiting for her. Aware of her power. Respectful of her.

Or maybe just afraid.

That feels good too.

“You can take the trials. Or you can leave. Do you understand?”

His face, covered in blood and bruises, nods. It sounds wet when he breathes.

“Great!” she says, retracting quickly enough that the air makes a snap sound as she moves. “Trials are through there, and the exit’s that way.”

At her words, a part of the organic curvature of the temple unfolds, opening wide to showcase a descent down into a long, lightless tunnel. On the opposite side of it, parts of the architecture move apart, revealing the pathway to the exit (which, ironically, also has a little trial to it!).

And then she waits. Staring down at the crowd.

They very quietly and very cautiously start moving towards each other. Like herd animals, they congregate, circling around their strongest members, the icons that they cling to for stability. Colors stick with colors, palettes with palettes, and the ones without clear allegiances tend to move towards each other for support in turn. Cliques and tribes form in real time, and while many of them do look towards the exit, no one actually takes it.

Which is a bit annoying, in some ways. She kind of wants them gone, almost like she also kind-of-sort-of wants them to stop being in her body, which is kind of like how she wants them to stay here so she can think things and learn. But they’re useful, and she likes helping people and-

Oh. That’s interesting.

She… likes helping people.

Interesting.

“Gratitude, senior.”

She turns to see the young man in dark blue bow to her, respectfully but not too deeply.

She doesn’t start beating his skull in, which he seems to take as a good result, and he nods, turning with the group of similarly dressed cultivators around him and descending down into the trials.

Others quickly emulate him, bowing politely before they turn to descend, one after another.

Only once the last group has descended does she remember that she probably needs to start working on the actual trials. They kind of just started going down a long tunnel.

A few copies of herself later and she has a working set of minds and able-bodied hands working kinda-sorta in tandem. The ground beneath the temple expands, opening new cave systems, tunnels, labyrinthine corridors and more. She fills them with hidden traps, starting off with the very basic spike-pit but making sure to hide some routes behind amalgamations of flesh and danger that could put up a hell of a fight against anything on her surface.

Which serves as a lovely reminder about the fact that there are other people around still.

As an afterthought, she connects the chambers her original trial-requesters are in to the wider labyrinth forming beneath the surface, letting them out to explore as they please as she turns the attention of herself, rather than her newer selves, back out into the world around her.

There are maybe twenty or so cultivators left of the hundred or more that originally arrived. She can taste where every body has fallen, smell every death blow between rivals or from unexpected dangers. They killed each other as they arrived, and killed each other as they explored, and while the bodies left behind are almost all bereft of any tools or artifacts, she doesn’t care about spirit stones or blades.

In unison, the landscape folds around the corpses, pulling them into the soft flesh of her outer layers and beginning to digest.

So much to do. So much she needs. But fresh corpses, imbued with bloodlines or Qi flavors or techniques or alchemies, are very much in line with her wants.

More will join them, she knows. Most of the cultivators who didn’t accept her summons are running about with increased panic, grabbing at anything precious they find with little regard for their own safety. Using the opportunities gifted by their opponents vanishing, no doubt. Some remain in groups, heading for higher challenges, and there are one or two individuals walking around independently, their cultivation or foundations high enough that they can afford to do stupid shit.

She’s not going to kill them all. Not for things they couldn’t know, or for ignoring her summons, anyways. But every one that dies is one that she has more ability to feed from, to digest and learn from.

She felt that island go dark.

Something’s still killing her. Piece by piece, rather than all at once, but killing her nonetheless.

Beneath the ground, two-dozen cultivators traverse ever more dangerous hallways as she watches, tailoring and preparing tools and augmentations best suited for each of them. Above it, more of them run amok, harvesting and eating and killing and taking what they please.

In both cases, they itch.

Still, there’s always room for more tools to work with.

A woman falls onto the hard-packed flesh-dirt, screaming in pain as her arm lies severed a few feet away. The man she killed in the battle just fought cut it off with an impulse of thought, his technique invisible and unblockable without a concept to confront it, but she’ll bleed out in minutes, and the medicine that Raika can taste in her storage ring isn’t strong enough to reattach so messy a torn-off limb.

Emerging from out of the ground, Raika smiles at the poor little cultivator all lost and alone.

“Hey there! I see you’re missing some pieces. Have you considered getting some improvements?”
