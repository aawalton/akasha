“We’ve found that tracking down the artifact Last Light,” Zephyr continued, “will be exceptionally difficult. The lands across the ocean west are not well known, but the compilation of sources suggests a vast variety of tribal groups. The Orswyth of the vision spent many, many years navigating this hostile environment, chasing myths as he learned the area and the peoples like a bona fide explorer. I don’t think we can shortcut that very easily, even with our resources. The people problem doesn’t go away with speed and money. We came to the estimate of six months in the most ideal circumstances.”

We could make inroads east, still, just in case. But I wouldn’t send anyone critical, or count on it to be acquired on your timetable, Madam.”Samantha frowned. It was all about countering that ‘bad vision’ of Orswyth, where she was corrupted by Entropy via the Sage of Truth’s developed and refined ultimate ability, during the last altercation at the Capital before going on to face the True Sage. Apparently, her levels and tricks were still not enough, and they needed something exceptionally special.

In order to develop it in the short timeframe of her planned, momentum-seizing, relative blitzkrieg through the Dominion territories, she was ensnaring many different creeds and experts in collaboration. Tashome had cobbled the solution together with Orswyth, requiring two main components: truly massive amounts of pure, divine [Light] energy, and a focal object. That object would have a sacrificial component: from her. She’d have to give up a piece of herself to truly provide the level of purity needed. Just a drop, a shard, like Last Light, which was a surviving shard of a perished deity of [Light]. They had to emulate that, and she was the only source feasible.

This was not entirely Tashome’s wheelhouse, sadly, as he was more about corrupting or repurposing a curse, not ensuring purity. As such, he could only provide his services for the conceptualization and synthesis of the components. Ultimately, it was up to Orswyth to devise the focus.

To this end, he first worked with Tashome to make a half-baked rough draft, and ‘give it the ole college try.’ It was doomed to failure, of course — yet that was the idea. His special class ability, [Deep Contemplation], allowed a very slow-brew retry of a failed task.

And that was what he was doing: devoting his every waking moment to redevelopment. The monumental nature of the task meant it would be a post-Matrazere thing. Samantha wasn’t going to wait, and she understood the Sage of Truth was not going to defend the fortress. For one thing, the entire Dominion wouldn’t think they needed him, as Matrazere was expressly designed to perpetually contain the defenders necessary. Everything the Dominion had sent her way in two huge battles? Other than their two champions — whom they had regarded as invincible — they were all extras. That was just the scale the bastards were on.

Fortunately, a combination of cowardice and a very staid, rigid hierarchical structure to how they did things precluded them from going ‘all-in’ like she was. It helped that her early alliance with Zaddy had allowed them to assassinate a key general very early on, and then also threw their hierarchy into chaos with what happened at the Battle of Wortboro. They hadn’t dealt well with the leadership hits, clearly. Why would they, when centuries of experience at the very top had suddenly gone up in smoke?

Matrazere is all-in enough, though.

They had a very capable general in the fortress: the new Sage of Judgment after the old one perished in the Battle of Wortboro. He went by the name Unobus. He was less powerful, had far less status, but apparently was a better tactician. The sage had not come out of the fortress, not even once, since attaining his role. While Samantha and her allies could label it cowardice, it was entirely the right play from the Dominion perspective. A general didn’t need to fight.

Dax generally didn’t either. Her ability, which needed her close to battle, was too tempting to remove her entirely from the battlefield, but there was little reason to risk her beyond being hidden as she was in the Rivermount siege. Had she had an epic duel with an enemy general in that battle? Fuck no. That was just stupid — that was what the various other champions were for. Dax had not even been touched, just as Linnore had not been as the second commander, who was even more removed from the battle.

Unobus worried her, from all the reports on him. He’d been a friend and compatriot of Kerakora, the assassinated famous general. Unobus seemed like he was a humble, low-status genius, and they’d knocked down everyone above him. There would be no avoiding him in Matrazere. Assassination inside the fortress was a pipedream likely doomed to catastrophe. Thankfully, she had Dax to match against him.

She’ll have her ‘duel’ for that battle. But not crossing swords so much as chess pieces.

“Send an expedition,” Samantha finally said to those gathered at the table. “I want it hastened as much as possible for first contact, but we’re not sending vital members, in consideration of Matrazere’s needs. I know it’ll be light compared to the likes of Gelmak Por sailing in the southeast, so make sure there is provision for more summoning circles for Servitors on the ships, to act as reinforcements, if needed. Anyway, the purpose isn’t just for the random shot at Last Light. Other benefits could make themselves known. Bring gifts. Let’s make nice with these tribes if possible.”

Synchronization with our western allies and helping them move their asses, getting some key people healed up, taking advantage of the new recruitment incentives and processes, so on and so on. Skirmishing is already bloody well upriver, though. Our advance teams have taken the fortress at Swiftpoint without much trouble. Fieldcarn, just past the final foothills before the Dominion’s southern heartland, will be well-defended, and they will begin harrying us in earnest once we are trying to secure these river territories. The foothills throughout it make it a problem. We’ll need to keep the recruitment train pouring in behind our main force to have decent garrisons.”

Samantha nodded, though she couldn’t help grimacing behind her mask. She’d made some compromises in her ideals to fill the army numbers out quickly, and almost certainly risked more traitors and terrorism. But the truth was, Rivermount would simply need to hold down the fort once they were marching on Matrazere. It didn’t really need to be perfect, and even if she wanted it, it was impossible. Going forward into Dominion territory, everything would be more and more corrupt. Spies, petty sabotage, assassination plots — these were a given.

Meanwhile, making it easy to soldier up as ‘former’ borderlander Dominion would far and away bring beneficial numbers. The vast majority were concerned about simple things: coin, food, and stability. Most members were cogs in a wheel in such a massive army. A huge number of Rivermount citizens had joined the armies as camp aides and so forth, starving for the generous coin offered.

Despite what happened to the refugees in the battle, they just kept pouring in, and there was no hesitation in getting into an army role if they could. Whether they believed Samanthian claims about the responsibility of the slaughter didn’t even seem to matter. Being employed, instead of not employed, trumped that matter easily, and everyone knew this army was good for the coin and gave generous, already well-respected contracts. They’d built a hell of a reputation thanks to the supreme organization Dax, Linnore, Jeeves, and others had engendered.

But mainly? Thanks to the money.

And if you didn’t really like supreme discipline in every facet of life, well, you could just go hang out with the Naugites. They’d adopted literal ‘sub-clans’ of ‘Northerners,’ i.e., humans, now comprising around twenty percent of the forces under their control. They’d wanted to write strict contracts for these people, but Samantha forced them to tone them down with the help of the Cat Sith, Sancus, and, well, both sides were happier overall, if she said so herself. But she supposed ‘mutual benefit’ worked out well when the conquest cup was overflowing. The Naugites were in continuous good spirits.
