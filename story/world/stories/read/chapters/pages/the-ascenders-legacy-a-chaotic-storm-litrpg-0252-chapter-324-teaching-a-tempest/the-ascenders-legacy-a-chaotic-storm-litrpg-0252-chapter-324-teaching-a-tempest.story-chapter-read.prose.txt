Aodhán clasped his hand, then accepted the brief embrace from Crixis's mother—who somehow managed it without releasing her son.

"Thank you for taking Crixis on as your aide," she said, her voice thick with emotion. "Maverick and I intentionally kept our distance these past few days. We didn’t want to complicate things during the initiation.”

"He's coy about it," Crixis's mother smiled. "He's the youngest of six and our second awakened. After five unawakened children, we'd all but given up hope."

"I had," Maverick corrected with a grin. "You can imagine my shock when, just a month ago, this little cub awakened."

"Alright, alright—" Crixis laughed, finally extracting himself from his mother's arms. "You're going to bore Aodhán to tears. And we're being terribly rude keeping Elder Sylvie waiting."

"Don't apologize. I adore reunions like this." Elder Sylvie smiled warmly at Crixis's parents. "He's done remarkably well. You must be very proud."

"Proud doesn't begin to cover it," Crixis's mother beamed. "To be chosen as aide to a transmigrant as powerful as Aodhán—it's beyond anything we dreamed."

Aodhán shifted uncomfortably under the weight of her praise, his polite smile straining as he shook his head. “I’m happy to have Crixis. He’s not a burden at all. Any transmigrant would be lucky to have him as an aide.”

Maverick beamed. “Perhaps, we should be congratulating you instead. You’ve gained yourself a remarkably capable aide.”

“I have." Aodhán’s smile softened, and he chuckled, genuinely this time. “I am truly fortunate. But Crixis is more than an aide—he's my friend.”

Crixis smiled at him, a hint of emotion in his eyes as he nodded. “You’re my friend too. I’m glad I met you when I did.”

They both laughed, remembering how they’d met, and elder Sylvie shook her head at their silliness, a small smile on her face. When their laughter died down, she spoke.

“It’s always a joy to see people bonding in the sect as they should. This is exactly what the sects were created for.”

“It is,” Maverick nodded, his gaze a little serious as he glanced back at his son. “It is important never to forget that. We are a family. All of us.”

Crixis and his mother nodded, and though Aodhán did the same, he wasn’t quite sure he knew it as intimately as the others did.

"Well then," Elder Sylvie said brightly, her cheery tone slicing through his somber thoughts like a knife, "I’m sure you have a lot of resting and celebrating to do, so I won’t take too much of your time.

She glanced at Crixis and extended a neatly folded pile of gold-rimmed robes toward him. "Congratulations. You missed the distribution."

Crixis accepted them with a wide smile. "Thank you, Elder Syl—"

The rest of his words died as Elder Sylvie produced a small rune-carved box from her robes. Its surface glimmered with inscribed patterns, and everyone paused at the sight of it. Crixis accepted it with both hands, cradling it like something newborn.

"You left the core sect before these were distributed to the new initiates," she explained. "Congratulations again."

Crixis looked from his parents to Aodhán, eyes wide. “My own soulseed. I’ll finally have my own bloodline manifestation.”

Aodhán grinned. “Let’s hope it doesn’t turn out as severe as mine.”

“Hummph!” Crixis’s mother snorted. “Severe bloodline manifestations are the best. You’ll appreciate it when you eventually have offspring of your own.”

The words, though uttered casually, echoed in Aodhán’s mind like thunder—a blessing and a threat rolled seamlessly into one. Aodhán had never given thought to birthing offspring before. He’d been too busy making sure he lived to see the next decade.

And now, with another war looming on the horizon, offspring were the farthest things from his mind. He, however, found himself not averse to the idea, a small smile blooming on his lips as he wondered what it would be like to start a family of his own.

He didn’t get to wonder long though, as Crixis opened the box, runes flaring violet and gold to reveal a bright blue soulseed no larger than his thumbnail, radiating a distinct sense of heaviness.

“By the Storm,” Crixis breathed. “It’s aligned with my path.”

“It sure is,” Maverick smiled proudly. “Your mother and I may or may not have had anything to do with it.”

Crixis smiled up at them, genuine gratitude in his gaze. “Thank you, Dad. Thank you, Mum. I really appreciate this.”

“Anything for you,” his mother beamed. “You have made us proud. This is thanks.”

Crixis smiled widely, but when he glanced back at the soulseed, the smile dimmed slightly, overshadowed by curiosity.

“Do you know what manifestations it'll have?" Crixis asked.

Elder Sylvie shook her head. "We never do—not truly. Not until it merges with your spirit and finds expression. Though I'm certain we'll be seeing a great deal of blue."

"Or silver," Aodhán added, noting a subtle flash within the seed's energy.

Crixis’s mother nodded, agreeing with him. “Your brother will be eternally jealous. He’s always loved silver.”

Crixis laughed. “Where is he anyway?”

“He’s on a mission.” Maverick chuckled. “He should be back in a month’s time.”

“Or less,” Crixis’s mother added. “What with the war and all that.”

The mention of war subdued the atmosphere a bit, but Maverick spoke before things got too grim.

“So, when will you absorb the seed?” he asked, nudging his son gently. “I want to be able to recognize you when next I see you.”

“As soon as possible,” Crixis responded immediately. “Aodhán bought me some treasures. I have so much to absorb.”

Aodhán paused, having had a different plan in mind. Crixis’s parents paused too, but their concerns were much different.

They glanced at him in surprise, and slowly, Maverick asked. “You bought treasures for our son?”

“Yes,” Aodhán replied, a bit hesitantly, not sure if he’d offended them. “I recently came into a sizeable amount of sect points, so I decided to use a part of it on Crixis. He’s my aide after all. It’s my duty to make sure he’s strong.”

Crixis’s parents exchanged a glance, and then his mother turned to Aodhán. "We were told Crixis was in good hands. It's something else to see it for ourselves."

"Indeed." Maverick reached into his robes and produced two treasure cases—both at the Mythic rank—and held one out to each of them. "Consider these a successful initiation gift. From both of us."

Aodhán accepted the mythic treasure, core sense sharpening to analyze the treasure within. It was an azure pebble of solidified water essence that made his aspect hum gently within him.

“We didn’t know your path,” Maverick continued, "so we chose something to aid your aspect. It's excellent for water aspect refinement."

Aodhán cradled the treasure and bowed. "Thank you. Truly."

"And we thank you for taking on our son." Crixis's mother smiled. "We are in your debt. If you ever need anything—we're just a sect away."

“We’ll remember that," Crixis said warmly, and Aodhán echoed the sentiment. A few minutes of easy conversation passed, and then after embraces that lasted just a bit longer than expected, Crixis's parents left, reiterating that Crixis had better call after he absorbed the soul-seed.

“I will,” Crixis laughed as he watched them rise into the air and zoom off, leaving them with Elder Sylvie, who lingered a moment.

Her gaze dropped to Aodhán's clothing, and a small smile formed. "I see you're not wearing your robes. Didn't they come with the house?"

"They did," Aodhán said, cringing slightly. "I haven't gotten used to wearing them yet."

"Get used to it quickly," she said, and though her tone remained light, her eyes were serious. "Those robes are your identity. And in times like these, blending in matters more than you might think."

The implication settled over him fully. He nodded. "I will."

She smiled once more, offered a final word of encouragement, and left.

Crixis immediately turned toward the apartment, eager to begin cultivating, but Aodhán had other plans. "Don't start yet. I want to go first—for safety.”

Crixis frowned. "I understand not going together, but I can't do anything to help once the twisted effect takes you.”

Aodhán smiled. “Let me worry about that. I promise it's better this way.”

Crixis exhaled disappointedly. “Fine. Is there anything you want me to do in the meantime?"

Aodhán's smile widened. "Yes. Come to the training room—there's a skill I want to teach everyone."

"Everyone?"

“Yes,” Aodhán nodded. “I’ve already texted the others. They should be here shortly."

“I hear that’s what pride does to a person,” Sophran teased loudly. “A visit to the elder pavilion, and now we are being summoned.” He shook his head. “If this is a solicitation for friendship, we are not signing it.”

Aodhán nodded. “The skill is called Tempest of Transformation, though for a long time it was known simply as Miracle Rain. And it’s proven to be one of the greatest power amplifiers I’ve ever seen.” He paused, then added with a small shrug. "I turned the tide of a full-scale war with it."

He did. He told them about the miracle rain, about the fine from the awakened council, about the Sunstonian war, and about the effects the rain had rippled through their ranks. By the time he finished, they were practically vibrating to learn it.

Aodhán smiled. His reasons were simple enough. He'd spent so much time giving to others—he wanted to experience his own miracle rain firsthand. With so many treasures to absorb at once, he couldn't afford to miss a single thing.

"How do we learn it?" Crixis asked.

“Why don't we go to the training room for that?"

They headed to the training room, and Aodhán summoned Varéc out of his spirit to settle in a corner. After everyone said hello, they gathered around him, expressions expectant.

Still, the difference between each person's cloud was obvious. Crixis's cloud seemed darker and heavier—a testament to his path—but compared to the intricacy of Darien's clouds, it seemed lacking in definition.

Sophran's cloud was the one that seemed most like his own, heavy not in sight but in essence—realer than the others in a way Aodhán couldn't explain yet. But the difference in clouds would have little to no effect on the next step, and as simply as he could, Aodhán began explaining the process of chaos, quintessence, and willpower weaving.

Chuckles rang out, but it turned out not to be as tricky as Aodhán had thought.
