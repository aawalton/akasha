There were a lot of ways I could have reacted to Anguis’s sudden…request.

Anger, for one. I was entitled to that, I believe. This was a Dwarf who had literally just admitted to not only being partially culpable for everything I’d gone through in Addersfield, but present as well. He’d dispassionately watched without lifting a finger as his deranged progeny had tortured and killed innocents by the dozen.

Laughter, for another. The idea that I could suddenly just drop everything and align myself with Prince Anguis of House Savoy, ruler of Vittolia and the surrounding lands, was laughable. Probably right in his face, too.

But frankly, what I felt at that moment…

Was mostly bewilderment.

I blinked rapidly for a moment, and I couldn’t help but notice that Elysael was visibly taken aback as well. “Repeat that for me?”

A tinge of what seemed to be real amusement crossed Anguis’s face, then. “Come with me,” He said patiently. “I am, formally and directly, offering my hand to you and yours in alliance, Marshal.”

He…

He even, actually, for real, lifted a hand and held it out to me, palm open in a plaintive gesture.

I just stared at it for a moment, lost and wary, as if those muscled fingers were serpents rearing up to strike me.

Frankly, they essentially were.

I genuinely couldn’t believe this was happening.

Elysael regained her composure first. “As ever, you are bold, Prince Anguis,” She said, open disdain dripping from her lips. “To think you would attempt to steal mine own allies from underneath me, in my very presence.”

Anguis’s eyes flickered over to Elysael for only an instant. “Quiet now,” He said mildly. “The leading men are speaking. We require no commentary from the peanut gallery.”

The most affronted look I’d ever seen crossed Elysael’s face for an instant, before it hardened into a mask of ice and calculation.

Most nobles, I think, would have immediately burst into offended recriminations at the dismissal. But Ely…she was shrewder than that. She flickered a glance my way, with a hidden request I was only able to read because of my familiarity with the woman.

Keep him talking.

I let out a long, slow sigh and didn’t bother to hide my exhaustion. It was both relevant and believable.

“What could have possibly,” I started incredulously. “Convinced you I would even entertain that request, Prince Anguis?”

“Why, it’s simple,” Anguis answered pleasantly. “You’ll die if you don’t. And that, truly, would be such a waste.”

I raised a placid eyebrow his way, unruffled. “Is that a threat?”

“Oh no,” Anguis tsked and shook his head. “Threats are so gauche. I make observations, instead. As surely as flame burns and wind blows, this is an inevitability. If you persist in your alliance with the Florens, you and all of your fellows shall die shortly. Along with the rest of this squallid collection of rust-red hovels. Answer me this, Marshal Hart. Why are you even here?”

“Why, I’m here because you requested my presence,” I said, deliberately misunderstanding him.

Anguis visibly rolled his eyes, easily seeing through the gambit. “Oh, posh. You know what I mean. I meant, why are you even assisting the Rhoscarans, Marshal? You may not have been born in Herztal-”

(Indirect confirmation he already knew I was a Precursor. Color me shocked.)

“-but your allegiance ultimately lies with them,” Anguis continued patiently. “This is a Velancian matter. And an internal one. This campaign is meant to squash a rebellious province of the Principality. It makes very little sense for you to waste your life in defense of this pact of rebels. From what I know of the good Regent Lord von Steinmark, I doubt he signed off on such direct interference in the governance of another country. Especially considering the current…state of the world.”

A frown of irritation crossed my lips. “You cannot possibly believe that anyone from Herztal would choose to align themselves with your faction, Lord Savoy,” I pointed out. “The Kingdom of Herztal rightfully considers the act of enslavement a blasphemy, and you are the biggest slave-master on the continent. And, frankly, my forces and I are in Velancia because of the circumstances.”

Time to go for the throat.

“As you say, Vereden is currently in the midst of a crisis,” I started slowly. “We came here because we have actionable intelligence that the corruption of the world’s Aether originates from the north. From within the Barren Forest and its inhabitants. Which you and yours have entered into an alliance with.”

The implicit accusation hung heavy in the air.

Anguis of Savoy tilted his head to study me for a moment, a considering look in his eye.

And then he smirked.

“Oh, this?” He said airily, waving a hand skyward in an almost careless manner. “Yes, we’re aware of its origin. It’s only right that a god reassert his grip upon his ancient domain, after all. The transition period is simply a tad…fraught. Rest assured, it will pass in time.”

…what?

“And why shouldn’t the Principality of Velancia enter into an alliance with Elvendom?” Anguis smiled broadly, opening his arms wide. “Finally, after long millennia of dormancy, a once-thought-lost god has awoken and taken a firm hand with his unruly children. Those whom we have warred with, who Rhoscara have warred with for centuries, are being brought into the light of civilization once more. A nation at once ancient and new rises within their ancestral lands, slowly shedding their trappings of barbarity. I rejoice at the chance to help guide the fledgling Confederation of Lasgach back into the global community that is Vereden. As should all who espouse their own virtue.”

Silence.

Utter, all-consuming silence across not only the docks we stood upon, but the surrounding battlements as well. I…suppose everyone around us could hear Anguis’s words as well.

Elysael was one to break the quiet. “Are you suggesting…” She said disbelievingly. “That the Mad God has, what,…regained his sanity? That he now rules the Elves in truth, and does not exist merely as an object of worship to them?”

Anguis took a long, deep breath…and smiled.

“Yes.”

Immediate whispers erupted all along the walls at Anguis’s pronouncement. Shocked, disbelieving ones at that. But within them was a measure of…well.

Hope.

That…was dangerous.

I didn’t blame them, though. But I didn’t believe it.

I wasn’t being hyperbolic, either. I literally didn’t believe that was possible. The idea that Fynneas had, somehow, regained his actual sanity, and was no longer the Mad God and just…a god?

Maybe even the God of Freedom once more.

Bullshit.

And I wasn’t the only one.

In a shower of leaves, Alveron appeared to teleport from his position up on the walls where he had been watching with the rest of the expedition Captains. From one moment to the next, he was standing not far from where I was, close enough that I could reach out to touch him. The Elf’s shoulders were tighter than I had ever seen before, and he was gripping his staff hard enough that his knuckles were white. To my surprise, he had lowered his hood, allowing his long, blade-like ears to bob in the air from the force of his furious shudders.

And display the outright snarl of rage that had overtaken his ancient, ringed features.

“Still thy wagging tongue, serpent,” Alveron hissed, the air thickening with his power. Not enough to manifest his Mantle, but enough to tease it. A scent wafted over the docks, fragrant and wooded, somehow overpowering the smell of the river. “Such a thing is not possible.”

I immediately tensed, and I noticed I wasn’t the only person to do so. Elysael’s Corvid Knights laid broad, plated palms over sheathed longswords, and Anguis’s soldiers did the same.

I didn’t blame them. I was a little scared of the ancient Paragon myself.

I’d never seen him like this.

In stark contrast to the ratcheting temperature, Anguis merely leaned on the table with one arm and regarded Alveron with a bright, almost mocking gaze. “You…I’ve heard of you,” He mused, before a sudden dagger-thin grin crossed his lips. “Alveron, is it not? The grandson. The last surviving blood of poor, fraught Fynneas. What a joy it is that you are here as well. What serendipity that I have the chance to reveal this…to his primary tormentor.”

For one, I sincerely doubted that Anguis hadn’t known about Alveron’s presence.

Two…

What was he talking about?

I wasn’t the only one confused.

Alveron clenched his jaw hard enough that I was surprised I didn’t hear shattering teeth. “Be mindful of your next words, child. These tin soldiers could not protect you from my wrath if I so chose.”

“No doubt, no doubt,” Anguis chuckled, still entirely at ease. “You are mighty indeed, from what I’ve learned. Do you know, Your Highness-”

Alveron visibly twitched at those words.

“-they have a different name for you, within Lasgach? After all, it’s been millennia since House Coillmhòr has ruled the forest in anything other than name,” Anguis continued, with a sad shake of his head. “How can your people be expected to know you by who you are? Instead…they only know you for what you do. To the poor, frightened people of Lasgach…you are the shadows under their bed. You are the beast who stalks the woods for a meal of babe-flesh. You are the storm that sweeps away the harvest, and the thief of hope. They have a different name for you, Prince Alveron of House Coillmhòr. The Elves call you Gréim Coill. The Death Hand of the Forest. Home sunderer. Forest burner. God-killer.”

Alveron…blinked, and his grip on his staff loosened. “What…?”

Anguis immediately struck at the weakness. “Why wouldn’t they?” He crooned, slowly standing up from the table and approaching Alveron. He almost looked like he was stalking the elderly Elf, as he began to circle around him. “For millennia have you hounded them. Every few centuries, you have appeared out of the gloom to strike at their very god. With unrelenting force, you slay him, sending his soul careening, screaming back to the safety of his Mantle, until he could incarnate once more. Then, you would repeat this process, over and over and over again. For thousands of years, you did this thing. What brutality, what hatred you must have possessed for your forebear, to commit to such a course of action.”

“No…” Alveron whispered, shaking his head. “I did not…I could not hate him. All I did, I did for my people. He was mad, and his madness has crouched upon the soul of my people since the War in Heaven. It was the only thing I could do to free them.”

I didn’t care for the desperation I heard in his voice. I made to take a step forward and stop this farce, but when I did...

I felt strong, heavy hands fall on either one of my shoulders, stopping me. Blinking in shock, I turned in place to see that two of Elysael’s Knights were now standing behind me. It was their hands stopping me. The Dwarves had to reach relatively high on my frame to do so, but that didn’t diminish the strength of their grip. Stunned, I shifted my eyes to the Florens Prince for a reason, but she wasn’t even looking at me.

Instead, she was watching the confrontation with sharp eyes.

“Was it, though?” Anguis hissed from behind the Paragon, still circling. “Did you ever really try to cure Fynneas of his madness, My Prince? Did you ever attempt to purge the poor man of his malady? To cure him? Or did you jump straight to violence? Was it resentment, I wonder? Did the long years he spent on the battlefront, fighting for your people in the War in Heaven and away from your family, engender distaste in your heart for your forebear?”

“No…” Alveron said weakly, no leaning heavily on his staff. I couldn’t help but notice he was shaking. “There was nothing to be done! He consumed his very family! Our family! Grandfather…grandfather ate his own son. There was…there was nothing left of him to save…”

Except, now Anguis was saying there was.

“Ah…” Anguis abruptly stopped in front of Alveron and smiled up at him thinly. “At last, we come to the real reason.”

“Revenge.”

Tears began to stream from Alveron’s tired, ancient, profoundly sad eyes.

“It was all about revenge, wasn’t it, Your Highness?” Anguis said softly. He tsked and shook his head. “What a tragic thing Fynneas was forced to do. No doubt he was under some malediction from the Chaos gods, to drive him to such madness. Perhaps, in that bygone time, he could have been roused much easier from such a state. But you were never interested in such a thing, were you? Instead, you wanted only revenge for the truly tragic end that befell the Lasgachian Royal Family. And thus a millennia long campaign was born in your heart…all for vengeance. How…petty.”

Alveron fell silent, bowing his head. The only sound in the docks was the strangely intense howling of the wind, and the creaking of the docks and ships in the river. The battlements had gone silent.

This was…

“That…” I said quietly, my voice nonetheless carrying easily. “Is the worst possible framing of everything, Lord Savoy.”

Anguis tilted his head to look at me. Not turned.

Tilted it backwards.

He must be seeing me upside down, from that angle. The smile that crept upon his lips instead appeared, to my eyes, as if it were a faint frown. “Perhaps,” He granted, Adam’s apple visibly bobbing. “Perhaps that is so, Marshal. And yet…see how he does not deny it?”

My eyes flickered over to Alveron, still silent.

“Even so,” I rallied, aware of the heavy hands on my shoulders. “Even if there was a partial desire for vengeance in Alveron’s heart, the reason he did it was to free his people from the Mad G-Fynneas’s madness.”

Anguis turned, then, to meet my eyes normally, in a languid movement. “And yet…it wasn’t needed. I have already begun the process of freeing the Elven people from the curse laid upon Fynneas by the Chaos gods. With the help of a new associate, we have excised Fynneas’s corrupted Mantle of Divinity from his poor, tortured body…and cast it aloft!”

In an incredibly dramatic movement, he swept one hand upwards…

To point directly at the sky.

And the frozen, magenta lighting that dominated it.

I think Anguis might have been expecting more shock from me in particular, judging by the faint moue of disappointment on his lips, but he didn’t get it.

Still…

There were plenty of gasps from the onlookers.

Including Elysael.

That…didn’t bode well.

“So you admit it.” My voice cracked sharply through the renewed murmurs. “It was you who brought this calamity upon us. You who facilitated the corruption of Vereden and the death of thousands. You engineered the Skyfall.”

Anguis met my eyes confidently…

And smiled.

“Yes.”
